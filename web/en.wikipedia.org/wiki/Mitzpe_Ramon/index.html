Mitzpe Ramon is a local council in the Negev desert of southern Israel, situated on the northern ridge of the Ramon Crater (Makhtesh Ramon), the world's largest erosion crater, at an elevation of roughly 840 metres above sea level.

History

The town was founded in 1951 as a camp for workers building the road to Eilat, and it developed as one of Israel's "development towns", populated largely by immigrants from North Africa and, later, from the former Soviet Union and elsewhere. The nearby Ramon Airbase and the Israel Defense Forces' training grounds also shaped its growth.

Tourism and astronomy

Mitzpe Ramon is a centre for desert tourism: visitors come for the crater's geology, hiking and cycling trails, the ancient Nabataean Incense Route sites nearby, and desert eco-tourism. Its clear skies and low light pollution have made it a notable destination for stargazing; the area around the town was designated an International Dark Sky Park by the International Dark-Sky Association in 2017, the first such designation in the Middle East. The Wise Observatory of Tel Aviv University is located a few kilometres east of the town.
