Five prime untranslated region

The five prime untranslated region (5′ UTR), also known as the leader sequence, is the region of an mRNA that is directly upstream (5′) of the initiation codon. This region is transcribed from the DNA and present in the mature messenger RNA but is not translated into protein. Though not coding for protein, the 5′ UTR plays a central role in the regulation of translation and hence gene expression.

Structure and function

In eukaryotes the 5′ UTR begins at the transcription start site, just after the 5′ cap, and ends immediately before the AUG start codon. Its length varies widely — typically around 100 to 200 nucleotides in humans. Features within the 5′ UTR that influence translation include:

Secondary structure — stable stem-loops can slow or block the scanning pre-initiation complex.

Upstream open reading frames (uORFs) — short ORFs in the leader can capture ribosomes and repress translation of the main coding sequence; they are central to the regulation of genes such as GCN4 in yeast and ATF4 in mammals.

Internal ribosome entry sites (IRES) — structured elements, common in viral RNAs, that recruit ribosomes internally, bypassing cap-dependent scanning.

Sequence motifs — including the Kozak sequence around the start codon, iron-responsive elements, and binding sites for regulatory proteins and microRNAs.

Prokaryotes

In bacteria, the 5′ UTR contains the Shine–Dalgarno sequence, a purine-rich motif that base-pairs with the 16S rRNA of the small ribosomal subunit to position the ribosome at the start codon.
