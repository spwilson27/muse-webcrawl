Puebla Cathedral, dedicated to the Immaculate Conception, is a Roman Catholic cathedral in the historic centre of Puebla, Mexico. It is considered one of the finest examples of colonial Baroque architecture in the country.

History

Construction began in 1575 to designs associated with Francisco Becerra and Juan de Cigorondo, and the cathedral was consecrated in 1649 under Bishop Juan de Palafox y Mendoza, though work continued afterwards. Its two towers, about 70 metres high, are among the tallest church towers in Mexico.

Interior

The interior contains an ornate main altar designed by Manuel Tolsá, fine choir stalls, and notable paintings and organs. The cathedral forms part of Puebla's UNESCO World Heritage historic centre.
