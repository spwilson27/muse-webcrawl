World's Columbian Exposition

Location: Chicago, Illinois
Dates: May – October 1893
Attendance: About 27 million visits

The World's Columbian Exposition, also known as the Chicago World's Fair of 1893, was a world's fair held in Chicago to celebrate the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus's arrival in the Americas. It profoundly influenced American architecture, urban planning, and popular culture.

The White City

Designed under Daniel Burnham and Frederick Law Olmsted, the fair's neoclassical, white-stuccoed "White City" in Jackson Park dazzled visitors with electric lighting on an unprecedented scale. The Midway Plaisance introduced the first Ferris wheel, built by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr. to rival the Eiffel Tower.

Legacy

The fair inspired the City Beautiful movement and introduced many Americans to products from Juicy Fruit gum to the hamburger's popularization. The Museum of Science and Industry now occupies the former Palace of Fine Arts, the fair's only major building constructed with brick.
