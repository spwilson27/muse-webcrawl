The 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey (2dFGRS) was a major redshift survey of galaxies conducted with the Anglo-Australian Telescope at Siding Spring Observatory between 1997 and 2002. Using the 2dF instrument, which could obtain spectra of up to 400 objects simultaneously across a two-degree field, the survey measured redshifts for more than 220,000 galaxies, at the time the largest such survey ever undertaken.

Results

The survey mapped the large-scale structure of the local universe in two wedge-shaped slices of sky. Its analyses provided precise measurements of the matter density of the universe and evidence for baryon acoustic oscillations, and detected the influence of dark energy in combination with cosmic microwave background data. The 2dF team was led by astronomers including Matthew Colless and John Peacock.

Legacy

The final data release in 2003 made spectra and catalogues publicly available. The survey was succeeded by larger projects such as the Sloan Digital Sky Survey and the 6dF Galaxy Survey of the southern sky.
