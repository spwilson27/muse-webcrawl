FormY = A·Lβ·Kα
AuthorsCharles Cobb, Paul Douglas (1928)
FieldEconomics
Returns to scaleConstant when α + β = 1

In economics, the Cobb–Douglas production function is a particular functional form widely used to represent the technological relationship between the amounts of two or more inputs — typically physical capital (K) and labour (L) — and the amount of output (Y) that can be produced by those inputs:

Y = A · Lβ · Kα

where A represents total factor productivity. The exponents are interpreted as output elasticities; if α + β = 1 the function exhibits constant returns to scale, and α and β also give the shares of output paid to capital and labour under competitive assumptions.

History

The form was proposed by mathematician Charles Cobb and economist (later US senator) Paul Douglas in a 1928 paper fitting US manufacturing data; related ideas had been anticipated by Knut Wicksell and others. Robert Solow used the function in his growth model, and it remains a workhorse of macroeconomics and growth accounting despite criticism of its restrictive substitution properties — the elasticity of substitution is fixed at one.
