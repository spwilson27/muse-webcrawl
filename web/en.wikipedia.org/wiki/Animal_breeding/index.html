Animal breeding

[Image: Champion pedigree livestock at an agricultural show]

Animal breeding is a branch of animal science that addresses the evaluation of the genetic value of livestock and companion animals, and the selection of breeding stock to produce offspring with desired heritable traits. Choosing animals for breeding is commonly based on estimated breeding values (EBVs), records of performance, and pedigree information.

Methods

Techniques include purebred breeding, crossbreeding to exploit hybrid vigour (heterosis), inbreeding and linebreeding to fix traits, and outcrossing to introduce new variation. Modern programmes increasingly use artificial insemination, embryo transfer and genomic selection, in which DNA markers predict an animal's merit before it has offspring.

Breeding goals

In livestock, goals include growth rate, milk yield, feed efficiency, fertility, disease resistance and temperament. In companion animals, breeders select for conformation to breed standards, health and behaviour. Excessive emphasis on a narrow set of traits can reduce genetic diversity and increase inherited disorders, an issue addressed by outcrossing programmes and health-screening schemes.
