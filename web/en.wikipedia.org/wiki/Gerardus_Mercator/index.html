Overview

Gerardus Mercator (1512–1594) was a Flemish geographer and cartographer, the most influential map-maker of the sixteenth century. Born Gerard de Kremer — Mercator is the Latin for 'merchant', as Kremer is in Flemish — he studied at Leuven, made globes and instruments, and in 1552 settled in Duisburg, beyond the reach of the religious persecution he had suffered in the Low Countries.

His 1569 world map introduced the Mercator projection, drawn explicitly 'for the use of navigators'. He also issued the first modern maps of Europe and Britain and revised Ptolemy's maps with a scholar's care.

The Atlas

Mercator spent his last decades on a great cosmography of the whole creation. The map volumes appeared in parts from 1585, and the complete work was published by his son Rumold in 1595, the year after his death, under the title Atlas — the first time the word was used for a book of maps, after the Titan of Greek myth whom Mercator pictured on the title page. The name, and the standard he set for cartography, have lasted ever since.
