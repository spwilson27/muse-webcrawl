Lincoln Logs are an American children's construction toy consisting of notched miniature logs used to build small forts, cabins, and other structures. The toy was invented in 1916 by John Lloyd Wright, son of architect Frank Lloyd Wright, and marketed by the John Lloyd Wright Manufacturing Company of Chicago.

Design

The logs have notches near their ends that allow them to be stacked at right angles, imitating log cabin construction, supplemented by roof slats, chimneys, and other pieces. The name evoked Abraham Lincoln and the log cabin of his childhood, appealing to American pioneer themes.

History

Wright said he was inspired by the interlocking timber foundation of the Imperial Hotel in Tokyo, designed by his father to resist earthquakes. The toy passed through several owners, including Milton Bradley and Hasbro, and in 1999 it was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame. Production of the wooden sets later returned to the United States after a period of overseas manufacture.
