The Battle of Pusan Perimeter (4 August – 18 September 1950) was a large-scale engagement of the Korean War between United Nations Command forces and the North Korean Korean People's Army. After being driven down the peninsula in the war's first weeks, UN and Republic of Korea forces made a final stand around the port of Pusan (Busan), behind a defensive line anchored on the Naktong River. Repeated North Korean offensives failed to break the perimeter, and the battle ended when UN forces broke out following the landings at Inchon.

Background

North Korea invaded South Korea on 25 June 1950. Under-equipped US occupation troops rushed from Japan and South Korean forces were pushed steadily south. By early August, General Walton Walker's Eighth Army had withdrawn behind the Naktong River into a perimeter roughly 140 miles around the port of Pusan, issuing his famous "stand or die" order.

The fighting

Through August and September the North Koreans launched a series of attacks — at the Naktong Bulge, Kyongju, Tabu-dong, Taegu and along the coast — in several places crossing the river and penetrating the line. The defenders, using interior lines, air superiority and growing reinforcements shipped through Pusan, sealed off each penetration. The Great Naktong Offensive of early September came closest to success but ultimately failed with heavy losses.

Aftermath

On 15 September UN forces landed at Inchon deep behind North Korean lines; on 16 September the Eighth Army broke out of the perimeter. The North Korean army around Pusan collapsed, and UN forces advanced rapidly northward.
