Fort-de-France

Fort-de-France is the capital of Martinique, an overseas department of France in the Caribbean. Located on the island’s west coast at a large natural bay, it is Martinique’s largest city and main port, with a population of roughly 75,000 in the commune and more in its urban area.

Fort-de-France
Country: France (Martinique)
Status: Capital and prefecture
Founded as fort: 1638
Former name: Fort-Royal
Population: ~75,000

History

The French built Fort Royal in 1638 overlooking the bay. The town, long overshadowed by Saint-Pierre, became capital after Mount Pelée destroyed Saint-Pierre in 1902. Renamed Fort-de-France in the 19th century, it hosted writer Aimé Césaire, its mayor from 1945 to 2001 and a founder of the Négritude movement.

Sights

Landmarks include Fort Saint-Louis, the Schoelcher Library (designed in Paris and shipped in 1889), Saint-Louis Cathedral with its tall spire, the La Savane park, and statues (the Empress Joséphine statue, decapitated by activists in 1991 and toppled in 2020). Ferries cross the bay to Trois-Îlets.
