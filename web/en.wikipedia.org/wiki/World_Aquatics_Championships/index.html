World Aquatics Championships

The World Aquatics Championships (formerly the FINA World Championships) are the world championships for six aquatic disciplines — swimming, diving, high diving, open water swimming, artistic swimming, and water polo — organised by World Aquatics (formerly FINA). The event is one of the largest global sporting competitions, second only to the Olympic Games in scale for aquatic sports.

History

The first championships were held in Belgrade, Yugoslavia, in 1973. Initially held at irregular intervals of two to five years, from 2001 the championships settled into a biennial cycle in odd-numbered years. The governing body renamed the event from the "FINA World Championships" after FINA rebranded as World Aquatics in 2023.

Host cities

Notable editions include Perth (1991, 1998), Rome (1994, 2009), Barcelona (2003, 2013), Montreal (2005), Melbourne (2007), Shanghai (2011), Kazan (2015), Budapest (2017, 2022), Gwangju (2019), and Fukuoka (2023). The Doha championships of 2024 were the first held in an Olympic year, an unusual schedule caused by COVID-19 postponements.

Medals and records

The United States has historically topped the all-time medal table, followed by China, Russia, and Australia. Michael Phelps holds the record for most World Championship gold medals in swimming (26 golds between 2001 and 2011), and Katie Ledecky is the most decorated female swimmer in the event's history. China has dominated diving, frequently sweeping events.

Disciplines

Swimming is contested in a 50-metre (long course) pool with roughly 40 events. Open water races are held over 5 km, 10 km, and team distances; high diving was added in 2013 with 20 m (women) and 27 m (men) platforms; artistic swimming (formerly synchronised swimming) introduced male competitors and a mixed duet in the 2010s and 2020s; water polo tournaments for men date to 1973 and for women to 1986.
