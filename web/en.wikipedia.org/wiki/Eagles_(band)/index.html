The Eagles are an American rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1971 by Glenn Frey, Don Henley, Bernie Leadon, and Randy Meisner. They are one of the best-selling bands of all time, having sold more than 150 million records worldwide, including 100 million in the United States alone. Their compilation Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975) is the best-selling album in U.S. history, and their studio album Hotel California (1976) is one of the most acclaimed rock albums ever made.

The Eagles pioneered a polished fusion of rock, country, and folk that came to define the Southern California sound of the 1970s. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1998, their first year of eligibility.

History

1971–1975: Formation and early success

Frey and Henley first played together in 1970 as members of Linda Ronstadt's backing band, alongside Leadon and Meisner. With encouragement from Ronstadt and her manager, the four formed their own group and signed to David Geffen's Asylum Records. Their self-titled debut album Eagles (1972) produced the hits "Take It Easy" (co-written by Frey and Jackson Browne), "Witchy Woman", and "Peaceful Easy Feeling".

The follow-up Desperado (1973), a concept album about Old West outlaws, was initially a modest seller but is now regarded as a classic. Guitarist Don Felder joined during the recording of On the Border (1974), which yielded their first number-one single, "Best of My Love". One of These Nights (1975) was a major commercial breakthrough, topping the Billboard 200 and producing three top-five singles.

1975–1980: Hotel California and breakup

Leadon left in 1975, disillusioned with the band's increasingly rock-oriented direction, and was replaced by Joe Walsh. The resulting lineup recorded Hotel California (1976), whose title track, "New Kid in Town", and "Life in the Fast Lane" became signature songs. The album has sold over 42 million copies worldwide and won two Grammy Awards.

Meisner departed in 1977 and was replaced by Timothy B. Schmit. The troubled, protracted sessions for The Long Run (1979) produced the number-one single "Heartache Tonight" but exhausted the band. Tensions boiled over at a concert in Long Beach, California, on July 31, 1980, and the Eagles broke up. Henley famously said the band would reunite "when hell freezes over".

1994–present: Reunions

In 1994 the band reunited for the Hell Freezes Over live album and tour. Felder was dismissed in 2001. The double album Long Road Out of Eden (2007) was their first studio album in 28 years and debuted at number one. After Glenn Frey died in January 2016, the band resumed touring in 2017 with Frey's son Deacon and country artist Vince Gill. From 2019 to 2023 they performed Hotel California in full on an extended tour, and in 2023 they launched the farewell "Long Goodbye" tour.

Musical style and legacy

The Eagles' music blends rock and roll with country and folk influences, characterized by meticulous production and close vocal harmonies. Early songs were written primarily by Frey and Henley, who became one of the most successful songwriting partnerships in rock. The band won six Grammy Awards and had five number-one singles and six number-one albums in the United States. Rolling Stone ranked them among the 100 greatest artists of all time.
