The Blue Line is a border demarcation between Lebanon and Israel published by the United Nations on 7 June 2000 for the purposes of determining whether Israel had fully withdrawn from Lebanon following its military campaign there.

Background

Israel invaded Lebanon in 1978 and again in 1982, occupying a "security zone" in the south until its withdrawal in May 2000. UN Security Council Resolution 425 had called for Israel's withdrawal and the restoration of Lebanon's sovereignty over its territory.

Demarcation

UN cartographers identified a line conforming to the internationally recognised boundaries of Lebanon, based largely on the 1923 Paulet–Newcombe line between the British and French mandates. The line is marked on the ground by blue barrels, giving it its name. Lebanon disputed parts of the line, notably around the Shebaa Farms and the village of Ghajar, and the line has remained a flashpoint, particularly during and after the 2006 Lebanon War and subsequent cross-border exchanges.
