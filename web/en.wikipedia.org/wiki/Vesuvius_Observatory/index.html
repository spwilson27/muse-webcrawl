Vesuvius Observatory

The historic observatory building on the flank of Mount Vesuvius.

The Vesuvius Observatory (Italian: Osservatorio Vesuviano) is an institute of the Italian Istituto Nazionale di Geofisica e Vulcanologia (INGV) responsible for monitoring volcanic activity in the Campania region, including Mount Vesuvius, the Phlegraean Fields (Campi Flegrei), and the island of Ischia. Founded in 1841 by Ferdinand II of the Two Sicilies, it is the oldest volcanological observatory in the world.

History

The observatory building was inaugurated in 1845, sited on the western slope of Vesuvius between Ercolano and Torre del Greco. Its first director was the physicist Macedonio Melloni. The volcanologist Giuseppe Mercalli, famous for his intensity scale for earthquakes, served as director from 1911. During the 1944 eruption of Vesuvius the observatory was used by Allied forces, and its then-director Giuseppe Imbò documented the event. In 1970 a new operational centre was built in Naples, now the main monitoring headquarters, while the historic building hosts a museum and exhibitions.

Monitoring

The observatory operates dense networks of seismometers, GPS and tiltmeters for ground deformation, gravimeters, and geochemical sensors sampling fumarole gases and groundwater. A 24-hour surveillance room in Naples follows the activity of the Neapolitan volcanoes, which sit within one of Europe's most densely populated volcanic regions — the Vesuvius "red zone" alone is home to some 700,000 people covered by evacuation planning.
