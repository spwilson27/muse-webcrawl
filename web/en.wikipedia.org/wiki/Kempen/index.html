Kempen is a town in the district of Viersen, in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, near the Dutch border and the Lower Rhine cities of Krefeld and Venlo. With around 35,000 inhabitants, it is known for its well-preserved medieval old town.

History

Kempen was first mentioned in documents in the 9th century and received town rights in the 13th century under the Archbishops of Cologne, whose territory it belonged to. The town was fortified and prospered on cloth production and trade. Its most famous son is the cleric and writer Thomas à Kempis (c. 1380–1471), author of The Imitation of Christ, who was born here as Thomas Hemerken.

Sights

Landmarks include the provost church of St. Mariae Geburt, the remains of the Kurkölnische Landesburg (the electors' castle), the Kuhtor gate and many burgher houses in the old town centre. The town also lends its name to the wider Kempenland region (De Kempen) straddling the Dutch–Belgian–German borderlands, though that historic region lies mainly in Belgium and the Netherlands.
