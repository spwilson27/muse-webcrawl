Pall Mall, in the St James's district of Westminster.

Pall Mall is a street in the St James's area of the City of Westminster, Central London. It connects St James's Street to Trafalgar Square and is a section of the regional A4 road. The street's name is derived from "pall-mall", a ball game played there during the 17th century, which in turn is derived from the Italian pallamaglio, literally "ball mallet".

The area was built up during the reign of Charles II and became known for high-class shopping and for the residences of the aristocracy. In the 18th century, Pall Mall was a centre for the booksellers and publishers, while in the 19th century it became famous for its gentlemen's clubs, several of which — including the Athenaeum, the Reform Club, and the Travellers Club — still stand along the street in buildings of considerable architectural distinction. It also hosted the Royal Academy's early exhibitions and Schomberg House, once home to the painter Thomas Gainsborough.

History

Although the road has been a thoroughfare since Saxon times, the name Pall Mall dates to the 17th century, when an earlier ground for playing the mallet-and-ball game of pall-mall lay nearby in St James's Field. After the game moved elsewhere, the street was laid out in 1661 and quickly developed into a fashionable address. In 1701 it became one of the first streets in London to be lit by gas.

Gentlemen's clubs

Pall Mall is best known for the gentlemen's clubs founded in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The United Service Club, the Athenaeum (founded 1824 and housed in a neoclassical building by Decimus Burton), the Travellers Club (founded 1819, in a palazzo-style building by Charles Barry), the Reform Club (founded 1836, also by Barry, and modelled after Italian Renaissance palaces), the Army and Navy Club, and the Royal Automobile Club all line or have lined the street.

Other notable buildings

Other landmarks include Schomberg House, Marlborough House — built for Sarah Churchill, Duchess of Marlborough, and later a royal residence — and the former premises of the War Office. The street also forms part of the ceremonial route near St James's Palace.
