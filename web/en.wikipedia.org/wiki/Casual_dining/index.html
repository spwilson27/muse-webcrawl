Casual dining

Casual dining restaurants offer table service in a relaxed setting.

Casual dining is a category of restaurant that serves moderately priced food in a relaxed atmosphere with table service. It sits between fast food and fine dining: meals are ordered from a menu and served by wait staff, but dress codes are informal, décor is often themed, and alcohol—including a full bar in many North American examples—is commonly served.

Characteristics

Casual dining establishments typically offer broad menus of familiar dishes, family-friendly seating, and per-person prices above fast-food levels but below upscale restaurants. In the United States the segment grew rapidly from the 1960s and 1970s with chains such as TGI Fridays, Applebee’s, Chili’s, and Olive Garden, many of which became fixtures of suburban shopping districts.

Related segments

The category overlaps with “family dining,” which often omits a bar, and contrasts with fast casual, where customers usually order at a counter but food quality and prices are higher than fast food. Since the 2000s, casual dining chains have faced competition from fast casual concepts and independent restaurants, prompting menu changes and remodels.
