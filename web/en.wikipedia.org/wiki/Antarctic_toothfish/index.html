Antarctic toothfish

The Antarctic toothfish (Dissostichus mawsoni), also known as the Antarctic cod, is a large, black or brown fish found in very cold (subzero) waters of the Southern Ocean. It is mainly caught in the Ross Sea. Together with the Patagonian toothfish it is sold commercially as "Chilean sea bass".

Biology

The species can exceed 2 metres in length and 130 kg in weight and live for nearly 50 years. Like other Antarctic notothenioids it produces antifreeze glycoproteins that prevent ice crystals forming in its blood. It is a top predator, feeding on fish and squid, and is itself preyed upon by Weddell seals, orcas and sperm whales.

Fishery

The longline fishery for Antarctic toothfish, regulated by CCAMLR (the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources), is valuable and controversial; the Ross Sea fishery has been certified by the Marine Stewardship Council despite objections from some scientists and conservation groups concerned about data gaps and bycatch.
