Life

James Burbage (c. 1531 – 2 February 1597) was an English actor and theatre impresario, a joiner by trade who became leader of Leicester's Men, one of the leading playing companies of Elizabethan London. In partnership with his brother-in-law John Brayne, a grocer, Burbage conceived the first successful purpose-built public playhouse in England since Roman times.

The Theatre

In 1576 Burbage leased land in Shoreditch, outside the City of London's jurisdiction, and built The Theatre — the venue whose name became the generic word for playhouses. A polygonal timber amphitheatre of perhaps 20 sides, it hosted Burbage's company and, from 1594, the Lord Chamberlain's Men, the company of William Shakespeare, with Burbage's son Richard as its leading tragedian. When the ground lease expired in 1597, the company famously dismantled The Theatre one winter night and carried its timbers across the Thames to build the Globe in 1599.

Blackfriars

In 1596 Burbage bought and converted part of the former Blackfriars monastery into an indoor playhouse; prevented by residents' petition from using it for adult playing, he leased it to a children's company. After his death it passed to his sons and in 1608 became the King's Men's winter theatre — the venue for the late romances of Shakespeare and the tragedies of Webster.

Legacy

Burbage's entrepreneurial vision — permanent playhouses owned by sharers in the acting company itself — created the commercial structure within which Shakespeare's career was made. His son Richard Burbage was the first Hamlet, Othello, Lear, and Richard III; his other son Cuthbert managed the family theatres.
