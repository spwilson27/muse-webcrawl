Meier-Gorlin syndrome

Meier-Gorlin syndrome (MGS), also known as ear, patella, short stature syndrome, is a rare autosomal recessive genetic disorder characterised by the triad of microtia (small ears), absent or hypoplastic patellae (kneecaps), and severe prenatal and postnatal growth retardation resulting in short stature. It is a form of primordial dwarfism.

Genetics

Meier-Gorlin syndrome is caused by biallelic mutations in genes encoding components of the DNA replication machinery, including ORC1, ORC4, ORC6, CDT1 and CDC6 — proteins involved in the origin recognition complex and replication licensing — as well as CDC45, GMNN and other replication-associated genes identified later. These defects impair cell proliferation, which is thought to underlie the growth failure.

Clinical features

In addition to the classic triad, features may include microcephaly, distinctive facial appearance, small mouth with full lips, congenital lung emphysema, feeding difficulties in infancy, and in females, underdevelopment of mammary glands. Intelligence is usually normal. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary.
