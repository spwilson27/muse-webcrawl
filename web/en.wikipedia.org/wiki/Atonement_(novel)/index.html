Atonement (novel)

Atonement is a 2001 British metafictional novel by Ian McEwan. Set in three time periods—1935 England, the Second World War, and present-day England—it tells the story of Briony Tallis, a thirteen-year-old girl whose misunderstanding of an act she witnesses, and the false accusation that follows from it, destroys the lives of her older sister Cecilia and Cecilia's lover Robbie Turner. Briony spends the rest of her life, and her career as a novelist, attempting to atone for her crime.

Author: Ian McEwan

Country: United Kingdom

Language: English

Genre: Metafiction, literary fiction

Published: 2001 (Jonathan Cape)

Pages: 371

Plot summary

In the summer of 1935, at the Tallis family estate, aspiring writer Briony misinterprets a charged encounter between Cecilia and Robbie, the housekeeper's Cambridge-educated son, and later, after her cousin Lola is assaulted in the grounds, falsely testifies that Robbie was the attacker. Robbie is imprisoned, then released to fight in France; the second part follows his retreat to Dunkirk in 1940. The third part follows the older Briony, training as a nurse in wartime London, confronting her guilt. A final section, set in 1999, reveals that the reunion of Cecilia and Robbie described earlier was Briony's invention: in reality both died in 1940, and her novel is her attempt at atonement.

Reception

The novel was widely acclaimed, shortlisted for the Booker Prize in 2001, and won several other awards; it frequently appears on lists of the greatest novels of the century. It is notable for its metafictional structure and its pastiche of literary styles. A 2007 film adaptation directed by Joe Wright, starring Keira Knightley and James McAvoy, was also critically successful.
