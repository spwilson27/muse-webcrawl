Counter-battery fire

[Image: Counter-battery fire]

Counter-battery fire is battlefield tactics employed to defeat the enemy's indirect fire elements (multiple rocket launchers, artillery and mortars). The aim is to neutralise or destroy enemy artillery systems, as well as their supporting logistics, communications and observation assets.

History

Counter-battery techniques developed rapidly during the First World War, when indirect artillery fire became dominant. Methods for locating enemy guns included sound ranging, flash spotting, and aerial observation, coordinated through dedicated counter-battery staffs and plotted on counter-battery charts.

Modern methods

Modern counter-battery operations rely heavily on weapon-locating radar (counter-battery radar), which detects incoming projectiles in flight and computes their point of origin from the trajectory within seconds. This information is passed through digital fire-control systems to friendly artillery, which can fire before the enemy battery displaces ("shoot-and-scoot"). Unmanned aerial vehicles and acoustic sensors supplement radar.

Because modern self-propelled guns can fire and move within minutes, counter-battery duels are highly time-sensitive. Counter-battery fire was a major feature of the Russo-Ukrainian War, where radar, drones and long-range rocket artillery were extensively used by both sides.
