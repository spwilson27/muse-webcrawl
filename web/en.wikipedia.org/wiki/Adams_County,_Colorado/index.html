Adams County, Colorado

Adams County is a county located in the U.S. state of Colorado. As of the 2020 census, its population was about 519,000, making it one of the most populous counties in the state. The county seat is Brighton. Adams County lies immediately northeast of Denver and forms part of the Denver–Aurora–Lakewood metropolitan statistical area. It is named for Alva Adams, a former governor of Colorado.

History

Adams County was created by the Colorado General Assembly in 1901–1902 from the northern portion of Arapahoe County, with voters approving the split and choosing Brighton as county seat in 1902. The county grew with Denver's suburban expansion in the later 20th century, absorbing farmland into suburbs such as Thornton, Westminster (partly), Northglenn, Commerce City and Federal Heights.

Geography

The county covers about 1,200 square miles on the high plains east of the Front Range, drained by the South Platte River and its tributaries. Denver International Airport lies east of the county, adjoining it, and the Rocky Mountain Arsenal National Wildlife Refuge, a former chemical weapons site turned wildlife refuge, sits largely within the county near Commerce City.

Communities

Incorporated places wholly or partly in Adams County include Aurora, Brighton, Commerce City, Federal Heights, Northglenn, Thornton and parts of Arvada, Bennett, Lochbuie, Strasburg and Westminster. The county government is led by an elected Board of County Commissioners.
