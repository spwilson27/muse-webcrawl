Caterina Cornaro (1454–1510) was the last monarch of the Kingdom of Cyprus, reigning from 1474 until her forced abdication in 1489, after which Cyprus passed under the direct rule of the Republic of Venice. A member of the wealthy Venetian Cornaro family, she was married by proxy to King James II of Cyprus in 1468, at the age of fourteen, as part of Venice's strategy to extend its influence over the island.

Reign

James II died in 1473, shortly before the birth of their son, James III, who himself died in infancy in 1474. Caterina was then proclaimed queen, but real power lay with Venetian counsellors and the island was effectively a Venetian protectorate. A revolt in her favour by Catalan nobles was suppressed. Increasingly sidelined, she was compelled to abdicate on 26 February 1489 and to hand Cyprus over to Venice — an annexation of lasting strategic and commercial importance to the republic.

Later life

In compensation, Venice granted her the town and lordship of Asolo, where she held a small court that became a celebrated centre of Renaissance culture; the philosopher Pietro Bembo set his dialogue on love, Gli Asolani, at her court. She died in Venice in 1510.

Legacy

Cornaro's story inspired numerous works of art, including operas by Donizetti (Caterina Cornaro, 1844) and Fromental Halévy, and Gentile Bellini's portrait of her survives in Budapest.
