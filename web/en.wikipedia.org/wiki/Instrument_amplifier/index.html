Instrument amplifier

An instrument amplifier is an electronic device that converts the often barely audible or purely electronic signal produced by an electric or electro-acoustic instrument into a louder audio signal via a loudspeaker. Instrument amplifiers are designed for use with instruments such as the electric guitar, electric bass, electric piano, synthesizers, and drum machines.

Types

Instrument amplifiers commonly combine a preamplifier, tone controls, and a power amplifier in a single unit with one or more loudspeakers (a "combo" amplifier), or are sold as separate amplifier "heads" and speaker cabinets. Guitar amplifiers are often designed to add pleasant distortion or "overdrive" when driven hard, whereas keyboard and acoustic instrument amplifiers aim for a cleaner, full-range sound.

History

Instrument amplifiers emerged in the 1930s as Hawaiian and jazz guitarists sought more volume. Companies such as Rickenbacker, Gibson, and later Fender developed influential designs, first using vacuum tubes and later solid-state and digital modeling technology.

Use

Amplifiers are used on stage, in recording studios, and for practice; small practice amplifiers may be only a few watts, while concert systems can reach several hundred watts. Many modern amplifiers include built-in effects such as reverb and chorus.
