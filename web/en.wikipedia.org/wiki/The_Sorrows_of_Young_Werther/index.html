The Sorrows of Young Werther

Author: Johann Wolfgang von Goethe
Published: 1774

The Sorrows of Young Werther (German: Die Leiden des jungen Werthers) is an epistolary novel by Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, published in 1774. Written in a burst of inspiration when Goethe was 24, it made him famous across Europe and became a defining work of the Sturm und Drang movement.

Plot

Told mostly through letters from Werther to his friend Wilhelm, the novel follows a sensitive young artist who moves to the village of Wahlheim and falls hopelessly in love with Charlotte ("Lotte"), who is engaged to — and then marries — the respectable Albert. Unable to renounce his passion or find a place in bourgeois society, and humiliated in an aristocratic gathering, Werther descends into despair and shoots himself with pistols borrowed from Albert.

Werther fever and the "Werther effect"

The novel sparked an international sensation: young men dressed in Werther's blue frock coat and yellow waistcoat, merchandise was sold, and reports of imitative suicides led some authorities to ban the book — the phenomenon later studied as the "Werther effect" in suicide research. Napoleon claimed to have read the novel seven times. Partly autobiographical — Goethe drew on his love for Charlotte Buff and the suicide of his acquaintance Karl Wilhelm Jerusalem — the book also launched the psychological novel of sentiment.
