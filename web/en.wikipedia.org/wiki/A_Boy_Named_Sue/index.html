A Boy Named Sue

"A Boy Named Sue" is a song written by humorist, children's author and poet Shel Silverstein and made famous by Johnny Cash. Cash recorded the song live in concert on 24 February 1969 at San Quentin State Prison for his At San Quentin album. The recording became one of Cash's biggest hits, reaching number one on the US country chart, number two on the Billboard Hot 100, and winning the Grammy Award for Best Male Country Vocal Performance.

Content

The song tells the comic, bitter story of a boy given a girl's name by his father, who then abandoned the family. Growing up ridiculed and forced to fight constantly, the boy vows to kill the man who gave him "that awful name". He finally finds his father in a bar in Gatlinburg, Tennessee, and after a brutal brawl the father explains that he named him Sue so he would grow up tough in a hard world, knowing he would not be there to raise him. The son, reconciled, resolves that if he ever has a son of his own, he will name him "Bill or George, anything but Sue".

Background

Silverstein had performed the song himself, and Cash learned it shortly before the San Quentin concert; his wife June Carter had encouraged him to include it. The title was partly inspired by Jean Shepherd, the humorist, who was named Jean and often joked about growing up with a girl's name. The live recording includes a censored expletive, bleeped on the album's single release.
