Saltoro Mountains

The Saltoro Mountains are a subrange of the Karakoram, lying southeast of the Siachen Glacier in the region administered by India near the Line of Control with Pakistan. The range runs along the Saltoro Ridge, which forms the watershed between the Siachen Glacier system to the east and the valleys draining westward. Its highest peaks include Saltoro Kangri (7,742 m), one of the highest summits in the Karakoram, as well as K12 (7,428 m), Ghent Kangri and Sherpi Kangri.

Strategic significance

The Saltoro Ridge dominates the Siachen Glacier and has been militarised since 1984, when India launched Operation Meghdoot to occupy the glacier and the main passes of the ridge—Bilafond La, Sia La and Gyong La—before Pakistani forces could do so. The resulting Siachen conflict made the area the highest battlefield in the world, with troops deployed above 6,000 metres, where extreme cold and altitude have caused more casualties than combat. The Actual Ground Position Line (AGPL) runs along the ridge; its alignment beyond the terminus point NJ9842 was left undefined by the 1972 Simla Agreement, the root of the dispute.

Climbing

Saltoro Kangri was first climbed in 1962 by a joint Japanese–Pakistani expedition approaching from the west. Mountaineering in the range remains heavily restricted by the military presence on both sides.
