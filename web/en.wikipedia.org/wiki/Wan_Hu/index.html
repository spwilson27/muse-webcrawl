Wan Hu

Wan Hu (also romanised as Wan-Hu or Wan Hoo) is a legendary Chinese figure said to have attempted to fly to space using a chair festooned with rockets. According to the story, around the end of the Ming dynasty (or, in some tellings, as early as about 2000 BC), Wan Hu attached 47 gunpowder rockets and two kites to a chair, sat in it holding large kites, and ordered servants to light the fuses. The rockets exploded, and when the smoke cleared, neither Wan Hu nor the chair could be found.

Origin and evidence

No contemporary Chinese source for the story has been found. The tale entered Western awareness in the 20th century through rocketry popular histories, such as Herbert S. Zim's Rockets and Jets (1945), which dated the event to about 1500 and named Wan Hu a Chinese official. Historians treat the account as legend, though it is often retold as an early (if fatal) experiment in rocket propulsion.

Legacy

A crater on the far side of the Moon, Wan-Hoo, is named after him. The story is a staple of popular spaceflight history and has featured in television programmes and museum exhibits as a symbol of early human ambition to fly into space.
