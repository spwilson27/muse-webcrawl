Arne Dekke Eide Næss (27 January 1912 – 12 January 2009) was a Norwegian philosopher and mountaineer who coined the term "deep ecology" and was an influential figure in the environmental movement of the late 20th century.

Academic career

Næss became Norway's youngest full professor when he was appointed professor of philosophy at the University of Oslo in 1939, a chair he held until 1970. He studied in Vienna in the 1930s and was influenced by the Vienna Circle, though his work on interpretation and argumentation — including his textbook on argumentation widely used in Norwegian schools — developed an empirical semantics that examined how ordinary speakers actually use words. After the Second World War he led a UNESCO project on the ideological bases of political disagreement.

Deep ecology

In a 1973 article, "The Shallow and the Deep, Long-Range Ecology Movement", Næss distinguished mainstream environmental reform ("shallow ecology") from a deeper questioning of purposes and values. He called his own philosophy "ecosophy T", after his mountain hut Tvergastein. The eight-point deep ecology platform, formulated with George Sessions in 1984, affirms the intrinsic value of all living beings and calls for substantial changes in human economies and ideologies.

Mountaineering

An avid climber from youth, Næss led the first expedition to reach the summit of Tirich Mir (7,708 m) in Pakistan in 1950, the highest mountain in the Hindu Kush.
