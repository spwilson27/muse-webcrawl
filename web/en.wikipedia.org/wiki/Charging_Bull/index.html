Charging Bull

Charging Bull, sometimes called the Wall Street Bull or the Bowling Green Bull, is a bronze sculpture by Italian artist Arturo Di Modica that stands at Bowling Green park in the Financial District of Manhattan, New York City. The 7,100-pound (3,200 kg) bull, poised as if charging, has become an internationally recognised symbol of Wall Street and of financial optimism and prosperity.

The bull, installed as guerrilla art in 1989, now draws throngs of visitors.

History

Di Modica spent some $350,000 of his own money creating the bull as a symbol of American resilience after the 1987 stock market crash. In the early hours of December 15, 1989, he and friends trucked the sculpture to Broad Street and installed it without permission in front of the New York Stock Exchange, beneath the exchange's Christmas tree. Police seized the sculpture that day, but public demand led the city to reinstall it at nearby Bowling Green, where it remains.

Symbolism and tourism

The bull represents aggressive financial optimism — a "bull market." It is one of New York's most photographed sculptures; visitors traditionally rub its horns, nose and testicles for luck. Di Modica retained copyright in the work and objected to its commercial use and to some later interventions, including the temporary installation of Fearless Girl facing it in 2017, which he argued altered its meaning; Fearless Girl was later moved to a site facing the Stock Exchange.
