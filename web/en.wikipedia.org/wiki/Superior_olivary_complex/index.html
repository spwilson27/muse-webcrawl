Superior olivary complex

The superior olivary complex lies in the caudal pons and is the first site of binaural convergence in the auditory pathway.

The superior olivary complex (SOC) or superior olive is a collection of brainstem nuclei in the pons that is part of the auditory system. It is the first structure in the ascending auditory pathway to receive input from both ears, and it is therefore central to sound localisation — the ability to determine the direction of a sound source in the horizontal plane.

Nuclei

The complex consists of several nuclei, the most prominent being:

  • Medial superior olive (MSO) — compares the arrival times of sounds at the two ears (interaural time differences) and is especially developed in animals that hear low frequencies
  • Lateral superior olive (LSO) — compares sound intensities between the ears (interaural level differences), receiving excitatory input from the ipsilateral cochlear nucleus and inhibitory input from the contralateral side via the medial nucleus of the trapezoid body
  • Medial nucleus of the trapezoid body (MNTB) — provides precisely timed inhibitory input, converting excitation from the globular bushy cells of the cochlear nucleus through the large calyx of Held synapse
  • Periolivary nuclei — include the source of the olivocochlear bundle, which projects back to the cochlea and modulates the activity of hair cells

Function

By comparing interaural time and level cues, the SOC computes the azimuth of a sound source. Its output ascends through the lateral lemniscus to the inferior colliculus. The olivocochlear efferent system arising in and around the complex is thought to improve signal detection in noise and protect the ear from acoustic injury.
