The pacification of Wujek (Polish: pacyfikacja kopalni Wujek) was the violent suppression, on 16 December 1981, of a strike at the Wujek hard coal mine in Katowice, Poland, by units of the ZOMO motorised police, supported by the army, during the first days of martial law. Nine miners were shot dead and many more were wounded. It was the bloodiest single episode of the martial-law crackdown on the Solidarity movement.

Background

Martial law was declared in Poland on 13 December 1981 by the government of General Wojciech Jaruzelski, aiming to crush the independent trade union Solidarity. Thousands of activists were interned overnight. Miners at Wujek, in the Silesian coalfield, came out on strike on 14 December, demanding the release of interned union activists — including the local Solidarity chairman Jan Ludwiczak, who had been detained after being beaten by militiamen — and the lifting of martial law. The miners fortified the pit, arming themselves with tools and makeshift weapons, and were supported by crowds of local residents.

The assault

On the morning of 16 December, security forces surrounded the mine. Talks failed, and shortly before noon ZOMO units, backed by tanks and armoured vehicles, moved in. Barricades were breached with armoured vehicles; water cannon, tear gas, and concussion grenades were used. When a special ZOMO platoon came under attack with tools and homemade weapons in the confined mine yard, its members opened fire with submachine guns. Nine miners were killed and more than twenty wounded; a number of police officers were also injured, some seriously.

The victims

The nine miners killed were Joachim Józef Gnida, Józef Krzysztof Giza, Ryszard Józef Gzik, Bogusław Kopczak, Andrzej Pełka, Zbigniew Wilk, Zenon Zając, Jan Stawisiński, and Joachim Pilarek. They became enduring symbols of resistance to the communist regime; the youngest was nineteen years old.

Aftermath and trials

The strike at Wujek was broken, but strikes continued at other Silesian mines for days — at the nearby Ziemowit mine, miners stayed down until 24 December, and underground protest at Piast lasted until 28 December. After the fall of communism in 1989, the Wujek shootings were repeatedly investigated. Trials of the ZOMO officers proved difficult: proceedings were twice discontinued, and only in 2008 did a court convict members of the special platoon, sentencing its commander and several subordinates to prison terms; the verdicts were later partly revisited on appeal. A memorial and museum now stand at the mine, and 16 December is commemorated annually in Katowice.
