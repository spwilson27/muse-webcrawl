Little Dorrit

Little Dorrit is a novel by Charles Dickens, first published in serial form between December 1855 and June 1857 and in book form in 1857. A satire on the shortcomings of government and society, and on the Marshalsea debtors' prison, it is one of Dickens's most sombre major works, organised in two books: "Poverty" and "Riches".

Plot summary

Amy Dorrit — "Little Dorrit" — was born in the Marshalsea Prison for debt in Southwark, London, where her father, William Dorrit, has been imprisoned for so long that he is known as the "Father of the Marshalsea". Amy works as a seamstress for the formidable Mrs Clennam. Mrs Clennam's son Arthur, returning to England after twenty years in China, is drawn to Amy and begins to investigate his mother's business affairs and the Dorrit family's history, suspecting a past wrong.

A codicil to a will reveals that William Dorrit is heir to a fortune; the family is released and becomes rich, travels in Europe, and — except for Amy — is corrupted by wealth and the desire to erase its prison past. Meanwhile Arthur's fortunes collapse through the fraudulent banking scheme of the financier Mr Merdle, and Arthur himself is imprisoned for debt in the same Marshalsea. Amy nurses him; long-buried secrets about the Clennam and Dorrit families emerge; and after her father's death Amy marries Arthur, content with a modest life.

Themes and background

Dickens's father, John Dickens, had been imprisoned in the Marshalsea in 1824, when Charles was twelve — an experience that shadowed the novelist's life and work. The prison itself had closed in 1842, before the novel was written. The book satirises the labyrinthine government bureaucracy embodied in the fictional Circumlocution Office, whose guiding principle is "How Not To Do It", and the speculative mania represented by Merdle, modelled in part on the fraudulent banker John Sadleir. The novel explores imprisonment in many forms — literal, social, psychological and self-imposed.

Principal characters

  • Amy Dorrit — the selfless heroine
  • William Dorrit — her vain, broken father
  • Arthur Clennam — the returning merchant who loves her
  • Mrs Clennam — Arthur's rigid, embittered mother
  • Mr Merdle — the celebrated financier and fraud
  • Flora Finching — Arthur's faded first love
  • Mr Pancks — the rent-collector who solves the Dorrit inheritance
  • John Chivery — the turnkey's son who loves Amy

Adaptations

Notable adaptations include the 1987 film by Christine Edzard, with Derek Jacobi and Alec Guinness, and the 2008 BBC television serial adapted by Andrew Davies, starring Claire Foy as Amy, Matthew Macfadyen as Arthur and Tom Courtenay as William Dorrit.
