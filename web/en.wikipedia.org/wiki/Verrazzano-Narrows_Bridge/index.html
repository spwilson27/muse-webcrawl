Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge

The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge is a double-decked suspension bridge connecting the New York City boroughs of Staten Island and Brooklyn. It spans the Narrows, the tidal strait linking Upper New York Bay with Lower New York Bay and the Atlantic Ocean, and is the gateway for ships entering the Port of New York and New Jersey.

Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge
Main span: 4,260 ft (1,298 m)
Opened: 1964
Designer: Othmar Ammann

History

Designed by Othmar Ammann and built under Robert Moses's Triborough Bridge and Tunnel Authority, construction began in 1959; the upper deck opened in November 1964 and the lower deck in 1969. Its 4,260-foot main span made it the longest suspension bridge in the world until the Humber Bridge surpassed it in 1981; it remains the longest in the Americas. The towers are so far apart that their tops are about 1⅝ inches farther apart than their bases because of the curvature of the Earth.

Name

The bridge is named for Giovanni da Verrazzano, the first European explorer to enter New York Harbor, in 1524. A long-running campaign to correct the spelling of his name (with two z's) succeeded in 2018 when New York State officially renamed it from "Verrazano-Narrows" to "Verrazzano-Narrows".

Use

The bridge carries Interstate 278 and is the starting point of the New York City Marathon each November.
