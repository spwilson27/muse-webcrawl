Prefecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A prefecture (from the Latin praefectura) is an administrative jurisdiction traditionally governed by an appointed prefect. The word derives from the office of prefect in the Roman Empire, and in modern usage designates a first- or second-level administrative division in a number of countries.

Examples

  • Japan — the 47 prefectures (todōfuken) are the country's primary subnational jurisdictions, each with an elected governor and assembly; they include the metropolis of Tokyo, the urban prefectures of Osaka and Kyoto, and the territory of Hokkaido.
  • France — a préfecture is both the office of the prefect and, by extension, the chief town of a department where the prefect sits; regional prefectures serve the regions.
  • China — prefecture-level cities and prefectures form the second level of administration below the provinces, a usage descending from the prefectures of imperial China.
  • Greece — prefectures (nomoi) were the second-level divisions until the Kallikratis reform of 2011 replaced them with regional units.
  • Mongolia and others — some translations render aimag and similar units as prefectures; several African states, including the Central African Republic, also use prefectures.

History

Late Roman administration grouped provinces into dioceses under vicars, supervised by the praetorian prefectures of the East, Illyricum, Italy, and Gaul — among the largest territorial units of the ancient world and the source of the modern term.
