New York Fashion Week (NYFW) is a semi-annual series of events held in February and September in New York City at which international fashion collections are shown to buyers, the press, and the public. It is one of the four major fashion weeks in the world, collectively known as the "Big Four," together with those of Paris, London, and Milan.

History

The first New York Fashion Week was created in 1943 by fashion publicist Eleanor Lambert as "Press Week," designed to draw attention to American designers during the Second World War, when the fashion press could not travel to Paris. The event's success helped establish New York as a fashion capital in its own right.

In 1993 the Council of Fashion Designers of America centralized the shows in Bryant Park, where the famous white tents hosted the event (sponsored as Mercedes-Benz Fashion Week) until 2010, when the shows moved to Lincoln Center. Since 2015 the official shows have been staged at venues in downtown Manhattan, including Spring Studios in Tribeca, while many designers present at locations across the city.

Schedule and significance

The February shows present autumn/winter collections and the September shows spring/summer collections for the following year. The official calendar, maintained by the CFDA, includes established houses such as Ralph Lauren, Michael Kors, Marc Jacobs, and Calvin Klein alongside emerging designers. New York traditionally opens the month-long circuit of the Big Four fashion weeks.
