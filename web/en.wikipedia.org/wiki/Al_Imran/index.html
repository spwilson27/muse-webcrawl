Al Imran

Al Imran (Arabic: آل عمران, Āl ʿImrān, "Family of Imran") is the third chapter (surah) of the Quran, with 200 verses (ayat). A Medinan surah, it takes its name from the family of Imran — in Quranic usage the family that includes Mary (Maryam), her son Jesus (Isa), and, in tradition, Moses and Aaron's father Amram.

Content

The surah opens with the mysterious letters Alif, Lam, Meem and an affirmation of God's unity and revelation. Its first part addresses the People of the Book (Jews and Christians), recounting the story of the family of Imran: the birth of Mary, her upbringing under Zechariah, the annunciation and miraculous birth of Jesus, and his miracles and disciples — affirming Jesus as a prophet and Messiah while rejecting his divinity. It also contains the famous verse of the Throne's companion themes and a passage (3:7) on clear versus allegorical verses.

Themes and context

The later part of the surah deals with the Muslim community's struggles, especially the Battle of Uhud (625 CE): it consoles the believers after their setback, analyses the causes of the defeat, praises the martyrs, and lays down rules on communal solidarity, charity, pilgrimage and the prohibition of usury. Tradition associates parts of the surah with the visit of a Christian delegation from Najran to Medina and the proposed mubahala (mutual imprecation), which the delegation declined.
