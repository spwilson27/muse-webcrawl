OPEC+

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

OPEC+ is a grouping of oil-exporting countries — the members of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) plus a set of non-OPEC producers led by Russia — that coordinate crude-oil production policy to influence prices. It originated in the Declaration of Cooperation of December 2016, when OPEC, then led in practice by Saudi Arabia, agreed with Russia and other producers to cut output in response to the price collapse caused by US shale growth; the arrangement was formalised by a charter in 2019. Angola's departure from OPEC (effective January 2024) reduced OPEC's membership to twelve.

The coalition manages supply through production quotas periodically adjusted (deep cuts in 2020 during the COVID demand collapse; contested increases as conditions changed), monitored against members' actual output, with chronic disputes over compliance and baselines. Its market share (well under half of world output, on the order of 40 per cent) is far below OPEC's 1970s dominance, yet its decisions move prices and geopolitics: the October 2022 cut drew US accusations of siding with Russia after the invasion of Ukraine, which the group rejected as market-based. OPEC+ exemplifies the modern Cartel problem in energy: formal coordination among sovereign producers, beyond antitrust's reach (the recurring US “NOPEC” bills have never been enacted).
