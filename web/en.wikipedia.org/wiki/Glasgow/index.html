Glasgow (Scots: Glesca; Scottish Gaelic: Glaschu) is the most populous city in Scotland and the fourth most populous in the United Kingdom. It stands on the River Clyde in west-central Scotland and forms the centre of the Greater Glasgow conurbation.

History

According to tradition, Glasgow was founded by the Christian missionary Saint Mungo in the 6th century, and its cathedral dates from the medieval period. The University of Glasgow was founded in 1451. After the Union of 1707 Glasgow's merchants prospered in transatlantic trade in tobacco, sugar and cotton, and in the 19th century the city became an industrial powerhouse — "the Second City of the Empire" — above all for shipbuilding on the Clyde, engineering and textiles.

Heavy industry's decline in the later 20th century brought severe economic and social problems, but from the 1980s Glasgow reinvented itself as a centre of culture, commerce, finance and education, hosting the 2014 Commonwealth Games and the COP26 climate summit in 2021.

Culture

Glasgow is known for its Victorian and Edwardian architecture, the works of Charles Rennie Mackintosh, major museums and galleries including Kelvingrove, a celebrated live-music scene, and the football rivalry between Celtic and Rangers — the Old Firm. Its people are called Glaswegians and are noted for a distinctive dialect and humour.
