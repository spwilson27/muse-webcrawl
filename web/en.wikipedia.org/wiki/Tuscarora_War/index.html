CombatantsTuscarora and allies vs. North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia colonists and Native allies

The Tuscarora War was fought in North Carolina from 1711 to 1715 between the Tuscarora people and their allies on one side and European settlers of North Carolina, South Carolina, and Virginia, together with their own Native American allies, on the other. It was one of the most destructive colonial wars in the early American South.

Causes

Tuscarora grievances included encroachment on their lands along the Neuse, Trent, and Pamlico rivers, fraudulent trading practices, and the kidnapping and enslavement of Tuscarora people by colonists. Tensions erupted after Baron Christoph von Graffenried founded the settlement of New Bern at the site of a Tuscarora town in 1710.

Course of the war

On 22 September 1711, Tuscarora warriors under Chief Hancock launched coordinated attacks on settlements, killing over a hundred colonists. North Carolina, weakened by internal division (Cary's Rebellion), relied on South Carolina, which sent expeditions under John Barnwell (1712) and James Moore (1713) composed largely of Yamasee and other Native troops. Moore's force stormed the principal Tuscarora stronghold, Fort Neoheroka, in March 1713, killing or capturing about a thousand people.

Aftermath

A treaty in February 1715 ended major fighting. Many surviving Tuscarora migrated north to New York, where they were adopted by the Iroquois as the Sixth Nation of the Confederacy; those who remained in North Carolina moved to a reservation on the Roanoke River. The war opened eastern North Carolina to settlement and intensified the enslavement of Native people in the region.
