Jurassic

Jurassic Period
Span201.4 ± 0.2 to 145.0 million years ago
Part ofMesozoic Era; preceded by the Triassic, followed by the Cretaceous
Named forThe Jura Mountains (France/Switzerland), where Alexander von Humboldt recognised the limestone formations in 1799; named by Alexandre Brongniart, 1829
SubdivisionsEarly, Middle and Late Jurassic epochs

The Jurassic is a geologic period and stratigraphic system that spanned 56.4 million years from the end of the Triassic Period 201.4 million years ago (Mya) to the beginning of the Cretaceous Period, approximately 145 Mya. It is the middle period of the Mesozoic Era and is named after the Jura Mountains. The Jurassic is famous as the age in which dinosaurs became the dominant land animals, giant sauropods reached their greatest sizes, and the first birds appeared.

Subdivisions

The Jurassic is divided into three epochs — Early (201.4–174.7 Mya), Middle (174.7–161.5 Mya) and Late (161.5–145.0 Mya) — and eleven stages, from the Hettangian to the Tithonian. The boundaries are defined by marine fossil records, above all ammonites, and by international boundary stratotypes (GSSPs).

Geography and climate

During the Jurassic the supercontinent Pangaea continued to break up: the Central Atlantic began opening between North America and Africa, and Gondwana started to fragment in the south. Sea levels rose overall, flooding continental margins with shallow epicontinental seas such as those that covered much of Europe. The world was warm, with no polar ice caps and a strong greenhouse climate; deserts persisted in continental interiors, and lush forests of conifers, cycads and ginkgoes grew at high latitudes.

Life

Following the end-Triassic mass extinction, marine ecosystems recovered and diversified. Ammonites became the characteristic fossils of the period, alongside belemnites, bivalves, brachiopods, crinoids, reef-building corals, ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs and early marine crocodilians. On land, dinosaurs dominated: enormous sauropods such as Diplodocus, Brachiosaurus and Apatosaurus; plated stegosaurs; and large theropod predators like Allosaurus. In the Late Jurassic the Solnhofen limestones of Germany preserved Archaeopteryx, the earliest known bird. Mammals remained small and mostly nocturnal, pterosaurs ruled the skies, and the first flowering plants had yet to appear, though some early angiosperm relatives are debated.

End of the period

The Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary at about 145 Mya is one of the least sharply defined in the Phanerozoic, marked by gradual faunal turnover and falling sea level rather than a mass extinction. The oceans, climate system and ecosystems passed into the Cretaceous with dinosaurs still dominant.
