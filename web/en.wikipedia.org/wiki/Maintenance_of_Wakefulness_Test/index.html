Maintenance of Wakefulness Test

The Maintenance of Wakefulness Test (MWT) is a daytime polysomnographic procedure used in sleep medicine to measure a person's ability to stay awake in a quiet, soporific environment. It is used to assess the severity of excessive daytime sleepiness and to evaluate the effectiveness of treatment, particularly in people whose occupation requires sustained alertness, such as commercial drivers and pilots.

Procedure

The standard protocol consists of four sessions, each up to 40 minutes long, conducted at two-hour intervals across the day. The patient sits in a dimly lit, quiet room, typically reclined in bed or a comfortable chair, and is instructed to try to remain awake without using extraordinary measures such as singing or slapping the face. Electroencephalography and other polysomnographic signals are recorded, and the test ends when sleep onset occurs or after 40 minutes without sleep.

Interpretation

The mean sleep latency across the four trials is the principal measure; a mean latency of 40 minutes is considered normal, while shorter latencies indicate impaired ability to maintain wakefulness. The MWT contrasts with the Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT), in which the patient is instructed to try to fall asleep; the MWT instead measures the capacity to resist sleep, which is more relevant to safety-critical performance and to demonstrating treatment response.
