Johnny Pacheco

Johnny Pacheco (born Juan Pablo Pacheco Knipping; March 25, 1935 – February 15, 2021) was a Dominican musician, arranger, composer, bandleader and record producer. A central figure in the creation of salsa music, he co-founded Fania Records and the Fania All-Stars, and is credited with coining the term "salsa" as a label for the music.

Early life

Pacheco was born in Santiago de los Caballeros, Dominican Republic, and moved with his family to New York City at age eleven. He studied percussion at the Juilliard School and became one of the city's most in-demand flutists, popularising the pachanga dance craze with his charanga orchestra in the early 1960s.

Fania Records

In 1964 Pacheco and lawyer Jerry Masucci founded Fania Records, which became the engine of the salsa boom, signing artists including Celia Cruz, Héctor Lavoe, Willie Colón and Rubén Blades. Pacheco directed the label's supergroup, the Fania All-Stars, whose concerts — including a famous 1971 show at the Cheetah nightclub and a 1974 appearance in Zaire — spread salsa worldwide.

Legacy

Across a career of more than 150 albums as leader or sideman, Pacheco shaped the sound of Afro-Cuban dance music in New York. He was inducted into the International Latin Music Hall of Fame and received a Latin Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award.
