Kami are the spirits worshipped in Japan's Shinto tradition. The word is often translated 'gods' or 'deities', but kami range from great mythological beings such as Amaterasu, the sun goddess, to the spirits of particular mountains, rivers, trees, rocks, ancestors, and even revered persons and natural forces. An old phrase speaks of yaoyorozu no kami — 'eight million kami' — meaning an innumerable host.

Veneration

Kami are enshrined in shrines (jinja) where a sacred object (shintai) houses their presence; worship aims at purity, gratitude, and requests for blessing rather than doctrine or moral command. The syncretic history with Buddhism made many kami also guardians or manifestations of buddhas, a fusion largely (but never completely) undone in the Meiji separation edicts of 1868.
