The states of Mexico are the first-order administrative divisions of the United Mexican States. There are 31 states, together with Mexico City, which is a federal entity with a status comparable to a state. Each state is free and sovereign in its internal government, elects its own governor and state congress, and has its own constitution, subject to the federal constitution of 1917.

List of states

The 31 states, with their capitals, include: Aguascalientes (Aguascalientes), Baja California (Mexicali), Baja California Sur (La Paz), Campeche (San Francisco de Campeche), Chiapas (Tuxtla Gutiérrez), Chihuahua (Chihuahua), Coahuila (Saltillo), Colima (Colima), Durango (Victoria de Durango), Guanajuato (Guanajuato), Guerrero (Chilpancingo), Hidalgo (Pachuca), Jalisco (Guadalajara), México (Toluca), Michoacán (Morelia), Morelos (Cuernavaca), Nayarit (Tepic), Nuevo León (Monterrey), Oaxaca (Oaxaca de Juárez), Puebla (Puebla), Querétaro (Santiago de Querétaro), Quintana Roo (Chetumal), San Luis Potosí (San Luis Potosí), Sinaloa (Culiacán), Sonora (Hermosillo), Tabasco (Villahermosa), Tamaulipas (Ciudad Victoria), Tlaxcala (Tlaxcala de Xicohténcatl), Veracruz (Xalapa), Yucatán (Mérida), and Zacatecas (Zacatecas).

History

The federal structure descends from the Constitution of 1824, which organised the new nation into states and territories. Over the following two centuries borders were repeatedly redrawn: territorial losses to the United States in the 19th century, the elevation of territories such as Baja California (1952) and Quintana Roo and Baja California Sur (both 1974) to statehood, and the 2016 reform that converted the Federal District into Mexico City, an autonomous federal entity. The two most recent states, Baja California Sur and Quintana Roo, joined the federation in 1974.

Government

Each state is headed by a governor elected for a single six-year term and a unicameral state congress. States are subdivided into municipalities (municipios), more than 2,400 in total, each with an elected municipal president and council. Mexico City is divided into 16 boroughs (alcaldías).
