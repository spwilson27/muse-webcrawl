Holden Special Vehicles

Industry: Automotive
Founded: 1987
Headquarters: Clayton, Victoria, Australia
Products: High-performance vehicles

Holden Special Vehicles (HSV) was the officially designated performance vehicle division of Australian manufacturer Holden. Established in 1987 as a joint venture between Holden and Tom Walkinshaw Racing (TWR), HSV produced modified, higher-performance versions of Holden vehicles, principally the Commodore, from its plant in Clayton, Victoria.

History

HSV replaced Holden's earlier performance partner, Peter Brock's HDT organisation, after the controversial "Energy Polarizer" affair of 1987. Its first car, the VL SS Group A SV "Walkinshaw" (1988), was a homologation special for Group A touring car racing, famous for its large body kit. Through the 1990s and 2000s HSV built a range including the Clubsport, Senator, Maloo ute, and the flagship GTS, fitting increasingly powerful V8 engines — Holden's 5.0-litre iron V8, then Chevrolet LS-series engines.

Later models

Notable late models included the supercharged LSA-powered GTS (2013) and the limited-edition GTSR W1 (2017), powered by the 474 kW LS9 supercharged V8 from the Corvette ZR1 — the most powerful production car built in Australia. After Holden ceased local manufacturing in 2017, HSV converted Chevrolet Camaros and Silverado pick-ups to right-hand drive under the GMSV banner before the HSV operation wound down around 2020.
