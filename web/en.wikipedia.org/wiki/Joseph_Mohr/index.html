Joseph Franz Mohr (11 December 1792 – 4 December 1848) was an Austrian Roman Catholic priest and writer, best known as the lyricist of the Christmas carol "Silent Night" ("Stille Nacht, heilige Nacht").

Early life

Mohr was born in Salzburg, the illegitimate son of a musketeer who deserted before his birth and an unmarried knitter. Recognised for his musical and scholarly gifts by the cathedral vicar Johann Nepomuk Hiernle, he was educated at the gymnasium and the Kremsmünster lyceum, and was ordained a priest in 1815 after receiving a special dispensation because of his illegitimate birth.

"Silent Night"

In 1816, while serving at the pilgrimage church of Mariapfarr, Mohr wrote the six-stanza poem "Stille Nacht". In 1817 he was posted to Oberndorf bei Salzburg as assistant priest, where he befriended the schoolteacher and organist Franz Xaver Gruber. On Christmas Eve 1818, Mohr asked Gruber to set the poem to music for voice and guitar; the two men performed it that night at Midnight Mass at St Nicholas Church in Oberndorf — Mohr singing tenor and playing guitar, Gruber singing bass. The carol spread through travelling folk singers and is now sung worldwide.

Later life

Mohr served in several parishes, known for generosity to the poor and for founding schools and a fund for elderly care. He spent his last years as vicar of Wagrain, where he died of a lung infection in 1848, aged 55, leaving almost nothing but his guitar. A memorial and the Silent Night Chapel in Oberndorf commemorate him and Gruber.
