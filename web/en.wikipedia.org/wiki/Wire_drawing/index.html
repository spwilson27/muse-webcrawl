Wire drawing

Wire drawing is a metalworking process used to reduce the diameter of a wire by pulling the wire through a single, or series of, drawing dies. The process is a type of forging; it is cold working, although it may also be performed at elevated temperatures for large sections. Wire drawing improves the wire's surface finish, dimensional accuracy and mechanical properties such as tensile strength.

Process

The wire stock, prepared by cleaning and lubrication, is pointed at one end and fed through a die — typically made of tungsten carbide or, for fine wire, diamond — whose hole is smaller than the wire's diameter. The wire is gripped and pulled through by a rotating draw bench or a motorised capstan. Successive passes through progressively smaller dies reduce the diameter in stages, with intermediate annealing used to restore ductility when the metal has work-hardened.

Applications

Drawn wire is used for electrical conductors, springs, fasteners, wire rope, musical instrument strings, fencing, and countless other products. Very fine wires, such as those used in electronics bonding, can be drawn to diameters of a few micrometres.
