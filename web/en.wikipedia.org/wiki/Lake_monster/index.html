Lake monster

A lake monster is a lake-dwelling creature in folklore or cryptozoology, typically described as a large serpent-like or long-necked animal inhabiting a freshwater lake. Such legends occur on every inhabited continent and are a staple of local folklore, tourism, and pseudoscientific speculation.

Famous examples

The most famous lake monster is the Loch Ness Monster ("Nessie") of Loch Ness, Scotland, reported since the 6th-century account of St Columba and popularized after a 1933 sighting and the 1934 "Surgeon's photograph," later admitted to be a hoax. Other celebrated examples include Champ of Lake Champlain (North America), Ogopogo of Okanagan Lake (Canada), the Nahuelito of Nahuel Huapi Lake (Argentina), Morag of Loch Morar, and the Bunyip of Australian billabongs in Aboriginal tradition.

Explanations

Scientific investigations consistently find no evidence of unknown large animals in these lakes. Proposed explanations for sightings include misidentified known animals (otters, swimming deer, large fish such as sturgeon or wels catfish), boat wakes, floating logs, wind-driven waves and seiches, and outright hoaxes. DNA surveys of Loch Ness (2019) found no reptilian DNA but abundant eel DNA. In cultural terms, lake monsters persist as folklore, mascots, and drivers of tourism.
