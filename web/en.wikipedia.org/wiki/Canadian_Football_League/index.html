The Canadian Football League (CFL; French: Ligue canadienne de football) is a professional sports league in Canada. The CFL is the highest level of competition in Canadian football. The league consists of nine teams, each located in a city in Canada, divided into East and West divisions. Its championship game, the Grey Cup, was first awarded in 1909 and is one of Canada's largest annual sporting and television events.

History

Canadian football evolved from rugby football in the 19th century; the sport's governing bodies included the Canadian Rugby Union, and eastern and western unions contested the Grey Cup for decades before formally uniting. The CFL was officially founded on 19 January 1958 out of the Interprovincial Rugby Football Union (the "Big Four") and the Western Interprovincial Football Union. The league experimented with expansion into the United States between 1993 and 1995 — the Baltimore Stallions even won the 1995 Grey Cup — but the American experiment collapsed, and the league restabilised in Canada, with Ottawa returning as the Redblacks in 2014.

The Canadian game

Canadian football differs from the American game in key respects: a larger field (110 yards long and 65 wide, with 20-yard end zones), 12 players per side, three downs to advance ten yards, a one-point "rouge" for certain kicks into the end zone, and looser motion and kicking rules that encourage a wide-open, pass-heavy style.

Teams

The nine teams are the BC Lions, Calgary Stampeders, Edmonton Elks, Saskatchewan Roughriders, and Winnipeg Blue Bombers (West Division), and the Hamilton Tiger-Cats, Toronto Argonauts, Ottawa Redblacks, and Montreal Alouettes (East Division). The Toronto Argonauts have won the most Grey Cups.

Season

The regular season runs from June to late October, with each team playing 18 games, followed by playoffs in November culminating in the Grey Cup, traditionally held on a Sunday in late November.
