Iran–Israel relations

The relations between Iran and Israel have swung from close cooperation under the Pahlavi monarchy to open hostility since the Iranian Revolution of 1979. Iran was among the first countries to recognise Israel, and under Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi the two states cooperated on trade, oil, and intelligence against common Arab adversaries, though relations were kept largely discreet.

After 1979

The Islamic Republic severed ties, and Ayatollah Khomeini's regime adopted a strongly anti-Israel ideology, supporting groups opposed to Israel including Hezbollah in Lebanon and Palestinian factions such as Hamas. Israel, in turn, has viewed Iran's nuclear programme and regional network of allies as its principal strategic threat. During the Iran–Iraq War, Israel nonetheless covertly sold arms to Iran, a flow partly exposed in the Iran–Contra affair.

Proxy conflict and direct confrontation

For decades the rivalry was fought largely through proxies and covert action: assassinations of Iranian nuclear scientists, cyber operations such as Stuxnet, and Israeli air strikes on Iranian-linked targets in Syria. In 2024 the confrontation turned direct, with an Israeli strike on an Iranian consular building in Damascus, followed by Iranian missile and drone attacks on Israel and Israeli retaliatory strikes — the first open exchanges of fire between the two states.
