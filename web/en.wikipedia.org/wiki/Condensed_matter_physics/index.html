Condensed matter physics

Condensed matter physics is the field of physics that deals with the macroscopic and microscopic physical properties of matter, especially the solid and liquid phases that arise from electromagnetic forces between atoms and electrons. It is the largest subfield of contemporary physics, encompassing the study of superconductivity, magnetism, semiconductors, superfluidity, and the behaviour of complex materials.

Condensed matter physics studies phases of matter such as crystals, magnets and superconductors.

Scope

Condensed matter physicists seek to understand how the collective behaviour of vast numbers of interacting particles gives rise to emergent properties — Philip W. Anderson's dictum "More is different" (1972) is a touchstone of the field. Topics include electronic band structure, phase transitions and critical phenomena, topological phases of matter, strongly correlated electron systems such as high-temperature superconductors, and soft condensed matter such as polymers, colloids and liquid crystals.

Applications

Research in condensed matter underlies much of modern technology: the transistor and semiconductor electronics, magnetic storage, lasers, and advances toward quantum computing. It employs experimental techniques including neutron and X-ray scattering, scanning probe microscopy and ultra-low-temperature measurement, alongside theoretical methods from quantum field theory and computational physics.
