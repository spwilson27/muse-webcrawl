Stand-up comedy

Stand-up comedy is a comedic performance in which a comedian performs in front of a live audience, speaking directly to them through a microphone. The performer is commonly known as a comic, stand-up comic, comedian, comedienne, stand-up comedian, or simply a stand-up.

Stand-up comedy consists of one-liners, stories, and observations, usually mixed, known as a monologue, act, routine, or set. Some stand-up comedians use props, music, or magic tricks. Performances take place in comedy clubs, bars, nightclubs, theatres, and increasingly in arenas and via streaming specials.

History

The roots of stand-up lie in vaudeville and the British music hall traditions of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and in the monologists of the Chitlin' Circuit and Borscht Belt. In the United States, figures such as Bob Hope, Jack Benny, and later Lenny Bruce and Mort Sahl reshaped the form toward topical and personal material in the 1950s and 1960s.

The 1970s and 1980s saw a stand-up boom, with comedians such as Richard Pryor, George Carlin, Steve Martin, and Robin Williams achieving rock-star fame. In the United Kingdom, the alternative comedy movement of the 1980s, centered on London's Comedy Store, produced a new generation of performers.

Formats and craft

A set is built from individual jokes ("bits"), each typically having a setup and a punchline, sometimes followed by "tags". Comedians develop material at open mics and refine it through repetition. Specialized roles in club line-ups include the host (MC), the feature act, and the headliner.
