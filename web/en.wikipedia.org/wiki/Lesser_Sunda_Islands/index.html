The Lesser Sunda Islands (Indonesian: Kepulauan Nusa Tenggara, formerly also called the Lesser Sundas) are a group of islands in Maritime Southeast Asia, north of Australia, forming the eastern portion of the Sunda Islands arc. Together with the Greater Sunda Islands (Borneo, Java, Sumatra, Sulawesi) they make up the Sunda Islands; politically most belong to Indonesia, with the eastern half of Timor forming the independent state of East Timor.

Geography

The volcanic island arc runs east from Bali — separated from Java by the Bali Strait — through Lombok, Sumbawa, Flores, the Solor and Alor archipelagos to Timor. The Wallace Line, the famous biogeographical boundary separating Asian from Australasian fauna, passes between Bali and Lombok, so the Lesser Sundas east of Lombok have increasingly Australian-influenced flora and fauna; Komodo island is home to the Komodo dragon. The climate is markedly drier than western Indonesia, with savanna landscapes in the east.

Administration

Indonesian Lesser Sundas are divided among the provinces of Bali, West Nusa Tenggara and East Nusa Tenggara. The islands have a combined population of over 15 million and economies based on agriculture, fishing and tourism — Bali being one of the world's best-known tourist destinations.
