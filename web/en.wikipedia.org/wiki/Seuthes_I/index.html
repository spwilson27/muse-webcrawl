Seuthes I

Seuthes I was a king of the Odrysian kingdom of Thrace from the death of his uncle Sitalces in 424 BC until about 410 BC. He was the son of Sparadocus and the nephew and successor of Sitalces, under whom he had already been a powerful figure at court.

Reign

According to Thucydides, Seuthes was, after Sitalces, the most influential man at the Odrysian court; he married Stratonice, the sister of the Macedonian king Perdiccas II, whose diplomacy helped secure his position. When Sitalces was killed campaigning against the Triballi in 424 BC, Seuthes succeeded him. Thucydides reports that under Seuthes the tribute and gifts flowing into the Odrysian treasury reached their height — some 400 talents of gold and silver in tribute alone, with gifts of comparable value — making the kingdom the richest in Europe between the Ionian Sea and the Black Sea.

Thucydides also records that the Athenian exile and historian's near namesake, the general Xenophon's era, Seuthes appears again in the next generation: the mercenary leader Seuthes who employed the Ten Thousand was Seuthes II, a descendant and subordinate prince of the same dynasty. Seuthes I appears to have maintained his uncle's friendly relations with Athens for much of his reign, though the kingdom's power began to fragment after his death into rival dynasts such as Medocus (Amadocus I) and Maisades.
