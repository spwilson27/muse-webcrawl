Tobacco smoke

Tobacco smoke is a sooty aerosol produced by the incomplete combustion of tobacco products during smoking. It is a complex, dynamic, and highly toxic mixture of more than 5,000 chemicals, including nicotine, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and carbon monoxide.

Composition

Tobacco smoke consists of a gas phase and a particulate phase. The particulate phase, often called "tar", contains nicotine, water, and hundreds of carcinogens such as benzo[a]pyrene. The gas phase includes carbon monoxide, hydrogen cyanide, nitrogen oxides, and volatile organic compounds. At least 70 of the chemicals in tobacco smoke are known to cause cancer.

Mainstream and sidestream smoke

Mainstream smoke is the mixture inhaled by the smoker, while sidestream smoke is emitted from the smouldering tip of a cigarette between puffs. Sidestream smoke contains higher concentrations of many toxicants and is a major component of secondhand smoke, also known as environmental tobacco smoke.

Health effects

Prolonged exposure to tobacco smoke causes lung cancer, cardiovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and many other conditions. Secondhand smoke exposure is also a recognized cause of disease in non-smokers, leading to smoke-free legislation in workplaces and public places in many countries.
