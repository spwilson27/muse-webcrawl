Phon

Phon
Quantity: Loudness level
Definition: Loudness level in phons equals the sound pressure level in dB SPL of a 1 kHz tone judged equally loud
Related unit: Sone (perceived loudness)
Standard: ISO 226 (equal-loudness contours)

The phon is a unit of loudness level for a sound, reflecting the human perception of its strength. A sound has a loudness level of N phons if it is judged by listeners to be as loud as a pure tone of frequency 1,000 Hz whose sound pressure level is N decibels above the threshold of hearing.

Definition and equal-loudness contours

Because the ear's sensitivity varies greatly with frequency, equal physical intensities do not sound equally loud. Curves of equal loudness — the Fletcher–Munson curves and their modern successors in ISO 226 — plot the sound pressure level required at each frequency for a tone to be judged as loud as a 1 kHz reference. Every point on one such contour has the same loudness level in phons: at 1 kHz, phons equal decibels SPL by definition, while at low frequencies much higher sound pressure is needed to reach the same number of phons.

Relation to the sone

The phon measures loudness level on a scale anchored to a reference tone; the related sone measures perceived loudness itself on a ratio scale, with 1 sone defined as the loudness of a 1 kHz tone at 40 dB SPL (40 phons). A sound of 2 sones is judged twice as loud as one of 1 sone; roughly, an increase of 10 phons corresponds to a doubling of loudness in sones for sounds well above threshold.
