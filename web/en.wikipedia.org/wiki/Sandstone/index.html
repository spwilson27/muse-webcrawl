Sandstone is a clastic sedimentary rock composed mainly of sand-sized (0.0625 to 2 mm) silicate grains. Sandstones comprise about 20–25% of all sedimentary rocks. Most sandstone is composed of quartz or feldspar (both silicates) because they are the most resistant minerals to the weathering processes involved in the formation of sandstones at the Earth's surface.

Formation

Sandstone forms when sand deposits from rivers, beaches, deltas, deserts or shallow seas are buried, compacted and cemented by minerals such as silica, calcium carbonate or iron oxides. Iron oxide cement gives red and orange hues, as in the rocks of the Colorado Plateau; the red colour of many deserts comes from sandstone and its soil.

Classification

Geologists classify sandstones by their framework composition: quartz arenites (over 90% quartz), arkoses (rich in feldspar), lithic arenites (rich in rock fragments) and greywackes (poorly sorted, matrix-rich, often deep-marine). The QFL (quartz–feldspar–lithics) ternary diagram is the standard plotting scheme.

Uses

Sandstone has been used since prehistory for construction and carving — the Red Fort in Delhi, Petra in Jordan and countless cathedrals are sandstone. Porous sandstones are major aquifers and hydrocarbon reservoirs; their pore spaces store groundwater, petroleum and natural gas.
