[Image: A harbour at low tide with boats resting on mud, then imagined at high water]

Tides are the rise and fall of sea levels caused by the combined effects of the gravitational forces of the Moon and Sun and Earth's rotation. Most coasts experience two high and two low tides a day; ranges vary from centimetres in enclosed seas to over 16 metres in Canada's Bay of Fundy.

Energy and effects

Tides drive currents, mix nutrients, shape coasts and intertidal ecosystems, and can generate electricity (the turbine cousins of the sea). The Moon slowly recedes and Earth's day lengthens as tidal friction transfers angular momentum — the same locking that keeps the Moon's same face toward us.
