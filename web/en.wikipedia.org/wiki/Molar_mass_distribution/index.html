Molar mass distribution

In polymer chemistry, the molar mass distribution (also called the molecular weight distribution) describes the spread of molar masses present in a polymer sample. Unlike small molecules of fixed molar mass, a polymer consists of chains of different lengths, so its molar mass must be described statistically by a distribution and by various average values.

Averages

The most common averages are:

  • Number-average molar mass (Mn) — the arithmetic mean, weighting each molecule equally; measured by osmometry or end-group analysis.
  • Weight-average molar mass (Mw) — weighting molecules by their mass fraction; measured by light scattering.
  • Z-average (Mz) and viscosity-average (Mv) molar masses, which emphasise the longest chains.

The ratio Mw/Mn is the dispersity (Đ, formerly polydispersity index). It equals 1 for a perfectly uniform (monodisperse) sample and is typically around 2 for polymers made by conventional free-radical or step-growth polymerisation; controlled ("living") polymerisations can approach 1.0–1.1.

Measurement

The full distribution is most commonly measured by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC), also called gel permeation chromatography (GPC), in which chains are separated by hydrodynamic size and detected by refractive index, light scattering or viscometry. Mass spectrometry (e.g. MALDI-TOF) can resolve distributions chain-by-chain for lower-mass polymers.

Significance

The distribution affects processing and properties: short chains act as plasticisers and lower strength, while very long chains raise melt viscosity and toughness. Theoretical distributions include the Schulz–Zimm and Flory (most probable) distributions for idealised polymerisations.
