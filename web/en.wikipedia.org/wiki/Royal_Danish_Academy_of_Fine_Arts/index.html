Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts

Charlottenborg Palace, seat of the academy since 1754

The Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts (Danish: Det Kongelige Danske Kunstakademi) is a Danish institution of higher education in Copenhagen, founded in 1754. It is Denmark's oldest institution of higher learning in the arts and comprises schools of visual arts, architecture, design and conservation.

History

The academy was inaugurated on 31 March 1754, the birthday of King Frederick V, and was based in Charlottenborg Palace on Kongens Nytorv. It was established to educate artists and architects for the royal building projects and to raise Danish art to European academic standards. The painter Nicolai Eigtved was a driving force in its creation. From its earliest decades the academy administered Denmark's most important art prizes, including the gold medals that financed study journeys to Rome for painters of the Danish Golden Age.

Structure

The academy today includes the Schools of Visual Arts, the School of Architecture, the School of Design and the School of Conservation, together with institutes for research. The Kunsthal Charlottenborg exhibition hall shares the palace complex and is one of Denmark's largest venues for contemporary art.

Notable people

Associated figures include sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen, Golden Age painters Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg and Christen Købke, architect Bjarke Ingels and artist Olafur Eliasson.
