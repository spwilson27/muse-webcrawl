Sanctuary

A sanctuary, in its original meaning, is a sacred place, such as a shrine. By the use of such places as a safe haven, by extension the term has come to be used for any place of safety. This secondary use can be categorized into human sanctuary, a safe place for humans, such as a political sanctuary; and non-human sanctuary, such as an animal or plant sanctuary.

Religious sanctuary

In many religions the sanctuary is the holiest part of a sacred building: in a church it denotes the area around the altar, often raised and railed, containing the altar and reserved for clergy; in the ancient Israelite Tabernacle and Temple it corresponded to the Holy of Holies. Temples in the Greek and Roman world, and medieval churches, also served as places of asylum.

Sanctuary as asylum

The legal and religious right of sanctuary allowed fugitives from justice to take refuge in a church or temple and claim immunity from arrest for a period. In England the practice was regulated from the Middle Ages — some chartered sanctuaries, such as those at Westminster, Beverley, and Durham, protected even serious offenders indefinitely — before being progressively abolished in the 16th and 17th centuries. The tradition survives in modern forms: the sanctuary movement of the 1980s, in which churches sheltered Central American refugees, and modern "sanctuary cities" that limit cooperation with immigration enforcement.

Wildlife sanctuary

A wildlife sanctuary is a protected area where animals are safeguarded from hunting and disturbance; in India, wildlife sanctuaries form a category of protected areas under the Wildlife Protection Act of 1972. Animal sanctuaries also exist privately, providing permanent refuge for rescued farmed, exotic, or laboratory animals, distinct from zoos in that residents are not bred or traded.
