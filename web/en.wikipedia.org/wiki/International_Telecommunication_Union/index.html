The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for matters related to information and communication technologies (ICT). Founded in Paris on 17 May 1865 as the International Telegraph Union, it is the oldest UN agency and one of the oldest international organisations in the world.

Functions

The ITU's mandate covers the global coordination of telecommunication networks and services. Its principal tasks include:

  • Radio spectrum and satellite orbits: through its Radiocommunication Sector (ITU-R) and the Radio Regulations, it allocates global radio-frequency spectrum and registers satellite orbits to prevent harmful interference.
  • Standardisation: the Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) produces technical standards (Recommendations) for networks and services, including telephone numbering (E.164), video compression (such as H.264/H.265) and fibre-optic systems.
  • Development: the Development Sector (ITU-D) works to expand access to ICTs in developing countries and tracks global connectivity statistics.

History

The ITU began with the first International Telegraph Convention, signed in 1865 by 20 European states to harmonise telegraphy across borders. It incorporated radiotelegraphy after 1906, took its present name in 1934, and became a UN specialised agency in 1947. In 1989 it famously allocated country calling codes and has continued to evolve with the internet era, convening the World Summit on the Information Society (2003–2005). On 1 January 2023 Doreen Bogdan-Martin took office as the first woman Secretary-General in the organisation's history.

Structure

The ITU's supreme body is the Plenipotentiary Conference, held every four years, which sets general policy and elects the Council and the Secretary-General, Deputy Secretary-General and directors of the three sector bureaux. Its membership uniquely combines 194 member states with more than 900 companies, universities and other "Sector Members" that participate in its technical work.
