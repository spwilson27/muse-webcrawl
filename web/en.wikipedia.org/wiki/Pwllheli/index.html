Pwllheli

Pwllheli is a market town and community on the Llŷn Peninsula in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It is often described as the unofficial capital of the Llŷn, and is a popular seaside resort with a marina, sandy beaches and a strong Welsh-speaking community.

History

The town's name combines pwll ("pool") and heli ("brine"), referring to the salt-water pool of its harbour. Pwllheli was granted a borough charter in the 14th century. It developed as a port and shipbuilding centre, and in the 19th century became a market town serving the surrounding agricultural peninsula. The railway arrived in 1867, boosting its growth as a holiday destination.

Attractions

Pwllheli has a large modern marina, one of the finest sailing centres in Wales, and hosts sailing events at Plas Heli, the Welsh National Sailing Academy. Nearby attractions include the South Beach, the headland of Pen-y-Graig, and the village of Llanbedrog with its art gallery. The town hosts a weekly market and is a gateway to the Wales Coast Path.

Language and culture

A large majority of residents speak Welsh. The town was the birthplace of the Welsh nationalist movement's early gatherings, and in 1925 the meeting at Pwllheli led to the founding of Plaid Cymru, the Welsh nationalist party.
