Princess of Asturias Awards

The annual ceremony at the Teatro Campoamor, Oviedo.

The Princess of Asturias Awards (Spanish: Premios Princesa de Asturias), formerly the Prince of Asturias Awards (1981–2014), are a series of annual prizes awarded in Spain by the Princess of Asturias Foundation, recognising scientific, technical, cultural, social, and humanitarian work performed by individuals, institutions, or organisations worldwide.

Established1981 (first awarded as Prince of Asturias Awards)
Presented byPrincess of Asturias Foundation
VenueTeatro Campoamor, Oviedo, Asturias
PrizeA Joan Miró-designed sculpture, diploma, insignia, and €50,000 per category

Categories

Eight awards are presented each year: Arts; Communication and Humanities; Social Sciences; Sports; Literature; Technical and Scientific Research; International Cooperation; and Concord. Juries for each category meet in Oviedo in the spring and summer, and the awards ceremony takes place each October, presided over by the Spanish royal family.

History

The Prince of Asturias Foundation was founded in 1980, and the first awards were made in 1981. When Felipe, Prince of Asturias, became king in 2014 and his daughter Leonor became Princess of Asturias, both the foundation and the awards took her title; Leonor has presided over the ceremony since 2019.

Selected laureates

  • Arts: Woody Allen, Paco de Lucía, Meryl Streep, Marina Abramović
  • Literature: Mario Vargas Llosa, Margaret Atwood, Leonard Cohen, Haruki Murakami
  • Technical and Scientific Research: Tim Berners-Lee (with others), Jane Goodall, CRISPR pioneers
  • Sports: Rafael Nadal, Michael Schumacher, the New Zealand All Blacks
  • Concord: Yad Vashem, the city of Berlin, health workers during the COVID-19 pandemic
