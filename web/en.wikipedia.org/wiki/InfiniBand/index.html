InfiniBand (IB) is a computer networking communications standard used in high-performance computing and enterprise data centres, featuring very high throughput and very low latency. It is used for data interconnect both among and within computers and serves as a direct or switched interconnect between servers and storage systems.

History and architecture

InfiniBand was created in 1999 from the merger of two competing designs, Future I/O and Next Generation I/O, and is maintained by the InfiniBand Trade Association. Its architecture is a switched fabric built from point-to-point links and switches managed by a subnet manager, with channel adapters at each node. InfiniBand natively supports remote direct memory access (RDMA), allowing one computer to transfer data directly into another's memory without involving either operating system, plus transport offloads that minimise CPU overhead. Signalling generations have progressed through SDR, DDR, QDR, FDR, EDR, HDR (200 Gbit/s), and NDR (400 Gbit/s).

Use

InfiniBand became the dominant interconnect for large supercomputing clusters, appearing in a large share of TOP500 systems, and was widely adopted in financial and enterprise data centres. The vendor Mellanox, acquired by Nvidia in 2020, became the principal supplier; InfiniBand fabrics are extensively used to interconnect GPUs in large AI training clusters, where it competes with high-speed Ethernet (including RoCE) and Nvidia's proprietary NVLink for intra-rack connections.
