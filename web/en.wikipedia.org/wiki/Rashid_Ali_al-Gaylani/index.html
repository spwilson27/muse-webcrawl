Rashid Ali al-Gaylani

Rashid Ali al-Gaylani (1892 – 28 August 1965) was an Iraqi politician who served as Prime Minister of Iraq on three occasions, most notably in 1941, when his pro-Axis government brought Iraq into confrontation with the United Kingdom during the Second World War.

Born1892, Baghdad, Ottoman Empire
Died28 August 1965, Beirut, Lebanon
OfficePrime Minister of Iraq (1933; 1940–41; 1941)

Early career

Born in Baghdad into the prominent al-Gaylani (al-Kaylani) family, Rashid Ali trained as a lawyer and entered politics after the First World War, serving in several ministerial posts, including Minister of Justice and Minister of the Interior, before his first brief premiership in 1933.

The 1941 coup and the Anglo-Iraqi War

In April 1941, with support from the nationalist "Golden Square" officers, Rashid Ali seized power and displaced the pro-British regent. His government sought support from Germany and Italy and refused British demands for troop transit rights under the Anglo-Iraqi Treaty. The resulting Anglo-Iraqi War of May 1941 ended with British forces occupying Baghdad; Rashid Ali fled, first to Iran and then to Germany, where he spent the war years.

Later life

Sentenced to death in absentia, Rashid Ali lived in exile in Saudi Arabia. After the 1958 Iraqi revolution overthrew the Hashemite monarchy, he returned to Iraq but was soon arrested for plotting against Abd al-Karim Qasim's government; his death sentence was commuted and he was released in 1961. He died in Beirut in 1965.
