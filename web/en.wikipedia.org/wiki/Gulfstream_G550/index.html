Gulfstream G550

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Gulfstream G550 is a business jet aircraft produced by General Dynamics' Gulfstream Aerospace unit in Savannah, Georgia. It is an improved development of the Gulfstream V, with increased range, a seventh cabin window pair and the PlaneView flight deck with an enhanced vision system. First delivered in 2003, it was for two decades among the most popular ultra-long-range corporate jets; more than 600 were built before production ended in 2021.

Design and performance

Powered by two Rolls-Royce BR710 turbofans, the G550 can cruise at Mach 0.80–0.885 at up to 51,000 ft, flying non-stop routes such as New York–Tokyo. Its cockpit introduced Gulfstream's PlaneView avionics suite with cursor-control devices and an infrared enhanced vision system displayed on the head-up display. The cabin, nearly 51 ft long, typically seats 14–18 passengers with a full galley and crew rest.

Military and special-mission variants

The type serves many governments as a VIP transport and as the basis for special-mission aircraft, most notably the US Air Force C-37B, the Israeli Air Force's Eitam airborne early warning aircraft (G550 CAEW), Singapore's AEW fleet, and electronic-intelligence and maritime patrol conversions. A further development with reduced range is the G500 (GVII family excluded — the earlier "G500" was a derated G550 variant marketed from 2004).
