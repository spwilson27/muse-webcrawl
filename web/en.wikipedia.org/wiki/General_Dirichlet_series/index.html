General Dirichlet series

A general Dirichlet series is an infinite series of the form Σ an e−λn s, where (an) are complex coefficients and (λn) is a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative real numbers tending to infinity. It generalizes the ordinary Dirichlet series, which corresponds to the choice λn = log n, and the ordinary power series, which corresponds to λn = n.

Convergence

Like ordinary Dirichlet series, a general Dirichlet series converges in a right half-plane of the complex plane. There is an abscissa of convergence σc and an abscissa of absolute convergence σa; the difference σa − σc is bounded by a quantity depending on the spacing of the exponents, and equals zero when the exponents grow like log n sufficiently closely.

Properties

Within its half-plane of convergence a general Dirichlet series defines a holomorphic function. Growth estimates relate the order of this function to the density of the exponent sequence. The theory, developed by authors including G. H. Hardy and Marcel Riesz, unifies results about power series, ordinary Dirichlet series, and almost periodic functions.

Examples

Examples include the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet L-functions (the log n case), exponential generating series, and series arising in the study of the mean values of arithmetic functions.
