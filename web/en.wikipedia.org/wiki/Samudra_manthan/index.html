Samudra manthan

Sources: Mahabharata, Ramayana, Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana

Key figures: Vishnu (as Kurma and Mohini), Shiva, Indra

Object obtained: Amrita, the nectar of immortality

The Samudra manthan (Sanskrit: समुद्रमन्थन, "churning of the ocean") is a major episode in Hindu mythology, recounted in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana and several Puranas. It describes how the devas (gods) and asuras (demons) cooperatively churned the Ocean of Milk (Kshira Sagara) to recover the nectar of immortality, amrita, and other treasures.

The myth

After Indra lost his power through a curse, the devas allied with the asuras to churn the ocean. Mount Mandara served as the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope; Vishnu took the form of the tortoise Kurma to support the mountain on his back. As the churning proceeded, treasures emerged — the goddess Lakshmi, the moon Chandra, the wish-fulfilling cow Kamadhenu, the elephant Airavata, the apsaras, and the physician Dhanvantari bearing the pot of amrita — but first came the deadly poison halahala, which Shiva drank, turning his throat blue.

The nectar

When the amrita finally appeared, the asuras seized it. Vishnu then assumed the enchantress form Mohini, distracted the asuras and distributed the nectar to the devas alone. The asura Svarbhanu, disguised as a deva, drank a sip before being beheaded; his head and body became Rahu and Ketu, associated with eclipses.

Cultural legacy

The churning is a celebrated subject of Indian and Southeast Asian art, most famously in the great bas-relief at Angkor Wat in Cambodia, and the drops of nectar said to have fallen to earth are commemorated at the sites of the Kumbh Mela festival.
