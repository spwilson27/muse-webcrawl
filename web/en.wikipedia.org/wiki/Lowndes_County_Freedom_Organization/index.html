The Lowndes County Freedom Organization (LCFO) was an independent political party founded in 1965 in Lowndes County, Alabama, by local African American residents working with organizers of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), including Stokely Carmichael. Its emblem, a black panther, later inspired the name of the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense in Oakland, California.

Background

Lowndes County, nicknamed "Bloody Lowndes," had an 80-percent Black population yet, at the start of 1965, not a single Black registered voter, owing to violent white supremacist repression. Following the Selma to Montgomery marches and the Voting Rights Act of 1965, residents began registering in large numbers and organized to contest county offices directly rather than work through the segregationist local Democratic Party.

The 1966 election

The LCFO nominated a full slate of Black candidates for county offices, including sheriff and tax assessor, in the November 1966 election. Although none won — amid widespread intimidation and the advantages of incumbency — the campaign was a landmark assertion that Black voters would exercise independent political power.

Legacy

The LCFO's black panther symbol was adopted in 1966 by Huey Newton and Bobby Seale for the Black Panther Party. The organization's model of independent Black political organizing influenced later movements, and John Hulett, an LCFO leader, went on to become Lowndes County's first Black sheriff in 1970.
