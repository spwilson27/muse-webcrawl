Arts Council England is a non-departmental public body of the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) and the national development agency for creativity and culture in England. It distributes public money from the government and income from the National Lottery to artists, arts organisations, museums, and libraries across the country.

History

The organisation descends from the Arts Council of Great Britain, founded in 1946 with John Maynard Keynes as its first chairman. In 1994 the British body was split into separate councils for England, Scotland, and Wales. The Arts Council of England merged with the regional arts boards in 2002 and adopted its present name in 2003. In 2011 it took on responsibility for museums and libraries formerly held by the Museums, Libraries and Archives Council, and it later assumed functions relating to cultural education and the National Lottery Heritage Fund's predecessor arrangements. From 2015 its national office has been based in Manchester.

Funding programmes

Its principal funding stream is the National Portfolio, which provides multi-year core funding to a portfolio of organisations—roughly a thousand organisations in the 2023–26 round, funded at about £444 million per year across grant-in-aid and lottery sources. Project-based funding is offered through National Lottery Project Grants, open to individuals and organisations, and through strategic funds for touring, capital development, and diversity programmes. Investment decisions in the 2023–26 round, which moved funding out of London toward other regions under the government's "levelling up" agenda and cut several prominent London institutions, were the subject of substantial public debate.

Strategy: Let's Create

The council's ten-year strategy Let's Create (2020–2030) sets three outcomes: creative people, cultural communities, and a creative and cultural country, underpinned by investment principles covering ambition and quality, dynamism, environmental responsibility, and inclusivity and relevance.
