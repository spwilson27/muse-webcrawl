Giant African land snail

Scientific nameLissachatina fulica (syn. Achatina fulica)
FamilyAchatinidae
Native rangeEast Africa
Shell lengthUp to c. 20 cm

The giant African land snail (Lissachatina fulica) is one of the largest terrestrial gastropods in the world, a species of large land snail in the family Achatinidae native to East Africa. Widely introduced — as food, through the pet trade and accidentally — it has become a notorious invasive species in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

Description

Adults have a conical brownish shell with darker banding, typically 7–10 cm long and occasionally approaching 20 cm. Hermaphroditic and highly fecund, a single snail can lay hundreds of eggs several times a year, enabling explosive population growth in new habitats.

Invasiveness

The snail feeds on more than 500 plant species, damaging crops and native flora, outcompeting native snails, and even eating stucco and plaster for calcium. It can carry the rat lungworm parasite Angiostrongylus cantonensis, a cause of eosinophilic meningitis in humans. Florida has twice mounted costly eradication campaigns after escapes from the pet trade (1966 and 2011). Introduction of predatory snails such as the rosy wolfsnail to control it has devastated endemic Pacific island snails.

Uses

In West Africa related giant snails are an important traditional food; L. fulica is also kept in the pet trade in some countries, though import is banned in the United States and strictly controlled elsewhere.
