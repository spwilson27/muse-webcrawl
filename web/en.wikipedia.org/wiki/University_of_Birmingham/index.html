University of Birmingham

University
Founded: 1900 (royal charter)
Location: Edgbaston, Birmingham, England
Students: about 38,000
Motto: Per Ardua ad Alta

The University of Birmingham is a public research university in Edgbaston, Birmingham, United Kingdom. It received its royal charter in 1900 as a successor to Queen's College, Birmingham (1825), and Mason Science College (1875), making it the first English civic or "redbrick" university to gain full university status.

History

The university's creation was championed by Joseph Chamberlain, its first chancellor, who wanted a great civic university for the industrial Midlands. The Edgbaston campus, donated by Lord Calthorpe, opened in 1909 with the domed Chancellor's Court designed by Aston Webb. Its clock tower, "Old Joe," standing 100 metres tall, is the tallest free-standing clock tower in the world. During World War II, Birmingham physicists contributed to the cavity magnetron, a key radar breakthrough.

Academics

The university is organized into five colleges, spanning arts and law, engineering and physical sciences, life and environmental sciences, medical and dental sciences, and social sciences. It is a founding member of the Russell Group and of the Universitas 21 network. Alumni and staff include eleven Nobel laureates.

Campus

Facilities include the Barber Institute of Fine Arts, the Lapworth Museum of Geology, Winterbourne House and Garden, and its own railway station — the only university campus station in Britain.
