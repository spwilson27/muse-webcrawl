Polish notation

Polish notation (PN), also known as normal Polish notation (NPN), Łukasiewicz notation, Warsaw notation, Polish prefix notation or simply prefix notation, is a mathematical notation in which operators precede their operands, in contrast to the more common infix notation, in which operators are placed between operands, as well as reverse Polish notation (RPN), in which operators follow their operands. It does not need any parentheses as long as each operator has a fixed number of operands. The description "Polish" refers to the nationality of logician Jan Łukasiewicz, who invented Polish notation in 1924.

The term Polish notation is sometimes used (as the opposite of infix notation) in reference to the related concepts of reverse Polish notation and Polish notation as used in computer science, where the operators are placed either before or after the operands.

History

A paper on notation by Łukasiewicz, published in 1929, also mentions his earlier development of the system. Łukasiewicz stated that he developed his notation in 1924 while lecturing at the University of Warsaw. When Polish notation is used as a representation for mathematical expressions by programming language interpreters, its operators can generally be seen as an abbreviation for a call to the corresponding operator, and its syntax does not require any parentheses, which allows the expressions to be parsed easily.

Explanation

In Polish notation, an expression such as a + b is written as + a b. For example, the expression (a + b) × c in infix notation becomes × + a b c in Polish notation. More complex expressions can be written without parentheses because the order of operations is determined by the position of the operators.
