Flower of Scotland

"Flower of Scotland" (Scottish Gaelic: Flùr na h-Alba) is a Scottish patriotic song, frequently performed at special occasions and sporting events as an unofficial national anthem of Scotland. Written by Roy Williamson of the folk group The Corries in the mid-1960s, it commemorates the victory of the Scots, led by Robert the Bruce, over Edward II of England at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314.

History

The Corries first performed the song in the 1960s, and it grew in popularity through the 1970s. The Scottish national rugby union team adopted it, singing it before matches from 1990, when it was famously sung before Scotland's Grand Slam-deciding victory over England at Murrayfield. In 1993 the Scottish Football Association adopted it for the national football team as well. It is also used by Team Scotland at the Commonwealth Games, where it has served as the victory anthem since 2010, replacing "Scotland the Brave".

Lyrics

O flower of Scotland, when will we see your like again,
That fought and died for your wee bit hill and glen,
And stood against him, proud Edward's army,
And sent him homeward to think again.

The song's verses recall the "flower" of Scottish manhood lost at Bannockburn and call on modern Scots to "rise now and be the nation again". Its martial focus on defeating the English has drawn occasional criticism, and there have periodically been public discussions about whether Scotland should adopt a different official anthem, but "Flower of Scotland" remains by far the most popular choice in opinion polls.

In popular culture

The song is sung by Scotland supporters at football and rugby internationals, appears at weddings and Burns suppers, and has been recorded by numerous artists. It was performed at the opening ceremony of the 2014 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow.
