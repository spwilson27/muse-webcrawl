Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis (1738–1805) was a British Army officer and colonial administrator. In the United States he is best remembered for surrendering his army at Yorktown in 1781, effectively ending major fighting in the American Revolutionary War. Despite that defeat, his career flourished: as Governor-General of India he defeated Tipu Sultan in the Third Anglo-Mysore War and enacted the Cornwallis Code and the Permanent Settlement of Bengal, and as Lord Lieutenant of Ireland he suppressed the Irish Rebellion of 1798 and helped bring about the Act of Union.

American war

Cornwallis served under Howe and Clinton, winning the Battle of Camden (1780) and Pyrrhic victories in the Carolinas, including Guilford Court House (1781). Marching into Virginia, he fortified Yorktown, where a Franco-American army and the French fleet trapped him; he surrendered on 19 October 1781, pleading illness and sending his subordinate to hand over his sword.

India and Ireland

In India (1786–1793) Cornwallis reformed the East India Company's civil service, reorganised the judiciary, and in 1792 forced Tipu Sultan to cede half his territory. In Ireland he combined military suppression of the 1798 rebellion with efforts to restrain reprisals, and supported the Union of 1801. He negotiated the Treaty of Amiens with France in 1802. Appointed Governor-General of India a second time in 1805, he died at Ghazipur shortly after arrival.
