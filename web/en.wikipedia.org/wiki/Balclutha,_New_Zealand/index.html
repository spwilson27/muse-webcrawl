Balclutha (Māori: Iwikatea) is a town in Otago, South Island, New Zealand. It lies towards the end of the Clutha River, on the east coast of the South Island, and is often described as the gateway to the Catlins. It is the largest town and seat of the Clutha District Council.

Geography

Balclutha lies on State Highway 1, roughly 81 kilometres south-west of Dunedin. The Clutha River / Mata-Au flows through the town and is crossed by the distinctive Balclutha Road Bridge, a concrete bowstring arch bridge opened in 1935. The river splits around Inch Clutha, a large fertile island downstream of the town.

History

The town was named after the Scottish Gaelic Baile Clutha, meaning "town on the Clyde", reflecting the Scottish heritage of many of its early settlers. James McDonald, one of the earliest European settlers in the district, arrived in the late 1840s. Balclutha grew as a river port and service centre for the surrounding farming district, and suffered severe flooding on several occasions, notably in 1878 and again in October 1978, when the Clutha and Pomahaka rivers inundated much of the town.

Economy

The local economy is based largely on agriculture and related industries, including sheep and dairy farming, freezing works, and a large dairy factory at nearby Stirling operated by Danone. The town serves as a retail and service hub for South Otago.

Notable people

  • Sarah Ulmer, Olympic gold medal-winning cyclist, was born in Auckland but raised in the district; the town is associated with several All Blacks.
  • Balclutha is also known as the hometown of musician and broadcaster figures in the region.
