Fried rice

Fried rice
CourseMain course
Place of originChina
RegionGreater China; widespread in East and Southeast Asia
Serving temperatureHot
Main ingredientsCooked rice, oil, egg, vegetables, meat or seafood

Fried rice is a dish of cooked rice that has been stir-fried in a wok or a frying pan and is usually mixed with other ingredients such as eggs, vegetables, seafood, or meat. It is often eaten by itself or as an accompaniment to another dish. Fried rice is a popular component of East Asian, Southeast Asian and certain South Asian cuisines, as well as a staple national dish of Indonesia.

As a homemade dish, fried rice is typically made with ingredients left over from other dishes, leading to countless variations. It is thought to have originated in Yangzhou, in China's Jiangsu province, during the Sui dynasty; Yangzhou fried rice remains one of the best-known varieties.

Preparation

Cooked rice is the main ingredient; day-old rice that has dried in the refrigerator is preferred because its lower moisture content produces a better texture and prevents clumping. The rice is stir-fried over high heat with oil, commonly with beaten egg, diced vegetables such as peas, carrots and scallions, and proteins such as chicken, pork, shrimp or Chinese sausage (lap cheong). Soy sauce and other seasonings are added for flavour and colour.

Varieties

Well-known varieties include Yangzhou fried rice (with shrimp, ham and egg), Indonesian nasi goreng (sweetened with kecap manis), Thai khao pad, Japanese chāhan or yakimeshi, Korean bokkeum-bap, and Cantonese-style dishes such as Hokkien (Fujian) fried rice served with a thick sauce. In the Americas, variations such as Peruvian arroz chaufa reflect Chinese diaspora cuisines.
