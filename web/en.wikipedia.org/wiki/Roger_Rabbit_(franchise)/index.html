The Roger Rabbit franchise is an American media franchise centred on the cartoon rabbit Roger Rabbit, originating in Gary K. Wolf's 1981 novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit? and achieving worldwide fame with the 1988 live-action/animation hybrid film Who Framed Roger Rabbit, directed by Robert Zemeckis and produced by Walt Disney Pictures (released under its Touchstone banner) with Amblin Entertainment.

The 1988 film

Set in a 1947 Hollywood where animated characters ("toons") live alongside humans, the film follows hard-boiled detective Eddie Valiant (Bob Hoskins), hired to investigate cartoon star Roger Rabbit (voiced by Charles Fleischer), who is framed for murdering studio owner Marvin Acme. The film was a landmark in visual effects, seamlessly blending hand-drawn animation (supervised by Richard Williams) with live action, and famously brought Disney and Warner Bros. cartoon characters (Mickey Mouse and Bugs Bunny) together on screen. It won four Academy Awards, including Best Visual Effects, grossed over $329 million, and is credited with helping spark the Disney Renaissance.

Other media

The franchise expanded with three theatrical Roger Rabbit shorts — Tummy Trouble (1989), Roller Coaster Rabbit (1990), and Trail Mix-Up (1993) — screened before Disney features. Wolf wrote sequel novels, Who P-P-P-Plugged Roger Rabbit? (1991) and Who Wacked Roger Rabbit? (2013), closer in tone to the film. Roger Rabbit's Car Toon Spin, a dark ride, opened in Mickey's Toontown at Disneyland in 1994 and later at Tokyo Disneyland. Jessica Rabbit, Benny the Cab, Baby Herman, and the villainous Judge Doom remain popular characters in Disney merchandise and parks.

Sequel efforts

A direct sequel or prequel (including a project known as Roger Rabbit: The Toon Platoon) has been repeatedly developed and shelved since the early 1990s, owing to rights complexities between Disney and Amblin and the cost of the hand-drawn/live-action technique; the character's afterlife has instead continued through parks, cameo appearances, and re-releases.
