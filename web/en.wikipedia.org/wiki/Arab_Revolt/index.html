Arab Revolt

DateJune 1916 – October 1918
LocationHejaz, Transjordan, Syria (Ottoman Empire)
ResultArab victory; Ottoman loss of Arabia and the Levant
Key leadersHussein bin Ali, Faisal I, T. E. Lawrence
OpposingOttoman Empire

The Arab Revolt (Arabic: الثورة العربية, al-Thawra al-ʿArabiyya) was a military uprising of Arab forces against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War. It began in June 1916 when Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca, proclaimed the revolt, acting on the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence in which Britain appeared to promise Arab independence in exchange for support against the Ottomans.

Course of the revolt

Hussein's sons, notably Faisal and Abdullah, led the Arab forces. With British money, weapons, and advisers — most famously T. E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia") — the rebels seized Mecca, Jeddah, and Taif, then waged a guerrilla campaign against the Hejaz Railway, tying down Ottoman troops. In July 1917 they captured Aqaba, a turning point that allowed them to support the British Egyptian Expeditionary Force under General Allenby in Palestine.

Arab forces advanced up the east bank of the Jordan as Allenby's army won the Battle of Megiddo in September 1918. Faisal's army entered Damascus on 1 October 1918, shortly before the Armistice of Mudros ended Ottoman participation in the war.

Aftermath

The revolt's political hopes were thwarted by the secret Sykes–Picot Agreement (1916) between Britain and France and by the Balfour Declaration (1917). Faisal's Arab Kingdom of Syria was crushed by France at the Battle of Maysalun (1920); he was later made King of Iraq, while his brother Abdullah became Emir (later King) of Transjordan, and Hussein proclaimed himself King of the Hejaz — which was conquered by Ibn Saud's Nejd in 1924–25, uniting Saudi Arabia.

Legacy

The Arab Revolt remains a founding story of modern Arab nationalism. Its flag, designed with pan-Arab colours, inspired the flags of many Arab states. Lawrence's account, Seven Pillars of Wisdom, and the film Lawrence of Arabia (1962) made the revolt famous in the West.
