Julio C. Tello

Julio César Tello (April 11, 1880 – June 3, 1947) was a Peruvian archaeologist, regarded as the father of Peruvian archaeology. Of Indigenous Quechua-speaking background, he studied medicine in Lima and anthropology at Harvard, and dedicated his career to demonstrating the autochthonous origins of Andean civilisation.

Work

Tello identified the Chavín culture as the "mother culture" of the Andes following his 1919 investigations at Chavín de Huántar, and later defined the Paracas and early Nazca cultures through excavations of the Paracas necropolis, recovering famous Paracas textiles and trepanned skulls. He founded the Museum of Peruvian Archaeology in Lima and served in Peru's congress, advocating for the protection of national heritage.
