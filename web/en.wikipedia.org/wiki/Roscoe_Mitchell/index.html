Roscoe Mitchell

Roscoe Mitchell (born August 3, 1940) is an American composer, jazz instrumentalist, and educator who plays saxophone and flute, among many other instruments. A central figure in avant-garde jazz, he is a co-founder of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) in Chicago and of the Art Ensemble of Chicago.

Career

Mitchell grew up in Chicago and played in military bands while serving in the U.S. Army in Germany. His 1966 debut album Sound was a landmark of free jazz, noted for its use of silence, space, and unconventional instruments ("little instruments"). In 1969 he moved to Paris with the group that became the Art Ensemble of Chicago, which fused free improvisation with African music, theatre, and the slogan "Great Black Music — Ancient to the Future".

Later work

Mitchell has led numerous groups, including the Sound Ensemble and the Note Factory, written works for orchestra and chamber ensembles, and taught at institutions including Mills College, where he held the Darius Milhaud Chair in Composition. He was named an NEA Jazz Master in 2020.
