Genetically modified organism

A genetically modified organism (GMO) is any organism whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering techniques. The exact definition can vary: the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety uses the term "living modified organism", while food law in many jurisdictions applies the term to organisms modified in ways that do not occur naturally by mating or natural recombination.

History

The first genetically modified organism was a bacterium created by Herbert Boyer and Stanley Cohen in 1973. Rudolf Jaenisch created the first transgenic mouse in 1974. Commercial milestones include human insulin produced by GM bacteria (1982), the Flavr Savr tomato (1994), and widespread GM crops from 1996 onward.

Production

Creating a GMO generally involves isolating a gene of interest, combining it with regulatory sequences and a selectable marker, delivering the construct into cells (by transformation, transduction, or microinjection), and selecting and regenerating organisms carrying the modification. Genome editing tools such as CRISPR have made targeted changes faster and more precise.

Uses

GMOs are used in biological and medical research (including knockout mice and fluorescent reporter organisms), in the production of medicines such as insulin and vaccines, in agriculture (GM crops and, more rarely, GM livestock such as the AquAdvantage salmon), and increasingly in industrial biotechnology.

Controversy

Debates concern food safety, environmental release, gene flow, intellectual property, and ethical questions. Regulatory approaches range from process-based systems (as in the European Union) to product-based systems (as in the United States).
