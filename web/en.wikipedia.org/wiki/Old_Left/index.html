Old Left

A labour demonstration of the 1930s, the milieu of the Old Left.

The Old Left is a term used to describe Western left-wing political movements and parties that existed before and during the Second World War, in contrast to the New Left that emerged in the 1960s. In the United States the term commonly refers to socialist, communist, and labour movements from the late 19th century through the 1940s, including the Socialist Party of America, the Communist Party USA, Trotskyist groups, and the broader Popular Front milieu of the 1930s.

Characteristics

The Old Left was centred on the industrial working class, trade unions, and questions of economic organization: public ownership, workers' control, unemployment, and the redistribution of wealth. Its organizational style was hierarchical and party-based, and its culture was closely tied to immigrant communities, labour halls, and the union movement. Many Old Left organizations were connected, formally or informally, to the Communist International or to rival Marxist traditions, and disputes over the Soviet Union — especially Stalinism and its opponents — dominated its internal debates.

Contrast with the New Left

The New Left of the 1960s distinguished itself from the Old Left by emphasizing civil rights, the Vietnam War, participatory democracy, and later identity and cultural issues, and by rejecting bureaucratic party structures. Figures such as C. Wright Mills, in his "Letter to the New Left" (1960), announced the generational break. Historians of the American left often treat the Old Left as the last left movement deeply rooted in the institutional life of the working class, while noting continuities of personnel and ideas between the two formations.

Decline

In the United States the Old Left declined under the pressures of the Second Red Scare and McCarthyism, the revelations of Khrushchev's 1956 Secret Speech, deindustrialization's early stirrings, and the suburbanization and rising prosperity of the postwar workforce. Similar trajectories — though with strong surviving communist and socialist parties in France and Italy — occurred in Western Europe.
