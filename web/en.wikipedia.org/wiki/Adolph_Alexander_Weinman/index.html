Adolph Alexander Weinman (December 11, 1870 – August 8, 1952) was a German-born American sculptor and architectural sculptor. He designed two of the most celebrated United States coins: the Mercury dime (1916–1945) and the Walking Liberty half dollar (1916–1947), whose obverse was later reused for the American Silver Eagle bullion coin.

Career

Weinman immigrated to the United States as a teenager and studied at the Cooper Union and the Art Students League of New York, working in the studios of Augustus Saint-Gaudens and Daniel Chester French. Beyond coinage, he produced architectural sculpture for buildings including the Wisconsin State Capitol, the Louisiana State Capitol, and the Jefferson Memorial, as well as monumental works such as the sphinxes of the Scottish Rite Temple in Washington, D.C.

Legacy

His "Winged Liberty" dime design — popularly called the Mercury dime because Liberty's winged cap suggested the Roman god Mercury — is regarded as one of the finest American coin designs. Weinman also served on the U.S. Commission of Fine Arts in the 1920s.
