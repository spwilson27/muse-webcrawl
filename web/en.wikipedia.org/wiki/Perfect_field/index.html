Perfect field

In algebra, a perfect field is a field k such that every algebraic extension of k is separable. Equivalently, k is perfect if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

  • k has characteristic zero, or
  • k has characteristic p > 0 and every element of k is a pth power, that is, the Frobenius endomorphism is surjective.

Examples

Examples of perfect fields include every field of characteristic zero (such as the rational numbers), every finite field, and every algebraically closed field. The field of rational functions over a finite field is imperfect, since the variable is not a pth power.

Properties

Perfect fields are important in Galois theory: over a perfect field, every irreducible polynomial is separable, so finite extensions have primitive elements and the theory of the Galois correspondence applies cleanly. Every field of characteristic p embeds into a smallest perfect extension called its perfect closure, obtained by adjoining all pth roots iteratively.
