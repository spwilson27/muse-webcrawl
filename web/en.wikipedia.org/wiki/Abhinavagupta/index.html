Abhinavagupta

Abhinavagupta (c. 950 – c. 1016 CE) was a polymath philosopher, mystic, aesthetician and theologian from Kashmir, and the foremost systematiser of the Trika school of Kashmir Shaivism. One of the greatest intellectual figures of classical India, he wrote influential works on metaphysics, epistemology, tantra, poetics and dramaturgy.

Life

Born into a family of scholars in the valley of Kashmir, Abhinavagupta studied under as many as fifteen teachers, ranging across grammar, logic, Vedānta, Buddhism and the various Śaiva and Śākta tantric lineages. According to his disciple Kṣemarāja and later hagiography, he eventually withdrew with a band of followers into a cave (identified with the Bhairava cave near modern Beerwah), where he is said to have entered a meditative state and not returned.

Works and thought

His masterpiece, the Tantraloka ("Light on Tantra"), is a vast encyclopaedic synthesis of tantric ritual and philosophy. In the Īśvara-pratyabhijñā commentaries he gave classical form to the Pratyabhijñā ("Recognition") school: reality is the self-luminous consciousness of Śiva, and liberation is the recognition (pratyabhijñā) that one's own consciousness is identical with the divine. His aesthetic writings — the Abhinavabhāratī, a commentary on the Nāṭyaśāstra, and the Locana on Ānandavardhana's Dhvanyāloka — developed the rasa theory of aesthetic relish, holding that the tasting of rasa is akin to the tasting of brahman. He is credited with some forty works, many still unpublished or only recently edited.
