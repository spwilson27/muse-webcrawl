Opsin

Opsins are a group of proteins that are part of the family of G protein-coupled receptors and function as light-sensitive molecular switches. An opsin bound to the chromophore retinal forms a photopigment: when a photon isomerises the retinal from 11-cis to all-trans, the opsin changes shape and triggers a signalling cascade — the first step of vision.

Animal visual opsins

In vertebrate rod cells, the opsin rhodopsin mediates dim-light vision. Cone cells express photopsins tuned to different wavelengths: in humans, long-wave (red), medium-wave (green) and short-wave (blue) sensitive opsins enable trichromatic colour vision. Their genes (OPN1LW, OPN1MW, OPN1SW) are targets of common colour-vision deficiencies.

Non-visual opsins

Many opsins serve functions outside image formation. Melanopsin, found in intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells, entrains the circadian clock to the light–dark cycle. Others, including encephalopsin and neuropsin, occur in skin, brain and other tissues, in animals from fish to mammals.

Microbial opsins

Type I (microbial) opsins, such as bacteriorhodopsin and the channelrhodopsins of algae, are light-driven ion pumps or channels unrelated in sequence to animal opsins, but similar in structure. Channelrhodopsins are the key tools of optogenetics, in which neurons are genetically modified to respond to light.
