Kwanliso

Kwanliso (Korean: 관리소, "control centre") is the Korean term for the political prison camps in North Korea, in which persons deemed hostile to the regime — together with up to three generations of their families under the songbun classification system — are detained, often for life.

System

The camps, run by the Ministry of State Security, are divided into "total control zones", from which release is essentially impossible, and "revolutionary zones". Prisoners perform forced labour in mining, logging, farming and manufacturing under conditions of severe malnutrition and frequent abuse. Defectors' testimony and satellite imagery have documented camps such as Camp 14 (Kaechon), Camp 15 (Yodok), Camp 16 (Hwasong) and Camp 25 (Chongjin).

International response

The 2014 United Nations Commission of Inquiry on human rights in North Korea concluded that crimes against humanity, including extermination, enslavement and deliberate starvation, had been committed in the camps and recommended referral of the situation to the International Criminal Court. North Korea denies the camps' existence as political prisons.
