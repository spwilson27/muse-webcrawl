Delmonico steak

Delmonico steak is a preparation—and historically a specific cut—of beef steak associated with Delmonico's Restaurant in New York City, the celebrated 19th-century fine-dining institution run by the Delmonico family. The name has been applied inconsistently over time to several cuts, most often a thick, well-marbled boneless ribeye or a top sirloin / club steak.

Delmonico steak
Origin: Delmonico's, New York City
Era: Mid-19th century
Cut (variable): Ribeye, strip, or sirloin
Served: Grilled / broiled, often with Delmonico potatoes

History

Delmonico's, opened in the 1820s–30s and at its height located on Beaver Street and later Fifth Avenue, served a "Delmonico steak" cut, by many accounts, from the short loin / rib section, prepared simply to showcase quality beef. Chef Charles Ranhofer's The Epicurean (1894) describes Delmonico steaks cut from the rib sections. As the restaurant's fame spread, butchers elsewhere adopted the name for different premium cuts, creating lasting ambiguity.

Modern usage

Today a Delmonico is commonly a boneless ribeye (rib steak), sometimes a thick top sirloin, marketed by steakhouses and butchers as a tender, marbled, generously sized steak, typically grilled or broiled and served with sides such as Delmonico potatoes (a baked potato casserole with cream and cheese).
