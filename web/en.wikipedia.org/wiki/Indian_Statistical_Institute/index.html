Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata

The Indian Statistical Institute (ISI) is a research university and institute of national importance headquartered in Kolkata, India. Founded in 1931 by the statistician Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis, it grew out of the Statistical Laboratory at Presidency College and became central to the development of statistics in India, pioneering large-scale sample surveys and contributing to national economic planning.

History and work

Mahalanobis founded the journal Sankhyā in 1933 and developed the Mahalanobis distance and modern survey sampling methods at ISI. The institute's work underpinned India's National Sample Survey and the Second Five-Year Plan. In 1959 Parliament declared ISI an Institute of National Importance and empowered it to award degrees. Distinguished researchers associated with ISI include C. R. Rao, who derived the Cramér–Rao bound and Rao–Blackwell theorem while working there.

Organization

ISI has centres in Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Tezpur, and Giridih, and offers undergraduate and postgraduate programmes in statistics, mathematics, quantitative economics, computer science, and related fields, as well as extensive research in theoretical statistics, probability, economics, and computer science.
