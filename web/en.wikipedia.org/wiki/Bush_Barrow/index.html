Bush Barrow

Bush Barrow is a Bronze Age burial mound in the Normanton Down Barrows cemetery, near Stonehenge in Wiltshire, England. It is regarded as one of the richest Bronze Age burials in Britain, often described as the burial of a "king of Stonehenge".

Excavation and finds

The barrow was excavated in 1808 by William Cunnington under the patronage of Sir Richard Colt Hoare. The primary burial was of a tall man accompanied by exceptionally rich grave goods, including a large gold lozenge-shaped breastplate, a gold belt hook, bronze daggers (one with a handle decorated with thousands of tiny gold pins), a bronze axe, and a sceptre or macehead with bone mounts. The finds are displayed at the Wiltshire Museum in Devizes.

Significance

Dated to around 2000–1700 BC, the burial belongs to the Wessex culture and indicates a person of very high status with access to materials and craftsmanship from across Britain and continental Europe.
