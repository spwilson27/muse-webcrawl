Olympic weightlifting

CharacteristicsIndividual strength sport; barbell lifts: snatch and clean and jerk

Olympic weightlifting (often shortened to weightlifting) is a sport in which athletes compete in lifting a barbell loaded with weight plates from the ground to overhead, with the aim of successfully lifting the heaviest weights. Athletes compete in two specific ways of lifting the barbell overhead. The snatch is a wide-grip lift in which the weighted barbell is lifted overhead in one motion. The clean and jerk is a combination lift, in which the weight is first taken from the ground to the front of the shoulders (the clean), and then from the shoulders to overhead (the jerk).

Each lifter gets three attempts at the snatch and three at the clean and jerk, and the total of the best successful lift in each event counts toward the overall result. The sport is governed internationally by the International Weightlifting Federation (IWF), founded in 1905.

History

Competitions in lifting heavy stones date to antiquity, and weightlifting appeared at the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896 as part of the athletics programme, again in 1904, and as a standalone sport from 1920 onward. Women's weightlifting was added to the Olympic programme at the Sydney 2000 Games. Until 1972 a third lift, the clean and press, was also contested, but it was eliminated because of difficulties in judging proper form.

Competition

Lifters compete in bodyweight categories. The barbell is loaded in increments of at least one kilogram; lifters declare their opening attempts and may only increase the weight after a successful lift. If a lifter fails to make a valid lift in the snatch, they are eliminated from the competition. A lift is judged by three referees, and at least two must approve it for the lift to count.

Equipment

The men's Olympic barbell weighs 20 kg and the women's 15 kg. Weight plates follow a colour code: red 25 kg, blue 20 kg, yellow 15 kg, green 10 kg, white 5 kg, and smaller change plates down to 0.5 kg. Collars weigh 2.5 kg each.

Weight classes

Weight classes have been revised repeatedly by the IWF. Categories in use in recent years have included, for men, divisions beginning at 55 kg through to the super-heavyweight class above 109 kg, and for women, divisions from 45 kg to above 87 kg.
