2019 Turkish local elections

Election details
Date31 March 2019 (Istanbul re-run 23 June 2019)
Turnout84.66%
OfficesMetropolitan and district mayors, municipal councils, mukhtars
Key outcomeOpposition CHP won Istanbul, Ankara, İzmir, Antalya, Adana and Mersin

The 2019 Turkish local elections were held on 31 March 2019 across Turkey to elect mayors, municipal councillors and neighbourhood mukhtars. Taking place less than a year after President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's re-election under the new presidential system, the elections delivered a major blow to the ruling AK Party: the opposition Republican People's Party (CHP) won the mayoralties of Istanbul and Ankara — ending a quarter-century of Islamist-rooted rule in both cities — amid an economic downturn marked by recession and high inflation.

Campaign

The AK Party again campaigned in alliance (the People's Alliance) with the nationalist MHP, framing the elections as a matter of national "survival" (beka). The CHP and the nationalist İYİ Party formed the Nation Alliance, and the pro-Kurdish HDP strategically declined to field candidates in several western cities, urging its supporters to vote against the government. In Istanbul the CHP nominated the relatively little-known district mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu against former prime minister Binali Yıldırım.

Results

Nationwide, the People's Alliance won a majority of the total vote, and the AK Party retained many provinces, but the opposition captured the country's largest cities: Istanbul, Ankara (Mansur Yavaş), İzmir, Antalya, Adana and Mersin. İmamoğlu's Istanbul margin was razor-thin — about 13,700 votes — and the AK Party alleged irregularities.

Istanbul annulment and re-run

In May 2019 the Supreme Election Council controversially annulled the Istanbul metropolitan mayoral result and ordered a re-run, while leaving district results intact. The decision was widely criticised domestically and internationally. In the re-run on 23 June 2019, İmamoğlu won by a landslide of about 800,000 votes (54 per cent), a result seen as a landmark moment in Turkish politics and a springboard for his national profile.

Aftermath

Control of Istanbul and Ankara deprived the AK Party of major municipal resources and patronage networks; the new opposition mayors publicised alleged waste in city finances. The elections demonstrated the continued competitiveness of Turkish politics despite democratic backsliding concerns, and set the stage for the opposition's strategy in the 2023 general elections.
