The Aikikai Foundation (財団法人合気会) is the original organisation for the Japanese martial art of aikido, founded by the art's creator Morihei Ueshiba and headed by his descendants as Doshu ("master of the way"). It is the parent body of the largest grouping of aikido practitioners in the world, with affiliated organisations in more than 100 countries.

History

The foundation was officially recognised by the Japanese government in 1940, under the name Kobukai; after the Second World War it was re-established as the Aikikai. Kisshomaru Ueshiba, the founder's son, became the second Doshu on his father's death in 1969 and oversaw aikido's international spread. He was succeeded in 1999 by his son Moriteru Ueshiba, the third and current Doshu.

Hombu Dojo

The organisation's headquarters is the Aikikai Hombu Dojo in Shinjuku, Tokyo, a multi-storey dojo on the site of the original Kobukan Dojo opened by Ueshiba in 1931. The Doshu teaches there, and instructors (shihan) are dispatched worldwide from the Hombu. The Aikikai issues dan and kyu grades recognised by its member organisations.

Organisation and relations

Aikido splintered into several schools during and after the founder's lifetime — such as Yoshinkan, Iwama Ryu, Ki Society and Tomiki Aikido — but the Aikikai remains the mainstream lineage. The International Aikido Federation, founded in 1976, groups the Aikikai's national member organisations.
