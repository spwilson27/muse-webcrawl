Mount Taibai

Mount Taibai (Chinese: 太白山; pinyin: Tàibái Shān) is a mountain in Shaanxi Province, China, and the highest peak of the Qinling Mountains, rising to approximately 3,767 metres above sea level. Its name, "Great White," refers to its snow-covered summit.

Geography and nature

The Qinling range forms the watershed between the Yellow River and Yangtze River basins and a major climatic divide between northern and southern China. Mount Taibai's great elevation range gives it rich vertical biodiversity, from warm-temperate forest at its base to alpine meadows near the top; glacial cirque lakes such as Daye Hai mark its summit area. The mountain and surrounding forests are protected within Taibaishan nature reserves, which shelter species such as the giant panda, golden takin, and golden snub-nosed monkey.

Culture

Taibai has long been a site of Daoist practice and pilgrimage, dotted with temples, and is associated in folklore with the poet Li Bai (also read Li Taibai, after the mountain and the planet Venus, the "Great White").
