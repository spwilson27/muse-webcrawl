Group delay and phase delay

Group delay and phase delay are two measures of the time delay experienced by a signal passing through a linear time-invariant system such as a filter, a transmission line or an acoustic medium. Both are derived from the system's phase response φ(ω), but they answer different questions: phase delay describes the delay of a single sinusoidal component, while group delay describes the delay of the envelope of a modulated signal or pulse.

Definitions

For a system with transfer function H(jω) = |H(jω)| ejφ(ω), the phase delay at angular frequency ω is

τφ(ω) = −φ(ω) / ω,

the time by which a pure sinusoid of that frequency is shifted. The group delay is

τg(ω) = −dφ(ω) / dω,

the negative slope of the phase response, which gives the delay of the envelope (the "group") of a narrowband signal centred at ω.

Interpretation

If the phase response is exactly linear in ω, the two delays are equal and constant, and the system delays all signals without distorting their shape. Deviations — a nonlinear phase response — make group delay vary with frequency, so different parts of a pulse's spectrum arrive at different times, smearing the pulse. This distortion is a central concern in the design of audio crossovers, data-communication filters, radar systems and optical fibres.

Applications

Group delay is specified and measured for filters (Bessel filters are prized for their maximally flat group delay), antennas, loudspeakers and long transmission paths. In seismology and optics, dispersion — the frequency dependence of propagation speed — is expressed through the difference between group velocity and phase velocity, the propagation analogues of the two delays.
