René Leduc

Born1898, France
Died1968
NationalityFrench
Known forRamjet-powered aircraft (Leduc 0.10, 0.21, 022)
FieldAeronautical engineering

René Leduc (1898–1968) was a French engineer and aircraft designer who devoted his career to ramjet propulsion. Beginning with patents filed in the 1930s, he designed a series of experimental aircraft in which the fuselage itself formed the ramjet duct, with the pilot seated inside the intake.

Aircraft

Development was interrupted by the Second World War, but after the war the Leduc 0.10 flew in 1949, carried aloft by a mother aircraft because a ramjet cannot produce thrust at standstill. The larger Leduc 0.21 followed in 1953, and the supersonic interceptor prototype Leduc 022 flew in 1956–57. The programme was cancelled in 1958 in favour of the Dassault Mirage III.

Design

Leduc's aircraft were striking: long cylindrical fuselages with annular combustion chambers, and a small glazed cockpit capsule in the centre of the nose intake. His work established French leadership in early ramjet experimentation.
