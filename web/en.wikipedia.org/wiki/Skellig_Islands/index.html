Skellig Islands

The Skellig Islands (Irish: Na Scealaga) are two steep, rocky islands in the Atlantic Ocean off the Iveragh Peninsula in County Kerry, Ireland. The larger island, Skellig Michael (Sceilg Mhichíl), rises 217 metres above sea level and is famous for its remarkably preserved early Christian monastery; the smaller, Little Skellig (Sceilg Bheag), is closed to the public and hosts one of the world's largest colonies of northern gannets.

Skellig Michael

The monastery on Skellig Michael, founded between the 6th and 8th centuries and dedicated to Saint Michael, sits on a ledge near the summit, reached by steep stone staircases. It comprises beehive-shaped stone huts (clochán), the Church of St Michael, a monks' graveyard, and oratories, all built with dry-stone construction. The site survived Viking raids in the 9th century and remained occupied until the monks moved to Ballinskelligs on the mainland in the 12th century. Skellig Michael was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

Wildlife

Little Skellig supports tens of thousands of breeding gannets, while Skellig Michael hosts large colonies of Manx shearwaters and Atlantic puffins. Both islands are protected as a national nature reserve and Special Protection Area.

Access and culture

Boat trips from Portmagee, Valentia, and Ballinskelligs land visitors on Skellig Michael in calm weather during the summer season; visitor numbers are limited to protect the site. The island's dramatic scenery has appeared in popular culture, notably as the location of Luke Skywalker's hermitage in the films Star Wars: The Force Awakens and Star Wars: The Last Jedi.
