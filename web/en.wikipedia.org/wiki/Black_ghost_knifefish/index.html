Black ghost knifefish

The black ghost knifefish (Apteronotus albifrons) is a tropical freshwater fish native to South America, found in the Amazon and Paraná river basins and in the Pantanal. It is black in colour with a white stripe on its nose and a white band at the end of its tail, and it swims by undulating a long fin on its underside, moving forwards and backwards with equal ease. It can grow to about 50 cm (20 in) in length.

Electroreception

Like other knifefishes, the black ghost is a weakly electric fish: an organ in its tail generates a continuous high-frequency electric field, and electroreceptors over its body detect distortions of that field caused by nearby objects. This electrolocation allows the nocturnal fish to hunt insect larvae and small crustaceans in muddy water in total darkness. Local folklore held that the fish was inhabited by the ghosts of fallen warriors, giving it its common name.

In aquaria

The species is a popular, if demanding, aquarium fish. It requires a large tank, hiding places, and soft, slightly acidic water, and it is generally peaceful but will eat smaller fish.
