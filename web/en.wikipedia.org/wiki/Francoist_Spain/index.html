Francoist Spain

Francoist Spain (Spanish: España franquista) was the period of Spanish history between 1939 and 1975, when Spain was ruled by dictator Francisco Franco. Officially the Spanish State, the regime emerged from the Nationalist victory in the Spanish Civil War.

Character of the regime

Franco's government was authoritarian and nationalist, built around the single party FET y de las JONS, censorship, political repression, Spanish national-Catholicism and, initially, economic autarky. Tens of thousands of Republicans were executed or imprisoned in the postwar repression.

Evolution

After isolation in the 1940s, Cold War alignment brought US bases and UN admission in the 1950s. Technocrats liberalised the economy from 1959, producing the "Spanish miracle" of rapid growth, tourism and emigration in the 1960s, while political liberalisation remained minimal. Franco designated Juan Carlos as his successor in 1969; on Franco's death on 20 November 1975, Spain began its transition to democracy.
