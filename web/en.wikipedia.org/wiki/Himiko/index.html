Reignc. 180 – c. 248 AD

Himiko (c. 170 – 248 AD), also known as Pimiko, was a shamaness-queen of Yamatai-koku in Wa (ancient Japan). Early Chinese dynastic histories, principally the Records of Wei (part of the Records of the Three Kingdoms, late 3rd century), describe her rule and her diplomacy with the Cao Wei state.

Historical accounts

According to the Chinese chronicles, after decades of warfare among the small kingdoms of Wa, the people agreed upon a woman as their ruler. Himiko, said to have occupied herself with magic and sorcery, remained unmarried and secluded in a fortified palace guarded by a thousand women attendants, communicating through a single male intermediary, identified as her younger brother. She sent embassies to the Wei court in 238, 243 and 247, receiving in return the title "Queen of Wa, Friendly to Wei" and gifts including a gold seal and one hundred bronze mirrors.

When Himiko died, a great mound was raised for her and, the chronicle says, more than a hundred attendants were buried with her. A king who was set up in her place failed to command obedience, and order was restored only when a thirteen-year-old girl, Iyo, was enthroned.

Debates

The location of Yamatai remains one of the most contested questions in early Japanese history, with scholars divided between northern Kyushu and the Kinai (Yamato) region. Himiko has also been variously identified with figures of later Japanese tradition, such as Empress Jingū or the sun goddess Amaterasu, though these identifications are speculative. The huge keyhole-shaped tomb Hashihaka Kofun in Nara Prefecture has been proposed as her burial mound.

In popular culture

Himiko appears frequently in Japanese fiction, manga, anime and video games, often portrayed as a powerful shaman queen.
