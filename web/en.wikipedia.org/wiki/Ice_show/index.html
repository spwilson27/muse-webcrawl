Ice show

Ice show
TypeTheatrical entertainment on ice
Notable productionsIce Follies, Holiday on Ice, Disney on Ice, Ice Capades

An ice show is an entertainment production performed by figure skaters on an ice surface, combining skating with theatrical elements such as musical numbers, elaborate costumes, scenery, chorus lines, comedy routines, and specialty acts. Unlike competitive figure skating, ice shows emphasise entertainment and spectacle over judged athletic difficulty.

History

Exhibition skating appeared in music halls in the early twentieth century, and Sonja Henie's films and tours popularised the format in the 1930s. The great touring revues — the Ice Follies (from 1936), Ice Capades (from 1940), and Holiday on Ice (from 1943) — played arenas across North America and Europe for decades and were the principal professional outlet for competitive skaters.

The genre adapted with the times: Disney on Ice (from 1981) built story-based productions around licensed characters, while gala exhibitions attached to competitions and tours such as Stars on Ice showcased champion skaters. Modern ice shows may be performed in theatres, in arenas, on cruise ships, and even on synthetic or outdoor ice.
