Drift mining

Drift mining is method of accessing valuable geological material, such as coal or ore, by following a nearly horizontal seam or vein into the side of a hill or mountain. The horizontal tunnel used for access is called a drift, and it contrasts with shaft mining, in which access is by vertical shafts, and slope mining, which uses inclined tunnels.

Method

A drift is driven into the hillside at or near the level of the seam, allowing miners, equipment and extracted material to move in and out on a gentle grade, often by rail or conveyor. Because the workings are above the drainage level, water flows out of the mine by gravity, avoiding much of the pumping needed in shaft mines.

Uses

Drift mining has been widely used in coal mining in regions such as Appalachia, where seams outcrop along hillsides, and in placer mining, where ancient river gravels buried in hills — as in the California gold country — are reached by drifts driven to the buried channels.
