Gustafson's law

Gustafson's law
Field: Parallel computing
Proposed by: John L. Gustafson
Year: 1988

Gustafson's law (or Gustafson–Barsis's law) is a principle in computer engineering which states that computations involving arbitrarily large data sets can be efficiently parallelised. It offers a counterpoint to Amdahl's law, which focuses on a fixed workload.

Formulation

Proposed by John L. Gustafson in 1988, the law gives scaled speedup: S = s + p × N, where N is the number of processors, s is the serial fraction of the scaled workload, and p is the parallel fraction. As problem size grows with the number of processors, the serial portion becomes proportionally less significant.

Comparison with Amdahl's law

Amdahl's law assumes a fixed problem size, so speedup is limited by the serial fraction. Gustafson's law assumes the problem size scales with available processors, reflecting practical usage where larger machines are used to solve larger problems in the same time.

Applications

The law is widely cited in high-performance computing to justify large-scale parallel systems and weak scaling benchmarks.
