Great Blizzard of 1888

The Great Blizzard of 1888, also known as the Great Blizzard of '88 or the Great White Hurricane (March 11–14, 1888), was one of the most severe recorded blizzards in American history. The storm paralyzed the East Coast of the United States from the Chesapeake Bay to Maine, as well as the Atlantic provinces of Canada.

The storm

Snowfalls of 20 to 60 inches (51 to 152 cm) fell in parts of New Jersey, New York, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and Connecticut, and sustained winds of more than 45 miles per hour (72 km/h) produced snowdrifts in excess of 50 feet (15 m). Railroads were shut down and people were confined to their houses for up to a week. Emergency services were completely disabled, and communication lines were downed: the storm helped motivate the development of underground utility wiring in cities such as New York and Boston.

More than 400 people died in the storm, including over 100 seamen, as 200 vessels were grounded or wrecked along the coasts of New England. Telegraph infrastructure was destroyed, isolating cities. In New York, transportation was crippled; the blizzard gave impetus to the construction of the city's subway system, which opened in 1904.
