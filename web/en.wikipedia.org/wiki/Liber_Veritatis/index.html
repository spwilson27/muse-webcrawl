Liber Veritatis

The Liber Veritatis, or Book of Truth, is a portfolio of drawings recording the works of the French landscape painter Claude Lorrain (Claude Gellée, 1600–1682). Begun in the mid-1630s and maintained for the rest of his life, it comprises some 195 drawings, each reproducing one of Claude's paintings in pen, ink and wash.

Purpose

Claude kept the book so that he could authenticate his own compositions and distinguish them from the many imitations and forgeries circulating among collectors. On the back of each drawing he noted the name of the patron or owner for whom the corresponding painting was made. The drawings have since become an indispensable tool for establishing Claude's oeuvre and chronology.

Later history

The volume passed through several collections and was acquired by the British Museum in 1957, where it remains. In the 18th century the drawings were engraved by Richard Earlom and published in London, popularising Claude's compositions; J. M. W. Turner later produced his own Liber Studiorum in conscious imitation of the project.
