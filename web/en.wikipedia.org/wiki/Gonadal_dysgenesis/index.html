Gonadal dysgenesis

Gonadal dysgenesis is a group of congenital conditions belonging to the class of disorders of sex development, in which the gonads (ovaries or testes) fail to develop normally and are replaced by functionless, fibrous tissue known as streak gonads. Because the gonads do not produce normal amounts of sex hormones or gametes, affected individuals typically have underdeveloped secondary sexual characteristics and infertility.

Types and causes

Gonadal dysgenesis occurs in several karyotypic settings. In Turner syndrome (45,X), ovarian development fails. In Swyer syndrome (46,XY complete gonadal dysgenesis), mutations in genes such as SRY, MAP3K1, or NR5A1 prevent testis determination; individuals have a female phenotype with a uterus but streak gonads. Pure gonadal dysgenesis also occurs with a normal 46,XX karyotype, sometimes due to mutations in the FSH receptor or other ovarian-development genes. Mixed gonadal dysgenesis (often 45,X/46,XY mosaicism) presents with one streak gonad and one dysgenetic testis.

Clinical features and management

Presentation is commonly in adolescence with delayed puberty or primary amenorrhea. Streak gonads containing Y-chromosome material carry a substantial risk of gonadoblastoma, so prophylactic gonadectomy is usually recommended in XY cases. Hormone replacement therapy induces puberty and maintains bone and cardiovascular health; psychological support and fertility counselling (including oocyte donation for some patients) are standard parts of care.
