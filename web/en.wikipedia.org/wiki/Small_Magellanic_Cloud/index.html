The Small Magellanic Cloud (SMC), or Nubecula Minor, is a dwarf galaxy near the Milky Way. Classified as a dwarf barred spiral galaxy, it has a diameter of about 18,900 light-years and contains several hundred million stars. Located some 200,000 light-years away in the constellation Tucana, it is one of the most distant objects visible to the naked eye and, with the Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC), forms a pair of satellite galaxies named after Ferdinand Magellan, whose crew observed them during the first circumnavigation (1519–1522), though they were known to southern peoples for millennia and described by earlier travellers such as Amerigo Vespucci.

Structure and contents

The SMC has a central bar structure and a deep, elongated three-dimensional shape: Cepheid variable measurements show its near and far sides differ by up to ~20,000 light-years in distance, evidence that tidal interactions with the LMC and the Milky Way are pulling it apart. A stream of gas, the Magellanic Bridge, connects the two Clouds, and the trailing Magellanic Stream arcs around the Milky Way. Notable objects include the bright globular cluster 47 Tucanae (a foreground Milky Way object in the same line of sight), the open cluster NGC 346 — a vigorous star-forming region — and the supernova remnant 1E 0102.2-7219.

Scientific importance

Working at Harvard College Observatory, Henrietta Swan Leavitt discovered the period–luminosity relation of Cepheid variables by studying SMC stars (announced 1908–1912), providing the first reliable "standard candle" for measuring cosmic distances. Because the SMC is metal-poor (roughly one-fifth the Sun's metallicity), it serves as a local analogue for galaxies in the early universe, and its stellar populations are intensively studied to understand star formation in low-metallicity environments.

Future

The SMC orbits the LMC, and the pair are falling toward the Milky Way. Models suggest the SMC may eventually be absorbed, and the LMC itself is expected to collide with the Milky Way in a few billion years.
