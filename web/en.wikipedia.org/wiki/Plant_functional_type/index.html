Plant functional type

A plant functional type (PFT) is a grouping of plant species that share similar functional traits and play similar roles in ecosystem processes, regardless of their taxonomic relationships. PFTs are the basic units used to represent vegetation in dynamic global vegetation models (DGVMs) and Earth system models, where it would be impossible to model every species individually.

Definition

Typical PFT classifications combine life form (tree, shrub, grass), leaf type (needleleaf or broadleaf), phenology (evergreen or deciduous), and climate tolerance (tropical, temperate, boreal). Examples used in models such as LPJ-GUESS include "tropical broadleaf evergreen tree", "temperate needleleaf evergreen tree", and "C4 grass". Each PFT is assigned parameter values for photosynthesis, allocation, mortality, and establishment.

Purpose and criticism

Grouping by function lets models simulate how vegetation cover, carbon uptake, and water fluxes respond to climate change at continental scales. Critics note that PFT schemes may miss trait diversity within groups, underestimate the importance of rare species, and struggle to represent novel ecosystems; newer approaches use continuous plant trait distributions instead of discrete types.
