Quebec (French: Québec) is one of the thirteen provinces and territories of Canada. It is the largest province by area and the second-most populous, with about 8.5–9 million people, the great majority French-speaking — the only province where French is the sole official language and francophones the majority. Its capital is Quebec City, founded by Samuel de Champlain in 1608 and the only walled city in North America north of Mexico; its metropolis is Montreal.

History

New France grew along the St. Lawrence River from 1608 until Britain conquered it in the Seven Years' War (the Battle of the Plains of Abraham, 1759; ceded 1763). The Quebec Act of 1774 preserved French civil law and Catholic rights, anchoring a distinct society that survived within Confederation (1867) as a province. The "Quiet Revolution" of the 1960s modernised and secularised Quebec, nationalised hydroelectricity (Hydro-Québec) and asserted French through laws like the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977). Sovereignty referendums failed narrowly twice — 1980 (59.6% No) and 1995 (50.6% No) — leaving Quebec's status Canada's defining constitutional question, and its culture (from Cirque du Soleil to Céline Dion) a global export.
