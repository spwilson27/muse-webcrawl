Assistant referee (association football)

In association football, an assistant referee (often still called a linesman) is one of the match officials who assist the referee in controlling a match under the Laws of the Game. Two assistant referees are appointed to a standard match, each patrolling one touchline and one half of the pitch, carrying a brightly coloured flag used to signal decisions.

Duties

The assistant referee's principal duties, set out in Law 6 of the Laws of the Game, are to indicate:

  • when the whole of the ball has left the field of play (throw-in, goal kick or corner kick), and which team is entitled to the restart;
  • when a player should be penalised for an offside offence — historically the most scrutinised part of the role, since the assistant must stay level with the second-last opponent or the ball;
  • fouls and misconduct the referee may not have seen, including offences near the goal line;
  • whether, at a penalty kick, the goalkeeper moved off the goal line before the ball was struck;
  • when a substitution is requested, and help with administrative and procedural checks.

Assistant referees signal with flag movements — raising the flag straight up for offside, a flick for a throw-in, and distinctive positions to indicate which part of the field an offside occurred in. Decisions remain advisory: the referee makes the final call, though in practice assistants' offside and ball-out-of-play signals are almost always followed.

The officiating team

In senior matches the assistants are joined by a fourth official, and in top competitions by additional assistant referees behind each goal and by a video assistant referee (VAR) team. The title "assistant referee" replaced "linesman" and "lineswoman" in 1996 to reflect the broader responsibilities of the role. The famous "diagonal system of control" has the referee run a diagonal while assistants cover the opposite touchlines, maximising coverage of the field.
