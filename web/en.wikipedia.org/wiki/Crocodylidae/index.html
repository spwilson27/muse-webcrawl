Crocodylidae

Crocodylidae is a family of true crocodiles within the order Crocodilia. It includes the genera Crocodylus (the most widespread), Mecistops (slender-snouted crocodiles), and Osteolaemus (dwarf crocodiles), totalling roughly 17–18 recognised living species.

Characteristics

True crocodiles have broad, triangular heads and, when the jaws are closed, the enlarged fourth tooth of the lower jaw fits into a notch outside the upper jaw and remains visible — a feature distinguishing them from alligators. They possess salt-excreting glands, allowing several species, notably the saltwater crocodile, to inhabit marine environments.

Distribution

Members of the family occur in tropical regions of Africa, Asia, Australia, and the Americas, inhabiting rivers, lakes, wetlands, and coasts. Many species are threatened by habitat loss and hunting, though several have recovered under conservation programmes.
