Yevgeny Prigozhin

Born: 1 June 1961, Leningrad, Russian SFSR, USSR
Died: 23 August 2023 (aged 62), Tver Oblast, Russia
Occupation: Oligarch, mercenary leader
Known for: Founder and leader of the Wagner Group

Yevgeny Viktorovich Prigozhin (1 June 1961 – 23 August 2023) was a Russian oligarch and mercenary leader who founded and commanded the Wagner Group, a private military company that fought in Ukraine, Syria, and several African countries. A former convict who built a catering empire in Saint Petersburg, he became known as "Putin's chef" because his companies catered Kremlin events for president Vladimir Putin.

Early life and business career

Prigozhin was imprisoned in 1981 after convictions for robbery and related offences, serving nine years. After his release he sold hot dogs and then built a chain of restaurants and a catering conglomerate, Concord Management and Consulting. Concord won lucrative contracts to supply food to the Russian military and to Moscow schools, and Prigozhin personally served heads of state at Kremlin banquets.

Internet Research Agency and Wagner

United States investigations concluded that Prigozhin financed the Internet Research Agency, a Saint Petersburg "troll farm" accused of interfering in the 2016 US presidential election; he was indicted by special counsel Robert Mueller in 2018. The Wagner Group, founded around 2014, deployed to Crimea and eastern Ukraine, then to Syria in support of the Assad government, and later to Libya, Mali, the Central African Republic, and elsewhere, often exchanging military services for access to gold and diamond mining concessions.

Ukraine war and mutiny

During Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Wagner forces — increasingly recruited from Russian prisons in exchange for pardons — played a central role in the battle for Bakhmut. Prigozhin publicly feuded with the Russian Ministry of Defence, accusing it of starving his fighters of ammunition. On 23 June 2023 he launched an armed mutiny, seizing military facilities in Rostov-on-Don and marching toward Moscow before standing down under a deal brokered by Belarusian leader Alexander Lukashenko.

Death

Exactly two months later, on 23 August 2023, Prigozhin was killed when the business jet carrying him and other Wagner leaders crashed in Tver Oblast. Western governments widely suspected the crash was an assassination, which the Kremlin denied. He was buried in Saint Petersburg.
