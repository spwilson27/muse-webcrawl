A gelding is a castrated male horse or other equine, such as a pony, donkey or a mule. Castration, as well as the elimination of hormonally driven behavior associated with a stallion, allows a male horse to be calmer and better-behaved, making the animal quieter, gentler and potentially more suitable as an everyday working animal. The gerund and participle "gelding" and the infinitive "to geld" refer to the castration procedure itself.

History and terminology

The practice of gelding horses dates to antiquity; the Scythians were among the peoples credited with early use of castration of male horses. A male horse is normally gelded young, often between six and twelve months of age, unless it is intended for breeding or is unusually valuable or well-behaved. A horse castrated after maturity may retain some stallion-like behavior; a horse that is castrated but retains one undescended testicle is called a rig.

Reasons for gelding

Only a small fraction of male horses are needed as breeding stock, and stallions require specialized management because testosterone-driven aggression and mating behavior can make them difficult and dangerous to handle. Geldings are preferred for most riding, driving, police, and lesson-horse work, can be turned out with other horses, and generally form the majority of horses in most equestrian disciplines. Geldings are permitted in nearly all competition classes, and many notable racehorses and Olympic horses have been geldings.

Procedure

Gelding is a surgical procedure performed by a veterinarian, with the horse standing under sedation and local anesthesia or lying under general anesthesia. The testicles are removed through scrotal incisions, with the spermatic cord crushed and cut, commonly with emasculators; the incisions are usually left open to drain. Complications can include swelling, bleeding, infection, and, rarely, eventration of intestine.

Alternatives and concerns

Alternatives include immunocastration by vaccination against GnRH. Animal welfare discussions consider pain management, timing, and aftercare; modern practice emphasizes anesthesia and analgesia.
