Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum

The Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum (Chinese: 明孝陵; pinyin: Míng Xiào Líng) is the tomb of the Hongwu Emperor (Zhu Yuanzhang), the founder of the Ming dynasty of China, and his wife Empress Ma. It lies at the southern foot of Purple Mountain in Nanjing, the dynasty's first capital. Construction began in 1381 and continued for some twenty-five years, reportedly employing about 100,000 workers.

Layout

The mausoleum broke with tradition: rather than the square plan of earlier imperial tombs, its walls follow the contours of the mountain in a roughly circular or "dipper" shape, with the burial mound integrated into the natural hillside. The complex is approached along a Sacred Way famous for its stone sculptures — four pairs of stone officers and twelve pairs of stone animals, including lions, xiezhi, camels, elephants, qilin and horses, some standing and some kneeling. A second section, with pairs of ornamental pillars and stone officers, turns to align with the main tomb axis.

Buildings

The tomb precinct includes the Golden Water Bridges, the main gate, the Square City (Sifangcheng) pavilion housing a giant tortoise-borne stele extolling the emperor's merits, the Sacrificial Hall site, and the Soul Tower (Ming Lou) standing before the circular burial mound. Many wooden structures were destroyed during the Taiping Rebellion and only partly rebuilt under the Qing.

Heritage status

Ming Xiaoling is the largest of the Ming imperial tombs and served as the model for the Ming tombs near Beijing. In 2003 it was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties".
