Three Marys

The Three Marys
Subject: New Testament figures
Associated events: Crucifixion, Resurrection of Jesus
Venerated in: Christianity

The Three Marys (also spelled Three Maries) are women named Mary who appear in the canonical Gospels in connection with the Crucifixion and Resurrection of Jesus. In Western Christian tradition they are usually identified as Mary Magdalene, Mary of Clopas, and Mary Salome (the mother of the apostles James and John). They are traditionally depicted as present at the foot of the cross and as the first witnesses to arrive at the empty tomb on Easter morning, carrying spices and ointments to anoint the body of Jesus — hence their depiction as myrrh-bearers in Eastern Christianity.

The Three Marys at the sepulchre, a frequent subject of Renaissance art.

Identification

The Gospel accounts name different groups of women. Matthew names Mary Magdalene and "the other Mary"; Mark adds Salome; Luke speaks of Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James; John places Mary the mother of Jesus, her sister Mary of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene at the cross. Medieval harmonisation grouped these into three principal figures, although the inclusion of the Virgin Mary herself as one of the three at the tomb is less common in Western iconography.

Traditions

According to a Provençal legend, the Three Marys — Mary Magdalene, Mary Salome, and Mary of Clopas, sometimes with their servant Sarah — landed by boat at the mouth of the Rhône after the Crucifixion, giving their name to the town of Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer, which remains a major pilgrimage site, especially for the Romani people. In Spain and the Philippines, processions of the Tres Marías form part of Holy Week observances, and in Easter celebrations the three women feature in the traditional Salubong (meeting) rite.
