Kurt Alois Josef Johann von Schuschnigg (14 December 1897 – 18 November 1977) was an Austrian statesman who served as Chancellor of the Federal State of Austria from 1934 until the Anschluss with Nazi Germany in March 1938. He succeeded Engelbert Dollfuss, who was assassinated by Austrian Nazis during the July Putsch.

Early life

Schuschnigg was born in Riva del Garda (then in Austria-Hungary) into a military family and was raised Catholic and monarchist. He served as an artillery officer in the First World War, was captured by the Italians, studied law at Innsbruck, and practised as a lawyer. Elected to the National Council in 1927 for the Christian Social Party, he became Minister of Justice (1932) and Minister of Education under Dollfuss.

Chancellorship

After Dollfuss's assassination on 25 July 1934, Schuschnigg continued the Austrofascist Ständestaat under the Fatherland Front, seeking to preserve Austrian independence with Italian support. As Mussolini drew closer to Hitler, Austria became isolated; the July Agreement of 1936 forced Schuschnigg to accept German influence, amnesty Austrian Nazis and admit them into his government.

Summoned to Berchtesgaden on 12 February 1938, Schuschnigg was bullied by Hitler into further concessions, including appointing the Nazi Arthur Seyss-Inquart as interior minister. In a last attempt to save independence, he announced a plebiscite on Austrian independence for 13 March 1938 under the slogan "Für ein freies und deutsches, unabhängiges und soziales, für ein christliches und geeintes Österreich!" Hitler responded with an ultimatum; Schuschnigg resigned on 11 March, broadcasting a farewell address ending "Gott schütze Österreich" ("God protect Austria"). German troops entered the next day.

Later life

Schuschnigg was imprisoned by the Nazis, held in concentration camps including Dachau and Sachsenhausen, and liberated by American troops in 1945. He emigrated to the United States, taught political science at Saint Louis University (1948–1967), and later returned to Austria, where he died in Innsbruck in 1977.
