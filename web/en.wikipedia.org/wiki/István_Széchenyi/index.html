Count István Széchenyi (21 September 1791 – 8 April 1860) was a Hungarian politician, political theorist and writer, widely considered one of the greatest statesmen in Hungarian history. Lajos Kossuth famously dubbed him "the Greatest Hungarian".

Life

Born into the wealthy and influential Széchenyi family in Vienna, he served as a cavalry officer in the Napoleonic Wars and travelled widely in Western Europe, becoming convinced that Hungary lagged behind and needed modernisation. At the Diet of 1825 he offered a full year's income of his estates to found a learned society, a gesture that led to the creation of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.

Reforms

Széchenyi's writings Hitel ("Credit", 1830), Világ ("Light", 1831) and Stádium (1833) attacked feudal privileges and advocateded credit reform, abolition of entail, and economic development. His practical projects included regulation of the Danube and Tisza rivers, steam navigation on the Danube and Lake Balaton, the first National Casino, horse racing and the Széchenyi Chain Bridge between Buda and Pest (opened 1849). He favoured gradual reform in cooperation with Vienna, which put him in rivalry with Kossuth's more radical nationalism.

Later years

In the Revolution of 1848 Széchenyi served as Minister of Transport in the Batthyány government, but the conflict between Hungary and the Habsburgs drove him into a nervous breakdown; he spent most of his remaining years in the Döbling sanatorium near Vienna, where he continued to write. He died by suicide in 1860.

Legacy

Széchenyi's name is borne by the Chain Bridge, Budapest's Széchenyi thermal baths and the Széchenyi National Library; his portrait appears on the Hungarian 5,000-forint banknote.
