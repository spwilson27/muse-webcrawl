Water supply and sanitation in Iran

Water supply and sanitation in Iran has achieved high levels of access by regional standards, with almost the entire urban population connected to piped water, but the sector faces severe water scarcity, heavy reliance on groundwater, and underinvestment in wastewater collection and treatment.

Resources and access

Iran is largely arid or semi-arid, with average annual rainfall of about 250 mm, a third of the world average. Historically, underground aqueducts called qanats supplied towns and farms; today dams, deep wells and inter-basin transfers dominate. Access to an improved water source is near-universal in cities and high in rural areas, while sewerage coverage lags — many cities historically relied on septic tanks and absorption wells, though wastewater networks have expanded rapidly since the 1990s.

Institutions

Services are provided by provincial water and wastewater companies under the Ministry of Energy, with policy support from the National Water and Wastewater Engineering Company. Tariffs are heavily subsidised and have historically been too low to cover costs.

Challenges

Over-extraction of aquifers for agriculture — which consumes around 90 percent of water withdrawals — has caused falling water tables, land subsidence and the drying of wetlands such as Lake Urmia. Drought, population growth and climate change have intensified competition among cities, farmers and ecosystems, making efficiency and wastewater reuse national priorities.
