Megaloblastic anemia

Megaloblastic anemia is a type of macrocytic anemia characterised by the presence of abnormally large red blood cell precursors, called megaloblasts, in the bone marrow. It results from impaired DNA synthesis, most commonly due to deficiency of vitamin B12 (cobalamin) or folate (vitamin B9), while RNA and protein synthesis proceed relatively unimpaired, producing a characteristic nuclear–cytoplasmic asynchrony.

Signs and symptoms

General features of anemia include fatigue, pallor, dyspnoea and palpitations. Megaloblastic anemia may additionally present with a smooth, sore tongue (glossitis), mild jaundice from ineffective erythropoiesis, and, in the case of B12 deficiency, neurological symptoms such as peripheral neuropathy, difficulty with balance, and subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord, which may become irreversible if untreated.

Causes

Causes include dietary insufficiency (particularly of B12 in strict vegan diets), malabsorption (including pernicious anemia, an autoimmune condition destroying gastric parietal cells and intrinsic factor), gastrectomy or ileal disease, increased demand such as in pregnancy (for folate), and drugs that interfere with folate metabolism, such as methotrexate, or with B12 absorption, such as metformin and proton pump inhibitors.

Diagnosis

The mean corpuscular volume (MCV) is elevated, typically above 100 fL. The peripheral blood smear shows macro-ovalocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils (more than five lobes). Bone marrow examination reveals hypercellular marrow with megaloblastic erythroid precursors. Serum B12 and folate levels, methylmalonic acid and homocysteine help distinguish the cause: methylmalonic acid is elevated in B12 deficiency but not in folate deficiency, while homocysteine is raised in both.

Treatment

Treatment consists of replacing the deficient vitamin — intramuscular hydroxocobalamin or oral cyanocobalamin for B12 deficiency, and oral folic acid for folate deficiency — together with correcting the underlying cause. Folate must not be given alone in B12 deficiency, as it may correct the anemia while allowing neurological damage to progress.
