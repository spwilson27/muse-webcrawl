Response bias

Response bias is a general term for a wide range of tendencies for participants in research — especially surveys and self-report questionnaires — to respond inaccurately or falsely to questions. These biases distort measurement and are a common problem in the social sciences, marketing research, opinion polling and psychological assessment, because the data depend on what respondents choose to say rather than on direct observation.

Types

Social desirability bias leads respondents to over-report approved behaviour (voting, charitable giving) and under-report disapproved behaviour (drug use, tax evasion). Acquiescence bias ("yea-saying") is the tendency to agree with statements regardless of content. Demand characteristics prompt participants to guess the study's purpose and answer accordingly. Other forms include extreme or central-tendency responding on rating scales, recall bias, question-order and wording effects, and non-response bias, where those who answer a survey differ systematically from those who do not.

Countermeasures

Researchers mitigate response bias through careful questionnaire design: neutral wording, balanced scales with reversed items, anonymous or confidential administration, randomised response techniques for sensitive questions, validation against behavioural or administrative records, and statistical weighting for non-response. Awareness of response biases is one reason self-reported data are treated cautiously in fields from epidemiology to election forecasting.
