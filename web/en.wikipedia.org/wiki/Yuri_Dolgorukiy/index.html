Yuri Dolgorukiy (c. 1099 – 15 May 1157), also known as Yuri I Vladimirovich, was a Rurikid prince of Kievan Rus' remembered chiefly as the traditional founder of Moscow. His nickname, Dolgorukiy — "Long-Armed" — alludes to his persistent reach for the throne of Kiev from his north-eastern appanage.

Life

A son of Vladimir Monomakh, Yuri ruled the Rostov-Suzdal lands in the forested north-east, where he founded or fortified numerous towns, including Dmitrov and Zvenigorod. The chronicles record that in 1147 he invited an ally to a feast at a place called Moscow — the first known mention of the city, and the reason Yuri is honoured as its founder. After a long series of wars with his nephew Iziaslav II and other rivals, Yuri finally secured Kiev in 1155 and ruled as Grand Prince until his death in 1157, traditionally said to have followed a banquet at which he may have been poisoned.

Legacy

Yuri's son Andrei Bogolyubsky shifted the centre of Rus' power toward Vladimir-Suzdal, laying foundations for the later rise of Moscow. An equestrian monument to Yuri stands in Tverskaya Square opposite Moscow City Hall, erected for the city's 800th anniversary in 1947, and his name has been borne by warships and a nuclear submarine of the Russian Navy.
