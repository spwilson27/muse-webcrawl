Spin-off (business)

In media and business, a spin-off is a new work or organisation derived from an existing one. In entertainment, a spin-off is a television series, film, book or video game that focuses on characters or settings from an earlier work while existing within the same fictional universe. In general business usage, the term can describe any venture that "spins off" from a parent organisation, such as a research group commercialising a technology from a university.

Media spin-offs

Classic examples include Frasier, spun off from Cheers; Better Call Saul, a prequel spin-off of Breaking Bad; and the Star Trek and Law & Order franchises, in which spin-offs often run alongside the parent show. Spin-offs may follow a supporting character to a new setting, explore a different time period, or reuse a format in a new location.

Research and technology spin-offs

Space agencies and universities popularised the phrase "spin-off" for technologies developed for one purpose that find commercial application elsewhere — NASA publishes an annual Spinoff report on such transfers, from memory foam to water-filtration systems. University spin-offs (or spin-outs) are start-up companies founded to exploit intellectual property developed in academic research.
