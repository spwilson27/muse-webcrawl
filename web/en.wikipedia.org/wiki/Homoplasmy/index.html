Homoplasmy

Homoplasmy is the presence of identical copies of mitochondrial DNA (or chloroplast DNA) within a cell or individual. Because each cell contains many mitochondria, each with multiple genome copies, a mutation initially creates a mixture — heteroplasmy — which through random segregation in cell division may drift toward homoplasmy, where all copies carry the same sequence, normal or mutant.

Clinical relevance

Homoplasmic mitochondrial mutations cause disease only when the variant is pathogenic in all copies; severity in heteroplasmic conditions depends on the proportion of mutant DNA and tissue energy demands. Homoplasmy also underlies maternal lineage tracing in genetics.
