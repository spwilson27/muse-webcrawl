Case citation

Case citation is a system of legal citation used to identify and locate judicial decisions. A typical citation gives the names of the parties, a volume number, the abbreviation of the reporter (the series of books or database in which the decision is published), a page or paragraph number, and the court and year.

Practice by jurisdiction

In the United States, citations follow The Bluebook, and federal cases are cited to reporters such as the United States Reports, the Federal Reporter and the Federal Supplement. In the United Kingdom, neutral citations — court-issued identifiers combining year, court and case number — have been used since 2001 alongside traditional law report citations such as those of the Appeal Cases or the Weekly Law Reports.
