Yellow anaconda

Scientific nameEunectes notaeus
FamilyBoidae
LengthTypically 3–4 m (10–13 ft)
RangeSouthern South America

The yellow anaconda (Eunectes notaeus), also known as the Paraguayan anaconda, is a species of boa endemic to southern South America. It is smaller than its close relative, the green anaconda, but is still one of the largest snakes in the Americas.

Description

Adults typically measure 3 to 4 metres in length, with females larger than males. The body is yellow, golden-tan, or greenish-yellow, overlaid with a pattern of black or dark brown saddles, blotches, and streaks. Like other anacondas, it is a heavy-bodied, non-venomous constrictor with eyes and nostrils positioned on top of the head, adaptations for an aquatic lifestyle.

Distribution and habitat

The species occurs in southern Brazil, Paraguay, Bolivia, Uruguay, and northeastern Argentina, including the Pantanal, the Gran Chaco, and the Iberá wetlands. It inhabits swamps, marshes, and the banks of slow-moving rivers and streams, preferring shallow, warm, heavily vegetated water.

Behaviour and diet

Yellow anacondas are largely aquatic ambush predators. They feed on fish, water birds, small mammals, other reptiles, and occasionally juvenile caimans, subduing prey by constriction. They are viviparous, giving birth to litters of 4 to 30 live young. The species is generally shy but will bite repeatedly if provoked or handled.

Conservation

The yellow anaconda is listed in CITES Appendix II and its international trade is regulated. It has been hunted for its skin and captured for the pet trade; introduced populations in Florida, where released animals have established themselves in Big Cypress National Park, are considered invasive.
