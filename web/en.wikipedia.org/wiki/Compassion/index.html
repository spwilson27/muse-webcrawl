Compassion

Compassion is a social feeling that motivates people to go out of their way to relieve the physical, mental, or emotional pains of others and themselves. It is often regarded as a sensitivity to suffering coupled with a desire to alleviate it.

Etymology and definitions

From Latin compati, 'to suffer with'. Compassion differs from empathy and sympathy in emphasising action to help. Psychologist Paul Gilbert defines it as sensitivity to suffering with commitment to relieve it.

In religions

Compassion (karuna) is central to Buddhism, where the bodhisattva embodies it. It is also a virtue in Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism and Jainism. The Dalai Lama frequently cites compassion as a universal value.

Psychology and neuroscience

Research links compassion to well-being, reduced stress, and prosocial behaviour. Compassion-focused therapy and loving-kindness meditation aim to cultivate it. Compassion fatigue is a risk among caregivers. See altruism.
