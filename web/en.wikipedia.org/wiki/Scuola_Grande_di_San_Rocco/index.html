Scuola Grande di San Rocco

LocationSan Polo, Venice, Italy
Built1515–1549
Known forCycle of paintings by Tintoretto

The Scuola Grande di San Rocco is a building in Venice, Italy, the seat of a confraternity (scuola) dedicated to Saint Roch, founded in 1478. It is renowned above all for the cycle of more than sixty paintings by Tintoretto that decorate its halls, among the greatest achievements of Renaissance art.

History

The confraternity, one of Venice's wealthy lay brotherhoods devoted to charitable work, grew powerful after the relics of St Roch were brought to Venice in 1485. Construction of its grand headquarters in the San Polo district began in 1515 under Bartolomeo Bon and continued under Sante Lombardo and Antonio Abbondi (lo Scarpagnino), who designed the ornate facade.

Tintoretto's cycle

In 1564 Tintoretto won the commission for the Sala dell'Albergo, famously presenting a finished painting rather than a sketch. Over the following decades he filled the building with vast canvases: the Crucifixion (1565) in the Sala dell'Albergo, Old and New Testament scenes in the upper hall (Sala Capitolare), and the Life of the Virgin cycle in the ground-floor hall. The writer John Ruskin and the novelist Henry James were among the many visitors overwhelmed by the ensemble.

Today

The confraternity still owns the building, which functions as a museum; the church of San Rocco opposite holds further works by Tintoretto.
