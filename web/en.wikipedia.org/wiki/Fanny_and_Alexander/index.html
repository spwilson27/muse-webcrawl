Fanny and Alexander (Swedish: Fanny och Alexander) is a 1982 period drama film written and directed by Ingmar Bergman. The plot focuses on two siblings and their large family in Uppsala, Sweden, during the first decade of the 20th century. Bergman intended the film to be his final feature, and it exists in a three-hour theatrical version and a five-hour television miniseries version, the latter of which Bergman considered definitive. It won four Academy Awards, including Best Foreign Language Film.

Plot

In 1907 the Ekdahl family, headed by the widowed matriarch Helena and centred on the town theatre, celebrates a sumptuous Christmas. When Alexander's father Oscar, the theatre's actor-manager, dies suddenly, his widow Emilie marries the austere Bishop Edvard Vergérus. The bishop moves the children into his bare, forbidding household, confiscates their toys, and punishes Alexander harshly for lying and storytelling, locking him in the attic. Family friend Isak Jacobi spirits the children away, hidden in a trunk, and shelters them in his antique shop among puppets and his mysterious nephew Ismael. The bishop dies in a fire after his bedside lamp is overturned during a confrontation, and the family reunites at the theatre, where Helena reads from Strindberg's A Dream Play: "Anything can happen, all is possible and probable."

Themes and reception

Semi-autobiographical, the film contrasts the Ekdahls' warm world of theatre, imagination, and sensual pleasure with the bishop's cold world of dogma and discipline. It distils the themes of Bergman's career — faith, family, ghosts, and storytelling — into what many critics regard as his crowning achievement. The film won Academy Awards for Best Foreign Language Film, Best Cinematography (Sven Nykvist), Best Art Direction, and Best Costume Design, and took the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Film.
