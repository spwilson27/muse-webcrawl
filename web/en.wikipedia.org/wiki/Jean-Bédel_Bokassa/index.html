Jean-Bédel Bokassa
Born: 22 February 1921, Bobangui
Died: 3 November 1996, Bangui
President of the Central African Republic: 1966–1976
Emperor Bokassa I: 1976–1979

Jean-Bédel Bokassa (22 February 1921 – 3 November 1996) was a Central African political and military leader who ruled the Central African Republic from a 1966 coup until his overthrow in 1979, proclaiming himself Emperor Bokassa I of the Central African Empire in 1976.

Rule

A former French colonial soldier, Bokassa seized power from his cousin David Dacko. His regime was authoritarian and extravagant; his 1977 coronation ceremony, modelled on Napoleon's, cost an enormous share of the national budget. In 1979, after international outrage over the killing of schoolchildren protesting in Bangui, France removed him in Operation Barracuda and restored Dacko.

Trial and death

Bokassa lived in exile in Côte d'Ivoire and France, then returned in 1986, was tried, and was sentenced to death — commuted to life imprisonment and later reduced. He was released in 1993 and died in Bangui in 1996. He was posthumously rehabilitated in 2010.
