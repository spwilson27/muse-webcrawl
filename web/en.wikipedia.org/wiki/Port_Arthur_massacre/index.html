Port Arthur massacre
Location: Port Arthur, Tasmania, Australia
Date: 28–29 April 1996
Deaths: 35
Injured: 23
Perpetrator: Martin Bryant

The Port Arthur massacre was a mass shooting that occurred on 28 April 1996 at Port Arthur, a tourist town and former convict site in Tasmania, Australia. The gunman, Martin Bryant, killed 35 people and wounded 23 others before being captured the following day. It was the deadliest mass shooting in modern Australian history.

Bryant pleaded guilty to all charges and was sentenced to 35 life sentences plus additional years without the possibility of parole.

Aftermath and gun law reform

The massacre prompted the federal government of Prime Minister John Howard to implement sweeping gun control reforms within months, including the National Firearms Agreement, a nationwide buyback and amnesty scheme that collected and destroyed more than 650,000 firearms, and bans on certain semi-automatic rifles and shotguns. The reforms are frequently cited in international debates on gun control.
