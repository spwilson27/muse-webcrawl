Multiplexing

In telecommunications and computer networks, multiplexing (sometimes contracted to muxing) is a method by which multiple analogue or digital signals are combined into one signal over a shared medium. The aim is to share a scarce resource, such as a physical transmission medium.

Types

Common techniques include frequency-division multiplexing (FDM), in which each signal occupies a separate frequency band; time-division multiplexing (TDM), in which signals take turns using the channel in short time slots; wavelength-division multiplexing (WDM), used in optical fibre systems; and code-division multiplexing. Multiplexed signals are separated at the receiving end by a demultiplexer.

Applications

Multiplexing is fundamental to telephone networks, cable television, digital subscriber lines and optical backbone networks, allowing many conversations or data streams to share a single cable or fibre.
