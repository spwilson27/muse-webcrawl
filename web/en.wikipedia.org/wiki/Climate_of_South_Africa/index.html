Climate of South Africa

The climate of South Africa is determined by the country's position between 22°S and 35°S latitude, in the Southern Hemisphere's subtropical zone, and its location between the Atlantic and Indian Oceans. South Africa has a generally temperate climate, partly because it is surrounded by ocean on three sides, lies in the climatically milder Southern Hemisphere, and has an average elevation that rises steadily towards the north and further inland.

Overview

The country has a wide range of climatic conditions, from the Mediterranean climate of the Western Cape, with winter rainfall, to the subtropical climate of KwaZulu-Natal, and the arid conditions of the Karoo and the Kalahari in the interior and northwest. The interior plateau (the Highveld) has warm summer days with afternoon thunderstorms and cold, dry winters with frost.

Temperature

Average annual temperatures range from around 15 °C in the interior highlands to above 23 °C along the subtropical northeast coast. Extreme temperatures above 40 °C are recorded in the Northern Cape, while snow falls regularly on the high mountain ranges such as the Drakensberg in winter.

Rainfall

Mean annual rainfall is about 464 mm, well below the world average, and most of the country is semi-arid. The eastern regions receive considerably more rain than the west; the west coast is influenced by the cold Benguela Current and is very dry, while the warm Agulhas Current moderates the east coast.
