Provincial administration

Provincial administration is the system of government of a province — a first- or second-level territorial division found in many countries — encompassing the executive, legislative and bureaucratic organs that manage public services, infrastructure and local law within the province. The powers of provincial administrations range from the extensive competencies of federal units, such as the Canadian provinces or Argentine provinces, to the largely supervisory role of provinces in unitary states, where they implement national policy.

Structure

A provincial administration is typically headed by a governor, premier, or president, who may be elected locally or appointed by the central government. A provincial legislature or council enacts bylaws and approves budgets, while departments (ministries) administer areas such as health, education, transport, policing and land use. Below the province, administration is usually divided into districts, counties and municipalities.

Federal and unitary systems

In federations — Canada, Argentina, Pakistan, South Africa, Spain's autonomous communities in functional terms — provincial governments hold constitutionally protected powers and raise significant revenues. In unitary states such as China, Thailand or the Philippines, provincial administrations are arms of the central state, though elected councils may provide limited local input. China's provincial-level divisions, for example, are administered by people's governments under the dual leadership of the State Council and provincial people's congresses.

History

The term derives from the Roman provincia, the territory administered by a Roman magistrate outside Italy. Successive empires — including the Ottoman, Spanish and British empires — governed through provincial administrations, and the model carried into their successor states.
