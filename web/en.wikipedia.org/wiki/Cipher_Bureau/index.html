Cipher Bureau (Poland)

The Cipher Bureau (Polish: Biuro Szyfrów) was the Polish interwar agency charged with both cryptography (the use of ciphers and codes) and cryptanalysis (the breaking of other nations' encrypted communications). Formed in 1931 from an earlier Cipher Section of the Polish General Staff, it is famous for achieving the first break of the German Enigma machine cipher and for handing its methods to Britain and France on the eve of World War II.

Breaking Enigma

In late 1932 the bureau's young mathematician-cryptanalysts Marian Rejewski, Jerzy Różycki, and Henryk Zygalski reconstructed the internal wiring of the military Enigma and developed methods to recover daily keys, aided by documents supplied by French intelligence from the German agent Hans-Thilo Schmidt. The Poles built Enigma doubles, the cyclometer, perforated "Zygalski sheets", and the electromechanical bomba kryptologiczna. On 25 July 1939, at a meeting in the Kabaty Woods near Warsaw, the bureau disclosed its achievements to British and French intelligence — a contribution that seeded the Allied Ultra program at Bletchley Park.

World War II

After Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, bureau personnel evacuated through Romania to France, where they continued work at "Cadix" near Uzès, then dispersed after the German occupation of Vichy France in November 1942. Różycki died in 1942; Rejewski and Zygalski reached Britain in 1943.
