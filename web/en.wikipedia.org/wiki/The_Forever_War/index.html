The Forever War is a military science fiction novel by American author Joe Haldeman, published in 1974. Widely regarded as one of the finest works of the genre, it won the Nebula Award for Best Novel in 1975 and the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1976. The novel is commonly read as an allegory of the Vietnam War, in which Haldeman served as a combat engineer and from which he was discharged after being wounded.

Plot summary

The novel is narrated by William Mandella, a physics graduate conscripted into the United Nations Exploratory Force to fight an interstellar war against the alien Taurans. Because travel between star systems takes place at relativistic speeds through "collapsar" jump points, months of subjective time for the soldiers correspond to years or decades back on Earth. Each time Mandella returns, human society has changed beyond recognition, while the war drags on for reasons nobody can clearly explain. Mandella survives engagement after engagement largely by luck, is repeatedly promoted, and eventually returns to an Earth so alienated from its veterans that he and his lover Marygay Potter choose to settle on a "time capsule" colony world reserved for returning soldiers.

Themes

The book explores the disorientation of veterans, the futility and bureaucratic momentum of war, the effects of time dilation on human relationships, and the way societies change while soldiers are absent. Haldeman's later novella A Separate War (1998) retells part of the story from Marygay Potter's point of view, and the novel Forever Free (1999) is a direct sequel. A thematic companion, Forever Peace (1997), also won the Hugo and Nebula awards.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted as a stage play, as the celebrated Belgian comic series by Marvano, and has long been in development as a film. It remains a staple of science fiction reading lists and a defining work of military science fiction alongside Starship Troopers, to which it is often contrasted.
