Makerspace

A makerspace is a community-operated, shared workspace where people with common interests — often in computers, machining, technology, craft, and electronics — can meet, socialize, and collaborate. Makerspaces typically provide access to tools and equipment that would be impractical for individuals to own, such as 3D printers, laser cutters, CNC machines, soldering stations, and woodworking machinery.

History and movement

Makerspaces grew out of the hackerspace movement and the broader maker movement of the 2000s, popularized by Make magazine (founded 2005) and Maker Faire events. Spaces such as Noisebridge in San Francisco, NYC Resistor in New York, and c-base in Berlin became models for community workshops. Libraries, schools, and universities have also adopted makerspaces as centres for hands-on STEM and STEAM learning.

Organization

Most makerspaces are run by nonprofits or member collectives, funded by membership dues and donations, and governed by their communities. Fab labs — a related concept developed at MIT's Center for Bits and Atoms — follow a shared charter and a standard inventory of digital fabrication tools. Makerspaces emphasize open access, skill-sharing through workshops and classes, and a do-it-yourself ethos of building, repairing, and inventing.
