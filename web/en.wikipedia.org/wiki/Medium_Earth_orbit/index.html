A medium Earth orbit (MEO) is an Earth-centered orbit with an altitude above a low Earth orbit (2,000 km) and below a high Earth orbit (35,786 km, the altitude of geostationary orbit). It is sometimes called an intermediate circular orbit (ICO).

Uses

MEO is the home of the global navigation satellite systems: the United States' GPS satellites orbit at about 20,200 km, Russia's GLONASS at about 19,100 km, the European Union's Galileo at about 23,222 km, and China's BeiDou partly at similar altitudes, typically in semi-synchronous orbits of roughly 12-hour periods. Communications constellations such as O3b also use MEO, where the greater altitude (compared with LEO) means fewer satellites are needed for coverage, at the cost of higher signal latency than LEO systems.

Characteristics

Satellites in MEO pass through the Van Allen radiation belts, so their electronics require radiation hardening. Orbital periods range from about 2 hours at the lower boundary to just under a day near geostationary altitude. The region between about 8,000 and 20,000 km is relatively sparsely populated compared with LEO and GEO.
