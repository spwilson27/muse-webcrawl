Blu-ray

A Blu-ray Disc

Blu-ray (Blu-ray Disc, BD) is a digital optical disc data storage format designed to supersede the DVD format. It was developed by the Blu-ray Disc Association and released worldwide in 2006. The plastic disc is 120 millimetres (4.7 in) in diameter — the same size as DVDs and CDs — and can hold up to 25 GB (single layer) or 50 GB (dual layer) of data, compared with 4.7 GB for a standard DVD.

Technology

The format's name derives from the blue-violet laser used to read the disc, which has a shorter wavelength (405 nm) than the red laser used for DVD, allowing data to be packed much more tightly. Blu-ray supports high-definition video at 1080p resolution, along with lossless audio formats and interactive features via BD-Java.

Format war

Blu-ray competed with Toshiba's HD DVD in a high-definition format war reminiscent of the VHS–Betamax contest. The war ended in 2008 when Toshiba conceded after major studios and retailers, including Warner Bros. and Walmart, sided exclusively with Blu-ray. The inclusion of a Blu-ray drive in Sony's PlayStation 3 console is widely credited as a decisive factor.

Successors and legacy

Ultra HD Blu-ray, launched in 2016, extended the format to 4K resolution with capacities of 66–100 GB. Although streaming services have eroded physical media sales, Blu-ray remains valued by collectors for its picture and sound quality, and the format is used for video game discs on PlayStation and Xbox consoles.
