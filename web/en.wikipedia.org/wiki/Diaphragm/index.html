Diaphragm

The diaphragm is a dome-shaped sheet of skeletal muscle and tendon that separates the chest (thoracic cavity) from the abdomen. It is the principal muscle of breathing in mammals.

Action

When the diaphragm contracts (via the phrenic nerves, C3–C5) it flattens, expanding the chest and drawing air into the lungs; relaxation lets it dome upward as air leaves. It provides most of quiet breathing's volume change. The diaphragm is pierced by the oesophagus, aorta and inferior vena cava.

Problems

Hiccups are involuntary diaphragm spasms. Hernias can push abdominal contents through it (hiatus hernia causing reflux; congenital hernias in newborns are emergencies). Phrenic nerve damage paralyses one side, visible as a raised hemidiaphragm on X-ray.
