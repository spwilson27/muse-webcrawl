Robin Milner


Born: January 13, 1934, Plymouth, England
Died: March 20, 2010, Cambridge, England
Occupation: Computer scientist
Awards: Turing Award (1991)

Arthur John Robin Gorell Milner (January 13, 1934 – March 20, 2010), known as Robin Milner, was a British computer scientist whose work shaped programming languages and concurrency theory. He received the ACM Turing Award in 1991.

Work

At the University of Edinburgh, Milner developed ML, a programming language arising from the LCF theorem prover, in which he devised polymorphic type inference — a system that guarantees well-typed programs cannot produce certain errors, summarised in the slogan "well-typed programs don't go wrong". ML became the ancestor of languages including Standard ML, OCaml and, by influence, Haskell and F#.

Milner's other great contribution was to the theory of concurrent systems: the Calculus of Communicating Systems (CCS, 1980) and its successor the π-calculus (developed with Joachim Parrow and David Walker), foundational models of processes that interact by message passing. His later work at Cambridge, where he headed the Computer Laboratory from 1996 to 1999, included bigraphs as a general model of computation and interaction.
