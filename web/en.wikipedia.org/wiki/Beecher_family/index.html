Beecher family

The Beecher family was a prominent American family of the 19th century, notable in religion, education and social reform. Descended from the preacher Lyman Beecher, the family's members included the abolitionist novelist Harriet Beecher Stowe, the preacher Henry Ward Beecher, and the educator Catharine Beecher. Their influence on antebellum American culture was such that they were sometimes called "the most famous family in America".


Lyman Beecher and his children

Lyman Beecher

Lyman Beecher (1775–1863) was a Presbyterian and Congregationalist minister, a leader of the Second Great Awakening, and president of Lane Seminary in Cincinnati. He fathered thirteen children, nearly all of whom became prominent in public life; he was said to have declared that his family would help shape the nation's morals.

Notable members

  • Catharine Beecher (1800–1878) — educator and author who founded schools for women and wrote influential works on domestic economy.
  • Harriet Beecher Stowe (1811–1896) — author of Uncle Tom's Cabin (1852), the anti-slavery novel credited with galvanising abolitionist sentiment in the North.
  • Henry Ward Beecher (1813–1887) — Congregationalist clergyman at Plymouth Church, Brooklyn, a celebrated orator, abolitionist and advocate of women's suffrage.
  • Isabella Beecher Hooker (1822–1907) — leader of the women's suffrage movement.
  • Thomas K. Beecher and Charles Beecher — ministers and reformers.

Legacy

The Harriet Beecher Stowe House in Hartford, Connecticut, and the Lyman Beecher House and Harriet Beecher Stowe House in Cincinnati are preserved as historic sites. The family's mixture of evangelical religion and reform activism typified the "benevolent empire" of antebellum Protestant America.
