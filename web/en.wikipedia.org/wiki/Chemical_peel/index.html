Chemical peel

Application of a chemical peeling solution in a dermatology clinic.

A chemical peel is a technique used to improve the appearance of the skin by applying a chemical solution that causes controlled injury to the skin's outer layers, which then exfoliate and are replaced by smoother new skin. Peels are used mainly on the face, neck and hands to treat photoageing, fine wrinkles, acne and acne scarring, and pigmentary disorders such as melasma and sun spots; the practice has roots in ancient Egypt and was developed into a medical procedure in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Types by depth

Chemical peel
Superficial: AHAs (glycolic, lactic), salicylic acid, low-strength TCA
Medium: Trichloroacetic acid (TCA) 35–50%
Deep: Phenol-based formulas
Uses: Photoageing, acne, dyschromia, keratoses
Risks: Scarring, pigment change, infection

Superficial peels remove only the epidermis using alpha-hydroxy acids (such as glycolic and lactic acid), beta-hydroxy acid (salicylic acid), or low concentrations of trichloroacetic acid (TCA); they have minimal downtime and are used for mild discoloration and texture problems. Medium-depth peels, typically TCA at 35–50%, reach the upper dermis and address moderate sun damage and actinic keratoses, with about a week of visible peeling. Deep peels use phenol-based solutions to reach the mid-dermis and produce the most dramatic improvement in coarse wrinkles, but carry the greatest risks and longest recovery.

Risks and precautions

Possible complications include infection, herpes simplex reactivation, scarring — particularly with deeper peels — and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation or hypopigmentation, a special concern in darker skin types. Phenol peels require cardiac monitoring because phenol absorbed through the skin can cause arrhythmias, and they are unsuitable for some patients. Patients are generally advised to avoid sun exposure during healing and to use sunscreen afterwards. Outcomes depend on the agent, concentration, application technique and the patient's skin type, and peels are best performed by trained clinicians.
