El Niño

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a climate pattern in the Pacific Ocean that occurs irregularly every two to seven years. During El Niño, unusually warm ocean water develops in the central and east-central equatorial Pacific, weakening the trade winds and shifting rainfall patterns worldwide.

Effects

El Niño events are associated with droughts in Australia, Indonesia, and parts of South Asia; heavy rainfall and flooding in the southern United States and coastal Peru and Ecuador; warmer winters in northern North America; and reduced Atlantic hurricane activity. They also depress fish stocks off Peru by suppressing nutrient-rich upwelling — the phenomenon from which the name (referring to the Christ child, because it often peaks near Christmas) derives.

Major events

Exceptionally strong events occurred in 1982–83, 1997–98, and 2015–16, causing billions of dollars in damage globally. The opposite, cool phase of ENSO is called La Niña.
