Zakia Jafri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zakia Jafri is an Indian human rights activist and the widow of Ehsan Jafri, a former member of parliament who was killed in the Gulbarg Society massacre during the 2002 Gujarat riots. She became widely known for her long legal struggle seeking accountability from senior state officials for alleged complicity in the riots.

Legal case

In 2006 Zakia Jafri filed a complaint alleging a wider conspiracy behind the 2002 violence and naming then–Gujarat Chief Minister Narendra Modi and dozens of other officials. The Supreme Court of India directed a Special Investigation Team (SIT) to examine the complaint; the SIT filed a closure report in 2012 finding no prosecutable evidence against the accused. Jafri's protest petition against the closure report was rejected by a magistrate in 2013 and by the Gujarat High Court in 2017.

On appeal, the Supreme Court heard the matter at length and on 24 June 2022 dismissed her plea, upholding the SIT's clean chit. The judgment's observations led to the arrest the following day of activist Teesta Setalvad, who had supported Jafri's case. Jafri continued to be regarded by supporters as a symbol of the victims' pursuit of justice.
