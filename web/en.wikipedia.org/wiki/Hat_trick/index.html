Hat-trick

A hat-trick or hat trick is the achievement of a generally positive feat three times in a single game or another context. The term first came into use in 1858 in cricket, when H. H. Stephenson took three wickets with three consecutive deliveries; a collection was held for him and he was presented with a hat bought with the proceeds.

Use in sport

In association football, a hat-trick occurs when a player scores three goals in a single match. A "perfect hat-trick" is sometimes defined as scoring once with the left foot, once with the right foot, and once with the head. By tradition, a player who scores a hat-trick may keep the match ball.

In ice hockey, a hat-trick is three goals by one player in a game; fans traditionally throw hats onto the ice to celebrate. When a player scores three consecutive goals it is sometimes called a "natural hat-trick". The Gordie Howe hat trick — a goal, an assist, and a fight in the same game — is a humorous informal variant.

In cricket the original meaning survives: a bowler taking three wickets in three consecutive balls achieves a hat-trick, a rare feat in all formats of the game.

Other uses

Beyond sport, the term is used generally for any triple success, such as a performer winning three awards in succession or a politician winning three elections.
