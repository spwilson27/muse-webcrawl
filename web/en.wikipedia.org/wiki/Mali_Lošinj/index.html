Mali Lošinj (Italian: Lussinpiccolo, literally "Little Lošinj") is a town on the island of Lošinj in the northern Adriatic Sea, in Primorje-Gorski Kotar County, Croatia. Despite its name meaning "little", it is the largest settlement on the island — bigger than Veli Lošinj ("Great Lošinj") — a paradox dating from the centuries when the smaller settlement outgrew its elder neighbour.

History

The town grew from the 13th century around the church of St Martin and a settlement of Croatian families; through the maritime era it developed into a significant shipbuilding and seafaring centre under Venetian and then Austro-Hungarian rule. In the 19th century Lošinj's shipyards and merchant captains were renowned across the Adriatic, and grand captains' houses still line the harbour. After the Second World War the town passed to Yugoslavia and many Italian-speaking inhabitants emigrated.

Tourism and nature

Mali Lošinj is a popular resort known for its mild climate, pine forests, the sheltered bays of Čikat and Sunčana Uvala, and clear waters frequented by a resident population of bottlenose dolphins, protected by a marine reserve. The nearby Museum of Apoxyomenos displays a remarkably preserved ancient Greek bronze athlete statue, recovered from the seabed off Lošinj in 1999.

Transport

The town is connected by ferry to Rijeka, Zadar and Venice, and has a small airport on the island.
