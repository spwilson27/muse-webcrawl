Hall–Petch relation

The Hall–Petch relation (or Hall–Petch equation) predicts that a material's yield strength increases as its grain size decreases. It is expressed as σy = σ0 + ky d−1/2, where d is average grain diameter, σ0 is a friction stress and ky is the Hall–Petch slope.

History

The relation was established from work by E. O. Hall in 1951 on mild steel and N. J. Petch in 1953 on cleavage fracture. It is explained by dislocation pile-ups at grain boundaries: smaller grains mean more boundaries impeding dislocation motion.

Inverse Hall–Petch

At very small grain sizes, typically below about 10–30 nm, the relation breaks down and strength may decrease with further refinement — the inverse Hall–Petch effect — as grain-boundary sliding and other mechanisms dominate in nanocrystalline materials.
