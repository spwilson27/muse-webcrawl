Cristóvão Ferreira

Born: c. 1580, Torres Vedras, Portugal
Died: 4 or 5 November 1650, Nagasaki, Japan
Occupation: Jesuit missionary

Cristóvão Ferreira (c. 1580 – 1650) was a Portuguese Jesuit missionary in Japan who became famous for committing apostasy after being tortured during the persecution of Christians under the Tokugawa shogunate. His case shocked European Christendom and became a byword for the limits of martyrdom.

Life

Ferreira arrived in Japan in 1609 and rose to a senior position in the Jesuit mission, acting as an administrator of the province after the expulsion of missionaries began in 1614. He remained in hiding until his capture in 1633. After several hours of torture by suspension in a pit (ana-tsurushi), Ferreira recanted his faith — reportedly the first European missionary in Japan to do so.

He took the Japanese name Sawano Chūan, married a Japanese widow, registered as a resident at a Buddhist temple, and lived in Nagasaki assisting the authorities as an interpreter and censor of imported books. He is credited with works on Western astronomy and medicine written in Japanese, and with a 1636 tract, The Deception Revealed, attacking Christian doctrine. He died in Nagasaki in 1650.

Legacy

Ferreira's apostasy is a central theme of Shūsaku Endō's novel Silence (1966) and its film adaptations, in which a fictionalised Ferreira explains his recantation to a younger missionary.
