Piper PA-30 Twin Comanche

The PA-30 Twin Comanche, Piper's twin-engine derivative of the PA-24

The Piper PA-30 Twin Comanche is an American twin-engine, four- to six-seat light aircraft designed by Ed Swearingen and built by Piper Aircraft. It was developed as a twin-engine version of the single-engine PA-24 Comanche and first flew in November 1962; production ran from 1963 to 1972 at Piper's Lock Haven, Pennsylvania plant.

Design

The Twin Comanche is a low-wing monoplane of all-metal construction with retractable tricycle landing gear. The standard PA-30 is powered by two 160 hp Lycoming IO-320 engines with counter-rotating propellers on later models, eliminating the critical-engine handling problem of conventional twins. It cruises at around 170 knots and is noted for fuel efficiency. Variants included the turbocharged PA-30 Turbo Twin Comanche and the PA-39 Twin Comanche C/R, which introduced counter-rotating engines as standard.

History

Production ended in 1972 after flooding from Hurricane Agnes destroyed the Lock Haven plant's tooling. A total of about 2,155 PA-30s and PA-39s were built. Despite its age the type retains an enthusiastic owner community and a reputation as an economical personal twin, though it demands careful handling in single-engine and stall conditions, as with other light twins of its era.
