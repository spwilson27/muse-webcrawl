Marxism–Leninism is a communist ideology developed by Joseph Stalin and the Communist Party of the Soviet Union from the theories of Karl Marx and Vladimir Lenin. It was the official ideology of the USSR and of the ruling parties of most socialist states during the twentieth century.

Doctrine

Marxism–Leninism combines Marx's historical materialism with Lenin's theory of the vanguard party, democratic centralism, and imperialism as the highest stage of capitalism. It supports a transitional socialist state on the road to a classless communist society.

Spread and variants

After 1945 the ideology was adopted by ruling parties in Eastern Europe, China (where it evolved alongside Mao Zedong Thought), Cuba, Vietnam, and elsewhere. Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991 its global influence sharply declined.
