Andromeda (constellation)

Andromeda is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and one of the 88 modern constellations. Located in the northern celestial hemisphere, it is named for Andromeda, the princess of Greek mythology who was chained to a rock to be devoured by the sea monster Cetus and rescued by Perseus. The constellation is most notable for containing the Andromeda Galaxy (M31), the nearest major galaxy to the Milky Way.

Stars

Andromeda's brightest star, Alpheratz (Alpha Andromedae), also historically shared with Pegasus as part of the Great Square of Pegasus, forms the northeastern corner of that asterism. Other notable stars include Mirach (Beta Andromedae), a red giant, and Almach (Gamma Andromedae), a colorful multiple star popular with amateur astronomers. Upsilon Andromedae was one of the first stars found to have a multi-planet extrasolar system.

Deep-sky objects

The Andromeda Galaxy (Messier 31) is a barred spiral galaxy approximately 2.5 million light-years away and is visible to the naked eye under dark skies as a small, elongated smudge. It is accompanied by the satellite elliptical galaxies M32 and M110. The constellation also contains the planetary nebula NGC 7662, known as the Blue Snowball Nebula, and the open cluster NGC 752.

Mythology

In Greek myth, Andromeda was the daughter of Cepheus and Cassiopeia, king and queen of Aethiopia. Cassiopeia's boast that her daughter's beauty surpassed the Nereids angered Poseidon, who sent the monster Cetus to ravage the coast. Chained to a rock as a sacrifice, Andromeda was rescued by the hero Perseus, who later married her. The constellations Andromeda, Perseus, Cepheus, Cassiopeia and Cetus all lie in the same region of the sky.
