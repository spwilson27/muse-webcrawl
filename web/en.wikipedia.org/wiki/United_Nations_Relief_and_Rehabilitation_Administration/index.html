United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration

The United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration (UNRRA) was an international relief agency created in 1943, before the United Nations itself was founded, to provide food, medicine, clothing and assistance to victims of the Second World War in areas liberated from Axis control. It later helped operate displaced-persons camps and repatriate millions of people.

Creation and leadership

Representatives of 44 nations signed the UNRRA agreement in Washington on 9 November 1943. Its largest financial contributor was the United States. The first director-general was Herbert H. Lehman, followed by Fiorello H. La Guardia and Lowell Rooks. UNRRA worked with national governments and military authorities rather than usually administering territory directly.

Operations

UNRRA supplied emergency food, seeds, livestock, fertilizer, medical services, sanitation and industrial rehabilitation in Europe and Asia, including major programmes in Italy, Poland, Yugoslavia, Greece, Czechoslovakia and China. After Germany's defeat, it assumed responsibility for many camps housing concentration-camp survivors, former forced labourers, prisoners of war and refugees. It organized transport and documentation for large-scale repatriation, while some displaced people resisted return to Soviet-controlled homelands.

Dissolution and legacy

Most European operations ended in 1947 and the agency was wound up by 1949. Its remaining functions were divided among successor bodies and new international organizations, including the International Refugee Organization, World Health Organization, Food and Agriculture Organization and UNICEF. UNRRA was a major experiment in multilateral humanitarian action and a precursor to modern United Nations relief, refugee and development agencies.
