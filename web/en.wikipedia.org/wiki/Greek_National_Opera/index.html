The Greek National Opera (Greek: Εθνική Λυρική Σκηνή, Ethniki Lyriki Skini) is Greece's national opera company, based in Athens. Founded in 1939–1940, it is the country's principal producer of opera, operetta, ballet and contemporary music theatre. Since 2017 the company has been housed in the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Cultural Center (SNFCC) in Kallithea, on the Athenian Riviera, in a building designed by Renzo Piano which it shares with the National Library of Greece.

History

The company emerged from the opera ensemble of the National Theatre of Greece and gave its first independent productions in 1940, opening with Johann Strauss II's Die Fledermaus. It was formally established as an autonomous state organisation in 1944. The young Maria Callas performed with the company during the Second World War, singing roles including Tosca and Leonore in Fidelio.

From 1958 the company was based at the Olympia Theatre on Akadimias Street in central Athens, which remained its home for nearly six decades. In 2017 it moved to the Stavros Niarchos Hall at the SNFCC, a 1,400-seat lyric theatre funded by a donation of the Stavros Niarchos Foundation. The move allowed the company to expand its repertoire, co-productions and audience.

Repertoire and activities

The Greek National Opera stages the standard Italian, French, German and Russian operatic repertoire alongside Greek works and new commissions. It maintains an orchestra, chorus, ballet company (the GNO Ballet) and a children's chorus, and runs education and outreach programmes across Greece. The company has presented notable productions directed by internationally known artists and regularly collaborates with European opera houses.
