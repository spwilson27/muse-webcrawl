Aira Caldera is a gigantic volcanic caldera in the south of the island of Kyushu, Japan. Measuring roughly 17 by 23 kilometers, it forms the northern part of Kagoshima Bay and was created by one of the largest eruptions of the last 100,000 years. The still-active stratovolcano Sakurajima rises from the caldera's southern rim, opposite the city of Kagoshima.

The Aira-Tn eruption

The caldera formed about 29,000 years ago (radiocarbon-calibrated to roughly 30,000 years before present) in the catastrophic Aira-Tn eruption, rated VEI 7. The eruption began with the Osumi pumice fall and culminated in the colossal Ito pyroclastic flow, which covered much of southern Kyushu in ignimbrite — visible today as the Shirasu plateau — and spread the Aira-Tn tephra (AT) ash layer across the whole Japanese archipelago and beyond. The AT ash is one of the most important stratigraphic marker horizons in Japanese archaeology and geology, separating Paleolithic cultural layers. The volume of erupted material is estimated at several hundred cubic kilometers; collapse of the emptied magma chamber created the caldera basin, which was subsequently flooded by the sea.

Sakurajima

About 13,000 years ago, a new volcanic edifice, Sakurajima, began growing within the southern part of the caldera. Originally an island, it was joined to the Osumi Peninsula by lava flows during its great 1914 eruption, which also lowered the island by subsidence as magma drained. Sakurajima is one of Japan's most active volcanoes, with persistent summit activity from the Showa crater and Minamidake, producing frequent ashfalls on Kagoshima city. A further large magma chamber beneath Aira Caldera is slowly refilling, and the caldera floor and Sakurajima are monitored continuously for deformation and seismicity by the Sakurajima Volcano Research Center.

Setting

The deep waters of the caldera basin, partly enclosed by Sakurajima, are used by Kagoshima port and by aquaculture, and the area is designated within the Kirishima-Kinkowan National Park. The caldera's formation is linked to the subduction of the Philippine Sea Plate beneath Kyushu along the Nankai Trough system.
