Quality of life

Access to green space, safety, and services are common quality-of-life factors.

Quality of life (often discussed as quality of living) is the degree of well-being felt by an individual or a group of people. It comprises both objective conditions — such as health, income, housing, environment, education, and safety — and subjective perceptions such as life satisfaction and happiness. The term is used in a wide range of contexts, including international development, healthcare, politics, and employment.

Measurement

Several indices attempt to quantify quality of life. The Human Development Index combines life expectancy, education, and income. The OECD Better Life Index uses eleven dimensions including housing, jobs, community, environment, and work–life balance. Consultancy surveys of expatriate living conditions, such as Mercer's Quality of Living Survey, rank cities on political stability, healthcare, banking, schools, and infrastructure, and are used by companies to set hardship allowances.

Health-related quality of life

In medicine, health-related quality of life (HRQoL) refers to how a person's health affects their physical, mental, and social functioning. It is measured with standardised questionnaires and is used alongside clinical outcomes in evaluating treatments, particularly for chronic conditions.

Quality of life vs standard of living

Standard of living is primarily an economic measure — income and consumption — whereas quality of life is broader, embracing environmental quality, leisure, social relationships, and personal fulfilment even where material standards are equal.
