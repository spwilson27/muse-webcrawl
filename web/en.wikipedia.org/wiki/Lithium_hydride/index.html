Lithium hydride

Lithium hydride
Formula: LiH
Molar mass: 7.95 g/mol
Appearance: colourless to grey solid
Density: 0.78 g/cm³
Melting point: 688.7 °C
CAS: 7580-67-8

Lithium hydride is an inorganic compound with the formula LiH. It is an alkali metal hydride — a colourless solid, although commercial samples are often grey. A salt-like hydride consisting of lithium cations and hydride anions, it is produced by treating lithium metal with hydrogen gas at high temperature.

Properties and reactions

LiH is unreactive towards oxygen and chlorine at moderate temperatures but reacts vigorously with water to produce hydrogen gas and lithium hydroxide: LiH + H2O → LiOH + H2. It is a strong reducing agent and a source of hydride in the synthesis of other complex hydrides, including lithium aluminium hydride and lithium borohydride.

Uses

Lithium hydride is used as a desiccant, as a precursor to other hydrides, in hydrogen storage research, and as a lightweight shielding material. Its deuterated form, lithium deuteride (LiD), is the fusion fuel used in thermonuclear weapons, in which neutrons convert lithium-6 into tritium in situ.

Safety

The compound reacts exothermically with water and moist air and must be handled under dry, inert conditions; the dust is irritating and the hydrogen evolved is flammable.
