Mount Doom, also called Orodruin ("Fiery Mountain" in Sindarin) and Amon Amarth ("Hill of Doom"), is a fictional volcano in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. It stands on the plateau of Gorgoroth in the heart of Mordor, the realm of the Dark Lord Sauron, and is the central destination of the quest in The Lord of the Rings.

Role in the legendarium

In the Second Age, Sauron built his fortress of Barad-dûr near the mountain and, in the fires of the Sammath Naur — the Chambers of Fire within Orodruin — forged the One Ring, pouring into it much of his own power. Because the Ring was made there, it could be destroyed nowhere else. In the Third Age, after Sauron's defeat by the Last Alliance, the Ring passed through many hands until the hobbit Frodo Baggins was charged with carrying it to the Cracks of Doom. At the very edge of the fire Frodo claimed the Ring for himself, but the creature Gollum bit it from his finger and fell with it into the abyss, destroying the Ring, unmaking Sauron, and causing the mountain to erupt in the cataclysm that ended the War of the Ring.

Depiction

In Peter Jackson's film trilogy, Mount Doom was represented principally by Mount Ngauruhoe and Mount Ruapehu in New Zealand's Tongariro National Park, augmented with digital effects. Tolkien's conception has often been linked to his experiences of industrial landscapes and, by some accounts, to the eruption-prone volcanoes he read about, such as Stromboli.
