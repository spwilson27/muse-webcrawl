Bursa is a city in northwestern Turkey and the administrative center of Bursa Province. The fourth most populous city in the country, it was the first major capital of the Ottoman Empire, from 1335 until the capital moved to Edirne and later Constantinople. Bursa lies in the foothills of Mount Uludağ, south of the Sea of Marmara.

History

The city was founded as Prusa (Greek: Προῦσα) by King Prusias I of Bithynia around 202 BC, reportedly on the advice of Hannibal, who had taken refuge at the Bithynian court. It passed to Rome, then to the Byzantine Empire, and was renowned in antiquity for its thermal baths. The Ottoman Turks under Orhan conquered Prusa in 1326 after a long siege, making it the first Ottoman capital; Orhan and his father Osman, founder of the dynasty, are buried there in türbes (tombs).

Sights and economy

Important monuments include the Grand Mosque (Ulu Cami, 1399), the Green Mosque and Green Tomb (Yeşil Camii and Yeşil Türbe), and the complexes of the early Ottoman sultans. The historic commercial district, with its hans and bazaars, together with the nearby village of Cumalıkızık, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2014 as "Bursa and Cumalıkızık: the Birth of the Ottoman Empire".

Bursa is a major center of Turkey's automotive industry, textiles (it was a historic terminus of the Silk Road and remains known for silk and towels), and food processing. The city is famous in Turkish cuisine as the home of İskender kebab, and the nearby mountain Uludağ is one of Turkey's leading ski resorts.
