Germanus of Auxerre

Bornc. 378, Auxerre, Gaul
Died31 July 448, Ravenna, Italy
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church; Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast31 July

Germanus of Auxerre (French: Saint Germain d'Auxerre; c. 378 – 448) was a bishop of Auxerre in late Roman Gaul and one of the most prominent saints of the early medieval West. Trained as a lawyer and provincial governor, he was ordained against his will and became a vigorous monastic founder and missionary.

Missions to Britain

In 429, at the request of British bishops, Germanus travelled to Britain with Lupus of Troyes to combat the Pelagian heresy, which denied original sin and the necessity of grace. According to his near-contemporary Life by Constantius of Lyon, he debated the Pelagians publicly, healed the blind daughter of a man of tribunician rank, and led British forces to the bloodless "Alleluia victory" over Saxon and Pictish raiders. He probably visited Britain a second time in the 440s.

Later life

Germanus interceded with the imperial court at Ravenna on behalf of the people of Armorica, where he died in 448 and was mourned by the court of Valentinian III and Galla Placidia. His body was returned to Auxerre, where the abbey church of Saint-Germain still bears his name.

Legacy

Germanus taught St Patrick, according to Irish tradition, and his cult spread widely in France and Britain. He is the patron saint of Auxerre.
