Thráin I is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, appearing in the appendices to The Lord of the Rings. A Dwarf of the line of Durin and son of Náin I, Thráin I was the founder of the Kingdom under the Mountain at Erebor, the Lonely Mountain, and the first of his people to bear one of the Seven Rings of Power given to the Dwarf-lords. He should not be confused with his descendant Thráin II, father of Thorin Oakenshield.

Biography

Thráin was born in the year 1934 of the Third Age in Khazad-dûm (Moria), ancestral home of Durin's Folk. His father Náin I was slain by the Balrog in 1981 T.A., and the dwarves abandoned Moria. Thráin led the remnant of his people into exile; after years of wandering he came in 1999 T.A. to the Lonely Mountain in the east of Rhovanion and founded there the Kingdom under the Mountain, becoming its first king. Under his rule the dwarves mined and delved in Erebor, grew rich trading with the Men of Dale and the Long Lake, and discovered the great jewel known as the Arkenstone, the Heart of the Mountain.

Thráin's ring, the last of the Seven to remain in dwarf hands over the ages, was said in dwarf tradition to have been given to Durin III by the Elven-smiths of Eregion rather than by Sauron. Unlike the Nine given to Men, the Seven could not turn the dwarves to wraiths, but inflamed their greed for gold. Thráin died in 2190 T.A. after a reign of nearly two centuries, and was succeeded by his son Thorin I, who later led much of the folk north to the Grey Mountains.

Later history of the line

The Ring of Thráin passed down his house until Thrór, grandfather of Thorin Oakenshield, and was lost when Thrór's son Thráin II was captured by Sauron in Dol Guldur — the ring was taken from him before his death in 2850 T.A., a loss revealed by Gandalf and central to the back-story of The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings.
