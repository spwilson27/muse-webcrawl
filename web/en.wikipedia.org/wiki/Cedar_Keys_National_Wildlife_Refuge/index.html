Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge

The refuge protects a cluster of small islands off Florida's Gulf coast.

The Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge is a United States National Wildlife Refuge located along the Gulf coast of Florida, near the town of Cedar Key in Levy County. Established in 1929 by President Herbert Hoover, it is one of the oldest refuges in the National Wildlife Refuge System and covers roughly 891 acres (361 hectares) across 13 small offshore islands.

History

The Cedar Keys were historically important nesting and rookery sites for wading birds, whose plumage was heavily hunted in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The refuge was created to protect these colonial nesting birds. Seahorse Key, the largest island in the group, once hosted one of the largest bird rookeries on Florida's Gulf coast, with tens of thousands of nesting ibis, herons, egrets, and cormorants, though nesting activity there declined markedly in the 2010s for reasons still studied by biologists.

Wildlife and habitat

The islands support salt marsh, mangrove, and coastal hardwood hammock habitats. Wildlife includes white ibis, various herons and egrets, brown pelicans, bald eagles, cottonmouth snakes (for which Seahorse Key is notable), and numerous shorebirds and migratory species. Several of the islands are designated wilderness areas under the Wilderness Act.

Access

Most refuge islands are closed to public entry to protect nesting birds; Atsena Otie Key is open for day use and is reached by boat from Cedar Key. The refuge is administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service as part of a complex that also includes the Lower Suwannee National Wildlife Refuge.
