The Santa Fe River is a 75-mile (121 km) river in northern Florida. It rises at Lake Santa Fe in Alachua County and flows westward, forming parts of the boundaries of several counties, before joining the Suwannee River near Branford. The river is celebrated for its numerous crystal-clear springs and for an unusual natural feature: it disappears entirely underground for about three miles.

The sink and rise

At O'Leno State Park the whole river sinks into a limestone sinkhole — the Santa Fe Sink — and travels through subterranean caverns before re-emerging at the River Rise Preserve State Park. The underground passage is part of the Floridan aquifer system, and dye-tracing studies have confirmed the connection. Cave divers explore parts of the conduit.

Springs and recreation

The lower Santa Fe is fed by first-magnitude and other springs including Ichetucknee Springs (on the tributary Ichetucknee River), Ginnie Springs, Poe Springs, Devil's Den-adjacent springs, and Blue Springs; these make it a major destination for swimming, tubing, paddling, and freshwater cave diving. The river's dark, tannin-stained water contrasts with the clear spring runs. Environmental concerns include nitrate pollution from agriculture and septic systems, which fuels algal growth in the springs, and declining spring flows from groundwater pumping.
