Pretoria Pit disaster

A Lancashire colliery of the early 20th century

Date21 December 1910
LocationWesthoughton, Lancashire, England
CauseUnderground explosion (firedamp)
Deaths344 men and boys

The Pretoria Pit disaster was a mining accident on 21 December 1910 at the Pretoria Pit, part of the Hulton Colliery in Westhoughton, Lancashire, England. An underground explosion killed 344 men and boys, making it the second-worst mining disaster in British history, exceeded only by the Senghenydd colliery disaster in Wales three years later.

The explosion

The Pretoria Pit consisted of several shafts sunk by the Hulton Colliery Company; the disaster occurred in the No. 3 Bank Pit, in the Plodder seam. On the morning of 21 December, around 900 men and boys were underground across the colliery's workings. At about 7:50 a.m. a roof fall in the Plodder mine is believed to have released a large quantity of firedamp (methane), which was ignited — possibly by a damaged safety lamp — producing a massive explosion that propagated through the workings aided by coal dust.

Of the 344 who died, the victims were men and boys from Westhoughton and the surrounding communities; many families lost multiple members. Only a handful of those in the affected workings survived. Rescue operations were hampered by afterdamp (carbon monoxide) and further falls, and recovery of bodies continued for days.

Aftermath

The disaster devastated the town of Westhoughton: it was said that there was scarcely a street without a house in mourning. A relief fund raised substantial sums for the widows and orphans. A memorial to the victims stands in Westhoughton cemetery, where many of the dead were buried, and services of remembrance are still held on the anniversary, including a major centenary commemoration in 2010.

The official inquiry examined the causes of the explosion and the state of safety in the pit, contributing to the wider debate that led to the Coal Mines Act 1911, which tightened safety requirements in British mines, including provisions on safety lamps, coal dust suppression, and rescue apparatus.
