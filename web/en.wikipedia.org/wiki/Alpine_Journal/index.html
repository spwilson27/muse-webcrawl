The Alpine Journal (AJ) is an annual publication of the Alpine Club of London, devoted to mountaineering and mountain exploration. First published in March 1863, it is among the oldest mountaineering journals in continuous existence and serves as a principal record of climbing in the Alps, the Himalaya, and the other great ranges of the world.

History

The journal was founded under the editorship of Hereford Brooke George, drawing on the Alpine Club's earlier Peaks, Passes and Glaciers publications. From its earliest volumes it combined narratives of ascents with scientific observations, book reviews, obituaries, and official Alpine Club proceedings. Through the late nineteenth century it documented the "golden age" and "silver age" of alpinism, including first ascents of major Alpine peaks; in the twentieth century it carried authoritative accounts of expeditions to Everest, K2, and other eight-thousanders.

Contents

A typical volume contains expedition reports, articles on climbing history and mountain culture, area notes compiling new ascents reported from around the world, book reviews, and an index of climbing literature. Expeditions supported by the Mount Everest Foundation and the Alpine Club often report in its pages. The journal's area notes and obituaries of climbers make it a standard reference for mountaineering historians.
