Rake (character)

In a historical context, a rake (short for rakehell, analogous to "hellraiser") was a man habituated to immoral conduct, particularly womanising. The rake is a stock character of Restoration comedy and, later, of the novel and opera.

Characteristics and examples

The rake is typically a witty, charming aristocrat who squanders his fortune on gambling, drink and seduction, defying social convention. Famous literary examples include the title figure of William Hogarth's pictorial series A Rake's Progress (1732–34), Lovelace in Samuel Richardson's Clarissa, the eponymous hero of Mozart's opera Don Giovanni, and Dorimant in George Etherege's The Man of Mode. In many narratives the rake is either punished or reformed by the love of a virtuous heroine — a pattern common in Regency romance fiction, where the "reformed rake" remains a popular hero type.
