Stryfe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stryfe is a supervillain appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. The character is most often depicted as an adversary of Cable and the X-Men. Created by writer Louise Simonson and artist Rob Liefeld, Stryfe first appeared in The New Mutants #86 (1990).

Fictional character biography

Stryfe is a clone of Nathan Summers, the mutant who would grow up to become Cable. In a dystopian future ruled by Apocalypse, the infant Nathan was infected with a techno-organic virus. Believing the child might not survive, Apocalypse's enemies in the Clan Chosen created a clone, intending it as a healthy replacement. Apocalypse's forces seized the clone and raised him under the name Stryfe, grooming him as a potential host body and heir.

Raised in cruelty and indoctrinated in Apocalypse's philosophy of survival of the fittest, Stryfe grew to hate both his progenitor and his "brother" Cable. He founded the Mutant Liberation Front, a terrorist organisation, and travelled back in time to the present day, where he became a recurring enemy of Cable, X-Force, and the X-Men. He played a central role in the 1992 crossover storyline "X-Cutioner's Song", during which he framed Cable for an assassination attempt on Professor X and released the Legacy Virus, a plague that would afflict mutants for years in the comics' chronology.

Powers and abilities

As a clone of Cable, Stryfe possesses powerful telepathic and telekinetic abilities, unrestrained by the techno-organic virus that limits Cable's own powers. His telekinesis grants him enhanced strength and durability, and he typically wears distinctive spiked armour.

In other media

Stryfe has appeared in animated series and video games associated with the X-Men franchise, and the character was referenced in connection with Cable's live-action film debut, though Stryfe himself did not appear.
