Erythromycin is an antibiotic used for the treatment of a number of bacterial infections. This includes respiratory tract infections, skin infections, chlamydia infections, pelvic inflammatory disease, and syphilis. It may also be used during pregnancy to prevent Group B streptococcal infection in the newborn, as well as to improve delayed stomach emptying (gastroparesis). Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that works by inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis through binding to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome.

History

Erythromycin was first isolated in 1952 from the metabolic products of a strain of Streptomyces erythreus (now Saccharopolyspora erythraea) found in a soil sample from the Philippines by scientists at Eli Lilly, led by Abelardo B. Aguilar. It was marketed under the trade name Ilosone (after the Philippine region of Iloilo where it was collected). Erythromycin was the first macrolide antibiotic and remains the prototype of the class that includes clarithromycin and azithromycin.

Side effects and interactions

Common side effects include abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, reflecting erythromycin's action as a motilin receptor agonist — a property exploited in its use as a prokinetic agent. It is a known inhibitor of cytochrome P450 3A4 and can prolong the QT interval, so it interacts with many other medications. Resistance among streptococci and staphylococci is common.
