The NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament, branded as March Madness, is a single-elimination tournament played each spring in the United States to determine the national champion of NCAA Division I men's college basketball. It has been held annually since 1939.

Format

The field comprises 68 teams: automatic bids for conference tournament champions and at-large selections chosen by a selection committee, whose seedings are announced on Selection Sunday. The First Four reduces the field to 64, after which teams advance through regional rounds to the Final Four and the national championship game.

History and culture

UCLA under John Wooden won ten titles between 1964 and 1975, the tournament's defining dynasty. The 1979 final between Magic Johnson's Michigan State and Larry Bird's Indiana State propelled the event's television popularity. Office pools built around the tournament bracket have become a widespread cultural phenomenon, and unexpected runs by low-seeded "Cinderella" teams are a hallmark of the event.
