Prototype

Testing a prototype vehicle.

A prototype is an early sample, model, or release of a product built to test a concept or process. It is a term used in a variety of contexts, including semantics, design, electronics, and software programming. A prototype is generally used to evaluate a new design to enhance precision by system analysts and users.

In engineering and design

Prototyping serves to allow engineers and designers to explore design alternatives, test theories, confirm performance, and ensure the product is safe and meets requirements before committing to tooling and production. Prototypes range from rough sketches and foam mock-ups ("low-fidelity") to fully functional pre-production units. Technologies such as 3D printing and CNC machining have made rapid prototyping routine, shortening development cycles for everything from aircraft (the traditional "X-plane" and prototype airframes) to consumer electronics.

In software

A software prototype is a working model of an application used to validate requirements and user-interface design with stakeholders. Evolutionary prototyping refines the model into the final system, while throwaway prototyping discards it after evaluation. The "prototype" concept also appears inside languages: JavaScript objects delegate to a prototype object, and prototype theory in cognitive psychology (after Eleanor Rosch) holds that categories are organised around typical exemplars.

Related ideas

In biology, the term is superseded by the concepts of holotype and archetype descriptions; in linguistics and philology, a proto-language is the reconstructed ancestor of a language family — not a prototype in the engineering sense (compare Proto-Finnic).
