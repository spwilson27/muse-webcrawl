The Durrani Empire (Pashto: د درانيانو ټولواکمني) was an Afghan empire founded by Ahmad Shah Durrani in 1747, with its capital at Kandahar. At its greatest extent it stretched from Mashhad in the west to Kashmir and Delhi in the east, and from the Amu Darya (Oxus) in the north to the Arabian Sea in the south. It is regarded as the founding polity of the modern state of Afghanistan, and Ahmad Shah is honoured as its national founder ("Baba", the Father).

Founding

After the assassination of the Persian ruler Nader Shah in 1747, Ahmad Khan, a young commander of the Abdali Pashtuns, was elected king at a loya jirga in Kandahar and took the title Durr-i-Durran ("Pearl of Pearls"), from which the dynastic name Durrani derives. He rapidly subdued Kabul, Herat, and Peshawar, and launched repeated campaigns into India.

Campaigns in India

Ahmad Shah invaded India eight times. His most famous victory was the Third Battle of Panipat (1761), where he crushed the Maratha Confederacy in one of the largest battles of the 18th century. The booty and tribute from India financed the empire, though his campaigns did not establish lasting Afghan rule east of the Punjab.

Decline

After Ahmad Shah's death in 1772, his son Timur Shah moved the capital to Kabul. Succession disputes among Timur's many sons, pressure from the Sikhs in the Punjab, Persia in the west, and the expanding British and Russian empires fragmented the state in the early 19th century. The Durrani dynasty fell in 1823, replaced eventually by the Barakzai dynasty of Dost Mohammad Khan.
