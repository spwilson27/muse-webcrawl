Nasal vowel

A nasal vowel is a vowel produced with the velum lowered so that air escapes through both the nose and the mouth. In the International Phonetic Alphabet, nasalisation is marked with a tilde above the vowel, as in [ã].

Occurrence

Nasal vowels are phonemic in languages such as French (as in bon [bɔ̃]), Portuguese, Polish and many others. In languages like English, vowels are nasalised only allophonically before nasal consonants.

Development

Nasal vowels commonly arise when a vowel absorbs the nasality of a following nasal consonant that is subsequently lost, a process seen in the history of French and Portuguese.
