The second impeachment of Donald Trump was the impeachment of the 45th president of the United States, adopted by the House of Representatives on 13 January 2021, one week after the attack on the United States Capitol. The single article of impeachment charged Trump with "incitement of insurrection" over his statements and actions surrounding the rally that preceded the riot, in which his supporters sought to disrupt the counting of Electoral College votes certifying Joe Biden's victory in the 2020 presidential election.

House proceedings

Trump thus became the only U.S. president — and only federal official — to be impeached twice, following his first impeachment in December 2019. Ten Republicans joined all Democrats in voting to impeach, making it the most bipartisan presidential impeachment vote in American history. The House acted after Vice President Mike Pence declined calls to invoke the Twenty-fifth Amendment.

Senate trial

The trial took place after Trump had left office on 20 January 2021; the Senate voted that trying a former president was constitutional. House managers, led by Representative Jamie Raskin, presented video evidence from the attack, while Trump's defence argued his speech was protected by the First Amendment and that the trial was unconstitutional. On 13 February 2021 the Senate voted 57–43 to convict — seven Republicans joining all 50 Democrats — the most bipartisan conviction vote in a presidential impeachment trial, but ten votes short of the two-thirds majority required, so Trump was acquitted.

Aftermath

Senate Republican leader Mitch McConnell, voting to acquit on jurisdictional grounds, stated that Trump was "practically and morally responsible" for provoking the attack and remained liable to criminal and civil process. The impeachment formed part of the wider political and legal fallout from January 6, including the House Select Committee investigation and later federal and state prosecutions related to efforts to overturn the 2020 election.
