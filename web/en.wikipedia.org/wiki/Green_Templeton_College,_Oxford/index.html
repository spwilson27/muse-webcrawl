Green Templeton College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. It was created in 2008 by the merger of Green College and Templeton College, making it one of the university's youngest colleges. It is a graduate-only college with a particular academic focus on subjects related to human welfare, including medicine, public health, management and the social sciences.

History

Green College was founded in 1979 with a benefaction from the Texas Instruments co-founder Cecil Howard Green and his wife Ida, and was centred on medicine. Its site incorporates the eighteenth-century Radcliffe Observatory, whose Tower of the Winds is one of Oxford's landmarks. Templeton College began in 1984 as the Oxford Centre for Management Studies, endowed by the investor and philanthropist Sir John Templeton, and specialised in business and management education. The two colleges merged on 1 August 2008 to form Green Templeton College.

Site and buildings

The college occupies the former Green College site on Woodstock Road, adjoining the Radcliffe Observatory Quarter. The Observatory building houses common rooms and event spaces, while surrounding buildings provide a library, lecture facilities, dining and accommodation for graduate students. The former Templeton site at Egrove Park in Kennington serves as an executive education and conference centre.

Academic profile

Green Templeton admits graduate students only, in disciplines including medicine, public and global health, business and management, education, social policy and other applied social sciences. Its fellowship has included senior figures in medicine and management studies, and the college hosts centres concerned with topics such as the future of healthcare and emerging markets.
