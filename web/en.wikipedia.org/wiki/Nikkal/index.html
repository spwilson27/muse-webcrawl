Nikkal

Nikkal (Ugaritic: 𐎐𐎋𐎍, nkl) was a goddess worshipped in the ancient Levantine city of Ugarit and elsewhere in the ancient Near East. Her name derives from Sumerian Ningal, "Great Lady", and she was associated with orchards and fruit. In Ugaritic religion she was the wife of the moon god Yarikh.

Nikkal

Origin: Mesopotamian (from Ningal)

Cult centre: Ugarit

Consort: Yarikh (moon god)

Domain: Orchards, fruit

Mythology

The Ugaritic poem known as Nikkal and the Kathirat recounts the marriage negotiations between Yarikh and Nikkal's father, Khirkhib, king of summer. Yarikh offers lavish bride-gifts of gold, silver and lapis lazuli, and the Kathirat, goddesses of conception and childbirth, are invoked to bless the union with offspring. The text is fragmentary but is notable for its lyrical descriptions of Nikkal as a fruitful bride.

The Hurrian Hymn

Nikkal is also the addressee of the Hurrian Hymn to Nikkal, preserved on a clay tablet (h.6) from Ugarit dating to roughly 1400 BCE. The tablet pairs the hymn's text with one of the oldest known examples of musical notation, making it among the earliest surviving written melodies in the world. The song appears to be a prayer for fertility.
