Kolomyia

Kolomyia, Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, Ukraine

Kolomyia (Ukrainian: Коломия; Polish: Kołomyja; German: Kolomea) is a city located on the Prut River in Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast in western Ukraine. It is the administrative centre of Kolomyia Raion and serves as a separate city of regional significance. Kolomyia is known as the capital of the Pokuttia region and is famous throughout Ukraine for its tradition of pysanky (decorated Easter eggs), including the landmark Pysanka Museum.

History

The settlement was first mentioned in 1240 in the Galician–Volhynian Chronicle. It developed as a trading post on the salt routes between Galicia and Moldavia. Kolomyia received Magdeburg rights in 1405. Over the centuries it passed between Poland, the Ottoman sphere of influence, the Habsburg Monarchy (from 1772), and after the First World War returned to Poland. In 1939 it was annexed by the Soviet Union, suffered under Nazi occupation during the Second World War, and became part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Culture

The Pysanka Museum, opened in 2000, is shaped like a giant Easter egg, some 13 metres high, and houses a collection of over 10,000 decorated eggs from Ukraine and around the world. The city also hosts the Kolomyia Museum of Folk Art of Hutsulshchyna and Pokuttia, founded in 1926, with rich collections of Hutsul woodcarving, pottery, textiles and icons.

Geography

Kolomyia lies in the foothills of the Carpathian Mountains, on the left bank of the Prut River, about 65 kilometres south-east of Ivano-Frankivsk. Its moderate continental climate and proximity to the mountains make it a gateway for tourism in the Pokuttia and Hutsul regions.

Demographics

According to the 2001 census, the population was about 61,000, composed overwhelmingly of Ukrainians, with small Russian, Polish and Jewish communities historically present. The broader urban community has remained a regional centre for education and light industry.
