Basque Y

The Basque Y (Spanish: Y vasca; Basque: Euskal Y) is a high-speed rail network under construction in the Basque Country, Spain, named because the layout of its three branches, viewed on a map, resembles the letter Y. The project will connect the three Basque provincial capitals — Bilbao, Vitoria-Gasteiz, and San Sebastián (Donostia) — with each other and with the French border at Irún/Hendaye, forming part of the EU's Atlantic Corridor.

Project

The network totals roughly 172 km of new high-speed line, much of it in tunnel because of the mountainous terrain (around 60% of the route is in tunnels and a further share on viaducts). Lines are built to standard gauge for mixed passenger service at design speeds up to about 250 km/h, replacing the winding conventional lines and cutting the Bilbao–San Sebastián journey to well under an hour. Construction began in the mid-2000s under the Spanish rail infrastructure manager Adif, with the Gipuzkoa sections managed by the Basque Government's Euskal Trenbide Sarea. The project has faced repeated cost increases and delays, with completion targets slipping into the late 2020s and beyond; the cross-border connection depends on French high-speed plans between Bordeaux and the Spanish border, which have progressed slowly.

Controversy

The Basque Y has been a major political controversy: supporters present it as essential for regional cohesion and freight shift from road, while opponents — including environmental groups and Basque nationalist parties such as EH Bildu — criticise its cost, environmental impact, and uncertain cross-border utility, and the project was targeted by ETA threats in its early years.
