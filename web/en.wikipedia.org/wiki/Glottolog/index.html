Glottolog is a bibliographic database of the world's lesser-known languages, developed and maintained at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, Germany (formerly at the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Human History in Jena). Its main curators include Harald Hammarström and Martin Haspelmath.

Overview

Glottolog aims to provide a comprehensive catalogue of the world's languages, language families and dialects, together with an extensive bibliography of descriptive materials — grammars, dictionaries and text collections — especially for endangered and little-documented languages. Each language variety in the database is assigned a unique identifier, the Glottocode, a stable code of four letters and four digits.

Classification

The classification of languages into families in Glottolog is deliberately conservative: a family is recognised only where the curators judge the published evidence for genealogical relationship to be convincing. As a result, Glottolog recognises several hundred independent families and isolates, and its classification sometimes differs from that of Ethnologue, which assigns ISO 639-3 codes and tends to be more inclusive.

Relationship to other resources

Glottolog Glottocodes are used as stable identifiers across linguistic typology databases such as the World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS) and AUTOTYP, and the database feeds into larger aggregations of linguistic data. The entire database is freely available online and downloadable under an open licence, with versioned releases (Glottolog 4.x and 5.x series).
