Mincemeat

Mincemeat, the traditional filling of British mince pies at Christmas.

Mincemeat is a mixture of chopped dried fruit, distilled spirits, and spices, traditionally including beef suet and historically minced meat, used chiefly as the filling of mince pies, a staple of the British Christmas table. Despite the name, most modern mincemeat contains no meat at all, though suet (often vegetarian) remains common.

History

Mincemeat descends from medieval English cooking, in which minced mutton or beef was combined with suet, dried fruits such as raisins and currants, sugar, and warming spices — cinnamon, cloves, nutmeg — brought back from the Crusades and the spice trade. Meat originally served as a means of preservation, and over the centuries the proportion of fruit and sugar rose while the meat fell away; by the 20th century, recipes typically included only shredded suet for richness. Traditional recipes call for grated apple, citrus peel and juice, brown sugar, and brandy or rum, mixed and matured for several weeks before use.

Variations

Mincemeat fills mince pies and tarts and features in Christmas puddings' accompaniments and Eccles-style cakes. In the United States, "mincemeat" pies — especially in New England — sometimes retain ground beef alongside fruit, echoing the older tradition. The name also survives in the unrelated Second World War deception codenamed Operation Mincemeat, after the dish's chopped contents.
