Lockheed T-33

Lockheed T-33 Shooting Star
Role: Jet trainer
Manufacturer: Lockheed
First flight: 22 March 1948
Number built: 6,557
Developed from: Lockheed P-80 Shooting Star

The Lockheed T-33 Shooting Star (or T-Bird) is an American subsonic jet trainer. It was produced by Lockheed and made its first flight in 1948. The T-33 was developed from the Lockheed P-80/F-80 starting as the TP-80C/TF-80C during development, then designated T-33A. It was used by the U.S. Navy initially as the TO-2, then TV-2, and after 1962, T-33B.

A T-33 Shooting Star, among the most widely used jet trainers ever built.

Design and development

The T-33 was created by lengthening the F-80 fuselage by about 3 feet (0.9 m) to add a second seat, with wing tip fuel tanks becoming a signature feature. More than 6,500 were built, including licence production by Canadair (as the CT-133 Silver Star) and Kawasaki in Japan. The type trained generations of pilots from more than 40 nations during the transition to jet aircraft.

Operational history

The T-33 served as the standard advanced jet trainer of the United States Air Force for decades and was also adapted for reconnaissance (RT-33), target towing, drone direction, and as a testbed. Some were converted into the F-94 Starfire interceptor line via the related YF-94. The U.S. Air Force retired its last T-33s in the 1980s, but a few remained in service with smaller air forces — Bolivia operated the type into the 2010s — and many survive as warbirds and gate guardians.

Specifications

The T-33A is powered by a single Allison J33-A-35 centrifugal-flow turbojet of 5,400 lbf thrust (with water injection), giving a maximum speed of about 600 mph (970 km/h) at altitude, a range near 1,275 miles (2,050 km) with tip tanks, and a service ceiling of 48,000 ft (14,600 m). Crew: two, in tandem.
