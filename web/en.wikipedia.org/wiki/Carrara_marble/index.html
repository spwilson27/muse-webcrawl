Carrara marble, sometimes spelled Carara marble, is a type of white or blue-grey marble quarried near the city of Carrara in the Apuan Alps of Tuscany, Italy. Prized since Roman times for its purity and fine grain, it is one of the most prestigious stones for sculpture and building decoration. Landmark works in Carrara marble include the Pantheon and Trajan's Column in Rome, the façade of Siena Cathedral, and Michelangelo's David and the Pietà.

Geology

The marble formed from a pure Jurassic limestone (the Marmi Formation) that was buried and metamorphosed during the Tertiary Alpine orogeny as the Apuan Alps were uplifted. Because the original limestone was unusually free of impurities, the resulting marble is exceptionally white; grey veining derives from trace minerals and organic material smeared out during deformation. The quarries, over 600 in number (many now abandoned), line the valleys of Fantiscritti, Colonnata and Torano above Carrara.

History

Systematic quarrying began under the Romans in the 1st century BC, supplying the building boom of Augustan Rome; the harbour of Luni shipped blocks across the Mediterranean. After a medieval decline, quarrying revived in the Renaissance: Michelangelo personally selected blocks at Carrara for the tomb of Julius II, the Medici Chapel and David (1501–1504). Blocks were historically slid down mountain tracks on wooden sledges called lizzatura — a dangerous operation still re-enacted annually. Since the 19th century, industrial methods including wire saws, railways, and modern cutting machinery have made Carrara a global centre of the dimension-stone trade, producing hundreds of thousands of tonnes per year, much of it now exported as powder and aggregate as well as block and slab.

Varieties and uses

Trade grades include the classic Bianco Carrara, the whiter Statuario (favoured for statuary), Calacatta (bolder, gold-toned veining, largely from the Carrara area), Bardiglio (grey) and Arabescato. The stone is used for sculpture, flooring, countertops, cladding and funerary monuments.
