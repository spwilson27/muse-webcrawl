Háttatal

Háttatal (Old Norse: "list of metres") is the third and final main section of the Prose Edda by Snorri Sturluson. It consists of a long poem of 102 stanzas composed by Snorri himself around 1222–1223, in praise of King Hákon Hákonarson of Norway and his father-in-law and co-regent Jarl Skúli Bárðarson, together with Snorri's own prose commentary explaining the metrical form of each stanza.

Content and purpose

The poem demonstrates a catalogue of Old Norse verse forms (hættir): it is composed chiefly in dróttkvætt variants but passes through dozens of metres and rhetorical devices — differing line lengths, internal rhyme schemes, alliteration patterns, and figures such as refrains — with the commentary naming and analysing each in turn. Háttatal thus serves as both a panegyric and a technical manual exemplifying the skaldic art for younger poets, complementing the mythology of Gylfaginning and the poetics and kennings of Skáldskaparmál.

Composition and context

Snorri composed the poem after his first visit to Norway (1218–1220), where he had become a retainer of the young king Hákon and Jarl Skúli. The split praise between the two rulers later became politically delicate when Skúli rebelled and was killed in 1240. The oldest surviving manuscript is the Codex Upsaliensis (DG 11), from the early 14th century.
