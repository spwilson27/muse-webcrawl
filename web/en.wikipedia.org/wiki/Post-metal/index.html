Post-metal

Stylistic origins: Post-rock, sludge metal, doom metal, shoegaze
Era: 1990s–2000s onwards
Key artists: Neurosis, Isis, Pelican, Cult of Luna, Russian Circles

Post-metal is a music genre combining the heaviness and intensity of heavy metal — particularly sludge and doom metal — with the textures, dynamics and song structures of post-rock. It typically features long, slowly developing compositions that build from quiet, atmospheric passages to massive climaxes.

History

The genre's foundations were laid in the 1990s by Neurosis, whose albums Souls at Zero (1992) and Through Silver in Blood (1996) fused hardcore, metal and ambient experimentation. The term "post-metal" gained currency in the 2000s around the band Isis, led by Aaron Turner, whose Oceanic (2002) and Panopticon (2004) became defining records; the style was sometimes called "atmospheric sludge" or, by Turner himself, "thinking man's metal". Instrumental bands such as Pelican and Russian Circles, and European acts including Cult of Luna and Amenra, developed the sound further.

Characteristics

Post-metal songs often run eight minutes or longer, use heavily distorted guitars layered with clean arpeggios, and feature minimal or buried vocals. Live visuals, oceanic and natural imagery, and influences from ambient, industrial and shoegaze music are common. In the 2010s bands such as Deafheaven blended post-metal with black metal and shoegaze ("blackgaze"), bringing the style wider attention.
