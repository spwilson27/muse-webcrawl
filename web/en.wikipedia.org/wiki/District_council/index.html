District council

A district council is a type of local government body found in several countries, typically responsible for administering a district — a subdivision of a county, region, or metropolitan area. The meaning and powers of district councils vary considerably by country.

United Kingdom

In England, district councils are one tier of local government in two-tier areas, responsible for services such as housing, planning, waste collection, leisure, and council tax collection, while county councils handle education and social services. Since the 1970s their number has been reduced by mergers and the creation of unitary authorities; the 2023 local government structure had 164 district councils in England. Scotland and Wales use unitary council areas, of which several are formally called councils rather than district councils.

Hong Kong

Hong Kong's District Councils are local consultative bodies for the territory's 18 districts, advising on local affairs and managing community facilities. Their composition and powers were substantially changed by reforms in 2023, which reduced the share of directly elected seats.

Other countries

District councils also exist in parts of Pakistan, India, New Zealand (as district councils for territorial authorities), and Israel, with roles ranging from purely advisory to substantial executive responsibilities.
