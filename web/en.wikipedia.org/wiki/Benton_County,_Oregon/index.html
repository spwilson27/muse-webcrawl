Benton County is a county in the U.S. state of Oregon. As of the 2020 census its population was about 95,000. The county seat and largest city is Corvallis, home of Oregon State University. The county lies in the west-central Willamette Valley, between the Willamette River to the east and the Oregon Coast Range to the west.

History

The county was created by the Provisional Government of Oregon in 1847 from part of Polk County and named for Senator Thomas Hart Benton of Missouri, a champion of westward expansion and the Oregon Country. The Kalapuya peoples inhabited the area for thousands of years before Euro-American settlement. Corvallis, originally called Marysville, was founded in 1845 and became the county seat; it briefly served as Oregon's territorial capital in 1855.

Geography

Benton County covers about 679 square miles, of which a small fraction is water. Mary's Peak, at 4,097 feet (1,249 m) the highest point in the Oregon Coast Range, rises in the county's west within the Siuslaw National Forest. Major waterways include the Willamette River on the eastern border and the Alsea and Marys rivers. The William L. Finley National Wildlife Refuge protects wetlands and oak savanna in the south of the county.

Economy and education

Oregon State University, founded in 1868 and located in Corvallis, is the county's largest employer and a major land-grant research institution. Other significant employers include Hewlett-Packard's Corvallis campus, Samaritan Health Services, and agriculture and forestry in the surrounding countryside — grass seed, wine grapes (the county lies partly in the Willamette Valley AVA), hazelnuts and timber.

Communities

Cities in Benton County include Corvallis, Philomath, Monroe, Adair Village and part of Albany. Unincorporated communities include Alsea, Bellfountain, Blodgett and Wren. The county is consistently ranked among the most highly educated and politically liberal counties in Oregon.
