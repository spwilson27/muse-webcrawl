Western United States

The Western United States (also called the American West, the Far West, and the West) is the region comprising the westernmost U.S. states. The United States Census Bureau defines the West as the 13 states of the Mountain and Pacific divisions: Montana, Wyoming, Colorado, New Mexico, Idaho, Utah, Nevada, Arizona, Washington, Oregon, California, Alaska, and Hawaii.

Geography

The West contains the Rocky Mountains, the Great Basin, the Sierra Nevada and Cascade ranges, the Mojave and Sonoran deserts, and the Pacific coast. It includes the highest point in the contiguous United States, Mount Whitney, and the lowest, Death Valley. Much of its land is federally owned, a distinctive feature of the region.

History

Long inhabited by Native American peoples, the region was explored and claimed by Spain, Mexico, Britain, Russia, and the United States. The Louisiana Purchase (1803), the annexation of Texas (1845), the Mexican Cession (1848), and westward migration along trails such as the Oregon Trail brought it into the United States, followed by gold rushes, railroad building, and rapid settlement.

Demographics

The West is the fastest-growing region of the United States and includes major metropolitan areas such as Los Angeles, Phoenix, Seattle, Denver, and Las Vegas. Its population is ethnically diverse, with large Hispanic and Asian American communities.
