Tully–Fisher relation

The Tully–Fisher relation is an empirical correlation between the intrinsic luminosity of a spiral galaxy and the width of its spectral lines, which measures the galaxy's maximum rotation velocity. Published by R. Brent Tully and J. Richard Fisher in 1977, the relation — approximately L ∝ v4 — allows astronomers to estimate a galaxy's true luminosity from an observable line width and hence, by comparing with its apparent brightness, its distance. It became one of the principal rungs of the extragalactic distance ladder beyond the reach of Cepheid variables.

Baryonic form

The baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, which relates total baryonic mass (stars plus gas) to rotation velocity, is remarkably tight over many orders of magnitude and holds even for gas-rich, low-surface-brightness galaxies. Its tightness is a key observational constraint discussed in both dark-matter and modified-gravity (MOND) frameworks.
