Compagnie Financière Richemont AG, commonly Richemont, is a Swiss-based luxury goods holding company founded in 1988 by South African businessman Johann Rupert. It is one of the world's three largest luxury groups, alongside LVMH (Louis Vuitton) and Kering, with a particular strength in "hard luxury" — jewellery and watches.

History

Rupert spun off the non-South African assets of the Rembrandt Group into Richemont, which assembled stakes in Cartier, Dunhill and Montblanc, then acquired watchmakers including Vacheron Constantin, Jaeger-LeCoultre, IWC and A. Lange & Söhne, and jeweller Van Cleef & Arpels. The group expanded into online luxury retail with Net-a-Porter and YOOX, later agreeing to sell YNAP to Mytheresa in 2024–2025.

Business

Richemont organises its "maisons" in jewellery (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Buccellati), specialist watchmaking (its Swiss manufactures), and other businesses including Chloé, Alaïa, Montblanc and Delvaux. The Rupert family controls the company through voting 'B' shares despite a minority economic stake — a dual-class structure typical of the sector.
