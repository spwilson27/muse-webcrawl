Graham Priest, leading advocate of dialetheism.

Graham Priest (born 1948) is a British-Australian philosopher known principally for his defence of dialetheism — the view that some contradictions are true — and for his foundational work in paraconsistent logic, the study of logical systems in which a contradiction does not entail everything. He has held posts at the University of Queensland, the University of Melbourne, and the City University of New York Graduate Center.

Work

Priest studied mathematics and logic at Cambridge and the London School of Economics, where his doctorate dealt with the logic of paradox. His book In Contradiction (1987, second edition 2006) is the classic statement of dialetheism, arguing that paradoxes such as the Liar show that the boundary of thought includes true contradictions. Other works include Beyond the Limits of Thought (1995), Towards Non-Being (2005), on the logic of intentionality and nonexistent objects, and One (2014), a study of being and unity with connections to Buddhist philosophy.

Priest has also written extensively on non-classical logic, the philosophy of language, metaphysics, and Asian thought, and is a co-editor of the history of philosophy. His views are controversial: critics argue dialetheism undermines rationality, while Priest maintains that classical logic itself cannot consistently handle the paradoxes it generates. He was elected a Fellow of the Australian Academy of the Humanities.
