Enguri Dam

The Enguri Dam is a hydroelectric arch dam on the Enguri River in Georgia. Currently it is the world's second highest concrete arch dam, with a height of 271.5 metres (891 ft). It is located in the town of Jvari in the Tsalenjikha Municipality, at the foot of the Caucasus Mountains in north-western Georgia, near the breakaway region of Abkhazia.

History

The Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev initially proposed a major dam and hydroelectric power scheme on the Bzyb River, but experts advised that it would have catastrophic effects on the beaches of Pitsunda; instead, the Enguri River site was chosen. Construction began in 1961 and the first generators entered service in 1978, with the power station completed in 1987. At the time of its inauguration it was the tallest arch dam in the world, a record it later lost to China's Jinping-I Dam.

Operation

The dam's five 260 MW generators give a total capacity of 1,300 MW, supplying a large share of Georgia's electricity — historically around 40 per cent of national consumption. The powerhouse is underground on the far side of the river in territory controlled by Abkhazia, while the dam itself stands in Georgian-controlled territory, an arrangement that has required continuous cross-line cooperation since the war in Abkhazia of 1992–93. Under informal agreements Georgia receives about 60 per cent of the output free of charge and Abkhazia about 40 per cent. Rehabilitation works in the 1990s and 2000s, supported by the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development, restored units that had fallen into disrepair.

Tourism

Since 2015 the dam has been developed as an industrial-heritage attraction, with a visitor centre and viewing platforms, and it has been proposed for inscription on Georgia's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.
