The Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (明石海峡大橋) is a suspension bridge which links the city of Kobe on the Japanese mainland of Honshu to Iwaya on Awaji Island. It is part of the Kobe-Awaji-Naruto Expressway, and crosses the busy and turbulent Akashi Strait. On its completion in 1998 it had the longest central span of any suspension bridge in the world, at 1,991 metres (6,532 ft) — a record it held until 2022.

History

Before the bridge, passengers crossed the Akashi Strait by ferry; dangerous storms and collisions, including the 1955 sinking of two ferries with 168 deaths, spurred demands for a fixed crossing. Construction began in 1988. The 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake, whose epicentre lay between the two towers, shifted the towers almost a metre apart, lengthening the span while the bridge was under construction; the design accommodated the change without delay.

Design

The bridge has three spans: a central span of 1,991 m and two side spans of 960 m, for a total length of 3,911 m. Its two steel towers rise 282.8 m above the sea. The bridge is designed to withstand winds of 286 km/h, earthquakes up to magnitude 8.5, and the strait's fierce currents. Tuned mass dampers in the towers limit oscillation. Nearly 300,000 km of wire were used in the main cables.

It is also called the Pearl Bridge, and its towers offer a visitor promenade and observation facilities. Illumination of the cables changes with seasons and occasions.
