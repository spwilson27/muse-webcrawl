Product placement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Product placement, also known as embedded marketing, is a form of advertising in which branded goods and services are featured within a work of entertainment — such as a film, television programme, video game, music video or novel — usually in return for payment or promotional consideration to the producer.

History

Early examples date to the beginnings of cinema; by the mid-20th century, studios maintained placement departments. The modern industry expanded sharply after the success of placements such as Reese's Pieces in E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982), which was followed by a large reported rise in the candy's sales, and Ray-Ban sunglasses in Risky Business (1983) and Top Gun (1986). The James Bond franchise is noted for extensive automotive, watch and drinks placements.

Types

Placements range from background props and signage, through products used and handled by characters, to "plot integration" in which a brand becomes part of the story. In games and virtual worlds, placement extends to in-game advertising and branded items; in influencer content, to sponsored integrations.

Regulation and criticism

Many jurisdictions require disclosure that content contains paid placement (for example, the "P" logo in the UK and EU rules), and placement of tobacco and alcohol is heavily restricted. Critics argue placements bypass viewers' advertising defences — especially children's — while marketers value the inability to skip embedded brands and the prestige transfer from admired characters.

Effectiveness research

Studies generally find that prominent, well-integrated placements improve brand recall and attitudes, though effects on actual purchases are harder to isolate.
