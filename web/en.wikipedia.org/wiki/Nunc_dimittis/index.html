Nunc dimittis

The Nunc dimittis (Latin for "now lettest thou depart"), also known as the Song of Simeon or the Canticle of Simeon, is a canticle taken from the second chapter of the Gospel of Luke. It records the words spoken by Simeon when, holding the infant Jesus in the Temple in Jerusalem, he recognised him as the promised Messiah.

Text

The traditional English text, from the Book of Common Prayer, reads:

Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word:
For mine eyes have seen thy salvation,
Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people;
A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.

Liturgical use

The Nunc dimittis forms a central part of the evening offices of several Christian traditions. In the Anglican tradition it is sung at Evensong, paired with the Magnificat. In Roman Catholic practice it is the Gospel canticle of Compline (Night Prayer), and in Orthodox worship it is sung at Vespers. Countless composers have set the text to music, often as part of a paired Magnificat and Nunc dimittis ("Mag and Nunc") for cathedral choirs.
