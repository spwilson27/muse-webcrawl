Laurales

The Laurales are an order of flowering plants (angiosperms), comprising around 2,500–2,800 species in some 85–90 genera, grouped into seven families. They belong to the magnoliids, one of the early-diverging lineages of flowering plants. The order includes many familiar and economically important aromatic plants: bay laurel, cinnamon, avocado, sassafras and the spicebushes, as well as timber trees and ornamentals such as the tulip tree's relatives in other orders are sometimes confused with them.

Characteristics

Members of the Laurales are mostly woody plants — trees and shrubs, with a few vines and one parasitic genus (Cassytha). Their leaves typically contain spherical oil cells that produce aromatic compounds, giving many species their characteristic fragrance. The flowers are often small and hypogynous or perigynous with a cup-shaped receptacle (hypanthium); the perianth is usually undifferentiated into sepals and petals (tepals), and the carpels are frequently separate or embedded in the receptacle. The fruit is commonly a drupe or berry seated on or enclosed by the enlarged floral cup.

Classification

The seven families recognised by the APG IV system (2016) are:

  • Lauraceae — by far the largest family (~2,500 species), including laurel, cinnamon, camphor tree and avocado
  • Monimiaceae — including boldo (Peumus boldus)
  • Hernandiaceae — mostly tropical trees, some with winged fruits adapted to ocean dispersal
  • Calycanthaceae — sweetshrubs (Calycanthus) and the former Idiospermaceae
  • Atherospermataceae — southern sassafrases of Australia and South America
  • Siparunaceae — neotropical and African shrubs and trees
  • Gomortegaceae — a single Chilean species, Gomortega keule (queule)

Fossil evidence, including flowers such as Mauldinia and early fruits from the Cretaceous, shows that the Laurales were already diverse and widespread roughly 100 million years ago; the order is thought to have originated in the former supercontinent Gondwana or Laurasia, with modern families distributed pantropically and in warm-temperate regions.

Economic importance

The Lauraceae supply culinary herbs and spices (bay leaves, cinnamon, cassia), the avocado fruit, camphor and aromatic essential oils, and valuable timber (e.g. greenheart, stinkwood). Calycanthus species and the tulipwood relatives are planted ornamentally. Some members are of conservation concern, such as the endangered Chilean queule and the avocado's wild relatives.
