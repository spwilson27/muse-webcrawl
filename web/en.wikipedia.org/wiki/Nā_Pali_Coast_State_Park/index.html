Nā Pali Coast State Park

Nā Pali Coast State Park is a 6,175-acre (2,499 ha) state park on the northwest shore of the island of Kauaʻi, Hawaiʻi. Its name means "the cliffs" in Hawaiian, referring to the dramatic sea cliffs (pali) that rise as much as 4,000 feet (1,200 m) above the Pacific Ocean along roughly 16 miles (26 km) of rugged coastline.

Description

The park is inaccessible to vehicles; it can be reached only by hiking, boat, kayak or helicopter. Its landscape of fluted cliffs, hanging valleys, waterfalls and remote beaches — including Kalalau Beach and Honopū Beach — is considered among the most beautiful coastlines in the world. The area is within the Hono O Nā Pali Natural Area Reserve, which protects native ecosystems.

Kalalau Trail

The famous Kalalau Trail, about 11 miles (18 km) long, traverses the park from Keʻe Beach to Kalalau Valley. Permits are required for hiking beyond Hanakāpīʻai Valley and for camping. The trail is known for narrow, eroded sections, stream crossings and spectacular views, and is frequently listed among America's most rewarding — and dangerous — hikes.

History

The Nā Pali valleys were inhabited by Native Hawaiians for centuries, supported by taro cultivation and fishing. After the 19th century, the remote valleys were gradually abandoned, though they briefly hosted small settlements and, in the 1960s–70s, informal encampments. Archaeological sites, heiau (temples) and agricultural terraces remain throughout the park.
