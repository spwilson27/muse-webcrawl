Politics of Brazil

The politics of Brazil take place in a framework of a federal presidential representative democratic republic, whereby the President is both head of state and head of government, and of a multi-party system. The political and administrative organization of Brazil comprises the federal government, the 26 states and a federal district, and the municipalities.

The federal government exercises control over the central government and is divided into three independent branches: executive, legislative, and judicial. Executive power is exercised by the President, advised by a cabinet of secretaries and ministers. Legislative power is vested in the National Congress, a two-chamber legislature comprising the Federal Senate and the Chamber of Deputies. Judicial power is exercised by the judiciary, consisting of the Supreme Federal Court, the Superior Court of Justice and other tribunals, the National Council of Justice and the state, federal district, and municipal courts.

Constitution

Brazil has had seven constitutions. The current one, promulgated in 1988, is known as the "Citizen Constitution" because it was drafted after the end of the military dictatorship that ruled the country from 1964 to 1985, and was designed to enshrine civil rights and liberties.

Political parties

Brazil has a highly fragmented multi-party system, with dozens of parties represented in Congress. Major parties have included the Workers' Party (PT), the Brazilian Social Democracy Party (PSDB), the Brazilian Democratic Movement (MDB), and the Liberal Party (PL). Coalition presidentialism — building governing coalitions across many parties — is a defining feature of the system.
