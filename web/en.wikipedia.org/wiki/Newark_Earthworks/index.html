Newark Earthworks

The Newark Earthworks in Newark and Heath, Ohio, are the largest surviving set of geometric earthworks built by the Hopewell culture (100 BCE – 500 CE). They form part of the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023.

Description

The complex originally covered more than four square miles and includes three preserved sections: the Great Circle Earthworks, a nearly perfect circle about 1,200 feet in diameter; the Octagon Earthworks, an octagon connected to a smaller circle and aligned to lunar cycles; and the Wright Earthworks, a fragment of a large square enclosure.

Astronomy

The Octagon is aligned to the 18.6-year cycle of the Moon's rising and setting extremes, evidence of sophisticated Hopewell astronomical knowledge and engineering.

Preservation

Much of the complex was destroyed by farming, canals, railroads, and urban growth. Surviving portions were preserved in the 19th century; the Octagon grounds were long used as a golf course under lease, a use that ended after litigation, with public access expanded in the 2020s. The site is managed by the Ohio History Connection in partnership with tribal nations.
