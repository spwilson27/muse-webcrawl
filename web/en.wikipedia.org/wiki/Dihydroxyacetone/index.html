Dihydroxyacetone

Dihydroxyacetone (DHA), also known as glycerone, is a simple saccharide (a triose) with the formula C3H6O3. It is the simplest of all ketoses and, uniquely among common sugars, has no chiral centre. DHA is a hygroscopic white crystalline powder with a sweet taste.

Biochemistry

Its phosphate ester, dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP), is a central intermediate in glycolysis, interconverted with glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate by triose phosphate isomerase, and also participates in the Calvin cycle and lipid metabolism.

Sunless tanning

DHA is the active ingredient in most sunless tanning (self-tanning) products. It reacts with amino acids in the dead surface layer of the skin in a Maillard reaction, producing brown melanoidin pigments within a few hours. The colour lasts several days until the treated cells are shed. DHA has been approved for cosmetic use by the US FDA since the 1970s and does not significantly penetrate beyond the stratum corneum.
