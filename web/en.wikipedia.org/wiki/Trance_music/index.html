Trance music

Trance is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged from the British new-age music scene and the German techno and hardcore scenes of the early 1990s. It is characterised by tempos generally between 125 and 150 beats per minute, repeating melodic phrases, and a musical form that distinctly builds tension and elements throughout a track, often culminating in one or two "peaks" or "drops". Vocals, when present, are often sung by female vocalists ranging from mezzo-soprano to soprano. The genre is named for its intended hypnotic, trance-inducing effect on the dancefloor.

History

Early trance drew on acid house, techno and the electronic body music scene; landmark early tracks include "We Came in Peace" by Dance 2 Trance (1990), "The Age of Love" (1990) and Jam & Spoon's remix work. A parallel psychedelic tradition grew out of Goa, India, producing Goa trance and later psytrance. In the late 1990s trance became a global commercial phenomenon through DJs and producers such as Paul van Dyk, Tiësto, Armin van Buuren, Ferry Corsten and Above & Beyond, with events such as Trance Energy and A State of Trance drawing large audiences.

Subgenres

Subgenres include uplifting trance, vocal trance, progressive trance, tech trance, hard trance, psychedelic trance (psytrance), Goa trance and balearic trance. While commercial popularity ebbed in the 2010s, trance retains a large international following.
