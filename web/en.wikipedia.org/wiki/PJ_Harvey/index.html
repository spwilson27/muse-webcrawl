Polly Jean "PJ" Harvey MBE (born 9 October 1969) is an English singer-songwriter, musician, poet and artist. Primarily known as a vocalist and guitarist, she is also proficient with a wide range of instruments. Rising from the independent-rock scene of the early 1990s, she is one of the most acclaimed British musicians of her generation, and the only artist to have won the Mercury Prize twice.

Career

Harvey grew up on a farm near Corscombe, Dorset, and formed the trio PJ Harvey with Rob Ellis and Steve Vaughan in 1991. Their raw, blues-influenced debut album Dry (1992) and its successor Rid of Me (1993, produced by Steve Albini) won wide critical praise. After the trio split, she continued as a solo artist with the more eclectic To Bring You My Love (1995), featuring singles such as "Down by the Water", and collaborated with Nick Cave and John Parish.

Stories from the City, Stories from the Sea (2000), recorded in New York and featuring Thom Yorke, won the 2001 Mercury Prize. Subsequent albums ranged across styles: the aggressive Uh Huh Her (2004), the piano-based White Chalk (2007), and Let England Shake (2011) — a song cycle about war and English identity, written largely on autoharp, which won her a second Mercury Prize in 2011. The Hope Six Demolition Project (2016) grew out of reporting trips to Kosovo, Afghanistan and Washington, D.C. In 2023 she released I Inside the Old Year Dying, built on poems from her verse novel Orlam, written in Dorset dialect.

Other work

Beyond records, Harvey has composed for theatre, film and dance, exhibited sculpture, and published poetry collections including The Hollow of the Hand (2015, with photographer Seamus Murphy) and Orlam (2022). She was appointed a Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE) in 2013 for services to music.
