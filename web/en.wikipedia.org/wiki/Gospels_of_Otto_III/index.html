Gospels of Otto III

The Gospels of Otto III (Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Clm 4453) is an illuminated manuscript produced around the year 1000, probably in the scriptorium of Reichenau Abbey, for the Holy Roman Emperor Otto III.

The manuscript contains the four Gospels and is celebrated for its cycle of miniatures, including a famous portrait of the emperor enthroned, flanked by clergy and soldiers and receiving the homage of the four provinces of the empire — Sclavinia, Germania, Gallia, and Roma. The miniatures of the evangelists and scenes from the life of Christ show the refined style of the Reichenau school, a pinnacle of Ottonian art.

The book later passed to Bamberg Cathedral, where Otto's successor Henry II donated it, and eventually to the Bavarian State Library in Munich during secularisation. It is regarded as one of the supreme achievements of medieval book illumination.
