History of Poland (1945–1989)

The history of Poland from 1945 to 1989 covers the period of the Polish People's Republic, a Soviet-aligned communist state established after the Second World War. Poland's borders shifted westward, and millions of Poles and Germans were resettled.

Consolidation and Stalinism

The communist Polish United Workers' Party consolidated power by 1948, imposing a planned economy, collectivization attempts, and political repression. After Stalin's death, Władysław Gomułka's return in 1956 brought a partial thaw.

Opposition and Solidarity

Worker protests in 1956, 1970, and 1976, the election of Pope John Paul II in 1978, and the Gdańsk strikes of 1980 produced Solidarity, the first independent trade union in the Soviet bloc, led by Lech Wałęsa. The regime imposed martial law in December 1981.

Fall of communism

Economic crisis and popular pressure forced the Round Table Talks of 1989 and semi-free elections in June, which Solidarity won decisively, ending communist rule and inspiring change across Eastern Europe. See also Polish People's Republic.
