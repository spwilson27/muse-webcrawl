Hedgerow

A hedgerow is a line of closely spaced shrubs and sometimes trees, planted and trained to form a barrier or to mark the boundary of an area, such as between neighbouring fields. Hedgerows that retain rural roads or one or both sides, may be considered as incorporated or integrated with the road's design. A hedgerow is the oldest and most common form of field enclosure in many parts of the world.

Hedgerows may also act as a source of wood and may act as habitat for pollinating insects, birds and other animals. In many parts of Europe and the United States, hedgerows are a recognized habitat for wildlife, often harbouring native plants and providing corridors for wildlife movement.

History

The development of hedgerows over the centuries includes evidence of hedges in use at least as far back as the Bronze Age. Hedges were an important consideration in the enclosure of common land during the 18th and 19th centuries in England, when thousands of miles of hedgerow were planted as land was enclosed. The typical English hedgerow consists of hawthorn, blackthorn, holly, hazel and other shrubs, often interwoven by the traditional craft of hedge laying, in which stems are partially cut and bent over to form a dense, stock-proof barrier.

In France, the bocage landscape of Normandy and Brittany is defined by dense hedgerows on earthen banks. During the Second World War, these hedgerows proved formidable obstacles to Allied armoured vehicles in the Battle of Normandy in 1944, leading to the fitting of hedgerow-cutting devices to tanks.

Ecology

Hedgerows provide shelter and food for a wide variety of species. They serve as corridors connecting fragmented woodland habitats, allowing animals such as hedgehogs, dormice and bats to move through agricultural landscapes. Hedgerow trees such as oak, ash and field maple add further habitat value. The loss of hedgerows in the 20th century, through field enlargement and mechanisation, has been linked to declines in farmland bird populations, and many countries now protect hedgerows by law.
