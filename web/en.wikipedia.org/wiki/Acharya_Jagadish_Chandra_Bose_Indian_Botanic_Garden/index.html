The Acharya Jagadish Chandra Bose Indian Botanic Garden, commonly known as the Calcutta Botanical Garden or Indian Botanic Garden, Howrah, is a large botanical garden at Shibpur, Howrah, on the western bank of the Hooghly River opposite Kolkata, India. Founded in 1787, it is among the oldest and largest botanical gardens in South Asia and is administered by the Botanical Survey of India.

The Great Banyan Tree, the garden's most famous specimen.

History

The garden was founded in 1787 by Colonel Robert Kyd of the East India Company, initially to identify new plant species of commercial value and to grow spices for trade. William Roxburgh, regarded as the father of Indian botany, became its superintendent in 1793 and built up its herbarium and scientific work. The garden was renamed in 2009 in honour of the Bengali scientist Jagadish Chandra Bose.

Collections

The garden holds more than 12,000 perennial plants and well over a million dried specimens in the Central National Herbarium. Collections include palms, bamboos, orchids, citrus, and an extensive range of medicinal and economic plants, arranged across 25 divisions connected by broad avenues.

The Great Banyan

The garden's chief attraction is the Great Banyan Tree (Ficus benghalensis), a single banyan whose canopy — supported by thousands of aerial prop roots — covers more than a hectare and a half, making it one of the widest trees in the world. The original trunk, struck by lightning and diseased, was removed in 1925, but the tree survives clonally through its prop roots and is estimated to be over 250 years old.
