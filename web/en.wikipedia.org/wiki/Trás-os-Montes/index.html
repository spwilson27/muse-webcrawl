Trás-os-Montes (literally "behind the mountains") is a historical region in the far north-east of Portugal, lying east of the mountain ranges that separate it from the Minho and the coast, and bordering Spain to the north and east. Together with the northern Douro valley it formed the former province of Trás-os-Montes e Alto Douro. Its principal cities are Vila Real, Bragança, Chaves and Mirandela.

Geography

The region's isolation — summed up in the local saying that there are "nine months of winter and three months of hell" (nove meses de inverno e três de inferno) — is a product of its rugged terrain of schist mountains, deep valleys and high plateaus such as the Terra Fria and Terra Quente. The Douro river forms its southern boundary and much of its border with Spain.

Culture

Trás-os-Montes is known for distinctive customs that survive from a long-isolated rural society: the masked winter-solstice festivals of the caretos, bagpipe music shared with Galicia, the communal village traditions of the Mirandese-speaking plateau (Mirandese is Portugal's second official language), and a hearty cuisine based on smoked meats, bread soups (açorda-like dishes), chestnuts, olive oil and the famous posta mirandesa steak from Mirandela cattle. Remittances from emigration, especially to France, reshaped village life in the 20th century, and depopulation remains a central challenge.

History

Roman roads and gold mines, Suevic and Visigothic kingdoms, and centuries as a frontier zone against León and Castile left the region studded with castles and pillories. The former province was abolished in administrative reforms, and today its territory is divided among the districts of Bragança and Vila Real within the Norte region.
