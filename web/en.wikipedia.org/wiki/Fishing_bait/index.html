Fishing bait is any material, substance, or food item placed on a fish hook or inside a fish trap to attract fish and induce them to bite or be captured. Baits are normally used in conjunction with a fishing rod and line, and the choice of bait depends on the target species, the habitat, and local regulations. The term is also used for the scent materials, collectively called groundbait, that are thrown into the water to draw fish to the fishing spot.

Natural baits

Natural baits have been used since prehistoric times. Common examples include worms (such as earthworms and lugworms), maggots and casters (fly pupae), insects and their larvae, small fish (live or dead), prawns and shrimps, mussels and other shellfish, bread, cheese, sweetcorn, luncheon meat, and dough-based pastes. Live bait fish are widely used for predatory species such as pike and bass, though their use is restricted in some jurisdictions because of the risk of spreading disease and invasive species.

Artificial baits

Artificial baits are manufactured materials designed to imitate natural food items or to attract fish through colour, scent, or flavour. They include soft plastic imitations of worms, grubs and baitfish; boilies, the hard, round, flavoured baits popular in carp fishing; pellets; and scent-enhanced doughs and pastes. Artificial fly patterns used in fly fishing are also a form of bait in the broad sense, though they are usually classed as lures or flies. The related category of fishing lures relies mainly on movement and appearance rather than food value.

Groundbait and prebaiting

Groundbait is a mixture of crushed particles — typically breadcrumb, crushed pellets, seeds, and flavourings — formed into balls and thrown or catapulted into the swim to attract and hold fish without feeding them excessively. Prebaiting, the introduction of bait over days before fishing, is a common tactic in specimen carp and catfish angling.

Regulation and environmental concerns

Many fisheries restrict certain baits; live vertebrate bait, trout pellets, and some nuts have been banned at individual venues. Discarded bait and groundbait can affect water quality, and the movement of live bait between waters is regulated in many regions to protect native ecosystems.
