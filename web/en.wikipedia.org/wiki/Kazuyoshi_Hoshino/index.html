Kazuyoshi Hoshino

Kazuyoshi Hoshino (星野 一義, born 1 July 1947) is a Japanese former racing driver and team owner, regarded as one of Japan's greatest racing drivers of his era. Nicknamed the "fastest man in Japan", he dominated domestic single-seater and sports car racing for more than two decades and founded the successful Hoshino Racing team, better known as Team Impul.

Career

Hoshino began on motorcycles before switching to cars with Nissan, racing touring cars in the late 1960s. In single-seaters he won the Japanese Formula 2000/Formula 2 championship multiple times (including 1975, 1977 and 1978) and later the All-Japan Formula 3000 championship in 1987 and 1990. In sports cars he won the All Japan Sports Prototype Championship repeatedly in the 1980s and early 1990s and was a factory Nissan driver at the 24 Hours of Le Mans; he also raced at Daytona, winning his class at the 1992 24 Hours of Daytona in a Nissan.

Hoshino contested two Formula One World Championship Grands Prix, both the Japanese Grand Prix at Fuji, in 1976 and 1977, driving for the privateer Heros Racing team; he retired in 1976 and finished 11th in 1977. Restricted by the limited international opportunities for Japanese drivers of his generation, his reputation rests primarily on his domestic dominance.

Team ownership

After retiring from driving in the late 1990s, Hoshino concentrated on running Hoshino Racing/Team Impul, which has won championships in Formula Nippon/Super Formula and Super GT with drivers including his son Kazuki Hoshino.
