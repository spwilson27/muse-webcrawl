Gucci

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gucci is an Italian luxury fashion house based in Florence, Italy. Its product lines include handbags, ready-to-wear, footwear, accessories and home decoration; it also licenses its name and branding to Coty for fragrance and cosmetics. Founded in 1921 by Guccio Gucci, it is one of the world's most valuable luxury brands and the flagship label of the French group Kering.

History

Guccio Gucci, who had worked as a luggage porter at the Savoy Hotel in London, opened a leather-goods shop and luggage atelier in Florence in 1921. His sons expanded the firm after the Second World War, opening stores in Milan, New York and London; the interlocking double-G logo, the bamboo-handled handbag and the horsebit loafer became signature designs favoured by figures such as Grace Kelly and Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis.

Estate and licensing disputes among the third generation of the family in the 1980s and early 1990s — culminating in the 1995 murder of Maurizio Gucci, commissioned by his ex-wife Patrizia Reggiani — nearly wrecked the company. Under CEO Domenico De Sole and creative director Tom Ford it revived spectacularly from the mid-1990s. Kering (then PPR) gained full control in 2004. Alessandro Michele, creative director from 2015 to 2022, drove another period of rapid growth with an eclectic, maximalist aesthetic; he was succeeded by Sabato De Sarno in 2023, and Demna was announced as artistic director in 2025.

The brand

Gucci consistently ranks among the most valuable luxury brands in the world, with annual revenue exceeding €10 billion at its early-2020s peak, and operates several hundred directly run stores worldwide.
