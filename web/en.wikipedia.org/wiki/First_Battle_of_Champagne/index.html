First Battle of Champagne

The First Battle of Champagne (French: 1ère Bataille de Champagne) was fought from 20 December 1914 to 17 March 1915 in World War I in the Champagne region of France against the German Empire's 3rd Army. It was the first major Allied offensive of the war on the Western Front after the front had congealed into trenches following the First Battle of the Aisne and the Race to the Sea.

The French Fourth Army under General Fernand de Langle de Cary attacked toward the railway between Châlons and Verdun, in coordination with the attack in Artois. The Germans, well dug in on high chalk ground with interlocking trenches, wire and machine-gun posts, repelled successive French assaults around Perthes, Beauséjour, and Massiges. The French advanced at most a few hundred yards in places and suffered approximately 90,000 casualties; German losses were roughly 46,000.

The battle demonstrated the difficulty of breaking through prepared trench defenses without far greater weights of artillery, lessons the French attempted to apply in the Second Battle of Champagne in autumn 1915.
