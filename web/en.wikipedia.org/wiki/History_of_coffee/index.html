History of coffee

The history of coffee begins in the highlands of Ethiopia, where, according to legend, the goatherd Kaldi noticed his goats' liveliness after eating coffee cherries. Cultivation and trade began on the Arabian Peninsula, where by the 15th century coffee was grown in Yemen and drunk in Sufi monasteries and Meccan coffeehouses.

Spread to Europe

Coffee reached Europe through Venetian trade in the 17th century. Coffeehouses spread across London, Paris, and Vienna, becoming centres of commerce and news — London's coffeehouses gave rise to Lloyd's of London and the stock exchange culture.

Colonial cultivation

The Dutch and French carried coffee plants to colonies in Java, the Caribbean, and South America. Brazil became the world's largest producer in the 19th century. Production relied heavily on enslaved and later low-wage labour, shaping colonial economies.

Modern era

Instant coffee, espresso culture, and the late-20th-century specialty coffee movement transformed consumption. Coffee is now one of the world's most valuable traded commodities. See also Coffee.
