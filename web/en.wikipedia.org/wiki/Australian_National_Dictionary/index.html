The Australian National Dictionary: Australian Words and Their Origins (AND) is a historical dictionary of Australian English, documenting words and meanings that originated in Australia, that have a special significance in Australia, or whose history in Australia differs from elsewhere. It is produced by the Australian National Dictionary Centre at the Australian National University in Canberra and published by Oxford University Press.

Editions

The first edition, edited by W. S. (Bill) Ramson, was published in 1988, Australia's bicentennial year. It contained some 10,000 headwords supported by about 57,000 dated quotations drawn from a citation database of Australian print sources extending back to the late 18th century. Like the Oxford English Dictionary, the AND is a dictionary on historical principles: each sense is documented with quotations showing its development over time.

The second edition, edited by Bruce Moore, was published in 2016 in two volumes. It expanded the work to more than 16,000 headwords and over 123,000 quotations, covering topics from convict slang and Aboriginal loanwords to the vocabularies of Australian rules football, surfing, mining, and the First World War "digger" lexicon. Words documented include fair dinkum, sheila, budgie smugglers, and selfie (first recorded in an Australian online forum in 2002).

Australian National Dictionary Centre

The Centre, established in 1988 as a joint venture between the Australian National University and Oxford University Press Australia, conducts research into Australian English, maintains the citation database from which the dictionary is compiled, and publishes related works such as the Australian Pocket Oxford Dictionary and studies of Australian vocabulary. It also selects, with public participation, the Australian Word of the Year each December.
