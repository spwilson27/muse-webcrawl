The Kriegsmarine (German pronunciation: [ˈkʁiːksmaˌʁiːnə]; lit. "War Navy") was the navy of Nazi Germany from 1935 to 1945. It superseded the Imperial German Navy of the German Empire (1871–1918) and the inter-war Reichsmarine (1919–1935) of the Weimar Republic. The Kriegsmarine was one of three official branches, together with the Heer and the Luftwaffe, of the Wehrmacht, the armed forces of Nazi Germany.

History

In violation of the Treaty of Versailles, the Kriegsmarine grew rapidly during German naval rearmament in the 1930s. The 1935 Anglo-German Naval Agreement authorised a German surface fleet nominally equal to 35 per cent of the Royal Navy's tonnage, and submarine construction resumed the same year. The ambitious Plan Z of 1939 envisioned a balanced battle fleet by the mid-1940s, but the outbreak of the Second World War that September left the navy far short of its goals.

Second World War

The Kriegsmarine's most significant contribution to the war was the Battle of the Atlantic, in which its U-boat fleet came close to severing Britain's transatlantic supply lines. Surface forces conducted commerce raiding with battleships such as Bismarck and Tirpitz, pocket battleships, and auxiliary cruisers, and supported operations including the invasion of Norway in 1940. After the failure of surface operations and the heavy losses of May 1943 ("Black May"), the U-boat campaign declined in effectiveness against Allied convoy defences, anti-submarine aircraft, and signals intelligence.

Command and strength

Grand Admiral Erich Raeder commanded the navy until January 1943, when Karl Dönitz, commander of the U-boat arm, succeeded him. At its peak the Kriegsmarine numbered well over a million personnel. It operated more than 1,100 U-boats during the war, of which roughly three-quarters were sunk. After Germany's surrender in May 1945 the navy was dissolved; surviving ships were divided among the Allies, and many German naval personnel were put to work clearing mines.
