Histone deacetylase

Histone deacetylases (EC 3.5.1.98, HDAC) are a class of enzymes that remove acetyl groups (O=C-CH3) from an ε-N-acetyl lysine amino acid on a histone, allowing the histones to wrap the DNA more tightly. This is important because DNA is wrapped around histones, and DNA expression is regulated by acetylation and de-acetylation. HDACs act in opposition to histone acetyltransferases (HATs).

Function

Acetylation neutralises the positive charge of lysine residues on histone tails, weakening the interaction between histones and negatively charged DNA and generally opening chromatin to transcription. By removing these acetyl groups, HDACs restore the positive charge, condense chromatin, and repress gene expression. HDACs also deacetylate many non-histone proteins, including transcription factors, and are recruited to promoters by co-repressor complexes such as Sin3, NuRD and CoREST.

Classes

In humans, eighteen HDACs are grouped into four classes based on homology to yeast enzymes. Class I (HDAC1, 2, 3 and 8) and class II (HDAC4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10) enzymes are zinc-dependent. Class III enzymes, the sirtuins (SIRT1–7), require NAD+ as a cofactor and are mechanistically distinct. Class IV contains only HDAC11. HDAC6 is notable for deacetylating cytoplasmic substrates such as α-tubulin.

HDAC inhibitors

Pharmacological HDAC inhibitors (HDACi) interfere with these enzymes and can reactivate silenced genes, induce differentiation and trigger apoptosis in tumour cells. Several are approved as anticancer drugs, including vorinostat (SAHA), romidepsin, belinostat and panobinostat, principally for cutaneous and peripheral T-cell lymphomas and multiple myeloma. HDAC inhibitors are also under investigation for neurological, inflammatory and infectious diseases. Naturally occurring inhibitors include trichostatin A, sodium butyrate and valproic acid.

Clinical significance

Deregulation of HDAC activity is implicated in cancer, neurodegenerative disorders and developmental syndromes; for example, HDAC8 mutations cause a form of Cornelia de Lange syndrome, and HDAC4 haploinsufficiency is associated with brachydactyly-mental retardation syndrome.
