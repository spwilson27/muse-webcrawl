Group of pictures

The group of pictures (GOP) is a concept in video compression: a group of successive pictures within a coded video stream. A GOP consists of one or more frames and is headed by an intra-coded (I) frame, followed by predictive (P) and bidirectionally predictive (B) frames. The GOP structure defines the order in which intra- and inter-frames are arranged and determines properties such as random access, error resilience and editing granularity.

Structure

A typical GOP in MPEG video begins with an I-frame, which is coded without reference to other frames and so serves as a point of random access. P-frames predict from earlier I- or P-frames, while B-frames use both past and future reference frames, achieving the highest compression. Common patterns are written, for example, as IBBPBBPBBPBB, giving a GOP length of 12.

GOP length and applications

Shorter GOPs improve seekability and error recovery but reduce compression efficiency; longer GOPs compress better but make editing and channel changing slower. Broadcast and streaming systems typically use GOPs of about half a second to two seconds, while storage-oriented formats may use longer ones.

Closed and open GOPs

In a closed GOP, frames do not reference pictures outside the group; in an open GOP, B-frames at the boundary may reference the I-frame of the following group, slightly improving compression.
