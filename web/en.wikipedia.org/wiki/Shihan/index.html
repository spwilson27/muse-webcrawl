Shihan

Shihan (師範) is a Japanese term that is used in many Japanese martial arts as an honorific title for expert or senior instructors. It can be translated as "master instructor". The term is the mid-level of instructor certification in many arts, commonly placed between renshi and hanshi in the hierarchy of shōgō (titles) used in traditional ryūha.

Usage

The use of the term differs between styles and organisations. In many karate organisations, shihan is customarily used by practitioners who have reached a high dan grade (often 5th dan and above) and who serve as senior teachers. In Aikido, particularly within the Aikikai Foundation, the title Shihan is a formal certification granted to senior instructors, typically of 6th dan and above, recognising them as master teachers authorised to represent the art. In some arts the title is further graded, for example dai shihan ("great master instructor") in the Bujinkan, where it denotes the most senior students of the sōke.

Within a dojo, shihan denotes the chief or senior instructor as distinct from the head of the style (such as a sōke or kanchō) and from other teachers: sensei (teacher), sempai (senior student), and assistant instructors. Because criteria vary widely, the title signals recognised teaching mastery within its own organisation rather than a universal standard of rank.

Etymology

The word combines the characters 師 (shi, "teacher" or "master") and 範 (han, "model" or "example"), so a shihan is literally a "teacher who serves as a model" — someone whose conduct and technique junior practitioners should emulate.
