Music documentary

A music documentary is a documentary film centred on a musician, band, musical event or musical culture. The genre encompasses the concert film, the behind-the-scenes tour film, and retrospective biographical studies. Music documentaries have been a staple of documentary cinema since the 1960s, when lightweight synchronised-sound equipment enabled direct cinema filmmakers to follow performers on the road.

Notable examples

Landmarks include Dont Look Back (1967), D. A. Pennebaker's portrait of Bob Dylan's 1965 English tour; Monterey Pop (1968) and Woodstock (1970), which defined the festival film; Gimme Shelter (1970), the Maysles brothers' account of the Rolling Stones' ill-fated Altamont concert; Martin Scorsese's The Last Waltz (1978); Jonathan Demme's Talking Heads film Stop Making Sense (1984), often cited as the greatest concert film of all time; and Metallica: Some Kind of Monster (2004), a candid study of a band in crisis.

Contemporary era

Streaming platforms have driven a boom in music documentaries and docuseries since the 2010s, from archival retrospectives such as The Beatles: Get Back (2021) to artist-controlled portraits, prompting debate about access, image management and the blurring of documentary and branding.
