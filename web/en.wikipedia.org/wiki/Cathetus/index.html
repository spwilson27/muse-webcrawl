Cathetus

In a right triangle, a cathetus (plural: catheti), commonly known as a leg, is either of the sides that are adjacent to the right angle. It is occasionally called a "side about the right angle". The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse. In the context of the hypotenuse, the catheti are sometimes referred to simply as "the other two sides".

Usage

If the catheti of a right triangle have different lengths, distinction can be made between the minor (shorter) and major (longer) cathetus. The ratio of the lengths of the catheti defines the trigonometric functions tangent and cotangent of the acute angles in the triangle: the ratio of a cathetus to the other defines the tangent of the angle adjacent to the first cathetus and the cotangent of the angle adjacent to the second.

By the Pythagorean theorem, the length of the hypotenuse equals the square root of the sum of the squares of the lengths of the catheti.

Etymology

The name comes from the Latin word cathetus, itself from the Greek word κάθετος (kathetos), meaning "let down, perpendicular".
