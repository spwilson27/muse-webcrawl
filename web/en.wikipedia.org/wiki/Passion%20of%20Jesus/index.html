Passion of Jesus

The Passion of Jesus (from Latin passio, "suffering") is the narrative of the final period of the life of Jesus Christ, encompassing his triumphal entry into Jerusalem, the Last Supper, prayer and agony in the garden of Gethsemane, his arrest, trials before the Sanhedrin and Pontius Pilate, crucifixion and death, and burial. The accounts are found in the four canonical Gospels and are commemorated in the Christian liturgy of Holy Week.

The Gospel accounts

In the synoptic Gospels and in John, the sequence of events includes the Last Supper, at which Jesus institutes the Eucharist; the agony in Gethsemane and the betrayal by Judas Iscariot; interrogation before Jewish and Roman authorities; the scourging and mocking by soldiers; the carrying of the cross to Golgotha; crucifixion between two thieves; the last words of Jesus; and his death and burial in the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea. The narratives differ in detail and emphasis, and the Passion is central to Christian doctrines of atonement and redemption.

Devotion and art

The Passion has generated an enormous body of devotion, art, music, and drama, including the Stations of the Cross, medieval mystery and passion plays such as that of Oberammergau, and musical settings by composers including J. S. Bach (the St Matthew Passion and St John Passion). The term "Passion" also gives its name to passion plays and to artistic depictions of the instruments of the Passion (the arma Christi). The episodes are traditionally read publicly during Holy Week services.
