Pome

In botany, a pome is a type of fruit produced by flowering plants in the subtribe Malinae of the family Rosaceae. A pome is an accessory fruit composed of one or more carpels surrounded by accessory tissue. The central core, containing the seeds, derives from the ovary, while much of the edible flesh derives from the hypanthium — the swollen base of the flower that surrounds the ovary.

Characteristics

A pome is identifiable by the "core" of seeds enclosed in a papery or cartilaginous membrane (the endocarp), surrounded by edible tissue. The remains of the flower's sepals, stamens and style can often be seen at the end opposite the stalk, as in the "eye" at the base of an apple. Because the ovary is inferior (positioned below the flower parts), the fruit develops beneath the floral tube that becomes the flesh.

Examples

Well-known pomes include:

  • Apple
  • Pear
  • Quince
  • Loquat
  • Rowan (mountain ash) berries
  • Hawthorn berries (haws)
  • Medlar
  • Serviceberry

Etymology

The word "pome" entered English in the late 14th century from Old French pome, "apple" or "fruit", ultimately from Latin pomum, "fruit, apple". This root also survives in French pomme (apple), the culinary term pomme de terre (potato, literally "apple of the earth"), and the name of the pomegranate.
