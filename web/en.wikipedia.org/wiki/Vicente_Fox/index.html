Vicente Fox Quesada (born 2 July 1942) is a Mexican businessman and politician who served as the 62nd president of Mexico from 2000 to 2006. His election ended 71 years of uninterrupted rule by the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI), marking Mexico's transition to a genuinely competitive democracy.

Early life and business career

Fox was born in Mexico City and raised on the family ranch in San Francisco del Rincón, Guanajuato. He studied business administration at the Universidad Iberoamericana and worked for Coca-Cola, rising from route supervisor to president of Coca-Cola's Mexican operations, before running the family's agricultural and footwear businesses.

Political career

Fox joined the conservative National Action Party (PAN), was elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1988, and became governor of Guanajuato in 1995. Campaigning as a plain-spoken, cowboy-booted outsider, he won the presidential election of 2 July 2000 with about 42% of the vote, defeating the PRI's Francisco Labastida.

Presidency

Fox's administration maintained macroeconomic stability, expanded the Oportunidades conditional cash-transfer programme and introduced the Seguro Popular health scheme. An ambitious immigration agreement with the United States stalled after the September 11 attacks. Relations with Cuba frayed, and Fox's hopes for major structural reforms were frustrated by an opposition-controlled Congress. He was succeeded by Felipe Calderón, also of the PAN, in 2006. After leaving office he founded the Centro Fox library and study centre in Guanajuato and has remained an outspoken commentator, memorably clashing with U.S. president Donald Trump over the proposed border wall.
