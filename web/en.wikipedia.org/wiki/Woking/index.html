Woking

Woking is a town in Surrey, England, about 23 miles (37 km) south-west of central London. It is the main town of the Borough of Woking and had a population of around 100,000 at the 2021 census. Woking is a significant commuter town on the South West Main Line, with fast trains to London Waterloo taking about 25 minutes.

History

Woking appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as "Wochinges". The original village, now Old Woking, grew around St Peter's Church; the modern town developed after the railway arrived in 1838 at what is now Woking station. In the Victorian era the area became known for the Brookwood Cemetery, once the largest burial ground in the world, served by a dedicated funeral railway from London. The Shah Jahan Mosque, built in 1889, was the first purpose-built mosque in the United Kingdom. H. G. Wells lived in Woking while writing The War of the Worlds (1898), whose Martians land on nearby Horsell Common.

Economy and culture

Woking hosts the headquarters of the McLaren Group, whose Technology Centre lies just outside the town, and offices of other major employers. Cultural venues include the New Victoria Theatre and the Lightbox gallery and museum. The town centre has been extensively redeveloped with residential towers, offices, and the Peacocks shopping centre.
