Filecoin is an open-source, public cryptocurrency and digital payment system intended to be a blockchain-based cooperative digital storage and data retrieval method. It was developed by Protocol Labs, the organisation behind the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS), and launched its mainnet in October 2020.

History

Filecoin was first described in a 2014 white paper by Juan Benet and Protocol Labs as an incentive layer for IPFS. A further white paper followed in 2017. In August–September 2017, Filecoin's initial coin offering raised approximately $257 million, at the time one of the largest token sales ever, including a pre-sale to accredited investors through a SAFT (Simple Agreement for Future Tokens) arranged with CoinList. The mainnet launched on 15 October 2020 after a incentivised testnet "space race".

How it works

The Filecoin network is a decentralised storage marketplace. Clients pay storage providers ("miners") in FIL to store data for set periods, and pay retrieval providers to fetch it. Providers must continually prove they are storing the data correctly using novel consensus mechanisms:

  • Proof of Replication (PoRep) — proving that a provider has created a unique physical copy of the data.
  • Proof of Spacetime (PoSt) — proving, over time, that the data continues to be stored, audited through randomised challenges in each block epoch.

Providers pledge collateral, which can be slashed if they fail proofs, and earn block rewards proportional to the storage power they contribute. Data is content-addressed through IPFS conventions, allowing verification of integrity.

Uses and ecosystem

Filecoin has been used to archive large public datasets, back up NFT assets, and store portions of institutional collections; projects such as the Internet Archive and various Web3 applications have used the network for durable storage. In March 2023 the Filecoin Virtual Machine (FVM) launched, enabling smart contracts and user programmability on the network, including liquid staking and data-computation services.

Criticism

Commentators have questioned the economics of decentralised storage compared with cheap centralised cloud providers, the large share of tokens allocated to insiders and early backers, volatility of the FIL price, and the energy and hardware demands of its proofs.
