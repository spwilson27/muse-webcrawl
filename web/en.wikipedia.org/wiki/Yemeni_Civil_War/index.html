Overview

The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014 when the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), allied with forces loyal to former president Ali Abdullah Saleh, seized the capital Sanaa and drove out President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi's government. In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition (with the UAE and Western support) intervened to restore Hadi; the war fragmented — Saleh broke with the Houthis and was killed by them in 2017, the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council seized Aden from the government in 2019, and fronts multiplied.

Course and humanitarian crisis

The war produced what the UN long called the world's worst humanitarian crisis: famine conditions, the largest cholera outbreak in recorded history (from 2016, over two million suspected cases), economic collapse and millions displaced; UNDP estimated 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021, most from indirect causes such as hunger and disease. An April 2022 UN-brokered truce dramatically reduced fighting and, though formally lapsed that October, its terms largely held as a de facto ceasefire while Saudi–Houthi talks proceeded. From late 2023 the Houthis' Red Sea shipping campaign internationalised the conflict again, drawing US and UK strikes. Yemen's formal government is now the Presidential Leadership Council, seated in Aden.
