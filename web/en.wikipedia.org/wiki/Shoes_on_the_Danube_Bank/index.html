Shoes on the Danube Bank

LocationPest bank of the Danube, Budapest, Hungary
Created16 April 2005
ArtistsGyula Pauer (sculptor), Can Togay (concept)
MaterialIron

Shoes on the Danube Bank (Hungarian: Cipők a Duna-parton) is a Holocaust memorial in Budapest, Hungary, on the eastern (Pest) bank of the Danube near the Hungarian Parliament Building. It consists of sixty pairs of life-sized iron shoes set into the stone embankment at the water's edge, commemorating the Jews murdered there by Arrow Cross militiamen in the winter of 1944–45.

History commemorated

During the Arrow Cross regime of late 1944 and early 1945, groups of Jews from Budapest were marched to the Danube embankment, ordered to remove their shoes — valuable and stealable property — and shot at the edge of the water so that the river carried the bodies away. Victims were often tied together in groups so that a single shot could push several people into the freezing river. Thousands were murdered in this way in the last months of the Second World War in Hungary.

The memorial

Conceived by film director Can Togay and realised by sculptor Gyula Pauer, the memorial was unveiled on 16 April 2005, the 60th anniversary of the end of the war in Hungary. The shoes — men's, women's, and children's, modelled on 1940s footwear — are cast in iron and fixed to the embankment. A plaque in Hungarian, English, and Hebrew reads: "To the memory of the victims shot into the Danube by Arrow Cross militiamen in 1944–45." Visitors leave candles, flowers, and stones among the shoes. The memorial has become one of Budapest's best-known and most-visited Holocaust sites, though it has occasionally been vandalised and the shoes periodically require repair.
