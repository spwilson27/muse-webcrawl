Sacred tradition

Sacred tradition, also called holy tradition or apostolic tradition, is a theological concept in the Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Oriental Orthodox churches, and the Assyrian Church of the East, holding that the faith and practice of the Church are transmitted not only through Sacred Scripture but also through a living, Spirit-guided tradition handed down from the apostles through their successors.

Meaning

In Catholic teaching (codified at the Council of Trent and restated in the Second Vatican Council's Dei Verbum, 1965), Scripture and Tradition together form the single "deposit of faith" (depositum fidei): Tradition is the Word of God as preached by the apostles and handed on in the Church's teaching, life and worship — including the liturgy, the writings of the Church Fathers, and the doctrinal definitions of councils — while the Magisterium (the teaching office of pope and bishops) authentically interprets both. Dei Verbum presents Scripture and Tradition not as two competing sources but as flowing from the same divine wellspring and mutually interpreting each other.

Eastern Orthodox theology similarly regards Holy Tradition as the life of the Holy Spirit in the Church, encompassing Scripture (as its foremost expression), the liturgy and sacraments, the ecumenical councils, the Fathers, canon law, hagiography and iconography.

Distinction from "traditions"

Theologians distinguish Sacred Tradition (the apostolic deposit, unchangeable in substance) from ecclesiastical traditions and customs (disciplinary or devotional practices that can develop or be reformed). Catholic apologists likewise distinguish it from the mere "traditions of men" criticised in the Gospels.

Protestant critique

Most Protestant traditions reject the Catholic/Orthodox position under the doctrine of sola scriptura, holding that Scripture alone is the infallible rule of faith, and that church tradition — while useful as a fallible guide (norma normata) — cannot bind the conscience with doctrines not taught in Scripture. The status of tradition was one of the central controversies of the Reformation. Some Anglican and Methodist theologians adopt a middle position (the Wesleyan Quadrilateral or prima scriptura), in which Scripture is primary but interpreted within tradition, reason and experience.
