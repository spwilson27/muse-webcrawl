Prayer rope

A prayer rope (Greek: komboskini; Russian: chotki) is a loop made of complex woven knots formed from wool or silk, used in Eastern Christian tradition to count the repetitions of prayers, most commonly the Jesus Prayer. Prayer ropes are part of the practice of Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Eastern Catholic monks and are increasingly used by laypeople.

Description

Traditional ropes are made of black wool — symbolising mourning for one's sins and the flock of Christ — tied with intricate knots, each knot formed of seven interlocking crosses. Common lengths are 33 knots (for the years of Christ's earthly life), 50, 100, 150, and 300 knots. The rope usually ends in a knotted cross and a tassel. Devotees hold each knot between thumb and forefinger while saying the prayer: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner."

History

The knotted rope is traditionally attributed to Saint Pachomius in the fourth century, who is said to have devised knotted cords to help illiterate monks keep count of their prayers; legend adds that the complicated knot form emerged after the devil repeatedly untied simpler knots. The rope became integral to hesychast practice on Mount Athos, where the Jesus Prayer was combined with prostrations and regulated breathing.

Related devices

Similar counting devices include the Russian lestovka, a leather ladder-like prayer counter used by Old Believers, and — outside Eastern Christianity — the Roman Catholic rosary and the prayer beads (misbaha) of Islam.
