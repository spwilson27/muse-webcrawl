Unicornfish

Unicornfish are fishes of the genus Naso in the surgeonfish family Acanthuridae. They are named for the horn-like rostral projection extending forward from the forehead of adults of some species.

Description

Unicornfishes have laterally compressed, oval bodies and small mouths with fixed incisor-like teeth, suited to feeding on zooplankton (as juveniles and in some species) or algae. Species range from about 30 cm to over 1 metre in length; the largest is the spotted unicornfish (Naso brevirostris). Like other surgeonfishes they bear sharp, forward-curving spines on the caudal peduncle.

Distribution and habitat

The roughly 20 species occur in tropical and subtropical waters of the Indo-Pacific and the Red Sea, usually on outer reef slopes and drop-offs, often in schools.

Species

Species include the bluespine unicornfish (Naso unicornis), orangespine unicornfish (Naso lituratus), spotted unicornfish (Naso brevirostris) and elegant unicornfish (Naso elegans).
