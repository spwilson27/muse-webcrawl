Personal development

Personal development or self-improvement is a lifelong process of developing one's talents, potential, and quality of life — encompassing activities that build human capital, improve self-awareness and identity, enhance employability, and facilitate the realisation of dreams and aspirations. It may take place through education, work, relationships, coaching, or self-directed practice.

Scope

Common areas of personal development include improving communication and leadership skills, health and fitness, emotional intelligence, financial habits, time management, and spiritual growth. Approaches range from structured degree programmes and professional training to the self-help industry of books, seminars, and coaching, whose roots run from Samuel Smiles's Self-Help (1859) through Dale Carnegie and Stephen Covey to contemporary productivity methods.

In organisations

Beyond the individual level, personal development relates to human resource development in organisations: mentoring, coaching, performance feedback, personal development plans (PDPs), and 360-degree appraisal are used to align individual growth with institutional goals. Abraham Maslow's hierarchy of needs, culminating in self-actualisation, is a foundational framework often invoked in the field.

Criticism

Critics argue that the self-help industry can overpromise results, individualise structural problems, and lack an evidence base; others note that structured reflection, goal-setting, and feedback do show measurable benefits.
