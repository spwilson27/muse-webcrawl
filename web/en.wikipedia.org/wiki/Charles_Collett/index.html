Charles Collett

Charles Benjamin Collett (10 September 1871 – 5 April 1952) was a British locomotive engineer who served as Chief Mechanical Engineer of the Great Western Railway (GWR) from 1922 to 1941. He succeeded George Jackson Churchward and developed the GWR's famous Castle and King class express locomotives.

Career

Collett joined the GWR at Swindon Works and rose through the drawing office under William Dean and Churchward. On Churchward's retirement in 1922 he became Chief Mechanical Engineer, a post he held for nearly two decades.

Locomotive designs

Collett's work largely developed Churchward's standardized designs. His best-known classes include the 4073 Castle class 4-6-0 (introduced 1923), a development of the Star class that became one of Britain's most successful express designs, and the 6000 King class (1927), built for the heaviest West of England expresses and the most powerful 4-6-0s in the country at the time. Other designs included the Hall class, Grange and Manor classes, numerous pannier tank classes such as the 5700 class, and the streamlined diesel railcars.

Legacy

Collett was appointed OBE and was a member of the Institution of Civil Engineers. His locomotives, many built in large numbers, remained in service into the 1960s, and numerous examples of his Castle, Hall, Manor and pannier tank classes are preserved.
