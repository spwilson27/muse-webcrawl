Italian Riviera

The Italian Riviera (Italian: Riviera ligure) is the narrow coastal strip of Liguria in north-western Italy, lying between the Ligurian Sea and the mountain chains of the Maritime Alps and the Apennines. It stretches from the French border near Ventimiglia to Capo Corvo near Tuscany, with the regional capital Genoa roughly at its centre.

The coast west of Genoa is called the Riviera di Ponente ("coast of the setting sun") and the coast to the east the Riviera di Levante ("coast of the rising sun"). The Riviera di Ponente includes the famous resort towns of Sanremo, Bordighera and Alassio; the Riviera di Levante includes Portofino, Santa Margherita Ligure, Rapallo and the five villages of the Cinque Terre.

Climate and history

Sheltered from cold northern winds by the mountains, the Riviera enjoys a mild Mediterranean climate that made it, from the mid-19th century, a favoured winter resort for the British and Russian aristocracy. Grand hotels and villa gardens — often planted with exotic species, as at the Hanbury Gardens near Ventimiglia — date from this era. Sanremo hosts Italy's most famous song festival and an annual Milan–San Remo cycling classic finishes there.

Tourism

The Riviera remains one of Italy's principal tourist regions, known for its seaside resorts, historic port towns, olive oil and basil-based cuisine (pesto alla genovese), and the UNESCO-listed landscapes of Portovenere and the Cinque Terre.
