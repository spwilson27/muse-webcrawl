Operation Tidal Wave

Date: 1 August 1943

Location: Ploiești, Romania

Part of: Oil campaign, Second World War

Belligerents: United States vs. Germany, Romania

Result: Heavy US losses; refineries partially damaged, output quickly restored

Operation Tidal Wave was an air attack by the United States Army Air Forces on nine oil refineries around Ploiești, Romania, on 1 August 1943, during the Second World War. Part of the strategic oil campaign against Nazi Germany, it was a daring low-level raid by 177 B-24 Liberator heavy bombers flying from Benghazi, Libya, against one of the most heavily defended targets in Europe. The Ploiești fields supplied a large share — often estimated at around a third — of Germany's petroleum.

The raid

The bombers flew at treetop height on the final approach to achieve surprise and precision. Navigation errors, the loss of the lead navigator's aircraft over the Ionian Sea, and a wrong turn at Târgoviște disrupted the carefully rehearsed attack and alerted the defences; German and Romanian anti-aircraft guns, fighters, barrage balloons and smoke screens exacted a heavy toll. The bombers attacked their targets at extremely low altitude, some flying through refinery explosions and flames, in what became one of the most costly US air missions of the war.

Results and legacy

Of 177 B-24s, 53 were lost and many more damaged; over 300 airmen were killed and others became prisoners or were interned in Turkey. Refinery damage was substantial at some plants but output losses were temporary: Ploiești had spare capacity, and production recovered within weeks, in some assessments exceeding pre-raid levels. Five Medals of Honor were awarded for the mission — the most for a single air action in the war. Sustainable attrition of Ploiești came only in 1944, when the Fifteenth Air Force attacked repeatedly from Italy and Soviet forces approached, finally shutting the refineries in August 1944.
