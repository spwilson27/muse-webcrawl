Gymnasium (school)

Gymnasium (and variants of the name) is a term in various European languages for a secondary school that emphasises academic learning and prepares students for higher education at a university. The word derives from the ancient Greek gymnasion, a place for physical and intellectual training. In the German educational system, the Gymnasium is the most advanced of the secondary school tracks, culminating in the Abitur qualification.

A German Gymnasium building

Germany

German Gymnasien typically teach pupils from grade 5 (age about 10) through grade 12 or 13. The curriculum traditionally stresses languages (including Latin and Greek in humanities-focused schools), mathematics, natural sciences, history, and the arts. After passing the Abitur examinations, students receive the Allgemeine Hochschulreife, which qualifies them for admission to any university. The modern Gymnasium developed from the humanist schools of the Renaissance and was standardised during the Prussian educational reforms of the early 19th century under Wilhelm von Humboldt.

Other countries

Schools called gymnasia, or local equivalents, exist in many countries influenced by the German or classical tradition, including Austria, Switzerland, the Netherlands (gymnasium, distinguished by compulsory Latin and Greek), Denmark, Sweden (gymnasium/gymnasieskola), Norway, Poland, the Czech Republic (gymnázium), Slovakia, Hungary (gimnázium), Croatia, Serbia, and Greece (gymnasio, there a lower secondary school). In Russia and other post-Soviet states, a gymnasium (gimnaziya) is a prestigious secondary school often specialising in humanities or languages.
