John Ray Grisham Jr. (born February 8, 1955) is an American novelist, lawyer, and former politician, known for his bestselling legal thrillers. His books have sold more than 300 million copies worldwide and have been translated into over forty languages; nine have been adapted into films.

Early life and legal career

Grisham was born in Jonesboro, Arkansas, and grew up in Mississippi. He studied accounting at Mississippi State University and law at the University of Mississippi, practised criminal and civil law in Southaven for a decade, and served in the Mississippi House of Representatives from 1984 to 1990.

Writing career

His first novel, A Time to Kill (1989), inspired by a harrowing court case he observed, sold modestly at first. His second, The Firm (1991), became the bestselling novel of the year and a hit film starring Tom Cruise, launching a run of legal thrillers — The Pelican Brief, The Client, The Rainmaker, The Runaway Jury — that made him one of the world's most popular authors. He has also written the nonfiction The Innocent Man (2006), short stories, the Theodore Boone children's series, and novels outside the legal genre such as A Painted House and Skipping Christmas.

Advocacy

Grisham serves on the board of the Innocence Project and has campaigned on wrongful convictions and against excessive incarceration. He lives in Virginia and Mississippi.
