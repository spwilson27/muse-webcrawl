Stalking

For other uses, see Stalking (disambiguation).

Stalking is unwanted and/or repeated surveillance or contact by an individual (or group) toward another person. Stalking behaviors are interrelated to harassment and intimidation and may include following the victim in person or monitoring them. The term stalking is used with some differing definitions in psychiatry and psychology, as well as in some legal jurisdictions as a term for a criminal offense.

According to a 2002 report by the U.S. National Center for Victims of Crime, "virtually any unwanted contact between two people [that directly or indirectly] communicates a threat or places the victim in fear can be considered stalking", although in practice the legal standard is usually somewhat stricter.

Definition and classification

Stalking generally involves a pattern of harassing or threatening behavior. According to criminologist Paul Mullen and colleagues, stalkers can be classified into types including the rejected stalker, the intimacy-seeking stalker, the incompetent suitor, the resentful stalker, and the predatory stalker. The rejected type arises from the breakdown of a close relationship; intimacy seekers pursue a relationship with a stranger or acquaintance, often fueled by delusional beliefs (erotomania); resentful stalkers pursue vendettas; and predatory stalkers plan a sexual attack.

Prevalence

Statistics from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention indicate that roughly one in three women and one in six men experience stalking during their lifetimes. Most victims are stalked by someone they know, commonly a current or former intimate partner.

Effects on victims

Victims commonly report anxiety, insomnia, depression, and social dysfunction. Many change their daily routines, move residence, or change jobs in response to being stalked.

Legislation

California enacted the first anti-stalking law in the United States in 1990, following several high-profile murders including that of actress Rebecca Schaeffer. All U.S. states, the federal government, and many other countries have since enacted stalking statutes. In the United Kingdom, the Protection from Harassment Act 1997 and the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 address stalking; cyberstalking laws address online conduct.
