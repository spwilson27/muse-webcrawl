High jump

The high jump is a track and field event in which competitors must jump unaided over a horizontal bar placed at measured heights without dislodging it. It has been an Olympic event since the first modern Games in 1896 for men and since 1928 for women.

Technique

Early jumpers used the scissors, western roll, and straddle techniques. The event was revolutionized at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics by Dick Fosbury, whose back-first "Fosbury flop" — running a curved approach, taking off from the outside foot, and arching over the bar head-first on his back — became universal among elite jumpers. The flop exploits a fast curved run-up and allows the jumper's center of mass to pass below the bar while the body passes over it.

Competition format

Competitors have three attempts at each height; the bar rises in set increments, and athletes may pass at a height. The winner is the jumper clearing the greatest height, with ties broken by fewest misses. Landing is onto a foam pit that became standard with the flop era.

Records

The men's world record of 2.45 m was set by Javier Sotomayor of Cuba in 1993. The women's record of 2.10 m was set by Yaroslava Mahuchikh of Ukraine in 2024, breaking Stefka Kostadinova's mark that had stood since 1987.
