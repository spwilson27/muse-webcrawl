Separation axiom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a separation axiom is a condition imposed on a topological space that requires distinct points (or sets) to be separable by neighbourhoods in some sense. The classical axioms are designated T0, T1, T2, and so on, after the German word Trennung ("separation").

The axioms

NameCondition
T0 (Kolmogorov)For any two distinct points, at least one has a neighbourhood not containing the other.
T1 (Fréchet)Each point has a neighbourhood not containing the other; equivalently, singleton sets are closed.
T2 (Hausdorff)Any two distinct points have disjoint neighbourhoods.
T2½ (Urysohn)Any two distinct points have neighbourhoods with disjoint closures.
T3Regular and T0: a point and a closed set not containing it can be separated by neighbourhoods.
T4Normal and T1: any two disjoint closed sets can be separated by neighbourhoods.
T5Completely normal and T1: every subspace is normal.

Relationships

Most of the axioms form a hierarchy: T5 implies T4, which implies T3, which implies T2, which implies T1, which implies T0. Most spaces studied in analysis are Hausdorff, and metric spaces satisfy all of the classical axioms. Non-Hausdorff spaces nevertheless arise naturally, for example the Zariski topology in algebraic geometry is T1 but not T2, and the particular point topology is not even T0 analogues aside, specific examples separate the various levels of the hierarchy.
