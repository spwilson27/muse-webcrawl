Idol (also known as Pop Idol, Super Idol, and American Idol in its various local versions) is a reality television singing competition franchise created by British entertainment executive Simon Fuller and developed by Fremantle. It originated with the British series Pop Idol, first broadcast on ITV in 2001, and became one of the most widely exported television formats in the world, with local versions produced in dozens of countries.

Format

Across versions, the format follows unsigned singing talent through open auditions before a panel of judges, theatre rounds, and weekly live shows in which the public votes for contestants by phone, text, or app. The winner typically receives a recording contract. Judges' critiques — especially, in early seasons, the acerbic persona of Simon Cowell — are a hallmark of the franchise, as are catchphrases such as host Ryan Seacrest's "Dim the lights... this is the result."

American Idol and global spread

The United States version, American Idol (Fox, 2002–2016; revived on ABC in 2018), became a cultural phenomenon, launching the careers of Kelly Clarkson, Carrie Underwood, Jennifer Hudson, Adam Lambert, and others. Versions have aired in countries including India (Indian Idol), Indonesia, Australia, Canada, Germany (Deutschland sucht den Superstar), France (Nouvelle Star), Brazil, the Philippines, South Africa, and Vietnam, alongside regional editions such as Arab Idol and Latin American Idol. The franchise was for years among the world's most-watched television formats and is credited with popularizing interactive voting in reality TV.

Legal history

The creation of the format was the subject of litigation between Simon Fuller and Simon Cowell over the similarity of Cowell's later show The X Factor; the dispute was settled out of court in 2005, with Fuller receiving an executive producer credit and a stake in the X Factor format.
