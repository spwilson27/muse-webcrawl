The Phantom of the Opera (French: Le Fantôme de l'Opéra) is a novel by the French author Gaston Leroux, first serialised in Le Gaulois from September 1909 to January 1910 and published in book form in 1910. It is partly inspired by real events at the Paris Opéra in the nineteenth century and by an earlier novel, Trilby (1894).

Plot

The Palais Garnier opera house is haunted by a mysterious "Opera Ghost" who extorts money from the managers and demands that Box Five be kept empty for him. The ghost is Erik, a horribly disfigured musical genius who lives in the cellars beneath the theatre and has fallen in love with the young singer Christine Daaé, whom he secretly tutors. When Christine falls in love with her childhood friend Raoul, Vicomte de Chagny, Erik abducts her during a performance and drops the great chandelier on the audience. In the climax, Christine's compassion — she kisses Erik, the first kiss of his life — moves him to release the lovers; he dies of a broken heart weeks later.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times, most famously the 1925 silent film starring Lon Chaney, and Andrew Lloyd Webber's 1986 stage musical, which became the longest-running show in Broadway history. A film adaptation of the musical was released in 2004.
