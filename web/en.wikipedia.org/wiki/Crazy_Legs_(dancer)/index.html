Crazy Legs (dancer)

Born: Richard Colón
January 1, 1966, The Bronx, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: B-boy, dancer, choreographer

Richard "Crazy Legs" Colón (born January 1, 1966) is an American b-boy who was a pivotal figure in the early development of breakdancing. He is best known as the longtime president of the Rock Steady Crew, one of the most influential breaking crews in hip hop history.

Early life and career

Born in the Bronx to Puerto Rican parents, Colón began dancing at around age nine, inspired by his older brother. He took his nickname from a description of his energetic, leg-driven style. He joined the Rock Steady Crew in its early years and, as president, helped expand the crew beyond New York, establishing chapters in other cities and countries and bringing breaking to a global audience.

Media and influence

Crazy Legs appeared in early hip hop films including Wild Style (1982) and Style Wars (1983), as well as the mainstream film Flashdance (1983), in which the Rock Steady Crew performed a famous street scene. He contributed choreography and consulting to numerous stage productions, documentaries and television programmes, and has been credited as an ambassador for hip hop culture worldwide, receiving honours for his role in preserving and teaching the dance.

He is credited with developing or popularising several signature moves, including variations of the backspin and the "W" freeze.
