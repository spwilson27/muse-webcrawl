Dolerite sea cliffs of the Tasman Peninsula

Tasman National Park is a national park in eastern Tasmania, Australia, about 56 kilometres east of Hobart. It occupies much of the rugged coastline of the Tasman and Forestier peninsulas and includes offshore islands such as Tasman Island. The park is celebrated for some of the tallest sea cliffs in the Southern Hemisphere, rising some 300 metres above the Tasman Sea.

Features

Characteristic landforms of eroded dolerite include the Tessellated Pavement, Tasman Arch, the Blowhole, Devils Kitchen, Remarkable Cave, and the sea columns of the Totem Pole and the Candlestick at Cape Hauy, popular with rock climbers. The Three Capes Track, a multi-day walk opened in 2015, traverses the park between Port Arthur and Fortescue Bay via Cape Pillar and Cape Hauy.

Flora and fauna

The park protects eucalypt forest, coastal heathland and patches of cool temperate rainforest. It provides habitat for species including the endangered swift parrot and the wedge-tailed eagle. Australian fur seals haul out on offshore rocks, and the waters below the cliffs support dolphins, migrating whales and rich kelp forests. The park forms part of the South-east Tasmania Important Bird Area.

History

The area adjoins the Port Arthur Historic Site, the convict-era penal settlement, and includes the former semaphore and signalling stations of the peninsula. The park was proclaimed in 1999, unifying earlier reserves around Eaglehawk Neck, Tasman Arch, Fortescue Bay and Tasman Island.
