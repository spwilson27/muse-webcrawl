Waterland (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈʋaːtərlɑnt]) is a municipality in the province of North Holland in the Netherlands, located just north of Amsterdam. It takes its name from the low-lying, water-rich polder landscape of the Waterland region, an area of peat meadows, canals and lakes much of it below sea level.

Geography and settlements

The municipality was formed on 1 January 1991 from the merger of several older municipalities. Its principal population centre is the historic town of Monnickendam, whose harbour once served trade in cheese, herring and shipbuilding. Other settlements include Broek in Waterland — often described as one of the Netherlands' most picturesque villages — Ilpendam, Katwoude, Landsmeer-adjacent villages, Marken (the former island, connected to the mainland by a causeway since 1957), Purmer (in part), Uitdam, Watergang and Zuiderwoude.

Character

Waterland is known for its green peat-meadow landscape, protected in large part as national landscape, and for the traditional green-painted wooden houses with white trim characteristic of the region. Popular with cyclists and day-trippers from Amsterdam, the municipality maintains a largely rural, village character despite its position within the Amsterdam metropolitan area. The Volendam and Edam area lies immediately to the north-east; to the south across the IJ lies Amsterdam itself.
