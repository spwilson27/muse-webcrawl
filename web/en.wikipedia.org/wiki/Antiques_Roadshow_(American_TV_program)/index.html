Antiques Roadshow is an American television program broadcast on PBS since January 9, 1997. Based on the long-running British series of the same name produced by the BBC, the show is produced by WGBH Boston. In each episode, the show tours American cities, where local residents bring antiques, collectibles, and family heirlooms to be appraised free of charge by experts in fields such as furniture, paintings, jewelry, folk art, musical instruments, and militaria.

Appraisers evaluate items brought by the public at touring events across the United States

Format

At each tour stop, thousands of ticket holders bring items for evaluation. Appraisers first assess items at triage tables; the most interesting pieces—whether surprisingly valuable or historically significant—are filmed for broadcast. During the on-camera appraisal, the expert discusses the item's origin, history, and craftsmanship before revealing an estimated value, frequently producing a memorable reaction from the owner. A recurring "Feedback Booth" segment at the end of episodes features brief comments and additional items from attendees.

Hosts

The series was originally hosted by antiques dealer Chris Jussel (1997–2000), followed by Dan Elias, Lara Spencer, and Mark L. Walberg, who hosted from 2006 through the 2019 season. Coral Peña became host in 2020, when the program shifted its format to filming at historic venues and estates rather than convention centers.

Notable appraisals

Among the most valuable items featured on the American series are a collection of five Qing dynasty Chinese cups carved from rhinoceros horn, appraised at $1 million to $1.5 million in Tulsa in 2011, and a 1904 Diego Rivera oil painting appraised at approximately $800,000 to $1 million. A Boston episode featured a Rolex "Paul Newman" Daytona watch purchased in the 1960s for about $345, appraised in 2020 at roughly $500,000 to $700,000.
