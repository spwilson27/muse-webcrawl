Cromford

Cromford is a village in Derbyshire, England, in the valley of the River Derwent. It is famous as the site of Cromford Mill, built by Sir Richard Arkwright in 1771 — the world's first successful water-powered cotton spinning mill.

History

Arkwright's mills and the workers' housing he built, including North Street, transformed the small lead-mining village into a pioneering factory community. The village forms part of the Derwent Valley Mills UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised for its central role in the Industrial Revolution.

Features

Landmarks include Masson Mills (1783), St Mary's Church, the Cromford Canal wharf, and the Greyhound Hotel built by Arkwright in 1778. The village is a popular base for visiting the southern Peak District.
