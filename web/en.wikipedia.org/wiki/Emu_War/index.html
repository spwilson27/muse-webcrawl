Emu War

The Emu War, also known as the Great Emu War, was a nuisance wildlife management military operation undertaken in Australia over the later part of 1932 to address public concern over the number of emus said to be running amok in the Campion district around Chandler, Western Australia. The unsuccessful attempts to curb the population of emus, a large flightless bird indigenous to Australia, employed Royal Australian Artillery soldiers armed with Lewis guns — leading the press to adopt the name "Emu War" when referring to the incident.

Background

After the First World War, ex-soldiers were settled as farmers on marginal land around Campion under a soldier-settlement scheme. Their difficulties were compounded by the Great Depression and by the arrival, during their annual post-breeding migration, of some 20,000 emus, which ate and trampled the wheat crop and broke down fences, letting rabbits in. Frustrated farmers — many of them ex-soldiers — demanded machine guns, and a deputation persuaded the government to deploy military personnel.

The operation

In November 1932 Major G. P. W. Meredith of the Seventh Heavy Battery, Royal Australian Artillery, took command of a party armed with two Lewis guns. Operations proved farcical: the emus split into small groups and scattered at speeds of up to 50 km/h, outrunning the soldiers; rain delayed the start; a truck-mounted gun crashed into a fence; and the birds absorbed astonishing amounts of fire. Meredith's men claimed several hundred kills, but ammunition expenditure per bird was enormous, and press coverage was mocking. The operation was withdrawn in December after Meredith conceded that the emus had shown "the manoeuvrability of tanks".

Aftermath

Farmers requested military assistance again in 1934, 1943 and 1948, and were refused. More effective proved the exclusion barrier fence and, later, government bounty schemes. The episode became a staple of Australian humour and folklore, retold in books, songs and films, and is often cited as a cautionary tale about pest-control policy.
