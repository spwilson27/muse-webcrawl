Responsive architecture

FieldArchitecture, interaction design
Key elementsSensors, actuators, control systems
ExamplesInstitut du Monde Arabe, Paris

Light-sensitive apertures, Institut du Monde Arabe

Responsive architecture is a field of architectural research and practice in which structures and spaces measure environmental conditions or occupants' behaviour and adapt their form, colour, or function in response, using sensors, control systems, and actuators.

History

The term was developed in the work of designers and theorists from the 1960s onward, including Nicholas Negroponte, who imagined architecture as a form of machine intelligence. Jean Nouvel's Institut du Monde Arabe in Paris (1987) is a landmark built example: its south façade has photosensitive apertures that open and close like camera irises to regulate daylight.

Applications

Responsive systems are used for climate control (kinetic façades and shading), energy efficiency, interactive public art, and spaces that reconfigure for changing uses. The field overlaps with kinetic architecture, smart buildings, and ubiquitous computing.
