Avestan is an Eastern Iranian language known only from its use as the language of Zoroastrian scripture, the Avesta, from which it takes its name. It was once the language of large parts of present-day Iran, Afghanistan and Central Asia. Along with Old Persian, it is one of two Iranian languages attested from the ancient period.

An Avestan manuscript of the Yasna.

Stages

Avestan texts fall into two strata: Old Avestan, the language of the Gathas (hymns attributed to Zoroaster) and the Yasna Haptanghaiti, closely related to the Sanskrit of the Rigveda; and Younger Avestan, the language of the bulk of the Avesta. The texts were transmitted orally for centuries before being committed to writing — in the Sasanian period a purpose-built alphabet, notable for its phonetic precision, was used.

Survival

No native speakers survived into the historical record; Avestan persisted as a liturgical language of Zoroastrian priests. Its study was foundational for comparative Indo-European linguistics after European scholars gained access to manuscripts in the eighteenth century.
