Anonymity
DefinitionState of a person's identity being unknown or untraceable
Related conceptsPseudonymity, privacy, confidentiality
Technical toolsTor, VPNs, mix networks, anonymous remailers

Anonymity describes a state in which a person's identity is unknown or cannot be linked to their actions, statements or data. The word derives from the Greek anonymia, "without a name". Anonymity is distinct from pseudonymity, in which a consistent false name may still accumulate a reputation and history, and from confidentiality, where identity is known but information is kept private.

Social and legal aspects

Anonymous speech has a long history: pamphleteers such as the authors of the Federalist Papers (writing as "Publius") used anonymity to argue without personal risk, and courts — notably the US Supreme Court in McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission (1995) — have protected anonymous political speech. Anonymous ballots underpin secret voting, while anonymous sources, juries, donations and twelve-step fellowships (such as Alcoholics Anonymous) serve protective functions. Conversely, anonymity can shield fraud, harassment and crime, and debates over online real-name policies turn on this tension.

Anonymity on the Internet

Online, anonymity is undermined by IP addresses, browser fingerprinting, metadata and behavioural patterns; researchers have shown that supposedly anonymised datasets can often be re-identified by cross-referencing. Anonymity networks such as Tor route traffic through layered relays to resist tracing, and mix networks and anonymous credentials aim to provide stronger guarantees. Formal models include the "anonymity set" (the crowd within which a user hides) and k-anonymity for datasets.
