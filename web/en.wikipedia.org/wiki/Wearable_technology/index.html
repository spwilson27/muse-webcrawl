Wearable technology
ExamplesSmartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, hearables, smart clothing
Key componentsSensors, microprocessors, wireless connectivity, batteries

Wearable technology, or wearables, is a category of electronic devices that can be worn as accessories, embedded in clothing, or attached to the body, incorporating microprocessors, sensors, and wireless connectivity to collect and exchange data. Common examples include smartwatches, activity trackers, smart glasses, wireless earbuds with sensing features ("hearables"), and medical devices such as continuous glucose monitors.

History

Early antecedents include the calculator watch of the 1970s and 1980s and wearable computers developed for research in the 1990s. The category expanded rapidly in the 2010s with fitness bands such as Fitbit, the launch of the Apple Watch in 2015, and experiments with head-mounted displays such as Google Glass (2013), which, though withdrawn as a consumer product, influenced later augmented-reality devices.

Applications

Wearables are used for health and fitness monitoring — tracking steps, heart rate, sleep, blood oxygen, and electrocardiograms — for notifications and payments, for navigation and workplace assistance, and in clinical medicine for remote patient monitoring. Smart textiles integrate conductive fibres and sensors directly into garments.

Issues

Challenges include limited battery life, measurement accuracy compared with medical-grade instruments, and privacy concerns arising from the continuous collection of sensitive biometric and location data.
