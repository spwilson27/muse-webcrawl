AMES Type 7

AMES Type 7
Role: Ground-controlled interception (GCI) radar
Country: United Kingdom
In service: 1942–
Frequency: about 200 MHz (1.5 m band)

The AMES Type 7, also known as the Final GCI, was a ground-controlled interception (GCI) radar developed by the Royal Air Force during the Second World War. "AMES" stood for Air Ministry Experimental Station, the cover designation for British radar projects.

Role and design

Earlier Chain Home radars gave early warning of approaching aircraft but lacked the precision to direct night fighters onto targets. The Type 7 provided a plan-position display covering the area around its station, allowing controllers to see both bombers and intercepting fighters on a single screen and guide the fighter by voice radio until its own airborne radar could take over.

Deployment

Type 7 stations began entering service from 1942 and formed the backbone of Britain's night air defence for the remainder of the war. After the war the system remained in use while more modern radars were developed, and the GCI concept shaped post-war air defence networks.
