Valle de Guadalupe


Location: Ensenada Municipality, Baja California, Mexico

The Valle de Guadalupe (Guadalupe Valley) is an agricultural region in the Ensenada Municipality of Baja California, Mexico, northeast of the city of Ensenada. It is Mexico's premier wine-producing region, accounting for the great majority of the country's wine production.

History

Missionaries planted vines in the valley in the 19th century, and in 1904 a community of Russian Molokan settlers founded the village of Guadalupe and expanded viticulture. Commercial winemaking grew from the late 20th century, led by wineries such as Monte Xanic (founded 1987), L.A. Cetto and Santo Tomás, and has since attracted many boutique producers.

Wine and tourism

The valley's Mediterranean-like climate, granite soils and ocean breezes suit grapes such as Nebbiolo, Tempranillo, Cabernet Sauvignon and Chenin Blanc. Wine tourism, restaurants and the annual Fiestas de la Vendimia harvest festival draw large numbers of visitors, along with ongoing debate about water scarcity and development pressure in the valley.
