Cushing is a city in Payne County, Oklahoma, United States. Population was about 7,800 at the 2020 census.

Cushing is a city in Payne County, Oklahoma, United States. It is best known as a major hub of the North American oil industry: since 1982 it has been the delivery and pricing point for the West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures contract traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange, earning it the nickname "Pipeline Crossroads of the World."

History

The area was part of the Sac and Fox Reservation. The town was founded in 1891 and named for Marshall Cushing, private secretary to Postmaster General John Wanamaker. Oil was discovered nearby in 1912 in the Cushing–Drumright oil field, sparking a boom that made the town a refining and pipeline center. The deep-water port at Cushing grew into one of the largest crude oil storage facilities in the world, with tens of millions of barrels of tank capacity.

Geography and economy

Cushing lies in central Oklahoma, about 50 miles northeast of Oklahoma City. Its economy is dominated by oil storage, pipelines and refining; major operators include Enbridge, Plains All American and Magellan Midstream. Because oil held at Cushing is reported weekly by the U.S. Energy Information Administration, the Cushing inventory figure is closely watched by energy markets worldwide.
