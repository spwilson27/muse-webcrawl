The Deutsche Bundesbank (pronounced [ˈdɔʏtʃə ˈbʊndəsˌbaŋk]), literally "German Federal Bank", is the central bank of the Federal Republic of Germany and, as such, part of the European System of Central Banks (ESCB). Because of its strength and former size, the Bundesbank is the most influential member of the ESCB. Both the Bundesbank and the European Central Bank are located in Frankfurt am Main.

History

The Bundesbank was established in 1957, succeeding the Bank deutscher Länder, and was responsible for issuing the Deutsche Mark. It became famous for its strict anti-inflation stance and its independence from political instruction, a model that strongly influenced the design of the European Central Bank. Until 1999 it conducted German monetary policy; with the introduction of the euro, that competence passed to the ECB Governing Council, on which the Bundesbank president sits.

Tasks

Today the Bundesbank implements ECB monetary policy in Germany, manages Germany's foreign reserves, oversees payment systems, contributes to banking supervision alongside BaFin, and holds the world's second-largest gold reserves, stored partly in Frankfurt, London and New York.
