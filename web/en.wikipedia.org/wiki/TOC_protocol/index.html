TOC protocol

The TOC protocol, or Talk to OSCAR protocol, was a protocol developed by AOL for use by third-party instant messaging clients to communicate with the AIM (AOL Instant Messenger) service. Unlike the proprietary OSCAR protocol used by the official AIM client, TOC was a simpler, plain-text protocol, documented by AOL so that independent developers could build compatible clients.

Operation

A TOC client connected to a TOC server, which acted as a proxy translating between the text-based TOC commands and the binary OSCAR protocol spoken by the AIM network. Commands were sent as space-separated ASCII strings, and the protocol handled sign-on, buddy lists, sending and receiving instant messages, and chat rooms, though with fewer features than full OSCAR clients.

Clients

TOC was used by a number of early open-source and multi-protocol clients, including TiK, TAC and early versions of Gaim (now Pidgin). A later revision, TOC2, added further capabilities. Use of TOC declined as clients moved to OSCAR and as AOL wound down AIM, which was discontinued on December 15, 2017.
