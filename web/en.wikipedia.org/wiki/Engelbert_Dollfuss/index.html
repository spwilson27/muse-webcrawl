Engelbert Dollfuss

Born: 4 October 1892, Texingtal, Austria-Hungary
Died: 25 July 1934 (aged 41), Vienna, Austria
Office: Chancellor of Austria, 1932–1934
Party: Christian Social Party; later Fatherland Front

Engelbert Dollfuss (4 October 1892 – 25 July 1934) was an Austrian politician who served as Chancellor of Austria from 1932 until his assassination in 1934. He established the authoritarian, corporatist regime known as Austrofascism, suspending parliament and ruling by decree while resisting absorption of Austria into Nazi Germany.

Rise to power

The son of a peasant family, Dollfuss studied law, served with distinction in the First World War, and rose through the Christian Social Party as an agricultural organiser and Minister of Agriculture. In May 1932 he became Chancellor at the head of a coalition with a one-vote majority. In March 1933, exploiting a procedural dispute, he prevented parliament from reconvening — the "self-elimination of parliament" — and governed under a wartime emergency law.

The Austrofascist state

In February 1934 government forces crushed the Social Democratic paramilitaries in a brief civil war, after which Dollfuss banned opposition parties and promulgated the corporatist May Constitution, organising society into his Fatherland Front. Aligned with Mussolini's Italy for protection against Hitler, he outlawed the Austrian Nazi Party and survived its provocations.

Assassination

On 25 July 1934, Austrian SS men disguised as soldiers stormed the Federal Chancellery in the July Putsch. Dollfuss was shot and, denied medical help, bled to death. The putsch failed; Mussolini mobilised troops at the Brenner Pass, and Hitler disowned the coup. Dollfuss was succeeded by Kurt Schuschnigg, who maintained the regime until the Anschluss of 1938.
