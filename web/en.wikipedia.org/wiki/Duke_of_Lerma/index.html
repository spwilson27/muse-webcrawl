Duke of Lerma

Duke of Lerma is a Spanish noble title created in 1599 for Francisco de Sandoval y Rojas, the powerful valido (favourite) of King Philip III of Spain. The town of Lerma in the province of Burgos was transformed by the first duke into a monumental courtly centre.

Francisco Gómez de Sandoval, 1st Duke

Francisco de Sandoval y Rojas (1553–1625), Marquis of Denia and from 1599 Duke of Lerma, dominated Spanish politics from 1598 to 1618. He persuaded Philip III to move the court to Valladolid from 1601 to 1606, enriching himself through property dealings, and masterminded the expulsion of the Moriscos in 1609. Accusations of corruption grew until his fall in 1618; he protected himself by obtaining a cardinal's hat. He died in 1625.

Lerma

The first duke rebuilt Lerma as a showcase of his power: the ducal palace, designed by Francisco de Mora, faces a great plaza; the collegiate church of San Pedro and several convents completed a programme that made the small town a secondary court. The palace and ensemble survive as a noted example of Herrerian architecture.
