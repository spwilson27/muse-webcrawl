Cat colony

A cat colony is a group of free-roaming cats that live and forage in the same place, drawn together by the availability of food and shelter. Colonies are most often formed by feral cats around a food source such as refuse areas, farms, fishing ports, or deliberate feeding by caretakers, and are usually structured around related females and their offspring.

Social structure

Unlike solitary stereotypes, feral cats in colonies form loose social groups. Related females may cooperatively nurse and guard kittens, while adult males tend to range more widely and interact mainly at mating and feeding times. Colony size depends on resources; a rich food source can support dozens of animals, and the composition is fluid, with cats joining and dispersing.

Management

The most widely endorsed method of managing cat colonies is trap–neuter–return (TNR): cats are trapped, sterilised, vaccinated, ear-tipped for identification, and returned to their territory. Supporters argue that stable, non-breeding colonies decline over time and that sterilised residents guard resources against fertile newcomers; critics — including many wildlife conservationists — argue that free-roaming cats kill large numbers of birds and small mammals and that colonies should be removed near sensitive habitats. Famous long-standing colonies include those at the Largo di Torre Argentina sanctuary in Rome and the cats cared for by the Ernest Hemingway Home in Key West, Florida.

Feral cats and the law

Some municipalities formally recognise and register colonies, licensing volunteer caretakers who provide food, shelter and veterinary care. In Spain, for example, the 2023 animal welfare law made municipalities responsible for managing feral cat colonies through ethical (CES/TNR) methods.
