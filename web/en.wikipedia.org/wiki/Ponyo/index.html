Ponyo (Japanese: 崖の上のポニョ, Gake no Ue no Ponyo, "Ponyo on the Cliff by the Sea") is a 2008 Japanese animated fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki and produced by Studio Ghibli. It is Miyazaki's eighth film for Ghibli and his tenth overall. The story centres on a goldfish-like sea creature named Ponyo who befriends a five-year-old boy, Sōsuke, and longs to become a human girl.

Plot

Ponyo is the daughter of Fujimoto, a former human turned sorcerer who lives beneath the sea, and Granmamare, the goddess of the ocean. Escaping her father's submarine home, she is rescued by Sōsuke, a boy living in a cliff-top house, who names her Ponyo and keeps her in a bucket. Ponyo uses her magic to grow arms and legs and transforms into a human girl, but her use of magic upsets the balance of nature, drawing the moon closer and causing catastrophic storms and flooding. With the help of Sōsuke's mother Lisa and the residents of a seaside nursing home, the children journey to restore balance; Ponyo passes a test of love and is allowed to live as a human, giving up her magic.

Production

Miyazaki decided to make the film with entirely hand-drawn animation, drawing the sea and waves himself and avoiding computer-generated imagery. The production produced some 170,000 individual drawings. The seaside town setting was modelled on Tomonoura, a port town in Hiroshima Prefecture where Miyazaki had stayed. The story draws loosely on Hans Christian Andersen's "The Little Mermaid" and on the Japanese folktale of Urashima Tarō.

Reception

The film premiered at the Venice International Film Festival in 2008, where it was warmly received, and it became a major box-office success in Japan, grossing over ¥15 billion domestically and more than $200 million worldwide. The English-language version, released by Disney in 2009, featured the voices of Noah Cyrus as Ponyo and Frankie Jonas as Sōsuke, with Cate Blanchett, Matt Damon, Tina Fey and Liam Neeson in supporting roles. The theme song became a hit in Japan, sung by child actress Nozomi Ōhashi with the duo Fujioka Fujimaki.
