Gondar

Gondar (Amharic: ጎንደር, Gonder) is a city in northern Ethiopia, in the Amhara Region. It was the capital of the Ethiopian Empire for more than two hundred years, and is renowned for the castles and churches of the Fasil Ghebbi fortress-city, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Gondar was founded by Emperor Fasilides around 1635 and became Ethiopia's permanent capital — the first after centuries of itinerant royal camps. The emperors of the Gondarine period built a series of castles within the imperial compound, blending Ethiopian, Portuguese, Moorish, and Indian influences. The city remained the imperial capital until 1855 and continued as a major religious and commercial centre. It suffered during the Mahdist invasions of the 1880s and was occupied by Italy in the 1930s.

Sights

The Fasil Ghebbi royal enclosure contains Fasilides' Castle, the castles of Iyasu I and Mentewab, a library, and banqueting halls. Nearby are the Debre Berhan Selassie church, famous for its painted ceiling of cherubic faces, and the Fasilides Bath, filled with water for the Timket (Epiphany) celebrations each January.
