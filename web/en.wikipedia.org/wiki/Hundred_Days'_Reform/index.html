Hundred Days' Reform

The Hundred Days' Reform was a failed 103-day national, cultural, political, and educational reform movement that occurred from 11 June to 22 September 1898 in late Qing-dynasty China. It was undertaken by the young Guangxu Emperor and his reform-minded supporters, notably Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao, following China's defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War.

Reforms

The Emperor issued a series of edicts aimed at sweeping modernization: abolishing the traditional eight-legged essay in the civil service examinations, establishing modern schools and a university in Peking, promoting industry, commerce, and modern agriculture, reorganizing the army and navy, and streamlining the bureaucracy.

Failure and coup

The reforms alarmed conservative officials and the Empress Dowager Cixi. On 21 September 1898 Cixi staged a coup, placed the Emperor under house arrest, and rescinded most of the edicts. Six reformers, including Tan Sitong, were executed; Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao escaped into exile.
