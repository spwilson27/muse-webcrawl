Sir Alexander Arnold Constantine Issigonis (18 November 1906 – 2 October 1988) was a British-Greek automotive designer, best known as the creator of the Mini, one of the most influential cars of the 20th century. His packaging innovations — a transverse engine, front-wheel drive and wheels pushed to the corners of the car — reshaped small-car design worldwide.

Early life

Issigonis was born in Smyrna (now İzmir, Turkey) to a Greek father and a German mother. His family was evacuated to Malta and then England during the Greco-Turkish War. He studied engineering at Battersea Polytechnic and, after failing mathematics, joined the motor industry, working at Humber and Morris Motors, where he developed an independent suspension system used on MG cars.

Morris Minor and the Mini

At Morris he designed the Morris Minor (1948), a landmark post-war British car. After a spell at Alvis, he returned to the British Motor Corporation in 1955 and, spurred by the Suez fuel crisis, designed the Mini, launched in 1959 as the Morris Mini-Minor and Austin Seven. Its space-efficient layout made it an icon of the 1960s.

Later designs and honours

Issigonis's later BMC designs included the 1100/1300 range (Britain's best-selling car for a decade), the Landcrab 1800 and the Austin Maxi. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1967 and knighted in 1969. He retired in 1971 but continued consulting, and died in 1988.
