Matthew Boulton

Matthew Boulton (3 September 1728 – 17 August 1809) was an English manufacturer and business partner of the Scottish engineer James Watt. In the last quarter of the 18th century, their firm Boulton & Watt installed hundreds of steam engines in Britain and abroad, an achievement central to the Industrial Revolution. Boulton was a key member of the Lunar Society of Birmingham, a circle of scientists, engineers, and thinkers whose meetings shaped the intellectual life of the Midlands Enlightenment.

Early life and the Soho Manufactory

Boulton was born in Birmingham, the son of a manufacturer of small metal goods — "toys" such as buttons, buckles, and snuff boxes. After his father's death in 1759 he took over the family business and in 1761 opened the Soho Manufactory near Birmingham, one of the largest factories of its day, employing around a thousand people and exporting ornamental goods, silverware, and ormolu across Europe.

Partnership with James Watt

In 1775 Boulton entered partnership with James Watt, whose improved steam engine design used a separate condenser to greatly reduce fuel consumption. Boulton provided capital, manufacturing capacity, and commercial drive; famously declaring of his market, "I sell here, Sir, what all the world desires to have — POWER." The pair obtained an extension of Watt's patent to 1800 and supplied engines to mines, mills, and canals throughout Britain.

Coinage

Frustrated by the poor state of British copper coinage and its widespread counterfeiting, Boulton built the Soho Mint and, from 1797, struck the large "cartwheel" pennies and twopences for the government, followed by coins for the East India Company and many foreign states. His steam-powered mint machinery set new standards for precision coinage.

Legacy

Boulton died at Soho House in 1809. He, Watt, and William Murdoch appear on the Bank of England £50 note issued in 2011, commemorating their partnership.
