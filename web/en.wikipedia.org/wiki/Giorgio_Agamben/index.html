Giorgio Agamben

Giorgio Agamben (born 22 April 1942) is an Italian philosopher best known for his Homo Sacer project, a multi-volume investigation of sovereignty, law and "bare life" that has been widely influential — and controversial — in political theory, legal studies and the humanities.

Life and work

Agamben studied law and philosophy in Rome, where his thesis concerned Simone Weil, and attended Martin Heidegger's seminars at Le Thor. He edited the Italian edition of Walter Benjamin's works and taught at universities in Italy and abroad, including the University of Macerata, the University of Verona, and the Università Iuav di Venezia. Early works such as The Man Without Content (1970), Stanzas (1977) and The Coming Community (1990) treated aesthetics, language and community.

Homo Sacer

Homo Sacer: Sovereign Power and Bare Life (1995) revived an obscure figure of archaic Roman law — the homo sacer, a person who could be killed with impunity but not sacrificed — to argue that Western politics is founded on the inclusion of "bare life" (zoē, mere biological life) into the political order through its exclusion, with the concentration camp as the hidden paradigm of modern power. In State of Exception (2003) he analysed how emergency powers, meant to be temporary, become normalised techniques of government; the multi-volume project continued with works on the oath, monastic rules, civil war, and use. Agamben's writings on the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2021), which attacked emergency restrictions as disproportionate, attracted sharp public criticism in Italy and abroad.
