Australian of the Year

Australian of the Year is a national award presented annually on the eve of Australia Day, 25 January, honouring an Australian citizen judged to have made an outstanding contribution to the nation or to serve as an inspiring role model.

History

The award began in 1960, when it was founded by the Victorian Australia Day Council; the first recipient was Nobel Prize-winning immunologist Sir Frank Macfarlane Burnet. It became a national award administered by the National Australia Day Council in 1979.

Categories

Alongside the main award, categories include Young Australian of the Year (since 1979), Senior Australian of the Year (since 1999) and Australia's Local Hero (since 2003). State and territory finalists are chosen first, with national winners announced in Canberra.

Notable recipients

Recipients have included cricketer Sir Donald Bradman-era figures such as artist Arthur Boyd, eye surgeon Fred Hollows, tennis player Evonne Goolagong Cawley, quantum physicist Michelle Simmons, cave-diving rescuers Craig Challen and Richard Harris (jointly, 2019), and eye health advocate Grace Tame (2021).
