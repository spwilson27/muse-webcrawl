Amusement rides, sometimes called carnival rides, are mechanical devices or structures that move people to create enjoyment and thrills. They are found at amusement parks, traveling carnivals, fairs and festivals around the world. Rides are generally categorised as flat rides (such as carousels and spinning rides), gravity rides (such as roller coasters), and water rides.

History

Simple mechanical rides appeared at European fairs in the Middle Ages, and by the 17th and 18th centuries ice slides in Russia inspired early wooden roller coasters. The 19th century saw steam-powered carousels and, in 1893, the original Ferris wheel at the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. The 20th century brought electrification, steel coasters of increasing height and speed, and increasingly elaborate themed rides at permanent parks.

Types

  • Flat rides — carousels, Ferris wheels, swing rides, pendulum rides, drop towers.
  • Roller coasters — gravity-driven trains on steel or wooden track, including launched and inverted designs.
  • Water rides — log flumes, river rapids rides and shoot-the-chute rides.
  • Dark rides — guided vehicles through indoor themed scenes.
  • Transport rides — park railways, monorails and sky rides that also move guests around a park.

Safety

Ride safety is governed by standards such as those developed by ASTM International's F24 committee and the European EN 13814 standard, and by inspection regimes that vary by jurisdiction. Modern rides use redundant restraint systems, block systems on coasters, and daily inspection checklists. Statistically, fixed-site amusement rides are very safe, with injuries far more often associated with rider behaviour than mechanical failure.
