Cholinesterase

The enzyme cholinesterase (EC 3.1.1.8) is a family of enzymes that catalyze the hydrolysis of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine into choline and acetic acid, a reaction necessary to allow a cholinergic neuron to return to its resting state after activation. There are two types of cholinesterase: acetylcholinesterase (AChE) and butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE, also called pseudocholinesterase or plasma cholinesterase).

Cholinesterase
EC number3.1.1.8 (butyrylcholinesterase); 3.1.1.7 (acetylcholinesterase)
SubstrateAcetylcholine and other choline esters
ProductsCholine + acetate
LocationSynapses, neuromuscular junctions, blood plasma, liver

Types

Acetylcholinesterase is found primarily in the synapses of the nervous system and at neuromuscular junctions, where it rapidly terminates signal transmission. It is one of the fastest enzymes known, hydrolysing about 25,000 molecules of acetylcholine per second. Butyrylcholinesterase is produced in the liver and circulates in blood plasma; its precise physiological function is less clear, though it acts as a scavenger of certain toxins and drugs.

Clinical significance

Inhibition of cholinesterases by organophosphate compounds and nerve agents (such as sarin) causes acetylcholine to accumulate, producing a cholinergic crisis. Conversely, reversible cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil are used therapeutically in Alzheimer's disease, and physostigmine and neostigmine are used in anaesthesia and myasthenia gravis. Inherited deficiency of butyrylcholinesterase can cause prolonged paralysis after administration of the muscle relaxant succinylcholine.
