Lý Thái Tổ

Lý Thái Tổ (974 – 31 March 1028), personal name Lý Công Uẩn, was a Vietnamese emperor and the founder of the Lý dynasty, reigning from 1009 to 1028. Raised in a Buddhist temple after a childhood marked by legend—he was said to have been adopted by the abbot Vạn Hạnh—he rose to command the palace guard under the Later Lê dynasty.

Accession

On the death of the emperor Lê Long Đĩnh in 1009, whose reign had become notoriously cruel, the court official Đào Cam Mộc and the monk Vạn Hạnh arranged Lý Công Uẩn's accession. He proclaimed the era name Thuận Thiên and founded the Lý dynasty, which would rule Vietnam for over two centuries.

Move to Thăng Long

In 1010 Lý Thái Tổ issued the Edict on the Transfer of the Capital (Chiếu dời đô), arguing that Hoa Lư was cramped and ill-fated, and moved the capital to Đại La, which he renamed Thăng Long ("Ascending Dragon")—modern Hanoi—after, according to tradition, seeing a golden dragon rise from the site. Thăng Long remained the capital of Vietnam for centuries.

Reign and legacy

His reign consolidated the state of Đại Việt: he reorganised the administration, reduced taxes, granted amnesties, patronised Buddhism, and conducted campaigns to secure the frontiers, including expeditions against Champa and rebellious regions. He was succeeded by his son Lý Thái Tông. Lý Thái Tổ is commemorated throughout Vietnam, notably by an equestrian-style statue near Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi.
