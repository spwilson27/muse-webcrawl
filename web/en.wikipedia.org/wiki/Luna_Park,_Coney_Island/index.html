Luna Park, Coney Island

Luna Park was an amusement park at Coney Island, Brooklyn, New York City, that operated from 1903 to 1944. Together with Steeplechase Park and Dreamland, it was one of the three great original Coney Island parks. A new park bearing the Luna Park name opened on the former Astroland site in 2010.

Original park (1903–1944)

Luna Park was created by Frederic Thompson and Elmer "Skip" Dundy on the site of the former Sea Lion Park. Thompson had designed the hit "A Trip to the Moon" ride at the 1901 Pan-American Exposition, and the park took its name from that attraction and, by some accounts, from Dundy's sister Luna. The park was a fantasy landscape of towers, minarets and domes in plaster, dramatically lit at night by hundreds of thousands of electric light bulbs fed by its own power plant.

Attractions included the Shoot-the-Chutes ride into a central lagoon, the "Twenty Thousand Leaks Under the Sea" ride, exotic animal shows, infant incubators displaying premature babies, and re-creations of foreign scenes and disasters. Luna Park's success was enormous — it drew millions of visitors and its name was copied by amusement parks around the world.

Decline and revival

The Great Depression and changing tastes hurt the park, and fires in 1944 destroyed much of it; it closed that year and the site was later redeveloped for housing. In 2010, a new Luna Park opened on Surf Avenue with modern rides, operated by the Italian ride manufacturer Zamperla, reviving the historic name and entrance arch design.
