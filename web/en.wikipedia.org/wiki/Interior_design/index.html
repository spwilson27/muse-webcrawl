Interior design

A residential interior combining furniture, lighting, and colour planning.

Interior design is the art and science of enhancing the interior of a building to achieve a healthier and more aesthetically pleasing environment for the people using the space. An interior designer plans, researches, coordinates, and manages such enhancement projects, which may involve spatial planning, furniture and materials selection, lighting, colour, and compliance with building codes and accessibility standards.

History

Decorated interiors date to ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome. The profession distinct from architecture emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; Elsie de Wolfe is often credited as the first professional interior decorator, and firms such as Syrie Maugham's popularised designed interiors. Post-war professional associations established education and licensing standards in many countries.

Practice

Designers work on residential, commercial, hospitality, healthcare, and institutional projects. The process typically includes programming (defining needs), concept and schematic design, design development with drawings and specifications, and construction administration. Computer-aided design and building information modelling are standard tools.

Styles and elements

Historical and contemporary styles include Victorian, Art Deco, Mid-century modern, Scandinavian, Industrial, and Minimalist design. Core elements are space, line, form, light, colour, texture, and pattern, organised by principles such as balance, rhythm, emphasis, and harmony.

Interior design vs decorating

Interior design involves structural and technical planning and often formal qualifications, whereas interior decorating focuses on surface aesthetics such as furnishings and colour schemes.
