Music video

A music video is a short film that integrates a song or album track with imagery, produced for promotional or artistic purposes. Modern music videos are primarily marketing devices intended to promote the sale and streaming of recordings, though many are recognised as works of visual art in their own right.

History

Precursors include illustrated songs, 1920s–40s "soundies" and Scopitone films, and 1960s promotional clips by artists such as the Beatles ("Strawberry Fields Forever", "Penny Lane") and Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" (1975). The launch of MTV in the United States on 1 August 1981 — opening with The Buggles' "Video Killed the Radio Star" — made the video an essential part of pop stardom. Directors such as Russell Mulcahy, David Fincher, Spike Jonze, Michel Gondry and Hype Williams developed a distinctive visual language; Michael Jackson's "Thriller" (1982, directed by John Landis) expanded the form into a long-form event, and Madonna and Jackson made the video central to their artistry.

MTV's shift toward reality programming in the 2000s pushed videos online; YouTube (founded 2005) became the dominant platform, with Psy's "Gangnam Style" (2012) the first video to reach one billion views, and Luis Fonsi's "Despacito" among the most-viewed of all time. Short-form platforms such as TikTok have since reshaped video style and marketing.

Types

Videos are commonly classed as performance-based (the artist performs), narrative (a story illustrates the lyrics) or concept-based (experimental or themed imagery), often combining all three. Lyric videos and visual albums (such as Beyoncé's Lemonade, 2016) are more recent forms.
