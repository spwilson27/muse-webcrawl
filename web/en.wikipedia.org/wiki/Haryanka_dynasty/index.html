Haryanka dynasty

The Haryanka dynasty was the second ruling dynasty of the Magadha empire in ancient India, succeeding the legendary Barhadratha dynasty. It is generally dated by historians to have ruled from about the mid-6th century BCE to about 413 BCE, when it was replaced by the Shishunaga dynasty. The dynasty is best remembered for its founder Bimbisara and his son Ajatashatru, under whom Magadha rose to become the dominant power of the Gangetic plain.

History

According to the Buddhist text Mahavamsa, the dynasty was founded by Bimbisara. Bimbisara (c. 544–492 BCE) expanded his kingdom through conquest and through matrimonial alliances with the royal houses of Kosala, Licchavi and Madra. He is said to have built the city of Rajagriha, which served as the capital of Magadha until the reign of Udayin.

Bimbisara was imprisoned and killed by his son Ajatashatru (c. 492–460 BCE), who pursued an aggressive policy of expansion, defeating Kosala and waging a long war against the Licchavi confederacy of Vaishali. Ajatashatru fortified the village of Pataligrama, which later grew into the city of Pataliputra.

Later rulers

Ajatashatru was succeeded by Udayin (c. 460–444 BCE), who founded Pataliputra at the confluence of the Ganges and Son rivers and moved the capital there. The later Haryanka kings — Anuruddha, Munda and Nagadasaka — are described in Buddhist sources as patricides, and the dynasty's unpopularity led to its overthrow by the minister Shishunaga around 413 BCE.
