Romano Prodi
Born: 9 August 1939, Scandiano, Italy
Prime Minister of Italy: 1996–1998, 2006–2008
President of the European Commission: 1999–2004

Romano Prodi (born 9 August 1939) is an Italian politician and economist who served as President of the European Commission from 1999 to 2004 and twice as Prime Minister of Italy, from 1996 to 1998 and from 2006 to 2008. He is considered the founder of the Italian centre-left coalition and one of the most prominent figures of the Second Republic.

Career

A professor of economics at the University of Bologna, Prodi served as Minister of Industry in 1978 and twice headed the state industrial holding IRI. Leading the Olive Tree coalition, he won the 1996 general election; his government achieved Italy's qualification for the euro. As President of the European Commission he oversaw the introduction of the euro, the 2004 enlargement to Central and Eastern Europe, and the start of work on the European Constitution.

Prodi returned to national politics and won the 2006 election at the head of The Union coalition, defeating Silvio Berlusconi. His second government fell in January 2008 after losing a confidence vote in the Senate. He later served as a UN envoy for the Sahel.
