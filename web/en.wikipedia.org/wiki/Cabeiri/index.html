Cabeiri

The Cabeiri (Greek: Κάβειροι, Kabeiroi) were a group of enigmatic chthonic deities worshipped in mystery cults on the islands of Lemnos and Samothrace and at Thebes in Boeotia. Their origins were considered non-Greek by ancient authors — possibly Phrygian or Pelasgian — and their rites were kept secret from the uninitiated.

Nature and mythology

Ancient sources disagree on the number, names, and gender of the Cabeiri. On Lemnos they were associated with the smith-god Hephaestus and were sometimes depicted as his sons or assistants, crablike or dwarfish craftsmen of the forge. At Samothrace, initiates into their mysteries believed the gods protected sailors from shipwreck; the sanctuary there drew worshippers from across the Greek and later Roman world, including, by tradition, Philip II of Macedon and Olympias.

The Mysteries of Samothrace

The Samothracian cult ranked second in prestige only to the Eleusinian Mysteries. Excavations at the Sanctuary of the Great Gods have uncovered the Hieron, the Arsinoeion (one of the largest round buildings in the Greek world), and the original setting of the Winged Victory of Samothrace. Unlike Eleusis, Samothrace admitted initiates of any status, including slaves and women, and initiation could occur at any time of year.
