Baqer Khan

Baqer Khan
Born: c. 1870, Tabriz, Qajar Persia
Died: November 1911, near Qasr-e Shirin
Known for: Hero of the Persian Constitutional Revolution; defender of Tabriz

Baqer Khan (Persian: باقرخان; c. 1870 – November 1911) was a key figure in the Persian Constitutional Revolution. Alongside Sattar Khan, he led the defense of Tabriz against royalist forces during the civil war that followed Mohammad Ali Shah's coup against the constitutional government in 1908. He was honored with the title Sālār-e Mellī ("National General" or "Leader of the Nation"), while Sattar Khan received the title Sardār-e Mellī ("National Commander").

Early life

Baqer Khan was born in Tabriz around 1870, the son of Hajji Reza Banna, a builder. He worked as a mason and stone-cutter and was a figure of standing in his neighborhood of Tabriz, belonging to the class of popular strongmen (lutis) who played a significant role in urban life.

Defense of Tabriz

When Mohammad Ali Shah bombarded the Majlis (parliament) in Tehran in June 1908 and moved to crush the constitutionalists, Tabriz rose in resistance. Baqer Khan commanded the fighters of the Khiaban quarter of the city, while Sattar Khan led the Amirkhiz quarter. Together their forces withstood an eleven-month siege by royalist troops under Rahim Khan Chalabianloo and later government forces, enduring severe deprivation until Russian troops entered the city in April 1909, ostensibly to protect European nationals.

Tehran and death

After the constitutionalists captured Tehran in July 1909 and deposed Mohammad Ali Shah, Baqer Khan and Sattar Khan traveled to the capital in 1910, where they were received as heroes. The new government, however, moved to disarm the revolutionary fighters; a confrontation at Atabak Park in Tehran ended with many of their men killed or disarmed. Baqer Khan left Tehran and was killed in November 1911 near Qasr-e Shirin, reportedly by Kurdish tribesmen while traveling toward the Ottoman frontier.

Legacy

Baqer Khan and Sattar Khan remain celebrated national heroes in Iran. Their house in Tabriz has been preserved as a museum of the Constitutional Revolution.
