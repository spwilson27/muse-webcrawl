Tara (Buddhism)

Tara (Sanskrit: तारा, Tārā; Tibetan: རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་, Jetsün Dölma) is a female Buddha and bodhisattva in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, revered as the "mother of liberation" and a saviouress who embodies compassion in action.

Origins and forms

Tara emerged in Indian Buddhism by about the sixth century CE, possibly drawing on Hindu goddess traditions, and became especially important in Tibet. She is said to have arisen from a tear of the bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara. Her two principal forms are Green Tara, associated with active protection from the eight fears, and White Tara, associated with long life and healing. A system of twenty-one Taras is also widespread.

Practice

Tara's mantra, oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā, is among the most recited in Tibetan Buddhism. Devotees invoke her for swift aid, protection, and longevity, and her image — youthful, seated with one foot extended, ready to rise and help — is found throughout the Himalayan world.
