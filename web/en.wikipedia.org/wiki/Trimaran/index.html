Trimaran

A trimaran is a multihull boat consisting of a main hull and two smaller outrigger hulls, called amas, connected to the main hull by lateral beams (akas). The design descends from the outrigger canoes and proas of the Pacific and Southeast Asia, and offers high stability and speed for its weight.

Sailing trimarans

Because stability comes from beam rather than ballast, trimarans can carry large sail plans on light hulls, making them among the fastest sailing craft. Designers such as Arthur Piver popularised plywood trimarans for home builders in the 1960s, and modern offshore racing trimarans — including giant "Ultim" class boats and the MOD70 one-designs — regularly exceed 40 knots and hold transoceanic records. Folding systems allow cruising trimarans, such as the Farrier/Corsair designs, to be trailered.

Powered and naval trimarans

The trimaran form is also used for fast ferries (for example Austal's Benchijigua Express) and naval vessels: the US Navy's Independence-class littoral combat ships and the British demonstrator RV Triton exploit the hull's large deck area, efficiency at speed, and seakeeping. Disadvantages include greater dockage width, structural loads on the crossbeams, and cost.
