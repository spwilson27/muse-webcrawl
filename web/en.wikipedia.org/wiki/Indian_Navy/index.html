Indian Navy

INS Vikrant, India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier, commissioned in 2022.

The Indian Navy is the maritime branch of the Indian Armed Forces. The President of India is its Supreme Commander. A blue-water navy, it operates two aircraft carriers and a fleet of destroyers, frigates, corvettes, submarines — including nuclear-powered ballistic-missile submarines — and naval aircraft, with its history tracing to the East India Company's Marine of 1612 and the Royal Indian Navy.

History

The navy distinguished itself in the 1971 war, when missile boats raided Karachi harbour in Operations Trident and Python, and the carrier INS Vikrant blockaded East Pakistan. Post-war growth emphasised sea control in the Indian Ocean; the Arihant-class SSBNs give India the sea leg of its nuclear triad.

Organisation and operations

Three commands — Western (Mumbai), Eastern (Visakhapatnam), and Southern (Kochi, the training command) — operate the fleet, supported by the MARCOS special forces and naval air stations flying P-8I patrol aircraft and MiG-29K fighters. The navy conducts constant anti-piracy patrols in the Gulf of Aden, humanitarian missions, and exercises such as Malabar with the United States, Japan, and Australia.
