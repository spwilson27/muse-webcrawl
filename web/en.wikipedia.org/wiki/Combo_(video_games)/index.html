Combo (video games)

A combo counter tracking consecutive hits in a fighting game

In video games, a combo (short for combination) is a sequence of actions performed in quick succession, typically while an opponent is unable to respond, which yields a benefit or reward greater than carrying out the actions individually. The concept originated in fighting games and has spread to many other genres.

History

Combos emerged prominently with Capcom's Street Fighter II (1991), where players discovered that certain moves could be cancelled into others so that a blocking or reeling opponent was struck repeatedly without a chance to escape; Capcom embraced the unintended behaviour and formalised combo counters in later titles. The term and mechanics were further popularised by 1990s fighting games and beat 'em ups such as Mortal Kombat and Killer Instinct, the latter known for its announcer calling out combo levels ("C-C-C-Combo Breaker").

Mechanics

In fighting games, combos rely on concepts such as hitstun, juggles, cancels, and links; skilled play revolves around confirming hits into high-damage sequences while managing damage scaling. Outside the genre, the word "combo" is used for chained actions in hack-and-slash games (for example the stylish action systems of Devil May Cry), rhythm games, skateboarding games such as Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, and puzzle games where chained matches multiply the score.
