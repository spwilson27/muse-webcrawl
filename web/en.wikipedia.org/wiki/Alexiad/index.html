The Alexiad (Greek: Ἀλεξιάς, Alexias) is a medieval Greek historical and biographical text written around 1148 by the Byzantine princess Anna Komnene, daughter of Emperor Alexios I Komnenos. In fifteen books it recounts the reign of her father (1081–1118) and is one of the most important primary sources for the Byzantine Empire and the First Crusade.

Contents

The work describes Alexios's seizure of the throne, his wars against the Normans of Robert Guiscard and Bohemond, the Pechenegs and the Seljuk Turks, and the passage of the First Crusade through Constantinople (1096–1097). Anna's account of the crusaders — whom she regarded as dangerous barbarians — provides an invaluable Byzantine counterpoint to Latin chronicles. She also describes court politics, military technology (including an early mention of the crossbow in Byzantine service) and the heresy trials of her father's reign.

Style and assessment

Written in an artificial Atticising Greek modelled on Thucydides, Xenophon and Homer, the Alexiad is openly partisan: it is both history and panegyric of Alexios, and Anna wrote partly to defend her father's memory and her own claim within the imperial family. Despite its bias, its detail and literary quality make it among the finest works of Byzantine historiography and a landmark of medieval literature by a woman.
