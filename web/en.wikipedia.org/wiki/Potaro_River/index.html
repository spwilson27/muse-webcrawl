Potaro River

Kaieteur Falls, on the middle course of the Potaro River.

The Potaro River is a river in Guyana, in northern South America, and a tributary of the Essequibo River. It runs for about 225 kilometres (140 miles) from the Pakaraima Mountains near Mount Ayanganna, close to the border with Brazil, eastward to its confluence with the Essequibo at Bartica. The river gives its name to the Potaro-Siparuni region of Guyana.

Course and features

The Potaro flows through dense rainforest and descends from the Guiana Highlands over a series of rapids and waterfalls. The most famous is Kaieteur Falls, where the river plunges about 226 metres (741 feet) in a single drop — roughly four and a half times the height of Niagara Falls — making it one of the most powerful waterfalls in the world. Other falls on the river include Amatuk Falls and Waratuk Falls. Below the falls the river is navigable in stretches and has long served as a route into the interior.

Human use

The Potaro valley has been prospected for gold and diamonds since the nineteenth century, and mining remains part of the local economy, with settlements such as Mahdia serving the mining districts. The river and its falls lie within or near Kaieteur National Park, established in 1929, one of Guyana's principal protected areas and tourist attractions. A hydroelectric scheme at Amaila Falls on the neighbouring Kuribrong River, a tributary of the Potaro, has been proposed to supply Georgetown with power.
