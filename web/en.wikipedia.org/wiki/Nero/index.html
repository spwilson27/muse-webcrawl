Nero

Nero Claudius Caesar Augustus Germanicus (15 December AD 37 – 9 June AD 68) was the fifth Roman emperor, ruling from AD 54 until his death, the last of the Julio-Claudian dynasty.

Predecessor: Claudius
Successor: Galba
Mother: Agrippina the Younger

Reign

Adopted by Claudius, Nero came to power at 16, guided early by Seneca and Burrus and his mother Agrippina — whom he later had killed (AD 59), as he did his wife Octavia. He pursued public popularity through games, performances (he sang and played the lyre publicly), tax cuts and building.

Fire and persecution

The Great Fire of Rome (AD 64) devastated the city; Nero rebuilt and built his vast Domus Aurea palace. Ancient sources say he blamed and persecuted Christians; the legend that he "fiddled while Rome burned" is anachronistic. Revolts, including Boudica's in Britain (AD 60–61) and the Jewish revolt (AD 66), marked his reign.

Death

Declared a public enemy amid revolts by Galba and others, Nero died by suicide in AD 68, sparking the Year of the Four Emperors. Historians note hostile senatorial sources colour his tyrannical image. See Julio-Claudian dynasty, Great Fire of Rome.
