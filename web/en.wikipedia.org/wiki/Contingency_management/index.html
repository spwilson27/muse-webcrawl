Contingency management

Contingency management (CM) is a behaviour modification intervention based on operant conditioning in which tangible rewards are delivered contingent on objective evidence of a target behaviour, most commonly abstinence from drugs verified by urine testing. It is among the most effective psychosocial treatments for substance use disorders, particularly stimulant use disorder, for which no approved medication exists.

TypeBehavioural therapy
BasisOperant conditioning
Primary useSubstance use disorders
Common formatsVoucher-based reinforcement, prize draws ("fishbowl")

Methods

In voucher-based reinforcement therapy, developed by Stephen Higgins and colleagues, patients earn vouchers exchangeable for goods and services for each drug-negative sample, with values escalating for continuous abstinence and resetting after a positive test. Prize-based CM, developed by Nancy Petry, lets patients draw from a bowl for chances to win prizes of varying value. Rewards are delivered immediately and frequently, and target behaviours are verified objectively, for example by urinalysis or breath carbon monoxide for smoking.

Evidence and use

Meta-analyses consistently find CM produces some of the largest effect sizes of any psychosocial treatment for substance use during the intervention, though benefits may diminish after rewards end. CM is used in the US Department of Veterans Affairs health system and in programmes targeting opioid, cocaine, methamphetamine, tobacco, and cannabis use. Beyond addiction, contingency approaches have been applied to medication adherence and weight loss. Barriers to wider adoption include cost, logistics, and ethical concerns about paying patients, which proponents answer by pointing to outcomes and cost-effectiveness.
