Financial technology

Financial technology (fintech) Key areas: digital payments, online lending, robo-advisers, insurtech, blockchain and cryptocurrencies
Notable centres: United States, China, United Kingdom, India

Financial technology (abbreviated fintech) is the technology and innovation that aims to compete with traditional financial methods in the delivery of financial services. It encompasses software, applications, and business models used in mobile banking, digital payments, peer-to-peer lending, automated investment advice, insurance, and distributed-ledger technologies.

History

Although the term gained widespread use in the 2010s, technology has long underpinned finance, from the transatlantic telegraph cable of 1866 and the first ATMs in the late 1960s to online banking in the 1990s. After the 2008 financial crisis, a wave of startups exploited smartphones, cloud computing, and open APIs to offer services that were cheaper or more convenient than those of incumbent banks.

Sectors

Major fintech sectors include payment processing and mobile wallets; digital-only ("neobanks") and challenger banks; marketplace and peer-to-peer lending; robo-advisers and retail trading apps; insurtech; regtech for compliance; and blockchain-based services such as cryptocurrencies and decentralised finance.

Regulation

Fintech firms are typically subject to financial regulation covering consumer protection, anti-money-laundering, and prudential soundness. Several jurisdictions have introduced regulatory sandboxes allowing firms to test products under supervision, and open-banking rules in the European Union and United Kingdom require banks to share customer account data with authorised third parties.
