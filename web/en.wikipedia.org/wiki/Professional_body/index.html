Professional body

A professional body (also called a professional association, professional organization, or professional society) is an organization, usually non-profit, which seeks to further a particular profession, the interests of individuals engaged in that profession and the public interest.

Professional bodies are commonly responsible for setting standards of education, training, ethics and professional conduct for their members. Many issue professional qualifications, maintain registers of practitioners, and offer membership grades such as Member, Fellow, or Associate. In some fields, membership or licensure by a professional body is legally required to practise.

Roles

Roles of professional bodies may include:

  • Setting and maintaining standards of professional competence and ethical conduct
  • Accrediting courses and qualifications
  • Providing continuing professional development (CPD)
  • Publishing journals and research
  • Representing the profession to government and the public
  • Disciplining members who breach codes of conduct

Examples

Examples include the General Medical Council in medicine, the American Bar Association in law, the Institution of Civil Engineers, and the Royal Society of Chemistry. Some, such as medical regulators, are statutory bodies created by legislation, while others are voluntary associations.
