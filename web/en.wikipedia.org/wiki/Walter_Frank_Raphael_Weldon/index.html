Walter Frank Raphael Weldon

Walter Frank Raphael Weldon FRS (15 March 1860 – 13 April 1906), usually called Raphael Weldon, was an English evolutionary biologist and a founder, with Francis Galton and Karl Pearson, of the science of biometrics — the statistical study of biological variation, which sought to test Darwinian natural selection quantitatively.

Career

Educated at King's College London and St John's College, Cambridge, Weldon began as a marine zoologist, working at the Plymouth Marine Laboratory. Influenced by Galton's Natural Inheritance (1889), he began measuring variation in wild populations of shrimp and crabs, fitting distributions to his data — groundbreaking empirical work showing measurable natural selection in shore crabs (Carcinus maenas) at Plymouth, where he demonstrated that frontal breadth ratios changed in response to harbour siltation.

He became Jodrell Professor of Zoology at University College London (1891) and Linacre Professor at Oxford (1899). With Karl Pearson he founded the journal Biometrika in 1901. Weldon initially opposed Mendelian genetics after its rediscovery in 1900, engaging in a bitter controversy with William Bateson; his early death from pneumonia at 46 cut short the biometry–Mendelism dispute, later healed by the modern evolutionary synthesis.
