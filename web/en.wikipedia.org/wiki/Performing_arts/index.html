Performing arts

The performing arts are arts in which artists use their voices, bodies or inanimate objects to convey artistic expression, in contrast to the visual arts, in which artists use paint, canvas or other materials to create static works. Performing arts include theatre, music, dance, opera, mime, spoken word and circus arts.

Types

  • Theatre — the branch concerned with acting out stories in front of an audience, using speech, gesture, music and spectacle.
  • Music — an art form whose medium is sound and silence; performance may be live or recorded.
  • Dance — movement of the body, usually rhythmic and to music, as a form of expression and entertainment.
  • Opera — a form of theatre in which music is a fundamental component and dramatic roles are taken by singers.
  • Circus — acrobatics, juggling, clowning and aerial acts.

History

Performance traditions are found in every culture, from ritual dance and storytelling to the codified theatres of ancient Greece, India, China and Japan. In the West, medieval religious drama, Renaissance court spectacle and the public playhouses of Shakespeare's London developed into the modern proscenium theatre, while the 20th century added experimental, site-specific and interdisciplinary performance art. Recorded and broadcast media have transformed how performances are distributed and preserved.

Performers

Those who participate in performing arts in front of an audience are called performers, including actors, comedians, dancers, magicians, circus artists, musicians and singers. They are supported by behind-the-scenes workers in fields such as set design, lighting, sound and stage management.
