Super Mario Bros. is a 1985 platform game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES). Designed by Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka, it is the first game in the Super Mario series and among the most influential video games ever made. Players control Mario — and, in two-player mode, his brother Luigi — on a quest through the Mushroom Kingdom to rescue Princess Toadstool (Peach) from King Koopa (Bowser).

Gameplay

The game consists of 32 side-scrolling levels ("courses") arranged in eight worlds of four levels each. Mario runs, jumps and swims through environments of plains, underground caverns, underwater stages and castles, defeating enemies such as Goombas, Koopa Troopas and Hammer Bros. by stomping or with fireballs gained from the Fire Flower power-up. Coins, question-mark blocks, hidden warp pipes that skip ahead between worlds, and the famous "Minus World" glitch became enduring elements of gaming lore. Koji Kondo's score — especially the "Ground Theme" of World 1-1 — is one of the most recognisable pieces of video game music.

Development and reception

Miyamoto's team designed the game as a culmination of the NES hardware's capabilities, following their work on Excitebike and Kung Fu. Released in Japan in September 1985 and in North America later that year (its exact US date is disputed), it sold more than 40 million copies for the NES, making it the console's best-selling game. Super Mario Bros. is credited with helping end the North American video game crash of 1983, popularising the side-scrolling platformer, and establishing conventions — scrolling levels, power-ups, secret areas — that shaped decades of game design. It has been re-released on nearly every Nintendo platform and consistently ranks among the greatest video games of all time.
