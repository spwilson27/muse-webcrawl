Sagami Bay

Japanese: Sagami-wan

Location: Kanagawa Prefecture, Honshu, Japan

Ocean: Pacific Ocean

Maximum depth: About 1,500 m

Sagami Bay (Japanese: Sagami-wan) is a bay on the Pacific coast of Honshu, Japan, in Kanagawa Prefecture, roughly 40 kilometres southwest of Tokyo. It is bordered by the Miura Peninsula to the east, the Izu Peninsula to the west, and the Shonan coastline to the north, with the island of Izu Oshima marking its southern entrance. The bay overlies the Sagami Trough, where the Philippine Sea Plate subducts beneath the Okhotsk Plate.

Geography and environment

The deep trough brings deep-sea fauna unusually close to shore, and the bay is famous for bioluminescent organisms and deep-sea fish specimens studied by Emperor Hirohito, a keen marine biologist who collected at the imperial villa at Hayama. Coastal cities include Odawara, Chigasaki, Fujisawa (with the island of Enoshima), Kamakura, Zushi and Yokosuka at the entrance to Tokyo Bay.

History

The bay's tectonic setting generated the Great Kanto earthquake of 1923, centred beneath the bay, which devastated Tokyo and Yokohama. Commodore Matthew Perry's squadron anchored in and near the bay in 1853–54 during the opening of Japan. Today the Shonan coast is a popular resort and surfing area within day-trip distance of Tokyo.
