Chroma subsampling

Chroma subsampling
FieldDigital video compression
Common schemes4:4:4, 4:2:2, 4:2:0, 4:1:1
Used byJPEG, MPEG, H.264, HEVC, AV1
BasisHuman vision is more sensitive to brightness than to colour

Chroma subsampling is the practice of encoding images by implementing less resolution for chroma (colour) information than for luma (brightness) information, taking advantage of the human visual system's lower acuity for colour detail relative to luminance. It is a key data-reduction step in virtually all consumer video formats, including JPEG, DVD, Blu-ray, broadcast television and most streaming codecs.

Sampling schemes

Schemes are commonly described using the J:a:b notation of a 4-pixel reference block:

  • 4:4:4 — no subsampling; full chroma resolution. Used in high-end production and RGB computer graphics.
  • 4:2:2 — chroma halved horizontally. Used in professional formats such as ProRes 422 and XDCAM.
  • 4:2:0 — chroma halved in both dimensions (one-quarter resolution). Used in JPEG, H.264, HEVC and virtually all distribution video.
  • 4:1:1 — chroma quartered horizontally; used in some NTSC-era formats.

In 4:2:0, a pixel block shares one pair of chroma values, reducing colour data to a quarter while luma stays full-resolution. Because the eye derives sharpness and detail mainly from luminance, the quality loss is usually imperceptible at normal viewing distances, though heavy subsampling can show colour fringing on fine coloured text or graphics — relevant for computer monitor displays.
