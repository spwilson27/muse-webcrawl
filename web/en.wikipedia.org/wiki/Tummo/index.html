Tummo

Tummo (Tibetan: གཏུམ་མོ, gtum-mo; Sanskrit: caṇḍālī) is a meditative practice in Tibetan Buddhism, particularly within the traditions of the Six Dharmas of Naropa. Often translated as "inner fire" or "inner heat," tummo involves visualisation, breath retention and manipulation of the subtle body's channels (nadi) and winds (prana) to generate intense bodily warmth and, ultimately, blissful states said to lead toward awakening.

Practice

The practitioner visualises the central channel running along the spine and, at the navel chakra, a short syllable or flame. Through a forceful breathing technique sometimes called "vase breathing" (kumbhaka), air is drawn into the lower abdomen and held, stoking the inner fire, which is imagined to rise through the central channel, melting a white drop at the crown and generating the "four joys."

Physiological effects

Tummo became famous in the West through accounts of monks drying wet sheets on their bodies in freezing temperatures. Scientific studies, beginning with Herbert Benson's work in the 1980s, documented that experienced practitioners could raise peripheral body temperature substantially and modestly increase core temperature, effects attributed to the breathing technique and visualisation.
