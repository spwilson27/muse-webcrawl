Biophysics

Hero image: a protein structure rendered as a ribbon diagram — structural biology is a core branch of biophysics.

Biophysics is an interdisciplinary science that applies the theories and methods of physics to understand how biological systems work. It addresses questions at every scale, from the molecular mechanics of single proteins to the population dynamics of ecosystems, and shares methods and concepts with biochemistry, molecular biology, physical chemistry, and physiology.

Scope

Molecular biophysics studies the structures and dynamics of proteins, nucleic acids, and membranes using techniques such as X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, cryo-electron microscopy, and single-molecule manipulation with optical tweezers. Other branches examine the mechanics of cells and tissues, the electrical activity of neurons (quantified by the Hodgkin–Huxley model of the action potential), radiation biology, and the physics of sensory systems such as hearing and vision. Medical physics — imaging and radiotherapy — is a closely related applied field.

History

The discipline has roots in nineteenth-century physiology (Hermann von Helmholtz measured the speed of nerve conduction) and was decisively shaped by Erwin Schrödinger's 1944 book What Is Life?, which drew physicists into biology. The determination of the structure of DNA in 1953 by Watson, Crick, Franklin, and Wilkins depended on X-ray diffraction physics, as did the first protein structures of myoglobin and hemoglobin by Kendrew and Perutz. The Biophysical Society, founded in 1958, and its Biophysical Journal anchor the field institutionally.

Modern developments

Computational biophysics, including molecular dynamics simulations of folding and membrane transport, has grown with supercomputing. The resolution revolution in cryo-EM, recognized by the 2017 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, made atomic structures of huge molecular machines routine. Biophysical questions — protein folding, molecular motors, mechanosensation — remain central to both fundamental biology and drug discovery.
