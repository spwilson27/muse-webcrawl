The Yellowhead Highway is a major interprovincial highway in western Canada that runs from Prince Rupert, British Columbia, on the Pacific coast, eastward through Alberta and Saskatchewan to Winnipeg, Manitoba. It forms the northern branch of the Trans-Canada Highway system and is numbered Highway 16 in all four provinces it crosses.

History

The highway takes its name from Pierre Bostonais, nicknamed "Tête Jaune" ("Yellow Head") for his blond hair, an Iroquois-Métis trapper and guide who worked for the Hudson's Bay Company in the early 19th century and blazed trails through the Rocky Mountains near the pass that now bears his name, Yellowhead Pass. The route was officially opened in stages during the 1960s and 1970s and was designated part of the Trans-Canada Highway network in 1984.

Route description

From Prince Rupert the highway follows the Skeena River to Terrace and Prince George, crosses the Rockies at Yellowhead Pass (1,131 m), passes through Jasper National Park and Edmonton, then continues across the prairies through Saskatoon-area communities and Yorkton before reaching Winnipeg. Major branches include Highway 5 (the "Yellowhead" spur to Kamloops via Clearwater) in British Columbia.

Communities along the route

Communities served include Prince Rupert, Terrace, Smithers, Prince George, Jasper, Edson, Edmonton, Lloydminster, North Battleford, Saskatoon (via Highway 16), Dauphin-area junctions, and Portage la Prairie before Winnipeg.
