Student exchange program

A student exchange program is a program in which students from a secondary school (high school) or university study abroad at one of their institution's partner institutions. In a reciprocal exchange, a student from the partner institution studies at the host institution in return, though many programs are one-directional.

Exchange programs may involve international travel, but do not necessarily require the student to study outside their home country. Programs vary in length from a few weeks to a full academic year or longer, and participants typically live with a host family or in student accommodation.

History

Organized youth exchange grew rapidly after the Second World War, promoted by organizations such as AFS Intercultural Programs (which grew out of the American Field Service) and Rotary Youth Exchange, with the aim of fostering international understanding. At the university level, the European Union's Erasmus Programme, launched in 1987, became the largest scheme of its kind, enabling millions of students to study in another European country.

Types

  • Short-term exchange: programs lasting from one week to three months, often during school holidays.
  • Long-term exchange: programs lasting six to ten months, typically involving immersion in a host family and local school.
  • University exchange: semester- or year-long study at a partner university, with credit transferred back to the home institution.

Benefits and challenges

Research associates exchange participation with gains in foreign-language proficiency, intercultural competence, independence, and employability. Challenges include homesickness, culture shock, cost, and the recognition of credits earned abroad.
