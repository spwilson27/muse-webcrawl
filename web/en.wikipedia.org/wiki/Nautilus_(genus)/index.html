Nautilus (genus)

Nautilus is a genus of cephalopods in the family Nautilidae, comprising species of nautilus including the best-known, the chambered nautilus (Nautilus pompilius). They inhabit deep reef slopes in the Indo-Pacific.

Anatomy

Nautiluses have a coiled, chambered external shell used for buoyancy, up to about 90 tentacles without suckers, simple pinhole eyes, and a siphuncle regulating gas in the shell chambers. They are nocturnal predators and scavengers of fish and crustaceans. Overfishing for the shell trade has led to CITES protection for the family.
