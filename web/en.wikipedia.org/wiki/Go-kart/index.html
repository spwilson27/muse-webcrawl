Go-kart

A go-kart, also written as go-cart (often referred to as simply a kart), is a type of small, open-wheel car or quadracycle. Go-karts come in all shapes and forms, from non-motorised models to high-performance racing machines. Some, such as Superkarts, are able to beat racing cars or motorcycles on long circuits.

Go-carts were originally created in the United States in the 1950s. Post-war surplus engines, originally intended for lawn mowers, were used as a novel means of recreation. Go-kart manufacturers soon sprang up, and the sport quickly spread to other countries; it remains hugely popular internationally today.

History

Art Ingels is generally accepted to be the father of karting. A veteran hot rodder and a race car builder at Kurtis Kraft, he built the first kart, the Caretta, in Southern California in 1956. Early karting events were held at car parks and attracted small crowds, but they proved popular enough for dedicated purpose-built kart tracks to be built. The first manufacturer was the American company Go Kart Manufacturing Co. (1957). McCulloch was the first company to produce kart engines, and its McCulloch MC-10, adapted from a chainsaw engine, became a standard of the sport.

Components

Chassis

The chassis is made of chromoly tubing. There is no suspension, so the chassis must have enough flexibility to work as a suspension and enough stiffness not to break or give way on a turn. Kart chassis are classified in the United States as "Open", "Caged", "Straight" or "Offset". All Commission Internationale de Karting (CIK) or Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) homologated chassis are "Straight" and "Open".

Engine

Amusement park go-karts are typically powered by four-stroke engines or electric motors, while racing karts use small two-stroke or four-stroke engines. Most are single-cylinder. Some racing karts, such as Superkarts, use two engines or have gearboxes with up to six speeds.

Transmission

Karts do not have a differential. The lack of a differential means one rear tire must slide while cornering; this is achieved by designing the chassis so that the inside rear tire lifts up slightly when the kart turns the corner. Power is transmitted from the engine to a rear axle by a chain. Both engine and axle sprockets are removable.

Racing

Kart racing is widely accepted as an affordable way to get into motor racing, and many professional racing drivers, including Formula One champions, began their careers in karts. Racing karts can reach speeds exceeding 130 km/h (80 mph) in sprint classes, while Superkarts on long circuits can exceed 250 km/h (155 mph).
