Elizabeth Bishop (1911–1979) was an American poet and short-story writer, celebrated for precise, observant, formally accomplished poems about place, travel, loss and memory. She was Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress (1949–50, now Poet Laureate).

Life

After an unsettled childhood in Massachusetts and Nova Scotia following her father's death and mother's institutionalisation, Bishop attended Vassar College, befriended Marianne Moore, and travelled widely. She lived for many years in Key West and, from 1951, in Brazil with architect Lota de Macedo Soares — the setting of Questions of Travel (1965). She later taught at Harvard.

Poetry

A slow, perfectionist writer, she published few books: North & South (1946), Poems: North & South — A Cold Spring (Pulitzer, 1956), Questions of Travel and Geography III (1976), containing "One Art", "In the Waiting Room" and "The Moose". Her villanelle "One Art" on the art of losing is among the best-known American poems.
