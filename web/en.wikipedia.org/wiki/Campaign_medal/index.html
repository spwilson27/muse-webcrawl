Campaign medal

A campaign medal is a military decoration which is awarded to a member of an armed force who serves in a designated military operation or performs duty in a geographical theatre. Campaign medals are very similar to service medals but carry a higher status as the award usually involves deployment in hostile conditions or actual combat.

History

Campaign medals were first issued by European powers in the 19th century. The British Waterloo Medal of 1815 was the first British campaign medal awarded to all ranks present at a battle. Earlier precedents include the medals issued by the Honourable East India Company and the naval and military gold medals for senior officers. The practice spread widely: France, Prussia, and later the United States (beginning with Civil War-era and later campaign medals such as the Civil War Campaign Medal) all instituted campaign medals for particular wars and expeditions.

Design and award

Campaign medals typically consist of a medallion suspended from a ribbon with colours symbolic of the campaign. Named battles and operations may be recognised by clasps (bars) attached to the ribbon. Modern examples include the Iraq Campaign Medal and Afghanistan Campaign Medal of the United States and the Operational Service Medal of the United Kingdom.
