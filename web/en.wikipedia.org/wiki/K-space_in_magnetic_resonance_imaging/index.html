K-space in magnetic resonance imaging

FieldMedical imaging / MRI physics
Related toFourier transform, spatial frequency

In magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), k-space is a two- or three-dimensional array of spatial frequency data acquired by the scanner. The final MR image is obtained by applying an inverse Fourier transform to the k-space data.

Acquisition

During scanning, magnetic field gradients cause the resonance frequency and phase of spins to vary with position, so that each measured signal samples a particular point of k-space. The trajectory through k-space is determined by the pulse sequence — most commonly a line-by-line Cartesian path, though radial and spiral trajectories are also used.

Interpretation

Points near the centre of k-space correspond to low spatial frequencies and determine overall image contrast and brightness, while points near the periphery provide high spatial frequencies that define fine detail and edges. This property underlies accelerated techniques such as partial Fourier imaging and parallel imaging, in which undersampled k-space data are reconstructed using coil sensitivity information.
