Four Articles of Prague

The Four Articles of Prague were the common programme of the Hussites, agreed in Prague in 1420 during the Hussite Wars. They summarised the demands of the movement inspired by the teachings of Jan Hus, who had been burned at the stake at the Council of Constance in 1415.

The articles

The four articles demanded:

  1. That the word of God be preached freely throughout the kingdom of Bohemia.
  2. That the sacrament of the Eucharist be administered in both kinds — bread and wine (sub utraque specie) — to all Christians not in mortal sin.
  3. That the clergy be deprived of their secular property and temporal power, returning to an apostolic life.
  4. That all mortal sins, especially those committed publicly, be duly punished by those responsible, regardless of the offender's rank.

History

The articles united the moderate Prague Utraquists and the more radical Taborites, though the factions interpreted them differently. They formed the basis of Hussite negotiations at the Council of Basel, which in 1436 produced the Compactata, granting Bohemia the right to communion in both kinds. The radical Taborites, who rejected compromise, had already been defeated at the Battle of Lipany in 1434.
