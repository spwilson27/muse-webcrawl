Minaret of Jam

The Minaret of Jam, a 65-metre Ghurid tower in western Afghanistan.

The Minaret of Jam is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in western Afghanistan, in the Shahrak District of Ghor Province, at the confluence of the Hari Rud and Jam rivers. The 65-metre (213 ft) tapering brick minaret, built around 1190, is the second tallest brick minaret in the world after the Qutb Minar in Delhi, and a masterpiece of Ghurid architecture.

Architecture

The minaret stands on an octagonal base and consists of four tapering cylindrical shafts, the lower encased in elaborate baked-brick decoration: geometric patterns, Kufic calligraphy, and bands of Quranic text, notably from Sura 19, Maryam. Inside, a double spiral staircase rises to balconies. An inscription attributes the structure to the Ghurid sultan Ghiyas ad-Din Muhammad (r. 1163–1202) and dates it most probably to 1193/94, possibly commemorating the Ghurid victory over the Ghaznavids and marking the site of the summer capital Firozkoh.

History and preservation

Firozkoh was sacked by the Mongols around 1222 and the site forgotten by the outside world until reported to scholars in the 19th and 20th centuries. Remote and protected by its isolation, the minaret nevertheless suffers from river erosion, a slight lean, looting of surrounding ruins, and periods of conflict. In 2002 it became Afghanistan's first World Heritage Site, simultaneously inscribed on the List of World Heritage in Danger.
