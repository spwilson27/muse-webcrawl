Mahatma

Mahatma
LanguageSanskrit: mahātman, "great soul"
MeaningHonorific for a person of great virtue or spiritual attainment

Mahatma is a Sanskrit honorific meaning "great soul" (from mahā, "great", and ātman, "soul/self"). In Indian religious traditions it is applied to saints, sages and revered spiritual teachers, and by extension to persons admired for exceptional moral stature.

Usage

In the Theosophical writings of the late 19th century, "Mahatmas" referred to the hidden adepts or Masters said to guide the movement, a usage that carried the word into Western languages. In Jainism and Hinduism the title is bestowed on ascetics and reformers; figures such as Mahatma Jyotirao Phule were so honoured for their social work.

Gandhi

The title is most famously associated with Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, to whom it was popularly applied from about 1914–1915; the poet Rabindranath Tagore is often credited with popularising it, though the exact first bestowal is disputed and Gandhi himself expressed ambivalence about it, writing that the odour of the title was often less pleasant than the work it demanded. In India he is also called Bapu ("father") and the Father of the Nation. The Government of India has never formally conferred the title.

Other uses

"Mahatma" has also been used satirically or critically, and appears in the names of institutions, awards and places across India and the diaspora honouring Gandhi and other recipients.
