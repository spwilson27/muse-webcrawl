Bran, also known as miller's bran, is the hard outer layer of cereal grains, consisting of the combined aleurone layer and pericarp. Along with the germ, it is an integral part of whole grains and is often produced as a by-product of milling in the production of refined grains. Bran is present in cereal grain, including rice, corn (maize), wheat, oats, barley, rye, and millet.

Nutrition

Bran is particularly rich in dietary fibre and essential fatty acids and contains significant quantities of starch, protein, vitamins, and dietary minerals. Wheat bran is about 43% fibre by weight, mostly insoluble, and is a good source of B vitamins, magnesium, and iron. Rice bran contains a relatively high proportion of oil (up to about 20%), including gamma-oryzanol, and is prone to rancidity unless stabilised by heat treatment immediately after milling.

Uses

Bran is widely used in breakfast cereals and baked goods — bran muffins, bran flakes, and added back into wholemeal flour — and as a component of animal feed, especially for horses and livestock. Oat bran gained prominence for its soluble beta-glucan fibre, which has been shown to help lower LDL cholesterol. Historically, the removal of bran in milling white flour improved shelf life and baking quality at the cost of the fibre and micronutrients concentrated in the bran and germ.
