Vincenc Strouhal (Czech: Čeněk Strouhal; 10 April 1850 – 23 January 1922) was a Czech physicist specializing in experimental physics. He was one of the founders of the physics department at Charles University in Prague and served as its rector in 1903–1904.

Biography

Strouhal was born in Seč in eastern Bohemia. He studied mathematics and physics at Charles University in Prague, where he became an assistant to Ernst Mach. He completed his habilitation there in 1876, and after the university split into Czech and German institutions in 1882, he became professor of experimental physics at the Czech university. He designed and oversaw the construction of the university's physics institute building on Ke Karlovu street in Prague.

Work

In 1878, while studying the "singing" of wires stretched in the wind, Strouhal described the phenomenon of periodic vortex shedding behind a bluff body. He found that the frequency of the tones produced was proportional to the wind speed and inversely proportional to the diameter of the wire. The dimensionless quantity expressing this relationship, fL/V, is now known as the Strouhal number in his honour. The phenomenon he described is closely related to the Kármán vortex street investigated later by Theodore von Kármán.

Strouhal also wrote influential textbooks of physics in Czech, including works on mechanics, acoustics, optics and electricity, and played a central role in establishing Czech scientific terminology.
