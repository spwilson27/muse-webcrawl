Hampton Roads

Hampton Roads is the name given both to a body of water in southeastern Virginia, United States — the channel through which the James, Nansemond and Elizabeth rivers flow into Chesapeake Bay — and to the metropolitan region surrounding it, also known as the Virginia Beach–Norfolk–Newport News metropolitan area.

The waterway

The harbour is one of the world's largest natural harbours and supports the Port of Virginia, one of the busiest commercial ports on the US East Coast. It is crossed by the Hampton Roads Bridge–Tunnel and the Monitor–Merrimac Memorial Bridge–Tunnel, which link the Virginia Peninsula with the South Hampton Roads cities.

The region

The metropolitan area, with a population of around 1.8 million, comprises cities including Norfolk, Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Newport News, Hampton, Portsmouth and Suffolk. The region is dominated by the United States military, above all Naval Station Norfolk, the world's largest naval base, and is a major centre for shipbuilding, including Newport News Shipbuilding. Hampton Roads was the site of the 1862 Battle of Hampton Roads, the first engagement between ironclad warships (USS Monitor and CSS Virginia).
