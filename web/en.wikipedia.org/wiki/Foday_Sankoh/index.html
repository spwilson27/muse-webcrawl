Foday Saybana Sankoh (17 October 1937 – 29 July 2003) was a Sierra Leonean rebel leader, founder of the Revolutionary United Front (RUF), whose insurgency launched the Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002). Backed by Liberian warlord Charles Taylor and financed substantially through illicit "blood diamonds", the RUF became notorious for atrocities including mass amputations and the widespread use of child soldiers.

Early life

Sankoh was born in Masang Mayoso in Tonkolili District, the son of a farmer of mixed Temne and Loko parentage. After primary school he worked various jobs before joining the Sierra Leone Army in 1956, rising to corporal and serving with peacekeeping forces in the Congo. He was imprisoned for several years after alleged involvement in a 1971 mutiny. In the late 1980s he travelled to Libya, where he received guerrilla training alongside figures including Charles Taylor, and resolved to overthrow the one-party APC government of President Joseph Momoh.

Civil war

On 23 March 1991 the RUF invaded Sierra Leone from Liberia, opening an eleven-year civil war. The rebels seized the diamond-rich Kono District; the conflict killed tens of thousands and displaced millions. Under the Abidjan (1996) and Lomé (1999) peace agreements, Sankoh was released from death row — he had been sentenced to death in 1998 — and became Vice-President of Sierra Leone and chairman of the commission overseeing the country's diamond resources. In May 2000, after RUF forces took hundreds of UN peacekeepers hostage, crowds marched on Sankoh's Freetown home; his bodyguards opened fire, killing protesters, and Sankoh was captured days later.

Death

Sankoh was indicted by the UN-backed Special Court for Sierra Leone for war crimes and crimes against humanity, but he died in custody on 29 July 2003, of complications following a stroke, before his trial could be held.
