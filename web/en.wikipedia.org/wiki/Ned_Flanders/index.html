Ned Flanders

Nedward "Ned" Flanders Jr. is a fictional character in the animated television series The Simpsons, voiced by Harry Shearer. He is the Simpson family's extremely religious, relentlessly cheerful next-door neighbour at 744 Evergreen Terrace in Springfield, and is often considered the moral foil to Homer Simpson.

Character

Flanders is a devout evangelical Christian whose unfailing friendliness, honesty and optimism contrast with Homer's selfishness and incompetence. He peppers his speech with folksy interjections such as "Okely-dokely!", "Hi-diddly-ho, neighborino!" and other "-diddly" formations — a speech pattern that has made "Flanders-isms" famous. His politeness occasionally cracks under extreme provocation, most memorably in the episode "Hurricane Neddy", which revealed that his serenity resulted from a childhood course of experimental anger-suppression therapy.

Storylines

Flanders owns the Leftorium, a shop in the Springfield Mall selling left-handed goods — a business that initially struggles until Homer's conscience prompts him to send customers its way ("When Flanders Failed"). His first wife Maude was killed in the episode "Alone Again, Natura-Diddily" (2000), leaving him to raise sons Rod and Todd alone; he later married teacher Edna Krabappel, who was retired from the series following the death of her voice actress, Marcia Wallace.

Cultural impact

"Ned Flanders" has entered popular usage as a byword for a conspicuously pious, mild-mannered person, and a metal band, Okilly Dokilly, performs in Flanders-inspired attire under the self-described genre "Nedal". The character is frequently cited in discussions of the portrayal of religion in American popular culture.
