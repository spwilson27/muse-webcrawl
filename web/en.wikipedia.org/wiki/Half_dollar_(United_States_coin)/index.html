The half dollar, sometimes referred to as the fifty-cent piece, is a United States coin worth 50 cents, or one half of a dollar. It is the largest United States circulating coin currently produced in both size and weight, at 1.205 inches (30.61 mm) in diameter and 0.085 inches (2.16 mm) in thickness, and is twice the weight of a quarter.

History

Half dollars were first struck in 1794 with the Flowing Hair design, followed by the Draped Bust, Capped Bust, Seated Liberty, Barber, Walking Liberty and Franklin types. The coin was struck in 90% silver until 1964, reduced to a 40% silver-clad composition from 1965 to 1970, and has been copper-nickel clad since 1971.

Kennedy half dollar

Following the assassination of President John F. Kennedy in November 1963, Congress authorised a new half dollar bearing his portrait. Designed from Gilroy Roberts's obverse and Frank Gasparro's reverse, the Kennedy half dollar was released in March 1964 and was widely hoarded as a memento, contributing to the denomination's disappearance from everyday circulation. A special Bicentennial reverse was issued in 1975–1976.

Circulation

Although still minted and legal tender, half dollars circulate only rarely in the United States; banks seldom stock them, vending machines do not accept them, and most production since 2002 has been sold directly to collectors. Nevertheless, the denomination remains popular with coin collectors and is sometimes requested at banks by the roll.
