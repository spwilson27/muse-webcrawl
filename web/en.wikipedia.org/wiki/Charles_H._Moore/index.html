Charles H. Moore

Charles Havice Moore II (born 9 September 1938), better known as Chuck Moore, is an American computer engineer and programmer, best known as the inventor of the Forth programming language in 1968. Moore's work is characterised by a radical pursuit of simplicity: he repeatedly redesigned both software and computer hardware to eliminate complexity, later designing stack-processor chips that execute Forth directly.

Forth

Moore developed Forth while working at the United States National Radio Astronomy Observatory in the late 1960s, building on a personal programming system he had refined since 1958 to control telescope equipment. The language is stack-based, extensible and highly compact, using reverse Polish notation and a dictionary of user-defined "words". Named for the fourth-generation of software Moore hoped to build (shortened to fit a five-character filename limit), Forth became widely used in astronomy, embedded systems and boot firmware, and was standardised by ANSI in 1994.

Hardware design

Frustrated by conventional processors, Moore co-founded Forth, Inc. in 1973 and later turned to silicon design, producing stack machines such as the Novix NC4016 (1985) and the Sh-Boom and MISC (Minimal Instruction Set Computer) chips. In the 2000s he co-founded GreenArrays, which produced the GA144, a chip with 144 small asynchronous Forth cores intended for extremely low-power applications.

Philosophy

Moore advocates keeping software small enough for one person to understand entirely, discarding features rather than adding them, and factoring code relentlessly. His maxim — "do not speculate!" — and his book Programming a Problem-Oriented Language have influenced generations of minimalists in computing.
