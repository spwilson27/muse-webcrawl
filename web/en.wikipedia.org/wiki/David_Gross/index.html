David Gross

David Jonathan Gross (born 19 February 1941) is an American theoretical physicist and string theorist. Along with Frank Wilczek and David Politzer, he was awarded the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery of asymptotic freedom in the theory of the strong interaction (quantum chromodynamics). He was the founding director of the Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics at the University of California, Santa Barbara.

Biography

Gross was born in Washington, D.C., and partly raised in Jerusalem, where his father helped establish the Hebrew University's school of business administration. He received his bachelor's degree from the Hebrew University in 1962 and his PhD from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1966 under Geoffrey Chew. After a period at Harvard, he joined Princeton University in 1969, becoming Eugene Higgins Professor of Physics, and in 1997 he moved to UC Santa Barbara to direct its Institute for Theoretical Physics (now the Kavli Institute), a post he held until 2012.

Research

In 1973, working with his first graduate student, Frank Wilczek, Gross discovered asymptotic freedom: the property that the strong nuclear force weakens at higher energies and shorter distances. David Politzer independently reached the same result. The discovery made quantum chromodynamics (QCD) a viable field theory of the strong interaction and explained why quarks behave almost as free particles inside hadrons at high energies while being confined at low energies. Gross also made major contributions to string theory, notably the 1985 construction of the heterotic string with Jeffrey Harvey, Emil Martinec, and Ryan Rohm (the "Princeton string quartet"), and to the use of quantum field theory methods in scattering calculations.
