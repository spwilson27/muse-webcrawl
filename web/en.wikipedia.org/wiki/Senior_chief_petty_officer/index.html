Senior chief petty officer

Senior chief petty officer is an enlisted rank in the United States Navy and United States Coast Guard, designated pay grade E-8, above chief petty officer (E-7) and below master chief petty officer (E-9). It is the second-highest tier of the Navy's chief petty officer grades, whose members form the senior enlisted leadership of their commands.

Advancement

Promotion to senior chief is by a central selection board convened annually, which reviews the records of eligible chief petty officers with at least three years in grade. Senior chiefs typically serve as departmental leading chiefs, senior enlisted advisors ashore, or in demanding technical leadership billets. Their insignia adds a silver star above the fouled anchor of the chief petty officer's badge, and eight years of cumulative good-conduct service is marked by gold rating stripes.

Role

Along with master chiefs, senior chiefs are regarded as technical experts and deckplate leaders responsible for training junior officers and enlisted sailors, maintaining discipline and standards within the Chiefs' Mess, and advising commanding officers. Fewer than about 2.5 percent of Navy enlisted personnel serve in the E-8 and E-9 grades combined, by statutory limits on senior enlisted strength.
