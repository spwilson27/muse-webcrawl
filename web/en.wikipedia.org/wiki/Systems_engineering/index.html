Systems engineering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The V-model relates design stages to corresponding test stages

Systems engineering is an interdisciplinary field of engineering and engineering management that focuses on how to design, integrate and manage complex systems over their life cycles. At its core it is concerned with work processes, optimisation methods and risk management tools that keep large projects coherent as a whole rather than as disconnected parts.

Scope and activities

Core activities include eliciting and analysing stakeholder requirements, defining system architectures, managing interfaces between subsystems, planning verification and validation, configuration management, and managing technical risk and trade-offs. The well-known V-model maps each stage of decomposition and design to a corresponding stage of integration and testing. Modern practice increasingly uses model-based systems engineering (MBSE), with languages such as SysML, in place of purely document-based approaches.

History

The discipline emerged in the 1940s at Bell Telephone Laboratories, where engineers recognised that the properties of whole systems (such as telephone networks) could not be understood from components alone. It matured through Cold War defence and aerospace programmes — missiles, radar networks and the Apollo programme — and through organisations such as the RAND Corporation. The professional society INCOSE (International Council on Systems Engineering), founded in 1990, now coordinates much of the field's body of knowledge.

Applications

Systems engineering is applied wherever complexity and integration dominate: aerospace and defence, transportation infrastructure, energy systems, healthcare delivery, and large software-intensive systems, where it overlaps with software and project engineering.
