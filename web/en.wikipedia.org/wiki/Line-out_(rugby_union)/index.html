Line-out (rugby union)

Line-out
SportRugby union
PurposeRestart play after the ball goes into touch
ThrowerUsually the hooker
Governing lawsWorld Rugby Law 18

A line-out or lineout in rugby union is a means of restarting play after the ball has gone into touch (out of the field of play at the side). When the ball goes into touch, the side that did not put it there usually throws it back in, from between the touchline and the five-metre line, into a corridor between two lines of opposing forwards who compete to catch it.

Procedure

The two teams form single lines, at least two players each, parallel to and one metre apart, between the 5-metre and 15-metre lines. A player — usually the hooker — throws the ball straight down the middle of the corridor. Designated jumpers, supported and lifted by team-mates, compete for the ball in the air; lifting, once prohibited, was legalised in 1999 and is now universal at higher levels. The throw must be straight and the ball must travel at least five metres. Teams use coded calls to disguise the intended target, and may vary the number of players in the line (from two to fourteen) to outmanoeuvre the opposition.

Tactics

Winning clean line-out ball is a major source of attacking possession. After a catch, the forwards often bind together into a driving maul, using the set piece as a platform to advance before releasing the ball to the backs. Defending teams contest the throw, attempt to intercept or disrupt the catch, and defend the subsequent maul. Infringements include jumping early, closing the gap, barging, and throwing not-straight, sanctioned with a free kick or a scrum or line-out to the opposition.

Quick throws and related restarts

A player may instead take a quick throw-in from the correct place before a line-out forms, provided the same ball is used and it has not touched a spectator or another player; the throw may be backwards. A line-out also results from certain penalties kicked to touch, where the kicking team retains the throw.
