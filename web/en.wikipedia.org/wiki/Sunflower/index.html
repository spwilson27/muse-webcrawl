Sunflower

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sunflower (Helianthus annuus) is a tall annual plant of the daisy family, native to North America and cultivated worldwide for its edible seeds and oil. Its huge flower head — actually thousands of tiny florets — can exceed 30 cm across, and young plants track the sun across the sky (heliotropism).

Biology and uses

Domesticated by Indigenous peoples of North America thousands of years ago, the sunflower became a major oil crop after reaching Europe and being developed in Russia in the 18th–19th centuries; Ukraine and Russia are the largest producers of sunflower oil. Seeds are eaten as snacks and bird feed; the plant is also used for phytoremediation — sunflowers drew radioactive caesium from ponds near Chernobyl — and as a cheerful garden flower and national symbol of Ukraine.

In culture

Van Gogh's Sunflowers series (1888–89) made the flower an icon of art. Fibonacci spirals in the seed head are a classic example of mathematical patterns in nature (phyllotaxis). Sunflowers symbolise loyalty, longevity and, after 2022, solidarity with Ukraine.
