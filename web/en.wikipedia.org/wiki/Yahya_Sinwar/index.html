Yahya Sinwar

Born: 29 October 1962, Khan Yunis, Gaza Strip
Died: 16 October 2024, Rafah, Gaza Strip
Role: Leader of Hamas in the Gaza Strip (2017–2024); chairman of the Hamas political bureau (2024)

Yahya Ibrahim Hassan Sinwar (Arabic: يحيى السنوار; 29 October 1962 – 16 October 2024) was a Palestinian militant and political leader who led Hamas in the Gaza Strip from 2017 and briefly served as chairman of the organization's political bureau in 2024.

Early life and imprisonment

Sinwar was born in the Khan Yunis refugee camp in the Egyptian-occupied Gaza Strip; his family had fled Ashkelon during the 1948 war. He studied Arabic at the Islamic University of Gaza and was an early member of Hamas at its founding in 1987, organizing its internal security apparatus, which targeted suspected collaborators. Arrested by Israel in 1988, he was sentenced to multiple life terms. In prison he learned Hebrew, studied Israeli society, and rose to lead Hamas prisoners. He was released in 2011 as one of 1,027 prisoners exchanged for the captured Israeli soldier Gilad Shalit; his brother Mohammed helped negotiate the deal.

Leadership

In 2017 Sinwar was elected Hamas's leader inside Gaza, combining political and military authority with a reputation for hardline strategy and tactical pragmatism, including periods of negotiated calm and efforts to reconcile with Fatah. Israel and the United States regarded him as a principal planner of the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel that killed about 1,200 people and sparked the Gaza war. After the assassination of Ismail Haniyeh in July 2024, Sinwar was named overall head of Hamas. He was killed by Israeli forces in Rafah on 16 October 2024 during a chance encounter, confirmed by the IDF and later by Hamas.
