Joseph Hooton Taylor Jr.


Joseph Hooton Taylor Jr.
Born: 29 March 1941, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Alma mater: Haverford College; Harvard University
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1993)
Known for: Discovery of the first binary pulsar

Joseph Hooton Taylor Jr. (born 29 March 1941) is an American astrophysicist and Nobel laureate known, with Russell Alan Hulse, for the discovery of the first binary pulsar, PSR B1913+16, which provided indirect evidence for the existence of gravitational waves.

Career

Taylor studied at Haverford College and received his PhD in astronomy from Harvard in 1968. After work at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory he joined the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where with Hulse, using the Arecibo Observatory, he discovered the binary pulsar in 1974. The gradual decay of its orbit matched the predictions of general relativity for energy loss through gravitational radiation. He later moved to Princeton University, where he became a distinguished professor.

Amateur radio

A lifelong radio amateur (call sign K1JT), Taylor developed the WSJT family of weak-signal digital communication protocols, including FT8, widely used by amateur radio operators for moonbounce and meteor-scatter contacts.

Awards

Taylor and Hulse shared the 1993 Nobel Prize in Physics "for the discovery of a new type of pulsar, a discovery that has opened up new possibilities for the study of gravitation".
