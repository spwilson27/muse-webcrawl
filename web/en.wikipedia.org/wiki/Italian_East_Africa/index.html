Italian East Africa (Italian: Africa Orientale Italiana, AOI) was a short-lived Italian colony in the Horn of Africa, formed in 1936 by uniting Italian Eritrea, Italian Somaliland and the newly conquered Ethiopian Empire after the Second Italo-Ethiopian War. It was proclaimed on 9 May 1936 by Benito Mussolini, who declared King Victor Emmanuel III Emperor of Ethiopia. The colony was divided into six governorates: Eritrea, Amhara, Galla-Sidamo, Harar, Scioa (Shewa) and Somalia, with its capital at Addis Ababa.

Conquest and administration

Italy's invasion of Ethiopia began in October 1935. Italian forces under Pietro Badoglio entered Addis Ababa on 5 May 1936; Emperor Haile Selassie fled into exile. The conquest was accompanied by the use of chemical weapons and mass reprisals, notably after the attempted assassination of Viceroy Rodolfo Graziani in February 1937, which triggered the Yekatit 12 massacres in Addis Ababa. Ethiopian arbegnoch (patriot) guerrillas continued resistance throughout the occupation. The regime pursued settler colonialism, racial segregation laws anticipating the 1938 racial legislation in Italy itself, and an extensive road-building programme; tens of thousands of Italian settlers arrived, though far fewer than propaganda promised.

Second World War and collapse

When Italy entered the Second World War in June 1940, Italian East Africa was isolated from Italy by British control of the Suez Canal and the Mediterranean. Forces under the Viceroy, Prince Amedeo, Duke of Aosta, briefly occupied Kassala in Sudan and conquered British Somaliland in August 1940. In January 1941 British and Commonwealth forces, together with Ethiopian patriots, invaded from Sudan and Kenya. Addis Ababa was liberated on 5 April 1941 and Haile Selassie restored to his throne on 5 May 1941. The Duke of Aosta surrendered at Amba Alagi in May; the last Italian stronghold, Gondar, fell in November 1941, though some Italian units waged guerrilla warfare until 1943.

Aftermath

Under the 1947 Paris Peace Treaty Italy formally renounced its colonies. Italian Somaliland returned to Italian administration in 1950 as a UN trust territory, becoming independent in 1960 as part of Somalia. Eritrea was federated with Ethiopia in 1952 and later annexed, a step that led to the Eritrean War of Independence and eventual Eritrean independence in 1991–1993.
