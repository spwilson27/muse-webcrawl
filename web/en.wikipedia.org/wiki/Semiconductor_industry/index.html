Semiconductor industry

The semiconductor industry is the aggregate of companies engaged in the design and fabrication of semiconductors and semiconductor devices, such as transistors and integrated circuits. Formed around 1960 following the adoption of the planar process and the MOS transistor, it has grown into a global business with annual sales exceeding US$500 billion, underpinning the wider electronics and computing industries.

Structure

The industry is organised around several business models: integrated device manufacturers (IDMs) such as Intel and Samsung design and fabricate their own chips; fabless companies such as NVIDIA, AMD and Qualcomm design chips manufactured by others; pure-play foundries such as TSMC fabricate designs for customers; and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) firms package and test finished devices. Equipment suppliers such as ASML, Applied Materials and Lam Research provide the lithography and process tools on which fabrication depends.

Geography and economics

Fabrication is concentrated in East Asia—Taiwan, South Korea, Japan and China—while design is led by the United States. The industry is famously cyclical and capital-intensive: a leading-edge fab can cost over US$20 billion. Because semiconductors are critical to defence, communications and AI, governments have adopted industrial policies such as the US CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 to localise supply chains.
