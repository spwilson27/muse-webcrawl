Blood alcohol content (BAC), also called blood alcohol concentration or blood alcohol level, is a measurement of alcohol intoxication used for legal and medical purposes. It expresses the mass of ethanol in a given volume or mass of blood, commonly as a percentage.

Effects by level

At 0.02–0.05% drinkers typically feel relaxed with mildly impaired judgement. At 0.08%, the legal driving limit in much of the United States, motor skills and reaction times are significantly impaired and crash risk rises sharply. Levels of 0.15–0.30% bring slurred speech, vomiting and blackouts; above roughly 0.40%, coma and death from respiratory depression become likely.

Measurement

BAC is measured directly from a blood sample or estimated from breath using a breathalyser, which applies a blood-to-breath partition ratio of about 2100:1. It can also be estimated retrospectively with the Widmark formula, which takes account of body mass, sex, and the amount consumed. The body metabolises alcohol at a roughly constant rate of about 0.015% per hour.

Legal use

Most jurisdictions criminalise driving above a specified BAC ("per se" limits), which vary from 0.00% for novice or commercial drivers in some countries to 0.08%. Higher aggravated thresholds apply in many places for enhanced penalties.
