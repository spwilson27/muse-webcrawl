Pyotr Stolypin

Pyotr Arkadyevich Stolypin
Born: 14 April 1862, Dresden
Died: 18 September 1911, Kiev
Office: Prime Minister of Russia (1906–1911)

Pyotr Arkadyevich Stolypin (1862–1911) was a Russian statesman who served as prime minister and minister of internal affairs under Tsar Nicholas II from 1906 until his assassination in 1911. He is remembered both for repressive measures against revolutionaries after the 1905 Revolution — the swift field courts-martial earned the hangman's noose the nickname "Stolypin's necktie" — and for ambitious agrarian reforms.

Stolypin reform

The Stolypin reform sought to dissolve the peasant commune (the obshchina) and create a class of prosperous, conservative individual farmers: peasants could withdraw their allotments as private property and consolidate scattered strips into enclosed farms (khutora and otruba). Stolypin famously declared his "wager on the strong and sober" and asked for twenty years of internal and external peace to transform Russia. Resettlement of millions of peasants to Siberia and Central Asia, supported by the Peasant Land Bank, accompanied the reform.

Assassination

In September 1911, while attending a performance of Rimsky-Korsakov's The Tale of Tsar Saltan at the Kiev Opera House in the Tsar's presence, Stolypin was shot by Dmitry Bogrov, a revolutionary and police informant, and died four days later.
