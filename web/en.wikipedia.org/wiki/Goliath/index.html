Goliath is a Philistine warrior described in the Book of Samuel in the Hebrew Bible, famous for his single combat with the young Israelite shepherd David. The story — the heavily armed giant defeated by an unlikely underdog — made "David and Goliath" a universal metaphor for a contest between unequal opponents.

Biblical narrative

In 1 Samuel 17, the armies of Israel and the Philistines face each other in the Valley of Elah. Goliath, a champion from Gath wearing bronze armor and carrying a spear "like a weaver's beam", challenges Israel to settle the battle by single combat, morning and evening for forty days. The shepherd boy David, visiting his brothers, accepts, refuses Saul's armor, and strikes Goliath in the forehead with a stone from his sling, then beheads him with the giant's own sword. The Philistines flee, and David's path to the throne begins.

Textual questions

Goliath's height varies between textual traditions: the Masoretic text gives "six cubits and a span" (about 2.9 m), while the Dead Sea Scrolls, Septuagint and Josephus give "four cubits and a span" (about 2.0 m). Second Samuel 21 also credits a warrior named Elhanan with killing "Goliath the Gittite", a discrepancy harmonized in Chronicles by making the victim Goliath's brother, Lahmi.

Legacy

The duel has been retold in art from medieval manuscripts to Caravaggio and Michelangelo, and medical writers have speculatively linked Goliath's stature and David's victory to acromegaly and its associated visual defects. The names "Goliath" and the "David and Goliath" framing pervade modern sports, business and political commentary.
