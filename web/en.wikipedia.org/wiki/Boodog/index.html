Boodog

Boodog (Mongolian: боодог) is a traditional Mongolian dish of goat or marmot cooked from the inside out using heated stones. The animal is slaughtered and skinned, its cavity is filled with stones heated in a fire, and the skin is tied closed; the carcass is then singed or torched from the outside. The stones cook the meat from within while the skin acts as a pressure vessel, producing tender meat and a rich broth collected inside.

The hot, greasy stones from the finished boodog are traditionally passed between the diners' hands, a practice believed to relieve fatigue and promote circulation. Boodog is closely related to khorkhog, in which pieces of meat are cooked with hot stones in a sealed container.
