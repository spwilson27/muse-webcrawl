Kull of Atlantis, or Kull the Conqueror, is a fictional character created by writer Robert E. Howard. He first appeared in the short story "The Shadow Kingdom" in the pulp magazine Weird Tales in August 1929. An Atlantean barbarian who becomes king of Valusia, the greatest kingdom of his age, Kull is a direct literary precursor of Howard's better-known creation, Conan the Barbarian.

Character

Kull was born in Atlantis into the Tiger Valley tribe, but was orphaned by a flood and raised by another clan before being captured and enslaved. Escaping, he became in turn a pirate, an outlaw, a gladiator, and finally a mercenary captain in the service of Valusia, whose throne he seized by strangling the mad king Borna with his bare hands. As king, Kull is a brooding, introspective figure, constantly at odds with the ancient laws and priesthoods of the decadent empire he rules, and troubled by metaphysical questions about identity and illusion.

His most trusted companion is the Pictish ambassador Ka-nu, and his great enemies include the serpent men — survivors of a pre-human race who infiltrate Valusian society in disguise — and the skull-faced sorcerer Thulsa Doom.

Stories

Howard completed only a handful of Kull stories, several published posthumously: "The Shadow Kingdom" (1929), "The Mirrors of Tuzun Thune" (1929), "Kings of the Night" (1930), "By This Axe I Rule!" (unpublished in Howard's lifetime; later reworked into the first Conan story, "The Phoenix on the Sword"), "The Cat and the Skull", "The Screaming Skull of Silence", and the poem "The King and the Oak". Later authors, notably Lin Carter, completed Howard's fragments and wrote additional Kull tales.

Setting

The stories are set in Howard's Thurian Age, thousands of years before Conan's Hyborian Age. Valusia, the "Land of Enchantment", is the oldest and most powerful of the Seven Empires, already old and decadent when Kull takes its throne; Atlantis is still a land of savage tribes, and beyond the western sea lie the Pictish Isles.

Adaptations

Kull appeared in Marvel Comics series in the 1970s and 1980s and was played by Kevin Sorbo in the 1997 film Kull the Conqueror, which drew heavily on the Conan stories. Dark Horse Comics published further Kull series from 2008.
