United States Air Force Reserve

The United States Air Force Reserve (USAFR) is the federal air reserve force of the United States Air Force. Established as a separate component in 1948, it provides trained units and qualified individuals for active duty in war or national emergency and supports peacetime missions around the world. Its major command is Air Force Reserve Command (AFRC), headquartered at Robins Air Force Base, Georgia.

Organization

Air Force Reservists serve in unit programmes, as individual mobilization augmentees attached to active-duty organizations, and in full-time Active Guard and Reserve and civilian technician roles. Reserve units operate fighters, bombers, tankers, airlift, rescue, space, cyber, aeromedical and support missions. Many are “associate” units in which reservists share aircraft and facilities with active-duty counterparts.

History and operations

Reserve airmen were mobilized for the Korean, Vietnam and Persian Gulf wars and for operations after the attacks of 11 September 2001. The Air Force Reserve also performs domestic missions including aerial firefighting, hurricane reconnaissance through the “Hurricane Hunters”, aerial spray, search and rescue and disaster relief. It evolved from a strategic reserve held mainly for major war into an operational reserve used continuously for combat and support deployments.

Personnel

Members commonly combine military service with civilian careers or education, training at least one weekend a month and additional periods each year, though many serve much more. Collectively known as Citizen Airmen, they provide experienced personnel whose skills can be expanded rapidly when required by national policy.
