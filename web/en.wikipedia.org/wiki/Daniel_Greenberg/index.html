Daniel Greenberg

Daniel Greenberg (born 1934) is an American educational philosopher and author, best known as a co-founder of the Sudbury Valley School in Framingham, Massachusetts, established in 1968, and a leading advocate of democratic education and the "Sudbury model" of schooling.

Career

Greenberg studied physics at Columbia University, earning his undergraduate degree and doctorate there, and worked in theoretical physics before turning to education. With his wife Hanna Greenberg and others, he founded Sudbury Valley School, where students are free to direct their own activities and the school is governed democratically by a School Meeting in which students and staff have equal votes.

Works

Greenberg has written extensively on education, children's rights and free schooling. His books, published by the Sudbury Valley School Press, include Free at Last: The Sudbury Valley School (1987), The Sudbury Valley School Experience, Education in America: A View from Sudbury Valley, and numerous collections of essays. The Sudbury model has inspired democratic schools around the world.
