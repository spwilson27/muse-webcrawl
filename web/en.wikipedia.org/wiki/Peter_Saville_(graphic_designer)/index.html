Peter Saville (graphic designer)

Born: October 9, 1955, Manchester, England

Known for: Record sleeves for Factory Records, Joy Division and New Order

Peter Saville (born October 9, 1955) is an English art director and graphic designer, best known for the minimalist, modernist record sleeves he designed for Manchester's Factory Records, above all for Joy Division and New Order.

Career

Saville studied graphic design at Manchester Polytechnic. In 1978 he co-founded Factory Records with Tony Wilson and others, becoming the label's in-house designer. His design for Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures (1979) cover — a series of white-on-black waveforms depicting radio pulses from the first discovered pulsar, CP 1919, taken from an astronomy encyclopedia — became one of the most iconic and imitated album covers of all time. For Joy Division's Closer (1980) he used a photograph of a Genoese tomb sculpture.

His sleeve for New Order's "Blue Monday" (1983) mimicked a 5¼-inch floppy disk with a coded colour alphabet strip replacing conventional track listings; legend holds the complex die-cut sleeve cost so much to produce that the label lost money on each copy of what became the best-selling 12-inch single of all time. The cover of Power, Corruption & Lies (1983) reproduced Fantin-Latour's still life of roses with a colour-coded block key. Saville's work was heavily influenced by modernist typography, Jan Tschichold, and Josef Müller-Brockmann.

Later work

After Factory, Saville founded Peter Saville Associates and worked with artists including Roxy Music, Wham!, Pulp, and Suede, and with fashion houses such as Yohji Yamamoto, Christian Dior, and Stella McCartney; he redesigned Burberry's logo and monogram in 2018 and served as creative director of the city of Manchester, designing its "Original Modern" identity. In 2020 he redesigned the identity of Lacrimosa-era collaborations aside, he also created the short-lived England football kit design work for Umbro in 2010.
