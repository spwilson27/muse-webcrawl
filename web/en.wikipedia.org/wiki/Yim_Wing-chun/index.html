Yim Wing-chun

Yim Wing-chun (simplified Chinese: 严咏春; traditional Chinese: 嚴詠春; Cantonese Yale: Yìhm Wihng-chēun) is a legendary Chinese martial artist credited in the folklore of Wing Chun kung fu as the art's founder or namesake. According to legend, she lived during the Qing dynasty and learned a new style of fighting from the Buddhist nun Ng Mui, one of the legendary Five Elders of the Shaolin Temple.

Legend

In the most common version of the story, Yim Wing-chun was a young woman — often described as the daughter of a beancurd seller — who was being forced into marriage by a local warlord or bully. Ng Mui taught her a compact, efficient style of boxing suited to a smaller person fighting a stronger opponent; Wing-chun used it to defeat her unwanted suitor. She later married her fiancé, Leung Bok-chau, to whom she taught the art; he named it after her.

Historicity

No reliable historical records confirm Yim Wing-chun's existence, and accounts vary between Wing Chun lineages. She is nevertheless venerated as the art's first practitioner and remains a cultural icon, portrayed in numerous Hong Kong martial arts films.
