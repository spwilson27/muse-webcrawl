Tribune

Tribune (Latin: tribunus) was the title of various elected officials in ancient Rome, most importantly the tribunes of the plebs, who protected the interests of the common people.

Tribune of the plebs

Created in 494 BC after the first secession of the plebs, plebeian tribunes — eventually ten — were sacrosanct, could veto (intercessio) acts of magistrates and the Senate, convene the Plebeian Council and propose legislation. Famous holders include Tiberius and Gaius Gracchus.

Other tribunes

Military tribunes commanded legions; tribunes of the treasury and consular tribunes also existed. Under the Empire, emperors held tribunician power (tribunicia potestas) as a basis of authority.

Later use

The title was revived in medieval Rome and the French Revolution, and survives in newspaper names such as the Chicago Tribune. See Roman Republic.
