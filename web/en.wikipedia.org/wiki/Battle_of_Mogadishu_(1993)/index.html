The Battle of Mogadishu was fought on 3–4 October 1993 in Mogadishu, Somalia, between United States forces and Somali militiamen loyal to Mohamed Farrah Aidid. Part of Operation Gothic Serpent, the battle is also known from the book and film Black Hawk Down.

Background

Following the US-led intervention to relieve the Somali famine (Operation Restore Hope), the United Nations mission UNOSOM II clashed with Aidid's Somali National Alliance. Task Force Ranger, built around Delta Force operators and Army Rangers, was deployed to capture Aidid and his lieutenants. On 3 October 1993 the force launched a daylight raid to seize two of Aidid's senior aides in the Bakara Market district.

The battle

The initial assault succeeded in capturing the targets, but rocket-propelled grenades brought down two MH-60 Black Hawk helicopters — Super Six-One and Super Six-Four. Ground convoys attempting to reach the crash sites were ambushed in the maze of streets, and a desperate overnight defence ensued around the first crash site. Chief Warrant Officer Michael Durant, pilot of Super Six-Four, was captured after snipers Gary Gordon and Randy Shughart sacrificed themselves defending the wreck; both received the Medal of Honor posthumously. A relief column of UN and US forces finally extracted the survivors on the morning of 4 October.

Aftermath

Eighteen Americans were killed and more than seventy wounded; Somali casualties were estimated in the hundreds to over a thousand. Images of dead US soldiers dragged through the streets shocked American public opinion. President Bill Clinton ordered a phased withdrawal, completed by March 1994, and the battle profoundly influenced US reluctance to intervene in later crises of the 1990s.
