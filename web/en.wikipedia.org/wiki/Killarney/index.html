Killarney (Irish: Cill Airne, meaning "church of the sloes") is a town in County Kerry, south-western Ireland. The town is on the north-eastern shore of Lough Leane, part of Killarney National Park, and is home to St Mary's Cathedral, Ross Castle, Muckross House and Abbey, the Lakes of Killarney, and the MacGillycuddy's Reeks mountains. Killarney is one of Ireland's leading tourist destinations and a starting point for the famous Ring of Kerry touring route.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Bronze Age, and the monastery on Innisfallen Island in Lough Leane, founded in the seventh century, produced the Annals of Innisfallen, an important chronicle of early Irish history. Tourism came to Killarney in the eighteenth century and was greatly boosted by Queen Victoria's visit to Muckross House in 1861. The traditional way for visitors to tour the lakes remains the horse-drawn "jaunting car".

Killarney National Park

Ireland's first national park, established in 1932 when the Muckross estate was donated to the state, now covers over 10,000 hectares of lakes, oak and yew woodlands, and mountain. It is home to Ireland's only remaining native herd of red deer and to reintroduced white-tailed eagles, and around it are sights such as Torc Waterfall, the Gap of Dunloe, Ladies View and Dinis Cottage.

Economy

Tourism dominates the local economy; the town is said to have one of the highest ratios of hotel beds to residents in Ireland. Killarney also hosts conferences at the INEC arena and is a gateway for walkers on the Kerry Way long-distance trail.
