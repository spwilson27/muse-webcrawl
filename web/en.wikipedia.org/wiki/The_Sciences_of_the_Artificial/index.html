The Sciences of the Artificial

The Sciences of the Artificial is a 1969 book by American economist and cognitive scientist Herbert A. Simon, based on the Karl Taylor Compton Lectures given at MIT. In it, Simon argues that artificial phenomena — things made by human beings — deserve a science of their own, distinct from the natural sciences, and lays foundations for the study of design.

Content

Simon distinguishes between the natural sciences, concerned with how things are, and the sciences of the artificial, concerned with how things ought to be in order to attain goals. An artifact is characterised as an interface between an "inner environment" (its substance and organisation) and an "outer environment" (the surroundings in which it operates). The book treats topics including the psychology of problem solving, bounded rationality, hierarchy and near-decomposability in complex systems, and heuristic search.

Influence

The book profoundly influenced design theory, management science, computer science and artificial intelligence research. Its concept of bounded rationality and its treatment of design as a discipline underpin much of modern design thinking. A third, substantially expanded edition appeared in 1996.
