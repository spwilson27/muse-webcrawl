Mount Yari (槍ヶ岳, Yari-ga-take) is a mountain in the Hida Mountains (Northern Alps) of Japan, on the border of Nagano and Gifu prefectures. Rising to 3,180 m — the fifth-highest summit in Japan — its sharp, spear-shaped peak has made it one of the country's most recognisable and beloved mountains, sometimes called "Japan's Matterhorn".

History

The peak was first climbed in 1828 by Banryū, a Buddhist monk and mountain ascetic, who founded a small shrine near the summit. The English missionary Walter Weston popularised the Japan Alps abroad in the 1890s and praised Yari's distinctive profile. Today the mountain forms part of Chūbu-Sangaku National Park and is listed among Japan's 100 Famous Mountains.

Climbing

Most climbers approach from Kamikōchi via the Yarisawa valley, overnighting at the large Yari-ga-take Sansō hut below the summit; the final section to the tiny summit shrine uses chains and ladders on exposed rock. A celebrated long traverse, the "Yari-Hotaka" ridge route, links Yari with Mount Hotaka via the jagged Daikiretto gap, one of Japan's classic alpine routes.
