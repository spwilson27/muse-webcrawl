Haji Bektash Veli (Turkish: Hacı Bektaş-ı Veli; Persian: حاجی بکتاش والی; c. 1209–1271) was an Alevi Muslim mystic, saint, sayyid and philosopher from Khorasan who lived and taught in 13th-century Anatolia. He is revered as the founder of the Bektashi order of Sufism and as a central figure of Alevi Islam — alongside Ali and Husayn, one of the tradition's defining sacred figures — although the Bektashi order in its institutional form was organised after his death, chiefly by Balım Sultan in the early 16th century.

Life

Historical details of Haji Bektash's life are sparse, and his biography is known mainly through the hagiographic Vilayetname (completed centuries later) and later tradition. He is said to have been born in Nishapur, to be a descendant of Musa al-Kazim, the seventh Shia Imam, and to have been a disciple in the lineage of Ahmed Yasawi, the great Turkic Sufi of Central Asia, whose emissary Lokman Perende is named as his teacher. After pilgrimage to Mecca — the source of his title Haji — he migrated to Anatolia around the time of the Mongol invasions and settled at Sulucakarahöyük, now the town of Hacıbektaş in Nevşehir Province, where he taught, gathered disciples and died in 1271.

Teachings

The Makalat ("Articles"), a work in Arabic and Turkish attributed to him, sets out a path of four gates and forty stations (Dört Kapı Kırk Makam): Sharia (religious law), Tariqa (the Sufi path), Marifa (gnosis/knowledge) and Haqiqa (truth/reality). His teaching emphasised humanity, tolerance, education and the unity of existence; sayings attributed to him include "Seek knowledge even if it is in China", "Whatever you seek, seek in yourself", and "Treat others' faults as your own". Alevis and Bektashis regard him as a proponent of religious tolerance between Muslims, Christians and others in multi-confessional Anatolia.

The Bektashi order and the Janissaries

After his death his shrine became the centre (the Pir Evi, house of the saint) of the Bektashi order, which spread widely through Ottoman Anatolia and the Balkans. The order became closely associated with the Janissary corps, who called themselves the "sons of Haji Bektash"; when Sultan Mahmud II abolished the Janissaries in 1826, Bektashi lodges (tekkes) were also suppressed, and the order went underground, surviving strongly in Albania, where its world headquarters later moved to Tirana. In 1964 the Haji Bektash complex in Hacıbektaş became a museum; an annual festival in mid-August draws large numbers of Alevi and Bektashi pilgrims. UNESCO marked 2021 as the year of Haji Bektash Veli, commemorating the 750th anniversary of his death.
