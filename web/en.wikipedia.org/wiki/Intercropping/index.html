Intercropping

Intercropping is the agricultural practice of growing two or more crops in proximity in the same field during a growing season, to use resources more efficiently and increase total yield and resilience.

Types

Forms include mixed intercropping, row intercropping, strip intercropping and relay intercropping (staggered planting). A classic example is the Three Sisters of maize, beans and squash: beans fix nitrogen, maize provides a trellis, squash shades the soil.

Benefits and limits

Benefits include better land-equivalent ratios, pest and disease disruption, weed suppression and risk spreading. Drawbacks include management complexity, competition if poorly designed, and difficulty mechanising — why monoculture dominates industrial farming, while intercropping remains central to smallholder and agroecological systems.
