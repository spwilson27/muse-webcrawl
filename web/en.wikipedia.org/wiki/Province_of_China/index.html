Province of China

Provinces (Chinese: 省; pinyin: shěng) are the most common type of province-level division in China. The People's Republic of China administers 22 provinces directly; it also claims Taiwan as its 23rd province, while Taiwan is administered by the Republic of China. Together with autonomous regions, municipalities and special administrative regions, provinces form the first level of the administrative hierarchy below the central government.

Government

Each province has a provincial people's government headed by a governor, overseen by a provincial people's congress. In practice, the provincial secretary of the Chinese Communist Party is the most powerful official in the province. Provincial governments administer prefecture-level cities, which in turn oversee counties and districts.

History

The provincial system originated under the Yuan dynasty (1271–1368), whose branch secretariats (xíng zhōngshū shěng) became the template for provinces under the Ming and Qing dynasties. The number and borders of provinces changed repeatedly; the present set largely stabilised in the 1950s, with Hainan established as a separate province in 1988 and Chongqing split from Sichuan as a municipality in 1997.

List

The PRC's provinces include Hebei, Shanxi, Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Fujian, Jiangxi, Shandong, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Guangdong, Hainan, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Gansu and Qinghai, plus the claimed province of Taiwan.
