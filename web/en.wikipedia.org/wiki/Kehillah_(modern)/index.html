Kehillah (modern)

The modern kehillah (Hebrew: קהילה, plural kehillot) is an organised Jewish community structure that emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, adapting the traditional pre-modern communal body to the conditions of emancipation, urbanisation and voluntary association.

History

The traditional kehillah governed religious, educational, charitable and judicial affairs of Jewish communities. After emancipation in Western Europe and immigration to the United States, communal leaders sought new forms. The New York Kehillah (1908–1922), led by Judah L. Magnes, attempted to unite the city's Jewish organisations for education, social work and representation.

Contemporary usage

Today the term is used by synagogues, federations and independent prayer communities to describe a self-defined congregation or community, often emphasising inclusivity and member participation. Jewish community federations in North America descend in part from kehillah experiments.
