Bus rapid transit (BRT) is a public transport system in which buses operate on dedicated rights-of-way with rail-like features — enclosed stations, off-board fare collection, level boarding, and priority at intersections — to deliver higher capacity and speed than conventional bus services at lower cost than rail.

Characteristics

The BRT Standard, published by the Institute for Transportation and Development Policy, scores systems on features including dedicated median busways, platform-level boarding, off-board fare collection, intersection treatments, and service frequency. High-capacity systems use articulated or bi-articulated buses and passing lanes at stations to run express and local services on the same corridor.

History

The first widely recognised BRT system opened in Curitiba, Brazil, in 1974, integrating dedicated busways with land-use planning under mayor Jaime Lerner. Quito and Bogotá followed in the 1990s; Bogotá's TransMilenio (2000) demonstrated that BRT could carry metro-scale passenger volumes. Systems have since spread worldwide, including in Mexico City, Istanbul, Guangzhou, and Johannesburg. Critics note that "BRT creep" — the dilution of dedicated infrastructure — can reduce systems to little more than rebranded bus routes.
