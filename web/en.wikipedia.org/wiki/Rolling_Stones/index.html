The Rolling Stones are an English rock band formed in London in 1962. Active across seven decades, they are one of the most popular and enduring bands of the rock era. Their early line-up consisted of vocalist Mick Jagger, guitarist Keith Richards, multi-instrumentalist Brian Jones, bassist Bill Wyman, and drummer Charlie Watts.

History

Rooted in blues and early rock and roll, the Stones first became known as the "bad boys" counter-image to the Beatles during the British Invasion. After early success with covers, the Jagger–Richards songwriting partnership produced classics such as "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", "Paint It Black", and "Sympathy for the Devil". Their run of albums from Beggars Banquet (1968) through Exile on Main St. (1972) is regarded as their creative peak. Later successes include Some Girls (1978), Tattoo You (1981), and Hackney Diamonds (2023), their first album of original material in 18 years.

Legacy

The Rolling Stones have sold an estimated 250 million records worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989. Their logo — the tongue and lips design created by John Pasche — is one of the most recognisable in popular culture. The band's stadium tours have repeatedly ranked among the highest-grossing concert tours of all time.
