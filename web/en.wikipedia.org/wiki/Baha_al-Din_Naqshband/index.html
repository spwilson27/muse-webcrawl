Baha al-Din Naqshband Bukhari (Persian: بهاءالدین محمد نقشبند بخاری; 1318–1389), also known as Khwaja Baha al-Din, was the eponymous founder of the Naqshbandi order of Sufism, one of the most widely spread Sufi orders in the world. He lived near Bukhara in present-day Uzbekistan.

Life

Born in the village of Qasr-i Hinduvan, later renamed Qasr-i Arifan, Baha al-Din was trained by Muhammad Baba al-Sammasi and Amir Kulal in the tradition of the Khwajagan masters of Central Asia. He is said to have received spiritual instruction from the long-dead Abdul Khaliq Ghijduwani, whose principles he systematised. He worked as an engraver of designs on cloth and metal — the origin of the name "Naqshband", meaning "pattern-maker" or "embroiderer".

Teachings

He emphasised silent remembrance of God (dhikr), earning a living by lawful work, sobriety, and adherence to the sharia and the sunna, summarised in the maxim "the heart with the Beloved, the hand at work". His mausoleum complex outside Bukhara remains one of Central Asia's most visited pilgrimage sites.
