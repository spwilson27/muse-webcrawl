The Lamborghini Gallardo is a sports car built by the Italian automotive manufacturer Lamborghini from 2003 to 2013. It is Lamborghini's second car released under parent company Audi, and at the time of its discontinuation it was the best-selling Lamborghini model ever, with 14,022 units built throughout its production run.

Overview

The Gallardo was named after a famous breed of Spanish fighting bull. Unlike the larger V12 flagship models, the Gallardo used a V10 engine and was intended as a more accessible entry point to the Lamborghini range, competing with cars such as the Ferrari 360/F430 and the Porsche 911 Turbo. It was offered with a six-speed manual transmission or an automated manual "E-gear" gearbox, and most versions featured all-wheel drive.

The first-generation Gallardo (2003–2008) used a 5.0-litre V10 producing around 500 PS (493 hp). A convertible Spyder version followed in 2005, and the lightweight Superleggera coupé in 2007.

Second generation (LP 560-4)

In 2008 the Gallardo was substantially updated as the LP 560-4, with a new 5.2-litre V10 producing 560 PS (552 hp), revised styling, and improved performance. Numerous special editions followed, including the rear-wheel-drive LP 550-2 Valentino Balboni, the LP 570-4 Superleggera, and the hardcore LP 570-4 Squadra Corse of 2013. The Gallardo also served in police service, with cars donated to the Italian State Police.

Replacement

Production ended on 25 November 2013. The Gallardo was replaced by the Lamborghini Huracán, unveiled at the 2014 Geneva Motor Show.
