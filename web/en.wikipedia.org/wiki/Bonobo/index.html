Bonobo

The bonobo (Pan paniscus), historically the ‘pygmy chimpanzee’, is an endangered great ape found only south of the Congo River in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Together with the Chimpanzee it is the closest living relative of humans.

Society

Bonobo society is female-led and strikingly peaceful by primate standards: conflicts are defused with grooming, food sharing and frequent sexual behaviour (including genital rubbing between females), which functions as social glue. Aggression and lethal violence — documented in chimpanzees — are rare to absent.

Bonobos are smaller and more gracile than chimpanzees, with longer legs, a black face with pink lips and a distinctive hair parting. They are accomplished tool users and, in captivity, the bonobo Kanzi learned to comprehend spoken English and use lexigram symbols.

Conservation

Bonobos are Endangered (see Endangered species): logging, poaching for bushmeat and civil conflict in their only homeland have fragmented a population estimated in the low tens of thousands.
