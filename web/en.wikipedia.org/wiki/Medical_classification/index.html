Medical classification

[Image: A classification arranges diseases and procedures into a coded hierarchy]
Standardised codes structure health data

A medical classification (or medical coding system) is a system that transforms descriptions of medical diagnoses, procedures and other health data into universal alphanumeric codes. Medical classifications form a core part of health informatics and are used for statistics, reimbursement, epidemiology, research and the management of health services.

Major systems

The best-known classification is the World Health Organization's International Classification of Diseases (ICD), whose eleventh revision (ICD-11) took effect in 2022; in the United States, a clinical modification, ICD-10-CM, is used for billing together with ICD-10-PCS and the Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes for procedures. Other major systems include the DSM-5 for mental disorders, SNOMED CT as a comprehensive clinical terminology, LOINC for laboratory and clinical observations, MeSH for indexing biomedical literature, and the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) system for drugs.

Purposes

Classifications allow mortality and morbidity statistics to be compared across places and times, underpin diagnosis-related group (DRG) payment systems, support clinical audit and quality measurement, and enable large-scale research in electronic health records. Because classifications impose a fixed hierarchy, they involve choices about how conditions are grouped and named — choices that can shape funding and the recognition of diseases — and mappings between systems (for example ICD-9 to ICD-10, or ICD to SNOMED CT) are a continuing practical challenge for health systems.
