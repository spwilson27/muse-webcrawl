The Knudsen number compares the mean free path λ with a characteristic length L

The Knudsen number (Kn) is a dimensionless number defined as the ratio of the molecular mean free path length λ to a representative physical length scale L: Kn = λ / L. It is named after the Danish physicist Martin Knudsen.

Interpretation

The Knudsen number indicates whether a gas flow should be treated as a continuum or by statistical mechanics. For Kn ≪ 1 the continuum hypothesis holds and the Navier–Stokes equations apply. As Kn grows, flow passes through the slip-flow regime (Kn roughly 0.01–0.1), the transitional regime (0.1–10), and finally free molecular flow (Kn > 10), where molecules collide with surfaces far more often than with each other and kinetic theory is required. High Knudsen numbers occur in rarefied gases — in the upper atmosphere, in vacuum systems, and in micro- and nano-scale channels.

Relations

The Knudsen number is related to the Mach number Ma and Reynolds number Re by Kn = (Ma / Re) · √(γπ/2), where γ is the heat capacity ratio. Martin Knudsen developed much of the underlying theory of rarefied gas flow while studying effusion, molecular flow through small apertures (Knudsen effusion), and the behaviour of gases at low pressure.
