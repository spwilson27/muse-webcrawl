The Randolph Caldecott Medal, frequently shortened to just the Caldecott, annually recognises the preceding year's "most distinguished American picture book for children". It is awarded to the illustrator by the Association for Library Service to Children (ALSC), a division of the American Library Association (ALA). The Caldecott and Newbery Medals are considered the most prestigious American children's book awards.

History

The award was established in 1937 by Frederic G. Melcher, who had earlier proposed the Newbery Medal, and was named for the 19th-century English illustrator Randolph Caldecott. The medal itself, designed by René Paul Chambellan, depicts one of Caldecott's illustrations for "The Diverting History of John Gilpin". The first medal was awarded in 1938 to Animals of the Bible, illustrated by Dorothy P. Lathrop.

Criteria and selection

Eligible illustrators must be citizens or residents of the United States, and the book must have been published in English in the United States during the preceding year. A committee of ALSC members selects one medal winner and, in most years, several runner-up "Honor Books".

Notable winners

Winners include Make Way for Ducklings (1942), The Snowy Day (1963), Where the Wild Things Are (1964), Jumanji (1982), The Polar Express (1986) and The Lion & the Mouse (2010). Marcia Brown, Leo and Diane Dillon, and Chris Van Allsburg are among the few illustrators to have won three times (Dillon counting consecutive wins in 1976 and 1977).
