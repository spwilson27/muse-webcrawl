Douglas–Rachford splitting

Douglas–Rachford splitting is an algorithm for solving convex optimisation problems by splitting them into simpler subproblems handled through proximity operators and reflected resolvents.

Method

Introduced by Jim Douglas and Henry Rachford in 1956 for heat-equation problems, it was generalised by Lions and Mercier. It relates closely to the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM).

Applications

The method is used in signal and image processing, phase retrieval and feasibility problems such as finding points in the intersection of convex sets.
