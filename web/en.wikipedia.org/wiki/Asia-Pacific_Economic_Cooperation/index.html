APEC
Founded: 1989
Headquarters: Singapore
Members: 21 "member economies"
Purpose: Regional economic integration

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) is an inter-governmental forum for 21 member economies in the Pacific Rim that promotes free trade throughout the Asia-Pacific region. It was established in 1989 in response to the growing interdependence of Asia-Pacific economies and the advent of regional trade blocs in other parts of the world.

Member economies

Members include China, Japan, South Korea, the United States, Canada, Australia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines, Mexico, Chile, Peru, Russia and others. Members are styled "economies" rather than countries, allowing participation by Hong Kong and Chinese Taipei alongside China.

Summits

APEC's annual Economic Leaders' Meeting draws heads of government and is famous for the "family photo" in which leaders wear attire reflecting the host economy's culture. Decisions are reached by consensus and commitments are non-binding.
