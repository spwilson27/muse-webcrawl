In photography, the 35 mm equivalent focal length is a measure that indicates the angle of view of a camera–lens combination by stating the focal length that would be required on a 35 mm (full-frame) camera to produce the same field of view. It allows lenses for different sensor sizes to be compared on a common scale.

Calculation

The equivalent focal length is found by multiplying a lens's actual focal length by the format's crop factor — the ratio of the diagonal of the 35 mm frame (≈43.3 mm) to the diagonal of the sensor in question. For example, a 50 mm lens on an APS-C camera with a 1.5× crop factor behaves like a 75 mm lens on full frame in terms of angle of view.

Usage

Manufacturers of compact and bridge cameras commonly quote 35 mm equivalent focal lengths (and zoom ranges) because their small sensors use very short actual focal lengths that would otherwise be unfamiliar. The concept applies to field of view only: depth of field and light-gathering also depend on the actual focal length and aperture, so "equivalence" discussions often extend to equivalent aperture as well.
