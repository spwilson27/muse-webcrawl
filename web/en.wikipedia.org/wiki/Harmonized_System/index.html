Harmonized System

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System, generally referred to as the Harmonized System or simply HS, is a multipurpose international product nomenclature developed and maintained by the World Customs Organization (WCO). It came into effect in 1988 and is used by more than 200 economies as the basis for their customs tariffs and international trade statistics.

Structure

The HS organizes goods into 21 sections and 99 chapters. Each product is assigned a six-digit code: the first two digits designate the chapter, the next two the heading, and the last two the subheading. Countries may add further digits for national purposes — for example, the United States uses ten-digit Schedule B and HTS codes. Over 5,000 product groups are covered, and classification is governed by the General Rules of Interpretation.

Revisions

The WCO updates the system roughly every five years (editions of 1992, 1996, 2002, 2007, 2012, 2017, 2022 and beyond) to reflect changes in technology and trade patterns. Correct HS classification determines duty rates, so classification disputes are a significant area of customs law.
