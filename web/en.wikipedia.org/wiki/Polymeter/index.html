Polymeter

Polymeter is a musical technique in which two or more different metres (time signatures) are used simultaneously — for example, one instrument playing in 3/4 while another plays in 4/4 against the same underlying pulse.

Distinction from polyrhythm

Polymeter differs from polyrhythm: in polyrhythm the beats themselves are subdivided differently but bar lengths coincide, whereas in polymeter the bar lengths differ, so the parts drift out of phase and realign only after a number of bars equal to the least common multiple of the two metres.

Usage

Polymeter appears in works by composers such as Charles Ives and Conlon Nancarrow, and is common in progressive rock and progressive metal, where bands such as Meshuggah layer phrases of different lengths over a steady 4/4 pulse.
