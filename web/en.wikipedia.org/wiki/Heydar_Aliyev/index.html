Heydar Aliyev (1923–2003) was an Azerbaijani politician who led Soviet Azerbaijan from 1969 to 1982 and served as President of the independent Republic of Azerbaijan from 1993 until his death in 2003. He was succeeded by his son Ilham Aliyev.

Career

Aliyev rose through the KGB, becoming head of the Azerbaijan SSR's security service and then First Secretary of the Communist Party of Azerbaijan. Recalled to Moscow in 1982 as a First Deputy Premier and Politburo member, he fell from favour under Gorbachev in 1987. Returning to Azerbaijan amid the collapse of the USSR and war over Nagorno-Karabakh, he took power in 1993, secured a ceasefire in 1994, and signed the "Contract of the Century" oil agreements that tied Azerbaijan's Caspian energy to Western markets.

Legacy

Supporters credit him with stabilising the state; critics point to authoritarian rule and a pervasive personality cult, visible in the many institutions bearing his name, including Baku's Heydar Aliyev International Airport and the Heydar Aliyev Center designed by Zaha Hadid.
