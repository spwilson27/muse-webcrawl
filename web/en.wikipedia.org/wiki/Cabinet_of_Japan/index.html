Cabinet of Japan

The Cabinet of Japan (Japanese: 内閣, Naikaku) is the chief executive body of the government of Japan. It is headed by the Prime Minister of Japan, who is appointed by the Emperor after being designated by the National Diet, and consists of the Ministers of State, most of whom head government ministries. Under the Constitution of Japan, executive power is vested in the Cabinet, which is collectively responsible to the Diet.

Composition

The Prime Minister appoints the Ministers of State, a majority of whom must be members of the Diet, and may dismiss them at will. The number of ministers is limited by the Cabinet Law. The Chief Cabinet Secretary is a key minister who acts as the government's spokesperson and coordinates policy. Other offices include the Minister for Foreign Affairs, Minister of Finance, Minister of Defense, and Minister of Justice.

History

The modern cabinet system was established in 1885 under Prime Minister Itō Hirobumi during the Meiji period, modelled in part on European systems. The 1889 Meiji Constitution made the cabinet responsible to the Emperor; the postwar 1947 constitution subordinated it to the Diet and established parliamentary cabinet government.

Functions

The Cabinet conducts affairs of state, manages foreign affairs, concludes treaties subject to Diet approval, administers the civil service, prepares the budget, and issues cabinet orders to execute the constitution and laws. In practice the Cabinet Secretariat and Cabinet Office support policy coordination, and the Prime Minister's official residence is the Kantei in Nagatachō, Tokyo.
