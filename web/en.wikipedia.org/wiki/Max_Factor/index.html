Max Factor is a cosmetics line founded in 1909 by Maksymilian Faktorowicz (1877–1938), a Polish-born cosmetician from Congress Poland, known professionally as Max Factor Sr. Beginning as the makeup supplier to early Hollywood, the company became one of the most influential beauty brands of the 20th century and is credited with popularising the word "makeup" itself.

History

Faktorowicz emigrated to the United States in 1904 and settled in Los Angeles in 1908, selling cosmetics to film actors. Stage greasepaint looked harsh on film, so in 1914 he introduced a flexible greasepaint in cream form in a range of tints, the first makeup made specifically for motion pictures. His innovations included "Colour Harmony" makeup matched to hair and complexion, Pan-Cake foundation (1937), lip gloss, waterproof makeup, and the Beauty Micrometer device used to measure facial proportions.

Max Factor products were used by stars such as Jean Harlow, Clara Bow, Bette Davis, and Joan Crawford, and the company received an honorary Academy Award in 1929 for its contribution to film. After Factor's death in 1938 the firm was led by his son Frank, who renamed himself Max Factor Jr.

Later ownership

The company was sold to Norton Simon in 1973, passed through Playtex and Revlon, and was acquired by Procter & Gamble in 1991. It is now owned by Coty, which relaunched the brand in various markets after a period in which it had been withdrawn from the United States.
