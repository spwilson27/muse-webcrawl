Round Tower (Copenhagen)

FunctionAstronomical observatory, church tower, exhibition space

The Round Tower (Danish: Rundetårn) is a 17th-century tower in central Copenhagen, Denmark, built as an astronomical observatory for the University of Copenhagen. It is part of the Trinitatis Complex, which also comprises a student church and university library, and was constructed on the initiative of King Christian IV, with Hans van Steenwinckel the Younger among its designers.

Architecture

Instead of stairs, a 209-metre spiral ramp — a helix corridor winding 7.5 times around the hollow core — leads to the viewing platform at the top, allowing instruments (and, legend says, the king on horseback) to be carried up easily. Peter the Great reportedly rode up in 1716 and his wife followed in a carriage. The platform offers panoramic views of old Copenhagen; the observatory at the top is Europe's oldest functioning observatory. A rebus inscription by Christian IV decorates the facade.

Today

The former library hall above the church now hosts exhibitions and concerts, and the tower is one of Denmark's best-known landmarks and most visited attractions. An annual unicycle race up the ramp is held in the tower.
