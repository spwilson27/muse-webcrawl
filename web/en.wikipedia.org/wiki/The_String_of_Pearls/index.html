The String of Pearls: A Romance is a penny dreadful serial published in Edward Lloyd's The People's Periodical between November 1846 and March 1847. It is the first literary appearance of Sweeney Todd, the "Demon Barber of Fleet Street," and of his accomplice Mrs. Lovett, whose pie shop disposes of the barber's victims.

Plot

Lieutenant Thornhill, returning from the South Seas bearing a valuable string of pearls for his friend's beloved, Johanna Oakley, visits Todd's barber shop in Fleet Street to be shaved — and is never seen again. Johanna, disguised as a boy, takes work in the shop to investigate. Todd murders his customers by pulling a lever that drops them through a trapdoor into the cellar, then robs and slits the throats of any who survive the fall. The bodies are delivered to Mrs. Lovett's pie shop in Bell Yard and baked into pies. The villains are eventually exposed and Todd is hanged.

Authorship and legacy

The serial was published anonymously and has been attributed to Thomas Peckett Prest, James Malcolm Rymer, or both. An expanded edition appeared in 1850. The story was quickly adapted for the stage (1847), and Todd became a durable figure of urban legend. The tale was later adapted by Christopher Bond (1970) and by Stephen Sondheim and Hugh Wheeler as the musical Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (1979), filmed by Tim Burton in 2007.
