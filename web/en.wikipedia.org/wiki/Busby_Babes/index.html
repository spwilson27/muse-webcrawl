Busby Babes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Busby Babes were the group of footballers recruited and trained by Manchester United under manager Matt Busby and his assistant Jimmy Murphy, who progressed from the club's youth team into the first team in the 1950s. Notable for youthful brilliance, the team won consecutive league championships in 1956 and 1957 with an average age of only 21 and 22 respectively.

Munich air disaster

On 6 February 1958, returning from a European Cup match in Belgrade, the team's aeroplane crashed on its third attempt to take off from a slush-covered runway at Munich-Riem Airport. Eight players — Geoff Bent, Roger Byrne, Eddie Colman, Duncan Edwards, Mark Jones, David Pegg, Tommy Taylor, and Liam Whelan — were among the 23 people killed. Edwards, considered one of England's finest players, died of his injuries 15 days later.

Legacy

Survivors including Bobby Charlton and Bill Foulkes helped a rebuilt United side, and Busby's reconstructed team won the European Cup a decade later in 1968. The Busby Babes remain an enduring symbol in Manchester United's history, commemorated by the Munich clock and memorial at Old Trafford.
