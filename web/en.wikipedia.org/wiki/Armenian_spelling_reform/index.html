Armenian spelling reform

The Armenian spelling reform was a change to the orthography of the Armenian language introduced in Soviet Armenia in the 1920s. The reform simplified several spelling conventions of Classical (Mesropian) orthography and remains the standard spelling in the Republic of Armenia today, while most of the Armenian diaspora continues to use the traditional orthography.

Background

Discussions of simplifying Armenian spelling began in the 19th century. After the establishment of Soviet rule in Armenia, a commission associated with the linguist Manuk Abeghian prepared a reform that was officially adopted in 1922, with further adjustments in 1940 that restored a few traditional elements.

Main changes

The reform abolished several letters' positional uses, replaced the digraph for the vowel /u/ with a single ligature letter, simplified the spelling of diphthongs, and removed the silent letter conventions inherited from classical spelling. Word-initial and word-internal spellings were made more phonetic.

Reception

Within Armenia the reformed orthography became universal in education and publishing. Diaspora communities, particularly in Lebanon, Syria, Iran and the West, largely retained the classical orthography, and the divergence is sometimes cited as a symbolic divide between the homeland and the diaspora.
