A third mate (3/M) or third officer is a licensed member of the deck department of a merchant ship. The third mate is a watchstander and customarily the ship's safety officer, ranking below the chief mate (first mate) and second mate.

Duties

The third mate typically stands the 8–12 and 20–24 watches as officer of the watch (OOW) on the bridge, responsible for the safe navigation of the vessel during those periods. As safety officer, the third mate is responsible for the inspection and maintenance of life-saving and firefighting equipment, including lifeboats, life rafts, fire extinguishers, and alarms. In port, the third mate assists with cargo operations and typically compiles documentation. In an emergency, the third mate may be stationed on the bridge or supervise a fire party or lifeboat party, according to the ship's muster list.

Licensing

Third mates hold an internationally recognized certificate of competency issued under the STCW Convention, obtained after completing an approved maritime academy program or sea-time requirements and passing examinations in navigation, seamanship, cargo handling, and safety. In the United States, the license is issued by the U.S. Coast Guard after graduation from a federal or state maritime academy or equivalent sea service plus examinations.

Role in other fleets

Equivalent junior deck officer ranks exist in naval services and on passenger ships, where third officers may also assist with passenger safety duties and tender operations. On some vessels the position is styled "junior officer" or combined with second officer watch duties.
