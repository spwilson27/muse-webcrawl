DNA mismatch repair

DNA mismatch repair (MMR) is a highly conserved system for correcting base–base mismatches and small insertion–deletion loops that arise during DNA replication and recombination.

Mechanism

In eukaryotes, MutS homolog complexes (MSH2–MSH6 or MSH2–MSH3) recognise mismatches, recruit MutL homolog complexes (principally MLH1–PMS2), and coordinate excision of the error-containing strand followed by resynthesis. In E. coli, strand discrimination uses the transient under-methylation of the new strand.

Clinical significance

Inherited defects in MMR genes cause Lynch syndrome, a predisposition to colorectal, endometrial and other cancers; MMR-deficient tumours show microsatellite instability and respond notably well to immune checkpoint therapy.
