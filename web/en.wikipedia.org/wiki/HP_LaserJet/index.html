HP LaserJet

LaserJet is a line of laser printers sold by HP Inc. (formerly Hewlett-Packard). The first LaserJet, introduced in May 1984, was the world's first desktop laser printer and became one of the most successful products in the history of personal computing, helping to launch the desktop publishing revolution alongside the Apple LaserWriter and Adobe PostScript.

History

The original LaserJet printed at 300 dots per inch, produced eight pages per minute, and sold for about US$3,495. It was built around a Canon print engine and used HP's Printer Command Language (PCL). The quieter, smaller LaserJet II of 1987 further popularised laser printing in offices. Over subsequent decades HP released hundreds of models, from small personal monochrome printers to high-volume colour multifunction devices for enterprise workgroups.

Technology

LaserJet printers use electrophotography: a laser beam (or, in some models, an array of LEDs) draws an electrostatic image on a rotating photoconductor drum, which attracts toner particles that are transferred to paper and fused by heat and pressure. Most models have used Canon engines, and HP toner cartridges traditionally integrate the drum and toner supply in one replaceable unit.

Printer languages and connectivity

LaserJets are known for PCL, HP's page description language, and many models also support PostScript emulation. Connectivity evolved from parallel and serial ports to USB, Ethernet, and wireless networking; management features such as the embedded JetDirect print server became standards in office printing.
