Italian Renaissance sculpture

Italian Renaissance sculpture was a central art form of the Italian Renaissance, flourishing roughly between the early 15th and late 16th centuries. Drawing on renewed study of classical antiquity, sculptors revived free-standing nude figures, naturalistic anatomy, and one-point perspective in relief.

Early Renaissance

The period is often dated from the 1401 competition for the bronze doors of the Florence Baptistery, won by Lorenzo Ghiberti over Filippo Brunelleschi. Donatello, the dominant sculptor of the quattrocento, produced the marble and bronze Davids, the St George, and the equestrian monument to Gattamelata in Padua.

High Renaissance and after

Michelangelo brought the art to its peak with the Pietà (1498–99), the colossal marble David (1501–04), and the Medici tombs. Later in the 16th century, Mannerist sculptors such as Benvenuto Cellini and Giambologna favoured elongated, serpentine figures designed to be viewed from multiple angles.
