Peig Sayers (1873 – 8 December 1958) was an Irish seanchaí (traditional storyteller) and memoirist from the Dingle Peninsula in County Kerry. Her dictated autobiography Peig, published in 1936, became one of the best-known Irish-language books of the 20th century and for decades was a set text for Irish school examinations — a status that made her name both famous and, among generations of students, notorious.

Life

Born Margaret Brosnan in Ballymore near Dunquin, she went into domestic service as a young girl. She married Pádraig Sayers of the Great Blasket Island and moved there, living in the small Irish-speaking island community. Of her children, several died young or emigrated; her son Tomás fell to his death from the island's cliffs. After the island's evacuation she returned to the mainland and spent her final years in Dingle hospital.

Works and legacy

Peig could not write Irish herself; her stories and memoirs were dictated to her son Mícheál and to visiting scholars. Folklorist Seán Ó Súilleabháin recorded hundreds of her folktales for the Irish Folklore Commission, and the Norwegian scholar Carl Marstrander and English scholar Robin Flower also visited her. Peig recounts the hardships of island life with stoicism, and generations of schoolchildren memorised its opening reflection that she was an old woman now, with one foot in the grave and the other on its edge. A second volume, Machnamh Seanmhná ("An Old Woman's Reflections"), appeared after her death. The book was removed as a compulsory school text in the 1990s, though scholarly assessment of Sayers as a major oral storyteller has grown. See also Great Blasket Island.
