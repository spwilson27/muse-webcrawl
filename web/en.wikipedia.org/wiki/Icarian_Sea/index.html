Icarian Sea

The Icarian Sea (Greek: Ικάριο Πέλαγος) is a subdivision of the Aegean Sea in the eastern Mediterranean, lying between the Cyclades and the coast of Asia Minor (Turkey). It is named after the island of Icaria and, in Greek mythology, after Icarus, who plunged into these waters after flying too close to the sun on wings of wax and feathers.

Looking across the Icarian Sea from the island that gives it its name.

Geography

The Icarian Sea is bordered by the islands of Icaria, Samos, and Patmos to the north and east, the Dodecanese including Kos to the south, and the Cyclades (Mykonos, Naxos) to the west. The Turkish mainland lies to the east. It is one of the windiest parts of the Aegean in summer, swept by the Etesian (meltemi) winds, and its deep waters form part of the Hellenic arc region of the Aegean.

Mythology and history

According to the myth told by Apollodorus and Ovid, Daedalus fashioned wings for himself and his son Icarus to escape Crete; Icarus ignored his father's warning, the sun melted the wax, and he fell into the sea that took his name. In historical times these waters carried trade between Ionia and the Aegean islands and saw naval operations from the Persian Wars to the Second World War.
