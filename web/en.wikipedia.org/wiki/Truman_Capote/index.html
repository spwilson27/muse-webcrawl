Truman Garcia Capote (born Truman Streckfus Persons; September 30, 1924 – August 25, 1984) was an American novelist, screenwriter, playwright and actor. Several of his short stories, novels and plays have been praised as literary classics, and his work — including the novella Breakfast at Tiffany's (1958) and the true crime book In Cold Blood (1966) — has been adapted into more than twenty films and television dramas.

Early life

Capote was born in New Orleans and spent much of his childhood in Monroeville, Alabama, where he was a neighbour and friend of the young Harper Lee, who later modelled the character Dill in To Kill a Mockingbird on him. He moved to New York City as a teenager and worked at The New Yorker while writing short stories that quickly won critical attention.

Career

His first novel, Other Voices, Other Rooms (1948), made him famous at twenty-three. He followed it with the story collection A Tree of Night, the novella The Grass Harp, the musical House of Flowers, and Breakfast at Tiffany's, whose heroine Holly Golightly became one of American literature's most famous characters and was immortalised on screen by Audrey Hepburn in 1961.

After reading of the 1959 murders of the Clutter family in Holcomb, Kansas, Capote spent six years researching the crime with Harper Lee's help. The result, In Cold Blood (1966), was a pioneering work of the "nonfiction novel" and an enormous bestseller, though Capote's closeness to the killers — he attended their executions — left a lasting mark on him.

Later life

A socialite as celebrated for his wit and his voice as for his writing, Capote held the famous Black and White Ball in 1966. His unfinished novel Answered Prayers, excerpts of which appeared in the 1970s, scandalised the high society it portrayed and effectively ended many of his friendships. Increasingly troubled by alcohol and drug dependence, he died in Los Angeles in 1984.
