Koryolink

Koryolink (Chosongul: 고려링크) was a mobile telecommunications provider in North Korea. It was established in 2008 as a joint venture between the Egyptian company Orascom Investment Holding (formerly Orascom Telecom Holding) and the state-owned Korea Post and Telecommunications Corporation, with Orascom holding a 75% stake and the North Korean partner 25%.

History

Koryolink received a licence in January 2008 to operate a WCDMA 3G network in North Korea. The service launched on 15 December 2008 in Pyongyang, initially targeting elite users, and expanded coverage to additional cities and transport corridors in subsequent years. Subscriber numbers grew rapidly; the company reported passing one million subscribers in 2012 and around three million by 2015.

Services and restrictions

Koryolink's 3G network was unusual in that it operated two largely segregated user groups: North Korean citizens, who could call each other but could not access international calls or the global Internet, and foreign residents and visitors, who could make international calls but were barred from calling domestic subscribers. Mobile internet for foreigners, offered briefly from early 2013, was suspended shortly after launch. Domestic subscribers had access only to North Korea's closed Kwangmyong intranet.

Competition and status

A second state-owned network, Kang Song Net, emerged as a competitor around 2015, and Orascom's effective control over Koryolink's operations and profits was curtailed by international sanctions and North Korean government measures. The company became a point of contention over repatriation of earnings and Orascom later consolidated the operation outside its reporting amid the deterioration of its position.
