Metagame

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A metagame (from the Greek prefix meta-, "beyond") is a set of strategies, knowledge, and behaviour that arises from interactions and information outside the formal rules of a game, or an approach to a game that transcends or operates outside the prescribed rules. The term and its shortening "the meta" are used in several senses in gaming, game theory, and role-playing.

In competitive gaming

In competitive video games, collectible card games, and tabletop wargaming, the metagame is the prevailing set of strategies, character/class/deck choices, and tactics considered strongest or most common in a given period and competitive environment. Players analyse tournament results and patches to predict opponents' likely choices and pick "meta" options or deliberate counters. Because popular strategies invite counter-strategies, the meta continually evolves — a dynamic sometimes compared to rock-paper-scissors. Balance patches from developers directly reshape the meta, and in games such as League of Legends, Magic: The Gathering, and StarCraft, "the meta" is a routine subject of community analysis.

In role-playing games

In tabletop role-playing games, metagaming usually has a negative sense: a player using knowledge their character could not have — for example, acting on monster statistics the player read in a manual — to gain an advantage. Game masters commonly discourage or prohibit it as breaking immersion and fairness.

In game theory

In Nigel Howard's metagame analysis (developed from the 1960s), the options available to players depend on the strategies they expect others to adopt, and a "metarational" analysis is used to study conflicts and cooperation beyond classical game-theoretic equilibrium assumptions. The term has also been used for games about games, and in business for competitive dynamics beyond a single market interaction.
