Bologna massacre

Date: 2 August 1980, 10:25 CEST
Location: Bologna Centrale railway station, Italy
Deaths: 85
Injured: over 200
Perpetrators: members of the Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari (convicted)

The Bologna massacre (Italian: strage di Bologna) was a terrorist bombing of the Bologna Centrale railway station in Bologna, Italy, on the morning of 2 August 1980. The explosion killed 85 people and wounded more than 200. It was the deadliest terrorist attack in modern Italian history and one of the worst atrocities of Italy's "Years of Lead".

The attack

At 10:25 local time an improvised explosive device, hidden in a suitcase left in the station's air-conditioned second-class waiting room, detonated. The blast, caused by some 23 kilograms of explosive (a mixture of TNT and T4), demolished the waiting room and part of the station roof, and struck a train waiting at the first platform. The station clock, stopped at 10:25, became the enduring symbol of the attack; it is preserved in the rebuilt station.

Investigations and trials

After a long and controversial investigation, the Italian courts convicted Valerio Fioravanti and Francesca Mambro, leading members of the neo-fascist group Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari (NAR), as the material perpetrators; both maintained their innocence. Luigi Ciavardini was later also convicted. Two officers of the Italian military intelligence service SISMI and Licio Gelli, head of the clandestine Propaganda Due (P2) masonic lodge, were convicted of conspiring to obstruct the investigation. In 2020, Paolo Bellini was convicted of complicity in the massacre, and investigations into the attack's financiers and instigators have continued into the 2020s.

Context and legacy

The bombing is generally placed within the strategia della tensione ("strategy of tension") — a series of attacks intended to destabilise Italian democracy and provoke authoritarian responses. The city of Bologna commemorates the victims every 2 August, and the Association of the Families of the Victims has campaigned for decades for full disclosure of state archives relating to the massacre.
