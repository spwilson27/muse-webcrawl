Diego Armando Maradona (30 October 1960 – 25 November 2020) was an Argentine professional football player and manager. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players in the history of the sport, he shared the FIFA Player of the 20th Century award with Pelé. A playmaker of supreme dribbling, vision and balance at just 5 ft 5 in, Maradona was the first player to set the world transfer record twice.

His career peaked at the 1986 FIFA World Cup in Mexico, where he captained Argentina to victory and produced — in one quarter-final against England — both the infamous "Hand of God" goal and, four minutes later, the "Goal of the Century", a 60-yard solo dribble past five players. At club level he led unfashionable Napoli to its first two Serie A titles (1987, 1990) and the 1989 UEFA Cup, becoming a folk hero in Naples. His career was marred by cocaine addiction: he was banned for 15 months in 1991 and sent home from the 1994 World Cup after failing a drug test.

Legacy

Maradona later managed Argentina at the 2010 World Cup and worked in the UAE and Mexico. His death in 2020 prompted three days of national mourning in Argentina; Napoli renamed their stadium the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona. The debate over Maradona versus Pelé — and latterly Lionel Messi — over the title of greatest ever remains a fixture of the sport. Association football history has few larger figures.
