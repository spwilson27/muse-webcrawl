Ecocriticism

Ecocriticism is the study of literature and other cultural artefacts from an environmental perspective — the interdisciplinary investigation of the relationship between literature and the physical environment. The term was coined by William Rueckert in his 1978 essay "Literature and Ecology: An Experiment in Ecocriticism", and the field was consolidated in the 1990s with the founding of the Association for the Study of Literature and Environment (ASLE) in 1992 and Cheryll Glotfelty and Harold Fromm's anthology The Ecocriticism Reader (1996).

Scope and development

Early ecocriticism focused on nature writing, Romantic poetry and the literature of wilderness, celebrating texts that represented the non-human world with fidelity. Later "waves" of the field broadened its concerns: to environmental justice and the environments of poor and marginalised communities; to urban and built environments; to ecofeminism, postcolonial ecologies and Indigenous literatures; and to global questions of climate change, extinction and the Anthropocene. Ecocritics analyse how cultural representations shape attitudes toward nature, and how concepts such as wilderness, pollution and sustainability are constructed in texts.

Related fields

Ecocriticism overlaps with environmental history, environmental philosophy and the broader environmental humanities. Critics have debated whether ecocriticism should be primarily celebratory, scholarly or activist in orientation; many practitioners understand it as scholarship with an explicit commitment to environmental awareness and action.
