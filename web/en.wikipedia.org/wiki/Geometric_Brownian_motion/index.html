Geometric Brownian motion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A geometric Brownian motion (GBM), also known as exponential Brownian motion, is a continuous-time stochastic process in which the logarithm of the randomly varying quantity follows a Brownian motion (also called a Wiener process) with drift. It is an important example of a stochastic process satisfying a stochastic differential equation (SDE); in particular, it is used in mathematical finance as the standard model of stock price behavior, most famously in the Black–Scholes option pricing model.

Definition

A stochastic process St is said to follow a GBM if it satisfies the stochastic differential equation

dSt = μSt dt + σSt dWt,

where Wt is a Wiener process, μ is the drift (the percentage trend), and σ is the volatility (the percentage standard deviation). Solving the equation with Itô's lemma gives

St = S0 exp[(μ − σ²/2)t + σWt],

so that at any time t the value is log-normally distributed, and the expected value is S0eμt.

Properties and use

Because the increments are proportional to the current value, GBM paths remain positive if they start positive, making the process suitable for quantities that cannot go negative, such as prices and population sizes. The process is the continuous-time analogue of multiplicative random growth. In finance it underlies the Black–Scholes–Merton model and Monte Carlo option valuation. Its known defects as a price model — constant volatility, continuous paths without jumps, and thin-tailed returns — have motivated extensions such as stochastic volatility and jump-diffusion models.
