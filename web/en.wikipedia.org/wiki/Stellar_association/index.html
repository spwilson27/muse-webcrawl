Stellar association

A stellar association is a very loose star cluster, looser than both open clusters and globular clusters. Stellar associations will normally contain from 10 to 100 or more visible stars. An association is primarily identified by the common movement vectors and ages of its stars, and also by common chemical compositions.

Types

Stellar associations are principally categorised by the types of stars they contain: OB associations consist of young, massive blue stars and are found in spiral arms where star formation is recent; R associations contain young stars surrounded by reflection nebulae; and T associations contain low-mass T Tauri stars still contracting toward the main sequence. Moving groups are older, dispersed associations identified mainly by kinematics.

Formation and fate

Associations form from a single giant molecular cloud but are not gravitationally bound, so they disperse into the galactic field within about 10 to 100 million years as the residual gas is expelled and galactic tides act. The Scorpius–Centaurus association is the nearest OB association to the Sun.
