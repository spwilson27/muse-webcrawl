Wamsutta

Wamsutta (c. 1634 – 1662), also known by the English name Alexander, was a sachem (leader) of the Wampanoag people of southeastern Massachusetts. He was the eldest son of Massasoit (Ousamequin), who had maintained peace with the Plymouth colonists, and the elder brother of Metacomet, known to the English as King Philip.

Life

On his father's death in 1661, Wamsutta succeeded him as sachem, in accordance with the treaty arrangements under which the Wampanoag leaders took English names—Wamsutta becoming Alexander, and his brother Metacomet becoming Philip. Like his father he sought to preserve the alliance with Plymouth, travelling to the colony to renew agreements and to trade.

In 1662 Wamsutta was summoned to Plymouth to answer charges that he was plotting with the Narragansett. He was seized at gunpoint and taken to the home of Josiah Winslow, where he fell ill—accounts differ over whether from disease, stress, or poison—and died on the journey home. His death, widely viewed by the Wampanoag as a murder, deepened distrust between the Wampanoag and the colonists and set the stage for the tensions that culminated in King Philip's War (1675–1678) under his brother.
