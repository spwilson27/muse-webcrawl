Serial killer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Serial murder
DefinitionA person who murders three or more people, with separate events separated by a "cooling-off" period
Term coinedPopularized in the 1970s, associated with FBI profiler Robert Ressler

A serial killer (also called a serial murderer) is a person who commits a series of murders, typically three or more, over a period of time, with a significant break — a "cooling-off period" — between them. The murders are often committed in a similar way, and the victims may have something in common. Serial killing is distinguished from mass murder, in which multiple victims are killed in a single event, and from spree killing, in which murders occur at multiple locations within a short period without a cooling-off interval.

Characteristics

Most identified serial killers are male. Motives identified by criminologists include sexual gratification, thrill-seeking, financial gain, anger, and attention-seeking. The FBI and other researchers classify serial killers as "organized" (planning their crimes and concealing evidence) or "disorganized" (acting impulsively), though later research regards this typology as an oversimplification. Many serial killers show histories of childhood abuse or neglect, but researchers caution that such traits are common and not predictive.

History

Although the term is modern, cases now described as serial murder predate it, including the unidentified "Jack the Ripper," who murdered at least five women in London in 1888. The phenomenon became a major focus of law-enforcement profiling in the United States in the 1970s and 1980s, and serial killers occupy a prominent, often sensationalized place in popular culture.
