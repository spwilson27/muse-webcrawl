Hilbert modular form

In mathematics, a Hilbert modular form is a generalization of a classical modular form to functions of several complex variables, one for each embedding of a totally real number field into the real numbers. They are named after David Hilbert, who first considered the idea, though the theory was developed substantially by Erich Hecke and others in the mid-20th century.

Definition

Let F be a totally real field of degree n with ring of integers O. A Hilbert modular form of weight k = (k1, ..., kn) is a holomorphic function on the product of n upper half-planes that transforms under the action of GL(2, O) by a prescribed automorphy factor and satisfies growth conditions at the cusps. The classical theory corresponds to F = the rational numbers (n = 1).

Significance

Hilbert modular forms are central examples in the Langlands program: their associated L-functions encode arithmetic information about F, and Hilbert modular varieties provide rich examples in arithmetic geometry.
