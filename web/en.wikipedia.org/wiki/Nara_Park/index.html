Nara Park (Japanese: 奈良公園, Nara Kōen) is a large public park in the city of Nara, Japan, at the foot of Mount Wakakusa. Established in 1880, it is one of Japan's oldest parks and encompasses about 502 hectares (or roughly 660 hectares including the grounds of the temples and shrines within it). The park includes the sites of Tōdai-ji, Kōfuku-ji and Kasuga Grand Shrine, together forming part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara".

Deer

The park is famous for its population of around 1,200 wild sika deer (Cervus nippon) that roam freely. According to Shinto legend, the deity Takemikazuchi arrived in Nara riding a white deer, and the deer of Kasuga Shrine were thereafter treated as sacred messengers of the gods. For centuries killing a deer was a capital offence; today the deer are designated a national natural treasure. Vendors sell shika senbei (deer crackers) for visitors to feed them, and the deer are well known for bowing to visitors in apparent imitation of the Japanese bow.

Features

Within or around the park are the Nara National Museum, the Great Buddha Hall of Tōdai-ji with its giant bronze Buddha, the five-storey pagoda of Kōfuku-ji, the lantern-lined paths of Kasuga Shrine, Sarusawa Pond and Mount Wakakusa, where the Wakakusa Yamayaki grass-burning festival is held each January.
