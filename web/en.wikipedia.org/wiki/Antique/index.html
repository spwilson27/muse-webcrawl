Antique

An antique (from Latin antiquus, "old" or "ancient") is an old collectable item. It is collected or desirable because of its age, beauty, rarity, condition, utility, personal emotional connection, and/or other unique features. It is an object that represents a previous era or time period in human history. Antiques are usually objects that show some degree of craftsmanship, or a certain attention to design. They can be bought at antique shops, estate sales, auction houses, or online, and are passed down through families as heirlooms.

Definition

The common legal definition of an antique is an object that is at least 100 years old. The United States customs authorities, for example, historically exempted from duty articles produced before 1830 and later adopted the 100-year rule. Younger collectables are often described as "vintage" or simply "collectable". In China, antiques have traditionally meant artefacts of the Ming dynasty or earlier periods sought by connoisseurs.

History of collecting

Collecting old objects is ancient: Roman collectors prized Greek sculpture, and Renaissance scholars assembled cabinets of curiosities mixing antiquities with natural specimens. The modern antiques trade developed in the 18th and 19th centuries alongside the Grand Tour, country-house sales, and the growth of museums. In the 20th century, television programmes such as the BBC's Antiques Roadshow (1979–present) popularised valuation and collecting for a mass audience.

Categories

Furniture

Antique furniture is graded by period and style — for example, Georgian, Regency, and Victorian in Britain, or federal and colonial styles in America — and value depends on originality of finish and hardware, provenance, and rarity of the maker.

Other fields

Major collecting areas include ceramics and glass, silver and jewellery, clocks and watches, books and maps, scientific instruments, rugs and textiles, and antique vehicles. Antique automobiles are generally defined by bodies such as the Antique Automobile Club of America as vehicles over 25 years old, a separate convention from the 100-year rule.

Trade and authentication

Because age and authenticity drive value, the trade relies on provenance research, expert appraisal, and scientific techniques such as dendrochronology for wood, thermoluminescence for ceramics, and X-ray analysis of paintings. Fakes and heavily restored pieces are a persistent problem, and reputable dealers typically offer guarantees of authenticity.
