The UK research councils are publicly funded agencies responsible for investing in science and research in the United Kingdom. Research Councils UK (RCUK), formed in 2002, was the partnership organisation that brought the councils together to provide a collective voice and coordinate common policies. In April 2018, RCUK and the seven councils became part of the new umbrella body UK Research and Innovation (UKRI).

The seven councils

  • Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC)
  • Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council (BBSRC)
  • Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council (EPSRC)
  • Economic and Social Research Council (ESRC)
  • Medical Research Council (MRC)
  • Natural Environment Research Council (NERC)
  • Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC)

Role and funding

Together the councils invested well over £3 billion a year in research grants, studentships, fellowships, institutes, and major facilities such as research ships, synchrotrons, and laboratories. Funding is allocated competitively, principally through peer review, under the "dual support" system alongside block funding from the UK's higher education funding bodies. The councils also supported international partnerships, including a RCUK office network in the United States, China, India, and Brussels.

History

The research council system traces its origins to the Medical Research Committee of 1913 and the Haldane principle of 1918, which holds that decisions on research funding should be made by researchers rather than politicians. The number and remit of councils changed repeatedly over the twentieth century before the seven-council structure was settled in the 1990s and 2000s.
