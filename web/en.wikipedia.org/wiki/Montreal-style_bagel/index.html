Montreal-style bagel

The Montreal-style bagel or Montreal bagel is a distinctive variety of handmade and wood-fired bagel. In contrast to the New York-style bagel, the Montreal bagel is smaller, thinner, sweeter, and denser, with a larger hole, and is always baked in a wood-fired oven.

Method

Montreal bagels are made with malt, egg, and no salt, and are boiled in honey-sweetened water before being baked in a wood-fired oven. The dough is hand-rolled into long strips and joined into rings. The two most common varieties are the sesame bagel (called "all dressed" in some shops' vocabulary does not apply here; the standard distinction is) — in practice the vast majority are topped either with sesame seeds or poppy seeds, unlike the wide range of toppings common in New York.

History

The Montreal bagel was brought to North America by Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe in the early 20th century, and the style developed in Montreal's Mile End neighbourhood. The city's two best-known bakeries, St-Viateur Bagel (founded 1957) and Fairmount Bagel (founded 1919 as the Montreal Bagel Bakery), both bake continuously in open shops where customers can watch the process. In 2008, Montreal bagels even travelled to space aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery with astronaut Greg Chamitoff.

Comparison with New York bagels

The Montreal bagel is generally smaller and chewier, with a crisp, slightly caramelised crust from the honey water and wood fire, while the New York bagel is larger, softer, saltier, and doughier, typically baked in a conventional oven. The rivalry between the two styles is a well-known culinary debate.
