Barrow-wight

Barrow-wights are undead creatures in the writings of J. R. R. Tolkien, haunting the burial mounds of the Barrow-downs in Middle-earth. The word "wight" derives from Old English wiht, meaning a creature or being.

In The Lord of the Rings

In The Fellowship of the Ring, Frodo Baggins and his companions are captured by a barrow-wight after becoming lost in the fog on the Barrow-downs. The wight prepares them for a ritual sacrifice, dressing them in white garments with gold circlets and laying a sword across their necks. Frodo resists the spell and calls for help; Tom Bombadil arrives, sings a song that banishes the wight, and distributes ancient swords from the barrow's treasure to the hobbits.

Origins

Tolkien drew on Norse and Old English folklore — the mound-dwelling draugar of Icelandic saga, such as Glámr in Grettis saga — and on his own philological interests. In Tolkien's legendarium, the wights were evil spirits sent by the Witch-king of Angmar to inhabit the bones of the Dúnedain buried in the mounds of Cardolan.
