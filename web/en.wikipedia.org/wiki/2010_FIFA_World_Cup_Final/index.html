The 2010 FIFA World Cup Final was the final match of the 2010 World Cup, the nineteenth edition of FIFA's competition for national football teams. It was played at Soccer City in Johannesburg, South Africa, on 11 July 2010 between the Netherlands and Spain — neither of whom had previously won the tournament, guaranteeing a first-time champion and the first European winner outside Europe. Spain won 1–0 after extra time through a goal by Andrés Iniesta.

Route to the final

Spain, reigning European champions, recovered from a shock opening defeat by Switzerland to top their group and then ground out successive 1–0 knockout wins over Portugal, Paraguay and Germany, with David Villa scoring five goals in the tournament. The Netherlands won all six of their matches, upsetting Brazil 2–1 in the quarter-finals and beating Uruguay 3–2 in the semi-final, with Wesley Sneijder and Arjen Robben their outstanding players.

Match

The final was a fractious, physical contest. Referee Howard Webb showed a World Cup final record 14 yellow cards, sending off Dutch defender John Heitinga in extra time, and was criticised for not dismissing Nigel de Jong for a chest-high challenge on Xabi Alonso. Robben missed two one-on-one chances against Iker Casillas; late in the second half of extra time, Iniesta latched onto a Cesc Fàbregas pass and volleyed the winner, celebrating by revealing a T-shirt honouring Dani Jarque, the Espanyol captain who had died in 2009.

Legacy

Spain's triumph completed a sequence of three consecutive major titles — Euro 2008, the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012 — widely regarded as one of the greatest international dynasties. The Netherlands lost their third World Cup final, extending their reputation as the strongest nation never to win the trophy.
