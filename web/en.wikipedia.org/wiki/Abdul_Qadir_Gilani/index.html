Abdul Qadir Gilani (Arabic: عبد القادر الجيلاني; 1077/78 – 1166 CE), known in Persian as Abdolqader Gilani and honoured as al-Ghawth al-A'zam, was a Persian Sunni Muslim preacher, ascetic and jurist of the Hanbali school who spent most of his life in Baghdad. He is regarded as the eponymous founder of the Qadiriyya, one of the oldest and most widespread Sufi orders in the Islamic world, and remains one of the most venerated saints of popular Islam, especially in South Asia.

Life

Born in Gilan on the Caspian coast (his birthplace is traditionally given as the village of Na'if), Gilani travelled to Baghdad in 1095 to study. He mastered Hanbali law, hadith and literature, and was trained in Sufism by Abu'l-Khayr Hammad ibn Muslim al-Dabbas. After years of ascetic retreat in the ruins around Baghdad, he began teaching around 1127 at a madrasa in the Bab al-Azj quarter given to him by his teacher, and his sermons drew enormous crowds of Muslims, Christians and Jews.

Teachings and works

Gilani preached a Sufism firmly anchored in Qur'an and Sunna, stressing humility, generosity, service and obedience to the sharia. Works attributed to him include the sermon collection Futuh al-Ghayb ("Revelations of the Unseen"), Al-Ghunya li-Talibi Tariq al-Haqq ("Sufficient Provision for Seekers of the Path of Truth"), and Al-Fath ar-Rabbani ("The Sublime Revelation").

Legacy

After his death in 1166 his sons organised his following into the Qadiriyya order, which spread through the Middle East, Africa and Asia and remains one of the largest Sufi tariqas. His tomb and mosque in Baghdad are major pilgrimage sites, and his death anniversary (Gyarvi Sharif, 11 Rabi' al-Thani) is celebrated by devotees worldwide.
