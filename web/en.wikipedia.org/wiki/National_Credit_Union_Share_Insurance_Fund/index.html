National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund

The National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund (NCUSIF) is a fund created by the United States Congress in 1970 to insure deposits at federally insured credit unions. It is administered by the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA).

The fund insures member share accounts — the credit union equivalent of bank deposits — up to $250,000 per member, per credit union, comparable to the deposit insurance provided by the FDIC for banks. It is funded by premiums and deposits paid by insured credit unions, and is backed by the full faith and credit of the United States government.

History

The fund was established by Public Law 91-206, signed in 1970. In 1985 it was recapitalized when insured credit unions deposited 1 percent of their insured shares into the fund, a capitalization structure that remains in place.

Coverage

The NCUSIF covers share savings, share draft (checking), money market, share certificate and IRA accounts at federally insured credit unions. Coverage is automatic for members of insured credit unions.
