Lucent Technologies, Inc. was an American multinational telecommunications equipment company headquartered in Murray Hill, New Jersey. It was created in 1996 when AT&T spun off its equipment businesses, including Western Electric and most of Bell Labs, retaining the long-distance services business.

History

Lucent was one of the most widely held stocks of the dot-com era, and at its peak was a major supplier of telephone switches, optical networking equipment, and wireless infrastructure. After the collapse of the telecom bubble in 2000–2001, the company suffered steep revenue declines, accounting restatements, and layoffs. Its research arm, Bell Labs, remained a source of major innovations and Nobel Prize–winning work.

In 2006 Lucent merged with the French company Alcatel to form Alcatel-Lucent, which was itself acquired by Nokia in 2016. The name Lucent, chosen from thousands of candidates, derived from the Latin for "light-bearing" or "shining."
