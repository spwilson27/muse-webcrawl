Omaha hold 'em, often called simply Omaha, is a community card poker game similar to Texas hold 'em, in which each player is dealt four private cards ("hole cards") and must make the best five-card hand using exactly two of them, together with three of the five community cards dealt face-up on the table.

History

Omaha is believed to have originated in Detroit in the 1970s, where a version called "Two Pair" or "Twice Three" was played, and it was introduced to Las Vegas casinos in the early 1980s. The game spread widely through the World Series of Poker, where Pot-Limit Omaha became one of its flagship events.

Rules

Each player receives four hole cards. Three betting rounds are interspersed with the flop, turn, and river community cards. At showdown a player must use exactly two hole cards and three board cards — a rule that distinguishes Omaha from Texas hold 'em and is the most common source of beginner mistakes, for example believing that a flush on the board alone counts.

Variants

The most popular betting structure is Pot-Limit Omaha (PLO). Other variants include No-Limit Omaha, Omaha hi-lo (eight-or-better), five-card Omaha ("Big O"), and Courchevel.

Strategy

Because players hold four cards, strong made hands are more common than in hold 'em; draws with many "outs" — so-called wrap straight draws — can even be statistical favourites over made hands.
