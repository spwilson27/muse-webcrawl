Bisexuality

The bisexual pride flag, designed by Michael Page in 1998.

Bisexuality is romantic attraction, sexual attraction, or sexual behavior toward both males and females, or toward more than one gender. The term is principally used in the context of human attraction to denote the capacity for romantic or sexual feelings toward people of more than one sex or gender, and is one of the main classifications of sexual orientation along with heterosexuality and homosexuality.

Definitions and models

Sexual orientation is commonly described along a continuum rather than as discrete categories. Alfred Kinsey's scale, developed in the 1940s, rated individuals from 0 (exclusively heterosexual) to 6 (exclusively homosexual), with bisexuality in the middle range; later models, such as the Klein Sexual Orientation Grid and Michael Storms' two-dimensional model, treat attraction to different genders as independent axes. Some people who are attracted to multiple genders prefer related labels such as pansexual or queer, and the umbrella term "bi+" is sometimes used collectively.

History and community

Behavior that would now be called bisexual has been documented in many societies throughout history, though the identity category is modern. The bisexual pride flag, with pink, purple and blue stripes, was designed by Michael Page in 1998; pink represents attraction to the same gender, blue attraction to a different gender, and the overlapping purple attraction across genders. Bisexual people report distinctive experiences, including "double discrimination" from both heterosexual and gay and lesbian communities and the erasure of bisexual identity, and surveys in several countries consistently find bisexual people form the largest single group within LGBT populations.
