Brighton and Hove

The seafront of Brighton and Hove, with the Palace Pier

Brighton and Hove is a city on the south coast of England, in the ceremonial county of East Sussex. It was formed as a unitary authority in 1997 by the merger of the boroughs of Brighton and Hove, and was granted city status in 2000. The conurbation also includes the smaller towns of Portslade and Rottingdean. Its population is around 290,000, making it one of the largest settlements in South East England outside London.

History

Brighton grew from the fishing village of Brighthelmstone into a fashionable seaside resort after Dr Richard Russell promoted its seawater cures in the 1750s, and especially after the patronage of the Prince Regent, who built the exotic Royal Pavilion. The arrival of the railway in 1841 brought mass tourism. Hove developed in the nineteenth century as a more sedate, residential neighbour, famous for its Regency and Victorian terraces along Brunswick Square and Adelaide Crescent.

Geography and economy

The city lies between the South Downs to the north and the English Channel to the south, about 47 miles (76 km) south of London. Its economy is based on tourism, conferences, education — it is home to the University of Sussex and the University of Brighton — and a large digital and creative sector that has earned it the nickname "Silicon Beach". Brighton and Hove is also known for its nightlife, arts festivals including the Brighton Festival and Brighton Fringe, and its large LGBT community.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the Royal Pavilion, the Palace Pier, the earlier West Pier (destroyed by fire in 2003), the British Airways i360 observation tower, the Volk's Electric Railway — the oldest operating electric railway in the world — and the pebble beach running the length of the seafront.
