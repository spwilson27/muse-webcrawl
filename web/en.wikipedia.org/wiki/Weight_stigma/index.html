Weight stigma

Weight stigma, also known as weight bias or weight-based discrimination, is discrimination or stereotyping based on a person's weight. Such discrimination is pervasive and largely socially acceptable, occurring in employment, healthcare, education, interpersonal relationships, and the media. Perpetrators and victims may not be aware of discrimination targeting heavier people because of the prevailing social acceptability of negative attitudes toward higher body weight.

Manifestations

Common stereotypes include assumptions that people with obesity are lazy, unmotivated, lacking in self-discipline, or less competent. Weight-based teasing and bullying are common among school-age youth, and adults report discrimination in hiring, wages, and promotion. In healthcare settings, studies document that some providers spend less time with higher-weight patients and attribute unrelated symptoms to weight.

Health effects

Experiencing weight stigma is itself associated with adverse outcomes, including psychological distress, depression, anxiety, disordered eating, avoidance of medical care, physiological stress responses, and — paradoxically — weight gain. Internalized weight bias, in which individuals apply negative stereotypes to themselves, is linked to poorer mental and physical health.

Legal and social responses

Few jurisdictions prohibit weight discrimination explicitly; Michigan's Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act is a notable exception in the United States, and some cities, including New York City (2023), have adopted height and weight protections. Activists within the fat acceptance movement and body positivity campaigns challenge weight stigma, and researchers advocate including weight in anti-discrimination policies.
