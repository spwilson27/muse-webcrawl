Khorasan Province (Persian: استان خراسان) was a province of Iran located in the northeast of the country, named after the historical region of Greater Khorasan. Bordering Turkmenistan and Afghanistan, it was for decades Iran's largest province by area, with its capital at Mashhad, the country's second-largest city and the site of the Imam Reza shrine.

History

The modern province took shape in the Pahlavi era as the Ninth Province and long encompassed the whole of Iran's northeast. Its size made administration difficult: covering deserts, mountain ranges, and agricultural oases, it had a population of several million spread over roughly 300,000 square kilometres. Repeated proposals to divide it were debated through the 1990s.

Division of 2004

On 29 September 2004, Khorasan Province was formally divided into three new provinces:

  • Razavi Khorasan, centered on Mashhad, retaining most of the population and the provincial institutions;
  • North Khorasan, with its capital at Bojnord;
  • South Khorasan, with its capital at Birjand.

The division followed legislation passed by the Iranian parliament in 2003–2004 and at times provoked local protests, particularly over the assignment of certain counties and the location of capitals. The three successor provinces together still correspond closely to the Iranian portion of historical Khorasan.
