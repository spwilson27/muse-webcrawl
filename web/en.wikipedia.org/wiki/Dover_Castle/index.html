Overview

Dover Castle is a medieval castle in Dover, Kent, founded in the 11th–12th centuries and described as the "Key to England" for its strategic position at the narrowest part of the Channel. The present great keep was built by Henry II in the 1180s; an Iron Age hillfort, a Roman lighthouse (pharos) and the Saxon church of St Mary-in-Castro occupy the same hill.

History

The castle withstood the siege of 1216–17 during the First Barons' War, when Prince Louis of France nearly took England. A network of tunnels was cut in the Napoleonic Wars and massively extended in the Second World War, when they housed the command that directed the Dunkirk evacuation (Operation Dynamo, May–June 1940) under Admiral Bertram Ramsay. The tunnels later served as a Cold War regional government bunker. The castle is managed by English Heritage.
