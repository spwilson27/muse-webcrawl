Lurcher

A lurcher is a crossbred dog resulting from mating a sighthound with a dog of another type, most often a pastoral breed or a terrier. The term derives from the Romani word lur, meaning thief, and Middle English lorchen, to lurk or watch.

History

Lurchers were traditionally bred in Britain and Ireland for hunting and poaching. Because commoners were at various times forbidden to own purebred sighthounds such as greyhounds, crosses combining sighthound speed with the intelligence, herding ability or hardiness of collies and other working breeds became the poacher's dog of choice. Common crosses include greyhound × Border Collie, whippet × terrier, and deerhound or saluki crosses.

Characteristics

Lurchers vary widely in appearance but generally retain the deep chest, long legs and tucked waist of the sighthound. They are bred for speed, stamina, intelligence and hunting ability rather than to a kennel-club standard, and they are not recognised as a breed by major kennel clubs. Beyond hunting, lurchers compete in lure coursing and racing, and are increasingly kept as companion dogs; several rescue organisations specialise in rehoming working lurchers and their sighthound relatives.
