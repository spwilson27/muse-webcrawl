New Car Assessment Program

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A frontal crash test of a passenger car.

A New Car Assessment Program (or Programme, NCAP) is a government or independent car safety programme tasked with evaluating new automobile designs for performance against various safety threats. NCAPs conduct crash tests and other evaluations beyond legal requirements and publish consumer-friendly ratings, typically as star ratings, to inform buyers and encourage manufacturers to improve vehicle safety.

History

The first NCAP was established by the United States National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) in 1979; in 1993 it introduced the familiar five-star rating system for frontal crash test results. The European programme, Euro NCAP, was founded in 1996 and published its first results in 1997. Programmes have since been created in Australia and New Zealand (ANCAP), Japan (JNCAP), Korea (KNCAP), China (C-NCAP), Latin America (Latin NCAP), Southeast Asia (ASEAN NCAP), India (Bharat NCAP, launched 2023) and for the global market (Global NCAP).

Testing

Depending on the programme, tests typically include:

  • Full-width and offset-deformable frontal impact tests
  • Side impact tests against a moving barrier and a rigid pole
  • Whiplash seat tests using rear-impact sleds
  • Pedestrian and cyclist (vulnerable road user) protection tests
  • Child occupant protection assessments with child restraint systems
  • Safety assist evaluations: autonomous emergency braking, lane support, speed assistance and seat belt reminders

Test protocols are periodically tightened, so the same star rating in different years does not necessarily represent the same level of performance.

Impact

NCAP ratings have been credited with driving substantial improvements in crashworthiness and in the fitment of electronic stability control, multiple airbags and driver assistance systems, as manufacturers compete for top ratings that feature prominently in marketing.
