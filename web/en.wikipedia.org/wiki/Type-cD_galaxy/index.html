Type-cD galaxy

A type-cD galaxy (cD galaxy) is a galaxy morphology classification, a subtype of the giant elliptical galaxy of type D. It is characterised by a large, diffuse halo of stars surrounding a normal elliptical core. cD galaxies are found almost exclusively at the centres of rich galaxy clusters, where they are also the brightest cluster galaxies.

Formation

The extended envelope is thought to grow through the merging of many smaller galaxies and the tidal stripping of stars from cluster members, a process sometimes called "galactic cannibalism". The stripped stars may become part of the intracluster light. cD galaxies are among the largest and most luminous galaxies known, with diameters that can exceed a megaparsec when the faint halo is included; examples include IC 1101 and the central galaxies of the Coma Cluster.
