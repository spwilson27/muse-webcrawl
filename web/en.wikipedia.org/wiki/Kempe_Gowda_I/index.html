Kempe Gowda I (1510–1569), also known as Hiriya Kempe Gowda, was a chieftain (palegar) under the Vijayanagara Empire who is credited with founding the city of Bengaluru (Bangalore) in 1537.

Founding of Bengaluru

According to tradition, Kempe Gowda conceived the idea of a new fortified town during a hunting expedition with his overlord's blessing. In 1537 he built a mud fort at Bengaluru, laid out streets and quarters for different trades (petes), constructed temples and tanks, and organised water supply. He is said to have erected four watchtowers marking the limits to which the city would grow — towers that still stand in modern Bangalore. His reign saw the building of numerous lakes and temples, including patronage of the Gavi Gangadhareshwara and Bull temples.

Later life and legacy

Kempe Gowda later quarrelled with the Vijayanagara court and was imprisoned for several years before being released. He died around 1569 and was succeeded by Kempe Gowda II. Bengaluru's international airport (Kempegowda International Airport), a major bus station and metro stations in the city are named in his honour, and his birthday is celebrated in Karnataka.
