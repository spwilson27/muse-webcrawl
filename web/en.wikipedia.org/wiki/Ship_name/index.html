Ship name

A ship name is the proper name given to a ship, displayed on its hull and used in official documents and communication. Nearly all vessels of any size carry names, which are subject to conventions that differ between navies, merchant fleets, and private owners, and many countries maintain formal registries of ship names.

Naval naming conventions

Navies typically name ships by class-based schemes. In the United States Navy, for example, aircraft carriers have often been named for presidents and notable figures, destroyers for naval heroes, submarines for cities, states, or fish, and amphibious ships for battles or places. Royal Navy names frequently repeat names of earlier warships, carrying battle honours forward. Naval ships also carry a ship prefix such as HMS or USS, denoting ownership and status.

Merchant and civilian naming

Merchant vessels are usually named by their owners and are required to be registered under a unique name in their flag state; names are painted on the bow and stern together with the port of registry. Passenger liners and cruise ships often follow company naming themes. Many ships are renamed when sold, though some names carry superstitions—sailors have long considered renaming a vessel unlucky unless done with a formal ceremony.

History

Ancient ships were commonly named for gods, goddesses, and virtues, a tradition continued in classical and later navies. The practice of formally christening and launching ships with ceremony, including breaking a bottle against the hull, became standard in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
