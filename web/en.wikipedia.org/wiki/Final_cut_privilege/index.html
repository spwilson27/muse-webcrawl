Final cut privilege

Final cut privilege (also known as final cut rights) is the right of a film director to determine the final version of a film for distribution. In the standard studio system, this right usually rests with the studio, and only established, commercially successful directors are typically granted it.

Practice

Directors known for holding final cut include Stanley Kubrick, the Coen brothers, and Christopher Nolan. Directors who lack it may release alternate "director's cut" editions later; famous disputes include the studio-imposed changes to Brazil and the multiple versions of Blade Runner.
