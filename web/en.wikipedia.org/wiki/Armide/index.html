Armide

Armide is an opera in five acts by Jean-Baptiste Lully, first performed at the Paris Opéra on 15 February 1686. Its libretto, by Philippe Quinault, is based on Torquato Tasso's epic poem Gerusalemme liberata and tells of the Saracen sorceress Armida and her love for the crusader knight Renaud (Rinaldo).

Plot and reception

Armida, who has enchanted the crusaders, finds she cannot bring herself to kill the sleeping Renaud because she has fallen in love with him. His rescue by fellow knights and her final despair form the dramatic arc. The Act 2 scene of Renaud's sleep and Armida's monologue "Enfin il est en ma puissance" is among the most celebrated passages in French Baroque opera.

The subject was later set by many composers, including Gluck, whose Armide (1777) used Quinault's libretto almost unchanged.
