The Scott Paper Company was an American manufacturer of sanitary paper products, founded in Philadelphia in 1879 by brothers E. Irvin and Clarence Scott. It pioneered the marketing of toilet paper on rolls — a product long considered unmentionable — and grew into the world's largest maker of tissue products, with brands including Scotties tissues, Cottonelle, Viva towels, and Scott bathroom tissue.

History

Scott popularized the toilet roll (from about 1890) and introduced paper towels (1907) and the first paper napkins sold for home use. In 1994, CEO Albert J. Dunlap ("Chainsaw Al") implemented drastic cost-cutting and layoffs, sharply raising the share price; in 1995 the company merged into Kimberly-Clark for about .4 billion, ending its independence while the Scott brand survived.
