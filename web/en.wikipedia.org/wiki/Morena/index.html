Morena

Morena (Spanish: Movimiento Regeneración Nacional, "National Regeneration Movement") is a major political party in Mexico. Founded by Andrés Manuel López Obrador as a civil association in 2011 and registered as a political party in 2014, it is generally described as left-wing and populist. The word morena also colloquially refers to a brown-skinned woman and to Our Lady of Guadalupe, giving the acronym cultural resonance.

Rise to power

Morena grew out of López Obrador's movements following his disputed 2006 presidential defeat and his 2012 campaign. In the 2018 general election, López Obrador won the presidency with about 53 percent of the vote, and Morena and its allies won majorities in both chambers of Congress and numerous governorships. The party's program, dubbed the "Fourth Transformation," emphasises combating corruption, austerity in government, expanded social programs and pensions, and state-led energy policy.

Recent years

In the 2024 general election Morena's candidate, Claudia Sheinbaum, a former Mexico City mayor, won the presidency by a wide margin, becoming Mexico's first woman president, while the party and its allies secured supermajorities enabling constitutional reforms, including a controversial overhaul of the judiciary introducing elected judges.
