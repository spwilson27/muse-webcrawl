Samguk sagi

Author: Kim Pusik and team of scholars
Completed: 1145
Language: Classical Chinese
Commissioned by: King Injong of Goryeo
Length: 50 volumes

The Samguk sagi (삼국사기, 三國史記, "History of the Three Kingdoms") is the oldest surviving Korean history book. Compiled by the scholar-official Kim Pusik and a team of younger scholars at the order of King Injong of Goryeo, it was completed in 1145 and covers the history of the Three Kingdoms of Korea—Silla, Goguryeo, and Baekje—down to the fall of Silla in 935.

Contents

The work comprises 50 volumes (gwon): 28 volumes of annals (bongi) for the three kingdoms—twelve for Silla, ten for Goguryeo, and six for Baekje—plus monographs on astronomy, geography, music, and official posts, chronological tables, and biographies of notable figures. It was written in Classical Chinese and modelled on Sima Qian's Shiji, applying Confucian historiographical principles.

Sources and criticism

Kim Pusik drew on earlier Korean records, most now lost, and on Chinese dynastic histories. Modern historians value the Samguk sagi as the main source for the Three Kingdoms period but criticise its Silla-centred and Confucian bias, its moralising judgments, and its treatment of myths—such as the founding legends of the kingdoms—as literal history. It complements the Samguk yusa (1281), the monk Iryeon's collection of legends and folklore omitted by Kim Pusik's sober account.
