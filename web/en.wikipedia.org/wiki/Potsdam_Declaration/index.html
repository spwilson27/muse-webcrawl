Potsdam Declaration

The declaration was issued during the Potsdam Conference in occupied Germany

The Potsdam Declaration, formally the Proclamation Defining Terms for Japanese Surrender, was a statement issued on 26 July 1945 by the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Republic of China during the Potsdam Conference, calling for the unconditional surrender of all Japanese armed forces in the Second World War. It warned that the alternative was "prompt and utter destruction". The declaration outlined the terms on which the war in the Pacific would end and Japan would be occupied and rebuilt.

Terms

  • Elimination of the authority of militarist leaders who had led Japan into war
  • Allied occupation of points in Japanese territory until the terms were fulfilled
  • Japanese sovereignty limited to Honshu, Hokkaido, Kyushu, Shikoku, and minor islands determined by the Allies, implementing the Cairo Declaration of 1943 — stripping Japan of its overseas empire
  • Disarmament of Japanese forces, after which soldiers could return to peaceful lives
  • Stern justice for war criminals, removal of obstacles to democratic tendencies, and establishment of freedom of speech, religion, and thought
  • Permission for Japan to maintain a peaceful, productive economy and, eventually, access to world trade
  • Withdrawal of occupation forces once a peacefully inclined and responsible government, freely chosen by the Japanese people, was established

Notably, the declaration did not specify the future of the Emperor, an omission debated ever since; drafts that would have explicitly permitted retention of a constitutional monarchy were revised before issue. Japan's prime minister Suzuki Kantarō responded with the word mokusatsu — "to kill with silence" — which was interpreted by the Allies as rejection. The Soviet Union, not yet at war with Japan, did not sign.

Outcome

Following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war and invasion of Manchuria, Japan accepted the declaration on 14 August 1945, with a reservation regarding the Emperor's prerogatives that the Allies answered ambiguously. The formal surrender was signed aboard USS Missouri on 2 September 1945, and the Potsdam terms provided the framework for the Allied occupation of Japan.
