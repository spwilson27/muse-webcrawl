Bézier curve

A Bézier curve is a parametric curve used in computer graphics and related fields, defined by a set of control points. The curve passes through the first and last control points and is tangent to the polygon they form; intermediate points pull the curve without generally lying on it.

Definition

Linear, quadratic and cubic Bézier curves are built by successive linear interpolation (de Casteljau's algorithm). In general a Bézier curve of degree n is a weighted sum of Bernstein polynomials evaluated at the curve parameter. Common special cases include quadratic curves in TrueType fonts and cubic curves in PostScript, SVG and CSS animation.

History

The curves are named after Pierre Bézier, who used them in the 1960s to design automobile bodies at Renault; the underlying mathematics had been developed by Paul de Casteljau at Citroën in 1959.
