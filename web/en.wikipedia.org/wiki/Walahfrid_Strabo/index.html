Walahfrid Strabo

Born: c. 808, Swabia

Died: 18 August 849, Loire River (drowned)

Occupation: Benedictine monk, poet, abbot of Reichenau

Known works: Hortulus, Visio Wettini, De exordiis

Walahfrid Strabo (c. 808 – 18 August 849), sometimes spelled Walafrid, was a Carolingian Benedictine monk, poet and theologian, and one of the leading literary figures of the Carolingian Renaissance. His byname Strabo ("the squinter") refers to a squint. He was educated at the abbey of Reichenau in Lake Constance and at Fulda under Hrabanus Maurus.

Life

Born of humble parents in Swabia, Walahfrid was offered to Reichenau as a child oblate. From 829 to 838 he served at the court of the emperor Louis the Pious as tutor to the young prince Charles (later Charles the Bald). He became abbot of Reichenau in 838 but was driven out during the civil wars among Louis's sons, finding refuge at Speyer; he was later restored to his abbey. He drowned in 849 while crossing the Loire on a diplomatic mission to Charles the Bald.

Works

Walahfrid's best-known work is the Hortulus ("Little Garden"), a poem of 444 hexameters describing the plants of his monastery garden at Reichenau and their medicinal uses, written in the tradition of Virgil's Georgics. His earliest work, the Visio Wettini (824), versified a vision of the afterlife experienced by his teacher Wetti. Later writings include the influential liturgical and historical treatise De exordiis et incrementis quarundam in observationibus ecclesiasticis rerum, saints' lives of St Gall and St Otmar, theological commentaries, and a prologue to Einhard's Life of Charlemagne in the edition he prepared. The Glossa ordinaria on the Bible was long attributed to him, though he probably contributed to the tradition rather than authoring it.
