A-infinity category

In mathematics, an A∞-category (A-infinity category) is a generalization of a category in which the morphism sets are graded vector spaces (or chain complexes) equipped with a sequence of higher composition operations that satisfy associativity only up to coherent homotopy. The notion extends that of an A∞-algebra, introduced by Jim Stasheff in the 1960s in his study of loop spaces.

Definition

An A∞-category consists of a set of objects, graded morphism spaces between them, and operations mk taking k composable morphisms to one, of degree 2 − k, satisfying an infinite family of quadratic relations (the A∞-relations). The first operation m1 is a differential, m2 is composition, associative up to the homotopy supplied by m3, and so on.

Applications

A∞-categories are central to homological mirror symmetry: Maxim Kontsevich's 1994 conjecture describes the derived category of coherent sheaves on one space as equivalent to the Fukaya category — an A∞-category built from Lagrangian submanifolds — of a mirror symplectic space. They also appear in string topology and the theory of triangulated categories via pretriangulated envelopes.
