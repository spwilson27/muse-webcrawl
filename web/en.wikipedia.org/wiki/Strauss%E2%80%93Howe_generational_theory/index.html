Strauss–Howe generational theory

The Strauss–Howe generational theory, devised by William Strauss and Neil Howe, describes a theorised recurring generation cycle in American and Western history. According to the theory, historical events are associated with recurring generational personas called archetypes, and a cycle of four "turnings" — a High, an Awakening, an Unravelling and a Crisis — repeats roughly every 80 to 90 years, a period the authors call a saeculum.

Archetypes

The four archetypes, in order, are Prophet (born during a High), Nomad (born during an Awakening), Hero (born during an Unravelling) and Artist (born during a Crisis). Each archetype's character is shaped by the era of its childhood and comes to dominate successive phases of adult life as the cycle turns.

Works and reception

Strauss and Howe set out the theory in Generations (1991) and The Fourth Turning (1997), and coined the term "Millennial" for the Hero generation born from 1982 onward. The theory has been influential in business, marketing and political commentary, though academic historians and sociologists have criticised it as unfalsifiable, overly deterministic and insufficiently supported by empirical evidence.
