The Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period (五代十國時代; 907–979) was an era of political fragmentation and rapid regime change in imperial China following the collapse of the Tang dynasty in 907. In the north, five short-lived dynasties succeeded one another at Kaifeng and Luoyang — Later Liang, Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han, and Later Zhou — while in the south and west some ten (in fact more) more durable regional kingdoms, including Wu, Southern Tang, Wuyue, Min, Chu, Former and Later Shu, Southern Han, Jingnan, and Northern Han, coexisted.

The north

The warlord Zhu Wen, having forced the last Tang emperor to abdicate, founded Later Liang in 907. The northern dynasties were dominated by military governors and by Shatuo Turkic commanders (Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han). In 936 Shi Jingtang founded Later Jin with help from the Khitan Liao dynasty, ceding the Sixteen Prefectures of Yan and Yun — the strategic passes north of the North China Plain — in return, a loss that exposed China to northern invasion for four centuries. The reforming Later Zhou emperor Chai Rong strengthened the army and administration, preparing the way for reunification.

The south

The southern kingdoms, comparatively stable and prosperous, developed maritime trade, tea and silk production, printing (the first printed Buddhist canon was produced in Shu), and the arts — Southern Tang, whose last ruler Li Yu was a celebrated poet, is remembered as a cultural golden age. Wuyue's kings built the sea walls protecting Hangzhou and the Qiantang coast.

Reunification

In 960 the Later Zhou general Zhao Kuangyin seized the throne at Chenqiao and founded the Song dynasty. Following a strategy of "south first, then north", Song absorbed the southern kingdoms one by one — Jingnan and Wuchang (963), Later Shu (965), Southern Han (971), Southern Tang (975), Wuyue's peaceful submission (978) — and finally conquered Northern Han in 979, reuniting China proper, though the Sixteen Prefectures remained with Liao. The era is chronicled in the Old History of the Five Dynasties and Sima Guang's Zizhi Tongjian.
