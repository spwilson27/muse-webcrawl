Alnilam

Alnilam is the central star of Orion's Belt, designated Epsilon Orionis (ε Ori). A blue supergiant about 1,200–2,000 light-years away, it is one of the most luminous stars known and lights the reflection nebula NGC 1990 around it.

Properties

Alnilam is a hot B-type supergiant with a powerful stellar wind losing mass at millions of times the Sun's rate. It is a suspected Alpha Cygni variable, varying slightly in brightness. At roughly 4–5 million years old and tens of solar masses, it will evolve into a red supergiant and eventually explode as a supernova.

Name

The name derives from Arabic al-niẓām, "string (of pearls)", referring to the Belt asterism, whose other stars are Alnitak and Mintaka. Because the Belt points toward Sirius and the Pleiades, Alnilam has long served navigation and seasonal calendars.
