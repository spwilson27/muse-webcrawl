Johann Bernoulli (6 August 1667 – 1 January 1748) was a Swiss mathematician, one of the many prominent mathematicians in the Bernoulli family. He is known for his contributions to infinitesimal calculus and for educating Leonhard Euler. He was the younger brother of Jacob Bernoulli, with whom he collaborated — and bitterly quarrelled — over the foundations of the new calculus.

Life and work

Bernoulli studied medicine at Basel but turned to mathematics, learning calculus from his brother Jacob. While tutoring the marquis Guillaume de l'Hôpital in Paris under a famous contract, he discovered the rule for evaluating indeterminate forms 0/0 now known as L'Hôpital's rule, published in l'Hôpital's 1696 textbook, the first on differential calculus.

Bernoulli posed and solved (with Leibniz, Newton and his brother) the brachistochrone problem in 1696 — the curve of fastest descent is the cycloid — a challenge that helped launch the calculus of variations. He also solved the catenary equation, developed the calculus of exponential functions, and contributed to differential equations (the Bernoulli equation) and fluid mechanics. Appointed professor at Groningen in 1695, he returned to Basel in 1705, succeeding to his late brother's chair.

Family feuds

Johann's rivalry with Jacob was notorious, and in later life he competed even with his own son Daniel Bernoulli, controversially backdating a publication to claim priority over Daniel's Hydrodynamica. His students included Euler, whom he mentored at Basel, and his other sons Nicolaus II and Johann II, who continued the family mathematical dynasty.
