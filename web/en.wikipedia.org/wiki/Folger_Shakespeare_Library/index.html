The Folger Shakespeare Library is an independent research library on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C. It has the world's largest collection of the printed works of William Shakespeare and is a primary repository for rare materials from early modern Britain.

Collection

The library holds 82 copies of Shakespeare's First Folio of 1623 — roughly a third of all known surviving copies — together with early editions of his works, the world's largest collection of Shakespeare-related material, and substantial holdings of other early English books printed between 1475 and 1700.

History and programmes

Oil executive Henry Clay Folger and his wife Emily amassed the collection over decades. The building, designed by Paul Philippe Cret in a stripped classicist style with Art Deco touches, opened in 1932, two years after Henry Folger's death. The Folger also runs the Folger Theatre, Folger Consort, exhibitions, and the Folger Institute for scholarly research, and publishes the New Folger Library editions of Shakespeare's plays.
