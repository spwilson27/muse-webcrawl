Public-key cryptography (asymmetric cryptography) is a cryptographic system that uses pairs of related keys: a public key that may be shared widely and a private key kept secret. What one key encrypts, only the other can decrypt, enabling secure communication and digital signatures without any prior shared secret.

History

Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman published the key-exchange concept in 1976; Rivest, Shamir and Adleman described RSA in 1977. It later emerged that James Ellis and colleagues at Britain's GCHQ had developed equivalent ideas in secret in the early 1970s. Public-key methods secure the modern web (TLS), underpin the ideas behind the codebreaking era's successor systems, and are threatened in principle by large-scale quantum computing, motivating post-quantum cryptography.
