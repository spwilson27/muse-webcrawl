Indomie is a brand of instant noodle produced by the Indonesian company Indofood. It is one of the best-selling instant noodle brands in the world, exported to more than 80 countries, and is especially popular in Indonesia, Nigeria, Australia, and parts of the Middle East and Africa.

History

Indomie was introduced in 1972 by the company that became Indofood, founded by Sudono Salim. The name is a portmanteau of "Indo" (Indonesia) and "mie" (the Indonesian spelling of "noodle"). The brand's most famous flavour, Mi Goreng — modelled on the Indonesian fried noodle dish mie goreng — was launched in 1982 and became the first dry (soup-less) instant noodle variant in Indonesia. It went on to achieve global cult status.

Products

The brand covers dozens of variants, including the Mi Goreng range (original, spicy, rendang, satay, and regional specials), soup noodles such as Soto Mie and Ayam Bawang (onion chicken), and cup and jumbo formats. Nigerian production, begun in the 1990s under licence, made Indomie so ubiquitous that "indomie" became a generic word for instant noodles in Nigeria.

Popularity

Indomie Mi Goreng has repeatedly appeared in instant-noodle rankings compiled by reviewers and has been named among the world's best instant noodles. Annual production runs to many billions of packets, and the brand is a staple food for students and low-income households across much of the developing world because of its low price and distinctive seasoning: a combination of sweet soy sauce (kecap manis), chilli sauce, flavoured oil, seasoning powder, and fried shallots.
