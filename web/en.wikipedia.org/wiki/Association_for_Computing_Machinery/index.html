The Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) is a US-based international learned society for computing. Founded in 1947, it is the world's largest scientific and educational computing society, with about 110,000 student and professional members as of the 2020s.

Activities

ACM publishes journals and magazines including Communications of the ACM and maintains the ACM Digital Library. It organises conferences through Special Interest Groups (SIGs) such as SIGGRAPH, SIGCHI and SIGCOMM, and sponsors programming contests including the International Collegiate Programming Contest historically.

Awards

ACM administers the Turing Award, often called the Nobel Prize of computing, as well as the ACM Prize in Computing and the Grace Murray Hopper Award.

Structure

ACM is led by an elected president and council. It has professional and student chapters worldwide and a Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct first adopted in 1992 and updated in 2018.

History

ACM was founded at a meeting at Columbia University in 1947 as the Eastern Association for Computing Machinery, dropping “Eastern” the following year. Its first president was John Mauchly, co-inventor of ENIAC.
