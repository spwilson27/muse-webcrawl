Stickney (crater)

Location: Phobos, moon of Mars

Diameter: ~9 km (5.6 mi)

Named after: Angeline Stickney

Stickney is the largest crater on Phobos, the larger of the two moons of Mars. It is about 9 km (5.6 mi) in diameter, taking up a substantial proportion of the moon's volume, Phobos itself having a mean radius of only about 11 km. The crater dominates the appearance of the moon.

Stickney was named after Angeline Stickney, the wife of astronomer Asaph Hall, who discovered Phobos and its companion moon Deimos in 1877. The name was approved in 1973.

Characteristics

The impact that created Stickney must have nearly shattered Phobos. The surface of the moon is marked by grooves, thought to be fractures caused by the impact, radiating from the crater. A smaller crater, Limtoc, lies inside Stickney. Later spacecraft imagery, including from Viking, Mars Global Surveyor, and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, revealed chains of small craters and a textured ejecta blanket within the crater.
