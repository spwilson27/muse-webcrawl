Release (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the music industry, a release is any creative output distributed for public consumption, most commonly a recording — a single, EP, album, or music video — issued by an artist or record label in a particular format and market. The term covers both the act of making the work available and the product itself, and a recording may have many distinct releases (original editions, reissues, remasters, and regional variants), a distinction important in discography and music databases.

Terminology

A work's first release in a given territory is sometimes called its street date; advance or promotional releases circulate to radio, press and streaming services before that date. In rights administration, a release is distinguished from a recording: one recording (master) may appear on many releases, and publishing and neighbouring-rights royalties are tracked per release and per territory.

Release strategies

Historically, albums were released on set days of the week; in 2015 the industry standardised on Friday as the global release day for new music. Digital distribution has enabled surprise releases (announced at or after availability), staggered single-led rollouts, and deluxe editions, while catalogue reissues and anniversary editions remain a major commercial category. Release metadata — titles, credits, ISRC codes for tracks and UPC codes for products — is compiled and exchanged through systems such as label copy and DDEX feeds.
