Estuary English

Estuary English is an English accent, or group of accents, associated with the area along the River Thames and its estuary, especially London, Essex and Kent. The term was coined by the British phonetician David Rosewarne in 1984.

Features

Estuary English is often described as intermediate between Received Pronunciation (RP) and Cockney. Characteristic features include glottal stops for /t/ in some positions, l-vocalisation (pronouncing milk with a vowel-like l), yod-coalescence, and the use of a broad A in words such as bath close to RP. Th-fronting is generally less common than in Cockney.

Distribution and status

The variety spread through social and geographical mobility, commuting, and the media. Some commentators have associated it with politicians and public figures adopting features perceived as more approachable, a phenomenon discussed as "mockney" when adopted by speakers from more prestigious backgrounds. Linguists caution that Estuary English is not a single uniform accent but a continuum of speech in south-eastern England.
