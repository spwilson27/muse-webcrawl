Davi Kopenawa Yanomami (born c. 1956) is a Yanomami shaman and spokesman for the Yanomami people of the Brazilian Amazon, internationally recognised for his defence of indigenous rights and the Amazon rainforest. His name "Kopenawa" refers to the hornet (kopenari), given for his fighting spirit.

Life and activism

Born in a Yanomami community near the Orinoco headwaters, Kopenawa lost much of his family to epidemics of measles and other diseases brought by outsiders in the 1950s–60s. After learning Portuguese while working for the National Indian Foundation (FUNAI), he campaigned against the gold-mining invasion (garimpo) of Yanomami territory during the 1980s, which brought disease and violence. His advocacy contributed to the Brazilian government's demarcation of the Yanomami Indigenous Territory in 1992, one of the largest protected indigenous areas in the Amazon. He co-founded the Hutukara Yanomami Association in 2004.

The Falling Sky

With anthropologist Bruce Albert, Kopenawa produced The Falling Sky: Words of a Yanomami Shaman (French 2010; English 2013), a landmark first-person account of Yanomami cosmology and a critique of the "merchandise" economy of white people and its threat to the forest. In 2019 he received the Right Livelihood Award jointly with Hutukara. He continues to campaign against illegal mining and mercury contamination in Yanomami territory.
