Alpini

The Alpini are the Italian Army's specialist mountain infantry, founded on 15 October 1872 — the oldest active mountain warfare corps in the world. They are instantly recognisable by the cappello alpino, a felt hat bearing a black raven feather (golden-eagle feather for officers).

History

Raised to defend Italy's Alpine borders, the Alpini were recruited locally from mountain districts. In the First World War they fought the gruelling "White War" against Austro-Hungarian Kaiserjäger in the Dolomites and on Monte Ortigara. In the Second World War Alpini divisions fought in France, Greece and on the Eastern Front, where the ARMIR's Alpine corps was largely destroyed during the Soviet offensives of the winter of 1942–43 — a sacrifice commemorated in the retreat from the Don.

Today

The modern Alpini comprise two brigades, "Taurinense" and "Julia", under the Alpine Troops Command in Bolzano, and have served in peacekeeping and combat operations including Afghanistan. Their veterans' association, the Associazione Nazionale Alpini, is one of Italy's largest, and its annual national gathering draws hundreds of thousands of participants.
