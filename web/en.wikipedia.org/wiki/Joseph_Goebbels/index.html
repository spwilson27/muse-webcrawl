Joseph Goebbels


Paul Joseph Goebbels
Born: 29 October 1897, Rheydt, German Empire
Died: 1 May 1945 (aged 47), Berlin, Nazi Germany
Office: Reich Minister of Propaganda (1933–1945); Chancellor of Germany (30 April – 1 May 1945)

Paul Joseph Goebbels (29 October 1897 – 1 May 1945) was a German Nazi politician and philologist who was the Gauleiter of Berlin and, from 1933, Reich Minister of Propaganda. One of Adolf Hitler's closest associates and most devoted followers, he was known for his public speaking, his organisation of Nazi propaganda, and his virulent antisemitism.

Early life

Goebbels, who had a deformed right foot caused by a congenital deformity or childhood osteomyelitis, earned a doctorate in philology from Heidelberg University in 1921. He joined the Nazi Party in 1924 and became Gauleiter of Berlin in 1926, where he founded the newspaper Der Angriff.

Propaganda minister

After the Nazis seized power in 1933, Goebbels's ministry took control of the press, radio, film and other media. He promoted the cult of Hitler, organised rallies and book burnings, and played a central role in inciting Kristallnacht in November 1938. During the Second World War he pushed for total war, notably in his Sportpalast speech of February 1943.

Death

Named Chancellor in Hitler's political testament, Goebbels succeeded Hitler on 30 April 1945. On 1 May 1945, he and his wife Magda had their six children poisoned and then killed themselves in Berlin.
