Frédéric Joliot-Curie

Jean Frédéric Joliot-Curie (19 March 1900 – 14 August 1958) was a French physicist and husband of Irène Joliot-Curie. Together they won the 1935 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of artificial radioactivity — the synthesis of new radioactive elements.


Born: Paris, France
Spouse: Irène Curie
Award: Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1935

Career

Joliot worked at the Radium Institute under Marie Curie, whose daughter Irène he married in 1926; both adopted the surname Joliot-Curie. Their 1934 discovery that stable elements could be made radioactive opened fields in medicine and research.

War and postwar

Active in the French Resistance, Joliot later became France's first High Commissioner for Atomic Energy and oversaw the first French reactor, Zoé, in 1948. His communist affiliation led to his removal in 1950. He was a leading figure in the World Peace Council. See Frisch–Peierls memorandum for parallel wartime nuclear work.
