Tokyo Skytree

Tokyo Skytree
Height: 634 m
Location: Sumida, Tokyo
Opened: 2012

Tokyo Skytree (Japanese: 東京スカイツリー) is a broadcasting and observation tower in Sumida, Tokyo, Japan. At 634 metres (2,080 ft), it is the tallest tower in the world and the third tallest structure overall, surpassed only by the Burj Khalifa and Merdeka 118. It was completed in February 2012 and opened to the public on 22 May 2012.

Design

The tower's lattice structure, designed by Nikken Sekkei, blends a traditional Japanese aesthetic with futuristic engineering: the silhouette shifts gradually from a triangular base to a circular top, echoed by subtle convex and concave curves evoking the blade of a katana. Its earthquake resistance includes a central reinforced-concrete shaft, modelled on the five-storey pagoda principle, that acts as a mass damper.

Use

Skytree replaced Tokyo Tower as the main television and radio broadcasting site for the Kantō region, since the older tower was no longer tall enough to give complete digital terrestrial coverage amid surrounding high-rises. Two observation decks, at 350 m and 450 m, and the adjoining Tokyo Solamachi shopping complex attract millions of visitors annually. The 634 m height was chosen because the digits "6-3-4" can be read as Mu-sa-shi, the old name of the region.
