Batavia (1628 ship)

Batavia was a ship of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), launched in 1628. On her maiden voyage she was wrecked on Morning Reef, near Beacon Island off the coast of Western Australia, on 4 June 1629. The events that followed — a mutiny led by the undermerchant Jeronimus Cornelisz and a massacre of many of the shipwreck survivors — have made the Batavia one of the most notorious shipwreck stories in maritime history. Of around 322 people aboard, roughly 40 were lost in the wreck itself and more than 100 survivors were murdered during the mutiny.

Construction and voyage

The Batavia was built in Amsterdam in 1628 as the flagship of a fleet of VOC vessels bound for the East Indies. She carried a rich cargo, including silver coins, trade goods, and building blocks for a portico intended for the port of Batavia (now Jakarta). The ship was commanded by Francisco Pelsaert, with Ariaen Jacobsz as skipper and Jeronimus Cornelisz as second-in-command (undermerchant). Cornelisz and Jacobsz plotted a mutiny during the voyage, which was interrupted when the ship struck the reef in the Houtman Abrolhos islands.

Wreck and massacre

After the wreck, Pelsaert sailed in an open boat with a small party to Batavia to seek rescue — an epic voyage of some 1,500 nautical miles. Left in charge, Cornelisz established a reign of terror on the islands, organising the systematic murder of survivors to reduce competition for supplies and to consolidate his power. When Pelsaert returned with the rescue ship Sardam, Cornelisz and his followers were captured after a battle with a group of loyal soldiers under Wiebbe Hayes, who had resisted the mutineers on West Wallabi Island. Cornelisz and several of his men were hanged on Seal Island; others were taken to Batavia for trial and punishment.

Legacy

The wreck site was identified in 1963, and artefacts, including the ship's stern, cannons and a reconstructed portico, were recovered in the 1970s and are displayed at the WA Shipwrecks Museum in Fremantle, Western Australia. A full-size replica of the ship was built in the Netherlands under the direction of Willem Vos and launched in 1995. The Batavia story has been retold in numerous books, an opera, and television documentaries, and is sometimes compared to the saga of the Bounty and to Lord of the Flies.
