Actinide

Seriesf-block, period 7
ElementsActinium (89) through lawrencium (103)
Electron filling5f subshell

The actinide series comprises the 15 metallic chemical elements with atomic numbers from 89 to 103, actinium through lawrencium. Named after actinium, the first element of the series, the actinides occupy the second row of the f-block in the periodic table and are characterised by the progressive filling of the 5f electron shell.

Properties

All actinides are radioactive, and most have short half-lives. Only thorium and uranium occur in significant quantities in nature; neptunium and plutonium occur in trace amounts in uranium ores, while the remaining actinides are produced artificially in nuclear reactors or particle accelerators. The early actinides (thorium, protactinium, uranium) show varied oxidation states resembling transition metals, whereas the later members behave more like the lanthanides, favouring the +3 state.

Seaborg's actinide concept, proposed in the 1940s, placed these elements in a distinct series analogous to the lanthanides and guided the discovery of the transuranium elements.

Applications

Uranium and plutonium are fuels for nuclear reactors and weapons. Americium-241 is used in household smoke detectors, curium and californium serve as neutron sources, and thorium has been investigated as an alternative nuclear fuel. The management of long-lived actinides in spent nuclear fuel is a central problem of radioactive waste disposal.
