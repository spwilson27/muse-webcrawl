Christianization of Lithuania

The Christianization of Lithuania occurred in 1387, when Grand Duke Jogaila (Władysław II Jagiełło) was baptized Catholic, married Jadwiga of Poland, and ordered the conversion of Lithuania — the last pagan state in Europe. Samogitia followed in 1413–1417.

Background

Earlier attempts included Mindaugas's baptism in 1251, later repudiated. Lithuanian paganism, centred on sacred groves and fire worship, persisted while the Teutonic Knights used its pagan status to justify crusades. The Union of Krewo (1385) linked conversion to the Polish alliance.

Process

Jogaila personally preached in Lithuanian, destroyed sacred sites, and founded Vilnius Cathedral and parishes. Mass baptisms were conducted in groups. Pagan customs survived in folklore for centuries.
