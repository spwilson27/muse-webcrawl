Espionage

Espionage is the clandestine gathering of confidential information.

Espionage, or spying, is the act of obtaining secret or confidential information from non-disclosed sources, or divulging it without the permission of the holder, for political, military, commercial or security advantage. A person who commits espionage is called an espionage agent or spy. Espionage is a clandestine component of intelligence gathering, distinct from open-source intelligence.

Methods

Spies may be recruited agents with access to classified material, or officers operating under diplomatic or non-official cover. Techniques include human intelligence (HUMINT), interception of communications (SIGINT), covert photography, dead drops, encrypted communication and, increasingly, cyber espionage against computer networks. "Moles" are agents placed or recruited inside a target organisation for long-term penetration.

History

Espionage is ancient: the biblical account of spies sent into Canaan, Sun Tzu's Art of War chapter on spies, and the intelligence networks of imperial Rome and China all attest to its antiquity. The modern era saw professional services emerge in the First and Second World Wars—Britain's MI6, Germany's Abwehr, and the American OSS, precursor of the CIA. During the Cold War, espionage figured prominently in confrontations between the United States and the Soviet Union, with famous cases including the Cambridge Five and the Rosenbergs.

Law

Most states criminalise espionage against them; in the United States the Espionage Act of 1917 remains a key statute, while the United Kingdom replaced its Official Secrets Acts framework with the National Security Act 2023. Under international humanitarian law, captured spies operating clandestinely behind enemy lines may be denied prisoner-of-war status, though they retain rights to a fair trial. Diplomatic spies caught in the act are typically declared persona non grata and expelled.

Industrial and cyber espionage

States and corporations also conduct espionage to steal trade secrets, technology and negotiating positions. Cyber espionage—intrusions attributed to state-sponsored groups—has become a dominant twenty-first-century concern, targeting governments, defence contractors and intellectual property.
