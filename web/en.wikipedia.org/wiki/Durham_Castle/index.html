Durham Castle

Durham Castle is a Norman castle in the city of Durham, England, standing on a hill above the River Wear opposite Durham Cathedral. It has been occupied continuously since the 11th century: first as the fortress and palace of the prince-bishops of Durham, and since 1837 as University College, Durham, the founding college of Durham University. Together with the cathedral, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

History

The castle was built on the orders of William the Conqueror in 1072, shortly after the Norman conquest and the Harrying of the North, to control the Scots-facing frontier and the restive north. It was entrusted to the Bishops of Durham, who as prince-bishops wielded near-regal palatine powers. Notable surviving features include the Norman chapel (c. 1078, among the oldest parts), the great hall built by Bishop Antony Bek in the early 14th century, the Black Staircase, and the rebuilt keep.

University College

When Durham University was founded in 1832, Bishop Edward Maltby gave the castle to the university, and it became University College in 1837. Around a hundred students still live within the castle walls, dining in the great hall, making it one of the few castles in England in everyday residential use as a student hall.
