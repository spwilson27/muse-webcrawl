E–Z notation

E–Z notation (or E/Z isomerism) is the IUPAC nomenclature system for describing the absolute stereochemistry of double bonds, such as the carbon–carbon double bond in alkenes. The labels derive from the German entgegen ("opposite") and zusammen ("together").

Assignment

At each end of the double bond, the two substituents are ranked by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) priority rules, based on the atomic number of the atoms directly attached. If the two higher-priority groups are on the same side of the double bond, the configuration is Z (zusammen); if they are on opposite sides, it is E (entgegen). The descriptor is written in italics in parentheses before the name, e.g. (E)-but-2-ene.

Relation to cis/trans

The older cis/trans notation works only when each carbon bears one identical or comparable group; E–Z notation generalises the description to any substitution pattern, including trisubstituted and tetrasubstituted alkenes and C=N and N=N bonds. For simple cases, cis usually corresponds to Z and trans to E, but this is not always so, because CIP priorities may reverse the apparent correspondence.
