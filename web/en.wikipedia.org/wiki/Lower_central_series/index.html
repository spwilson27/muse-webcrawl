In mathematics, the lower central series of a group G is the descending chain of subgroups defined by γ1(G) = G and γn+1(G) = [γn(G), G], where [H, G] denotes the subgroup generated by all commutators of elements of H with elements of G.

Properties

Each term of the series is a characteristic — hence normal — subgroup of G. The successive quotients γn(G)/γn+1(G) are abelian. A group is called nilpotent of class c if the series reaches the trivial subgroup in c+1 steps, i.e. γc+1(G) = {1}. Free groups have lower central series that intersect trivially, and the associated graded Lie algebra formed from the quotients is a central tool in the study of free groups and in algebraic topology, where the series arises for fundamental groups and in the theory of link invariants via Milnor invariants.

Examples

For an abelian group, γ2(G) is already trivial. For the Heisenberg group of upper-triangular 3×3 matrices over a ring, the second term is the centre and the third is trivial, so the group is nilpotent of class 2.
