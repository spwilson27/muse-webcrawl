Telenovela

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A telenovela is a type of television serial drama, particularly popular in Latin America, typically featuring a limited run of roughly 120 episodes and a melodramatic storyline centred on romantic relationships. Unlike open-ended soap operas, telenovelas end with a planned conclusion in which storylines are resolved.

History

The genre descends from Latin American radionovelas and fotonovelas. Television production began in Cuba, Brazil, and Mexico in the 1950s; Mexican broadcaster Televisa and Brazil's TV Globo became dominant producers. The Venezuelan production Cristal (1985) and Mexican Los ricos también lloran (1979) achieved enormous international success, the latter becoming a phenomenon in post-Soviet Russia and China.

Characteristics

Telenovelas usually air in prime time five or six nights per week, rely on cliffhangers, and mix romance with themes of class conflict, family secrets, and social mobility. Remakes and format sales are common across countries; successful titles have been exported to more than 100 countries.

Variants

Subgenres include historical, youth (juvenil), comedy, and "narco-novelas". The Philippine teleserye and Korean drama share some structural features but developed independently.
