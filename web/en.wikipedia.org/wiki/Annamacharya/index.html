Annamacharya

Born22 May 1408, Tallapaka, Andhra Pradesh, India
Died4 April 1503 (aged 94), Tirupati
OccupationSaint, composer, poet
Known forSamkirtanas in praise of Venkateswara

Annamacharya (1408–1503), also known as Annamayya, was a 15th-century Hindu saint and composer from Tallapaka in present-day Andhra Pradesh. He is regarded as one of the earliest Indian musicians to compose devotional songs known as samkirtanas, and is honoured as the "grandfather of Telugu song-writing" (Pada-kavita Pitamaha).

Life

Annamacharya was born into a Nandavarika Brahmin family in the village of Tallapaka, near Tirupati. According to tradition, he was an incarnation of the sword of Lord Vishnu. As a youth he climbed the Tirumala hills and entered the service of Venkateswara, the deity of the Tirumala temple, to whom nearly all his songs are addressed. He is also said to have debated with the Advaita scholar and later accepted initiation into Sri Vaishnavism.

Works

Tradition credits Annamacharya with composing 32,000 samkirtanas, of which about 12,000 survive, engraved on copper plates discovered in a sealed chamber of the Tirumala temple in the 20th century. His songs, written in Telugu, combine devotional fervour with sharp social comment, rejecting caste distinctions and declaring that devotion alone brings salvation. Famous compositions include devotional lyrics still sung daily at Tirumala and popularised in the 20th century by singers such as M. S. Subbulakshmi and Mangalampalli Balamuralikrishna.

Legacy

The Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams promotes the Annamacharya Project to publish and record his compositions. Temples and statues honour him across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and his life was depicted in the 1997 Telugu film Annamayya.
