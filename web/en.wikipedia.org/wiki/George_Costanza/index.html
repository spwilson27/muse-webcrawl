George Louis Costanza is a fictional character in the American television sitcom Seinfeld (1989–1998), played by Jason Alexander. He is Jerry Seinfeld's best friend since childhood and a regular member of the show's central quartet, alongside Jerry, Elaine Benes and Cosmo Kramer.

George is characterised as neurotic, insecure, cheap, dishonest and perpetually unlucky in work and romance. The character was largely based on the show's co-creator, Larry David, and Alexander's performance won widespread acclaim.

Character

George grew up in Brooklyn and attended public school with Jerry. He lives in the shadow of his overbearing parents, Frank and Estelle Costanza, whose shouting matches are a recurring feature of the series. Despite brief periods of employment — most notably as assistant to the traveling secretary of the New York Yankees — George is frequently unemployed and schemes constantly to avoid work while appearing busy.

Notable storylines

George's storylines include his engagement to Susan Ross, who dies after licking the cheap toxic envelopes George chose for their wedding invitations; his invention of the alias "Art Vandelay," a supposed architect and importer/exporter; his pretence of being a marine biologist to impress a woman, culminating in him removing a golf ball from the blowhole of a beached whale; and the episode "The Opposite," in which George decides to do the opposite of every instinct and is rewarded with success. He also co-creates, with Jerry, a "show about nothing" pitched to NBC — a metafictional version of Seinfeld itself.

Reception

George Costanza is widely regarded as one of the greatest television characters of all time. Jason Alexander was nominated for seven consecutive Primetime Emmy Awards for the role, in addition to Golden Globe nominations.
