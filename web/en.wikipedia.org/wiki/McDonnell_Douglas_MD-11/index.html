The McDonnell Douglas MD-11 is an American wide-body airliner manufactured by McDonnell Douglas and, later, by Boeing. A lengthened and more efficient development of the DC-10 trijet, it features a stretched fuselage, winglets, a two-crew glass cockpit, and more powerful engines. Two hundred were built between 1988 and 2000.

Development

The MD-11 programme was launched in December 1986 with orders from airlines including British Caledonian and Swissair. The aircraft first flew in January 1990 and entered service with Finnair later that year. Variants included the passenger MD-11, the all-freight MD-11F, convertible and combi versions, and the extended-range MD-11ER.

Operational history

Early aircraft fell short of promised range and fuel-burn figures, and the type lost ground to twin-engine rivals such as the Boeing 777 and Airbus A330/A340, which benefited from ETOPS liberalisation. Passenger operators progressively retired the type — KLM operated the last scheduled passenger MD-11 flight in October 2014 — but the freighter version remained popular with cargo carriers such as FedEx Express, UPS Airlines, and Lufthansa Cargo. Following Boeing's merger with McDonnell Douglas, production ended in 2000, with the final aircraft delivered in 2001.

Specifications (MD-11ER)

  • Crew: 2 flight crew
  • Capacity: up to 410 passengers (single class)
  • Length: 61.2 m (200 ft 10 in)
  • Wingspan: 51.7 m (169 ft 6 in)
  • Engines: 3 × General Electric CF6-80C2 or Pratt & Whitney PW4000 turbofans
  • Range: about 13,400 km (7,240 nmi)
