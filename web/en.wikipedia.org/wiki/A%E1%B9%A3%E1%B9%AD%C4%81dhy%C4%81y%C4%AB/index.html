Aṣṭādhyāyī

The Aṣṭādhyāyī (अष्टाध्यायी, "Eight Chapters") is a Sanskrit treatise on grammar written by the ancient Indian scholar Pāṇini, conventionally dated to roughly the 5th–4th century BCE. It is one of the earliest surviving works on descriptive linguistics and the foundational text of the Vyākaraṇa tradition, defining Classical Sanskrit morphology and syntax with extraordinary precision.

Structure

The text comprises about 4,000 aphoristic rules (sūtras) arranged in eight chapters of four quarters each. The sūtras operate as a highly compressed generative system: using meta-rules, markers, and rule-ordering conventions, they generate all and only the correct forms of the language. Its introductory Śiva-sūtras organize the sounds of Sanskrit into classes exploited throughout the grammar.

Influence

Pāṇini's grammar was preserved and elaborated by commentators such as Kātyāyana and Patañjali. In the 19th century it profoundly influenced Western linguistics; its formal methods have often been compared to modern formal grammars and computing, and Leonard Bloomfield called Pāṇini's work "one of the greatest monuments of human intelligence."
