Salsette Island

Location: Arabian Sea, Maharashtra, India
Area: 619 km²
Population: over 15 million (approx.)
Major cities: Mumbai (part), Thane, Mira-Bhayandar

Salsette Island (Marathi: साष्टी) is a large island on the western coast of Maharashtra, India. It is one of the most densely populated islands in the world, containing most of the city of Mumbai, the city of Thane, and the Mira-Bhayandar municipality. The island is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Vasai Creek to the north, Thane Creek to the east, and Mumbai Harbour to the southeast.

Geography

Salsette is roughly 619 square kilometres. Much of its interior is occupied by Sanjay Gandhi National Park, one of the largest national parks within a metropolis, which contains the ancient Kanheri Caves carved by Buddhist monks. The island's highest point is about 450 metres. Historically it was separated from the smaller island of Bombay to its south; the two were joined by causeways and land reclamation during the 19th century, so that greater Mumbai now spreads across them.

History

The island's caves attest to settlement since ancient times. The region was ruled successively by Hindu and Muslim dynasties, then by Portugal from 1534, when it was ceded by the Sultan of Gujarat. Under Portuguese rule many churches were built, and East Indian Catholic communities formed. The Marathas under Chimaji Appa conquered the island in 1739, and the British took it by the Treaty of Salbai in 1782. Today it forms the core of the Mumbai Metropolitan Region.
