Digital marketing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Digital marketing is the component of marketing that uses the Internet and online-based digital technologies — such as desktop computers, mobile phones, and other digital media and platforms — to promote products and services. It developed during the 1990s and 2000s and changed the way brands reach consumers, enabling precise targeting, real-time measurement, and interactive, two-way communication rather than one-way broadcast advertising. It is sometimes called online marketing, and is often contrasted with traditional marketing through print, television, radio, and direct mail.

History

The first clickable web banner advertisement ran in 1994 on HotWired.com for AT&T, reportedly achieving a 44% click-through rate. Search engines soon followed: Google (founded 1998) launched AdWords in 2000, introducing pay-per-click advertising auctioned against search keywords. The rise of social media platforms in the 2000s (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram) added social marketing and influencer marketing, while the smartphone shifted much digital activity to mobile devices and apps. Programmatic advertising automated the buying of ad impressions in real time.

Channels and techniques

  • Search engine optimisation (SEO) — improving a site's visibility in organic search results.
  • Search engine marketing (SEM) — paid search advertising, typically pay-per-click.
  • Content marketing — creating articles, videos, and other media to attract and retain an audience.
  • Social media marketing — promotion through social platforms, organic and paid.
  • Email marketing — direct messages to opted-in lists, including automation and newsletters.
  • Display and video advertising — banners and pre-roll ads across the web.
  • Affiliate and influencer marketing — paying third parties commissions or fees for promotion.
  • Marketing analytics — measuring funnels, attribution, and return on ad spend.

Regulation and privacy

Digital marketing depends heavily on data about users, which has brought it under increasing regulation: the EU's GDPR (2018) and ePrivacy rules govern consent and cookies, and similar laws have followed elsewhere. The advertising industry has faced criticism over behavioural tracking, fraud, and brand safety, while platform changes — such as Apple's App Tracking Transparency (2021) and the deprecation of third-party cookies in browsers — have pushed marketers toward first-party data and contextual targeting.
