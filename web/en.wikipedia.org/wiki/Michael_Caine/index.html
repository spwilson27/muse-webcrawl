Sir Michael Caine (born Maurice Joseph Micklewhite; 14 March 1933) is an English retired actor. Known for his Cockney accent and distinctive horn-rimmed glasses, he appeared in more than 160 films over a career spanning eight decades and is regarded as one of the most accomplished and recognisable British actors of his generation, one of only a handful nominated for an Academy Award for acting in five different decades.

Career

Born in Bermondsey to a working-class family, Caine did National Service in Korea before years of repertory theatre and small television roles. His breakthrough came in Zulu (1964), playing the upper-class officer Gonville Bromhead — against his own background — and stardom followed as spy Harry Palmer in The Ipcress File (1965) and the womanising title character of Alfie (1966), which earned an Oscar nomination. Iconic roles of the era include The Italian Job (1969), famous for the line "You're only supposed to blow the bloody doors off," and Get Carter (1971).

Caine won Academy Awards for Best Supporting Actor for Hannah and Her Sisters (1986) and The Cider House Rules (1999), with further acclaimed performances in Sleuth (1972), The Man Who Would Be King (1975), Educating Rita (1983), Little Voice (1998), and The Quiet American (2002). A younger generation knows him as Alfred Pennyworth in Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy (2005–2012) and for roles in Inception (2010) and Interstellar (2014); Caine appeared in eight of Nolan's films. He was knighted in 2000 under his birth name, as Maurice Micklewhite. In October 2023, at age 90, he confirmed his retirement from acting following The Great Escaper.

Cultural image

His catchphrase in popular culture, "Not a lot of people know that," originated in an anecdote by Peter Sellers and was adopted by Caine himself. He is one of the highest-grossing actors of all time, with his films grossing over $7.8 billion worldwide.
