Root canal treatment

Root canal treatment (also known as endodontic therapy, endodontic treatment, or root canal therapy) is a treatment sequence for the infected pulp of a tooth which is intended to result in the elimination of infection and the protection of the decontaminated tooth from future microbial invasion.

Indications

Root canal therapy is used when the dental pulp becomes inflamed (pulpitis) or infected, most often due to deep tooth decay, repeated dental procedures on the tooth, or a crack or chip. Irreversible pulpitis and pulp necrosis cannot heal on their own, and treatment consists of either extraction or removal of the pulp.

Procedure

Under local anaesthesia, the dentist or endodontist opens the tooth, removes the pulp tissue, and cleans, shapes and disinfects the root canals using endodontic files and irrigants such as sodium hypochlorite. The canals are then filled and sealed, most commonly with gutta-percha, and the tooth is restored, usually with a crown.

Outcomes

When performed well, root canal treatment has a high success rate, and treated teeth can function for many years. Possible complications include persistent infection, instrument fracture, and reinfection if the coronal seal fails.
