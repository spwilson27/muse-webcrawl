101955 Bennu (provisional designation 1999 RQ36) is a carbonaceous near-Earth asteroid in the Apollo group, discovered by the LINEAR project in 1999. It is named after Bennu, an ancient Egyptian deity associated with the Sun, creation, and rebirth, usually depicted as a heron. About 490 metres across, Bennu is a loosely bound "rubble pile" with a distinctive spinning-top shape, rotating once every 4.3 hours.

OSIRIS-REx mission

Bennu was the target of NASA's OSIRIS-REx sample-return mission, launched in 2016. The spacecraft arrived in December 2018, mapped the asteroid, and on 20 October 2020 touched down at the Nightingale site to collect surface regolith, gathering at least 121.6 grams of material. The sample capsule returned to Earth on 24 September 2023, landing in Utah. Analysis of the samples revealed abundant water-bearing clay minerals, carbon, nitrogen, and amino acids and other organic compounds of interest to the study of the origin of life.

Impact hazard

Bennu is classified as a potentially hazardous object. Orbital calculations show a small cumulative probability of impact with Earth in the late 22nd century, with the most significant potential encounter on 24 September 2182, at a probability of roughly 1 in 2,700. The Yarkovsky effect, measured precisely by OSIRIS-REx, slowly alters its orbit.
