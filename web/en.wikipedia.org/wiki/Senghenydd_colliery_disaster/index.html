Senghenydd colliery disaster

The Senghenydd colliery disaster, also known as the Senghenydd explosion, occurred at the Universal Colliery in Senghenydd, near Caerphilly, in Glamorgan, south Wales, on 14 October 1913. A devastating explosion of firedamp and coal dust killed 439 miners and one rescuer, making it the worst mining accident in the history of the United Kingdom. The colliery had already suffered an explosion in 1901 that killed 81 men.

Background

The Universal Colliery, owned by the Lewis Merthyr Consolidated Collieries group, worked the steam-coal seams of the South Wales Coalfield. The earlier explosion of May 1901 had killed 81 of the 82 men underground on the night shift. Despite safety recommendations made after 1901, and despite new requirements in the Coal Mines Act 1911, firedamp remained poorly controlled in the pit's west side workings, and electric signalling equipment capable of sparking was in use.

The explosion

At about 8:00 a.m. on 14 October 1913, shortly after the day shift of some 950 men had descended, a huge explosion tore through the western workings. It is generally believed that firedamp accumulated at the coalface was ignited by a spark from electric signalling apparatus, and that the initial blast raised coal dust which propagated the explosion through miles of roadways. Fire followed the blast, and rescuers fought heat, falls of roof and poisonous afterdamp; some survivors were recovered alive days later, famously including a group found behind falls after more than a week of rescue work. In all, 439 miners and one rescuer died, leaving hundreds of widows and fatherless children in the small village.

Aftermath

The subsequent inquiry found fault with the colliery's ventilation and safety practices, but the penalties imposed on the company and its manager were widely regarded as derisory — the manager was fined a small sum, and charges against the company were largely dismissed. The disaster became a lasting symbol of the human cost of the coal industry and of the inadequacy of early mining-safety enforcement. A national memorial to all who died in the Welsh mining industry, centred on a statue of a rescuer, was unveiled at Senghenydd on the centenary of the disaster in 2013.
