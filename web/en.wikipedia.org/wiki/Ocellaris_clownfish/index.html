Ocellaris clownfish

Scientific nameAmphiprion ocellaris
FamilyPomacentridae
LengthUp to 11 cm
RangeIndo-Pacific reefs

The ocellaris clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris), also known as the common clownfish, is a marine fish belonging to the family Pomacentridae, which includes clownfishes and damselfishes. It is widely known from the 2003 animated film Finding Nemo, whose title character is an ocellaris clownfish.

Description

The ocellaris clownfish grows to about 11 cm in length. Its body is bright orange with three white stripes outlined in black: one behind the eye, one in the middle of the body bulging forward, and one at the base of the tail. It closely resembles the true percula clownfish (Amphiprion percula), but the two can be distinguished by stripe thickness and the number of dorsal spines.

Mutualism with anemones

Ocellaris clownfish live in a mutualistic symbiosis with sea anemones, sheltering among their stinging tentacles, to which the fish are protected by a mucus coating. The anemone gains nutrients from the fish's waste and defence against polyp-eating fish, while the clownfish gains protection from predators. Host species include the magnificent sea anemone, the giant carpet anemone, and the Mertens' carpet sea anemone.

Social structure and reproduction

Clownfish live in hierarchical groups in which the largest individual is the breeding female and the second-largest the breeding male; the species is a protandrous hermaphrodite, so if the female dies the dominant male changes sex. Eggs are laid on a flat surface near the anemone and guarded by the male until they hatch.

Aquarium trade

The species is one of the most popular marine aquarium fish and is widely bred in captivity, which has reduced pressure on wild populations. Colour variants, including black-and-white and "snowflake" forms, have been selectively bred.
