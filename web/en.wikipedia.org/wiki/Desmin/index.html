Desmin

Desmin intermediate filaments surround the Z-discs of muscle fibres
GeneDES (human)
TypeType III intermediate filament protein
Location2q35 (human chromosome)
Expressed inSkeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle

Desmin is a type III intermediate filament protein found in muscle cells, where it is a key structural component of the cytoskeleton. In humans it is encoded by the DES gene. Desmin filaments encircle the Z-discs of the sarcomere, linking individual myofibrils laterally to one another and to the cell membrane and nucleus, and thereby maintaining the structural and mechanical integrity of the contractile apparatus.

Function

Desmin is one of the earliest protein markers of muscle differentiation and is expressed in skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle. Beyond a passive scaffolding role, the desmin network helps position mitochondria and other organelles and participates in mechanical signalling within the muscle cell.

Clinical significance

Mutations in DES cause desmin-related myofibrillar myopathy, a group of disorders characterised by abnormal aggregates of desmin and other proteins in muscle fibres, leading to progressive skeletal muscle weakness, cardiac conduction defects and cardiomyopathy. Desmin immunostaining is also used diagnostically in pathology to identify tumours of muscle origin.
