Uskoks

Active: 16th–17th centuries

Base: Senj, Croatia

Opponents: Ottoman Empire, Republic of Venice

The Uskoks (Croatian: Uskoci) were irregular soldiers and maritime raiders of the Croatian Military Frontier during the 16th and early 17th centuries. Refugees from Ottoman-conquered lands, they settled first at Klis and later at Senj under Habsburg protection, from where they raided Ottoman territory and, notoriously, Venetian shipping in the Adriatic.

Name and origins

The name derives from the Croatian word uskočiti, "to jump in" — referring to deserters or refugees who joined their ranks. Recruited from Croats, Serbs, Vlachs and others fleeing Ottoman rule, the Uskoks combined guerrilla warfare on land with piracy at sea in small, fast boats.

Conflicts

Uskok raids provoked repeated crises between the Habsburgs and Venice, culminating in the Uskok War of 1615–1618. By the Treaty of Madrid (1617), the Habsburgs agreed to remove the Uskoks from Senj; many were resettled inland in the Žumberak hills, ending their career as Adriatic corsairs. They remain heroic figures in Croatian folk tradition and literature.
