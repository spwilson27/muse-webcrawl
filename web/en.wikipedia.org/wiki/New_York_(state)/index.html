New York, also called New York State, is a state in the Northeastern United States. One of the original thirteen colonies, it was the eleventh state to ratify the U.S. Constitution in 1788. With about 19.6–20 million residents it is the fourth-most populous state. Its capital is Albany and its largest city is New York City, the most populous city in the United States and a global centre of finance, culture, and media; nearly half of the state's population lives in the city and its metropolitan area.

History

The region was inhabited by Algonquian peoples and the Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee). Henry Hudson's 1609 voyage for the Dutch led to the colony of New Netherland (1614) and New Amsterdam (1626), which the English seized in 1664, renaming it New York after the Duke of York. The state was a major theatre of the American Revolutionary War, including the pivotal Battle of Saratoga (1777). The Erie Canal (completed 1825), linking the Hudson River to the Great Lakes, made New York City the nation's premier port and spurred upstate growth. New York was the most populous state from the 1810s until California passed it in the 1960s.

Geography

New York borders Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Vermont, Lake Ontario, Lake Erie, and the Canadian provinces of Ontario and Quebec. Its terrain includes the Adirondack Mountains — the largest state park in the contiguous United States — the Catskills, the Finger Lakes, the Hudson Valley, Long Island, and Niagara Falls on the Ontario border. The state is informally divided into "Downstate" (New York City, Long Island, and the lower Hudson Valley) and "Upstate" (everything north and west).

Economy

New York's gross state product is roughly $2 trillion, among the largest subnational economies in the world. New York City dominates finance (Wall Street), media, technology, fashion, and tourism; upstate centres include Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse, Albany (including its "Tech Valley"), and Binghamton. Agriculture is significant, with New York a top producer of apples, grapes, and dairy products.

Demographics and culture

New York is one of the most ethnically diverse states, with large African American, Italian, Irish, Jewish, Puerto Rican, Dominican, and Asian communities; Ellis Island processed millions of immigrants arriving through New York Harbor. The state is home to world-renowned institutions including Broadway theatre, major museums, Ivy League Cornell University, and the State University of New York (SUNY) system.

Government

The state government, seated in Albany, comprises an elected governor and the bicameral New York State Legislature (Senate and Assembly). New York sends two senators and 26 representatives to the U.S. Congress (as apportioned after the 2020 census). The state is known for landmark policies from the abolition of slavery (1827) to the first U.S. women's suffrage referendum movement launched at Seneca Falls (1848).
