Company (military unit)

Typical size80–250 soldiers
Commanded byCaptain or Major
Subunits3–5 platoons
Part ofBattalion or regiment

A company is a military unit, typically consisting of 80–250 soldiers and usually commanded by a major or a captain. Most companies are formed of three to seven platoons, although the exact number may vary by country, unit type, and functional specialty. Companies are the smallest military unit normally commanded by a commissioned officer above the rank of lieutenant, and they are usually the largest sub-unit of a battalion.

Organization

In most modern armies, an infantry company consists of a company headquarters and three rifle platoons, often with a weapons platoon providing mortars, machine guns, and anti-armour weapons. The company commander is assisted by an executive officer (second-in-command), a first sergeant or company sergeant major, and headquarters personnel such as signallers, medics, and supply staff.

Several companies grouped together form a battalion, commanded by a lieutenant colonel. In the British Army and many Commonwealth forces, the equivalent cavalry and armoured unit is a squadron, and in artillery units the equivalent is a battery.

Historical development

The company emerged as a standard tactical unit in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, as the companies of pikemen and musketeers of Spanish, Dutch, and Swedish armies were grouped under captains who often raised and financed the men themselves. The term derives from the Latin companio, originally denoting those who share bread. During the Napoleonic Wars and the American Civil War, the company of roughly one hundred men was the basic manoeuvre and administrative unit of infantry regiments.

Specialized companies

Beyond rifle companies, armies field headquarters companies, support companies, signal companies, engineer companies, medical companies, and military police companies, each providing a specific function to a battalion or brigade. In the United States Marine Corps, companies are also the building blocks of Marine Expeditionary Units.
