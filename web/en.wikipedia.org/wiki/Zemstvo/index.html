A zemstvo (Russian: земство, plural zemstva) was an institution of local self-government established in the Russian Empire by the reform edict of Tsar Alexander II in 1864, in the wake of the emancipation of the serfs. Zemstvos were created at district (uezd) and provincial (guberniya) levels in most provinces of European Russia.

Functions

Elected assemblies — with seats allocated among landowners, urban property holders, and peasant communities — were responsible for roads, primary education, public health, veterinary services, famine relief, agricultural improvement, and statistics. They employed a professional staff of doctors, teachers, agronomists, and statisticians known as the "third element", and their statistical studies became famous sources on rural Russia.

Significance

The zemstvos gave educated Russians experience of public administration and became seedbeds of liberal politics; zemstvo liberals campaigned for a national representative body and fed into the Constitutional Democratic Party after 1905. Although their powers were repeatedly curtailed by the autocracy, the zemstvos' practical achievements in medicine and schooling were widely admired. They were dissolved following the October Revolution.
