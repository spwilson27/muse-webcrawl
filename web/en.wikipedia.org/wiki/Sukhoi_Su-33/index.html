The Su-33 (NATO: Flanker-D) is Russia's carrier-based fighter.

The Sukhoi Su-33 (Russian: Сухой Су-33; NATO reporting name: Flanker-D) is a Soviet/Russian all-weather carrier-based twin-engine air superiority fighter designed by Sukhoi and manufactured by Komsomolsk-on-Amur Aircraft Production Association. It is a derivative of the Su-27 'Flanker' adapted for operations from aircraft carriers.

Design and development

Developed in the 1980s as the Su-27K for the Soviet Navy's new carriers, the Su-33 features folding wings and tailplanes, an arrestor hook, strengthened landing gear, canard foreplanes for low-speed lift, and an in-flight refuelling probe. It operates from the carrier Admiral Kuznetsov using a ski-jump ramp and arrested landings (STOBAR). Armament includes a 30 mm GSh-30-1 cannon and up to 12 hardpoints for R-27 and R-73 air-to-air missiles.

Operational history

The type entered service in 1998 with the Russian Navy's 279th Shipborne Fighter Aviation Regiment. In November 2016 Su-33s flew combat sorties from Admiral Kuznetsov off Syria, the type's first combat deployment; two aircraft were lost in landing accidents during the cruise. Russia has modernised part of the small fleet while the MiG-29K takes on a growing share of carrier aviation duties. China's Shenyang J-15 is an unlicensed development based on an Su-33 prototype acquired from Ukraine.
