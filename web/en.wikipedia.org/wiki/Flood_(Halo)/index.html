Flood (Halo)

The Flood is a parasitic alien species in the Halo science fiction universe, created by Bungie and first appearing in the 2001 video game Halo: Combat Evolved. The Flood is driven by a desire to infect any sentient life of sufficient biomass, converting hosts into Flood forms and assimilating their knowledge into a collective intelligence coordinated by a Gravemind. In the series' backstory, the Flood's war with the ancient Forerunners led them to fire the Halo Array — ringworld weapons that destroyed all sentient life in the galaxy to starve the Flood — 100,000 years before the games' events.

Forms and biology

The Flood spreads through infection forms — small, balloon-like creatures that latch onto hosts, insert a proboscis into the nervous system, and rewrite the host's DNA, transforming it into a shambling combat form. Decomposed or damaged hosts become carrier forms that incubate new infection forms. Flood "pure forms," introduced in Halo 3, are created directly from biomass without a host and can shapeshift between stalker, ranged, and tank morphologies. As biomass accumulates, Flood colonies form a hive mind; at sufficient scale a Gravemind emerges, a highly intelligent coordinating intelligence. Gameplay-wise, the Flood is distinguished by relentless swarming, the ability to wield weapons via combat forms, and (in most games) vulnerability to energy weapons and melee.

Appearances

The Flood debuts midway through Halo: Combat Evolved in the level "343 Guilty Spark," a famous tonal shift from military science fiction to horror. It returns in Halo 2 (where the Gravemind speaks), Halo 3 (invading Earth and the Ark), and in spin-offs and expanded-universe fiction, including the Forerunner Saga novels by Greg Bear, which depict the Flood's origin as a corrupted remnant of the Precursors. The Flood appears as an enemy faction in Halo Wars and Halo Wars 2's Awakening the Nightmare expansion.

Reception

The Flood's mid-game reveal is frequently cited among gaming's most memorable twists, though some players criticize Flood levels (notably "The Library") as repetitive. Critics have praised the creature design and the way the Flood recontextualizes the Covenant war storyline.
