The United Front Work Department (UFWD) is a department of the Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) responsible for "united front" work: managing relations with, and seeking to influence, social and political groups outside the Party, both inside China and abroad. It reports directly to the CCP Central Committee and has counterpart organs at every level of the party-state.

Functions

Domestically, the UFWD liaises with China's eight legally permitted minor ("democratic") parties, the All-China Federation of Industry and Commerce, ethnic minority and religious affairs bodies, and prominent non-party figures. Following institutional reforms in 2018, the department absorbed the State Administration for Religious Affairs and the Overseas Chinese Affairs Office, expanding its remit over religious policy and diaspora affairs.

Internationally, the department and affiliated organizations work with overseas Chinese communities, students, businesses, and cultural associations. Foreign governments and analysts frequently describe these activities as a channel of Chinese influence operations; several countries have scrutinized UFWD-linked bodies in debates over foreign interference, while Beijing maintains that united front work is a legitimate, open part of its political system aimed at building consensus and national unity.

History

United front strategy dates to the CCP's alliances of the 1920s–40s, including cooperation with the Kuomintang against Japan. A central united front organ was formalized in the 1940s; Li Weihan was a prominent early head. The department was sidelined during the Cultural Revolution and restored after 1978 under Deng Xiaoping, when united front work was revived to mobilize expertise and capital for reform. Its prominence rose further in the 2010s, with a central leading small group (later commission) on united front work established in 2015.
