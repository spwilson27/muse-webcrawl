University Circle

University Circle is a district on the east side of Cleveland, Ohio, notable for its concentration of educational, cultural, and medical institutions within about one square mile. It is home to Case Western Reserve University, the Cleveland Museum of Art, Severance Hall (home of the Cleveland Orchestra), University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center, and the Cleveland Clinic's main campus is nearby. The district is often described as the cultural and educational heart of Northeast Ohio.

History

The area takes its name from a streetcar turnaround — a literal circle — at Euclid Avenue and what is now Stokes Boulevard. In the 1880s, Western Reserve University and the Case School of Applied Science moved to the district, followed by cultural institutions attracted by its park-like setting along Wade Park. The Cleveland Museum of Art opened in 1916, and Severance Hall followed in 1931. The two universities merged in 1967 to form Case Western Reserve University.

Development

The nonprofit University Circle Inc., founded in 1957, coordinates planning, policing, and development for the district. Major recent projects have included the Uptown mixed-use development along Euclid Avenue and new residential construction, as the district's institutions together employ tens of thousands of people and attract more than two million visitors annually. The area is served by RTA rapid transit on the Red Line and the HealthLine bus rapid transit corridor.
