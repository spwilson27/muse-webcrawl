Plymouth is a ghost town and the de jure capital of the island of Montserrat, an overseas territory of the United Kingdom in the Caribbean Sea. Located on the southwest coast of the island, the town was evacuated and abandoned in 1997 after being destroyed by eruptions of the Soufrière Hills volcano. It remains Montserrat's official capital, making it the only abandoned capital city in the world, while the de facto seat of government is at Brades.

History

Plymouth was founded on the site of an early settlement near the mouth of a ravine on Montserrat's leeward coast. For centuries it served as the island's main port and commercial centre, with a population of around 4,000 at its peak. Its Georgian-era architecture, built of local stone, made it one of the more attractive small towns in the Caribbean.

Volcanic eruption and abandonment

In July 1995, the Soufrière Hills volcano, which had been dormant for centuries, began erupting. Plymouth lay within the zone threatened by pyroclastic flows and ash fall. The city was evacuated in August 1995; residents were briefly allowed to return later that year, but renewed activity forced a final evacuation in 1996.

In 1997, major eruptions sent pyroclastic flows and heavy ash falls over the abandoned city, burying much of it under layers of volcanic debris, in places more than a metre deep. Buildings collapsed or burned, and the port facilities were destroyed. The southern half of Montserrat, including Plymouth, was declared an exclusion zone, which remains in force.

Present day

Plymouth remains the de jure capital of Montserrat, although government functions moved first to Brades in the northwest of the island. A new capital is being developed at Little Bay. The ruined city, sometimes compared to a modern Pompeii, can be viewed from designated vantage points and from the sea; entry into the exclusion zone is strictly controlled.
