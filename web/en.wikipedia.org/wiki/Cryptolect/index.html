Cryptolect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Placeholder image

A cryptolect is a language variety — a form of cant, argot or secret language — deliberately designed to be unintelligible to outsiders, typically to conceal communication within a group. M. A. K. Halliday coined the related term anti-language for such varieties used by an "anti-society" set against the mainstream.

Characteristics

Cryptolects usually keep the grammar of the surrounding language while replacing much of the vocabulary — through borrowing, metaphor, reversal, syllable games or invented words — so that eavesdroppers cannot follow the content. They serve functions of concealment (from police, rivals or customers) and of group identity and solidarity.

Examples

  • Polari — used in British gay subculture, theatre and merchant navies.
  • Rotwelsch — a thieves' cant of German-speaking Europe.
  • Shelta — spoken by Irish Travellers.
  • Verlan and louchébem — French word-game argots.
  • Thieves' cant — of early modern England.
  • Lunfardo — of the Buenos Aires underworld, now general Rioplatense slang.
  • Grypsera — a Polish prison slang.
