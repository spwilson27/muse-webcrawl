Yucatán

Yucatán is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the northern part of the Yucatán Peninsula. Its capital and largest city is Mérida. The name is also applied more broadly to the peninsula, which comprises the states of Yucatán, Campeche and Quintana Roo, plus parts of Belize and Guatemala.

History

The region was a heartland of the Maya civilization, home to cities such as Chichén Itzá, Uxmal and Mayapán; Chichén Itzá is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site and major tourist destination. The Spanish conquest of Yucatán (1527–1546) was prolonged, and Maya resistance flared again in the Caste War (1847–1901). In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the state prospered from henequen (sisal) exports. The peninsula is low limestone karst, noted for cenotes (natural sinkholes) and for the Chicxulub crater, linked to the extinction of the dinosaurs.
