The zero trust security model, also known as zero trust architecture (ZTA) or perimeterless security, is an approach to information security that requires strict identity verification for every person, device and workload attempting to access resources, regardless of whether the request originates inside or outside the organisation's network. It abandons the traditional "castle-and-moat" model, in which everything inside the corporate perimeter was trusted by default.

Principles

NIST Special Publication 800-207 codifies the model's tenets: all data sources and services are resources; all communication is secured regardless of network location; access to each resource is granted per-session, after authentication and authorisation; access decisions are driven by dynamic policy incorporating identity, device posture and behaviour; the integrity and security posture of all assets are continuously monitored; and authentication is strict and continuously evaluated. The familiar summary is "never trust, always verify," together with two operational assumptions: assume a breach has already occurred, and grant only least-privilege access.

History

The term "zero trust" was coined in 2010 by John Kindervag of Forrester Research. Google's BeyondCorp programme, begun after the 2009 Operation Aurora attacks and described in papers from 2014, demonstrated the model at scale by dispensing with the corporate VPN and granting access based on user and device identity. In 2021 a US executive order directed federal agencies to move toward zero trust architecture, with NIST SP 800-207 as the reference framework.

Implementation

Typical components include strong multifactor authentication and single sign-on, device identity and health checks, micro-segmentation of networks, a policy decision point (policy engine and administrator) mediating a policy enforcement point in front of each resource, and pervasive logging and analytics. Critics note that zero trust is a strategy rather than a product, and that vendor marketing has stretched the label; implementation is also difficult in estates with legacy systems lacking modern identity support.
