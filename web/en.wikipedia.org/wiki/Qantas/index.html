Qantas Airways is the flag carrier of Australia and its largest airline by fleet size, international flights, and international destinations. The airline's name is an acronym for Queensland and Northern Territory Aerial Services, its original name, founded in Winton, Queensland, in 1920—making it the third-oldest continuously operating airline in the world. It is widely regarded as one of the world's leading long-distance carriers.

History

Qantas began with joy flights and airmail in outback Queensland, flying Avro 504s, before launching the legendary outback services linking western Queensland towns. In 1935 it began international flying to Singapore via Imperial Airways, and from 1947—when the Australian government nationalised it—it built an international network, including the "Kangaroo Route" to London. The 1970s brought 747s and nonstop services; the airline was fully privatised in the 1990s. Qantas launched low-cost subsidiary Jetstar in 2003.

Safety record and fleet

Qantas has long held a reputation as one of the world's safest airlines, notable for never having a fatal jet-aircraft accident. Its fleet includes Airbus A380s, Boeing 787 Dreamliners, Airbus A330s, and domestic 737s, with A350-1000s ordered for the "Project Sunrise" programme of nonstop Sydney–London and Sydney–New York flights. The airline's kangaroo tail logo, introduced in 1944, is one of the most recognised in aviation.
