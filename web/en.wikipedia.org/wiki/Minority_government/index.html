Minority government

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A minority government is a government formed when a political party or coalition in a parliamentary system does not have an overall majority of legislators, yet is sworn into office to govern. It must seek support bill by bill from other parties, or stabilise itself through formal arrangements — coalition agreements short of cabinet participation, or "confidence and supply" deals in which outside parties guarantee survival on confidence votes and budgets in exchange for concessions.

Practice

Minority governments are routine in Scandinavia and common in Canada, Spain and (as governments or near-governments) elsewhere; the UK's 2017–19 government relied on a confidence-and-supply agreement with the DUP. Scholarship finds them neither rare nor necessarily unstable or ineffective — many serve full terms by trading legislative wins — though they can be vulnerable to opportunistic no-confidence votes and may campaign on grievance against obstruction. Their frequency tracks electoral-system proportionality and party-system fragmentation.
