Unit disk

In mathematics, a unit disk (or disc) is a disk of radius one. More precisely, the open unit disk around a point P in a plane is the set of points whose distance from P is less than 1, while the closed unit disk is the set of points whose distance from P is less than or equal to 1. Usually, unless otherwise specified, "the unit disk" means the open unit disk centered at the origin of the Cartesian plane.

Properties

The unit disk is a fundamental domain in complex analysis: every holomorphic function on the disk is the subject of study of the Poincaré disk model of hyperbolic geometry, in which "lines" are arcs of circles orthogonal to the boundary circle. By the Riemann mapping theorem, every simply connected open subset of the complex plane other than the plane itself is conformally equivalent to the unit disk.

Related concepts

The unit disk generalizes to the unit ball in higher dimensions and to unit disks in other metric spaces. In computational geometry, a unit disk graph is the intersection graph of a family of unit disks, commonly used to model wireless communication networks.
