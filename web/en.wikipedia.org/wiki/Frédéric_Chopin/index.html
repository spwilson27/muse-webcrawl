Frédéric Chopin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Frédéric Chopin in the Delacroix portrait: dark, sensitive face emerging from shadow]

Frédéric François Chopin (1 March 1810 – 17 October 1849) was a Polish composer and virtuoso pianist of the Romantic period who wrote primarily for solo piano. He has maintained worldwide renown as a leading musician of his era, one whose 'poetic genius was based on a professional technique that was without equal in his generation'.

Born near Warsaw to a French father and Polish mother, Chopin settled in Paris in 1831 after the failed Polish uprising, giving few public concerts but teaching and composing for the salon aristocracy. His relationship with the writer George Sand lasted a decade. Nearly all his works involve the piano: nocturnes, mazurkas (rooted in Polish dance), polonaises, waltzes, ballades, scherzos, two concertos and the 24 Preludes. Frail throughout his life, he died in Paris at 39, probably of tuberculosis; his heart was taken to Warsaw, where it rests in the Church of the Holy Cross.
