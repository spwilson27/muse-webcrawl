Øresund

Location: Between Zealand (Denmark) and Scania (Sweden)

Length: 118 km (73 mi)

Min. width: 4 km

Crossing: Øresund Bridge (opened 2000)

The Øresund (Danish: Øresund; Swedish: Öresund) is the strait separating the Danish island of Zealand from the Swedish province of Scania. Together with the Great Belt and Little Belt, it forms the connection between the Baltic Sea and the Kattegat leading to the North Sea, making it one of the world's busiest shipping lanes.

Geography

The strait is 118 kilometres long; its width ranges from 4 kilometres between Helsingør and Helsingborg in the north to about 28 kilometres further south. Islands in the strait include Amager, Saltholm and the artificial Peberholm, built for the bridge-tunnel crossing. The principal cities facing each other across the water are Copenhagen and Malmö.

History

Control of the Øresund was strategically vital for centuries. From 1429 to 1857 Denmark levied the Sound Dues on foreign ships passing through, a major source of crown revenue, collected at Helsingør beneath Kronborg Castle — the Elsinore of Shakespeare's Hamlet. The Treaty of Roskilde (1658) transferred the eastern shore to Sweden, so the strait became an international border.

Øresund Bridge

Since 1 July 2000 the fixed link of bridge, artificial island and tunnel has carried road and rail traffic between Copenhagen and Malmö, integrating the two cities into a single Øresund Region labour market. Ferry services continue on the northern Helsingør–Helsingborg route.

Ecology

Brackish Baltic water flows northward at the surface while saltier Kattegat water flows southward at depth, producing a halocline; the strait's fisheries were historically rich in herring, whose medieval shoals underpinned Scania's Hanseatic trade.
