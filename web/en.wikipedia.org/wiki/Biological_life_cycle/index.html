Biological life cycle

Alternation of generations in a fern.

In biology, a biological life cycle (or just life cycle) is a series of stages in form and reproductive mode that an organism undergoes, from the time a primordial germ cell matures to when it terminates; a whole generation is thought to be completed when the cycle returns to the same stage and repeats.

Types of life cycle

Life cycles are classified by the relative prominence of haploid (n) and diploid (2n) phases. In a diplontic cycle, as in most animals, the diploid organism is the main stage and only the gametes are haploid. In a haplontic cycle, as in many fungi and some algae, the haploid stage predominates and the zygote is the only diploid cell, dividing by meiosis. In a haplodiplontic cycle — the alternation of generations — multicellular haploid and diploid stages both occur, as in land plants, where a diploid sporophyte produces spores that grow into a haploid gametophyte.

Complex life cycles

Many organisms have complex life cycles involving different stages in different hosts or environments. Parasites such as malaria-causing Plasmodium alternate between human and mosquito hosts; many marine invertebrates pass through free-swimming larval stages; amphibians undergo metamorphosis from aquatic larvae to terrestrial adults.

History of study

The alternation of generations was elucidated in the mid-19th century, notably by Wilhelm Hofmeister in plants, and the cytological basis — fertilisation and meiosis — was clarified by the end of that century, giving the modern understanding of life cycles in terms of chromosome number.
