Louisiade Archipelago

LocationMilne Bay Province, Papua New Guinea
Major islandsMisima, Vanatinai (Tagula), Rossel
Total islandsAbout 90
Named byLouis Antoine de Bougainville, 1768

The Louisiade Archipelago is a string of some ninety islands and coral reefs extending about 200 kilometres south-east of the eastern tip of New Guinea, in Milne Bay Province of Papua New Guinea. The French explorer Louis Antoine de Bougainville named the group in 1768 after Louis XV.

Geography

The largest islands are Vanatinai (also called Tagula or Sudest), Misima and Rossel, each mountainous and covered in rainforest, surrounded by extensive lagoon systems including the Calvados Chain. The archipelago is rich in endemic species and is considered a distinct bioregion; Rossel Island is noted for its complex shell-money system and its language, Yélî Dnye, famous among linguists for its unusual sound inventory.

History

The islands were probably settled by Austronesian-speaking voyagers more than two thousand years ago and were drawn into trade networks including the Kula ring documented by anthropologists further north. Gold was mined on Misima from the late 19th century, and the area saw fighting and Allied air bases during the Second World War, following the nearby Battle of the Coral Sea in 1942.
