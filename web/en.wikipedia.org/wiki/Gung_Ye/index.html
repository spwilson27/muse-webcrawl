Gung Ye

Gung Ye (Korean: 궁예; c. 869–918)
King of Taebong (Hugoguryeo)
Reign: 901–918

Gung Ye (Korean: 궁예; c. 869 – 24 July 918) was the founder and only king of the short-lived Korean kingdom of Taebong (originally Hugoguryeo, "Later Goguryeo") during the Later Three Kingdoms period. Originally a Buddhist monk, he rose as a rebel commander against the declining Silla kingdom and established his own state in the north of the peninsula.

Early life

According to tradition, Gung Ye was a son of King Heonan or King Gyeongmun of Silla, abandoned at birth after an unfavourable prophecy and raised in a monastery after being blinded in one eye. As Silla's authority collapsed amid peasant rebellions in the late 9th century, he joined the rebel forces of the monk-general Yang Gil and soon surpassed his master.

Taebong

In 901 Gung Ye founded Hugoguryeo, renaming it Majin and later Taebong, with his capital at Songak (modern Kaesong) and then Cheorwon. He attracted defectors from Silla and campaigned successfully against Later Baekje. Among his ablest commanders was Wang Geon, the future founder of Goryeo.

Tyranny and fall

In his later years Gung Ye proclaimed himself the living Maitreya Buddha, claimed supernatural powers to read minds, and governed with mounting paranoia, executing officials, monks, and even his wife and sons on suspicion of treason. In 918 his generals, led by Hong Yu, Bae Hyeon-gyeong, Sin Sung-gyeom, and Bok Ji-gyeom, deposed him and placed Wang Geon on the throne. Gung Ye fled and was killed, and his state continued as Goryeo.
