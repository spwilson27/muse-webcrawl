Arthur Edward Waite

Arthur Edward Waite (1857–1942), occultist and co-creator of the Rider–Waite tarot

Arthur Edward Waite (2 October 1857 – 19 May 1942) was a British poet, author, and mystical scholar. He is best known as the co-creator, with artist Pamela Colman Smith, of the Rider–Waite tarot deck (1909), the most widely used tarot pack in the English-speaking world.

Life and work

Born in Brooklyn, New York, and raised in London, Waite joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1891 and later led a reformed faction of it. He wrote extensively on Freemasonry, Rosicrucianism, alchemy, the Holy Grail, and ceremonial magic, including The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts (1898), The Holy Kabbalah (1929), and The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1911), the companion to his deck.

Legacy

Waite sought to present the tarot as a coherent symbolic system drawing on Christian mysticism and the Grail legends. His emphasis on illustrated Minor Arcana — scenes on every pip card, designed by Smith — revolutionised tarot design and practice.
