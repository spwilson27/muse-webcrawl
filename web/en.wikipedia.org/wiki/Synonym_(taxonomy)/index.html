Synonym (taxonomy)

In taxonomy, a synonym is one of two or more scientific names that apply to the same taxon. Synonyms arise when a species is described more than once, when a taxon is moved between genera (creating new name combinations), or when two previously separate taxa are judged to be the same. Each taxon has one correct (accepted) name under the relevant nomenclatural code; the remainder are its synonyms.

Objective and subjective synonyms

Objective (homotypic) synonyms are names based on the same type specimen, so their equivalence is a matter of nomenclatural fact — for example, a species described in one genus and later transferred to another generates a new combination based on the same type. Subjective (heterotypic) synonyms rest on a taxonomist's judgement that two type specimens belong to the same taxon, and may be reversed if opinion changes.

Zoology and botany

Usage differs slightly between codes. In zoology, governed by the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature, synonyms include available names for the same taxon, and the senior synonym normally has priority. In botany, under the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants, a synonym is a name not currently accepted for the taxon; botanists further distinguish homotypic (nomenclatural) and heterotypic (taxonomic) synonyms, and long synonym lists in floras document each species' naming history.

Junior and senior synonyms

Where two names compete, the earlier published is the senior synonym and usually prevails under the principle of priority; the later is the junior synonym. Exceptions occur when a younger name is conserved, or a little-used senior name is suppressed to protect a familiar junior one (in zoology, a "reversal of precedence").
