Cuckmere Haven

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cuckmere estuary and its meanders, with the Seven Sisters cliffs to the east.

Cuckmere Haven is an estuary and flood plain in East Sussex, England, where the River Cuckmere meets the English Channel between Seaford and Eastbourne. It lies within the South Downs National Park and forms part of the Seven Sisters Country Park. The area is famed for the sweeping meanders of the river's lower course and for the view eastward to the white chalk cliffs of the Seven Sisters, with the Coastguard Cottages at the cliff edge forming one of the most photographed landscapes in southern England.

Geography and nature

Unlike most Sussex rivers, whose mouths have been trained by harbours and groynes, the Cuckmere still reaches the sea through a largely natural estuary, its cut-off meanders surviving as oxbow lakes on the flood plain. The valley is a Site of Special Scientific Interest and an important habitat for overwintering and migratory birds, grazing marsh, and saltmarsh plants. The shingle beach and the river mouth shift with storms, and periodic breaching and flooding of the meanders is part of the estuary's natural dynamics.

History

The haven was historically used by smugglers bringing contraband ashore along this isolated stretch of coast. During the Second World War the valley was fortified against invasion: pillboxes, anti-tank defences, and ditches were built across the flood plain, some of which survive, and the meanders were deliberately made conspicuous from the air so that enemy aircraft would not mistake the valley for a landing ground — the nearby town of Seaford was protected in part by decoy lighting schemes. The Coastguard Cottages date from the 19th century, when coastguards watched for smugglers wrecking and smuggling along the shore.

Coastal management

The shingle ridge and flood defences protecting the valley have long been debated. The Environment Agency has favoured a policy of managed realignment or no active intervention for the estuary, allowing natural processes to reshape the mouth and periodically flood the meanders, while local campaigns have pressed for continued maintenance of the defences protecting the cottages and valley floor.

In popular culture

The view of the Coastguard Cottages and the Seven Sisters from Cuckmere Haven has appeared in numerous films and television productions, standing in for the white cliffs of Dover in period dramas and appearing in films such as Atonement and Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, as well as in countless advertisements and travel features.
