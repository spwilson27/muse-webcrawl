The MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL) is a research institute at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), formed in 2003 by the merger of the Laboratory for Computer Science (LCS) and the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. It is the largest on-campus laboratory at MIT.

CSAIL is housed in the Ray and Maria Stata Center, a distinctive deconstructivist building designed by Frank Gehry, and conducts research spanning robotics, systems, theory, machine learning, and human-computer interaction.

History

The AI Laboratory grew out of Project MAC, founded in 1963, where pioneers such as Marvin Minsky and John McCarthy advanced early artificial intelligence. LCS, spun off in 1970, produced work on computer systems, the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) under Tim Berners-Lee, and the RSA encryption algorithm.

Research

CSAIL's roughly sixty research groups work on areas including cryptography, networks, programming languages, computational biology, and natural language processing. Notable projects have included the Oxygen ubiquitous computing initiative and early self-driving and humanoid robotics research.

People

Faculty and alumni associated with CSAIL include Tim Berners-Lee, Rodney Brooks, Shafi Goldwasser, Silvio Micali, and Daniela Rus, who became director in 2012.
