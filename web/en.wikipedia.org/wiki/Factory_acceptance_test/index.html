Factory acceptance test
AbbreviationFAT
LocationManufacturer's premises
CounterpartSite acceptance test (SAT)

A factory acceptance test (FAT) is a set of functional tests performed on equipment at the manufacturer's factory before it is delivered to the purchaser's site. The purpose of the FAT is to verify that the equipment or system has been built and performs in accordance with the design specifications, purchase order requirements, and applicable standards, while defects can still be corrected conveniently at the factory.

Process

The FAT is normally carried out according to a test procedure agreed in advance between the manufacturer and the customer. Representatives of the customer, and sometimes of an independent inspection agency or the end user, witness the tests. Typical checks include dimensional and visual inspection, verification of materials and documentation, electrical and insulation tests, functional and performance runs, alarm and safety-system checks, and verification of nameplate data.

Punch lists and approval

Discrepancies found during the FAT are recorded on a punch list (snag list) and classified by severity. Items that prevent safe or contractual operation must usually be resolved before shipment, while minor items may be carried over for correction at site. Successful completion is documented by signing a FAT certificate or report, which typically authorises the manufacturer to pack and ship the equipment.

Relation to site acceptance tests

After delivery and installation, a site acceptance test (SAT) repeats or extends functional testing under actual operating conditions, confirming that transport and installation have not impaired performance and that the system interfaces correctly with the rest of the plant. In process and software industries the FAT/SAT sequence forms part of the broader commissioning and validation workflow.
