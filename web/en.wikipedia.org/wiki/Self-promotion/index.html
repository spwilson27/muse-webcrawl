Self-promotion

Placeholder image: promoting one's own work and accomplishments.

Self-promotion is the act of promoting or publicising oneself or one's achievements, with the intention of improving one's social status, career prospects, or reputation. In social psychology it is classified as a form of self-presentation in which a person highlights their competence, skills, and accomplishments so that others perceive them as able and accomplished.

Psychology and social effects

Self-promotion is a double-edged strategy: it can raise perceptions of competence but risks being judged boastful or arrogant, so effective self-promoters often combine claims of ability with expressions of modesty or attribute successes partly to luck or help. Research finds cultural and gender differences in how self-promotion is practised and received; for instance, women who self-promote may be penalised in some settings for violating modesty norms, a phenomenon related to the "self-promotion gap" in workplaces. Self-promotion is common in job interviews, academic and artistic careers, politics, and on social media, where it shades into personal branding.
