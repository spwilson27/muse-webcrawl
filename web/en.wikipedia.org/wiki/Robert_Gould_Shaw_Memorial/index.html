Robert Gould Shaw Memorial

The Robert Gould Shaw Memorial, also known as the Shaw Memorial or the Memorial to Robert Gould Shaw and the Massachusetts Fifty-Fourth Regiment, is a bronze relief sculpture by Augustus Saint-Gaudens located on Boston Common in Boston, Massachusetts. It commemorates Colonel Robert Gould Shaw and the men of the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, one of the first African-American regiments raised in the North during the American Civil War.

History

Shaw and nearly half his regiment were killed in the assault on Fort Wagner, South Carolina, on 18 July 1863. A memorial fund was begun almost immediately, largely through the efforts of Joshua Benton Smith, a Black caterer, state legislator, and friend of the Shaw family. Saint-Gaudens was commissioned in 1884 and worked on the sculpture for fourteen years; it was unveiled on 31 May 1897 in a ceremony attended by veterans of the regiment and the philosopher William James, who delivered the dedication address.

Description

The high-relief bronze shows Shaw on horseback, accompanied by marching infantrymen of the 54th — each soldier individually modelled from life studies of Black men — while above them hovers an allegorical female figure carrying poppies and an olive branch. The memorial stands opposite the Massachusetts State House at the top of Boston Common and forms part of the Boston African American National Historic Site. A famous plaster version is held by the National Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C.
