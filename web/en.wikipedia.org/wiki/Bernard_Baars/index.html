Bernard J. Baars (born 1946) is a Dutch-American cognitive neuroscientist best known as the originator of global workspace theory (GWT), an influential account of consciousness and cognition. He developed the theory in his 1988 book A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness.

Career

Baars was born in the Netherlands and emigrated to the United States as a teenager. He received his doctorate in psychology from the University of California, Los Angeles. He has held positions at the State University of New York at Stony Brook and at The Neurosciences Institute in San Diego, California, where he worked as a senior fellow in theoretical neurobiology.

Global workspace theory

Global workspace theory proposes that the brain consists of many specialised, largely unconscious processors competing for access to a limited-capacity "global workspace". Information that wins this competition is broadcast widely across the brain, and this global availability is identified with conscious experience. Baars illustrated the idea with a theatre metaphor: consciousness is like a bright spot on a stage, shaped by an audience of unconscious processes working behind the scenes. The theory has been developed further in collaboration with Stanislas Dehaene and others under the name "global neuronal workspace", and it remains one of the leading scientific frameworks for studying consciousness.

Selected works

  • A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness (1988)
  • In the Theater of Consciousness: The Workspace of the Mind (1997)
  • On Consciousness: Science & Subjectivity (2019)

Baars also co-founded the journal Consciousness and Cognition and has written textbooks aimed at reviving the scientific study of consciousness within mainstream psychology.
