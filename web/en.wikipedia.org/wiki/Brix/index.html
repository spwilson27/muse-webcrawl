Degrees Brix (symbol °Bx) is a measure of the sugar content of an aqueous solution. One degree Brix is 1 gram of sucrose in 100 grams of solution and represents the strength of the solution as a percentage by mass. If the solution contains dissolved solids other than pure sucrose, then the °Bx reading approximates the total dissolved solid content; the scale is nonetheless the standard measure used throughout the sugar, wine, fruit juice, soft drink and honey industries.

Measurement

Brix is measured either with a hydrometer, which infers concentration from specific gravity, or with a refractometer, which measures refractive index; both instruments are calibrated against pure sucrose solutions at a reference temperature (typically 20 °C), and tables convert the physical reading to °Bx. The refractometer method requires only a drop of liquid and is standard for checking fruit ripeness in the field: grapes for table wine are typically harvested at 20–25 °Bx, corresponding to a potential alcohol of roughly 11.5–14 per cent if fully fermented.

History

The scale is named after the German engineer and mathematician Adolf Ferdinand Wenceslaus Brix (1798–1870), who recalculated the earlier Balling scale in the 19th century. It belongs to a family of saccharometer scales that includes the Balling and Plato scales used in brewing, the Oechsle scale used in German winemaking and the KMW (Babo) scale used in Austria; all report essentially the same quantity with slightly different definitions and conversion tables. In practice, Brix and Plato readings for brewing wort are close enough that brewers use them nearly interchangeably.

Applications

Beyond wine and brewing, Brix is used to grade fruit juices and concentrates (orange juice concentrate is traded at standard values such as 65 °Bx), to assess quality and payment in the sugar industry, to control soft-drink syrup concentrations, and in agriculture to judge the maturity of fruit, since soluble solids — mostly sugars — rise as fruit ripens. It is also used as an index of quality in tomatoes, melons and other produce.
