Giselle

Giselle is a romantic ballet in two acts with music by Adolphe Adam and choreography originally by Jean Coralli and Jules Perrot. The libretto, by Théophile Gautier and Jules-Henri Vernoy de Saint-Georges, was inspired by a prose passage in Heinrich Heine's De l'Allemagne describing the Wilis — the spirits of young women who died before their wedding day. It was first performed by the Ballet du Théâtre de l'Académie Royale de Musique at the Salle Le Peletier in Paris on 28 June 1841, with Carlotta Grisi in the title role.

Synopsis

Act I. In a Rhineland village, the peasant girl Giselle loves Albrecht, whom she believes to be a fellow villager but who is in fact a nobleman already engaged to Bathilde. When the deception is revealed during the autumn grape-harvest festival, Giselle loses her reason and dies of a broken heart, in the ballet's famous "mad scene."

Act II. At midnight in the forest, Giselle's spirit is summoned by Myrtha, Queen of the Wilis, to join the vengeful sisterhood that dances men to death. When Albrecht comes to her grave, Giselle protects him, dancing with him until dawn breaks the Wilis' power, and her spirit is released — a triumph of love and forgiveness over death.

Legacy

Giselle is regarded as one of the supreme achievements of the Romantic ballet and a cornerstone of the classical repertoire. The version most often performed today derives from Marius Petipa's revivals for the Imperial Ballet in St. Petersburg. The title role is considered one of the most demanding in ballet, requiring both brilliant technique and profound dramatic gifts, and the Act I peasant pas de deux and the Act II dances of the Wilis are among the most celebrated passages in all choreography.
