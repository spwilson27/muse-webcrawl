Harajuku (原宿) is a district in Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan. Harajuku is the common name given to the area on the east side of the JR Yamanote Line between Shinjuku and Shibuya, and is internationally known as a centre of Japanese youth culture and fashion. Shopping and dining options include many small, youth-oriented boutiques and cafés, together with larger international chains on Omotesandō avenue.

Fashion

Harajuku became famous in the 1990s as a gathering place for teenagers dressed in distinctive street styles, including Lolita, decora, visual kei, and cosplay. The pedestrian bridge over the railway near Harajuku Station was a celebrated meeting point for fashionably dressed young people on Sundays. The fashion magazine FRUiTS, founded by photographer Shoichi Aoki in 1997, documented the scene and brought it to international attention. Styles spread through nearby shopping streets such as Takeshita Street, Cat Street, and Ura-Harajuku.

History

The area was a rural village before the Edo period and developed as a post town. In the modern era it hosted housing for Allied occupation personnel (Washington Heights) and was reshaped for the 1964 Tokyo Olympics, after which boutiques and youth-oriented retail steadily colonised its back streets. Adjacent landmarks include the Meiji Shrine and Yoyogi Park.
