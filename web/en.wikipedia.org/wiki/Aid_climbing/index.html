Aid climbing

Aid climbing is a style of rock climbing in which the climber makes progress by weighting equipment placed in the rock — such as nuts, pitons, camming devices, hooks and etriers (webbing ladders) — rather than by using only hands and feet on the rock, as in free climbing.

Aid routes are graded on the A (or C, for clean aid without a hammer) scale from A0 to A5, reflecting the security of placements and the consequences of a fall. Long aid routes on big walls such as those of Yosemite's El Capitan may take days, with climbers sleeping on portaledges.

Historically dominant for hard big-wall ascents, aid climbing declined in popularity as free-climbing standards rose and many former aid lines were freed, but it remains essential for some extreme terrain and for exploratory mountaineering.
