John Davies Cale (born 9 March 1942) is a Welsh musician, composer, and record producer who was a founding member of the American rock band the Velvet Underground. Over a career spanning six decades, he has worked in rock, drone, classical, and avant-garde music, and produced albums for artists including the Stooges, Nico, Patti Smith, and the Modern Lovers.

Early life and avant-garde

Cale, a gifted violist from a Welsh-speaking mining village, studied music at Goldsmiths College, London, and moved to New York in 1963 to study with Aaron Copland and work in the avant-garde. He performed with John Cage and joined La Monte Young's Theatre of Eternal Music, an immersion in long sustained drones that marked his music permanently.

The Velvet Underground

In 1965 Cale co-founded the Velvet Underground with Lou Reed. His viola drones, piano, and bass, and his classical and avant-garde training, shaped the band's first two albums, The Velvet Underground & Nico (1967) and White Light/White Heat (1968). Creative friction with Reed led to his departure in 1968.

Solo career and production

Cale's solo work ranges from the orchestral pop of Paris 1919 (1973) to the fractured intensity of Fear (1974) and the live Sabotage/Live (1979); with Reed he recorded the Andy Warhol tribute Songs for Drella (1990), and with Bob Neuwirth Last Day on Earth (1994). His recording of Leonard Cohen's "Hallelujah" (1991) became the template for Jeff Buckley's celebrated version. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Velvet Underground in 1996, appointed OBE in 2010, and continues to release albums including Mercy (2023) and Poptical Illusion (2024).
