Delage

Delage was a French luxury automobile and racing car company founded in 1905 by Louis Delage in Levallois-Perret near Paris. It built some of the most prestigious sporting cars of the interwar period before being absorbed by Delahaye in 1935.

History

Louis Delage had worked for Peugeot before striking out on his own. Early cars used De Dion-Bouton engines, but Delage soon produced its own. The company gained fame in Grand Prix racing, winning the 1927 European Championship and setting land speed records. Its road cars, notably the D6 and D8 models, featured elegant coachwork by firms such as Letourneur et Marchand, Chapron and Figoni et Falaschi.

Decline and legacy

The Great Depression hit the luxury market hard. In 1935 Delage was taken over by Delahaye, which continued producing cars under the Delage name until 1953. The marque was revived in the 21st century with a limited-production hypercar, the Delage D12.
