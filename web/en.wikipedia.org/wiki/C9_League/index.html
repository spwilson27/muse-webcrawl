The C9 League groups nine of China's leading research universities.

The C9 League (Chinese: 九校联盟) is an alliance of nine leading universities in China, formed in 1998 with the support of the Chinese government under the former Project 985 initiative to promote the development of world-class research universities. The league is sometimes compared to the Ivy League in the United States or the Russell Group in the United Kingdom, and its members consistently rank among the top universities in China and Asia.

Members

  1. Fudan University (Shanghai)
  2. Harbin Institute of Technology (Harbin)
  3. Nanjing University (Nanjing)
  4. Peking University (Beijing)
  5. Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Shanghai)
  6. Tsinghua University (Beijing)
  7. University of Science and Technology of China (Hefei)
  8. Xi'an Jiaotong University (Xi'an)
  9. Zhejiang University (Hangzhou)

Activities

The member universities cooperate on student exchange, credit recognition, joint graduate training and shared academic resources. Admission to C9 institutions is through the highly competitive national college entrance examination (gaokao), and the league's members receive a large share of national research funding. Graduates of C9 universities are heavily represented in Chinese academia, government and industry.
