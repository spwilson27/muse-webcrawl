Žabljak (Montenegrin Cyrillic: Жабљак) is a small town in northern Montenegro, the administrative centre of Žabljak Municipality. Situated in the heart of the Durmitor mountain range at an altitude of about 1,456 metres (4,777 ft), it is the highest-situated town in the Balkans and the principal gateway to Durmitor National Park.

Geography

The town lies on a high plateau surrounded by the peaks of Durmitor, the highest of which is Bobotov Kuk (2,523 m). Nearby attractions include the Black Lake (Crno Jezero), the largest of Durmitor's glacial lakes, and the Tara River Canyon, one of the deepest river canyons in Europe. Winters are long and snowy, and the town is a centre for skiing at the Savin Kuk resort; summers bring hiking, rafting and mountaineering tourism.

History

The area was settled in the medieval period, and the town's name derives from the Slavic word žaba ("frog"), after frogs that lived in nearby ponds. During the Second World War Žabljak was a centre of partisan resistance and was burned by occupying forces; it was afterwards rebuilt. With the creation of Durmitor National Park in 1952 and its later inscription as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the town developed into Montenegro's leading mountain resort.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census, the town of Žabljak had 1,723 inhabitants, while the municipality as a whole had 3,569. The population is composed mainly of Montenegrins and Serbs.
