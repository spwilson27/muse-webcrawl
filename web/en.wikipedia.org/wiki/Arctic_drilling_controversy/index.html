The Arctic drilling controversy concerns the long-running political and environmental debate over oil and natural gas exploration and production in the Arctic, particularly in the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge (ANWR) in northeastern Alaska and in offshore waters of the Arctic Ocean. The dispute pits arguments about energy security, revenue, and jobs against concerns about wildlife, wilderness values, Indigenous rights, oil spills, and climate change.

The coastal plain of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge

Background

The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980 set aside the coastal plain of the refuge, known as Section 1002, and prohibited oil development there without congressional authorization. Geological surveys have suggested the area may hold billions of barrels of recoverable oil, though estimates vary widely. The plain is also calving ground for the Porcupine caribou herd and habitat for polar bears and migratory birds.

Arguments

Supporters of drilling argue it would reduce dependence on imported oil, create jobs, and generate revenue for Alaska and the federal government; development would use modern techniques with a small surface footprint. Opponents argue that the refuge is one of the last intact Arctic ecosystems, that spills in remote and ice-covered waters would be nearly impossible to clean up, that the Gwich'in people depend on the caribou herd for subsistence and culture, and that new fossil fuel development conflicts with climate goals.

Developments

Congressional attempts to authorize ANWR drilling failed repeatedly over several decades. In 2017, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act directed the Interior Department to conduct lease sales in the refuge, and a first lease sale was held in January 2021, drawing limited industry interest. The Biden administration subsequently suspended the leases and ordered a new environmental review; policy has shifted with successive administrations. Offshore Arctic drilling in the Chukchi and Beaufort seas has also been contentious, notably Royal Dutch Shell's exploration program, which it abandoned in 2015 after disappointing results and high costs.
