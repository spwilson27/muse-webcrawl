Mephibosheth

Mephibosheth (Hebrew: מְפִיבֹשֶׁת, also spelled Merib-baal in Chronicles) was a son of Jonathan and grandson of King Saul. He is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, appearing in the books of Samuel. According to the biblical narrative, he was five years old when his father and grandfather died at the Battle of Mount Gilboa. On hearing of their deaths, his nurse fled with him in panic and dropped him, leaving him lame in both feet (2 Samuel 4:4).

At David's court

Years later, King David, wishing to show kindness to the house of Saul for Jonathan's sake, learned of Mephibosheth from Ziba, a former servant of Saul. David restored to Mephibosheth all the land of Saul and invited him to eat continually at the king's table "like one of the king's sons" (2 Samuel 9). Mephibosheth had a young son named Mica.

During Absalom's revolt

When David fled Jerusalem during Absalom's rebellion, Ziba met him with provisions and claimed that Mephibosheth had stayed behind hoping to recover the kingdom. David granted Ziba all of Mephibosheth's property. On David's victorious return, Mephibosheth—unkempt and in mourning dress—explained that Ziba had deceived and abandoned him, and that he, being lame, had been unable to come. David divided the land between the two men. Later, when David spared Mephibosheth during the episode of the Gibeonites (2 Samuel 21), it is noted that he protected him because of the oath between David and Jonathan.

Name

The name appears as Merib-baal ("Baal contends") in 1 Chronicles, with "Mephibosheth" possibly reflecting a later scribal substitution of bosheth ("shame") for the divine name Baal, a pattern seen in other Saulide names such as Ish-bosheth.
