Merce Cunningham

Merce Cunningham
Born: April 16, 1919, Centralia, Washington
Died: July 26, 2009, New York City
Occupation: dancer, choreographer

Mercier Philip "Merce" Cunningham (April 16, 1919 – July 26, 2009) was an American dancer and choreographer who was at the forefront of American modern dance for more than 50 years. He is considered one of the most important choreographers of the twentieth century. Together with his life partner, composer John Cage, and visual artist Robert Rauschenberg, Cunningham created works that radically reimagined the relationship between dance, music, and design.

Career

Cunningham trained at the Cornish School in Seattle and danced as a soloist with the Martha Graham Dance Company from 1939 to 1945. He presented his first solo concert with John Cage in 1944 and founded the Merce Cunningham Dance Company at Black Mountain College in 1953. The company toured worldwide for six decades.

Choreographic method

Cunningham famously used chance procedures—such as coin tosses based on the I Ching—to determine movement sequences, freeing choreography from narrative and musical phrasing. In his works, dance, music, and décor were created independently and combined only at performance. He treated any point in space as equally significant, rejecting the traditional hierarchy of center stage. From the 1990s he choreographed extensively with the software program LifeForms (later DanceForms), pioneering computer-assisted choreography.

Collaborations and legacy

His collaborators included John Cage (musical director for life), Robert Rauschenberg, Jasper Johns, Andy Warhol, and musicians such as David Tudor. Late works include Biped (1999) and Ocean. After his death, the company carried out a two-year farewell "Legacy Tour" and dissolved, as Cunningham had planned, while the Merce Cunningham Trust preserves his works through licensing and teaching.
