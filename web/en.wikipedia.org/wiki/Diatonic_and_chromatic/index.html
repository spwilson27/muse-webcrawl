Diatonic and chromatic

Diatonic and chromatic are terms in music theory used to describe scales, melodies, harmonies and instruments. A diatonic scale is a seven-note scale built from five whole steps and two half steps — the pattern of the major scale and the natural minor scale. Notes or chords belonging to a given key are diatonic to it; notes outside the key are chromatic.

Usage

The chromatic scale contains all twelve pitches of the octave. Melodies that stay within a key are diatonic, whereas chromatic passages use additional semitones for colour, tension or modulation. Instruments may be described as diatonic (such as a harmonica tuned to one key, or a diatonic accordion) or chromatic (capable of all twelve notes, like the piano).

History

The terms derive from ancient Greek music theory: diatonikos and chrômatikos named two of the genera of tetrachord tuning, alongside the enharmonic genus.
