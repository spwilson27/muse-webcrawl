The NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) is the highest level of college football in the United States. Comprising 134 schools as of the 2024 season, mostly large public universities, FBS teams play a regular season followed by postseason bowl games, with the national championship decided by the College Football Playoff.

Overview

FBS was created in 1978 when Division I football split into Division I-A (now FBS) and I-AA (now the FCS). Unlike the FCS, whose champion is decided by an NCAA-run playoff, the FBS postseason historically centred on invitational bowl games, the oldest being the Rose Bowl (first played in 1902). For decades the national champion was crowned by polls (AP and Coaches); a single championship game followed under the Bowl Championship Series (1998–2013), and a four-team College Football Playoff began in 2014, expanding to twelve teams for the 2024 season.

Conferences

The "Power" conferences — the SEC, Big Ten, ACC, Big 12 and (until its effective collapse in 2024) the Pac-12 — hold the largest media contracts and most playoff access, while the "Group of Five" leagues (AAC, Mountain West, Sun Belt, C-USA and MAC) and a small number of independents, headed by Notre Dame, complete the subdivision.

Eligibility and bowls

Teams generally need at least six wins to be bowl eligible; more than forty bowl games are staged each December and January. FBS programmes may award up to 85 full scholarships, higher than the FCS limit of 63 equivalencies.
