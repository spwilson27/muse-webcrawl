National Weather Service

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Weather Service (NWS) is an agency of the United States federal government that is tasked with providing weather forecasts, warnings of hazardous weather, and other weather-related products to organisations and the public for the purposes of protection, safety, and general information. It is a part of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) branch of the Department of Commerce, and is headquartered in Silver Spring, Maryland.

The agency was known as the United States Weather Bureau from 1891 until it adopted its current name in 1970.

History

Federal weather services began in 1870 when President Ulysses S. Grant signed a resolution authorising the Secretary of War to establish meteorological stations; the work was assigned to the Army Signal Corps. The service was transferred to civilian control under the Department of Agriculture in 1891 as the U.S. Weather Bureau. It moved to the Department of Commerce in 1940, became part of the Environmental Science Services Administration in 1965, and in 1970 was placed under the newly created NOAA and renamed the National Weather Service.

Organization

The NWS operates through national centres and a network of 122 local Weather Forecast Offices (WFOs), each responsible for forecasts, warnings and statements for its county warning area. Thirteen River Forecast Centers issue river and flood forecasts, and the National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP) include:

  • Storm Prediction Center (Norman, Oklahoma) — convective outlooks and severe thunderstorm and tornado watches.
  • National Hurricane Center (Miami, Florida) — tropical cyclone forecasts for the Atlantic and eastern Pacific.
  • Weather Prediction Center — national precipitation and surface analysis guidance.
  • Ocean Prediction Center, Space Weather Prediction Center, Climate Prediction Center, Environmental Modeling Center, Aviation Weather Center and the National Centers for Environmental Information.

Products and technology

The NWS issues public, aviation, marine, fire-weather and hydrologic forecasts, and life-saving watches and warnings for tornadoes, severe thunderstorms, flash floods, winter storms and hurricanes. Its principal observing systems include the NEXRAD network of WSR-88D Doppler weather radars, automated surface observing stations (ASOS), upper-air balloon soundings, and NOAA's GOES geostationary satellites. Warnings are disseminated through NOAA Weather Radio, the Emergency Alert System, Wireless Emergency Alerts to mobile phones, and weather.gov. Numerical weather prediction models used operationally include the GFS and the high-resolution HRRR.
