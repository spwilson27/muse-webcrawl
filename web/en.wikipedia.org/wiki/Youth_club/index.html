Youth club

A youth club or youth centre is a place where young people can meet and take part in a variety of activities, such as sports, games, music, drama and discussion groups. Youth clubs and centres are run by youth workers, teachers and volunteers, and typically open in the evenings and at weekends. Many are attached to schools, churches or community centres, or provided by local authorities and voluntary organisations.

History

The youth club movement grew in nineteenth-century Britain and other industrialising countries, where reformers sought to give working-class adolescents healthy recreation and moral guidance outside working hours. Organisations such as the Boys' Brigade, the Scouts and the Girls' Friendly Society combined club activities with uniformed or religious elements. In the twentieth century, state-funded youth services expanded, providing professional youth workers and purpose-built centres.

Activities and purpose

Clubs offer informal education: alongside recreation they may give advice on health, careers and relationships, support schoolwork, and provide a safe social space. Open-access clubs accept any young person in an area, while targeted projects work with groups such as young carers or those at risk of exclusion. Funding cuts in several countries since 2010 have closed many clubs, prompting debate about the consequences for youth crime and wellbeing. See also youth hostel and Scouting.
