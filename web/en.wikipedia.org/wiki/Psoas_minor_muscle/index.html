Psoas minor muscle

The psoas minor is a long, slender muscle of the abdomen and pelvis, located anterior to the psoas major muscle. It is present in only about 40–60% of the population, making it a variable or inconstant muscle; when absent, it is often replaced by a tendinous expansion of the iliac fascia.

Structure

The psoas minor arises from the sides of the bodies of the T12 and L1 vertebrae and the intervertebral disc between them. It descends as a small fleshy belly before continuing as a long, flat tendon that inserts onto the pectineal line of the pubis and the iliopubic eminence. It is innervated by the ventral ramus of the first lumbar nerve (L1).

Function

The psoas minor is a weak flexor of the trunk and the lumbar vertebral column. It also acts to tense the iliac fascia. Because of its variability and small size, its functional contribution is modest compared with the psoas major.

Clinical significance

A taut or hypertrophied psoas minor tendon has occasionally been implicated in abdominal or pelvic pain, and the muscle is a landmark in retroperitoneal surgery. Its absence is a normal anatomical variant and has no clinical consequence.
