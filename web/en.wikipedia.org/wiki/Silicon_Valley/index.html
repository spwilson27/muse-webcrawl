Overview

Silicon Valley is a region in the southern part of the San Francisco Bay Area in Northern California that serves as a global centre for high technology, innovation and venture capital. Its name, coined in 1971 by journalist Don Hoefler, refers to the silicon used in semiconductor chips — the industry that first defined it.

History

The Valley's roots run from Frederick Terman's Stanford electronics programmes and the 1939 Hewlett-Packard garage, through William Shockley's semiconductor laboratory (1956), the "traitorous eight" who founded Fairchild Semiconductor (1957), and their spin-offs (Intel, AMD and the venture firm Kleiner Perkins), to the personal-computer era (Apple, 1976; Xerox PARC's innovations), the internet boom (Google, Yahoo, eBay), social media (Facebook/Meta), and the 2020s artificial-intelligence surge centred on Nvidia and OpenAI-era firms. Stanford and Berkeley, defence research funding, California's ban on non-compete agreements and deep venture capital made the ecosystem self-reinforcing. Its costs — housing unaffordability, inequality and congestion — are the standing critique; the name is now used generically for tech clusters worldwide.
