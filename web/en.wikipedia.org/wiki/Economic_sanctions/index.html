Economic sanctions

[Image: a closed border checkpoint with barriers stopping freight trucks]

Economic sanctions are restrictions on trade, finance or other economic relations imposed by one country or group of countries on another to coerce a change in behaviour, punish wrongdoing or signal disapproval. Instruments range from tariffs and export controls to asset freezes, exclusion from financial messaging systems (SWIFT) and full embargoes.

Use and effectiveness

Modern sanctions regimes include those on Iran (nuclear programme; the 2015 JCPOA relief and 2018 US reimposition), North Korea, Cuba (US embargo since the 1960s) and — the largest in scale — Russia after its 2022 invasion of Ukraine, including central-bank asset freezes and oil Price caps. Research (Hufbauer and successors) finds sanctions succeed in their stated aims in a minority of cases, more often for modest goals against dependent targets, while imposing humanitarian costs that motivated 'smart' (targeted) sanctions on elites and specific sectors. Sanctions interact with trade policy, evasion networks and third-country intermediaries, and their expanding use in great-power rivalry is a live question in international political economy.
