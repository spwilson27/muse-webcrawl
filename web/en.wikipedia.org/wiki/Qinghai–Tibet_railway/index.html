Qinghai–Tibet railway

The Qinghai–Tibet railway (Qingzang railway) is a high-altitude railway connecting Xining in Qinghai Province to Lhasa in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. Opened in 2006, it is the world's highest railway, reaching 5,072 metres (16,640 ft) at the Tanggula Pass.

Length1,956 km (Xining–Lhasa)
Highest point5,072 m (Tanggula Pass)
Opened1 July 2006

Construction

The first section, from Xining to Golmud, was completed in 1984. The far more difficult Golmud–Lhasa section, begun in 2001, crosses more than 550 km of permafrost; engineers used crushed-rock embankments, cooling pipes and bridges to keep the frozen ground stable. The line includes the Fenghuoshan Tunnel, the world's highest rail tunnel, and wildlife crossings for migrating Tibetan antelope.

Operation

Passenger trains are pressurized and carry supplemental oxygen to reduce altitude sickness. The railway has transformed travel and trade between Tibet and the rest of China, and has since been extended by lines from Lhasa to Shigatse and Nyingchi.
