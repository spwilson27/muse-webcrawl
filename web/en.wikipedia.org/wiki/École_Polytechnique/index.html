École Polytechnique (French: [ekɔl pɔlitɛknik]), nicknamed l'X, is a French public institution of higher education and research in Palaiseau, near Paris, and one of the most prestigious of France's grandes écoles. Founded in 1794 during the French Revolution by the mathematician Gaspard Monge and others, it trains engineers, scientists, and senior civil servants, and remains a military academy under the Ministry of the Armed Forces.

History

The school was founded in 1794 as the École centrale des travaux publics to supply the Revolutionary state with engineers, and renamed École Polytechnique in 1795. Napoleon militarised it in 1804, giving it its motto "Pour la Patrie, les Sciences et la Gloire" ("For the Homeland, Science and Glory"). Students, admitted by a famously demanding competitive examination after classes préparatoires, hold military status during their studies. In 1976 the school moved from Paris's Latin Quarter to Palaiseau.

Academics

The flagship cycle ingénieur is a four-year multidisciplinary programme strong in mathematics, physics, computer science, economics and engineering, followed by specialisation at partner institutions. The school also offers bachelor's, master's and doctoral programmes, and is a founding member of the Institut Polytechnique de Paris (2019). Notable alumni include the mathematicians Cauchy, Fourier, and Poincaré's teachers' generation, the physicist Marie Curie's contemporaries, and numerous French presidents, ministers and CEOs; several alumni have won Nobel Prizes and Fields Medals.

Traditions

Students wear the grand uniforme with bicorne hat and sword at ceremonies, and the school marches in the Bastille Day parade. Alumni are known as X followed by their class year (e.g., "X1975").
