Orc

An orc (or ork) is a fictional humanoid monster creature found in folklore and modern fantasy. The concept was popularised by J. R. R. Tolkien in The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, where orcs are the brutal foot-soldiers of the Dark Lords Morgoth and Sauron. Orcs have since become a stock creature of fantasy fiction, role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, and video games.

Etymology and early uses

The word derives from Old English orc, a demon or ogre, ultimately from Latin Orcus, a god of the underworld. It appears in Beowulf among the monstrous races descended from Cain, and sporadically in early modern literature as a sea-monster or ogre, before Tolkien revived it.

Tolkien's orcs

In Tolkien's writings, orcs are a corrupted race of humanoids, bred in mockery of Elves (his texts offer several, not fully reconciled, accounts of their origin). They are smaller than Men, fanged, bow-legged, and hateful of sunlight, though Saruman's Uruk-hai are larger and can endure the sun. They speak debased tongues and the Black Speech imposed by Sauron.

In later fantasy

Through Dungeons & Dragons (1974) and Games Workshop's Warhammer setting — where greenskins are boisterous, football-hooligan-like "Orks" — the orc became a standard fantasy "race", often green-skinned, warlike and tribal. Later works have increasingly portrayed orcs with greater nuance or as playable, sympathetic peoples.
