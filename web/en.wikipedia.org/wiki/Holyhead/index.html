Holyhead

Holyhead (Welsh: Caergybi) is a town in Anglesey, Wales, and the largest town in the county. It is known for its busy ferry port with services to Dublin, Ireland, across the Irish Sea.

History

The town grew around St Cybi's Church, founded in the 6th century within the walls of a late Roman fort. In the 19th century Holyhead became the terminus of the London mail route and railway, with a vast harbour and breakwater — at 2.7 km, the longest in the UK — completed in 1873.

Geography

The town stands on Holy Island, connected to Anglesey by causeways. Nearby Holyhead Mountain and South Stack lighthouse and seabird cliffs are popular with walkers.

Transport

Holyhead railway station and the A55 expressway link the port to Chester and the Welsh mainland via the Britannia Bridge.
