History of paleontology

DisciplinePaleontology
Key figuresMary Anning, Cuvier, Owen, Darwin

The history of paleontology is the history of understanding fossils — from ancient curiosities to the science of life's evolution over geological time.

Before science

Fossils were collected in antiquity and the Middle Ages and explained as sports of nature or remnants of the biblical Flood. Leonardo da Vinci and Nicolaus Steno (17th century) argued fossils were remains of once-living organisms and laid stratigraphic principles.

The heroic age

Georges Cuvier demonstrated extinction and founded comparative anatomy around 1800. Mary Anning's Lyme Regis discoveries (ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs) fed British "heroic age" geology; William Buckland and Gideon Mantell described the first dinosaurs, named by Richard Owen in 1842. Charles Lyell's uniformitarianism and Darwin's Origin of Species (1859) made fossils evidence of evolution.

Modern paleontology

The American "Bone Wars" of Cope and Marsh, 20th-century finds in China and Africa, radiometric dating, cladistics and the dinosaur renaissance (from the 1960s, linking dinosaurs to birds) reshaped the field; CT scanning and geochemistry now read life's history in unprecedented detail.
