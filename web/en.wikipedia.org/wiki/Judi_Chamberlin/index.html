Judi Chamberlin

Judi Chamberlin (born Judith Rosenberg; October 30, 1944 – January 16, 2010) was an American activist, author and educator who helped found the psychiatric survivors movement. After being involuntarily committed in 1966 following a depression, she became a leading critic of forced psychiatric treatment.

Activism

Chamberlin joined the Mental Patients Liberation Front in Boston in the early 1970s and helped establish the Ruby Rogers Drop-In Center, one of the first peer-run centres for psychiatric survivors. In 1978 she published On Our Own: Patient-Controlled Alternatives to the Mental Health System, a foundational text arguing for alternatives controlled by former patients themselves. She co-founded the National Empowerment Center and worked internationally for the rights of people with psychosocial disabilities, influencing the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
