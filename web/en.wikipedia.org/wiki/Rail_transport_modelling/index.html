Rail transport modelling

A model railway layout with detailed scenery.

Rail transport modelling (American English: railroad modeling) is a hobby in which rail transport systems are modelled at a reduced scale. It encompasses miniature trains, track, scenery, buildings, and signalling, arranged on a layout and often operated to a timetable.

Scales

Popular scales include HO (1:87), the most popular worldwide; N (1:160), favoured where space is limited; OO (1:76) in Britain; O (1:43 or 1:48); and Z (1:220). Larger scales, including live-steam garden railways, are also popular. Standards for track, wheels, and couplings are maintained by bodies such as the NMRA in North America and MOROP (NERM standards) in Europe.

Control

Traditional layouts vary track voltage to control speed (analogue control). Digital Command Control (DCC), standardised in the 1990s, sends coded signals through the track so multiple locomotives can be controlled independently on the same line, with sound and lighting effects.

The hobby

Modellers range from collectors of ready-to-run equipment to scratch-builders who construct locomotives and scenery from raw materials. Modular standards such as Free-Mo allow clubs to join layouts for exhibitions. Notable public layouts include Miniatur Wunderland in Hamburg, the world's largest model railway.
