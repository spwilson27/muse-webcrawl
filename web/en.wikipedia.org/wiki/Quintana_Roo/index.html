Quintana Roo is one of the 32 states of Mexico, occupying the eastern part of the Yucatán Peninsula. It is bordered by the states of Campeche and Yucatán to the west, the Caribbean Sea to the east, and the nation of Belize to the south. Its capital is Chetumal, on Chetumal Bay near the Belizean border, while its largest and best-known city is Cancún.

History

The region formed part of the heartland of the Maya civilisation; the pre-Columbian ruins of Tulum, Cobá, and Chacchoben still stand within its borders. During the colonial era and the nineteenth century the area was a remote frontier, and during the Caste War of Yucatán (1847–1901) it was the stronghold of the independent Maya of Chan Santa Cruz. President Porfirio Díaz created the federal territory of Quintana Roo in 1902, naming it for the independence hero Andrés Quintana Roo. The territory became Mexico's 31st state on 8 October 1974.

Tourism and economy

From the 1970s the Mexican government deliberately developed Cancún from a near-empty barrier island into one of the world's leading beach resorts. The resort corridor south of the city — the Riviera Maya, including Playa del Carmen and Tulum — together with the islands of Cozumel and Isla Mujeres makes tourism the overwhelming driver of the state's economy and population growth; Quintana Roo has repeatedly been Mexico's fastest-growing state. The offshore Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the world's second-largest barrier reef system, supports diving and fishing. The state is also crossed by sections of the Tren Maya railway, inaugurated in 2023–24.
