West Germany was the common name for the Federal Republic of Germany between its formation on 23 May 1949 and German reunification on 3 October 1990, when the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) acceded to it. Formed from the American, British and French occupation zones (the Trizone), it comprised the western two-thirds of Germany's post-war territory.

History

Under Chancellor Konrad Adenauer, the new state adopted the Basic Law, anchored itself in the West — joining NATO in 1955 and co-founding the European Economic Community in 1957 — and pursued the Wirtschaftswunder ("economic miracle") under Economics Minister Ludwig Erhard's social market economy. The Hallstein Doctrine claimed sole representation of Germany; Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik in the 1970s normalised relations with the East, including the 1972 Basic Treaty with the GDR. West Berlin, an exclave 160 km inside East Germany, remained under Allied occupation but was closely integrated, most famously during the Berlin Airlift crisis of 1948–49 and after the Berlin Wall's construction in 1961.

Reunification

Following the fall of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989, Chancellor Helmut Kohl negotiated the Two Plus Four Treaty and the GDR's accession; on 3 October 1990 West Germany's Basic Law extended eastward and the Federal Republic simply continued, enlarged — so "West Germany" ceased to be a meaningful separate designation.
