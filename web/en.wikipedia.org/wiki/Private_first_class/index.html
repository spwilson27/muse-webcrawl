Private first class

Placeholder image: U.S. Army PFC insignia (single chevron over rocker)

Private first class (PFC) is a military rank recognised, in different forms, by many armed forces around the world. In the United States Army and Marine Corps it is an enlisted rank above private and below corporal or specialist, typically held by soldiers with about a year of service who have demonstrated proficiency in basic duties.

United States

In the U.S. Army, private first class is the third enlisted rank (pay grade E-3), above private E-2 and below specialist or corporal (E-4); promotion is generally automatic after 12 months' time in service and 4 months in grade. In the U.S. Marine Corps, PFC is the second enlisted rank (E-2), above private and below lance corporal, and is awarded after six months' service. Historically, the Army's First World War-era PFC was a specialist appointment rather than a grade, and the rank was restructured several times before the modern pay-grade system.

Other countries

Equivalent or similarly named ranks exist elsewhere: Singapore's armed forces have a private first class rank; the Philippine Army and Marine Corps use PFC; and several other armies influenced by the U.S. system employ the title. France's soldat de première classe is a related distinction rather than a full rank.
