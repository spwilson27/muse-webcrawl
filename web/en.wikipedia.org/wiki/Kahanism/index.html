Kahanism

Kahanism is a religious Zionist ideology based on the views of Rabbi Meir Kahane (1932–1990), founder of the Jewish Defense League in the United States and of the Kach party in Israel. It combines Jewish nationalism with religious fundamentalism, advocating a theocratic Jewish state governed by halakha (Jewish religious law), the expulsion of Arabs from Israel and the occupied territories, and annexation of the "Greater Land of Israel." Kach was barred from Israel's Knesset in 1988 under anti-racism legislation and designated a terrorist organization by Israel in 1994; it is also listed as a terrorist group by the United States, Canada, and the European Union.

Origins and doctrine

Kahane, born in Brooklyn, founded the Jewish Defense League in 1968, promoting Jewish self-defense under the slogan "Never Again." Immigrating to Israel in 1971, he founded Kach ("Thus!"), which won a single Knesset seat in 1984. Kahane's program — spelled out in works such as They Must Go (1980) — held that Arab citizens of Israel posed a demographic and religious threat that could be resolved only by their removal, compensated or otherwise; that democracy and halakha were ultimately incompatible; and that the state should be reorganized on religious law. Kahane was convicted in both the US and Israel of various offences, including conspiracy to manufacture explosives, and was assassinated in New York in 1990 by El Sayyid Nosair.

Banning and later movements

After Kahane's sole Knesset term, Israel's Central Elections Committee disqualified Kach in 1988 as racist. Following the 1994 Cave of the Patriarchs massacre, in which Kahane follower Baruch Goldstein murdered 29 Palestinian worshippers in Hebron, the Israeli government outlawed Kach and its offshoot Kahane Chai as terrorist organizations. Kahanist ideas nonetheless persisted in movements such as Lehava and in parties of the far right; Itamar Ben-Gvir, leader of Otzma Yehudit (Jewish Power) and a self-described former Kahane follower, entered government as national security minister in 2022, though mainstream Kahanist organizations remain banned.
