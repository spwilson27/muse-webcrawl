Islamic Republic of Iran

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia — see also Iran


Flag of the Islamic Republic of Iran, adopted in 1980

The Islamic Republic of Iran is the formal name of the Iranian state as constituted following the Iranian Revolution of 1979. Articles dealing with Iran's history, geography, economy and society are collected under Iran; this article concerns the political system established in 1979.

Establishment

After the overthrow of Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, a referendum in March 1979 approved the creation of an Islamic republic, and a new constitution — adopted in December 1979 — institutionalised the doctrine of Velayat-e Faqih (Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist), under which a senior cleric, the Supreme Leader, holds ultimate authority over the state. Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, the revolution's leader, became the first Supreme Leader; on his death in 1989 he was succeeded by Ali Khamenei.

Political system

The system combines elected institutions — a president, a unicameral parliament (the Islamic Consultative Assembly, or Majles) and an Assembly of Experts that selects the Supreme Leader — with clerical oversight bodies. The Guardian Council vets candidates and legislation for conformity with Islam and the constitution; the Expediency Council arbitrates disputes. The Supreme Leader commands the armed forces, including the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), appoints the head of the judiciary and state broadcasting, and sets the broad direction of policy.

Character and debates

Scholars describe the Islamic Republic as a hybrid theocratic-republican or authoritarian system, with political competition permitted within limits set by the clerical establishment. Periodic protest movements — including the 2009 Green Movement and the 2022–2023 protests following the death of Mahsa Amini — have challenged the system's clerical rule, compulsory hijab laws and economic conditions, and have been met with repression. Iran's foreign policy under the Islamic Republic emphasises resistance to the United States and Israel, support for allied regional movements, and the pursuit of a nuclear programme that has brought extensive international sanctions.
