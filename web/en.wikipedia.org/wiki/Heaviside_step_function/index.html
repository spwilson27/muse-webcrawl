The Heaviside step function, or unit step function, usually denoted by H, θ, or u, is a discontinuous function named after Oliver Heaviside, whose value is zero for negative arguments and one for positive arguments: H(x) = 0 for x < 0 and H(x) = 1 for x > 0. The value at zero is a matter of convention — commonly 1, 0, or 1/2 (the symmetry-preserving choice).

Properties

The step function is the integral of the Dirac delta function: H(x) = ∫₋∞ˣ δ(s) ds, and conversely the delta function is its derivative in the sense of distributions. It is also the cumulative distribution function of a degenerate random variable concentrated at zero. Products and shifts of step functions represent signals switched on at a chosen time.

Applications

Heaviside introduced the function in his operational calculus for solving differential equations in electrical engineering, where H(t) models turning on a voltage or current at t = 0. Step functions are central to Laplace transform methods, signal processing (as the step response of a system), control theory, and probability. Smooth approximations, such as the logistic function, converge to H as a steepness parameter grows.

General forms

Definitions with H(0) = 1 are used in computer algebra systems and signal-processing conventions where maximal right-continuity is convenient; the ramp function is the integral of the step function, and any piecewise-constant function can be built as a sum of shifted, scaled step functions.
