Famous examplesJuan Fernández Islands (Alexander Selkirk); Clipperton Island; Devon Island

A desert island, or deserted island, is an island, islet, or atoll that is not permanently populated by humans. Uninhabited islands are often depicted in films and stories about shipwrecked people, and are also used as stereotypes for the concept of "paradise". Here "desert" is used in its older sense of "deserted or unoccupied", rather than meaning an arid climate; many desert islands are lush tropical islands.

Notable examples and castaways

The most famous real-life castaway account is that of Alexander Selkirk, marooned on an island in the Juan Fernández archipelago from 1704 to 1709, an experience that inspired Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe (1719), the archetype of the desert-island story. Among the largest uninhabited islands is Devon Island in the Canadian Arctic. Some uninhabited islands are protected as nature reserves, such as Aldabra in the Seychelles.

In culture

The desert island is a durable setting and thought experiment: "desert island discs" (the BBC programme Desert Island Discs, running since 1942) asks guests which recordings they would take if cast away; the trope recurs in works from The Swiss Family Robinson and Lord of the Flies to Gilligan's Island and Cast Away.
