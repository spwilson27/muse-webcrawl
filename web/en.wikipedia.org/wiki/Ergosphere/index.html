Ergosphere

The ergosphere is a region located outside the event horizon of a rotating black hole. Its name was proposed by Remo Ruffini and John Archibald Wheeler, derived from the Greek word ergon meaning "work", because it is theoretically possible to extract energy and mass from a black hole within this region. The ergosphere has an oblate spheroidal shape that coincides with the event horizon at the poles and extends out to a greater equatorial radius.

Properties

A black hole with angular momentum has an ergosphere whose outer boundary is the static limit (ergosurface) and whose inner boundary is the event horizon. Within the ergosphere, the frame-dragging effect of the Kerr spacetime is so extreme that no object can remain stationary with respect to a distant observer: everything is forced to rotate in the same direction as the black hole. Nevertheless, unlike objects inside the event horizon, objects in the ergosphere can still escape to infinity.

Energy extraction

In the Penrose process, a particle entering the ergosphere splits into two parts; one falls into the black hole with negative energy (as measured at infinity), and the other escapes carrying more energy than the original particle brought in, extracting rotational energy from the black hole. A related mechanism, the Blandford–Znajek process, is thought to power relativistic jets from active galactic nuclei.
