Josiah

Josiah (c. 648 – 609 BCE) was the sixteenth king of Judah, reigning from approximately 640 to 609 BCE according to the Hebrew Bible. Ascending the throne at age eight after the assassination of his father Amon, he is portrayed in the Books of Kings and Chronicles as one of Judah's most righteous kings, famous for a sweeping religious reform centred on the "Book of the Law" discovered in the Temple — a discovery many scholars associate with the composition or promulgation of Deuteronomy.

Reign and reform

According to 2 Kings, in the eighteenth year of his reign (622 BCE), during repairs to the Temple in Jerusalem, the high priest Hilkiah found "the book of the law". When it was read to him, Josiah tore his clothes in distress, consulted the prophetess Huldah, and launched a thoroughgoing reform: he purged the Temple of vessels for Baal and Asherah, deposed idolatrous priests, destroyed high places and altars throughout Judah and the former northern kingdom (including the altar at Bethel), abolished mediums and household gods, and centralized sacrifice at Jerusalem. He then celebrated a Passover described as unmatched since the days of the judges.

Death

In 609 BCE Pharaoh Necho II of Egypt marched north through Judah to aid Assyria against Babylon. Josiah confronted him at Megiddo and was killed in the battle, despite (in the Chronicler's telling) Necho's warning that he came by divine command. Josiah was buried in Jerusalem amid great lamentation; the prophet Jeremiah is said to have composed laments for him. His sons Jehoahaz and Jehoiakim succeeded him as the kingdom moved toward its destruction by Babylon in 586 BCE.

Historical assessment

Josiah's reform is a landmark in the history of Israelite religion and biblical studies: since W. M. L. de Wette (1805), scholars have linked the found lawbook with Deuteronomy and the "Deuteronomistic" editing of Israel's history. Whether the discovery was genuine or a pious construction remains debated, as does the extent of Josiah's expansion into the former northern territories.
