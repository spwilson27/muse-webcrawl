Institut Laue–Langevin
Location: Grenoble, France
Established: 1967
Type: International research centre
Named after: Max von Laue and Paul Langevin

The Institut Laue–Langevin (ILL) is an international research centre at the leading edge of neutron science and technology, located in Grenoble, France. It operates one of the most intense continuous neutron sources in the world, a 58 MW high-flux research reactor.

Founded in 1967 as a Franco-German institute and later joined by the United Kingdom, the ILL is named after physicists Max von Laue and Paul Langevin. Its neutron scattering instruments serve thousands of visiting researchers each year in fields from condensed matter physics and chemistry to biology and materials science.
