Mickey Mouse (film series)

Mickey Mouse as he appeared in the classic theatrical shorts

Mickey Mouse is a series of American animated short films produced by Walt Disney Productions and released through various distributors. The series stars the studio's mascot, Mickey Mouse, who debuted in the landmark sound cartoon Steamboat Willie on November 18, 1928. Between 1928 and 1953, more than 130 theatrical shorts were produced in the series, making it one of the most successful animated film series of the Golden Age of American animation.

History

Mickey was created by Walt Disney and Ub Iwerks after Disney lost the rights to his earlier character, Oswald the Lucky Rabbit. The first two Mickey cartoons, Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho, were produced as silent films, but it was the third, Steamboat Willie, with synchronized sound, that made Mickey a star. The series introduced recurring characters including Minnie Mouse, Pluto, Goofy, and Donald Duck, the last of whom gradually eclipsed Mickey in popularity and received his own series.

Notable films

Highlights of the series include The Band Concert (1935), the first Mickey cartoon in colour; Brave Little Tailor (1938), nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Short Film; and Lend a Paw (1941), which won the Oscar. Regular theatrical production ended in 1953 with The Simple Things. Mickey later returned in featurettes such as Mickey's Christmas Carol (1983) and the short Get a Horse! (2013).

Legacy

The early Mickey Mouse cartoons are landmark works of animation history. Steamboat Willie entered the United States public domain on January 1, 2024, when its 95-year copyright term expired, an event widely covered in discussions of copyright law and the public domain.
