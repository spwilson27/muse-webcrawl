Heike Kamerlingh Onnes

Born21 September 1853, Groningen, Netherlands
Died21 February 1926 (aged 72), Leiden, Netherlands
Known forLiquefaction of helium; discovery of superconductivity
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1913)

Heike Kamerlingh Onnes (21 September 1853 – 21 February 1926) was a Dutch physicist. He founded the cryogenics laboratory at Leiden University, achieved the first liquefaction of helium in 1908, and in 1911 discovered Superconductivity — the abrupt disappearance of electrical resistance in mercury cooled below about 4.2 K. He received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1913 "for his investigations on the properties of matter at low temperatures".

Leiden laboratory

Appointed professor at Leiden in 1882, Kamerlingh Onnes built his laboratory into the world centre of low-temperature physics, guided by the motto Door meten tot weten ("through measurement to knowledge"). His liquefaction of helium on 10 July 1908 reached temperatures below 1 K and opened the temperature range in which superconductivity and superfluidity would later be found.

Discovery of superconductivity

In April 1911 his team found that the resistance of a mercury wire dropped to immeasurably small values at 4.2 K. He coined the term "superconductivity" for the phenomenon. His group's measurements, and his liquefaction techniques, laid the experimental foundations later explained by BCS theory (1957).
