The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction (usually referred to as F&SF) is a US science fiction and fantasy magazine, first published in 1949 as The Magazine of Fantasy. It is the longest-running magazine of its type, known for its literary quality and its digest format.

History

The magazine was founded by Lawrence E. Spivak's Mercury Press, with Anthony Boucher and J. Francis McComas as editors. From the start it aimed at a more sophisticated readership than the pulp magazines, publishing fantasy, horror and science fiction alongside book reviews and, later, science columns by Isaac Asimov, whose "Science" essays ran in the magazine for over thirty years.

Contents and significance

F&SF published many landmark stories, including Daniel Keyes's "Flowers for Algernon", Robert A. Heinlein's Starship Troopers (serialised as "Starship Soldier"), Stephen King's The Dark Tower stories, and works by Harlan Ellison, Ursula K. Le Guin, Roger Zelazny and Shirley Jackson. Its stories have won numerous Hugo and Nebula Awards. Under editor Edward L. Ferman (1966–1991) the magazine reached particular prominence; Gordon Van Gelder became editor in 1997 and later publisher.
