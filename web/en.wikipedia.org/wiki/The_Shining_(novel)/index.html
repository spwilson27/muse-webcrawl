The Shining is a 1977 horror novel by American author Stephen King. It is King's third published novel and first hardcover bestseller. The novel follows Jack Torrance, a struggling writer and recovering alcoholic who accepts a job as the winter caretaker of the isolated Overlook Hotel in the Colorado Rockies, bringing his wife Wendy and five-year-old son Danny, who possesses psychic abilities called "the shining". As a winter storm cuts the hotel off from the outside world, the building's violent past and malevolent supernatural forces drive Jack toward madness and murder.

Plot summary

Jack Torrance, fired from a teaching job after assaulting a student, takes the caretaker post hoping to rebuild his family and finish a play. The hotel's cook, Dick Hallorann, recognizes Danny's gift and warns him about the hotel. Over the winter, the Overlook manifests apparitions — including the woman in Room 217, the ghostly bartender Lloyd, and a masked ball — exploiting Jack's alcoholism and resentment. Danny summons Hallorann telepathically; in the climax, Jack attacks his family but momentarily recovers himself, and the neglected boiler explodes, destroying the hotel as Wendy, Danny, and Hallorann escape.

Inspiration and themes

King was inspired by a stay at the Stanley Hotel in Estes Park, Colorado, in 1974, when he and his wife were the only guests as the hotel closed for the season; the idea also drew on Ray Bradbury's and Shirley Jackson's haunted-house fiction and on King's own struggles with alcohol and anger. Themes include addiction, family breakdown, and the cyclical nature of violence — the novel famously includes the repeated typed line "All work and no play makes Jack a dull boy".

Adaptations

Stanley Kubrick's 1980 film adaptation, starring Jack Nicholson, became a landmark of horror cinema, though King disliked its departures from the novel and later wrote a 1997 television miniseries closer to his text. A sequel novel, Doctor Sleep (2013), follows the adult Danny Torrance and was itself filmed in 2019.
