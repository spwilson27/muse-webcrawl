HTTP/2

HTTP/2 is the second major version of the Hypertext Transfer Protocol, published by the IETF in 2015 as RFC 7540 and updated by RFC 9113 in 2022. It succeeded HTTP/1.1, which had been in use since the late 1990s, and was itself later joined by HTTP/3.

Design

Whereas HTTP/1.1 messages are textual and each TCP connection effectively handles one request at a time (pipelining being rarely deployed), HTTP/2 introduces a binary framing layer. Requests and responses are broken into frames belonging to numbered streams, which are multiplexed over a single connection. Header fields are compressed with the HPACK algorithm, and servers may push resources to clients proactively.

Adoption

HTTP/2 was based on Google's SPDY protocol. All major browsers support it, almost exclusively over TLS connections negotiated via ALPN. It accounts for a large share of traffic to major websites, and its successor HTTP/3 retains most of its semantics while replacing TCP with QUIC.
