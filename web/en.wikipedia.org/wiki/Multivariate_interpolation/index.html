Multivariate interpolation

In numerical analysis, multivariate interpolation (also called spatial interpolation in some contexts) is interpolation on functions of more than one variable; the unknown function is interpolated at known points and the method gives values at arbitrary points in the domain. It generalizes one-dimensional interpolation to surfaces and volumes, and is much less straightforward: there is no universal analogue of polynomial interpolation for scattered data.

Regular grids

When data lie on a regular grid, tensor-product methods apply: bilinear and trilinear interpolation extend linear interpolation dimension by dimension, while bicubic interpolation and multivariate splines (tensor-product B-splines) give smoother results. For smooth periodic functions, multivariate trigonometric interpolation via the discrete Fourier transform is standard.

Scattered data

For irregularly distributed points, common methods include:

  • Nearest-neighbour interpolation — assigns the value of the closest known point (equivalently, a Voronoi tessellation).
  • Inverse distance weighting (Shepard's method) — a weighted average with weights decreasing with distance.
  • Radial basis function interpolation — represents the interpolant as a sum of radial kernels (e.g., Gaussian, multiquadric, thin-plate spline) centred at the data points.
  • Triangulation-based methods — piecewise-linear or Clough–Tocher interpolation over a Delaunay triangulation.
  • Kriging — a geostatistical method giving best linear unbiased predictions under a spatial covariance model.

Applications

Multivariate interpolation is used in image resampling and resizing, terrain modelling from surveyed elevations, weather and climate data gridding, geological and mining estimation, computer graphics (surface reconstruction, texture mapping), and in the construction of surrogate models in engineering design and machine learning.
