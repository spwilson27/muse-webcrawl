Lester Keith Piggott (5 November 1935 – 29 May 2022) was an English professional jockey and trainer, widely regarded as one of the greatest flat-racing jockeys of all time. Nicknamed "The Long Fellow" for his height and unusually upright style, he rode 4,493 winners in Britain and won the Epsom Derby a record nine times.

Career

From a famous racing family, Piggott rode his first winner at age twelve in 1948 and his first Derby at eighteen, on Never Say Die in 1954. His other Derby winners included Crepello, Sir Ivor, Nijinsky (completing the English Triple Crown in 1970), Roberto, The Minstrel and Teenoso. He was champion jockey eleven times and was especially associated with trainers Noel Murless and Vincent O'Brien.

Later life

Piggott retired from riding in 1985 to train, but in 1987 was jailed for tax fraud and stripped of his OBE. In one of sport's most famous comebacks he returned to the saddle in 1990, winning the Breeders' Cup Mile on Royal Academy ten days after his release, aged 54. He retired finally in 1995 and died in Switzerland in 2022.
