Danish Crown Jewels

The Danish Crown Jewels are the symbols of the Danish monarchy. They consist principally of a crown, a sceptre, a globus cruciger, the Sword of State and an ampulla. The crown jewels are kept at Rosenborg Castle in Copenhagen, where they form part of the collection of the Danish Royal Collections.

History

The older Danish regalia date from the reign of Christian IV in the early 17th century. The crown currently used by Danish monarchs is the Crown of Christian V, made in 1670–1671 by the goldsmith Paul Kurtz in Copenhagen. It is made of gold and decorated with enamel and table-cut stones, and is surmounted by a monde and cross.

The crown has not been worn by a Danish monarch since the coronation of Christian VIII in 1840, as Denmark's later monarchs have been proclaimed rather than crowned. The jewels are displayed to the public in the treasury of Rosenborg Castle alongside the older Crown of Christian IV and the magnificent crown of Queen Sophie Magdalene.

The collection

Among the most notable pieces are the sceptre and orb of Frederick III, the electoral sword, and the celebrated set of Queen Caroline Amalie's jewellery. The collection also includes the Rosenborg wine, one of the finest pieces of Renaissance goldsmith work in Denmark.
