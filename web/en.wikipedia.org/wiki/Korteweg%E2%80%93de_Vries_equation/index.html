Korteweg–de Vries equation

The Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation is a nonlinear partial differential equation that models waves on shallow water surfaces, combining weak nonlinearity with weak dispersion. It takes the form ∂tu + 6u∂xu + ∂xxxu = 0 and is named after Diederik Korteweg and Gustav de Vries, who derived it in 1895.

Solitons

The KdV equation famously admits soliton solutions — localised waves that travel unchanged and pass through one another elastically. It explains the solitary wave observed by John Scott Russell in a canal in 1834. Rediscovered numerically by Kruskal and Zabusky in 1965, the soliton concept transformed the study of nonlinear waves.

Integrability

The equation is exactly solvable by the inverse scattering transform (developed in 1967) and possesses infinitely many conserved quantities, making it a prototype integrable system with applications from fluid dynamics to plasma physics and optical fibres.
