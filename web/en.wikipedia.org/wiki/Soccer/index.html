Soccer

Soccer, known as football in most of the world and formally as association football, is a team sport played between two teams of eleven players on a rectangular field with a goal at each end. The object is to score by getting the ball into the opposing goal; players mainly use their feet, and the goalkeepers are the only players permitted to handle the ball within their penalty area. It is the world's most popular sport, played by over 250 million people in more than 200 countries.

History

Ball games resembling football were played in ancient China (cuju), Greece and Mesoamerica, and folk football was common in medieval England. The modern game was codified in England in 1863 when the Football Association was founded and the Laws of the Game were drawn up, separating association football from rugby. The international governing body, FIFA, was founded in 1904.

Laws and gameplay

A standard match lasts 90 minutes, in two 45-minute halves. The Laws of the Game, maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB), govern the field, ball, offside rule, fouls and misconduct, free kicks, penalty kicks, throw-ins, goal kicks and corner kicks. If a knockout match is level, it may go to extra time and a penalty shoot-out.

Competitions

The FIFA World Cup, held every four years since 1930, is the most prestigious tournament and the most-watched sporting event in the world. Continental championships include the UEFA European Championship, Copa América and Africa Cup of Nations. Top club competitions include the UEFA Champions League and domestic leagues such as the English Premier League, Spain's La Liga and Germany's Bundesliga. The name "soccer" derives from Oxford "-er" slang for "association" and is standard in the United States, Canada, Australia and several other countries where another code of football dominates.
