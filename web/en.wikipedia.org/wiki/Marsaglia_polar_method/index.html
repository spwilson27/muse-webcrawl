Marsaglia polar method

The Marsaglia polar method is a method for generating a pair of independent standard normal random variables from uniform random numbers, introduced by George Marsaglia in 1964. It is a variant of the Box–Muller transform that avoids trigonometric functions.

Algorithm

Generate u and v uniformly on (−1, 1) and let s = u² + v². If s ≥ 1 or s = 0, discard and retry. Otherwise compute the factor √(−2 ln s / s); then x = u·factor and y = v·factor are two independent standard normal samples.

Discussion

Points (u, v) are accepted only inside the unit circle (probability π/4 ≈ 78.5% per trial), and the method uses the point's polar representation implicitly — hence "polar". Because it needs only a logarithm and a square root, it was historically faster than Box–Muller on many machines and remains a standard technique in simulation software.
