The Cast Courts are two large gallery halls at the Victoria and Albert Museum in London, housing one of the world's most extensive collections of plaster casts of sculpture. Opened in 1873 as the Architectural Courts, they were designed so that students, artists and the public could study faithful full-size reproductions of Europe's greatest monuments without travelling.

Collection

The courts contain casts of medieval and Renaissance works, including Michelangelo's David (which famously had a detachable plaster fig leaf for royal visits), the pulpit by Giovanni Pisano, Ghiberti's Gates of Paradise, and the Pórtico de la Gloria from Santiago de Compostela. The cast of Trajan's Column from Rome is so tall that it is displayed in two halves. The collection was assembled in the spirit of Henry Cole, the museum's first director, and the 1867 international convention promoting the exchange of reproductions of works of art.

History

In the 20th century, as originals became prized over reproductions, many cast collections worldwide were destroyed or dispersed; the V&A's survived and have been restored and reinterpreted. The two courts — the Weston Cast Court and the Chitra Nirmal Sethia Cast Court — reopened after major refurbishment in 2014 and 2018 respectively.
