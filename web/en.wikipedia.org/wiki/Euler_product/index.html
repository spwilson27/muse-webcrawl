Euler product

Image: Euler product (placeholder)

An Euler product is an expansion of a Dirichlet series into an infinite product indexed by prime numbers. It is named after Leonhard Euler, who proved the identity for the Riemann zeta function in 1737. Euler products are fundamental in analytic number theory because they encode the unique prime factorisation of integers in analytic form.

The zeta function

For the Riemann zeta function, Euler showed that for Re(s) > 1,

ζ(s) = Σn≥1 n−s = Πp prime (1 − p−s)−1.

The identity follows by expanding each factor as a geometric series and using the fundamental theorem of arithmetic: every positive integer appears exactly once as a product of prime powers. Because ζ(s) diverges as s → 1, the product must run over infinitely many primes, giving a new proof of Euclid's theorem that there are infinitely many primes.

General theory

More generally, if f is a completely multiplicative arithmetic function, its Dirichlet series factors as a product over primes of simple local factors. Dirichlet L-functions and the L-functions of modular forms and elliptic curves all admit Euler products, and the shape of the local factors encodes deep arithmetic information, such as the number of points on an elliptic curve modulo p.
