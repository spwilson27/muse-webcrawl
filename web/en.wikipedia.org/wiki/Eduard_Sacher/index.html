Eduard Sacher (8 October 1843 – 22 November 1892) was an Austrian hotelier and restaurateur, founder of the Hotel Sacher in Vienna and son of Franz Sacher, the confectioner credited with creating the Sachertorte in 1832.

Life and career

Eduard was born in Vienna, the son of Franz Sacher, who as a sixteen-year-old apprentice cook had created the famous chocolate cake for Prince Metternich. Eduard trained in the delicatessen and restaurant trade and built up a successful provisions business in Vienna, marketing his father's cake and fine foodstuffs. He opened a renowned restaurant and in 1876 established the Hotel Sacher on the site of the demolished Kärntnertor theatre, opposite the Vienna State Opera.

Hotel Sacher

Under Eduard and then, after his early death in 1892, under his formidable widow Anna Sacher (née Fuchs), the hotel became one of the great social institutions of imperial Vienna — famous for its restaurant, its private dining rooms (the chambres séparées), and its clientele of aristocrats, artists and politicians. Anna Sacher, recognisable by her French bulldogs and cigars, ran the hotel until 1929 and made it a legend of Viennese hospitality.

The Sacher enterprise also guarded the "Original Sacher-Torte" name, leading in the 20th century to a famous legal dispute with the Demel bakery over which house could claim the authentic cake — settled with the Hotel Sacher using the "Original" designation and a round chocolate seal, Demel an "Eduard-Sacher-Torte" variant with a triangular seal.
