Multiple-unit train control

Multiple-unit train control, often abbreviated to multiple-unit or MU, is a system for controlling several self-propelled rail vehicles or locomotives simultaneously from a single driving cab, using electrical connections (jumper cables or couplers) that carry control signals through the train.

History

The concept was developed in the 1890s, most notably by Frank J. Sprague, whose system for electric traction cars was first demonstrated in Chicago in 1897. It made the modern electric and diesel multiple unit train possible: each car contributes traction power, yet the whole consist responds to one driver's controls.

Operation

Control circuits operate contactors that regulate traction motors, along with braking, doors, and auxiliary systems. On locomotives, MU allows several diesel-electric units to be coupled and driven as one — the standard arrangement for heavy freight in North America — and permits distributed power placements elsewhere in the train. Compatibility between manufacturers led to standardized MU connections such as the AAR 27-pin system.
