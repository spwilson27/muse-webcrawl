Roe Erister "Rick" Hall (January 31, 1932 – January 2, 2018) was an American record producer, songwriter, and musician who founded FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals, Alabama. Known as the "Father of Muscle Shoals Music", he produced landmark soul and pop recordings by artists including Aretha Franklin, Etta James, Wilson Pickett, Clarence Carter, and Otis Redding.

Early life

Hall was raised in poverty in rural Mississippi and Alabama. After working as a musician and songwriter in Florence, Alabama — where he wrote the 1960 hit "You Better Move On", recorded by Arthur Alexander — he used the song's royalties to build his own recording studio.

FAME Studios

Hall established FAME (Florence Alabama Music Enterprises) in 1959 and moved the studio to Muscle Shoals in the early 1960s. With his house rhythm section, later known as the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section ("the Swampers"), FAME created a distinctive blend of soul, R&B, and country. In 1967 Hall produced Aretha Franklin's breakthrough Atlantic sessions, including "I Never Loved a Man (The Way I Love You)". Other classic FAME recordings include Percy Sledge's "When a Man Loves a Woman" and hits by Wilson Pickett, Etta James, and Clarence Carter.

In 1969 the Swampers left to found the competing Muscle Shoals Sound Studio, but Hall rebuilt with a new rhythm section and continued producing hits through the 1970s and beyond, expanding into country music and winning a Grammy Trustees Award in 2014.

Legacy

Hall's story and FAME's rivalry with Muscle Shoals Sound were told in the 2013 documentary Muscle Shoals. He was inducted into the Alabama Music Hall of Fame. He died at his home in Muscle Shoals on January 2, 2018.
