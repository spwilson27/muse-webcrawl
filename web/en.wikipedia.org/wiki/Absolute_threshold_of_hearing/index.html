Absolute threshold of hearing

The absolute threshold of hearing (ATH) is the minimum sound level of a pure tone that an average human ear with normal hearing can hear with no other sound present. It is a frequency-dependent quantity, typically measured in decibels of sound pressure level (dB SPL), and forms the lower boundary of the equal-loudness contours.

Measurement

The threshold is most sensitive in the range of about 2–5 kHz, where tones of roughly 0 dB SPL (corresponding to a sound pressure of 20 micropascals) can be detected. Sensitivity falls off steeply at low frequencies: at 20 Hz the threshold is around 70–80 dB SPL. Thresholds also rise at very high frequencies, particularly with age and noise exposure (presbycusis).

Reference level

The reference sound pressure level of 20 µPa in air was chosen to approximate the threshold of hearing at 1 kHz for a typical young listener. Because the actual threshold at 1 kHz is close to this value, the ATH at 1 kHz is often quoted as approximately 0 dB SPL.

Psychoacoustic relevance

The absolute threshold is fundamental to psychoacoustics and audio compression: perceptual audio codecs such as MP3 and AAC discard signal components that lie below the threshold of hearing or are masked by louder sounds.
