Kasparov versus the World

Kasparov versus the World was a consultation chess game held over the Internet in 1999, in which world champion Garry Kasparov played a single game against a "World Team" whose moves were chosen by majority vote of tens of thousands of participants. Kasparov won after 62 moves in a game widely praised as one of the finest ever played by a collective.

Format

The game was hosted on MSN from June to October 1999. Four young masters — Étienne Bacrot, Florin Felecan, Irina Krush and Elisabeth Pähtz — publicly recommended moves, and over 50,000 players from more than 75 countries voted on each move, with one move played per day. Analysis was coordinated via bulletin boards, where grandmaster Yuri Averbakh and others contributed.

The game

The World, playing Black, chose the Sicilian Defence. A novel queen sacrifice idea on move 10 (suggested by Krush) produced a richly complex middlegame; Kasparov later said he had never exerted as much effort on a single game. After a long queen endgame, the World resigned on move 62, the vote for resignation passing narrowly.

Legacy

The match was an early landmark of internet collaboration and online voting, and is still studied for its high quality and for Kasparov's book Kasparov Against the World (2000), written with Daniel King.
