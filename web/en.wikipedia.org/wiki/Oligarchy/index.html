Oligarchy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Oligarchy is a form of power structure in which power rests with a small number of people. These may be distinguished by nobility, wealth, education, corporate or religious control, or military power. In Aristotle's classification, oligarchy was the corrupt form of rule by the few (its healthy counterpart being aristocracy), specifically rule by the wealthy in their own interest; the related term plutocracy stresses rule by wealth directly.

Concepts and examples

Robert Michels' "iron law of oligarchy" (1911) argued that all organisations, even democratic parties and unions, develop oligarchic leadership — a thesis debated ever since. Classical examples include Sparta and the Venetian Republic; modern usage describes, among others, post-Soviet "oligarchs" (business magnates with political influence in Russia and Ukraine in the 1990s–2000s) and, more loosely, political systems alleged to be captured by donor or corporate elites — an application tested empirically in political-science studies of policy responsiveness, with contested conclusions.
