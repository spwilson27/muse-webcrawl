Dental anatomy is a field of anatomy dedicated to the study of human tooth structures. The development, appearance, and classification of teeth fall within its scope. (The function of teeth as they contact one another falls elsewhere, under dental occlusion.) Tooth formation begins before birth, and the teeth's eventual morphology is dictated during this time. Dental anatomy is also a taxonomic science: it is concerned with the naming of teeth and the structures of which they are made, this information serving a practical purpose in dental treatment.

Structure of a tooth

Each tooth consists of a crown, visible above the gumline, and one or more roots anchored in the jawbone. The crown is covered by enamel, the hardest substance in the human body, composed largely of hydroxyapatite. Beneath the enamel lies dentin, a resilient mineralised tissue, and at the centre the pulp, containing nerves and blood vessels. The root is covered by cementum and held in its socket by the periodontal ligament.

Types of teeth

Humans have four classes of teeth. Incisors (eight) are chisel-shaped front teeth used for cutting; canines (four) are pointed teeth for tearing; premolars (eight) crush and grind; and molars (twelve, including wisdom teeth) grind food. The permanent dentition numbers 32 teeth, whereas children have 20 deciduous (primary) teeth.

Notation

Systems such as the FDI World Dental Federation notation, the Universal Numbering System used in the United States, and the Palmer notation identify individual teeth for clinical records.
