The Lugou Bridge (simplified Chinese: 卢沟桥; traditional Chinese: 盧溝橋; pinyin: Lúgōu Qiáo), also known as the Marco Polo Bridge, is a stone bridge located 15 km (9.3 mi) southwest of Beijing's city center in the Fengtai District. It bridges the Yongding River, a major tributary of the Hai River. Situated at the eastern end of the bridge is the historic fortified town of Wanping.

The Lugou Bridge, famous for its hundreds of carved stone lions

History

Construction of the original bridge began in 1189, the final year of the reign of Emperor Shizong of Jin, and was completed under his successor in 1192. Following damage from floods, the bridge was reconstructed under the Kangxi Emperor of the Qing dynasty in 1698. The Lugou Bridge is 266.5 metres (874 ft) in length and 9.3 metres (31 ft) in width, supported on 10 piers and 11 segmental arches.

The bridge is well known because it was highly praised by the Venetian traveler Marco Polo during his visit to China in the 13th century (leading the bridge to become known in Europe simply as the Marco Polo Bridge), and for the Marco Polo Bridge Incident, which marked the beginning of the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945).

Stone lions

The bridge is famous for the hundreds of stone lions carved on its balustrade posts. Estimates of the number of lions range from 482 to 501. Each lion is unique, and folk tradition holds that it is impossible to count them all exactly. The lions range in size from a few centimetres to over a metre tall and date from the Jin, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties and the modern era.
