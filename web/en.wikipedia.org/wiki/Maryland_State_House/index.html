Maryland State House

LocationAnnapolis, Maryland, U.S.
Built1772–1779
ArchitectJoseph Horatio Anderson
StyleGeorgian

The Maryland State House is located in Annapolis, Maryland, and is the oldest state capitol in continuous legislative use in the United States, dating to 1772. It houses the Maryland General Assembly, as well as the offices of the Governor and Lieutenant Governor. The building is crowned by the largest wooden dome in the United States constructed without nails.

History

Construction of the present building, the third state house on the site, began in 1772 but was delayed by the American Revolutionary War and was not completed until 1779. From November 1783 to August 1784, Annapolis served as the capital of the United States, and the State House hosted the Congress of the Confederation. There, on 23 December 1783, George Washington resigned his commission as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army, and on 14 January 1784 Congress ratified the Treaty of Paris, formally ending the war.

Architecture

The building was designed by architect Joseph Horatio Anderson in the Georgian style. Its distinctive wooden dome, completed in the 1790s, is constructed of cypress beams held together with wooden pegs. The interior includes the Old Senate Chamber, restored to its 18th-century appearance, where Washington's resignation took place. A lightning rod designed to specifications associated with Benjamin Franklin crowns the dome.

Recognition

The Maryland State House was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
