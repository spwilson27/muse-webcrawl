Fattoush

Fattoush (Arabic: فتوش, also fattush, fatush or fattouche) is a Levantine salad made from chopped vegetables and herbs mixed with pieces of toasted or fried khubz (pita bread). It belongs to the family of dishes known as fattat (plural of fatteh), which incorporate stale flatbread as a base ingredient — a traditional way for farmers in the Levant to use up bread that had gone dry.

Ingredients

Fattoush typically includes lettuce, tomatoes, cucumbers, radishes, spring onions and fresh herbs such as parsley and mint, and often purslane where available. The bread is cut into pieces, toasted or fried until crisp, and added just before serving so it keeps some crunch. The dressing is based on olive oil and lemon juice and is characteristically seasoned with sumac, the deep-red ground spice that gives the salad its tart flavour; some recipes also add pomegranate molasses.

Regional context

The salad is a staple of Lebanese, Syrian, Jordanian, Palestinian and Israeli tables, commonly served as part of a meze spread or alongside grilled meats. It is frequently compared with the Italian bread salad panzanella, another dish devised to use stale bread. During Ramadan fattoush is a popular choice for breaking the fast at iftar.
