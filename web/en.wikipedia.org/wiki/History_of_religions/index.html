History of religions

The history of religions follows humanity's beliefs and practices from prehistoric ritual through the rise of organised religions in the ancient world, the Axial Age transformations, and the global spread of the major faiths to the present.

Prehistoric religion

Deliberate burial with grave goods appears among early humans over 100,000 years ago. Cave art such as Lascaux suggests shamanic practice, and Göbekli Tepe (c. 9500 BCE) shows monumental ritual architecture preceding agriculture. Neolithic and Bronze Age societies developed goddess cults, ancestor veneration and sky gods.

Ancient religions

The literate civilisations produced complex polytheisms: Egyptian religion with its afterlife literature, Mesopotamian temple cities, Vedic religion in India, Greek and Roman pantheons, and Chinese ancestral and state cults. Zoroastrianism in Persia introduced a cosmic dualism and eschatology that influenced Judaism, which itself developed from ancient Israelite religion into monotheism during and after the Babylonian exile.

The Axial Age

Between roughly 800 and 200 BCE, thinkers across Eurasia reframed religion ethically and universally: the Upanishads and the Buddha and Mahavira in India, Confucius and Laozi in China, the Hebrew prophets, Zoroaster, and Greek philosophers. Buddhism spread across Asia under Ashoka and along the Silk Road.

The world religions expand

Christianity grew from a Jewish sect to the religion of the Roman Empire by the 4th century and later of Europe and, through missions, the globe. Islam, founded in 7th-century Arabia, spread rapidly from Iberia to Indonesia, developing Sunni and Shia branches. Medieval centuries saw Sikhism arise in Punjab, the Bhakti movement reshape Hinduism, and Buddhist schools flourish in Tibet and East Asia.

Modern era

The Reformation split Western Christianity; Enlightenment thought, colonialism and print transformed religious life; the 19th and 20th centuries brought new religious movements, secularisation in much of Europe, global Pentecostal growth, and interfaith dialogue. Today Christianity and Islam each count adherents in the billions, alongside Hinduism, Buddhism and a large religiously unaffiliated population.
