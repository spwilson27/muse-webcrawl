Éowyn is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. A noblewoman of Rohan, niece of King Théoden and sister of Éomer, she is a shieldmaiden who, disguised as the rider "Dernhelm", defies orders to remain behind, rides to the Battle of the Pelennor Fields, and there destroys the Witch-king of Angmar — fulfilling the prophecy that he would not fall "by the hand of man".

Biography

Éowyn was born in 2995 of the Third Age, daughter of Éomund, chief Marshal of the Riddermark, and Théodwyn, sister of Théoden. Orphaned young — her father killed by Orcs and her mother dead of grief — she and Éomer were raised in Théoden's household at Edoras. By the time of the War of the Ring she tended her uncle as he declined under the malign influence of his counsellor Gríma Wormtongue. She longed for deeds of glory and chafed at being left to govern Rohan while the men rode to war; she also conceived an unreciprocated love for Aragorn, who gently refused her.

Ordered to stay behind when Théoden led the Rohirrim to Gondor's aid, Éowyn disguised herself as the young rider Dernhelm, took the hobbit Meriadoc Brandybuck with her, and rode with the host. In the battle before Minas Tirith (15 March 3019), she confronted the Witch-king after he struck down Théoden. When the Nazgûl lord boasted that no living man could hinder him, she revealed herself — "I am no man!" — and, aided by Merry's barrow-blade, which broke the spell knitting the Witch-king's form together, she slew him, fulfilling Glorfindel's ancient prophecy.

Aftermath

Stricken by the Black Breath and her wounds, Éowyn was healed in the Houses of Healing by Aragorn, where she met Faramir, Steward of Gondor. Renouncing war, she declared she would be a healer and take joy in growing things; she married Faramir and lived with him in Ithilien, where he became Prince. Critics have discussed Éowyn extensively as Tolkien's most prominent female character and in relation to the shieldmaidens of Old Norse literature and the figure of Hervör.

Portrayals

Éowyn was played by Miranda Otto in Peter Jackson's film trilogy (2002–2003), and voiced by Karen Hurley in the 1978 animated film and by Nellie Bellflower in the 1980 animated The Return of the King.
