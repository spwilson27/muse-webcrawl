Phreaking

A "blue box", an electronic device used to generate the control tones of the telephone network.

Phreaking is a slang term coined to describe the activity of a culture of people who study, experiment with, or explore telecommunication systems, such as equipment and systems connected to public telephone networks. The term phreak is a sensational spelling of the word freak with the ph- from phone, and may also refer to the use of various audio frequencies to manipulate a phone system.

History

Phreaking originated in the late 1950s and 1960s, when hobbyists discovered that the telephone network's in-band signalling could be manipulated with whistles and tone generators. A famous early observation was that a toy whistle given away in boxes of Cap'n Crunch cereal produced a tone at 2600 Hz, the frequency used by AT&T long-distance trunks to indicate an idle line. Whistling this tone into a telephone could seize control of a trunk and allow free long-distance calls.

Devices such as the blue box (which generated signalling tones), the red box (which simulated payphone coin-deposit tones) and the black box were widely built and traded. John Draper, known as "Captain Crunch", became one of the most famous phreaks. In 1971 an article in Esquire magazine publicised the subculture, and a young Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs built and sold blue boxes before founding Apple.

Decline

As telephone networks moved from in-band analogue signalling to out-of-band digital signalling systems such as SS7 in the 1980s and 1990s, the classic techniques ceased to work. The phreaking community overlapped heavily with the emerging computer hacker subculture, and many of its members moved on to computer networks and the Internet.
