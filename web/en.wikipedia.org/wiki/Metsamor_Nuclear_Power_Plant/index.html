Metsamor Nuclear Power Plant

The Metsamor Nuclear Power Plant, also known as the Armenian Nuclear Power Plant, is the only nuclear power plant in the South Caucasus. It is located about 36 km west of Yerevan, near the town of Metsamor in Armavir Province, close to the Turkish border. The plant originally had two Soviet VVER-440 pressurized water reactors; Unit 2 remains in operation and supplies roughly 30–40% of Armenia's electricity.

History

Construction began in 1969. Unit 1 entered service in 1976 and Unit 2 in 1980, using the VVER-440/V270 design adapted for the site's seismic conditions but lacking a full secondary containment building. Following the 1988 Spitak earthquake, which the plant survived without damage, both units were shut down in 1989 amid safety concerns. Faced with a severe energy crisis after the collapse of the USSR, Armenia restarted Unit 2 in 1995 with Russian assistance; Unit 1 was never restarted and is being decommissioned.

Safety debate and future

The plant's age, seismic setting, and lack of containment have made it a long-standing concern for the European Union and Turkey, which have pressed for its closure, while the Armenian government argues it is indispensable for energy security. Extensive safety upgrades were carried out with IAEA support, and Russian-financed life-extension work on Unit 2 aims to keep it operating into the 2030s. Armenia has discussed building a replacement plant, including possible small modular reactors.
