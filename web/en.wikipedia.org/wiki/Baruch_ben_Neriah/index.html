Baruch ben Neriah (Hebrew: בָּרוּךְ בֶּן־נֵרִיָּה, "Baruch, son of Neriah") was the scribe and loyal companion of the prophet Jeremiah in the final decades of the kingdom of Judah. According to the Book of Jeremiah, Baruch wrote down Jeremiah's oracles at his dictation, read them publicly in the Temple, and re-wrote the scroll after King Jehoiakim burned the first copy.

Biblical account

Baruch accompanied Jeremiah through the Babylonian siege and fall of Jerusalem (586 BCE) and was carried with him into exile in Egypt. Jeremiah 45 preserves a personal oracle addressed to Baruch, promising him his life "as a prize of war" amid the coming destruction. Later Jewish and Christian traditions credit Baruch with the deuterocanonical Book of Baruch and works such as 2 Baruch and 3 Baruch.

Archaeology

In the 1970s–1990s, bullae (clay seal impressions) bearing the name "Baruch son of Neriah the scribe", one with a fingerprint, surfaced on the antiquities market and in excavation contexts; their authenticity and provenance have been debated but they are widely cited as rare candidates for artefacts of a named biblical individual.
