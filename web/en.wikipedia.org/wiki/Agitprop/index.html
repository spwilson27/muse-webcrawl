Agitprop

Agitprop
Origin: Soviet Russia, from "agitation and propaganda"
Forms: Posters, theatre, trains, film

Agitprop (from Russian: агитпроп, short for "agitation and propaganda") is political propaganda disseminated through popular media such as literature, plays, pamphlets, films, and other art forms, with an explicit and unapologetically political message, originally in Soviet Russia.

History

The term originated with the Department of Agitation and Propaganda of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union, established in 1920. Agit-trains and agit-ships toured the country during the Russian Civil War carrying films, speakers, and printed matter. Artists such as Vladimir Mayakovsky, El Lissitzky, and Alexander Rodchenko created iconic posters and photomontage.

Theatre and beyond

Agitprop theatre troupes, including Germany's Piscator and the British Unity Theatre, spread the style internationally. The term later broadened to describe any strongly political art intended to mobilise audiences, often used pejoratively.
