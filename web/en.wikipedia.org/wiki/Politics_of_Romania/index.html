Politics of Romania

The politics of Romania take place within a semi-presidential republic under the Constitution of 1991 (revised 2003). Romania is a unitary state; legislative power is vested in a bicameral Parliament consisting of the Senate and the Chamber of Deputies, elected by proportional representation for four-year terms.

Executive

The President, directly elected for up to two five-year terms, represents the state, designates the Prime Minister, and has significant roles in foreign policy and defence. The Prime Minister leads the government, which must win a vote of confidence in Parliament. Tensions between presidents and prime ministers — "cohabitation" — have been a recurring feature, most sharply during the 2012 constitutional crisis and attempted suspension of President Traian Băsescu.

Parties

The largest party since 1989 has generally been the Social Democratic Party (PSD), successor in part to the post-communist National Salvation Front. Its chief rivals have included the National Liberal Party (PNL) and, since 2016, the reformist Save Romania Union (USR). The nationalist Alliance for the Union of Romanians (AUR) rose rapidly after 2020. Reserved seats are allocated to ethnic minority organisations, including the Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (UDMR), which has joined several coalition governments.

Politics since 1989 has also been marked by anti-corruption prosecutions led by the National Anticorruption Directorate, large street protests (notably in 2017 against a corruption-pardoning ordinance), and Romania's integration into NATO (2004) and the European Union (2007).
