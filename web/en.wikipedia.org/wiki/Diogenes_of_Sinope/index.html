Diogenes of Sinope

Diogenes of Sinope (c. 412/404 – c. 324/321 BC), also known as Diogenes the Cynic, was a Greek philosopher and one of the founders of Cynic philosophy. Born in Sinope, he was exiled for defacing the currency, moved to Athens, and made a virtue of poverty, living in a large ceramic jar.

Life and anecdotes

Our main source is Diogenes Laërtius. Famous stories tell of Diogenes carrying a lamp in daylight "looking for a man", discarding his only cup after seeing a child drink from cupped hands, and replying to Alexander the Great, who asked if he wanted anything, "Yes — stand out of my sunlight." Captured by pirates, he was sold as a slave in Corinth, where he lived freely thereafter.

Philosophy

Diogenes taught that happiness lies in living according to nature, in self-sufficiency (autarkeia) and shameless rejection of convention. He declared himself a "citizen of the world" (kosmopolitēs), an early statement of cosmopolitanism. His performance-like philosophy influenced the Stoics through his pupil Crates of Thebes, teacher of Zeno.
