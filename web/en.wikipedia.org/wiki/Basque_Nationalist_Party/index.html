Basque Nationalist Party

The Basque Nationalist Party (Basque: Euzko Alderdi Jeltzalea, EAJ; Spanish: Partido Nacionalista Vasco, PNV) is a Basque nationalist and regionalist political party in Spain and France. Founded in 1895 by Sabino Arana, it is one of the oldest nationalist parties in Europe and has governed the Basque Autonomous Community for most of the period since Spain's return to democracy.

History

Sabino Arana founded the party to defend Basque identity, language, and the traditional fueros (charters) against centralisation and industrial immigration. The party achieved the first Basque autonomous government in 1936 under José Antonio Aguirre during the Spanish Civil War, then endured exile and clandestinity under Franco. After 1975 it became the dominant force in Basque politics, leading regional governments under lehendakaris including Carlos Garaikoetxea, José Antonio Ardanza, Juan José Ibarretxe, and Iñigo Urkullu.

Ideology and position

The PNV combines Basque nationalism with broadly centrist, pro-business and pro-European positions, and has often been a pragmatic kingmaker in the Spanish parliament, trading support to Madrid governments for fiscal and autonomy concessions under the Basque Economic Agreement. Its youth organisation is Euzko Gaztedi. A French branch operates in the Northern Basque Country.
