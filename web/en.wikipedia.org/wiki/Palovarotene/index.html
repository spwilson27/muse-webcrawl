Palovarotene

Trade nameSohonos
ClassRetinoic acid receptor gamma (RARγ) agonist
IndicationFibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP)
RouteOral
DeveloperClementia Pharmaceuticals / Ipsen

Palovarotene, sold under the brand name Sohonos, is a medication used to reduce the formation of heterotopic ossification in people with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP), an extremely rare genetic disorder in which soft tissues progressively turn to bone. It is a highly selective agonist of the retinoic acid receptor gamma (RARγ), and works by interfering with the signalling pathways that drive the abnormal formation of bone in muscle, tendons and ligaments.

Medical use

Palovarotene was approved in Canada in 2022 and by the US Food and Drug Administration in August 2023 for adults and children (females aged 8 years and older, males aged 10 years and older) with FOP, making it the first approved treatment for the condition. It is taken orally, with dosing that increases during flare-ups. In the European Union, the marketing application was refused after the regulator concluded the benefits did not outweigh the risks.

Mechanism of action

FOP is caused by activating mutations in the ACVR1/ALK2 receptor that render it abnormally responsive to activin A, triggering endochondral bone formation in soft tissue. As a RARγ agonist, palovarotene suppresses chondrogenesis — the cartilage stage through which the ectopic bone forms — by modulating BMP signalling and downstream transcription, thereby reducing new heterotopic bone volume in clinical studies.

Adverse effects

Like other retinoids, palovarotene carries significant risks. It is teratogenic and contraindicated in pregnancy, and a boxed warning concerns premature closure of the growth plates (epiphyseal closure) in growing children, which requires monitoring of skeletal growth. Common adverse effects include dry skin and mucous membranes, itching, rash, hair loss, redness of the skin and other mucocutaneous reactions typical of the retinoid class.

History

Palovarotene was originally investigated for emphysema by Roche before being repurposed by Clementia Pharmaceuticals for FOP; Ipsen acquired Clementia in 2019 and completed the late-stage MOVE trial programme that supported approval.
