V. S. Naipaul

Sir Vidiadhar Surajprasad Naipaul (17 August 1932 – 11 August 2018), commonly known as V. S. Naipaul, was a Trinidadian-born British writer of Indo-Trinidadian descent, known for his works of fiction and nonfiction set in the Caribbean, Africa, and Asia. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2001.

Life

Naipaul was born in Chaguanas, Trinidad and Tobago, then a British colony, to parents descended from Indian indentured labourers. He won a scholarship to Oxford University, where he studied English, and settled in England. His early humorous novels of Trinidad life, including The Mystic Masseur and A House for Mr Biswas (1961), widely considered his masterpiece, established his reputation.

Later work

His later fiction, such as A Bend in the River (1979), explored postcolonial societies in Africa and the Caribbean, while travel books including Among the Believers (1981) examined the Islamic world. His unsparing assessments of the developing world made him a controversial figure. He won the Booker Prize in 1971 for In a Free State and was knighted in 1990.
