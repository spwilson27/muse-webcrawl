Joseph M. Juran

Joseph Moses Juran (December 24, 1904 – February 28, 2008) was a Romanian-born American engineer, management consultant, and a towering figure in quality management. His Quality Control Handbook, first published in 1951, became the standard reference of the field, and his lectures in Japan in the 1950s helped ignite that country's quality revolution. He lived to 103, publishing and consulting into his late nineties.

Early life and career

Juran was born in Brăila, Romania, and emigrated with his family to Minnesota in 1912. He studied electrical engineering at the University of Minnesota and joined Western Electric in 1924, working at the Hawthorne Works in Chicago, where he was exposed to the early statistical quality control work of Walter Shewhart's circle. During the Second World War he served in Washington on Lend-Lease administration. Afterwards he left industry to become an independent consultant and professor, writing and lecturing on management.

Contributions to quality management

Juran framed quality as "fitness for use" and taught that quality problems are overwhelmingly management-controllable rather than worker-controllable. He popularised the application of the Pareto principle — the "vital few and the useful many" (he later corrected his earlier phrase "trivial many") — arguing that a small share of causes accounts for most quality losses. His "Juran trilogy" organises quality management into three processes: quality planning, quality control, and quality improvement.

Invited to Japan by the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers in 1954, following W. Edwards Deming's earlier lectures, Juran addressed top and middle management and helped shift Japanese quality practice from inspection and shop-floor statistics toward company-wide management. In 1979 he founded the Juran Institute. He received Japan's Order of the Sacred Treasure and the U.S. National Medal of Technology (1992).
