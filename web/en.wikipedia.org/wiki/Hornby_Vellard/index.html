Hornby Vellard

The Hornby Vellard was a major engineering project carried out in Bombay (now Mumbai) between 1782 and 1784 under William Hornby, Governor of Bombay from 1771 to 1784. The project built a causeway — a "vellard" (from the Portuguese vallado, an embankment or dike) — across the Great Breach at Mahalaxmi, closing the gap through which the sea flooded the low-lying central parts of the island at high tide.

Background

Bombay originally consisted of seven separate islands divided by tidal flats and creeks. At high tide, the sea poured through the breach between the islands near Mahalaxmi, inundating the low ground of the central island and separating areas such as Worli from Mahim and the southern districts. Earlier proposals to close the breach had been rejected by the East India Company in London on grounds of cost.

Construction

Governor Hornby proceeded with the work without waiting for formal approval. Between 1782 and 1784 the causeway was built across the breach at a cost of about ₹100,000. The embankment permanently joined the islands at this point, allowing reclamation of the flooded flats behind it and creating continuous land communication between the northern and southern parts of the city.

Legacy

The Hornby Vellard was the first of the great causeways and reclamations that eventually merged the seven islands of Bombay into the single peninsula of modern Mumbai. The present-day road Lala Lajpatrai Marg (formerly Vellard Road) follows the line of the causeway, beside the Haji Ali Dargah and the Mahalaxmi Racecourse, which stands on land reclaimed behind the embankment.
