Isothermal process

An isothermal process is a type of thermodynamic process in which the temperature T of a system remains constant: ΔT = 0. This typically occurs when a system is in contact with an outside thermal reservoir, and a change in the system happens slowly enough for heat exchange to keep the temperature constant.

Ideal gases

For an ideal gas, the product of pressure and volume is constant during an isothermal process, in accordance with Boyle's law: pV = constant. Because the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, ΔU = 0, so by the first law of thermodynamics all heat absorbed by the gas equals the work it performs: Q = W.

Work

The work done in a reversible isothermal expansion of n moles of ideal gas from volume VA to VB is W = nRT ln(VB/VA). On a pressure–volume diagram the process follows a hyperbola known as an isotherm.

Examples

Isothermal steps occur in the Carnot cycle, and phase changes such as melting and boiling at constant pressure are effectively isothermal. The isothermal process contrasts with the adiabatic process, in which no heat is exchanged.
