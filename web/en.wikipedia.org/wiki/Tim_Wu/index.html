Tim Wu (born 1972) is a Canadian-American legal scholar and professor at Columbia Law School. He is best known for coining the phrase "network neutrality" in his 2003 paper Network Neutrality, Broadband Discrimination, and for his books on media, technology and antitrust, including The Master Switch (2010), The Attention Merchants (2016) and The Curse of Bigness (2018).

Career

Wu clerked for Judge Richard Posner and Justice Stephen Breyer, worked in the technology industry in Silicon Valley, and taught at the University of Virginia and Stanford before joining Columbia. He has served as an advisor to the U.S. Federal Trade Commission, ran for Lieutenant Governor of New York in 2014 on an anti-monopoly platform, and from 2021 to 2023 served as Special Assistant to the President for Technology and Competition Policy in the Biden administration, where he helped shape executive action on competition.

Ideas

In The Master Switch, Wu describes "the Cycle" by which open information industries — telephony, radio, film, television — historically consolidated into closed empires. The Curse of Bigness argues for reviving the anti-monopoly tradition associated with Louis Brandeis, making Wu a leading figure in the "New Brandeis" movement in antitrust.
