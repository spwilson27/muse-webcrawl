Cuatro Ciénegas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cuatro Ciénegas (Spanish for "four marshes") is a municipality and valley in the Mexican state of Coahuila, in the Chihuahuan Desert. The Cuatro Ciénegas Basin is internationally known for its system of spring-fed pools (pozas), streams and wetlands that harbour an exceptional concentration of endemic species, and it is protected as a Flora and Fauna Protection Area and a Ramsar wetland.

Ecology

Isolated by surrounding desert mountains, the basin's gypsum-rich springs support living stromatolites — layered microbial communities similar to those that dominated early Earth — together with dozens of endemic fishes (including the Cuatro Ciénegas pupfish and the cushioned cichlid), turtles such as the endemic Coahuilan box turtle, snails, and other organisms found nowhere else. Scientists regard the valley as a natural laboratory for studying evolution in isolation, and NASA-funded researchers have studied its microbial ecosystems as analogues for ancient environments on Earth and possibly Mars.

Threats

The wetlands are threatened by groundwater extraction for alfalfa irrigation in surrounding valleys, which has lowered water levels and dried some pools, and by invasive species such as jewel cichlids. Conservation efforts by Mexican authorities and NGOs seek to reconcile local water use with protection of the endemic fauna.

The town

The municipal seat, Cuatro Ciénegas de Carranza, is the birthplace of Venustiano Carranza, leader of the Mexican Revolution's Constitutionalist forces and President of Mexico (1917–1920); his house is now a museum. The town is designated a Pueblo Mágico and is a gateway for visitors to the pools and the white gypsum Dunas de Yeso.
