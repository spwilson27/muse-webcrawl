Om mani padme hum

Sanskritॐ मणि पद्मे हूं (oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ)
Tibetanཨོཾ་མ་ཎི་པདྨེ་ཧཱུྃ
Associated withAvalokiteśvara (Chenrezig)

Om mani padme hum (Sanskrit: ॐ मणि पद्मे हूं, IPA: [õːː mɐɳɪ pɐdmeː ɦũː]) is the six-syllabled Sanskrit mantra particularly associated with the four-armed Shadakshari form of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. It first appeared in the Mahayana Kāraṇḍavyūhasūtra, where it is also referred to as the sadaksara (six syllables) and the paramahrdaya, or "innermost heart" of Avalokiteshvara.

Meaning

The mantra is commonly rendered in English as "Hail to the jewel in the lotus", though scholars note that a literal translation is difficult: maṇi means "jewel" and padme is the locative of padma, "lotus". In Tibetan Buddhism, each of the six syllables is said to purify one of the six realms of existence and to correspond to one of the six perfections (pāramitās): generosity, ethics, patience, diligence, renunciation and wisdom.

Practice

The mantra is the most common mantra in Tibet and is found carved on rocks and stones (mani stones), written on prayer wheels, and printed on prayer flags. Recitation with a mala of 108 beads is a central devotional practice; the 14th Dalai Lama, regarded as an emanation of Avalokiteshvara, has described the mantra as a condensation of the Buddha's teachings on compassion.
