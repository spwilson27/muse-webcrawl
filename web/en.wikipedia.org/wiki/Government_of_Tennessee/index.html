Government of Tennessee

The government of Tennessee is established by the Constitution of Tennessee, the current version of which was adopted in 1870 and has been amended numerous times since. Like the federal government, it is divided into three branches: executive, legislative and judicial. The seat of government is the State Capitol in Nashville.

Executive branch

The executive branch is headed by the Governor of Tennessee, elected statewide to a four-year term and limited to two consecutive terms. The governor signs or vetoes legislation, commands the Tennessee National Guard, grants pardons and commutations, and appoints the heads of the executive departments. Tennessee is unusual in that most other executive-branch officials historically were not popularly elected: the lieutenant governor is chosen by the state Senate (the title held by the Senate's Speaker), while the secretary of state, treasurer and comptroller are elected by a joint vote of the General Assembly. The attorney general of Tennessee is appointed by the Tennessee Supreme Court for an eight-year term — unique among the states. The executive departments (about twenty) administer areas such as transportation, health, education, revenue and correction.

Legislative branch

The legislature is the Tennessee General Assembly, a bicameral body consisting of the Senate (33 members, four-year terms) and the House of Representatives (99 members, two-year terms). The General Assembly convenes annually in Nashville, enacts the state budget, and may override a gubernatorial veto by a simple majority of both houses. Constitutional amendments must be approved by the legislature in successive sessions and ratified by referendum.

Judicial branch

The judiciary is headed by the Tennessee Supreme Court, five justices chosen under the Tennessee Plan (merit selection with retention elections) for eight-year terms. Below it sit the intermediate Court of Appeals and Court of Criminal Appeals, and the trial courts of general jurisdiction — circuit, chancery and criminal courts — organised into 31 judicial districts, alongside limited-jurisdiction general sessions courts and municipal courts.

Local government

Tennessee has 95 counties and more than 340 municipalities. Counties are governed by county commissions and an elected county mayor (formerly county executive), while cities operate under mayor–council, council–manager or commission charters; Nashville and Davidson County form a consolidated metropolitan government.
