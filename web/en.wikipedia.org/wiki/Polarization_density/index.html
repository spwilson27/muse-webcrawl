Polarization density

In classical electromagnetism, polarization density (or electric polarization) is the vector field P that expresses the density of permanent or induced electric dipole moments in a dielectric material. When a dielectric is placed in an external electric field, its molecules acquire or align dipole moments, and the polarization is the dipole moment per unit volume. Its SI unit is the coulomb per square metre (C/m²), the same dimension as electric displacement.

Relation to bound charge

The polarization is directly tied to the bound charge that appears in a polarized dielectric: the volume bound charge density is ρb = −∇·P, and the surface bound charge density on the boundary of the material is σb = P·n̂. The electric displacement field is defined as D = ε₀E + P, which allows Maxwell's equations in matter to be written in terms of free charges and currents alone.

Constitutive relations

In a linear, isotropic dielectric the polarization is proportional to the electric field, P = ε₀χeE, where χe is the electric susceptibility; combined with the definition of D this gives the familiar relation D = εE with ε the permittivity. In anisotropic materials the response is described by a tensor, and in ferroelectrics the polarization is nonlinear, history-dependent, and can persist when the field is removed (remanent polarization). Microscopic mechanisms contributing to polarization include electronic displacement of electron clouds, ionic displacement in crystals, orientation of permanent molecular dipoles, and interfacial (space-charge) polarization, each dominating in different frequency ranges.
