Washington, D.C. International Stakes

Washington, D.C. International Stakes
LocationLaurel Park, Laurel, Maryland
Inaugurated1952
Distance1½ miles (about 2,400 m) on turf

The Washington, D.C. International Stakes was an American thoroughbred horse race held at Laurel Park Racecourse in Laurel, Maryland. Founded in 1952 by John D. Schapiro, it pioneered international competition in American racing by inviting top horses from around the world to race on grass — then a novelty in the United States.

History

Run over a mile and a half on turf, the race attracted champions from Europe, South America, Australia and Japan, and became a forerunner of events such as the Arlington Million and the Breeders' Cup Turf. Famous winners included Kelso, who set a course record in 1964, and Sir Ivor, the English Derby winner, in 1968. The race later showcased horses such as Manila and Paradise Creek (1994, the race's swan song era as a Grade I event).

Legacy

Although the International declined in status in the 1990s as rival turf races multiplied, it is credited with demonstrating that foreign horses would travel to race in America and with helping establish turf racing in the United States.
