Tensor product

In mathematics, the tensor product V ⊗ W of vector spaces V and W is a vector space together with a bilinear map V × W → V ⊗ W, universal among bilinear maps: any bilinear function on V × W factors uniquely through it. If V has dimension m and W dimension n, V ⊗ W has dimension mn; in bases, the tensor product of vectors is given by the Kronecker product of their coordinate arrays.

Definition

The tensor product can be constructed as a quotient of the free vector space on V × W by relations enforcing bilinearity. Pure tensors are written v ⊗ w; general elements are sums of pure tensors. The construction generalises to modules over a ring and to tensor products of algebras, chain complexes and Hilbert spaces.

Properties

The tensor product is associative and commutative up to canonical isomorphism, distributes over direct sums, and satisfies adjunctions such as Hom(V ⊗ W, U) ≅ Hom(V, Hom(W, U)). The tensor algebra of a space is the direct sum of all its tensor powers.

Applications

Tensor products describe composite quantum systems (and thereby quantum entanglement), multilinear algebra, differential forms via exterior products, and representations of product groups.
