Artemis I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Artemis I, formerly Exploration Mission-1, was an uncrewed test flight of NASA's Artemis program: the first integrated launch of the Space Launch System rocket and Orion spacecraft, flown from 16 November to 11 December 2022. Orion travelled to a distant retrograde orbit around the Moon — farther from Earth than any crew-capable spacecraft had flown — and returned to a Pacific splashdown.

The mission followed years of delays and two scrubbed attempts in August and September 2022 (engine sensor faults and hydrogen leaks), plus a rollback for Hurricane Ian. Once flying, it performed nearly flawlessly: Orion's European service module, cameras and systems worked, ten CubeSats were deployed (with mixed success), and radiation and engineering data were collected for the crewed follow-on.

Heat shield findings

Post-flight inspection found the Orion heat shield had charred and lost material differently than models predicted — cracks and liberated fragments in the Avcoat ablator — prompting a two-year investigation. NASA concluded the crew could fly safely on Artemis II with the same shield by changing the reentry trajectory, a decision scrutinised by its own inspector general and safety advisers.
