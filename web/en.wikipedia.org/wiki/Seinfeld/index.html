Seinfeld is an American television sitcom created by Larry David and Jerry Seinfeld for NBC. Often described as "a show about nothing", it follows a fictionalized Jerry Seinfeld and his friends — George Costanza, Elaine Benes, and neighbour Cosmo Kramer — in New York City, mining everyday minutiae for comedy.

History

After a 1989 pilot ("The Seinfeld Chronicles"), the series grew from modest ratings into the defining sitcom of the 1990s, topping the Nielsen ratings in its later seasons. Episodes such as "The Contest", "The Soup Nazi", "The Marine Biologist", and "The Junior Mint" entered popular culture, popularizing phrases like "yada yada" and "no soup for you". Storylines famously interlock, with a strict "no hugging, no learning" ethos attributed to David.

Finale and legacy

The 1998 finale, watched by about 76 million viewers, divided audiences. Reruns and streaming kept the show culturally central; it is routinely ranked among the greatest television series ever made and strongly influenced later single-camera and cringe comedy, including David's Curb Your Enthusiasm.
