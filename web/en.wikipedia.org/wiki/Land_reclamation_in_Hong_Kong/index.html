Land reclamation in Hong Kong

Land reclamation in Hong Kong has been extensive since the mid-19th century because of the territory's mountainous terrain and scarce flat land. Around 7,000 hectares — roughly 6–7% of Hong Kong's total area, and about a quarter of its developed land — has been created by filling in the sea, mainly around Victoria Harbour and on Lantau Island.

Major projects include the Praya Reclamation Scheme of the 1890s, post-war reclamations at Tsuen Wan and Sha Tin, the Central and Wan Chai reclamation that created land for the financial district, and the massive airport platform at Chek Lap Kok (opened 1998) and Tung Chung new town.

Reclamation became controversial for narrowing Victoria Harbour and harming marine ecology; the Protection of the Harbour Ordinance (1997) established a presumption against further harbour reclamation. Later proposals, such as the Lantau Tomorrow Vision artificial islands, remain debated on cost and environmental grounds.
