White balance

White balance (WB) is the process of removing unrealistic colour casts from an image, so that objects which appear white to the eye are rendered white in a photograph. Because different light sources have different colour temperatures — candlelight and tungsten bulbs are warm (orange), while shade and overcast sky are cool (blue) — a camera must estimate the colour of the illumination and scale the red, green and blue channels accordingly. The human visual system performs this automatically as colour constancy; cameras must do it computationally.

Methods

Cameras offer automatic white balance (AWB), presets for common lighting (daylight, cloudy, tungsten, fluorescent), and manual calibration against a neutral grey or white reference such as an 18% grey card. Photographers shooting in raw format can set white balance freely in post-processing, since the unprocessed sensor data is preserved. In film and video production, white balancing the camera to the set lighting serves the same purpose.
