Kähler manifold

A Kähler manifold, named after the German mathematician Erich Kähler, is a complex manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric and a symplectic form that are mutually compatible in a specific way, so that the manifold carries three integrable structures — Riemannian, complex, and symplectic — pairwise compatible.

Definition

A Kähler manifold is a Hermitian manifold whose associated (1,1)-form, the Kähler form, is closed. Equivalently, the metric is locally given by a real-valued Kähler potential, and the Levi-Civita connection preserves the complex structure. Holonomy of a Kähler metric is contained in the unitary group U(n).

Examples

Complex Euclidean space with its flat metric, complex projective space with the Fubini–Study metric, and any complex submanifold of a Kähler manifold are examples. Compact Kähler manifolds include all smooth projective algebraic varieties; the Kodaira embedding theorem characterises those compact Kähler manifolds that are projective.

Properties

On a compact Kähler manifold, Hodge theory gives strong topological restrictions: the Betti numbers in odd degree are even, and hard Lefschetz holds. The Calabi conjecture, proved by Shing-Tung Yau, guarantees Kähler metrics with prescribed Ricci form, giving rise to Calabi–Yau manifolds important in string theory.
