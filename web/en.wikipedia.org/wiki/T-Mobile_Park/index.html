T-Mobile Park is a retractable-roof baseball stadium in Seattle, Washington, United States. Located in the city's SoDo neighbourhood, just south of downtown, it is the home ballpark of the Seattle Mariners of Major League Baseball. The stadium opened in 1999 as Safeco Field, replacing the Kingdome, and took its present name in January 2019 under a 25-year naming-rights agreement with T-Mobile US.

History

The Mariners' future in Seattle was secured in 1995 after the team's dramatic playoff run prompted the Washington State Legislature to approve a funding package for a baseball-only stadium. Construction began in 1997 on a site immediately south of the Kingdome. The first game was played on July 15, 1999, against the San Diego Padres. The ballpark hosted the 2001 MLB All-Star Game and the 2023 All-Star Game.

Features

The stadium's most distinctive feature is its three-panel retractable roof, which slides on rails to cover the seating bowl while leaving the structure open at the sides; it is designed to withstand earthquakes and the region's wind and rain rather than to create an indoor climate. The roof weighs about 22 million pounds (10,000 tonnes) and moves on 128 wheels powered by electric motors. The park seats 47,929 for baseball and is known for its views of the downtown Seattle skyline, its natural grass playing surface, and features such as the "Pen" gathering area in left-centre field and a museum and Mariners Hall of Fame.

The stadium has also hosted college football bowl games, WrestleMania XIX in 2003, concerts, and the 2026 venue programme for international soccer events in the region is centred on the neighbouring Lumen Field.
