Gerard Hendrik (Geert) Hofstede (2 October 1928 – 12 February 2020) was a Dutch social psychologist, mechanical engineer and writer, best known for pioneering research on cross-cultural groups and organisations and for developing cultural dimensions theory, one of the most influential frameworks in cross-cultural psychology and international management.

Career

Hofstede trained as a mechanical engineer at Delft University of Technology and spent a decade in industry before earning a doctorate in social psychology from the University of Groningen (1967). While working at IBM Europe, he gained access to an exceptionally large survey database of employee values covering more than 100,000 respondents in dozens of countries. His statistical analysis of this material, published as Culture's Consequences (1980), identified systematic national differences in work-related values.

Cultural dimensions

Hofstede's model describes national cultures along dimensions originally comprising power distance, individualism versus collectivism, masculinity versus femininity, and uncertainty avoidance. Later research added long-term versus short-term orientation (with Michael Harris Bond) and indulgence versus restraint (with Michael Minkov), producing the widely used six-dimensional model presented in Cultures and Organizations: Software of the Mind (1991, with Gert Jan Hofstede and Minkov).

Although criticised for essentialism, the age of the IBM data and the equation of nation with culture, the framework remains a standard teaching and consulting tool in international business, diplomacy and cross-cultural communication. Hofstede taught at institutions in the Netherlands, Belgium and Hong Kong, and was professor emeritus at Maastricht University.
