Arthur Cronquist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Arthur Cronquist, botanist

Arthur John Cronquist (March 19, 1919 – November 22, 1992) was an American biologist, botanist and a specialist on Compositae. He is considered one of the most influential botanists of the 20th century, owing to his formulation of the Cronquist system and his two major works, An Integrated System of Classification of Flowering Plants (1981) and, with Henry A. Gleason, the two-volume Manual of Vascular Plants of Northeastern United States and Adjacent Canada (1963).

Life and career

Cronquist was born in San Jose, California, and grew up in Pocatello, Idaho. He studied botany at Idaho State College and Utah State University, receiving his doctorate from the University of Minnesota in 1944. After wartime work with the U.S. Rubber Plantations Company, he held positions at the University of Georgia, Washington State University, and from 1952 the New York Botanical Garden, where he spent the remainder of his career.

The Cronquist system

The Cronquist system of plant classification arranged the flowering plants (Magnoliophyta) into two classes, Magnoliopsida (dicotyledons) and Liliopsida (monocotyledons), subdivided into subclasses, orders, and families. It was one of the most widely used classification schemes for flowering plants until the adoption of the molecular-phylogenetic APG systems beginning in 1998.
