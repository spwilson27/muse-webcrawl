Wildlife sanctuary

Purpose: Protection of wild species and habitat

Legal basis (India): Wildlife Protection Act, 1972

A wildlife sanctuary is a protected natural habitat area where wild plants and animals are safeguarded from hunting, poaching, predation threats, and competition with human activity. The term is used in several countries' legal systems, most prominently in India, where wildlife sanctuaries are a formal protected-area category.

India

Under India's Wildlife Protection Act of 1972, state governments may declare an area a sanctuary if it is considered to be of adequate ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological, natural, or zoological significance. India has more than 570 wildlife sanctuaries covering roughly 123,000 square kilometres, about 3.7% of the country's land area. Well-known examples include Bharatpur (Keoladeo), Periyar, and Bandipur.

Unlike national parks, sanctuaries may allow some regulated human activities, and private land within them is not necessarily acquired by the state; grazing and collection rights may continue under regulation. They are classified by the IUCN largely as Category IV protected areas.

Other uses of the term

In other countries, "wildlife sanctuary" is often used less formally, and may refer to facilities that rescue, rehabilitate, and provide lifelong care for individual animals — such as big-cat sanctuaries or farm-animal sanctuaries — as distinct from zoos, because the animals are not bred, traded, or exhibited for entertainment.
