Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan

Santa Maria delle Grazie ("Holy Mary of Grace") is a church and Dominican convent in Milan, northern Italy, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The convent contains the mural of The Last Supper by Leonardo da Vinci, which is in the refectory.

History

Duke Francesco I Sforza ordered the construction of a Dominican convent and church on the site of a prior chapel dedicated to Saint Mary of the Graces. The architect Guiniforte Solari designed the main church, built between 1463 and 1497. Ludovico Sforza later commissioned Donato Bramante to rebuild the apse and crossing, creating the distinctive domed tribune; Bramante's cloister, known as the Cloister of the Frogs, adjoins the church.

The Last Supper

Leonardo da Vinci painted The Last Supper on the north wall of the refectory between about 1495 and 1498, for Ludovico Sforza. Because Leonardo experimented with an oil-tempera technique on dry plaster, the painting began to deteriorate early; after damage in the Second World War — when the refectory was hit by bombing but the mural was protected by sandbagging — a major restoration was completed in 1999. Opposite the Last Supper is Giovanni Donato da Montorfano's fresco of the Crucifixion (1495).

World heritage

The church and convent were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980, citing the building as a masterpiece of Renaissance architecture and the unique artistic value of Leonardo's mural.
