Black rhinoceros

The black rhinoceros (Diceros bicornis), also called the hook-lipped rhinoceros, is a species of rhinoceros native to eastern and southern Africa. Despite its name, its colour varies from brown to grey; the name distinguishes it from the white rhinoceros, a distinction said to derive from a mistranslation of the Afrikaans wyd ("wide") referring to lip shape.

Description

The black rhinoceros has a pointed, prehensile upper lip adapted for grasping twigs and leaves, in contrast to the square lip of the grazing white rhinoceros. It bears two keratin horns, the front one typically about 50 cm long and occasionally much longer. Adults are solitary and notoriously ill-tempered, relying on keen hearing and smell rather than their poor eyesight.

Behaviour and diet

Black rhinos are browsers, feeding on leafy plants, branches, shoots and fruit. They are generally solitary except for mothers with calves, and males defend territories marked with dung middens. They commonly wallow in mud, which protects the skin from sun and insects.

Conservation

Poaching for horn, driven by demand in traditional medicine markets and for dagger handles, reduced the population from an estimated 65,000–100,000 in 1960 to fewer than 2,500 by the mid-1990s. Intensive protection and translocation programmes have since raised numbers to over 6,000, but the species remains Critically Endangered and poaching continues. The western black rhinoceros subspecies was declared extinct in 2011.
