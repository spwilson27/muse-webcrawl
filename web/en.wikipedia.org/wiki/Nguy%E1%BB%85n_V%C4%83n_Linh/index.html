Nguyễn Văn Linh (1 July 1915 – 27 April 1998) was a Vietnamese revolutionary and politician who served as General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam from 1986 to 1991. He is best known as the political architect of Đổi Mới, the economic renovation programme that moved Vietnam toward a market economy.

Career

A veteran of the anti-French and anti-American resistance and long-time party chief in Saigon, Linh experimented with market reforms as party secretary of Ho Chi Minh City in the early 1980s. Elevated to general secretary at the Sixth Party Congress in December 1986, amid economic crisis, he launched Đổi Mới — legalising private business, decollectivising agriculture and opening to foreign investment — while retaining one-party rule.

Legacy

Linh's reforms transformed Vietnam's economy and are compared with perestroika, though Vietnam's version proved far more durable. He retired in 1991 and later became a critic of corruption within the party.
