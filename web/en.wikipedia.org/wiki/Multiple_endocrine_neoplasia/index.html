Multiple endocrine neoplasia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) is a group of inherited disorders in which tumours develop in two or more endocrine glands. The syndromes are transmitted in an autosomal dominant pattern and are classified into several types according to the glands involved and the causative gene.

Clinical features

MEN 1 usually presents with hyperparathyroidism from multigland parathyroid disease, often in early adulthood, together with hormone-secreting pancreatic tumours (gastrinomas causing Zollinger–Ellison syndrome; insulinomas causing hypoglycaemia) and pituitary adenomas such as prolactinomas. MEN 2 is dominated by medullary thyroid carcinoma, which in MEN 2B can arise in infancy; phaeochromocytomas occur in about half of cases. Genetic testing for RET mutations guides prophylactic thyroidectomy in childhood for MEN 2 carriers.

Management

Management combines surgery (for example, parathyroidectomy, removal of phaeochromocytoma before thyroid surgery, and prophylactic thyroidectomy), medical therapy for hormone excess, lifelong biochemical and imaging surveillance, and cascade genetic screening of relatives.
