Torrens title is a system of land registration in which a government-maintained register of land holdings is itself conclusive evidence of ownership. Under the system, the state guarantees the accuracy of the register, and a person shown on it as the owner holds an indefeasible title — one that generally cannot be challenged on the basis of defects in earlier transactions, subject only to limited statutory exceptions.

History

The system is named after Sir Robert Torrens, premier of South Australia, who modelled it on the registration of ships under British merchant shipping law and drove its enactment in the Real Property Act 1858. Torrens's goal was to replace the cumbersome old system of conveyancing by deeds — under which every transfer required tracing a chain of documents back decades — with a single register entry that could be relied upon absolutely. The reform was rapidly adopted across Australia and New Zealand and spread through much of the British Empire and beyond.

Principles

Scholars conventionally describe three principles. The mirror principle holds that the register should reflect all material facts about the title. The curtain principle holds that a purchaser need not look behind the register to earlier dealings. The insurance principle holds that anyone who suffers loss through the operation of the system is compensated from a public assurance fund.

Because title depends on registration rather than on the validity of the underlying documents, an entry procured even by a forged transfer may (depending on the jurisdiction's version of indefeasibility) confer a good title on a bona fide registered purchaser, with the true owner's remedy lying against the assurance fund rather than the land.

Adoption

Torrens registration operates throughout Australia and New Zealand, in western Canada and several other Canadian jurisdictions, in Singapore, Malaysia, Fiji, and various other states, and in modified form in parts of the United States. England and Wales operate a related (though not identical) registered-title system under the Land Registration Act 2002.
