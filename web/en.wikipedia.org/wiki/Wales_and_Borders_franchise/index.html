Wales and Borders franchise

The Wales and Borders franchise (Welsh: Masnachfraint Cymru a'r Gororau) is a passenger railway franchise covering most train services in Wales, the Welsh Marches, and parts of the North West and West Midlands of England, including services to Birmingham, Manchester, Liverpool and Chester. It is one of the railway franchises created following the privatisation of British Rail.

History

From 2003 to 2018 the franchise was operated by Arriva Trains Wales, which ran over 1,000 services per day serving 247 stations. In October 2018 KeolisAmey Wales took over under the new brand Transport for Wales Rail Services. In February 2021, amid the COVID-19 pandemic, the Welsh Government nationalised the operation through Transport for Wales Rail Limited, a publicly owned operator of last resort, which continues to run the services.

Services

The franchise covers the South Wales Main Line, North Wales Coast Line, Cambrian Line, Heart of Wales line, Valley Lines network around Cardiff, and cross-border routes into England. Major intercity services link Cardiff and Swansea with Manchester and Birmingham, and Holyhead with Cardiff and (historically) London via other operators.

South Wales Metro

The franchise incorporates plans for the South Wales Metro, an integrated transport network centred on Cardiff involving electrification of the Valley Lines, new trains, and tram-train services, delivered by Transport for Wales.
