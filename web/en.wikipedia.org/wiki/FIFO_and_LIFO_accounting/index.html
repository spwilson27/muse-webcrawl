FIFO and LIFO accounting

FIFO ("first-in, first-out") and LIFO ("last-in, first-out") are methods used in accounting to determine the value of inventory and the cost of goods sold when identical items are purchased at different prices over time. They specify the assumed flow of costs — not necessarily the physical flow of goods.

The methods

Under FIFO, the oldest purchase costs are assigned to cost of goods sold first, so ending inventory is valued at the most recent prices. Under LIFO, the newest costs go to cost of goods sold first, leaving the oldest costs in inventory. In periods of rising prices, LIFO therefore produces higher cost of goods sold, lower reported profit and lower taxable income, but a balance-sheet inventory figure that may lag far behind replacement cost. The difference between LIFO inventory and what it would be under FIFO is disclosed as the LIFO reserve.

Regulation

US GAAP permits both methods (subject to the LIFO conformity rule for taxes), but IFRS prohibits LIFO because it can leave unrealistic old costs in the balance sheet. Companies that switch away from LIFO, or are acquired under IFRS reporting, must restate accordingly.
