Overview

The Bonneville Salt Flats are a densely packed salt pan in northwestern Utah, a remnant of the Pleistocene Lake Bonneville, which once covered much of the Great Basin. The flats are public land managed by the Bureau of Land Management and are famous as the venue for land-speed records since 1914, when Teddy Tetzlaff first raced there.

Speed Weeks

The Bonneville Speedway hosts Speed Week (August) and the World Finals under SCTA/BNI sanction, where streamliners have exceeded 400 mph; Sir Malcolm Campbell, Gary Gabelich (Blue Flame, 630 mph in 1970) and the Burkland family are legends of the salt. Seasonal flooding lays down a fresh, hard surface each year when the brine evaporates. Salt thickness has thinned over decades, a concern blamed on nearby potash extraction and climate, prompting restoration pumping programmes.
