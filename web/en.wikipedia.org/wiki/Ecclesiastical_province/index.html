Ecclesiastical provinces group neighbouring dioceses.

Ecclesiastical province

An ecclesiastical province is a large jurisdiction of a Christian church, consisting of several neighbouring dioceses grouped together under the oversight of a metropolitan archbishop. The term is used principally in the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox churches and the churches of the Anglican Communion. It derives from the provinces of the late Roman Empire, whose civil structure early church organisation followed.

Catholic Church

In the Latin Church of Catholicism, a province normally comprises one metropolitan archdiocese and several suffragan dioceses. The metropolitan archbishop has limited supervisory authority over the suffragans and presides at provincial councils. Some dioceses are immediately subject to the Holy See and belong to no province. There are several hundred ecclesiastical provinces worldwide.

Anglican Communion

In Anglican usage, "province" is also the term for the member churches of the Communion themselves — autonomous national or regional churches such as the Province of Canterbury's own Church of England — as well as, in some churches, internal groupings of dioceses under an archbishop. The Church of England has two provinces, Canterbury and York, each headed by an archbishop.

Orthodox churches

Historically the Orthodox Church organised metropolises corresponding to Roman provinces, and the title "metropolitan" survives widely, though modern usages vary.
