AFL–NFL merger

AFL–NFL merger
Announced: 8 June 1966
Fully effective: 1970 season
Parties: National Football League and American Football League
Result: NFL with AFC and NFC conferences

The AFL–NFL merger was the merger of the National Football League (NFL) and the rival American Football League (AFL), agreed in 1966 and completed in 1970. It ended the bidding war for players between the leagues and created the structure of modern professional American football.

Background

The AFL, founded in 1960, challenged the NFL's dominance, competing for players, television contracts and fans. Escalating player salaries pushed both leagues toward an agreement, negotiated largely by NFL commissioner Pete Rozelle and AFL founder Lamar Hunt.

Terms and outcome

The leagues agreed to a common player draft from 1967, an interleague championship game — the Super Bowl — and full integration by 1970. All ten AFL teams joined the NFL, most forming the American Football Conference (AFC), balanced by the National Football Conference (NFC). The merger also required, and received, an exemption from antitrust laws, granted by the United States Congress in 1966.
