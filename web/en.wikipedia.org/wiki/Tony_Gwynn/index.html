Tony Gwynn

Anthony Keith Gwynn Sr. (May 9, 1960 – June 16, 2014), nicknamed "Mr. Padre", was an American professional baseball right fielder who played 20 seasons in Major League Baseball, all of them for the San Diego Padres.

Gwynn was one of the greatest hitters in baseball history. He won eight National League batting titles, tying the league record, finished with a career batting average of .338, and collected 3,141 hits. A 15-time All-Star, he was renowned for his ability to hit to all fields and for striking out rarely — fewer than 500 times in his entire career. He led the Padres to their only two World Series appearances, in 1984 and 1998.

Later life and honours

After retiring in 2001, Gwynn coached baseball at his alma mater, San Diego State University, and worked as a broadcaster. He was inducted into the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 2007, in his first year of eligibility. He died of salivary gland cancer in 2014.
