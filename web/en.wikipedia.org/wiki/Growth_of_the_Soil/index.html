Growth of the Soil

Growth of the Soil (Norwegian: Markens Grøde) is a novel by Norwegian author Knut Hamsun, published in 1917. It is generally regarded as Hamsun's masterpiece and played a major role in his being awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1920.

The novel tells the story of Isak, a simple, taciturn man who settles in an uninhabited valley in northern Norway, clears the land, and builds a farm he names Sellanraa. Joined by Inger, a woman with a cleft lip who becomes his wife, Isak's life follows the rhythms of the seasons and the slow expansion of his holding. Around this central narrative Hamsun weaves subplots involving neighbours, mining speculation, infanticide, and the encroachment of modern commerce.

The work celebrates agrarian life, self-sufficiency and man's rootedness in the earth, in deliberate contrast to the restlessness of modern urban civilisation.
