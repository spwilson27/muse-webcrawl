The 2010–11 Queensland floods were a series of floods in Australia, beginning in November 2010 and culminating in January 2011, primarily in the state of Queensland. Driven by one of the strongest La Niña events on record — which ended a decade of drought — record rainfall flooded vast areas, forced the evacuation of thousands of people, and affected more than 70 towns and cities. Thirty-three people were confirmed dead, most of them in the Lockyer Valley.

Course of the disaster

Flooding built through December 2010 across central and southern Queensland, inundating towns including Emerald, Bundaberg, and Rockhampton. On 10 January 2011 an intense flash flood — described by witnesses as an "inland tsunami" — swept down the range through Toowoomba and into the Lockyer Valley towns of Grantham, Gatton, and Withcott, killing 21 people there. The Brisbane River then rose through 12–13 January, peaking at 4.46 metres in Brisbane, with around 20,000 homes inundated across the capital and neighbouring Ipswich. The flood was Brisbane's largest since 1974.

Response and aftermath

The disaster triggered a massive volunteer clean-up — the "mud army" — and national fundraising appeals. A Commission of Inquiry examined the management of Wivenhoe Dam, whose flood releases were criticised; it found the dam's operators had not followed the manual in some respects. The floods were declared Australia's most expensive natural disaster at the time in insured terms and dented national GDP through losses to coal mining, agriculture, and tourism. They were followed within weeks by Severe Tropical Cyclone Yasi striking north Queensland.
