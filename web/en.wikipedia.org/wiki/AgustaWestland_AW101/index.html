The AgustaWestland AW101 is a medium-lift helicopter in military and civil use. Large enough to carry 24 troops seated or 45 standing, it was developed as a joint venture between Westland Helicopters of the United Kingdom and Agusta of Italy in response to national requirements for a modern naval utility helicopter. Prior to delivery of the first model, the merged company EH Industries designated it the EH101; the type is named Merlin in British service.

Development

Studies began in 1977 when the Royal Navy issued a requirement for an anti-submarine warfare helicopter to replace the Westland Sea King. A memorandum of understanding between the British and Italian governments led to the formation of EH Industries in 1980. The first prototype flew on 9 October 1987. Following the merger of Westland and Agusta in 2000 to form AgustaWestland, the type was re-designated AW101 in 2007. Production and final assembly have been carried out at Yeovil in England and Vergiate in Italy.

Design

The AW101 is powered by three engines—either Rolls-Royce Turbomeca RTM322 or General Electric CT7 turboshafts—driving a five-blade main rotor with BERP (British Experimental Rotor Programme) blades. The airframe is of aluminium-lithium alloy with a rear loading ramp, and the type features an active vibration control system, crashworthy seating, and extensive defensive aids in military variants. Naval versions carry dipping sonar, sonobuoys, torpedoes, and anti-ship missiles depending on role.

Operational history

The Merlin HM1 entered Royal Navy service in 1999 for anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare, operating from frigates, carriers, and shore bases. Upgraded Merlin HM2s followed, and RAF Merlin HC3/3A transport variants were later transferred to the Royal Navy's Commando Helicopter Force as the HC4/4A. Italy operates the type in anti-submarine, airborne early warning, and utility roles. Denmark flies the AW101 for search and rescue and troop transport; Norway ordered the type for all-weather SAR; other operators include Algeria, Canada (as the CH-149 Cormorant for SAR), Japan, Poland, Portugal, Saudi Arabia, and Turkmenistan. Civil and VVIP variants have also been sold, and a version designated VH-71 Kestrel was developed for the United States presidential helicopter programme before its cancellation in 2009.

Variants

  • Merlin HM1/HM2 – Royal Navy anti-submarine and maritime patrol versions.
  • Merlin HC3/HC4 – transport versions for commando support.
  • CH-149 Cormorant – Canadian search and rescue variant.
  • VH-71 Kestrel – cancelled US presidential transport derivative.

Specifications (general)

  • Crew: 3–4
  • Capacity: 24 seated or 45 standing troops; up to 5,600 lb (approx.) internal cargo
  • Maximum takeoff weight: 14,600 kg (32,188 lb)
  • Maximum speed: 309 km/h (192 mph)
  • Range: approximately 1,390 km (860 mi)
