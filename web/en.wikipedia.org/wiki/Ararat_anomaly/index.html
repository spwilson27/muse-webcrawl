Ararat anomaly

The Ararat anomaly is an object appearing on photographs of the snowfields near the summit of Mount Ararat in Turkey, which some have claimed to be the remains of Noah's Ark. Mount Ararat, a dormant volcano near the Turkish border with Armenia and Iran, is traditionally associated with the biblical account in the Book of Genesis, in which the ark came to rest "on the mountains of Ararat".

Satellite and aerial imagery

The feature first attracted attention through declassified United States reconnaissance imagery, including photographs taken in 1949 by a US Air Force aircraft and later satellite images released in the 1990s and 2000s through Freedom of Information requests. The object appears as an elongated, dark feature at roughly 4,700 metres elevation on the mountain's western plateau, partly covered by glacial ice. Commercial satellite imagery obtained by researchers and media outlets has been examined by analysts with differing conclusions.

Interpretations

Advocates of the ark interpretation point to the object's apparent dimensions and boat-like outline. Skeptical analysts, including some who examined the imagery for intelligence agencies, have concluded the anomaly is consistent with a natural rock formation, with shadows and ice creating the impression of an artificial structure. Expeditions to the site are restricted because of the mountain's sensitive military-zone location, and no scientific expedition has produced verified evidence of an artificial vessel there.

Related claims

The Ararat anomaly is distinct from the nearby Durupınar site, a boat-shaped geological formation south of the mountain promoted since the mid-20th century as the ark's remains, which geologists regard as a natural mudstone formation. Searches for Noah's Ark on Ararat have a long history and remain a topic of popular and religious interest.
