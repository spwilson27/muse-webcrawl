Impersonator

An impersonator is someone who imitates or copies the behaviour, appearance, or sound of another person, usually a celebrity. Impersonation differs from acting in that the goal is not to portray a character but to replicate a real, recognisable individual as faithfully as possible. Impersonators work in entertainment, comedy, tribute performance, and voice-over, and range from look-alike "tribute artists" to impressionists who mimic voices and mannerisms.

Types

  • Celebrity look-alikes and tribute artists — performers who resemble and re-create the shows of famous figures, most famously Elvis Presley impersonators, who number in the thousands and hold conventions and contests; similar scenes exist for Michael Jackson, Marilyn Monroe, ABBA, and the Beatles.
  • Impressionists — comedians whose acts consist of vocal impersonations of politicians and celebrities, a staple of radio, television sketch comedy and satire shows.
  • Sound-alikes and voice doubles — singers and voice actors hired to replicate a known voice for animation, advertising, or when the original artist is unavailable.
  • Historical and political decoys — body doubles used for security or ceremonies by heads of state; wartime decoys such as M. E. Clifton James, who impersonated Field Marshal Montgomery during the Second World War, are a serious, non-entertainment application.

Legality

Entertainment impersonation is generally lawful, but impersonating a police officer, official, or other person with intent to deceive for gain is a criminal offence in most jurisdictions (identity fraud). Commercial use of a celebrity's likeness may also raise right-of-publicity issues, though parody and tribute performance enjoy varying protection.

In culture

Impersonation has a long lineage, from music-hall impressionists to modern viral videos and deepfake technology, which applies machine learning to synthesise convincing impersonations digitally and has raised new concerns about fraud and misinformation.
