Korean Air Lines Flight 902 (KAL902) was a scheduled flight from Paris to Seoul via Anchorage that was shot at by Soviet air defence interceptors on 20 April 1978 after it strayed into prohibited Soviet airspace near Murmansk. The crippled Boeing 707 made a forced landing on a frozen lake in Karelia; two passengers were killed.

Incident

Because of a navigational error—generally attributed to a mistaken compass setting compounded by failure to cross-check position—the aircraft reversed course over the Arctic and flew southeast into the Soviet Union instead of toward Alaska. Soviet Su-15 interceptors were scrambled; the Soviets said the airliner ignored signals to land, while the Korean crew maintained they tried to communicate. An interceptor fired two missiles; one struck the left wing and shrapnel punctured the fuselage, killing two passengers and causing rapid decompression.

Captain Kim Chang-kyu brought the aircraft down through cloud and made a successful emergency landing on the ice of Lake Korpiyarvi near Loukhi. All surviving occupants were rescued by Soviet helicopters.

Aftermath

Passengers were released after two days and flown home via Helsinki; the captain and navigator were detained for investigation but released after about two months, following an apology and a settlement in which Korean Air Lines paid a Soviet invoice (reported at about $100,000) for the "rescue" costs. The incident foreshadowed the far deadlier shootdown of Korean Air Lines Flight 007 in 1983.
