Barangaroo

Barangaroo is a suburb and major urban renewal precinct on the northwestern edge of the Sydney central business district, New South Wales, Australia. The 22-hectare site, a former container wharf on Darling Harbour known as the Hungry Mile, has been redeveloped since the 2010s into three parts: Barangaroo South, a commercial and residential district anchored by the three International Towers; Barangaroo Central, a low-rise cultural and residential area; and Barangaroo Reserve, a six-hectare harbour headland park opened in 2015 that recreates the pre-1836 shoreline with sandstone blocks and native planting.

Name

The area is named for Barangaroo, a Cammeraygal woman and fisherwoman who was a prominent figure in the early colony of Sydney and the wife of Bennelong. The name was chosen by public vote in 2006.

Development and controversy

The redevelopment, overseen by the New South Wales government and built principally by Lendlease, has been among Australia's largest urban projects. Its most contested element is the 275-metre Crown Sydney resort tower, completed in 2020, which houses a hotel and apartments but whose planned casino operation was barred after a 2021 inquiry found Crown Resorts unsuitable to hold a casino licence. Litigation over view corridors from Observatory Hill and disputes over the size of the project marked its planning history. Barangaroo railway station on the Sydney Metro City & Southwest line opened in August 2024.
