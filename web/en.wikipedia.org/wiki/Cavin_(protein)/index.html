Cavin (protein)

The cavins are a family of cytoplasmic proteins that associate with caveolae, the small flask-shaped invaginations of the plasma membrane, and are required together with the caveolins for caveola formation. Four cavin proteins are known in vertebrates: cavin-1 (PTRF), cavin-2 (SDPR), cavin-3 (SRBC) and cavin-4 (MURC).

Members

Cavin-1, originally identified as polymerase I and transcript release factor (PTRF), is the founding and most essential family member; its loss eliminates caveolae in most cell types. Cavin-2 (serum deprivation-response protein, SDPR) helps regulate caveolar shape and is enriched in endothelial cells. Cavin-3 (SRBC) is a protein kinase C substrate related to signalling. Cavin-4 (muscle-related coiled-coil protein, MURC) is largely restricted to skeletal and cardiac muscle.

Structure and function

Cavins are coiled-coil proteins that assemble into a coat complex on the cytoplasmic surface of caveolae, interacting with caveolin-1 and membrane phospholipids such as phosphatidylserine and phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate. The cavin coat is thought to stabilise the curved caveolar bulb and recruit additional machinery involved in caveolar endocytosis, mechanoprotection and signalling.

Clinical significance

Mutations in cavin-1 cause congenital generalised lipodystrophy type 4, with muscular dystrophy and cardiac arrhythmias, while mutations in cavin-4 are associated with dilated cardiomyopathy. Loss of cavin expression has also been reported in several cancers.
