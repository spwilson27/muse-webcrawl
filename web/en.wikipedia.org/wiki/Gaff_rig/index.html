Gaff rig

Gaff rig is a sailing rig (configuration of sails, mast and stays) in which the sail is four-cornered, fore-and-aft rigged, controlled at its peak by a gaff — a spar that runs diagonally up the mast from the jaw at the mast to support the head (top edge) of the sail. The sail's other edges are the throat (along the gaff), the foot (often attached to a boom) and the luff (along the mast).

Description

The gaff rig became the predominant fore-and-aft rig of working sailing craft in the 18th and 19th centuries — schooners, ketches, yawls, sloops and cutters — because it sets a large area of sail on a relatively short mast, handles well with small crews, and performs especially well off the wind. The gaff is hoisted by throat and peak halyards, and a topsail may be set above it.

Comparison with Bermuda rig

In the 20th century the triangular Bermuda (Marconi) rig largely replaced gaff rig in yachts, being simpler, lighter aloft and more efficient to windward. Gaff rig nevertheless remains in use on traditional and classic boats, charter vessels and some modern "gaffer" designs, prized for its appearance and its power on reaches and runs.
