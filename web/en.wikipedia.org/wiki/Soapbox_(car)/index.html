Soapbox (car)

A soapbox car (also known as a gravity racer, billy cart or go-cart in some regions) is a motorless vehicle, usually homemade, that is raced downhill powered only by gravity. The name derives from the wooden soap crates originally used to build the earliest examples during the Great Depression.

History

In 1933, Dayton, Ohio news photographer Myron Scott observed boys racing homemade cars down a hill and organised a race; the event grew into the Soap Box Derby, first held nationally in 1934 and later based in Akron, Ohio, where the All-American Soap Box Derby World Championship is still held each year at Derby Downs.

Construction

Traditional soapbox cars are built from a wooden crate or planks mounted on four wheels — often pram wheels or bicycle wheels — with a simple steering mechanism worked by the driver's hands or feet and a rudimentary brake. Modern derby kits use moulded shells, precision axles and strict weight limits, with classes for different ages.

Racing

Races are typically run on a closed, sloping road or dedicated track, two cars at a time in timed heats. Because there is no engine, success depends on low rolling resistance, good aerodynamics, careful alignment and the combined weight of car and driver. Adult and charity "soapbox races", such as the Red Bull Soapbox Race, emphasise creative, often whimsical designs as much as speed.
