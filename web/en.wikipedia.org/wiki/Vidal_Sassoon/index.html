Vidal Sassoon CBE (17 January 1928 – 9 May 2012) was a British hairstylist, businessman, and philanthropist. He is credited with creating a simple geometric hair style called the five-point cut, a model of which is the bob cut. Influenced by Bauhaus architecture, Sassoon's styles liberated women from the beehive and other high-maintenance styles, replacing them with precision cuts that fell naturally back into place.

Career

Born in Hammersmith, London, to a Sephardic Jewish father from Greece and an Ashkenazi mother from Kyiv, Sassoon spent part of his childhood in an orphanage after his father's departure. He was apprenticed to hairdresser Adolph Cohen at 14. As a teenager he fought with the 43 Group against fascist organisations in post-war London, and in 1948 he joined the Haganah and fought in the Arab–Israeli War.

Sassoon opened his first salon in Mayfair in 1954. His breakthrough came in the 1960s with geometric cuts such as the "five point" and his 1967 asymmetric crops for clients including fashion designer Mary Quant, actress Nancy Kwan, and model Grace Coddington. He expanded into a global salon chain and a successful hair-care product line, whose slogan "If you don't look good, we don't look good" became famous. In 1983 he sold his name and the product business to Richardson-Vicks, later part of Procter & Gamble, and devoted much of his later life to philanthropy, including anti-poverty campaigns and a centre for the study of antisemitism at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. He died of leukaemia at his Los Angeles home in 2012.
