Heribert Rosweyde

Heribert Rosweyde (20 January 1569, Utrecht – 5 October 1629, Antwerp) was a Dutch Jesuit scholar and hagiographer, best known as the originator of the vast project of critically edited lives of the saints that became the Acta Sanctorum.

Life and work

Rosweyde was born in Utrecht and studied at Douai and Leuven before entering the Society of Jesus in 1588. While teaching philosophy at Douai, he spent his leisure studying the manuscripts of saints' lives in the libraries of Belgian monasteries, and became convinced that the medieval legends needed to be published in their original, unedited form rather than in the abridged and polished versions then current, such as those of Laurentius Surius.

The Acta Sanctorum project

In 1603 Rosweyde conceived a plan for an eighteen-volume collection of saints' lives arranged by feast day, and in 1607 he published the plan as the pamphlet Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis bibliothecis manuscriptae. Although he did not live to see the first volume appear, his colleague Jean Bolland took up the project; the first January volumes of the Acta Sanctorum were published in Antwerp in 1643. The work, continued by the Bollandists, is still in progress today.

Other works

Rosweyde edited the Vitae Patrum (1615), a collection of lives of the Desert Fathers, and published an edition of the letters of St. Paulinus of Nola. His critical, source-based approach laid the foundations of modern hagiography.
