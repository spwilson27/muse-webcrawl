Elections in Brazil are held at the federal, state, and municipal levels. Brazil elects a president and vice president, the National Congress (Chamber of Deputies and Federal Senate), state governors and legislatures, and municipal mayors and councils. General and municipal elections alternate every two years.

System

The president, governors, and mayors of larger municipalities are elected by absolute majority in up to two rounds, the second round being a runoff between the top two candidates. Federal deputies and most local councillors are elected by open-list proportional representation, while senators are elected by plurality in staggered terms of eight years. Voting is compulsory for literate citizens between 18 and 70, making turnout among the highest in the world; 16- and 17-year-olds may vote voluntarily.

Administration and voting technology

Elections are administered by the Electoral Justice system headed by the Superior Electoral Court (TSE). Since 1996 Brazil has voted on domestically developed electronic voting machines, allowing results to be tallied within hours; the system has also been the subject of political debate and audit proposals.

History

Direct elections were restored after the military dictatorship (1964–1985); the first direct presidential election of the new republic was held in 1989, and universal suffrage including illiterate citizens was enshrined in the 1988 Constitution.
