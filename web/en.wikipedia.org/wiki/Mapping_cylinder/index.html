Mapping cylinder

In mathematics, specifically algebraic topology, the mapping cylinder of a continuous function f : X → Y between topological spaces X and Y is the quotient space obtained by gluing one end of the cylinder X × [0,1] to Y along f. It is denoted Mf and may be seen as a way of replacing an arbitrary map by a cofibration without changing the homotopy type involved.

Definition

The mapping cylinder is defined as Mf = ((X × [0,1]) ⊔ Y) / ~, where the equivalence relation identifies (x, 0) with f(x) for all x in X. The space X sits inside Mf as the subspace X × {1}, and Mf deformation-retracts onto Y, so the inclusion of Y is a homotopy equivalence. The composite X → Mf → Y equals f, with the first map a cofibration and the second a homotopy equivalence.

Applications

Mapping cylinders are a basic technical tool in homotopy theory: any map factors as a cofibration followed by a homotopy equivalence, allowing general maps to be treated with the convenient properties of inclusions. The mapping cylinder is used in the definitions of the mapping cone and Puppe sequence, and in the theory of cofiber sequences. For a homeomorphism of a surface, the analogous construction with the ends glued together gives the mapping torus.
