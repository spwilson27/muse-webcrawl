The Polavaram Project is a multipurpose irrigation and hydroelectric project on the Godavari River in Andhra Pradesh, India. It comprises a large earth-cum-rockfill dam near Polavaram village, designed to irrigate roughly 290,000 hectares, generate 960 MW of hydropower, supply drinking water, and transfer surplus Godavari water to the Krishna River basin through a right canal — a key element of India's river-interlinking ambitions. It was declared a national project under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014.

Construction of the Polavaram dam and spillway on the Godavari River.

History

The scheme was first conceived in 1941 by engineer K. L. Rao and later championed by administrators including Sir Arthur Cotton's successors on the Godavari system. Foundation stone ceremonies occurred in 1980 and again in 2004, but sustained construction began only in the 2010s. The project set a Guinness-recognised concreting record during spillway works in 2019.

Controversies

The reservoir's submergence zone extends into Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and Telangana, prompting inter-state disputes and litigation. Rehabilitation of displaced communities — estimated at around 200,000 people, many of them Adivasi — has been a persistent point of contention, as have cost escalations far beyond the original estimates and disputes between the state and central governments over funding and execution.
