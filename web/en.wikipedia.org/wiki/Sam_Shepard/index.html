Sam Shepard

Born: Samuel Shepard Rogers III, 5 November 1943, Fort Sheridan, Illinois, U.S.
Died: 27 July 2017 (aged 73), Midway, Kentucky, U.S.
Occupations: Playwright, actor, director, screenwriter
Notable work: Buried Child, True West, Paris, Texas

Sam Shepard (born Samuel Shepard Rogers III; November 5, 1943 – July 27, 2017) was an American playwright, actor, director, screenwriter, and author whose career spanned over half a century. He wrote 58 plays as well as several books of short stories, essays, and memoirs, and won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama in 1979 for his play Buried Child.

Playwriting

Shepard began writing plays in New York's Off-Off-Broadway scene in the mid-1960s, producing a stream of short, surreal works such as Cowboys and The Rock Garden. In the 1970s he wrote his celebrated "family plays"—Curse of the Starving Class (1978), Buried Child (1978), True West (1980), Fool for Love (1983), and A Lie of the Mind (1985)—which combine mythic images of the American West with violent, decaying family relationships and won him ten Obie Awards.

Film work

Shepard wrote the screenplay for Wim Wenders' Paris, Texas (1984), which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes. As an actor he was nominated for an Academy Award for playing test pilot Chuck Yeager in The Right Stuff (1983) and appeared in films including Days of Heaven, Steel Magnolias, Black Hawk Down, The Notebook, and August: Osage County. He had a long relationship with the actress Jessica Lange.

Death and legacy

Shepard died at his home in Kentucky in 2017 from complications of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), which he had kept private. He is regarded as one of the most important American dramatists of his generation; his final book, the novel Spy of the First Person, was published posthumously.
