General Atomics MQ-1 Predator

The General Atomics MQ-1 Predator is an American remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) built by General Atomics Aeronautical Systems and used by the United States Air Force and the Central Intelligence Agency. Initially conceived in the early 1990s for aerial reconnaissance and forward observation roles, the Predator was later modified to carry and fire two AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, becoming one of the first armed unmanned combat aerial vehicles. It pioneered the modern era of long-endurance armed drone operations.

Development

The Predator derived from the GNAT 750 and the DARPA Amber projects of Abraham Karem's Leading Systems. The RQ-1 first deployed to the Balkans in 1995 for surveillance over Bosnia and Kosovo. After the September 11 attacks, an armed version, redesignated MQ-1, conducted strikes in Afghanistan, Pakistan, Yemen, Somalia, and elsewhere, operated by crews via satellite links from ground stations in the United States.

Design

The aircraft is powered by a Rotax 914F piston engine driving a pusher propeller, cruises at about 84 mph, and can remain aloft for more than 24 hours at altitudes up to 25,000 feet. Its sensor ball carries electro-optical/infrared cameras and a synthetic aperture radar, and the airframe's distinctive inverted V-tail protects the propeller during takeoff and landing.

Operational history

Predators flew more than two million hours, most of them in combat theaters, and the type became emblematic of remote warfare and its controversies. The USAF retired the MQ-1 in March 2018, transitioning squadrons to the larger, faster MQ-9 Reaper; Italian and other operators flew related versions, and NASA operated the unarmed Altair and Ikhana research variants.
