Reserve power

Reserve power, also known as standby power, is electrical power provided by a secondary source that takes over when the primary supply fails or is interrupted. Reserve power systems are used in settings where continuity of supply is critical, such as hospitals, data centres, telecommunications exchanges, water treatment plants and emergency services. Typical sources include battery-based uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), diesel or gas generators, and flywheels.

Overview

A reserve power system normally sits idle or in a standby condition while the mains supply is available. When a failure, sag or outage is detected, an automatic transfer switch disconnects the load from the primary source and connects it to the reserve source. In large installations, a UPS bridges the gap between the failure of the mains and the start-up of a standby generator, which may take several seconds to reach full output.

Applications

Standby generators are required by building and fire codes in many jurisdictions for hospitals, high-rise buildings and facilities handling hazardous materials. Data centres classify their reserve power provision in tiers, with redundancy expressed as N+1 or 2N configurations. Reserve capacity on electrical grids — generating plant held ready to cover the loss of the largest infeed — is also sometimes described as reserve power, related to the concept of spinning reserve.
