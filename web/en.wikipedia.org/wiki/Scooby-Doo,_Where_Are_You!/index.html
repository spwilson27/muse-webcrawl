Scooby-Doo, Where Are You!

Genre: Animated mystery comedy

Created by: Joe Ruby, Ken Spears

Production: Hanna-Barbera Productions

Network: CBS

Original run: 13 September 1969 – 31 October 1970 (first run); later episodes 1976–1978 on ABC as part of package shows

Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! is an American animated television series produced by Hanna-Barbera for CBS. It premiered on 13 September 1969 and launched the long-running Scooby-Doo franchise. The show follows four teenagers — Fred Jones, Daphne Blake, Velma Dinkley and Norville "Shaggy" Rogers — and their talking Great Dane, Scooby-Doo, as they travel in their van, the Mystery Machine, solving mysteries involving supposedly supernatural creatures.

Premise and formula

Each episode follows the durable formula the series made famous: the gang arrives somewhere, encounters a "monster" or "ghost" terrorising the locals, investigates while being chased (often in musical chase sequences), sets a trap, and unmasks the villain — invariably a flesh-and-blood criminal using a costume and tricks for profit. The culprit's catchphrase complaint that he "would have gotten away with it too, if it weren't for you meddling kids" became a cultural byword. Scooby and Shaggy, the show's comic cowards, are motivated by food, especially the comically tall "Scooby Snacks" used to bribe them into danger.

Development

The series was developed by writers Joe Ruby and Ken Spears with character designer Iwao Takamoto, under CBS executive Fred Silverman, who wanted a non-violent mystery show for Saturday mornings in response to parental concern about superhero cartoons. Early concepts featured a dog as a sidekick; the Great Dane was promoted to co-star during development, and Silverman took the name "Scooby-Doo" from the scat syllables in Frank Sinatra's recording of "Strangers in the Night". Casey Kasem voiced Shaggy and Don Messick voiced Scooby-Doo in the original series.

Broadcast history

Two seasons of 25 episodes were produced for CBS in 1969–1970. The show was an immediate hit, and Hanna-Barbera built numerous successors on its model, beginning with The New Scooby-Doo Movies (1972). Sixteen further episodes were produced in the late 1970s for ABC and aired within Scooby's All-Star Laff-A-Lympics, later syndicated under the Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! title, bringing the total to 41 episodes.

Legacy

The series defined the template for decades of Scooby-Doo incarnations in television, direct-to-video films and two live-action theatrical films, and its characters and catchphrases are deeply embedded in popular culture. In 2013, TV Guide ranked Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! the fifth-greatest television cartoon of all time.
