Order of magnitude

An order of magnitude is an approximation of the logarithm of a value relative to some contextually understood reference value, usually 10, interpreted as the base of the logarithm and the representative of values of magnitude one. Two numbers are "within an order of magnitude" of each other if the larger is no more than about ten times the smaller.

Definition and use

Orders of magnitude are used to make approximate comparisons, particularly in science, where quantities range over many powers of ten. The order of magnitude of a number is, roughly, its position on a logarithmic scale: the number 1,000,000 (106) is six orders of magnitude larger than 1. Scientific notation expresses numbers explicitly in this form, with a coefficient multiplied by a power of ten.

Estimates

Order-of-magnitude reasoning underlies Fermi estimates — back-of-the-envelope calculations that combine rough inputs to obtain a result reliable within a factor of ten. Typical examples: there are about 1080 atoms in the observable universe, and the mass of the Earth is about 1024 kg.

Other bases

Although base 10 is most common (a "decade"), orders of magnitude are also used with base 2 in computing (factors of 1,024, e.g., kilobytes versus megabytes) and with the natural logarithm in some scientific contexts.
