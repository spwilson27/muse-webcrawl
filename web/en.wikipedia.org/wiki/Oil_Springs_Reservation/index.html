Oil Springs Reservation

Oil Springs Reservation, also known as Oil Spring Reservation, is an Indian reservation of the federally recognized Seneca Nation of Indians located in western New York State, United States. The reservation covers approximately one square mile (about 640 acres) straddling the border of Allegany County and Cattaraugus County, near the village of Cuba, New York. It is one of three reservations of the Seneca Nation, along with the Allegany Reservation and the Cattaraugus Reservation.

History

The reservation takes its name from a natural petroleum spring, known as the Oil Spring, located within its boundaries. In 1627, the Franciscan missionary Joseph de La Roche Daillon described the spring, producing the earliest recorded reference to petroleum in North America. Native peoples had long used the oil from such seeps for medicinal and ceremonial purposes.

The Oil Springs Reservation was reserved to the Seneca under the Treaty of Big Tree in 1797, when the Seneca ceded most of their lands in western New York but retained several reservations. Because of ambiguities in the treaty's description, the Oil Spring tract was omitted from some later conveyances, and the Seneca were dispossessed of it for a period in the 19th century before successfully recovering title through litigation and negotiation.

Geography and government

The reservation is governed by the Seneca Nation of Indians, whose government consists of an elected council and president under an 1848 constitution. Population on the reservation itself is small. The Seneca Nation operates governmental and community facilities there, and the territory is significant chiefly for its historical associations with the petroleum spring.
