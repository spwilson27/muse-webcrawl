The National Women's Law Center (NWLC) is a United States nonprofit organization founded in 1972 that advocates for women's rights and LGBTQ rights through litigation, policy research and public education, with an emphasis on issues affecting low-income women and girls.

History

The Center began as a programme of the Center for Law and Social Policy, when female administrative staff and law students demanded better pay, professional roles for women attorneys, and a formal women's programme. It became an independent organization under founding leadership that included Marcia Greenberger.

Work

The NWLC works on education (including Title IX enforcement in school athletics), employment and pay equity, reproductive rights and health care access, childcare, and income security. It has participated in major Supreme Court litigation and administers the Time's Up Legal Defense Fund, launched in 2018 to support workers challenging sexual harassment.

Campaigns

Notable campaigns include pressing for enforcement of equal athletic opportunity in schools, analysing the gender wage gap in its annual reports, and legal advocacy surrounding contraceptive coverage under the Affordable Care Act.
