Hali Meiðhad

Hali Meiðhad
Language: Early Middle English
Date: c. 1190–1230
Genre: Prose treatise on virginity
Group: Katherine Group

Hali Meiðhad ("Holy Maidenhood") is an early Middle English prose treatise, written around 1190–1230 in the West Midlands, addressed to women — probably anchoresses — who had taken vows of virginity. It forms part of the Katherine Group of devotional texts, alongside the lives of Saints Katherine, Margaret and Juliana, and is connected with Ancrene Wisse.

Content

The text praises consecrated virginity above marriage, vividly cataloguing the hardships of medieval married life — a demanding husband, pregnancy, childbirth and household drudgery — in order to persuade its readers of the superiority of the contemplative, celibate life. Its lively, sometimes satirical depiction of marriage is among the most quoted passages in early Middle English prose.

Language

Hali Meiðhad is written in the AB language, a highly regular written standard of the West Midlands also used in Ancrene Wisse, notable for its alliterative prose rhythms.
