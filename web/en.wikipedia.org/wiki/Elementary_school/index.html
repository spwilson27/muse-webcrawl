Elementary school


An elementary school classroom

An elementary school, a primary school, or a grade school in North America is a school for primary education of children who are 4 to 11 years of age (and in many cases, 4 to 10 years of age). Primary schooling follows preschool and precedes secondary schooling.

The International Standard Classification of Education considers primary education as a single phase where programmes are typically designed to provide fundamental skills in reading, writing, and mathematics and to establish a solid foundation for learning. This is ISCED Level 1.

Curriculum

Elementary education emphasizes literacy and numeracy, alongside foundational work in science, social studies, art, music, and physical education. In most systems a single classroom teacher handles most subjects for a class of children, in contrast to the subject-specialist teachers of secondary school.

Around the world

In the United States, elementary schools usually cover kindergarten through fifth or sixth grade, after which pupils move to middle school. In the United Kingdom, primary schools take children from ages 4 or 5 to 11, divided into infant and junior stages in some areas. Japan has six years of elementary school (shōgakkō) beginning at age six, as part of nine years of compulsory education. In Germany, the Grundschule typically lasts four years (six in Berlin and Brandenburg) before pupils are tracked into different secondary school types.

History

Mass elementary schooling spread in the nineteenth century with compulsory-education laws — Prussia's early system, England's Elementary Education Act 1870, and the American common-school movement — making basic literacy a near-universal public provision.
