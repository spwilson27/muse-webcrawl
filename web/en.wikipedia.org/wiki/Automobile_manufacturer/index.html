Automobile manufacturer

An automobile manufacturer is a company engaged in the design, development, manufacture, marketing and sale of motor vehicles. The automobile industry is one of the world's largest manufacturing sectors by revenue and is dominated by a relatively small number of multinational groups.

Major manufacturers

By production volume, the largest groups have historically included Toyota, Volkswagen Group, Hyundai Motor Group, General Motors, Ford, Stellantis, Honda and Nissan. Industry rankings are published annually by OICA (Organisation Internationale des Constructeurs d'Automobiles). Electric-vehicle makers such as Tesla and BYD have risen rapidly in market capitalisation and sales since the 2010s.

Structure

Manufacturers (OEMs) typically design vehicles and assemble them from components supplied by Tier 1 suppliers such as Bosch, Magna, Continental and ZF. Many brands share platforms and are organised under holding groups selling numerous marques.

History

The industry began in the late 19th century with firms such as Benz, Peugeot and Oldsmobile; mass production pioneered by Ford's assembly line (1913) made cars affordable. Post-war growth of Japanese manufacturers and later Korean and Chinese makers reshaped the global market.
