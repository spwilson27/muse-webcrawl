Salzburg

Country: Austria
State: Salzburg
Population: approx. 155,000 (city)
Elevation: 424 m
UNESCO: Historic Centre, World Heritage Site (1996)

Salzburg is the fourth-largest city in Austria and the capital of the federal state of Salzburg. Situated on the banks of the Salzach River near the German border and the northern rim of the Alps, it is renowned for its baroque old town, its role in the history of salt trading, and as the birthplace of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart.

History

The site was settled in Roman times as the municipium of Iuvavum. In the late 7th century St. Rupert founded a monastery there, and Salzburg grew into the seat of powerful prince-archbishops whose wealth came largely from salt mined at nearby Hallein—hence the name Salzburg, "salt castle". The archbishops ruled an independent ecclesiastical principality within the Holy Roman Empire until secularisation in 1803; the territory passed finally to the Austrian Empire after the Congress of Vienna.

Culture

Mozart was born in Salzburg in 1756, and the city trades heavily on his legacy through the Mozarteum, two Mozart museums, and the Salzburg Festival of music and drama, founded in 1920. The film The Sound of Music (1965), set and largely filmed in and around the city, draws large numbers of visitors. Major landmarks include Hohensalzburg Fortress, one of Europe's largest and best-preserved medieval castles, Salzburg Cathedral, Mirabell Palace, and Hellbrunn Palace.

Geography and economy

Salzburg lies about 300 km west of Vienna and 150 km southeast of Munich. Tourism, conferences, and trade fairs dominate its economy; it is also the birthplace of the Red Bull energy drink company, whose global headquarters is nearby at Fuschl am See. The historic centre was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996.
