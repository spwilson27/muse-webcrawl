Porfirio Díaz (15 September 1830 – 2 July 1915) was a Mexican general and politician who ruled Mexico for most of the period from 1876 to 1911, known as the Porfiriato. A hero of the war against the French intervention, he seized power promising "no re-election", then repeatedly re-elected himself.

The Porfiriato

Under the slogan "Order and Progress", Díaz's regime of positivist technocrats (the científicos) encouraged foreign investment, built railways and telegraphs, and modernised the economy, while concentrating land in large haciendas and suppressing dissent through the rurales and a controlled press. Economic growth came with deep inequality and political stagnation.

Fall and exile

In 1910 Francisco Madero challenged Díaz's seventh re-election and launched the Mexican Revolution under the Plan of San Luis Potosí. Unable to contain the uprising, Díaz resigned in May 1911 and sailed into exile in France, famously remarking that Madero had "unleashed a tiger". He died in Paris in 1915.
