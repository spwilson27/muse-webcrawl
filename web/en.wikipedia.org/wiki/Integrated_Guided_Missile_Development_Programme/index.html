Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme

The Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) was an Indian Ministry of Defence programme to develop a comprehensive range of missiles, managed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO). Launched in 1983 under the leadership of A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, the programme produced five missile systems — Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Nag and Trishul — covering surface-to-surface ballistic missiles, a re-entry technology demonstrator that became a strategic ballistic missile family, medium- and short-range surface-to-air missiles, and an anti-tank guided missile. The programme was formally concluded in 2008 after its main systems were developed or transferred to follow-on projects.

History

India's earlier missile work, including Project Devil and Project Valiant in the 1970s, had been discontinued. In 1982–83 the government sanctioned the IGMDP to achieve self-reliance in missile technology, particularly as access to foreign technology became restricted. Kalam, who had led the SLV-3 satellite launch vehicle project, was appointed programme director. The programme built an extensive network of DRDO laboratories, academic institutions and public and private industries.

Missile systems

  • Prithvi — a short-range surface-to-surface ballistic missile, in variants for the Army, Air Force and Navy (Dhanush), with ranges of 150–350 km.
  • Agni — begun as a technology demonstrator for re-entry vehicle technology, it evolved into a family of medium- to intercontinental-range ballistic missiles forming the core of India's nuclear deterrent.
  • Akash — a medium-range surface-to-air missile system with a range of about 25–30 km, later inducted by the Army and Air Force.
  • Nag — a third-generation "fire-and-forget" anti-tank guided missile, with a helicopter-launched variant (HELINA/Dhruvastra).
  • Trishul — a short-range surface-to-air missile; it served as a technology demonstrator and was closed in 2008 without full induction.

Legacy

The IGMDP is credited with establishing India's missile industrial base and capabilities in guidance, propulsion, materials and re-entry technology under technology-denial regimes. Its successor efforts include the Agni series' further development, the K-series submarine-launched missiles, BrahMos (a joint venture with Russia), and India's ballistic missile defence programme.
