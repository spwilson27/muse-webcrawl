Manuel I Komnenos (28 November 1118 – 24 September 1180), Latinized as Comnenus, was a Byzantine emperor of the 12th century who reigned over a crucial period in the history of the Byzantine Empire and the Mediterranean. The fourth son of John II Komnenos, he succeeded his father in 1143 and reigned for 37 years, pursuing an energetic and ambitious foreign policy aimed at restoring the empire's influence in Italy, the Balkans and the Crusader states of the Levant.

Reign

Manuel inherited a strong empire from his father and grandfather Alexios I, and continued the Komnenian restoration. He dealt with the passage of the Second Crusade (1147) through imperial territory, fought wars against the Normans of Sicily, the Hungarians and the Serbs, and intervened repeatedly in the politics of the Crusader principality of Antioch, whose prince, Raynald of Châtillon, he humbled in 1159 in a famous ceremonial entry into the city.

His attempt to reconquer Norman southern Italy in alliance with the papacy ultimately failed, and his ambition in the east ended with defeat by the Seljuk Turks at the Battle of Myriokephalon in 1176, a setback Manuel himself compared to Manzikert, though the empire's Anatolian frontier largely held afterward.

Legacy

Manuel was famed in the West as a powerful and chivalrous ruler, generous to Latin Christians and interested in theological dialogue with Rome. Historians debate whether his far-reaching ambitions overstrained the empire's resources. He was the last Komnenian emperor to rule effectively; within five years of his death the dynasty collapsed amid usurpations and the catastrophe of the Fourth Crusade followed in 1204.
