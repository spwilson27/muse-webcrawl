Victorious Youth

Yearc. 300–100 BC
MediumBronze
Dimensions151.5 cm (59⅝ in)
LocationGetty Villa, Pacific Palisades, California

The Victorious Youth, also known as the Getty Bronze, is a life-size ancient Greek bronze statue of a young athlete, dated to roughly 300–100 BC. One of the very few life-size Greek bronzes to survive from antiquity, it depicts a nude youth wearing an olive wreath — the prize of a victor in athletic games — and is attributed to the tradition of Lysippos and his school.

The statue was recovered from the Adriatic Sea off the coast of Fano, Italy, in 1964, when it was snagged in the nets of an Italian fishing trawler. It had presumably been lost in a Roman-era shipwreck while being transported as loot or cargo. After passing through the antiquities market, it was purchased by the J. Paul Getty Museum in 1977.

Dispute and legal proceedings

Italy has long claimed the statue was illicitly exported and has sought its return. Italian courts ruled in favor of forfeiture, a decision upheld by Italy's Court of Cassation in 2018, and the European Court of Human Rights ruled in 2024 that Italy's claim did not violate the Getty's rights. The Getty has maintained that it acquired the work in good faith and has resisted the forfeiture, making the Victorious Youth one of the most prominent contested antiquities in the world. The statue remains on display at the Getty Villa in Pacific Palisades, California, where it is a centerpiece of the museum's antiquities collection.
