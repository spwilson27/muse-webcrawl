Conwy

Conwy is a walled market town and community in Conwy County Borough on the north coast of Wales. The town, which had a population of about 14,700 in the 2021 census (together with adjoining Deganwy), lies on the west bank of the River Conwy estuary, facing Llandudno across the water.

History

Conwy was founded by Edward I during his conquest of Wales between 1283 and 1289. Its castle and town walls were built as part of Edward's ring of fortifications in north Wales; together with the castles of Beaumaris, Caernarfon, and Harlech, Conwy Castle is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd. The walled town retains much of its medieval street pattern, 21 towers, and three original gateways, and is considered one of the finest surviving examples of a fortified medieval town in Britain.

The site was previously occupied by the Cistercian Aberconwy Abbey, founded by Llywelyn the Great, which Edward moved upriver to Maenan. Plas Mawr, an Elizabethan townhouse built in 1576–1585, survives in the high street, as does the reputed smallest house in Great Britain, a red-painted terrace house just 72 inches (1.8 m) wide on the quay.

Transport

Three bridges cross the estuary at Conwy: Thomas Telford's Conwy Suspension Bridge (1826), Robert Stephenson's tubular railway bridge (1849), and a modern road bridge. The town is served by the North Wales Coast Line and the Conwy Valley Line.
