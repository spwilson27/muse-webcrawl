Walloon (walon) is a Romance language that is spoken in much of Wallonia, the predominantly French-speaking southern region of Belgium, together with pockets of northern France. Walloon is a member of the langues d'oïl family — the closest relative of French — but it is recognised as a distinct language, not a dialect of French, by the French Community of Belgium.

The Walloon-speaking region covers most of Wallonia in southern Belgium.

History and status

Walloon literature flourished from the 17th century in theatre and poetry. Once the everyday tongue of most Walloons, it declined sharply through the 20th century as standard French became the language of schooling and media. It is now considered endangered by UNESCO, though revival efforts include theatre, music and the unified orthography known as rifondou walon.

Features

Walloon preserves Latin sounds lost in French, retains a distinctive vocabulary (e.g. ouy "today", from hodie), and has four main dialect groups: central, eastern (Liège), western and southern.
