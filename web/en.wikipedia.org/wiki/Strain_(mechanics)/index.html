Strain (mechanics)

In mechanics, strain is a description of deformation in terms of relative displacement of particles in a material body. In its simplest one-dimensional form, engineering (nominal) strain is the change in length divided by the original length, ε = ΔL/L0, a dimensionless quantity often expressed as a percentage or in microstrain. Strain quantifies how much a body stretches, compresses or shears, independent of the forces that caused it.

Types of strain

Normal strain measures elongation or contraction along an axis; shear strain measures the change in angle between two originally perpendicular lines, expressed in radians. For small deformations the strain at a point is summarised by the infinitesimal strain tensor, a symmetric 3×3 matrix built from gradients of the displacement field. For large deformations, finite-strain measures such as the Green–Lagrange strain and the engineering, true (logarithmic), Green and Almansi strains differ and must be used with their conjugate stress measures.

Relation to stress

Within the elastic range, strain is proportional to stress through the elastic moduli — Hooke's law, σ = Eε in one dimension, with E Young's modulus. Beyond the yield point, plastic (permanent) strain accumulates. Lateral contraction accompanying axial extension is described by Poisson's ratio.

Measurement

Strain is measured with extensometers, bonded electrical-resistance strain gauges (whose resistance changes with deformation), photoelasticity, and optical techniques such as digital image correlation and moiré methods. Strain gauges are ubiquitous in structural health monitoring, load cells and experimental stress analysis.
