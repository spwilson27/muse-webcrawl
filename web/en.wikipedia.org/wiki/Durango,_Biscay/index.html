Durango, Biscay

Durango is a town and municipality in the province of Biscay, in the Basque Country of northern Spain. It is the main town of the comarca of Durangaldea and lies in the valley of the Ibaizabal river, roughly 30 km southeast of Bilbao.

History

Durango received its town charter in the early 13th century and was an important stop on routes between the Meseta and the Cantabrian coast. In the 15th century the town was the scene of the Durango heresy trials. On 31 March 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, Durango was bombed by the German Condor Legion and Italian aircraft in support of Franco's forces; churches were hit during Mass and several hundred civilians were killed, weeks before the better-known bombing of Guernica.

Landmarks

The old town preserves the church of Santa María de Uribarri with its large wooden portico, the church of Santa Ana and its baroque tower, and the Kurutziaga Cross, a richly carved 15th-century stone cross in the Museo de Arte e Historia. The town is also a gateway to the Urkiola Natural Park.

Culture

Durango hosts the Durangoko Azoka, the major annual Basque book and music fair held each December since 1965, a central event in Basque-language culture. The town has rail connections to Bilbao and San Sebastián via Euskotren.
