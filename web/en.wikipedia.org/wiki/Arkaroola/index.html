Arkaroola is a wilderness sanctuary and tourism resort in the northern Flinders Ranges of South Australia, about 600 kilometres north of Adelaide. The property, on Adnyamathanha country, is renowned for its rugged mountain scenery, ancient geology, wildlife, astronomy, and the Arkaroola Wilderness Sanctuary founded by Reg and Griselda Spriggs in 1968.

Geology and landscape

The Arkaroola–Mount Painter region exposes some of Australia's most ancient and geologically complex rocks, including granites and metamorphic basement more than a billion years old, folded sedimentary ranges, and the radiogenic Mount Painter uranium province. Attractions include the Mawson Valley, Sillers Lookout, the Ridgetop Track, gorges such as Arkaroola Creek, and natural springs.

Tourism and science

The resort offers four-wheel-drive tours, bushwalking, an astronomical observatory taking advantage of exceptionally dark, dry skies, and scenic flights. Wildlife includes yellow-footed rock-wallabies, emus, and wedge-tailed eagles. In 2011–2012 the South Australian government moved to protect the area from mining, legislating the Arkaroola Protection Area after public controversy over uranium exploration at Mount Gee.
