Tây Sơn dynasty

Period1778 – 1802
CountryVietnam (Đại Việt)
FoundersThe Tây Sơn brothers: Nguyễn Nhạc, Nguyễn Huệ, Nguyễn Lữ
Notable rulerEmperor Quang Trung (Nguyễn Huệ)

The Tây Sơn dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Tây Sơn) was a Vietnamese dynasty that rose from a peasant rebellion led by three brothers from the village of Tây Sơn in Bình Định province. Between 1771 and 1802 the brothers overthrew the ruling Nguyễn and Trịnh lords, reunified Vietnam after two centuries of division, and repelled invasions from Siam and Qing China.

Rise and unification

The rebellion began in 1771 under Nguyễn Nhạc, Nguyễn Huệ, and Nguyễn Lữ, promising land redistribution and relief from taxation. The Tây Sơn captured Quy Nhơn, defeated the Nguyễn lords in the south (survivor Nguyễn Ánh repeatedly escaped), then marched north: Nguyễn Huệ destroyed the Trịnh forces in 1786 and briefly reunified the country. The brothers divided Vietnam between them, with Nhạc as emperor in the centre, Huệ in the north, and Lữ in the south.

Quang Trung and fall

Nguyễn Huệ, enthroned as Emperor Quang Trung, won the dynasty's most famous victories: over a Siamese army at Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút (1785, by Nguyễn Lữ's forces) and over a Qing invasion at the Battle of Ngọc Hồi–Đống Đa at Tết 1789. He promoted chữ Nôm, reformed taxation and education, and planned further campaigns, but died suddenly in 1792, aged about 40. His young son Quang Toản could not hold the dynasty together against the returning Nguyễn Ánh, who, with French assistance, took Huế and finally Thăng Long in 1802, founding the Nguyễn dynasty.
