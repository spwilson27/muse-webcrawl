WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control

Signed: 21 May 2003, Geneva, Switzerland

Effective: 27 February 2005

Parties: More than 180

Depositary: Secretary-General of the United Nations

The WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (WHO FCTC) is a treaty adopted by the 56th World Health Assembly on 21 May 2003 and entered into force on 27 February 2005. It was the first treaty negotiated under the auspices of the World Health Organization and one of the most rapidly and widely embraced treaties in United Nations history, providing a legal framework for tobacco control measures implemented by its parties across the world.

Background

The idea of an international instrument for tobacco control arose in the mid-1990s as evidence accumulated that tobacco use was a global epidemic projected to kill hundreds of millions of people. In 1999 the World Health Assembly formally launched negotiations, and after several rounds of intergovernmental talks the text was adopted unanimously in 2003.

Provisions

The Convention commits parties to implement comprehensive tobacco control policies, including:

  • Price and tax measures to reduce demand for tobacco (Article 6);
  • Protection from exposure to tobacco smoke in indoor workplaces, public transport and indoor public places (Article 8);
  • Regulation of the contents of tobacco products and of tobacco product disclosures (Articles 9 and 10);
  • Large health warnings and restrictions on misleading packaging and labelling (Article 11);
  • Education, communication, training and public awareness (Article 12);
  • A comprehensive ban on tobacco advertising, promotion and sponsorship (Article 13);
  • Measures concerning tobacco dependence and cessation (Article 14);
  • Elimination of illicit trade in tobacco products (Article 15);
  • Prohibition of sales to and by minors (Article 16);
  • Protection of public health policies from the commercial and other vested interests of the tobacco industry (Article 5.3).

Governance

The Conference of the Parties (COP) is the Convention's governing body and meets regularly to review implementation and adopt guidelines and protocols. In 2012 the COP adopted the Protocol to Eliminate Illicit Trade in Tobacco Products, the first protocol to the Convention, which entered into force in 2018. The United States has signed but not ratified the treaty.
