List of Cistercian abbeys in Ireland

The Cistercians arrived in Ireland in 1142 with the foundation of Mellifont Abbey, the first Cistercian house in the country, established under the patronage of Donnchad Ua Cerbaill, king of Airgíalla, on the initiative of Saint Malachy of Armagh, who had encountered the order at Clairvaux. Over the following century more than thirty abbeys were founded, mostly as daughter houses of Mellifont; nearly all were suppressed during the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII in the 1530s and 1540s.

Abbeys (selected)

  • Mellifont Abbey (1142), County Louth — mother house of most Irish Cistercian foundations.
  • Baltinglass Abbey (1148), County Wicklow.
  • Bective Abbey (1147), County Meath.
  • Boyle Abbey (1161/1218), County Roscommon.
  • Jerpoint Abbey (c. 1180), County Kilkenny — noted for its carved cloister arcade.
  • Grey Abbey (1193), County Down — founded by Anglo-Norman patrons.
  • Inch Abbey (c. 1180), County Down.
  • Corcomroe Abbey (c. 1194), County Clare, in the Burren.
  • Duiske Abbey (Graiguenamanagh) (1204), County Kilkenny.
  • Kilcooly Abbey (1182), County Tipperary.
  • Abbeyknockmoy (1190), County Galway.
  • Assaroe Abbey (1179), County Donegal.
  • Tintern Abbey (c. 1200), County Wexford — a daughter of Tintern in Wales.
  • Dunbrody Abbey (1182), County Wexford.
  • Hore Abbey (1272), County Tipperary, near the Rock of Cashel.
  • Abbeyleix Abbey (1183), County Laois.

Modern revival

The order returned to Ireland in the 19th century with the foundation of Mount Melleray Abbey (1832) in County Waterford, followed by Mount St Joseph Abbey, Roscrea (1878), and Bolton Abbey in County Kilkenny. Portglenone Abbey (Our Lady of Bethlehem) was founded in Northern Ireland in 1948.
