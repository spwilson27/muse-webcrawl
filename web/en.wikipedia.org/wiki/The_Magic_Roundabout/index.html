The Magic Roundabout is an English-language version of the French stop-motion children's television series Le Manège enchanté, created by Serge Danot in 1964. For the BBC version, first broadcast in 1965, actor Eric Thompson wrote entirely new scripts — loosely inspired by, rather than translated from, the French — and performed all the voices as narrator.

Characters and style

The stories centre on a group of animals and friends gathered around a roundabout in a garden: Dougal, a shaggy, sugar-loving Skye terrier (named Pollux in French); Florence, a sensible young girl; Brian, a cheerful snail; Ermintrude, a pink cow; Dylan, a laid-back, guitar-playing rabbit; and Zebedee, a jack-in-the-box magician on a spring whose catchphrase "Time for bed!" closed each five-minute episode. Thompson's witty, deadpan narration gave the programme a surreal, gentle humour that appealed to adults as much as children; at its peak it drew audiences of some eight million in an early-evening slot.

Later versions

Thompson recorded 441 episodes before his death in 1982. The show was revived in the 1990s on Channel 4 with narration by Nigel Planer. A CGI feature film, The Magic Roundabout (2005), featured the voices of Tom Baker, Kylie Minogue and Robbie Williams (with a heavily re-edited American version titled Doogal), and a new CGI television series followed in 2007.
