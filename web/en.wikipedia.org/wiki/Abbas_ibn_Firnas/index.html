Abbas ibn Firnas

Bornc. 809/810, Izn-Rand Onda (near Ronda), Emirate of Córdoba
Died887, Córdoba, Al-Andalus
Full nameAbbas ibn Firnas ibn Wirdas al-Takurini
FieldsAstronomy, engineering, poetry, medicine
Known forReported early flight attempt; planetarium; glass-making

Abbas ibn Firnas (Arabic: عباس بن فرناس; c. 809/810 – 887 CE) was an Andalusian polymath — inventor, engineer, aviator, physician, chemist, poet and musician — of Berber descent who lived in the Emirate of Córdoba. He is famous in popular accounts for an early attempt at human flight in the ninth century.

The flight attempt

Later sources, most prominently the seventeenth-century historian al-Maqqari drawing on earlier material, report that ibn Firnas constructed wings of wood and silk covered with feathers and launched himself from a height near Córdoba (some accounts place the feat around 852 or c. 875, and one earlier report describes him covering himself in feathers and gliding from a minaret). He is said to have flown a considerable distance before crash-landing and injuring his back, an outcome attributed to his failure to include a tail for landing. Historians debate the details, which rest on late and partly legendary sources, but the episode made him an iconic pioneer of aviation in the Arab world.

Other work

Ibn Firnas is credited with a planetarium with mechanical representations of the heavens, an early process for making colourless glass from sand (stimulated by reading about glass manufacture), lenses and "reading stones", a water clock (al-Maqata), and contributions to medicine and poetry at the court of Abd al-Rahman II and Muhammad I. He also reportedly devised a method of cutting rock crystal in al-Andalus, ending reliance on Egyptian craftsmen.

Legacy

A crater on the Moon (Ibn Firnas), a bridge in Córdoba, and an airport road statue in Baghdad commemorate him; Libya issued a stamp in his honour, and he is frequently cited as one of the earliest people to attempt heavier-than-air flight.
