Assyrian Neo-Aramaic

Assyrian Neo-Aramaic, also known as Assyrian, Suret, or Sureth, is a Northeastern Neo-Aramaic language spoken by Assyrian Christians, originally in northern Iraq, northwestern Iran, southeastern Turkey, and northeastern Syria, and today widely in the Assyrian diaspora. Number of speakers is commonly estimated in the hundreds of thousands. It is written in the Syriac script.

History

Neo-Aramaic languages descend from the Aramaic varieties spoken across the Near East for over two thousand years. Assyrian Neo-Aramaic crystallised from the dialects of the Mosul plain and the Urmia region; a written standard based on the Urmia dialect emerged in the 19th century with printing and missionary activity. The Assyrian genocide of 1915 and later upheavals, including the Simele massacre of 1933 and emigration from Iraq, dispersed speakers worldwide.

Dialects and standard

Major dialect groups include Urmian (Iran), Nineveh Plains dialects (such as Alqosh and Tel Keppe), and the dialects of Tur Abdin's neighbours; Chaldean Neo-Aramaic is closely related and sometimes classified together with Assyrian as "Suret". The language has official recognition in Iraq, and there are media, literature, and a popular music scene in Assyrian.
