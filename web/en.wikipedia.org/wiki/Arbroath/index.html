Arbroath (Scots: Aiberbrothock; Scottish Gaelic: Obar Bhrothaig) is a former royal burgh and the largest town in the council area of Angus, Scotland, with a population of about 23,500. It lies on the North Sea coast, some 16 miles (26 km) northeast of Dundee. The town is best known as the place where the Declaration of Arbroath, Scotland's famous letter of independence, was sent to the Pope in 1320.

History

Arbroath Abbey was founded in 1178 by William the Lion, who dedicated it to Saint Thomas Becket and was buried there in 1214. The abbey became one of the richest in Scotland. On 6 April 1320, during the Wars of Scottish Independence, the Scottish Parliament met at the abbey and sent the Declaration of Arbroath to Pope John XXII, asserting Scotland's independence and popular sovereignty — a document often credited as an influence on later declarations of independence.

The town developed as a fishing port and industrial centre for textiles, jute, and engineering. The Bell Rock Lighthouse, completed in 1811 by Robert Stevenson on a reef 11 miles offshore, is celebrated through the Signal Tower Museum in the town.

Arbroath smokie

The town's culinary speciality is the Arbroath smokie: a haddock that is salted, dried, and hot-smoked over hardwood in a traditional barrel, giving it a distinctive copper colour and flavour. It holds protected geographical indication (PGI) status, meaning genuine smokies must be produced within a defined area around the town. Legend holds that the dish originated in the nearby fishing village of Auchmithie after a barrel of salted fish caught fire.
