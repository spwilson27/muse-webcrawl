Keshena is a census-designated place in Menominee County, Wisconsin, United States, and the county seat of Menominee County. It is the administrative centre of the Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin and lies within the Menominee Indian Reservation.

Geography and community

Keshena is located in northeastern Wisconsin, along the Wolf River. The community is named after Keshena, a 19th-century chief of the Menominee people. Facilities in Keshena include the tribal government offices, the College of Menominee Nation, and the Menominee Casino Resort. The surrounding reservation is heavily forested and is known for its sustainably managed timberlands, managed by Menominee Tribal Enterprises.

Demographics

As of the 2020 census, Keshena had a population of roughly 1,250, the majority of whom are members of the Menominee tribe. Menominee County, which is coterminous with the reservation, is among the most impoverished counties in Wisconsin but also notable for its forest ecology and tribal self-governance, restored by the Menominee Restoration Act of 1973 after the tribe's federal recognition had been terminated in the 1950s and 1960s.
