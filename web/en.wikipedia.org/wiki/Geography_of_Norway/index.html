Geography of Norway

Location: Northern Europe
Area: 385,207 km² (incl. Svalbard)
Highest point: Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m)
Coastline: 100,915 km (with islands)

The geography of Norway is dominated by the Scandinavian Mountains and one of the longest and most rugged coastlines in the world, deeply indented by fjords. Norway occupies the western part of the Scandinavian Peninsula, shares borders with Sweden, Finland and Russia, and includes the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard and the island of Jan Mayen.

Terrain

The Scandinavian mountain chain runs the length of the country, with its highest peak, Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m), in the Jotunheimen range. Glaciers, including Jostedalsbreen — the largest glacier in mainland Europe — cap several high plateaus. Fjords such as the Geirangerfjord and Nærøyfjord (UNESCO World Heritage Sites) and the Sognefjord, Norway's longest and deepest fjord, cut far inland from the Atlantic coast. Finnmark in the northeast is a high, barren plateau extending above the Arctic Circle, where the North Cape marks the northernmost point of mainland Europe.

Climate

Despite its northern latitude, coastal Norway has a relatively mild climate for its latitude owing to the North Atlantic Current; ports remain ice-free in winter. Inland and northern areas have colder, more continental conditions, while the western coast is among the wettest places in Europe — Bergen receives over 2,000 mm of precipitation annually. Northern Norway experiences the midnight sun in summer and the polar night in winter.

Resources

Norway is a major producer of petroleum and natural gas from the North Sea, Norwegian Sea and Barents Sea, and generates nearly all its electricity from hydropower. Its waters are rich fishing grounds, and the country has significant deposits of iron ore, titanium and other minerals.
