Elephant Island is an ice-covered, mountainous island off the coast of Antarctica in the outer reaches of the South Shetland Islands, in the Southern Ocean. Its name, recorded by early sealers, is usually attributed either to its appearance or to sightings of elephant seals along its shores.

The Shackleton expedition

The island is best known as the refuge of Ernest Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition in 1916. After their ship Endurance was crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea, the crew reached Elephant Island in lifeboats in April 1916 — their first landfall in 497 days. Shackleton and five companions then sailed the lifeboat James Caird some 1,300 kilometres to South Georgia to fetch rescue, while the remaining 22 men, under Frank Wild, survived four and a half months at Point Wild, sheltering under upturned boats, until rescued on 30 August 1916. All survived.

Geography and wildlife

The island has no permanent population and no safe anchorage. It supports colonies of chinstrap and gentoo penguins, southern giant petrels, and haul-outs of Antarctic fur seals and southern elephant seals. A Brazilian Antarctic research refuge was formerly maintained at Point Wild.
