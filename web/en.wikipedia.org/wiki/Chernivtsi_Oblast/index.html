Chernivtsi Oblast (Ukrainian: Чернівецька область) is an oblast (province) in western Ukraine, located at the foothills of the Carpathian Mountains along the borders with Romania and Moldova. Its administrative centre is the historic city of Chernivtsi. It is the smallest oblast of Ukraine by area.

History

The territory corresponds largely to the northern part of the historical region of Bukovina, together with the area around Khotyn in historical Bessarabia. Bukovina belonged successively to Moldavia, the Ottoman sphere, the Austrian Empire (from 1775), and Romania (from 1918). In 1940, following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, northern Bukovina was annexed by the Soviet Union and the Chernivtsi Oblast was formed within the Ukrainian SSR.

Geography and population

The oblast is drained by the Dniester, Prut, and Siret rivers. It is ethnically diverse: Ukrainians form the majority, with significant Romanian, Moldovan, Russian, and other minorities, particularly in the southern districts. The region is known for the UNESCO-listed Residence of Bukovinian and Dalmatian Metropolitans in Chernivtsi and for the medieval fortress of Khotyn.
