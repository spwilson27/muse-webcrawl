President of Equatorial Guinea

The president of Equatorial Guinea is the head of state and head of government of the Republic of Equatorial Guinea. The country has had only two presidents since independence from Spain in 1968.

History

Francisco Macías Nguema, elected at independence, became an increasingly brutal dictator and was overthrown in 1979 by his nephew, Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo, who has held the presidency ever since — making him one of the longest-serving heads of state in the world. Obiang was initially chairman of the Supreme Military Council before assuming the presidential title.

Elections and term

Under the constitution the president is elected for a seven-year term. Elections have been regularly criticised by observers for irregularities, and Obiang has been re-elected with overwhelming official majorities. The Democratic Party of Equatorial Guinea is the dominant party.

Powers

The presidency wields extensive powers in an effectively authoritarian system, controlling the executive, influencing the judiciary, and commanding the armed forces. The prime minister handles day-to-day government administration.
