Prudhoe Bay is an area of Alaska's North Slope on the coast of the Arctic Ocean, best known as the site of the Prudhoe Bay Oil Field, the largest oil field in North America. The bay itself was named in 1826 by British explorer John Franklin after Captain Algernon Percy, Baron Prudhoe. The adjacent community, formally a census-designated place, consists almost entirely of oil-industry workers housed in large man-camp facilities.

Oil field

Oil was discovered at Prudhoe Bay on 12 March 1968 by ARCO and Exxon, with the confirmation well at nearby Sag River. The field contained an estimated 25 billion barrels of oil originally in place. Its remoteness — roughly 400 air miles north of Fairbanks and separated from tidewater by the Brooks Range — required construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, completed in 1977, which carries crude 800 miles south to the ice-free port of Valdez.

Production peaked in 1988 at about 2 million barrels per day, when Prudhoe Bay supplied roughly a quarter of US oil output, and has since declined to a fraction of that level, though enhanced recovery and satellite fields continue to extend its life. The adjacent Deadhorse airport and the Dalton Highway serve the field.

Environment and incidents

Prudhoe Bay lies within the Arctic coastal plain tundra, habitat for caribou, grizzly bears and migratory birds. In March 2006, a corrosion leak in a transit line spilled over 200,000 gallons of crude oil — the largest oil spill on the North Slope at the time — leading to fines, pipeline replacement and heightened scrutiny of operator BP.
