John Kenneth Galbraith

John Kenneth Galbraith (15 October 1908 – 29 April 2006) was a Canadian-American economist, public official and diplomat, and a leading figure of post-war Keynesian and institutional economics. His bestselling books reached a wide public readership.

John Kenneth Galbraith
Born: 15 Oct 1908, Ontario, Canada
Died: 29 Apr 2006, Cambridge, Massachusetts
Institutions: Harvard University
Posts: US Ambassador to India (1961–63)

Career

Born in Iona Station, Ontario, Galbraith studied agricultural economics, joined Harvard, administered US price controls in World War II, helped design postwar economic policy, and advised Democratic presidents from Roosevelt to Johnson, serving Kennedy as ambassador to India.

Major works

American Capitalism (1952) proposed countervailing power; The Affluent Society (1958) coined "conventional wisdom" and lamented private affluence amid public squalor; The New Industrial State (1967) analysed the technostructure of large corporations; and the BBC series The Age of Uncertainty (1977) popularised economic history.

Legacy

Critics disputed his claims about corporate planning and advertising's "dependence effect", but his literary style and institutional critique influenced generations of policymakers and readers.
