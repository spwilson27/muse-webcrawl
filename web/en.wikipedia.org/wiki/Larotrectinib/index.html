Larotrectinib, sold under the brand name Vitrakvi, is an anti-cancer drug used for the treatment of solid tumours that harbour a neurotrophic tyrosine receptor kinase (NTRK) gene fusion. It is a selective inhibitor of the tropomyosin receptor kinases TRKA, TRKB, and TRKC encoded by the NTRK1, NTRK2, and NTRK3 genes.

Larotrectinib was notable as one of the first "tissue-agnostic" cancer drugs: it was approved on the basis of the tumour's molecular alteration rather than the organ in which the cancer originated. It was developed by Loxo Oncology in collaboration with Bayer, and received approval from the United States Food and Drug Administration in November 2018.

Medical uses

Larotrectinib is indicated for the treatment of adults and children with solid tumours that have an NTRK gene fusion without a known acquired resistance mutation, that are either metastatic or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity, and that have no satisfactory alternative treatments or have progressed following treatment. Response rates in clinical trials were high — around 75–80% across a variety of tumour types — with many responses durable.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include fatigue, nausea, dizziness, vomiting, cough, constipation, diarrhoea, and increases in liver enzymes. Because TRK signalling is involved in nervous system function, neurological effects such as dizziness and paraesthesia can occur, and withdrawal pain has been reported on discontinuation. Resistance can develop through secondary NTRK kinase domain mutations, which motivated the development of next-generation inhibitors such as selitrectinib and repotrectinib.

History

The drug was discovered and developed by Loxo Oncology. The FDA granted it breakthrough therapy designation, and its approval in 2018 followed the pooled results of three basket trials (LOXO-TRK-14001, SCOUT, and NAVIGATE). Bayer acquired full marketing rights through its collaboration with Loxo, which was itself acquired by Eli Lilly in 2019.
