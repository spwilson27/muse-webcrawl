P-Funk

P-Funk (also spelled P Funk or P-Funk music) is a style of funk music and the name of the musical collective led by George Clinton, built around the bands Parliament and Funkadelic — collectively Parliament-Funkadelic. Emerging in the 1970s, P-Funk combined psychedelic rock, rhythm and blues, jazz and gospel with elaborate science-fiction mythology, stage spectacle and humour. The term is commonly said to stand for "Parliament-Funkadelic", "pure funk" or "Plainfield funk", after Clinton's home town of Plainfield, New Jersey.

Characteristics

P-Funk recordings are built on deep, syncopated bass lines — most famously those of Bootsy Collins — interlocking horn arrangements by Fred Wesley and Maceo Parker, synthesizer bass and keyboard textures from Bernie Worrell, and group chant vocals. Songs frequently dissolved into extended grooves and call-and-response refrains such as "We want the funk" and "Ain't no party like a P-Funk party".

Mythology

Clinton constructed an elaborate science-fiction narrative across the Parliament albums, in which Starchild, an alter ego arriving aboard the Mothership, does battle with Sir Nose D'Voidoffunk to bring funk to humanity. The mythology drew on Afrofuturist themes and culminated on stage with a full-size Mothership prop landing amid the band — a spectacle first staged on the 1976–77 Earth Tour and preserved in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Influence

P-Funk is one of the most sampled bodies of work in popular music. Its records underpin much West Coast hip hop and G-funk, and its approach influenced Prince, the Red Hot Chili Peppers and Outkast, among many others. Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and George Clinton and Parliament-Funkadelic received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019.
