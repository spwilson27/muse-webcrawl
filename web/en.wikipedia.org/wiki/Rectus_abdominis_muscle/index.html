Rectus abdominis muscle

Rectus abdominis
OriginPubic symphysis, pubic crest
InsertionXiphoid process, costal cartilages 5–7
NerveThoraco-abdominal nerves (T6–T11)
ActionsFlexion of trunk, compression of abdomen

The rectus abdominis muscle, also known as the "abdominal muscle" or simply the "abs", is a paired straight muscle situated on each side of the anterior wall of the human abdomen. It is separated from its fellow by the linea alba and is contained within the rectus sheath.

Structure

The muscle is crossed by three or four tendinous intersections, which give the muscle its segmented appearance — the "six-pack" visible in lean individuals. It arises from the pubic crest and symphysis and inserts on the xiphoid process and the cartilages of the fifth, sixth, and seventh ribs.

Function

The rectus abdominis flexes the lumbar spine and trunk, tilts the pelvis, and compresses the abdominal viscera, assisting in forced expiration, defecation, urination, and childbirth.
