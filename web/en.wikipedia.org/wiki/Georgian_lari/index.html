The lari (Georgian: ლარი; ISO code: GEL) is the currency of Georgia. It is divided into 100 tetri (თეთრი). The name lari derives from an old Georgian word for a hoard or property, while tetri ("white") recalls medieval Georgian silver coins.

History

After independence from the Soviet Union, Georgia used a transitional coupon currency that suffered hyperinflation. The lari was introduced on 2 October 1995 under President Eduard Shevardnadze, replacing the coupon at a rate of one million to one. It has remained a floating currency since, managed by the National Bank of Georgia. In 2014 the lari received an official symbol, ₾, based on the Georgian letter ლ.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 5, 10, 20 and 50 tetri and 1 and 2 lari. Banknotes are issued in 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 lari; a redesigned series introduced from 2016 depicts figures such as Queen Tamar, Ilia Chavchavadze, Ivane Javakhishvili and the writer and statesman Akaki Tsereteli, alongside Georgian cultural heritage imagery.
