Ableton Live, also known as Live or sometimes colloquially as "Ableton", is a digital audio workstation for macOS and Windows developed by the German company Ableton. In contrast to many other software sequencers, Live is designed to be an instrument for live performances as well as a tool for composing, recording, arranging, mixing, and mastering. It is also used by DJs, as it offers a suite of controls for beat-matching, crossfading, and other effects used in turntablism, and it was one of the first music applications created to automatically beat-match songs.

Live is available directly from Ableton in three editions: Intro (with limited key features), Standard, and Suite. The software is also bundled with the hardware controller Push, a dedicated instrument for song creation manufactured by Ableton.

History

Ableton was founded in 1999 in Berlin by Gerhard Behles and Robert Henke, who had worked together at the company Native Instruments. The first version of Live was released on 30 October 2001 as a purely audio-only application; MIDI support arrived with version 4 in 2004. The program's two main views, the Arrangement View and the Session View, were present from the beginning: the Arrangement View resembles a traditional timeline-based sequencer, while the Session View presents a grid of clips that can be triggered non-linearly, an approach well suited to improvisation and live performance.

Live 9, released in 2013, introduced the Push hardware controller. Live 10 followed in 2018 with the Wavetable synthesizer, and Live 11 in 2021 added comping and MPE support. Live 12, released in March 2024, introduced the Meld synthesizer, the Roar saturator, and generative MIDI tools.

Features

Live's Session View allows clips of audio and MIDI to be launched in any order, with each clip able to loop independently; scenes arrange clips horizontally so an entire row can be triggered at once. The software is known for its audio time-stretching algorithms ("warping"), which allow samples to be synchronised to a project tempo in real time. Instruments include Sampler, Simpler, Operator (FM synthesis), Wavetable, Drum Rack, and, in the Suite edition, integration with Max for Live, which lets users build custom instruments and effects using the Max/MSP environment.
