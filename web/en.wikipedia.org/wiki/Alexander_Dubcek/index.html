Alexander Dubček

Alexander Dubček
Born: 27 November 1921, Uhrovec, Czechoslovakia
Died: 7 November 1992 (aged 70), Prague
First Secretary of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia: 1968–1969
Known for: Prague Spring, "socialism with a human face"

Alexander Dubček (Slovak: [ˈaleksander ˈduptʂek]; 27 November 1921 – 7 November 1992) was a Czechoslovak and Slovak statesman who served as the First Secretary of the Presidium of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (KSČ) from January 1968 to April 1969. He attempted to reform the communist government during the Prague Spring, an effort that ended with the invasion of Czechoslovakia by the Warsaw Pact in August 1968.

Early life

Dubček was born in Uhrovec, grew up partly in Kyrgyzstan where his communist father helped build a cooperative, and joined the Slovak resistance against the Nazi-backed Slovak State during World War II.

Prague Spring

Succeeding Antonín Novotný in January 1968, Dubček presided over a liberalisation programme — "socialism with a human face" — loosening censorship, rehabilitating purge victims, encouraging free discussion and proposing federalisation of Czech and Slovak republics. The reforms alarmed Moscow. After failed negotiations, troops of the USSR and four Warsaw Pact allies invaded on 20–21 August 1968. Dubček was arrested and taken to Moscow, where he signed the Moscow Protocol accepting limited reversal of reforms.

Later life

Replaced by Gustáv Husák in April 1969, Dubček was expelled from the party in 1970 and worked in the Slovak forestry service in obscurity. He returned to prominence in the Velvet Revolution of 1989, becoming chairman of the Federal Assembly. He died in 1992 from injuries sustained in a car crash on the Prague–Brno motorway.
