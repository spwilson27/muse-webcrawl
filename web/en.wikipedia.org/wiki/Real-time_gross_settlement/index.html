Real-time gross settlement

Real-time gross settlement (RTGS) systems are specialist funds transfer systems where the transfer of money or securities takes place from one bank or lender to another on a "real-time" and on a "gross" basis. "Real-time" means the payment transaction is not subjected to any waiting period and is settled as soon as the payment is processed. "Gross" means the transaction is settled on a one-to-one basis without bundling or netting with any other transaction. Once processed, payments are final and irrevocable.

Overview

RTGS systems are typically operated by a country's central bank, because they are seen as a critical infrastructure for a country's economy. Payments are normally settled in central bank money, which eliminates settlement risk between participating institutions. RTGS is the fastest possible mechanism for transferring large amounts of money between banks.

Comparison with net settlement

In contrast to deferred net settlement systems, where payments accumulate and are offset against each other before a net amount is transferred at designated times, RTGS processes each payment individually and continuously. Net settlement exposes participants to the risk that a counterparty fails before settlement, whereas RTGS largely removes this systemic risk at the cost of requiring greater liquidity.

Examples

Well-known RTGS systems include Fedwire in the United States, TARGET2 (and its successor T2) in the euro area, CHAPS in the United Kingdom, and BOJ-NET in Japan. Many countries adopted RTGS during the 1990s and 2000s following recommendations from the Bank for International Settlements.
