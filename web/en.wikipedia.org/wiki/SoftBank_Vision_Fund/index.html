The SoftBank Vision Fund is a venture capital fund founded in 2017 by the Japanese conglomerate SoftBank Group. At its launch it was the world's largest technology-focused investment fund, raising approximately US$100 billion in committed capital. A successor vehicle, Vision Fund 2, was launched in 2019 and is funded primarily by SoftBank itself.

History

SoftBank founder Masayoshi Son announced the fund in 2016. Its major outside investors included Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (which committed about $45 billion), Abu Dhabi's Mubadala Investment Company, Apple, Foxconn, Qualcomm, and Sharp. The fund made very large minority investments — often hundreds of millions or billions of dollars — in late-stage technology companies, a strategy that reshaped venture capital by inflating round sizes and valuations across the industry.

Investments

Notable Vision Fund investments have included Uber, WeWork, DoorDash, ByteDance, Coupang, Grab, Paytm (One97 Communications), OYO Rooms, Slack, Arm Holdings, and the Indian e-commerce company Flipkart. The fund's bet on WeWork became one of its most notorious losses after the office-sharing company's failed 2019 IPO. Market downturns in 2022 produced record quarterly losses for the fund, prompting SoftBank to slow new investment sharply. Gains from Arm's 2023 Nasdaq listing and a recovery in technology valuations later improved results.

Vision Fund 2 and later vehicles

Vision Fund 2, announced in 2019, is financed largely from SoftBank's own balance sheet and has backed hundreds of smaller, earlier-stage companies, including many in artificial intelligence. SoftBank has also operated Latin America funds and, through SB Northstar and other vehicles, public-equity investments.
