Robert FitzRoy

Born: July 5, 1805, Ampton, Suffolk, England
Died: April 30, 1865, Upper Norwood, London
Service: Royal Navy; Vice-Admiral
Known for: Captain of HMS Beagle; founder of the Met Office

Robert FitzRoy (July 5, 1805 – April 30, 1865) was an English naval officer, hydrographer, and meteorologist. He captained HMS Beagle during Charles Darwin's famous voyage, served as Governor of New Zealand, and founded the Meteorological Office, issuing the first daily weather forecasts.

Beagle voyages

An aristocratic and brilliant but mercurial officer, FitzRoy took command of the Beagle in 1828 after her captain's suicide, completing a survey of South American waters. On the second voyage (1831–1836), fearing the loneliness that afflicted his predecessor, he sought a gentleman companion of scientific interests—and obtained the young Charles Darwin. FitzRoy's own meticulous surveying produced charts used into the twentieth century, and his Narrative of the voyage appeared alongside Darwin's Journal of Researches. Their relationship later cooled as FitzRoy, a devout Anglican, defended biblical literalism against Darwin's theory of evolution, publicly expressing regret for his role in the voyage at the 1860 Oxford debate.

New Zealand and Parliament

FitzRoy sat in Parliament for Durham and in 1843 became the second Governor of New Zealand, where his attempts to balance Māori land rights against settler demands during the Flagstaff War crisis made him enemies in both camps; he was recalled in 1845.

Meteorology

In 1854 FitzRoy was appointed head of a new government department to collect weather data at sea—the origin of the Met Office. After the wreck of the Royal Charter in a storm in 1859, he established a storm-warning system for ports and began publishing weather forecasts in The Times in 1861, popularizing the very word "forecast." His Weather Book (1863) synthesized his observations. Overworked, depressed, and fearful of the errors in his forecasts, he took his own life in 1865. The shipping forecast sea area "FitzRoy" (renamed from Finisterre in 2002) commemorates him.
