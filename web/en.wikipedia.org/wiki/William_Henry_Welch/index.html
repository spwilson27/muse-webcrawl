William Henry Welch

William Henry Welch (April 8, 1850 – April 30, 1934) was an American physician, pathologist, bacteriologist and medical-school administrator, known as the "Dean of American Medicine". He was one of the "Big Four" founding professors of the Johns Hopkins Hospital and School of Medicine, and the first dean of the Johns Hopkins School of Medicine.

Career

After graduating from Yale in 1870, Welch studied medicine at Columbia, receiving his MD in 1875, then spent several years in Germany studying pathology with Julius Cohnheim and bacteriology with Robert Koch's circle. Returning to New York, he established one of the first scientific pathology laboratories in the United States at Bellevue Hospital Medical College.

In 1884 he was recruited to Baltimore as the first professor of pathology at the new Johns Hopkins University. When the medical school opened in 1893, Welch became its first dean (1893–1898) and the hospital's first pathologist-in-chief. With William Osler, William Stewart Halsted and Howard Kelly he formed the "Big Four" whose portraits hang at Johns Hopkins. Welch's laboratory trained a generation of American medical scientists, and he discovered the gas-gangrene bacillus Bacillus aerogenes capsulatus, long named Clostridium welchii after him.

Institution building

Beyond Hopkins, Welch was the founding director of the Johns Hopkins School of Hygiene and Public Health (1916), helped shape the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research and the China Medical Board, and served as president of the American Medical Association and the National Academy of Sciences.
