Perry Smith (murderer)

Perry Smith

Born: October 27, 1928, Huntington, Nevada

Died: April 14, 1965 (aged 36), Lansing, Kansas

Cause of death: Execution by hanging

Conviction: Four counts of first-degree murder

Victims: The Clutter family

Perry Edward Smith (October 27, 1928 – April 14, 1965) was an American mass murderer who, with Richard Hickock, murdered the four members of the Clutter family in Holcomb, Kansas, on November 15, 1959. The crime became famous through Truman Capote's 1966 non-fiction novel In Cold Blood.

Early life

Smith was born in Nevada to rodeo performers; his childhood was marked by poverty, his mother's alcoholism and suicide, and periods in orphanages and detention. He served as a paratrooper in the Korean War, but a motorcycle accident left him with chronic leg pain and an addiction to aspirin and alcohol. He met Hickock while serving a prison sentence in Kansas for burglary.

The murders and execution

Acting on a tip that farmer Herbert Clutter kept a safe of cash (he did not), Smith and Hickock invaded the Clutter home and murdered Herbert, his wife Bonnie, and their children Nancy and Kenyon. Smith later confessed to killing all four. The pair were arrested in Las Vegas, convicted in 1960, and hanged at the Kansas State Penitentiary on April 14, 1965. Capote's portrayal of Smith as the more sensitive and remorseful of the two shaped public memory of the case.
