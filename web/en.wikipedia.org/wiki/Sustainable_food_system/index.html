Sustainable food system

A sustainable food system is a type of food system that provides healthy food to people while creating sustainable environmental, economic, and social systems that surround food production. Sustainable food systems start with the development of sustainable agricultural practices, more sustainable food distribution systems, the creation of sustainable diets, and the reduction of food waste throughout the system.

Local food networks are one element of sustainable food systems

Components

The concept spans the whole chain from inputs — seed, soil, water, and fertiliser — through farming, processing, transport, retail, and consumption, to disposal. Key concerns include greenhouse-gas emissions (food systems are estimated to produce roughly a third of anthropogenic emissions), biodiversity loss, freshwater use, soil degradation, and the social conditions of farm and food workers. Frameworks such as agroecology, organic farming, regenerative agriculture, and permaculture are commonly proposed production models.

Challenges

Analysts debate how to feed a growing population within planetary boundaries: proposed measures include shifting diets away from ruminant meat, halving food loss and waste, closing yield gaps, and internalising environmental costs in food prices. The UN Food and Agriculture Organization and the UN Food Systems Summit (2021) have made sustainable food systems a central policy theme, linked to the Sustainable Development Goals, especially Zero Hunger (SDG 2) and Responsible Consumption and Production (SDG 12).
