Tewkesbury

Country: England
County: Gloucestershire
District: Tewkesbury Borough
Population: approx. 10,700

Tewkesbury is a medieval market town and civil parish in the Borough of Tewkesbury in Gloucestershire, England. It stands at the confluence of the River Severn and the River Avon, approximately 10 miles north of Gloucester. The town is known for its well-preserved medieval and Tudor buildings and for Tewkesbury Abbey, one of the finest Norman churches in England.

History

The name Tewkesbury is thought to derive from Theoc, a Saxon hermit who founded a cell here in the 7th century. The town grew around its abbey, founded in the early 12th century. In 1471 the Battle of Tewkesbury, one of the decisive battles of the Wars of the Roses, was fought just south of the town; the Lancastrian army was defeated and Edward, Prince of Wales, was killed. Because of its position on the Severn, Tewkesbury was a significant inland port and milling centre, and it later became known for mustard, praised in Shakespeare's works. In July 2007 the town was severely affected by widespread flooding.

Landmarks

Tewkesbury Abbey, dedicated to St Mary, survived the Dissolution of the Monasteries in 1540 after local people purchased it from the Crown for use as their parish church. The town has one of the best-preserved medieval street patterns in England, with numerous alleyways and timber-framed buildings, including the Royal Hop Pole Hotel, mentioned in Dickens's The Pickwick Papers.

Events

The Tewkesbury Medieval Festival, held each July near the anniversary of the battle, is one of the largest medieval re-enactments in Europe. The town also hosts a mop fair each October.
