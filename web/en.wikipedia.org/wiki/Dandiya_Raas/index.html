Dandiya Raas

Dandiya Raas is a traditional folk dance from the Indian state of Gujarat, performed prominently during the Hindu festival of Navaratri. Dancers move in circles while striking together pairs of wooden sticks, called dandiyas, in rhythm with the music. Together with garba, it is one of the most popular forms of celebration during the nine nights of Navaratri.

Origins and symbolism

The dance is traditionally associated with the goddess Durga and her victory over the demon Mahishasura. The paired sticks are said to represent Durga's swords, and the dance dramatizes the battle between goddess and demon. It is related to the lezim dances of Maharashtra and to stick dances found across western India.

Performance

Men and women dressed in colourful traditional attire — embroidered chaniya cholis for women and kediyu for men — form concentric circles and strike sticks with their partners while moving clockwise or anticlockwise to the beat of dhol drums and folk songs. Rhythms range from simple two-beat patterns to complex sequences such as the six-beat hinch. Modern Navaratri celebrations in Gujarat and in Indian diaspora communities worldwide feature large organised dandiya events with live bands and recorded music.
