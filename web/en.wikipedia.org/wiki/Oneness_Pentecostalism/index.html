Oneness Pentecostalism

Oneness Pentecostalism (also known as Apostolic Pentecostalism, Jesus' Name Pentecostalism, or the Jesus Only movement) is a nontrinitarian movement within Protestant Christianity that emphasises the absolute oneness of God and the deity of Jesus Christ. It emerged in the United States in the years following the Azusa Street Revival, taking distinct shape around 1914–1916, and its adherents number in the tens of millions worldwide.

Doctrine

Oneness theology holds that God is absolutely one in person, and that "Father", "Son" and "Holy Spirit" are titles or manifestations of the one God, Jesus Christ being the full incarnation of that God. Against the doctrine of the Trinity, Oneness teachers argue that God is not a plurality of persons. The movement is often compared by critics to the ancient heresy of modalism (Sabellianism), an identification many Oneness theologians reject as imprecise.

Practice

The most distinctive practice is baptism in the name of Jesus only, based on the baptismal accounts in the Acts of the Apostles, rather than with the Trinitarian formula of Matthew 28:19. Oneness churches also teach the necessity of receiving the Holy Spirit with the evidence of speaking in tongues, following the pattern of Acts 2. Major Oneness bodies include the United Pentecostal Church International and the Assemblies of the World; the movement's 1916 split from the Assemblies of God is known as the "New Issue". Many Oneness congregations also maintain conservative holiness standards of dress and conduct.
