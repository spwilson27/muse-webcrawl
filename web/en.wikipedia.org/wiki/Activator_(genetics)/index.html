In genetics, an activator is a protein (a transcription factor) that binds to an enhancer or promoter region of DNA and increases the transcription of a nearby gene. Most activators work by recruiting or stabilising the binding of RNA polymerase and the general transcription machinery to the promoter, often through protein–protein interactions and chromatin remodelling.

An activator bound at an enhancer loops to the promoter to stimulate transcription by RNA polymerase.

Mechanism

Activators typically have a DNA-binding domain recognising a specific sequence and an activation domain that contacts components of the transcriptional machinery, such as mediator complex subunits or histone acetyltransferases that loosen chromatin. Some activators are themselves regulated by small molecules: in bacteria, the catabolite activator protein (CAP/CRP) activates the lac operon genes only when bound to cyclic AMP, signalling low glucose.

Examples

Well-studied activators include CAP in Escherichia coli, GAL4 in yeast (widely exploited in the GAL4/UAS experimental system), and the viral protein VP16, a potent activator whose activation domain is used in synthetic constructs. In multicellular eukaryotes, combinations of activators and repressors pattern gene expression during development.
