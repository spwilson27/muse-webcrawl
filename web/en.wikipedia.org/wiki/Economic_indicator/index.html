An economic indicator is a statistic about an economic activity that allows analysis of economic performance and predictions of future performance. Indicators are published by government agencies, central banks and private organisations, and are used by policymakers, businesses and investors to gauge the health of an economy and the stage of the business cycle.

Classification

Indicators are conventionally grouped by their timing relative to the business cycle:

  • Leading indicators change before the economy as a whole — for example, building permits, new orders for durable goods, consumer expectations, the yield curve and stock prices. Composite measures such as the Conference Board's Leading Economic Index combine several of them.
  • Coincident indicators move with the economy — for example, industrial production, personal income and employment in non-agricultural sectors.
  • Lagging indicators change after the economy has turned — for example, the unemployment rate, outstanding commercial loans and the consumer price index for services.

Major indicators

Among the most closely watched releases are gross domestic product (GDP), the unemployment rate, inflation measures such as the consumer price index (CPI), retail sales, housing starts and purchasing managers' indices (PMI). Because single indicators are noisy and frequently revised, analysts generally interpret them in combination.
