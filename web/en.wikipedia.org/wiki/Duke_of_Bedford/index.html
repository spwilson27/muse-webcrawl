Duke of Bedford (named after Bedford, England) is a title that has been created six times in the Peerage of England. The current holder is Andrew Russell, 15th Duke of Bedford. The Russell family has held land at Woburn since the 16th century, and the family's London estate in Bloomsbury produced place-names such as Russell Square, Bedford Square, and Tavistock Square.

History

The first creation was in 1414 for John of Lancaster, third son of Henry IV, who served as regent of France during the Hundred Years' War. Later creations in 1470 and 1485 became extinct. The Russells entered the peerage as Barons Russell in 1539 and became Earls of Bedford in 1551. William Russell, 5th Earl of Bedford, was created Duke of Bedford in 1694 after the Glorious Revolution.

Notable holders

John Russell, 4th Duke of Bedford (1710–1771), was a leading 18th-century statesman. John Russell, 1st Earl Russell, a younger son of the 6th Duke, twice served as Prime Minister. The philosopher Bertrand Russell was grandson of the Prime Minister and brother of the 13th Duke. The 13th Duke, John Russell, opened Woburn Abbey and its safari park to the public to pay death duties.
