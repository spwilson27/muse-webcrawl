X.509

Standard: ITU-T X.509
First published: 1988
Subject: Public key certificates

In cryptography, X.509 is an International Telecommunication Union (ITU) standard defining the format of public key certificates. X.509 certificates are used in many Internet protocols, including TLS/SSL, which underpins HTTPS, the secure protocol for browsing the web.

Structure

A certificate binds a public key to an identity (such as a hostname or an organisation) through the digital signature of a certificate authority (CA). It contains fields for the version, serial number, issuer, validity period, subject, subject public key info, and extensions such as Subject Alternative Names, encoded in ASN.1 and typically serialised in DER or PEM format.

Trust and revocation

Browsers and operating systems ship with lists of trusted root CAs, forming chains of trust to end-entity certificates. Revoked certificates are published in certificate revocation lists (CRLs) or queried via the Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP). The system's history includes notable failures and mis-issuances that have led to the distrust of compromised authorities.
