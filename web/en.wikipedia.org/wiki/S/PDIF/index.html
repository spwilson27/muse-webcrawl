S/PDIF

TypeDigital audio interconnect
Full nameSony/Philips Digital Interface
StandardIEC 60958 (consumer use)
ConnectorsRCA coaxial; TOSLINK optical
Derived fromAES3 (AES/EBU)

S/PDIF (Sony/Philips Digital Interface) is a type of digital audio interface used in consumer audio equipment to carry digital audio signals between components, typically over short distances. It is the consumer counterpart of the professional AES3 (AES/EBU) standard, with which it shares the same basic signal structure defined in IEC 60958.

Connectors and media

S/PDIF signals are carried either electrically over 75-ohm coaxial cable terminated with RCA connectors, or optically via the TOSLINK fibre-optic connector system developed by Toshiba. The optical variant is common on televisions, soundbars, game consoles, and AV receivers, and provides electrical isolation between devices, eliminating ground loops.

Capabilities

The interface carries two channels of uncompressed PCM audio (commonly up to 24-bit/192 kHz, though many implementations are limited to 48 or 96 kHz) plus status bits that can carry compressed surround formats such as Dolby Digital (AC-3) and DTS for decoding by an AV receiver. Because the clock is embedded in the signal using biphase mark code, signal quality depends on the receiver's clock recovery, a phenomenon popularly discussed as "jitter." S/PDIF does not support the lossless high-definition multichannel formats (Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio), which require HDMI; it also carries a form of SCMS copy-management information.

Applications

S/PDIF outputs appear on CD, DVD and Blu-ray players, MiniDisc decks, computers and sound cards, digital television receivers, and home-theatre equipment. It remains a common way to connect TVs to soundbars via the optical output.
