Aussiedler

Aussiedler (German for "resettlers"; singular the same) is a term in German law for ethnic Germans from Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union who immigrated to the Federal Republic of Germany under the right of return. Since 1993, newly arriving resettlers have formally been called Spätaussiedler ("late resettlers").

Legal basis

Article 116 of the German Basic Law (Grundgesetz) granted German citizenship to ethnic Germans expelled or fleeing from Eastern territories after the Second World War, and to their descendants. The Federal Expellees Law (Bundesvertriebenengesetz) of 1953 defined the status of Aussiedler: persons of German ethnic origin (deutsche Volkszugehörige) living in Eastern Europe or the Soviet Union who wished to take up residence in Germany.

Waves of immigration

Numbers were small for decades because Eastern Bloc states restricted emigration: roughly 1.4 million ethnic Germans arrived between 1950 and 1987, mainly from Poland and Romania. After 1987, liberalisation under Gorbachev and the collapse of communism triggered a surge — nearly 400,000 arrived in 1989 and a similar number in 1990, now largely from the Soviet Union (Russia Germans and Kazakhstan Germans). Between 1950 and the 2010s, more than 4.5 million Aussiedler and Spätaussiedler settled in Germany.

Restriction and integration

The flood prompted legislative tightening: the 1990 Resettler Admission Act, and from 1993 the War Consequences Compensation Act, which capped annual admissions (around 220,000 initially, later effectively lower), required proof of ethnic German descent and basic German language ability, and imposed a language test from 1996. Integration challenges included unrecognised qualifications, limited German proficiency among later arrivals from the former USSR, and concentration in particular housing estates. Reception processing was centred on camps such as Friedland and later Bramsche.
