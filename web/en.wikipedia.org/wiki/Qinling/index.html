The Qinling (Chinese: 秦岭; pinyin: Qínlǐng) or Qin Mountains are a major east–west mountain range in southern Shaanxi Province, China. The mountains stretch for about 1,500 kilometres from Gansu in the west to Henan in the east, forming the watershed between the Yellow River basin to the north and the Yangtze River basin to the south. Because the range blocks the cold dry air of the north from reaching the subtropical south, it is regarded as the principal geographical dividing line between northern and southern China, in climate, agriculture, dialect, and culture.

Geography

The Qinling forms the eastern continuation of the Kunlun Mountains. Its western portion is higher and drier, while the central and eastern parts receive more rainfall. The highest peak is Mount Taibai, at 3,767 metres the highest point in eastern China. Major passes and plank roads historically connected the Wei River valley around Xi'an with the Han River valley and Sichuan to the south, including the famous Baoxie Road and the routes chronicled in the era of the Three Kingdoms.

Climate and ecology

The Qinling marks the boundary between the temperate zone of the north and the subtropical zone of the south; the January 0 °C isotherm and the 800 mm rainfall line run roughly along it. North of the range, wheat and millet are staples, while rice dominates to the south. The mountains' mixed forests are among China's most biodiverse temperate habitats. The Qinling is home to the Qinling panda, a subspecies of the giant panda, as well as golden snub-nosed monkeys, takins, crested ibises, and golden pheasants. Several nature reserves, including Changqing and Foping, protect this wildlife.

History

The range takes its name from the Qin, the state that unified China under Qin Shi Huang in 221 BCE, whose heartland lay in the Wei valley north of the mountains. For centuries the Qinling was a formidable barrier defended by passes such as Tong Pass in the east, and the ancient plank roads cut into its cliffs were marvels of engineering.
