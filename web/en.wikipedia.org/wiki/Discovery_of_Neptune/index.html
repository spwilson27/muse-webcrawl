Discovery of Neptune

Discovery of Neptune
Date: 23–24 September 1846
Observers: Johann Galle, Heinrich d'Arrest (Berlin)
Predictors: Urbain Le Verrier; John Couch Adams
Related: Neptune, Uranus

The planet Neptune was discovered on 23 September 1846, within a degree of a position predicted mathematically from irregularities in the orbit of Uranus — a triumph of celestial mechanics.

Predictions

French mathematician Urbain Le Verrier and English mathematician John Couch Adams independently calculated where an unseen planet should be. Le Verrier sent his coordinates to Berlin Observatory.

Observation

Astronomer Johann Gottfried Galle, assisted by Heinrich Louis d'Arrest, found Neptune the first night of searching, using a new star chart to spot a star that was not on the map.

Credit dispute

The near-simultaneous predictions sparked a Franco-British priority dispute; modern consensus credits both men, with Le Verrier's published prediction directly leading to the telescopic discovery. Galileo had recorded Neptune as a 'star' in 1612–13 without recognizing it as a planet.
