The Ellora Caves are a multi-religious rock-cut cave complex with inscriptions dating from the 6th century CE onwards, located in the Aurangabad District of Maharashtra, India. There are over 100 caves at the site, all excavated out of the vertical face of the Charanandri hills, 34 of which are open to public. These consist of 17 Hindu (caves 13–29), 12 Buddhist (caves 1–12), and 5 Jain (caves 30–34) caves, built between the 6th and 11th centuries. The proximity of the monuments of three different religions demonstrates the religious harmony and spirit of tolerance that was prevalent during this period in India.

The caves were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

Location

The Ellora caves are located in the Indian state of Maharashtra about 29 kilometres (18 miles) north-west of the city of Aurangabad, and about 300 kilometres (190 miles) east-northeast of Mumbai. Ellora occupies a relatively flat rocky region of the Western Ghats, where ancient volcanic activity had formed layered basalt formations, called Deccan Traps. The volcanic activity that formed the west-facing cliff at Ellora occurred during the Cretaceous period.

The Hindu caves

The Hindu caves were constructed during the Kalachuri period, from the mid-6th century to the end of the 8th century in two phases. Caves 28, 27 and 19 were the earliest excavated. These were followed by caves 29 and 21, which were excavated along with a flight of stairs. Caves 20 and 26 were built during the Rashtrakuta period, followed by cave 17.

Kailasa temple (Cave 16)

Of all the cave temples in Ellora, the central attraction is the Kailasa temple (Cave 16), which is the largest rock-cut Hindu temple in the world. It features the largest single monolithic rock excavation in the world. The temple covers an area twice the size of the Parthenon in Athens, and was built by excavating approximately 400,000 tons of rock. It was built under the direction of the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I (c. 756–773 CE), and is dedicated to the Hindu god Shiva, representing Mount Kailasa, Shiva's Himalayan home.

The Buddhist caves

The Buddhist caves were the earliest structures at Ellora, created between the 5th and 8th centuries. These consisted of twelve caves (caves 1–12). They were built between 630 and 700 CE. These caves should be considered in two groups: an earlier group of caves 1–5 and a later group of caves 6–12.

The Jain caves

The five Jain caves at Ellora belong to the 9th and 10th centuries. They all belong to the Digambara sect. Jain caves reveal specific dimensions of Jain philosophy and tradition. They reflect a strict sense of asceticism – they are not relatively large compared to others, but they present exceptionally detailed art works. The most remarkable Jain shrines are the Chhota Kailash (Cave 30), the Indra Sabha (Cave 32) and the Jagannath Sabha (Cave 33).
