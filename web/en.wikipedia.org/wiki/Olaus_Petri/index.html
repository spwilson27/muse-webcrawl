Olaus Petri

Olaus Petri (1493–1552), born Olof Pettersson, was a Swedish clergyman, writer, and a central figure of the Protestant Reformation in Sweden. Working with his brother Laurentius Petri, the first Lutheran archbishop of Sweden, he helped establish the Swedish Church as a national Lutheran body under King Gustav Vasa.

Career

Educated at the University of Wittenberg, where he encountered the teachings of Martin Luther, Olaus returned to Sweden and became chancellor to Gustav Vasa. He translated the New Testament into Swedish (1526) and authored chronicles, plays, and theological works that shaped the Swedish literary language.

Conflict and legacy

His criticism of the king's seizure of church property led to a death sentence in 1540, later commuted. A statue of Olaus Petri stands outside Stockholm Cathedral, where he served as pastor.
