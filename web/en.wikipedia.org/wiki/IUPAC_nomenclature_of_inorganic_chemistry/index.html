IUPAC nomenclature of inorganic chemistry

The IUPAC nomenclature of inorganic chemistry is a systematic method of naming inorganic chemical compounds, as recommended by the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC). The recommendations are published in Nomenclature of Inorganic Chemistry, commonly known as the Red Book. Ideally, every inorganic compound should have a name from which an unambiguous formula can be determined.

Systematic names

Compositional nomenclature builds names from the elements present, using multiplicative prefixes (mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, etc.) and suffixes such as -ide for anions: for example, NaCl is sodium chloride and Fe₂O₃ is diiron trioxide, though the Stock system, which indicates oxidation states with Roman numerals — iron(III) oxide — is widely used for transition metal compounds. Additive nomenclature, used for coordination compounds, lists ligands alphabetically before the central atom, e.g. pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride.

Acids, salts and hydrides

The Red Book gives rules for naming oxoacids and their salts (such as sulfuric acid and sodium sulfate, systematically tetraoxidosulfuric acid and related forms), hydrides (parent hydrides such as silane and phosphane), and retained traditional names for a small set of common compounds such as water and ammonia.

History

IUPAC first published inorganic nomenclature rules in 1940, with major revisions in 1957, 1971, 1990 and 2005. The 2005 Red Book (prepared under the editorship of Neil G. Connelly and Ture Damhus) is the current comprehensive version.
