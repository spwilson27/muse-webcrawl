The Treaty of Teschen (German: Frieden von Teschen) was signed on 13 May 1779 in Teschen (now Cieszyn/Český Těšín) between Austria and Prussia, ending the War of the Bavarian Succession — the bloodless "Potato War" fought over the succession to the Electorate of Bavaria after the death of Maximilian III Joseph in 1777.

Terms

Austria abandoned most of its claims to Bavarian territory, returning the land it had occupied, but retained the Innviertel, the strip of Bavaria east of the Inn river (with Braunau), which remains part of Austria today. In compensation for other claims, Austria paid six million florins to Saxony, whose elector's dynastic claims were also recognised. Prussia's rights of succession to the Franconian margraviates of Ansbach and Bayreuth were confirmed. Russia and France acted as mediators and guarantors — a notable step in Russia's emergence as a guarantor of the imperial German constitution.

Significance

The treaty preserved the Austro-Prussian dualism within the Holy Roman Empire and confirmed that neither power could rearrange Germany at the other's expense. The Innviertel was briefly restored to Bavaria in 1809–1816 but returned to Austria at the Congress of Vienna.
