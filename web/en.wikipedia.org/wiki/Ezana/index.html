Ezana (Ge'ez: ዒዛና) was a ruler of the Kingdom of Aksum in present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea during the fourth century AD. He is best known as the first Aksumite king to embrace Christianity, making Aksum one of the earliest states in the world to adopt the Christian faith, and for his military campaigns, which extended Aksumite power into Nubia.

Conversion to Christianity

Ezana was tutored as a boy by Frumentius, a Christian from Tyre who had risen to a position of influence at the Aksumite court and who later became the first bishop of Aksum. Ezana's conversion is dramatically visible in his coinage: his early coins bear the South Arabian disc-and-crescent symbol, while his later issues replace it with the Christian cross — among the earliest appearances of the cross on coinage anywhere.

Inscriptions and campaigns

Ezana left a series of royal inscriptions in Ge'ez, Sabaean and Greek recording his victories. His inscriptions describe campaigns against the Beja, the Noba, and other peoples, and a major expedition down the Nile against the declining kingdom of Meroë (Kush) around AD 350, commemorated on a stele found at Meroë itself. In his inscriptions he styled himself "king of kings" and ruler of Aksum, Himyar, Raydan, Ethiopia, Saba, Salhen, Tsiyamo, Beja and Kush.

Legacy

Under Ezana, Aksum reached the height of its power as a trading empire linking the Roman world with Arabia and India through the Red Sea port of Adulis. Ezana and his brother Saizana are venerated as saints in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.
