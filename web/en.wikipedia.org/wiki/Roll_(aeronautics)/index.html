Roll (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, roll is the rotational movement of an aircraft about its longitudinal axis — the axis running from the nose to the tail. Together with pitch (rotation about the lateral axis) and yaw (rotation about the vertical axis), roll describes the three-dimensional attitude of an aircraft in flight.

Control

Roll is controlled primarily by the ailerons, surfaces on the trailing edges of the wings that deflect in opposite directions: one wing's lift increases while the other's decreases, producing a rolling moment. On some aircraft, spoilers assist the ailerons, and large aircraft may use differential thrust in emergencies. The resulting bank angle causes the lift vector to tilt, which is what actually turns a fixed-wing aircraft; coordinated flight requires the pilot to combine roll with rudder input to counter adverse yaw.

Roll rate and manoeuvres

Roll rate is usually expressed in degrees per second. High-performance aerobatic and military aircraft can roll at several hundred degrees per second. Named manoeuvres built on roll include the barrel roll, the aileron roll, the snap roll and the slow roll. In spacecraft and missiles, roll control is achieved with reaction control thrusters or thrust vectoring.
