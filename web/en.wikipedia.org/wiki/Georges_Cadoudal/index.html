Georges Cadoudal (1 January 1771 – 25 June 1804) was a Breton counter-revolutionary and leader of the Chouans during the French Revolution. A committed royalist, he led insurgent forces in Brittany against the Republic and later plotted the assassination of First Consul Napoleon Bonaparte, for which he was executed.

Early life

Cadoudal was born in Brech, near Auray in Brittany, the son of a miller. Loyal to the monarchy and the Catholic Church, he joined the counter-revolutionary rising in the west of France after the execution of Louis XVI in 1793.

The Chouannerie

He became one of the principal leaders of the Chouannerie, the royalist insurrection in Brittany and Normandy. A physically imposing and charismatic commander, he repeatedly refused amnesties and reconciliations that other Chouan leaders accepted. After the peace negotiations of 1800 he travelled to London, where he received support from the British government and the comte d'Artois, the future Charles X.

Conspiracy and execution

In August 1803 Cadoudal landed secretly in France as part of a conspiracy, involving generals Charles Pichegru and Jean Victor Moreau, to assassinate Bonaparte and restore the Bourbons. The plot was uncovered by the police; Cadoudal was arrested in Paris in March 1804 after a dramatic chase. He was tried, refused to plead for mercy, and was guillotined on 25 June 1804, reportedly declaring his devotion to the royal cause to the end.

Legacy

Cadoudal remains a hero figure in Breton royalist memory; Charles X posthumously ennobled his family, and he was made a Marshal of France posthumously by Louis XVIII. His name is commemorated in Brittany, including the commune near his birthplace renamed in his honour.
