The SIS Building, also known as the MI6 Building or Vauxhall Cross, is the headquarters of the British Secret Intelligence Service (SIS, commonly known as MI6). It stands at 85 Albert Embankment in Vauxhall, on the south bank of the River Thames in London, beside Vauxhall Bridge.

Design and construction

Designed by architect Terry Farrell and completed in 1994, the building is a postmodern structure of stepped, ziggurat-like terraces clad in cream and green stone and glass, with references to 1930s architecture, Aztec and Mayan temples, and the great London power stations. Beneath the flamboyant exterior lies a heavily protected government building: its defences reportedly include thick walls, blast protection, and extensive below-ground levels, and the design allows the building's facilities to keep functioning independently in an emergency.

The government bought the site in the 1980s after SIS had been dispersed among several London buildings, and the service moved in during 1994 from its previous home at Century House, Lambeth. The actual construction cost was kept secret on security grounds.

In popular culture

Uniquely for a real intelligence headquarters, the building has repeatedly played itself in the James Bond films — as MI6 headquarters in GoldenEye (1995) and subsequent films, exploding in the plot of Skyfall (2012) and being attacked again in Spectre (2015). In September 2000 the real building was struck by a Russian-made anti-tank rocket fired by dissident Irish republicans; the attack caused only superficial damage.
