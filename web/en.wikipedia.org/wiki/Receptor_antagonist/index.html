Receptor antagonist

A receptor antagonist is a type of receptor ligand or drug that blocks or dampens a biological response by binding to a receptor rather than provoking a response like an agonist. Antagonist drugs interfere in the natural operation of receptor proteins: they are sometimes called blockers, as in beta blockers, calcium channel blockers and angiotensin receptor blockers.

Types

Competitive antagonists bind reversibly to the same site on the receptor as the endogenous ligand, and their effect can be overcome by increasing the concentration of agonist. Non-competitive antagonists bind to a separate (allosteric) site or irreversibly to the active site, so increasing agonist concentration cannot fully restore the response. A silent antagonist occupies the receptor with no intrinsic activity, while an inverse agonist reduces the basal, constitutive activity of the receptor below baseline.

Pharmacology

Antagonists are described by their affinity for the receptor (often expressed as a dissociation constant or pA2 value) and by their lack of efficacy. Many widely used medicines are antagonists, including naloxone at opioid receptors, atenolol at beta-adrenergic receptors, losartan at angiotensin II type 1 receptors, and naltrexone.
