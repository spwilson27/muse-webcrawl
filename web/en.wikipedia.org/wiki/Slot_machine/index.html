Slot machine

A slot machine (American English), known variously as a fruit machine (British English), puggy (Scottish English), the slots (Canadian English and American English), poker machine/pokies (Australian English and New Zealand English), or gaming machine, is a casino gambling machine with three or more reels which spin when a button is pushed. Slot machines are also known pejoratively as one-armed bandits, alluding to the large mechanical levers affixed to the sides of early machines and to the machines' ability to empty players' pockets and wallets as thieves would.

History

The first modern slot machine was developed in the late 19th century. Charles August Fey of San Francisco built the Liberty Bell machine in 1894–1895; it had three spinning reels and paid out up to 50 cents when three bells lined up. Because cash payouts were restricted in many places, early machines often dispensed prizes such as drinks, cigars, or gum — the origin of fruit symbols and the "BAR" symbol, derived from the logo of the Bell-Fruit Gum Company.

Electromechanical machines appeared in the 1960s, notably Bally's Money Honey (1963), which allowed much larger payouts. From the late 1970s onwards, video slots using computer random number generators replaced mechanical reels, and microprocessors allowed machines to "weight" symbols so that losing combinations appear more often than the physical reels would suggest.

Operation

Modern slot machines are controlled by computers. When a player inserts money or a ticket and presses a button or pulls a lever, a random number generator (RNG) determines the outcome instantly; the spinning reels or video display are an entertaining presentation of that predetermined result. Each symbol combination pays according to a pay table, and the machine's long-run return to player (RTP) is set by the weighting of symbols and the pay table, typically between about 75% and 98%.

Terminology

Traditional mechanical slots have three reels, each with symbols such as fruits, bells, lucky sevens and bars. Video slots may have five or more "reels" and dozens or hundreds of paylines. Progressive slots contribute a fraction of each bet to a shared jackpot that grows until won. Bonus rounds and free-spin features are common in contemporary machines.

Regulation and criticism

Slot machines are heavily regulated in most jurisdictions, with minimum payout percentages and testing of RNGs. Critics argue that rapid play, near-miss programming and immersive design features contribute to problem gambling; slots account for a large share of gambling revenue in many markets and a disproportionate share of gambling addiction cases.
