Air Force Global Strike Command (AFGSC) is a major command of the United States Air Force, headquartered at Barksdale Air Force Base, Louisiana. It is responsible for the Air Force's entire intercontinental ballistic missile force and its long-range bomber force, providing the air and land legs of the United States nuclear triad in support of US Strategic Command.

History

The command was activated on 7 August 2009, following nuclear surety failures in 2007–2008 — including the accidental transport of nuclear-armed cruise missiles on a B-52 and the mistaken shipment of nuclear fuses to Taiwan — which led the Air Force to consolidate all nuclear-capable bomber and missile forces under a single command. The Eighth Air Force (bombers) transferred to AFGSC in early 2010, followed by the Twentieth Air Force (ICBMs).

Forces

AFGSC's subordinate units include the Eighth Air Force, operating the B-1B Lancer, B-2 Spirit and B-52H Stratofortress fleets, and the Twentieth Air Force, operating approximately 400 Minuteman III ICBMs across missile fields in Montana, North Dakota and Wyoming. The command also oversees the 377th Air Base Wing at Kirtland AFB and other supporting units, with around 33,000 personnel assigned.

Modernisation

The command is fielding major replacements for its Cold War systems: the B-21 Raider stealth bomber, the LGM-35 Sentinel ICBM to replace Minuteman III, and the AGM-181 Long Range Stand Off nuclear cruise missile.
