Birthday party

TypeSocial celebration
OccasionAnniversary of a person's birth
Typical elementsBirthday cake with candles, gifts, singing "Happy Birthday", party games

A birthday party is a party held to celebrate the anniversary of the birth of a person, typically the guest of honour. Such parties are especially common for children in many cultures, though milestone birthdays — such as 18th, 21st, 30th, 50th, and 100th birthdays — are often marked with larger celebrations for adults as well.

Customs

Common features include a birthday cake decorated with the guest of honour's name and candles representing their age (or a single candle), which the celebrant blows out after making a silent wish while guests sing "Happy Birthday to You" — written in 1893 by sisters Patty and Mildred J. Hill as "Good Morning to All" and long one of the most recognised songs in the English language. Guests typically bring wrapped gifts, and parties for children often feature games such as pin the tail on the donkey, musical chairs, and piñatas, as well as party favours and goody bags for attendees.

History

Celebrations of birthdays have roots in ancient customs of protecting the celebrant from evil spirits on the day their fortune was thought to change; the ancient Greeks offered moon-shaped cakes with candles to Artemis, goddess of the Moon. In medieval and early modern Europe, birthday observances were mainly for royalty and saints' days; birthday parties for ordinary children (Kinderfeste) developed in Germany and spread during the 19th and 20th centuries with rising prosperity.

Around the world

Cultures mark coming-of-age birthdays in distinctive ways, including the Latin American quinceañera for a girl's 15th birthday, the Jewish bar and bat mitzvah, and the North American "sweet sixteen". In China, longevity noodles are eaten for elders' birthdays; in Korea, seaweed soup (miyeok-guk) is traditional birthday food.
