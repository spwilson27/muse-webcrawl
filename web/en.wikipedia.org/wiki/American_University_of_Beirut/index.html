American University of Beirut

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The American University of Beirut (AUB; Arabic: الجامعة الأميركية في بيروت) is a private, non-sectarian, and independent university chartered in New York with its campus in Beirut, Lebanon. It was founded in 1866 as the Syrian Protestant College by American missionaries Daniel and Abby Bliss, and renamed the American University of Beirut in 1920.

Established: 1866
Type: Private university
Location: Bliss Street, Beirut, Lebanon
President: Fadlo R. Khuri (since 2016)
Students: about 9,000

History

The university opened on December 3, 1866, with sixteen students. Its campus occupies a 61-acre site overlooking the Mediterranean Sea in the Ras Beirut district. The institution's medical center, founded in 1867, grew into a leading regional teaching hospital. AUB remained open through the First and Second World Wars, the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), and the financial crisis of 2019, though president Malcolm H. Kerr was assassinated in 1984 during the civil war.

Academics

AUB comprises seven faculties: Agricultural and Food Sciences; Arts and Sciences; Engineering and Architecture; Health Sciences; Medicine; Nursing; and the Suliman S. Olayan School of Business. Instruction is in English. The university is widely ranked among the top universities in the Arab world.

Notable alumni

Alumni include numerous prime ministers and heads of state across the Middle East, as well as figures such as architect Zaha Hadid, philosopher and diplomat Charles Malik, and writer and activist Philip Khuri Hitti, the university's first Muslim graduate.
