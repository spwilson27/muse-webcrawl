Polyploid

Polyploidy is the condition of a cell or organism having more than two complete sets of chromosomes. Organisms with three sets are triploid, four tetraploid, and so on. Polyploidy is very common in plants, occurs in some animals (especially fishes, amphibians, and invertebrates), and is rare and usually lethal in mammals.

Chromosome sets in haploid, diploid, and polyploid cells.

Types

Autopolyploidy arises from genome doubling within one species, typically through unreduced gametes or errors in mitosis or meiosis. Allopolyploidy combines the genomes of two species through hybridisation followed by chromosome doubling, which restores fertility to the hybrid. Bread wheat is a natural allohexaploid combining three ancestral genomes.

Significance

Polyploidy is a major mechanism of plant evolution and speciation, since a new polyploid may be instantly reproductively isolated from its parents. Many crops are polyploid — wheat, cotton, potato, coffee, sugarcane, and strawberry among them — and polyploid plants often show larger cells and organs ("gigas" effects), exploited in breeding; seedless watermelons and bananas are triploid. Ancient genome duplications also occurred in the ancestry of vertebrates and teleost fish. In humans, triploidy and tetraploidy occur in a small percentage of conceptions and almost always result in miscarriage.
