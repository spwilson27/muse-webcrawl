Luhn algorithm

The Luhn algorithm, also known as the modulus 10 or mod 10 algorithm, is a simple check-digit formula used to validate a variety of identification numbers, most famously payment card numbers. It was created by IBM scientist Hans Peter Luhn, described in a patent filed in 1954 and granted in 1960, and is now specified in ISO/IEC 7812-1.

Description

To verify a number against its included check digit:

  1. Starting from the rightmost digit (the check digit), move left and double the value of every second digit.
  2. If doubling a digit gives a two-digit number, add its digits (equivalently, subtract 9).
  3. Sum all the resulting digits together with the undoubled digits.
  4. The number is valid if the total is a multiple of 10.

Strengths and weaknesses

The algorithm detects any single-digit error and almost all transpositions of adjacent digits, except the pair 09 ↔ 90. It is not a cryptographic function and cannot protect against deliberate fraud; it is designed only to catch accidental errors in transcription or data entry. It is used for credit and debit card numbers, IMEI numbers, and various national identification numbers.

Example

The classic test number 79927398713 sums to 70 under the algorithm, a multiple of 10, so it is valid; its final digit 3 is the check digit for the payload 7992739871.
