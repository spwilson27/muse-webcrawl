Arrhythmia

An arrhythmia, also known as cardiac arrhythmia or heart arrhythmia, is a group of conditions in which the heartbeat is irregular, too fast, or too slow.

Arrhythmia
SpecialtyCardiology / electrophysiology
SymptomsPalpitations, dizziness, fainting, shortness of breath, or none
DiagnosisECG, Holter monitor

Classification

A heart rate below 60 bpm is bradycardia; above 100 bpm is tachycardia. Types include atrial fibrillation — the most common serious arrhythmia — atrial flutter, supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, which causes cardiac arrest.

Causes

Causes include coronary artery disease, heart failure, electrolyte imbalances, thyroid disorders, alcohol, caffeine and certain drugs. Diagnosis uses electrocardiography, ambulatory monitoring and electrophysiology studies.

Treatment

Treatments include lifestyle changes, antiarrhythmic medication, anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation, cardioversion, catheter ablation, pacemakers and implantable defibrillators.
