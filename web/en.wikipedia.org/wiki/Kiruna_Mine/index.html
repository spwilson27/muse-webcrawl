Kiruna Mine

The Kiruna Mine (Swedish: Kirunavaara) is the largest and most modern underground iron ore mine in the world, located in Kiruna in northern Sweden and operated by the state-owned company LKAB. The orebody is a massive magnetite-apatite deposit about 4 km long and up to 2 km deep.

Operations

Mining began in the early 20th century, moving underground as open pits deepened; the mine uses sublevel caving with largely automated trains and hoisting. It produces tens of millions of tonnes of crude ore annually, railed to the ports of Narvik and Luleå.

City relocation

Because mining-induced subsidence threatens the town of Kiruna, LKAB and the municipality are relocating the city centre several kilometres east — one of the largest urban relocation projects ever undertaken, including moving the historic Kiruna Church.
