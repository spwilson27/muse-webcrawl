Aranyaka

The Aranyakas (Sanskrit: आरण्यक, āraṇyaka) are a part of the Vedas, the ancient sacred scriptures of Hinduism. Their name means "forest texts" or "wilderness texts", reflecting their association with study and meditation in the forest, away from settled life. They form a layer of the Vedic corpus between the Brahmanas and the Upanishads.

Content

The Aranyakas discuss the symbolism and inner meaning of rituals described in the Brahmanas, offering philosophical interpretations and instructions for rites considered too esoteric or dangerous for ordinary village settings. They mark a transition from the ritualism of the earlier texts toward the speculative philosophy of the Upanishads, which often appear as their concluding portions. Major Aranyakas include the Aitareya Aranyaka and the Taittiriya Aranyaka; the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad — "the great forest text" — is the concluding part of the Shatapatha Brahmana tradition.

Place in the Vedic corpus

Each Veda is traditionally divided into Samhita (hymns and mantras), Brahmana (ritual commentary), Aranyaka (forest texts) and Upanishad (philosophical teachings). The Aranyakas are associated with the vanaprastha, or forest-dweller, stage of life in the ashrama system.
