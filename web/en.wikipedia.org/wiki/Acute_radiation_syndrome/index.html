Acute radiation syndrome

Acute radiation syndrome (ARS), also known as radiation sickness or radiation poisoning, is a collection of health effects caused by exposure of most or all of the body to a high dose of penetrating ionizing radiation over a short period of time, usually minutes.

Presentation

ARS classically proceeds through a prodromal phase (nausea, vomiting, fatigue), a latent phase of apparent recovery, and a manifest illness phase. Depending on dose, the dominant form may be hematopoietic (bone marrow failure, roughly 0.7–10 Gy), gastrointestinal (roughly 10–50 Gy), or cardiovascular/central nervous system (above about 50 Gy), the last being invariably fatal. The median lethal dose without treatment (LD50/60) is around 4–5 gray.

Causes and treatment

Cases have followed nuclear weapons use at Hiroshima and Nagasaki, reactor accidents such as Chernobyl, criticality accidents and lost radiotherapy or industrial sources. Treatment is supportive: decontamination, antiemetics, infection control, blood products and hematopoietic growth factors, with bone marrow transplantation in selected cases.
