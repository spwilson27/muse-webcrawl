Fantasy Records

Fantasy Records is an American independent record label company founded by brothers Max and Sol Stanley Weiss in 1949 in San Francisco, California. It is best known as a major jazz label and, later, as the original label of the rock band Creedence Clearwater Revival.

History

Fantasy began as a jazz label, releasing records by Dave Brubeck, who was among its earliest successes, as well as Gerry Mulligan, Chet Baker, and Vince Guaraldi, whose "Cast Your Fate to the Wind" and Jazz Impressions of A Boy Named Charlie Brown were hits for the label. In the 1950s the company also operated the Galaxy Records subsidiary.

In 1967 the label was purchased by Saul Zaentz and partners. Under Zaentz, Fantasy signed the Berkeley band the Golliwogs, who renamed themselves Creedence Clearwater Revival and became one of the most successful American rock bands of the late 1960s and early 1970s. The band's relationship with Zaentz and the label later became famously acrimonious over royalties and copyright control.

Expansion and catalogues

In the 1970s Fantasy acquired the catalogues of several important jazz labels, including Prestige, Riverside, and Milestone, becoming one of the largest holders of classic jazz recordings. Zaentz used the label's profits to finance films through Fantasy Films, including One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975) and Amadeus (1984), both of which won the Academy Award for Best Picture.

In 2004 Fantasy was sold to the Concord Music Group, which merged it into Concord Records; the combined company became known as Concord. The historic Fantasy Studios in Berkeley closed in 2018.
