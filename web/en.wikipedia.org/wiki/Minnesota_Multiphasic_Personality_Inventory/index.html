Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory

The Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI) is the most widely used standardized psychometric test of adult personality and psychopathology. Developed by Starke R. Hathaway and J. C. McKinley at the University of Minnesota and first published in 1943, it was constructed by empirical criterion keying: items were retained if they statistically distinguished clinical patients from normal comparison groups, regardless of their apparent content.

Scales and versions

The original test's ten clinical scales include Hypochondriasis, Depression, Hysteria, Psychopathic Deviate, Paranoia, Psychasthenia, Schizophrenia, Hypomania, and Social Introversion, alongside validity scales (L, F, K) designed to detect lying, random responding, and defensiveness. The MMPI-2 (1989) restandardized the test on a modern normative sample with 567 true–false items; the MMPI-2-RF (2008) shortened it to 338 items organized around restructured clinical scales. The current edition, the MMPI-3, was released in 2020. An adolescent version, the MMPI-A, also exists.

Use and criticism

The MMPI is used in clinical assessment, forensic evaluations, and personnel screening for sensitive occupations. Interpretation relies on profile patterns ("code types") rather than single scales. Critics have raised concerns about cultural bias, the test's length, and misuse by untrained interpreters, but it remains one of the most researched instruments in psychological testing.
