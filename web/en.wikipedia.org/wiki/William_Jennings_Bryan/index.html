William Jennings Bryan (19 March 1860 – 26 July 1925) was an American orator and politician, one of the dominant figures in the populist wing of the Democratic Party. He was the Democratic nominee for President of the United States three times (1896, 1900 and 1908) and served as Secretary of State under Woodrow Wilson from 1913 to 1915.

"Cross of Gold" and presidential campaigns

Bryan, a congressman from Nebraska, electrified the 1896 Democratic National Convention with his "Cross of Gold" speech, denouncing the gold standard and championing the free coinage of silver on behalf of farmers and debtors. Nominated at only 36, he campaigned across the country in an unprecedented speaking tour but lost to William McKinley. He lost the rematch in 1900 (campaigning against imperialism after the Spanish–American War) and again in 1908 to William Howard Taft.

Secretary of State

As Secretary of State, Bryan negotiated a series of "cooling-off" arbitration treaties and championed progressive causes. He resigned in June 1915, judging Wilson's second note to Germany after the sinking of the Lusitania too belligerent.

Later life and the Scopes trial

In retirement Bryan became a leading voice of the fundamentalist and anti-evolution movement. In July 1925 he assisted the prosecution in the Scopes "Monkey" Trial in Dayton, Tennessee, facing Clarence Darrow, who cross-examined him on the Bible in a famous courtroom confrontation. Bryan died in his sleep five days after the trial ended.
