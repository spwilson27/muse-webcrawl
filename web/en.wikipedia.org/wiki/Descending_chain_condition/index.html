Descending chain condition

The descending chain condition (DCC) is a finiteness condition in mathematics, used in order theory, abstract algebra and related areas. A partially ordered set (or a family of subsets, submodules, ideals, and so on) satisfies the descending chain condition if every strictly descending sequence of elements

x1 > x2 > x3 > ⋯

eventually terminates — that is, becomes constant after finitely many steps. Equivalently, every nonempty subset has a minimal element. The condition is the order-theoretic dual of the ascending chain condition (ACC).

Examples and applications

In ring theory, a ring satisfies the descending chain condition on ideals precisely when it is an Artinian ring; by the Artin–Wedderburn theory such rings have a well-behaved structure, and Artinian rings are automatically Noetherian by the Akizuki–Hopkins theorem. A module satisfying the DCC on submodules is called an Artinian module.

The natural numbers with their usual ordering satisfy the DCC (this is the principle of well-ordering, and underlies proofs by infinite descent), whereas the integers, the rational numbers and the real numbers do not: each admits infinite strictly descending sequences. More generally, any well-founded relation satisfies a form of the descending chain condition, which justifies transfinite and structural induction arguments in set theory and computer science — for instance, termination proofs for rewriting systems and recursive programs.
