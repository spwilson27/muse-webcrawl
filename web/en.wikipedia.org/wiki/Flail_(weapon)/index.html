Flail (weapon)

A flail is a weapon consisting of a striking head attached to a handle by a flexible rope, strap, or chain. The chief tactical virtue of the flail was its capacity to strike around a defender's shield or parry. Its chief liability was a lack of precision and the difficulty of using it in close formations, or in the confined spaces of single combat — indeed, the wielder could strike himself if he missed.

Origins

The flail derives from the agricultural threshing tool of the same name, used to separate grain from husks. Peasant levies in medieval Europe frequently carried the agricultural flail into battle, and an adapted military form — with a shorter striking arm, sometimes studded or shod with iron — emerged by the early Middle Ages. The Hussites of 15th-century Bohemia famously employed war flails from their wagon forts, and the weapon appears widely in German and Swiss service.

Design

Military flails vary from simple one-handed implements with a short chain and ball to two-handed versions with long hafts and heavy spiked heads. The related morning star has a spiked head fixed rigidly to the shaft, while the flail's flexible connection lets the head gain momentum and wrap around obstacles. East Asian analogues include the Chinese two- and three-section staves and the Japanese nunchaku and kusarigama family.
