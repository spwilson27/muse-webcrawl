Tree of Gernika

The Tree of Gernika (Basque: Gernikako Arbola) is an oak tree in the town of Gernika in Biscay, in the Basque Country of Spain. It is a symbol of the traditional freedoms (the fueros) of the Basque people.

History

For centuries, the lords of Biscay and later the kings of Castile swore oaths beneath the tree to respect the Basque fueros, and the assemblies (Juntas Generales) of Biscay met in its shade beside the Casa de Juntas. Successive trees have occupied the site: the "father tree" planted in the 14th century, the "old tree" whose trunk is preserved in a pavilion in the grounds, and the current tree, planted in the 19th century. Saplings grown from its acorns have been planted around the world as symbols of Basque identity.

Symbolism

The tree survived the bombing of Guernica in 1937, deepening its symbolic power. The Lehendakari (Basque president) traditionally takes the oath of office beneath it, and it appears on the coat of arms of Biscay and features in the Basque anthem Gernikako Arbola.
