Cry of Dolores

The Cry of Dolores (Spanish: Grito de Dolores) occurred in Dolores, Mexico, on 16 September 1810, when Roman Catholic priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla rang his church bell and gave the call to arms that triggered the Mexican War of Independence.

Cry of Dolores

Date: 16 September 1810
Location: Dolores, Guanajuato
Leader: Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla

Events

Hidalgo, with Ignacio Allende and Juan Aldama, had conspired against Spanish colonial rule. When the conspiracy was discovered, Hidalgo rang the bell in the early hours, gathered parishioners, and exhorted them to revolt. The exact words are not recorded verbatim; traditional versions include cries against bad government and for independence and the Virgin of Guadalupe.

Commemoration

Each year on the night of 15 September, the President of Mexico re-enacts the Grito from the balcony of the National Palace in Mexico City, ringing Hidalgo's bell and shouting '¡Viva México!' Independence Day is celebrated on 16 September.
