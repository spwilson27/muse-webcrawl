Bartending

Bartending is the occupation of a bartender, a person who formulates and serves alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks behind a bar in a licensed establishment. Bartenders typically also maintain the supplies and inventory for the bar, handle payment, and are responsible for verifying customers' ages and for responsible service of alcohol.

Duties and skills

A bartender's work combines mixology — knowledge of cocktail recipes, spirits, beer and wine — with speed, memory, cleanliness and customer interaction. Common techniques include shaking, stirring, building, muddling, straining and flaming garnishes. A "flair bartender" adds juggling and performance elements to drink preparation.

History

The profession has ancient antecedents in innkeepers and tavern keepers, but modern bartending is often dated to the nineteenth-century United States. Jerry Thomas, author of How to Mix Drinks (1862), the first bartender's guide published in America, is regarded as the father of American mixology. Prohibition (1920–1933) drove many American bartenders abroad and reshaped cocktail culture.
