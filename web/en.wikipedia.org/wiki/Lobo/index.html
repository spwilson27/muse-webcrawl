Lobo (born Roland Kent LaVoie, 31 July 1943) is an American singer-songwriter who achieved international success in the early 1970s with soft rock hits including "Me and You and a Dog Named Boo" (1971), "I'd Love You to Want Me" (1972) and "Don't Expect Me to Be Your Friend" (1973). "Lobo" is Spanish for "wolf".

Early life and career

LaVoie was born in Tallahassee, Florida, and raised in Winter Haven. He began playing in local bands in the early 1960s, including The Rumours, whose members later included Gram Parsons and drummer Jon Corneal. In 1964 he was briefly a member of the group Sugar Beats, and by the end of the decade he was working as a songwriter and recording solo material.

Breakthrough

In 1971, recording under the name Lobo for Big Tree Records, he released "Me and You and a Dog Named Boo", which reached No. 5 on the US Billboard Hot 100 and was a hit internationally. The album Introducing Lobo followed. His biggest success came in 1972 with "I'd Love You to Want Me", which reached No. 2 in the United States and No. 1 in Germany, where it became one of the best-selling singles of the era. "Don't Expect Me to Be Your Friend" (1973) reached the US top 10, as did his albums Calumet (1973).

Later career

Lobo's commercial momentum in the US faded in the late 1970s, but he retained a large following in Europe and especially in Asia, where his songs remained staples of radio and he toured frequently. He recorded for several labels, released country material under his own name, and continued to record and perform into the 21st century, including albums aimed at his Asian audience.
