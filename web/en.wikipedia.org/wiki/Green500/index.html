Green500

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Green500 is a biannual ranking of supercomputers in the TOP500 list in terms of energy efficiency, measured as floating-point operations per second per watt (FLOPS per watt). It aims to encourage the development of energy-efficient high-performance computing and to provide a metric complementary to raw performance.

The list was first published in 2007 by computer scientists Kirk W. Cameron and Wu-chun Feng of Virginia Tech, arising from their work on power-aware computing and the "Supercomputing in Small Spaces" project.

Methodology

Entries report the power consumed while running the LINPACK benchmark used for the TOP500 ranking, and efficiency is expressed in gigaflops per watt. Reporting standards were tightened over time, distinguishing measurements of the whole system from partial measurements. Recent leaders have exceeded 60 gigaflops per watt, aided by accelerator-heavy designs using GPUs.

Significance

As supercomputers grew toward the exascale, power consumption — often tens of megawatts — became a primary design constraint, making the Green500 a closely watched indicator of progress in efficient architectures, power management, and cooling.
