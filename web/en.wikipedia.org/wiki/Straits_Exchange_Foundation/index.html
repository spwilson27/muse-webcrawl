Straits Exchange Foundation

SEF headquarters in Taipei, Taiwan

The Straits Exchange Foundation (SEF; Chinese: 海峽交流基金會; pinyin: Hǎixiá Jiāoliú Jījīnhuì) is a semi-official organisation set up by the government of the Republic of China (Taiwan) to handle technical and business matters with the People's Republic of China (mainland China). Because the two governments do not recognise each other, the SEF conducts contacts, negotiations and document authentication on behalf of Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council through its mainland counterpart, the Association for Relations Across the Taiwan Straits (ARATS).

History

The SEF was established on 21 November 1990 and formally began operations on 8 March 1991, with the businessman and statesman Koo Chen-fu as its first chairman. In April 1993, Koo met ARATS chairman Wang Daohan in Singapore in the Koo–Wang talks, the first high-level semi-official meeting across the Taiwan Strait since 1949. A second round was held in Shanghai in 1998. Talks were suspended for a decade after 1999, then resumed in June 2008 following the election of Ma Ying-jeou, producing agreements on direct transport, trade and tourism. Dialogue was again suspended after 2016, when the Tsai Ing-wen administration declined to affirm the "1992 Consensus" framework Beijing demanded.

Functions

The foundation verifies and authenticates documents issued on the mainland, assists Taiwanese citizens in emergencies in mainland China, handles repatriation and family matters, and serves as the channel for cross-strait agreements negotiated with ARATS. In practice, when official talks are frozen, the SEF's role is largely confined to document authentication and casework.
