Common carrier

A common carrier in common law countries is a person or company that transports goods or people for any person or company and is responsible for any possible loss of the goods during transport. A common carrier offers its services to the general public under a licence or authority provided by a regulatory body, in contrast to a contract carrier, which serves particular customers under individual contracts such as a charter.

Characteristics

Common carriers typically:

  • Hold themselves out to serve the public indiscriminately
  • Operate over regular routes or between fixed termini, on regular schedules
  • Charge standard, published rates
  • Are subject to a heightened duty of care and, historically, near-strict liability for loss of goods, subject to exceptions such as acts of God and inherent vice

Regulation

Railways, trucking companies, airlines, shipping lines, pipelines and telecommunications providers have all been treated as common carriers in various jurisdictions. In the United States, motor freight common carriers were long regulated by the Interstate Commerce Commission and later under the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's safety regime. Telecommunications common carriage underlies principles such as net neutrality.

Common carrier in telecommunications

Under the US Communications Act, a telecommunications provider is treated as a common carrier to the extent it provides telecommunications services for a fee directly to the public, with obligations of non-discrimination and just and reasonable charges.
