Charter

A charter is the grant of authority or rights, stating that the granter formally recognises the prerogative of the recipient to exercise the rights specified. In transport and logistics, the term refers to the hiring of a vehicle, vessel or aircraft, or of freight capacity, for the exclusive use of the hirer under a contract of carriage, rather than carriage offered to the general public on fixed schedules.

Transport chartering

A charter service is provided by contract carriers, who undertake to transport passengers or goods for a particular customer or group. Examples include charter flights operated by airlines for tour operators or private clients, chartered buses and coaches, and chartered trains. Charter carriage contrasts with common carriage, in which a carrier offers service to the public at large.

Maritime chartering

In shipping, a charterparty is the contract for the hire of a vessel. The main types are the voyage charter (hire for a single voyage), the time charter (hire for a period, with the owner retaining crew and navigation), and the bareboat or demise charter (hire in which the charterer crews and operates the vessel). Charter rates for bulk shipping are tracked by indices such as the Baltic Dry Index.

Other meanings

Historically, a charter is a formal document granting rights or privileges, such as a royal charter incorporating a city, university or company, or a constitutional document such as Magna Carta. In project management, a project charter formally authorises a project.
