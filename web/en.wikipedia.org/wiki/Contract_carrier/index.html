Contract carrier

A contract carrier is a transportation company or individual that transports goods or passengers for specific clients under individual, negotiated contracts, rather than offering service to the general public. Contract carriers stand in contrast to common carriers, which hold themselves out to serve anyone willing to pay their published rates.

Characteristics

A contract carrier typically:

  • Serves a limited number of shippers or customers under continuing agreements;
  • Negotiates rates, routes, and service levels individually for each contract;
  • May dedicate particular vehicles or equipment to a single customer;
  • Is not obliged to serve the general public or to file public tariffs in the manner of a common carrier.

Regulation

In the United States, contract carriers in interstate commerce are regulated by the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration and must obtain operating authority. Historically, the distinction mattered greatly under the Interstate Commerce Act and the Motor Carrier Act of 1935, which subjected common and contract carriers to different licensing and rate rules; deregulation in the late twentieth century narrowed many of the practical differences, though the legal categories persist. Similar distinctions appear in air transport, where contract (charter) operations differ from scheduled common carriage.
