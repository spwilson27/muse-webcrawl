Freight transport

Freight transport, also referred to as freight forwarding or shipping, is the physical process of transporting commodities and merchandise goods and cargo. The term shipping originally referred to transport by sea but in American English has been extended to refer to transport by land or air as well.

Modes

  • Ship: Maritime transport carries the great majority of world trade by volume, in bulk carriers, tankers and container ships.
  • Air: Air cargo is used for high-value and time-sensitive goods, carried in dedicated freighters and passenger aircraft belly holds.
  • Rail: Rail freight moves bulk commodities and intermodal containers over land.
  • Road: Trucking provides flexible first- and last-mile delivery; loads are described as full truckload or less-than-truckload (LTL).

Intermodal freight

Intermodal freight transport involves moving goods in standardised containers that transfer between ships, trains and trucks without handling the cargo itself. Containerisation, developed in the 1950s, dramatically reduced the cost of international trade.

Organisation

Freight may be carried by common carriers serving the public, by contract carriers serving particular shippers (sometimes under charter arrangements), or by private fleets. Freight forwarders and third-party logistics providers arrange transport, warehousing and customs clearance on behalf of shippers. Legal terms of trade are codified in the Incoterms rules, and the contract of carriage is evidenced by documents such as the bill of lading.
