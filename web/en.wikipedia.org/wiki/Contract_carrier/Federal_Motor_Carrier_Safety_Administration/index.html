Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) is an agency within the United States Department of Transportation that regulates the trucking industry in the United States. The primary mission of the FMCSA is to reduce crashes, injuries and fatalities involving large trucks and buses.

History

The FMCSA was established by the Motor Carrier Safety Improvement Act of 1999 and began operating on 1 January 2000. Previously, motor carrier safety functions had been housed in the Federal Highway Administration and, before that, the Interstate Commerce Commission.

Functions

The agency issues and enforces the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations (FMCSRs), which govern areas including:

  • Commercial driver's licences and driver qualification
  • Hours of service rules limiting driving time and requiring rest
  • Vehicle inspection, repair and maintenance
  • Transportation of hazardous materials by road
  • Registration (operating authority and USDOT numbers) for interstate motor carriers, including common and contract carriers

FMCSA also runs safety measurement and compliance programmes, such as the Compliance, Safety, Accountability (CSA) programme, and administers grants to state enforcement partners.
