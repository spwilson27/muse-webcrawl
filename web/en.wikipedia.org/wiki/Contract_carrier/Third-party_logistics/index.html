Third-party logistics

Third-party logistics (abbreviated as 3PL) in logistics and supply chain management is an organisation's use of third-party businesses to outsource elements of its distribution, warehousing and fulfilment services. Third-party logistics providers typically specialise in integrated operations of warehousing and transportation services that can be scaled and customised to customers' needs, based on market conditions.

Services

3PL services commonly include:

  • Transportation management and freight forwarding, arranging carriage with common carriers and contract carriers
  • Warehousing, cross-docking and inventory management
  • Order fulfilment, pick and pack, and distribution
  • Customs brokerage and international shipping documentation
  • Returns processing (reverse logistics) and value-added services such as kitting and labelling

Types of providers

Providers range from asset-based companies owning trucks and warehouses to non-asset-based freight brokers and forwarders. Fourth-party logistics (4PL) providers act as integrators, managing a client's entire supply chain and coordinating multiple 3PLs.

Development

The 3PL industry grew rapidly from the 1980s as companies outsourced logistics to focus on core activities and as globalisation lengthened supply chains. Major global providers include DHL Supply Chain, Kuehne + Nagel, DB Schenker and XPO. E-commerce growth has driven demand for fulfilment-centre services offered by 3PLs and by platforms such as Amazon.
