Marquess of Queensberry is a title in the Peerage of Scotland. The Marquessate (with its subsidiary titles) was created in 1682 for William Douglas, 3rd Earl of Queensberry. The 4th Marquess was created Duke of Queensberry in 1684. After the death in 1810 of William Douglas, 4th Duke of Queensberry ("Old Q"), the dukedom of Queensberry passed to the 3rd Duke of Buccleuch, while the marquessate was inherited by a cousin, Sir Charles Douglas of Kelhead, 5th Baronet, from whom the present line descends.

The title is best known to the public through John Sholto Douglas, 9th Marquess of Queensberry (1844–1900), an enthusiastic sportsman whose name is attached to the Marquess of Queensberry Rules that govern modern boxing. The 9th Marquess also played a notorious public role in the downfall of the writer Oscar Wilde: his feud with Wilde, whose friendship with the Marquess's son Lord Alfred Douglas he bitterly opposed, precipitated the libel trial of 1895 and Wilde's subsequent prosecution and imprisonment.

History

The Earldom of Queensberry had been created in 1633 for William Douglas, 1st Viscount of Drumlanrig, a descendant of the Douglas lords of Drumlanrig in Dumfriesshire. The 3rd Earl, a leading figure in Restoration Scotland, received the marquessate in 1682 and the dukedom two years later. The 3rd Duke of Queensberry was a prominent supporter of the Union of 1707. On the separation of the dukedom and marquessate in 1810, the marquessate passed to the Douglas of Kelhead line. Later holders included John Douglas, 7th Marquess, a noted athlete, and Sholto Charles Douglas, Lord Aberdour, father of mountaineer Lord Francis Douglas who died on the first ascent of the Matterhorn.

Marquesses of Queensberry (1682)

The marquesses include William Douglas, 1st Marquess (1637–1695); James Douglas, 2nd Marquess (1672–1711); James Douglas, 3rd Marquess (1697–1715), who was declared incapable and passed over; Charles Douglas, 3rd Duke and 4th Marquess (1698–1778); William Douglas, 4th Duke, "Old Q" (1724–1810); Charles Douglas, 6th Marquess (1777–1837); John Douglas, 7th Marquess (1779–1856); Archibald William Douglas, 8th Marquess (1818–1858); John Sholto Douglas, 9th Marquess (1844–1900); and their successors to the present holder.
