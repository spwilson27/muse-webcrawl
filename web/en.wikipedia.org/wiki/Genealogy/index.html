Genealogy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hand-drawn family tree spreading across a parchment chart
A hand-drawn family tree spreading across a parchment chart

Genealogy is the study of families, family history and the tracing of lineages. Once the preserve of heralds validating noble descent, it is now among the world's most popular hobbies, transformed by digitised records (censuses, parish registers, passenger lists) and consumer DNA testing. Genealogists reconstruct pedigrees from primary evidence under standards such as the Genealogical Proof Standard; genetic genealogy has added chromosome-level corroboration — and forensic uses that have solved notorious cold cases.

Method and records

The craft works backwards from the known: civil registration, church books, wills, land deeds, military files, newspapers. In England parish registers begin (patchily) in 1538; many countries' censuses anchor the nineteenth century. Mass digitisation — FamilySearch (the LDS Church's archive, the world's largest), Ancestry, national archives — plus crowdsourced trees have industrialised the hobby while importing its characteristic error: the copy-pasted unsourced pedigree.

Genetic genealogy

Autosomal DNA tests (from 2010) let adoptees find birth families and cousins find cousins; in 2018 investigators identified the Golden State Killer through relatives' profiles on GEDmatch, launching investigative genetic genealogy — hundreds of identifications since, and a continuing privacy reckoning over whose DNA can expose whom.
