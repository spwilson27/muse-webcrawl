Walter Raleigh

Sir Walter Raleigh (c. 1552 – 29 October 1618) was an English statesman, soldier, writer and explorer, and a favourite courtier of Queen Elizabeth I. He is best known for sponsoring the Roanoke Colony, the first English attempt at permanent settlement in North America, and for popularising tobacco in England.

Career under Elizabeth I

Born in Devon, Raleigh served in Ireland, where he took part in suppressing the Desmond Rebellions and received confiscated land. Knighted in 1585, he rose high in the Queen's favour and held offices including captain of the guard. In 1584 he obtained a patent to explore and colonise North America; expeditions he sponsored founded the ill-fated Roanoke Colony in present-day North Carolina, whose settlers vanished by 1590. Raleigh fell from favour after secretly marrying Elizabeth Throckmorton, one of the Queen's maids of honour, and was briefly imprisoned in the Tower of London in 1592.

Later life and death

In 1595 Raleigh sailed to South America seeking the legendary El Dorado, later publishing The Discoverie of Guiana. After James I succeeded Elizabeth in 1603, Raleigh was convicted of treason in a controversial trial and spent thirteen years in the Tower, where he wrote his History of the World. Released for a final expedition to Guiana in 1617, he was executed in 1618 after his men attacked a Spanish outpost, in breach of the terms of his release. Raleigh remains one of the most famous figures of the Elizabethan age; the capital of North Carolina is named after him.
