Macbeth (character)

Lord Macbeth, the thane of Glamis and later the thane of Cawdor, is the title character and main protagonist of William Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth (c. 1606). The character is loosely based on the historical king Macbeth of Scotland and largely derived from the account in Holinshed's Chronicles (1587).

Role in the play

Macbeth is introduced as a brave and honoured general of King Duncan's army. Returning from battle, he and his friend Banquo meet three witches, who prophesy that Macbeth will become thane of Cawdor and then king, and that Banquo's descendants will be kings. When Duncan makes him thane of Cawdor, fulfilling the first part, Macbeth's ambition is awakened. Urged on by his wife, Lady Macbeth, he murders Duncan in his sleep and seizes the crown.

To secure his throne, Macbeth arranges the murders of Banquo and of Macduff's family, descending into tyranny and paranoia while Banquo's ghost haunts him. The witches' further prophecies — that he cannot be harmed by any "of woman born" and is safe until Birnam Wood comes to Dunsinane — give him false confidence. Both prove deceptive: Macduff's soldiers advance camouflaged with branches of Birnam Wood, and Macduff, delivered by caesarean section, kills Macbeth in single combat. Malcolm, Duncan's son, is restored as king.

Analysis

Macbeth is a classic tragic hero: an admired figure destroyed by ambition and a fatal moral choice rather than by innate villainy. His soliloquies, including "Is this a dagger which I see before me" and the nihilistic "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow", chart his psychological disintegration from valour to guilt-ridden tyranny. Actors traditionally refer to the play itself as "the Scottish play" because of a superstitious curse.
