Licence

A licence (Commonwealth spelling) or license (American spelling) is an official permission or permit to do, use, or own something, granted by a competent authority. Licences are issued for activities that are otherwise restricted or regulated, such as driving a vehicle, practising a profession, operating a business, broadcasting, or using intellectual property belonging to another party.

Types

Regulatory licences

Governments license activities in the interest of public safety, health, and order. Common examples include driving licences, pilot licences, firearms licences, liquor licences, and professional licences for doctors, lawyers, and tradespeople. Obtaining such a licence usually requires meeting specified qualifications and may involve examinations and fees; licences can be suspended or revoked for misconduct.

Intellectual property licences

In intellectual property law, a licence is a grant by the owner of a patent, copyright, or trademark permitting another party to use the protected work or invention under defined conditions, often in exchange for royalties. Software licences — including proprietary end-user licence agreements and free and open-source licences such as the GNU General Public License — are a familiar example. A "licence" is also the agreement by which a broadcaster or venue obtains rights to perform or exhibit works.

Terminology

In British English, licence is the noun and license the verb ("to license", "licensed"); American English uses license for both. A person or business holding a licence is a licensee; the authority granting it is the licensor.
