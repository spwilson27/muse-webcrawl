Austrian State Treaty

Signed15 May 1955, Belvedere Palace, Vienna
Effective27 July 1955
PartiesAustria, US, USSR, UK, France

The Austrian State Treaty (German: Österreichischer Staatsvertrag) of 1955 re-established Austria as a free, independent and democratic state after Allied occupation following the Second World War. It was signed on 15 May 1955 at the Schloss Belvedere in Vienna by Austria and the four occupying powers.

Terms

The treaty ended the occupation, restored Austria's 1937 borders, prohibited Anschluss (union with Germany) and Nazi organisations, and protected minority rights. In parallel, Austria committed to permanent neutrality, enacted in the Neutrality Act of 26 October 1955 — now Austria's national day — which enabled Soviet agreement to withdraw.

Legacy

Foreign Minister Leopold Figl famously showed the treaty from the Belvedere balcony declaring “Österreich ist frei!” (“Austria is free!”). More than 150,000 Allied troops withdrew by October 1955.
