Grumman F4F Wildcat

The Grumman F4F Wildcat is an American carrier-based fighter aircraft that entered service in 1940 with the United States Navy and the British Royal Navy, where it was initially known as the Martlet. First used by the British in the North Atlantic, the Wildcat was the only effective fighter available to the US Navy and Marine Corps in the Pacific Theater during the early part of the Second World War. The Wildcat was outperformed by the faster, more maneuverable and longer-ranged Mitsubishi A6M Zero, but held its own partly because its airframe could withstand a great deal of damage, and thanks to tactics such as the Thach Weave developed by John S. Thach.

Operational history

Wildcats fought in the battles of Wake Island, the Coral Sea, Midway and Guadalcanal, and in the North African landings and Atlantic convoy escort. Lessons learned from the Wildcat were later applied to the faster F6F Hellcat, which could still outperform the Zero on its own terms. Including the FM variants built by General Motors' Eastern Aircraft division, 7,885 Wildcats were produced. FM-2s served from escort carriers until the end of the war.

Specifications

The F4F-4 was powered by a Pratt & Whitney R-1830-86 Twin Wasp radial engine of 1,200 hp, with a maximum speed of 318 mph (512 km/h) at 19,400 ft, a range of 770 miles, and armament of six .50 in (12.7 mm) M2 Browning machine guns. Its landing gear was retracted by a hand-cranked mechanism, a distinctive and laborious feature for pilots.
