Five wisdoms

TraditionMahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism
Sanskritpañca-jñāna

The five wisdoms (Sanskrit: pañca-jñāna; Tibetan: ye shes lnga) are five qualities of awakened awareness in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. They describe how the enlightened mind experiences reality once the five poisons (delusion, attachment, aversion, pride and jealousy) are transmuted, and they are embodied by the Five Wisdom Buddhas (Tathāgatas).

The five wisdoms

  • Dharmadhātu wisdom — the wisdom of the all-encompassing expanse of reality, associated with Vairocana.
  • Mirror-like wisdom — clear, impartial reflection of things as they are, associated with Akṣobhya.
  • Wisdom of equality — perceiving the equal nature of all phenomena, associated with Ratnasambhava.
  • Discriminating wisdom — precise discernment of each phenomenon's distinct character, associated with Amitābha.
  • All-accomplishing wisdom — spontaneous, effective action for the benefit of beings, associated with Amoghasiddhi.

Each wisdom is linked with one of the five Buddha families, a colour, an element, a direction and one of the five skandhas, forming an integrated mandala of transformation used in tantric practice.
