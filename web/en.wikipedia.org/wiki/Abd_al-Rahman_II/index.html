Abd al-Rahman II (Arabic: عبد الرحمن الثاني; 792–852) was the fourth Umayyad Emir of Córdoba, ruling the Emirate in al-Andalus from 822 until his death. His thirty-year reign brought internal consolidation after the revolts of his father's time and a brilliant flowering of court culture modelled on Abbasid Baghdad.

Reign

Abd al-Rahman succeeded his father al-Hakam I in 822 and spent his first years suppressing revolts in Toledo and Mérida and campaigning against the Christian kingdoms of the north and the Franks of the Spanish March. In 844 a Viking fleet raided Lisbon and Seville; after defeating the raiders he built dockyards and a standing fleet that ended large-scale Norse attacks on al-Andalus. He also conducted diplomacy with Constantinople, exchanging embassies with the emperor Theophilos.

Court and culture

His reign is remembered for the arrival in 822 of the Persian musician Ziryab, who transformed Andalusi music, cuisine, dress and etiquette. Abd al-Rahman reorganised the administration on Baghdad lines, with a vizier and specialised departments, expanded the Great Mosque of Córdoba (enlargement of 833–848), and struck coinage and built extensively in the capital. Late in the reign began the episode of the voluntary Christian "Martyrs of Córdoba", whose public blasphemies against Islam the authorities tried, largely in vain, to defuse without executions.

Death

Abd al-Rahman II died in 852 and was succeeded by his son Muhammad I.
