Historical negationism

Historical negationism, also called denialism, is a falsification or distortion of the historical record. The term, coined by the French historian Henry Rousso in 1987 in connection with Holocaust denial, distinguishes illegitimate denial or minimisation of established historical facts from legitimate historical revisionism, in which scholars reinterpret the past on the basis of evidence and argument.

Negationism is defined by its rejection of an established documentary record

Characteristics

Historians characterise negationism by several recurring techniques: outright denial that an event occurred; minimisation of its scale or significance; blame-shifting onto the victims; cherry-picking or fabrication of sources; manufacturing false equivalence between unequal events; and presenting denial as forbidden knowledge suppressed by a conspiracy. Because such positions reject the normal standards of historical evidence, the mainstream scholarly community does not treat them as legitimate revisionist scholarship.

Examples

The paradigm case is Holocaust denial, which is illegal in a number of European countries and in Israel. Other widely discussed examples include denial of the Armenian genocide, denial or minimisation of Japanese war crimes such as the Nanjing Massacre, apologetic rewriting of the history of the Soviet Union under Stalin, "Lost Cause" narratives of the American Civil War that minimise the role of slavery, and state-sponsored distortion in various national historiographies. In each case the stakes are political as well as scholarly, touching on memory, identity, and claims for recognition or redress.

Responses

Responses to negationism have included criminal laws against denial (criticised by some historians and free-speech advocates), public commemoration and education, digitisation of archives to make primary evidence widely accessible, and scholarly refutation — most famously the expert evidence assembled for the 2000 London libel trial in which the writer David Irving sued Deborah Lipstadt and Penguin Books and was found by the court to be a Holocaust denier who had deliberately distorted evidence.
