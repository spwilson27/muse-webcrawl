Old Julfa cemetery

The cemetery of Old Julfa (Armenian: Ջուղայի գերեզմանատուն) was a medieval Armenian cemetery near the town of Julfa in the Nakhchivan exclave of Azerbaijan, on the north bank of the Aras River opposite Iran. It was famous as the largest surviving collection of khachkars — intricately carved cross-stones — in the world.

History

Old Julfa was a prosperous trading city on the silk routes, settled by Armenian merchants. Its cemetery grew from the early medieval period through the sixteenth century, eventually containing thousands of khachkars and ram-shaped tombstones, many with elaborate carvings of crosses, rosettes, and scenes of the deceased's trade.

Destruction

Reports of damage began in 1998, and between roughly 1998 and 2006 the cemetery was destroyed. Satellite imagery analysed by the American Association for the Advancement of Science confirmed the complete removal of the khachkars and the levelling of the site by 2009. Azerbaijan has denied the destruction and the historical Armenian presence at the site, and international access has been refused. UNESCO and the European Parliament have condemned the loss.
