Tiberias

Tiberias (Hebrew: טבריה, Ṭəḇeryā) is an Israeli city on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee (Lake Tiberias/Kinneret) in the Lower Galilee. Founded around AD 20 by Herod Antipas and named in honour of the Roman emperor Tiberius, it became one of the four holy cities of Judaism, a centre of rabbinic scholarship where much of the Jerusalem Talmud was compiled, and today is a resort town known for its hot springs and lakeside tourism.

History

Herod Antipas built the city near the hot springs of Hammat Tiberias, partly over a burial ground, which initially made observant Jews reluctant to live there. After the destruction of Jerusalem (AD 70) and the Bar Kokhba revolt, Tiberias became the leading centre of Jewish life in the Galilee: the Sanhedrin moved there, and Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi's academy flourished nearby; the Mishnah's redaction and much of the Jerusalem Talmud are associated with the region. The Tiberian system of vocalisation (niqqud) of the Hebrew Bible was developed by the Masoretes of Tiberias in the early Middle Ages.

The city was conquered by Muslim forces in the 630s, taken by Crusaders in 1099 (becoming the capital of the Principality of Galilee), and retaken by Saladin after the Battle of Hattin in 1187. In the sixteenth century, under Ottoman rule, Doña Gracia Nasi and Joseph Nasi attempted to rebuild Tiberias as a Jewish refuge. The city was badly damaged by the Galilee earthquake of 1837.

Modern city

During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War the Arab population of Tiberias left or was evacuated, and the city developed within Israel as a lakeside resort and pilgrimage destination. Sites include the tombs of Rabbi Akiva and Maimonides, the ancient hot springs, and churches commemorating New Testament events around the lake.
