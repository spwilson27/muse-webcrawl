The Belgian or Belgian Draft is a breed of heavy draught horse from the Brabant region of modern Belgium. One of the strongest and heaviest horse breeds, it was developed for farm and haulage work and exported widely in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Characteristics

Belgians are large, muscular horses with a relatively small, well-shaped head, a short, heavily muscled neck, powerful hindquarters and feathered lower legs. They commonly weigh between 1,800 and 2,400 pounds (820–1,090 kg), and exceptional stallions have exceeded 3,000 pounds. In North America the typical colour is sorrel or chestnut with a flaxen mane and tail and a white blaze, while European Brabants are often roan or bay and somewhat heavier in build.

History and use

The breed descends from the heavy horses of medieval Flanders, often identified with the "great horse" used by knights. The Belgian stud book society was founded in 1885, and American importers established the Belgian Draft Horse Corporation of America in 1887. Belgians became the most numerous draught breed in the United States. Today they are used for pulling competitions, logging, farm work, carriage driving and showing. The breed is predisposed to certain conditions including chronic progressive lymphedema and junctional epidermolysis bullosa in some lines.
