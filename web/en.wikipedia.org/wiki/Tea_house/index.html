Tea house

A tea house (or teahouse) is an establishment primarily serving tea and other light refreshments. Tea houses are central social institutions in many cultures, particularly in China, Japan, the Middle East, Central Asia and parts of Europe, where they function as places for conversation, business, games and relaxation.

China

The Chinese tea house (cháguǎn or chálóu) has a history stretching back more than a thousand years. Tea houses flourished in the Song dynasty and became fixtures of urban life. In Sichuan, tea houses are renowned for their relaxed pace, bamboo chairs and covered-bowl (gaiwan) tea service, and many feature performances such as storytelling, opera excerpts and ear-cleaning services.

Japan

In Japan, the chashitsu is a purpose-built space for the Japanese tea ceremony (chanoyu), developed under the influence of Zen Buddhism and masters such as Sen no Rikyū. Separately, ochaya are exclusive establishments where geisha entertain guests.

Middle East and Central Asia

The chaikhana (tea house) is widespread from Iran and Turkey through Central Asia. Often doubling as caravanserai rest stops, chaikhanas serve strong tea from samovars and provide a space for socialising, board games and news exchange. In Azerbaijan, the çayxana is traditionally a male social space.

Europe

Tea rooms became popular in Britain in the late 19th century, offering respectable public dining spaces for women. In the Czech Republic and Slovakia, čajovna tea houses proliferated after 1989, offering teas from around the world in quiet, eclectic interiors.
