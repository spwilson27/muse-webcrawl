Noise figure

The noise figure (NF) and noise factor (F) are measures of the degradation of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) caused by components in a signal chain, such as amplifiers and receivers. The noise factor is the ratio of the SNR at the input to the SNR at the output:

F = SNRin / SNRout

The noise figure is the noise factor expressed in decibels: NF = 10 log10(F). A noiseless device has F = 1 and NF = 0 dB.

Cascaded systems

For a cascade of stages, the Friis formula gives the total noise factor:

F = F₁ + (F₂ − 1)/G₁ + (F₃ − 1)/(G₁G₂) + …

where G denotes available power gain. Because later terms are divided by the gain of earlier stages, the first stage — typically a low-noise amplifier — dominates the overall noise performance of a receiver.

Definitions and temperature

The standard definition assumes a source temperature of 290 K. Noise figure is related to noise temperature by Te = (F − 1)·290 K. Noise figure is meaningless without also stating the measurement bandwidth context, since noise power scales with bandwidth; it is widely used to specify receivers, amplifiers, mixers and antennas in radio-frequency engineering.
