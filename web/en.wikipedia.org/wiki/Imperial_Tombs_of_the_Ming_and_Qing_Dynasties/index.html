Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties

The Imperial Tombs of the Ming and Qing Dynasties are a collection of imperial mausolea built by the emperors of China's Ming dynasty (1368–1644) and Qing dynasty (1644–1912). Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List beginning in 2000 and extended in 2003 and 2004, the sites illustrate the development of Chinese imperial tomb architecture and feng shui principles over more than five centuries.

Sites

The listing includes the Ming tombs near Beijing (the famous Thirteen Tombs in the Changping district), the Ming Xiaoling mausoleum in Nanjing, the Qing dynasty Eastern Qing tombs near Zunhua and Western Qing tombs near Baoding, the Qing tombs at Shenyang (the Fuling, Zhaoling and Yongling tombs), and the tombs of individual emperors and princes in Hubei, Jiangsu and elsewhere.

Design

The tomb complexes follow principles of feng shui, set against hills with a sacred way (shendao) lined with stone statues of officials, soldiers and animals leading to ceremonial halls, memorial towers and the burial mound covering the underground palace. Together they present a fusion of architecture and landscape intended to express imperial power and the harmony between humanity and nature.
