The Arecibo Observatory, also known as the National Astronomy and Ionosphere Center, was an astronomical observatory in the municipality of Arecibo, Puerto Rico. Its principal instrument was the Arecibo Telescope, a 305-metre (1,000 ft) spherical reflector dish built into a natural limestone sinkhole — for over five decades the world's largest single-aperture telescope, until surpassed by China's FAST in 2016.

Research

The telescope served radio astronomy, atmospheric science, and planetary radar. It mapped the surface of Venus through its clouds, measured Mercury's rotation period (1965), produced the first radar images of an asteroid (1989), and observed the first confirmed exoplanetary system (around pulsar PSR B1257+12, 1992). In 1974 it transmitted the Arecibo message, a pictorial interstellar radio message directed at the globular cluster M13, devised with Frank Drake and Carl Sagan.

Collapse

After auxiliary and main cable failures in August and November 2020, the National Science Foundation decided to decommission the telescope. On 1 December 2020 the remaining cables gave way and the 900-ton instrument platform crashed into the dish, destroying the telescope. Plans have since centred on a proposed next-generation educational and research facility at the site.

In popular culture

The observatory featured prominently in the film GoldenEye (1995), the film Contact (1997), and episodes of The X-Files.
