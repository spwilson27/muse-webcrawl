Constitutional AI

Constitutional AI is a method for training artificial intelligence systems, particularly large language models, to be helpful and harmless, developed by the AI company Anthropic and described in a 2022 paper. Instead of relying primarily on human feedback to judge harmful outputs, the method gives the model an explicit set of principles — a "constitution" — against which it critiques and revises its own responses.

Method

Training proceeds in two phases. In the supervised phase, the model generates responses to potentially harmful prompts, critiques them using the constitutional principles, and produces revised, safer responses, which are used as fine-tuning data. In the reinforcement-learning phase (RLAIF — reinforcement learning from AI feedback), the model compares pairs of its own responses according to the constitution and those preferences train a reward model used for further optimisation.

Significance

Constitutional AI is used to align Anthropic's Claude family of models. The approach reduces the need for large volumes of human labelling of harmful content and makes the values guiding the model explicit and inspectable, though critics note that the choice of principles remains a human design decision.
