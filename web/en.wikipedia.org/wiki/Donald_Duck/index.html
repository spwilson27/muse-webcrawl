Donald Fauntleroy Duck is an American cartoon character created by Walt Disney. An anthropomorphic white duck with a yellow-orange bill and feet, he usually wears a sailor shirt and cap but no trousers. He debuted in the Silly Symphony The Wise Little Hen (1934) and became one of Disney's most popular characters, famous for his semi-intelligible voice, explosive temper and misadventures — and, in comics, for the rich Duckburg world built around him.

History

Voiced for fifty years by Clarence Nash, Donald starred in more films than Mickey Mouse, including the wartime propaganda cartoon Der Fuehrer's Face (1943), which won an Academy Award. His comic-book incarnation, developed above all by Carl Barks — who created Scrooge McDuck, Gladstone Gander and much of Duckburg — made him arguably more beloved in print, especially in Europe, than on screen. Italian and Scandinavian Donald comics remain best-sellers.

Character

Donald's defining traits are his bad luck, short temper and perseverance; stories variously cast him as a put-upon everyman, a treasure hunter with Uncle Scrooge, or a doting (if exasperated) uncle to Huey, Dewey and Louie. His birthday is officially 9 June 1934, the release date of his first film.
