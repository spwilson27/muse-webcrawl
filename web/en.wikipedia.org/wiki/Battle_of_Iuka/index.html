The Battle of Iuka was fought on September 19, 1862, in Iuka, Mississippi, during the American Civil War. In the opening battle of the Iuka–Corinth Campaign, Union Maj. Gen. William S. Rosecrans stopped the advance of the Confederate Army of the West commanded by Maj. Gen. Sterling Price.

After the Union Army's victory in the Siege of Corinth in May 1862, most forces were dispersed to other operations. In September, Price's army occupied Iuka to prevent Rosecrans from reinforcing Maj. Gen. Don Carlos Buell in Kentucky. Maj. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant planned a two-pronged attack on Iuka, with Rosecrans advancing from the southwest and Maj. Gen. Edward Ord from the northwest.

Background

In early September 1862, Confederate Gen. Braxton Bragg launched his invasion of Kentucky. To prevent Union forces in Mississippi from reinforcing Buell, Price was ordered to move toward Nashville. Price reached Iuka on September 14 and occupied the town. Grant, commanding the District of West Tennessee, decided to attack Price before the Confederate forces could unite with those of Earl Van Dorn.

Battle

Grant's plan called for Rosecrans to approach Iuka from the southwest along the Jacinto Road while Ord advanced from the northwest. Ord's force was to wait within earshot and attack once Rosecrans engaged. However, an acoustic shadow — a phenomenon in which sound fails to carry due to atmospheric conditions — prevented Ord's men from hearing the battle, so they never attacked.

Rosecrans's leading division, under Brig. Gen. Charles S. Hamilton, collided with Confederate pickets around 4:30 p.m. near the intersection of the Mill Road and Jacinto Road, about a mile southwest of Iuka. Fierce fighting continued until after dark. A key moment came when the 11th Missouri and supporting regiments defended a six-gun battery against repeated Confederate assaults; the battery was eventually overrun, but the Union line held.

Aftermath

Price had planned to attack the following day but instead withdrew during the night along the Fulton Road, which the Union forces had failed to block. Rosecrans's men were too exhausted to pursue vigorously. Casualties totaled 782 Union (144 killed, 598 wounded, 40 captured or missing) and 1,516 Confederate (263 killed, 692 wounded, 561 captured or missing). Price joined Van Dorn shortly afterward, and the combined Confederate force attacked Corinth on October 3–4, 1862, in the Second Battle of Corinth, where they were repulsed.
