The Hadith al-Thaqalayn (Arabic: حديث الثقلين, "Hadith of the Two Weighty Things") is a saying (hadith) attributed to the Islamic prophet Muhammad in which he describes the Quran and his family, the Ahl al-Bayt ("People of the House"), as two weighty things that he leaves behind for his community. The hadith is narrated in both Sunni and Shia sources and is one of the most important texts in the Shia case for the religious authority of Muhammad's family.

Text

A well-known version, narrated from Zayd ibn Arqam in Sahih Muslim, reads:

"I am leaving among you two weighty things: the first is the Book of God, in which is guidance and light, so hold fast to the Book of God and adhere to it... and the people of my House. I remind you of God regarding the people of my House; I remind you of God regarding the people of my House; I remind you of God regarding the people of my House."

Another version, prominent in Shia sources and also recorded in Sunni collections such as the Musnad of Ahmad ibn Hanbal, links the two more strongly:

"I leave among you two weighty things: the Book of God and my kindred, the People of my House. So long as you hold fast to them both, you will never go astray after me. The one is greater than the other... and they shall never separate until they return to me at the Pond."

Context

Versions of the saying are associated with several occasions in Muhammad's final year (632 CE), including the Farewell Sermon at Arafat and the stop at Ghadir Khumm on the return journey to Medina. Chains of transmission and exact wordings vary among collections, and scholars have long graded the hadith as authentic (sahih) or at least well-attested (mutawatir in meaning, according to some).

Interpretation

Shia view

In Twelver Shia Islam, the hadith is regarded as a clear designation of the Ahl al-Bayt — specifically the Imams descended from Ali and Fatimah — as authoritative guides alongside the Quran, whose protection from error follows from their inseparability from the Book until the Day of Judgement. It is cited together with the Event of Ghadir Khumm and the Hadith of the Cloak as the textual pillars of the doctrine of the Imamate.

Sunni view

Sunni scholars accept the hadith's authenticity in its Sahih Muslim form and interpret it as an exhortation to honour and care for the Prophet's family and to hold fast to the Quran (and, in related narrations, the Prophet's Sunnah), without entailing the Shia doctrine of divinely appointed Imams.
