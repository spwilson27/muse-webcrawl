Racial disparity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Racial disparity refers to systematic differences in outcomes — such as income, wealth, health, education, housing and treatment by the criminal justice system — between racial or ethnic groups within a society. The term is used most extensively in research on the United States, where large and persistent gaps between white Americans and Black, Hispanic and Native American populations have been documented across many domains, though racial disparities are studied in many other countries as well.

Domains

Criminal justice

Studies consistently find disparities in arrest rates, sentencing lengths and incarceration rates. Black Americans, about 13% of the US population, have made up a much larger share of the prison population, and research on sentencing has found racial gaps in outcomes even after controlling for offence and criminal history, though the size and interpretation of the residual gap are debated.

Wealth, income and health

Median white household wealth in the United States has long been a multiple — often estimated around eight to ten times — of median Black household wealth, a gap attributed by many researchers to historical policies including slavery, redlining and unequal access to homeownership and education. Health disparities include differences in life expectancy, infant and maternal mortality, and rates of chronic disease, which researchers link to socioeconomic conditions, environmental exposure and access to care as well as to differential treatment within healthcare.

Explanations and debate

Explanations offered for racial disparities range from the legacy of historical discrimination and structural or institutional racism, through current interpersonal discrimination (documented in audit studies of hiring and housing), to differences in family structure, culture, geography and class. Scholars and policymakers disagree about the relative weight of these causes and about remedies such as affirmative action, reparations, or race-neutral policies aimed at disadvantaged groups.
