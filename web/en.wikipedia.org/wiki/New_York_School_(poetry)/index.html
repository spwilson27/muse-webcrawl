New York School (poetry)

The New York School was an informal group of American poets active from the 1950s onward, centred in New York City and closely connected with the city's avant-garde painters, especially the Abstract Expressionists. Its first generation comprised Frank O'Hara, John Ashbery, Kenneth Koch, James Schuyler and Barbara Guest.

Style

The New York School poets wrote in a conversational, witty, often improvisatory voice, favouring everyday incident, urban life, friendship, art and popular culture over solemn confession or formal academic verse. O'Hara's "I do this, I do that" poems and his doctrine of "Personism" exemplify the group's approach; Ashbery developed a more elusive, dreamlike lyricism that made him one of the most decorated American poets.

Second generation and legacy

A second generation — including Ted Berrigan, Alice Notley, Ron Padgett, Joe Brainard and Anne Waldman — gathered around the Poetry Project at St. Mark's Church in the East Village. The school's influence runs through much subsequent American avant-garde and experimental poetry.
