2017 Iraqi–Kurdish conflict

The 2017 Iraqi–Kurdish conflict was a military confrontation between the federal government of Iraq and the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) in October 2017. It followed the Kurdish independence referendum of 25 September 2017, in which over 92 percent of voters backed independence — a vote Baghdad declared unconstitutional. Iraqi forces retook the disputed city of Kirkuk and most other territories the Kurdish Peshmerga had held since 2014.

2017 Iraqi–Kurdish conflict

Date: 15–27 October 2017
Location: Northern Iraq (Kirkuk and disputed territories)
Belligerents: Iraq vs. Kurdistan Region
Result: Iraqi federal victory; Baghdad takes Kirkuk and disputed areas

Background

During the war against the Islamic State, Kurdish Peshmerga forces had taken control of Kirkuk and other disputed territories in 2014 after the Iraqi army withdrew. The independence referendum, organised by KRG president Masoud Barzani, was opposed by Baghdad, Turkey, Iran and the United States. Iraq's Supreme Court ruled the vote unconstitutional, and the federal government demanded its results be annulled.

Course of the conflict

On 15–16 October, Iraqi security forces and Popular Mobilization Forces advanced on Kirkuk. Many Peshmerga units withdrew after an agreement between Baghdad and commanders aligned with the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, and federal forces seized the city, its oil fields and military bases with limited fighting. Over the following days Iraqi forces took control of most remaining disputed areas, including Sinjar, Makhmur and Altun Kupri, where heavier clashes occurred. Tens of thousands of civilians were displaced.

Aftermath

Barzani announced his resignation effective 1 November 2017. The KRG lost control of the Kirkuk oil fields, roughly halving its oil revenue, and offered to "freeze" the referendum results in favour of dialogue, which Baghdad rejected in favour of full annulment.
