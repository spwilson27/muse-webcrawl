Lal Bal Pal

Lal Bal Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, and Bipin Chandra Pal) were a triumvirate of assertive nationalists in British-ruled India in the early 20th century, from 1906 to 1918. They advocated the Swadeshi movement involving the boycott of all imported items and the use of Indian-made goods.

Background

The three leaders represented Punjab, Maharashtra, and Bengal respectively. Their assertive approach marked a shift from the moderate methods of the early Indian National Congress, especially after the Partition of Bengal in 1905. They demanded swaraj (self-rule) and mobilised students, workers, and the urban middle class.

Legacy

The Lal Bal Pal era transformed the independence movement into a mass movement and laid the groundwork for the later Gandhian campaigns.
