The Beast (game)

The Beast was an alternate reality game (ARG) created in 2001 as a viral marketing campaign for Steven Spielberg's film A.I. Artificial Intelligence. Designed by a team at Microsoft led by creative director Jordan Weisman, with game designer Elan Lee and writer Sean Stewart among its principal architects, it is widely regarded as the foundational work of the ARG genre. The game took its name from the players' own nickname for its sprawling, tentacular mass of content.

Design and discovery

The entry point (or "rabbit hole") was concealed in the film's promotional materials: a credit on the trailer and posters for a "sentient machine therapist" named Jeanine Salla. Searching the name led players to a network of fictional websites set in the year 2142, the film's future world. Over roughly twelve weeks the game expanded to dozens of websites, telephone numbers that could actually be called, faxes, live events, and a murder mystery narrated through the sites of fictional corporations, families and anti-robot activists. Clues demanded skills from players collectively — the game was designed for a community, not an individual — including Japanese, German, Tamil, binary code, and knowledge of subjects from organ design to Martin Luther.

Players and impact

The central player community, the Cloudmakers mailing list, grew to several thousand members who solved puzzles collaboratively in real time. Estimates place the total audience in the millions. The Beast established the ARG aesthetic of "this is not a game" (TINAG) — the designers never acknowledged its fictional nature in character — and its success inspired later landmark ARGs such as I Love Bees (2004) for Halo 2 and Year Zero (2007). It won awards including a Webby and helped create the commercial field of transmedia storytelling.
