Handshaking lemma

In graph theory, a branch of mathematics, the handshaking lemma is the statement that, in every finite undirected graph, the number of vertices that touch an odd number of edges is even. The lemma is a consequence of the degree sum formula, which states:

Σ deg(v) = 2|E|

that is, the sum of the degrees of all vertices in a graph equals twice the number of edges, since each edge contributes exactly two to the total degree count (one at each of its endpoints).

Name and motivation

The name comes from a popular restatement: at a party, if guests shake hands with one another, the number of people who have shaken hands an odd number of times is even. This version is a common introduction to reasoning with parity and invariants.

Proof and corollaries

The proof of the degree sum formula is a simple double counting argument: count the pairs (vertex, incident edge) in two ways. One immediate corollary is that a graph cannot have exactly one vertex of odd degree. Leonhard Euler proved the result in his famous 1736 paper on the Seven Bridges of Königsberg, the work that founded graph theory.
