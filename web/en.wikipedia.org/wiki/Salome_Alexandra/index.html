Salome Alexandra

Queen of Judea
Reign: 76–67 BCE
Predecessor: Alexander Jannaeus
Successor: Hyrcanus II
Died: 67 BCE

Salome Alexandra, also known by her Hebrew name Shelomzion (שְׁלוֹמְצִיּוֹן), (141–67 BCE) was the queen regnant of the Hasmonean kingdom of Judea from 76 BCE until her death. She was the wife of Alexander Jannaeus and one of the last rulers of an independent Jewish state before Roman domination. Rabbinic tradition remembers her reign, together with that of her brother Simeon ben Shetach, as a golden age.

Reign

On his deathbed, Alexander Jannaeus is said by Josephus to have advised his wife to conciliate the Pharisees, whose popular influence he had fought during his reign. Salome Alexandra followed this counsel: she reversed her husband's policies, released Pharisaic prisoners, recalled exiles, and allowed the Pharisees a leading role in government, while the Sadducees withdrew to fortresses. Under her rule the Sanhedrin was reorganized under Pharisaic leadership, and Simeon ben Shetach, traditionally described as her brother, became its head.

Foreign policy

Salome Alexandra strengthened the army, maintained fortresses, and avoided major wars, though she sent her son Aristobulus with an army toward Damascus. She preserved Judea's independence during a period when the Armenian king Tigranes the Great threatened the region; diplomacy and gifts, along with Tigranes' preoccupation with the Romans, averted invasion.

Death and legacy

Salome Alexandra died in 67 BCE at about seventy-three years of age. Her elder son Hyrcanus II succeeded her, but his brother Aristobulus II soon rebelled, igniting a civil war that ended with Roman intervention under Pompey. In rabbinic literature her reign is associated with miraculous abundance—rain falling on Sabbath eves and wheat grains growing to the size of kidneys.
