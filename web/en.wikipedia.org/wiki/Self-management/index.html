Self-management

Self-management or self-governance refers to the conduct of a person, organisation or system by itself, without external direction. The term is used in several senses: in political economy, workers' self-management of enterprises; in organisational theory, self-managing teams and companies; in medicine, patients' management of chronic conditions; and in psychology, an individual's regulation of their own behaviour and goals.

Workers' self-management

Workers' self-management (French: autogestion) is a form of organisational management in which the workforce of a firm decides on matters such as production, working conditions and the distribution of surplus, rather than owners or appointed managers. It is associated with anarchist, syndicalist and some socialist traditions. Notable implementations include the Yugoslav system of workers' councils introduced from 1950, worker cooperatives such as the Mondragon Corporation in Spain, and factory occupations in Argentina after the 2001 crisis.

Chronic disease management

In healthcare, self-management programmes — such as the Stanford Chronic Disease Self-Management Program — teach patients skills in monitoring symptoms, managing medication, exercise, and communicating with clinicians, improving outcomes in conditions such as diabetes and arthritis.

Personal self-management

In management literature, self-management denotes the ability to plan, prioritise and regulate one's own work: setting goals, managing time, and monitoring performance without direct supervision.
