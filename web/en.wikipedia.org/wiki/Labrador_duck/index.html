Labrador duck

Labrador duck
Scientific name: Camptorhynchus labradorius
Status: Extinct (c. 1878)

The Labrador duck (Camptorhynchus labradorius) was a North American sea duck, striking in its black-and-white pied plumage in the male. It is considered the first known endemic North American bird species to become extinct after European colonization, with the last reliable record in 1875 and a specimen taken in 1878.

Description and habits

The species had an unusual bill, broad and flattened with many lamellae, suggesting it fed on small molluscs and crustaceans in shallow coastal waters. It bred somewhere in the far northeast of Canada, possibly Labrador, and wintered along the Atlantic coast from Nova Scotia to Chesapeake Bay.

Extinction

The causes of its extinction remain unclear. It was hunted, but not heavily, and its eggs were likely collected; changes in its food supply after human alteration of coastal ecosystems may have contributed. Some 55 museum specimens survive worldwide.
