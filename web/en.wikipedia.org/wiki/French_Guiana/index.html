French Guiana

French Guiana (French: Guyane) is an overseas department and region of France located on the northern coast of South America. It is bordered by Brazil to the east and south and Suriname to the west, with the Atlantic Ocean to the north. Its capital is Cayenne.


Capital: Cayenne
Area: about 83,534 km²
Status: Overseas department of France
Currency: Euro

Geography

The territory is largely covered by Amazon rainforest. A narrow coastal plain holds most of the population. The Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, used by the European Space Agency and Arianespace for rocket launches near the equator, is a major economic asset.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region long before French colonisation in the 17th century. France used it as a penal colony, including Devil's Island, from the 19th century until 1953 — a system made famous by Henri Charrière's Papillon. It became an overseas department in 1946.

Demographics and economy

The population, a few hundred thousand, is ethnically diverse — Creole, mainland French, Haitian, Brazilian, Hmong and Indigenous communities among others. See also French West Indies for other French overseas territories in the Americas.
