Province

Provinces are major subnational divisions in many states.

A province is almost always an administrative division within a country or state. The term derives from the ancient Roman provincia, the major territorial and administrative unit of the Roman Empire's possessions outside Italy. Since the term province has become a standard designation for the primary sub-governmental entities in many countries, the word has also acquired the broader sense of any territory outside the capital or metropolis — "the provinces" as opposed to the centre.

Modern usage

Provinces are the first-level divisions of, among others, Canada (ten provinces), China (22 provinces plus autonomous regions and municipalities), Italy, Spain (alongside autonomous communities), the Netherlands, Pakistan, South Africa, Argentina, Indonesia, and the Philippines. In federal systems such as Canada's, provinces hold constitutionally protected powers comparable to those of U.S. or Australian states. In unitary states they are administrative areas with varying degrees of self-government.

Other meanings

The word is also used for ecclesiastical provinces — groupings of dioceses under an archbishop in the Roman Catholic, Anglican and Orthodox churches — and for the administrative units of religious orders. In geology and biogeography, a "province" denotes a region with distinctive rock types or fauna.

Examples in this project

Specific provinces treated separately include the Italian Province of Brindisi and Province of Foggia, the historical Province of Massachusetts Bay, and the Provinces of Belgium.
