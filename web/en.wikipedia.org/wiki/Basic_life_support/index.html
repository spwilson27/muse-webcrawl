Basic life support

Basic life support (BLS)
Field: Emergency medicine, first aid
Key skills: CPR, AED use, airway obstruction relief
Guidelines: AHA, ERC, ILCOR
Sequence (adult): C-A-B (compressions, airway, breathing)

Basic life support (BLS) is the level of medical care used for victims of life-threatening illnesses or injuries until full medical care can be given at a hospital. It is provided by trained responders, including bystanders, and focuses on maintaining airway, breathing, and circulation without advanced equipment.

Chain of survival

BLS is the first links of the chain of survival: early recognition and call for help, early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), early defibrillation with an automated external defibrillator (AED), and early advanced life support. High-quality CPR emphasises chest compressions at 100–120 per minute, depth of 5–6 cm in adults, full recoil, and minimal interruptions.

Technique

The rescuer checks responsiveness and breathing, activates emergency services and sends for an AED, then begins compressions in the centre of the chest. Rescue breaths are given at a ratio of 30 compressions to 2 breaths by trained rescuers; untrained bystanders are encouraged to do compression-only CPR. Choking is treated with back blows and abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre) for adults and children over one year.

Organisations and training

Guidelines are issued and periodically revised by the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR), the American Heart Association, and the European Resuscitation Council. BLS certification is required for healthcare professionals and widely taught to the public and workplace first-aiders.
