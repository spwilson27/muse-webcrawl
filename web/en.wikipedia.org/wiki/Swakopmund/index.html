Swakopmund ("Mouth of the Swakop") is a coastal city in western Namibia, on the Atlantic at the mouth of the Swakop River, where the Namib Desert meets the sea. It is Namibia's premier beach resort and a centre for adventure tourism.

History

Founded in 1892 as the main harbour of German South West Africa — because Walvis Bay was British — Swakopmund filled with German colonial architecture: the Woermannhaus, the old railway station, the lighthouse and the jetty. After the First World War it passed to South African administration, and to independent Namibia in 1990.

Tourism

Visitors come for sandboarding and quad-biking on the dunes, skydiving, dolphin cruises and the nearby Moon Landscape and Welwitschia plains. Its cool Benguela-current climate, often foggy, contrasts sharply with the hot desert inland.
