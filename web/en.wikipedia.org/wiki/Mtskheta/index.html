Mtskheta (Georgian: მცხეთა) is a city in the Mtskheta-Mtianeti region of Georgia, about 20 km (12 mi) northwest of Tbilisi, at the confluence of the Mtkvari (Kura) and Aragvi rivers. One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Georgia, it was the capital of the ancient Kingdom of Iberia and remains a spiritual centre of the Georgian Orthodox Church.

History

According to tradition, Mtskheta was founded in the distant past and became the capital of Iberia in the early centuries of the first millennium. It was here that Saint Nino is said to have converted the Iberian king Mirian III to Christianity in the 330s, making Georgia one of the earliest Christian kingdoms. The capital was moved to Tbilisi by King Dachi in the early 6th century, but Mtskheta retained its religious importance as the seat of the Catholicos-Patriarch of Georgia.

Monuments

The city's great monuments are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Svetitskhoveli Cathedral, built in the 11th century on the site of earlier churches, is traditionally held to be the burial place of Christ's robe and of several Georgian kings. The Jvari Monastery (6th century) stands on a mountaintop overlooking the confluence of the two rivers, and the Samtavro Monastery, north of the centre, is associated with Saint Nino and the tombs of Mirian III and Queen Nana.
