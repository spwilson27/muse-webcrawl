Pérotin (fl. c. 1200) was a European composer, believed to be French, who worked at the cathedral of Notre Dame in Paris. He was the most famous member of the Notre Dame school of polyphony and one of the very few composers of his era known by name.

Life and sources

Almost nothing is known of Pérotin's life. He is mentioned by the anonymous English music theorist known as Anonymous IV, who praised him as "the best composer of discant" (optimum discantor) and credited him with revising Léonin' great book of organum, the Magnus liber organi, and with composing organa in three and four voices.

Works

Attributed to Pérotin are the four-voice organa Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes, written around 1198 for performance at Notre Dame — among the earliest surviving polyphonic works for four voices — as well as three-voice organa, conductus, and clausulae. His music stretches chant tenors into long rhythmic patterns beneath elaborate upper voices, a landmark in the development of Western polyphony and of rhythmic notation.
