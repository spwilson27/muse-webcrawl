Steering

Steering is the set of components and linkages that allows a vehicle (car, truck, bicycle, boat or aircraft) to follow a desired course. In wheeled vehicles the steering system turns the pivoted front wheels (and sometimes the rear wheels) in response to the driver's input at the steering wheel.

Mechanisms

The most common automobile arrangement is the rack and pinion, in which rotation of the pinion moves a toothed rack connected to the wheels; older and heavier vehicles often use recirculating-ball mechanisms. Steering geometry, notably the Ackermann geometry, arranges for the inner wheel to turn through a greater angle than the outer wheel so all wheels roll around a common centre.

Power steering

Power steering assists the driver using hydraulic pressure or an electric motor, reducing effort and allowing quicker steering ratios. Electric power steering has become dominant in new cars, and steer-by-wire systems, which replace the mechanical link with electronic signals, are entering production.
