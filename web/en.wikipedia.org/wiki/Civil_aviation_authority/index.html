Civil aviation authority

A civil aviation authority (CAA) is a national body responsible for regulating civil aviation within a country, including the licensing of personnel, certification of aircraft, oversight of airlines and air navigation services, and the implementation of safety standards. Most civil aviation authorities operate within the framework of the Chicago Convention of 1944 and the standards of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO).

Civil aviation authorities regulate airlines, aircraft and airspace safety.

Functions

Typical responsibilities include:

  • Issuing and renewing pilot, engineer and air traffic controller licences;
  • Certifying aircraft types and registering aircraft;
  • Approving airline operations through air operator certificates;
  • Regulating airports and air navigation services;
  • Investigating or overseeing responses to safety occurrences, often in cooperation with a separate accident investigation body;
  • Enforcing aviation security and consumer-protection rules.

Examples

Examples of civil aviation authorities include the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) in the United States, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) together with national authorities in Europe, the Civil Aviation Authority of the United Kingdom, the Civil Aviation Authority of New Zealand, the Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) in Australia, the Directorate General of Civil Aviation in India, and the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC).

History

National regulation of civil aviation began in the years after the First World War, with the 1919 Paris Convention establishing early international rules. The Chicago Convention of 1944 created ICAO and the modern framework under which national authorities harmonise their regulations while retaining sovereign control of their airspace.
