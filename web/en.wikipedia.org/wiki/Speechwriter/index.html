A speechwriter is a person who is hired to write speeches that will be delivered by another person. Speechwriters are employed by many senior-level elected officials and executives in both government and private industry, by university presidents and by celebrities.

Role

The speechwriter's task is to produce text that captures the speaker's voice, style and positions, and that suits the audience and occasion. The work typically involves researching the subject, consulting with the speaker and policy staff, and revising drafts through several rounds of edits. Because the speech is delivered as the speaker's own words, speechwriting is a form of ghostwriting, and the speechwriter usually remains anonymous.

Notable examples

Famous political speechwriters include Judson Welliver, often regarded as the first modern presidential speechwriter; Theodore Sorensen, who worked closely with John F. Kennedy; Peggy Noonan, who wrote for Ronald Reagan and coined phrases such as "a thousand points of light"; and Jon Favreau, chief speechwriter for Barack Obama. In the United States, the White House Office of Speechwriting is headed by the Director of Speechwriting.
