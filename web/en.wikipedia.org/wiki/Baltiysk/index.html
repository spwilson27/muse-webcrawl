Baltiysk

Baltiysk (Russian: Балтийск), prior to 1946 known by its German name Pillau, is a seaport town and the administrative centre of Baltiysky District in Kaliningrad Oblast, Russia, located on the northern part of the Vistula Spit, on the shore of the Strait of Baltiysk separating the Vistula Lagoon from the Baltic Sea. It is the westernmost town in Russia and the main base of the Russian Navy's Baltic Fleet.

History

The Prussian village of Pillau developed around a fortress built in the seventeenth century; the famous pentagonal Pillau citadel was begun by Sweden during the Thirty Years' War and completed by Prussia. Pillau became an important ice-free port for Königsberg, connected to the lagoon by the strait. In 1945, during World War II, Pillau was a major embarkation point for the evacuation of German refugees and troops by sea (Operation Hannibal). After the war the area passed to the Soviet Union, and in 1946 the town was renamed Baltiysk. The German population was expelled and replaced by Soviet settlers.

Naval base

Baltiysk is the principal base of the Baltic Fleet and one of Russia's few ice-free Baltic ports, giving it enduring strategic importance. A ferry service connects Baltiysk with St. Petersburg, and a monument to Peter the Great stands on the harbourfront.
