Mower

A mower is a machine designed to cut grass or other plants growing on the ground to an even height. Mowers range from small hand-pushed lawn mowers for domestic gardens to large tractor-drawn or self-propelled agricultural machines used to cut hay and forage crops.

History

The first lawn mower was patented by Edwin Beard Budding of Stroud, England, in 1830, adapted from machinery used to trim the nap on cloth. Early mowers were heavy cast-iron devices pushed or pulled by hand or horse. The advent of steam, petrol, and later electric power transformed mowing in the 20th century.

Types

Common types include rotary mowers, which use a spinning horizontal blade; cylinder or reel mowers, which shear grass between rotating blades and a fixed bedknife; and flail mowers for rough vegetation. Agricultural mowers include sickle-bar mowers, disc mowers, and mower-conditioners that crimp cut hay to speed drying.

Power sources

Modern mowers are powered by petrol engines, mains electricity, rechargeable batteries, or human effort. Robotic lawn mowers that operate autonomously have become increasingly common in domestic settings.
