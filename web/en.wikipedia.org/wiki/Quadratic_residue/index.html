Quadratic residue

In number theory, an integer q is a quadratic residue modulo n if it is congruent to a perfect square modulo n; otherwise it is a quadratic non-residue. The concept was central to Gauss's Disquisitiones Arithmeticae.

Properties

For an odd prime p, exactly half of the non-zero residues are quadratic residues. Euler's criterion states q(p−1)/2 ≡ ±1 (mod p), with +1 for residues. The law of quadratic reciprocity, proved by Gauss, relates solvability of x² ≡ p (mod q) and x² ≡ q (mod p) and is a cornerstone of algebraic number theory.

Applications

Quadratic residues are used in cryptography (Goldwasser–Micali, Rabin), pseudorandom generation (Legendre sequences), and acoustics (quadratic residue diffusers).
