Noise barrier

A noise barrier (also called a sound wall, sound berm or acoustic barrier) is an exterior structure designed to shield inhabitants of noise-sensitive areas from noise pollution, most commonly from roads, railways and industrial sites. By blocking the direct path of sound and forcing it to diffract over the top, a barrier typically reduces noise levels at receptors behind it by about 5–10 decibels.

Design

A barrier must be tall enough to break the line of sight between the noise source and the receiver, continuous without gaps, and massive enough (typically at least 20 kg/m²) that sound transmitted through it is negligible compared with sound diffracted over it. Materials include concrete, masonry, timber, metal and transparent acrylic panels; earth berms are used where space permits. Absorptive facings can reduce reflected sound toward the opposite side of the road.

History and use

Noise barriers were first built in the United States in the late 1960s and spread widely after the US Noise Control Act of 1972 and similar legislation elsewhere. They are now a common feature of highways in Europe, North America, Japan and Australia, where they mitigate traffic noise for nearby housing.

Limitations

Barriers are less effective for tall buildings overlooking them and for distant receptors, and they can raise aesthetic, shading and air-flow concerns. Their benefit is greatest close behind the barrier, in its "shadow zone".
