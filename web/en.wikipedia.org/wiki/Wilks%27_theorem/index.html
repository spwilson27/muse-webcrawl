Wilks' theorem

Wilks' theorem, named after the statistician Samuel S. Wilks, is a result in mathematical statistics concerning the asymptotic distribution of the likelihood-ratio test statistic. It is a cornerstone of likelihood-based hypothesis testing.

Statement

Consider a parametric model with parameter vector θ, and a null hypothesis that constrains θ to a sub-model. Wilks' theorem states that, under regularity conditions and as the sample size grows, minus twice the logarithm of the likelihood ratio — −2 ln λ, where λ is the ratio of the maximum likelihood under the null to the maximum likelihood under the full model — converges in distribution to a chi-squared distribution. The degrees of freedom equal the difference in the number of free parameters between the full model and the null model.

Use

The result allows p-values and confidence regions to be computed for likelihood-ratio tests without simulating the null distribution, and underlies deviance analysis in generalized linear models.
