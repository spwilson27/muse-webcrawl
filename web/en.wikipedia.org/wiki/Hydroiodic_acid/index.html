Hydroiodic acid

Hydroiodic acid (or hydriodic acid) is the aqueous solution of hydrogen iodide (HI). It is a strong acid — among the strongest of the common hydrohalic acids — and a powerful reducing agent. Commercial hydroiodic acid typically contains 48–57% HI by mass; the constant-boiling mixture (about 57% HI) boils at roughly 127 °C.

Properties and preparation

Hydrogen iodide is a colourless gas that fumes in moist air; its solution darkens on standing as dissolved oxygen oxidises iodide to iodine. HI can be prepared by reacting iodine with hydrazine or hydrogen sulfide, or industrially by the catalytic combination of hydrogen and iodine. Unlike hydrogen fluoride, HI is a strong acid because the large iodide ion stabilises charge and the H–I bond is weak; the acid strength of the hydrohalic acids increases down the group: HF < HCl < HBr < HI.

Uses

Hydroiodic acid is used in organic synthesis to cleave ethers (the Zeisel method for methoxy determination), to reduce various functional groups, and to prepare iodide salts. It is an intermediate in the Cativa process for acetic acid production, where HI is part of the catalytic cycle. Because it is also used in the illicit manufacture of methamphetamine, its sale is regulated in many jurisdictions.
