Terrain

Terrain or relief (also topographic relief) involves the vertical and horizontal dimensions of land surface. The term bathymetry is used when describing underwater relief, while hypsometry studies terrain relative to sea level. Terrain is commonly expressed in terms of elevation, slope, and the orientation (aspect) of landforms.

Shaded relief map of mountainous terrain

Description

Terrain is formed by the interaction of endogenic processes (tectonic uplift, volcanism) and exogenic processes (weathering, erosion and deposition by water, wind, ice and gravity). Major terrain types include mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys, hills and deserts. The scientific study of landforms and the processes that shape them is geomorphology.

Importance

Terrain strongly influences climate and weather patterns — mountain ranges force air to rise, producing orographic precipitation and rain shadows — as well as the distribution of water, ecosystems, agriculture, transport routes and settlement. Militaries study terrain for its effects on movement, cover and observation; engineers and planners analyse slope and aspect for construction, flood risk and land use. In cartography, terrain is represented by contour lines, spot heights, hillshading and digital elevation models (DEMs), increasingly derived from satellite radar, lidar and photogrammetry.
