Appropriation (art)

Appropriation in art is the use of pre-existing objects or images with little or no transformation applied to them. The use of appropriation has played a significant role in the history of the arts (literary, visual, musical and performing arts).

History

Appropriation can be traced to the cubist collages and constructions of Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque made from 1912 onward. Marcel Duchamp's readymades, notably the Fountain (1917), radically extended the idea. In the mid-20th century Pop artists such as Andy Warhol and Roy Lichtenstein appropriated commercial imagery.

Pictures Generation

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, artists including Sherrie Levine, Richard Prince, Barbara Kruger and Cindy Sherman — associated with the Pictures Generation — rephotographed or reused existing images to question authorship, originality and gender. Levine's After Walker Evans (1981) is a canonical example.

Copyright and fair use

Appropriation raises legal questions about copyright infringement and fair use. Landmark cases include Rogers v. Koons (1992) and Cariou v. Prince (2013), which found some of Prince's works transformative. Debates continue over sampling in music and digital remix culture.
