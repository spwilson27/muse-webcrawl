LogMAR chart

A LogMAR chart (Logarithm of the Minimum Angle of Resolution) is a chart consisting of rows of letters that is used by ophthalmologists, optometrists and vision scientists to estimate visual acuity. It was developed at the National Vision Research Institute of Australia in 1976 by Ian Bailey and Jan Lovie, and is designed to enable a more accurate estimate of acuity than other charts.

A Bailey–Lovie LogMAR chart.

Design

The chart follows three principles: equal legibility of letters on each line, equal spacing between letters and rows, and a logarithmic progression of letter sizes (each row is 0.1 log units, a ratio of about 1.26, smaller than the one above). Each line has five letters, and scores can be calculated per letter (0.02 log units each), giving finer gradations and allowing partial-line credit, unlike the all-or-nothing lines of the traditional Snellen chart.

Scoring

LogMAR scores run opposite to intuition: 0.0 corresponds to standard 6/6 (20/20) vision, negative values indicate better-than-standard acuity, and each 0.1 increase in LogMAR corresponds to one line worse on the chart. Because of its statistical properties, LogMAR measurement — often implemented as the ETDRS chart, developed for the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study — is considered the gold standard in vision research and clinical trials.

Advantages

Compared with Snellen charts, LogMAR charts give more consistent measurements at all acuity levels, especially for low vision, and their scores are suitable for statistical averaging and analysis.
