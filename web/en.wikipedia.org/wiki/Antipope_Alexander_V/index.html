Antipope Alexander V

Bornc. 1339, Crete
Died3 May 1410, Bologna
ClaimElected pope by the Council of Pisa, 1409
Birth namePetros Philarges

Alexander V (born Petros Philarges, Peter of Candia) is considered by the Catholic Church an antipope. A Franciscan friar of Greek origin born on Crete, he studied at Oxford and Paris, became Archbishop of Milan in 1402 and a cardinal in 1405.

Pisan claim

During the Western Schism, the Council of Pisa (1409) deposed both the Roman pope Gregory XII and the Avignon pope Benedict XIII and elected Philarges as Alexander V in June 1409. Because neither rival resigned, the Church was left with three claimants. Alexander enjoyed wide recognition — in France, England, Bohemia and much of Italy — but died suddenly at Bologna in May 1410, amid later rumours (unsubstantiated) of poisoning by his successor, the Pisan antipope John XXIII. The schism was finally resolved at the Council of Constance (1414–1418).
