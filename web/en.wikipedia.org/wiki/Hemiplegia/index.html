Hemiplegia

Hemiplegia is a condition in which one side of the body is paralysed, caused by damage to the motor areas of the opposite cerebral hemisphere or to the corticospinal tract. It differs from hemiparesis, which denotes weakness rather than complete paralysis on one side.

Causes

The most common cause is stroke; other causes include traumatic brain injury, cerebral palsy (spastic hemiplegia is a common subtype in children), brain tumours, multiple sclerosis, and infections. Transient hemiplegia can accompany migraine (hemiplegic migraine) or follow seizures (Todd's paresis).

Signs and management

Hemiplegia in adults typically shows a spastic pattern, with stiffness and exaggerated reflexes in the affected arm and leg. Treatment centres on the underlying cause and on rehabilitation—physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy where language is affected—together with management of spasticity and prevention of complications such as contractures and shoulder pain.
