Otome game

An otome game (Japanese: 乙女ゲーム, otome gēmu, literally "maiden game") is a story-based romance video game targeted towards women, in which the player controls a female protagonist who interacts with — and usually pursues romantic relationships with — a cast of male characters. Generally one of the goals, besides developing the romance, is to develop a relationship by choosing dialogue and actions that raise the affection of the selected partner. Otome games are a major segment of the Japanese dating sim and visual novel market.

History

The genre is generally credited to begin with Angelique, released in 1994 by Koei for the Super Famicom. Developed by Ruby Party, Koei's all-female development studio, Angelique combined elements of strategy games, in which the heroine competed to become queen of a cosmos, with romance options among nine male guardians. Its success established Ruby Party as the pioneer of the genre.

Subsequent landmark titles include Konami's Tokimeki Memorial Girl's Side (2002) and Idea Factory's Otomate label, launched in 2007, whose titles such as Hakuoki (2008) — a historical fantasy set among the Shinsengumi — became multimedia franchises spawning anime, manga, stage plays and merchandise. From the 2010s, otome games found a large new audience on smartphones, with free-to-play and episodic titles such as Ikemen Sengoku and Voltage Inc.'s story apps, and the genre has increasingly been localised for Western markets.

Gameplay and characteristics

Most otome games follow the visual novel format: the story is told through text, character sprites and voiced dialogue (the heroine is often unvoiced and sometimes shown with obscured eyes so players can project themselves onto her). Players make choices that branch the story into routes for each love interest, leading to multiple endings. Common settings include school life, historical Japan, fantasy kingdoms and show-business. Many titles feature celebrated male voice actors, whose performances are themselves a major selling point.

Related genres

Otome games are the female-audience counterpart of bishōjo games aimed at men. Boys' love (BL) games, which depict male–male romance, form a closely related but distinct market, also largely consumed by women.
