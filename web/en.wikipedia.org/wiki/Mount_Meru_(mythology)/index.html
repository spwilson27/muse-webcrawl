Mount Meru (mythology)

Mount Meru in Hindu cosmology

Mount Meru, also called Sumeru, is the sacred five-peaked mountain of Hindu, Jain and Buddhist cosmology. It is considered the centre of all the physical, metaphysical and spiritual universes — the axis of the world around which the sun, moon and stars revolve.

Description

In Hindu texts such as the Puranas, Meru is described as being 84,000 yojanas high, with its peak made of gold and serving as the abode of Brahma and other deities. The great rivers, including the Ganges, are said to flow from its summit. The continent of Jambudvipa surrounds it, and the mountain's slopes are associated with different heavens and realms.

In different traditions

In Jain cosmology, Meru stands at the centre of the middle world (Madhyaloka) and features prominently in the geography of the universe and in the rituals surrounding the birth of a Tirthankara, who is anointed on its summit. In Buddhism, Sumeru is the centre of the world-system, surrounded by concentric seas and mountain ranges, with the heaven of the Thirty-Three Gods (Trāyastriḿśa) on its summit, ruled by Śakra. Buddhist temples such as Angkor Wat in Cambodia were designed as architectural representations of Mount Meru, with five towers symbolising its peaks.
