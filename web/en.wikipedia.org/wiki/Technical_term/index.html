Technical term

A technical term is a word or phrase that has a specialised, precise meaning within a particular field of knowledge, profession, or activity. Technical terms form part of terminology — the vocabulary of a discipline — and are closely related to jargon, though "jargon" often carries a negative connotation of unnecessarily obscure language, whereas technical terms exist primarily to express concepts exactly and economically.

Purpose

Technical terms allow practitioners to communicate complex ideas precisely and briefly. A single term may encapsulate a concept that would otherwise require a lengthy description, and its meaning is fixed by definition within the field, reducing ambiguity. For example, in law, medicine, engineering, and computing, terms such as "tort", "myocardial infarction", "torque", and "cache" have definitions that differ from, or are narrower than, their everyday senses.

Formation

New technical terms arise through several processes: coining from Greek and Latin roots (common in the natural sciences and medicine), borrowing from other languages, acronyms and initialisms, eponyms (terms named after people), and the specialisation of ordinary words given a technical sense. Standardisation bodies, professional societies, and nomenclature commissions (for example, IUPAC in chemistry and the ICZN in zoology) regulate official terminology in many disciplines.

Criticism and accessibility

Heavy use of technical terms can exclude lay audiences and is sometimes criticised as obfuscation, whether deliberate or not. Plain-language movements in law, medicine, and government encourage writers to explain or avoid jargon when communicating with the public, while retaining technical precision where it is needed.
