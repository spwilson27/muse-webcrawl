Wuppertal Schwebebahn

The Wuppertal Schwebebahn (Wuppertal Suspension Railway) is a suspension railway in Wuppertal, Germany. Opened in 1901, it is the oldest electric elevated railway with hanging cars in the world and remains in daily use as a public transport system. The line is 13.3 kilometres (8.3 mi) long, much of it running about 12 metres (39 ft) above the surface of the river Wupper, and carries tens of thousands of passengers per day.

History

The system was designed by Eugen Langen, who had earlier trialled a suspended monorail at Deutz. Construction began in 1898, and the first section opened on 1 March 1901; Kaiser Wilhelm II had taken a test ride in the imperial car (Kaiserwagen) in October 1900. The railway proved a success in the industrial Wupper valley, where the river and the crowded streets left little room for a conventional elevated line.

Incidents

In 1950 the Althoff Circus organised a publicity stunt in which a young elephant named Tuffi was carried on the train; frightened, she charged through the side of the car and fell into the river below, suffering only minor injuries — Tuffi became a local mascot and her image is still used in Wuppertal. The railway's only fatal accident occurred on 12 April 1999, when a train struck a steel claw left on the track during maintenance work near Robert-Daum-Platz station and derailed into the Wupper, killing five people.

Operation

The line has 20 stations and a journey time of about 30 minutes. New Generation 15 trains, built by Vossloh Kiepe, entered service from 2016, replacing the fleet that had operated since the 1970s.
