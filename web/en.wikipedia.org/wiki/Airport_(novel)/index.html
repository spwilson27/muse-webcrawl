Airport (novel)

Airport is a bestselling 1968 novel by British-Canadian writer Arthur Hailey. Set over roughly seven hours of a single stormy evening at the fictional Lincoln International Airport near Chicago, the novel interweaves the personal and professional crises of airport manager Mel Bakersfeld, his brother Keith, an air traffic controller haunted by guilt over a past collision, a veteran pilot, and a desperate unemployed man who boards a Rome-bound flight with a homemade bomb in order that his wife may collect his life insurance.

First-edition cover of Airport (1968).

Plot and reception

Hailey researched the novel by spending time in airports and with airline staff, and the book is noted for its detailed, procedural depiction of airport operations during a blizzard: a disabled aircraft blocking the main runway, a threatened closure, stowaways, protesters against noise, and the race to save the damaged airliner whose bomb has exploded in flight. Airport spent 64 weeks on the New York Times bestseller list and was the best-selling novel of 1968.

Film adaptation

The 1970 film Airport, produced by Ross Hunter and starring Burt Lancaster and Dean Martin, was a major box-office success and helped launch the 1970s cycle of disaster films, followed by sequels Airport 1975, Airport '77 and The Concorde ... Airport '79, as well as the parody Airplane! (1980). See also Arthur Hailey and Airport (1970 film).
