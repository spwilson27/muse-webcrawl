An identity document (also called a piece of identification or ID) is any document that may be used to prove the identity of a person. Issued in a standard format, such as a card, it typically bears the holder's photograph, name, date of birth and other identifying details, and increasingly a chip storing biometric data. The most widely recognised identity documents are passports for international travel and national identity cards for domestic use.

History

Early identification papers included letters of introduction and internal passports in imperial Russia and Qing China. Systematic photographic identification developed in the nineteenth century, and the First World War made passports a standard requirement for crossing borders. Many European states introduced compulsory national identity cards in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries, while other countries — notably the United Kingdom and the United States — rely on documents issued for other purposes, such as driving licences, for day-to-day identification.

Types

  • Passport: certifies identity and nationality for international travel; modern e-passports follow ICAO standards with a machine-readable zone and contactless chip.
  • National identity card: compulsory in many countries (e.g. the German Personalausweis, the Spanish DNI, China's resident identity card, India's Aadhaar card).
  • Driving licence: certifies permission to drive and serves as de facto ID in countries without a national card.
  • Residence permits and voter ID cards serve specific populations and purposes.

Security features and biometrics

Documents incorporate holograms, microprinting, UV-visible inks, laser engraving and embedded chips to resist forgery. Biometric identifiers — facial images, fingerprints and iris scans — bind the document to its holder. Databases linked to national ID systems raise ongoing debates about privacy, surveillance and exclusion of people unable to register.
