The International Harvester Company (IHC, later IH) was an American manufacturer of agricultural and construction equipment, automobiles, commercial trucks and household products. It was formed in 1902 by the merger of the McCormick Harvesting Machine Company, the Deering Harvester Company and three smaller firms.

History

The merger, financed by J. P. Morgan, created a dominant force in farm machinery. The company became known for the Farmall line of tractors introduced in 1924, the Scout off-road vehicle, and its range of trucks.

Decline and breakup

Financial difficulties and a costly strike in 1979–80 weakened the company. In 1985 it sold its agricultural division to Tenneco's Case Corporation, forming Case IH, and renamed itself Navistar International, continuing as a truck and engine manufacturer.
