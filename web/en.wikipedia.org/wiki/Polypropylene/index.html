Polypropylene

Polypropylene (PP), also known as polypropene, is a thermoplastic polymer used in a wide variety of applications. It is produced via chain-growth polymerization from the monomer propylene. Polypropylene belongs to the group of polyolefins and is partially crystalline and non-polar. Its properties are similar to polyethylene, but it is slightly harder and more heat resistant.

History

Crystalline isotactic polypropylene was first produced in 1954 by Giulio Natta and the German chemist Karl Rehn, using Ziegler–Natta catalysts; Natta shared the 1963 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for this work. Commercial production began in 1957.

Properties and uses

Polypropylene is the second-most widely produced commodity plastic (after polyethylene), with a global market measured in tens of millions of tonnes. It is used in packaging, textiles (ropes, carpets), laboratory equipment, automotive components, reusable containers, and banknotes in some countries. It carries the resin identification code 5.
