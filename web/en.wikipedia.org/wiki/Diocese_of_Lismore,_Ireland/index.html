Diocese of Lismore, Ireland

The Diocese of Lismore was a historic diocese in Ireland centred on the monastic town of Lismore, County Waterford. Founded in the 7th century around the monastery established by St Carthage (Mochuda) around 635, Lismore became one of the great schools of early medieval Ireland, famed for its learning and its metalwork, including the Lismore Crozier.

At the Synod of Rathbreasail in 1111 Lismore was recognised as a diocese in the province of Cashel, and its bishops attended later medieval synods and parliaments. Its cathedral, dedicated to St Carthage, stands on the site of the early monastery beside Lismore Castle.

Following the Reformation, separate Church of Ireland and Roman Catholic successions continued. In the Church of Ireland the see was united with Waterford in the 19th century (and later became part of the Diocese of Cashel and Ossory), while the Roman Catholic diocese was united with Waterford in 1363 in practice and formally as the Diocese of Waterford and Lismore, which continues today.
