John Mackie

John Leslie Mackie (25 August 1917 – 12 December 1981) was an Australian philosopher, originally from Sydney, who spent most of his career at the University of Oxford. He made significant contributions to the philosophy of religion, metaphysics, and ethics, and is best known for defending moral skepticism and error theory.

Life

Mackie studied at the University of Sydney under John Anderson, whose philosophical realism influenced his early work. During World War II he served in the Middle East. He held chairs at the University of Otago in New Zealand and the University of Sydney before moving to Oxford in 1967, where he became a fellow of University College.

Ethics and error theory

In Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong (1977), Mackie argued that moral judgments are systematically false because they claim objective, categorical values that do not exist — his "argument from queerness." He summarized his position with the famous opening line: "There are no objective values."

Philosophy of religion

His posthumously completed The Miracle of Theism (1982) is a major critique of arguments for the existence of God, particularly the argument from evil, and is regarded as a classic of atheist philosophy.
