Jardin des Plantes

The Jardin des Plantes is the main botanical garden in France and one of seven departments of the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle. It is situated in the 5th arrondissement of Paris, on the left bank of the river Seine, and covers 28 hectares (280,000 m²).

History

Founded in 1626 as a royal garden of medicinal plants under Guy de La Brosse, physician to Louis XIII, the garden opened to the public in 1640. During the French Revolution it became part of the newly founded Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle.

Features

The garden includes formal French gardens, an alpine garden, greenhouses, a rose garden, and the Ménagerie, one of the oldest zoos in the world, founded in 1794. The Grande Galerie de l'Évolution and the Gallery of Paleontology and Comparative Anatomy are also located on the site.
