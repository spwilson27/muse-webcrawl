Speyside single malt

Speyside single malts are single malt Scotch whiskies produced in the Speyside region of north-east Scotland, along the valley of the River Spey and its tributaries. Speyside contains more than half of all Scotland's malt whisky distilleries — around fifty — making it the most densely distillery-populated whisky region in the world, and it is legally recognised as a distinct "locality" within the Highland region under the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009.

Character

Speyside whiskies are traditionally regarded as the most complex and elegant of Scotch malts. Two broad styles predominate: light, grassy, fruity whiskies such as Glenlivet and Glenfiddich, and rich, sherried whiskies such as Macallan, Aberlour, and Glenfarclas, matured in former sherry casks and noted for dried-fruit, spice, and chocolate flavours. Peat smoke, dominant in Islay malts, is usually absent or very light in Speyside.

Notable distilleries

  • The Glenlivet — founded 1824, the first licensed distillery in the parish; long the benchmark Speyside style.
  • Glenfiddich — founded 1886 in Dufftown; pioneer of the single malt category in the 1960s and one of the world's best-selling single malts.
  • The Macallan — famed for sherry-oak maturation and some of the most valuable whiskies sold at auction.
  • Aberlour, Balvenie, Glenfarclas, Cardhu, Mortlach, Cragganmore, Strathisla — among many other well-known producers.

Dufftown, the region's unofficial capital, hosts an annual whisky festival, and the Malt Whisky Trail links several distilleries open to visitors. The region's abundance of distilleries reflects its pure water sources, barley-growing land, and a history of illicit distilling in remote glens during the era of heavy excise duty, followed by legalisation after the Excise Act 1823.
