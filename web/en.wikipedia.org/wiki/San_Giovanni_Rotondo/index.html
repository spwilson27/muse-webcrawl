San Giovanni Rotondo

San Giovanni Rotondo
Country: Italy
Region: Apulia (province of Foggia)
Population: about 26,000
Known for: Shrine of Padre Pio

San Giovanni Rotondo is a town and comune in the province of Foggia, Apulia, southern Italy. It is world-famous as the home of Saint Pio of Pietrelcina (Padre Pio), the Capuchin friar and stigmatist who lived and died there, and its Sanctuary of Saint Mary of the Graces and the Padre Pio Pilgrimage Church draw millions of pilgrims each year, making it one of the most visited Catholic shrines in the world.

History

The town was founded in 1095 on a site where, according to tradition, a temple of Janus had stood; its name references a circular (rotondo) baptistery dedicated to Saint John the Baptist. For most of its history it was a modest agricultural town in the Gargano hills.

Padre Pio

In 1916 the young friar Padre Pio was assigned to the friary of Santa Maria delle Grazie at San Giovanni Rotondo, where he remained until his death in 1968. He received the stigmata in 1918 and became famous as a confessor and mystic. Near the shrine he founded the Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza ("Home for the Relief of Suffering"), a large hospital inaugurated in 1956. The modern pilgrimage church, designed by Renzo Piano and consecrated in 2004, can hold thousands; Padre Pio's body rests in its crypt and was displayed for veneration after his exhumation in 2008.
