Harrow on the Hill

Harrow on the Hill is a locality and historic village in the London Borough of Harrow in Greater London, England. The village occupies a prominent hill — one of the highest points in London at about 124 metres (408 feet) — and is best known as the home of Harrow School, founded in 1572.

History

The Church of St Mary, dating in part to the 11th century, stands at the hill's summit; its spire is a landmark visible across north-west London. The suicide of a schoolboy viewer inspired a famous 1822 poem associated with the churchyard. Notable former residents and visitors include Lord Byron, who spent school holidays nearby and wrote of the view in "Lines Written Beneath an Elm".

The area today

High Street and the surrounding conservation area preserve Georgian and Victorian buildings, pubs, and the school's historic core, contrasting with the modern suburb of Harrow below. Harrow-on-the-Hill station provides Metropolitan line and Chiltern Railways services.
