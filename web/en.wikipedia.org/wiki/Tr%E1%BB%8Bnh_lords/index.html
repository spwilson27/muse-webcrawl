The Trịnh lords (Vietnamese: Chúa Trịnh) were a family of de facto rulers of northern Vietnam (Đàng Ngoài, known to Europeans as Tonkin) from 1545 to 1787. Nominally loyal ministers of the Later Lê dynasty emperors, the Trịnh in practice held all real power, ruling from Thăng Long (modern Hanoi) while the emperors reigned as figureheads.

History

The family rose under Trịnh Kiểm, son-in-law of Nguyễn Kim, who led the struggle against the Mạc dynasty to restore the Lê emperors. His son Trịnh Tùng completed the capture of Thăng Long in 1592 and established the hereditary dominance of the family. From 1627 to 1672 the Trịnh fought the long Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War against the Nguyễn lords of the south, which ended in a de facto partition of Vietnam along the Gianh River.

During the 17th and 18th centuries, the Trịnh regime (in which the Trịnh lord held the title of Vương, "King/Prince," beside the Lê emperor) maintained a large court and army, conducted diplomacy with China and European trading companies, and presided over a flourishing commercial life at Phố Hiến and Thăng Long. In the 1780s, internal rebellions and the Tây Sơn offensives destroyed Trịnh power; the last lord, Trịnh Bồng, fled the capital in 1786.
