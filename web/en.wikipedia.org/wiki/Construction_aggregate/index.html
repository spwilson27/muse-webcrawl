Construction aggregate

Construction aggregate, or simply aggregate, is a broad category of coarse- to medium-grained particulate material used in construction, including sand, gravel, crushed stone, slag, recycled concrete and geosynthetic aggregates. Aggregates are the most mined materials in the world.

Uses

Aggregates are used as a stable foundation or road/rail base with predictable, uniform properties, or to help bind other materials together. In concrete, aggregates provide compressive strength and bulk while reducing the cement required; in asphalt, they form the structural skeleton of pavements.

Types

  • Coarse aggregate: crushed stone or gravel, typically 5–40 mm, used in concrete and road base.
  • Fine aggregate: sand and crushed fines below about 5 mm, used in mortar and concrete.
  • Recycled aggregate: crushed concrete, brick or asphalt reclaimed from demolition.
  • Lightweight aggregate: expanded clay, shale or pumice for lightweight concrete.

Sources

Natural aggregates are won from quarries and pits. Specifications such as ASTM C33 govern grading, cleanliness and durability, since contaminated or weak aggregate degrades concrete performance.
