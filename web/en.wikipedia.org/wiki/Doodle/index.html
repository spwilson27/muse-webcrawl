Doodle

A doodle is a drawing made while a person's attention is otherwise occupied. Doodles are simple drawings that can have concrete representational meaning or may be composed of random and abstract lines or shapes, generally without ever lifting the drawing device from the paper, in which case it is usually called a scribble.

Description

Doodling most often takes place during lectures, meetings, or telephone conversations, and in the margins of notebooks. Common subjects include geometric shapes, patterns, faces, cartoons, landscapes, and written words. Children and students doodle frequently, but the habit appears across ages and professions: doodles preserved in manuscripts range from medieval monks' marginalia to the drawings of presidents and poets.

Psychology

Psychologists have studied doodling in relation to memory, concentration and daydreaming. A widely cited 2009 study by Jackie Andrade found that participants who doodled while listening to a recorded message recalled more details than a control group, suggesting doodling may help maintain arousal and attention during monotonous tasks. Other interpretations relate doodles to creativity and mind-wandering.

Etymology

The word doodle first appeared in the early 17th century to mean a fool or simpleton. Its modern sense of an aimless drawing is recorded from the early 20th century, possibly influenced by the verb dawdle.

In popular culture

The song "Yankee Doodle" popularised the term in America. The search engine Google has made the word familiar worldwide through its "Google Doodles", temporary logo illustrations celebrating holidays and notable people.
