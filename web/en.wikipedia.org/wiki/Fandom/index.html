Fandom

Fandom
DefinitionSubculture of fans sharing an interest in a work, person or hobby
ActivitiesConventions, fan fiction, fan art, cosplay, wikis
Notable fandomsScience fiction, anime, K-pop, sports, video games

A fandom is a subculture composed of fans characterised by a feeling of empathy and camaraderie with others who share a common interest — in a book, film, television series, band, sports team, game or celebrity. Fans typically spend significant time and money on their interest, produce creative works and build communities ranging from local clubs to vast online networks.

History

Organised fandom emerged in the 1920s and 1930s among science-fiction readers, whose letters to pulp magazines led to fan clubs, fanzines and conventions; the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) was held in 1939. Media fandom expanded with Star Trek in the 1960s–70s, whose largely female fan community pioneered fan fiction and fan conventions. The internet radically enlarged fandom from the 1990s onward, enabling instant global communities, fan wikis, and archives such as Archive of Our Own (AO3), founded in 2008.

Activities

Common fan activities include writing fan fiction, drawing fan art, cosplay, editing fan videos, maintaining wikis and databases, collecting memorabilia, and attending conventions. Some fandoms adopt collective names — such as "Trekkies", "Whovians", "ARMY" (BTS) or "Swifties" (Taylor Swift). Scholars of fan studies, such as Henry Jenkins, describe fans as "textual poachers" who actively remake media rather than passively consuming it.

Fan–creator relations

Creators and rights holders have mixed relationships with fandom: fan labour provides free promotion, but fan works raise copyright questions generally handled through tolerance or licences. Some franchises encourage participation, while organised fan campaigns have influenced casting, renewals and content decisions.
