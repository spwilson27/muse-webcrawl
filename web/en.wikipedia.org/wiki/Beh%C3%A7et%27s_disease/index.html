Behçet's disease

SpecialtyRheumatology, immunology
Named afterHulusi Behçet
DistributionMost common along the historic Silk Road

Behçet's disease is a rare chronic inflammatory disorder of unknown cause, characterised by recurrent oral and genital ulcers, eye inflammation (uveitis) and skin lesions. It is a form of variable-vessel vasculitis that can affect arteries and veins of all sizes, as well as the joints, gastrointestinal tract and nervous system. It is named after the Turkish dermatologist Hulusi Behçet, who described the syndrome in 1937.

Signs and symptoms

Recurrent mouth ulcers are usually the first manifestation and occur in almost all patients. Genital ulcers, acne-like skin lesions and erythema nodosum are common. Eye involvement can cause blindness if untreated; vascular involvement may produce aneurysms and thrombosis, and neurological disease (neuro-Behçet's) affects a minority of patients. The pathergy test — an exaggerated skin response to a needle prick — is a characteristic finding.

Diagnosis and treatment

There is no specific laboratory test; diagnosis is clinical, using criteria such as those of the International Study Group (1990) and the International Criteria for Behçet's Disease. Prevalence is highest in Turkey and along the ancient Silk Road through the Middle East to Japan, and the disease is associated with the HLA-B51 allele. Treatment uses immunosuppressive drugs — colchicine, corticosteroids, azathioprine and anti-TNF agents — tailored to the organs involved.
