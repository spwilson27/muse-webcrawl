Regulation B governs how creditors may evaluate credit applications

Regulation B is a United States federal regulation, codified at 12 CFR Part 1002, that implements the Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA). It prohibits creditors from discriminating against credit applicants on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (provided the applicant has the capacity to contract), because all or part of the applicant's income derives from a public assistance program, or because the applicant has in good faith exercised any right under the Consumer Credit Protection Act.

History

The regulation was originally issued by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System in 1975 following the enactment of ECOA in 1974, and was substantially revised in 1976 when the Act's protected bases were extended beyond sex and marital status. Rulemaking authority transferred to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) under the Dodd–Frank Act in 2011, when the regulation was republished as Part 1002.

Key provisions

Regulation B restricts the information a creditor may request (for example, it generally may not ask about an applicant's spouse except in specified circumstances, and may not require signatures of a spouse on individual credit), and it prescribes rules for evaluating applications, including requirements to consider reliable income such as part-time work, alimony and child support if the applicant chooses to disclose it. It requires creditors to notify applicants of action taken within 30 days and, on adverse action, to provide a statement of specific reasons or notice of the right to obtain them. It also governs record retention, credit scoring system standards, special purpose credit programs, and the collection of monitoring information in mortgage lending.

Enforcement

ECOA and Regulation B are enforced by the CFPB, federal banking regulators, the Department of Justice (for pattern-or-practice violations), and through private lawsuits by aggrieved applicants. The regulation is a principal legal tool against redlining and discriminatory lending in consumer and business credit.
