Constantin von Economo

Constantin Alexander Freiherr Economo von San Serff (21 August 1876 – 21 October 1931) was an Austrian psychiatrist and neurologist of Greek origin. He is best known for identifying encephalitis lethargica (von Economo's encephalitis) and for his work on the cytoarchitecture of the human cerebral cortex.

Work

Working in Vienna, Economo described the epidemic of encephalitis lethargica that began during the First World War, a disease that left many survivors with severe parkinsonism. With Georg N. Koskinas he published the monumental atlas Die Cytoarchitektonik der Hirnrinde des erwachsenen Menschen (1925), which divided the cortex into 107 areas. He also described the large spindle-shaped neurons of the frontoinsular cortex and anterior cingulate cortex, now known as von Economo neurons, which are implicated in social cognition.

Life

Economo was born in Brăila, Romania, to a wealthy Greek family, and grew up in Trieste. Besides medicine, he was a pioneer aviator and balloonist. He died in Vienna in 1931.
