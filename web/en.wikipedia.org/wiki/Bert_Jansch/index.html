Herbert Jansch (3 November 1943 – 5 October 2011) was a Scottish folk musician and founding member of the band Pentangle. Regarded as one of the most influential acoustic guitarists of all time, he pioneered the "folk baroque" style, blending British traditional music with blues and jazz.

Career

Born in Glasgow and raised in Edinburgh, Jansch was inspired by American bluesmen such as Big Bill Broonzy and by the guitarist Davy Graham, whose piece "Anji" he recorded on his self-titled debut album in 1965. That album, recorded on borrowed equipment, established his reputation in the London folk clubs. With fellow guitarist John Renbourn he recorded Bert and John (1966) and in 1967 the two formed Pentangle with Jacqui McShee, Danny Thompson and Terry Cox, fusing folk, jazz and blues to international acclaim.

Influence and later life

Jansch's intricate fingerstyle playing influenced generations of guitarists, including Jimmy Page — Led Zeppelin's "Black Mountain Side" was adapted from Jansch's arrangement of "Black Water Side" — as well as Johnny Marr, Bernard Butler and Neil Young, who called him the Jimi Hendrix of the acoustic guitar. After Pentangle's original run ended in 1973 he continued a prolific solo career, recording more than twenty albums. He received two Lifetime Achievement Awards from BBC Radio 2's Folk Awards and was honoured, with Pentangle, for his contribution to British folk music. He died of lung cancer in 2011.
