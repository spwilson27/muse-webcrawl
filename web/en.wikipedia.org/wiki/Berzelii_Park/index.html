Berzelii Park (Swedish: Berzelii park) is a small public park in central Stockholm, Sweden, in the Norrmalm district at the head of the Nybroviken bay, between Nybroplan and the Hallwyl Museum area. It is named after the Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius (1779–1848), whose statue stands at its centre.

History

The park was laid out in the 1850s on land reclaimed at the inner end of Nybroviken, part of the 19th-century reshaping of central Stockholm. The bronze statue of Berzelius, by the sculptor Carl Gustaf Qvarnström, was unveiled in 1858 and is regarded as the first publicly erected statue of a non-royal person in Sweden — a monument to a scientist funded by the Swedish Academy of Sciences. Surrounding the park are several notable institutions: the Royal Dramatic Theatre (Dramaten) at Nybroplan, the Hallwyl Museum, and the classic restaurant Berns Salonger, which opened in 1863 facing the park.

Layout

The park is a formally planted urban square with lawns, flower beds, and rows of trees, overlooked by the China Theatre (Chinateatern). It serves as a busy pedestrian junction linking Strandvägen and the Östermalm district with the city centre.
