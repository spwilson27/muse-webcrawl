Abarth & C. S.p.A. is an Italian racing and road car maker founded by Austro-Italian Carlo Abarth in 1949. Its logo is a scorpion, the founder's zodiac sign, on a red and yellow shield. Long associated with Fiat, Abarth became the performance division of the Fiat group and, since 2021, part of Stellantis.

History

Carlo Abarth, a former motorcycle racer and technical director of the Cisitalia racing team, established Abarth & C. in Bologna in 1949, moving later to Turin. The company first produced exhaust systems and tuning kits for Fiat, Alfa Romeo, Lancia, and Simca cars, then complete competition cars.

Racing success

Abarth cars dominated small-displacement racing in the 1950s and 1960s, setting numerous speed records at Monza. After Fiat acquired the company in 1971, Abarth became its official racing department, preparing rally cars such as the Fiat 131 Abarth, which won World Rally Championship manufacturers' titles in 1977, 1978, and 1980. The department later contributed to Lancia's rally programme, including the Delta S4.

Relaunch

In 2007 Fiat relaunched Abarth as a standalone marque with models based on the Fiat 500 and Punto. The Abarth 500/595 range of hot hatches and the 124 Spider roadster became its best-known modern products. From 2023 the brand began electrifying with the Abarth 500e and 600e.

Models

Historic models include the Abarth 750, 850 TC, 1000, OT series, and the Simca-Abarth 1300. Modern production centres on tuned versions of Fiat platforms wearing the scorpion badge.
