Israeli–Lebanese conflict

The Israeli–Lebanese conflict is a long-running series of military engagements involving Israel, Lebanon, and various armed groups operating from Lebanese territory. Major episodes include the 1978 and 1982 Israeli invasions of Lebanon and the 2006 Lebanon War with Hezbollah.

LocationIsrael–Lebanon border, southern Lebanon
PartiesIsrael; Lebanon; PLO; Hezbollah; Amal
Key events1978 Litani operation, 1982 Lebanon War, 2006 Lebanon War

Background

After the Palestine Liberation Organization established bases in southern Lebanon following its expulsion from Jordan in 1970, cross-border attacks and Israeli reprisals escalated. Israel invaded in March 1978 (Operation Litani) and again in June 1982, besieging Beirut and forcing the PLO to evacuate to Tunis.

Occupation and withdrawal

Israel maintained a "security zone" in southern Lebanon until its unilateral withdrawal in May 2000. The Shia movement Hezbollah, formed during the 1980s occupation, became the dominant armed force in the south.

2006 war

A Hezbollah cross-border raid in July 2006 triggered a 34-day war involving heavy Israeli air strikes, a ground incursion, and thousands of Hezbollah rockets fired into northern Israel. UN Security Council Resolution 1701 ended the fighting and expanded the UNIFIL peacekeeping force.
