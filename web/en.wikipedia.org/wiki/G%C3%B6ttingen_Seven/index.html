Memorial to the Göttingen Seven in Hanover

The Göttingen Seven (German: Göttinger Sieben) were a group of seven professors at the University of Göttingen who in 1837 protested against the abolition of the liberal constitution of the Kingdom of Hanover by the new king, Ernest Augustus. All seven were dismissed, and three were expelled from the kingdom.

The protest

When Ernest Augustus I came to the Hanoverian throne in 1837, he annulled the 1833 constitution, to which civil servants and professors had sworn an oath. Led by historian Friedrich Christoph Dahlmann, the seven — Dahlmann, the brothers Wilhelm and Jacob Grimm, historian Georg Gottfried Gervinus, physicist Wilhelm Eduard Weber, orientalist Heinrich Ewald, and jurist Wilhelm Eduard Albrecht — signed a declaration that they still considered themselves bound by their oath to the constitution.

Consequences

The king dismissed all seven and banished Dahlmann and the Grimms from Hanover. The affair became a cause célèbre across Germany, celebrated as an early stand for constitutional government and academic freedom. The dismissed professors found posts elsewhere — Weber later worked with Gauss's telegraph and returned to Göttingen in 1849 — and the seven are commemorated by statues and memorials in Göttingen and Hanover.
