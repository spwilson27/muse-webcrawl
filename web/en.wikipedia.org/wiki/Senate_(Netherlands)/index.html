Senate (Netherlands)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Senate (Dutch: Eerste Kamer der Staten-Generaal) — 75 seats, The Hague. Upper house of the States General.

The Senate (Dutch: Eerste Kamer der Staten-Generaal, literally "First Chamber of the States General") is the upper house of the bicameral parliament of the Netherlands, the States General, the other being the House of Representatives (Tweede Kamer). Its 75 members, called senators, are not directly elected: they are chosen every four years by the members of the provincial councils and by electoral colleges for the Caribbean Netherlands and for Dutch citizens abroad, shortly after the provincial elections.

Powers

The Senate's legislative role is more restrained than that of the House of Representatives. It cannot initiate or amend legislation; it may only approve or reject bills passed by the lower house, considering them as a whole. It also lacks the right of inquiry in its own right in the same form, though it can hold debates and put written questions to ministers. In practice the Senate acts as a chambre de réflexion, testing bills against the constitution, treaty obligations, legal quality, and practical enforceability, and Dutch governments must negotiate to secure a Senate majority, which they may not possess even when they command the lower house.

History and composition

The Senate was created in 1815, at the insistence of Belgian representatives in the United Kingdom of the Netherlands, and was retained after Belgian independence; membership originally lay with the king's appointees from the nobility and wealthiest citizens. The constitutional revision of 1848 made senators elected by the provincial councils. Senators serve part-time, meeting normally one day a week, and most combine the office with other careers. The Senate sits in the Binnenhof in The Hague, in a chamber dating to the seventeenth century.
