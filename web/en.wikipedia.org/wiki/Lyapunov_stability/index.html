Lyapunov stability

Various types of stability may be discussed for the solutions of differential equations or difference equations describing a dynamical system. The most important type is that concerning the stability of solutions near to a point of equilibrium. This may be discussed by the theory of Aleksandr Lyapunov.

Informal description

In simple terms, if the solutions that start out near an equilibrium point stay near forever, then the equilibrium is Lyapunov stable. If solutions that start out near the equilibrium not only stay near but also eventually converge to the equilibrium, then it is asymptotically stable. If solutions starting near the equilibrium move away from it, the equilibrium is unstable.

History

Aleksandr Lyapunov developed the theory in his 1892 doctoral thesis The General Problem of the Stability of Motion. His two methods — the indirect (linearisation) method and the direct method using Lyapunov functions — remain central to control theory and the analysis of nonlinear dynamical systems.
