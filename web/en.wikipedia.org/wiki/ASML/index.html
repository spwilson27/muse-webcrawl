ASML Holding N.V. is a Dutch multinational corporation headquartered in Veldhoven, Netherlands. It is the world's dominant supplier of photolithography machines used to print integrated circuits, and the only maker of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems — making it one of the most strategically important companies in the global technology supply chain.

History

ASML was founded in 1984 as a joint venture between Philips and ASM International, working out of a shed beside a Philips building in Eindhoven; it was spun off and listed in 1995. Its PAS 5500 and later TWINSCAN platforms won the market for deep-ultraviolet scanners against Japanese rivals Nikon and Canon.

The company bet heavily on EUV — using 13.5 nm light from a tin-plasma source — through the 2000s, helped by a 2012 "customer co-investment" programme in which Intel, TSMC and Samsung bought stakes to fund development. Commercial EUV systems shipped in volume from the late 2010s, and the newer High-NA EUV machines, costing on the order of €350–380 million each, began shipping in 2023–2024.

Geopolitics

Under U.S. pressure, the Dutch government has never licensed EUV exports to China, and controls have since extended to advanced DUV tools, making ASML a central point of leverage in the U.S.–China technology rivalry. The company's machines contain hundreds of thousands of parts from suppliers such as Zeiss (optics) and are assembled in cleanrooms in Veldhoven.
