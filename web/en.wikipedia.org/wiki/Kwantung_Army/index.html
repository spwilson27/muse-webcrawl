The Kwantung Army (Japanese: 関東軍, Kantō-gun) was a major army group of the Imperial Japanese Army, formed in 1906 to guard the Kwantung Leased Territory and the South Manchuria Railway zone in north-eastern China. It became one of the most powerful and politically autonomous formations of the Japanese military.

History

After the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905), Japan took over Russian leaseholds in southern Manchuria. The garrison force guarding the railway zone grew into the Kwantung Army, headquartered first at Port Arthur (Lüshun) and later at Hsinking, capital of the puppet state of Manchukuo created in 1932.

Mukden Incident and Manchukuo

On 18 September 1931, officers of the Kwantung Army staged the Mukden (Manchurian) Incident, blaming Chinese saboteurs for a small explosion on the railway near Mukden, and used it as a pretext to occupy all of Manchuria — acting largely without authorisation from Tokyo. The conquest led to the creation of Manchukuo under the former Qing emperor Puyi, and to Japan's withdrawal from the League of Nations.

Second Sino-Japanese War and World War II

The Kwantung Army supplied forces for the invasion of China proper after 1937 and fought border conflicts with the Soviet Union, including the disastrous Battle of Khalkhin Gol (Nomonhan) in 1939. In August 1945 the Soviet invasion of Manchuria overwhelmed it; hundreds of thousands of its soldiers were taken prisoner to Siberia, and the army was dissolved with Japan's surrender.
