Pes planus

Pes planus
Other namesFlat feet, fallen arches
SpecialtyOrthopedics, podiatry
SymptomsOften none; sometimes foot pain

Pes planus, also known as flat feet or fallen arches, is a postural deformity in which the arches of the foot collapse, with the entire sole of the foot coming into complete or near-complete contact with the ground. In functional terms, the foot rolls inward excessively (overpronation) during weight bearing.

Flat feet are normal in infants and toddlers, because the arch has not yet developed and baby fat masks it. The arch typically forms during childhood; some people never develop arches. Estimates suggest that between 20% and 37% of adults have some degree of flat feet.

Causes

Pes planus may be congenital (present from birth, often hereditary) or acquired. Acquired flat feet in adults commonly result from dysfunction of the posterior tibial tendon, which supports the arch; other causes include injury, arthritis, obesity, diabetes, and ageing. Flexible flat feet, in which the arch appears when the foot is not bearing weight, are far more common than rigid flat feet, which may indicate an underlying bone abnormality such as tarsal coalition.

Symptoms and treatment

Many people with flat feet have no symptoms. Others experience foot pain, particularly in the heel or arch area, leg fatigue, or pain in the ankles, knees, hips, or lower back due to altered alignment. Treatment is usually needed only when symptoms occur and may include arch supports or orthotic insoles, stretching exercises, supportive footwear, weight management, physiotherapy, and, in severe or rigid cases, surgery.

Military service

Flat feet were historically a common reason for rejection from military service, on the assumption that flat-footed soldiers would break down on long marches. Modern studies have found little evidence that flat feet significantly impair physical performance, and many armed forces no longer disqualify asymptomatic recruits.
