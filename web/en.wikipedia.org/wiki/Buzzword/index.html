Buzzword

A buzzword is a word or phrase, new or already existing, that becomes popular for a period of time. Buzzwords often derive from technical terms yet often have much of the original technical meaning removed through fashionable use, being simply used to impress others. Some buzzwords, such as leverage or synergy, retain meaningful definitions in their home fields, while others become near-synonymous with fashionable jargon in business, politics, technology and education.

Buzzword
Etymology"Buzz" + "word"; attested from the 1940s (Harvard slang)
Related termsJargon, cliché, weasel word, hype
ExamplesSynergy, paradigm shift, low-hanging fruit, web 2.0, metaverse

Characteristics

Buzzwords typically sound authoritative and futuristic, promise efficiency or innovation, and are vague enough to fit many situations. They spread through management literature, consulting, marketing and media, and often fade within a few years — a lifecycle parodied in games such as "buzzword bingo", in which meeting attendees mark off jargon terms as they are uttered. Hallgren and colleagues noted buzzwords can serve real functions: signalling group membership, coordinating around new concepts, and lending legitimacy to initiatives.

Criticism

Critics from George Orwell ("Politics and the English Language", 1946) onward have attacked fashionable abstractions as a means of obscuring thought and evading responsibility. Corporate buzzwords feature prominently in satire, from Dilbert to lists of "most hated business jargon" published annually by business media. Nevertheless, some buzzwords mature into standard vocabulary: internet, blog and cloud computing all began as modish terms.
