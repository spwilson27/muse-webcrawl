The Treaty of Turkmenchay (Persian: عهدنامه ترکمنچای; Russian: Туркманчайский договор) was an agreement between Qajar Persia and the Russian Empire that concluded the Russo-Persian War of 1826–1828. Signed on 22 February 1828 in the village of Torkamanchay near Tabriz, it confirmed Persia's territorial losses in the South Caucasus and is remembered in Iran, along with the earlier Treaty of Gulistan (1813), as a national humiliation.

Terms

Persia ceded to Russia the Erivan Khanate (most of present-day Armenia) and the Nakhichevan Khanate, and the boundary between the two empires was fixed along the Aras River — a frontier that largely survives today as the border between Iran and Armenia and Azerbaijan. Persia paid an indemnity of ten kurors in gold (about 20 million silver roubles), granted Russia exclusive navigation rights on the Caspian Sea and capitulatory trading privileges for Russian merchants, and recognised Russia's right to repatriate Armenians living in Persia — tens of thousands of Armenians subsequently moved into the ceded territories.

Diplomatic aftermath

The Russian negotiator was the diplomat and playwright Alexander Griboyedov, who had helped draft the treaty and was sent to Tehran to enforce its terms. In February 1829 he was killed when a mob stormed the Russian mission, an event for which the Shah sent a formal embassy of apology to St Petersburg bearing gifts, including the great Shah Diamond.

Turkmenchay fixed the last border changes between Iran and Russia in the Caucasus and, together with Gulistan, left a lasting legacy in Iranian political memory of great-power encroachment during the "Great Game".
