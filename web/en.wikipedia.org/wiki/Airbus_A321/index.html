The Airbus A321 is a member of the Airbus A320 family of short- to medium-haul, narrow-body, twin-engine jet airliners developed and produced by Airbus. It is the longest variant of the family, with a fuselage stretched by about 6.9 metres over the A320, and typically seats 185–236 passengers. The A321 first flew in 1993 and entered service with Lufthansa in 1994.

Development

Launched in 1988 as the first derivative of the A320, the A321 added fuselage plugs ahead of and behind the wing, a strengthened structure and landing gear, and higher-thrust engine options (the CFM56 and IAE V2500). Sub-variants included the heavier, longer-range A321-200, introduced in 1997.

A321neo

The re-engined A321neo (new engine option), with CFM LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1100G engines and sharklet wingtip devices, entered service in 2017 and offers roughly 15–20% lower fuel burn per seat than the earlier model. It also underpins two long-range derivatives: the A321LR (2018), with additional fuel tanks enabling transatlantic routes, and the A321XLR (extra long range), which entered service in 2024 with a range of up to about 4,700 nautical miles — the longest of any single-aisle airliner — allowing airlines to operate thin long-haul routes economically.

Production and operators

A321s are assembled in Hamburg, Germany, and Mobile, Alabama, among other sites. The original A321 (ceo) accumulated more than 1,700 deliveries, and the A321neo has become the best-selling member of the A320neo family, with orders numbering in the thousands from airlines worldwide, including major fleets at Delta, American, IndiGo, Wizz Air, and Turkish Airlines.

Specifications (typical)

  • Length: 44.51 m; wingspan 35.8 m (with sharklets)
  • Typical range: ~3,200 nmi (A321ceo); ~4,000 nmi (A321LR); ~4,700 nmi (A321XLR)
  • Engines: 2 × turbofan, 30,000–35,000 lbf class
