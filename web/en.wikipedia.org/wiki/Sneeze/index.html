Sneeze

A sneeze, or sternutation, is a semi-autonomous, convulsive expulsion of air from the lungs through the nose and mouth, usually caused by foreign particles irritating the nasal mucosa. A sneeze expels air forcibly from the mouth and nose in an explosive, spasmodic involuntary action. This action allows for mucus to escape through the nasal cavity and saliva to escape from the oral cavity.

Physiology

Sneezing is triggered when sensory nerves in the nasal lining — branches of the trigeminal nerve — detect irritants such as dust, pollen, pepper, cold air, or viral infection. Signals pass to the medulla in the brainstem, which coordinates a deep inhalation followed by closure of the vocal cords, a rise in intrathoracic pressure, and a forceful exhalation that expels droplets at high speed. Sneezing also occurs in response to bright light in people with the photic sneeze reflex (ACHOO syndrome), an inherited trait.

Disease transmission

Sneezes aerosolize droplets containing infectious particles, making them a route of transmission for respiratory diseases such as influenza and the common cold. Health guidance advises covering the mouth and nose with a tissue or the crook of the elbow to limit spread.

Cultural responses

Many cultures respond to a sneeze with a blessing — "bless you" in English, "Gesundheit" in German, "salud" in Spanish. A common folk explanation connects the custom with the era of plague, or with beliefs that the soul leaves the body during a sneeze. In several languages, sneezing is used in proverb and superstition.
