Patala

Patala (Sanskrit: पाताल, Pātāla) is, in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain cosmology, the netherworld — a realm beneath the earth inhabited by nagas (serpent beings), demons and other creatures. It is often translated as the underworld or nether realm, though unlike the hellish Naraka, Patala is described as a place of beauty, wealth and splendour, in some accounts more opulent than the heavens.

The seven realms

Hindu scriptures such as the Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana divide the netherworld into seven regions: Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Gabhastimat, Mahatala, Sutala and Patala proper. Each is ruled by different beings — kings, demons and serpent lords. Sutala is ruled by the virtuous demon king Bali, while Patala proper is the home of the nagas, ruled by Vasuki.

Description

The Bhagavata Purana describes Patala's cities with jewelled walls, lakes, gardens and palaces. The nagas wear gems on their hoods that illuminate their kingdoms. The sage Narada is said to have returned from Patala praising it above heaven. At the bottom of the universe below Patala lies the serpent Shesha, who supports the world.

In other traditions

In Jain cosmology the lower world contains hellish regions distinct from Patala, while tantric and regional traditions variously identify earthly entrances to Patala in caves, wells and temples across India.
