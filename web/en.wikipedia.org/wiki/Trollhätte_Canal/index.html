Trollhätte Canal

The Trollhätte Canal (Swedish: Trollhätte kanal) is a canal in Sweden forming part of the navigable waterway between Lake Vänern and the Kattegat at Gothenburg. Of the 82 kilometres of the Göta River route, about 10 kilometres are artificial canal sections bypassing the rapids and falls at Trollhättan, Lilla Edet and Vargön.

History

The falls at Trollhättan long obstructed transport on the Göta älv. Early canals, including the Karl IX canal at Eskilstuna-era works and the lock at Lilla Edet (1607), attested to centuries of attempts. The present Trollhätte Canal was built under the direction of engineer Nils Ericson and opened in 1800, with locks dimensioned for the vessels of the day. It was rebuilt with larger locks in 1916, which remain in use, allowing vessels of up to about 88 metres in length, 13.2 metres in beam and 5.4 metres draught to reach Lake Vänern.

The canal today

Around 10–11 million tonnes of freight pass through the canal each year, together with thousands of leisure boats in summer. The locks at Trollhättan, grouped in staircases beside the dramatic old falls, are a popular tourist attraction. A long-discussed project would build a new, larger lock flight to secure the route for bigger ships through the 21st century.
