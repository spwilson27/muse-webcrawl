Euphorbia is a very large and diverse genus of flowering plants, commonly called spurge, in the family Euphorbiaceae. With roughly 2,000 species, it is one of the largest genera of flowering plants. Members range from small annual herbs to woody shrubs and trees, and many are succulent, resembling cacti.

Description

All species exude a milky latex when cut, which is often toxic and irritating. The flowers are typically reduced and grouped in a specialised pseudanthium called a cyathium, unique to the genus.

Distribution

The genus is distributed worldwide, with centres of diversity in Africa and the Americas. Notable species include the poinsettia (Euphorbia pulcherrima), the crown of thorns, and the candelabra tree.

Uses and toxicity

Some species are grown as ornamentals, while others are significant weeds. The latex of several species has traditional medicinal uses but can cause severe skin and eye irritation.
