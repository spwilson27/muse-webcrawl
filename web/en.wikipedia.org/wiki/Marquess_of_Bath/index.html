Marquess of Bath is a title in the Peerage of Great Britain, created in 1789 for Thomas Thynn, 3rd Viscount Weymouth, a former Secretary of State under George III. The family name is pronounced "thine". The family seat is Longleat House in Wiltshire, an Elizabethan prodigy house, and the family also owns Cheddar Gorge and the former village of Cheddar.

History

The Thynn family rose to prominence in the sixteenth century; John Thynn built the first Longleat, and his descendants acquired the viscountcy of Weymouth in 1682. The third viscount became marquess in 1789 as a reward for political service. Later holders have included the 7th Marquess, Alexander Thynn (1932–2020), a flamboyant figure who opened Longleat to the public in 1949 — the first stately home in Britain to do so — and created the Longleat Safari Park in 1966. The current holder, Ceawlin Thynn, succeeded his father in 2020.

Longleat

Longleat House, rebuilt in classical Renaissance style by John Thynn the elder, stands in Capability Brown–landscaped parkland. The estate's safari park, maze, and adventure attractions draw hundreds of thousands of visitors a year, making it a significant business as well as a historic house.
