Argyll and Bute (Scottish Gaelic: Earra-Ghàidheal agus Bòd) is one of the 32 unitary council areas of Scotland and a lieutenancy area. It covers much of Scotland's west coast in the southern Highlands: the historic counties of Argyll and Bute (the latter comprising the Isle of Bute and the Cowal peninsula), together with part of Dunbartonshire around Helensburgh. It is the second-largest Scottish council area by land, yet sparsely populated, with an indented coastline of sea lochs and peninsulas longer than that of France.

Geography

The area includes the peninsulas of Kintyre, Cowal and Rosneath, the shores of Loch Fyne, Loch Lomond's western side, and 23 inhabited islands, among them Islay, Jura, Mull, Tiree, Coll, Colonsay, Lismore, Bute, Arran's neighbours in the Firth of Clyde being in North Ayrshire. Oban is the principal ferry port — the "Gateway to the Isles" — for Caledonian MacBrayne services to the Inner and Outer Hebrides. The town of Dunoon and the Clyde coast settlements face the Glasgow conurbation across the firth, while Helensburgh adjoins Faslane naval base, home of the UK's nuclear submarine fleet on the Gare Loch.

Economy and culture

Tourism, whisky distilling (Islay's peated malts and Campbeltown's revived distilleries), aquaculture and shellfish, forestry, renewable energy and the public sector dominate the economy. The area has one of the highest proportions of Gaelic speakers in mainland Scotland, and standing stones, crannogs and early Christian sites such as Iona (in the council area) and Kilmartin Glen attest to its antiquity: the kingdom of Dál Riata had its stronghold at Dunadd.

Governance

Argyll and Bute Council was created in 1996 from the Argyll and Bute district of Strathclyde Region; its headquarters are at Kilmory Castle, Lochgilphead. The area returns members to the Scottish and UK parliaments under the same name.
