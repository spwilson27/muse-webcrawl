Henry the Lion (German: Heinrich der Löwe; c. 1129/1131 – 6 August 1195) was a member of the Welf dynasty and Duke of Saxony (as Henry III) from 1142 and Duke of Bavaria (as Henry XII) from 1156 until 1180. One of the most powerful princes of the Holy Roman Empire, he was for decades the chief ally — and eventual rival — of his cousin, Emperor Frederick Barbarossa. His fall in 1180 reshaped the political map of Germany.

Rise

Son of Henry the Proud and grandson of Emperor Lothair III, Henry recovered Saxony as a boy and was granted Bavaria by Frederick Barbarossa in 1156. He supported the emperor's Italian campaigns in return for a free hand in the north-east, where he waged war against the Slavic peoples east of the Elbe, founded and refounded cities and bishoprics (including Lübeck in 1159, after refounding it, Munich in 1158, and Lüneburg), and promoted German settlement and trade in the Baltic. At his court city of Brunswick he erected the great bronze Brunswick Lion (c. 1166) as a symbol of his power, and had the lavish Gospels of Henry the Lion produced. His second marriage, to Matilda, daughter of Henry II of England, in 1168 tied the Welfs to the Angevin court.

Fall and exile

The breach came when Henry refused to support Barbarossa's Italian campaign of 1176, which ended in defeat at Legnano. At a series of diets in 1179–80 the princes charged Henry with disturbing the peace; he was placed under the imperial ban and his duchies were divided — Westphalia went to the archbishop of Cologne, the Saxon duchy was split, and Bavaria passed to Otto of Wittelsbach. Henry submitted in 1181 and went into exile at the court of his father-in-law in England and Normandy. He was allowed to return to Brunswick in 1185 (with a second exile 1189–90), reconciled with the new emperor Henry VI, and died at Brunswick in 1195.

His son Otto of Brunswick later became Emperor Otto IV. Henry is remembered as a city founder, a patron of art, and a central figure of German medieval history.
