Lip balm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lip balm or lip salve is a wax-like substance applied topically to the lips to moisturise and relieve chapped or dry lips, angular cheilitis, stomatitis or cold sores. Lip balm often contains beeswax or carnauba wax, camphor, cetyl alcohol, lanolin, paraffin, and petrolatum, among other ingredients. Some have sunscreen or flavouring, and a few are lightly tinted, blurring the boundary with lipstick.

Function

The skin of the lips is thin and lacks oil glands, so it loses moisture readily. A lip balm works primarily as an occlusive: its waxy or greasy layer slows evaporation from the lip surface, allowing the tissue to rehydrate. Humectant ingredients such as hyaluronic acid or glycerin may be combined with the occlusive base. Dermatologists caution that some ingredients — menthol, camphor, phenol, fragrances and salicylic acid — can irritate or dry the lips and encourage a cycle of reapplication.

History

Home remedies for chapped lips, including beeswax and earwax, are ancient. Commercial lip balm became a mass product in the late nineteenth century: in the 1880s Charles Browne Fleet marketed ChapStick in Virginia, selling the rights cheaply before the product eventually made it a household name; Carmex (1937) and Blistex (1947) followed, and EOS popularised the spherical balm in the 2010s. The most popular primary market for lip balm is the treatment of dry lips.
