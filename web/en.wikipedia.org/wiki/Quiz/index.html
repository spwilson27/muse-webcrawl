Quiz

Quiz
Type: Game of questions and answers
Formats: Pub quiz, quiz show, academic quiz bowl

A quiz is a form of game or test of knowledge in which players (as individuals or in teams) attempt to answer questions correctly, usually for points, prizes, or simply for entertainment. Quizzes range from short informal tests of a few questions to large organised competitions, and they are a staple of education, broadcasting, and pub culture.

Etymology

The origin of the word "quiz" is uncertain. It is first attested in the late 18th century meaning an odd or eccentric person, and took on the sense of a practical joke or interrogation in the 19th century. A popular but almost certainly apocryphal story holds that Dublin theatre owner Richard Daly coined the word on a bet in 1791 by having it chalked on walls around the city, so that Dubliners would debate its meaning the next day.

Forms

The pub quiz, established in the United Kingdom in the 1970s, is a weekly trivia night in which teams compete in a pub, typically paying a small entry fee toward a prize pot. Quiz shows are a long-running genre of radio and television, including programmes such as Mastermind, University Challenge, Jeopardy!, and Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?. In schools, a quiz is a brief assessment, usually smaller than a test or exam. Academic competitions such as quiz bowl and the National Academic Quiz Tournaments pit student teams against each other on toss-up and bonus questions.

In education

Research on the "testing effect" shows that frequent low-stakes quizzing improves long-term retention of material compared with rereading alone, making quizzes a common formative-assessment tool in classrooms and online learning platforms.
