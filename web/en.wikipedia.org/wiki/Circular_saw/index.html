Circular saw

A circular saw is a power-saw using a toothed or abrasive disc or blade to cut different materials using a rotary motion spinning around an arbor. A hole saw and ring saw also use a rotary motion but are different from a circular saw.

History

The circular saw was invented around the end of the 18th century as a riff-saw to convert logs into lumber. Suggestions of its origin in China, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, and the United States vary. American inventor Tabitha Babbitt is often credited with inventing a circular saw in 1810 while working at the Harvard Shaker community.

Types

Besides hand-held circular saws, common uses include table saws, radial arm saws, cut-off saws, and cold saws. Blades are designed for the material they cut: wood, hardwoods, softwoods, metal, and tile each require different blade designs.

Safety

Circular saws are among the most dangerous common power tools; proper technique, guards, and kickback prevention are essential.
