The Verse of Purification (Arabic: آية التطهير, Āyat al-Taṭhīr) is the latter part of verse 33 of Surah Al-Ahzab (the 33rd chapter) of the Quran. It declares God's intention to remove all impurity (rijs) from the Ahl al-Bayt ("People of the House") and to purify them completely. The verse is one of the most discussed passages in the Quran, owing to disagreement between Sunni and Shia Muslims over the identity of the Ahl al-Bayt it refers to and whether it implies their infallibility (ʿiṣma).

Text

"…Allah intends only to remove from you the impurity [of sin], O people of the [Prophet's] household, and to purify you with [extensive] purification." (Q 33:33, Saheeh International rendering)

The passage occurs in the middle of a verse addressed to the wives of Muhammad, instructing them on conduct in their households. The purification clause shifts grammatically from the feminine plural used for the wives to a masculine plural, a change that forms a central point in theological debate.

Interpretations

Shia view

In Twelver Shia Islam, the Ahl al-Bayt of this verse are the Ahl al-Kisa ("People of the Cloak"): Muhammad, his daughter Fatimah, his cousin and son-in-law Ali, and his grandsons Hasan and Husayn. Shia scholars argue that the verse's shift in gender and the Hadith of the Cloak (in which Muhammad gathered these four under his cloak and recited the verse) show that "the House" here means these five, and that their complete purification implies infallibility — a foundation of the doctrine of the Imamate.

Sunni view

Sunni scholars generally hold that the verse, in context, primarily addresses Muhammad's wives, while many also include Ali, Fatimah, Hasan, and Husayn within the Ahl al-Bayt on the basis of the Hadith of the Cloak. They typically do not derive a doctrine of infallibility from the verse in the Shia sense, interpreting purification as protection from sin and devotion to obedience.

Hadith of the Cloak

Reports in both Sunni and Shia collections — including a well-known narration in Sahih Muslim from Aisha — relate that Muhammad went out one morning wearing a striped cloak, and admitted Hasan, Husayn, Fatimah, and Ali beneath it, then recited: "Allah intends only to remove from you the impurity, O people of the household, and to purify you extensively."
