Hans Haacke

Born12 August 1936, Cologne, Germany
MovementConceptual art, institutional critique, systems art
Notable worksCondensation Cube (1963–65), MoMA Poll (1970), Shapolsky et al. (1971), Germania (1993)

Hans Haacke (born 12 August 1936) is a German-born American conceptual artist living in New York. A pioneer of institutional critique, he is known for artworks that expose the workings of physical, biological, social, and political systems — from condensation forming inside a sealed acrylic cube to the real-estate holdings of slumlords and the public-relations machinery of corporations and museums.

Early work: systems

Haacke studied in Kassel and Paris and worked early on with the ZERO group. In the 1960s he made works governed by natural processes: the Condensation Cube (1963–65), in which water condenses and evaporates inside a transparent box according to gallery conditions, and pieces using water, ice, plants, and live animals. He described his approach as making something that "reacts to its environment" rather than a static object.

Institutional critique

Around 1970 Haacke turned from physical to social systems. His MoMA Poll (1970) invited museum visitors to vote on a question linking Governor Nelson Rockefeller, a museum trustee, to US policy in Indochina. His 1971 solo exhibition at the Guggenheim Museum was cancelled six weeks before opening because works documented, with photographs and charts, the Manhattan property empires of slumlords — most famously Shapolsky et al. Manhattan Real Estate Holdings, a Real-Time Social System, as of May 1, 1971. The cancellation became a landmark case in debates over censorship and the politics of museums.

Later work

Haacke went on to dissect corporate sponsorship, art-market entanglements, and national memory: works addressed Mobil, Philip Morris, and the South African apartheid regime, and for the German pavilion at the 1993 Venice Biennale he smashed the marble floor laid down in the Nazi era beneath a photograph of Hitler's visit, sharing the Golden Lion. His 2000 installation DER BEVÖLKERUNG ("To the Population") in the Reichstag in Berlin invites members of parliament to bring soil from their constituencies to a planted trough, deliberately echoing and contesting the building's inscription "Dem Deutschen Volke".
