Charles Theodore Dotter

Born14 June 1920, Boston, Massachusetts, USA
Died15 February 1985
Known forAngioplasty; interventional radiology

Charles Theodore Dotter (1920–1985) was an American radiologist regarded as the father of interventional radiology. In 1964 he performed the first transluminal angioplasty, using catheters to dilate a narrowed artery in a patient's leg and saving it from amputation.

Career

Dotter joined the University of Oregon Medical School, where he built a pioneering department of radiology. He developed catheter techniques, angiographic equipment, and early vascular stents, and famously predicted many of the minimally invasive image-guided procedures now routine in medicine.

Recognition

Dotter was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, and the Dotter Interventional Institute in Portland, Oregon, is named in his honour.
