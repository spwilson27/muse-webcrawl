The Berlin Marathon (German: Berlin-Marathon) is a marathon event held annually in Berlin, Germany, on the last weekend of September. It is one of the six World Marathon Majors and is famous for its flat, fast course, on which more marathon world records have been set than at any other race — including Eliud Kipchoge's 2:01:09 in 2022.

History

The race was founded by baker and running enthusiast Horst Milde; the first edition, on 13 October 1974, attracted 286 participants and ran through the Grunewald forest in West Berlin. From 1981 the race moved onto the streets of West Berlin. After German reunification, the 1990 race — held three days before formal reunification — ran through both halves of the city, with runners passing through the Brandenburg Gate, an image broadcast worldwide.

Course

The modern course starts and finishes near the Brandenburg Gate, looping through Charlottenburg, Tiergarten, Kreuzberg, Neukölln, Schöneberg, Steglitz and Mitte. It is exceptionally flat, with wide streets and few sharp turns, and passes landmarks including the Reichstag, the Berlin Cathedral, Potsdamer Platz and the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.

World records

Berlin has hosted a succession of world records: Christa Vahlensieck's women's record in 1977; Ronaldo da Costa (1998); the first sub-2:05 (Paul Tergat, 2003) and the first sub-2:04 (Haile Gebrselassie, 2008); further records by Patrick Makau, Wilson Kipsang and Dennis Kimetto, whose 2:02:57 in 2014 broke the 2:03 barrier; and Eliud Kipchoge's 2:03:32 (2017), 2:01:39 (2018) and 2:01:09 (2022). On the women's side, Tigist Assefa ran 2:11:53 in 2023, a world record at the time. The event also includes an inline skating marathon, the largest in the world, and races for handcyclists and wheelchair athletes.
