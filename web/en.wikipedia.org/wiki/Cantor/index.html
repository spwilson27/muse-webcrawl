Cantor

A cantor is a person who leads congregational singing and prayer in worship. The word derives from the Latin cantor, "singer". The role exists in both Jewish and Christian traditions, though with different histories and functions.

In Judaism

In Judaism the cantor, called a hazzan (also spelled chazzan), leads the congregation in sung prayer in the synagogue, often as a trained professional with knowledge of liturgy and the traditional chant modes (nusach). The cantor serves as the sheliach tzibbur, the emissary of the congregation, and in many communities works alongside the rabbi as clergy. The late 19th and early 20th centuries are remembered as a "golden age of cantors", when virtuoso hazzanim such as Yossele Rosenblatt became recording stars.

In Christianity

In Christian usage, a cantor sings the solo verses of psalms and other chants to which the choir or congregation responds. In the Roman Catholic Church the cantor leads the responsorial psalm and other sung parts of the Mass. In Lutheran tradition the Kantor — a post famously held by Johann Sebastian Bach at St Thomas's, Leipzig — directed church music and often the church school. In Eastern Orthodox practice the cantor or reader chants services, frequently without instrumental accompaniment.
