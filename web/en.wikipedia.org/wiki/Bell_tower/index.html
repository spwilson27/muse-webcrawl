Bell tower

A bell tower is a tower that contains one or more bells, or that is designed to hold bells even if it currently has none. Such a tower commonly serves a church and usually forms part of a church building, but many are freestanding structures. A bell tower may also be called a campanile, an Italian term especially used of detached towers, or a belfry, referring strictly to the part of the tower housing the bells.

History

Bells were rung from towers in early medieval Europe to summon communities to worship and to mark the hours. In Italy, freestanding campaniles became prominent civic symbols; the most famous is the Leaning Tower of Pisa, begun in 1173. Bell towers later spread to civic buildings such as town halls and university campuses.

Functions

The primary purpose of a bell tower is to carry bells high enough for their sound to carry over a town or parish. Bells historically signalled services, weddings, funerals, alarms and celebrations. Towers often also carry clocks, forming clock towers.

Types

Forms include the Italian campanile, the church steeple, the German Glockenturm, wooden bell towers of Scandinavia and Eastern Europe, and the bell-gables (espadañas) of Spain and Latin America. Some towers house a carillon, an instrument of at least 23 bells played from a keyboard.
