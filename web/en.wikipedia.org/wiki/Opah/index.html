Opah

Opahs, also called moonfish, sunfish (not to be confused with the ocean sunfish), kingfish, and Jerusalem haddock, are large, colorful, deep-bodied pelagic lampriform fishes belonging to the family Lampridae. They live in deep waters of the open oceans worldwide.

Description

Opahs have a distinctive round, laterally compressed body with bright silvery-bluish coloration, white spots, and vivid red-orange fins and mouth. They commonly reach about 1 metre in length and can weigh well over 100 kilograms. Species include the common opah Lampris guttatus and several more recently distinguished relatives identified through genetic and morphological work.

Endothermy

In 2015 researchers reported that the opah is the first fish known to be fully warm-bodied: it generates heat through constant flapping of its large pectoral fins and maintains elevated body temperatures throughout its body using counter-current heat exchangers in the gills. This whole-body endothermy gives it advantages in vision, muscle performance, and cognition in the cold deep waters (around 50–300 metres deep) where it hunts squid and fish.

Fisheries

Opahs are not targeted by a major dedicated fishery but are caught as bycatch in tuna and swordfish longline fisheries, including off Hawaii, where they are sold as a valued food fish with rich, varied flesh.
