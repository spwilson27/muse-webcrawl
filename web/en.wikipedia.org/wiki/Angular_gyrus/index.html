Angular gyrus

The angular gyrus is a region of the inferior parietal lobule in the cerebral cortex, roughly corresponding to Brodmann area 39. It lies near the junction of the temporal, parietal and occipital lobes and receives converging information from visual, auditory and somatosensory systems.

Functions

The angular gyrus is associated with language processing, reading, number cognition, spatial attention, memory retrieval and aspects of social cognition. In the dominant hemisphere it helps connect visual word forms with meaning and sound. It is also part of networks involved in the default mode and in out-of-body or perspective-taking phenomena studied in neurology.

Clinical significance

Damage to the dominant angular gyrus can contribute to Gerstmann syndrome, classically described with agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia and left–right disorientation, although the purity and localisation of the syndrome are debated. Lesions and stimulation studies also link the region to alexia and semantic deficits.
