Paranal Observatory is an astronomical observatory operated by the European Southern Observatory (ESO). It is located in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile on top of Cerro Paranal at 2,635 m altitude, 120 km south of Antofagasta. By total light-collecting area, it is the largest optical-infrared observatory in the Southern Hemisphere; worldwide it is second only to Mauna Kea Observatory in Hawaii. Its flagship instrument, the Very Large Telescope (VLT), consists of four 8.2-metre Unit Telescopes and four movable 1.8-metre Auxiliary Telescopes forming the VLT Interferometer (VLTI).

Site and facilities

The Atacama offers around 300 clear nights per year, extremely dry air and excellent seeing. The summit was levelled to form a plateau for the telescope enclosures; astronomers and staff are housed at the Residencia, a partly subterranean building whose dome featured as the villain's lair in the James Bond film Quantum of Solace (2008). The site also hosts the VISTA survey telescope and the VLT Survey Telescope, and nearby Cerro Armazones is the construction site of ESO's Extremely Large Telescope.

Discoveries and work

Paranal instruments have produced landmark results, including the first direct image of an exoplanet (2M1207b, 2004), long-term tracking of stars orbiting the Milky Way's central black hole Sagittarius A* (work recognised by the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics), the first spectrum of a super-Earth atmosphere, and ESPRESSO measurements of exoplanet winds. The planetary-nebula luminosity function and the cosmic distance scale have likewise been advanced from Paranal data.
