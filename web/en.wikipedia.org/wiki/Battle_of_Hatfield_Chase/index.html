The Battle of Hatfield Chase was fought on 12 October 632 (or 633) at Hatfield Chase near Doncaster, between the Northumbrian king Edwin and an alliance of Penda, the pagan king of Mercia, and Cadwallon ap Cadfan, the Christian king of Gwynedd. Edwin was defeated and killed, ending Northumbria's brief supremacy over the other Anglo-Saxon kingdoms.

Background

Edwin, Bretwalda and the first Christian king of Northumbria, had driven Cadwallon into exile and dominated much of Britain. Cadwallon allied with Penda and invaded Northumbria, meeting Edwin's army on the marshy plain of Hatfield Chase, near the Roman road north of Doncaster.

Battle

Details of the fighting are sparse; Bede records that Edwin was killed along with his son Osfrith, while another son, Eadfrith, was captured and later killed. The Northumbrian army was destroyed, and Cadwallon and Penda ravaged the kingdom, sacking York.

Aftermath

Northumbria split back into its constituent kingdoms of Bernicia and Deira. Edwin's widow and Paulinus, the missionary bishop, fled south. A year later Edwin's nephew Oswald returned from exile and defeated and killed Cadwallon at Heavenfield, restoring Northumbrian power. Edwin came to be venerated as a saint.
