Blue box

A blue box used by phone phreaks in the 1960s–70s (placeholder).

A blue box was an electronic device used in the mid-twentieth century American telephone network to make free long-distance calls by imitating the in-band signalling tones that controlled trunk lines. Playing a 2600 Hz tone into the phone would seize a trunk; subsequent multi-frequency tones could then route a call anywhere in the network without billing. Blue boxes were the central tool of the "phone phreak" subculture.

History

The vulnerability arose because the Bell System routed calls using audible tones carried on the same voice channel. Hobbyists discovered the system in the 1950s and 1960s: Joe Engressia ("Joybubbles") could whistle the 2600 Hz tone, and John Draper ("Captain Crunch") used a toy whistle from a cereal box that produced the same frequency. In 1971 an Esquire article, "Secrets of the Little Blue Box", brought phreaking to public attention. Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs built and sold blue boxes before founding Apple — an episode both later described as formative.

Decline

Blue boxes became obsolete as telephone networks moved to out-of-band, digital signalling (SS7), which separated control signals from the voice path, and as carriers deployed tone-detection equipment and prosecuted offenders. The devices remain iconic artefacts of early hacker culture and telecommunications history.
