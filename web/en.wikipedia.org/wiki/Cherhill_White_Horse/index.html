The Cherhill White Horse is a hill figure on Cherhill Down in Wiltshire, England, cut into the chalk grassland below the Lansdowne Monument near the village of Cherhill, east of Calne. It is the second-oldest of Wiltshire's white horses and a well-known landmark visible from the A4 road.

History

The horse was cut in 1780 under the direction of Dr Christopher Alsop of Calne, who is said to have directed the work by shouting instructions through a megaphone from the road below. The figure measures roughly 129 feet (39 m) long and 142 feet (43 m) high. Its eye was originally formed of upturned glass bottles set into the ground, which glittered in the sun; the bottles were repeatedly dug up by souvenir hunters, and on restoration in 2002 the eye was replaced with stone and concrete.

Restoration

Like all chalk hill figures, the horse requires regular "scouring" to remove weeds and refresh the chalk. Major restorations took place in 2002, led by a local preservation group with support from the landowner, and the figure has since been maintained by volunteers of the Cherhill White Horse Preservation Group.
