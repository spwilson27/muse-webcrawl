Saxony (German: Sachsen; Upper Sorbian: Sakska), officially the Free State of Saxony (Freistaat Sachsen), is a landlocked federal state of Germany in the country's east, bordering Poland and the Czech Republic. Its capital is Dresden and its largest city is Leipzig; other major cities include Chemnitz and Zwickau. Saxony is the tenth-largest and sixth-most-populous of Germany's sixteen states.

History

The name derives from the Saxons, a Germanic people whose original homeland lay further northwest, in what is now Lower Saxony. The medieval Duchy and later Electorate of Saxony, ruled by the Wettin dynasty from Meissen, became one of the most powerful states of the Holy Roman Empire; Elector Augustus the Strong also became King of Poland. The Kingdom of Saxony (1806–1918) was the first German state to industrialize, with dense networks of textile, engineering and mining industries around Chemnitz and the Ore Mountains. After the Second World War Saxony belonged to East Germany until reunification in 1990, when the state was re-established.

Economy and culture

Modern Saxony is a centre of microelectronics — Dresden's "Silicon Saxony" cluster hosts major semiconductor fabs — as well as automotive manufacturing (Volkswagen, BMW and Porsche plants) and mechanical engineering. The state is renowned for its cultural heritage: the Baroque old town of Dresden, the Leipzig book fair and Gewandhaus orchestra, Meissen porcelain, and the Christmas traditions of the Ore Mountains. A Sorbian minority in Upper Lusatia maintains the only recognized Slavic minority culture in Germany.
