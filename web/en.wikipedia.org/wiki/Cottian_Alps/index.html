Cottian Alps

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cottian Alps (French: Alpes Cottiennes; Italian: Alpi Cozie) are a mountain range in the southwestern part of the Alps, straddling the border between France (Hautes-Alpes and Savoie) and Italy (Piedmont). They take their name from Marcus Julius Cottius, a king of the Ligurian tribes who allied with Augustus in the 1st century BC.

Geography

The highest peak is Monte Viso (Monviso), at 3,841 metres, a prominent pyramid-shaped summit visible across the plains of Piedmont. Other notable peaks include Monte Granero, Monte Orsiera, and Rocciamelone, long believed in the middle ages to be the highest mountain in the Alps. The Fréjus Road Tunnel and Fréjus Rail Tunnel, major crossings between France and Italy, pass through this general sector of the Alpine chain near Modane and Bardonecchia.

Passes

Historic passes include the Col du Mont Cenis, the Col de Montgenèvre — one of the oldest and most used Alpine crossings, on the ancient Via Domitia route — the Col d'Izoard, and the Col Agnel (Colle dell'Agnello), among the highest paved passes in Europe.
