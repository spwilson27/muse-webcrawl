Royal Dramatic Theatre

Native nameKungliga Dramatiska Teatern (Dramaten)
LocationNybroplan, Stockholm, Sweden
Opened1788 (company); 1908 (present building)
ArchitectFredrik Lilljekvist

The Royal Dramatic Theatre (Swedish: Kungliga Dramatiska Teatern, colloquially Dramaten) is Sweden's national stage for spoken drama. Founded by King Gustav III in 1788, it is housed in an Art Nouveau building at Nybroplan in Stockholm, inaugurated in 1908.

History

Gustav III, a passionate patron of theatre, established the company to cultivate Swedish-language drama. The present theatre, designed by Fredrik Lilljekvist with contributions from artists including Carl Milles and Carl Larsson, opened with a production of Strindberg's Master Olof.

Company

Dramaten stages around a thousand performances a year across several stages, from classics by Shakespeare, Ibsen and Strindberg to new Swedish writing. Ingmar Bergman was closely associated with the theatre for decades, and its ensemble has included such actors as Greta Garbo (a student at its acting school in the 1920s), Ingrid Bergman, Max von Sydow and Stellan Skarsgård. Its acting school was the forerunner of the Stockholm Academy of Dramatic Arts.
