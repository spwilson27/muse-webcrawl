Fiddler on the Roof (film)

Fiddler on the Roof is a 1971 American musical comedy-drama film produced and directed by Norman Jewison, adapted from the 1964 Broadway musical of the same name, itself based on the stories of Sholem Aleichem about Tevye the Dairyman. The film stars Topol as Tevye, a poor Jewish milkman in the village of Anatevka in Tsarist Russia, struggling to maintain his religious and cultural traditions as his daughters choose their own husbands and outside forces threaten his community.

Plot

Tevye, guided by "Tradition", narrates the life of his shtetl. His three eldest daughters successively defy the custom of arranged marriage by the matchmaker: Tzeitel marries the tailor Motel instead of the wealthy butcher Lazar Wolf; Hodel falls for the revolutionary student Perchik; and Chava marries a Russian Christian, Fyedka — a step Tevye cannot accept. In the end, the Jews of Anatevka are ordered to leave the village, dispersing to America and elsewhere.

Reception

The film was a major commercial and critical success. It was nominated for eight Academy Awards, winning three — for cinematography, sound, and scoring (John Williams, in his first Oscar win). Songs include "Tradition", "Matchmaker, Matchmaker", "If I Were a Rich Man" and "Sunrise, Sunset". The fiddler of the title appears on a roof as a symbol of the precarious balance of life.
