Narathu

Narathu (Burmese: နရသူ; 1118–1171) was king of the Pagan dynasty of Burma (Myanmar) from 1167 to 1171. Remembered in Burmese chronicles as a usurper and parricide, he seized the throne by murdering his father Alaungsithu and his elder brother Min Shin Saw.

Reign

Narathu had governed during his aged father's illness and, when Alaungsithu reportedly recovered and named Min Shin Saw heir, Narathu had his father smothered and his brother poisoned on the day of his enthronement, according to the chronicles. To expiate his sins he built the Dhammayazika Pagoda east of Pagan, a vast pentagonal stupa still standing, and made donations to the Sangha, yet chroniclers portray a reign haunted by guilt and portents.

His reign saw tensions with Sri Lanka and the lower Burma coast. In 1171 Narathu was assassinated: the chronicles relate that the chief of Pateikkaya, whose daughter the king had slighted, sent eight men disguised as Brahmin astrologers who slew him in the palace. He was succeeded by his son Naratheinkha.
