Michael Kenneth Mann (born February 5, 1943) is an American film director, producer, and screenwriter, regarded as one of the most influential filmmakers of modern crime cinema. Known for his meticulous research, stylised realism, nocturnal cityscapes, and use of contemporary music, Mann's work frequently explores professionalism, codes of conduct, and men defined by their work. He is a four-time Academy Award nominee.

Career

Born in Chicago, Mann studied at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and the London Film School, beginning in documentaries and commercials before writing for American television (Starsky & Hutch, Police Story) and winning an Emmy for the TV film The Jericho Mile (1979). His theatrical debut, Thief (1981) with James Caan, established his signature themes. Manhunter (1986), the first screen adaptation of Thomas Harris's Hannibal Lecter novels, became a cult classic. As executive producer of Miami Vice (1984–1989) and Crime Story, he reshaped American television aesthetics.

The Last of the Mohicans (1992) was a popular success; Heat (1995), his Los Angeles crime epic pairing Al Pacino and Robert De Niro on screen together for the first time, is widely considered his masterpiece and one of the greatest crime films ever made. The Insider (1999), about tobacco whistleblower Jeffrey Wigand, earned seven Academy Award nominations including Best Picture and Best Director. He followed with the biopic Ali (2001); the digital-noir thriller Collateral (2004), a landmark in digital cinematography; Miami Vice (2006); Public Enemies (2009); Blackhat (2015); and Ferrari (2023), about Enzo Ferrari. A sequel to Heat, based on his 2022 novel Heat 2, has been in development.

Style

Mann's films are marked by rigorous procedural detail, blue-and-silver night photography, modernist architecture, and an empathy for disciplined professionals on both sides of the law. Critics place him among the leading American auteurs of his generation.
