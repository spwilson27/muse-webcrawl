iCloud is a cloud computing and cloud storage service operated by Apple Inc. Launched on October 12, 2011, it allows users to store data such as documents, photos, and music on remote servers for download to iOS, macOS, and Windows devices, to share and send data among users, and to manage Apple devices if lost or stolen.

iCloud replaced Apple's MobileMe service, which was discontinued in June 2012. The service provides email, contacts, calendar, bookmarks, notes, reminders, Keychain passwords, photos, and file storage, and acts as a hub for services such as Find My, iCloud Backup, and Family Sharing.

Features

  • iCloud Drive — file storage and synchronization across devices.
  • iCloud Photos — full-resolution photo and video library stored in the cloud.
  • iCloud Backup — automatic daily backups of iOS devices.
  • Find My — locating Apple devices, AirTags, and consenting contacts.
  • iCloud Mail — an @icloud.com email account.
  • iCloud+ — a subscription tier adding Private Relay, Hide My Email, HomeKit Secure Video support, and larger storage plans.

Storage plans

Every Apple account includes 5 GB of free storage. Paid tiers have historically ranged from 50 GB to 12 TB. By 2023 the service was reported to have hundreds of millions of users worldwide.

Criticism and incidents

iCloud has faced scrutiny over privacy and security, including the 2014 celebrity photo leak attributed to targeted phishing and weak password practices, and debate in 2021 over a proposed on-device scanning system for child sexual abuse material, which Apple paused and later abandoned in favour of expanded end-to-end encryption options such as Advanced Data Protection (2022).
