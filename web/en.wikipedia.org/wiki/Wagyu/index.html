Wagyu

Country of originJapan
BreedsJapanese Black, Japanese Brown, Japanese Polled, Japanese Shorthorn
Known forIntensive marbling, rich flavour, high price

Wagyu (和牛, wa-gyū, "Japanese cattle") is the collective name for the four principal Japanese breeds of beef cattle. All Wagyu cattle derive from cross-breeding in the early twentieth century of native Japanese cattle with imported stock, mostly European.

Wagyu beef is known worldwide for its intense marbling — abundant intramuscular fat dispersed through the meat — which gives it a rich, fatty flavour and a buttery, tender texture. The most famous regional brands include Kobe beef (from Tajima strain Japanese Black cattle raised in Hyōgo Prefecture), Matsusaka beef, and Ōmi beef.

History

Cattle were introduced to Japan from China via the Korean peninsula in about the second century AD, as draft animals used in rice cultivation. For most of Japanese history, eating beef was limited by Buddhist traditions and, during the Edo period, formal prohibitions. After the Meiji Restoration of 1868, beef consumption was promoted and foreign breeds — including Brown Swiss, Devon, Shorthorn, Simmental, Ayrshire, Korean, Aberdeen Angus and Hereford — were imported for cross-breeding with native stock to increase size and milk yield.

By the middle of the twentieth century, regional populations had been consolidated into the four recognized Wagyu breeds: the Japanese Black (Kuroge Washu), which accounts for roughly 90% of Wagyu production; the Japanese Brown (Akage Washu); the Japanese Shorthorn (Nihon Tankaku Washu); and the Japanese Polled (Mukaku Washu).

Grading

In Japan, beef is graded by the Japan Meat Grading Association using a yield grade (A–C) and a quality grade (1–5) based on marbling, meat colour and brightness, firmness and texture, and fat colour and quality. The beef marbling standard (BMS) scale runs from 1 to 12; the highest grade, A5, denotes the greatest yield and quality, and A5 Wagyu from celebrated brands commands some of the highest prices of any meat in the world.

Wagyu outside Japan

From the 1970s onward, small numbers of Wagyu cattle and genetics were exported, and herds were established in Australia, the United States, Canada, and other countries. Because Japan later closed exports of live animals and genetics, overseas herds descend from a small founder population. Crosses with Angus and other breeds are common; such animals are often marketed as "American Wagyu" or "Wangus". Japan has moved to protect its geographic indications, including the name Kobe beef.
