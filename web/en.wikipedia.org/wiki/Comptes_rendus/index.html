Comptes rendus

Comptes rendus (French for "reports" or "proceedings"), in full Comptes rendus hebdomadaires des séances de l'Académie des sciences, is a French scientific journal founded in 1835 by the astronomer François Arago. Published by the French Academy of Sciences, it carried short communications of new research presented at the Academy's weekly meetings, and became one of the most important rapid-publication venues of nineteenth- and twentieth-century science.

History

Before 1835, results announced at the Academy appeared only slowly, or in memoirs published years later. Arago, as perpetual secretary, created the weekly Comptes rendus so that new discoveries could be printed within days, establishing priority. Much of French science of the period — the work of Cauchy, Pasteur, the Curies, Poincaré, and many others — first appeared in its pages. Foreign scientists also published there: announcements of discoveries in mathematics, physics, chemistry, astronomy, and biology routinely appeared as short notes, often just a few pages long.

Series and successors

Over time the journal was divided into subject series (mathematics, physics, chemistry, earth sciences, life sciences, and others). A major reorganisation in 2002 regrouped the titles as the Comptes Rendus series of the Academy — including Comptes Rendus Mathématique, Physique, Chimie, Géoscience, Biologies, Palevol, and Mécanique — published in French and English and, from 2020, as open-access journals on the Academy's own platform. The historical archive, digitised on Gallica, is a fundamental primary source for the history of modern science.
