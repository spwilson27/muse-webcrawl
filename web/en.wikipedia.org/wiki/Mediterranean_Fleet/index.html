Mediterranean Fleet

The Mediterranean Fleet, also known as the Mediterranean Station, was a formation of the Royal Navy. The Fleet was one of the most prestigious commands in the navy for the majority of its history, defending the vital sea link between the United Kingdom and the majority of the British Empire in the Eastern Hemisphere. The first Commander-in-Chief for the Mediterranean Fleet was the appointment of General at Sea Robert Blake in 1654; the fleet's permanent establishment dates from the late 17th century, and it was based principally at Gibraltar and, above all, Malta.

History

The Mediterranean Fleet guarded Britain's route to India through the Suez Canal after its opening in 1869, making Malta's Grand Harbour at Valletta one of the principal naval bases of the world. In the First World War the fleet supported the Dardanelles campaign and operated against the Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian navies.

During the Second World War, under Admiral Andrew Cunningham, the fleet fought the Italian Regia Marina across the Mediterranean, achieving successes at Taranto (November 1940) — the first all-aircraft naval raid in history — and Cape Matapan (March 1941). It sustained the siege of Malta, supported the North African campaign, and covered the landings in Sicily and Italy in 1943, though at heavy cost from Axis air attack, notably around Crete in 1941.

After the war the fleet declined with the British withdrawal from east of Suez. It played a role in the 1956 Suez Crisis, and was disbanded in 1967, its responsibilities passing to other commands as Malta's dockyard role faded following Maltese independence in 1964.

Commanders

Distinguished commanders-in-chief included John Jervis (Earl of St Vincent), Horatio Nelson (who held the command before Trafalgar), Lord Fisher, Roger Keyes, Dudley Pound, and Andrew Cunningham.
