Hunsrück

The Hunsrück (German pronunciation: [ˈhʊnsˌʁʏk]) is a low mountain range in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, bounded by the river valleys of the Moselle (north), the Nahe (south), and the Rhine (east). The landscape is one of rolling plateaus, deep river gorges, and extensive forests, and includes the Erbeskopf (816 m), the highest point in Rhineland-Palatinate.

Geography

The Hunsrück forms part of the Rhenish Massif, composed mainly of Devonian slate and quartzite. The plateau is cut by tributaries of the Moselle, Nahe and Rhine. The region is sparsely populated, with small towns such as Simmern, Kirchberg, Kastellaun and Morbach, and is known for the Hunsrückisch dialects of German.

Economy and culture

Historically poor agricultural land led to large emigration in the 19th century, notably to Brazil, where Riograndenser Hunsrückisch is still spoken. Today forestry, tourism (the Saar-Hunsrück Nature Park and Hunsrück-Hochwald National Park, established 2015) and slate heritage are important. The region gained fame as the setting of Edgar Reitz's television series Heimat (1984).

Emigration history

Thousands of Hunsrückers emigrated to southern Brazil from the 1820s onward, founding communities in Rio Grande do Sul; their dialect and customs persist there today.
