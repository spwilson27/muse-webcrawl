Happy Valley, Hong Kong

Happy Valley (Chinese: 跑馬地, lit. "horse-running place") is an affluent residential suburb of Hong Kong, located on Hong Kong Island, in Wan Chai District, surrounded by tall residential towers and known for the Happy Valley Racecourse. It is one of the oldest areas of the city.

History

The area was originally a marshy valley called Wong Nai Chung, drained by the Wong Nai Chung River. In the 1840s the British drained the marshes and built a racecourse on the flat ground; the malaria-ridden settlement earned the sarcastic name "Happy Valley", also a period euphemism for a graveyard — several cemeteries were indeed established there, including the Hong Kong Cemetery, St Michael's Catholic Cemetery and the Muslim and Parsee cemeteries.

The racecourse

The Happy Valley Racecourse, operated by the Hong Kong Jockey Club, held its first races in 1846 and remains one of Hong Kong's most famous landmarks. Floodlit "Happy Wednesday" night meetings draw large crowds to the course, which is ringed by skyscrapers. A disastrous fire in the grandstand in 1918 killed at least 590 people.

Transport

Happy Valley is served by trams on a loop branch of the Hong Kong Tramways, buses, and the Tin Hau and Causeway Bay stations of the MTR Island line nearby.
