Francesco II Sforza

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francesco II Sforza (4 February 1495 – 1 November 1535) was the last Sforza Duke of Milan, ruling from 1521 until his death. The second son of Ludovico Sforza ("il Moro") and Beatrice d'Este, he spent much of his youth in exile after his father was driven from Milan by the French invasions that opened the Italian Wars.

Life

Francesco grew up at the imperial court of Maximilian I, his cousin, after the French conquest of Milan in 1499–1500. When the Holy League drove the French from Italy, his elder brother Massimiliano was restored as duke in 1512, but the French victory at Marignano (1515) dispossessed the Sforza again. In 1521 the Emperor Charles V and Pope Leo X restored Francesco as duke, and Imperial forces secured him in Milan after the Battle of Bicocca (1522). Resentful of Imperial domination, Francesco joined the League of Cognac (1526) against Charles V, which brought an Imperial siege of the Castello Sforzesco and the sack of his duchy; he was nevertheless confirmed in Milan by the settlements concluding the war, including the Peace of Cambrai (1529) and the Treaty of Bologna.

Marriage and death

In 1534 Francesco married Christina of Denmark, the widowed daughter of Christian II of Denmark and niece of Charles V; the marriage, celebrated by proxy and then in Milan, produced no children. He died on 1 November 1535, the last of his line to rule Milan. With no heir, Charles V annexed the Duchy of Milan to the Spanish Habsburg monarchy, ending Milanese independence and beginning nearly two centuries of Spanish rule in Lombardy.
