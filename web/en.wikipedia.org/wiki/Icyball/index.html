The Icyball is an absorption refrigerator invented in the 1920s by the Australian engineer David Forbes Keith and manufactured in the United States by the Crosley Radio Corporation from about 1928. It required no electricity or moving parts, making it popular in rural areas lacking electrical service.

Operation

The device consists of two connected metal spheres joined by a tube, containing water and ammonia under pressure. To "charge" it, one sphere (the hot ball) is heated over a kerosene burner or other heat source for about 90 minutes, driving the ammonia into the second sphere (the cold ball), where it condenses. The cold ball is then placed inside an insulated cabinet; as the ammonia evaporates back, it absorbs heat and produces cooling for roughly a day before recharging is needed. One ball therefore serves as the generator/absorber and the other as the condenser/evaporator in alternating roles.

History

Crosley bought the American rights to Keith's patent and marketed the Icyball widely in rural America in the late 1920s and early 1930s. Rural electrification and cheaper compressor refrigerators ended its commercial life, but the design remains of interest for off-grid and solar refrigeration because it runs on heat alone.
