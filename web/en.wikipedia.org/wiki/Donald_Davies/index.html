Donald Davies

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Donald Davies
BornDonald Watts Davies, 7 June 1924, Treorchy, Wales
Died28 May 2000 (aged 75)
Known forCo-inventing Packet switching; coining the word 'packet'
[Image: Photograph of Donald Davies at the National Physical Laboratory in the 1960s]

Donald Watts Davies (7 June 1924 – 28 May 2000) was a Welsh computer scientist who, independently of Paul Baran in the United States, invented Packet switching — and gave it its name. His work at the UK's National Physical Laboratory (NPL) directly influenced the design of the ARPANET.

Davies had worked on Alan Turing's ACE computer project at NPL. In 1965, frustrated by the inefficiency of circuit-switched lines for bursty computer traffic, he conceived a store-and-forward network in which messages were divided into small fixed-size units — for which, consulting a linguist colleague, he chose the word packet. The NPL Mark I network went live in the early 1970s as the first local packet-switched network, and Davies's 1967 Gatlinburg paper brought the idea to the American team building the ARPANET, whose leader Lawrence Roberts adopted it.

Davies later turned to network security, leading work on encryption and banking security in the 1980s. Recognition came late — including posthumous induction into the Internet Hall of Fame — a modest fate for the man who named the atom of the Internet age.
