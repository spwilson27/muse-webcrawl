Provençal dialect

Provençal (Occitan: provençau) is a dialect of the Occitan language spoken in Provence, in south-eastern France, including the lower Rhône valley and much of the French Riviera hinterland. The term was formerly used for the whole of Occitan, whose medieval literary form — the language of the troubadours — was based largely on varieties from the south.

History

Occitan was the prestige literary language of medieval southern France and the vehicle of troubadour lyric poetry. Following the Albigensian Crusade and the administrative advance of French, it retreated to rural and domestic use. In the 19th century the Félibrige movement, led by Frédéric Mistral — who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904 and compiled the dictionary Lou Tresor dóu Felibrige — sought to revive Provençal letters using a Mistralian orthography.

Characteristics and status

Provençal comprises sub-dialects such as Maritime, Rhodanian and Niçard (around Nice, transitional toward Ligurian). Like other Occitan varieties it is severely endangered: intergenerational transmission largely ceased in the 20th century, though immersion schools (calandretas) and regional cultural programmes support revival.
