Boolean logic

Boolean logic is a system of logic based on the values "true" and "false," developed by the English mathematician George Boole in the mid-19th century, principally in The Mathematical Analysis of Logic (1847) and An Investigation of the Laws of Thought (1854). Its operations—conjunction (AND), disjunction (OR), and negation (NOT)—form the basis of Boolean algebra and of modern digital computing.

Operations

Conjunction yields true only when both operands are true; disjunction yields true when at least one operand is true; negation inverts a value. Further connectives such as NAND, NOR, and XOR can be built from these, and NAND alone is functionally complete. Boolean expressions can be manipulated using laws such as De Morgan's laws, distributivity, and absorption.

Applications

Boole's work, extended by Claude Shannon's application of Boolean algebra to switching circuits in 1937, underlies digital electronics, computer programming (conditional statements and Boolean data types), set theory, and database and search-engine queries.
