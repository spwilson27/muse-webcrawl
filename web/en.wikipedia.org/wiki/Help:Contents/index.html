Help:Contents

Help pages explain how to use and contribute to Wikipedia. This contents page organises help by task: reading the encyclopedia, editing articles, contributing media, communicating with other editors, and understanding Wikipedia's technical and community infrastructure.

ContentsReading · Searching · Editing · Links and references · Media · Talk and discussion · Accounts · Tracking changes · Policies and guidelines

Reading Wikipedia

Articles can be browsed by subject through the contents pages and portals, followed through hyperlinks, or found with the search box, which supports full-text search and suggestions as you type. Help is available on using the search effectively, on printing and exporting articles, and on accessing Wikipedia on mobile devices and in other languages.

Editing

Most pages can be edited by anyone. The introduction walks new editors through making a first edit, in either the visual editor or the source (wikitext) editor. Further help covers:

  • Formatting — headings, lists, bold and italic text, and the wikitext markup behind them.
  • Links — linking to other articles, to sections, and to external websites.
  • References — adding citations to reliable sources, using footnotes and reference lists, in line with verifiability policy.
  • Images and media — uploading files to Wikimedia Commons and placing them in articles with captions and thumbnails.
  • Tables, templates and categories — the building blocks that structure articles, including infoboxes and navigation templates.
  • Creating an article — notability requirements, draft space, and review of new articles by other editors.

Talk and communication

Every article has a talk page for discussing improvements. Help pages explain how to sign posts, indent replies, notify other editors, and request help or a third opinion when editors disagree.

Accounts and preferences

Registering an account is free and optional, and gives an editor a username, a user page, a watchlist of pages to follow, and preferences for appearance and editing. Help covers choosing a username, password security, and what to do about a compromised account.

Tracking changes

Readers and editors can follow the encyclopedia's constant change through recent changes, page histories showing every past version of an article, watchlists, and diffs comparing two versions.

Asking for help

If these pages do not answer a question, editors can ask at the help desk, the Teahouse for newcomers, or the reference desk for questions about facts and research. The community portal points to further resources.
