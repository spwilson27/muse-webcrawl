Pomology

Pomology (from Latin pomum, "fruit", + -logy, "study") is a branch of botany that studies fruit and its cultivation in any given place. It is closely related to horticulture and is sometimes considered a subdivision of it.

The study of stone fruits such as peaches, plums, and cherries forms a subdiscipline of pomology. Pomologists study trees, vines, and shrubs that produce edible fruits, including their taxonomy, breeding, physiology, and culture.

History

Pomology has been practised since antiquity in regions where fruit trees were cultivated, including the Mediterranean basin and China. The modern discipline developed in the nineteenth century as systematic descriptions of fruit varieties, often accompanied by detailed illustrations, were published in works such as Johann Hermann Knoop's Pomologia (1758) and the catalogues of national fruit collections.

In the United States, pomology became an organised science with the founding of the American Pomological Society in 1848, and the United States Department of Agriculture maintained a Division of Pomology whose watercolor collection documented thousands of fruit varieties.
