Kim is a novel by the English author Rudyard Kipling, first published in serial form in McClure's Magazine from December 1900 to October 1901 and in book form by Macmillan in October 1901. Set in British India against the backdrop of the Great Game — the political conflict between Britain and Russia in Central Asia — it follows the orphan Kimball O'Hara, known as Kim, who is drawn both into British espionage and into a spiritual quest as the chela (disciple) of a Tibetan lama. It is widely regarded as Kipling's masterpiece.

Plot summary

Kimball O'Hara is the orphaned son of an Irish soldier and a poor white mother in Lahore. Raised as a street child, fluent in the languages and ways of the bazaar, he befriends an elderly Tibetan lama who is searching for the legendary River of the Arrow, whose waters free a man from the Wheel of Things. Kim becomes the lama's disciple and travels with him across northern India.

At the same time, Kim is recruited into the British Secret Service by the Pathan horse-trader and agent Mahbub Ali and trained by Colonel Creighton and the sahib Lurgan, learning observation, disguise, and the famous memory exercise known as "Kim's Game" (the Play of the Jewels). His dual journeys — the lama's spiritual search and his own work in the Great Game, culminating in the interception of Russian agents in the Himalayas — run through the novel in parallel, and its ending leaves the two paths unresolved in tension.

Themes

The novel is celebrated for its panoramic, affectionate depiction of India's peoples, religions, languages, and landscapes along the Grand Trunk Road, and for its exploration of identity: Kim's constant question "Who is Kim?" remains unanswered. Postcolonial critics, notably Edward Said, have also examined the novel's imperial assumptions and its place in the literature of empire.

Legacy

Kim founded much of the modern espionage novel's tradition; its tradecraft details and "Great Game" setting influenced later spy fiction. "Kim's Game" is still used in military observation training and in Scouting. The novel has been adapted for film (notably MGM's 1950 version starring Errol Flynn and Dean Stockwell) and television.
