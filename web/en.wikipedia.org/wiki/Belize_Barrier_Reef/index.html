The Belize Barrier Reef is a series of coral reefs straddling the coast of Belize, roughly 300 metres offshore in the north and 40 kilometres in the south, extending for about 300 kilometres (190 miles). It is the largest barrier reef in the Northern Hemisphere and part of the greater Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System, the world's second-largest reef system after Australia's Great Barrier Reef.

Description

The reef system includes fringing reefs, the offshore barrier reef itself, hundreds of sand cays, mangrove forests, coastal lagoons and three of the Caribbean's four true atolls: Turneffe Atoll, Lighthouse Reef and Glover's Reef. The Great Blue Hole, a giant marine sinkhole in Lighthouse Reef made famous by Jacques Cousteau, is one of the world's most celebrated dive sites. Charles Darwin described the reef in 1842 as "the most remarkable reef in the West Indies".

Biodiversity

The reef supports more than 500 fish species, 65 species of stony coral, several hundred invertebrate species, West Indian manatees, sea turtles, American crocodiles and rich seabird colonies. Seven protected areas form the Belize Barrier Reef Reserve System, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

Conservation

Threats include climate-change-driven coral bleaching, coastal development, overfishing and invasive lionfish. The site was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2009 because of mangrove cutting and oil exploration, and removed in 2018 after Belize imposed a moratorium on offshore oil exploration and strengthened mangrove protections. Tourism — diving, snorkelling and sport fishing — is a mainstay of the Belizean economy.
