Appropriate technology

Appropriate technology is a movement and set of design principles encompassing technological choice and application that is small-scale, affordable, labour-intensive, energy-efficient, environmentally sound, and locally autonomous. It was originally articulated by the economist Ernst Friedrich Schumacher in his 1973 book Small Is Beautiful, where he called it "intermediate technology" — technology far more productive than traditional methods but far cheaper and simpler than the capital-intensive technology of industrialised economies.

Principles

Appropriate technology is designed to fit the economic, cultural, and environmental conditions of its users, particularly in developing regions. Designs favour local materials, skills, and maintenance capacity; examples include treadle pumps and low-cost water filters in rural Asia and Africa, bicycle-powered machinery, passive-solar building design, rocket stoves, and micro-hydro installations. The goal is to increase productivity and well-being without creating dependence on imported fuel, parts, or expertise.

History and influence

Schumacher founded the Intermediate Technology Development Group (now Practical Action) in 1966. The movement spread through development agencies in the 1970s and influenced later fields including sustainable design, permaculture, and the open-source appropriate technology movement, which publishes designs freely online. Critics have argued that "intermediate" solutions can entrench lower living standards, while supporters respond that appropriateness is contextual: a technology counts as appropriate if the people using it can own, repair, and benefit from it.
