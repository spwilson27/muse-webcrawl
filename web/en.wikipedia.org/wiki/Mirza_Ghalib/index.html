Mirza Asadullah Beg Khan (27 December 1797 – 15 February 1869), known by his pen name Ghalib, was an Indian poet widely regarded as the greatest master of the Urdu ghazal. Writing in both Urdu and Persian during the twilight of the Mughal Empire, he served as poet laureate at the court of the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar.

Life

Ghalib was born in Agra into a family of Turkish military descent and moved to Delhi as a young man, where he spent most of his life in genteel poverty, dependent on pensions and patronage. He witnessed the Indian Rebellion of 1857 and the destruction of old Delhi, which he chronicled in his Persian diary Dastanbu. His Urdu letters, written in a conversational style, are considered foundational works of Urdu prose.

Poetry

His Urdu divan is small but extraordinarily dense: his ghazals explore love, loss, wine, mysticism, and the paradoxes of existence with irony and philosophical depth, often in deliberately difficult language. Though he prized his Persian verse more highly, it is the Urdu ghazals — endlessly quoted, sung, and commented upon — that made him the most beloved poet of the Urdu language in India and Pakistan.
