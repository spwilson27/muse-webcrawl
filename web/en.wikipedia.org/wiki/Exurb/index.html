Exurb

An exurb (or exurban area) is an area outside the typically denser inner suburban area, at the edge of a metropolitan area, which has some economic and commuting connection to the metro area, low housing density, and growth. It is in the outskirts of a large city, or beyond the city's suburbs. Exurbs consist of "the outermost, newest, and fastest-growing rings" of metropolitan regions, often characterized by large-lot single-family housing and long commutes.

Definition and usage

The term was coined in the 1950s by writer Auguste Comte Spectorsky to describe the ring of prosperous communities beyond the suburbs that are commutable to a city. The Brookings Institution defined exurbs as communities located on the urban fringe with a significant share of residents commuting to the urban core. Exurban growth accelerated in the United States in the 1990s and 2000s, driven by cheaper land, larger houses, highway construction, and, later, remote work.

Characteristics and criticism

Exurbs typically have lower population density than suburbs, limited public transport, and high car dependence. Critics associate exurban development with sprawl, loss of farmland and habitat, and high infrastructure costs, while residents cite space, affordability, and perceived safety and school quality as attractions.
