The Stoa of Attalos (also spelled Attalus) is a covered portico on the east side of the Ancient Agora of Athens. It was built by and named after King Attalos II of Pergamon, who ruled between 159 and 138 BC, as a gift to the city in gratitude for the education he received there. Destroyed by the Heruli in AD 267, it was fully reconstructed in 1953–1956 by the American School of Classical Studies at Athens, with funding from the Rockefeller family, and now houses the Museum of the Ancient Agora.

Architecture

The stoa is about 115 metres long and two storeys high. Its façade combines a Doric colonnade on the ground floor with Ionic columns above; the back wall, of limestone, contained 21 shops on each floor. It is the most fully restored ancient building in the Agora and gives visitors a vivid impression of the commercial architecture of Hellenistic Athens.

Museum

The museum's collection displays artefacts excavated in the Agora—ostraka used in ostracism votes, jury ballots, measuring standards, coins and sculpture—illustrating the civic life of classical Athens.
