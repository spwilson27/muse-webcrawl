Heracles (Roman: Hercules) is the greatest hero of Greek mythology, a son of Zeus famed for superhuman strength and for completing the Twelve Labours imposed by King Eurystheus — including slaying the Nemean lion and the Hydra, cleaning the Augean stables, and fetching Cerberus from the underworld. Driven mad by Hera, he had killed his own family, and the labours were penance; on death by a poisoned robe he was lifted to Olympus as a god.

Legacy

Heracles was worshipped as both hero and god across the Greek and Roman worlds, patron of gymnasiums and model of virtue through toil — the philosopher Prodicus' tale of Heracles at the crossroads, choosing Virtue's hard path over Vice's easy one, became a moral staple. His name echoes in the Pillars of Hercules at Gibraltar and in countless artworks.
