Mkhitar Sparapet

Born: 17th century
Died: 1730
Allegiance: Armenians of Syunik

Mkhitar Sparapet (died 1730) was an Armenian military commander (sparapet means commander-in-chief) who led the Armenian liberation movement in Syunik (Zangezur) in the 1720s, succeeding David Bek.

Career

Mkhitar served under David Bek in the defense of the Armenian strongholds of Syunik against Ottoman and Persian forces and local Muslim khans allied with them. When David Bek died — by some accounts in 1728 — Mkhitar assumed command of the Armenian forces at Halidzor and the other fortresses of the region.

As commander, Mkhitar continued resistance against Ottoman campaigns in the Caucasus, at one point seeking aid from both Persia and Russia. Internal disputes with local Armenian meliks and clergy weakened his position; according to tradition, he was murdered in 1730 at Khndzoresk by fellow Armenians frustrated with his strict discipline, after which organized resistance in Syunik collapsed.

Legacy

Together with David Bek, Mkhitar Sparapet is remembered as a hero of Armenian national liberation, and his story is retold in Raffi's historical novel David Bek and in the opera of the same name.
