Cucurbita pepo

Cucurbita pepo
Family: Cucurbitaceae
Origin: North America
Includes: Pumpkin, zucchini, acorn squash

Cucurbita pepo is a cultivated plant of the genus Cucurbita, yielding varieties of winter squash and pumpkin, as well as summer squashes such as zucchini (courgette), yellow squash and pattypan. It has been domesticated in the Americas for thousands of years.

Domestication

Archaeological seeds from sites in Mexico and the eastern United States date domestication to at least 8,000–10,000 years ago, making it one of the earliest domesticated species in the New World. Indigenous peoples valued its seeds and flesh; thick-rinded forms stored well through winter.

Varieties

Cultivar groups include pumpkins (including Halloween jack-o'-lantern types and oil-seed Styrian pumpkins), acorn, delicata, spaghetti squash, crookneck, and ornamental gourds. The species' diversity of fruit shapes is controlled by relatively few genes.

Cultivation

Plants are monoecious, bee-pollinated annual vines or bushes, frost-sensitive and fast-growing. Seeds are eaten toasted (pepitas) and pressed for oil.
