Willow warbler

Willow warbler (Phylloscopus trochilus) is a very common and widespread leaf warbler which breeds throughout northern and temperate Europe and the Palearctic, from Ireland east to the Anadyr River basin in eastern Siberia, and migrates to winter in sub-Saharan Africa.

The willow warbler (Phylloscopus trochilus) is a very common and widespread leaf warbler which breeds throughout northern and temperate Europe and across the Palearctic to eastern Siberia. It is strongly migratory, with the whole population wintering in sub-Saharan Africa.

Description

The willow warbler is a small bird, 11–12.5 cm (4.3–4.9 in) long and weighing about 7–15 g. It has greenish-brown upperparts and off-white to yellowish underparts, a yellowish face and supercilium, and pale brownish-pink legs — the latter and its descending, liquid song distinguish it from the very similar chiffchaff. Its song is a musical descending cascade of notes, unlike the chiffchaff's repeated "chiff-chaff".

Distribution and habitat

It breeds in open woodland, scrub, birch and willow thickets and forest edges across northern Eurasia. Three subspecies are recognised, with eastern birds (P. t. yakutensis) undertaking some of the longest migrations, relative to body size, of any bird — up to 12,000 km to East Africa.

Behaviour and ecology

The willow warbler is insectivorous, feeding actively among foliage. The female builds a domed nest of grass on or near the ground and lays 4–8 eggs. Chicks fledge after about two weeks. The species remains abundant — historically one of Britain's commonest summer visitors — although southern populations have declined in recent decades, possibly owing to climate change and habitat loss on migration routes and wintering grounds. It is classified as Least Concern by the IUCN.
