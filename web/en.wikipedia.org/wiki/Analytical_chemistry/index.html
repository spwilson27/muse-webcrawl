Analytical chemistry is the science of identifying substances (qualitative analysis) and determining how much of them is present (quantitative analysis). It answers the chemist's most practical questions: what is this, and how pure is it?

Classical 'wet' methods — titration, precipitation and gravimetric analysis — remain in use, but modern analysis is dominated by instruments: chromatography to separate mixtures, spectroscopy to identify them, and mass spectrometry to weigh molecules and fragments with extreme precision.

[Image: A scientist titrating with a burette, and a chromatography instrument]

Applications

Analytical chemistry guards drinking water, verifies medicines, tests athletes for banned substances, dates archaeological finds, monitors pollution and quality-controls everything from steel to whisky. Detection limits now reach parts per trillion.

Accuracy and precision

Analysts distinguish accuracy (closeness to the true value) from precision (reproducibility), and use blanks, standards and calibration curves to control systematic error.
