Youm-e-Takbir (Urdu: یومِ تکبیر, "Day of Greatness") is a national day in Pakistan observed on 28 May each year. It commemorates the Chagai-I nuclear tests conducted on 28 May 1998, when Pakistan carried out its first public nuclear weapons tests in the Ras Koh hills of the Chagai District in Balochistan. The name derives from the takbir — the cry of Allahu Akbar ("God is greater") — associated with the celebrations that followed the tests.

Background

Pakistan's nuclear weapons programme began in the 1970s, following the 1971 war and India's first nuclear test in 1974. After India conducted the Pokhran-II tests on 11 and 13 May 1998, Pakistan responded in kind despite international pressure and offers of aid to refrain. On 28 May 1998, five underground devices were detonated at Chagai, and a sixth followed on 30 May at Kharan. Pakistan thereby became the seventh country in the world — and the first Muslim-majority country — to publicly test nuclear weapons.

Observance

The day was proposed as a national commemoration under the government of Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif, whose administration authorised the 1998 tests. It is marked by official ceremonies, speeches, flag-hoisting, tributes to the scientists and engineers of the Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission and Khan Research Laboratories — including Dr Abdul Qadeer Khan — and media programming on the history of the programme. In 2024 the federal government declared 28 May a public holiday in Pakistan.
