Coxsackieviruses are non-enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA viruses belonging to the genus Enterovirus in the family Picornaviridae. They are named after Coxsackie, New York, where they were first isolated from the faeces of children with paralytic disease in 1948 by Gilbert Dalldorf and Grace Sickles.

Coxsackieviruses are divided into two groups. Group A viruses (23 serotypes) typically infect skin and mucous membranes and cause hand, foot and mouth disease and herpangina. Group B viruses (6 serotypes) more often infect internal organs such as the heart, pleura, pancreas and liver, and are associated with myocarditis, pericarditis, pleurodynia (Bornholm disease), and, in some studies, a possible role in type 1 diabetes.

Transmission and symptoms

Transmission occurs mainly by the faecal–oral route and through respiratory droplets. Most infections are mild or asymptomatic, presenting as fever, sore throat, rash, or gastrointestinal upset, but severe disease can occur in newborns and in the immunocompromised.

Treatment and prevention

There is no specific antiviral treatment or licensed vaccine for coxsackieviruses in general use; care is supportive. Good hand hygiene and sanitation reduce transmission.
