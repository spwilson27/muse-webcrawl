Tibet Autonomous Region

The Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR), often shortened to Tibet, is an autonomous region of the People's Republic of China, established in 1965. It lies on the Tibetan Plateau in southwest China and is the second-largest province-level division of China by area, covering about 1.2 million square kilometres, though it has one of the smallest populations, at roughly 3.6 million people. Its capital and largest city is Lhasa.

Geography

The region occupies much of the Tibetan Plateau, the highest region on Earth, with an average elevation above 4,000 metres. It is bordered by Xinjiang, Qinghai, Sichuan and Yunnan within China, and internationally by India, Nepal, Bhutan and Myanmar. Mount Everest, the world's highest peak, stands on its border with Nepal. Major rivers including the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra), the upper Yangtze, Mekong and Salween have their sources in or near the region.

History

The autonomous region covers most of the area historically governed from Lhasa by the Ganden Phodrang government of the Dalai Lamas until 1959. Following the Seventeen Point Agreement of 1951, Tibet was incorporated into the People's Republic of China; after the 1959 uprising the 14th Dalai Lama fled to India, and the Tibet Autonomous Region was formally established in 1965. Other historically Tibetan areas were incorporated into the neighbouring provinces of Qinghai, Sichuan, Yunnan and Gansu.

Administrative divisions and economy

The region administers the prefecture-level city of Lhasa and the prefectures of Shannan, Xigazê, Nyingchi, Qamdo, Nagqu and Ngari. Its economy has traditionally rested on agriculture and animal husbandry — barley, wheat, yaks and sheep — though tourism, mining and infrastructure development have grown rapidly, aided by the Qinghai–Tibet railway, opened in 2006, which links Lhasa with Xining. The population is predominantly ethnic Tibetan, most of whom practise Tibetan Buddhism.
