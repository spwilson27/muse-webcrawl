Metabolite

In biochemistry, a metabolite is an intermediate or end product of metabolism. The term is usually used for small molecules. Metabolites have various functions, including fuel, structure, signalling, stimulatory and inhibitory effects on enzymes, catalytic activity of their own (usually as a cofactor to an enzyme), defence, and interactions with other organisms (e.g. pigments, odorants, and pheromones).

Primary and secondary metabolites

A primary metabolite is directly involved in the normal growth, development, and reproduction of an organism: examples include amino acids, nucleotides, organic acids such as lactic acid, vitamins, and ethanol produced by fermentation. Secondary metabolites, by contrast, are not directly required for growth and are often restricted to particular species or groups of organisms; they include antibiotics such as penicillin, pigments such as carotenoids, alkaloids, and terpenoids, many of which have important ecological functions and pharmacological uses.

The complete set of metabolites in a biological sample is called the metabolome, and its systematic study is metabolomics, typically using mass spectrometry and NMR spectroscopy. In pharmacology, the term metabolite also denotes the products of drug metabolism — the chemical transformations a drug undergoes in the body, which may be inactive, active (sometimes more potent than the parent drug), or toxic.
