Rita Levi-Montalcini (22 April 1909 – 30 December 2012) was an Italian neurologist who, with Stanley Cohen, discovered nerve growth factor (NGF), the first identified growth factor regulating cell growth and differentiation in the nervous system. For this work they received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1986. A senator for life in the Italian Senate, she was the first Nobel laureate to reach the age of 100.

Early life and wartime research

Born in Turin to a Jewish family, Levi-Montalcini overcame her father's belief that women should not pursue careers and entered the University of Turin medical school, graduating in 1936 and specialising in neurology and psychiatry under Giuseppe Levi. Italy's Fascist racial laws of 1938 barred her from academic work; during the Second World War she set up a makeshift laboratory in her bedroom and later in a cottage near Asti, studying nerve fibre growth in chick embryos with rudimentary equipment — the experiments that first suggested a diffusible factor controlling neuronal survival.

Discovery of NGF

In 1947 she accepted a position at Washington University in St. Louis with Viktor Hamburger, initially for ten months; she stayed for three decades. Working with biochemist Stanley Cohen in the 1950s, she isolated nerve growth factor — a protein secreted by target tissues (and, serendipitously, abundant in mouse tumour tissue, snake venom and mouse salivary glands) that causes nerve cells to grow and survive. The discovery transformed developmental neurobiology and opened the field of growth factor research, with implications for understanding neurodegenerative diseases and cancer.

Later life

Levi-Montalcini divided her career between the United States and Italy, founding the Institute of Cell Biology of the CNR in Rome in 1969. She was the first woman admitted to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences. In 2001 she was appointed Senator for Life by President Carlo Azeglio Ciampi. She championed education for African women through her foundation, continued publishing into her centenary year, and died in Rome in 2012 at the age of 103.
