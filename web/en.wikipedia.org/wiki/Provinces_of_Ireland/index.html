Provinces of Ireland

There are four provinces of Ireland: Connacht, Leinster, Munster and Ulster. The Irish word for a province is cúige, literally "a fifth", recalling a period when there were five provinces, the fifth being Meath. The provinces have no administrative or governmental functions today, but they remain important in sport, culture and identity, and are used in some ceremonial and statistical contexts.

Ulster straddles the border between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland: six of its nine counties are in the United Kingdom and three in the Republic. The other three provinces lie entirely within the Republic.

History

The provinces originate in the early medieval kingdoms of Ireland. In mythology and early history, Ireland was divided among five great kingdoms — Ulster, Connacht, Leinster, Munster and Meath — whose high kings ruled from Tara. Meath was absorbed into Leinster in the 18th century. The present boundaries were largely fixed under English administration in the 17th century; the province of Meath survived longest in the form of a bishopric and in the phrase "the Royal County".

Sport

The provinces field representative teams in rugby union — Leinster, Munster, Ulster and Connacht compete in the United Rugby Championship and European competitions — and provincial championships structure Gaelic games under the GAA.
