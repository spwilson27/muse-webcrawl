Robert Louis Stevenson (born Robert Lewis Balfour Stevenson; 13 November 1850 – 3 December 1894) was a Scottish novelist, essayist, poet and travel writer. He is best known for works such as Treasure Island, Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde, Kidnapped and A Child's Garden of Verses.

Life

Born in Edinburgh into a family of lighthouse engineers, Stevenson suffered from chronic respiratory illness throughout his life. He studied engineering and then law at the University of Edinburgh but devoted himself to writing. His travels in France produced his first book, An Inland Voyage (1878), followed by Travels with a Donkey in the Cévennes (1879). He met the American Fanny Osbourne in France, followed her to California, and married her in 1880, afterwards returning to Britain.

Major works

Treasure Island (1883), first serialised in a children's magazine, established the modern pirate mythos of treasure maps marked with an "X", one-legged seamen and talking parrots. Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886) became an immediate success and a lasting exploration of the duality of human nature; its title characters entered the language. The historical adventure Kidnapped (1886), set after the Jacobite rising of 1745, and its sequel Catriona followed. Later works include The Master of Ballantrae (1889) and the unfinished Weir of Hermiston.

Samoa and death

Seeking a climate for his health, Stevenson voyaged in the Pacific from 1888 and settled at Vailima in Samoa, where the Samoans named him Tusitala — "teller of tales". He wrote extensively about the Pacific and involved himself in local politics. He died there suddenly in 1894, probably of a cerebral haemorrhage, aged 44, and was buried on Mount Vaea overlooking the sea. His poem "Requiem", with the lines "Home is the sailor, home from sea", is inscribed on his tomb.

Legacy

Celebrated in his lifetime, Stevenson's reputation dipped among modernist critics who dismissed him as a children's writer, but he has since been restored to the canon and ranks among the most translated authors in the world.
