Academy Award for Best Cinematography

The Academy Award for Best Cinematography honours the director of photography of a film, presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences since the first Academy Awards in 1929 (won by Charles Rosher and Karl Struss for Sunrise).


Presented by: AMPAS
First awarded: 1929
Most wins: Leon Shamroy and Joseph Ruttenberg (4 each)

History

From 1939 to 1966 (with brief variation) separate awards were given for black-and-white and colour cinematography. Notable multiple winners include Freddie Young, Robert Surtees, Sven Nykvist, Vittorio Storaro, Conrad Hall, Janusz Kamiński, Emmanuel Lubezki — who won three consecutive awards for Gravity, Birdman and The Revenant — and Roger Deakins. Recent winners include Hoyte van Hoytema (Oppenheimer) and Lol Crawley (The Brutalist).
