Metroid is an action-adventure video game developed and published by Nintendo, first released in Japan in August 1986 for the Famicom Disk System and in North America in 1987 for the Nintendo Entertainment System. It is the first instalment of the Metroid series. The player controls bounty hunter Samus Aran, who infiltrates the planet Zebes to destroy the parasitic Metroid organisms stolen by the Space Pirates and their leader, the Mother Brain.

Gameplay

Unlike the linear platformers of its era, Metroid presents a large, interconnected world that the player explores non-linearly, backtracking through earlier areas as new abilities are acquired. Permanent power-ups — the Morph Ball, missiles, the Varia Suit, the Screw Attack — progressively open new routes, a design that, combined with Castlevania, later gave the genre the name "Metroidvania". The NES version used a password system to save progress. The game was also notable for its atmospheric isolation and for revealing, to players who finished quickly, that Samus was a woman — a surprise widely remarked upon at the time.

Development and legacy

The game was produced by Gunpei Yokoi, directed by Satoru Okada and Masao Yamamoto, with music by Hirokazu Tanaka, whose sparse, eerie score is often credited with much of the game's mood. Metroid sold over a million copies and spawned numerous sequels, including Metroid II, Super Metroid, and the Metroid Prime series. A remake, Metroid: Zero Mission (2004), retold the story with modernised mechanics. The original is regularly listed among the most influential games of the 1980s.
