Tic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tic is a sudden, rapid, recurrent, nonrhythmic motor movement or vocalization. Tics are involuntary or semi-voluntary actions that typically occur in bouts, worsen with stress, anxiety, excitement or fatigue, and can often be suppressed temporarily at the cost of mounting inner tension. They are a hallmark feature of tic disorders, most notably Tourette syndrome, in which both motor and vocal tics are present for more than a year.

Classification

Tics are classified as motor or vocal (phonic), and as simple or complex. Simple motor tics are brief, meaningless movements involving a limited number of muscle groups, such as eye blinking, nose wrinkling, head jerking and shoulder shrugging. Complex motor tics are coordinated patterns of movement resembling normal motor actions but performed inappropriately, and may include touching, tapping, jumping, or, rarely, obscene gestures (copropraxia). Simple vocal tics include throat clearing, sniffing, grunting and barking sounds, while complex vocal tics include repeating words or phrases (echolalia, palilalia) and, in a minority of cases, involuntary swearing (coprolalia).

Causes and neurobiology

The precise cause of tics is not fully understood, but evidence points to dysfunction in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits and to abnormalities in dopaminergic neurotransmission. Tic disorders run in families and are thought to arise from a combination of genetic vulnerability and environmental factors. Tics typically begin in childhood, peak in severity around ages 10 to 12, and often diminish or remit by late adolescence.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical, based on observation and history. Many people with mild tics require no treatment. When tics cause distress or impairment, behavioral therapies such as Comprehensive Behavioral Intervention for Tics (CBIT), including habit reversal training, are considered first-line. Medications, including alpha-2 adrenergic agonists and antipsychotics, may be used in more severe cases.
