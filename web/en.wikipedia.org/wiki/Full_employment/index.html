Full employment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Full employment is an economic situation in which all available labour resources are being used in the most efficient way possible. In practice it does not mean zero unemployment: economists define full employment as the level of employment at which the remaining joblessness consists only of frictional and structural unemployment — people between jobs or retraining — with no cyclical, demand-deficient unemployment. The unemployment rate consistent with full employment is often called the natural rate of unemployment or NAIRU (the non-accelerating inflation rate of unemployment).

Full employment: everyone willing and able to work at prevailing wages can find a job.

Definitions and measurement

British economist William Beveridge, in his 1944 book Full Employment in a Free Society, defined full employment as having more vacant jobs than unemployed workers, roughly corresponding to an unemployment rate around 3 per cent. Modern estimates of the NAIRU vary by country and period — often placed between 4 and 6 per cent for the United States — and are revised as labour markets change. Because the concept is unobservable, central banks such as the Federal Reserve, charged by law with pursuing "maximum employment", estimate it from inflation behaviour, vacancies and wage growth.

Policy

Keynesian economics holds that governments can and should use fiscal and monetary policy to restore full employment in a slump. Monetarist and new classical critics, notably Milton Friedman, argued there is a natural rate determined by labour-market structure, and that attempts to hold unemployment below it produce accelerating inflation — an argument embodied in the expectations-augmented Phillips curve. Post-war Western governments formally committed to full employment (for example Britain's 1944 White Paper and Australia's 1945 White Paper), and the goal remained central until the stagflation of the 1970s shifted priorities toward inflation control. Job guarantee and employer-of-last-resort schemes remain a minority proposal for achieving literal full employment.
