Charles Goodnight

Charles Goodnight (March 5, 1836 – December 12, 1929), also known as Charlie Goodnight, was an American rancher in the American West. Regarded as one of the most famous cattle barons of the Texas Panhandle, he is credited, with his partner Oliver Loving, with establishing the Goodnight–Loving Trail, one of the most heavily used cattle trails of the Southwest.

DiedDecember 12, 1929 (aged 93), Phoenix, Arizona, U.S.

Early life

Goodnight was born in Illinois and moved with his family to Texas in 1845. He worked as a cowboy and served with the Texas Rangers during the Civil War era, fighting Comanche raiders on the frontier.

Cattle career

In 1866 Goodnight and Oliver Loving blazed the Goodnight–Loving Trail from Texas through New Mexico to Colorado, avoiding the more dangerous routes. After Loving was killed by Comanches in 1867, Goodnight carried on their partnership obligations to Loving's family. In 1876 he drove cattle into Palo Duro Canyon and, with English investor John Adair, founded the JA Ranch, which grew to more than a million acres.

Bison preservation

At the urging of his wife Mary Ann ("Molly"), Goodnight preserved a small herd of American bison on the ranch in the 1880s, when the species stood near extinction. The surviving Goodnight herd later contributed animals to restock preserves including Yellowstone National Park, and descendants of the herd still live at Caprock Canyons State Park in Texas.

Legacy

Goodnight is credited with inventing the chuck wagon, the mobile kitchen that fed cowboys on trail drives. He was a model for the character Woodrow Call in Larry McMurtry's novel Lonesome Dove, loosely based on the Goodnight–Loving drives.
