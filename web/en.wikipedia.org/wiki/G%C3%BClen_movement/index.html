Gülen movement

The Gülen movement (Turkish: Gülen hareketi), known to followers as Hizmet ("the Service"), is a transnational Islamic social movement inspired by the teachings of the Turkish preacher Fethullah Gülen (1941–2024). It is known for its network of schools, tutoring centres, media outlets, businesses and charities in Turkey and around the world.

Origins

The movement emerged in Turkey in the 1970s around Gülen's sermons emphasising education, interfaith dialogue and service. Its schools spread across Central Asia, Africa, the Balkans and the West after the 1990s.

Conflict with the Turkish state

Once allied with Turkey's ruling AK Party, the movement fell out with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan after the 2013 corruption investigations. The Turkish government blamed the movement for the failed 2016 coup attempt, designated it a terrorist organisation ("FETÖ"), and carried out mass purges; Gülen, living in the United States, denied involvement. Many other countries did not adopt Turkey's designation.
