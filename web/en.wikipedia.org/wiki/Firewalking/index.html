Firewalking

Firewalking is the act of walking barefoot over a bed of hot embers or stones. It has been practised by many peoples and cultures in many parts of the world, with the earliest evidence from Iron Age India around 1200 BCE. Firewalking is often used as a rite of passage, as a test of strength and courage, or, in religion, as a test of one's faith. It has also become popular at corporate seminars and motivational workshops.

History and traditions

Firewalking traditions include the Anastenaria of Greece and Bulgaria, in which devotees of Saint Constantine walk on embers while carrying icons; Hindu festivals in India, Sri Lanka, Fiji and South Africa, including the Thimithi festival in honour of the goddess Draupadi; rituals in Sri Lanka, Japan (hikeshi and ceremonies at Mount Takao), and among Aboriginal, African and Native American peoples. On the Fijian island of Beqa, the Sawau people are renowned for walking on white-hot stones, a practice now also demonstrated for visitors.

Physics

Firewalking need not be miraculous: physicists attribute its feasibility to physics rather than supernatural powers. Wood embers have poor thermal conductivity, and a walker's feet are in contact with any given ember only briefly at a brisk pace. In addition, a thin layer of ash insulates the coals, and moisture on the feet may contribute a Leidenfrost-type vapour barrier. Burns do occur when participants move too slowly, when the bed contains metal or stones that conduct heat well, or when embers adhere to the feet — large-scale seminar firewalks have occasionally resulted in dozens of participants requiring treatment.

Modern practice

From the 1970s onward, firewalking was popularised in the United States as a self-help exercise, notably by Tolly Burkan and later in large motivational events. Scientists and sceptics have used public firewalks to teach critical thinking about extraordinary claims, demonstrating that the feat is explicable without altered states, while acknowledging its ritual and psychological significance for many communities.
