SubclassNautiloidea

The Nautiloidea are a large and diverse group of marine cephalopods in the subclass Nautiloidea, characterised by a chambered external shell and simple, concave sutures where the septa meet the shell wall. They first appeared in the Late Cambrian and were among the dominant marine predators of the Palaeozoic era.

Evolution

Early nautiloids diversified rapidly in the Ordovician, evolving straight, curved and coiled shells; some forms, such as the orthoconic endocerids, reached several metres in length. The group declined after the Devonian but survived in coiled forms through the Mesozoic alongside the related ammonoids.

Living representatives

Today the subclass survives only in the family Nautilidae — the genera Nautilus and Allonautilus — found in deep reef waters of the Indo-Pacific. Living nautiluses have numerous simple tentacles without suckers, pinhole eyes and a shell divided into gas-filled chambers used for buoyancy.
