Lalique

Lalique is a French luxury glassmaker and jewellery company founded in 1885 by the renowned glass designer and jeweller René Lalique. Based in Paris, with its glassworks at Wingen-sur-Moder in Alsace, the company is celebrated for its Art Nouveau jewellery and Art Deco glass, including vases, perfume bottles, chandeliers, and automobile hood ornaments.

History

René Lalique established his reputation in the 1890s as one of the leading designers of Art Nouveau jewellery, creating innovative pieces using glass, enamel, and horn alongside precious stones. In the early twentieth century he turned increasingly to glass, opening the Wingen-sur-Moder factory in 1921 and producing the moulded-glass designs that defined the brand.

Products

Lalique became famous for perfume bottles made for François Coty and other houses, elegant car mascots such as the "Victoire" (Spirit of the Wind), and illuminated glass interiors for ocean liners and trains. Today the company produces crystalware, jewellery, perfume, and interior design objects, and operates the Musée Lalique, opened in 2011 near the original factory.
