Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT), also known as hormone replacement therapy (HRT), is a treatment used to relieve symptoms of the menopause by supplementing the hormones — chiefly estrogen, usually combined with a progestogen — whose levels fall at menopause.

Uses

MHT is the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms such as hot flushes and night sweats, and it also helps prevent postmenopausal bone loss and fractures. Women who still have a uterus receive a progestogen alongside estrogen to protect the endometrium; women who have had a hysterectomy usually take estrogen alone.

Risks and evidence

Use of hormone therapy fell sharply after 2002, when the Women's Health Initiative trial reported increased risks of breast cancer, stroke and venous thromboembolism with combined therapy. Subsequent analyses support a more favourable risk–benefit profile for women beginning treatment under age 60 or within ten years of menopause, and current guidelines recommend individualised decisions using the lowest effective dose.
