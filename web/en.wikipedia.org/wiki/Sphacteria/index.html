Sphacteria

Sphacteria (Greek: Σφακτηρία) is a small island at the entrance to the bay of Pylos in the Peloponnese, Greece. Today also known as Sfaktiria, it is famous chiefly for the battle fought on it in 425 BC during the Peloponnesian War, and for events of the Greek War of Independence in 1825.

Battle of Sphacteria (425 BC)

During the Battle of Pylos, a force of Spartan hoplites was stranded on Sphacteria after the Athenian navy won control of the bay. Athenian forces under Cleon and the general Demosthenes landed on the island, using light-armed troops to wear down the heavily armed Spartans. The surviving 292 Spartans, including about 120 full Spartiates, surrendered — an event that stunned the Greek world, since Spartans were expected to die rather than yield. Thucydides' account of the episode is one of the most detailed battle narratives of the war.

Later history

In 1825, during the Greek War of Independence, Ottoman-Egyptian forces of Ibrahim Pasha stormed the island, killing its Greek defenders. During the Battle of Navarino in 1827, the allied fleet anchored in the bay nearby. Monuments on the island commemorate the Greek defenders and fallen allied sailors.
