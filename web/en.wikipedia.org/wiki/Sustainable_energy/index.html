Sustainable energy

Sustainable energy
Wind and solar power, the fastest-growing sustainable energy sources
DefinitionEnergy provision meeting present needs without compromising future generations
Main sourcesSolar, wind, hydroelectricity, geothermal, sustainable biomass; often nuclear (debated)
UN goalSustainable Development Goal 7 (affordable and clean energy)

Sustainable energy is energy produced and used in ways that meet the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. In practice it centres on low-carbon sources that can be maintained indefinitely — principally renewable energy such as solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal and sustainable biomass — combined with energy efficiency, storage and modernised grids. Whether nuclear power counts as sustainable is debated: it is low-carbon and long-lived but produces long-lived radioactive waste.

Role in climate policy

Energy production and use account for roughly three-quarters of global greenhouse-gas emissions, so transitioning to sustainable energy is the central task of climate change mitigation. Assessments by the IPCC find that wind, solar and efficiency measures offer the largest near-term emission reductions at falling cost: the price of solar electricity fell by around 90% in the 2010s, making renewables the cheapest new generation in much of the world. UN Sustainable Development Goal 7 commits to universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy by 2030.

Transition challenges

Variable renewables require storage (batteries, pumped hydro, hydrogen), transmission expansion and flexible demand; electrification of transport, heating and industry multiplies electricity demand; and supply chains for minerals such as lithium, cobalt and rare earths raise environmental and geopolitical concerns. Energy transition also has distributional stakes — jobs in fossil-fuel regions, energy poverty and the financing needs of developing economies — addressed in policy under the banner of a "just transition".
