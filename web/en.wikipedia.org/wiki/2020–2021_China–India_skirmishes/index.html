2020–2021 China–India skirmishes

The 2020–2021 China–India skirmishes were a series of standoffs and violent clashes between China and India along their disputed Himalayan frontier, the Line of Actual Control (LAC), beginning in May 2020. The confrontation included the deadly fighting in the Galwan Valley on 15 June 2020 — the first combat deaths on the border since 1975 — and only gradually de-escalated through military and diplomatic talks.

Background

China and India disagree over long stretches of their 3,488 km frontier, most acutely in Aksai Chin (administered by China, claimed by India) and Arunachal Pradesh (administered by India, claimed by China). A build-up of roads and infrastructure on both sides, and India's 2019 administrative reorganisation of Ladakh, increased friction ahead of the 2020 standoffs.

Clashes and standoff

In early May 2020, Chinese and Indian troops confronted each other at Pangong Tso and in Sikkim, with fistfights and stone-throwing. On the night of 15–16 June, a violent melee in the Galwan Valley using clubs and improvised weapons killed 20 Indian soldiers; China later confirmed four of its soldiers died. Firearms were not used, in line with border agreements. Further incidents followed: warning shots were fired near Pangong Tso in September 2020 — the first shots on the LAC in 45 years — and another clash occurred at Naku La in January 2021. Both sides deployed tens of thousands of troops, armour and artillery through two Himalayan winters.

Disengagement

After multiple rounds of corps-commander talks, both sides completed a synchronised disengagement at Pangong Tso in February 2021, and at Gogra in August 2021 and Hot Springs in September 2022. Relations remained strained, with India restricting Chinese apps and investment, and full normalisation remained incomplete for several years afterwards.
