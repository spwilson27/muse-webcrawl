Fátima

Fátima is a city in the municipality of Ourém, in central Portugal. It is one of the most important Catholic pilgrimage destinations in the world, owing to the reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary to three shepherd children — Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto — at the Cova da Iria in 1917.

The apparitions

According to Catholic tradition, the Virgin Mary appeared to the children on the 13th day of six consecutive months in 1917, beginning on 13 May. The events culminated on 13 October 1917 with the "Miracle of the Sun", witnessed by a large crowd. The apparitions were declared worthy of belief by the Catholic Church in 1930. Francisco and Jacinta, who died in the influenza pandemic shortly afterwards, were canonized by Pope Francis in 2017.

Sanctuary

The Sanctuary of Fátima includes the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (built 1928–1953), the Chapel of the Apparitions on the site of the appearances, and the much larger Basilica of the Holy Trinity, consecrated in 2007, one of the largest churches in the world. Millions of pilgrims visit each year, especially on 13 May and 13 October.

The town

Before 1917 Fátima was an obscure rural parish; its very name preserves a local legend from the Reconquista period about a Moorish princess named Fátima who converted to Christianity. Pilgrimage transformed it into a town of hotels, religious shops, and services for visitors.
