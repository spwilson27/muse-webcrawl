History of Lebanon

The history of Lebanon covers one of the oldest continuously inhabited regions in the world, from the Phoenician city-states of the ancient Levant through Roman, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule, the French Mandate, independence in 1943, the civil war of 1975–1990, and the crises of the 21st century.

Antiquity

The coastal cities of Byblos, Sidon and Tyre were leading centres of the Phoenicians, the maritime traders who spread the alphabet across the Mediterranean. Lebanon subsequently fell under Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Hellenistic and Roman rule; Baalbek became one of the grandest temple complexes of the Roman Empire. Christianity spread early, and the Maronite church took root in Mount Lebanon.

Medieval and Ottoman periods

Arab Muslim armies conquered the region in the 630s–640s. Crusaders held much of the coast in the 12th and 13th centuries before Mamluk reconquest. Under Ottoman rule from 1516, local emirs of the Ma'an and Shihab dynasties — notably Fakhr al-Din II and Bashir II — exercised considerable autonomy in Mount Lebanon, while Druze and Maronite communities shaped the mountain's confessional geography. Sectarian conflict in 1860 led European powers to impose an autonomous Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate.

Mandate and independence

After the First World War, France received the League of Nations mandate for Syria and Lebanon and in 1920 proclaimed the State of Greater Lebanon with roughly its present borders. Lebanon became independent in 1943 under the National Pact, an unwritten agreement distributing power by confession: a Maronite president, a Sunni prime minister and a Shia speaker of parliament. Beirut flourished as a banking and trading hub — the "Paris of the Middle East".

Civil war and after

Tensions over the armed Palestinian presence and the confessional system erupted into the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), which drew in Syria and Israel and killed an estimated 120,000–150,000 people. The Taif Agreement of 1989 rebalanced confessional shares and ended the war; Syrian forces remained until 2005. Hezbollah, formed during the Israeli occupation of the south, fought Israel in the 2006 Lebanon War. Since 2019 Lebanon has suffered a severe financial collapse, compounded by the Beirut port explosion of 4 August 2020, one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history.
