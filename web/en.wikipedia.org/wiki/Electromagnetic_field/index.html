Electromagnetic field
ComponentsElectric field E, magnetic field B
Governed byMaxwell's equations

An electromagnetic field (EMF) is a physical field produced by electrically charged objects and magnetic moments, consisting of interdependent electric and magnetic fields. It is the medium of the electromagnetic force, one of the four fundamental interactions.

Description

In classical physics the field is described by Maxwell's equations: charges create electric fields, currents create magnetic fields, and changing fields induce each other, so disturbances propagate as electromagnetic waves at the speed of light. In special relativity, E and B are components of a single electromagnetic tensor, mixing under Lorentz transformations. In quantum electrodynamics the field is quantised and its excitations are photons.

Spectrum and applications

Electromagnetic radiation spans radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. Fields underlie electronics, communications, motors, medical imaging and much of modern technology. Low-frequency household fields and possible health effects have been extensively studied.
