The Vasa Museum (Swedish: Vasamuseet) is a maritime museum in Stockholm, Sweden. Located on the island of Djurgården, it displays the only almost fully intact 17th-century ship that has ever been salvaged: the 64-gun warship Vasa, which sank on her maiden voyage in Stockholm harbour on 10 August 1628. Opened in 1990, the museum is the most visited museum in Sweden, attracting well over a million visitors in busy years.

The ship

Vasa was ordered by King Gustav II Adolf during the Thirty Years' War and was one of the most powerfully armed vessels of her time, richly decorated with hundreds of carved sculptures. Fatally top-heavy and unstable, she capsized after sailing barely 1,300 metres. Preserved by the cold, brackish, shipworm-free waters of the Baltic, the wreck was located by Anders Franzén in 1956 and raised in a widely publicized salvage operation on 24 April 1961.

The museum

After decades of conservation with polyethylene glycol at a temporary facility, the ship was moved into the purpose-built museum, designed by architects Marianne Dahlbäck and Göran Månsson, whose silhouette evokes the ship's masts and hull. Exhibitions around the vessel explore shipbuilding, life on board, the salvage, and the people who perished, including reconstructed faces of the victims based on their skeletons. Only a small percentage of the ship's material is replacement; the hull displayed is about 98 percent original.
