The Sieges of Nagashima (長島一向一揆) were a series of three campaigns (1571, 1573, 1574) by the warlord Oda Nobunaga against the Nagashima fortress complex in Ise Province, held by the Ikkō-ikki—armed leagues of Jōdo Shinshū (True Pure Land) Buddhists. Situated in a maze of river channels at the mouths of the Kiso, Nagara and Ibi rivers, Nagashima resisted two Oda invasions before Nobunaga reduced it in 1574 by starvation and fire, killing its entire population of perhaps 20,000 people.

Background

The Nagashima ikki, based at the Hongan-ji-affiliated temple complex of Ganshō-ji and fortified islands in the river delta, threatened Nobunaga's communications between Owari and Kyoto and ambushed his armies—among them at the crossing of the Nagara River in 1570, where his younger brother Oda Nobuharu and many retaineres were killed. The ikki's support for Nobunaga's enemies, the Asai and Asakura and the Hongan-ji in Osaka, made its destruction a priority.

First siege (1571)

In May 1571 Nobunaga advanced with some 30,000 men but found the island fortresses screened by water and palisades. Unable to force the channels, he withdrew in haste; the ikki counterattacked the retreat, inflicting serious losses, including the death of the clan elder Ujiie Naomoto.

Second siege (1573)

In September 1573 Nobunaga returned with a larger army, including arquebus units under Hashiba Hideyoshi, and stormed several outlying forts. Torrential rain, however, soaked his gunpowder and ruined his firearms; when he ordered a withdrawal the ikki counterattacked fiercely, killing a number of noted Oda retainers and wounding Shibata Katsuie. Nobunaga again abandoned the campaign.

Third siege and destruction (1574)

In 1574 Nobunaga changed tactics. Supported by the pirate fleet of Kuki Yoshitaka, which blockaded the fortress by sea, he invested the delta islands, seized the outer strongpoints one by one and drove the surviving defenders and refugees—some 20,000 people—into the two main forts, Ganshō-ji and Ōtori-jō. After weeks of starvation, in late September the Oda forces set the palisades and buildings ablaze; those who fled the fire were cut down. Virtually the entire population perished.

Aftermath

The annihilation of Nagashima was among the most notorious massacres of the Sengoku period and a warning to the Hongan-ji, with whom Nobunaga nonetheless continued a ten-year war until 1580. The site of the fortress, at present-day Kuwana, Mie Prefecture, retains few visible remains.
