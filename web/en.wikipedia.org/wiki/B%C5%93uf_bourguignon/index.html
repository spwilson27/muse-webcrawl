Bœuf bourguignon

Bœuf bourguignon (beef Burgundy) is a classic French beef stew braised in red wine — traditionally Burgundy wine — with beef stock, garlic, onions, pearl onions, mushrooms and lardons. It is one of the most famous examples of peasant dishes refined into haute cuisine.

History

The dish evolved in Burgundy, where Charolais cattle and Pinot noir wine were local staples. Auguste Escoffier published an influential recipe, and Julia Child's version in Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961) popularised it in the English-speaking world; she called it one of the most delicious beef dishes concocted by man.

Preparation

Cubes of beef are browned, then braised slowly for several hours until tender; the garnish of glazed pearl onions and mushrooms is cooked separately and added at the end. It is typically served with potatoes, pasta or crusty bread.
