Mötley Crüe is an American heavy metal band formed in Los Angeles in 1981 by bassist Nikki Sixx, drummer Tommy Lee, guitarist Mick Mars and vocalist Vince Neil. The band has sold over 100 million albums worldwide and is regarded as one of the defining acts of the 1980s glam metal scene on the Sunset Strip.

History

Their debut album, Too Fast for Love (1981), was followed by the breakthrough Shout at the Devil (1983). Successive albums Theatre of Pain (1985) and Girls, Girls, Girls (1987) cemented their fame, matched by a notorious reputation for excess chronicled in the bestselling group memoir The Dirt (2001). Their most commercially successful album, Dr. Feelgood (1989), was their first recorded sober and reached number one in the United States.

Neil left in 1992 and was replaced briefly by John Corabi before returning in 1997; Tommy Lee also spent several years away around the turn of the century. The reunited classic line-up toured extensively in the 2000s, released Saints of Los Angeles (2008), and signed a widely publicised "cessation of touring" agreement before a farewell tour ending on New Year's Eve 2015.

Return and legacy

The band reunited in 2018 to record new songs for the film adaptation of The Dirt (2019) and embarked on a large stadium tour in the 2020s. Their blend of heavy metal, glam imagery and arena rock, together with hits such as "Kickstart My Heart", "Home Sweet Home" and "Dr. Feelgood", made them staples of rock radio and MTV.
