Natal, Rio Grande do Norte

Natal (Portuguese for "Christmas") is the capital and largest city of the Brazilian state of Rio Grande do Norte, near the northeasternmost tip of South America. Founded on 25 December 1599 — hence its name — it sits at the mouth of the Potengi River. Natal is renowned for its beaches, giant sand dunes, and some of the purest air in the Americas, and for its strategic role during the Second World War, when its air base was a key American staging point for flights to Africa, earning the city the nickname "Trampoline of Victory."

History

The Portuguese built the Forte dos Reis Magos (Fortress of the Three Wise Men), a star-shaped fort at the river mouth, beginning in 1598, and founded the city the following Christmas Day. The Dutch occupied Natal from 1633 to 1654, renaming it New Amsterdam. In the Second World War, the Parnamirim airfield outside the city became one of the largest U.S. air bases outside the United States, a springboard for aircraft crossing the South Atlantic to Africa — the narrowest point between the Americas and Africa. Presidents Franklin D. Roosevelt and Getúlio Vargas met in Natal in January 1943 (the Potengi Conference). Natal also hosts the Barreira do Inferno launch centre, Brazil's first space launch base (1965), used for sounding rockets.

Tourism

The city's main tourist beach is Ponta Negra, dominated by the Morro do Careca ("Bald Hill"), a 107-metre dune now protected from climbing. Buggy tours cross the dunes of Genipabu, with its lagoons and dromedary rides, north of the city; to the south lie the dolphin bay of Pipa and the world's largest cashew tree, at Pirangi, whose canopy covers some 8,500 m². Natal was a host city of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, with matches at the Arena das Dunas. The city is also famous for the Carnatal, an out-of-season carnival held each December.
