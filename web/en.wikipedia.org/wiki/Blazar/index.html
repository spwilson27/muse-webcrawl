A blazar is an active galactic nucleus (AGN) with a relativistic jet — a stream of ionised matter travelling at nearly the speed of light — directed very nearly toward the observer. Relativistic beaming of the jet's emission makes blazars among the brightest and most rapidly variable objects in the universe.

Properties

Blazars emit across the entire electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays, and their light can vary dramatically on timescales of days or even minutes. Their spectra are dominated by non-thermal radiation from the jet, and the emission is strongly polarised. The class comprises two main subclasses: BL Lacertae objects, with weak or absent spectral emission lines, and flat-spectrum radio quasars, which show strong broad emission lines characteristic of quasars.

Unified model

In the unified scheme of AGN, a blazar is simply a radio galaxy whose jet happens to point at Earth: viewed side-on, the same object would be classified an ordinary radio galaxy, with parent populations of Fanaroff–Riley galaxies. Beaming both brightens the jet and compresses apparent timescales, explaining blazars' extreme properties without requiring intrinsically different engines. Blazars have also been implicated as sources of high-energy neutrinos — notably the 2017 association of a neutrino detected by IceCube with a flare of the blazar TXS 0506+056.
