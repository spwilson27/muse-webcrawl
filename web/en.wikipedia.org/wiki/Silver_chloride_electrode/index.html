Silver chloride electrode

The silver/silver chloride electrode is a common type of reference electrode used in electrochemistry. It consists of a silver wire coated with silver chloride, immersed in a solution of known chloride concentration, typically potassium chloride.

Principle

The electrode reaction is AgCl(s) + e− ⇌ Ag(s) + Cl−. With saturated KCl at 25 °C its potential is about +0.199 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode. It is inexpensive, stable and nontoxic compared with the calomel electrode, and is widely used in pH meters and other electrochemical measurements.
