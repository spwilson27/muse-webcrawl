Gwangju Inhwa School

Gwangju Inhwa School (Korean: 광주인화학교) was a school for hearing-impaired students founded in 1961 in Gwangju, South Korea. The school became notorious after it was revealed that a number of its students had been sexually abused by several of the school's employees, including its principal, over a period of years.

Abuse scandal

The abuses were first investigated in 2005 after a teacher reported them, but the case received little public attention and the offenders received light punishments, with some returning to work at the school. The case gained nationwide attention in 2011 following the release of the film Silenced (Korean: 도가니, Dogani), based on Gong Ji-young's 2009 novel The Crucible, which dramatized the events at the school.

Public outrage over the film led the South Korean National Assembly to pass the so-called "Dogani Law" in late 2011, which abolished the statute of limitations for sex crimes against minors and disabled people and increased maximum penalties. The Gwangju Metropolitan Office of Education revoked the school's license, and Gwangju Inhwa School was permanently closed in November 2011. A fresh police investigation was opened, and a former administrator was subsequently convicted in connection with the abuse of a student.
