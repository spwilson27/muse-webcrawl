Lake whitefish

The lake whitefish (Coregonus clupeaformis) is a species of freshwater whitefish in the salmon family (Salmonidae), native to the cold lakes of northern North America. It is one of the most important commercial freshwater fish in Canada and the Great Lakes region of the United States, prized for its mild, white flesh. The species is a bottom feeder as an adult, distinguished from related ciscoes by its blunt, overhanging snout and subterminal mouth.

Description

Lake whitefish are silvery, laterally compressed fish with small heads, tiny mouths, and the small adipose fin characteristic of salmonids. Adults commonly weigh 0.7–1.8 kg and reach 40–60 cm, though fish over 8 kg have been recorded; the largest known specimen weighed about 19 kg (42 lb), taken in Lake Superior in 1918. A distinctive hump behind the head develops in some older fish, especially in northern populations.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges across Canada and Alaska and into the northern United States, from the Atlantic watershed of Labrador west to British Columbia and the Yukon. It inhabits cold, deep lakes — including all of the Great Lakes — and is also found in some large rivers. Juveniles feed on plankton in surface waters; adults move to the bottom to eat insect larvae, molluscs, amphipods, and other invertebrates.

Life history

Lake whitefish spawn in autumn, usually November into December, broadcasting eggs over rocky shoals in shallow water. The eggs overwinter under the ice and hatch in spring. Growth is slow in northern lakes, and maturity may not be reached until age five to seven or later. The species' taxonomy is complicated by great morphological variation between lakes, once leading to many named "species" that are now treated as forms of one variable species.

Fisheries

Whitefish have been fished by Indigenous peoples for millennia and by commercial gill-net and trap-net fisheries since the 19th century. Great Lakes stocks collapsed in the mid-20th century under overfishing, sea lamprey predation, and pollution, then partly recovered; declines in Lake Michigan and Lake Huron since the 2000s, linked to invasive zebra and quagga mussels restructuring the food web, are a continuing concern. Smoked whitefish and whitefish salad remain regional delicacies.
