Fujita scale

The Fujita scale (F-Scale), or Fujita–Pearson scale (FPP scale), is a scale for rating tornado intensity, based primarily on the damage tornadoes inflict on human-built structures and vegetation. It was introduced in 1971 by Tetsuya Theodore Fujita of the University of Chicago, in collaboration with Allen Pearson.

History and use

Fujita devised the scale by connecting the Beaufort scale and the Mach scale, dividing tornado damage into six categories; he initially rated historical tornadoes back to 1950. In the United States, tornadoes from 1950 to the early 1970s were rated retroactively from damage reports and photographs. Because wind speeds are estimated from damage rather than measured, the scale is a damage scale rather than a direct wind measurement.

Enhanced Fujita scale

From 1 February 2007, the United States replaced the Fujita scale with the Enhanced Fujita (EF) scale, which refines the damage indicators and lowers the wind speeds assigned to the highest categories (an EF5 begins at 200 mph). Canada adopted the EF scale in 2013. The original Fujita scale remains in historical records and is still used in some other countries; the International Fujita scale was proposed in 2023 to harmonise ratings globally.
