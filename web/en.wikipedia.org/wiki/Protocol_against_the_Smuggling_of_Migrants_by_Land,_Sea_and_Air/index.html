Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air

The Protocol against the Smuggling of Migrants by Land, Sea and Air, supplementing the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime, was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 2000 in Palermo, Italy, and entered into force on 28 January 2004. It is one of the three Palermo protocols, alongside the protocols on trafficking in persons and on illicit firearms.

Provisions

The protocol defines smuggling of migrants as "the procurement, in order to obtain, directly or indirectly, a financial or other material benefit, of the illegal entry of a person into a State Party of which the person is not a national or a permanent resident". States parties are required to criminalise smuggling, document fraud and enabling illegal residence for financial benefit, with aggravated offences where lives are endangered or migrants are subjected to inhuman or degrading treatment.

Protection of migrants

The protocol expressly provides that migrants shall not become liable to criminal prosecution under it for having been the object of smuggling, and requires states to protect the rights of smuggled migrants, including the right to life and freedom from torture. It also contains provisions on cooperation against smuggling by sea, border measures, document security, and the return of smuggled migrants.
