The Chengdu J-20 Mighty Dragon is a twin-engine, all-weather stealth fighter aircraft developed by China's Chengdu Aircraft Industry Group for the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF). It is China's first operational fifth-generation fighter, designed for air superiority with emphasis on low observability, long range, and networked sensors.

Development

The J-20 programme grew out of the J-XX studies of the 1990s. A prototype made its first flight on 11 January 2011 — coinciding with a visit to Beijing by the US Secretary of Defense — and the type was publicly unveiled at the Zhuhai Airshow in November 2016. It entered PLAAF service in March 2017. Early production aircraft used Russian AL-31 engines or the Chinese WS-10; later variants are reported to use the more powerful WS-15. A two-seat variant, the J-20S, has also been observed, the first two-seat stealth fighter design anywhere.

Design

The J-20 combines a faceted, radar-absorbent-coated airframe with canard foreplanes and a delta wing, diverterless supersonic inlets, and internal weapons bays — a main bay for air-to-air missiles such as the long-range PL-15, plus side bays for short-range PL-10 missiles. Its sensor suite includes an active electronically scanned array radar and an electro-optical targeting system, integrated through sensor fusion in the style of other fifth-generation fighters.
