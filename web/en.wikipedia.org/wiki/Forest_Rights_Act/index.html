The Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006

Enacted: 2006 (in force 31 December 2007)
Country: India
Administered by: Ministry of Tribal Affairs
Beneficiaries: Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers

The Forest Rights Act (FRA), formally the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, is a law of India recognising the rights of forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers to forest land and resources from which they had historically been excluded under colonial and post-colonial forest law.

Rights recognised

The Act recognises individual rights to hold and live in forest land occupied for habitation or self-cultivation (up to four hectares), and community rights including use of minor forest produce, grazing, access to water bodies, and the right to protect, regenerate and manage community forest resources. It also recognises the right against eviction without rehabilitation and requires the free, informed consent of village assemblies (gram sabhas) for diversion of forest land in certain circumstances.

Procedure and implementation

Claims are initiated by the gram sabha, screened by sub-divisional committees and decided by district-level committees chaired by the district collector. Implementation has been uneven across states: millions of claims have been filed, a substantial share rejected, and the recognition of community forest rights has generally lagged behind individual titles. The Act remains politically contentious, balancing tribal welfare and historic justice against wildlife conservation concerns; in 2019 the Supreme Court ordered eviction of claimants whose claims had been rejected, an order it later stayed.
