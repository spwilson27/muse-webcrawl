Himyar

Himyar was an ancient kingdom in the southern highlands of Yemen, in the southwestern corner of the Arabian Peninsula. It emerged around 110 BCE and at its height dominated much of South Arabia, conquering the kingdoms of Saba, Qataban, and Hadhramaut.

History

The Himyarite capital was Zafar, near modern Yarim. In the late 4th century CE the Himyarite kings adopted Judaism, making it one of the few Jewish states of the ancient world outside the Levant. In the early 6th century the Christian king Dhu Nuwas persecuted Christians at Najran, prompting an invasion by the Ethiopian kingdom of Aksum, which conquered Himyar around 525 CE. The region later came under Sasanian Persian influence before the arrival of Islam in the 7th century.

Culture

The Himyarites used the South Arabian script and language in their inscriptions and were part of the caravan trade in frankincense and myrrh that linked South Arabia to the Mediterranean world.
