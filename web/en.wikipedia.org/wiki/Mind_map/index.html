Mind map

A mind map is a diagram used to visually organize information into a hierarchy, showing relationships among pieces of a whole. It is often created around a single concept, drawn as an image in the center of a blank page, to which associated representations of ideas such as images, words and parts of words are added. Major ideas are connected directly to the central concept, and other ideas branch out from those major ideas.

History

Visual thinking diagrams have been used for centuries — the philosopher Porphyry's tree (3rd century) classified Aristotle's categories, and Ramon Llull used similar structures. The modern mind map was popularized by British popular psychology author Tony Buzan, who promoted it from the 1970s in his BBC series and book Use Your Head (1974). Buzan claimed traditional outlines inhibit learning by failing to engage the brain's associative processes, and advocated guidelines such as using colors, curved branches, single keywords, and images.

Uses

Mind maps are used for note-taking, brainstorming, problem solving, studying, summarizing information, and project planning. They are a common feature of study-skills courses, and dedicated software supports collaborative and hand-drawn mapping.

Research

Some studies have found that mind mapping can improve students' recall of factual material compared with conventional notes, though results are mixed and the technique is generally considered one study aid among many, closely related to the concept map developed by Joseph Novak.
