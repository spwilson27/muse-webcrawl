Sholay

Sholay (transl. "Embers") is a 1975 Indian Hindi-language action-adventure film directed by Ramesh Sippy, produced by G. P. Sippy, and written by Salim–Javed. It stars Dharmendra, Amitabh Bachchan, Sanjeev Kumar, Amjad Khan, Hema Malini, and Jaya Bhaduri. The film is a "dacoit Western" set in the ravines of central India, in the fictional village of Ramgarh.

Plot

Retired police officer Thakur Baldev Singh hires two small-time criminals, Jai and Veeru, to capture the sadistic bandit Gabbar Singh, who murdered the Thakur's family and severed his arms. The narrative follows their defense of the village, Jai's romance with the widowed Radha, and Veeru's courtship of the talkative Basanti, culminating in Jai's death and Gabbar's capture.

Legacy

Initially receiving lukewarm reviews, Sholay became a record-breaking box-office success, running for over five years at Mumbai's Minerva cinema, and for decades was regarded as the highest-grossing Indian film adjusted for inflation. Amjad Khan's Gabbar Singh became one of Indian cinema's most iconic villains, and the film's dialogue entered everyday speech. It has been named the greatest Indian film of all time in polls, including the BFI's 2002 critics' poll and a 2005 India Today readers' poll. The dialogue album and R. D. Burman's songs, including "Yeh Dosti", remain widely popular.
