The Penobscot (Penobscot: Panawáhpskewi) are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands, centred on the Penobscot River in what is now Maine. The federally recognised Penobscot Nation is headquartered at Indian Island near Old Town, Maine.

History

The Penobscot were members of the Wabanaki Confederacy and speakers of an Eastern Abenaki language. Their homeland ranged along the Penobscot River and its tributaries, where they fished, hunted and travelled by birchbark canoe; the river and its salmon runs were central to subsistence and identity. Contact with French and English traders and colonists from the seventeenth century brought trade, missionaries, warfare and progressive loss of land despite treaty agreements.

The Penobscot Nation today

The Maine Indian Claims Settlement Act of 1980 recognised the Penobscot Nation's land claims and provided funds to purchase land and invest in the tribal economy. The Nation operates government services, cultural programmes and language revitalisation, and has played a leading role in defending the health of the Penobscot River, including the restoration of migratory fish through dam removals on its lower reaches.
