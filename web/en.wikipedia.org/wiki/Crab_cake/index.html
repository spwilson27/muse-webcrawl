Crab cake

A crab cake is a variety of fish cake popular in the United States, composed of crab meat and various other ingredients, such as bread crumbs, mayonnaise, mustard, eggs and seasonings. It is particularly associated with the Chesapeake Bay region, especially the state of Maryland.

Styles

Two classic Maryland styles are recognised. Boardwalk crab cakes are breaded, deep-fried and served on a bun, typical of seaside resorts. Restaurant or "gourmet" crab cakes use lump or jumbo lump blue crab meat with minimal filler and are broiled, sautéed or baked. Marylanders traditionally season the mixture with Old Bay seasoning and serve the cakes with lemon, tartar sauce or mustard.

Purists insist on meat from the blue crab (Callinectes sapidus) of the Chesapeake, though imported crab meat from other species is widely used because of cost and availability.

History

Crab-based croquettes appeared in American cookbooks in the nineteenth century, influenced in part by Native American and colonial cooking along the Atlantic coast. The term "crab cake" was popularised in the 1930s, notably by Crosby Gaige's New York World's Fair Cook Book (1939). The dish has since become an emblem of Maryland cuisine and a staple well beyond the Mid-Atlantic.
