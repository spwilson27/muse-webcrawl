Fascinator

TypeFormal headpiece
MaterialsFeathers, flowers, beads, sinamay
Worn atWeddings, race meetings, formal events

A fascinator is a formal, often elaborate headpiece worn as an alternative to a hat, typically attached to the hair with a comb, headband, or clip. Fascinators are especially associated with British formal occasions such as weddings and Royal Ascot.

History

The word originally described a lacy, knitted head shawl fashionable in the nineteenth century. In its modern sense, the fascinator emerged in the late twentieth century: milliner Stephen Jones is credited with popularizing the term in the 1980s and 1990s for small, decorative cocktail headpieces of feathers and veiling. The style was taken up by members of the British royal family — notably Catherine, Princess of Wales — and became a fixture of wedding-guest dress.

Design

Fascinators range from a simple spray of feathers on a comb to large sculptural constructions of sinamay, silk flowers, beads, and netting. Because they leave most of the head uncovered, they allow elaborate hairstyles to remain visible. At Royal Ascot, the dress code in the Royal Enclosure formally recognizes fascinators as headpieces, though hats with a solid base are encouraged in some enclosures.
