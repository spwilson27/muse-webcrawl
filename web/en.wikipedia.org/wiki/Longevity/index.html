Longevity
DefinitionLength of life, especially beyond the average
Longest verified human lifespanJeanne Calment, 122 years 164 days
Key factorsGenetics, lifestyle, environment, healthcare

The word longevity is sometimes used as a synonym for "life expectancy" in demography, but more precisely it refers to the ability to survive to an exceptionally old age, and in particular the long life of individuals or the longevity characteristics of a population. The longest verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who lived to 122 years and 164 days.

Factors

Significant factors in natural longevity include genetics — studies of twins suggest roughly 20–30% of variation in lifespan is inherited — along with gender (women live longer than men in almost all countries), diet, physical activity, avoiding smoking and excessive alcohol, and access to healthcare. So-called Blue Zones, regions such as Okinawa, Sardinia, and Ikaria with unusually high concentrations of centenarians, have been studied for common lifestyle patterns.

Longevity in other organisms

Maximum lifespans vary enormously across species: from a few weeks in some insects to over 200 years in the Galápagos tortoise and the bowhead whale, and millennia in clonal plants such as the bristlecone pine and quaking aspen colonies. Research into ageing (biogerontology) seeks to understand the biological mechanisms underlying these differences.
