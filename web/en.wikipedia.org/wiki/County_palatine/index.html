County palatine

A county palatine was an area ruled by a lord with special royal privileges — "palatine" powers normally exercised by the Crown — such as appointing judges and running an independent legal administration. The institution developed in medieval England and in the Holy Roman Empire.

Examples

In England the principal palatinates were the County Palatine of Durham, ruled by prince-bishops; the County Palatine of Lancaster, held by the Dukes (and later the Crown) of Lancaster; and Cheshire, ruled by the Earls of Chester. Durham's palatine court survived until 1971, and the Duchy of Lancaster retains certain palatine attributes today.
