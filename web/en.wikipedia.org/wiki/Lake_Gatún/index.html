Lake Gatún
Location: Panama
Surface area: 425 km²
Surface elevation: 26 m above sea level
Created: 1913 (Gatún Dam)

A vessel crossing Lake Gatún

Lake Gatún (Spanish: Lago Gatún) is a large artificial lake in Panama, created between 1907 and 1913 by the damming of the Chagres River at Gatún during construction of the Panama Canal. At the time of its creation it was the largest artificial lake in the world. Lying 26 metres (85 ft) above sea level, it forms the summit level of the canal: ships are lifted by locks to the lake at one end and lowered at the other, crossing the Continental Divide through the flooded valley.

Formation and role

The Gatún Dam, an earthen structure across the Chagres valley, flooded some 425 km² of rainforest; drowned treetops still mark the channel, and hilltops became islands, most famously Barro Colorado Island, now a protected reserve administered by the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute. The lake stores the water that operates the canal locks and generates hydroelectric power, so droughts in its watershed — notably in 2023–24 — directly restrict canal traffic.

Ecology

The flooding introduced a new aquatic ecosystem and isolated terrestrial species on the islands, making Barro Colorado one of the most intensively studied tropical forests on Earth. Peacock bass, introduced in the twentieth century, dominate the fishery and have altered the lake's native fish communities.
