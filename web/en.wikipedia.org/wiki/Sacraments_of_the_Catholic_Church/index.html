Sacraments of the Catholic Church

There are seven sacraments of the Catholic Church, which according to Catholic theology were instituted by Jesus Christ and entrusted to the Church. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the sacraments are "efficacious signs of grace, instituted by Christ and entrusted to the Church, by which divine life is dispensed to us". The seven sacraments are Baptism, Confirmation, the Eucharist, Penance (Reconciliation/Confession), the Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony. The number seven was formally defined by the Council of Trent (1545–1563) in response to Protestant reformers, though the list had been standard in Western theology since the 12th century, notably through Peter Lombard's Sentences.

Classification

The sacraments are traditionally grouped as:

  • Sacraments of initiation — Baptism, Confirmation, and the Eucharist, which lay the foundation of the Christian life.
  • Sacraments of healing — Penance and the Anointing of the Sick.
  • Sacraments of service (vocation) — Holy Orders and Matrimony.

Baptism, Confirmation and Holy Orders are said to impart an indelible sacramental "character" and therefore cannot be repeated.

Theology

Catholic doctrine holds that sacraments confer the grace they signify ex opere operato — by the very fact of the rite being validly performed — provided the recipient places no obstacle (obex) to grace through unrepented mortal sin. Each sacrament has a prescribed matter (material sign, e.g. water for Baptism, bread and wine for the Eucharist) and form (the accompanying words), and an authorised minister. The Eucharist holds a special place as the "source and summit of the Christian life", in which Catholics believe the bread and wine are transubstantiated into the body and blood of Christ.

Validity and other churches

The Catholic Church recognises the seven sacraments of the Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches as valid, and baptism administered in most other Christian communities. The Eastern Catholic Churches administer the same seven sacraments (called "mysteries"), though with differing practice, such as chrismation (confirmation) together with infant baptism and communion of infants.
