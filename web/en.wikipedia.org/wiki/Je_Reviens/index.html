Je Reviens

Image placeholder: Je Reviens

Je Reviens is a classic French perfume created by the house of Worth. Launched in 1932, it was composed by perfumer Maurice Blanchet and is regarded as one of the great aldehydic floral fragrances of the 20th century. The name, French for "I shall return," was associated with the couturier Charles Frederick Worth's fashion house, which had pioneered the presentation of perfume as an extension of couture.

Composition

Je Reviens is noted for its luminous floral bouquet built on aldehydes, with notes commonly described as including narcissus, violet, jasmine, ylang-ylang, and rose over a base of sandalwood, vetiver, tonka bean, and musk. The fragrance was originally presented in a distinctive Lalique glass bottle, with a frosted stopper and celestial-blue packaging designed to evoke the night sky.

History

The House of Worth, founded in Paris in 1858, introduced perfumes under Jacques Worth in the 1920s, including Dans la Nuit (1924), of which Je Reviens was a companion release. The fragrance was revived and reformulated several times during the 20th century, notably in the 1970s with the "Je Reviens Couture" edition. Its reputation rests on its association with the elegance of interwar Parisian couture.
