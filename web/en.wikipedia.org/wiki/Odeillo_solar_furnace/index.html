Odeillo solar furnace

The Odeillo solar furnace is the world's largest solar furnace. It is situated in Font-Romeu-Odeillo-Via, in the department of Pyrénées-Orientales, in south of France. It is 48 metres (157 ft) high and 54 m (177 ft) wide, and includes 63 heliostats. It was built between 1962 and 1968, and started operating in 1969.

The furnace can concentrate sunlight to produce temperatures up to 3,500 °C (6,330 °F). Because it relies on direct solar radiation, it is used by the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) for studies of materials at extreme temperatures, heat transfer and solar energy conversion.

Principle

A field of movable mirrors (heliostats) reflects sunlight onto a large parabolic concentrator, which focuses the rays onto a small target area at the top of a central tower, generating extreme heat in seconds.
