South African Police Service (SAPS)
Formed: 27 January 1995
Predecessor: South African Police
Headquarters: Pretoria
Jurisdiction: South Africa

The South African Police Service (SAPS) is the national police force of the Republic of South Africa. It was created in 1995 by amalgamating the police forces of the former homelands (Bantustans) with the South African Police (SAP), transforming a force associated with enforcing apartheid into a unified service for the democratic state.

Structure

SAPS is headed by a National Commissioner appointed by the President, with provincial commissioners for each of South Africa's nine provinces. It operates more than a thousand police stations and includes specialised units such as the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation (the Hawks), the Special Task Force, and forensic services.

History and challenges

The post-1994 transformation emphasised community policing, but SAPS has faced persistent challenges: high violent-crime rates, corruption scandals, and episodes such as the 2012 Marikana massacre, in which police shot dead 34 striking miners. Ranks in SAPS were remilitarised in 2010, restoring titles such as General and Colonel.
