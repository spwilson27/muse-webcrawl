Phenology

Phenology tracks the timing of seasonal events such as flowering and leaf unfolding.

Phenology is the study of periodic events in the biological life cycles of plants and animals and how these are influenced by seasonal and interannual variations in climate, as well as habitat factors such as elevation. The word is derived from the Greek φαίνω (phainō, "to appear, come into view") and λόγος (logos, "study"). Examples of phenological events include the flowering of plants, the unfolding of leaves, the arrival of migratory birds, egg-laying, and the emergence of insects.

History

Records of phenological events have been kept for centuries; the Japanese tradition of recording cherry blossom dates at Kyoto extends back over a millennium, and in Britain systematic networks of observers were organised in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The term itself was proposed by the Belgian botanist Charles Morren in the mid-19th century, and his son Edward Morren helped promote the discipline.

Phenology and climate change

Because phenological events are sensitive to temperature, long records serve as indicators of climate change. Many studies document advancing spring events — earlier flowering, earlier leaf-out, and earlier insect emergence — in response to warming. Shifts in timing can cause mismatches between species, for example between the peak abundance of caterpillars and the hatching of the birds that feed on them. Modern phenology also uses remote sensing, tracking the "green wave" of vegetation across continents from satellites.
