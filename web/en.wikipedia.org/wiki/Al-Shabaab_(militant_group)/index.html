Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen (حركة الشباب المجاهدين, "Movement of Striving Youth"), commonly known as al-Shabaab, is a Salafi jihadist militant organisation based in Somalia. It emerged in the mid-2000s as the militant youth wing of the Islamic Courts Union, and after the Ethiopian intervention of 2006 developed into an insurgency that at its height controlled much of southern and central Somalia, including parts of Mogadishu.

History

Driven from Mogadishu by African Union (AMISOM, now ATMIS) and Somali forces from 2011, al-Shabaab lost most major towns but retains control of rural areas, collects taxation, and conducts guerrilla operations and large-scale bombings. In 2012 its leadership formally pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda. Somali government offensives since 2022, supported by clan militias, have retaken territory in central regions, while al-Shabaab remains one of al-Qaeda's wealthiest and largest affiliates.

Major attacks

The group has carried out major attacks inside Somalia — including the October 2017 Mogadishu truck bombing, among the deadliest terrorist attacks worldwide — and abroad, notably the 2010 Kampala bombings, the 2013 Westgate mall attack in Nairobi, the 2015 Garissa University attack, and the 2020 attack on the Manda Bay airfield in Kenya.
