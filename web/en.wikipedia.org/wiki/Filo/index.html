Filo

Filo (or phyllo) is a very thin unleavened dough used for making pastries such as baklava and börek in Middle Eastern, Greek, and Balkan cuisines. The name comes from the Greek word φύλλο (phýllo), meaning "leaf", reflecting the paper-thin sheets of the dough.

Preparation

Filo dough is made from flour, water, and a small amount of oil or vinegar. The dough is rolled and stretched by hand or machine until it is nearly transparent. In use, sheets are layered with brushed butter or oil between them; the layers separate during baking to give a crisp, flaky texture. Because homemade filo is labour-intensive, most cooks use commercially produced frozen filo.

Dishes

Famous filo dishes include Greek baklava (layered with nuts and honey syrup), spanakopita (spinach and feta), tiropita (cheese), Turkish börek, and Balkan burek. Filo is also used for strudels, samosa-like parcels, and modern wrapped appetisers.
