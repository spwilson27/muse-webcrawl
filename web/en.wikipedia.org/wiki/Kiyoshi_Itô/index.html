Kiyosi Itô

Kiyosi Itô (伊藤 清, Itō Kiyoshi, 7 September 1915 – 10 November 2008) was a Japanese mathematician known for his pioneering work in stochastic analysis. He developed the concepts of the stochastic integral and stochastic differential equation, now known as Itô calculus, which are fundamental to probability theory and are applied extensively in mathematical finance, physics and engineering.

Life and work

Itô studied mathematics at the Imperial University of Tokyo, graduating in 1938. From 1939 to 1942 he worked at the Government Statistics Bureau, where, influenced by the work of Kolmogorov and Lévy, he developed his fundamental papers on stochastic processes. In his landmark 1942 paper "On stochastic processes" and the 1944 and 1946 follow-ups, he constructed stochastic integrals with respect to Brownian motion and formulated stochastic differential equations of the form dXt = μ dt + σ dWt.

He received his doctorate from the University of Tokyo in 1945 and became a professor at Kyoto University in 1952, where he remained until his retirement in 1979, apart from visiting appointments at Stanford, Cornell, Princeton and Aarhus. His "Itô's lemma" — the chain rule of stochastic calculus — became a cornerstone of the Black–Scholes option-pricing theory in finance.

Recognition

Itô received the Asahi Prize (1977), the Wolf Prize in Mathematics (1987, shared with Peter Lax), the Kyoto Prize (1998) and the first Gauss Prize of the International Mathematical Union (2006) for applications of mathematics. He died in Kyoto in 2008 at the age of 93.
