Gematria (Hebrew: גימטריה, gīmaṭriyyā) is the practice of assigning numerical values to words or phrases on the basis of the values of their letters, and interpreting texts — especially the Hebrew Bible — by identifying words of equal value. It is one of the traditional methods of Jewish exegesis and a central tool of Kabbalah.

The Hebrew letter values

In the standard system (mispar gadol), the 22 Hebrew letters take the values 1–9, 10–90 and 100–400: aleph = 1, bet = 2, … tav = 400, with final letter forms sometimes counted at 500–900. Variant systems include reduced value (mispar katan), in which values are compressed to single digits, and ordinal value.

Use in exegesis

Rabbanic interpreters used gematria to link verses and draw legal or homiletic conclusions; one classic example equates the value of "wine" (yayin, 70) with "secret" (sod, 70): "When wine enters, secrets come out." The best-known value is that of חי (chai, "life") = 18, which is why gifts and donations are traditionally given in multiples of 18. Kabbalists used elaborate gematria on divine names and scripture to map correspondences within the sefirot.

Analogues

Parallel systems existed wherever letters doubled as numerals: Greek isopsephy (behind, famously, the number 666 associated in Revelation with "Nero Caesar" transliterated into Hebrew) and the Arabic abjadi system. Modern enthusiasts extend gematria to other alphabets and languages, though such uses lie outside the classical tradition.
