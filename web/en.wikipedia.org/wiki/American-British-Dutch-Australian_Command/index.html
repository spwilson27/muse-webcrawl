The American-British-Dutch-Australian Command, usually known as ABDA Command or ABDACOM, was a short-lived joint Allied command in early 1942 charged with holding the "Malay Barrier" against Japanese expansion into South-East Asia and the South West Pacific during the Pacific War.

Formation

ABDA was created following the Arcadia Conference, with British General Sir Archibald Wavell as Supreme Commander, headquartered at Lembang in the Dutch East Indies. It unified American, British, Dutch, and Australian land, sea, and air forces across a vast area from Burma through Malaya and the East Indies to northern Australia. Its naval forces (ABDAFLOAT) were commanded first by U.S. Admiral Thomas C. Hart and then by Dutch Vice-Admiral Conrad Helfrich.

Dissolution

Outnumbered and lacking air cover, ABDA forces could not stop the Japanese advance. Singapore fell on 15 February 1942, and on 25 February Wavell dissolved the command, returning control to national commanders. The Allied naval striking force was effectively destroyed days later in the Battle of the Java Sea (27 February 1942).
