6.0 system

For other uses, see Figure skating (disambiguation).

The 6.0 system (sometimes called the traditional system) is the scoring method formerly used in figure skating. It is named for the maximum possible score of 6.0, a "perfect" mark. Under the system, judges awarded each performance two marks — one for technical merit and one for presentation — and skaters were ranked by ordinal placements rather than by accumulated points. The system was used at all major competitions until it was replaced by the ISU Judging System following the judging scandal at the 2002 Winter Olympics.

How it worked

Each judge independently awarded a skater two scores on a scale from 0.0 to 6.0, in increments of 0.1: the first mark for technical merit (in the short programme, required elements and their execution; in the free skate, difficulty and quality of the programme as a whole) and the second for presentation (choreography, interpretation, timing and expression). The two marks were added together to give each judge's total for the skater.

Rankings were then determined ordinally. Each judge's scores produced a ranking of the skaters; a skater was placed first overall by a judge whose total for them was highest. The final placement was decided by majority principles — essentially, the skater ranked first by a majority of the panel beat all others, and remaining places were resolved by majority comparisons and tie-breaking procedures involving the sums of ordinals and, if needed, the technical or presentation marks.

Criticism and replacement

The ordinal method was criticised for opacity and for allowing counterintuitive results: a skater could win more judges' first places yet lose, and placings could flip when a lower-ranked skater's result changed ("flipping" and violations of independence of irrelevant alternatives). The system's vulnerability to bloc judging and vote-trading became a public scandal in the pairs event at the 2002 Salt Lake City Winter Olympics, when the French judge admitted to being pressured to vote for the Russian pair. In response, the International Skating Union developed the Code of Points-based ISU Judging System, adopted for major events from the 2004–05 season, in which skaters accumulate points for each element rather than being ranked ordinally.

Legacy

"Six point oh" remains the popular shorthand for a perfect, iconic performance — marks of 6.0 were awarded sparingly, and famously celebrated skaters such as Michelle Kwan and Torvill and Dean collected many. Some smaller competitions and non-ISU events continued to use 6.0-style judging after the elite sport moved on.
