Business register

A business register (or commercial register) is a public register in which companies, traders and other business entities record legally relevant information, such as their name, legal form, registered office, directors and share capital. In many countries registration is a legal requirement before a company may trade, and the register serves as an authoritative source for third parties dealing with the business.

Functions

Business registers provide legal certainty: they disclose who is authorised to represent a company, what its capital is, and whether it is solvent or in liquidation. Creditors, customers and investors use registers to verify a company's existence and status before entering contracts. Statistical offices also maintain statistical business registers as sampling frames for economic surveys.

By country

In the United Kingdom, companies are registered with Companies House. Germany maintains the Handelsregister at local courts, while France uses the Registre du commerce et des sociétés. In the United States there is no single national register; incorporation is handled at state level, typically by the secretary of state. Many jurisdictions now provide free online access to register data, and the European Union operates the Business Registers Interconnection System (BRIS) linking national registers.
