The Fender Jazz Bass (often shortened to J-Bass) is the second model of electric bass guitar created by Leo Fender. Introduced in 1960 as a higher-end companion to the Precision Bass, it features an offset-waist body derived from the Jazzmaster guitar, a narrower nut width, and two single-coil pickups with two volume controls allowing players to blend the bright bridge pickup with the warmer neck pickup.

Design and sound

The Jazz Bass is known for a brighter, more articulate tone with pronounced midrange "growl", especially from the bridge pickup. Its slim neck profile made it popular with players who found the Precision Bass neck wide. Later variants added active electronics, five-string models, and signature editions.

Notable players

Famous Jazz Bass players include Jaco Pastorius, who famously removed the frets from his "Bass of Doom"; Geddy Lee of Rush; Marcus Miller; John Paul Jones of Led Zeppelin; and Noel Redding of the Jimi Hendrix Experience.
