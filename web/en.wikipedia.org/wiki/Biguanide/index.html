Biguanide

Biguanide is an organic compound with the formula HN(C(NH)NH2)2, and the parent of a class of drugs called biguanides that includes the widely used diabetes medication metformin. Biguanides were derived from galegine, a glucose-lowering compound found in the plant French lilac (Galega officinalis).

Pharmacology

Oral biguanides lower blood glucose in type 2 diabetes chiefly by reducing hepatic glucose production and improving insulin sensitivity, without causing weight gain or, on their own, hypoglycaemia. Their precise molecular mechanism involves effects on mitochondrial function and activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK).

Drugs in the class

Metformin is the only biguanide in widespread clinical use and is generally the first-line treatment for type 2 diabetes. Phenformin and buformin were withdrawn in many countries in the 1970s because of a risk of lactic acidosis, a rare but serious side effect also monitored with metformin. Related compounds include the antiseptic chlorhexidine and the antimalarial proguanil.
