Amleth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amleth, the legendary Danish prince who inspired Hamlet

Amleth (Old Norse: Amlóði) is a legendary Danish prince whose story is preserved in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum (c. 1200) and in earlier Icelandic tradition. He is the principal source for William Shakespeare's Prince Hamlet.

The legend

In Saxo's account, Amleth's father, King Horvendill of Jutland, is murdered by his brother Feng, who then marries the queen, Gerutha. Amleth feigns madness to survive, speaks in riddles, and eventually kills Feng at a banquet, burning the hall with Feng's courtiers inside. He then travels to England, outwits the English king's treacherous letter (altering it so that others are executed instead), marries the king's daughter, and later returns to Denmark, also marrying the Scottish queen Hermuthruda. He dies in battle against King Wiglek, who had seized his throne.

Influence

Through François de Belleforest's French retelling in his Histoires Tragiques (1570), the tale reached England, where it was adapted in a lost play (the Ur-Hamlet) and then in Shakespeare's Hamlet (c. 1600). The 2022 film The Northman retells the legend more directly.
