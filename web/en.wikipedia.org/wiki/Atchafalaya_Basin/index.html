Atchafalaya Basin

The Atchafalaya Basin, or Atchafalaya Swamp, is the largest wetland and swamp in the United States. Located in south central Louisiana, it is a combination of wetlands and river delta area where the Atchafalaya River and the Gulf of Mexico converge. The basin contains about 1.4 million acres, including the largest continuous tract of bottomland hardwood forest in North America and America's largest river swamp.

Geology and hydrology

The basin is the floodplain of the Atchafalaya River, a distributary of the Mississippi and Red Rivers. Historically the Atchafalaya captured an ever-growing share of the Mississippi's flow; the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Old River Control Structure, completed in 1963, now limits the Atchafalaya to about 30 percent of the combined flow to prevent the Mississippi from changing course. A floodway system, including the Morganza Spillway, uses the basin to relieve flood pressure on Baton Rouge and New Orleans.

Ecology

The basin supports bald cypress–tupelo swamps, bottomland hardwoods, and open marsh, providing habitat for alligators, black bears, egrets, herons, and the largest nesting concentration of bald eagles in the south-central United States. Its fisheries — especially crawfish, catfish, and Atchafalaya bass — are commercially and culturally important to Louisiana's Cajun communities.

Culture

The basin lies at the heart of Acadiana, and its bayous, camps, and swamp tours feature prominently in Cajun culture and cuisine. Interstate 10 crosses the basin on an 18-mile elevated bridge.
