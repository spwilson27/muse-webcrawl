Pantothenic acid

Pantothenic acid, also called vitamin B5, is a water-soluble B vitamin essential for the synthesis of coenzyme A (CoA), and thus for the metabolism of carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. Its name derives from the Greek pantos, "everywhere", because it is found in a wide variety of foods.

Sources and deficiency

Good sources include meat, whole grains, eggs, broccoli, and legumes. Deficiency is rare and usually occurs only with severe malnutrition; symptoms can include fatigue, numbness, and burning sensations in the feet.

Function

Through coenzyme A and the acyl carrier protein, pantothenic acid is central to the citric acid cycle, fatty acid synthesis and oxidation, and the production of steroid hormones and neurotransmitters.
