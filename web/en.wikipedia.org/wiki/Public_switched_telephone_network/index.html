Public switched telephone network

The public switched telephone network (PSTN) is the aggregate of the world's telephone networks operated by national, regional, or local operators, providing infrastructure and services for public telecommunication. It originated with manual switchboards and electromechanical exchanges and evolved into a largely digital, circuit-switched network interconnected by standardized signalling systems.

Architecture

The PSTN is organized as a hierarchy of exchanges or central offices. Subscribers connect to a local exchange by the local loop, historically a pair of copper wires. Calls are routed through tandem and toll switches, with international calls passing through gateway exchanges. Signalling was transformed by Signalling System No. 7 (SS7), which separated call control from the voice path and enabled services such as caller ID and toll-free numbers. Numbers are allocated under the international E.164 numbering plan administered by the ITU.

Modern developments

Since the late twentieth century the PSTN has been progressively digitized, and many operators are now retiring circuit-switched infrastructure in favour of Voice over IP carried over broadband networks, with interworking gateways preserving compatibility with legacy telephones.
