Terrarium

A terrarium (plural: terraria or terrariums) is a glass container containing soil and plants, maintained as a miniature garden or ecosystem. Sealed terraria are largely self-sustaining: plants transpire water that condenses and returns to the soil, while photosynthesis and bacterial decomposition cycle gases and nutrients. Open terraria suit dry-climate plants such as succulents and cacti.

History

The terrarium descends from the Wardian case, invented about 1829 by London physician Nathaniel Bagshaw Ward, who found ferns thriving in a sealed jar. Wardian cases revolutionised the transport of living plants by sea — enabling the global transfer of tea, rubber and cinchona — and sparked a Victorian fern craze (pteridomania).

Types and care

Closed terraria require indirect light, moisture-loving plants (ferns, mosses, fittonia, peperomia) and restraint with watering, since excess moisture invites mould. Terraria are also enclosures for animals such as reptiles and amphibians — in that sense, a vivarium — with controlled humidity, heating and lighting.
