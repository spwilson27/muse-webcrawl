Guards Division

The Guards Division was an infantry division of the British Army formed during the First World War in August 1915 from battalions of the regiments of Foot Guards. It fought on the Western Front for the remainder of the war.

First World War

Comprising the Grenadier, Coldstream, Scots, Irish and (from 1915) Welsh Guards, the division fought at Loos, the Somme, Passchendaele, Cambrai and in the Hundred Days Offensive of 1918, earning a reputation as an elite formation. It was disbanded in 1919.

Later formations

The name was revived in June 1945, when the Guards Armoured Division was reorganised as the Guards Division in Germany before being disbanded in 1946. In the modern army, "Guards Division" also denotes the administrative grouping of the five regiments of Foot Guards within the Household Division, responsible for public duties such as the King's Guard and Trooping the Colour.
