2015–16 El Niño event

2015–16 El Niño
Formed: March 2015
Dissipated: June 2016
Peak ONI: ~2.3 °C (Nov 2015–Jan 2016)
Comparable events: 1982–83, 1997–98

The 2015–16 El Niño event was one of the three strongest El Niño events since records began in 1950, rivalling those of 1982–83 and 1997–98. El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), characterised by unusually warm sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean.

Development

Conditions developed during 2014 and were officially declared an El Niño in March 2015. The event peaked in late 2015, with weekly sea surface temperature anomalies in the Niño 3.4 region briefly exceeding 3 °C, and it decayed during the first half of 2016, giving way to La Niña conditions later that year.

Impacts

The event contributed to 2015 and 2016 becoming the warmest years then on record globally. It brought flooding to parts of South America and East Africa, severe drought to southern Africa, Ethiopia, Indonesia and Australia, coral bleaching events including on the Great Barrier Reef, and an extremely active 2015 Pacific hurricane season, which included Hurricane Patricia — the strongest tropical cyclone ever recorded in the Western Hemisphere by sustained winds. Drought conditions also aggravated food insecurity for tens of millions of people.
