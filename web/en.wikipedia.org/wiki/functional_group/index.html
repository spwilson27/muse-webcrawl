Functional group

In organic chemistry, a functional group is a substituent or moiety in a molecule that causes the molecule's characteristic chemical reactions. The same functional group will undergo the same or similar chemical reactions regardless of the rest of the molecule's composition, allowing systematic prediction of reactions and the design of chemical synthesis.

Common functional groups

Well-known functional groups include the hydroxyl group of alcohols (–OH), the carbonyl group of ketones and aldehydes (C=O), the carboxyl group of carboxylic acids (–COOH), the amino group of amines (–NH2), and the carbon–carbon double and triple bonds of alkenes and alkynes.

Nomenclature

In IUPAC nomenclature, functional groups determine the suffix of a compound's name and the priority order used when several groups are present; the highest-priority group defines the parent structure.

Retrosynthesis

Functional group interconversions are a staple of retrosynthetic analysis, in which chemists plan syntheses by converting target molecules into simpler precursors.
