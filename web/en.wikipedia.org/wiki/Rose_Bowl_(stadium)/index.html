Rose Bowl (stadium)

The Rose Bowl is an outdoor athletic stadium in Pasadena, California, United States, opened in 1922. It is famous as the home of the annual Rose Bowl Game college football bowl game — "The Granddaddy of Them All" — played on New Year's Day as part of the Tournament of Roses. The stadium is a National Historic Landmark and one of the most recognizable venues in American sport.

History

Designed by architect Myron Hunt in the Arroyo Seco, the horseshoe-shaped stadium opened for the 1923 Rose Bowl Game and was enclosed into a full bowl in 1929. It hosted the 1932 and 1984 Olympic events, five Super Bowls (XI, XIV, XVII, XXI, XXVII), the 1994 FIFA World Cup Final, and the 1999 Women's World Cup Final — the most attended women's sporting event in history at the time. From 1982 it has been the home field of the UCLA Bruins.

The venue today

The stadium also hosts concerts, the monthly Rose Bowl Flea Market, and July Fourth fireworks. A major renovation completed in the 2010s added the Terry Donahue Pavilion with premium seating and a new press box, while preserving the historic facade and the iconic rose-red signage.
