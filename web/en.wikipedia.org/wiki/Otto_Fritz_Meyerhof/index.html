Otto Fritz Meyerhof

Otto Fritz Meyerhof (April 12, 1884 – October 6, 1951) was a German-American physician and biochemist who won the 1922 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (shared with Archibald Hill) for his discovery of the fixed relationship between the consumption of oxygen and the metabolism of lactic acid in muscle.

Born in Hanover, Meyerhof studied medicine at Heidelberg. His research on glycolysis — the breakdown of glycogen to lactic acid — led to the elucidation of the metabolic pathway known as the Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas pathway, and to the concept of the Pasteur effect, the suppression of fermentation by oxygen.

A professor at Kiel and later director of a department at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Medical Research in Heidelberg, Meyerhof, who was of Jewish descent, fled Nazi Germany in 1938, first to Paris and then, in 1940, to the United States, where he became a professor at the University of Pennsylvania. He died in Philadelphia in 1951.
