Ownership

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ownership is the state or fact of legal possession and title to property, carrying the right to use, enjoy, exclude others from, and transfer the thing owned, subject to law. A. M. Honoré's influential analysis (1961) unpacked ownership as a bundle of incidents — rights to possess, use, manage, take income and capital, security against expropriation, transmissibility, and the absence of term — no single one being indispensable in every system.

Ownership may be individual, joint (co-ownership), corporate or public; it may be split (between trustee and beneficiary, landlord and tenant, mortgagor and mortgagee) and limited by the rights of others and public regulation. Philosophical justifications range from Locke's labour theory and Hegel's personality theory to utilitarian and law-and-economics accounts that treat clear property rights as engines of prosperity. Critics note ownership's historical entanglement with dispossession — of commoners by enclosure, of indigenous peoples by colonial law — and contemporary concentrations of wealth.
