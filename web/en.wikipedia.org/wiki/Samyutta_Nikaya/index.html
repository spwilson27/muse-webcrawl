Samyutta Nikaya

The Samyutta Nikaya (Pali: Saṃyutta Nikāya, "Connected Discourses" or "Kindred Sayings") is a Buddhist scripture, the third of the five nikayas, or collections, in the Sutta Pitaka, which is one of the "three baskets" that compose the Pali Tipitaka of Theravada Buddhism. Because of the abbreviated way in which parts of the text are written, the total number of suttas is unclear, but the collection is generally counted as containing roughly 2,900 individual discourses grouped into 56 saṃyuttas (chapters or "connected" groups), each organised around a theme, a person, or a group of interlocutors.

Contents

The nikaya is divided into five large divisions, called vaggas:

  • Sagatha Vagga — discourses in verse, including dialogues with devas, Mara, and others;
  • Nidana Vagga — centred on dependent origination (paticca-samuppada) and causation;
  • Khandha Vagga — on the five aggregates of clinging;
  • Salayatana Vagga — on the six sense bases and feelings;
  • Maha Vagga — the "Great Division," covering the Noble Eightfold Path, the seven factors of enlightenment, mindfulness, and the Four Noble Truths (the Sacca Samyutta, containing the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta traditionally regarded as the Buddha's first sermon in its received form).

Parallels and translations

Parallels to many of its discourses survive in the Chinese Saṃyukta Āgama and in Sanskrit and Tibetan sources, making the collection an important witness to early Buddhist literature. English translations were published by the Pali Text Society as The Book of the Kindred Sayings (five volumes, 1917–30) and by Bhikkhu Bodhi as The Connected Discourses of the Buddha (2000).
