Marathwada

Marathwada is a proposed state and a region of the Indian state of Maharashtra. It comprises the eastern part of the state and consists of eight districts: Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar), Beed, Hingoli, Jalna, Latur, Nanded, Osmanabad (Dharashiv), and Parbhani. The region's administrative headquarters and largest city is Aurangabad.

Marathwada was historically part of the princely state of Hyderabad under the Nizam. After Indian independence, the region was integrated into the Indian Union following Operation Polo in 1948, initially as part of Hyderabad State. In 1956 it was transferred to Bombay State, and in 1960 it became part of the newly formed Maharashtra.

Geography

The region lies on the Deccan Plateau, drained largely by the Godavari River and its tributaries, including the Purna, Dudhna, and Manjra. Much of Marathwada falls in a rain-shadow zone and is prone to drought, which has had significant effects on its predominantly agricultural economy.

Culture and history

The region is known for the Ajanta and Ellora caves near Aurangabad, major UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and for its association with the saint-poets of the Varkari tradition, including Dnyaneshwar and Eknath. Aurangabad, founded in the early 17th century, served as a capital under Malik Ambar and later as a Mughal provincial capital under Aurangzeb, whose tomb lies at nearby Khuldabad.

Economy

Agriculture—particularly cotton, soybean, sugarcane, and pulses—dominates the regional economy, supplemented by industrial growth around Aurangabad, which hosts automotive and pharmaceutical manufacturing.
