March (music)

A march, as a musical genre, is a piece of music with a strong regular rhythm, originally written to accompany marching and to help soldiers keep step. Marches are typically composed in a duple meter such as 2/4, 4/4 (common time), or 6/8, with a brisk, regular tempo.

Structure

The classic concert march, as developed by composers such as John Philip Sousa, consists of several contrasting sections or "strains," a trio section in a related key (usually the subdominant), and often a "dogfight" or break strain. Marches frequently begin with a short introduction and feature melodies first played softly and then repeated with full instrumentation.

Types

Military marches regulate the pace of troops—typically 120 beats per minute for a quick march. Other varieties include funeral marches (such as that in Chopin's Piano Sonata No. 2 and Handel's Saul), wedding marches (notably Mendelssohn's from A Midsummer Night's Dream and Wagner's Bridal Chorus), coronation marches, and circus marches or "screamers," which are played at a fast tempo. Sousa, nicknamed the "March King," composed standards including "The Stars and Stripes Forever," "Semper Fidelis," and "The Washington Post."
