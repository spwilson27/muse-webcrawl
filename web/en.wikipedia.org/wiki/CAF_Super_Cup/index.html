CAF Super Cup

The CAF Super Cup is an annual association football competition organised by the Confederation of African Football (CAF), contested between the winners of the CAF Champions League and the winners of the CAF Confederation Cup. First played in 1993, it is African football's equivalent of the UEFA Super Cup, traditionally staged early in the year following the finals of the two club competitions.

History

The first edition was held in 1993 in Abidjan, where Africa Sports of Côte d'Ivoire beat Morocco's Wydad Casablanca on penalties. Until 2004 the second participant was the winner of the African Cup Winners' Cup; after that competition's abolition, the Confederation Cup winner took its place. The match was traditionally hosted by the Champions League winner's country; recent editions have sometimes been staged at neutral venues including Doha, Qatar.

Format

The Super Cup is a single match. If the score is level after 90 minutes, the winner is decided by a penalty shoot-out, without extra time in most editions.

Winners

Egypt's Al Ahly is by far the most successful club in the competition, with eight titles (2002, 2006, 2007, 2009, 2013, 2014, 2021 May edition, and 2021 December edition). Egyptian rivals Zamalek have won it five times, and TP Mazembe of DR Congo three times. Champions League winners have won the large majority of editions.
