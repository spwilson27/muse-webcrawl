Ossicles

The ossicles (also called the auditory ossicles) are three small bones in the middle ear — the smallest bones in the human body — that transmit sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear. From outermost to innermost they are the malleus (hammer), the incus (anvil) and the stapes (stirrup), named for their shapes.

The malleus is attached to the tympanic membrane (eardrum); vibrations pass through the incus to the stapes, whose footplate sits in the oval window of the cochlea. The lever action of the ossicular chain and the difference in area between the eardrum and the oval window amplify the pressure of sound waves, compensating for the impedance mismatch between air and the fluid of the inner ear.

Diseases affecting the ossicles include otosclerosis, in which the stapes becomes fixed, causing conductive hearing loss; it may be treated by stapedectomy. Discontinuity of the ossicular chain can result from trauma or chronic ear infection.
