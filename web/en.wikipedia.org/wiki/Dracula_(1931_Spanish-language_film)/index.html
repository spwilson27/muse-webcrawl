Dracula is a 1931 American Spanish-language horror film directed by George Melford and starring Carlos Villarías and Lupita Tovar. It was produced by Universal Pictures as a Spanish-language version of the English-language Dracula (1931) starring Bela Lugosi, using the same sets and largely the same script.

Production

In the early sound era, Hollywood studios commonly filmed foreign-language versions of their pictures on the same sets. The English-language cast filmed by day; Melford's Spanish-language company worked at night on the same Universal sets. The Spanish version runs considerably longer than the English one and is often praised by critics for Melford's more fluid camerawork and atmospheric staging, since the night crew could study the day crew's footage and improve upon it.

Cast

  • Carlos Villarías as Count Dracula
  • Lupita Tovar as Eva Seward
  • Barry Norton as Juan Harker
  • Pablo Álvarez Rubio as Renfield
  • Eduardo Arozamena as Professor Van Helsing

Legacy

Long considered a rarity, the film was restored and re-released on home video alongside the Lugosi version, and many modern critics rate it equal or superior to its English-language counterpart.
