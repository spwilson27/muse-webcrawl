Cabernet Franc

Cabernet Franc is one of the major black grape varieties worldwide. It is principally grown for blending with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot in the Bordeaux style, but can also be vinified alone, as in the Loire Valley, where it is made in Chinon and Bourgueil. In addition to being used in blends and produced as a varietal in Canada and the United States, it is sometimes made into ice wine in those regions.

History

Cabernet Franc is believed to have been established in the Libournais region of southwest France sometime in the 17th century, with cuttings later planted in the Loire. DNA profiling in the 1990s showed that Cabernet Franc crossed with Sauvignon blanc produced Cabernet Sauvignon, and with Magdeleine Noire des Charentes produced both Merlot and Carménère.

Viticulture and wine

The grape ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon and has thinner skins, giving lighter-coloured, softer wines with characteristic aromas of raspberry, bell pepper and tobacco leaf, attributable to methoxypyrazines. On Bordeaux's Right Bank it is the dominant variety in Château Cheval Blanc and a major component of Pomerol and Saint-Émilion blends.
