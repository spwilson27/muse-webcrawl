Personal information management

Personal information management
FieldInformation science, human–computer interaction
Key activitiesAcquiring, organising, maintaining, retrieving and using information

Personal information management (PIM) is the activities people perform in order to acquire or create, store, organize, maintain, retrieve, and use informational items such as documents (analog and digital), web pages, and email messages for everyday use to complete tasks (work-related or not) and fulfil a person's various roles (as parent, employee, friend, member of community, etc.). PIM is also the name of the field of study concerned with these activities.

Overview

Ideally, personal information management ensures that a person always has the right information in the right place, in the right form, and of sufficient completeness and quality to meet their current need. Tools and technologies such as personal information managers, file managers, and integrated search help, but PIM also encompasses the strategies people develop for organising their information: folder hierarchies, naming conventions, tagging, and piling versus filing.

Research

PIM emerged as a recognised area of study in the late 1980s and 1990s, drawing on information science, cognitive psychology and human–computer interaction. Research themes include how people re-find information they have seen before, the fragmentation of personal information across devices and applications, and the design of tools that unify collections. William Jones's book Keeping Found Things Found (2008) is a widely cited synthesis of the field.

Challenges

The growth of digital information has made PIM more difficult: people must manage email, files, photos, bookmarks and messages across multiple devices and cloud services. Researchers describe problems of information overload, fragmentation and the effort required to keep collections organised over long periods.
