First Mesa (Hopi: Wàlpi) is a census-designated place in Navajo County, Arizona, United States, on the Hopi Reservation. It consists of three Hopi and Hopi-Tewa villages built on top of the 570-foot-high mesa: Walpi, Sichomovi, and Tewa (Hano). The population was around 1,500 at recent censuses.

The villages

Walpi, founded around 900 AD, is one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the United States; its stone houses cling to the narrow southern tip of the mesa. Sichomovi was established in the late 18th century, and Tewa (Hano) was founded by Tewa refugees from the Rio Grande valley after the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, whose descendants still speak the Tewa language alongside Hopi. Below the mesa lies the community of Polacca.

Culture

First Mesa is known for its traditional Hopi pottery, revived in the late 19th and early 20th centuries by the potter Nampeyo of Hano, whose designs drew on ancient Sikyatki ware. The villages continue to observe a full ceremonial calendar, including katsina dances.
