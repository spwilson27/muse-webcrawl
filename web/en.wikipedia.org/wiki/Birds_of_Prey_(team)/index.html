The Birds of Prey is a superhero team featured in several American comic book series published by DC Comics. The team's initial line-up was Black Canary and Barbara Gordon — who had been paralyzed by the Joker in Batman: The Killing Joke and had reinvented herself as the information broker Oracle — and later expanded to include the Huntress and others.

Publication history

The concept originated with editor Jordan B. Gorfinkel; the name "Birds of Prey" was first applied in the 1996 one-shot Black Canary/Oracle: Birds of Prey by Chuck Dixon and Gary Frank. A sequence of one-shots and miniseries led to an ongoing series launched in 1999. Writer Gail Simone's run (beginning with issue #56, 2003) is regarded as definitive, adding the Huntress as the third core member and developing the team's blend of espionage missions and character-driven storytelling. The series was relaunched under The New 52 in 2011 and again during DC Rebirth (as Batgirl and the Birds of Prey), and a further incarnation led by Black Canary appeared in 2023.

Members

Beyond the core trio, membership has included Lady Blackhawk, Hawk and Dove, Big Barda, Batgirl (Cassandra Cain and later Barbara Gordon in the field), Catwoman, Poison Ivy, Katana, Starling, Harley Quinn, and Cassandra Cain as Orphan. Oracle typically coordinates missions from Gotham's clock tower, providing intelligence and computer support.

In other media

The team headlined a short-lived 2002 television series, Birds of Prey, and inspired the 2020 film Birds of Prey (and the Fantabulous Emancipation of One Harley Quinn), in which Harley Quinn teams with Black Canary, Huntress, Renee Montoya, and Cassandra Cain.
