Great Heathen Army

The Great Heathen Army (Old English: mycel hæþen here), also called the Great Danish Army or Great Viking Army, was a coalition of Scandinavian warriors, mainly Danes, who invaded England from 865 AD, conquering much of the north and east and founding the Danelaw.

Active865–878
LeadersIvar the Boneless, Halfdan Ragnarsson, Ubba, Guthrum
OriginDenmark / Scandinavia
OutcomeDanelaw established; Treaty of Wedmore 878

Campaign

Landing in East Anglia in 865, the army took York (866), conquered Northumbria and Mercia, and overwintered at fortified camps such as Repton and Torksey. According to saga tradition the leaders were sons of Ragnar Lothbrok avenging his death at the hands of King Ælla.

War with Wessex

Alfred the Great of Wessex fought the invaders for years; after defeat at Chippenham (878) he rallied and won at Edington, leading to Guthrum's baptism and the Treaty of Wedmore, dividing England between Wessex and the Danelaw. Many Vikings settled as farmers. See Alfred the Great and Danelaw.
