Jalal Talabani (12 November 1933 – 3 October 2017) was an Iraqi Kurdish politician who served as the President of Iraq from 2005 to 2014 — the first non-Arab president of the country — and founded the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK), one of the two main Kurdish political parties.

Early political career

Born near Kirkuk, Talabani joined the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) as a teenager and rose through its ranks while studying law in Baghdad. After disputes with KDP leader Mustafa Barzani, he broke away and in 1975 co-founded the PUK, which became the dominant force in eastern Iraqi Kurdistan, centered on Sulaymaniyah.

Presidency

Following the 2003 invasion of Iraq and the fall of Saddam Hussein, Talabani was chosen by the National Assembly as president in April 2005 and re-elected in 2006 and 2010. Known affectionately as "Mam Jalal" ("Uncle Jalal"), he cultivated a reputation as a consensus-builder among Iraq's feuding factions. A stroke in December 2012 curtailed his role, and he was succeeded by Fuad Masum in 2014.

Death and legacy

Talabani died in Berlin in October 2017. His funeral in Sulaymaniyah drew huge crowds. He is remembered as a central figure of the Kurdish national movement and of post-2003 Iraq.
