The Churches of Christ are autonomous Christian congregations associated with one another through shared beliefs and practices, arising from the Restoration Movement of the early nineteenth century in the United States. They have no central governing body or formal denomination; each local congregation is self-governing under its own elders. Historically they descend from the work of Barton W. Stone and Alexander Campbell, who sought to restore the church of the New Testament and to unite Christians on the basis of the Bible alone.

Beliefs and practices

Churches of Christ emphasize the authority of the New Testament as the pattern for church organization and worship. Baptism by immersion for the forgiveness of sins, weekly observance of the Lord's Supper, congregational a cappella singing (without instrumental accompaniment), and leadership by male elders are characteristic practices. Congregations commonly use the slogan-like summaries of the Restoration plea: "No book but the Bible, no creed but Christ," and "Where the Scriptures speak, we speak; where the Scriptures are silent, we are silent."

History

The movement began with Stone's Christians in Kentucky, following the Cane Ridge Revival of 1801, and Campbell's "Disciples" in western Pennsylvania and Virginia. The two groups united in 1832 in Lexington, Kentucky. After the American Civil War the movement divided; the Churches of Christ were recognized as a separate body from the Disciples of Christ in the 1906 United States religious census. In the twentieth century the churches spread through evangelism and missionary work, and related bodies such as the International Churches of Christ separated in later decades.

Worship

Sunday assemblies typically include congregational singing, prayers, Scripture reading, a sermon, the Lord's Supper, and a freewill contribution. Because they regard instrumental music as unauthorized by the New Testament pattern, most congregations sing a cappella, a practice that has produced a rich tradition of hymnody and shape-note singing schools.
