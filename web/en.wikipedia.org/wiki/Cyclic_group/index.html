Cyclic group


The 6th roots of unity form a cyclic group under multiplication (placeholder image)

In abstract algebra, a cyclic group is a group that can be generated by a single element: every element of the group is a power of that generator. Cyclic groups are necessarily abelian. Up to isomorphism there is exactly one cyclic group of each finite order n (denoted Zn or Cn) and one infinite cyclic group (Z, the integers under addition).

Examples and properties

Examples include the integers modulo n under addition, the n-th roots of unity under multiplication, and the rotations of a regular polygon. Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic, and cyclic groups have a completely known structure, making them the building blocks in the classification of finitely generated abelian groups. A group of prime order is always cyclic. Cyclic groups are central in cryptography, where the discrete logarithm problem is posed in cyclic groups, and in number theory through primitive roots modulo a prime.
