Bermuda Bowl

The Bermuda Bowl is the premier world championship tournament in contract bridge, contested by national teams in the open category. It is organised by the World Bridge Federation and held every two years (biennially), in odd-numbered years.

History

The tournament was first held in 1950 in Hamilton, Bermuda, from which it takes its name. The idea was promoted by bridge player and organiser Norman Bach. Early editions were dominated by the United States and Italy's famous Blue Team, which won ten consecutive titles between 1957 and 1969.

Format

Teams qualify through zonal championships from the world's bridge zones. The event is held alongside the Venice Cup (women's teams), the Senior Bowl, and the World Transnational Open Teams Championship. The format involves a round-robin qualifying stage followed by knockout matches of long duration.

Notable winners

Italy and the United States have been the most successful nations overall, with France, the Netherlands, Norway, and Poland also winning titles in recent decades.
