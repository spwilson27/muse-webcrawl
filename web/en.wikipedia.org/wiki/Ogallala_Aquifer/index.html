Ogallala Aquifer

Map of the Ogallala Aquifer beneath the High Plains.

The Ogallala Aquifer is a shallow water-table aquifer surrounded by sand, silt, clay and gravel, located beneath the Great Plains of the United States. One of the world's largest aquifers, it underlies an area of about 174,000 square miles across eight states, from South Dakota to Texas.

Use

The aquifer provides drinking water and irrigation for one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world, supporting roughly a third of US groundwater irrigation. Center-pivot irrigation transformed the High Plains into a major producer of corn, wheat, cotton and cattle after World War II.

Depletion

Withdrawal greatly exceeds natural recharge, especially in the southern plains, and water levels have fallen by more than 100 feet in parts of Texas and Kansas since pre-development. Conservation measures and falling pumping in some districts have slowed, but not reversed, depletion.
