Shawn Fanning

Born: November 22, 1980, Brockton, Massachusetts, U.S.
Occupation: Computer programmer, entrepreneur
Known for: Co-founding Napster

Shawn Fanning (born November 22, 1980) is an American computer programmer and entrepreneur. In 1999, while a freshman at Northeastern University in Boston, he developed Napster, the first widely used peer-to-peer file-sharing service, which let users share MP3 music files over the Internet and transformed the music industry.

Napster

Fanning wrote the original Napster software in 1999, taking the name from his nickname. The service grew explosively to tens of millions of users, but drew lawsuits from the Recording Industry Association of America and artists including Metallica; a federal injunction shut Napster down in 2001. Fanning — who had appeared on the cover of Time magazine at age 19 — became a symbol of the Internet's disruption of established media industries, even as the company entered bankruptcy.

Later ventures

After Napster, Fanning co-founded Snocap, a licensed digital music distribution company; Rupture, a social network for gamers acquired by Electronic Arts in 2008; Path, working with Dave Morin; and Airtime, a video chat service launched with Sean Parker in 2012. He also invested in and advised other Internet startups, and the Napster story was dramatized in the film The Social Network (2010), where Sean Parker's role is central.
