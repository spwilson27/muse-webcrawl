Actinomycetota (formerly known as Actinobacteria) is a phylum of Gram-positive bacteria characterised by DNA with a high guanine–cytosine content. They are among the most abundant and diverse bacteria in soil, fresh water and marine environments, and include filamentous forms that resemble fungi as well as simple rods and cocci.

Diversity and ecology

The phylum includes the genus Streptomyces, the largest genus of bacteria, whose species grow as branching hyphae and reproduce by spores; the plant-symbiotic nitrogen fixer Frankia; the gut commensal Bifidobacterium; and important pathogens such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, the cause of tuberculosis, Corynebacterium diphtheriae and species of Nocardia. Actinomycetota are major decomposers of organic matter in soil — their activity produces geosmin, the compound responsible for the earthy smell of rain on dry soil — and they play a central role in carbon cycling.

Importance to medicine

Actinomycetota, above all Streptomyces, are the source of the majority of naturally derived antibiotics, including streptomycin, tetracyclines, erythromycin and vancomycin, as well as antifungals, anticancer agents such as doxorubicin, and immunosuppressants. Their large genomes encode numerous biosynthetic gene clusters for secondary metabolites, making them a continuing focus of drug discovery.

Taxonomy

The phylum was renamed from Actinobacteria to Actinomycetota under the revised rules of prokaryotic nomenclature adopted in 2021, which require phylum names to be formed from a type genus. The principal class is Actinomycetia.
