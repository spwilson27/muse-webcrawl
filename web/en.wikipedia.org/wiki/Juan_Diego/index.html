Juan Diego

Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin, also known simply as Juan Diego (1474 – May 30, 1548), was a Nahua peasant and Roman Catholic saint. According to Mexican Catholic tradition, the Virgin Mary appeared to him in December 1531 on Tepeyac Hill near present-day Mexico City, an event known as Our Lady of Guadalupe.

The apparitions

Tradition holds that the Virgin appeared to Juan Diego four times, speaking in Nahuatl and asking that a church be built in her honor. When Bishop Juan de Zumárraga demanded a sign, Juan Diego gathered roses in his tilma (cloak) at the Virgin's direction; when he unfolded the tilma before the bishop, the image of Our Lady of Guadalupe was miraculously imprinted on it. The tilma is preserved in the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Mexico City.

Canonization and legacy

Juan Diego was beatified in 1990 and canonized by Pope John Paul II on July 31, 2002, becoming the first Roman Catholic indigenous saint of the Americas. His feast day is December 9. The historicity of Juan Diego has been debated by scholars, but he remains a potent symbol of Mexican Catholic identity and of the dignity of indigenous peoples.
