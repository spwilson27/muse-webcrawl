Internet censorship in China

Internet censorship in China is among the most extensive in the world. The Chinese government blocks numerous foreign websites and services, monitors domestic internet activity and requires platforms to remove prohibited content, under a system popularly known abroad as the Great Firewall of China.

Mechanisms

Blocking is implemented at the network level through DNS poisoning, IP blocking and keyword filtering of traffic crossing China's borders, combined with legal obligations on domestic companies to police their platforms. Major foreign services including Google, Facebook, YouTube, X (Twitter) and Wikipedia have been blocked at various times, fostering a domestic ecosystem of Chinese platforms such as Baidu, WeChat and Weibo. Circumvention tools such as VPNs are themselves restricted.

Legal framework

Censorship rests on a body of law and regulation including the Cybersecurity Law of 2017, real-name registration requirements and rules against content said to endanger national security, spread rumours or harm social stability. Enforcement bodies include the Cyberspace Administration of China, founded in 2014.

Debate

The Chinese government defends the system as an exercise of "internet sovereignty" and a protection against harmful content. Critics, including press-freedom organisations, argue it restricts free expression and access to information; China consistently ranks near the bottom of global internet freedom indices.
