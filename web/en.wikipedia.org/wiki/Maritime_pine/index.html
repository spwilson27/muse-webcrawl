Pinus pinaster, the maritime pine or cluster pine, is a pine native to the western Mediterranean region, from Portugal and Spain east to southern France, Italy, Morocco and Algeria. It is a medium-sized evergreen tree reaching 20–35 m tall, with thick, deeply fissured reddish-brown bark and long needles in pairs.

Distribution and uses

The species thrives on poor, sandy, acidic soils and is planted extensively for timber, resin and dune stabilisation. The great Landes forest in south-western France, about one million hectares, is largely maritime pine planted from the nineteenth century. It has become invasive in parts of South Africa, Australia and New Zealand.

Ecology

Maritime pine is adapted to wildfire: its serotinous cones open after fire, and thick bark protects mature trees. It is a host of the pine processionary moth and is threatened in places by the pine wood nematode.
