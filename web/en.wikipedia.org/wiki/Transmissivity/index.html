Transmissivity

Transmissivity is a term used in several scientific fields:

  • In optics and electromagnetism, transmissivity (or transmittance) describes the fraction of incident radiation that passes through a material or surface.
  • In hydrogeology, transmissivity is the rate at which groundwater flows horizontally through a unit width of an aquifer under a unit hydraulic gradient. It equals the hydraulic conductivity multiplied by the saturated thickness of the aquifer.

Hydrogeology

The transmissivity of an aquifer, usually denoted T, is defined as T = K · b, where K is the horizontal hydraulic conductivity and b is the saturated thickness of the aquifer. It is expressed in units of area per time (for example m²/day). Transmissivity is a central parameter in groundwater hydraulics, estimated from pumping tests using solutions such as the Theis equation, and it determines how much water a well can sustainably produce. Values range over several orders of magnitude depending on the aquifer material.

Optics

In optics, the closely related term transmittance is the ratio of transmitted to incident radiant power, and is connected to absorbance through the Beer–Lambert law. Internal transmittance corrects for surface reflection losses.
