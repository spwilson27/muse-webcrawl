Stamp collecting

Stamps arranged in a collector's stockbook.

Stamp collecting is the collecting of postage stamps and related objects, such as covers (envelopes), postal stationery and postmarks. It is one of the world's most popular hobbies, with tens of millions of collectors worldwide. The study of stamps and postal material is known as philately, a term coined in 1864 by the French collector Georges Herpin from Greek roots meaning, loosely, "love of tax-free things", referring to the fact that a stamp showed that postage had already been paid.

History

Collecting began almost as soon as the first stamp, the Penny Black, was issued by Great Britain in 1840. By the 1860s there were stamp dealers, catalogues and journals, and the first stamp album was published in 1862. The hobby grew enormously in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, aided by cheap international postage and colonial issues produced partly for the collector market. Famous rarities include the British Guiana 1c magenta (1856), the Swedish Treskilling Yellow and the United States Inverted Jenny (1918), each of which has sold for millions of dollars.

Collecting practices

Collectors may assemble stamps by country, period or theme (topical collecting), such as birds, trains or space exploration. Stamps are commonly stored in albums or stockbooks and handled with tongs to avoid damage from fingers. Value and identification depend on catalogue listings, condition, perforations, watermarks and printing varieties; standard catalogues include Stanley Gibbons, Scott, Yvert et Tellier and Michel. Organised philately is supported by national societies, local clubs and exhibitions, and by dealers and auction houses around the world.
