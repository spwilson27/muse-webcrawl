Capra is a genus of mammals, the goats, comprising nine species, including the markhor, the ibexes, and the domestic goat. Wild Capra species are found in mountainous regions of Europe, Asia, and northeastern Africa, where they are sure-footed climbers of steep, rocky terrain.

Species

Recognised species include the wild goat (C. aegagrus), ancestor of the domestic goat (C. hircus), the Alpine ibex (C. ibex), the Nubian ibex (C. nubiana), the Siberian ibex (C. sibirica), the Iberian ibex (C. pyrenaica), the walia ibex (C. walie), the West and East Caucasian turs, and the markhor (C. falconeri).

Biology

Goats are sexually dimorphic: males are larger and bear long, ridged horns that curve backwards in a scimitar shape, used in rut clashes. Females' horns are smaller. Both wild and domestic goats are browsers and grazers with a ruminant digestive system. The domestic goat, domesticated around 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent, is among humanity's most important livestock animals.
