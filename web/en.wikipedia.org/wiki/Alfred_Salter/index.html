Alfred Salter

Alfred Salter (16 June 1873 – 24 August 1945) was a British medical doctor, Labour politician and Quaker peace campaigner. He was Member of Parliament for Bermondsey West in London from 1922 to 1945 (with a break in 1931–1935).

Life and work

Salter qualified in medicine and settled in Bermondsey, then one of London's poorest districts, where he set up a pioneering medical practice providing low-cost care to the poor and worked with his wife, Ada Salter, a pioneering local councillor and mayor of Bermondsey. The Salters campaigned for public health, housing and green space in the inner city; Ada's "beautification" programme planted thousands of trees and created playgrounds.

Politics

A Christian socialist and pacifist, Salter opposed the First World War and was active in the Fellowship of Reconciliation. In Parliament he campaigned on public health, temperance and disarmament. The practice he founded in Bermondsey is regarded as a forerunner of the National Health Service. A pub, a bridge and other landmarks in Bermondsey commemorate the Salters, and the statue of Alfred Salter on the riverside at Bermondsey Wall East is a well-known local memorial.
