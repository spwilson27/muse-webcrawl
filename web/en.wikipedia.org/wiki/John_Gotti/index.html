John Joseph Gotti Jr. (October 27, 1940 – June 10, 2002) was an American mafioso and boss of the Gambino crime family in New York City. He ordered and helped to orchestrate the murder of Gambino boss Paul Castellano in December 1985 and took over the family, which became America's most powerful Mafia organisation.

Rise

Raised in poverty in the Bronx and Queens, one of thirteen children, Gotti joined the Fulton–Rockaway Boys gang and became a protégé of Gambino underboss Aniello Dellacroce. He served prison terms for hijacking and attempted manslaughter before rising to capo. On December 16, 1985, Castellano was shot outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan; Gotti seized control of the family days later.

The "Dapper Don"

Flamboyant, expensively dressed and unusually willing to court publicity, Gotti was dubbed the "Dapper Don" by the press and the "Teflon Don" after three acquittals in the 1980s — later shown to have involved jury tampering and witness intimidation. His headquarters at the Ravenite Social Club in Little Italy was under constant FBI surveillance.

Conviction and death

Wiretaps and the defection of his underboss Salvatore "Sammy the Bull" Gravano finally brought Gotti down. On April 2, 1992, he was convicted of five murders, conspiracy, racketeering, illegal gambling and tax fraud, and sentenced to life in prison without parole. He died of throat cancer on June 10, 2002, at the U.S. Medical Center for Federal Prisoners in Springfield, Missouri.
