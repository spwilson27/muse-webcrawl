Symbolα (alpha), RA

Right ascension (abbreviated RA; symbol α) is the angular distance of a particular point measured eastward along the celestial equator from the Sun at the March equinox to the hour circle of the point in question above the Earth. Together with declination, it specifies the position of a point on the celestial sphere in the equatorial coordinate system.

Explanation

Right ascension is the celestial equivalent of terrestrial longitude. Both measure an angle eastward from a primary direction: for right ascension, that direction is the March equinox, also called the First Point of Aries — the place in the sky where the Sun crosses the celestial equator at the start of northern spring. Unlike longitude, which is measured in degrees, right ascension is traditionally measured in hours, minutes, and seconds of time, from 0h to 24h, reflecting Earth's rotation: the sky appears to turn through one hour of right ascension per hour of sidereal time. One hour of right ascension equals 15 degrees of arc; one minute of RA equals 15 arcminutes.

History

The concept was used by ancient astronomers, including Hipparchus, who catalogued stars by equatorial coordinates in the second century BC. Because of the precession of the equinoxes, the right ascension and declination of a fixed star change slowly over time, so catalogues state the epoch (such as J2000.0) to which the coordinates refer.

Use

An equatorial-mounted telescope can be set to an object's right ascension and declination to locate it. At any moment, the right ascension of objects on the local meridian equals the local sidereal time, which is how observers plan which objects are visible during a night.
