Kapoor family

The Kapoor family is an Indian film dynasty that has been active in Hindi cinema (Bollywood) since 1929. Often called the "first family of Indian cinema", the Kapoors have produced actors, directors and producers across five generations, beginning with patriarch Prithviraj Kapoor and including his sons Raj, Shammi and Shashi Kapoor.

History

Prithviraj Kapoor (1906–1972), who began in silent films and founded the Prithvi Theatres, established the dynasty. His eldest son Raj Kapoor (1924–1988) became one of India's most celebrated actor-directors, founding R. K. Studios in 1948 and making classics such as Awaara (1951) and Shree 420 (1955). Shammi Kapoor (1931–2011) was a swashbuckling star of 1960s musicals, and Shashi Kapoor (1938–2017) combined Hindi stardom with English-language art cinema and revived Prithvi Theatre.

Later generations

Raj's sons Randhir, Rishi (1952–2020) and Rajiv Kapoor, and Shashi's son Kunal, carried the family into the 1970s–90s. The fourth generation includes Karisma and Kareena Kapoor (Randhir's daughters) and Ranbir Kapoor (Rishi's son), among Bollywood's leading stars of the 2000s–2020s. Through marriages, the family is connected to the Bachchan family (via the Nanda branch) and other film clans. R. K. Studios in Chembur, Mumbai, was sold in 2018 after a fire, closing a landmark chapter of the dynasty's history.
