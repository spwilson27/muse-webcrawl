Stein Rokkan (4 July 1921 – 22 July 1979) was a Norwegian political scientist and sociologist known for his macro-historical models of state formation, nation-building and party systems in Europe. He founded comparative political data archiving in Europe and helped establish political science at the University of Bergen.

Career

Rokkan studied in Oslo and Paris and worked at the Chr. Michelsen Institute in Bergen before becoming professor at the University of Bergen. His Norwegian election studies pioneered the use of survey and ecological data in Scandinavia — including the famous finding about Norwegian politics that "votes count, but resources decide". He was central to building European social-science infrastructure, including data archives and the European Consortium for Political Research.

Cleavage theory

With Seymour Martin Lipset, Rokkan argued that the party systems of Western Europe reflected social cleavages crystallised during two revolutions: the "national revolution", which produced centre–periphery and church–state conflicts, and the "industrial revolution", which produced land–industry and owners–workers conflicts. Their 1967 essay concluded that the party alternatives of the 1960s still largely reflected the cleavage structures of the 1920s — the celebrated "freezing hypothesis", a benchmark against which later scholars, including Giovanni Sartori, measured party-system change.

Rokkan's later work developed a conceptual "map of Europe", modelling how state formation, city networks and cultural boundaries along the old Roman limes and the trade routes of the "city belt" shaped territorial politics across centuries.

Major works

  • Party Systems and Voter Alignments (1967), co-edited with S. M. Lipset
  • Citizens, Elections, Parties (1970)
  • State Formation, Nation-Building, and Mass Politics in Europe (posthumous, 1999)
