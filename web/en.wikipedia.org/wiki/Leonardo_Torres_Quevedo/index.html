Leonardo Torres Quevedo

Leonardo Torres Quevedo (20 December 1852 – 18 December 1936) was a Spanish civil engineer and mathematician, a pioneering inventor in fields including cable cars, airships, remote control and early computing machines.

Born20 December 1852, Santa Cruz de Iguña, Spain
Died18 December 1936, Madrid, Spain
Known forEl Ajedrecista chess automaton, Telekino, Astra-Torres airships
FieldEngineering, mathematics

Inventions

Torres Quevedo designed funiculars and cable cars, including the Spanish Aerocar still operating at Niagara Falls. His Astra-Torres airships were used by Allied forces in the First World War. In 1903 he presented the Telekino, an early radio remote-control system for unmanned vessels.

Computing machines

Most famously, his chess endgame automaton El Ajedrecista (1912) could play a king-and-rook versus king endgame against a human opponent and is considered one of the earliest computer games. He also designed analogue calculating machines and wrote essays on automata that anticipated ideas in modern computing.
