Antonio Di Pietro (born 2 October 1950) is an Italian former magistrate and politician. As a Milan prosecutor in the early 1990s he became the public face of the Mani pulite ("Clean Hands") anti-corruption investigations that exposed the Tangentopoli scandal and brought down Italy's postwar party system.

Magistrate

Born to a farming family in Molise, Di Pietro worked as an emigrant factory hand in Germany before earning a law degree and joining the police, then the judiciary. In February 1992 his team arrested Socialist official Mario Chiesa taking a bribe, the first arrest of Mani pulite; the inquiry spread to thousands of politicians and businessmen. Di Pietro resigned from the judiciary in December 1994 after clashing with the government of Silvio Berlusconi.

Political career

He served as Minister of Public Works in Romano Prodi's first government (1996), then founded his own party, Italy of Values (Italia dei Valori), in 1998. He sat in the Italian Senate and Chamber of Deputies and was elected three times to the European Parliament, and again served as Minister of Infrastructure under Prodi in 2006–2008. He led Italy of Values until 2013.
