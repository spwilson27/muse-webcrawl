Cognitive radio

A cognitive radio (CR) is an intelligent radio that can be programmed and configured dynamically to use the best wireless channels in its vicinity to avoid user interference and congestion. Such a radio automatically detects available channels in the wireless spectrum, then accordingly changes its transmission or reception parameters.

History

The concept was first proposed by Joseph Mitola III and Gerald Q. Maguire Jr. in 1999, building on software-defined radio. Regulators such as the FCC in the United States later explored allowing unlicensed devices to opportunistically use "white spaces" in television bands.

Functions

The main functions of a cognitive radio transceiver are spectrum sensing (detecting unused spectrum), spectrum management (selecting the best available channel), spectrum sharing (coordinating access with other users) and spectrum mobility (vacating a channel when a licensed primary user appears).

Applications

Cognitive radio techniques underpin dynamic spectrum access, TV white-space broadband, public-safety networks and aspects of 5G and IEEE 802.22 wireless regional area networks.
