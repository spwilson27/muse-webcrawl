K-1 is a Japanese kickboxing promotion and brand founded in 1993 by Kazuyoshi Ishii, a master of Seidokaikan karate. Its rules — punches, kicks, and knee strikes, with clinching restricted — were designed to let fighters from different stand-up martial arts compete against each other, and the letter "K" is said to stand for karate, kickboxing, and kempo (and "Kakuto," fighting).

History

The first K-1 World Grand Prix was held at the Yoyogi National Gymnasium in Tokyo on 30 April 1993 and was won by the Croatian fighter Branko Cikatić. The heavyweight Grand Prix format — an eight-man single-elimination tournament culminating in a final each December — became the promotion's centrepiece. Dutch fighters dominated much of the early era: Ernesto Hoost and Peter Aerts each won multiple Grand Prix titles, while Remy Bonjasky and Semmy Schilt later added their names. In its heyday K-1 events filled the Tokyo Dome before crowds of more than 50,000 and were broadcast widely in Japan.

Rules and format

K-1 bouts are fought in a ring over three rounds of three minutes (with extension rounds in tournament matches). Punches, kicks, knee strikes, and spinning backfists are permitted; elbows and prolonged clinching are not. Fighters traditionally advanced through regional Grand Prix qualifiers into the World Grand Prix Final Elimination and the final eight-man tournament. A lighter-weight series, K-1 MAX (for fighters around 70 kg), was launched in 2002 and produced stars such as Masato, Buakaw Banchamek, and Andy Souwer.

Financial troubles and revival

The promotion's parent organisation, Fighting and Entertainment Group (FEG), accumulated massive debts and collapsed in 2011, bringing the brand's activity nearly to a halt. The K-1 trademarks were acquired by a Hong Kong–based company, K-1 Global, and from 2014 a revived Japanese organisation began staging events under the K-1 name, focusing on lighter weight classes and a new generation of Japanese fighters. The "K-1 World GP" events in Japan have continued since, rebuilding the brand's presence.

Influence

K-1 was instrumental in popularising heavyweight kickboxing worldwide in the 1990s and 2000s and served as a proving ground for many fighters who later succeeded in mixed martial arts, including Mirko Cro Cop, Alistair Overeem, and Mark Hunt.
