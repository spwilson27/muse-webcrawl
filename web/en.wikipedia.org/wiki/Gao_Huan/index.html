Gao Huan (496 – 13 February 547), courtesy name Heliuhun, was a Chinese military general and politician of the Northern Wei and Eastern Wei dynasties. He became the paramount authority of Eastern Wei and the de facto ruler of northern China east of the passes, and his family later founded the Northern Qi dynasty. After Northern Qi was established by his son Gao Yang, Gao Huan was posthumously honoured as an emperor.

Early life

Gao Huan was born in 496 at the northern garrison town of Huaishuo, one of the Six Garrisons guarding Northern Wei's frontier against the Rouran. His family, of Han Chinese background, claimed descent from the Gao clan of Bohai, but had long lived among the Xianbei population of the frontier and adopted many Xianbei customs. Impoverished in his youth, Gao Huan served as a courier before joining the uprisings that engulfed northern China in the 520s.

Rise to power

Gao Huan served successively under the rebel leaders Du Luozhou and Ge Rong and then under the Northern Wei general Erzhu Rong, rising through the ranks. After the Erzhu clan seized power and then fell into conflict, Gao Huan broke with them, won over the remnants of Ge Rong's army — largely ethnic Han and other non-Xianbei soldiers stationed in Hebei — and in 532 destroyed the Erzhu forces. He installed Emperor Xiaowu of Northern Wei, but relations soured; in 534 Emperor Xiaowu fled west to the general Yuwen Tai, and Gao Huan enthroned Yuan Shanjian as Emperor Xiaojing, moving the capital east to Ye. This split Northern Wei into Gao Huan's Eastern Wei and Yuwen Tai's Western Wei.

Rule of Eastern Wei

From his base at Jinyang (modern Taiyuan), Gao Huan controlled Eastern Wei's armies and administration while nominally serving its emperor. He fought a long series of campaigns against Western Wei — including the battles of Xiaoguan, Shayuan (537), Heqiao (538) and Mangshan (543), and the siege of Yubi — but never succeeded in conquering the west. Within his own domains he was regarded as a capable administrator and a shrewd judge of men, balancing the interests of his Xianbei soldiers and Han Chinese officials.

Death and legacy

Gao Huan died in 547, shortly after a failed campaign against Western Wei's fortress of Yubi. His eldest son Gao Cheng succeeded him as regent, and in 550 his second son Gao Yang deposed the Eastern Wei emperor and founded Northern Qi. Gao Huan was posthumously honoured with the temple name Gaozu and the posthumous name Emperor Shenwu.
