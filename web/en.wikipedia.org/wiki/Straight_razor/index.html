Straight razor

A straight razor is a razor with a blade that can fold into its handle. Also called an open razor and, colloquially, a cut-throat razor, it consists of a sharpenable steel blade (typically described by its width in eighths of an inch and by its grind, from wedge to full hollow) pivoted in protective scales of horn, wood, bone or synthetic material.

History

Straight razors were the principal shaving instrument from the 18th century until the early 20th, with Sheffield and Solingen the great centres of manufacture. Their dominance ended after King Camp Gillette's disposable-blade safety razor (1903) made shaving quicker and safer; barbers continued to use straights for finishing work, though health regulations later pushed many to replaceable-blade "shavettes".

Use and maintenance

The blade is maintained by stropping on leather before each shave and honed periodically on whetstones. Shaving is done with short strokes at roughly a 30-degree angle after preparing the skin with hot water and lather. A modern revival of "wet shaving" has renewed interest in straight razors for their close shave, longevity and freedom from disposable waste.
