Bessel filter

[Image placeholder: Bessel filter response curve]

In electronics and signal processing, a Bessel filter is a type of analog linear filter with a maximally flat group/phase delay (maximally linear phase response), which preserves the wave shape of filtered signals in the passband. Bessel filters are often used in audio crossover systems.

The filter's frequency response is derived from Bessel polynomials and is named after Friedrich Bessel. Unlike Butterworth or Chebyshev filters, a Bessel filter has a gentle roll-off and no ripple, but its group delay is almost constant across the passband, so a filtered pulse emerges with minimal distortion of its shape.

Characteristics

The transfer function is expressed using reverse Bessel polynomials. Bessel filters sacrifice stopband attenuation for phase linearity; for the same order, they attenuate high frequencies less steeply than Butterworth or elliptic designs. They are widely used where waveform preservation matters, such as in digital audio oversampling filters and measurement equipment.
