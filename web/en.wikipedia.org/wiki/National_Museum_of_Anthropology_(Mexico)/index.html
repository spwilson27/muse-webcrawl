National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico)

National Museum of Anthropology
Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología
Location: Chapultepec Park, Mexico City
Opened: 1964
Architect: Pedro Ramírez Vázquez
Type: Archaeology and anthropology museum

The National Museum of Anthropology (Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología, MNA) is a national museum of Mexico, located in Chapultepec Park in Mexico City. It is the largest and most visited museum in Mexico and contains significant archaeological and anthropological artifacts from Mexico's pre-Columbian heritage.

Building

The current building, designed by architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez and inaugurated in 1964, is a landmark of modern Mexican architecture. Its vast central courtyard is covered by an enormous umbrella-like roof supported by a single central pillar clad in bronze relief. Exhibition halls are arranged around the courtyard, with archaeology on the ground floor and ethnography of Mexico's Indigenous peoples above.

Collection

Famous exhibits include the Aztec Sun Stone (Calendar Stone), the colossal Olmec heads, treasures from the Maya tomb of Pakal at Palenque, and the Aztec sculpture of Coatlicue. The museum's collection traces the cultures of Mesoamerica, including the Olmec, Maya, Zapotec, Mixtec, Mexica (Aztec) and cultures of the Gulf Coast, West Mexico and the North. It is administered by the National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH).
