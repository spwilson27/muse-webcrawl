The Foreign Intelligence Service of the Russian Federation (Russian: Служба внешней разведки Российской Федерации, SVR RF) is Russia's external intelligence agency, focusing mainly on civilian affairs. It is the direct successor of the First Chief Directorate of the Soviet KGB.

History

The SVR was created in December 1991 when Boris Yeltsin reorganised the KGB after the collapse of the Soviet Union, converting its foreign-intelligence arm first into the Central Intelligence Service and then the SVR. Its headquarters at Yasenevo in south-west Moscow dates from the KGB era.

Role

The agency collects political, economic and scientific-technical intelligence abroad and runs networks of officers under diplomatic and other cover, as well as "illegals" — a programme exposed in the United States in 2010. It works alongside the FSB (internal security) and the GRU (military intelligence). Under Russian law, the SVR director reports directly to the President of Russia.
