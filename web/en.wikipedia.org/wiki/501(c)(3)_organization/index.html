501(c)(3) organization

The Internal Revenue Service grants and oversees 501(c)(3) status

A 501(c)(3) organization is a United States nonprofit organisation that is exempt from federal income tax under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. There are roughly 1.5 million such organisations, including charities, educational institutions, religious bodies, hospitals and private foundations.

Requirements

To qualify, an organisation must be organised and operated exclusively for exempt purposes — religious, charitable, scientific, educational, literary, amateur sports competition, prevention of cruelty to animals or children, or testing for public safety. No part of its net earnings may inure to the benefit of private individuals (private inurement), and its assets must be permanently dedicated to an exempt purpose upon dissolution.

Types

The IRS classifies 501(c)(3)s broadly as public charities or private foundations. Public charities receive support from a broad public base and include churches, schools and hospitals; private foundations are typically funded and controlled by a family or small group and are subject to additional rules, including a required annual distribution of about 5% of investment assets.

Political activity limits

501(c)(3) organisations are absolutely prohibited from intervening in political campaigns on behalf of or against candidates (the Johnson Amendment, added in 1954), and lobbying may form only an insubstantial part of their activities. Donations to 501(c)(3)s are generally tax-deductible for the donor — a key distinction from 501(c)(4) social welfare organisations.

Application and compliance

Most organisations apply for recognition using IRS Form 1023 (or the streamlined Form 1023-EZ) and file annual Form 990 information returns, which are public. Status can be revoked for failing to file for three consecutive years.
