The 1992 Consensus (Chinese: 九二共識) is a political term referring to the outcome of a meeting in November 1992 between semi-official representatives of the People's Republic of China (PRC) and the Republic of China (Taiwan). According to the account later promoted by the Kuomintang (KMT), the two sides reached an understanding that there is "one China," while each side maintains its own interpretation of what that means—often summarized as "one China, respective interpretations" (一中各表). This understanding enabled the 1993 Wang–Koo summit in Singapore and subsequent cross-strait talks.

Origins and naming

The November 1992 talks in Hong Kong between Taiwan's Straits Exchange Foundation (SEF) and the mainland's Association for Relations Across the Taiwan Straits (ARATS) concerned the authentication of documents. Correspondence exchanged afterward was later characterized by each side differently: Beijing emphasized agreement on the one-China principle, while Taipei emphasized that each side would express its position orally. The label "1992 Consensus" itself was coined only in 2000 by Su Chi, a former Taiwanese mainland-affairs official, who has acknowledged inventing the term to repackage the earlier statements.

Dispute over its existence and content

Whether a "consensus" was reached, and on what, is contested. The Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) has generally denied that any such consensus exists, and former president Lee Teng-hui—president at the time—denied its existence. Beijing insists on the consensus as a precondition for official dialogue but defines it strictly as adherence to one China, rejecting the "respective interpretations" formula. Taiwan's DPP governments since 2016 have declined to affirm the term, and cross-strait official contacts have consequently been suspended by Beijing.

Significance

The 1992 Consensus functioned for two decades as the diplomatic formula underpinning semi-official cross-strait engagement, including the 2015 Ma–Xi meeting in Singapore—the first meeting between the leaders of the two sides since 1949.
