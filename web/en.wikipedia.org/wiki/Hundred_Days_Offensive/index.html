Hundred Days Offensive
Date8 August – 11 November 1918
LocationNorthern France and southern Belgium

ResultDecisive Allied victory; Armistice of 11 November 1918

The Hundred Days Offensive was a series of massive Allied offensives on the Western Front in the final period of the First World War, from 8 August 1918 until the Armistice of 11 November. Following the failure of the German Spring Offensive and the Allied counterstroke at the Second Battle of the Marne, the Allies — under the overall coordination of Ferdinand Foch — launched successive attacks that pushed the German army back continuously, in contrast to the static trench warfare of previous years.

Allied infantry advancing in the autumn of 1918.

Course of the offensive

The offensive opened with the Battle of Amiens on 8 August 1918, when British, Australian, Canadian, and French forces, supported by massed tanks and aircraft, broke through German lines; Erich Ludendorff called it "the black day of the German Army." Successive operations followed: the Second Battle of the Somme, the advance to the Hindenburg Line, the Battle of St Quentin Canal (where the line was breached in late September), the Meuse-Argonne Offensive by American forces, and the liberation of northern France and Belgium.

Outcome

By November, German forces were in full retreat, the army's reserves were exhausted, and Germany's allies had collapsed. Facing military defeat, domestic unrest, and the Kiel mutiny, the German government sought an armistice, which took effect on 11 November 1918. Allied casualties during the Hundred Days were heavy — roughly 1.8 million across all armies engaged — but the campaign ended the war on the Western Front.
