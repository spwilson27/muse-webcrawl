Daegaya (대가야, "Great Gaya") was a city-state and, by the late 5th century, the leading polity of the Gaya confederacy in the Nakdong River region of southern Korea. Centred on modern Goryeong County, it rose to prominence after the decline of Geumgwan Gaya and survived until its conquest by Silla in 562.

History

According to legend, Daegaya was founded in 42 AD by King Ijinasi, said to have descended from the heavens alongside the founders of the other Gaya states. For centuries it was one of several Gaya polities enriched by iron production and trade with Baekje, Silla and Wa (Japan). As Geumgwan Gaya waned after Silla and Goguryeo interventions in the early 5th century, Daegaya became the confederacy's centre, reaching its height under rulers of the late 5th and early 6th centuries. King Haji attempted to rally the Gaya states and ally with Baekje against expanding Silla, but Silla annexed Daegaya in 562, completing its absorption of Gaya.

Archaeology

The Jisan-dong tomb cluster in Goryeong — large mounded royal burials, including tombs with sacrificed attendants — reveals Daegaya's wealth and hierarchy. Finds include iron armour and weapons, horse trappings, ornate pottery and the famous Daegaya long-necked jars and mounted cups. The Gaya Tumuli, including the Daegaya royal tombs, were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023. Goryeong also preserves the Daegaya Museum and is associated with the gayageum zither, credited to King Gasil of Gaya.
