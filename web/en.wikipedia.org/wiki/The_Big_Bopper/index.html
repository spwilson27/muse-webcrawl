The Big Bopper

Born: Jiles Perry Richardson Jr., October 24, 1930, Sabine Pass, Texas
Died: February 3, 1959 (aged 28), near Clear Lake, Iowa
Occupation: Disc jockey, singer, songwriter
Known for: "Chantilly Lace"

Jiles Perry "J. P." Richardson Jr. (October 24, 1930 – February 3, 1959), better known by his stage name The Big Bopper, was an American musician and disc jockey. His best-known compositions include "Chantilly Lace," "White Lightning," and "Running Bear," and he was one of the first artists to experiment with music videos. He died in a plane crash alongside Buddy Holly and Ritchie Valens, an event later known as "The Day the Music Died."

Career

Richardson worked as a disc jockey at KTRM radio in Beaumont, Texas, where he adopted the "Big Bopper" persona on his afternoon show. In 1957 he set a record by broadcasting for five days, two hours, and eight minutes straight. His novelty song "Chantilly Lace," with its spoken "Hellooo, baby!" opening, became a number-one hit in 1958, and his album Chantilly Lace followed.

Death

While touring with the Winter Dance Party in early 1959, Richardson — suffering from flu and, at roughly 240 pounds, uncomfortable on the tour bus — took the seat on the chartered plane that Waylon Jennings had given up. On February 3, 1959, the Beechcraft Bonanza crashed shortly after takeoff from Mason City, Iowa, killing Richardson, Holly, Valens, and pilot Roger Peterson. Don McLean's 1971 song "American Pie" immortalized the date as "the day the music died."

Legacy

"White Lightning," written by Richardson, became George Jones's first number-one country hit in 1959; "Running Bear" topped the charts for Johnny Preston in 1960. Richardson left behind one of rock and roll's most recognizable voices and a small but enduring catalog.
