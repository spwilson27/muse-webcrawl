Eeben Barlow is a South African soldier and security entrepreneur best known as the founder of Executive Outcomes (EO), the private military company whose operations in Angola and Sierra Leone in the 1990s made it the prototype of the modern private military industry.

Military career

Barlow was born in Northern Rhodesia and served in the South African Defence Force during the Border War period, in reconnaissance and special-operations roles. He later acknowledged working with the Civil Cooperation Bureau, an apartheid-era covert unit. With the end of apartheid approaching and the SADF's elite units being demobilised, Barlow founded Executive Outcomes in 1989, initially to provide clandestine training; it soon expanded into full-scale combat support contracts staffed largely by veterans of units such as 32 Battalion and the Reconnaissance Commandos.

Executive Outcomes

Executive Outcomes won its most famous contract in 1993, when it was hired by the Angolan government to retake the oil installations at Soyo from UNITA rebels, and thereafter to train and support the Angolan armed forces; its operations are credited with helping drive UNITA to the Lusaka peace negotiations. In 1995 EO was hired by Sierra Leone's military government to fight the Revolutionary United Front, whose advance on Freetown it reversed within months. The company's effectiveness — and its links to mining interests paid through affiliated concessions — drew international scrutiny and condemnation, and South Africa's Regulation of Foreign Military Assistance Act (1998) effectively forced its closure; EO was dissolved at the end of 1998.

Later work

Barlow subsequently chaired STTEP International, a private military company that advised and trained African Union and national forces, including Nigerian troops fighting Boko Haram in 2015. He has written and lectured widely on private military companies, arguing that professional contractors can succeed where United Nations peacekeeping fails, and published the memoir Executive Outcomes: Against All Odds (2007, revised 2018) and Composite Warfare.
