Frank Marshall

Frank Wilton Marshall (born September 13, 1946) is an American film producer and director. With his wife, Kathleen Kennedy, he founded Amblin Entertainment with Steven Spielberg in 1981, and later The Kennedy/Marshall Company. He is one of the most commercially successful producers in Hollywood history.

Early life

Marshall was born in Glendale, California. His father, Jack Marshall, was a composer and conductor; his mother was a teacher. He attended UCLA, graduating in 1968 with a degree in political science, and played on the university's championship soccer team in 1966.

Career

Marshall entered the film business through director Peter Bogdanovich, working as a location manager on The Last Picture Show (1971) and going on to produce films such as Paper Moon (1973) and Nickelodeon (1976). He first worked with Steven Spielberg as executive producer on Raiders of the Lost Ark (1981).

As a producer, Marshall's credits include the Indiana Jones films, Poltergeist (1982), Gremlins (1984), The Color Purple (1985), Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), Hook (1991), The Sixth Sense (1999), Seabiscuit (2003), the Bourne films, The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (2008) and the Jurassic World films.

Directing

Marshall's directorial debut was the thriller Arachnophobia (1990). He went on to direct Alive (1993), about the Uruguayan rugby team whose plane crashed in the Andes, Congo (1995) and the survival drama Eight Below (2006).

Sports administration

A keen sportsman, Marshall served as a vice president of the United States Olympic Committee and was involved in organising the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics. He has competed in marathons and triathlons.

Personal life

Marshall married producer Kathleen Kennedy in 1987; they have two daughters. In 2012 he received the Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award jointly with Kennedy.
