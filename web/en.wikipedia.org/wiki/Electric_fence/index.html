Electric fence

An electric fence is a barrier that uses electric shocks to deter animals and people from crossing a boundary. The voltage of the shock may have effects ranging from discomfort to death. Most electric fences are used today for agricultural fencing and other forms of animal control, although they are also used to protect high-security areas and in some cases as a weapon.

Principle

An electric fence energizer (also called a charger or controller) converts mains, battery, or solar power into brief, high-voltage pulses — often several thousand volts — delivered about once per second. When an animal touches the electrified wire while standing on the ground, the circuit completes through its body back to the energizer's earth stake, delivering a short, sharp, memorable shock.

The pulses are carefully limited in energy for safety: the shock is designed to be painful but not harmful, teaching livestock to avoid the fence through psychological conditioning rather than physical restraint.

History

Early patents for electric animal fencing date from the 19th century, and H. G. Wells described an electrified fence in fiction. Practical energizers based on interrupter mechanisms were developed in the 1930s in New Zealand and the United States — notably by William Gallagher, whose company Gallagher Group became a major manufacturer.

Uses

Electric fences are used for rotational grazing and containing cattle, horses, sheep, pigs, and poultry, and for keeping out predators and wildlife such as elephants and bears. Security fences with lethal voltage have been used in prisons and military installations, though non-lethal systems are far more common.
