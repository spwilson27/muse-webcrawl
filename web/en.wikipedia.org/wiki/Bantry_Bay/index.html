Bantry Bay

Bantry Bay (Irish: Bá Bheanntraí) is a bay located in County Cork, Ireland. The bay runs approximately 35 km (22 mi) from northeast to southwest into the Atlantic Ocean. It is approximately 3 to 4 km (1.9 to 2.5 mi) wide at the head and 10 km (6.2 mi) wide at the entrance, and is one of the longest and deepest natural harbours in Europe. The town of Bantry sits at the head of the bay, and Whiddy Island and Bere Island lie within it.

History

In December 1796 a French expedition under General Hoche, carrying Wolfe Tone and some 15,000 troops intended to land in support of the United Irishmen, reached Bantry Bay but was scattered by storms; only part of the fleet anchored, and no landing took place. The failed expedition is commemorated in Bantry and in the folk song tradition.

During the Napoleonic and later periods the bay's anchorage at Berehaven was an important Royal Navy base, remaining a British Treaty Port until 1938. Whiddy Island became the site of a major oil terminal in the 1960s; in January 1979 the tanker Betelgeuse exploded there, killing 50 people, one of Ireland's worst disasters.

Geography and economy

The bay is bounded by the Beara Peninsula to the north and the Sheep's Head Peninsula to the south. Fishing, mussel farming, tourism and the oil storage terminal on Whiddy Island are the main economic activities. Glengarriff, at the bay's head on the Beara side, is a noted tourist village with the Italianate gardens of Garnish Island nearby.
