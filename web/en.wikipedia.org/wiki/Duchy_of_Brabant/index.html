Duchy of Brabant

Duchy of Brabant
Status: Duchy of the Holy Roman Empire
Existed: 1183–1795
Capitals: Brussels, Leuven
Languages: Dutch, French

The Duchy of Brabant was a state of the Holy Roman Empire, established in 1183 and centred on the cities of Brussels and Leuven in the Low Countries. It grew out of the Landgraviate of Brabant and became one of the most prosperous and influential principalities of north-western Europe.

History

Henry I became the first Duke of Brabant in 1183/84. The duchy expanded eastward and absorbed the Margraviate of Antwerp. Brabantine towns such as Leuven, Brussels, Antwerp and 's-Hertogenbosch grew rich on the cloth trade and commerce between England, France and the Rhineland. In 1356 the Joyous Entry charter limited ducal power and guaranteed urban liberties.

Through marriage the duchy passed to the Dukes of Burgundy in 1430 and thence to the Habsburgs. In the Dutch Revolt the duchy was split: the north (Staats-Brabant) came under the Dutch Republic as a generality land, while the south remained Spanish (later Austrian) Habsburg territory. The southern part formed the core of what would become Belgium. The duchy was abolished following French annexation in 1795.

Legacy

The name survives in the modern Belgian provinces of Flemish Brabant and Walloon Brabant, the Dutch province of North Brabant, and the former province of Brabant (split in 1995). The Brabantine lion remains the region's heraldic symbol.
