Hall County is a county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Georgia. Its county seat and largest city is Gainesville. The county was created in 1818 and named for Lyman Hall, a signer of the Declaration of Independence and governor of Georgia.

Geography

Much of the county's eastern boundary is formed by Lake Sidney Lanier, a major reservoir on the Chattahoochee River that supplies water and recreation for north Georgia. Hall County lies in the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains and is part of the Gainesville metropolitan area.

Economy

Poultry processing is a dominant industry — Gainesville styles itself the "Poultry Capital of the World" — alongside manufacturing, healthcare and logistics.

Communities

Cities include Gainesville, Oakwood, Flowery Branch, Buford (partly), Braselton (partly), Clermont, Gillsville (partly) and Lula (partly).
