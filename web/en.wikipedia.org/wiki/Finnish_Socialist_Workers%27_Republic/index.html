The Finnish Socialist Workers' Republic (Finnish: Suomen sosialistinen työväentasavalta), commonly known as Red Finland, was a short-lived socialist state established in 1918 during the Finnish Civil War. It controlled the industrialised south of Finland, including Helsinki, Tampere, and Turku, and was governed by the Finnish People's Delegation.

History

Following Finland's declaration of independence from Russia in December 1917, tensions between the socialist Red Guards and the conservative White Guards erupted into civil war in late January 1918. The Reds proclaimed a socialist republic and signed a treaty with Soviet Russia on 1 March 1918, by which Russia recognised the republic and ceded territory including the Petsamo area. In practice, the republic's administration struggled with food shortages, military organisation, and a collapsing economy.

Defeat

The Whites, assisted by a German expeditionary force that landed in April 1918, defeated the Reds by May. The Socialist Workers' Republic collapsed; its leaders fled to Soviet Russia, and tens of thousands of Red prisoners were held in prison camps, where many died of disease and malnutrition.
