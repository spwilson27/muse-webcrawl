Dale Mortensen

Dale Thomas Mortensen (February 2, 1939 – January 9, 2014) was an American economist and Nobel laureate. He received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2010, jointly with Peter A. Diamond and Christopher A. Pissarides, "for their analysis of markets with search frictions."

Career and research

Mortensen earned his bachelor's degree from Willamette University and his Ph.D. from Carnegie Mellon University in 1967, joining the faculty of Northwestern University, where he spent most of his career. His research pioneered the study of labour markets in which job seekers and employers take time to find each other — "search frictions." Together with Pissarides he developed the Diamond–Mortensen–Pissarides (DMP) model, in which unemployment arises naturally from the matching process between workers and vacancies even in equilibrium, providing a framework for analysing unemployment benefits, hiring costs and labour-market policy.

He also held a Niels Bohr visiting professorship at Aarhus University in Denmark. Mortensen died of cancer in 2014 at the age of 74.
