Pierre-Paul Riquet, Baron de Bonrepos (1609–1680), was a French engineer and salt-tax farmer best known as the originator and builder of the Canal du Midi, the canal linking Toulouse to the Mediterranean Sea, considered one of the greatest engineering works of the 17th century.

Life and the canal project

Riquet was born in Béziers in Languedoc and made a fortune as a farmer-general of the gabelle (salt tax) in Languedoc. The idea of joining the Atlantic to the Mediterranean by canal had been discussed since Roman times, but the key problem was supplying water to the summit level. Living near the Montagne Noire, Riquet discovered that its streams could be dammed and diverted: he built the Bassin de Saint-Ferréol reservoir and a feeder channel (the Rigole) to the summit pound at the Seuil de Naurouze.

In 1662 Riquet persuaded Louis XIV's minister Jean-Baptiste Colbert of the scheme's feasibility, famously demonstrating it with a model at his estate of Bonrepos. Authorised by royal edict in 1666, construction ran from 1667 to 1681, employing around 12,000 workers, with Riquet spending much of his personal fortune on the works. He died in 1680, months before the canal — 240 km long, with over 100 locks, aqueducts and tunnels — was officially opened in May 1681. The Canal du Midi is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and an obelisk and statues in Béziers and Toulouse commemorate its creator.
