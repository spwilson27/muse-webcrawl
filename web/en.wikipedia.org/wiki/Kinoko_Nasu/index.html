Kinoko Nasu (Japanese: 奈須 きのこ, born November 28, 1973) is a Japanese author, best known for creating the Tsukihime and Fate series of visual novels and for co-founding the company Type-Moon with artist Takashi Takeuchi. His works are noted for their elaborate shared fictional universe (often called the "Nasuverse"), dense world-building, and themes of heroism, mortality, and the value of human life.

Career

Nasu wrote the novel The Garden of Sinners (Kara no Kyōkai) in 1998–99, publishing chapters online. With Takeuchi he formed the doujin circle Type-Moon and released the visual novel Tsukihime (2000), a major underground success. After going commercial, the company released Fate/stay night (2004), which became one of the best-known visual novels ever made and spawned the vast Fate franchise, including the mobile game Fate/Grand Order (2015), for which Nasu serves as lead writer and supervisor.

Style and themes

Nasu's writing is characterised by intricate magical systems with strict internal rules, extensive exposition delivered in a distinctive prose style, and protagonists who confront despair and self-destruction. He is famously reclusive, rarely appearing in public, and his pen name is written with "kinoko" (mushroom) in hiragana after the surname Nasu (eggplant).
