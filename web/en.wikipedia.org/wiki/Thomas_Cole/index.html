Thomas Cole

Thomas Cole (February 1, 1801 – February 11, 1848) was an English-born American artist and the founder of the Hudson River School of American landscape painting. Known for his romantic portrayals of the American wilderness, Cole is best remembered for multi-canvas allegorical series such as The Course of Empire (1833–1836) and The Voyage of Life (1838–1840).

Early life

Cole was born in Bolton-le-Moor (now Bolton), Lancashire, England, in 1801. His family emigrated to the United States in 1818, settling first in Philadelphia and then in Steubenville, Ohio. Largely self-taught, Cole worked as an engraver's assistant and itinerant portraitist before turning to landscape painting, moving to New York City in 1825.

Career

In the summer of 1825 Cole traveled up the Hudson River to the Catskill Mountains, producing sketches that became paintings such as Lake with Dead Trees and Falls of the Kaaterskill. These works were exhibited in a New York shop window and attracted the patronage of John Trumbull and other prominent figures, launching his career.

Cole traveled to Europe in 1829–1832 and again in 1841–1842, studying the Old Masters and painting Italian and English landscapes. His five-part series The Course of Empire, commissioned by the New York banker Jonathan Sturges, traces a civilization from savagery through empire to desolation and remains his most famous work. The Voyage of Life (1840), an allegorical series depicting a Christian's journey from childhood to old age, was widely reproduced as engravings.

Death and legacy

Cole died of pleurisy in Catskill, New York, in 1848, leaving his monumental series The Cross and the World unfinished. His home and studio in Catskill, Cedar Grove, is now the Thomas Cole National Historic Site. Cole's influence on American landscape painting, exercised through pupils such as Frederic Edwin Church, was profound.
