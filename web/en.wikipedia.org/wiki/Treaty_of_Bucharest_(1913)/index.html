Treaty of Bucharest (1913)

Signed: 10 August 1913

Location: Bucharest, Romania

Parties: Bulgaria; Romania, Serbia, Greece and Montenegro

Context: End of the Second Balkan War

The Treaty of Bucharest was concluded on 10 August 1913 by the delegates of Bulgaria, Romania, Serbia, Greece and Montenegro, ending the Second Balkan War. Bulgaria, attacked by its former allies and by Romania and the Ottoman Empire after it had moved against Serbia and Greece over the division of Macedonia, was forced to accept a settlement that stripped it of most of its gains from the First Balkan War.

Terms

Bulgaria ceded Southern Dobruja to Romania, up to a line from Tutrakan to Balchik; recognised Serbian gains in northern and central Macedonia, keeping only a portion of eastern Macedonia including Strumica; and accepted Greek possession of southern Macedonia, the Aegean coast including Kavala, and Crete. Bulgaria retained a short Aegean coastline at Western Thrace with the port of Dedeagach (Alexandroupoli), and its army was to demobilise. The Ottoman reconquest of Edirne was dealt with separately by the Treaty of Constantinople later that year.

Consequences

The treaty left Bulgaria deeply revisionist and embittered — described by observers as a national catastrophe. Its determination to recover Macedonia and Dobruja drove Bulgaria into the Central Powers camp in 1915 during the First World War. One diplomat present, surveying the settlement, remarked that Europe would not have peace for long; the Balkan settlement of 1913 proved unstable, and within eleven months the assassination at Sarajevo plunged the continent into general war.
