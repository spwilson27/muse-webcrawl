Wolfenstein 3D

Wolfenstein 3D is a first-person shooter video game developed by id Software and published by Apogee Software, released on 5 May 1992 for MS-DOS. It is widely credited with popularising the first-person shooter genre and establishing the template followed by Doom the following year.

Gameplay

The player controls Allied spy William "B.J." Blazkowicz, navigating maze-like levels of a Nazi fortress, fighting guards, dogs, SS troopers and mutant enemies with a knife, pistol and machine guns. Each episode ends with a boss fight. Floors use ray casting to render a pseudo-3D environment at high speed on contemporary PCs.

Development

Designed principally by John Romero, John Carmack and Tom Hall, the game drew inspiration from the 1981 game Castle Wolfenstein by Muse Software. Carmack's engine rendered textured walls in real time, a major technical advance.

Release and reception

Distributed as shareware, the first episode was free and the full version sold by mail order. The game sold an estimated 200,000 copies by the end of 1993 and received critical acclaim for its speed and action. It was controversial in Germany for its Nazi imagery and was initially indexed and effectively banned from public sale there.

Legacy

Wolfenstein 3D spawned the prequel Spear of Destiny, numerous ports, and the long-running Wolfenstein series, including Return to Castle Wolfenstein (2001) and MachineGames' reboot series beginning with The New Order (2014).
