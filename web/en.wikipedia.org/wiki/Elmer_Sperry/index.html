Elmer Ambrose Sperry

Elmer Ambrose Sperry Sr. (October 12, 1860 – June 16, 1930) was an American inventor and entrepreneur, most famous for having co-invented, with Herman Anschütz-Kaempfe, the gyrocompass and as the founder of the Sperry Gyroscope Company. He is known as the "father of modern navigation technology."

Early life

Sperry was born in Cincinnatus, New York, the son of Stephen Decatur Sperry and Mary Burst. He attended Cornell University briefly and soon embarked on a career in electrical engineering, working on arc lighting, dynamos, and electric mining machinery.

Gyroscopic inventions

Sperry's greatest contributions involved applying the gyroscope to practical problems. He developed a gyrocompass that, unlike a magnetic compass, points to true north, and gyroscopic stabilizers for ships and aircraft. His companies supplied equipment to navies around the world during the First World War.

In 1914, his son Lawrence Sperry demonstrated an automatic pilot for aircraft in France, a landmark in aviation history. Sperry also developed the first ball-and-race-milled gyroscopic turn and bank indicator and early fire-control systems.

Legacy

Sperry held over 400 patents. The Elmer A. Sperry Award for advancing engineering in transportation is named in his honor. His company eventually became part of Sperry Rand and later Unisys.
