5.45×39mm

The 5.45×39mm cartridge, standard issue for the AK-74 family.

The 5.45×39mm is an intermediate rifle cartridge developed by the Soviet Union and introduced into service in 1974 together with the AK-74 assault rifle. Inspired in part by the United States' 5.56×45mm round, it replaced the heavier 7.62×39mm as the Warsaw Pact's standard rifle cartridge and remains the principal service cartridge of Russia and several other states.

Design

The cartridge fires a slender 5.6 mm (nominal 5.45 mm) boat-tail bullet of about 3.4 grams at a muzzle velocity near 900 m/s. The standard 7N6 ball projectile has a steel core and a small hollow cavity in its nose beneath the jacket, which shifts the center of gravity rearward and promotes early yaw in tissue — a behavior that led to reports of unusually severe wounds during the Soviet–Afghan War and to the nickname "poison bullet" among the mujahideen. The bottle-necked, rimless steel case is 39.8 mm long with a more pronounced taper than 5.56×45mm, aiding reliable extraction in automatic weapons.

Performance and use

Compared with 7.62×39mm, the 5.45 mm round is lighter — soldiers can carry substantially more ammunition — shoots flatter, and produces less recoil, improving controllability in automatic fire. Its effective range is about 500 meters. Besides the AK-74 and its derivatives (AKS-74, AK-74M, AK-12), it is chambered in weapons such as the AN-94, RPK-74, and Bulgarian and other licensed variants, and in some civilian rifles.
