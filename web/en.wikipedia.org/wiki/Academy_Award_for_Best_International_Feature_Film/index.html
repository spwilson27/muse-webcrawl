Academy Award for Best International Feature Film

The Academy Award for Best International Feature Film (known as Best Foreign Language Film until 2019) is presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to a feature film produced outside the United States with predominantly non-English dialogue. Each eligible country may submit one film for consideration through an officially approved organization.

History

Foreign-language films were recognized with Special/Honorary Awards from 1947, beginning with Italy's Shoeshine (1946); recipients included Bicycle Thieves, Rashomon, and La Strada. The category became competitive at the 29th Academy Awards for 1956, when Federico Fellini's La Strada won for Italy. Italy and France have dominated the category historically, with Italian films winning 14 times and French films 12 times among competitive awards.

In April 2019 the Academy renamed the category Best International Feature Film and widened eligibility discussion, though the rule requiring predominantly non-English dialogue remains. The award is accepted by the film's director, but it is officially credited to the submitting country.

Notable winners

  • 1956: La Strada (Italy)
  • 1958: Mon Oncle (France)
  • 1963: 8½ (Italy)
  • 1972: The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie (France)
  • 1988: Pelle the Conqueror (Denmark)
  • 1999: All About My Mother (Spain)
  • 2000: Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (Taiwan)
  • 2006: The Lives of Others (Germany)
  • 2009: The Secret in Their Eyes (Argentina)
  • 2011: A Separation (Iran)
  • 2018: Roma (Mexico)
  • 2019: Parasite (South Korea) — also won Best Picture
  • 2023: The Zone of Interest (United Kingdom)
