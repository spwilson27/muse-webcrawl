Lithic flake

In archaeology, a lithic flake is a "portion of rock removed from an objective piece by percussion or pressure," typically during the manufacture of stone tools. Flakes are the basic unit of lithic analysis, and their characteristics allow archaeologists to reconstruct the techniques used by prehistoric knappers.

PeriodPalaeolithic onwards

Characteristics

A complete flake exhibits several diagnostic features on its ventral (inner) surface: the striking platform where the blow landed, the bulb of percussion, eraillure scars, and ripple marks radiating from the point of impact. The dorsal (outer) surface may retain cortex (the weathered rind of the original nodule) or scars from previous flake removals.

Production

Flakes are detached from a core using a hammerstone (hard percussion), an antler or bone billet (soft percussion), or by pressure with a pointed tool. Controlled flaking relies on the conchoidal fracture properties of fine-grained, cryptocrystalline rocks such as flint and chert, and of volcanic glass such as obsidian.

Use

Some flakes were used directly as cutting or scraping tools with little further modification; others were retouched into formal tool types such as scrapers, points and burins. Unused flakes and shatter are classed as debitage.
