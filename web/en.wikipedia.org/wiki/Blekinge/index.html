Blekinge

Blekinge is a traditional province (landskap) in southern Sweden, on the Baltic coast. It is Sweden's second-smallest province by area, covering about 3,000 km², and is known as "the garden of Sweden" for its mild climate, oak forests and archipelago. The provincial capital is Karlskrona, founded in 1680 as Sweden's principal naval base.

History

Blekinge belonged to Denmark until the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658, when it was ceded to Sweden along with Scania and Halland. To secure the new southern coast, Charles XI founded Karlskrona in 1680 and moved the main Swedish naval base there from Stockholm; its naval port was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1998. Ronneby, one of the province's older towns, was a noted spa in the 19th century.

Geography

The province has a coastline of sheltered bays and an archipelago of rocky islands, while the interior is forested with lakes, rising toward the Småland highlands. The Mörrum River (Mörrumsån) is famous for salmon and sea-trout fishing.

Culture

Blekinge's dialect is transitional between Scanian and standard Swedish. The province is associated with the folk costume tradition, the writer Selma Lagerlöf set part of The Wonderful Adventures of Nils in its landscape, and the Sweden Rock Festival is held at Sölvesborg. Modern Karlskrona hosts the Swedish Coast Guard headquarters, a naval base, and a technology sector centred on the Blekinge Institute of Technology.
