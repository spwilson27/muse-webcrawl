Ballet school

Young students at the barre during a ballet class.

A ballet school is an institution that teaches classical ballet technique, ranging from neighbourhood studios offering recreational classes for children and adults to the great vocational schools attached to national ballet companies, which train students full-time for professional careers.

Training

Ballet training traditionally begins in childhood, often around the age of seven or eight, with full-time vocational training commonly starting between the ages of ten and twelve. Instruction centres on the daily class, which progresses from exercises at the barre to centre work, allegro (jumping steps), and, for female students, pointe work. Major systems of pedagogy include the Russian Vaganova method, the French school, the Italian Cecchetti method, the Danish Bournonville tradition, the English Royal Academy of Dance syllabus, and the Balanchine technique taught at the School of American Ballet.

Vocational schools

Many leading companies maintain their own schools, which serve as the principal pipeline of dancers into the company. Examples include the Paris Opera Ballet School (founded 1713, among the oldest), the Vaganova Academy of Russian Ballet in St Petersburg, the Bolshoi Ballet Academy in Moscow, The Royal Ballet School in London, the School of American Ballet in New York, the Hamburg Ballet School, the Cuban National Ballet School, and the Royal Danish Ballet School.

Examinations and accreditation

Recreational schools frequently follow examination syllabuses offered by bodies such as the Royal Academy of Dance, the Imperial Society of Teachers of Dancing, and the Cecchetti Council, which grade students through progressive levels. Independent vocational schools may combine dance training with academic education and residential boarding.
