40 Gigabit Ethernet

40 Gigabit Ethernet (40GbE) and 100 Gigabit Ethernet (100GbE) are groups of computer networking technologies for transmitting Ethernet frames at speeds of 40 and 100 gigabits per second (Gbit/s), respectively. These technologies are defined by the IEEE 802.3ba-2010 standard and later amendments.

Development

The standards were developed in response to the bandwidth demands of data centres, internet exchanges, and high-performance computing. 40GbE was aimed at server and aggregation links, while 100GbE targeted network backbones. Both operate only in full-duplex mode over fibre-optic cabling and specialized twinaxial copper assemblies.

Physical layers

Common 40GbE interfaces include QSFP+ transceivers operating as four parallel 10 Gbit/s lanes, either multimode (40GBASE-SR4) or single-mode (40GBASE-LR4). The standards support breakout configurations allowing a 40 Gbit/s port to operate as four independent 10 Gbit/s links.
