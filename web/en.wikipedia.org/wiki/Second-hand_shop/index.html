Second-hand shop

A second-hand shop is a shop that sells used goods — items previously owned by another person — typically at a substantial discount from their original price. Second-hand shops range from charity shops and thrift stores to consignment boutiques, antique shops, pawnshops, and specialized resellers of books, records, clothing, and electronics.

Types

Charity shops and thrift stores

Charity shops, common in the United Kingdom, and thrift stores in North America, sell donated goods to raise funds for charitable organizations. Large operators include Oxfam, the British Heart Foundation, Goodwill Industries, and the Salvation Army. Donations are often tax-deductible for donors, and shops may be staffed largely by volunteers.

Consignment and buy-sell-trade

Consignment shops sell goods on behalf of their owners and take a percentage of the sale price. Other stores buy used goods outright for resale, as with used bookstores and record shops.

History and growth

Trade in used clothing and household goods is centuries old, conducted historically by peddlers and at rag fairs. Organized charity retailing grew in the late 19th and 20th centuries. The resale market expanded rapidly in the 21st century with online platforms such as eBay, Depop, and Vinted, and with growing consumer interest in sustainability, vintage fashion, and frugality. Industry reports project the global second-hand apparel market to reach hundreds of billions of dollars by the late 2020s.

Environmental aspects

Buying second-hand extends product life cycles and reduces demand for new manufacture, though critics note that cheap used goods can still encourage overconsumption, and unsold donations may be exported or landfilled.
