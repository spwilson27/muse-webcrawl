Battle of Ctesiphon (363)

The Battle of Ctesiphon took place in 363 outside the Sasanian capital of Ctesiphon in Mesopotamia, during the Emperor Julian's Persian expedition. Roman forces defeated a Sasanian army outside the city walls, but could not capture Ctesiphon itself.

Background

Julian invaded Persia in 363 with a large army, advancing down the Euphrates and then the Tigris, hoping to force a decisive settlement or even take the capital. A fleet accompanied the army with supplies.

Battle

Before Ctesiphon the Romans encountered a Persian force drawn up across a canal. Despite Sasanian cavalry and elephants, Roman infantry and cavalry drove the Persians back into the city. Sources, notably Ammianus Marcellinus, who was present, report low Roman losses.

Aftermath

Without siege equipment sufficient for Ctesiphon's walls and with a second Persian army approaching, Julian burned his fleet and marched north along the Tigris. Harassed constantly, he was mortally wounded at the Battle of Samarra. His successor Jovian signed a humiliating peace ceding Roman territory in Mesopotamia.
