The Interpretation of Dreams (German: Die Traumdeutung) is an 1899 book by the Austrian neurologist Sigmund Freud, the founder of psychoanalysis, in which he introduces his theory of the unconscious as it relates to dream interpretation. Freud called dreams "the royal road to a knowledge of the unconscious activities of the mind". The book, postdated 1900 by its publisher, sold slowly at first but went through eight editions in Freud's lifetime and became the founding text of psychoanalysis.

Theory

Freud argues that dreams are disguised fulfilments of repressed wishes, typically infantile and sexual in origin. He distinguishes the manifest content of the dream from its latent dream-thoughts, which the "dream-work" transforms through condensation, displacement, considerations of representability and secondary revision, evading the mind's internal censorship. His method of interpretation relies on the dreamer's free associations to each element of the dream and is illustrated with the famous analysis of his own "Irma injection" dream of July 1895. The book also contains the first full presentation of the Oedipus complex and of Freud's topography of the psyche.

Reception

Although many of its specific claims are now scientifically contested, the book transformed psychology's vocabulary and twentieth-century culture, influencing literature, art (notably Surrealism), and everyday language.
