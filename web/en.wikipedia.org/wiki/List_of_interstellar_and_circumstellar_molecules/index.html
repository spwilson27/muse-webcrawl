List of interstellar and circumstellar molecules

This is a list of molecules detected in the interstellar medium and circumstellar envelopes by astronomical spectroscopy. More than 300 distinct molecular species have been identified, ranging from simple diatomic molecules to complex organic molecules with more than a dozen atoms. Most are detected through their rotational transitions at millimetre and submillimetre wavelengths, observed with radio telescopes; others are found through infrared absorption or emission features.

The first interstellar molecules — CH, CH+ and CN — were detected optically in the late 1930s and early 1940s. The field expanded rapidly after 1968 with the discoveries of ammonia (NH3), water (H2O) and formaldehyde (H2CO), the first polyatomic organic molecule found in space.

Four-atom and larger molecules

Four-atom species include ammonia (NH3), formaldehyde (H2CO), thioformaldehyde (H2CS), acetylene (C2H2), methane (CH4) and the cyclopropenylidene ring (c-C3H2). Larger detections include methanol (CH3OH), ethanol (C2H5OH), dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3), ethyl formate, acetonitrile (CH3CN), benzene (C6H6), propylene, vinyl cyanide, and the amino acid precursor aminoacetonitrile. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are inferred from infrared emission bands, and the fullerene C60 and its cation C60+ were detected in the 2010s; C60+ is a confirmed carrier of several diffuse interstellar bands.

The largest confirmed interstellar molecules include cyanopolyynes such as HC11N and complex organics in hot cores and corinos such as Sagittarius B2 and IRAS 16293-2422.
