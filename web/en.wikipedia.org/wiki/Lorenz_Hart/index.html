Lorenz Hart

Lorenz Milton Hart (May 2, 1895 – November 22, 1943) was an American lyricist and, with composer Richard Rodgers, half of the songwriting team Rodgers and Hart. Their musical comedies and film songs produced standards such as "My Funny Valentine", "The Lady Is a Tramp", "Bewitched, Bothered and Bewildered", "Blue Moon", and "Manhattan". Hart's lyrics are celebrated for their wit, internal rhyme, and bittersweet blend of sophistication and melancholy.

Career

Hart met Rodgers in 1919 through a mutual friend; their first success came with the revue Garrick Gaieties (1925), featuring "Manhattan". Through the 1920s and 1930s they wrote a string of Broadway and London hits — The Girl Friend, A Connecticut Yankee (with "My Heart Stood Still" and "Thou Swell"), On Your Toes, Babes in Arms, The Boys from Syracuse, and Pal Joey (1940), whose cynical anti-hero and songs like "I Could Write a Book" marked a new maturity in the American musical. In Hollywood they wrote film scores including Love Me Tonight (1932), with "Isn't It Romantic?" and "Lover".

Style

Hart combined dazzling verbal technique — multisyllabic rhymes, puns, and literary allusions — with an undercurrent of loneliness, visible in lyrics like "Spring is here, why doesn't my heart go dancing?" His work is often contrasted with the sunnier style of Rodgers's later partner, Oscar Hammerstein II.

Personal life and death

Hart struggled with alcoholism and depression; his unreliability contributed to Rodgers's decision to work with Hammerstein on Oklahoma! (1943). Hart declined to write that show but worked with Rodgers on a revival of A Connecticut Yankee in 1943, contributing the elegiac "To Keep My Love Alive". He died of pneumonia on November 22, 1943, shortly after the revival opened.
