Lifeguard

A lifeguard is a rescuer who supervises the safety and rescue of swimmers, surfers, and other water sports participants at places such as swimming pools, water parks, beaches, and lakes. Lifeguards are trained in water rescue, first aid, and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), and they work to prevent accidents by enforcing safety rules and watching for swimmers in difficulty.

Duties

The primary duty of a lifeguard is prevention: scanning the water systematically, recognising distress — often signalled by the instinctive drowning response rather than calls for help — and intervening before an emergency develops. When a rescue is required, lifeguards use equipment such as rescue tubes, rescue buoys, and paddleboards, and provide first aid, including CPR and, where available, automated external defibrillators (AEDs). They may also monitor weather, water quality, and dangerous marine life, and designate safe swimming areas with flags.

Training and certification

Lifeguards are typically certified by national bodies such as the American Red Cross, the Royal Life Saving Society in Commonwealth countries, or national surf life saving associations, as in Australia, where volunteer surf lifesavers patrol beaches. Certification generally requires minimum swimming proficiency, rescue skills, and current qualifications in first aid and CPR, renewed periodically.

History

Organised lifesaving developed in the nineteenth century with the growth of seaside recreation; life saving societies were founded in Britain, the United States, and Australia, and beach patrols with distinctive uniforms and equipment — such as the lifeguard tower and the red-and-yellow flagged patrol area — became familiar features of bathing beaches worldwide.
