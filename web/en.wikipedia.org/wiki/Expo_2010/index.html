Expo 2010, officially the Expo 2010 Shanghai China, was a universal world exposition held on both banks of the Huangpu River in Shanghai, China, from 1 May to 31 October 2010. Its theme, "Better City, Better Life", addressed the challenges and opportunities of urbanisation in the twenty-first century. It was the first world exposition held in China and the largest in history by attendance and site area.

Preparations

Shanghai won the bid to host the exposition in December 2002, defeating bids from Russia, South Korea, Poland and Mexico. The Chinese government invested heavily in the event, clearing a 5.28 square kilometre site in the Nanpu and Lupu bridge districts, relocating factories and residents, and extending the Shanghai Metro to serve the site. Total expenditure is generally estimated to have exceeded that of the Beijing 2008 Summer Olympics.

The fair

A record 192 countries and 50 international organisations participated. The centrepiece was the China Pavilion, a 63-metre inverted-pyramid structure in vermilion red inspired by the traditional dougong bracket, which housed a celebrated animated version of the Qing-dynasty scroll painting Along the River During the Qingming Festival. Other noted pavilions included the Seed Cathedral of the United Kingdom, the woven-basket pavilion of Spain and the ice-cube pavilion of Finland. The Expo Axis, a kilometre-long boulevard beneath a vast membrane canopy, organised the site.

Attendance reached 73 million visits, with a single-day record of more than one million visitors on 16 October 2010 — both world exposition records. The mascot, Haibao, a blue figure resembling the Chinese character for "person", appeared throughout the city.

Legacy

Following the exposition, most national pavilions were demolished as required by BIE rules. The China Pavilion was converted into the China Art Museum, Shanghai, and the Expo Axis and several other structures were retained as part of a cultural and commercial district. The event reinforced Shanghai's standing as a global city and is widely regarded as a showcase of China's economic rise.
