Form W-4

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Form W-4, officially the Employee's Withholding Certificate, is an Internal Revenue Service (IRS) tax form completed by employees in the United States to indicate their tax situation to their employer. The information provided determines the amount of federal income tax to withhold from the employee's pay.

Purpose

Employees generally complete a W-4 when starting a job and may file a new form when their personal or financial situation changes, such as marriage, the birth of a child, or taking a second job. The employer uses the form together with IRS withholding tables to calculate payroll withholding.

2020 redesign

The form was substantially redesigned for 2020 following the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, which suspended personal exemptions. The new form eliminated withholding "allowances" and instead asks about multiple jobs, dependents, and other income and deductions, aiming to improve the accuracy of withholding.

Related forms

Form W-4 is related to Form W-2, the annual wage and tax statement an employer sends to employees and the IRS, and Form W-9, used for independent contractors.
