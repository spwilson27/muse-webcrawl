Triveni Sangam

The confluence of the Ganges and Yamuna at Prayagraj

In Hindu tradition, a Triveni Sangam is the confluence (sangam) of three rivers (triveni, "three strands"). The most famous and sacred is at Prayagraj (formerly Allahabad), where the Ganges and the Yamuna meet, together with the mythical, subterranean Sarasvati river.

The Prayagraj Sangam

At Prayagraj the muddy waters of the Ganges and the greener waters of the Yamuna can be seen to merge; the Sarasvati is believed to flow unseen beneath them. Bathing at the Sangam is considered highly meritorious, believed to wash away sins. It is the site of the Kumbh Mela, the largest religious gathering in the world, held in rotation among four river-bank sites, with Prayagraj's festival drawing tens of millions of pilgrims.

Other Triveni Sangams

Several other confluences in India are styled Triveni Sangam, including those at Bhagamandala in Karnataka (Kaveri, Kannike and the mythical Sujyoti), at Tirumakudalu Narasipura, and at Erode in Tamil Nadu, where the Kaveri meets the Bhavani and the unseen Amudha river.
