The Seven against Thebes were seven champions in Greek mythology who made war on Thebes. They were chosen to lead an army raised by Polynices, who had been denied his rightful share of the rule of Thebes by his brother Eteocles. The expedition ended in disaster: all seven champions died, and the brothers killed each other. Their story formed part of the Theban cycle of epic poetry and was dramatised by Aeschylus in his tragedy Seven Against Thebes (467 BC).

The myth

After the banishment of Oedipus, his sons Eteocles and Polynices agreed to rule Thebes in alternate years. Eteocles refused to surrender the throne at the end of his term, and Polynices fled to Argos, where King Adrastus gave him his daughter in marriage and promised to restore him. Adrastus raised an army commanded by seven champions, each assigned to attack one of Thebes' seven gates.

The seven champions

Lists vary between sources, but the canonical seven are: Adrastus (king of Argos, the sole survivor), Amphiaraus (a seer who foresaw his own death), Capaneus (who boasted not even Zeus could stop him and was struck by lightning), Hippomedon, Parthenopaeus, Tydeus, and Polynices himself. Eteocles defended the seventh gate against his brother, and the two killed one another.

Aftermath

Ten years later, the sons of the Seven — the Epigoni — marched on Thebes again and sacked the city. The refusal of Creon to allow burial of the Argive dead, and Antigone's defiance of that order, form the subject of Sophocles' Antigone.
