Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation may refer to several distinct concepts. This article outlines the principal uses of the term; for specific topics, see the linked articles.

Health

In medicine, rehabilitation is the process of restoring health, functioning and quality of life for people with disabilities or health conditions. It includes physical rehabilitation after injury, stroke or surgery; psychiatric rehabilitation; cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation; and drug rehabilitation for substance dependence. Rehabilitation is delivered by multidisciplinary teams including physicians, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech-language therapists and psychologists. The World Health Organization defines rehabilitation as "a set of interventions designed to optimize functioning and reduce disability in individuals with health conditions in interaction with their environment."

Criminal justice

In penology, rehabilitation is the process of reforming an offender so that they can re-enter society and refrain from further crime, as distinct from deterrence, incapacitation or retribution. Programmes may include education, vocational training, therapy and restorative justice.

Other uses

  • Land rehabilitation – restoring degraded land, such as mine sites, to a useful state.
  • Building rehabilitation – renovating a building while retaining its historic character.
  • Wildlife rehabilitation – care of injured or orphaned wild animals with the aim of release.
  • Political rehabilitation – the restoration of a person's reputation and rights after persecution, as with victims of purges.
