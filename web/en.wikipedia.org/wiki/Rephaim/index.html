Rephaim


Illustration placeholder: Rujm el-Hiri, sometimes associated with the "circle of the Rephaim"

The Rephaim (Hebrew: רפאים, rĕpāʾîm) are a people or group of peoples mentioned in the Hebrew Bible and in ancient Ugaritic and Phoenician texts. In the Hebrew Bible the term has two distinct usages: it refers in some passages to a legendary race of giants or mighty ancient inhabitants of Canaan, and in others to the shades or spirits of the dead in Sheol.

In the Hebrew Bible

Ancient inhabitants

Genesis 14 lists the Rephaim among the peoples defeated by Chedorlaomer at Ashteroth-Karnaim. Deuteronomy 2–3 recalls that the land east of the Jordan was formerly inhabited by Rephaim — the Emim, Zamzummim, and Anakim are counted as Rephaim — and that Og, king of Bashan, was "of the remnant of the Rephaim", whose iron bedstead was over thirteen feet long. Joshua 12–13 speaks of the Valley of Rephaim south-west of Jerusalem, a lowland also named in the David narratives (2 Samuel 5) as a Philistine battleground.

The dead

In poetic and prophetic texts — Isaiah 14:9 ("the shades [Rephaim] below are excited to meet you"), Isaiah 26:14, Psalm 88:10, Proverbs, and Job 26:5 — the Rephaim are the inhabitants of Sheol, the shadowy dead, sometimes described as former kings and warriors now powerless.

Ugaritic texts

Ugaritic literature of the Late Bronze Age (c. 14th–13th centuries BC) knows the rpum (Rapa'ūma) as deified royal ancestors, honoured with funerary meals; the "Rephaim texts" (KTU 1.20–1.22) describe them journeying to a banquet. This evidence suggests the two biblical usages derive from a single older conception of honoured ancestral dead, later recast in Israelite tradition as a race of pre-Israelite giants and as ordinary shades.

Interpretations

Scholars debate whether the Rephaim were thought of as a historical ethnic group, as heroic semi-divine ancestors akin to the Greek heroes, or as a purely literary survival. They are often compared with the Nephilim of Genesis 6. The name survives geographically in the Valley of Rephaim (Emek Refa'im) in modern Jerusalem.
