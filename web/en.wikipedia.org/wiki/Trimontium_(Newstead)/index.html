Trimontium (Newstead)

Trimontium was a Roman fort complex at Newstead, near Melrose, in the Scottish Borders — one of the largest and most important Roman sites in Scotland. Its name, "the place of the three hills," refers to the triple peaks of the nearby Eildon Hills. The fort stood on Dere Street, the main Roman road into Scotland, at a crossing of the River Tweed.

History

Trimontium was founded around AD 79–80 during Agricola's campaigns and was occupied, with interruptions, until the early 3rd century. Successive forts were built on the site — Flavian, Antonine and Severan phases are distinguished — together with extensive annexes, a civilian settlement (vicus), an amphitheatre (the northernmost known in the Roman Empire) and temporary marching camps nearby. At its height the complex covered over 30 hectares and garrisoned cavalry units, including the prestigious ala Vocontiorum.

Excavations

Major excavations by James Curle between 1905 and 1910 recovered an exceptional collection of finds, including ceremonial cavalry helmets and masks, armour, tools, and the famous rubbish pits whose contents illuminate daily life on Rome's northernmost frontier. Curle's publication A Roman Frontier Post and its People (1911) remains a classic. The site is interpreted today at the Trimontium Museum in Melrose.
