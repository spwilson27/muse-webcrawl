The United States Army Futures Command (AFC) is a United States Army command responsible for modernizing the force: developing future concepts, requirements, and capabilities. Activated on 24 August 2018 and headquartered in downtown Austin, Texas—deliberately located near universities and the technology industry rather than on a traditional post—it was the Army's first new four-star headquarters established in decades.

Organization and priorities

AFC consolidated the Army's modernization enterprise, absorbing the Army Capabilities Integration Center and, in 2019, the Research, Development and Engineering Command (renamed the Combat Capabilities Development Command, DEVCOM), as well as the Army's research laboratories. Its work is organized around cross-functional teams pursuing priority capability areas, originally six: long-range precision fires; next-generation combat vehicles; future vertical lift; the Army network; air and missile defense; and soldier lethality, later supplemented by synthetic training environment and assured positioning, navigation, and timing efforts.

Role

The command runs experimentation campaigns such as Project Convergence, linking sensors, shooters, and artificial intelligence tools across services. Its leaders have framed its mission as ensuring the Army can fight and win in multi-domain operations against peer adversaries. In 2025 the Army announced a further reorganization—merging Futures Command with Training and Doctrine Command into a new Transformation and Training Command—as part of a broader Army Transformation Initiative.
