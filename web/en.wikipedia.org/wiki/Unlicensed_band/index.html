Unlicensed band

An unlicensed band is a portion of the radio spectrum in which users may operate radio equipment without obtaining an individual station license, provided the equipment complies with technical rules such as maximum transmit power, duty cycle, and certification requirements. Regulators instead license the band "by rule," so any compliant device may be used.

Uses

Unlicensed bands underpin much of modern consumer wireless technology. The industrial, scientific and medical (ISM) bands at 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz host Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, cordless phones, and microwave ovens. Other unlicensed spectrum supports garage door openers, baby monitors, RFID readers, keyless-entry fobs, and wireless microphones, and in the United States rules such as Part 15 of the FCC regulations govern the devices.

Regulation and trade-offs

Because no user has exclusive rights, unlicensed devices must accept interference from other users and are usually limited to low power and short range. In exchange, unlicensed access dramatically lowers barriers to innovation, allowing new technologies to be deployed without spectrum auctions or licensing delays. Some bands, such as the Citizens Broadcast Radio Service at 3.5 GHz in the United States, use tiered or database-coordinated sharing between licensed and unlicensed users.
