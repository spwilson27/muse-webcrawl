Dax (French pronunciation: [daks]; Gascon: Dacs) is a commune in the Landes department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It is a sub-prefecture of the department and one of France's best-known spa towns, famed since Roman times for its hot springs and therapeutic mud.

The Fontaine Chaude, built over the town's Roman-era hot spring.

History

Known in Latin as Aquae Tarbellicae, the town was a watering place of the Tarbelli tribe and then of Roman Gaul, who prized its spring, whose water emerges at about 64 °C. The medieval town was fortified, and its cathedral of Notre-Dame dates largely from the 17th century after an earlier Gothic building collapsed. Dax's thermal industry developed strongly in the 19th century, and the town remains a leading centre of pelotherapy — treatment with mineral mud from the Adour river.

Geography and culture

Dax lies on the river Adour between Bayonne and Bordeaux. Its August festival, the Féria de Dax, draws hundreds of thousands of visitors for bullfighting, music and street celebrations in the Landes tradition. The town's rugby union club, US Dax, is one of the historic clubs of French rugby.
