J. F. C. Fuller

Major-General John Frederick Charles Fuller CB CBE DSO (1 September 1878 – 10 February 1966) was a senior British Army officer, military historian, and strategist, noted as an early theorist of modern armoured warfare. He developed Plan 1919, a proposal for a decisive tank offensive, and influenced the development of mechanised warfare in several countries.

Career

Fuller served in the Boer War and the First World War, joining the Tank Corps and becoming its chief of staff. His plan for the Battle of Cambrai (1917) helped achieve the first mass tank attack. After the war he lectured at staff colleges and wrote extensively on the future of war, arguing that tanks and aircraft would restore mobility and decision to battle.

Later life and writings

Frustrated by the Army's resistance to reform, Fuller retired in 1933. In the 1930s he became involved with Oswald Mosley's British Union of Fascists, a connection that damaged his reputation. He wrote more than forty books, including The Generalship of Ulysses S. Grant, A Military History of the Western World, and works on yoga and the occultist Aleister Crowley, with whom he had been associated in his youth.
