Buddhism in Vietnam (Vietnamese: Đạo Phật or Phật Giáo) is mainly of the Mahayana tradition and is one of the main religions of Vietnam. Buddhism arrived in Vietnam as early as the 3rd or 2nd century BCE via missionaries and merchants from India, and more substantially from China during the first millennium CE. Vietnamese Buddhism is generally syncretic, combining elements of Pure Land, Thiền (the Vietnamese form of Zen), and folk religious practices such as ancestor veneration.

History

Buddhism was firmly established under the Lý and Trần dynasties (11th–14th centuries), when it enjoyed royal patronage and deeply influenced Vietnamese culture, literature and architecture; the Trần era produced the uniquely Vietnamese Trúc Lâm school of Thiền. Under later dynasties Confucianism gained official precedence, but Buddhism remained the faith of the majority of the population. In the Mekong Delta, Khmer communities practise Theravada Buddhism, and the 20th century saw a distinct Theravada revival among ethnic Vietnamese.

Modern era

Buddhist organisations played a prominent political role in South Vietnam during the 1960s, notably in the 1963 Buddhist crisis protesting the policies of President Ngô Đình Diệm, during which the monk Thích Quảng Đức self-immolated in Saigon. After reunification in 1975, the state-sponsored Vietnam Buddhist Sangha was established in 1981; the independent Unified Buddhist Church of Vietnam remains unrecognised by the government. The teacher Thích Nhất Hạnh, founder of the Engaged Buddhism movement and the Plum Village tradition, became one of the world's best-known Buddhist figures.

Practice

Most Vietnamese Buddhists practise a devotion centred on the recitation of the name of Amitabha Buddha (Nam Mô A Di Đà Phật) and the bodhisattva of compassion, Quán Thế Âm (Avalokiteśvara), combined with meditation and observance of vegetarian days on the lunar calendar. Pagodas serve as centres of community life, and festivals such as Vesak (Phật Đản) and the Vu Lan (Ullambana) festival of filial piety are widely observed.
