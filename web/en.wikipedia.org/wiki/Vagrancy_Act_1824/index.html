Vagrancy Act 1824

The Vagrancy Act 1824 (5 Geo. 4. c. 83) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It criminalised a range of behaviours associated with poverty and homelessness, including rough sleeping and begging, and it remained substantially in force in England and Wales for nearly two centuries.

Provisions

The Act classified offenders as "idle and disorderly persons", "rogues and vagabonds", and "incorrigible rogues", with escalating penalties including imprisonment with hard labour. Section 4 made it an offence, among other things, to wander abroad and lodge in barns or unoccupied buildings without visible means of subsistence, to beg, and to be found sleeping outdoors. The Act also contained the notorious "sus" provision, under which a "suspected person or reputed thief" loitering with intent to commit an arrestable offence could be convicted — a provision repealed in 1981 after controversy over discriminatory use.

Repeal

After decades of campaigning by homelessness charities and parliamentarians, the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022 provided for the repeal of the Vagrancy Act in England and Wales, though commencement of the repeal was delayed while replacement measures were considered.
