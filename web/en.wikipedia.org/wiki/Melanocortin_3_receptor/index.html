Melanocortin 3 receptor

MC3R (melanocortin 3 receptor)

Gene: MC3R, chromosome 20q13.2 (human)

Family: G protein-coupled receptors

Ligands: α-, β-, γ-MSH, ACTH

The melanocortin 3 receptor (MC3R) is a G protein-coupled receptor that binds melanocortin peptides with a distinctive preference for γ-MSH in addition to α-MSH and ACTH. It is expressed in the brain (especially the hypothalamus), gut, placenta, heart, and immune cells.

Function

MC3R participates in the central regulation of energy homeostasis, complementary to MC4R: it modulates food intake timing, nutrient partitioning, feed efficiency, and the entrainment of circadian rhythms to feeding. It also has anti-inflammatory actions in peripheral tissues, with melanocortin peptides acting at MC3R reducing inflammation in models of arthritis and peritonitis.

Genetics

Mice lacking MC3R show increased fat mass and reduced lean mass with more efficient food use but are not hyperphagic, unlike MC4R-deficient mice. In humans, rare variants — including a fully inactivating variant reported in 2021 — are associated with later puberty, shorter stature, reduced lean mass, and increased adiposity, linking MC3R to growth and developmental timing as well as body composition.
