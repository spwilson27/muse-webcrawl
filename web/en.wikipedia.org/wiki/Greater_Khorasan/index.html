Greater Khorasan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Greater Khorasan (Middle Persian: Xwarāsān; Persian: خراسان بزرگ) is a historical eastern region in the Iranian Plateau in West and Central Asia that encompasses western and northern Afghanistan, northeastern Iran, the eastern parts of Central Asia (Turkmenistan) and parts of Tajikistan and Uzbekistan.

The name Khorasan derives from Middle Persian words meaning "where the sun arrives from" — the land of the sunrise, reflecting its position as the eastern frontier of the Persian world.

Geography

Historically, Greater Khorasan's boundaries were fluid, at times extending from the Caspian Sea in the west to the Hindu Kush and the borders of India in the east. Its great oasis cities — Nishapur, Merv, Herat, Balkh, Tus, and Samarkand and Bukhara in its wider definition — were major stops on the Silk Road.

History

Khorasan was a province of the Sasanian Empire and, after the Muslim conquest of Persia, one of the four quarters of the caliphate. Under the Abbasids its governors, the Tahirids, became effectively autonomous, and the region flourished under the Samanids as a centre of Persian learning. Khorasan was repeatedly devastated by invasions, most destructively by the Mongols in the 13th century, yet cities such as Herat revived under the Timurids.

Modern division

Today Greater Khorasan is divided among Iran (the provinces of Razavi Khorasan, North Khorasan, and South Khorasan), Afghanistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, and Tajikistan. The Iranian province of Khorasan was split into three provinces in 2004.
