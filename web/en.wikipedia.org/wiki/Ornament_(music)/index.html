Ornament (music)

Common Baroque ornaments: trill, mordent and turn.

In music, ornaments or embellishments are musical flourishes — typically rapid notes — that are not essential to carry the line of melody or harmony, but serve instead to decorate or "ornament" that line, provide additional rhythmic interest, or serve other purposes. Many ornaments are performed as fast notes around a central, main note.

Types

Common ornaments include the trill (rapid alternation with the note above), the mordent and inverted mordent, the turn (a figure circling the main note), the appoggiatura ("leaning note"), the acciaccatura (a "crushed" grace note), the slide or glissando, and the tremolo. In vocal music, ornaments such as melisma and coloratura passages serve similar decorative functions.

History

Ornamentation was central to Renaissance and Baroque performance practice, when singers and instrumentalists were expected to improvise divisions and graces, and composers published tables of ornaments — notably in French harpsichord music — specifying their realisation. The galant and classical styles used ornaments more sparingly and increasingly wrote them out as ordinary notes. Improvised ornamentation remains fundamental in jazz, blues, Indian classical music and traditional Irish music, where rolls, cuts and crans decorate the melody.
