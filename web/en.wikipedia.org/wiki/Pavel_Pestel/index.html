Pavel Ivanovich Pestel (Russian: Павел Иванович Пестель; 5 July 1793 – 25 July 1826) was a Russian revolutionary and an ideological leader of the Decembrist movement. A decorated officer of the Napoleonic Wars, he became the leading figure of the Decembrists' Southern Society and author of their most radical programme, Russkaya Pravda ("Russian Justice").

Life and military career

Pestel was born in Moscow to a family of German descent; his father served as governor-general of Siberia. Educated in Dresden and at the Corps of Pages in St Petersburg, he distinguished himself in the Patriotic War of 1812, fighting at Borodino, where he was seriously wounded, and in the foreign campaigns that followed. By his early thirties he had risen to the rank of colonel and commanded the Vyatka Infantry Regiment, gaining a reputation as a brilliant, severe, and ambitious officer.

Revolutionary activity

Disillusioned with autocracy and serfdom after witnessing constitutional government in western Europe, Pestel joined the first Decembrist secret societies and, after the dissolution of the Union of Welfare, founded and directed the Southern Society at Tulchin in Ukraine from 1821. His draft constitution, Russkaya Pravda, was the most radical Decembrist programme: it demanded the overthrow of the monarchy, the execution of the imperial family, a unitary republic ruled provisionally by a strong central government, universal male suffrage, the abolition of serfdom, and the partial redistribution of land, with half the country's land held as a public fund available to all citizens.

Arrest and execution

Betrayed by an informer, Pestel was arrested on 13 December 1825 — the day before the Decembrist uprising in St Petersburg — while the southern rising led by others collapsed within weeks. After interrogation in the Peter and Paul Fortress, he was sentenced to be quartered, commuted to hanging. He was executed on 25 July 1826 together with four fellow Decembrists: Pyotr Kakhovsky, Kondraty Ryleyev, Sergey Muravyov-Apostol, and Mikhail Bestuzhev-Ryumin.

Legacy

Pestel is remembered as the most systematic thinker of the Decembrist movement, and Russkaya Pravda remains a key document in the history of Russian revolutionary thought.
