Oriental turtle dove

Oriental turtle dove (Streptopelia orientalis).

The Oriental turtle dove or rufous turtle dove (Streptopelia orientalis) is a member of the bird family Columbidae (doves and pigeons). The species has a wide native distribution from Europe across Asia to Japan, with northern populations migrating south in winter.

Description

A medium-sized dove, roughly 30–35 cm long, it resembles the European turtle dove but is larger and more heavily built. The plumage is blue-grey above with rufous-scaled feathers on the back and wings, a pinkish breast, and a distinctive black-and-white barred patch on the side of the neck. The tail is wedge-shaped with white tips. Its call is a distinctive four-note "her-her, hu-hu" phrase, quite different from the purring of the European turtle dove.

Distribution and habitat

The species breeds in open woodland, farmland edges and gardens across Siberia, Central and East Asia, and parts of South Asia; the subspecies differ in size and in how migratory they are. It feeds mainly on seeds, grains, shoots and buds taken on the ground. In Japan it is a familiar bird of satoyama landscapes and is known as kijibato.

Taxonomy

Several subspecies are recognised, including S. o. orientalis in East Asia and S. o. meena in Central Asia; some authorities have proposed splitting the species into two.
