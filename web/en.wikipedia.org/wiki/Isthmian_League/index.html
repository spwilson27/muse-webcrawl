Isthmian League

The Isthmian League is a regional men's football league covering London, East and South East England, featuring mostly semi-professional clubs. It is one of the three leagues at the seventh and eighth levels of the English football league system, alongside the Northern Premier League and the Southern League.

History

The league was founded on 8 March 1905 by amateur clubs in the London area; its name derives from the Isthmian Games of ancient Greece, reflecting the amateur sporting ethos of its founders. For much of its history it was one of the leading amateur leagues in England, and it remained officially amateur until the Football Association abolished the amateur/professional distinction in 1974. In 1973 it became the first league outside the Football League to have a title sponsor.

Structure

The league currently consists of four divisions: the Premier Division (step 3, level 7), and Division One North, Division One South Central and Division One South East (step 4, level 8). The Premier Division champions are promoted to the National League South (or, depending on geography, the National League North), with play-off winners also promoted. The league has been sponsored under the name "Pitching In Isthmian League" in recent years.

Cup competitions

The league runs the Isthmian League Cup and other competitions. For many years its clubs also contested the FA Amateur Cup, and several Isthmian clubs have won the FA Trophy and reached notable rounds of the FA Cup.
