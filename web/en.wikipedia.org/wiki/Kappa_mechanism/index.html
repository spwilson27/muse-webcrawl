Kappa mechanism

FieldStellar astrophysics
Proposed byArthur Eddington (the "valve mechanism"); opacity role clarified by Sergei Zhevakin
DrivesCepheids, RR Lyrae variables, Delta Scuti and other pulsating stars
Key physicsOpacity (κ) rising on compression in partial ionisation zones

The kappa mechanism, also called the κ-mechanism or Eddington valve, is the process that drives the regular radial pulsations of variable stars such as Cepheids and RR Lyrae stars. It rests on the behaviour of stellar opacity — denoted κ (kappa) — in layers where hydrogen and helium are partially ionised.

How it works

In most stellar material, compression heats the gas and lowers its opacity, letting radiation escape and damping any pulsation. In a partial ionisation zone, however, compressional heating goes into further ionisation rather than raising the temperature, and the increased density raises the opacity: the layer traps heat when the star contracts, like a closed valve. Pressure builds until the star is pushed back outward; on expansion the gas recombines, opacity falls, the valve opens and heat escapes, and the cycle repeats. Arthur Eddington proposed the heat-engine picture; in the 1950s Sergei Zhevakin identified the second ionisation zone of helium as the critical layer in classical pulsators.

Applications

Because the location of the ionisation zones depends on temperature, the mechanism explains the instability strip on the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram within which stars pulsate, and underlies the period–luminosity relations used as cosmic distance indicators.
