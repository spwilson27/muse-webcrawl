Royal Small Arms Factory

Location: Enfield Lock, London, England
Founded: 1816
Closed: 1988
Products: Rifles, machine guns, small arms

The Royal Small Arms Factory (RSAF) was a British government-owned rifle factory at Enfield Lock in north London. It was the principal state manufactory of British military small arms for much of the 19th and 20th centuries, and gave its name to the "Enfield" family of weapons.

History

Production began in 1816 on a site chosen for its water power and access to the River Lea. The factory adopted American-style interchangeable manufacture in the 1850s. It produced the Pattern 1853 Enfield rifle-musket, the Martini–Henry, and the Lee–Enfield bolt-action rifles that armed British forces through both World Wars, as well as the Bren light machine gun and the L1A1 Self-Loading Rifle.

Closure

The factory was privatised as part of Royal Ordnance in 1987 and closed in 1988, with production moved to Nottingham. Much of the site was redeveloped as housing, known as Enfield Island Village, though some historic buildings survive.
