Crown Heights, Brooklyn

Crown Heights is a neighbourhood in the central portion of the borough of Brooklyn in New York City. It is bounded roughly by Washington Avenue to the west, Atlantic Avenue to the north, Ralph Avenue to the east and Empire Boulevard to the south. The area is known for its large Hasidic Jewish community — it hosts the world headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement at 770 Eastern Parkway — and its large Caribbean-American population.

History

The area, once partly farmland, developed rapidly after the extension of subway lines in the 1920s, becoming home to Jewish and later Caribbean immigrant communities. In August 1991 the neighbourhood was the scene of the Crown Heights riot, three days of unrest following a car accident that killed a seven-year-old Guyanese boy, Gavin Cato, and the subsequent stabbing of Yankel Rosenbaum.

Landmarks

Major institutions in or near Crown Heights include the Brooklyn Museum, the Brooklyn Botanic Garden, the Brooklyn Children's Museum and the main branch of the Brooklyn Public Library at Grand Army Plaza. Eastern Parkway hosts the annual West Indian Day Parade, one of New York's largest cultural festivals.
