Paquimé (Casas Grandes)
LocationCasas Grandes, Chihuahua, Mexico
CultureMogollon (Casas Grandes tradition)

StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1998)

Paquimé, also known as Casas Grandes, is a prehistoric archaeological site in the modern Mexican state of Chihuahua, near the modern town of Casas Grandes. It was the largest and most complex settlement of the Mogollon culture area, a major regional centre of the American Southwest and northern Mexico.

The site

During its Medio period florescence (about 1200–1450 AD), Paquimé contained multi-storey adobe apartment compounds of hundreds of rooms arranged around plazas, T-shaped doorways, an elaborate water-control system of reservoirs and stone-lined canals fed from the Casas Grandes River, ceremonial mounds, and Mesoamerican-style ball courts. Excavations directed by Charles C. Di Peso (1958–1961) uncovered evidence of craft production, turquoise working, copper bells, and pens interpreted as breeding aviaries for scarlet macaws traded from tropical Mesoamerica.

Decline and heritage

Paquimé was burned and abandoned around 1450 AD; its descendants include the modern settlements of the region, and its pottery tradition inspired the modern Mata Ortiz ceramic revival. The archaeological zone was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998.
