Mount Diablo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Diablo from the east, Contra Costa County, California.

Mount Diablo is a mountain of the Diablo Range in Contra Costa County, in the San Francisco Bay Area of Northern California, south of Clayton and northeast of Danville. Its summit rises to 3,849 feet (1,173 m). The mountain and its surrounding area are protected as Mount Diablo State Park.

Views

Although not especially high, Mount Diablo commands an exceptionally unobstructed panorama: on clear days the view from the summit is among the most extensive in the western United States, taking in the Sierra Nevada, Mount Lassen far to the north, the Farallon Islands in the Pacific, and 40 of California's 58 counties at least in part. The summit building, constructed in the 1930s, houses a museum and an aviation beacon — one of the "Eyes of Diablo" — which was lit after the attack on Pearl Harbor and is now relit each December 7 in remembrance, and annually on September 11.

Survey landmark

In 1851 the summit was chosen by Colonel Leander Ransom as the initial point for the Mount Diablo Base Line and Meridian, from which most land surveys in California and Nevada are still referenced. The mountain's name — "Devil's Mount" in Spanish — is traditionally explained by an 1805 incident in which a Native village escaped Spanish soldiers, an escape attributed to a spirit or "puy" appearing as the devil. In the mythology of the local Miwok peoples, Mount Diablo was the point of creation.
