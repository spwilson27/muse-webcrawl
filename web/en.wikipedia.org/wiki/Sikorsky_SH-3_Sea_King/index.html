The Sikorsky SH-3 Sea King (company designation S-61) is an American twin-engine anti-submarine warfare (ASW) helicopter designed and built by Sikorsky Aircraft. It was a landmark design — one of the first ASW helicopters to combine hunting and attack capabilities in a single airframe, and one of the first amphibious helicopters, with a boat hull and retractable floats.

Design and development

Developed in the late 1950s to replace the piston-engined HSS-1 Seabat, the Sea King's watertight hull allowed it to land on water, and its dipping sonar, search radar, and armament of torpedoes and depth charges made it a complete submarine hunter-killer. It first flew on 11 March 1959 and entered U.S. Navy service in June 1961.

Operational history

Sea Kings served in the Vietnam War (combat search and rescue and plane-guard duties), recovered Apollo program astronauts and capsules, and operated as the U.S. presidential transport "Marine One" (VH-3D). Variants included the HH-3A rescue, RH-3A minesweeping, and numerous export versions; license production included Agusta (Italy), Mitsubishi (Japan), and Westland (United Kingdom, as the Westland Sea King). U.S. Navy SH-3s were retired in 2006; the type remains in service with several navies.

Specifications (SH-3H)

  • Crew: 4; Length: 16.7 m; Rotor diameter: 18.9 m
  • Powerplant: 2 × General Electric T58-GE-10 turboshafts, 1,400 shp each
  • Maximum speed: 267 km/h; Range: about 1,005 km
  • Armament: Mk 44/46 torpedoes, depth charges, missiles in some variants
