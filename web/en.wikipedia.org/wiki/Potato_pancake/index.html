Potato pancake

Potato pancakes served with sour cream.

Potato pancakes are shallow-fried pancakes of grated or ground potato, matzo meal or flour, and a binding ingredient such as egg or applesauce, often flavoured with grated garlic or onion and seasonings. They are a traditional dish in many European and Jewish cuisines.

Regional varieties

  • Latkes — in Jewish cuisine, latkes are eaten especially during Hanukkah, fried in oil to commemorate the miracle of the oil in the Hanukkah story. They are typically served with sour cream or applesauce.
  • Draniki — the Belarusian version, a national dish of Belarus, also popular in Russia and Ukraine.
  • Reibekuchen / Kartoffelpuffer — German potato pancakes, commonly sold at Christmas markets and served with apple sauce.
  • Bramboráky — Czech pancakes flavoured with marjoram and garlic.
  • Placki ziemniaczane — Polish potato pancakes, sometimes served with goulash (placki po węgiersku).
  • Rösti — the Swiss dish of coarsely grated potato, usually formed into a larger cake without egg or flour.
  • Boxty — an Irish potato pancake combining grated raw and mashed potato.

Preparation

Raw potatoes are grated, excess liquid is squeezed out, and the mixture is combined with egg, flour or matzo meal, and seasoning, then spooned into hot oil and flattened into rounds, fried until crisp and golden on both edges.
