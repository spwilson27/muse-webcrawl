The Old Curiosity Shop

Author: Charles Dickens
Published: 1840–1841 (serial)
Genre: Novel

The Old Curiosity Shop is a novel by Charles Dickens, published serially in his weekly periodical Master Humphrey's Clock from 1840 to 1841 and in book form in 1841. It tells the story of Nell Trent — "Little Nell" — and her grandfather, keepers of a London shop of curiosities.

Plot

Driven into ruin by his secret gambling addiction, Nell's grandfather loses the shop to the grotesque moneylender Daniel Quilp. Grandfather and granddaughter flee London and wander the industrial Midlands, encountering kindly schoolmasters, waxwork showmen, and brutal furnace labour, while Quilp and the brass-souled Sampson Brass pursue them. The saintly Nell, worn down by the journey, dies in a quiet village — an ending that became one of the most famous scenes of Victorian sentimentality.

Reception

The serial's readership was enormous; legend has it that New York readers crowded the pier in 1841 shouting to incoming ships, "Is Little Nell dead?" Later critics, Oscar Wilde famously among them, mocked the death scene's pathos, yet the novel remains central to Dickens's early fame, and Quilp is ranked among his great villains.
