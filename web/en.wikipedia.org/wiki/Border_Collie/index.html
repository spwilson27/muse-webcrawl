The Border Collie is a British breed of herding dog of medium size. Widely considered the most intelligent dog breed, Border Collies were developed in the Anglo-Scottish border region for working livestock, especially sheep. They are famed for their intense stare (the "eye"), athleticism, energy, and trainability, and they excel at sheepdog trials and dog sports such as agility and flyball.

History

The breed descends from landrace collies of the British Isles. Most Border Collies trace to a single dog, Old Hemp (born 1893 in Northumberland), whose quiet, intense herding style became the breed standard; his descendants dominate pedigrees. The name "Border Collie" was adopted in 1915, and sheepdog trials under the International Sheep Dog Society (founded 1906) shaped the working type. Kennel Club recognition came much later (1976 in the UK), amid lasting debate between working and show breeding.

Characteristics

Border Collies are medium-sized, athletic dogs with rough (long) or smooth (short) double coats in many colours, most typically black with white markings. They gather stock using "eye" — a hypnotic, crouching stare — combined with stalking posture. Studies of canine cognition rank the breed at the top: the Border Collie Chaser learned the names of over 1,000 objects. Their drive and need for work make them demanding pets; under-stimulated dogs may develop destructive or obsessive behaviours such as shadow-chasing.

Health

Generally healthy and long-lived, the breed is predisposed to collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, epilepsy, and the MDR1 gene mutation affecting drug sensitivity. Trapped neutrophil syndrome and neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis occur in some lines and are managed by DNA testing.
