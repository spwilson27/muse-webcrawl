Overseas Chinese

Overseas Chinese (or overseas ethnic Chinese) are people of Chinese birth or ethnic descent who live outside the territories of the People's Republic of China and the Republic of China (Taiwan). The worldwide population is estimated at roughly 50 million, concentrated in Southeast Asia, North America, and Australasia, with significant communities on every continent.

History

Chinese emigration has a long history, from maritime traders in Southeast Asia to the labour migrations of the 19th century — miners, railway workers and merchants drawn to the Americas, Australia and Southeast Asian colonies during the California and Australian gold rushes and the building of transcontinental railways. Later waves included students, professionals and, after the late 1970s, emigrants from the PRC following economic reforms.

Communities

The largest populations are in Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, the United States, Singapore, Canada, the Philippines, Australia and Vietnam. Chinatowns in cities such as San Francisco, New York, London and Bangkok are visible centres of these communities. Overseas Chinese have played major roles in the commerce of Southeast Asia, and remittances and investment from the diaspora contributed significantly to China's economic development after 1978.
