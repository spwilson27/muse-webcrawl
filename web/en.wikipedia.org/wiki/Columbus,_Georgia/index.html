Columbus is a consolidated city-county in west-central Georgia, on the banks of the Chattahoochee River across from Phenix City, Alabama. It is the county seat of Muscogee County, with which it merged in 1971 — one of the first consolidated city-county governments in the United States. Columbus is the second-most populous city in Georgia outside the Atlanta metropolitan area.

History

Founded in 1828 by an act of the Georgia Legislature, Columbus was planned as a trading post at the head of navigation of the Chattahoochee River, on the site of a Creek village. It grew rapidly as a centre of the textile industry, exploiting water power from the river's fall line, and supplied the Confederacy during the Civil War; the Battle of Columbus in April 1865 was among the war's last battles. In the 20th century, the nearby military installation — Fort Benning, renamed Fort Moore in 2023 and back to Fort Benning in 2025 — became central to the local economy.

Geography and economy

The city lies about 100 miles (160 km) southwest of Atlanta at the fall line between the Piedmont and the coastal plain. Major employers include Fort Benning, Aflac (founded in Columbus in 1955), Total System Services (TSYS), and Columbus State University. The city developed a 15-mile riverwalk along the Chattahoochee and, in 2013, opened a 2.5-mile urban whitewater course created by removing two dams — promoted as the longest urban whitewater rafting course in the world.

Culture

Institutions include the National Infantry Museum, the National Civil War Naval Museum at Port Columbus, the Springer Opera House (the State Theatre of Georgia), and the Columbus Museum. The blues singer Gertrude "Ma" Rainey was born in Columbus.
