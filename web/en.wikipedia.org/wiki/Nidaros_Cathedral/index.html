Nidaros Cathedral (Norwegian: Nidarosdomen) is a cathedral of the Church of Norway located in the city of Trondheim in Trøndelag county. It is built over the burial site of King Olav II of Norway (c. 995–1030), who was killed at the Battle of Stiklestad and canonised as St Olav, the patron saint of Norway. The cathedral is the traditional location for the consecration of Norwegian monarchs and houses the Norwegian Crown Jewels.

History

A church was first raised over Olav's grave shortly after his death, and pilgrims flocked to his shrine from across northern Europe. The present stone cathedral was begun around 1070 and substantially completed by about 1300, in a mixture of Romanesque and Gothic styles closely related to English cathedral architecture. Fires repeatedly damaged the building, and after the Reformation of 1537 the shrine of St Olav was destroyed and much of the church fell into ruin. A major restoration began in 1869 and continued for well over a century, recreating the richly sculptured west front from the few surviving medieval fragments and early illustrations.

Features

The west front carries a gallery of carved statues of biblical figures and Norwegian kings and saints. Inside are two organs, including a notable Baroque instrument, and the octagonal chapel marking the traditional site of St Olav's grave. The cathedral remains a pilgrimage destination on the St Olav Ways.
