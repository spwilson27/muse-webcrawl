Tigrinya (ተԜ᢮Մ, təgrəɲɲa) is an Ethiopian Semitic language in the Afro-Asiatic family, spoken by the Tigrinya people of Eritrea and by the Tigrayans of northern Ethiopia. It is the most widely spoken language of Eritrea, where it serves as a working language of government, and an official language of Ethiopia's Tigray Region. A large diaspora also speaks it in Israel, Europe and North America.

History and script

Tigrinya descends from the ancient language Ge'ez, once spoken in the Aksumite Empire and still used as a liturgical language. The earliest written Tigrinya texts date to the 13th century. It is written with the Ge'ez (Ethiopic) script, an abugida in which each character represents a consonant-vowel syllable; Tigrinya uses seven vowel orders of each consonant sign. Unlike its close relative Tigre, Tigrinya is written rather than mainly oral, and unlike Amharic it preserves the pharyngeal consonants of Ge'ez.

Grammar

Like other Semitic languages, Tigrinya builds words from consonantal roots with interwoven vowel patterns. It is typically a subject–object–verb language, marks gender in the second and third person, and distinguishes singular and plural verbs and pronouns. Nouns may take definite suffixes, and the language has a rich system of derived verb stems expressing causative, passive and reciprocal meanings.
