Rational-legal authority

Rational-legal authority (also known as rational authority, legal authority, rational domination, legal domination, or bureaucratic authority) is a form of leadership in which the authority of an organisation or a ruling regime is largely tied to legal rationality, legal legitimacy and bureaucracy. The majority of the modern states of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries are rational-legal authorities, according to sociologists.

The concept originates in Max Weber's tripartite classification of authority, alongside traditional authority and charismatic authority. In a rational-legal system, legitimacy derives from a system of impersonal rules and laws; officials exercise power by virtue of their office, not their person, and their commands are obeyed because they fall within the legally defined scope of that office.

Characteristics

Weber described the corresponding administrative apparatus as bureaucracy: a hierarchy of offices, written records, specialised training, and rules applied impersonally. Rational-legal authority underlies modern constitutional states, corporations, and large organisations.
