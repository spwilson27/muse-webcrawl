Brett Andrew Hull (born 9 August 1964) is a Canadian-born American former professional ice hockey player and one of the most prolific goal scorers in National Hockey League (NHL) history. Nicknamed the "Golden Brett", he scored 741 regular-season goals, placing him among the top five goal scorers of all time when he retired.

Career

Hull, the son of hockey legend Bobby Hull, was drafted by the Calgary Flames in 1984 and traded to the St. Louis Blues in 1988, where he became a star. Playing on a line with Adam Oates, he recorded three consecutive seasons of 70 or more goals, including 86 goals in 1990–91, the third-highest single-season total in NHL history. That season he won the Hart Memorial Trophy as the league's most valuable player and the Lester B. Pearson Award.

Hull later won the Stanley Cup with the Dallas Stars in 1999 — scoring the controversial series-clinching goal in triple overtime against Buffalo — and again with the Detroit Red Wings in 2002. He also played for the United States internationally, winning the 1996 World Cup of Hockey.

Legacy

Hull was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 2009, the same year as his longtime teammate Steve Yzerman. He and his father Bobby are the only father-son pair in NHL history each to score 500 goals and each to win the Hart Trophy.
