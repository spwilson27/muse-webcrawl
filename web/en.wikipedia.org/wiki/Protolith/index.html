Protolith

Protolith
FieldMetamorphic petrology
DefinitionOriginal rock before metamorphism
ExamplesShale → slate; limestone → marble; basalt → amphibolite

A protolith (from Ancient Greek πρῶτος prôtos "first" and λίθος líthos "stone") is the original, unmetamorphosed rock from which a given metamorphic rock is formed. The protolith may be sedimentary, igneous or another older metamorphic rock. Identifying the protolith is a central task in metamorphic petrology, because a metamorphic rock's mineralogy and texture depend jointly on the protolith's chemistry and on the pressure and temperature conditions of metamorphism.

Common protolith–product pairs

  • Shale or mudstone (pelitic protolith) → slate, phyllite, schist, gneiss with increasing grade.
  • Limestone or dolomite → marble.
  • Sandstone (quartz-rich) → quartzite.
  • Basalt or gabbro (mafic protolith) → greenschist, amphibolite or eclogite depending on pressure and temperature.
  • Granite → orthogneiss.
  • Peridotite → serpentinite (with hydration).

Identification

Geologists infer protoliths from bulk-rock geochemistry — especially elements immobile during metamorphism such as Ti, Zr, Y and the rare earths — from relict textures and mineral pseudomorphs, and from field relations, such as transition zones where a rock passes gradationally into its metamorphosed equivalent. The prefix system of metamorphic nomenclature reflects protoliths: a metabasalt, for example, is a metamorphosed basalt.

Importance

Because two rocks with the same protolith can become completely different metamorphic rocks under different conditions, and the same metamorphic rock type can arise from several protoliths, distinguishing protolith effects from pressure–temperature effects is essential for reconstructing tectonic history, for example in subduction zones and mountain belts.
