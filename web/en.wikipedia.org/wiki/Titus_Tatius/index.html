Titus Tatius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Titus Tatius was, according to Roman tradition, the king of the Sabines who, after a war with Rome over the abduction of the Sabine women, became joint king of Rome with Romulus. His story is part of the legendary history of Rome's founding, told by Livy, Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Plutarch.

War and reconciliation

After Romulus's Romans seized Sabine women at a festival to provide wives for the new city, Tatius led the Sabine army against Rome. In the famous battle, the abducted women themselves intervened, rushing between the armies and pleading for peace between their fathers and husbands. The two peoples then united under a joint kingship: Romulus ruled on the Palatine Hill and Tatius on the Quirinal, with the combined community called Quirites after the Sabine town of Cures.

Death and legacy

Tatius is said to have been murdered about five years later at Lavinium while arbitrating a dispute, or in a quarrel over the plundering of ambassadors. Romulus is said to have declined to avenge him, and after Tatius's death the kingship passed to Romulus alone. The Sabine element in Roman society, language and institutions — including the Roman tribes and certain priesthoods — was traditionally credited to this union.
