C-segment

The C-segment is the third category of the European segments for passenger cars and is described as "medium cars". It is equivalent to the "compact car" size class in the United States, and to the "small family car" category in British terminology.

Definition

The European Commission-defined segments do not have formal size criteria, but C-segment cars are typically hatchbacks, saloons (sedans), estates (station wagons) and crossovers with a length in the region of 4.2 to 4.6 metres. The segment is one of the largest by sales volume in Europe, historically accounting for roughly a fifth to a quarter of the European passenger car market.

Examples

Long-running nameplates in the C-segment include the Volkswagen Golf, the Ford Focus, the Toyota Corolla (in its hatchback form), the Honda Civic, the Opel/Vauxhall Astra, the Peugeot 308, the Škoda Octavia and the Hyundai i30/Kia Ceed. Premium C-segment models include the Audi A3, BMW 1 Series and Mercedes-Benz A-Class.

Sub-categories

Alongside conventional hatchbacks and saloons, the segment includes compact MPVs (such as the Volkswagen Touran) and compact crossover SUVs (such as the Nissan Qashqai and Volkswagen T-Roc), the latter having grown rapidly in market share since the late 2000s. Electric models such as the Volkswagen ID.3 are also classified in the C-segment.
