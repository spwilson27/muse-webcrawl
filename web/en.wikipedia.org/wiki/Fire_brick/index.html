Fire brick

A fire brick, firebrick, fireclay brick, or refractory brick is a block of ceramic material used in lining furnaces, kilns, fireboxes, and fireplaces. A refractory brick is built primarily to withstand high temperature, but will also usually have a low thermal conductivity for greater energy efficiency. Usually dense fire bricks are used in applications with extreme mechanical, chemical, or thermal stresses, such as the inside of a wood-fired kiln or a furnace, which is subject to abrasion from wood, fluxing from ash or slag, and high temperatures. In other, less harsh situations, such as in an electric or natural gas fired kiln, porous bricks, commonly known as "insulating bricks", are a better choice.

Manufacture

In the making of firebrick, refractory clay is fired in the kiln until it is partly vitrified, and for special purposes may also be glazed. Fire bricks usually contain 30–40% alumina and the primary raw material is usually chamotte with other materials.

Types

Fire bricks are classified by their alumina content and their maximum service temperature. High-alumina bricks withstand greater temperatures and chemical attack, while silica bricks are used in glass furnaces. Insulating fire bricks (IFB) are lightweight and porous, providing insulation in kilns and furnaces where they are not subject to abrasion or slag attack.

Applications

  • Lining of furnaces, kilns, incinerators and reactors
  • Fireplaces and wood-burning stoves
  • Pizza ovens and forges
  • Steel and glass production
