Challenger 2

The Challenger 2 (MoD designation FV4034) is a British main battle tank in service with the British Army and the Royal Army of Oman. Designed and built by Vickers Defence Systems (now part of BAE Systems), it entered service in 1998 as the successor to the Challenger 1. The tank is noted for its heavy Chobham/Dorchester armour protection and for its rifled 120 mm main gun, unique among current NATO tanks.

A Challenger 2 of the British Army, distinguished by its rifled 120 mm gun and heavy armour.

History and design

Vickers developed the Challenger 2 as a private venture from 1986, and the Ministry of Defence ordered 127 tanks in 1989, with further orders following. Although it resembles its predecessor externally, fewer than five percent of components are shared: the turret was redesigned, the fire-control system replaced with a digital system derived from the M1A1 Abrams, and protection improved with second-generation Chobham armour, known as Dorchester. The L30A1 rifled gun fires separate-loading ammunition including HESH rounds, valued for their effect against light armour and fortifications but incompatible with NATO smoothbore ammunition.

Service history

Challenger 2s deployed to Kosovo and, in 2003, to Iraq, where they fought in the assault on Basra. In service it acquired a reputation for survivability: through the entire Iraq deployment no Challenger 2 was lost to enemy fire, although one was destroyed by friendly fire in March 2003 and another was penetrated by a shaped-charge weapon in 2007, injuring its driver. In January 2023 the United Kingdom announced the transfer of fourteen Challenger 2s to Ukraine, where they entered combat later that year — the first were confirmed lost in action in September 2023.

Challenger 3

Under the Challenger 3 programme, 148 Challenger 2 hulls are being rebuilt by Rheinmetall BAE Systems Land with a new turret, the L55A1 smoothbore gun and enhanced armour and electronics, with service entry planned for the late 2020s.
