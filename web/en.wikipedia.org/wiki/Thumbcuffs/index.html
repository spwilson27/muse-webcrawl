Thumbcuffs

Thumbcuffs are a metal restraining device that locks thumbs together, used as a form of portable restraint. They are similar in design to handcuffs, but are significantly smaller and lighter, and were sometimes used by law enforcement officers as a secondary or backup restraint.

Design

Thumbcuffs consist of two small metal cuffs connected by a short length of chain or a rigid bar. Each cuff closes around the base of a thumb and is secured with a key, typically the same standard handcuff key used on full-size restraints. A double-locking mechanism, operated with the key, prevents the cuffs from being tightened further once applied.

Use

Thumbcuffs were occasionally carried by plainclothes and off-duty officers who found full handcuffs too bulky. Because they restrain only the thumbs, they are considerably less secure than handcuffs and can cause injury, including dislocation, if a prisoner struggles. For this reason their use has been controversial, and they were prohibited for police use in some jurisdictions.
