Palio

A palio is an Italian festival, typically of medieval origin, centred on a competition between neighbourhoods, parishes, or towns, most often a horse race. The word derives from the Latin pallium, the banner of rich cloth awarded to the winner, which remains the prize — a painted silk standard — in the most famous races.

Palio di Siena

The most renowned is the Palio di Siena, held twice each summer, on 2 July and 16 August, in the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo. Ten of Siena's seventeen contrade compete in a bareback horse race of three laps around the piazza, preceded by months of rivalry, pageantry, and the blessing of the horses in church. The event's origins lie in medieval races run through the city streets.

Other palii

Many other Italian towns hold palii, including the Palio di Asti in Piedmont, among the oldest documented; the Palio di Legnano, commemorating the 1176 battle against Frederick Barbarossa; and the Palio del Golfo, a rowing regatta at La Spezia.
