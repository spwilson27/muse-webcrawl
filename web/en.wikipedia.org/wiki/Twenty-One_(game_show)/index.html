Twenty-One (game show)

Twenty-One was an American game show originally hosted by Jack Barry that aired on NBC from 12 September 1956 to 16 October 1958, and that became notorious as the centre of the 1950s quiz show scandals. Produced by Jack Barry and Dan Enright, the show pitted two contestants in isolation booths answering questions of graded difficulty, scoring points toward a target of 21, as in blackjack.

Format

Each contestant, sealed in a booth and wearing headphones so as not to hear the other's performance, selected categories and difficulty levels worth one to eleven points. A correct answer added the points; a wrong answer subtracted them. The first to reach exactly 21, or the higher scorer after a tied sequence of rounds, won, earning $500 per point of margin; champions could risk their winnings and return against new challengers, multiplying the stakes.

Charles Van Doren and the scandal

The show's most famous champion was Charles Van Doren, a Columbia University English instructor, who won $129,000 during a celebrated run in late 1956 and early 1957, defeating the reigning champion Herb Stempel. In 1958, after revelations about another quiz show, Dotto, Stempel charged that Twenty-One was rigged: he said producers had fed him answers, coached contestants' behaviour, and ordered him to lose to the more telegenic Van Doren. Congressional hearings in 1959 exposed the rigging across the industry; Van Doren, who had become a star and an editor at Encyclopædia Britannica, testified on 2 November 1959 that he had been given the questions and answers in advance. He lost his university and publishing positions and pleaded guilty to second-degree perjury. The scandal destroyed the big-money quiz format for a generation and led to amendments to the Communications Act making quiz rigging a federal crime.

Legacy and revivals

The story was dramatised in Robert Redford's 1994 film Quiz Show, with Ralph Fiennes as Van Doren. The format itself was revived by NBC in 2000, hosted by Maury Povich, and has been adapted in several other countries, including Britain's Twenty-One on ITV.
