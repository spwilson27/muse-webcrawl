Lisburn (from Irish Lios na gCearrbhach, "fort of the gamesters") is a city in Northern Ireland, situated on the River Lagan, about 13 km southwest of Belfast. It straddles the boundary between County Antrim and County Down and received city status in 2002 as part of the Golden Jubilee celebrations of Elizabeth II.

History

Lisburn originated in the 1620s as a settlement for English and Welsh planters and became a centre of the Irish linen industry, aided by Huguenot immigrants such as Louis Crommelin, who established fine linen manufacture from 1698. Christ Church Cathedral stands on one of the oldest church sites in the area. The Lagan Canal once linked the town to Belfast and Lough Neagh.

Economy and transport

The city is served by the M1 motorway and by railway stations on the Belfast–Dublin line. It forms the centre of the Lisburn and Castlereagh City Council area.
