Louis Ferdinand, Dauphin of France

Louis Ferdinand, Dauphin of France (4 September 1729 – 20 December 1765) was the elder and only surviving son of King Louis XV of France and Queen Marie Leszczyńska. As the heir apparent, he was Dauphin from birth until his early death from tuberculosis at Fontainebleau, predeceasing his father by nine years. Three of his sons became kings of France: Louis XVI, Louis XVIII, and Charles X.

Life

Louis Ferdinand married Infanta Maria Teresa Rafaela of Spain in 1745; she died in childbirth the next year. In 1747 he married Maria Josepha of Saxony, daughter of Augustus III of Poland, in a match arranged as France realigned its alliances. The Dauphin fought alongside Marshal Maurice de Saxe at the Battle of Fontenoy in 1745.

Devoutly Catholic and supported by the dévot party at court, he opposed his father's mistresses, particularly Madame de Pompadour, and was associated with conservative religious factions including sympathies for the Jesuits' opponents. He was the centre of hopes for reform among those alienated by Louis XV's court, but his death at 36 — followed two years later by that of his wife — left his eldest surviving son, the future Louis XVI, as heir.

Legacy

His reputation as an intelligent, pious prince meant his early death was widely mourned; historians have debated whether his reign might have averted the crises of the late Bourbon monarchy.
