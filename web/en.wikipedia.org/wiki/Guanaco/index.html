Guanaco

Guanaco (Lama guanicoe)
Family: Camelidae
Range: South America, sea level to 4,500 m
Status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The guanaco (Lama guanicoe) is a camelid native to South America, closely related to the llama, which is descended from domesticated guanacos. Adults stand about 1.0–1.2 m at the shoulder and weigh 90–140 kg. Their woolly coat is light brown above and white below.

Distribution and behaviour

Guanacos inhabit arid grasslands, steppe, and mountainous terrain from Peru and Bolivia through Argentina and Chile to Tierra del Fuego, at elevations from sea level to about 4,500 m. They live in herds of females and young led by a dominant male, or in bachelor groups. Well adapted to harsh conditions, they can go long periods without water and have a high red-blood-cell efficiency suited to thin mountain air. Their main predator is the puma.

Conservation and use

Once numbering perhaps 30–50 million, guanacos were greatly reduced by hunting and competition with livestock; numbers recovered to roughly 1.5–2 million, mostly in Argentina. Their fine fibre is valued for textiles, and managed shearing of live animals is practiced. Guanaco hunting was central to the cultures of Patagonian peoples such as the Tehuelche.
