Procyonidae

Procyonidae is a family of carnivoran mammals in the superfamily Musteloidea, native to the Americas. It includes the raccoons, coatis, kinkajous, olingos, olinguitos, ringtails and cacomistles — about 14 living species in six genera. Procyonids are small to medium-sized animals, generally weighing 1 to 14 kg, with long tails, dexterous forepaws and omnivorous diets.

Distribution and behaviour

The family ranges from southern Canada through the United States and Central America to northern Argentina. Most species are nocturnal and arboreal or semi-arboreal forest dwellers, though raccoons also thrive in wetlands and urban areas. The common raccoon (Procyon lotor) has been introduced to Europe and Japan, where it is considered invasive.

Evolution and classification

Procyonids diverged from mustelids roughly 30 million years ago; the earliest fossils are from the Oligocene of North America, and the family reached South America before the Great American Interchange, probably by island hopping. The red panda was historically placed in Procyonidae but is now assigned to its own family, Ailuridae. Molecular studies have reshuffled relationships among the olingos and coatis, and the olinguito (Bassaricyon neblina), described in 2013, was the first new carnivoran species announced from the Americas in 35 years.
