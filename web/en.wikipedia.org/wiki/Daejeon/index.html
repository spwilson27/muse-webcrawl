Daejeon

Daejeon is South Korea's fifth-largest metropolis, with a population of about 1.5 million. Located in the central region of the country, it serves as a major transport hub and is known as a centre of science and technology, home to the Daedeok Innopolis research complex and the university KAIST.

Geography

Daejeon lies in a basin surrounded by mountains, roughly 170 km south of Seoul. Three streams — Gapcheon, Yudeungcheon and Daejeoncheon — run through the city and join the Geum River. The city is divided into five administrative districts (gu), including Yuseong, known for its hot springs.

History

The area grew rapidly after railway lines were built in the early 20th century, connecting the Gyeongbu and Honam lines at Daejeon Station. During the Korean War, the Battle of Taejon (July 1950) briefly delayed the North Korean advance. The city hosted the World Expo in 1993 and was a venue of the 2002 FIFA World Cup.

Economy and education

Dozens of government research institutes are concentrated in Daedeok Science Town, and several government agencies have relocated to the Daejeon Government Complex. Notable universities include KAIST, Chungnam National University and Hannam University.
