FICO (legal name: Fair Isaac Corporation) is an American data analytics company based in Bozeman, Montana, focused on credit scoring services. Founded in 1956 by engineer Bill Fair and mathematician Earl Isaac as Fair, Isaac, and Company, it created the FICO Score, the best-known measure of consumer credit risk in the United States, used by the great majority of American lenders and by the secondary mortgage market.

History

Fair and Isaac founded their company in San Jose, California, selling decision-support systems based on statistical scoring. In 1958 they pitched credit scoring to American lenders, and in the following decades scoring spread through credit cards and consumer lending. The general-purpose FICO Score, built on credit bureau data, was introduced with Equifax in 1989 and soon adopted by Experian and TransUnion as well. The company changed its brand name to FICO in 2009. In 2023 the company moved its headquarters from San Jose to Bozeman, Montana; it has long had major offices in San Diego and elsewhere.

The FICO Score

The base FICO Score ranges from 300 to 850; higher scores indicate lower credit risk. It is computed from five categories of credit-report data, weighted approximately as: payment history (35%), amounts owed (30%), length of credit history (15%), new credit (10%) and credit mix (10%). Industry-specific versions (auto, bankcard, mortgage) use a 250–900 scale. Scores are generated separately from each bureau's data, so a consumer has many FICO Scores. The exact algorithms are proprietary, a point of long-running debate; US law nonetheless gives consumers free access to their credit reports, and FICO has sold score access directly through its myFICO service since the early 2000s.

Other products

Beyond consumer scores, FICO sells fraud-detection systems — its Falcon platform monitors a large share of the world's payment-card transactions — along with decision-management software, collections and customer-management tools, and the FICO Platform for automated decisioning. It also publishes the widely followed "average FICO Score" statistics for the United States, which reached record highs around 718 in the early 2020s.
