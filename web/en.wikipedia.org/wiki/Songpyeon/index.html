Songpyeon

Songpyeon (송편) is a traditional Korean rice cake made of rice flour dough, filled with ingredients such as sesame seeds and honey, chopped chestnuts, or sweetened mung bean or red bean paste, and steamed over a bed of pine needles. The pine needles give the cakes a distinctive fragrance and are said to prevent them sticking together. Songpyeon is the signature food of Chuseok, the Korean autumn harvest festival.

Preparation

The dough is made from finely ground rice flour mixed with hot water (and sometimes coloured with natural ingredients such as mugwort for green, cactus or berry juice for pink, and pumpkin or egg yolk for yellow). Small pieces are rolled into balls, indented, filled with a sweetened mixture — most commonly toasted sesame seeds with honey or sugar — then folded and sealed into a half-moon shape. The folded shape is said to symbolise the moon's promise of fullness; tradition holds that shaping beautiful songpyeon brings good fortune, and a Korean saying promises that one who makes pretty songpyeon will have beautiful daughters or a good spouse.

Cultural significance

Families traditionally gather on the eve of Chuseok to make songpyeon together, using rice from the new harvest, and the cakes are placed on the ancestral memorial table (charye) during the holiday. Songpyeon is among the oldest of Korean rice cakes, with origins traced to the Three Kingdoms period: according to legend recorded in the Samguk yusa, during the reign of King Yuri of Silla, court women competed in weaving contests ending on the full moon of the eighth lunar month, and rice cakes were prepared for the occasion.

Varieties

Regional varieties include flower-shaped kkot-songpyeon and versions made with glutinous rice, potato starch (in Gangwon Province), or ramie leaves (in the Jeolla region, using a dough similar to injeolmi). Thawed or day-old songpyeon is often pan-fried in sesame or perilla oil.
