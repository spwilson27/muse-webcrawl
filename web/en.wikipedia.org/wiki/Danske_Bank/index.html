Danske Bank

Danske Bank A/S is a Danish multinational banking and financial services corporation. Headquartered in Copenhagen, it is the largest bank in Denmark and a major retail bank in Northern Europe with operations in Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Northern Ireland and the Baltic region historically.

History

The bank was founded on 5 October 1871 as Den Danske Landmandsbank, Hypothek- og Vexelbank i Kjøbenhavn ("The Danish Farmers' Bank, Mortgage and Exchange Bank in Copenhagen"). It grew through acquisitions, notably the merger in 1990 with Handelsbanken (Kjøbenhavns Handelsbank) and Provinsbanken that formed Den Danske Bank, and adopted the name Danske Bank in 2000. It expanded abroad by acquiring banks including Sampo Bank in Finland (2007) and Northern Bank in Northern Ireland.

Money laundering scandal

From 2017 investigations revealed that Danske Bank's Estonian branch had channelled roughly €200 billion in suspicious transactions between 2007 and 2015, largely from Russia and other former Soviet states — the largest money-laundering scandal in European banking history. The affair forced the resignation of CEO Thomas Borgen in 2018, cost the bank its Estonian and other Baltic operations, and led to settlements including a 2022 agreement with US and Danish authorities involving about $2 billion in forfeiture and fines.

Operations

Danske Bank serves personal, business and institutional customers, offering banking, mortgage lending (through Realkredit Danmark), insurance, asset management and pension products, and employs around 20,000 people.
