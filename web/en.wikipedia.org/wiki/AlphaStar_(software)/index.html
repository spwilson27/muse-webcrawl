AlphaStar is an artificial intelligence program developed by DeepMind that plays the real-time strategy video game StarCraft II. In 2019 it became the first AI to reach Grandmaster status on the official StarCraft II ladder — ranking above 99.8% of human players — and defeated top professionals in televised show matches. It was presented as a landmark for reinforcement learning in complex, imperfect-information, real-time environments.

Background

StarCraft II presents difficulties absent from board games mastered earlier by AI: players act simultaneously and continuously, observe only a fog-of-war-limited view, must plan over thousands of time steps, and choose from a combinatorially vast action space while managing economy, production, and combat together. DeepMind selected it as a benchmark environment and worked with Blizzard Entertainment to build a training interface.

Method

AlphaStar's agent is a deep neural network combining recurrent (LSTM) components, attention mechanisms, and pointer networks to issue game actions. Training proceeded in two stages: initial imitation learning on replays of human games, followed by reinforcement learning in a "league" of agents — main agents, main exploiters, and league exploiters — that played against present and past versions of one another to discover robust strategies and avoid forgetting counters. The system trained on Google TPUs; DeepMind reported that the league ran for 44 days with each agent experiencing up to 200 years of real-time play.

Matches and results

In December 2018 AlphaStar beat the professional player MaNa 5–0 in a closed series (with a prototype camera-limited agent later losing the live rematch). In July 2019, playing anonymously on the European public ladder under constraints on perception and actions designed to be comparable to human limits, AlphaStar reached Grandmaster for all three races. The work was published in Nature in October 2019 ("Grandmaster level in StarCraft II using multi-agent reinforcement learning").

Reception

Commentators praised the league-training method and the system's long-horizon strategic play, while debating the fairness of its reaction speed and interface in early versions. AlphaStar became a reference point in reinforcement-learning research alongside AlphaGo and OpenAI Five.
