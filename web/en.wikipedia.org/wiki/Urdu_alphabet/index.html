Urdu alphabet


The Urdu alphabet in Nastaliq

The Urdu alphabet is the right-to-left script used for writing the Urdu language. It is an extended form of the Perso-Arabic alphabet, which itself derives from the Arabic script, and is almost always written in the cursive Nastaliq calligraphic style, in which letters slope and stack diagonally. A more angular Naskh style is sometimes used for religious texts and computing.

Letters

Depending on how special characters and aspirated digraphs are counted, the alphabet has around 39–40 base letters. Beyond the Arabic core it adds characters from Persian, such as pe (پ), che (چ), zhe (ژ) and gāf (گ), and letters invented for Indic sounds absent from Arabic and Persian: the retroflex consonants ṭe (ٹ), ḍāl (ڈ) and ṛe (ڑ). Aspiration is written with a following do-chashmī he (ھ), forming digraphs such as بھ, پھ, تھ.

Characteristics

Like Arabic, Urdu script is an abjad in which short vowels are normally omitted, though diacritics (aerab) can be added. Letters change shape according to their position in a word — initial, medial, final or isolated. Additional letters mark nasalisation (nūn ghunnā ں) and the glottal/affricate sounds of Arabic loanwords. Standardisation for computing came with the Unicode Arabic block and, in Pakistan, fonts such as Nafees Nastaleeq.
