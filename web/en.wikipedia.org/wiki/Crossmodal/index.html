Crossmodal

Crossmodal refers to interactions between two or more different sensory modalities. Examples include the influence of vision on hearing (as in the McGurk effect), of smell and taste on flavour perception, and of touch on visual judgments. Research in cognitive neuroscience has shown that the brain does not process each sense in isolation; rather, information from the senses is integrated at many levels of the nervous system.

Crossmodal correspondences

A crossmodal correspondence is a tendency for a sensory feature in one modality to be matched or associated with a feature in another modality. Well-known examples include the bouba/kiki effect, in which people across cultures associate rounded shapes with the sound "bouba" and angular shapes with "kiki", and the widespread association of high-pitched sounds with small, bright or elevated objects.

Crossmodal plasticity

Crossmodal plasticity refers to the reorganization of the brain's sensory systems following the loss of a sense. In people who are blind from an early age, the visual cortex can be recruited for tactile tasks such as Braille reading and for auditory processing, a phenomenon studied extensively in neuroscience.

Applications

Understanding crossmodal interactions has applications in food science and marketing (how colour and sound alter perceived flavour), in the design of multisensory interfaces and virtual reality, and in sensory substitution devices that convey visual information through sound or touch.
