Voxel

A set of voxels rendered as stacked unit cubes
Analogous toPixel (2D)
DimensionThree-dimensional grid element
UsesMedical imaging, video games, 3D modelling, scientific visualisation

A voxel represents a value on a regular grid in three-dimensional space. The name is a portmanteau of "volume" and "pixel" (itself from "picture element"): just as a pixel represents a value on a two-dimensional grid, a voxel represents a value on a three-dimensional grid. Voxels are frequently used in the visualisation and analysis of medical and scientific data, and in computer graphics and video games.

A voxel's position is typically inferred from its index in the grid rather than stored explicitly. The value associated with a voxel may represent a single property such as opacity or colour, a scalar measurement such as density, or multiple channels of data.

Rendering

Voxel data can be rendered directly by a volume renderer, which assigns colour and opacity to each voxel and composites the result, or converted to polygonal surfaces using algorithms such as marching cubes, which extract an isosurface from the voxel field. Direct volume rendering is common in medical imaging (CT and MRI scans), while surface extraction is common in games and simulation.

Applications

  • Medical imaging: CT, MRI and ultrasound scans produce voxel data representing tissue density or signal intensity.
  • Video games: Games such as Minecraft build worlds from large arrays of voxels, enabling fully destructible and user-modifiable terrain.
  • Scientific visualisation: Fluid dynamics, weather models and seismic surveys use voxel grids to represent three-dimensional fields.
  • 3D printing and modelling: Voxel-based editors allow sculpting volumetric models.

Data structures

Because dense voxel grids require large amounts of memory, sparse representations such as octrees and sparse voxel octrees are often used, storing only regions that contain data. Compression techniques and level-of-detail hierarchies make very large voxel worlds tractable on modern hardware.
