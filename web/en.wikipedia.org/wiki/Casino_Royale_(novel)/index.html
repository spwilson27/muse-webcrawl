Casino Royale is the debut novel of British author Ian Fleming, published in 1953. It introduces the character of James Bond, a British Secret Service agent (007), and is the first of Fleming's twelve Bond novels.

Plot

Bond is sent to the fictional French casino town of Royale-les-Eaux to bankrupt Le Chiffre, paymaster of a Soviet-backed trade union, in a high-stakes game of baccarat. Assisted by Vesper Lynd, René Mathis and CIA agent Felix Leiter, Bond wins the game after Leiter supplies emergency funds — but is captured and tortured by Le Chiffre, who is then executed by a SMERSH assassin. Bond falls in love with Vesper, only to discover she was a double agent; she takes her own life, leaving Bond embittered. The novel ends with his famous report: "The bitch is dead now."

Background and reception

Fleming, a former naval intelligence officer, wrote the novel in early 1952 at his Jamaican estate, Goldeneye. Drawing on wartime experiences — including a visit to a casino at Estoril, Portugal — he created a colder, more ruthless hero than earlier thriller protagonists. The book was a critical and commercial success in Britain and launched one of the most successful franchises in publishing and film history.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted as a 1954 television episode of Climax! starring Barry Nelson, as a 1967 parody film, and as the 2006 film Casino Royale, Daniel Craig's debut as Bond, which follows the novel's plot closely.
