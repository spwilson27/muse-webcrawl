Yangcheng Lake

Yangcheng Lake (阳澄湖)
Location: Jiangsu, China
Surface area: approx. 117–120 km²
Known for: Chinese mitten crabs

Yangcheng Lake (Chinese: 阳澄湖; pinyin: Yángchéng Hú) is a freshwater lake about 3 kilometres northeast of Suzhou in Jiangsu Province, China. Covering roughly 117 square kilometres, it lies between Lake Tai and the Yangtze River delta plain and is divided into eastern, central, and western sections.

Hairy crabs

The lake is celebrated throughout China as the home of the Chinese mitten crab (Eriocheir sinensis), marketed as Yangcheng Lake hairy crab (dàzháxiè). The crabs, prized for their rich golden roe in autumn, command high prices and carry an official protected-origin designation; demand has long exceeded the lake's output, leading to widespread counterfeiting in which crabs raised elsewhere are briefly "bathed" in the lake or simply relabelled. Genuine crabs are sold with anti-counterfeit tags issued by the local crab association, and the annual season opens in mid-to-late September around the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Surroundings

The Yangchenghu Peninsula on the lake's eastern shore has been developed with resorts, a theme park, and the Chongyuan Temple, whose 33-metre Guanyin statue stands in the lake shallows. The Shanghai–Nanjing intercity railway and several expressways cross the area, and the lake's crab restaurants draw seasonal crowds from Shanghai and Suzhou. Aquaculture reforms since the 2010s have reduced net-pen farming areas in the lake to improve water quality.
