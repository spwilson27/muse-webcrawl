Spencer Tracy

Spencer Bonaventure Tracy (April 5, 1900 – June 10, 1967) was an American actor. He was known for his naturalistic performing style and versatility, and appeared in 75 films from 1930 to 1967. Over a career in which he was one of MGM's biggest stars, Tracy was among the first actors nominated for nine Academy Awards for Best Actor, sharing the record with Laurence Olivier, and won the award twice — in consecutive years, for Captains Courageous (1937) and Boys Town (1938). The American Film Institute ranked him ninth on its list of the greatest male stars of classic American cinema.

Career

After Broadway success in The Last Mile (1930), Tracy signed with Fox and then moved to MGM in 1935. His partnership with director Victor Fleming and friend Clark Gable produced hits such as San Francisco (1936). Tracy's on-screen pairing with Katharine Hepburn, beginning with Woman of the Year (1942), spanned nine films and a 26-year off-screen relationship. His later work includes Father of the Bride (1950), Bad Day at Black Rock (1955), The Old Man and the Sea (1958), and four films for Stanley Kramer, concluding with Guess Who's Coming to Dinner (1967), completed 17 days before his death from a heart attack.

Acting style

Tracy was famous for an apparently effortless realism — colleagues said he made acting look "as easy as peeling a potato" — and for mastering long takes and minimal gesture. His personal struggles with alcoholism, and his guilt and devotion surrounding his deaf son John, shaped a private life kept deliberately apart from his public image.

Legacy

Tracy remains a benchmark of screen acting; his nine Oscar nominations were achieved across five decades. Consec­utive Best Actor wins were unmatched among men until Tom Hanks in 1993–94 (and earlier equalled only by Tracy himself and, in part, by others in different categories).
