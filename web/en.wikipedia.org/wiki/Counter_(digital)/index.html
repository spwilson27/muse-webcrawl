Counter (digital)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In digital logic and computing, a counter is a device which stores (and sometimes displays) the number of times a particular event or process has occurred, often in relationship to a clock pulse. The most common type is a sequential digital logic circuit with an input line called the clock and multiple output lines whose values represent the count in binary or binary-coded decimal.

Types

Asynchronous (ripple) counters clock each flip-flop from the output of the previous one, so state changes propagate with cumulative delay; synchronous counters clock all flip-flops simultaneously and are faster and free of glitches. Counters may count up, down, or both, and may be built to divide frequency — an n-bit counter divides its input clock frequency by up to 2ⁿ.

Applications

Counters are used for frequency division, timing, address generation in memory, and as the program counter in processors. Integrated examples include the classic 74-series counters such as the 74HC93 and the 74HC161.
