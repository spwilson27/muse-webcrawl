India pale ale

India pale ale (IPA) is a hoppy beer style within the broader category of pale ale. It originated in England in the 19th century in the pale ales exported to India under British rule, which were highly hopped and relatively strong so as to survive the long sea voyage. The style was later revived and transformed by American craft brewers, and IPA has become the flagship style of the modern craft beer movement.

History

In the late 18th century, London brewer George Hodgson of the Bow Brewery shipped pale ale to India for the East India Company; the beer was well hopped and conditioned in a way that withstood the months-long journey around the Cape of Good Hope. Brewers in Burton upon Trent — including Bass and Allsopp — took up the trade in the 1820s and 1830s, their sulphate-rich water producing a characteristically dry, hoppy beer. Advertisements for "pale ale prepared for the East and West India climate" appeared by the 1830s, and the term "India pale ale" was in print by 1835. Demand in Britain itself grew, and IPA became a popular domestic style before declining in the 20th century under pressure from lighter lagers and mild ales.

American craft revival

American homebrewers and early craft brewers revived IPA in the 1970s and 1980s, using assertive American hop varieties bred in the Pacific Northwest such as Cascade, Centennial and Chinook. Anchor Brewing's Liberty Ale (1975) and Sierra Nevada's Celebration Ale are landmark examples. American IPAs tend to be stronger and more intensely hopped than their English ancestors, emphasizing citrus, pine and tropical-fruit hop character.

Substyles

Numerous substyles have emerged, including English IPA, American IPA, West Coast IPA (dry, bitter, clear), New England or "hazy" IPA (juicy, soft, turbid, popularized in the 2010s), double or imperial IPA, session IPA, black IPA, Belgian IPA, and brut IPA. IPA is now brewed worldwide and is the best-selling craft beer style in the United States.
