Mars Pathfinder

Mars Pathfinder (MESUR Pathfinder) was an American robotic spacecraft that landed a base station with a roving probe on Mars on July 4, 1997. It consisted of a lander, renamed the Carl Sagan Memorial Station, and a lightweight (10.6 kg) wheeled robotic rover named Sojourner — the first rover to operate outside the Earth–Moon system. The mission was the second of NASA's Discovery Program of low-cost missions and demonstrated a novel airbag landing system.

Mission

Launched on December 4, 1996 aboard a Delta II rocket, Pathfinder entered the Martian atmosphere directly on July 4, 1997. A parachute, retrorockets, and a cocoon of giant airbags softened the landing in Ares Vallis, an ancient flood plain in the northern hemisphere chosen because it promised a variety of rock types washed down from the highlands. The lander bounced and rolled to a stop, then opened its petal-shaped panels.

The Sojourner rover — a six-wheeled, rocker-bogie vehicle the size of a microwave oven — drove onto the surface on July 6 and used its Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer to analyse rocks nicknamed Barnacle Bill, Yogi, and Moe. The lander returned panoramic images, weather data from its meteorology package, and evidence that Mars was once warmer and wetter.

End of mission

Pathfinder was designed to last one week to one month; it operated for nearly three months, returning 2.3 billion bits of information including more than 16,500 images from the lander and 550 from the rover. The final successful communication was on September 27, 1997; after further attempts, the mission was declared over on March 10, 1998. The mission's success — technically and in public engagement via the then-new World Wide Web — set the pattern for the rover missions that followed.
