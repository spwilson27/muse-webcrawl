Missouri Pacific Railroad

The Missouri Pacific Railroad (reporting mark MP), commonly abbreviated to MoPac, was one of the first railroads in the United States west of the Mississippi River. Headquartered in St. Louis, Missouri, it grew into a major system serving the central and southern United States, reaching from Chicago and Omaha through Kansas City and Little Rock to Houston, New Orleans, and the Mexican border at Laredo and El Paso.

History

The company originated as the Pacific Railroad of Missouri, chartered in 1849, which began construction westward from St. Louis in 1851 and reached Kansas City in 1865. After financial difficulties and reorganization, the line took the Missouri Pacific name in the 1870s. Under financier Jay Gould, who gained control in 1879, the railroad expanded aggressively, absorbing numerous smaller lines across Missouri, Kansas, Arkansas, Louisiana, and Texas.

Through mergers the Missouri Pacific came to control the Texas and Pacific Railway, the St. Louis, Iron Mountain and Southern Railway, and other properties, forming a system of more than 11,000 miles. The railroad was noted for its distinctive "eagle" emblem, referencing its streamlined Eagle passenger trains such as the Texas Eagle and Colorado Eagle.

Merger

In 1982 the Missouri Pacific merged with the Western Pacific Railroad and the Union Pacific Railroad under a common corporate parent, though the Missouri Pacific continued to operate as a separate subsidiary. Its corporate existence formally ended on January 1, 1997, when it was fully merged into Union Pacific.
