Battle of Killiecrankie

The Battle of Killiecrankie, also known as the Battle of Rinrory, was fought on 27 July 1689 during the first Jacobite rising in Scotland. Highland Jacobite forces under John Graham of Claverhouse, Viscount Dundee, defeated a government army commanded by General Hugh Mackay at the Pass of Killiecrankie in Perthshire — but Dundee was killed in the moment of victory, fatally weakening the rising.

Background

After the Glorious Revolution deposed James VII and II, Dundee raised the Highlands for the exiled king. Mackay marched north to secure the strategic pass where the road from Perth enters the Highlands. Dundee's clan army — some 1,800–2,400 Highlanders with a troop of horse — occupied high ground above the pass as Mackay's roughly 3,500–4,000 government troops, many of them Scots regiments in Dutch service, emerged from the defile below.

The battle

Dundee waited until late evening, around 8 p.m., letting the sun drop behind his men, then launched the Highland charge downhill. The clansmen fired a single volley at close range, threw down their muskets and crashed into Mackay's line with broadswords and targes before the government infantry — equipped with plug bayonets that had to be fixed in the musket muzzle — could respond. Two regiments in the centre were swept away in minutes. The government army lost perhaps 2,000 men killed, wounded or captured. Jacobite losses were far lower, around 600, but they included Dundee himself, shot below the breastplate while waving his men on.

Aftermath

Leaderless, the Jacobites failed to exploit the victory; under Colonel Alexander Cannon they were repulsed at Dunkeld in August, and the rising dissolved after the defeat at Cromdale in May 1690. Killiecrankie remains the classic demonstration of the Highland charge and is commemorated at the National Trust for Scotland visitor centre at the pass; the "Soldier's Leap" marks where a fleeing government soldier is said to have jumped the River Garry.
