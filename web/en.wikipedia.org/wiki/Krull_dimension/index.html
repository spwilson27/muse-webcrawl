Krull dimension

In commutative algebra, the Krull dimension of a commutative ring R, named after Wolfgang Krull, is the supremum of the lengths of all chains of prime ideals in R. The Krull dimension need not be finite even for a Noetherian ring. More generally, the Krull dimension can be defined for modules over possibly non-commutative rings as the deviation of the poset of submodules.

The Krull dimension of a ring is one of the fundamental numerical invariants of commutative algebra and plays a central role in algebraic geometry, where it corresponds to the dimension of the associated affine scheme.

Formal definition

Let R be a ring and let 𝔭0, 𝔭1, …, 𝔭n be prime ideals of R with 𝔭0 ⊊ 𝔭1 ⊊ … ⊊ 𝔭n. The length of such a chain is n. The Krull dimension of R, written dim R, is the supremum of the lengths of all such chains. Equivalently, dim R equals the supremum of the heights of all prime ideals of R, where the height of a prime ideal 𝔭 is the supremum of the lengths of chains of prime ideals contained in 𝔭.

Examples

A field has Krull dimension zero, since its only prime ideal is the zero ideal. More generally, any Artinian ring has dimension zero. The ring of integers ℤ has dimension one, with chains of the form (0) ⊊ (p) for prime numbers p. A polynomial ring in n variables over a field, k[x1, …, xn], has Krull dimension n. Formal power series rings over a field likewise have dimension equal to the number of variables.

Properties

For a Noetherian local ring (R, 𝔪), Krull's Hauptidealsatz (principal ideal theorem) states that a prime ideal minimal over an ideal generated by n elements has height at most n. The dimension of a finitely generated algebra over a field equals the transcendence degree of its fraction field over the base field. Krull's Hauptidealsatz is a cornerstone of dimension theory, implying that systems of polynomial equations in n variables cut out varieties whose components have codimension at most n.
