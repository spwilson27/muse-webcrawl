John Deere

John Deere (1804–1886) was an American blacksmith and manufacturer who founded Deere & Company, one of the world's largest makers of agricultural machinery. His steel plough, developed in 1837, helped open the prairies of the American Midwest to farming.

Life

Born in Rutland, Vermont, Deere worked as a blacksmith before moving west to Grand Detour, Illinois, in 1836. Finding that cast-iron ploughs fouled in the rich prairie soil, he fashioned a ploughshare from a broken steel saw blade whose polished surface scoured clean as it cut.

Company

Production grew from a handful of ploughs to thousands annually; Deere moved operations to Moline on the Mississippi River in 1848 and incorporated Deere & Company in 1868, with his son Charles as a key executive. The firm — famous for its green-and-yellow leaping deer trademark — remains headquartered in Moline.
