Battle of Sphacteria

The Battle of Sphacteria took place in 425 BC during the Peloponnesian War, on the island of Sphacteria in the bay of Pylos, off the southwest coast of the Peloponnese. Athenian forces under Cleon and Demosthenes forced the surrender of a body of Spartan hoplites trapped on the island — among them some 120 full Spartiates. The capture of so many citizen-soldiers, unthinkable in Greek warfare, was a profound shock to Sparta and to the Greek world.

Background

Athenians fortified Pylos inside Spartan territory; a Spartan assault failed and a landing force of hoplites was isolated on Sphacteria when Athens won the ensuing naval battle in the bay. Sparta sued for an armistice, but negotiations at Athens failed under Cleon's influence.

The assault

Cleon, who had boasted he would settle the affair within twenty days, joined Demosthenes with reinforcements. Athenian light-armed troops — archers, peltasts, and Messenian hoplites — landed at dawn and wore down the Spartans, who could not close with enemies who refused hand-to-hand combat on the rough, ash-covered ground left by a recent fire. Surrounded on a rise, the Spartans surrendered.

Aftermath

The prisoners were held at Athens as hostages against a Spartan invasion of Attica, which ceased thereafter. The affair destroyed the myth of Spartan invincibility and of Spartans never surrendering, and greatly strengthened Cleon's political position. Sparta's desire to recover the men was a principal motive for the Peace of Nicias in 421 BC. Sparta's humiliation was avenged in part at Mantineia in 418 BC.
