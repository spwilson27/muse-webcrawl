Arirang Festival

Thousands of performers form a human mosaic at the Arirang Mass Games.

The Arirang Festival (Korean: 아리랑 축제), also known as the Arirang Mass Games, was a mass gymnastics and artistic performance held in Pyongyang, North Korea, at the Rungrado 1st of May Stadium — the largest stadium in the world. The festival, first staged in 2002, involved more than 100,000 participants: gymnasts, dancers, acrobats and schoolchildren performing synchronised routines, while tens of thousands of students in the stands flipped coloured cards to form giant mosaic images.

Content and organisation

The performance told the official story of North Korea — the life of Kim Il Sung, the anti-Japanese struggle, socialist construction and the aspiration for Korean reunification — through chapters drawn from the folk song "Arirang". Participants trained for months each year. The festival was staged in most years between 2002 and 2013, with revivals in modified form later in the decade under different titles.

Reception

For foreign visitors, the Mass Games became the most famous spectacle of North Korean tourism and are often described as the largest choreographed performance on earth. The festival is entered in reference works as a record-setting mass event; at the same time, critics and defectors have described the gruelling training imposed on child performers as a human-rights concern. Imagery from the Games — vast, perfectly coordinated crowds — has become an emblem, to supporters and critics alike, of the North Korean state's capacity for mobilisation.
