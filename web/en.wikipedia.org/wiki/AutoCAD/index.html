AutoCAD is a commercial computer-aided design (CAD) and drafting software application developed and marketed by Autodesk. First released in December 1982, it was one of the first CAD programs to run on microcomputers with internal graphics controllers, at a time when most CAD ran on mainframes or minicomputers. AutoCAD is used by architects, engineers, and construction professionals to create precise 2D and 3D drawings.

History

AutoCAD originated in a 1977 program called Interact CAD, written by Autodesk co-founder Mike Riddle. Autodesk was founded in 1982 around the product, and AutoCAD was demonstrated at COMDEX that November. Successive releases added 3D modelling, customisation through AutoLISP (1986), and industry-specific toolsets (Architecture, Mechanical, Electrical). The annual "R" releases through the 1990s and 2000s made it the dominant drafting standard worldwide.

File formats

AutoCAD's native format is DWG, which became a de facto industry standard for CAD data exchange; DXF (Drawing Exchange Format) is its documented interchange format. The DWG format is periodically revised with major releases, and its dominance has been the subject of interoperability disputes and reverse-engineering efforts such as the Open Design Alliance libraries.

Variants and platform

Autodesk sells vertical products built on AutoCAD (including Civil 3D, AutoCAD Architecture, and AutoCAD Mechanical), the lower-cost AutoCAD LT, and since 2010 mobile and web versions (AutoCAD Web App). The software is extensible via AutoLISP, Visual LISP, VBA, .NET, and ObjectARX APIs.
