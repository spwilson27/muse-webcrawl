Islands of Croatia

SeaAdriatic Sea

Croatia's Adriatic coast is fringed by one of the world's most island-rich archipelagos: a total of 1,246 islands, islets, rocks and reefs are counted in Croatian waters, of which 79 islands are larger than one square kilometre and 48 are permanently inhabited. The indented Dalmatian coastline and its islands make Croatia's shoreline one of the longest in Europe when island coasts are included.

Groups and character

The northern Kvarner islands (Krk, Cres, Lošinj, Rab, Pag) give way to the Zadar and Šibenik archipelagos — including the uninhabited Kornati, protected as a national park — and then to the central and southern Dalmatian islands (Brač, Hvar, Korčula, Vis, Lastovo, Mljet). Island economies depend heavily on tourism, supplemented by fishing, olive oil, wine and, on Pag, sheep's-milk cheese. Many islands lose permanent residents to the mainland, a long-running depopulation concern, while gaining large seasonal populations in summer.
