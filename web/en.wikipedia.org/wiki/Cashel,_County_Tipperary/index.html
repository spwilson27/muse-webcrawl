Cashel (Irish: Caiseal Mumhan, "stone fort of Munster") is a town in County Tipperary, Ireland. It is dominated by the Rock of Cashel, a dramatic limestone outcrop crowned by medieval religious buildings, which rises above the Golden Vale.

History

The Rock was the traditional seat of the Kings of Munster for several centuries before the Norman invasion. According to legend, St Patrick converted King Aengus here in the 5th century. In 1101 the King of Munster, Muirchertach Ua Briain, granted the Rock to the Church. The surviving complex includes Cormac's Chapel (1134), a masterpiece of Irish Romanesque architecture, a 12th-century cathedral, a round tower and a high cross.

The town

Modern Cashel is a market and heritage town on the M8 Dublin–Cork motorway, with tourism centred on the Rock of Cashel, one of Ireland's most visited heritage sites. The town also retains the Bolton Library and the remains of its medieval walls and abbeys.
