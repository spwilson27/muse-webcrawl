Salve Regina

The Salve Regina (Latin for "Hail, Queen") is one of the four Marian antiphons of the Roman Catholic Church, traditionally sung at the end of Compline (Night Prayer) from the Saturday before Trinity Sunday until the Saturday before the first Sunday of Advent. It is among the most famous of all Marian hymns and a staple of Catholic devotion, also recited at the conclusion of the Rosary in many traditions.

Text and history

The antiphon addresses the Virgin Mary as queen and mother of mercy: "Salve, Regina, mater misericordiae: vita, dulcedo, et spes nostra, salve" ("Hail, Queen, mother of mercy: our life, our sweetness and our hope, hail"). It goes on to plead, "Eia ergo, advocata nostra" ("Turn then, our advocate") and closes with "O clemens, O pia, O dulcis Virgo Maria." Authorship is traditionally attributed to the 11th-century monk Hermann of Reichenau (Hermannus Contractus), though this is debated. St Bernard of Clairvaux is legendarily credited with adding the final triple invocation. The antiphon spread through the Cistercian, Dominican and Franciscan orders, and the University of Paris and many colleges adopted it as a daily devotion; "Salve Regina" is also the alma mater hymn of numerous schools and the name of Salve Regina University in Newport, Rhode Island.

Musical settings

The chant exists in both a solemn and a simple tone. Composers across the centuries have set the text polyphonically and concertante, including Josquin des Prez, Palestrina, Monteverdi, Pergolesi, Haydn, Schubert and Poulenc, whose setting dates from 1941.
