Abba Ptachya Lerner (28 October 1903 – 27 October 1982) was a Russian-born British and American economist. Brought up in London's East End, he studied at the London School of Economics under Friedrich Hayek and Lionel Robbins, and became one of the most creative interpreters and popularisers of Keynesian economics, as well as a major contributor to welfare economics and international trade theory.

Career

Lerner taught at the LSE in the 1930s, co-founding the Review of Economic Studies. After wartime work and a spell at the New School for Social Research in New York, he held posts at Roosevelt University, Michigan State University, the University of California, Berkeley, Queens College in New York, and finally Florida State University, where he remained until his death.

Contributions

Lerner's most influential work was The Economics of Control (1944), which set out the theory of functional finance: government taxing, spending and borrowing should be judged only by their effects on employment, price stability and the economy as a whole, not by whether the budget is "balanced". His other eponymous results include the Lerner index of monopoly power (the price–cost margin), the Lerner symmetry theorem (a tax on imports is equivalent to a tax on exports), and the Marshall–Lerner condition for when a devaluation improves the trade balance. Late in life he proposed the Market Anti-Inflation Plan (MAP), a tradable-permit scheme against inflation.
