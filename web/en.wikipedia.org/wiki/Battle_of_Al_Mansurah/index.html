Battle of Al Mansurah

The Battle of Al Mansurah was fought from 8 to 11 February 1250, during the Seventh Crusade, between the Crusader army led by King Louis IX of France and Egyptian Ayyubid forces reinforced by the Bahri Mamluks. It was a decisive Egyptian victory that doomed the crusade and led to Louis's capture.

Battle

Louis had seized Damietta in 1249 and marched up the Nile toward Cairo, encamping before the fortified town of Al Mansurah, founded after an earlier crusade. On 8 February a Coptic guide showed a ford; the vanguard under the king's brother Robert of Artois crossed and routed the Egyptian camp, killing commander Fakhr-ad-Din, but against orders charged into Mansurah's streets, where Mamluks under future sultan Baibars trapped and slaughtered them, including most of the Templar contingent. Louis's main force held a bridgehead under heavy attack, using Greek fire defences, but disease and blocked supplies destroyed the army.

Aftermath

The starving Crusaders attempted retreat in April and were destroyed at Fariskur; Louis was ransomed for an enormous sum and Damietta's return. The defeat hastened the end of Ayyubid rule: Sultan Turanshah was murdered weeks later, ushering in the Mamluk Sultanate. See also Seventh Crusade.
