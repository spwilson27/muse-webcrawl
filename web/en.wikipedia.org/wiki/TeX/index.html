TeX (pronounced "tek", and often typeset as TEX) is a typesetting program created by Donald Knuth, first released in 1978. It is popular in academia, especially in mathematics, computer science, and physics, because of the high quality of its typesetting of mathematical formulas and its stability. TeX is free software and is generally considered to be one of the most successful digital typographic systems.

History

Knuth began developing TeX in 1977 out of frustration with the declining quality of typesetting of his book series The Art of Computer Programming, particularly after the second edition of Volume 2 was reset with phototypesetting. He expected the project to take six months; it eventually occupied him, on and off, for a decade, with the definitive version frozen in 1989 as version 3. Alongside TeX, Knuth created the Metafont font-description language and the Computer Modern family of typefaces.

TeX's version number famously converges to π: each successive bug-fix release appends a digit, and the current version is 3.141592653. Knuth has declared that upon his death the version number will become exactly π and no further changes will be made. He also offers a monetary reward — a check for $327.68, one "hexadecimal dollar" — to the first finder of each genuine bug.

The system

TeX takes text files containing markup commands and produces device-independent DVI output (today usually converted to PDF). Its paragraph line-breaking algorithm considers each paragraph as a whole, minimising overall "badness" and producing exceptionally even text. Its hyphenation, ligature, and kerning handling, together with the mathematics typesetting designed by Knuth, set standards other systems still emulate.

TeX is a macro language with some 300 primitive commands, extended by format packages. The most widely used is LaTeX, created by Leslie Lamport, which provides high-level document structure commands. Modern engines extending TeX include pdfTeX, XeTeX, and LuaTeX, adding direct PDF output, Unicode, and system-font support. Distributions such as TeX Live and MiKTeX bundle the engine with thousands of packages maintained through the CTAN archive.

Use

TeX and LaTeX are the standard for scholarly publishing in mathematics and the physical sciences, and many publishers accept or require submissions in LaTeX. The name TeX derives from the Greek τέχνη (techne, "art" or "craft"), hence Knuth's prescribed pronunciation with the Greek chi.
