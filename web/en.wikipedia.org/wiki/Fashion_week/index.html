A fashion week is a fashion industry event, lasting approximately one week, where fashion designers, brands or houses display their latest collections in runway fashion shows to buyers and the media. These events influence the upcoming seasons' trends. The most prominent fashion weeks are held in the fashion capitals New York, London, Milan and Paris — known as the "Big Four" — though dozens of other cities now host their own.

History

The first organised fashion week was held in New York in 1943, organised by publicist Eleanor Lambert as "Press Week" to showcase American designers while the Second World War made travel to Paris impossible. Paris began coordinated shows in 1973 under the Chambre Syndicale; Milan and London established their weeks in 1958 and 1984 respectively. The traditional schedule runs New York first, then London, Milan and Paris, with womenswear collections shown in February/March (autumn/winter) and September/October (spring/summer), and menswear and haute couture weeks at other points in the year.

Format and changes

Shows historically presented collections six months ahead of retail, allowing buyers to place orders; the rise of fast fashion and social media has pressured this model, prompting "see now, buy now" experiments, livestreamed shows and digital presentations, accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Fashion weeks are also major media and celebrity events, and increasingly debated for their environmental footprint and the diversity of models cast.
