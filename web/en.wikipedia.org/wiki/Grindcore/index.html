Grindcore

Stylistic originsExtreme metal, hardcore punk, crust punk, death metal
Cultural originsMid-1980s, England and the United States
Key actsNapalm Death, Carcass, Terrorizer, Brutal Truth, Pig Destroyer
CharacteristicsBlast beats, very short songs, abrasive distortion, shouted or growled vocals

Grindcore is an extreme fusion genre of heavy metal and hardcore punk that originated in the mid-1980s, drawing inspiration from abrasive-sounding musical styles such as thrashcore, crust punk, hardcore punk, extreme metal, and industrial. Grindcore is considered a more noise-filled style of hardcore punk while employing most of that genre's characteristics.

Characteristics

The genre is defined by heavily distorted, down-tuned guitars, blast-beat drumming at extreme tempos, and songs that are often only seconds or a couple of minutes long. Napalm Death's "You Suffer" (1987), lasting about 1.3 seconds, is cited by Guinness World Records as the shortest song ever recorded. Vocals may be growled, shrieked, or shouted, and lyrics frequently address politics, gore, or dark humour.

History

Birmingham's Napalm Death pioneered the style with Scum (1987); the term "grindcore" is attributed to their drummer Mick Harris. British label Earache Records and bands such as Carcass and Bolt Thrower spread the genre, while American groups including Terrorizer, Repulsion, and later Brutal Truth and Pig Destroyer developed it further.

Subgenres and offshoots

Related styles include goregrind, pornogrind, deathgrind, powerviolence, and mathcore.
