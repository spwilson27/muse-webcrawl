The Orange Blossom Classic is an American college football game featuring historically black colleges and universities (HBCUs). It was founded in 1933 and originally held in Miami, Florida, at the Miami Orange Bowl, where it became one of the premier postseason-style events for Black college football during the era of segregation, when Black teams were barred from the major bowl games.

History

The inaugural Orange Blossom Classic was played in 1933, with Florida A&M among its most frequent participants. In its heyday the game drew large crowds and showcased legendary coaches such as Jake Gaither of Florida A&M. The classic featured a parade and festivities that made it a cultural centerpiece of Black college football. The game was discontinued after the 1978 edition as integration changed the landscape of college football and the big bowls began admitting Black players and teams.

Revival

After a hiatus of more than four decades, the Orange Blossom Classic was revived in 2021 at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens. The revived game is played over Labor Day weekend as a regular-season matchup between HBCU programs, typically featuring Florida A&M and an opponent such as Jackson State, accompanied by a week of community events, concerts, and a battle of the bands.
