Ōsumi Islands

The Ōsumi Islands (大隅諸島, Ōsumi-shotō) are an archipelago in the Nansei Islands, and form the northernmost group of the Satsunan Islands, which in turn are part of the Ryukyu island chain. Administratively the islands belong to Kagoshima Prefecture, Japan. The group stretches south-west from the Ōsumi Peninsula of Kyushu and includes volcanic islands formed along the Ryukyu arc.

Geography

The principal islands are Tanegashima, Yakushima, Kuchinoerabujima, Magejima, Kuroshima, Iōjima and Takeshima. Tanegashima, the largest and easternmost, is long and low, famous as the place where firearms were introduced to Japan in 1543 (the "Tanegashima" matchlock) and as the site of the Tanegashima Space Center, Japan's largest launch facility. Yakushima, a rugged, mountainous circular island, is covered by ancient cedar forests (including the famous Jōmon Sugi) and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993. Kuchinoerabujima is an active volcanic island whose eruptions, notably in 2015, have required the evacuation of its small population. Iōjima hosts the active Iōdake volcano and, like other remote islands of the region, is associated with the medieval tradition of political exiles banished from the capital.

History

The islands historically formed part of Ōsumi Province and were ruled by the Shimazu clan of Satsuma Domain. They were a gateway for trade and contact with the Ryukyu Kingdom and beyond; in 1543 a Portuguese ship drifted ashore at Tanegashima, introducing the arquebus to Japan. During the Second World War the islands saw kamikaze operations and were occupied by the United States after 1945, returning to Japanese administration in 1953 along with the rest of the Satsunan Islands south of 30°N.
