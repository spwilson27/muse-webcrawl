Dreidel

A dreidel (Yiddish: דרײדל, draydl; Hebrew: סביבון, sevivon) is a four-sided spinning top, played during the Jewish holiday of Hanukkah. Each side of the dreidel bears a Hebrew letter: נ (nun), ג (gimel), ה (hei) and ש (shin). These letters are an acronym for the Hebrew phrase Nes Gadol Haya Sham — "a great miracle happened there" — referring to the Hanukkah miracle of the oil in the Second Temple.

The game

Players sit in a circle with a central pot of tokens — often coins, nuts, raisins or chocolate coins (Hanukkah gelt). Each player antes a token into the pot and takes turns spinning the dreidel:

  • Nun — do nothing.
  • Gimel — take the whole pot.
  • Hei — take half the pot.
  • Shin — put one (or two) tokens into the pot.

A player who runs out of tokens is out; the game typically continues until one player has won all the tokens. Dreidels are made of plastic, wood, metal or, in decorative versions, crystal and silver, and are spun with a peg at the top between finger and thumb.

History

The dreidel game is generally understood to descend from the European gambling top game known in England and Ireland as "teetotum" (and in German as a game of N = Nichts, G = Ganz, H = Halb, S = Stell ein), adapted by German Jews in the early modern period. A popular folk tradition holds that Jews in the time of Antiochus IV used dreidel games to conceal Torah study from Greek soldiers. In Israel, dreidels replace the shin with a פ (pei), for Nes Gadol Haya Po — "a great miracle happened here". The dreidel is celebrated in the well-known Hanukkah song "I Have a Little Dreidel".
