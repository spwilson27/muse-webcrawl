The Kaveri River water dispute (also spelled Cauvery) is a long-running conflict over the sharing of the waters of the Kaveri River in southern India among the states of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala and the union territory of Puducherry. It is one of India's most politically charged interstate disputes, periodically erupting into protests and violence, notably in 1991 and 2016.

Background

The Kaveri rises in Karnataka and flows through Tamil Nadu to the Bay of Bengal. Agreements between the princely state of Mysore and the Madras Presidency in 1892 and 1924 regulated dam construction and water use. After independence and the reorganisation of states, Tamil Nadu argued for the protection of its established irrigation in the delta, while Karnataka sought to expand irrigation and meet the drinking-water needs of growing Bengaluru.

Tribunal and verdicts

The central government constituted the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal in 1990. Its final award (2007) allocated the river's yield among the four parties. On appeal, the Supreme Court of India in February 2018 slightly increased Karnataka's share — to supply Bengaluru's drinking water — and reduced Tamil Nadu's, while directing the creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority to oversee releases, especially in deficit monsoon years, when conflict recurs.
