Blue Mountain Peak is the highest mountain in Jamaica and one of the highest peaks in the Caribbean, at 2,256 metres (7,402 ft). It is the highest point in the Blue Mountains range in eastern Jamaica and lies on the border of Portland and Saint Thomas parishes. The surrounding Blue and John Crow Mountains National Park was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015.

Geography and climate

The summit is frequently shrouded in mist, which gives the Blue Mountains their name and bluish appearance. Temperatures at the peak can fall close to freezing, far cooler than the coast; frost has occasionally been recorded. The slopes support montane cloud forest rich in endemic species, including tree ferns, bromeliads and the Jamaican blackbird, and the lower slopes are famous for Blue Mountain coffee, among the most expensive coffees in the world.

Climbing

The standard route begins at Whitfield Hall near Mavis Bank, ascending about 11 km (7 miles) of trail. Hikers traditionally climb at night to reach the summit for sunrise, when on a clear day Cuba, some 145 km away, can be seen. The trail passes through the Portland Gap ranger station and is well maintained, though steep and often muddy.

Flora and fauna

The peak's elfin woodland is stunted by wind and cloud. The area is a stronghold for endemic birds such as the Jamaican tody and the ring-tailed pigeon, and for the giant swallowtail, the largest butterfly in the Americas.
