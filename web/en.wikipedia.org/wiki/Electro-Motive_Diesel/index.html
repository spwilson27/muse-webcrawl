Electro-Motive Diesel

Electro-Motive Diesel, Inc.
Founded: 1922 (as Electro-Motive Company)
Headquarters: La Grange, Illinois, USA
Industry: diesel-electric locomotives
Parent: Progress Rail (Caterpillar)

Electro-Motive Diesel (EMD) is an American manufacturer of diesel-electric locomotives, locomotive products, and diesel engines for the rail industry. Originally the Electro-Motive Division of General Motors, it was for much of the twentieth century the world's largest builder of diesel locomotives.

History

Founded in 1922, the company pioneered diesel-electric railcars and, in 1939, introduced the FT freight locomotive, which demonstrated that diesels could replace steam on main lines. Its E-units and F-units powered famous streamlined passenger trains, and the GP and SD series became staples of North American freight railroading.

Products

EMD is known for its two-stroke diesel engines, including the 567, 645, and 710 series. Locomotive models such as the GP9, SD40-2, and SD70 series were produced in the thousands and exported worldwide.

Ownership

General Motors sold the division in 2005 to private equity investors, who renamed it Electro-Motive Diesel. Caterpillar acquired it in 2010 through its Progress Rail subsidiary, and production shifted to plants including Muncie, Indiana.
