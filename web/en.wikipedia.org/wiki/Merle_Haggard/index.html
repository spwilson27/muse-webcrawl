Merle Ronald Haggard (April 6, 1937 – April 6, 2016) was an American country music singer, songwriter, guitarist, and fiddler. Widely regarded as one of the greatest country musicians of all time, he was a central figure in the Bakersfield sound and later the outlaw country movement.

Early life and prison years

Haggard was born in Oildale, California, during the Great Depression, to parents who had migrated from Oklahoma — the "Okie" experience that would shape his songwriting. His father died when Merle was nine, and the young Haggard turned rebellious, running away from home and committing a string of petty crimes. After a botched robbery attempt, he was sent to San Quentin in 1958. There, seeing Johnny Cash perform on New Year's Day 1959 inspired him to pursue music seriously. He was paroled in 1960; in 1972 Governor Ronald Reagan granted him a full pardon.

Career

Haggard signed with Capitol Records and scored his first top-10 hit with "(My Friends Are Gonna Be) Strangers" in 1965. Songs such as "Branded Man", "Sing Me Back Home", "Mama Tried" (1968) and "Hungry Eyes" drew on his prison years and working-class roots. His 1969 song "Okie from Muskogee", a defence of small-town values against the counterculture, became his signature hit and won the Country Music Association Single of the Year award; the follow-up "The Fightin' Side of Me" (1970) reinforced the image, though Haggard's views evolved over time and he later distanced himself from some of its sentiments.

With his band the Strangers, Haggard placed 38 number-one hits on the Billboard country charts between the 1960s and the 1980s, including "Workin' Man Blues", "If We Make It Through December", "Ramblin' Fever", "I Think I'll Just Stay Here and Drink", "Big City" and "Pancho and Lefty", a 1983 duet with Willie Nelson. His guitar playing, influenced by Bob Wills's western swing and Lefty Frizzell's phrasing, helped define the lean, electric Bakersfield sound pioneered with Buck Owens.

Legacy and honours

Haggard was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1994, received a Kennedy Center Honor in 2010, and won Grammy Awards including Best Country Vocal Performance for "That's the Way Love Goes" (1984) and a Grammy Hall of Fame induction for "Mama Tried". He has been cited as an influence by generations of country, rock and Americana musicians.

Death

Haggard, who had survived lung cancer surgery in 2008, died of complications from pneumonia at his home in Palo Cedro, California, on April 6, 2016 — his 79th birthday.
