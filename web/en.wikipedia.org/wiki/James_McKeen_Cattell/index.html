James McKeen Cattell

James McKeen Cattell (May 25, 1860 – January 20, 1944) was an American psychologist, the first professor of psychology in the United States, and a long-time editor and publisher who promoted the cause of science through journals including Science, The Scientific Monthly and American Men of Science.

Career

Cattell studied at Lafayette College, where his father was president, and in Germany under Wilhelm Wundt, whose assistant he became; he also worked with Francis Galton in England. He received his PhD from Leipzig in 1886. After teaching at the University of Pennsylvania, where he held the first American chair titled Professor of Psychology, he moved in 1891 to Columbia University, heading its psychology department for 26 years.

Contributions

Cattell pioneered the experimental study of individual differences and mental testing in America; his 1890 paper "Mental Tests and Measurements" introduced the term "mental test". He founded the Psychological Corporation in 1921 to apply psychological testing in industry. As editor of Science from 1894 and publisher of several other journals, and through his biographical directory American Men of Science (1906), he shaped American scientific institutions.

Dismissal and later life

Columbia dismissed Cattell in 1917 for his public opposition to conscription during World War I; he sued and won a settlement. He helped found the American Association of University Professors. He died in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, in 1944.
