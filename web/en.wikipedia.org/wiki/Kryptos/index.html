Kryptos

Kryptos is a sculpture by the American artist Jim Sanborn located on the grounds of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) headquarters, the George Bush Center for Intelligence in Langley, Virginia. Since its dedication on November 3, 1990, there has been much speculation about the meaning of the four encrypted messages it bears. Of these four messages, the first three have been solved, while the fourth message remains one of the most famous unsolved codes in the world; the sculptor has given several clues to it.

Description

The sculpture consists mainly of a large vertical S-shaped copper screen resembling a piece of paper emerging from a computer printer, half of which consists of encrypted text, together with a petrified-wood structure, a water-filled basin and other elements including a compass needle. The four passages of ciphertext contain 869 characters in total. The cryptographic systems were designed with the assistance of Ed Scheidt, a retired chairman of the CIA's Cryptographic Center; the first two passages use a Vigenère cipher with the keywords KRYPTOS and PALIMPSEST, the third uses a transposition cipher, and it quotes Howard Carter's account of opening Tutankhamun's tomb. The passage ends: "…the Berlin Clock?" — a clue Sanborn released in 2014, followed by "BERLIN" (2010) and the word "CLOCK" region, and in 2020 the word "NORTHEAST" as the plaintext for characters at positions 26–34 of the fourth passage.

The name comes from the Greek word for "hidden". Misspellings in the deciphered text (such as "IQLUSION" and "UNDERGRUUND") were deliberate, Sanborn has said, intended to add difficulty and texture. The unsolved fourth passage, known as K4, has attracted thousands of amateur attempts and professional cryptanalysis; in 2025 Sanborn announced that the solution would be auctioned.
