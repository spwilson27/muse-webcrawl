Palermo Protocol

The Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children, commonly known as the Palermo Protocol or the Trafficking Protocol, is a protocol to the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organized Crime. It was adopted in Palermo, Italy, in 2000 and entered into force on 25 December 2003.

Signed15 November 2000 (adopted), Palermo
Effective25 December 2003
PartiesMore than 180 states
DepositarySecretary-General of the United Nations

Contents

The Protocol provides the first internationally agreed definition of "trafficking in persons": the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons by means of threat, force, coercion, abduction, fraud, deception or abuse of power or of a position of vulnerability, for the purpose of exploitation. The consent of a victim is irrelevant where any of those means are used, and for children no improper means need be shown.

States parties undertake to criminalise trafficking, protect and assist victims — including with housing, counselling, and consideration of residence status — and cooperate on prevention, information exchange and border measures. The Protocol is one of three Palermo protocols, the others addressing the smuggling of migrants and illicit firearms.
