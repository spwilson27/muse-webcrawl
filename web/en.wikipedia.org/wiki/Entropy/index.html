Entropy is a scientific concept, most commonly associated with disorder or randomness, that is central to thermodynamics and statistical mechanics. Rudolf Clausius introduced it in 1865 in formulating the second law of thermodynamics: the entropy of an isolated system never decreases.

Meaning

Ludwig Boltzmann gave entropy a statistical meaning — it measures the number of microscopic arrangements (microstates) consistent with a system's macroscopic state, expressed on his tombstone as S = k log W. Entropy explains why heat flows from hot to cold, why heat engines cannot be perfectly efficient, and, in information theory, quantifies uncertainty in a message.
