Dialect continuum
Field: Dialectology, sociolinguistics
Examples: West Germanic, Romance, Arabic, Chinese
Related: Dialect, Mutual intelligibility

A dialect continuum or dialect chain is a series of language varieties spoken across a geographical area such that neighboring varieties are mutually intelligible, but distant ones may not be.

Examples

Classic examples include the West Germanic continuum from the Netherlands through Germany, the Romance continuum across France, Spain and Italy, the Arabic dialects from Morocco to Iraq, and the varieties often grouped as Chinese.

Implications

Continua blur the boundary between language and dialect: political borders and standard languages often cut across gradual linguistic change. Standardization and mass media can break up continua by leveling local varieties toward national standards.

Ring species analogy

Linguists sometimes compare continua to ring species in biology, where neighboring populations interbreed but endpoints cannot.
