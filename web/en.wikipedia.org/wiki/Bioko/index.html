Bioko (historically Fernando Po or Fernando Póo) is an island 32 kilometres off the west coast of Africa and the northernmost part of Equatorial Guinea, of which it hosts the capital, Malabo. The island has a population of about 335,000 and an area of 2,017 km². It is of volcanic origin and is dominated by the extinct volcano Pico Basilé, which rises to 3,011 metres.

Geography

Bioko lies in the Gulf of Guinea, about 100 km from Equatorial Guinea's mainland territory of Río Muni. The island is mountainous and densely forested, with a wet equatorial climate. Two administrative provinces, Bioko Norte and Bioko Sur, divide the island. The southern part is remote and largely covered by the Gran Caldera and Southern Highlands Scientific Reserve.

History

The island's indigenous inhabitants are the Bubi people, who speak a Bantu language distinct from those of the mainland. The Portuguese navigator Fernão do Pó sighted the island around 1472, and it passed between Portuguese, Spanish and British control; Britain leased it as a naval and anti-slavery base in the early nineteenth century, founding Port Clarence (now Malabo). Spain reasserted control and developed cocoa plantations worked by migrant labour, and the island became part of independent Equatorial Guinea in 1968. It was renamed Bioko in 1979, after the Bubi politician Cristino Seriche Bioko.

Wildlife

Bioko's forests are rich in endemic wildlife, including several primate species such as the drill and the red-eared guenon, and the island is an important nesting site for sea turtles. Conservation groups work to protect its wildlife from bushmeat hunting.
