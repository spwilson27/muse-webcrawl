Proa

A proa is a type of multi-hull sailing vessel with one main hull and a smaller outrigger float (the ama) connected by crossbeams (the aka). Developed by the Austronesian peoples of the Pacific and Indian Oceans, proas were among the fastest sailing craft of the pre-modern world and were used for voyaging, trade and fishing across Oceania.

Design

The classic Pacific proa keeps the outrigger always to windward and reverses direction end-for-end when tacking — "shunting" — so the bow becomes the stern; the sail, traditionally a crab-claw or Oceanic lateen, is repositioned accordingly. The hull is often asymmetric to generate lift and counteract the sideways force of the rig. Atlantic proas, by contrast, keep the outrigger to leeward and tack conventionally.

History

European explorers encountered proas in Micronesia and the Mariana Islands, where the vessels were called "flying proas" for their speed, reportedly exceeding 20 knots in favourable conditions. Traditional proas remain in use in parts of Micronesia and Indonesia, and modern designers have adapted the concept for racing and record attempts.
