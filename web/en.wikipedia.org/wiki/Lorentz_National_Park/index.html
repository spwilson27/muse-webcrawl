Lorentz National Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lorentz National Park Location: Central Papua, Indonesia
Area: 25,056 km² (9,674 sq mi)
Established: 1997
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 1999

Lorentz National Park is an Indonesian national park located in the province of Central Papua in the island of New Guinea. With an area of about 25,056 square kilometres (9,674 sq mi), it is the largest protected area in Southeast Asia. In 1999 Lorentz was declared a World Heritage Site by UNESCO.

Geography

The park is remarkable for being the only protected area in the Asia-Pacific region to contain a full altitudinal array of ecosystems, spanning marine areas, mangroves, tidal and freshwater swamp forest, lowland and montane rainforest, alpine tundra, and the equatorial glaciers of the Sudirman Range. At 4,884 metres, Puncak Jaya (Carstensz Pyramid) is the tallest mountain between the Himalayas and the Andes, and the park shelters some of the last remaining tropical glaciers in the world, which are retreating rapidly.

Biodiversity

Lorentz contains many unexplored and unmapped areas and is thought to hold exceptional biological diversity, including many endemic species. It is home to wildlife such as the southern cassowary, tree kangaroos, echidnas and numerous birds of paradise. Much of the park remains poorly surveyed scientifically because of its remoteness and rugged terrain.

People

The park is also notable for its cultural diversity: it is the traditional home of numerous indigenous peoples, including the Asmat, Amungme, Dani, Sempan and Nduga, many of whom continue to live within its boundaries. The park is named after Hendrikus Albertus Lorentz, a Dutch explorer who passed through the area on his 1909–10 expedition.
