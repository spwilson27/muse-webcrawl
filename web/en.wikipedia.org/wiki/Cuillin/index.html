The Cuillin (Scottish Gaelic: An Cuiltheann) is a range of rocky mountains on the Isle of Skye in Scotland, regarded as the finest mountain range in the British Isles for mountaineering. The main Cuillin ridge, the Black Cuillin, is formed of gabbro — a rough, dark, exceptionally grippy rock — and basalt, remnants of the eroded magma chamber of an ancient volcano active about 60 million years ago. Its highest point is Sgùrr Alasdair at 992 metres (3,255 ft). Separated from it by Glen Sligachan, the smoother, scree-covered Red Cuillin (the Red Hills) are of granite.

Peaks and traverse

The Black Cuillin includes twelve Munros (Scottish peaks over 3,000 ft), among them Sgùrr nan Gillean, Bruach na Frìthe, Am Basteir with its famous pinnacle the Basteir Tooth, and the Inaccessible Pinnacle ("In Pinn") of Sgùrr Dearg — the only Munro whose summit requires rock climbing, first climbed by the Pilkington brothers in 1880. The traverse of the full 11–12 km main ridge, with some 3,900 m of ascent and several graded rock climbs, is considered the most demanding mountaineering expedition in the United Kingdom, typically taking strong parties 15–20 hours or more in a single push. The ridge is also notorious for local magnetic anomalies caused by the iron-rich gabbro, which can deflect compasses.

Culture and ownership

The Cuillin have been celebrated in Gaelic song and in Sorley MacLean's poetry. In 2000 the range was offered for sale by the MacLeod clan chief, reportedly at £10 million to fund repairs to Dunvegan Castle, provoking a public controversy over land ownership; the sale did not proceed. Approaches are made from Sligachan, Glen Brittle, and Elgol, with Loch Coruisk — reachable by boat or on foot — at the range's heart.
