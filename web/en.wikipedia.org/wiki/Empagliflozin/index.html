Empagliflozin, sold under the brand name Jardiance among others, is an antidiabetic medication used to improve glucose control in people with type 2 diabetes. It is also used to reduce the risk of cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease, and to treat heart failure and chronic kidney disease. It was developed by Boehringer Ingelheim and is co-marketed with Eli Lilly and Company.

Mechanism of action

Empagliflozin is an inhibitor of the sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2), which is found almost exclusively in the proximal tubules of the kidney. By blocking SGLT2 it reduces glucose reabsorption and increases urinary glucose excretion, lowering blood sugar independently of insulin.

Clinical evidence

The EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial (2015) showed that empagliflozin reduced the composite outcome of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction or nonfatal stroke in patients with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk, and reduced hospitalisation for heart failure. Subsequent trials (EMPEROR-Reduced and EMPEROR-Preserved) extended its use to heart failure with reduced and preserved ejection fraction, and the EMPA-KIDNEY trial demonstrated benefit in chronic kidney disease.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include urinary tract infections and genital mycotic infections. Rare but serious risks include euglycaemic diabetic ketoacidosis, lower-limb amputation concerns shared with the class, and Fournier's gangrene.
