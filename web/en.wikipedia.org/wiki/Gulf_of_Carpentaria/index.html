Gulf of Carpentaria

Gulf of Carpentaria
Location: Northern Australia
Coordinates: 14°S 139°E
Area: about 300,000 km²

The Gulf of Carpentaria is a large, shallow sea enclosed on three sides by northern Australia and bounded on the north by the Torres Strait and the Arafura Sea. It lies between Cape York Peninsula in Queensland to the east and Arnhem Land in the Northern Territory to the west. The gulf covers about 300,000 square kilometres, and its shoreline is fringed by extensive mudflats, mangroves, and salt pans.

Geography

The gulf is generally shallow, with depths mostly between 55 and 66 metres and no more than about 82 metres. Major islands include Groote Eylandt, the Wellesley Islands, and Vanderlin Island. Rivers flowing into the gulf include the Flinders, Norman, Gilbert, Leichhardt, Nicholson, Roper, and McArthur rivers. During glacial periods of lower sea level, the gulf basin contained a large freshwater lake known as Lake Carpentaria.

Climate and phenomena

The region has a tropical monsoon climate with a wet season from December to April. From September to November the gulf gives rise to the Morning Glory cloud, a rare roll cloud that can grow hundreds of kilometres long and is prized by glider pilots. Tropical cyclones cross the gulf in the summer months.

History and exploration

The lands around the gulf have been inhabited by Aboriginal peoples for tens of thousands of years. The first European exploration is generally credited to the Dutch: Willem Janszoon's voyage of 1605–06 reached the western coast of Cape York, and the gulf was later named for Pieter de Carpentier, Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies. Matthew Flinders surveyed the coast in 1802–03, and Ludwig Leichhardt crossed the region overland in 1844–45.

Economy

The gulf supports important prawn and barramundi fisheries, and its shores host major mining and port developments, including manganese on Groote Eylandt and zinc at the McArthur River mine, shipped from Bing Bong.
