DVB-T

DVB-T, short for Digital Video Broadcasting — Terrestrial, is the DVB European-based consortium standard for the broadcast transmission of digital terrestrial television. It was first published by ETSI in 1997 and first deployed in the United Kingdom in 1998 by ONdigital.

Technology

DVB-T uses coded orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (COFDM) with either 2k or 8k carriers in a 6, 7 or 8 MHz channel. Modulation can be QPSK, 16-QAM or 64-QAM, giving net bit rates of roughly 5 to 31 Mbit/s per channel, typically carrying a multiplex of several standard-definition programmes in MPEG-2 or MPEG-4 AVC coding. The system supports hierarchical transmission and single-frequency networks.

Adoption and successor

DVB-T was adopted across Europe, Australia, and many countries in Asia, Africa and Latin America for the analogue switch-off. Its successor, DVB-T2, published in 2008, increases capacity by about 30–50% and is used for HD and UHD terrestrial services in many markets.
