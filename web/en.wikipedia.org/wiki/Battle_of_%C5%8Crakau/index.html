The Battle of Ōrākau was a battle fought from 31 March to 2 April 1864 during the Invasion of the Waikato, part of the New Zealand Wars. Around 300 Māori defenders, including women and children, held a hastily built pā at Ōrākau against some 1,400 British and colonial troops.

Background

After the fall of Rangiriri and Paterangi, Kīngitanga forces fell back into the Waikato interior. Rewi Maniapoto and others occupied a position at Ōrākau that lacked a water supply and was poorly sited, but the defenders refused to abandon it.

The siege

British forces under Brigadier General Carey surrounded the pā and bombarded it with Armstrong guns. When called on to surrender, the defenders famously replied — in words later much quoted — "Ka whawhai tonu mātou, ake! ake! ake!" ("We will fight on for ever and ever!"). On the third day, out of water and ammunition, the defenders broke out through the British lines; many were killed in the pursuit, but a substantial number escaped.

Legacy

Ōrākau became one of the most remembered episodes of the New Zealand Wars, symbolising Māori courage and resistance. Casualty figures are disputed, but Māori losses were heavy, with perhaps 160 killed.
