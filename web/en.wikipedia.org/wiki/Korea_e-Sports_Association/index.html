Korea e-Sports Association

The Korea e-Sports Association (KeSPA; Korean: 한국e스포츠협회) is a South Korean body, founded in 2000 under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, that manages and promotes professional competitive gaming (esports) in South Korea. At its height it oversaw 22 professional teams and more than a dozen corporate titles, and it set many of the institutional templates — licensed pro gamers, televised leagues, standardised rules and refereeing — later copied by the global esports industry.

History

KeSPA was created during the explosive growth of PC bang culture and televised StarCraft: Brood War. It administered the KeSPA Cup, certified professional gamers, negotiated broadcast rights with the cable channels OnGameNet and MBCGame, and ran the team-based Proleague (2003–2016), for years the premier StarCraft competition in the world. The association also selected national teams for international events and maintained the influential KeSPA player rankings.

Controversies

In 2010 a match-fixing scandal involving several professional StarCraft players led to bans and criminal convictions and shook public confidence in the sport; KeSPA responded with stricter oversight. The association also fought a long intellectual-property dispute with Blizzard Entertainment over broadcast rights to StarCraft competitions, settled in 2011. As Korean esports shifted toward League of Legends and publisher-run leagues in the 2010s, KeSPA's direct control over top-tier leagues diminished, and it refocused on amateur development, national teams, and school and regional competitions.
