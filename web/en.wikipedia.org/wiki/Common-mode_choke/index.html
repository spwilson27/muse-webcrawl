Common-mode choke

A toroidal common-mode choke used for EMI suppression.

A common-mode choke is an electrical component consisting of two coils wound on a single magnetic core. It is designed to block common-mode currents — currents flowing in the same direction on a pair of conductors — while passing differential-mode currents, which flow in opposite directions.

Principle of operation

The two windings are arranged so that the magnetic fluxes produced by differential-mode currents cancel each other, presenting only low impedance to the wanted signal. Common-mode currents, by contrast, produce fluxes that add, so the choke presents a high inductive impedance to them and attenuates common-mode noise. Remaining noise may also be absorbed as heat in a lossy ferrite core.

Applications

  • Suppression of electromagnetic interference (EMI) on power supply and data lines.
  • Filtering of noise on USB, Ethernet, HDMI and other balanced interfaces.
  • Reduction of conducted emissions in switched-mode power supplies to meet EMC regulations.
  • Baluns and impedance matching in radio-frequency circuits, in the related choke balun configuration.

Construction

Common-mode chokes are commonly wound on toroidal ferrite cores, E-cores, or implemented as coupled inductors in surface-mount packages for circuit boards. Design parameters include inductance, current rating, DC resistance, and the frequency range over which attenuation is required.
