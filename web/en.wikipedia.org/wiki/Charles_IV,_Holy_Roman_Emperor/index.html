Charles IV (Czech: Karel IV.; German: Karl IV.; Latin: Carolus IV; 14 May 1316 – 29 November 1378), also known as Charles of Luxembourg, born Wenceslaus, was the first King of Bohemia to become Holy Roman Emperor. A member of the House of Luxembourg, he was elected anti-king of the Romans in 1346, crowned King of Bohemia the same year, and crowned emperor in Rome in 1355. His reign is often regarded as a golden age of Bohemian history.

Early life

Charles was the eldest son of King John of Bohemia and Elizabeth, sister of Wenceslaus III, the last Přemyslid king. Raised at the French court of his uncle Charles IV of France, he received a thorough education, learned several languages, and took the name Charles at his confirmation. He served as regent in Bohemia from 1333 and administered northern Italy for his father.

Reign

As emperor, Charles issued the Golden Bull of 1356, which fixed the constitution of the Empire and the procedure for imperial elections for centuries, recognising seven prince-electors. He made Prague the imperial capital, founded Charles University in 1348 — the first university in Central Europe — and commissioned great building works including St. Vitus Cathedral, the New Town of Prague, Karlštejn Castle and the Charles Bridge. By purchase and diplomacy he expanded the Luxembourg lands, acquiring Brandenburg in 1373.

Legacy

Charles IV is remembered in the Czech lands as Otec vlasti ("Father of the Homeland"). In 2005 he was voted the greatest Czech historical figure in a national television poll. He was succeeded in Bohemia and the Empire by his son Wenceslaus IV.
