Ole Rømer

Ole Christensen Rømer (1644–1710) was a Danish astronomer who, in 1676, made the first quantitative measurement of the speed of light, demonstrating that light travels at a finite speed. He also invented the modern thermometer scale and served as Denmark's royal astronomer and chief of the Copenhagen police.

Measurement of the speed of light

Working at the Paris Observatory, Rømer noticed that the eclipses of Jupiter's moon Io appeared delayed when Earth was moving away from Jupiter and early when approaching. He correctly attributed the discrepancy to the time light takes to cross Earth's orbit, estimating light's speed at about 220,000 km/s — remarkably close to the true value given the era's instruments.

Other work

Rømer invented the meridian circle and the Rømer temperature scale, on which Daniel Fahrenheit later based his scale. Most of his manuscripts were destroyed in the Copenhagen fire of 1728.
