Monster group

In the area of abstract algebra known as group theory, the Monster group M (also known as the Fischer–Griess monster, or the Friendly Giant) is the largest sporadic simple group, having order

808,017,424,794,512,875,886,459,904,961,710,757,005,754,368,000,000,000
= 246 · 320 · 59 · 76 · 112 · 133 · 17 · 19 · 23 · 29 · 31 · 41 · 47 · 59 · 71

The finite simple groups have been completely classified. Every such group belongs to one of 18 countably infinite families, or is one of 26 sporadic groups that do not follow such a systematic pattern. The Monster group contains 20 sporadic groups as subquotients, including all but six of the others. Robert Griess has called those 20 groups the "happy family", and the remaining six sporadic groups "pariahs".

History

The existence of the Monster was predicted by Bernd Fischer and Robert Griess in 1973, based on work on a certain simple group containing double covers of the Fischer groups. Griess constructed the Monster explicitly in 1980, as the group of automorphisms of the Griess algebra, a 196,884-dimensional commutative non-associative algebra. John Conway and Simon Norton showed that the smallest faithful complex representation of the Monster has dimension 196,883.

Moonshine

The Monster is central to monstrous moonshine, a surprising connection discovered by Conway and Norton between the Monster and modular functions. The coefficients of the j-function's q-expansion (1, 196884, 21493760, ...) turned out to equal sums of dimensions of irreducible representations of the Monster (e.g. 196884 = 196883 + 1). Richard Borcherds proved the moonshine conjectures in 1992, work for which he received the Fields Medal in 1998.

Representations and character table

The character table of the Monster, published in the ATLAS of Finite Groups, is a 194 × 194 matrix (the Monster has 194 conjugacy classes). The smallest nontrivial representations have dimensions 196883, 21296876, 842609326, and so on. The Monster's maximal subgroups were largely determined through work of many group theorists; it has 46 conjugacy classes of maximal subgroups.
