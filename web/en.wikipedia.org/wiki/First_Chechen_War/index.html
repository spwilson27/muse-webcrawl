The First Chechen War, also known as the First Chechen Campaign, was a conflict between the Russian Federation and the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria, fought from December 1994 to August 1996. It began when Russian forces invaded Chechnya to restore federal control over the republic, which had declared independence under Dzhokhar Dudayev after the collapse of the Soviet Union.

Course of the war

The initial Russian assault on Grozny, launched on New Year's Eve 1994, ended in disaster as armoured columns were destroyed in the city streets. Russian forces eventually captured the devastated capital in March 1995 after weeks of artillery bombardment, but Chechen fighters waged guerrilla warfare from the mountains. The war was marked by atrocities and heavy civilian casualties, including the Budyonnovsk hospital hostage crisis in June 1995. In August 1996 Chechen forces retook Grozny, forcing Moscow to negotiate.

Aftermath

The Khasavyurt Accord of 31 August 1996 ended the war, with Russian troops withdrawing and Chechnya's status deferred. Tens of thousands of civilians were killed and Grozny was left in ruins. The fragile peace lasted until the Second Chechen War began in 1999.
