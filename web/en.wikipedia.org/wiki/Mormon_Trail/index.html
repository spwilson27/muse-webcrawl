Mormon Trail


Route of the Mormon Trail, 1846–1869.

The Mormon Trail is the 1,300-mile (2,092 km) long route from Illinois to Utah on which Mormon pioneers (members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints) travelled from 1846 to 1868. Today, the Mormon Trail is a part of the United States National Trails System, known as the Mormon Pioneer National Historic Trail.

The trail began at Nauvoo, Illinois, crossed Iowa (with a winter settlement at Winter Quarters, near present-day Omaha, Nebraska), followed the Platte River through Nebraska, crossed Wyoming via Fort Laramie, South Pass and Fort Bridger, and descended through Emigration Canyon into the Salt Lake Valley.

History

After the death of Joseph Smith in 1844 and continuing conflict in Illinois, Brigham Young led the main body of Latter-day Saints west beginning in February 1846. The vanguard pioneer company reached the Salt Lake Valley on 24 July 1847 — now commemorated as Pioneer Day in Utah. Over the following two decades roughly 70,000 pioneers travelled the trail, many in wagon companies and, from 1856, in handcart companies, some of which met disaster in the Willie and Martin handcart tragedy of 1856.

Much of the route shared corridors with the Oregon Trail and California Trail, though the Mormons generally travelled on the north bank of the Platte to avoid conflict with other emigrants. The trail fell out of use with the completion of the first transcontinental railroad in 1869.
