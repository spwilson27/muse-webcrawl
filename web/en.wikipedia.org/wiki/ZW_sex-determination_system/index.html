ZW sex-determination system

The ZW sex-determination system is a chromosomal mechanism for determining the sex of offspring found in birds, some reptiles (including many snakes and monitor lizards), some fish, some crustaceans, and many insects, notably butterflies and moths. In this system the female is the heterogametic sex, carrying two different sex chromosomes (ZW), while the male is homogametic (ZZ)—the reverse of the mammalian XY system, in which males are heterogametic.


ZW inheritance pattern

Mechanism

The ovum determines the sex of the offspring: eggs carrying a Z chromosome produce ZZ males, and eggs carrying a W chromosome produce ZW females. In birds, sex determination appears to depend largely on the dosage of the Z-linked gene DMRT1—two copies in ZZ embryos drive testis development—rather than on a dominant W-linked ovary determiner, though the W chromosome carries genes such as HINTW that may contribute. In the silkworm Bombyx mori, by contrast, a piRNA produced from the W chromosome is the primary sex determinant, silencing the Z-linked masculinising gene Masc.

Evolution

The ZW systems of different groups evolved independently; the sex chromosomes of birds are not homologous with those of snakes or Lepidoptera. Some lineages have moved between systems: certain lizards show both ZW determination and temperature-dependent sex determination, and the bearded dragon can undergo sex reversal of ZZ individuals at high incubation temperatures. Unlike the mammalian Y, the W chromosome has often, but not always, degenerated; ratite birds retain large, largely undifferentiated W chromosomes.

Consequences

Because females are heterogametic, Z-linked recessive traits show criss-cross inheritance opposite to the XY pattern—this is exploited commercially in chickens, where sex-linked feathering and colour genes allow day-old chicks to be sexed by appearance, and in silkworm breeding. The Z chromosome also underlies Haldane's rule patterns in birds and Lepidoptera, where the heterogametic (female) hybrids are the sterile or inviable sex.
