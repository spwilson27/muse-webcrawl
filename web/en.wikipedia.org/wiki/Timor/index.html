Timor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Timor is an island at the southern end of Maritime Southeast Asia, in the north of the Timor Sea. It is divided between the sovereign state of East Timor (Timor-Leste) in the east and Indonesian West Timor in the west. With an area of about 30,777 km², it is the largest of the Lesser Sunda Islands. The name derives from the Malay and Indonesian word timur, meaning "east".

Geography

The island is long and narrow, oriented southwest–northeast, with a mountainous spine whose highest point, Tatamailau (Mount Ramelau) in East Timor, rises to 2,963 metres. The climate is tropical with a pronounced dry season. Timor lies on the edge of the Australian continental shelf and its rugged terrain reflects the collision between the Australian plate and the Banda volcanic arc.

History

Portuguese traders arrived in the early 16th century, drawn by sandalwood, and the island was eventually split between Portuguese (eastern) and Dutch (western) spheres, a division formalised in the 19th century. During the Second World War Timor was occupied by Japan after fierce fighting involving Australian commandos. Indonesia invaded East Timor in 1975 and occupied it until a UN-supervised referendum in 1999 led to independence in 2002. The population of the island is about three million, divided among numerous ethnolinguistic groups speaking Papuan and Austronesian languages, with Tetum and Indonesian as lingua francas.
