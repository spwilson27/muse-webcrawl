ClassificationString instrument; plucked lute
OriginIndia (from the Afghan rubab)

The sarod is a stringed instrument of Hindustani classical music, known for its deep, weighty, introspective sound. Together with the sitar it is among the most popular and prominent instruments of North Indian classical music.

Design

The sarod developed in India from the Afghan rubab in the nineteenth century. It has a hollowed wooden body covered with a goatskin soundboard, a fretless neck faced with polished metal, and typically some 17–25 strings, including playing strings, drone strings, and sympathetic strings. The absence of frets and the smooth steel fingerboard allow the continuous slides (meend) essential to raga performance. The strings are stopped with the fingernails of the left hand and plucked with a plectrum of coconut shell, ebony, or bone called a java.

Performing traditions

Major lineages (gharanas) descend from masters such as Allauddin Khan of Maihar, whose son Ali Akbar Khan brought the sarod to international fame, and from Hafiz Ali Khan, father of Amjad Ali Khan. The sarod's repertoire and technique combine the rhythmic complexity of the older dhrupad tradition with lyrical khyal-style elaboration.
