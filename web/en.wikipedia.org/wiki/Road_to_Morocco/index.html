Road to Morocco is a 1942 American comedy film starring Bing Crosby, Bob Hope and Dorothy Lamour, directed by David Butler. It is the third film in the popular "Road to..." series, following Road to Zanzibar (1941) and preceding Road to Utopia (1946).

Plot

Jeff Peters (Crosby) and Orville "Turkey" Jackson (Hope) are shipwrecked off the coast of North Africa after their freighter explodes — Orville having lit a cigarette in the hold. Washed ashore in Morocco, they ride a camel to a desert city where Jeff sells Orville into slavery to raise money; Orville is bought by Princess Shalmar (Lamour), who intends to marry him because a prophecy — and her astrologers — suggest her first husband will die, after which she may marry the man she truly loves. The pair become entangled with the desert sheik Mullay Kassim, who also wants the princess, before the scheme resolves in a comic duel of wits and songs.

Songs

The score by Jimmy Van Heusen and Johnny Burke includes the title song "(We're Off on the) Road to Morocco" and "Moonlight Becomes You", which became a hit for Crosby.

Reception and legacy

The film is often ranked the best of the series, noted for its self-referential gags — the stars address the audience, a talking camel complains about its role — and was added to discussions of classic Hollywood comedy. It was a major box-office success on its release.
