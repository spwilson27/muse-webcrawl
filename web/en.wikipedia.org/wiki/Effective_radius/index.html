Effective radius

The effective radius or half-light radius (Re) of a galaxy is the radius of the isophote which encloses half of the total light emitted by that galaxy. This means the region inside the effective radius emits as much light as the region outside it. The effective radius is a fundamental parameter in describing the structure of galaxies and is central to empirical laws such as the fundamental plane of elliptical galaxies.

Definition

The effective radius is defined together with the surface brightness at that radius, Ie. For galaxies whose light profile follows a Sérsic profile — an r1/n law — the effective radius is the scale at which the profile has fallen to a specified fraction of its central value, and it marks the radius containing half the total luminosity.

Uses

The effective radius is used to characterise galaxy sizes in observational studies of galaxy formation and evolution. Size–mass relations connect the effective radius to the stellar mass of galaxies, and these relations evolve with redshift, showing that galaxies at earlier cosmic times were typically more compact than those of comparable mass today. In de Vaucouleurs' law, which describes the surface brightness of elliptical galaxies as an r1/4 profile, the effective radius is the natural scale length of the profile.
