Engine knocking

Knocking (also knock, detonation, spark knock, pinking or pinging) in spark ignition internal combustion engines occurs when combustion of some of the air/fuel mixture in the cylinder does not result from propagation of the flame front ignited by the spark plug, but when one or more pockets of mixture explode outside the envelope of the normal combustion front.

Causes

The fuel/air charge is meant to be ignited by the spark plug only, and burn smoothly. When the unburned "end gas" is compressed and heated beyond its autoignition limit, it detonates almost simultaneously, producing a sharp pressure wave heard as a metallic pinging sound. Contributing factors include low-octane fuel, excessive compression ratio, over-advanced ignition timing, high intake temperatures and heavy load at low speed.

Effects and control

Severe or prolonged knock can damage pistons, rings and cylinder heads. Modern engines use knock sensors to detect the characteristic vibration and retard ignition timing, and fuels are rated by octane number for knock resistance.
