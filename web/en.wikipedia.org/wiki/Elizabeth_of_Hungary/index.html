Elizabeth of Hungary (German: Heilige Elisabeth von Thüringen; Hungarian: Árpád-házi Szent Erzsébet; 7 July 1207 – 17 November 1231), also known as Elizabeth of Thuringia, was a Hungarian princess and landgravine of Thuringia who became one of the most beloved saints of the medieval church. Renowned for her charity to the poor and sick, she was canonised only four years after her early death at the age of twenty-four.

Life

Elizabeth was the daughter of King Andrew II of Hungary and Gertrude of Merania. Betrothed in childhood to the heir of Thuringia, she was sent to the Wartburg castle at the age of four and married Landgrave Louis IV in 1221, when she was fourteen. The marriage was a happy one, and Elizabeth used her position to practise works of mercy, distributing food during famine, funding a hospital for the poor, and — according to legend — carrying bread to the hungry in defiance of court disapproval, the episode remembered as the "miracle of the roses".

Widowhood and charity

Louis died of plague in 1227 while travelling to join the Sixth Crusade. Elizabeth, widowed at twenty, was driven from the Wartburg by her brother-in-law and later regained her dower. Under the harsh spiritual direction of Conrad of Marburg she devoted herself entirely to prayer and service, building a hospice at Marburg where she personally nursed the sick. She joined the Third Order of St. Francis and died in 1231, her health broken by austerity and labour.

Veneration

Miracles were reported at her tomb, and Pope Gregory IX canonised her at Perugia in 1235. The Elisabethkirche in Marburg, built over her shrine, became a major pilgrimage church and a landmark of early Gothic architecture in Germany. Elizabeth remains a patron saint of charitable works, nurses, and bakers, and is widely venerated in Hungary and Germany.
