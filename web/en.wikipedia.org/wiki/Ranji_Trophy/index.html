The Ranji Trophy is the premier domestic first-class cricket championship played in India, organised by the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI). It is named after the famous Indian cricketer Kumar Shri Ranjitsinhji ("Ranji"), who played for England and Sussex, and was first contested in the 1934–35 season.

Format

Regional and state teams — 38 in recent seasons following the expansion of 2018 — compete in group stages divided into tiers (Elite and Plate), followed by knockout rounds culminating in a final. Matches are played over four days (five for the final) under first-class rules.

History and records

Mumbai (formerly Bombay) is by far the most successful team, with more than 40 titles, including a streak of 15 consecutive championships from 1958–59 to 1972–73. The tournament has been the principal proving ground for Indian Test cricketers; notable record-holders include Wasim Jaffer, the competition's highest run-scorer.
