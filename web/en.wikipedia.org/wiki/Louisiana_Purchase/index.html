Louisiana Purchase

The Louisiana Purchase was the acquisition of the territory of Louisiana by the United States from Napoleonic France in 1803. For $15 million, the US bought about 828,000 square miles (2.14 million km²) west of the Mississippi River, roughly doubling the nation's size.

Louisiana Purchase
Date: 30 April 1803 (treaty)
Seller: France (Napoleon Bonaparte)
Buyer: United States (President Thomas Jefferson)
Price: $15 million
Area: ~828,000 sq mi

Background

President Thomas Jefferson sought control of New Orleans and Mississippi navigation. Napoleon, needing funds for war and abandoning American ambitions after failure in Saint-Domingue, offered the whole territory. Envoys James Monroe and Robert Livingston accepted.

Consequences

The purchase raised constitutional questions, enabled westward expansion and prompted the Lewis and Clark Expedition. All or part of 15 present US states were formed from it, and its lands were home to many Native American nations whose sovereignty the treaty ignored.
