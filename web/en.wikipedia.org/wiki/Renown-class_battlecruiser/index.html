Renown-class battlecruiser

The Renown class consisted of two battlecruisers built for the Royal Navy during the First World War: HMS Renown and HMS Repulse. Originally laid down as improved Revenge-class battleships, they were redesigned by Admiral Sir John Fisher as fast, lightly armoured capital ships carrying 15-inch guns.

Design

The ships displaced about 27,600 long tons, were armed with six 15-inch (381 mm) guns in three twin turrets, and could reach 32 knots. Their armour, only 6 inches on the belt, was criticised as inadequate — a deficiency underlined by the loss of three battlecruisers at Jutland, which occurred while the Renowns were still building, prompting some additional protection.

Service

Both ships saw limited action in the First World War. Renown was extensively reconstructed in the 1930s and served throughout the Second World War, including operations with Force H and the hunt for the Bismarck. Repulse, less modernised, was sunk by Japanese land-based aircraft off Malaya on 10 December 1941 along with the battleship Prince of Wales. Renown survived the war and was scrapped in 1948.
