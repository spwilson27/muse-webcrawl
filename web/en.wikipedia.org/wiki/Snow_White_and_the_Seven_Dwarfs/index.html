Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs is a 1937 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Productions and released by RKO Radio Pictures. Based on the 1812 German fairy tale by the Brothers Grimm, it is the first full-length traditionally animated feature film and the first Disney animated feature film.

The story follows the beautiful princess Snow White, who is exiled into the dangerous forest by her wicked stepmother, the Queen, who is jealous of her beauty. Snow White befriends a group of seven dwarf miners who take her in, but the Queen, disguised as an old hag, tricks her into eating a poisoned apple that places her in a death-like sleep, from which she can only be awakened by love's first kiss.

Plot

Having lost both of her parents, young princess Snow White lives with her vain stepmother, the Queen. Fearing that Snow White will surpass her in beauty, the Queen forces her to work as a scullery maid and asks her Magic Mirror daily who is the fairest of them all. The Mirror always answers that the Queen is fairest — until one day it proclaims Snow White the fairest.

The Queen orders her Huntsman to take Snow White into the forest and kill her, bringing back her heart as proof. Unable to do so, the Huntsman spares her and urges her to flee. Lost in the woods, Snow White finds a cottage belonging to seven dwarfs — Doc, Grumpy, Happy, Sleepy, Bashful, Sneezy, and Dopey — who work in a diamond mine. She cleans their home, and they allow her to stay.

Discovering from the Mirror that Snow White still lives, the Queen transforms herself into an old hag and creates a poisoned apple. She finds the cottage while the dwarfs are at work and tricks Snow White into biting it. The dwarfs pursue the Queen, who falls to her death from a cliff. Finding Snow White in a death-like sleep, the dwarfs place her in a glass coffin. A Prince who had earlier fallen in love with her arrives, kisses her, and breaks the spell; they ride off to his castle as the dwarfs celebrate.

Production

Development began in early 1934, and in June 1934 Walt Disney announced production of his first feature film to The New York Times. Skeptics in the film industry dubbed the project "Disney's Folly," doubting a feature-length cartoon could hold an audience. The film employed innovations including the multiplane camera, and animators studied live-action reference footage of the characters. The budget ballooned from an initial $250,000 to nearly $1.5 million.

Release and legacy

The film premiered at the Carthay Circle Theatre on December 21, 1937, to a wildly enthusiastic audience, receiving a standing ovation. It became the highest-grossing film of 1938 and, adjusted for inflation, remains one of the top-grossing films of all time. In 1939, Disney received an honorary Academy Award — one full-size statuette and seven miniature ones — presented by Shirley Temple. The film is widely considered a landmark that proved the viability of the animated feature and launched Disney's studio into a golden age. It was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1989.

Music

Songs by Frank Churchill and Larry Morey include "I'm Wishing," "One Song," "With a Smile and a Song," "Whistle While You Work," "Heigh-Ho," "Someday My Prince Will Come," and "The Silly Song." "Someday My Prince Will Come" later became a jazz standard.
