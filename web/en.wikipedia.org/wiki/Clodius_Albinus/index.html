Clodius Albinus

Decimus Clodius Septimius Albinus (c. 150 – 19 February 197) was a Roman imperial claimant during the Year of the Five Emperors. Born at Hadrumetum in North Africa into a distinguished senatorial family, he pursued a successful military career, serving on the Danube and in Britain, where by 191 he was governor of the province of Britannia, commanding its three legions.

Rise

After the murder of Pertinax in 193, Albinus was one of three provincial commanders proclaimed emperor by their armies, alongside Septimius Severus in Pannonia and Pescennius Niger in Syria. Severus, closest to Rome, secured the capital first and bought Albinus's neutrality by recognising him as Caesar and designated heir and sharing the consulship of 194 with him. While Severus campaigned in the east and destroyed Niger, Albinus remained in Britain and Gaul consolidating support in the western provinces.

Civil war and death

Once master of the east, Severus repudiated the arrangement, named his own son Caracalla as Caesar, and marched west. Albinus crossed from Britain into Gaul, established himself at Lugdunum (Lyon), and was proclaimed Augustus by his troops and the Senate's sympathisers in the west. After an indecisive clash near Tinurtium, the rivals met in the great Battle of Lugdunum on 19 February 197, one of the largest battles ever fought between Roman armies, reportedly involving some 150,000 men. Severus prevailed; Albinus died — by suicide or execution — and his severed head was sent to Rome. Lugdunum was sacked, Albinus's family was killed, and his supporters in the Senate were purged. Cassius Dio and Herodian give accounts of his career; the Historia Augusta biography is largely fictional.
