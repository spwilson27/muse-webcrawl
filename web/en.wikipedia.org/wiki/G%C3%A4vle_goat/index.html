Gävle goat
Location: Slottstorget, Gävle, Sweden
First built: 1966
Height: about 13 metres
Material: Straw on a wooden frame

The Gävle goat (Swedish: Gävlebocken) is a traditional Christmastime display of a giant version of a traditional Swedish Yule goat figure made of straw. It is erected each year by local community groups at the beginning of Advent over Castle Square (Slottstorget) in central Gävle, Sweden.

History of arson

The goat has become internationally famous for frequently being destroyed — most often by arson — despite security measures including guards, fences, flame retardant and webcams. Since its first construction in 1966, it has been burned or otherwise destroyed in most years, spawning betting markets on its survival and extensive media coverage each December. The tradition of the goat itself dates to 1966, when advertising consultant Stig Gavlén proposed erecting a giant Yule goat.
