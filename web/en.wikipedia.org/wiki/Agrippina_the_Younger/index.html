Julia Agrippina (6 November 15 – 23 March 59 AD), known as Agrippina the Younger, was a Roman empress and one of the most prominent women of the Julio-Claudian dynasty. She was the daughter of Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder, sister of Caligula, wife and niece of the emperor Claudius, and mother of the emperor Nero.

Early life and marriages

Agrippina was born at Oppidum Ubiorum, later renamed Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium (modern Cologne) in her honour. Her first marriage, to Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus, produced her only child, Nero, in 37 AD. Under Caligula she was exiled to the Pontine Islands on a charge of conspiracy (39 AD) but was recalled by Claudius. Her second husband, the wealthy Passienus Crispus, died leaving her a fortune.

Empress

In 49 AD Agrippina married her uncle Claudius — a union requiring a change in the law against uncle–niece marriage — and received the title Augusta, unprecedented for a living emperor's wife. She dominated the court, founded a colony in her birthplace, and secured Nero's adoption by Claudius ahead of his son Britannicus, marrying Nero to Claudius's daughter Octavia. Ancient sources accuse her of poisoning Claudius with mushrooms in 54 AD so that Nero could succeed.

Fall and death

As Nero's mother-regent in all but name, Agrippina initially controlled appointments and policy, but she progressively lost influence to Seneca and Burrus and to Nero's resentment of her interference. After she threatened to champion Britannicus's claim, relations collapsed. In March 59 AD Nero had her murdered at Baiae, after a failed attempt with a collapsible boat, as described by Tacitus, Suetonius, and Cassius Dio.

Legacy

Agrippina wrote memoirs, now lost but used by Tacitus and Pliny. Hostile senatorial tradition painted her as the archetypal scheming imperial woman; modern historians view her as a skilled dynastic politician operating within the constraints of a system that barred women from formal power.
