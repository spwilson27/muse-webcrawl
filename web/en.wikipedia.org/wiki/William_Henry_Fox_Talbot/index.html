William Henry Fox Talbot


Born 11 February 1800, Melbury, Dorset, England
Died 17 September 1877 (aged 77), Lacock, Wiltshire
Known for Calotype process; The Pencil of Nature

William Henry Fox Talbot (11 February 1800 – 17 September 1877) was an English scientist, inventor, and photography pioneer who invented the salted paper and calotype processes, the precursors of photographic processes of the later 19th and 20th centuries.

Early life and education

Talbot was educated at Harrow School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied mathematics and classics. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1831 and served briefly as a Member of Parliament for Chippenham.

Photography

Frustrated by his inability to sketch the scenery of Lake Como with a camera lucida in 1833, Talbot began experiments to fix images formed by light on paper. By 1835 he had produced small paper negatives at his home, Lacock Abbey — among them a famous image of the oriel window. His calotype process, patented in 1841, used silver iodide paper developed in gallic acid, allowing a negative from which multiple positive prints could be made. His book The Pencil of Nature (1844–46) was the first commercially published book illustrated with photographs.

Other work

Talbot made contributions to mathematics (including an early study of elliptic integrals), optics, etymology, and Assyriology, helping decipher cuneiform inscriptions. His patents on photography were partly relaxed in 1854 after the Collodion-era trial, opening the field to wider development in Britain.
