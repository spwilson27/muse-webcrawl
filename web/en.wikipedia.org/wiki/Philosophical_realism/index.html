Philosophical realism

Philosophical realism is the view that some entities — such as universals, physical objects, mathematical objects, or moral values — exist independently of our thoughts, perceptions, or linguistic conventions about them. Realism is usually contrasted with idealism, nominalism, and anti-realism.

Forms

Platonic realism holds that abstract objects and universals exist in their own right; Aristotelian or moderate realism locates universals in things. In contemporary philosophy, realism is debated domain by domain: scientific realism about unobservable entities, moral realism about ethical facts, and mathematical realism about numbers and sets.

History

The medieval problem of universals pitted realists such as William of Champeaux against nominalists such as Roscelin and William of Ockham. Realist positions remain central to analytic philosophy, from Bertrand Russell's realism to modern structural realism in philosophy of science.
