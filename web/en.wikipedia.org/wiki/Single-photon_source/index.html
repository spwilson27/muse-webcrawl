Single-photon source

FieldQuantum optics
Key metricg(2)(0) antibunching
UsesQuantum cryptography, quantum computing

A single-photon source is a light source that emits one photon at a time. Such sources are key components in quantum optics, quantum cryptography and quantum computing, where the emission of exactly one photon on demand enables protocols that are impossible with classical light.

Types

Common implementations include quantum dots, single atoms or ions in cavities, nitrogen-vacancy centres in diamond, single molecules, and heralded sources based on spontaneous parametric down-conversion, in which detection of one photon of a pair announces the other.

Characterisation

The purity of a single-photon source is measured by the second-order correlation function g(2)(0): an ideal source has g(2)(0) = 0, indicating perfect antibunching, whereas attenuated laser light has g(2)(0) = 1.

Applications

Single photons carry qubits in photonic quantum computing and are used in quantum key distribution systems such as BB84, where multi-photon pulses would open security loopholes.
