Upper Derwent Valley

Location: Peak District, Derbyshire, England

Reservoirs: Howden, Derwent, Ladybower

The Upper Derwent Valley is an area of the Peak District National Park in Derbyshire, England. It is dominated by three large reservoirs — Howden, Derwent and Ladybower — built in the first half of the 20th century to supply water to the cities of the East Midlands and South Yorkshire.

Reservoirs

Howden and Derwent reservoirs were constructed between 1901 and 1916 behind massive stone-faced dams. Ladybower Reservoir, completed in 1945, required the flooding of the villages of Derwent and Ashopton; the church spire of Derwent village remained above the water for a time before being demolished. In dry years the ruins of the drowned villages can reappear when water levels fall.

Dambusters

During the Second World War, the Derwent and Howden dams were used for practice by No. 617 Squadron RAF — the "Dambusters" — because of their resemblance to the German dams targeted in Operation Chastise in 1943. A memorial and museum in the west tower of Derwent Dam commemorate the association.

Recreation

The valley is popular for walking, cycling and birdwatching, with woodland and moorland surrounding the reservoirs. The area around Fairholmes visitor centre is a gateway to the Dark Peak moors of Kinder Scout and Bleaklow.
