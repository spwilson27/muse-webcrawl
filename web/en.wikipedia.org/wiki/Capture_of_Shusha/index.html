Capture of Shusha

The capture of Shusha was the decisive operation of the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war, in which Azerbaijani forces seized the strategic mountain city of Shusha (Shushi) between 4 and 8 November 2020. Control of Shusha, which overlooks the Lachin corridor and lies only a few kilometres from Stepanakert, effectively decided the war: a ceasefire agreement was signed two days later.

Shusha sits on a high plateau dominating the road between Stepanakert and Armenia.

Background

Shusha, a historic Azerbaijani cultural centre and a largely Armenian-populated city under the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh after its capture by Armenian forces in May 1992, occupies a commanding position in the Karabakh mountains. During the 2020 war, which began on 27 September, Azerbaijani forces advanced from the south through the Jabrayil and Fuzuli districts, then fought through difficult mountain terrain toward Shusha in late October.

The battle

Because the cliffs and single approach roads made a conventional armoured assault impractical, Azerbaijan committed special forces who infiltrated through forested ravines, in places scaling cliffs to reach the plateau. Fighting inside the city from 4 November was intense and largely conducted at close quarters with light weapons. By 8 November Azerbaijani troops controlled the city; President Ilham Aliyev announced its capture that day.

Consequences

The loss of Shusha left Stepanakert exposed and the Lachin corridor — Artsakh's only road link to Armenia — within Azerbaijani artillery range. On the night of 9–10 November Armenia, Azerbaijan and Russia signed a trilateral ceasefire statement ending the war, under which Azerbaijan retained its battlefield gains and Armenia returned the surrounding districts. A Russian peacekeeping contingent was deployed along the corridor. Shusha was subsequently promoted by Azerbaijan as a symbol of the victory and designated the country's cultural capital.
