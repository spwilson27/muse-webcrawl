NEXRAD


NEXRAD / WSR-88D
Type: Weather radar network
Country: United States
Introduced: 1988–1997 deployment
Number built: 160 sites

NEXRAD or Nexrad (Next-Generation Radar) is a network of 160 high-resolution S-band Doppler weather radars operated by the National Weather Service (NWS), an agency of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), together with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the U.S. Air Force. Its technical name is WSR-88D (Weather Surveillance Radar, 1988, Doppler).

Capabilities

The WSR-88D detects precipitation and atmospheric movement, producing data on reflectivity, radial velocity, and spectrum width. Beginning in 2011, the network was upgraded with dual-polarization technology, which transmits and receives both horizontal and vertical pulses, allowing forecasters to distinguish rain, hail, snow, and non-meteorological echoes, estimate rainfall more accurately, and identify debris lofted by tornadoes.

Deployment

Development followed deadly tornado and flood events of the 1970s that exposed the limits of earlier radars. The first WSR-88D was installed for operational use in Norman, Oklahoma, in the early 1990s, and network deployment was completed by 1997. Radar Operations Center support is based in Norman.

Data use

NEXRAD data are freely available to the public and underpin weather warnings, television weather graphics, commercial weather applications, and research into severe storms, hydrology, and aviation hazards. The network is complemented by smaller Terminal Doppler Weather Radars at major airports.
