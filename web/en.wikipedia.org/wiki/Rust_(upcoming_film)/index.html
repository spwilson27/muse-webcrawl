Rust is an American Western film written and directed by Joel Souza, from a story by Souza and Alec Baldwin, who stars as outlaw Harland Rust. Set in 1880s Kansas, it tells of a notorious outlaw who rescues his young grandson, Lucas, who has been sentenced to hang for an accidental killing, and the two go on the run from a U.S. Marshal and a bounty hunter.

The film became internationally known before release because of a fatal on-set shooting on 21 October 2021 at Bonanza Creek Ranch in New Mexico, in which a live round discharged from a revolver held by Baldwin killed cinematographer Halyna Hutchins and wounded Souza. Production was suspended, and the incident led to criminal proceedings, civil lawsuits, industry-wide debate over firearms on film sets, and reforms in prop-gun practice. Filming was completed in Montana in spring 2023 with Bianca Cline as cinematographer; the film premiered at the Camerimage festival in Poland in November 2024.

Plot

In 1880s Kansas, thirteen-year-old Lucas Hollister is orphaned and, after accidentally killing a rancher, sentenced to hang. His estranged grandfather, the ageing outlaw Harland Rust, breaks him out of custody, and the pair flee toward Mexico pursued by U.S. Marshal Wood Helm and the bounty hunter Fenton "Preacher" Lang.

Production

Development

Baldwin approached Souza with the story idea, and the project was announced in 2020 with Baldwin producing and starring. Principal photography began in October 2021 in New Mexico on a modest budget, with Hutchins — a rising cinematographer Baldwin had specifically sought — behind the camera.

2021 shooting incident

On 21 October 2021, during a rehearsal inside a church set, Baldwin pointed a Colt .45 revolver toward the camera; the gun, which had been loaded with a live round, discharged, killing Hutchins and injuring Souza. The production's armorer, Hannah Gutierrez-Reed, was convicted of involuntary manslaughter in March 2024 and sentenced to 18 months in prison. Charges against Baldwin were dismissed with prejudice in July 2024 after the court found that prosecutors had withheld evidence. The incident spurred legislative proposals, union campaigns to ban functional firearms and live ammunition on sets, and widespread adoption of digital and rubber-gun alternatives.

Completion and release

Under a settlement with Hutchins's widower, Matthew Hutchins — who became an executive producer — production resumed in April 2023 at Yellowstone Film Ranch in Montana, with the original cast returning. The completed film premiered at Camerimage in November 2024, dedicated to Hutchins, and was released in the United States in 2025.
