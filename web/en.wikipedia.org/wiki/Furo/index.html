Furo

Furo (風呂), or the more polite form ofuro (お風呂), is a Japanese bathtub, a central element of the Japanese bathing tradition. Unlike Western bathtubs, a furo is typically deeper, often made of wood (such as hinoki cypress), stainless steel, or plastic, and is designed for soaking in hot water rather than for washing.

Bathing practice

In Japanese bathing custom, the body is washed and rinsed thoroughly outside the tub — traditionally while sitting on a small wooden stool using a shower or a tap, soap, and a small basin — before entering the furo. Because bathers enter clean, the same hot water can be shared by all members of the household and kept at temperature with a lid or reheating system. The soak, usually at around 40 °C (104 °F), is intended for relaxation and warmth rather than cleansing.

History

The furo evolved from steam baths and ritual purification practices associated with Buddhist temples, and wooden soaking tubs became common in Japanese homes during the Edo period. The traditional wooden furo was heated by a wood-fired stove beneath or beside the tub. Modern homes generally use gas or electric water heaters with deep acrylic or steel tubs equipped with covers and remote-controlled temperature settings.
