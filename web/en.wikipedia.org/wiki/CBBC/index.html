CBBC

Country: United Kingdom
Network: BBC
Launched: 1985 (as Children's BBC programming block); channel launched 11 February 2002
Target audience: Children aged 6–12

CBBC (short for Children's BBC) is a British free-to-air children's television channel owned and operated by the BBC. It is also the brand used for all BBC content aimed at children aged 6 to 12. Its sister channel, CBeebies, serves younger children. CBBC broadcasts every day from 7:00 to 19:00 (previously until 21:00), timesharing with BBC Four.

History

The Children's BBC brand was launched on 9 September 1985 as a programming strand on BBC One, famously presented from the small "Broom Cupboard" studio at Television Centre by Phillip Schofield and his puppet Gordon the Gopher. The dedicated CBBC digital channel launched on 11 February 2002 alongside CBeebies. In 2012, CBBC production moved to MediaCityUK in Salford. The channel's evening hours were reduced in 2022, and proposals have been made to move CBBC to an online-only service as part of the BBC's digital strategy, though the linear channel continued thereafter.

Programming

CBBC programming includes drama, comedy, factual, entertainment and news. Long-running and popular titles include Blue Peter (shared with the wider BBC children's output), Newsround, The Story of Tracy Beaker and Tracy Beaker Returns, Horrible Histories, The Sarah Jane Adventures, Wolfblood, The Dumping Ground, Danger Mouse (2015 revival), Operation Ouch! and Deadly 60. The channel is known for its in-vision continuity presenters broadcasting from "CBBC HQ".
