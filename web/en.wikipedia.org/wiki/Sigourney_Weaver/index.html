Sigourney Weaver (born Susan Alexandra Weaver; October 8, 1949) is an American actress. A figure in science fiction and popular culture, she has received various accolades, including two Golden Globe Awards and a British Academy Film Award, in addition to nominations for three Academy Awards, four Primetime Emmy Awards, and a Tony Award. She is best known for playing Ellen Ripley in the Alien franchise (1979–1997), a role that pioneered the female action hero in film.

Early life

Weaver was born in Manhattan to Elizabeth Inglis, an English actress, and Sylvester "Pat" Weaver, a pioneering television executive who created The Tonight Show at NBC. She adopted the name Sigourney after a minor character in F. Scott Fitzgerald's The Great Gatsby. Educated at Stanford and the Yale School of Drama — where she appeared alongside Meryl Streep — she worked in off-Broadway theatre before a tiny film debut in Woody Allen's Annie Hall (1977).

Career

Weaver's breakthrough came as Ripley in Ridley Scott's Alien (1979). In James Cameron's sequel Aliens (1986) she earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Actress — an almost unprecedented honour for a science-fiction performance. She played Ripley in two further sequels and returned to Cameron's worlds as Dr. Grace Augustine in Avatar (2009), then the highest-grossing film of all time, and as the Na'vi teenager Kiri in Avatar: The Way of Water (2022).

Beyond science fiction she starred as Dana Barrett in Ghostbusters (1984), as primatologist Dian Fossey in Gorillas in the Mist (1988), and in Working Girl (1988) — receiving Oscar nominations for the latter two in the same year — as well as in The Ice Storm (1997), Galaxy Quest (1999), and A Monster Calls (2016).

Legacy

Ripley is consistently ranked among the greatest film heroines, and Weaver's dramatic credibility within genre material helped legitimise science fiction as a vehicle for serious acting. She has also been a prominent environmental activist, particularly on ocean conservation.
