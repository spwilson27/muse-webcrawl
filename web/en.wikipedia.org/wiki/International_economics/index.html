International economics

International economics is a field of economics concerned with economic interactions between countries, including international trade, foreign investment, exchange rates and international monetary arrangements. It is conventionally divided into two branches: international trade theory and policy, and international finance (or international macroeconomics).

International trade

Trade theory explains the pattern and gains from trade. The Ricardian model attributes trade to differences in comparative advantage; the Heckscher–Ohlin model to differences in factor endowments; and the new trade theory of economists such as Paul Krugman to economies of scale and product differentiation. Trade policy examines tariffs, quotas and trade agreements and their welfare effects.

International finance

International finance studies exchange rate determination, balance of payments adjustment, capital flows and currency crises. Key frameworks include purchasing power parity, the Mundell–Fleming model and the "impossible trinity" of monetary policy constraints. International institutions such as the International Monetary Fund, the World Bank and the World Trade Organization form the governance architecture within which these interactions occur.
