Base station

A base station is a fixed transceiver at a central location that serves as a hub through which portable or mobile radio units communicate. In cellular networks, base stations are the interface between mobile devices and the wider telephone or data network, each covering a geographic area called a cell.

Cellular base stations

In a cellular network, each cell is served by at least one fixed-location transceiver, known as a cell site or base transceiver station (BTS). A base station typically consists of antennas mounted on a tower, rooftop, or other structure, together with radio equipment in a shelter or cabinet, a power supply, and a backhaul connection to the operator's core network. As a mobile user moves, calls and data sessions are handed over from one base station to the next.

Types

Base stations vary greatly in coverage and capacity. Macrocells cover areas from about one to several tens of kilometres and form the backbone of rural and suburban networks. Microcells and picocells serve smaller areas such as city streets and building interiors, while femtocells are very small, low-power stations used in homes and small offices. Distributed antenna systems spread signals through many small antennas fed from a central unit.

Other uses

The term is also used for the fixed stations of two-way radio systems for police, fire, and taxi fleets; for wireless local loop systems; and for the charging or control hubs of cordless telephones. In surveying, a GPS base station at a known position broadcasts corrections to roving receivers.
