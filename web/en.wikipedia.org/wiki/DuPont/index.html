DuPont de Nemours, Inc., commonly known as DuPont, is an American multinational chemical and materials company based in Wilmington, Delaware. Founded in 1802 by the French-American chemist Éleuthère Irénée du Pont as a gunpowder mill on the Brandywine River, it grew into one of the world's largest chemical companies and a pioneer of polymer science.

History

Du Pont, a pupil of Antoine Lavoisier, emigrated from revolutionary France and established the Eleutherian Mills to manufacture high-quality black powder. The company supplied much of the Union's gunpowder during the American Civil War and expanded into dynamite and smokeless powder. In the twentieth century it diversified into paints, dyes and — transformatively — synthetic polymers, while divesting its explosives heritage. It was also a major shareholder in General Motors from 1914 to 1961.

In 2015 DuPont agreed to merge with Dow Chemical, forming DowDuPont in 2017; the combine split in 2019 into three companies — Corteva (agriculture), Dow Inc. (materials) and the present DuPont de Nemours (specialty products).

Inventions and products

DuPont's laboratories produced a remarkable sequence of materials: neoprene synthetic rubber (1930); nylon, developed by Wallace Carothers's team and commercialised in 1939; Teflon (polytetrafluoroethylene), accidentally discovered by Roy Plunkett in 1938; Mylar polyester film; Lycra spandex; Nomex and Kevlar aramid fibres, the latter developed by Stephanie Kwolek in 1965 and used in ballistic armour; Tyvek sheet; and Corian solid surfacing.

Environmental record

The company has faced extensive litigation over per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), notably PFOA ("C8") contamination around its Washington Works plant in West Virginia, which led to a landmark epidemiological study and large settlements, and its Chemours spin-off now carries much of that legacy.
