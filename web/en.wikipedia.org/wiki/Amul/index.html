Amul is an Indian dairy cooperative brand managed by the Gujarat Co-operative Milk Marketing Federation (GCMMF), jointly owned by several million milk producers in Gujarat. The name derives from the Sanskrit word amulya ("priceless") and from the Anand Milk Union Limited, founded in 1946 in Anand, Gujarat, by farmers resisting exploitation by middlemen, under the guidance of Tribhuvandas Patel and, from 1949, engineer Verghese Kurien.

The Amul model and White Revolution

The three-tier "Anand pattern" — village co-operative societies, district unions, and a state federation — eliminated intermediaries and returned most of the consumer price to producers. From 1970, the model was replicated nationally through Operation Flood, led by Kurien's National Dairy Development Board, transforming India into the world's largest milk producer in what is called the White Revolution. Amul's products include milk, butter, ghee, cheese, ice cream, and paneer, and the brand is one of India's best known.

Advertising

Since 1967 Amul's butter advertising has featured the "Amul girl" — a round-eyed moppet in a polka-dot frock created by Sylvester da Cunha's agency — commenting on current events in witty hoardings, one of the longest-running advertising campaigns in the world, with the slogan "Utterly Butterly Delicious."
