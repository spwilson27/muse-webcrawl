Fokker Eindecker

The Fokker Eindecker fighters were a series of German single-seat monoplane fighter aircraft of the First World War, the first purpose-built German fighter aircraft and the first to be fitted with a synchronisation gear allowing a machine gun to fire through the propeller arc.

Development

Developed by Fokker from the M.5 reconnaissance monoplane, the E.I entered service in mid-1915, followed by the E.II, E.III, and E.IV variants.

The synchronised armament gave Germany a period of air superiority known to the Allies as the 'Fokker Scourge' in late 1915 and early 1916.

Pilots and legacy

Aces such as Max Immelmann and Oswald Boelcke flew Eindeckers and helped develop early fighter tactics.

The advantage ended as Allied types such as the Nieuport 11 and Airco DH.2 arrived.
