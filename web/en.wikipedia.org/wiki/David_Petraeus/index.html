David Howell Petraeus (born 7 November 1952) is a retired United States Army general and former public official. He commanded coalition forces in Iraq during the 2007 "surge", commanded US Central Command and later forces in Afghanistan, and served as Director of the Central Intelligence Agency from 2011 until his resignation in 2012.

Petraeus led the 2007 Iraq troop surge and a rewrite of US counterinsurgency doctrine.

Military career

A 1974 graduate of West Point, Petraeus earned a PhD in international relations from Princeton University with a dissertation on lessons of the Vietnam War. He commanded the 101st Airborne Division during the 2003 invasion of Iraq and the subsequent occupation of Mosul. As head of the Combined Arms Center he oversaw the writing of the Army's counterinsurgency field manual FM 3-24. In February 2007 he took command of Multi-National Force – Iraq, implementing the surge of US troops credited, together with the Sunni Awakening, with a sharp reduction in violence.

CIA director and resignation

After commanding US Central Command (2008–2010) and coalition forces in Afghanistan (2010–2011), Petraeus retired from the Army to become CIA director in September 2011. He resigned in November 2012 after the FBI uncovered an extramarital affair with his biographer Paula Broadwell. In 2015 he pleaded guilty to a misdemeanour charge of mishandling classified information, receiving probation and a fine. He subsequently worked in private equity at KKR and in academia.
