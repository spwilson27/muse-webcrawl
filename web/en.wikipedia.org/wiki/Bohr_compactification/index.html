Bohr compactification

Bohr compactification
FieldHarmonic analysis, topological groups
Named afterHarald Bohr
NotationbG for a topological group G
Key objectsAlmost periodic functions; Pontryagin duality

In mathematics, the Bohr compactification of a topological group G is a compact Hausdorff topological group that plays the role of a universal compactification for G, constructed to capture the theory of almost periodic functions introduced by Harald Bohr in the 1920s. For the real line, the Bohr compactification bR is the space on which every almost periodic function on R extends continuously.

Definition

Given a locally compact abelian group G with dual group Ĝ, the Bohr compactification bG is defined as the Pontryagin dual of Ĝ equipped with the discrete topology. There is a canonical continuous homomorphism from G into bG with dense image, and bG is universal: every continuous homomorphism from G to a compact group factors uniquely through it. For non-abelian groups the construction generalizes using finite-dimensional unitary representations, in parallel with the Tannaka–Krein duality; the resulting object is still called the Bohr (or Bohr–van der Corput) compactification.

Almost periodic functions

A bounded continuous function on G is almost periodic if its translates form a relatively compact set in the supremum norm. The fundamental theorem of the subject states that the almost periodic functions on G are exactly the pullbacks of continuous functions on bG. For G = R, these include all trigonometric polynomials with arbitrary (incommensurable) frequencies and their uniform limits; the mean value of an almost periodic function is given by integration against Haar measure on the compactification.

Applications

The Bohr compactification is used in number theory and ergodic theory — for instance in the analysis of equidistribution, uniform distribution of sequences, and nilsequences — and in the study of almost periodic solutions of differential equations.
