Pulitzer Prize for Photography

The Pulitzer Prize for Photography was one of the American Pulitzer Prizes, awarded annually from 1942 to 1967 for distinguished examples of photography in American newspapers. In 1968 it was replaced by two separate awards, the Pulitzer Prize for Spot News Photography (later renamed Breaking News Photography) and the Pulitzer Prize for Feature Photography.

History

The photography prize was introduced in 1942, a quarter century after the Pulitzers were founded. The first winner was Milton Brooks of The Detroit News, for his photograph "Ford Strikers Riot," showing a confrontation during a strike at the Ford Motor Company. During the Second World War, winning images included Joe Rosenthal's photograph of the flag raising on Iwo Jima (1945), one of the most reproduced images of the war.

Other celebrated winners included Max Desfor for Korean War coverage (1951), John L. Gaunt Jr. for "Tragedy by the Sea" (1955), and Robert H. Jackson, who won in 1964 for his photograph of Jack Ruby shooting Lee Harvey Oswald, taken moments after the event. The final award under the single-category format went to Jack R. Thornell in 1967 for his photograph of the wounded civil rights worker James Meredith.

Successor categories

Recognizing the difference between immediate news coverage and longer-form photographic essays, the Pulitzer Board split the prize in 1968. The two successor categories continue today as the Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography and the Pulitzer Prize for Feature Photography.
