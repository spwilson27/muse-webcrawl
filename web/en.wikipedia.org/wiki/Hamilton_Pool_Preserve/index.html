Hamilton Pool Preserve is a scenic natural swimming hole and nature preserve in Travis County, Texas, about 23 miles west of Austin. The pool was created when the dome of an underground river collapsed, leaving a jade-green pool fed by Hamilton Creek, which spills over a limestone ledge in a 50-foot waterfall into the grotto below.

Geology and ecology

The collapsed grotto forms a natural amphitheatre of limestone, draped with maidenhair ferns and moss. The preserve covers 232 acres of the Balcones Canyonlands habitat, home to the endangered golden-cheeked warbler, chatterbox orchids and diverse native plants along the creek trail to the Pedernales River.

History and access

The site was once part of a ranch owned by the Reimers family, who opened it to the public; Travis County purchased the land in 1985. Extremely popular for swimming in summer, the preserve manages access through a reservation system, and swimming is sometimes closed after heavy rains due to water quality.
