Giorgio Vasari

Giorgio Vasari, painter, architect and art historian

Born30 July 1511, Arezzo, Tuscany
Died27 June 1574, Florence
Known forLives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects; the Uffizi

Giorgio Vasari (30 July 1511 – 27 June 1574) was an Italian Renaissance painter, architect, writer and historian, best known for his Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects, considered the ideological foundation of art-historical writing and the source of most modern knowledge of Renaissance artists' lives.

Career

Born in Arezzo, Vasari was a pupil in Florence in the circle of Michelangelo, whom he idolised, and became a successful Mannerist painter and the principal court artist of Duke Cosimo I de' Medici. His architectural works include the Uffizi palace in Florence (begun 1560) with its Vasari Corridor connecting to the Palazzo Pitti, and the church of Santa Maria Novella's remodelled interior. With teams of assistants he executed vast decorative schemes, including the Sala dei Cinquecento in the Palazzo Vecchio and the cupola frescoes of Florence Cathedral, left unfinished at his death.

The Lives

The first edition of the Lives appeared in 1550, with a greatly expanded second edition in 1568. Vasari structured Italian art history as a three-age progression, from Cimabue and Giotto through Masaccio and the High Renaissance, culminating in Michelangelo. His Tuscan (especially Florentine) bias, anecdotes, and occasional errors have been noted by later scholars, but the work remains foundational — it popularised the term "Renaissance" (rinascita) and the notion of the "Gothic" as a barbarous style.
