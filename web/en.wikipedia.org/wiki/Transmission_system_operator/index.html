Transmission system operator

A transmission system operator (TSO) is an entity entrusted with transporting energy in the form of natural gas or electrical power on a national or regional level, using fixed infrastructure. TSOs operate the high-voltage transmission networks and are responsible for keeping supply and demand in balance in real time, ensuring the security and reliability of the power system.

Electricity TSOs

An electricity TSO plans, operates and maintains the transmission grid, schedules power flows between generators, distribution networks and large consumers, and procures balancing and ancillary services to maintain system frequency. Because transmission is a natural monopoly, TSOs are typically regulated and, in many jurisdictions, required to be unbundled from generation and supply interests so that all market participants have non-discriminatory access to the grid. Examples include National Grid in Great Britain, RTE in France and the members of ENTSO-E, the European association of electricity TSOs.

Gas TSOs

Gas transmission system operators manage high-pressure pipeline networks, compressor stations and interconnections, balancing gas injections and withdrawals and allocating capacity to shippers. In Europe, gas TSOs cooperate through ENTSOG.
