Ahl al-Bayt

Ahl al-Bayt (Arabic: أَهْلُ ٱلْبَيْتِ, "People of the House") is a term in Islam referring to the family of the Prophet Muhammad. The phrase appears in the Quran (notably verse 33:33, the "verse of purification") and holds central importance in Islamic theology, though Sunni and Shia Muslims differ on exactly who is included.

Definitions

In Shia Islam, the Ahl al-Bayt are restricted to Muhammad, his daughter Fatimah, his cousin and son-in-law Ali, and their sons Hasan and Husayn — the five figures known as the Ahl al-Kisa ("People of the Cloak") from the Hadith of the Cloak — together with the line of Imams descended from Husayn. Shia tradition attributes to them divine knowledge and infallibility (isma).

Most Sunni scholars include Muhammad's wives, the Banu Hashim clan, and sometimes his wider household among the Ahl al-Bayt, while also honouring Ali, Fatimah, Hasan, and Husayn as foremost among them. The requirement to send blessings upon the Prophet's family appears in the daily prayers of all Muslims.

Significance

Devotion to the Ahl al-Bayt is expressed in the Hadith al-Thaqalayn, in which Muhammad is reported to have left behind "the Book of God and my Ahl al-Bayt." Commemorations such as Ashura, marking Husayn's martyrdom at Karbala in 680 CE, keep the family's memory at the heart of Shia religious life, while Sufi orders also trace many of their lineages through Ali.
