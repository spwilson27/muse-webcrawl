Jetty

A jetty is a structure that projects from land out into a body of water. Jetties are built for several purposes: to influence currents and protect a shoreline or harbour entrance from silting, to serve as a landing place for boats, or as a walkway. The term overlaps with pier, wharf, breakwater and groin, with distinctions varying between British and American usage.

Training jetties

At river mouths and tidal inlets, paired jetties — called training walls or, in American usage, jetties — confine the current so that it scours a navigable channel and prevents the formation of a shifting sand bar. Notable examples include the jetties at the mouth of the Columbia River and the South Pass of the Mississippi. The structures are usually built of rock rubble, concrete or timber piling.

Other uses

Smaller jetties serve as landing stages for ferries and fishing boats or as recreational fishing platforms. In architecture, the unrelated word "jetty" also describes the overhanging upper storey of a timber-framed building (jettied floors), common in medieval and Tudor construction.
