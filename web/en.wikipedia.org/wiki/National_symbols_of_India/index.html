National symbols of India

The Lion Capital of Ashoka, India's state emblem

The national symbols of India are the emblems officially recognised as representing the Republic of India. They are governed in part by law, including the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act and the Flag Code of India.

Principal symbols

  • Flag: the Tiranga, a horizontal tricolour of saffron, white and green with the navy-blue 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra at its centre, adopted on 22 July 1947.
  • State emblem: an adaptation of the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath, adopted on 26 January 1950, bearing the motto Satyameva Jayate ("Truth alone triumphs").
  • National anthem: "Jana Gana Mana" by Rabindranath Tagore, adopted in 1950; the national song is "Vande Mataram" by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee.
  • National animal: the Bengal tiger; national bird: the Indian peafowl; national flower: the lotus; national tree: the Indian banyan; national fruit: the mango.
  • National river: the Ganga (Ganges). The national aquatic animal is the Ganges river dolphin, and the national heritage animal is the Indian elephant.
  • Currency symbol: the Indian rupee sign (₹), adopted in 2010. The national calendar is the Saka calendar.
