General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party

[Image placeholder: Great Hall of the People]

The General Secretary of the Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party is the highest-ranking official of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the paramount leader of China. The officeholder presides over the Politburo and its Standing Committee, the party's top decision-making bodies, and since the 1990s has conventionally also held the posts of President of the People's Republic of China and Chairman of the Central Military Commission — the so-called "trinity" of party, state, and military leadership.

History

The title was used intermittently in the party's early decades (Chen Duxiu was the first general secretary in the 1920s) before being abolished in favour of the chairmanship held by Mao Zedong. The post was restored at the 12th Party Congress in 1982, initially as a more administrative role subordinate to senior elders, and grew in importance under Hu Yaobang, Zhao Ziyang, and Jiang Zemin. Jiang's successors Hu Jintao (2002–2012) and Xi Jinping (from November 2012) consolidated the pattern of the general secretary as the core of the leadership.

Powers and selection

Formally, the general secretary is elected by the Central Committee at its first plenum after a Party Congress and is responsible for convening meetings of the Politburo and its Standing Committee and chairing the Secretariat's work. In practice the officeholder directs personnel appointments through the nomenklatura system, sets the policy agenda, and commands the People's Liberation Army through the Central Military Commission. Under Xi Jinping, term and age norms that had regularised succession were loosened, and he began a third term as general secretary in October 2022.
