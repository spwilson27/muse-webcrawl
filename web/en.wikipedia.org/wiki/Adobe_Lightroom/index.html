Adobe Lightroom is a family of software products by Adobe Inc. for organising, editing and sharing digital photographs, with an emphasis on non-destructive editing of raw image files. Where Photoshop works at the level of individual pixels and layers, Lightroom addresses the photographer's whole workflow: importing and cataloguing large shoots, applying develop settings, and exporting for print, books, slideshows and the web.

[Image: The Adobe Lightroom Develop module interface]

Overview

Lightroom stores edits as metadata instructions rather than altered pixels: adjustments to exposure, white balance, tone curves, colour, sharpening, noise reduction and lens corrections are recorded in a catalogue database (or sidecar data) and rendered on the fly, so originals are never modified. Its modules are Library, Develop, Map, Book, Slideshow, Print and Web. The raw-processing engine is shared with Adobe Camera Raw.

History and editions

Lightroom began as a project (code-named "Shadowland") led by Mark Hamburg, an early Photoshop developer, and was released after an unusually public beta programme in 2006; version 1.0 followed in February 2007. It competed with Apple's Aperture (discontinued in 2015). In 2017 Adobe split the product line: the catalogue-based desktop application became Lightroom Classic, while a new cloud-first application, simply Lightroom (informally Lightroom CC), synchronises full-resolution files through Adobe's cloud and runs on desktop, mobile and web. Both are supplied by Creative Cloud subscription.
