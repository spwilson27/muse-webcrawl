Current account (balance of payments)

Trade in goods is the largest component of most countries' current accounts.

The current account is one of the two main components of a country's balance of payments (the other being the capital and financial account). It records the value of exports and imports of goods and services, net income from abroad, and net current transfers, over a given period.

Components

The current account balance is the sum of: (1) the trade balance in goods; (2) the balance on services, such as transport, travel, finance and software; (3) primary income — investment income and compensation of employees received from and paid to the rest of the world; and (4) secondary income — current transfers such as remittances and foreign aid.

Surpluses and deficits

A current account surplus means a country is a net lender to the rest of the world; a deficit means it is a net borrower, financed by capital inflows. By accounting identity, the current account balance equals national saving minus national investment, so persistent deficits reflect low saving or high investment rather than trade policy alone. Large, persistent imbalances — such as the long-running United States deficit and surpluses in China, Germany and oil exporters — are a recurring topic in debates about global macroeconomic stability.
