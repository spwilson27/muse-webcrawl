Indie game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indie games are known for distinctive art styles and experimental mechanics.

An indie game, short for independent video game, is a video game created by individuals or small development teams without the financial and technical support of a large game publisher. Indie games often focus on innovation, experimental gameplay, and artistic expression, relying on digital distribution rather than retail release.

History

Independent development is as old as home computing, when hobbyists shared games on cassette and floppy disk, but the modern indie movement crystallised in the mid-2000s with digital storefronts. Xbox Live Arcade and Steam (from 2003–05) let small teams reach global audiences; the successes of Braid, Super Meat Boy, and Minecraft around 2008–2011 demonstrated that tiny teams could outsell studio productions, and the 2012 documentary Indie Game: The Movie popularised the scene.

Development and distribution

Accessible engines, asset stores, and crowdfunding (Kickstarter, Patreon) lowered barriers further. Developers self-publish on itch.io and Steam, market through social media, streamers, and festivals such as the Independent Games Festival, whose Seumas McNally Grand Prize is a leading honour. Nintendo, Sony, and Microsoft run indie showcases, blurring the line with publisher-backed "triple-I" productions.

Characteristics

Indie games frequently revive retro genres (roguelikes, metroidvanias), use pixel art or minimal aesthetics, and tell personal stories — as in Undertale, Celeste, and Papers, Please. Low budgets constrain scope, but freedom from publisher oversight permits risk-taking major studios avoid.
