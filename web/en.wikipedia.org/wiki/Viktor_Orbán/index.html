Viktor Mihály Orbán (born 31 May 1963) is a Hungarian lawyer and politician who has been Prime Minister of Hungary since 2010, having previously held the office from 1998 to 2002. He has led the Fidesz political party since 1993, with a break between 2000 and 2003. He is the country's longest-serving prime minister.

Orbán, Hungary's longest-serving prime minister

Early life and rise

Orbán studied law at Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest. In 1988 he was a founding member of Fidesz, originally a liberal youth movement opposing the communist regime. He gained national fame in June 1989 with a speech at the reburial of Imre Nagy demanding the withdrawal of Soviet troops and free elections. After the transition to democracy, he was elected to the National Assembly in 1990.

Premierships

Orbán's first government (1998–2002) led Hungary into NATO in 1999. Returning to power in 2010 with a two-thirds parliamentary majority, his government adopted a new constitution, restructured the electoral system and state institutions, and pursued what Orbán described as an "illiberal state". His governments have clashed repeatedly with the European Union over rule-of-law issues, judicial independence, media freedom and migration policy; Hungary built a border fence during the 2015 European migrant crisis. Orbán won further elections in 2014, 2018 and 2022. Critics describe his rule as democratic backsliding, while supporters credit him with defending national sovereignty and conservative values.
