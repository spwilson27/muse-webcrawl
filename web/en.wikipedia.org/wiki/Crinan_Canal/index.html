The Crinan Canal is a canal in Argyll and Bute, in the west of Scotland, running for nine miles (14 km) between Ardrishaig on Loch Fyne and the village of Crinan on the Sound of Jura. By crossing the Kintyre peninsula, it gives boats a sheltered shortcut of some 85 miles compared with the exposed voyage around the Mull of Kintyre. Authorised by Act of Parliament in 1793 and opened in 1801, it is often described as one of Britain's most beautiful canals — Queen Victoria cruised its length in 1847 and called it the "Royal Route" — and it remains in use by fishing boats, yachts and the surviving Clyde puffers.

Construction

The canal was promoted to link the Clyde and the Hebrides with the fishing grounds and kelp shores of the west coast. John Rennie was consulting engineer, but construction was dogged by money shortages, landslips and the collapse of a reservoir dam; the canal opened in 1801 in an unfinished state and only became fully navigable in 1809 after government loans and remedial work supervised in part by Thomas Telford. Its 15 locks climb to a summit pound near Cairnbaan, where a cluster of canal-side buildings and the Cairnbaan Hotel served passing traffic, before descending through the Dunardry locks to Crinan.

History and traffic

In the nineteenth century the canal carried herring, slate, whisky and passengers; steamers ran through in conjunction with Clyde services, and the last cargo vessels — including the famous VIC-class puffers — used it into the late twentieth century. Management passed from the Crinan Canal Company to the British Transport Commission and in 2012 to Scottish Canals. Today the traffic is overwhelmingly leisure craft, and the canal is a scheduled monument and a centrepiece of the Argyll tourist economy, with a towpath cycle route between the two sea locks.

Features

Notable structures include the swing and rolling bridges at Ardrishaig and Lock 4, the Dunardry flight, the summit reach beside the Kilmartin Glen's prehistoric monuments, and Crinan basin itself, overlooked by the Crinan Hotel and famous for its views to Jura, Scarba and the Corryvreckan whirlpool beyond.
