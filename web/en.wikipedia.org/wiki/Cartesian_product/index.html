Cartesian product

In mathematics, the Cartesian product of two sets A and B, denoted A × B, is the set of all ordered pairs (a, b) where a ∈ A and b ∈ B. It is named after René Descartes, whose Cartesian geometry linked algebra to Euclidean geometry.

Definition and examples

If A = {1, 2} and B = {x, y, z}, then A × B = {(1,x),(1,y),(1,z),(2,x),(2,y),(2,z)} with cardinality |A|·|B| = 6. The Cartesian product of the real line with itself, ℝ × ℝ, is the Cartesian plane of coordinate geometry.

Properties

The product is not commutative — A × B ≠ B × A in general — but is associative up to natural isomorphism. It generalises to finite and infinite products of families of sets, foundational in set theory, relational algebra and product topology, where the product topology makes projections continuous.
