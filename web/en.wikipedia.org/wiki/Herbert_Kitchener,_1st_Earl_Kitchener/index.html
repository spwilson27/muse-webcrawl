Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener

Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener
Born: 24 June 1850, Ballylongford, County Kerry, Ireland
Died: 5 June 1916 (aged 65), HMS Hampshire, North Sea
Rank: Field Marshal
Commands: Sirdar of the Egyptian Army; Commander-in-Chief, India; Secretary of State for War

Field Marshal Horatio Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener (24 June 1850 – 5 June 1916) was an Irish-born senior British Army officer and colonial administrator. He first gained fame for his imperial campaigns, most notably the reconquest of Sudan in 1898, and played a central role in the early part of the First World War as Secretary of State for War.

Early life and career

Kitchener was born near Ballylongford in County Kerry, Ireland, the son of an army officer. He attended the Royal Military Academy, Woolwich, and was commissioned into the Royal Engineers in 1871. Before completing his officer training he volunteered to serve on the French side in the Franco-Prussian War. He worked on survey duties in Palestine and Cyprus before being posted to Egypt.

Sudan and the Boer War

As Sirdar (commander-in-chief) of the Egyptian Army, Kitchener led the Anglo-Egyptian forces that defeated the Mahdists at the Battle of Omdurman in 1898, avenging the death of General Gordon at Khartoum thirteen years earlier. He was created Baron Kitchener of Khartoum. During the Second Boer War (1899–1902) he served first as chief of staff and then as commander-in-chief, directing the later phases of the conflict, including the use of blockhouses, farm burnings and concentration camps against Boer guerrillas.

First World War

Appointed Secretary of State for War in August 1914, Kitchener correctly predicted a long war and raised the vast volunteer force known as "Kitchener's Army" or the New Army. His portrait, pointing out of the famous recruiting poster declaring "Lord Kitchener Wants You", became an enduring image of the war. He died on 5 June 1916 when the cruiser HMS Hampshire, carrying him on a diplomatic mission to Russia, struck a German mine off the Orkney Islands and sank.

Legacy

Kitchener remains a controversial figure: celebrated as an organiser of victory and imperial soldier, he has also been criticised for the scorched-earth policies of the Boer War. The city of Kitchener, Ontario, in Canada was renamed in his honour in 1916.
