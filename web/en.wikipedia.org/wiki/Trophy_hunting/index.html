Trophy hunting

Trophy hunting
DefinitionHunting of wild game animals for a trophy (horns, antlers, head, skin) as a memento of the hunt
Common quarryBig game including the African "Big Five", deer, bears
RegulationPermits and quotas; CITES controls trade in parts of listed species

Trophy hunting is hunting of wild game animals for recreation, where the hunter seeks a souvenir—such as the head, horns, antlers, or skin of the animal—to display as a trophy of the hunt, often in pursuit of officially measured records, such as those kept by the Boone and Crockett Club or Safari Club International.

Practice

Trophy hunts are typically conducted under licence, with fees that can be very high for sought-after species such as rhinoceros, elephant or lion. In parts of Africa, trophy hunting occurs in designated hunting areas and community conservancies, notably in Namibia, Zimbabwe, Tanzania and South Africa.

Debate

Supporters, including some conservation organisations, argue that well-regulated trophy hunting generates revenue and incentives for habitat protection and anti-poaching, and can fund conservation where photographic tourism is not viable. Critics contend it is cruel, selectively removes prime breeding males, and that revenue often fails to reach local communities; several studies dispute the magnitude of its conservation benefits. The 2015 killing of Cecil the lion in Zimbabwe drew global attention and prompted airlines and governments to tighten rules on trophy imports.
