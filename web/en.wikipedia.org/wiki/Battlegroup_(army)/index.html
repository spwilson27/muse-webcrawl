Battlegroup (army)

A battlegroup (British Army usage), or task force (United States Army usage), is a combined-arms military formation built around a headquarters — typically that of an armoured regiment or infantry battalion — to which sub-units of other arms are attached for a particular operation or period. Battlegroups are the basic tactical unit of manoeuvre in many NATO armies.

Composition

A typical British battlegroup is commanded by a lieutenant-colonel and consists of the headquarters of the parent unit, one or more tank squadrons or infantry companies in balanced combination (for example, two companies of infantry and two squadrons of tanks), and supporting elements: artillery batteries in direct support, mortar and anti-tank platoons, reconnaissance troops, engineers, air defence, signals and logistics. The mix is tailored to the mission, a practice known as "grouping", and sub-units may be exchanged between battlegroups as the tactical situation changes.

History

The concept descends from the German Kampfgruppe of the Second World War — ad hoc combined-arms groupings named after their commander, such as Kampfgruppe Peiper — and from the improvised columns and "Jock columns" used in the North African campaign. Post-war, the British Army institutionalised the battlegroup as the principal unit below brigade level, and the term was later adopted for the European Union's multinational battlegroups and for NATO formations such as the enhanced Forward Presence battlegroups deployed in the Baltic states and Poland from 2017.

Related terms

At sea, the equivalent Royal Navy term for a formation built around a major warship is a "task group"; in land warfare a larger ad hoc formation may be termed a brigade group. Battlegroups should not be confused with the United States Army's brigade combat teams, which are permanent rather than task-organised formations.
