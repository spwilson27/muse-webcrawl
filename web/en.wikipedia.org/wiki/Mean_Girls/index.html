Mean Girls is a 2004 American teen comedy film directed by Mark Waters and written by Tina Fey. It stars Lindsay Lohan, Rachel McAdams, Lacey Chabert, Amanda Seyfried (in her film debut), Tim Meadows, Ana Gasteyer, Amy Poehler and Fey. The film follows Cady Heron (Lohan), a naive teenager who was homeschooled in Africa by her scientist parents, as she transfers to an American public high school and is quickly adopted by the Plastics, the school's most popular clique, led by Regina George (McAdams).

Plot

Cady, newly arrived from Kenya, befriends outsiders Janis and Damian, who persuade her to infiltrate the Plastics to sabotage Regina. Cady genuinely falls for Regina's ex-boyfriend Aaron Samuels, becomes increasingly "plastic" herself, and ultimately takes Regina's place as queen bee — until a scandal over the group's scurrilous "Burn Book" erupts into a school-wide confrontation. Cady redeems herself by taking responsibility, winning the Mathletes championship, and sharing the Spring Fling crown with her classmates.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed over $130 million worldwide and was praised for Fey's script; McAdams's performance launched her career. It has become a cult classic, endlessly quoted ("On Wednesdays we wear pink"; "You can't sit with us"), and October 3 is celebrated by fans as "Mean Girls Day". A made-for-television sequel appeared in 2011, a Broadway musical premiered in 2018, and a film adaptation of the musical was released in 2024.
