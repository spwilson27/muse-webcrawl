Francis I, Holy Roman Emperor

Francis I (German: Franz I.; 8 December 1708 – 18 August 1765) was the Duke of Lorraine and Bar (1729–1737), and later Grand Duke of Tuscany (1737–1765), who married Maria Theresa of Austria and was made the Holy Roman Emperor (1745–1765) and nominal King in Germany in 1745. Although Francis was little more than a figurehead — Maria Theresa being the de facto ruler of the Habsburg domains — he was nonetheless an able administrator and businessman who funded the state coffers and his wife's government.

Early life

Born in Nancy, Duchy of Lorraine, Francis was the second surviving son of Leopold, Duke of Lorraine, and Élisabeth Charlotte d'Orléans. He was educated at the court of Vienna, where the Emperor Charles VI took him under his wing and arranged his marriage to Maria Theresa.

Reign

Under the terms ending the War of the Polish Succession, Francis exchanged Lorraine for the Grand Duchy of Tuscany. He married Maria Theresa in 1736. After Charles VI's death in 1740, Maria Theresa succeeded to the Habsburg hereditary lands, and Francis was elected Holy Roman Emperor in 1745, following the brief reign of the Wittelsbach emperor Charles VII. Francis took a keen interest in natural science and in the finances of the monarchy, founding a state lottery and managing the imperial estates profitably.

Francis and Maria Theresa had sixteen children, including Emperors Joseph II and Leopold II, and Marie Antoinette, Queen of France. He died suddenly at Innsbruck in 1765 and was succeeded as emperor by his eldest son, Joseph II.
