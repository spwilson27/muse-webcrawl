Spacewar!

DesignersSteve Russell, Martin Graetz, Wayne Wiitanen, et al.
PlatformPDP-1
Release1962
GenreShoot 'em up
ModeMultiplayer

Spacewar! is a space combat video game developed in 1962 by Steve Russell in collaboration with Martin Graetz, Wayne Wiitanen, and others at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. Written for the newly installed PDP-1 minicomputer, it is one of the earliest known digital computer games and the first to be widely distributed and played at multiple computing installations.

Gameplay

Two players each control a spaceship — the "needle" and the "wedge" — orbiting a central star whose gravity well pulls the ships inward. Players maneuver with thrust and rotation, fire torpedoes, and attempt to destroy the opposing ship while avoiding collision with the star or the opponent. A hyperspace feature allows a ship to disappear and reappear at a random location, at increasing risk of destruction with each use. The background starfield, "Expensive Planetarium", was written by Peter Samson using real star data.

Development and legacy

The game was created by members of the Hingham Institute and the Tech Model Railroad Club culture at MIT, inspired by the Lensman science fiction novels of E. E. Smith. It spread informally between universities on punched paper tape and became a standard demonstration program distributed by DEC with PDP computers. Spacewar! directly inspired the first commercial arcade video games, Computer Space (1971) and Galaxy Game (1971), and is frequently cited as one of the most influential video games ever made. In 2018 it was inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame.
