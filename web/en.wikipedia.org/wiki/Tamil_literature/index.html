Tamil literature

Tamil literature has a recorded history of over two thousand years, making Tamil one of the world's classical languages. Its earliest works, the Sangam poems, were composed in academies (sangams) at Madurai, while later centuries produced epic, devotional, philosophical and modern prose traditions.

Sangam literature

The oldest stratum, Sangam literature (roughly 3rd century BCE–3rd century CE), comprises anthologies such as the Ettuthokai (Eight Anthologies) and Pattuppattu (Ten Idylls), organised around the themes of akam (interior: love and domestic life) and puram (exterior: war and public life) and the landscape-based tinai system. The grammatical treatise Tolkappiyam is the oldest surviving Tamil work.

Didactic and epic periods

The post-Sangam age produced the Tirukkural of Tiruvalluvar, a collection of 1,330 couplets on ethics, politics and love, and the twin epics Silappatikaram and Manimekalai. From the 6th to 9th centuries, the Bhakti movement generated the hymns of the Alvars and Nayanmars; in the 12th century Kamban composed the Tamil Ramavataram.

Modern literature

Print culture in the 19th and 20th centuries brought the novel and short story, with figures such as Subramania Bharati, Kalki Krishnamurthy, Pudhumaipithan and Jayakanthan, followed by a vibrant contemporary scene including Sri Lankan and diaspora writers.
