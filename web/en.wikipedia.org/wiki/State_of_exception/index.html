State of exception

A state of exception (German: Ausnahmezustand) is a situation in which a government suspends normal legal order, typically in response to an emergency, exercising powers beyond ordinary constitutional limits.

Theory

Carl Schmitt opened his Political Theology (1922) with the claim that "Sovereign is he who decides on the exception". Giorgio Agamben's State of Exception (2003) argued that the exception has become a permanent technique of government, creating zones where law is suspended and life is exposed to power — a condition he traced from Roman law through wartime decrees and modern detention camps.

Practice

States of emergency, martial law, and emergency decrees are legal forms of the exception in many constitutions; debates concern their duration, proportionality, and the risk that temporary suspensions become permanent.
