PurposeProtein structure prediction and protein design

Rosetta@home is a volunteer computing project operated by the Baker laboratory at the University of Washington, running on the Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing (BOINC) platform. The project aims to predict protein structures and design new proteins using the Rosetta software suite, applying donated computing power from volunteers' home computers.

Purpose and method

Rosetta searches for the lowest-energy conformation of a protein chain, following the principle that a protein folds to its minimum-energy structure. Volunteers' computers run simulations that generate "decoys" — candidate structures — which are returned to the project's servers. The same methods allow the design of novel proteins with new functions, which are then synthesised and tested in the laboratory.

Applications

Research supported by Rosetta@home has contributed to the design of candidate drugs and vaccines, including work relevant to malaria, Alzheimer's disease and HIV. During the COVID-19 pandemic, computing power surged as volunteers joined the search for proteins that could block the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein; related Baker-lab design methods contributed to the SKYCovione vaccine, approved in South Korea in 2022. Project scientist David Baker shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for computational protein design.

Participation

Anyone can join by installing the BOINC client and attaching to the project; a screensaver displays the current simulation. At its peaks the project has sustained hundreds of teraflops of aggregate computing power.
