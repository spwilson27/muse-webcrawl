Sarona (colony)

Preserved Templer buildings in the Sarona compound, Tel Aviv.

Sarona was an agricultural colony founded in 1871 in Ottoman Palestine by members of the German Templer Society (Temple Society). Located northeast of Jaffa, it was one of several Templer settlements in the Holy Land and eventually became absorbed into the growing city of Tel Aviv. Today the site is a preserved heritage and commercial district in central Tel Aviv, known as the Sarona compound.

History

The Templers purchased land on the Plain of Sharon — from which the name Sarona derives — and laid out a village of stone houses along straight streets in the German style. The colonists developed citrus groves, vineyards, dairies and workshops, and the settlement became known for its agricultural products and its winery.

During the Second World War, British authorities interned many of Sarona's German residents as enemy aliens, and many were deported to Australia or repatriated to Germany. After the war the land passed to British and then Israeli government use; the area became part of the Israeli government and military complex known as the Kirya. In the 2000s and 2010s, many of the original Templer buildings were restored and the compound reopened as a shopping, dining and leisure district, anchored by the Sarona Market food hall.
