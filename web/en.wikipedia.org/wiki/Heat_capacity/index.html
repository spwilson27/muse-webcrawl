Heat capacity

Heat capacity or thermal capacity is a physical property of matter, defined as the amount of heat to be supplied to an object to produce a unit change in its temperature. The SI unit is joule per kelvin (J/K).

Specific heat capacity

Dividing by mass gives specific heat capacity; by amount of substance gives molar heat capacity. Water's unusually high specific heat moderates climate and makes it an effective coolant.

Measurement and theory

Heat capacities are measured with calorimeters. Classically the Dulong–Petit law predicts about 3R per mole for solids; quantum models by Einstein and Debye explain low-temperature behaviour. Values differ at constant pressure (Cp) and constant volume (Cv), with Cp > Cv for gases.
