Solar prominence

[Image: A solar prominence erupting from the Sun's limb, imaged in hydrogen-alpha light]

Overview

A prominence is a large, bright, gaseous feature extending outward from the Sun's surface into the corona, anchored to the photosphere and structured by magnetic fields. Seen against the solar disc in hydrogen-alpha light, the same structures appear dark and are called filaments. Prominences are composed of relatively cool (about 5,000–8,000 K) dense plasma suspended in the million-degree corona.

Behaviour

Quiescent prominences can persist for months along polarity-inversion lines; eruptive prominences blast material into space and are frequently associated with coronal mass ejections that drive space weather at Earth. They were first clearly recorded during eclipses (their pink-red colour remarked in chronicles) and are now monitored continuously by solar observatories such as SDO and by amateur H-alpha telescopes.
