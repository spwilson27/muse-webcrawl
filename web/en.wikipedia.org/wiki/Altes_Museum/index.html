Altes Museum

Altes Museum
LocationMuseum Island, Berlin, Germany
Built1825–1830
ArchitectKarl Friedrich Schinkel

The Altes Museum (English: Old Museum) is a listed building on the Museum Island in the historic centre of Berlin, Germany. Built between 1825 and 1830 by order of King Frederick William III of Prussia, it was designed by the architect Karl Friedrich Schinkel in the Neoclassical style, and is counted among the most significant works of that period. The building houses the Antikensammlung, the Berlin collection of classical antiquities.

Originally called the Königliches Museum (Royal Museum), it was the first museum on the island and the first public museum in Prussia. It received its present name, "Old Museum", in 1845 after the construction of the neighbouring Neues Museum ("New Museum"). The building faces the Lustgarten and the Berlin Cathedral.

Architecture

The museum is a rectangular building raised on a high podium. Its front is dominated by a colonnade of eighteen fluted Ionic columns, behind which a broad staircase leads to the entrance. Behind the colonnade lies a monumental rotunda, modelled on the Pantheon in Rome, which originally served as a gallery for classical sculpture and remains a centrepiece of the building. Frescoes depicting mythological scenes decorate the portico.

History

Heavily damaged by fire in the final days of the Second World War, when the building burned out, the museum was reconstructed under the direction of Hans Erich Bogatzky and Theodor Voissen, reopening in 1966 in East Berlin. Since German reunification it has been part of the Berlin State Museums (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin), and with the rest of Museum Island it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Collections

The Antikensammlung displays Greek and Roman art on two floors: sculpture, vases, jewellery, coins and the famous Etruscan collection, including the Praying Boy (Betender Knabe) and portraits such as the so-called Berlin Goddess. The Münzkabinett's coin collection is also partly exhibited in the building.
