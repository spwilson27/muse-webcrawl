Oireachtas na Gaeilge (Irish pronunciation roughly "ir-akh-tas na GWAYL-guh", meaning "The Irish Language Assembly/Festival") is an annual festival of Irish-language arts and culture, and the name of the organisation that runs it. First held in 1897 during the Gaelic Revival, it is the premier competitive festival for performers in the Irish language, encompassing traditional singing, dancing, storytelling, drama, poetry and literary competitions.

History

The Oireachtas was founded in 1897, modelled partly on the Welsh Eisteddfod and the Scottish Mòd, at a time when the Gaelic League (Conradh na Gaeilge), founded in 1893 by Douglas Hyde and others, was working to revive Irish as a living language. The festival quickly became a focus of the cultural nationalist movement and was supported by figures such as Patrick Pearse.

After a period of decline and interruption in the mid-twentieth century, the festival was revived and re-established on a rotating basis, being hosted in different towns and cities, including venues in the Gaeltacht (Irish-speaking districts) and in cities such as Dublin, Letterkenny and Galway.

Competitions

The most prestigious singing competition is Corn Uí Riada, the cup for sean-nós (old-style) unaccompanied traditional singing, named after the broadcaster and composer Seán Ó Riada. Other major events include:

  • Sean-nós dancing competitions
  • Storytelling (scéalaíocht) and recitation
  • Drama and puppetry in Irish
  • Literary competitions for poetry, prose and drama manuscripts, with winning works published
  • Competitions for children and young people in song, dance and storytelling

Media

The festival is broadcast extensively by the Irish-language media, including RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta and TG4, and is a major gathering point for Irish speakers from Ireland and the diaspora.
