CLIP (Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training) is a neural network model developed by OpenAI that learns visual concepts from natural language supervision. Introduced in 2021, it jointly trains an image encoder and a text encoder on a large dataset of image–caption pairs collected from the internet, using a contrastive objective: the model learns to predict which caption in a batch matches which image.

Method

CLIP was trained on a dataset of about 400 million (image, text) pairs. During training, the image and text encoders produce embeddings that are compared with cosine similarity; the contrastive loss maximises the similarity of correct pairs and minimises that of incorrect pairs. The image encoder used either a modified ResNet or a Vision Transformer (ViT), while the text encoder was a Transformer.

Zero-shot transfer

Because CLIP maps images and text into a shared embedding space, it can perform classification without task-specific training examples: given an image, one embeds candidate class descriptions (e.g. "a photo of a dog") and selects the closest. OpenAI reported that a zero-shot CLIP model matched the accuracy of a fully supervised ResNet-50 on ImageNet without using any of ImageNet's training images.

Applications and influence

CLIP became a foundational component in multimodal AI. It has been used for image search and moderation, as the conditioning encoder in text-to-image generation systems such as DALL·E 2 and Stable Diffusion, and as a building block in vision-language models. OpenAI released the code and weights under the MIT license, and open reproductions such as OpenCLIP followed.

Limitations

Researchers have noted weaknesses in counting, fine-grained classification, and spatial reasoning, and that biases present in web-scraped training data can be reflected in the model's behaviour.
