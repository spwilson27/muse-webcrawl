Inter-State River Water Disputes Act

Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956
Enacted by: Parliament of India
Enacted: 1956 (under Article 262 of the Constitution)
Status: In force, as amended

The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted under Article 262 of the Constitution, which empowers Parliament to provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers and river valleys and to bar the jurisdiction of courts in such matters.

Mechanism

When a state government complains that its water interests are prejudiced, the Central Government first attempts negotiation; failing settlement, it constitutes a Water Disputes Tribunal whose decision, when published, is final and binding. Tribunals have dealt with disputes over the Krishna, Godavari, Cauvery, Ravi–Beas, Narmada and Mahadayi rivers, among others.

Amendments and criticism

The Act was amended in 2002 to impose time limits following the Sarkaria Commission's recommendations, though proceedings have often extended far beyond them. A 2019 amendment bill proposed a single standing tribunal with benches and a Disputes Resolution Committee. Delays and non-implementation of awards have made the Act a persistent subject of debate in Indian federalism.
