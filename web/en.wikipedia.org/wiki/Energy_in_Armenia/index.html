Energy in Armenia

The Metsamor (Armenian) Nuclear Power Plant

Energy in Armenia describes energy production, consumption and policy in the Republic of Armenia. Armenia has no proven reserves of oil or natural gas and imports nearly all of its fossil fuels, mainly natural gas from Russia (and smaller volumes via Iran), while domestic electricity generation comes from nuclear power, hydropower and thermal plants, with a growing contribution from solar energy.

Electricity

The backbone of Armenia's power system is the Metsamor Nuclear Power Plant (the Armenian NPP), whose single operating VVER-440 unit supplies roughly a third or more of the country's electricity; work has been carried out to extend its operating life into the 2030s, and Armenia has discussed building new nuclear capacity. Hydropower, notably the Sevan–Hrazdan and Vorotan cascades, supplies a significant share, and gas-fired plants in Yerevan and Hrazdan provide the balance and winter capacity.

Armenia is interconnected with Georgia and Iran. An electricity-for-gas swap arrangement with Iran exchanges Iranian natural gas for exported Armenian electricity. Historically Armenia also had interconnections with Azerbaijan and Turkey, which are not currently in normal operation.

Renewables

Armenia has good solar resources and has attracted investment in utility-scale solar plants, such as the Masrik-1 project, alongside small hydropower and rooftop solar growth. Natural gas nonetheless dominates total primary energy supply, used heavily for heating and transport (a large share of vehicles run on compressed natural gas).

History

The severe energy crisis of the early 1990s — following the 1988 Spitak earthquake shutdown of Metsamor, the collapse of Soviet supply arrangements and regional blockades — led to years of blackouts. Restarting Metsamor's Unit 2 in 1995 stabilised supply. Energy security and diversification remain central policy concerns.
