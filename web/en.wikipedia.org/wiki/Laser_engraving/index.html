Laser engraving

Laser engraving is the practice of using lasers to engrave an object. A laser engraving machine consists of a laser source, a controller, and a work surface; the focused beam vaporises or melts material point by point as it traces a design, leaving a permanent mark with high precision.

Process and materials

Unlike mechanical engraving, the laser does not contact the surface and the bit does not wear out. CO₂ lasers are commonly used for wood, acrylic, glass, leather, and coated metals, while fiber lasers are preferred for bare metals and plastics. Laser marking differs from engraving in that it discolours rather than removes material, and laser etching melts the surface to create raised marks.

Applications

Applications include product serialisation and barcodes, personalised gifts and trophies, industrial part marking, signage, and fine art.
