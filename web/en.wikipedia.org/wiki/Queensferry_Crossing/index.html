The Queensferry Crossing (formerly the Forth Replacement Crossing) is a road bridge in Scotland, spanning the Firth of Forth between South Queensferry, near Edinburgh, and North Queensferry in Fife. It is the third bridge across the Forth at Queensferry, alongside the Forth Road Bridge (1964) and the Forth (rail) Bridge (1890), and at 2.7 kilometres it is the longest three-tower cable-stayed bridge in the world.

Background and construction

The crossing was commissioned after corrosion was discovered in the main cables of the Forth Road Bridge in 2004, raising doubts about its long-term capacity. The Forth Crossing Act was passed by the Scottish Parliament in 2011 and construction began the same year. The design uses three slender towers up to 207 metres tall with overlapping cable stays crossing at mid-span for stiffness, and windshielding intended to keep the bridge open in high winds that frequently closed its predecessor. Some 23,000 miles of cabling were used, and the works employed up to 1,200 people at peak.

Opening

The bridge opened to traffic on 30 August 2017 and was officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 4 September 2017 — 53 years to the day after she opened the Forth Road Bridge. The older bridge was retained for public transport, cyclists, and pedestrians. The Queensferry Crossing carries the M90 motorway across the Forth and quickly became one of Scotland's most recognisable modern landmarks.
