Kagi chart

A Kagi chart (kagi ashi, 鉤足) is a chart used in technical analysis to track the price movements of an asset. It is believed to have been developed in Japan in the 1870s, and was popularised in the West by Steve Nison's book Beyond Candlesticks. Unlike conventional charts, a Kagi chart is independent of time: the x-axis does not advance at fixed intervals.

Construction

A Kagi chart consists of a series of vertical lines connected by short horizontal lines, called "shoulders" (when a rising line turns down) and "waists" (when a falling line turns up). A line continues in the same direction until the price reverses by at least a predetermined reversal amount, at which point a new line is drawn in the opposite direction. Lines change thickness — thick "yang" lines when the price rises above the previous shoulder, thin "yin" lines when it falls below the previous waist.

Interpretation

Traders watch for the shifts between thick and thin lines as signals of changes in supply and demand: a change to a thick line after a series of thin lines suggests buyers have taken control, and vice versa. Because the reversal amount filters out small fluctuations, Kagi charts emphasise significant trends and suppress market noise.
