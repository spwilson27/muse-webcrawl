Napoleonic Code

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Napoleonic Code, officially the Civil Code of the French (Code civil des Français), is the French civil code established under Napoleon Bonaparte and promulgated on 21 March 1804. Prepared by a commission of four jurists (including Jean-Étienne-Marie Portalis) in remarkable speed after 1800, it was the first successful attempt at a comprehensive, systematic code for a modern state, and one of the most influential legal documents in history.

Content and principles

The Code embodied revolutionary principles: equality of (male) citizens before the law, abolition of feudal privilege, secular authority over marriage and civil status, absolute private property, and freedom of contract — while also entrenching patriarchal family law (wives' legal subordination, paternal authority) that was reformed only in the twentieth century. It was accompanied by companion codes of civil and criminal procedure, commerce and penal law, organising French law into accessible written form that ordinary citizens could read.

Influence

Spread by conquest, imitation and colonisation, the Code became the model for civil codes across continental Europe (Belgium, the Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Portugal), Latin America, the Middle East (Egypt), Japan (in part) and jurisdictions such as Louisiana and Quebec — a principal vehicle of the civil-law tradition worldwide. In France, the Code (renamed Code civil) remains in force, much amended; its bicentenary in 2004 was marked by legal conferences worldwide on codification's future in an age of European and global law.
