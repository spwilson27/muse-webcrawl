Butcher block

Placeholder image: an end-grain butcher block.

Butcher block is a style of assembled wood used for heavy-duty cutting boards, worktops and chopping surfaces. It is made by gluing together strips of wood, commonly hard maple, teak, birch or walnut, into a thick slab. In traditional end-grain butcher block, the wood fibres run vertically, so the cutting surface presents the ends of the fibres to the knife.

Construction and properties

End-grain construction is highly durable and self-healing: knife edges slide between the wood fibres rather than cutting across them, which keeps both the block and the blade in better condition, and shallow cuts tend to close up. Edge-grain and flat-grain variants also exist and are cheaper but less hard-wearing. Butcher block surfaces are finished with food-safe mineral oil or wax rather than varnish, and require periodic re-oiling to prevent drying and cracking.

Uses

Originally the work surface of butchers' shops — heavy freestanding blocks on legs, used for breaking down carcasses with cleavers — butcher block is now widely used for domestic kitchen countertops, island tops and chopping boards.
