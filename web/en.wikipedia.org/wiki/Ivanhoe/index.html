Ivanhoe

The tournament at Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a central scene of the novel.

Ivanhoe: A Romance is a historical novel by Sir Walter Scott, first published in three volumes in 1819 (dated 1820). It is set in 12th-century England, in the years after the Norman Conquest, and is credited with reviving popular interest in medievalism and chivalry in the Romantic era.

Plot

The hero, Wilfred of Ivanhoe, is a Saxon knight disinherited by his father Cedric for supporting the Norman king Richard the Lionheart and for loving Cedric's ward, the Lady Rowena. Returning disguised from the Crusades, Ivanhoe triumphs at the tournament at Ashby-de-la-Zouch, is wounded, and is nursed by Rebecca, the Jewish daughter of Isaac of York. The novel interweaves the conflict between Saxons and Normans, the villainy of Prince John and the Templar Brian de Bois-Guilbert, a trial for witchcraft brought against Rebecca, and the intervention of King Richard and of Robin Hood and his outlaws, who appear as Locksley and his men.

Themes and legacy

Scott presents the fusion of Saxon and Norman into a single English nation as the path to national unity, though modern critics have examined the novel's depiction of Jewish characters — Rebecca is among its most admired figures, and her prominence was progressive for its day even as Isaac is drawn partly from stereotype. Ivanhoe shaped the popular image of the Middle Ages for generations, influenced the depiction of Robin Hood as a noble Saxon outlaw, and has been adapted many times for film and television, notably the 1952 MGM film starring Robert Taylor and Elizabeth Taylor.
