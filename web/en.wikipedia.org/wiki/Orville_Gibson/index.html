Orville Gibson

An early Gibson mandolin built on Orville Gibson's archtop design.

Orville H. Gibson (1856 – August 19, 1918) was an American luthier who founded the Gibson Mandolin-Guitar Manufacturing Company in Kalamazoo, Michigan, in 1902. The company that bears his name became one of the world's best-known makers of guitars and mandolins.

Life and work

Born in Chateaugay, New York, Gibson settled in Kalamazoo and worked as a shoe clerk while building instruments in his spare time. His key innovation, patented in 1898, was to carve the tops and backs of mandolins and guitars from solid wood, like a violin's, rather than bending thin strips or using flat "tater bug" bowl backs. These archtop instruments were louder, more durable and easier to manufacture. In 1902 investors incorporated the Gibson company around his designs and name; Gibson himself worked for the firm only briefly, receiving payments under an endorsement arrangement, and his later years were clouded by illness. He died in Ogdensburg, New York, in 1918.

Legacy

Gibson's archtop principle underlies the Gibson L-5 guitar, the F-style mandolins later perfected by Lloyd Loar, and ultimately the company's famous electric guitars such as the Les Paul.
