Overview

Orthoclase, or orthoclase feldspar, is an important tectosilicate mineral and the potassium end-member of the alkali feldspar series, KAlSi₃O₈. It defines hardness 6 on the Mohs scale and is a major constituent of granite and other felsic igneous rocks. Its name comes from Greek for "straight fracture", referring to its two cleavages meeting at right angles.

Varieties and uses

Sanidine is the high-temperature form found in volcanic rocks; microcline (including green amazonite) is the low-temperature polymorph. Adularia shows schiller effects, and moonstone is a gem feldspar with adularescence. Orthoclase is used in ceramics and glassmaking, and its potassium-argon system helps date rocks. With plagioclase, feldspars make up about 60% of Earth's crust — the most abundant mineral group.
