Tamping machine

TypeRailway maintenance vehicle
PurposePacking (tamping) track ballast
Major manufacturersPlasser & Theurer, Harsco, Matisa

A tamping machine or ballast tamper is a self-propelled rail vehicle used to pack (tamp) the ballast under railway tracks so that the track geometry — level, alignment, and cross-level — is correct and durable. Tamping restores the support of the sleepers (ties) that is gradually lost as trains pass.

Operation

The machine lifts the track slightly while vibrating tamping tines are driven into the ballast on either side of each sleeper; the tines then squeeze the ballast beneath the sleeper to form a compacted bed. Modern continuous-action tampers combine tamping with measuring systems that record and correct track geometry, and many also sweep and profile the ballast afterwards.

History

Ballast was originally tamped by hand with shovels and picks. Mechanized tampers appeared in the early 20th century, and firms such as Austria's Plasser & Theurer, founded in 1953, developed the large multiple-sleeper tampers used on main lines worldwide today.
