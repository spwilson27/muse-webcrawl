Type: Anglican province
Primate: Archbishop of Armagh

The Church of Ireland (Irish: Eaglais na hÉireann) is a Christian church in Ireland and an autonomous province of the Anglican Communion. It operates across both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland and is the second-largest church on the island after the Roman Catholic Church.

History

The church traces its roots to St Patrick and the early Irish church. At the Reformation it became the established state church, retaining the historic cathedrals and the succession of bishops while adopting Protestant theology and the Book of Common Prayer (in Irish from 1608). It was disestablished by the Irish Church Act 1869, effective 1871, and thereafter governed itself through the General Synod.

Structure

The church has two provinces, Armagh and Dublin, led by archbishops, and eleven dioceses. St Patrick's Cathedral and Christ Church Cathedral, both in Dublin, are its best-known buildings. Worship follows the Book of Common Prayer (2004 revision).
