The Standing Rock Indian Reservation is a Native American reservation spanning parts of North Dakota and South Dakota in the United States. It is home to the federally recognized Standing Rock Sioux Tribe, composed principally of the Yanktonai, Hunkpapa, and Blackfeet (Sihasapa) bands of the Lakota and Dakota peoples.

History

The reservation was established in 1889 following the division of the much larger Great Sioux Reservation into several smaller reservations under the Dawes-era policy of allotment and surplus land sales. Earlier, the Treaty of Fort Laramie of 1868 had guaranteed the Sioux a vast territory. The famous Hunkpapa leader Sitting Bull lived at Standing Rock during his final years and was killed there in 1890 by Indian agency police attempting to arrest him, shortly before the Wounded Knee Massacre.

Geography

The reservation covers Sioux County, North Dakota, and Corson County, South Dakota, together with portions of neighboring counties. Its eastern boundary is formed by the Missouri River and Lake Oahe. The reservation seat is Fort Yates, North Dakota.

Dakota Access Pipeline protests

In 2016 and 2017, Standing Rock became the center of the Dakota Access Pipeline protests, in which tribal members and thousands of supporters, describing themselves as "water protectors," opposed construction of an oil pipeline just north of the reservation, arguing it threatened water supplies and sacred sites. The protests drew international attention and became one of the largest Native American-led movements in modern U.S. history.

Government and services

The tribe is governed by an elected tribal council under its constitution. Sitting Bull College, named for the Hunkpapa leader, provides higher education on the reservation. The Prairie Knights Casino and Resort, operated by the tribe, is a significant employer.
