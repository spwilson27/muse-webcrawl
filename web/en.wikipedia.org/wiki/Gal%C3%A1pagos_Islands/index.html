Galápagos Islands
Country: Ecuador
Location: Pacific Ocean, c. 900 km west of mainland Ecuador
Major islands: Isabela, Santa Cruz, San Cristóbal, Floreana
Famous for: Endemic species; Darwin's research

The Galápagos Islands are an archipelago of volcanic islands in the Eastern Pacific Ocean, located around the Equator, 900 kilometres west of the mainland of South America. They form the Galápagos Province of the Republic of Ecuador, with a population of slightly more than 33,000.

Wildlife and evolution

The islands are famed for their large number of endemic species — including giant tortoises, marine iguanas and flightless cormorants — which were studied by Charles Darwin during the second voyage of HMS Beagle. His observations of variation among mockingbirds and finches contributed to the development of his theory of evolution by natural selection.

Conservation

Much of the archipelago is protected as a national park and marine reserve and was among the first UNESCO World Heritage Sites, though invasive species and tourism pressures remain major concerns.
