Plastic recycling

Sorted plastic bottles at a recycling facility

Plastic recycling is the processing of plastic waste into new products. When performed correctly it can reduce dependence on landfill, conserve resources, and reduce greenhouse gas emissions compared with producing new plastic from raw materials.

Process

Recycling of thermoplastics typically involves collection, sorting (by polymer type and colour, increasingly with automated near-infrared sorters), washing, shredding into flakes, melting, and extrusion into pellets that can be remoulded. Resin identification codes 1 (PET) and 2 (HDPE) – drinks bottles and milk jugs – are the most widely recycled; other resins are collected in fewer places. Chemical recycling processes, such as pyrolysis and depolymerisation, break polymers down to monomers or feedstocks and can handle some contaminated or mixed streams.

Challenges and rates

Global plastic recycling rates remain low: the OECD has estimated that only about 9% of plastic waste is recycled, with the rest landfilled, incinerated, or mismanaged. Obstacles include contamination, mixed-polymer products that cannot easily be separated, the low price of virgin plastic, and degradation of polymer quality with each cycle ("downcycling").

For decades much of the developed world's collected plastic was exported for processing, principally to China, until China's 2018 "National Sword" import restrictions sharply reduced that trade and redirected flows to Southeast Asia and elsewhere.

Policy

Policy tools include deposit-return schemes for beverage containers, extended producer responsibility laws, recycled-content mandates, and the negotiations, begun in 2022 under the UN Environment Assembly, toward a global treaty on plastic pollution.
