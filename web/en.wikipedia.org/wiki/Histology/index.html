Histology

Histology is the study of the microscopic structure of tissues — how cells are organised into the tissues that build organs. It is a foundation of Anatomy and of diagnostic pathology.

Methods

Tissues are fixed, embedded in wax, sliced into micrometre-thin sections, mounted and stained — classically with haematoxylin (nuclei, blue-purple) and eosin (cytoplasm, pink). Newer methods include immunohistochemistry (antibody tags), electron microscopy and digital slide scanning for remote diagnosis.

The four tissue types

Epithelial (coverings and glands — e.g. the lining of the Intestine), connective (bone, blood, cartilage, fat — unified by extracellular matrix such as Collagen), muscle, and nervous tissue. Pathologists read biopsies histologically to diagnose cancer and diseases like Cirrhosis.
