Janet Jagan (née Rosenberg; October 20, 1920 – March 28, 2009) was a Guyanese politician who served as president of Guyana from 1997 to 1999. Born in Chicago, she was the first woman to hold the office, the first (and only) American-born head of state of Guyana, and one of the founders of the People's Progressive Party (PPP) alongside her husband, Cheddi Jagan.

Early life and arrival in Guyana

Janet Rosenberg was born to a middle-class Jewish family in Chicago and trained as a nurse. She met Cheddi Jagan, a Guyanese dentistry student, in 1942 and married him in 1943. She moved with him to British Guiana that year and became deeply involved in the colony's labour and independence politics.

Political career

In 1950 the Jagans founded the People's Progressive Party. Janet Jagan served for decades as the party's general secretary and as editor of its newspaper, Mirror. In 1953, in the colony's first election under universal suffrage, she became one of the first women elected to the House of Assembly; after the PPP victory the constitution was suspended by the British within months. She later served as minister of labour, health and housing (1957–1961) and in other cabinet and parliamentary posts across a political career spanning more than fifty years.

Presidency

Cheddi Jagan became president in 1992 and died in office in March 1997. Janet Jagan served first as prime minister and then won the December 1997 general election, becoming president. Her term was marked by post-election unrest and street protests by the opposition. Citing ill health, she resigned on August 11, 1999, and was succeeded by Finance Minister Bharrat Jagdeo.

Death and legacy

Jagan died in Georgetown in 2009. She remains a central figure in Guyana's modern political history, noted for her organisational role in the PPP and for breaking barriers as a foreign-born woman leading a South American state.
