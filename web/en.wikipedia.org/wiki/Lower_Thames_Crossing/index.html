The Lower Thames Crossing is a proposed road crossing of the River Thames in England, east of the existing Dartford Crossing. It would connect the A2 and M2 in Kent, south of the river, with the A13 and M25 in Essex, north of the river, via a bored tunnel beneath the Thames between Gravesend and Tilbury.

Background

The Dartford Crossing, the only road crossing of the Thames east of London, carries far more traffic than it was designed for and suffers chronic congestion. Successive studies since the 2000s examined options for additional capacity, and in April 2017 the government announced its preferred route for a new crossing at the Lower Thames location.

Scheme

The project comprises roughly 14.3 miles (23 km) of new road, including twin bored tunnels of 16.4 metres internal diameter — among the largest bored road tunnels in the world — carrying three lanes in each direction. The route would pass near Gravesend, Orsett, and South Ockendon. National Highways promotes the crossing as a means of nearly doubling road capacity across the Thames east of London and relieving the Dartford Crossing.

Planning and controversy

An application for a Development Consent Order was submitted in 2022 and examined during 2023; the government granted consent in March 2025. The scheme, whose cost estimates have risen to around £9–10 billion, has been opposed by some local councils, residents, and environmental groups concerned about traffic, air quality, and the loss of ancient woodland and green belt land. Supporters, including business groups, argue it is essential for trade through the Channel ports.
