Local Government Code

EnactedOctober 10, 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160)
CountryPhilippines
Signed byPresident Corazon Aquino

The Local Government Code of 1991, officially Republic Act No. 7160, is a Philippine law that devolved substantial powers, responsibilities, and resources from the national government to local government units (LGUs) — provinces, cities, municipalities, and barangays. It is regarded as a landmark in Philippine decentralisation.

Provisions

The Code transferred responsibility for basic services — including health, agriculture extension, social welfare, local infrastructure, and environmental management — to LGUs, and increased their share of national internal revenue (the Internal Revenue Allotment, now the National Tax Allotment) to 40 percent. It also mandated local special bodies, civil-society representation in local councils, and mechanisms of recall, initiative, and referendum.

Structure of local government

The Code sets out the powers, officials, and internal organisation of each tier of local government, from the barangay (village) up to the province, and provides for leagues of local governments and inter-local cooperation.

Impact

Assessments of the Code note expanded local autonomy and innovative local programmes, alongside challenges of uneven capacity, fiscal dependence on national transfers, and political dynasties. Successive reform proposals have sought to amend its allocation of functions and funds.
