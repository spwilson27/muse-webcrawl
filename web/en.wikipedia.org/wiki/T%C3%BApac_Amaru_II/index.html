Túpac Amaru II

BornJosé Gabriel Condorcanqui, 19 March 1738, Tinta, Peru
Died18 May 1781, Cuzco, Peru
Known forLeading the largest Indigenous rebellion in colonial Spanish America

Túpac Amaru II (born José Gabriel Condorcanqui Noguera, 1738–1781) was an Indigenous Peruvian cacique (chief) who led the largest rebellion against Spanish colonial rule in the Americas. Claiming descent from Túpac Amaru, the last Inca ruler executed in 1572, he took his ancestor's name and demanded the abolition of the mita forced-labour system, the alcabala sales tax, and corrupt colonial officials.

The rebellion

In November 1780, Túpac Amaru seized the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga and had him executed at Tungasuca, sparking a revolt that spread across the southern Andes and drew tens of thousands of Indigenous supporters, along with some mestizos and creoles. His forces won an early victory at Sangarará but failed to capture Cuzco. His wife Micaela Bastidas was a key strategist and commander. In 1781 he was captured and, after being forced to watch the execution of his family, was brutally executed in Cuzco's main plaza on 18 May 1781.

Legacy

The rebellion continued under his relatives until 1783 and shook Spanish America, prompting limited reforms. Túpac Amaru II became a foundational hero of Peruvian nationalism and Indigenous movements across Latin America; his name was later adopted by Uruguay's Tupamaros guerrillas and Peru's MRTA, and the American rapper Tupac Shakur was named after him.
