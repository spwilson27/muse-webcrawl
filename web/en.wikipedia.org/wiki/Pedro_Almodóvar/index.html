Pedro Almodóvar

Born: 25 September 1949, Calzada de Calatrava, Spain
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, producer
Years active: 1974–present

Pedro Almodóvar Caballero (born 25 September 1949) is a Spanish film director, screenwriter and producer, regarded as one of the most internationally acclaimed filmmakers of his generation. His films are marked by melodrama, irreverent humour, bold colour, glossy décor and complex narratives, often centred on strong female characters, desire, identity and family. He emerged as a leading figure of La Movida Madrileña, the countercultural movement that followed the death of Franco.

Career

Born in Calzada de Calatrava in La Mancha, Almodóvar moved to Madrid in the late 1960s and taught himself cinema by making Super 8 shorts, unable to attend film school. His first feature, Pepi, Luci, Bom (1980), was followed by a string of exuberant comedies and melodramas including Labyrinth of Passion (1982), Matador (1986) and Law of Desire (1987). International breakthrough came with Women on the Verge of a Nervous Breakdown (1988), nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Major works

With his brother Agustín he founded the production company El Deseo. Later films include All About My Mother (1999), which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film; Talk to Her (2002), which won him the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay; Volver (2006); The Skin I Live In (2011); and the semi-autobiographical Pain and Glory (2019), starring Antonio Banderas, a frequent collaborator alongside Penélope Cruz. His first English-language feature, The Room Next Door (2024), won the Golden Lion at Venice.

Style and themes

Almodóvar's style draws on classic Hollywood melodrama, Spanish popular culture, kitsch and the work of directors such as Douglas Sirk and Luis Buñuel. Recurring themes include mothers, death, Catholicism, queer identity, and the interplay of performance and real life. He has received numerous honours, including two Academy Awards, five BAFTAs, and Spain's National Film Award.
