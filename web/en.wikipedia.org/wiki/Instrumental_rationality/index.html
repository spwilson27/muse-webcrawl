Instrumental rationality

Instrumental rationality is a mode of practical reasoning focused on finding the most efficient or cost-effective means to achieve a given end, without reflecting on the worth of the end itself. It contrasts with value rationality, in which action is guided by values and beliefs regardless of consequences.

Origins

The concept is closely associated with the sociologist Max Weber, who described Zweckrationalität (goal-oriented rational action) as one of four types of social action. David Hume anticipated the idea in arguing that reason is the "slave of the passions," suited to discovering means rather than choosing ends.

Critique

Theorists of the Frankfurt School, notably Max Horkheimer and Theodor Adorno in Dialectic of Enlightenment, criticised instrumental reason as a form of thought that reduces nature and people to objects of control. Jürgen Habermas contrasted instrumental action with communicative action oriented toward mutual understanding.
