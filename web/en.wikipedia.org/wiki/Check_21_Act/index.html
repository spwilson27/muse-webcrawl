The Check Clearing for the 21st Century Act, known as Check 21, is a United States federal law enacted in 2003 that transformed cheque (check) processing by making it legally possible to truncate paper checks and substitute printed "substitute checks" for the originals. It took effect on October 28, 2004.

Background

Before Check 21, US law generally required that the original paper check be physically presented to the paying bank, so billions of checks travelled by air and ground courier across the country each year. The grounding of air transport after the September 11 attacks in 2001, which stranded vast numbers of checks in transit, highlighted the fragility of the system and spurred reform. Other countries had already adopted check truncation, but earlier US proposals had stalled over the question of whether banks could be forced to accept electronic images.

Substitute checks

Check 21's solution was the substitute check, also called an image replacement document (IRD): a paper reproduction of both sides of an original check, bearing the legend "This is a legal copy of your check. You can use it the same way you would use the original check." A conforming substitute check is the legal equivalent of the original, so any bank must accept it. Banks may therefore scan checks early in the clearing process, transmit the images electronically, and destroy or archive the paper originals; only banks that require paper receive a printed substitute. The law does not compel any bank to accept images directly, but the substitute-check mechanism makes electronic exchange workable end to end.

Consumer protections and effects

The Act gives consumers a right to expedited recredit when a substitute check was improperly charged to their account, and imposes warranties and indemnities allocating losses among banks in the chain. In practice, Check 21 dramatically reduced the cost and time of check clearing, enabled remote deposit capture (including later mobile phone deposits), and hastened the decline of "float". Check use itself continued to fall as electronic payments grew, but the infrastructure Check 21 created underpins the remaining system.
