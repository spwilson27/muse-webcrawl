Loughs Agency

Formed1999 (origins 1952)
JurisdictionFoyle and Carlingford areas, Ireland/Northern Ireland
ParentNorth/South Ministerial Council
HeadquartersDerry

The Loughs Agency (formally the Loughs Agency of the Foyle, Carlingford and Irish Lights Commission) is a cross-border body in Ireland responsible for the conservation, protection and development of inland fisheries and marine resources in the Foyle and Carlingford areas, which span the border between Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland.

History

The agency continues the work of the Foyle Fisheries Commission, established in 1952 by parallel legislation in Belfast and Dublin to manage the salmon fisheries of the River Foyle system. Under the Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement of 1998, its functions were transferred in 1999 to the Loughs Agency as one of the North/South implementation bodies overseen by the North/South Ministerial Council, and its remit was extended to Carlingford Lough and to marine tourism and aquaculture.

Functions

The agency licenses and regulates salmon, sea trout and shellfish fisheries, enforces fisheries law in its catchments, monitors water quality and fish stocks, and promotes angling tourism on rivers such as the Foyle, Finn and Mourne. It is funded jointly by the Irish government and the Northern Ireland Executive.
