Acupressure

Acupressure is an alternative medicine technique often used in conjunction with acupuncture or reflexology. It is based on the concept of life energy (qi) which flows through "meridians" in the body. In treatment, physical pressure is applied to acupuncture points, or ashi trigger points, with the aim of clearing blockages in these meridians. Pressure may be applied by hand, elbow, or with various devices.

Background

Acupressure developed within traditional Chinese medicine, in which specific points on the body are believed to lie along channels (meridians) carrying qi. Stimulating the points with finger pressure rather than needles is intended to restore balance and promote healing. Japanese shiatsu and other Asian bodywork traditions use closely related methods. In clinical practice, acupressure is commonly offered for nausea, pain, stress and fatigue; wrist bands that press the P6 (Neiguan) point are marketed for motion sickness and morning sickness.

Effectiveness

Systematic reviews have found some evidence that acupressure may help with nausea and vomiting, including post-operative and pregnancy-related nausea, but the overall quality of trials is low and evidence for most other conditions is inconclusive. Medical organisations generally classify acupressure as a complementary therapy lacking a plausible biological mechanism beyond placebo and the non-specific effects of touch and relaxation.
