REDD and REDD+

REDD+ (or REDD-plus) is a framework to encourage developing countries to reduce emissions and enhance removals of greenhouse gases through a variety of forest management options, providing financial value for the carbon stored in forests. It grew out of REDD — "Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and forest Degradation" — negotiated under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC).

The "+" in REDD+ refers to additional forest-related activities beyond avoiding deforestation and degradation: conservation of forest carbon stocks, sustainable management of forests, and enhancement of forest carbon stocks.

History

The idea was first introduced in 2005 by Papua New Guinea and Costa Rica on behalf of the Coalition for Rainforest Nations. It was taken up in the Bali Action Plan (2007), elaborated in the Cancun Agreements (2010), and given a full rulebook in the Warsaw Framework for REDD-plus (2013). The Paris Agreement (2015) enshrined REDD+ in its Article 5.

How it works

Developing countries establish forest reference emission levels, implement national strategies, build monitoring systems (MRV — measurement, reporting and verification), and put in place safeguards for biodiversity and the rights of indigenous peoples and local communities. In return they may receive results-based payments for verified emission reductions.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about land rights, benefit sharing, permanence of stored carbon, leakage of deforestation to other areas, and whether forest carbon credits are a substitute for reducing fossil-fuel emissions.
