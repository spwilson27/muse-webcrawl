José Luis Alberto Muñoz Marín (18 February 1898 – 30 April 1980) was a Puerto Rican journalist, politician, statesman, and poet regarded as the "Father of Modern Puerto Rico" and the "Architect of the Commonwealth." He was the first democratically elected governor of Puerto Rico, serving from 1949 to 1965.

Career

The son of politician and publisher Luis Muñoz Rivera, Muñoz Marín began as a poet and journalist in New York and Puerto Rico. He became president of the Puerto Rican Senate in 1941 and founded the Popular Democratic Party (PPD) in 1938, winning power on a program of social and economic reform summarized by the slogan "Bread, Land, and Liberty."

As governor he oversaw Operation Bootstrap, which industrialized the island's economy through tax incentives for U.S. manufacturers, and the creation in 1952 of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico (Estado Libre Asociado) under a locally drafted constitution, following the U.S. Congress's authorization of self-government in Public Law 600. His tenure brought expanded education, infrastructure, and emigration to the U.S. mainland, but also criticism for repression of the independence movement, including the era of the "Gag Law" (Law 53).

Muñoz Marín retired from the governorship in 1965 and remains the dominant figure of 20th-century Puerto Rican politics; San Juan's international airport bears his name.
