Direct marketing

Direct marketing is a form of communicating an offer, where organizations communicate directly to a pre-selected customer and supply a method for a direct response. Among practitioners, it is also known as direct response marketing. By contrast, advertising is a mass-message form of communication.

Direct marketing channels include direct mail, telemarketing, catalogues, email marketing, text messaging, and targeted digital advertising. A defining characteristic is that the marketer seeks a measurable response — an order, a request for information, or a visit to a store or website — and can therefore calculate the return on each campaign.

History

The practice dates to the mail-order catalogues of the 19th century, such as those of Montgomery Ward (1872) and Sears, Roebuck and Co., which brought goods to rural customers. The rise of customer databases and cheap computing in the late 20th century transformed the field, and email and internet marketing later became its fastest-growing forms.

Issues

Direct marketing is associated with concerns about privacy, unsolicited communications ("junk mail" and spam), and the environmental cost of printed mail. Many jurisdictions regulate it, and do-not-call registers and opt-out schemes are common.
