Dust Bowl

Dust Bowl
LocationGreat Plains, United States and Canada
Period1930–1936 (some areas to 1940)
CausesSevere drought; deep ploughing of native grasses
EffectsMass migration, farm failures, ecological damage

The Dust Bowl was the result of a period of severe dust storms that greatly damaged the ecology and agriculture of the American and Canadian prairies during the 1930s. The phenomenon was caused by a combination of natural factors (severe drought) and man-made factors: a failure to apply dryland farming methods to prevent wind erosion, most notably the destruction of the natural topsoil by settlers in the region.

The drought came in three waves: 1934, 1936, and 1939–1940, but some regions of the High Plains experienced drought conditions for as long as eight years. Massive dust storms — 'black blizzards' — carried topsoil as far as the East Coast; on Black Sunday, 14 April 1935, one of the worst storms swept from the Oklahoma Panhandle across the plains.

Causes

During the wet 1910s and 1920s, homesteaders and speculators ploughed up millions of acres of native grassland, whose deep roots had anchored the soil. Mechanised farming with tractors and one-way ploughs left the land bare between crops. When drought struck after 1930 and the Great Depression ruined crop prices, the exposed soil simply blew away.

Human impact

Hundreds of thousands of people — derided as 'Okies' after the worst-hit state, Oklahoma — abandoned failing farms and migrated, most famously to California along Route 66. The exodus was chronicled in John Steinbeck's novel The Grapes of Wrath (1939) and in the photographs of Dorothea Lange for the Farm Security Administration.

Government response

The Roosevelt administration created the Soil Conservation Service under Hugh Hammond Bennett, promoted contour ploughing and shelterbelts, bought back marginal land, and taught dryland techniques. Rains returning in the early 1940s, together with wartime demand, ended the crisis, though historians warn that modern groundwater depletion could allow a recurrence.
