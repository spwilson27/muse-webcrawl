Jorge Eliécer Gaitán Ayala (January 23, 1903 – April 9, 1948) was a Colombian lawyer and populist politician, leader of the Liberal Party and its most likely next president when he was assassinated in Bogotá on April 9, 1948. His murder triggered the riots known as El Bogotazo and opened the decade of partisan bloodshed known as La Violencia.

Career

Gaitán studied law at the National University of Colombia and earned a doctorate in Rome, where he studied under the criminologist Enrico Ferri. He rose to national prominence in 1929 with his congressional denunciation of the 1928 Banana Massacre, in which the army killed striking United Fruit Company workers near Ciénaga. He served as mayor of Bogotá (1936–1937), minister of education (1940–1942), and minister of labour, and built a mass following among urban workers and the poor with fiery speeches against the "oligarchy" and slogans such as "Yo no soy un hombre, soy un pueblo" ("I am not a man, I am a people").

Assassination and aftermath

After losing the 1946 presidential election—when a Liberal split let the Conservatives win—Gaitán unified the Liberal Party under his leadership and was widely expected to win the next election. On April 9, 1948, he was shot outside his office in downtown Bogotá by Juan Roa Sierra, who was immediately lynched by a mob. News of the assassination unleashed massive rioting that destroyed much of central Bogotá, and violence spread through the country, beginning La Violencia, a period of civil conflict that claimed some 200,000 lives. Gaitán remains one of Colombia's most revered and debated political figures; his daughter Gloria Gaitán became a writer and political activist.
