The Blue Cliff Record (Chinese: 碧巖錄; pinyin: Bìyán Lù; Japanese: Hekiganroku) is a collection of one hundred Chan Buddhist koans, compiled in China during the Song dynasty. It is regarded as one of the most important texts of the Zen tradition, sometimes called "the first book of Zen".

Composition

The monk Xuedou Zhongxian (980–1052) selected one hundred old cases — encounters and sayings of earlier masters — and composed a verse comment on each. A century later the master Yuanwu Keqin added introductions, interlinear remarks ("capping phrases"), and prose commentaries based on his lectures; his disciple published the whole in 1125. According to tradition, Yuanwu's own student Dahui Zonggao, fearing students would attach to the words rather than the insight, burned the woodblocks of the book; it was reconstructed and reprinted in the early 14th century.

Content and influence

Each case layers anecdote, verse and commentary, modelling the literary, allusive style of Song-dynasty Chan. The first case concerns Bodhidharma's audience with Emperor Wu of Liang. Together with the Gateless Barrier, the Blue Cliff Record forms the core of koan study, especially in the Japanese Rinzai school, where it was translated and commented on for centuries.
