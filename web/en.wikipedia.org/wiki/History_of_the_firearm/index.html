History of the firearm

The history of the firearm begins in China following the invention of gunpowder. The earliest gunpowder weapons, such as fire lances — tubes of bamboo or metal that projected flames and shrapnel — appeared by the tenth to twelfth centuries. Metal-barrel hand cannons are documented in China by the late thirteenth century, including the Heilongjiang hand cannon dated to about 1288.

Spread to Europe and the Islamic world

Gunpowder technology spread westward through the Islamic world to Europe by the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. Cannon were used at sieges such as Constantinople in 1453, while smaller arms evolved from hand cannons into the arquebus. The matchlock mechanism of the fifteenth century allowed a soldier to fire while holding the weapon with both hands.

Ignition systems

Successive ignition systems improved reliability: the wheellock (early 1500s), the snaplock and the flintlock (early 1600s), which remained standard for two centuries. In the early nineteenth century, percussion caps using fulminate compounds replaced flint, enabling more dependable firing in wet weather.

Breechloading and repeating arms

The nineteenth century brought rifled barrels, the Minié ball, breechloading designs such as the Dreyse needle gun, and metallic cartridges. Revolvers by Samuel Colt and lever-action rifles by Winchester made repeating fire practical. Hiram Maxim's recoil-operated machine gun (1884) transformed warfare.

Modern firearms

The twentieth century introduced widespread self-loading pistols and rifles, including the German Sturmgewehr 44 and the Soviet AK-47, the most widely produced assault rifle. Modern developments include polymer frames, modular designs, and precision manufacturing, alongside continuing debates over firearms regulation worldwide.
