Lake Taupō

Lake Taupō (Māori: Taupō-nui-a-Tia) is a large crater lake in New Zealand's North Island, in the caldera of the Taupō Volcano. With a surface area of about 616 square kilometres it is the largest lake by surface area in New Zealand, and the second largest freshwater lake in Oceania.

The lake was formed and enlarged by major volcanic eruptions, including the Oruanui eruption about 26,500 years ago and the Hatepe eruption around 232 CE, one of the most violent eruptions of the last 5,000 years. Taupō drains via the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest river.

The lake is a noted trout fishery, stocked with brown and rainbow trout introduced in the late 19th century, and a major tourist destination centred on the town of Taupō on its north-eastern shore.
