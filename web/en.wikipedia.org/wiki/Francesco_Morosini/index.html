Francesco Morosini

Francesco Morosini (26 February 1619 – 16 January 1694) was the Doge of Venice from 1688 to 1694, famous as a military commander during Venice's wars against the Ottoman Empire. Known as "il Peloponnesiaco" (the Peloponnesian) for his conquest of the Morea.

Early career and Candia

Born into a prominent Venetian patrician family, Morosini distinguished himself at sea from a young age. As Captain General he commanded the defence of Candia (Heraklion) during its long siege, negotiating its surrender to the Ottomans in 1669 after 21 years of siege — the longest in history. He was tried for the loss but acquitted.

Morean War

In the Morean War (1684–1699) Morosini led Venetian forces to conquer the Peloponnese and parts of mainland Greece. His bombardment of Athens in 1687 caused the explosion that severely damaged the Parthenon, then used as an Ottoman powder magazine. Elected doge in 1688 while still in command in the field — unprecedented — he died on campaign at Nauplia in 1694. His embalmed cat, famous in Venetian lore, is preserved with his effects in the Museo Correr.
