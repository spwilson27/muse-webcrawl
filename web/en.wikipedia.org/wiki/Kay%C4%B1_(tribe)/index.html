The Kayı (Turkish: Kayı boyu) were one of the Oghuz Turkic tribes, traditionally placed in the Bozok branch descended from the legendary Oghuz Khagan. According to Ottoman tradition, the Kayı were the tribe of Ertuğrul and his son Osman I, founder of the Ottoman Empire, a genealogy that gave the tribe immense prestige in Ottoman historiography.

Origins and migration

The Kayı appear in medieval sources such as Mahmud al-Kashgari's Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk (11th century), which lists their tamgha. Like other Oghuz groups, they migrated westward from Central Asia with the Seljuk expansion into Iran and Anatolia. Ottoman chronicles written in the 15th century traced the dynasty's line to the Kayı and through them to Oghuz Khagan; modern historians debate how much of this is later legitimising construction.

Symbolism

The Kayı tamgha — read as "IYI", formed like a bow flanked by arrows — was adopted as an emblem by the Ottomans and remains popular in modern Turkey as a symbol of Turkish heritage, appearing in public monuments and popular culture, including the television series Diriliş: Ertuğrul. Numerous villages and towns in Turkey still bear the name Kayı.
