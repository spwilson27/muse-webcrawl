The Newton–Cotes formulas are a family of rules for numerical integration (quadrature) that approximate an integral by evaluating the integrand at equally spaced points and fitting an interpolating polynomial through them. The simplest are the trapezoidal rule (two points) and Simpson's rule (three points); higher members include Simpson's 3/8 rule and Boole's rule.

Closed and open formulas

Closed formulas include the endpoints of the interval; open formulas use only interior points and are useful when the integrand is undefined at the endpoints. For large numbers of points the equally spaced interpolation becomes unstable (Runge's phenomenon), so in practice composite rules — applying a low-order formula on many small subintervals — are preferred.

History

The rules are named for Isaac Newton and Roger Cotes, whose work on interpolation in the early eighteenth century laid the foundation.
