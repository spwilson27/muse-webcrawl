Saatchi & Saatchi is a British multinational communications and advertising agency network with 114 offices in 76 countries and over 6,500 staff. It was founded in 1970 and is currently headquartered in London. Saatchi & Saatchi is part of the Publicis Groupe, the world's third-largest communications group.

History

Saatchi & Saatchi was founded in London by brothers Maurice and Charles Saatchi in 1970. Charles Saatchi had previously worked at the creative consultancy Collett Dickenson Pearce and at the agency CramerSaatchi, which he co-founded with art director Ross Cramer. The new agency grew rapidly through the 1970s on the back of high-profile, often provocative campaigns, including the famous "Labour Isn't Working" poster for the Conservative Party in the 1979 general election and the "Pregnant Man" advertisement for the Health Education Council.

During the 1980s the agency pursued aggressive international expansion, acquiring numerous firms and becoming, for a period, the largest advertising agency in the world. In 1995, after a shareholder dispute, Maurice and Charles Saatchi were forced out of the company; they went on to found M&C Saatchi. In 2000, Saatchi & Saatchi was acquired by the French Publicis Groupe.

Notable campaigns

The agency is associated with the philosophy of "Lovemarks", developed by former CEO Kevin Roberts, which holds that brands should aim to inspire loyalty beyond reason. Famous work includes campaigns for the Toyota, Procter & Gamble, Visa and General Mills accounts, and the long-running British Airways "Face" commercial directed by Hugh Hudson.
