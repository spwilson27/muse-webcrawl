Garcí Rodríguez de Montalvo

Garcí Rodríguez de Montalvo (c. 1450 – 1505) was a Spanish author who arranged the modern version of the chivalric romance Amadís de Gaula, originally written in the 14th century, and added a fourth book of his own, as well as the sequel Las sergas de Esplandián (The Adventures of Esplandián), in which the mythical island of California first appears.

Life

Little is known of Montalvo's life. He was a regidor (councilman) of Medina del Campo in Castile and is believed to have taken part in the war of Granada. He was an avid hunter and fathered several sons. His name is sometimes given as Garci Ordóñez de Montalvo.

Works

Montalvo reworked the first three books of Amadís de Gaula, which circulated in manuscript, and wrote the fourth book himself; the combined work was published in Zaragoza in 1508. His sequel, Las sergas de Esplandián, published around 1510, describes an island called California, ruled by black women under Queen Calafia, lying "on the right hand of the Indies" — the first literary mention of the name that would later be given to the peninsula and region of California in North America.

Legacy

The Amadís became the most influential of the Spanish romances of chivalry and the model against which Don Quixote was later written and parodied. Montalvo's invented island of California left a lasting mark on geography through the name of the modern U.S. state.
