The South Moravian Region (Czech: Jihomoravský kraj) is an administrative unit (kraj) of the Czech Republic, located in the south-western part of the historical region of Moravia. Its capital is Brno, the country's second-largest city. The region borders Austria to the south and Slovakia to the south-east.

Geography

The region is drained chiefly by the Morava river and its tributary the Dyje (Thaya). The landscape ranges from the lowlands of the Lower Morava valley to the wooded hills of the Bohemian-Moravian Highlands in the north-west. The Pálava hills, a limestone ridge near Mikulov, form a protected landscape area noted for rare flora.

Economy and culture

South Moravia is the heart of Czech wine production, accounting for the great majority of the country's vineyards, centred on towns such as Mikulov, Velké Pavlovice and Hodonín. Brno is a centre of higher education, technology and trade fairs. The region contains two UNESCO World Heritage Sites: the Lednice–Valtice Cultural Landscape and the Tugendhat Villa in Brno. The battlefield of Austerlitz (Slavkov), site of Napoleon's 1805 victory, lies east of Brno.

Administrative division

The region is divided into seven districts: Blansko, Brno-City, Brno-Country, Břeclav, Hodonín, Vyškov and Znojmo.
