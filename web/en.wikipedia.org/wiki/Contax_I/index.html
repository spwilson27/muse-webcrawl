The Contax I is a 35 mm rangefinder camera manufactured by Zeiss Ikon in Dresden from 1932 to 1936. It was designed under chief engineer Heinz Küppenbender as a high-end competitor to the Leica II, launching the Contax name.

Design

Unlike the Leica's cloth shutter, the Contax I used a precision vertical-travel metal-slat focal-plane shutter reaching 1/1000 second — built like a roller blind of hinged brass slats. It offered a long-base rangefinder, a removable back for easy loading, and a bayonet lens mount with focus driven by a wheel on the camera front. The die-cast body was heavy and famously complex.

Versions

Six main variants (Ia–If) differ in details such as slow-speed provision, tripod socket and finish; early models lacked slow speeds below 1/25 s.

Legacy

The Contax I established Zeiss's premium 35 mm system; its successor, the Contax II (1936), simplified the mechanism and became a photojournalist classic used by Robert Capa.
