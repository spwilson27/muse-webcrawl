Scooter-sharing system

A scooter-sharing system is a shared transport service in which electric motor scooters and/or kick scooters are made available to use for short-term rentals. They are a form of micromobility, typically operated as dockless systems in which vehicles can be picked up and dropped off anywhere within a service area, located and unlocked through a smartphone application.

History

Motor scooter sharing began in the early 2010s: Scoot Networks launched in San Francisco in 2012 with docked electric mopeds, and European services such as Coup (Berlin, 2016) and eCooltra followed. The sector transformed in 2017–2018 with the arrival of dockless electric kick scooters, pioneered by Bird (founded in Santa Monica in 2017) and Lime, which spread rapidly to cities around the world, attracting billions of dollars of venture capital investment.

Operation

Users register with an operator's app, supplying a payment method and sometimes a driving licence. Scooters are located on an in-app map, unlocked by scanning a QR code, and charged by the minute plus an unlock fee. Operators collect vehicles for recharging, maintenance and rebalancing, employing staff or paying gig-economy "chargers" to collect and charge kick scooters overnight. Fleets are tracked by GPS, and operators may use geofencing to create no-ride and no-parking zones.

Regulation and controversy

The rapid, often unpermitted roll-outs of 2018 provoked backlash over pavement clutter, obstruction of pedestrians, safety of riders and other road users, and the short lifespan and lifecycle emissions of the scooters. Many cities responded with permit schemes, fleet caps, parking rules and speed limits; some, such as Paris in 2023, voted to end rental e-scooter services entirely. Sharing schemes remain a common part of urban transport in many cities, increasingly integrated with public transport planning.
