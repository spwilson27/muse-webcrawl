.303 British

The .303 British (designated as the 303 British by the C.I.P. and SAAMI) or 7.7×56mmR, is a .303-inch calibre rimmed rifle cartridge developed in Britain as a black-powder round in 1888 and adopted with the Lee–Metford rifle.

Service history

It was the standard British and Commonwealth military cartridge from 1889 until the 1950s, chambered in the Lee–Enfield bolt-action rifles, Vickers machine guns, Bren light machine guns and many other arms. It saw service in both World Wars and numerous colonial conflicts before being replaced by the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge.

Surplus and sporting use

The cartridge remains popular for target shooting and hunting, and large numbers of surplus Lee–Enfield rifles are in civilian hands.
