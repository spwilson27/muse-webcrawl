Donald Bailey

Sir Donald Coleman Bailey, OBE (15 September 1901 – 5 May 1985) was an English civil engineer best known as the inventor of the Bailey bridge, a modular prefabricated truss bridge that played a vital role in the Allied war effort during the Second World War.

Life and career

Bailey was born in Rotherham, Yorkshire, and studied engineering at the University of Sheffield. He worked as a civil engineer and, in the 1930s, became a civil servant at the War Office, where he was associated with the Experimental Bridging Establishment at Christchurch, Dorset.

The Bailey bridge

Bailey's idea, developed in 1940–41, was a bridge built from interchangeable prefabricated steel panels pinned together, light enough to be transported and erected by hand without heavy equipment, yet strong enough for tanks. First used in action in North Africa and Italy from 1943, Bailey bridges were credited by senior commanders, including Field Marshal Montgomery, as a decisive contribution to Allied mobility. Over 200 miles of Bailey bridging were assembled during the war. The design remains in use worldwide for military and emergency civilian purposes.

Honours

Bailey was appointed OBE in 1945 and knighted in 1946 for his invention. He retired from government service in 1966 and died in Bournemouth in 1985.
