Syrian civil war spillover in Lebanon

Date2011 – 2017 (major phase)
LocationLebanon (Tripoli, Arsal, Beirut, Bekaa Valley)
PartiesHezbollah and pro-Assad groups; Sunni Islamist and anti-Assad groups; Lebanese Armed Forces

The Syrian civil war spillover in Lebanon refers to the violence, political instability, and refugee flows that spread from the Syrian civil war into neighbouring Lebanon from 2011 onward. Lebanon's own sectarian divisions mirrored Syria's: the Shia movement Hezbollah intervened openly in Syria in support of President Bashar al-Assad, while many Lebanese Sunnis sympathised with the Syrian opposition.

Major episodes

In the northern city of Tripoli, repeated rounds of fighting between the Sunni neighbourhood of Bab al-Tabbaneh and the Alawite neighbourhood of Jabal Mohsen killed scores between 2011 and 2014. In the border town of Arsal, Syrian-based jihadist groups including the al-Nusra Front and the Islamic State seized territory and captured Lebanese soldiers in August 2014, leading to a long hostage crisis and a major Lebanese Army operation in 2017 that expelled the militants from the border zone. Beirut suffered a series of bombings, including the November 2015 Burj el-Barajneh bombings, claimed by the Islamic State, which killed more than 40 people.

Hezbollah intervention and refugees

Hezbollah's deployment of thousands of fighters to Syria — announced publicly in 2013 and decisive in battles such as al-Qusayr — deepened Lebanon's internal tensions and drew retaliatory attacks. Lebanon also received well over one million Syrian refugees, roughly a quarter of its own population, straining public services and the economy, though the Lebanese state pursued a policy of official "dissociation" from the Syrian conflict.
