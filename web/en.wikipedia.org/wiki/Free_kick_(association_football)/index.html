Free kick (association football)

A free kick is a method of restarting play in association football. It is awarded after an infringement of the Laws of the Game by the opposing team and is taken from the spot where the offence occurred. Free kicks are governed by Law 13 and come in two types: direct, from which a goal may be scored directly against the opponents, and indirect, from which the ball must touch another player before a goal can be scored.

Direct free kicks

A direct free kick is awarded for offences such as careless, reckless or excessively forceful challenges, holding, handball, and other fouls listed in Law 12. If a direct free kick offence is committed by a defender inside their own penalty area, a penalty kick is awarded instead. When a direct free kick is taken close to the opponents' goal, the defending team typically forms a "wall" of players, who must stand at least 9.15 metres (10 yards) from the ball.

Indirect free kicks

An indirect free kick is awarded for offences including dangerous play, impeding an opponent without contact, dissent by action, and specified goalkeeper offences such as handling a deliberate back-pass. The referee indicates an indirect free kick by raising an arm, which is kept raised until the ball touches another player or goes out of play.

Procedure

The ball must be stationary and is in play once it is kicked and clearly moves (except for a free kick to the defending team in its own penalty area). Opponents must retire the required distance; since 2019, attacking players must remain at least one metre from a defensive wall of three or more players.
