Islandmagee

Islandmagee

Location: County Antrim, Northern Ireland

Type: Peninsula

Near: Larne, Whitehead

Islandmagee (from Irish Oileán Mhic Aodha, "island of Magee") is a peninsula and civil parish in County Antrim, Northern Ireland, situated between Larne Lough and the North Channel. Despite its name, it is a peninsula rather than an island. The area includes the villages of Ballystrudder, Mill Bay, and Portmuck.

History

Islandmagee was the site of a notorious massacre in 1642, during the Irish Confederate Wars, when local Protestant settlers were killed by Scottish Covenanter soldiers — an event long contested in scale and memory. In 1711, the area was also the setting of the last witch trial in Ireland, in which eight women were convicted at Carrickfergus of bewitching a young woman.

Points of interest

The Gobbins cliff path, first opened in the Edwardian era and restored in 2015, runs along the peninsula's basalt cliffs. Islandmagee also hosts a major underground gas storage project in salt caverns beneath Larne Lough, and the Ballylumford power station sits at its southern end.
