Jacques Lecoq (15 December 1921 – 19 January 1999) was a French stage actor and acting movement coach, one of the most influential theatre pedagogues of the twentieth century. He was best known for his teaching methods in physical theatre, movement and mime, which he taught at the school he founded in Paris, the École internationale de théâtre Jacques Lecoq, from 1956 until his death.

Life

Lecoq began as a sports teacher and physiotherapist before turning to theatre after the Second World War. In Italy from 1948 he worked with Giorgio Strehler at the Piccolo Teatro in Milan, choreographing movement and rediscovering the commedia dell'arte and its masks — work that decisively shaped his pedagogy. Returning to Paris in 1956, he founded his school to pursue his own research into movement and creation.

Pedagogy

Lecoq's two-year course rests on the study of movement: the neutral mask, which strips away individual habit to reveal a state of balance and availability; expressive and larval masks; the dynamics of the natural world — elements, materials, colours, animals — translated into the body; and the great dramatic territories of melodrama, commedia dell'arte, tragedy, bouffons and clown. His axiom "tout bouge" ("everything moves") summarised his belief that the actor's body is the primary instrument of theatre.

His writings include The Moving Body (Le Corps poétique) and Theatre of Movement and Gesture. Graduates of the school — including members of Théâtre de Complicité, Ariane Mnouchkine's generation of collaborators, and performers such as Geoffrey Rush — carried his influence into physical and devised theatre worldwide.
