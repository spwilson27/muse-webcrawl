Parent material

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parent material is the underlying geological material (generally bedrock or a superficial deposit) from which soil develops. It is the unconsolidated organic and mineral material in which soil formation begins. Parent materials influence the texture, mineral content, drainage and fertility of the resulting soil, and are one of the five soil-forming factors in the classic model of Hans Jenny, alongside climate, organisms, relief (topography) and time.

Types

Residual parent material

Residual parent material forms in place from the weathering of bedrock beneath the soil. Soils formed from residual material tend to inherit the chemistry of the underlying rock: soils over limestone, for example, differ markedly from soils over granite or basalt.

Transported parent material

Transported parent materials have been moved from their place of origin by natural agents and are classified by the agent of transport:

  • Alluvium — deposited by running water in floodplains and deltas; often fertile and stratified.
  • Colluvium — moved downslope by gravity at the base of hills and slopes.
  • Loess — wind-deposited silt, forming deep, fertile soils in regions such as the American Midwest and northern China.
  • Till — deposited directly by glaciers, unsorted and often stony.
  • Outwash — sorted sediments deposited by glacial meltwater.
  • Lacustrine deposits — fine sediments laid down in former lakes.
  • Marine deposits — sediments deposited in shallow seas and later exposed.

Organic parent material

In wetlands, accumulated plant remains can become the parent material of organic soils (histosols), such as peat.

Influence on soil properties

The composition of parent material strongly affects soil texture and mineralogy: sandy parent materials yield drought-prone soils, clay-rich materials yield slowly draining soils, and materials rich in weatherable minerals release more plant nutrients. Over long periods, climate and organisms may override the initial imprint of parent material, but young soils usually reflect it closely.
