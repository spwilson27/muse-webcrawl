Metal Gear is an action-adventure stealth video game developed and published by Konami for the MSX2 computer in 1987. Designed by Hideo Kojima, it is regarded as one of the founding titles of the stealth game genre, emphasizing infiltration and evasion over direct combat. It is the first game in the long-running Metal Gear series and introduces the series' protagonist, Solid Snake.

Plot

The game is set in 1995, two hundred kilometers north of South Africa, where the fortified state of Outer Heaven, founded by a mercenary leader, is rumored to possess a new superweapon. The special forces unit FOXHOUND sends its agent Gray Fox to infiltrate Outer Heaven, but contact is lost after he transmits the message "Metal Gear". FOXHOUND commander Big Boss then sends rookie operative Solid Snake on Operation Intrude N313 to rescue Gray Fox and destroy the weapon, revealed to be Metal Gear, a walking nuclear-equipped battle tank. Snake eventually discovers that Big Boss himself is the leader of Outer Heaven; after defeating him, Snake destroys Metal Gear and escapes as the fortress self-destructs.

Gameplay

The player controls Solid Snake through the interconnected screens of Outer Heaven. Snake begins unarmed, acquiring weapons and equipment such as handguns, explosives, binoculars, and key cards as he progresses. Guards patrol set routes and react when Snake enters their line of sight, triggering an alert state unless he hides or eliminates witnesses. Rather than relying on firepower, the game rewards avoiding detection. Boss encounters and hostage rescues that raise Snake's rank and carrying capacity punctuate the infiltration.

Ports and versions

A port for the Nintendo Entertainment System was released in 1987–88, developed by a separate team without Kojima's involvement, with significant changes to level design and the final confrontation. Versions for the Commodore 64 and MS-DOS followed. The Famicom/NES version proved commercially influential in North America and led to the NES sequel Snake's Revenge, as well as Kojima's own MSX2 sequel, Metal Gear 2: Solid Snake (1990). The original MSX2 version was later re-released in compilations and as an unlockable in Metal Gear Solid 3: Subsistence.

Legacy

Metal Gear is credited with popularizing stealth mechanics in video games and launched one of the medium's most acclaimed franchises, culminating in the Metal Gear Solid series. Its core design—an armed infiltration where combat is a fallback rather than the focus—remained the template for the series.
