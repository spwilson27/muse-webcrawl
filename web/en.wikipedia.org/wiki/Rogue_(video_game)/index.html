Rogue (video game)

Rogue is a dungeon crawling video game created by Michael Toy and Glenn Wichman, with later contributions by Ken Arnold, first released in 1980 for Unix systems at UC Berkeley and UC Santa Cruz.

The player descends through procedurally generated dungeon levels—rendered in ASCII characters on a text terminal—to retrieve the Amulet of Yendor. Rogue popularised permanent death (permadeath) and randomised levels, so no two games are alike; on death the player must start over.

Legacy

Rogue was included in the BSD Unix distributions and inspired a vast family of 'roguelike' games, including Hack, NetHack, Moria and Angband. Its design concepts—procedural generation and permadeath—remain central to the modern roguelike and roguelite genres. It was added to the World Video Game Hall of Fame discussions and is considered one of the most influential games of all time.
