Munich Airport (German: Flughafen München; IATA: MUC, ICAO: EDDM), officially named after former Bavarian minister-president Franz Josef Strauss, is the international airport of Munich, the capital of Bavaria. It is Germany's second-busiest airport after Frankfurt and one of Europe's major hubs, handling roughly 40 million passengers a year in normal times.

History

The airport opened on 17 May 1992, replacing the capacity-constrained Munich-Riem Airport, which closed the same night in one of the largest overnight airport relocations of its era. Terminal 2, built as a joint venture with Lufthansa, opened in 2003, followed by its satellite building in 2016. A long-planned third runway has been politically contested and repeatedly deferred.

Facilities

The two terminals are linked by the Munich Airport Center (MAC), an open-air plaza with shops, a beer garden and events, roofed by a large glass canopy. The airport is connected to the city by two S-Bahn lines (S1 and S8), taking about 40 minutes to the Hauptbahnhof, and is consistently rated among the world's best airports in passenger surveys, including repeated Skytrax five-star ratings.
