The Cathedral Basilica of St Stanislaus and St Ladislaus of Vilnius, commonly Vilnius Cathedral, is the main Catholic cathedral of Lithuania and the spiritual and ceremonial heart of the country, standing on Cathedral Square at the foot of Gediminas Hill in Vilnius Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Vilnius Cathedral with its freestanding bell tower, a converted castle tower.

History

The site is said to have held a temple to the Baltic god Perkūnas before a Christian church was built following Lithuania's conversion in 1387. Successive Gothic and Baroque cathedrals burned or were rebuilt; the present Neoclassical building was designed by Laurynas Gucevičius between 1783 and 1801, its temple-like portico adorned with sculptures by Tommaso Righi. Under Soviet rule the cathedral was closed in 1949 and used as an art gallery; it was returned to the Church and reconsecrated in 1989.

Features

The cathedral contains more than forty works of art from the 16th–19th centuries, royal mausoleums of Lithuanian rulers and queens, and the ornate Chapel of St Casimir, housing the relics of Lithuania's patron saint. In 2002, a hiding place rediscovered in the cathedral walls yielded a hoard of treasures concealed in 1939. The separate 57-metre bell tower stands on the foundations of a defensive tower of the Lower Castle.
