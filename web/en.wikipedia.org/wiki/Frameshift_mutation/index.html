Frameshift mutation

A frameshift mutation (also called a framing error or a reading frame shift) is a genetic mutation caused by indels (insertions or deletions) of a number of nucleotides in a DNA sequence that is not divisible by three. Because the ribosome reads the gene in codons — triplets of nucleotides — inserting or deleting bases shifts the reading frame, so that the sequence of codons downstream of the mutation is completely changed.

Effects

The altered reading frame typically produces an entirely different amino-acid sequence and often introduces a premature stop codon, yielding a truncated, usually nonfunctional protein; the aberrant mRNA may also be destroyed by nonsense-mediated decay. The closer the frameshift occurs to the start of the coding sequence, the more severe its effect generally is. Insertions or deletions of three (or a multiple of three) bases do not shift the frame and are called in-frame indels.

Causes and examples

Frameshifts can arise from polymerase slippage at repeated sequences or from errors in DNA repair. Well-known examples include many of the mutations causing Tay–Sachs disease, some mutations in cystic fibrosis, and frameshifts in tumour suppressor genes such as BRCA1 and BRCA2 in hereditary cancers. Because they generate novel peptide sequences, frameshift mutations in tumours can also produce neoantigens relevant to cancer immunotherapy.
