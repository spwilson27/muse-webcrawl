Squamous-cell carcinoma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Histopathology of squamous-cell carcinoma

Squamous-cell carcinoma (SCC), also known as epidermoid carcinoma, comprises a number of different types of cancer that begin in squamous cells. These cells form the surface of the skin, the lining of hollow organs, and the lining of the respiratory and digestive tracts. Squamous-cell carcinomas can occur in the skin, lips, mouth, oesophagus, urinary bladder, prostate, lung, vagina, and cervix, among other sites.

Cutaneous squamous-cell carcinoma

Squamous-cell carcinoma of the skin (cSCC) is the second most common type of skin cancer after basal-cell carcinoma. The main risk factor is cumulative exposure to ultraviolet radiation from sunlight. It typically presents as a firm, red nodule, a scaly or crusted sore that fails to heal, or a wart-like growth, usually on sun-exposed areas such as the face, ears, neck, and backs of the hands. Precancerous actinic keratoses may progress to invasive cSCC.

Unlike basal-cell carcinoma, cutaneous SCC has a significant (though generally low) risk of metastasis, and risk is increased in immunosuppressed patients, such as organ transplant recipients.

Other sites

  • Lung: squamous-cell carcinoma of the lung is a subtype of non-small-cell lung cancer strongly associated with smoking.
  • Head and neck: SCC accounts for the great majority of cancers of the oral cavity, pharynx, and larynx; a subset is driven by human papillomavirus (HPV).
  • Oesophagus: squamous-cell carcinoma is the dominant oesophageal cancer type worldwide.
  • Cervix: most cervical cancers are squamous-cell carcinomas related to HPV infection.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by biopsy and histological examination, which shows nests of squamous epithelial cells, keratinisation, and intercellular bridges. Treatment depends on the site and stage and may include surgical excision (including Mohs micrographic surgery for high-risk skin tumours), radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors such as cemiplimab and pembrolizumab for advanced cutaneous SCC.

Prevention

Sun protection, avoidance of tanning beds, smoking cessation, and HPV vaccination reduce the risk of squamous-cell carcinoma at various sites.
