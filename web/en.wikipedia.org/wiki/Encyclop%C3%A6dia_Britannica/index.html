The Encyclopædia Britannica is a general-knowledge English-language encyclopaedia, for many decades the oldest and one of the most authoritative of its kind. It was first published in Edinburgh, Scotland, in weekly instalments between 1768 and 1771, and grew into a multi-volume reference work written by hundreds of expert contributors. After 244 years in print, it ceased print publication in 2012 and continues as a digital encyclopaedia at Britannica.com.

History

The first edition, edited by William Smellie and issued by engraver Andrew Bell and printer Colin Macfarquhar, ran to three volumes. Successive editions expanded dramatically: the ninth edition (1875–1889) was famous for scholarly articles by leading thinkers, and the eleventh edition (1910–11), published with Cambridge University, became a landmark of popular scholarship. The continuously revised fifteenth edition (1974) reorganised the work into the Micropædia, Macropædia and Propædia.

Ownership and digital era

The work passed through several American owners, including Sears, Roebuck and the University of Chicago, before being acquired by Swiss financier Jacob Safra. A CD-ROM version appeared in the 1990s and the online edition in 1994. In March 2012 the company announced that the 2010 printing would be the last, ending the print era.
