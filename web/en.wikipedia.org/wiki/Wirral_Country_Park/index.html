The Wirral Country Park is a country park on the Wirral Peninsula in North West England, running along the disused trackbed of the former Birkenhead Railway branch line between Hooton and West Kirby. Opened in 1973, it was the first country park to be designated in Britain under the Countryside Act 1968. Its 12-mile (19 km) linear route, the Wirral Way, is used by walkers, cyclists, and horse riders, with an unsurfaced path for pedestrians alongside a separate bridleway/cycle track for much of its length.

Route and features

The line of the park follows the railway that opened in stages from 1866 and closed to passengers in 1956 and to freight in 1962. From Hooton the route passes through Willaston, Neston, and Parkgate—where the promenade overlooks the salt marsh of the Dee Estuary, famous for its bird life—and on past Heswall and Thurstaston to West Kirby, where it connects with the shore of the estuary and views across to the hills of Wales. At Thurstaston, a visitor centre, opened in 1973 in the former station area, provides exhibitions and access to the adjoining cliffs and the shore of the Dee, part of which is protected for its wildlife.

Wildlife

The park's habitats include railway cuttings, woodland, grassland, and the intertidal marshes of the Dee Estuary, an internationally important site for wintering waders and wildfowl such as pintail, oystercatcher, and redshank. The hedgerows along the old line support badgers, foxes, and a variety of breeding birds.
