Switchback (railway)

A switchback, or zig zag, is a railway engineering solution in which a train reverses direction at a set of points in order to climb or descend steep terrain within a limited distance, travelling alternately forward and backward along successive legs of track.

Switchback (railway)
Also calledZig zag
PurposeGaining elevation on steep gradients
Famous examplesDarjeeling Himalayan Railway, Lithgow Zig Zag

Operation

The train runs up the first leg to a reversing station, where the points are switched and the train backs (or runs in reverse) up the next leg, gaining height with each traverse. Multiple switchbacks may be stacked on a single hillside.

Examples

Famous switchbacks include the Batasia Loop and zig zags of the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway in India, the Lithgow Zig Zag in Australia's Blue Mountains, and the Chongqing and Andean railways of South America. The technique was also common on logging and mining railways.

Disadvantages

Switchbacks limit train length, require time-consuming reversals, and constrain capacity, so most have been replaced by spirals, loops or tunnels where traffic justified the cost.
