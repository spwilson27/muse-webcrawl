Wolfram Research, Inc. is an American multinational private company that creates computational technology. Its founder and chief executive officer is computer scientist and theoretical physicist Stephen Wolfram. The company is headquartered in Champaign, Illinois, with offices in several other countries.

The company's flagship product is Mathematica, a computer algebra and technical computing system first released in 1988, whose programming environment is now branded as the Wolfram Language. In 2009 the company launched Wolfram|Alpha, a computational knowledge engine that answers factual queries by computing answers from curated data.

Wolfram Research headquarters in Champaign, Illinois

History

Wolfram founded the company in 1987 after developing the computer algebra system SMP at Caltech. Mathematica's release in 1988 established Wolfram Research in scientific and technical computing. The company later created the Wolfram Demonstrations Project, MathWorld, and the Wolfram Science research program associated with Wolfram's 2002 book A New Kind of Science.

Products and services

Besides Mathematica and Wolfram|Alpha, the company offers the Wolfram Cloud for deploying computations, Wolfram SystemModeler for engineering simulation, Wolfram Data Drop, and extensive free reference websites, notably MathWorld and ScienceWorld. Its publishing arm has produced reference works based on Mathematica documentation and Wolfram's writings.
