Georgian Orthodox Church

Full name Georgian Apostolic Autocephalous Orthodox Church
Primate Catholicos-Patriarch of All Georgia (Ilia II, since 1977)
Headquarters Tbilisi, Georgia
Members approx. 3.5 million
Language Georgian

The Georgian Orthodox Church (full name: Georgian Apostolic Autocephalous Orthodox Church; Georgian: საქართველოს სამოციქულო ავტოკეფალური მართლმადიდებელი ეკლესია) is an autocephalous (independent) Eastern Orthodox church in full communion with the other churches of Eastern Orthodoxy. It is Georgia's dominant religious institution and one of the oldest Christian churches in the world, claiming apostolic foundation. The large majority of Georgia's population — over 80 percent — belongs to it.

History

Tradition holds that Christianity was first preached in Georgia by the apostles Andrew and Simon the Canaanite. The kingdom of Iberia (eastern Georgia) adopted Christianity as its state religion in the 320s–330s through the mission of Saint Nino of Cappadocia, making Georgia one of the earliest Christian nations. The church became autocephalous in the 4th–5th centuries, and in the 11th century its head took the title Catholicos-Patriarch. Medieval Georgia's "Golden Age" under David IV and Queen Tamar saw the building of great cathedrals and monasteries such as Gelati, Bagrati, Svetitskhoveli, and Alaverdi, and the flowering of Georgian literature and hymnography.

After the Russian Empire annexed Georgia, the church's autocephaly was abolished in 1811 and it was subordinated to the Russian Orthodox Church under an exarchate. Autocephaly was proclaimed again in 1917 after the fall of the Tsar but was suppressed under Soviet rule, though the Russian church formally recognized it in 1943. The Ecumenical Patriarchate recognized and confirmed Georgian autocephaly (and the patriarchal dignity) in 1990.

Modern role

Since Georgian independence in 1991, the church has undergone a major revival, with hundreds of churches built or restored, and enjoys a special constitutional status and a 2002 concordat-like constitutional agreement with the state. Catholicos-Patriarch Ilia II, enthroned in 1977, is a widely revered public figure. The church's seat is at Sameba (Holy Trinity) Cathedral in Tbilisi, consecrated in 2004, one of the largest Orthodox cathedrals in the world. Georgia's most sacred object, the robe of Christ, is traditionally held to be buried at Svetitskhoveli Cathedral in Mtskheta, the church's historic centre.
