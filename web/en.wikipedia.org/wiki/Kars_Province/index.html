Kars Province

Kars Province (Turkish: Kars ili) is a province in northeastern Turkey, bordering Armenia, with its capital at the city of Kars. It covers about 10,000 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 280,000. The province lies on a high, cold plateau of the Armenian Highlands and experiences severe continental winters.

History

The area formed part of historical Armenia, including the medieval Bagratid capital of Ani, the ruined "city of 1001 churches" near the Armenian border, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Control passed among Armenian kingdoms, Byzantines, Seljuks, Mongols, and Safavids before the Ottomans took the region in the 16th century. The Russian Empire annexed Kars in 1878 and held it for forty years, leaving distinctive Baltic-style architecture in the capital. The region changed hands during the First World War and the Turkish War of Independence before the 1921 Treaty of Kars fixed the Turkish–Soviet frontier.

Districts and economy

The province is divided into eight districts, including Kars, Kağızman, Sarıkamış (known for its ski resort and the 1914–15 battle), and Digor. The economy is based largely on livestock farming and dairy products, notably Kars kaşar cheese and honey. The Baku–Tbilisi–Kars railway, opened in 2017, passes through the province.
