Bidirectional reflectance distribution function

The bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF) is a function that describes how light is reflected at an opaque surface. It gives the ratio of reflected radiance in a particular outgoing direction to the incident irradiance from a particular incoming direction. BRDFs are fundamental in computer graphics rendering, remote sensing, and optics.

fr(ωi, ωr) = dLr(ωr) / dEi(ωi)

Properties

The BRDF is a function of four variables: the incoming and outgoing directions, each described by polar and azimuthal angles. Physical BRDFs obey reciprocity (reversing light direction gives the same value) and energy conservation (a surface cannot reflect more light than it receives). A perfectly diffuse (Lambertian) surface has a constant BRDF.

Models

Empirical and physically based models approximate real materials. Widely used models include the Phong and Blinn–Phong models, the Cook–Torrance microfacet model, and the Oren–Nayar model for rough diffuse surfaces. Microfacet models treat a surface as a collection of tiny mirror-like facets and underpin modern physically based rendering (PBR) workflows.

Measurement and applications

BRDFs can be measured with gonioreflectometers, which vary light source and detector angles, or estimated from photographs. Applications include realistic image synthesis, material appearance design, satellite remote sensing of land surfaces, and the design of optical coatings.
