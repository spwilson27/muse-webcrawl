Qutuz

Saif ad-Din Qutuz
Died: 24 October 1260
Reign: 1259–1260, Mamluk Sultan of Egypt

Saif ad-Din Qutuz (died 24 October 1260) was a Mamluk sultan of Egypt, ruling from 1259 until his assassination in 1260. A former slave-soldier of Turkic origin, he rose through the Bahri Mamluk ranks after the Mamluks seized power in the aftermath of the Seventh Crusade.

Battle of Ain Jalut

When Mongol armies under Hulagu sacked Baghdad (1258) and Damascus (1260), Qutuz refused submission, executed Mongol envoys, and marched into Palestine. On 3 September 1260, at the Battle of Ain Jalut, his forces under field commander Baibars destroyed Kitbuqa's Mongol army using feigned retreat tactics — a landmark check on Mongol expansion and a turning point preserving Islamic Egypt and the holy cities.

Death

On the triumphal return, Qutuz was assassinated during a hunt, almost certainly at Baibars's instigation; Baibars succeeded him and founded the mature Mamluk sultanate's power.
