Rudolf Höss

Born: November 25, 1901, Baden-Baden, German Empire
Died: April 16, 1947 (aged 45), Oświęcim, Poland
Known for: Commandant of Auschwitz concentration camp
Conviction: War crimes; executed by hanging

Rudolf Franz Ferdinand Höss (also spelt Höß; 25 November 1901 – 16 April 1947) was a German SS officer and the longest-serving commandant of Auschwitz concentration and extermination camp. He oversaw the mass murder of approximately 1.1 million people, most of them Jews, during the Holocaust.

Career

Höss joined the Nazi Party in 1922 and the SS in 1934, serving at Dachau and Sachsenhausen before being appointed commandant of Auschwitz in May 1940. Under his direction the camp expanded enormously, and he supervised the introduction of Zyklon B as a killing agent and the construction of the Birkenau extermination facilities. Captured by British forces in 1946, he testified at Nuremberg and gave detailed accounts of the killing process.

Trial and execution

Extradited to Poland, Höss was tried in Warsaw, sentenced to death, and hanged on 16 April 1947 at Auschwitz, on a gallows erected beside the former camp crematorium. His memoirs, written in custody, remain an important—if self-serving—primary source on the operation of the camp.
