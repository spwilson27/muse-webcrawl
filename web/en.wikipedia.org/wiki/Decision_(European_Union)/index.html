Decision (European Union)

In European Union law, a decision is one of the legal instruments available to the institutions of the European Union. Article 288 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) provides that a decision is binding in its entirety, and that a decision which specifies those to whom it is addressed is binding only on them.

Legal character

Decisions sit alongside regulations, directives, recommendations, and opinions as the instruments listed in Article 288 TFEU. Unlike a directive, which binds member states as to the result to be achieved but leaves the choice of form and methods to national authorities, a decision is binding in its entirety. Unlike a regulation, which has general application, a decision may be addressed to specific addressees — member states, companies, or individuals — and in that case binds only those addressees. Since the Treaty of Lisbon, decisions that do not specify addressees are also possible and have general, legislative character.

Uses

Decisions are used in many areas of EU activity, including:

  • Competition law — Commission decisions finding infringements of Articles 101 and 102 TFEU, imposing fines, or approving or blocking mergers and state aid.
  • Common Foreign and Security Policy — Council decisions define Union actions and positions, including restrictive measures (sanctions).
  • Administrative and implementing measures addressed to member states or specific undertakings.
  • Appointment decisions and decisions establishing agencies, programmes, or negotiating mandates.

Adoption and effect

Decisions may be adopted by the Council, the European Commission, the European Parliament together with the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure, or the European Central Bank in its fields of competence. They take effect upon notification to their addressees or, where they have no addressees, upon publication in the Official Journal of the European Union. Addressed decisions can be challenged before the Court of Justice of the European Union by an action for annulment under Article 263 TFEU.
