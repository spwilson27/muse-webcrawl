Dress code

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Common levels of formality in Western dress codes

A dress code is a set of rules, often written, governing what garments may be worn in a given setting, such as a workplace, school, place of worship, restaurant or social event. Dress codes may be imposed for reasons of safety, hygiene, professionalism, equality, or social convention, and they vary widely between cultures and occasions.

Western dress codes

In contemporary Western usage, invitations and venues commonly specify levels of formality:

  • White tie — the most formal: tailcoat, white waistcoat and bow tie for men; full-length evening gown for women.
  • Black tie — dinner jacket (tuxedo) with black bow tie; evening gown or cocktail dress.
  • Business formal — matching suit and tie; equivalent formal business attire.
  • Business casual — collared shirts, chinos or skirts, without ties or jeans in stricter settings.
  • Smart casual and casual — progressively relaxed attire, still neat in appearance.

Workplace and school codes

Employers use dress codes to project an image, comply with occupational safety requirements (for example personal protective equipment in industry and healthcare), and reduce visible status differences. School uniform policies and student dress codes are common in many countries and have been the subject of debate and litigation, particularly where rules are alleged to discriminate by gender, religion or culture, or to restrict freedom of expression.

Religious and cultural codes

Many religions prescribe standards of dress, such as head coverings in places of worship, modest dress requirements, or specific garments worn by clergy and adherents. Dress codes also govern mourning dress, wedding attire and ceremonial uniforms.
