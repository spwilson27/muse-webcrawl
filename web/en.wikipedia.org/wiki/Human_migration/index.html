Human migration

Human migration is the movement of people from one place to another, with intentions of settling, permanently or temporarily, at a new location. The movement often occurs over long distances and from one country to another (external migration), but internal migration (within a single country) is also possible. Migration may involve individuals, families or large groups, and may be voluntary or forced.

Prehistoric migration

Early human migration began when anatomically modern humans spread from Africa to other continents beginning roughly 70,000–100,000 years ago, eventually reaching Asia, Europe, Australia and the Americas. These movements shaped the genetic and cultural diversity of human populations worldwide.

Causes

Migration is commonly explained by "push" and "pull" factors: economic opportunity, employment, education and safety pull migrants toward destinations, while poverty, conflict, persecution, environmental change and natural disasters push them from their homes. Refugees fleeing war or persecution are a major category of forced migrants.

Modern patterns

Contemporary migration includes labour migration, family reunification, international students, and displacement by conflict and climate pressures. Receiving countries regulate entry through immigration law and visa regimes, and migration is a prominent topic in politics and public policy.

Effects

Migration affects both origin and destination societies: remittances support families in home countries, diasporas maintain cultural links, and destination countries gain labour and cultural diversity while facing challenges of integration and public services.
