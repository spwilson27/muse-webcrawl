CAMRA

CAMRA, the Campaign for Real Ale, is an independent voluntary consumer organisation headquartered in St Albans, England, which promotes traditional cask-conditioned ("real") ale, real cider and perry, and traditional British pubs and clubs as part of the country's cultural heritage. Founded in 1971, it is widely regarded as one of the most successful consumer campaigning organisations in Europe, with a membership of around 150,000 or more at its peak.

History

CAMRA was founded in Kruger's Bar, Dunquin, County Kerry, Ireland, in March 1971 by Graham Lees, Bill Meller, Michael Hardman, and Jim Makin, who were dismayed at the domination of the British brewing industry by large national brewers producing bland, pasteurised, pressurised keg beer at the expense of traditional cask ale. Originally named the Campaign for the Revitalisation of Ale, it soon adopted the name Campaign for Real Ale. The organisation's campaigns helped halt the decline of cask ale and encouraged the growth of microbreweries in Britain.

Activities

CAMRA publishes the annual Good Beer Guide (since 1974) and the newspaper-style magazine What's Brewing, runs hundreds of local beer festivals including the Great British Beer Festival in London, and maintains the National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors. Its campaigns have included defending pub planning protections, promoting beer duty reforms such as the Small Breweries' Relief (2002), and the community right to bid for pubs under the Localism Act 2011. Since 2018 its remit formally extends to campaigning for pubs and clubs as social centres more generally.

Definition of real ale

CAMRA coined the term "real ale" for beer brewed from traditional ingredients, matured by secondary fermentation in the container from which it is dispensed (typically a cask), and served without the use of extraneous carbon dioxide.
