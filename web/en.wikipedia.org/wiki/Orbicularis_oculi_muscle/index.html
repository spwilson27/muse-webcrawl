Orbicularis oculi muscle

Orbicularis oculi
Origin: frontal bone, medial palpebral ligament, lacrimal bone
Insertion: lateral palpebral raphe
Nerve: facial nerve (CN VII)
Action: closes the eyelids

The orbicularis oculi is a muscle in the face that closes the eyelids. It arises from the nasal part of the frontal bone, from the frontal process of the maxilla in front of the lacrimal groove, and from the anterior surface and borders of the medial palpebral ligament. It is one of the muscles of facial expression and is supplied by the facial nerve.

Structure

The muscle is conventionally divided into three parts. The orbital part forms a broad ring around the orbit and is responsible for forceful closure of the eye, as in winking against resistance or protecting the eye. The palpebral part lies within the eyelids themselves and produces the gentle closure of blinking and sleep. The lacrimal part (tensor tarsi) lies behind the medial palpebral ligament and lacrimal sac and assists the flow of tears.

Function

Contraction of the orbicularis oculi closes the eyelids, spreads the tear film over the cornea, and helps drive tears toward the lacrimal puncta and into the nasolacrimal duct. The palpebral part acts involuntarily in the blink reflex, which protects the eye from foreign bodies and bright light.

Clinical significance

Paralysis of the muscle, as in Bell's palsy, prevents complete closure of the eye (lagophthalmos) and can lead to drying and ulceration of the cornea. Involuntary spasm of the muscle is called blepharospasm. The muscle is also involved in the formation of "crow's feet" wrinkles at the lateral corner of the eye.
