The American black bear (Ursus americanus), also known as the black bear, is a species of medium-sized bear endemic to North America. It is the continent's smallest and most widely distributed bear species. The American black bear is an omnivore, with its diet varying greatly depending on season and location. It typically lives in largely forested areas but will leave forests in search of food, and is sometimes attracted to human communities due to the immediate availability of food.

Description

American black bears have short, smooth, curved claws suited to climbing trees, relatively small rounded ears, and a straight "dished" facial profile. Despite the name, coat color varies widely — from black to chocolate brown, cinnamon, blond, and even bluish-grey and white in rare coastal populations of British Columbia (the Kermode or "spirit bear"). Adult males typically weigh 60–300 kg (130–660 lb), with females about one-third smaller. They stand about 0.7–1 m (2–3 ft) at the shoulder and 1.3–1.9 m (4–6 ft) long.

Distribution and behavior

The species ranges across Canada, most of the United States' forested regions, and into northern Mexico, with an estimated population of roughly 600,000 in North America. Sixteen subspecies are commonly recognized, including the Florida black bear and the Louisiana black bear. Black bears are excellent climbers and swimmers, have a keen sense of smell, and enter a period of winter torpor (often called hibernation) lasting up to seven months in northern areas, during which cubs are born — usually two per litter.

Relationship with humans

The IUCN lists the American black bear as Least Concern. Populations are managed through regulated hunting in most jurisdictions. Encounters with humans are common where bears access garbage, bird feeders, or campsites; attacks are rare but can increase with food conditioning. The black bear inspired the original "Teddy bear" story of 1902, involving President Theodore Roosevelt — though the animal in that anecdote was a black bear caught in Mississippi.
