Mustelidae

The Mustelidae (/mʌˈstɛlɪdiː/; from Latin mustela, weasel) are a diverse family of carnivoran mammals, including weasels, badgers, otters, polecats, martens, and wolverines. It is the largest family in the order Carnivora, comprising roughly 66 species across all continents except Australia and Antarctica.

Characteristics

Mustelids vary greatly in size, from the least weasel (about 25 g) to the sea otter and giant otter (up to about 45 kg) and the wolverine. Common traits include an elongated body with short legs in many terrestrial forms, a long skull with powerful jaws, and well-developed anal scent glands used for marking territory and defence. Most mustelids are primarily carnivorous, feeding on mammals, fish, birds, eggs, invertebrates, and carrion; some, such as the honey badger, are notably omnivorous.

Many mustelids are solitary and nocturnal. Several northern species, including the stoat and long-tailed weasel, moult into white winter coats (ermine) in snowy regions.

Subfamilies

Modern classifications, informed by molecular phylogenetics, recognize about eight subfamilies:

  • Guloninae — martens, tayra, fisher, and wolverine
  • Helictidinae — ferret-badgers
  • Ictonychinae — grisons, African striped weasels, marbled polecat
  • Lutrinae — the 13 species of otters
  • Melinae — Eurasian badgers
  • Mellivorinae — honey badger
  • Mustelinae — weasels, stoats, minks, polecats, and the domestic ferret
  • Taxidiinae — American badger

Skunks and stink badgers, once classed as mustelids, are now placed in their own family, Mephitidae.

Relationship with humans

Several mustelids are trapped or farmed for their fur, including sable, mink, and ermine. The domestic ferret (Mustela furo) has been kept for thousands of years for hunting rabbits and as a pet. Otters are frequently flagship species for freshwater conservation. Some mustelids, such as fishers and weasels, are valued for controlling rodent pests, while introduced stoats and ferrets have become destructive invasive predators in New Zealand.
