Third Battle of Artois

Date: 25 September – 4 November 1915
Location: Artois, France
Result: Inconclusive; French territorial gains at heavy cost
Combatants: France and the British Empire vs. German Empire

French troops in Artois, autumn 1915

The Third Battle of Artois (25 September – 4 November 1915) was a battle of the First World War fought by the French Tenth Army against the German Sixth Army in the Artois region of northern France. It formed part of the Allied autumn offensive of 1915, coordinated with the French Second Battle of Champagne and the British attack at Loos.

Background

The French commander-in-chief, General Joseph Joffre, planned simultaneous offensives in Champagne and Artois to break the German trench lines and restore a war of movement. The Tenth Army under General Victor d'Urbal was to attack toward the Vimy Ridge and the Douai plain. A prolonged artillery preparation, with some 1.4 million shells, preceded the assault.

Course of the battle

The attack began on 25 September 1915. French troops captured the village of Souchez and advanced toward Vimy Ridge, but could not seize the crest. German counter-attacks, poison gas use by both sides, autumn rain, and the exhaustion of reserves brought the offensive to a halt by mid-October; fighting sputtered out by 4 November.

Casualties and significance

French casualties were approximately 48,000; German losses about 20,000. The battle, like the parallel Champagne offensive, demonstrated the difficulty of breaking entrenched defences with the tactics and artillery methods of 1915, and it set the pattern of attrition that would characterize the Western Front.
