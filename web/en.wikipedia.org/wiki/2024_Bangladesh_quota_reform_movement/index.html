The 2024 Bangladesh quota reform movement, also known as the July Revolution, was a series of protests led primarily by students demanding reform of the quota system for government jobs in Bangladesh. The movement escalated into a nationwide uprising that ended the fifteen-year rule of Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina.

Background

Bangladesh reserved 56 per cent of government jobs under various quotas, including 30 per cent for descendants of veterans of the 1971 Liberation War. After protests in 2018, the government abolished the quota system for senior posts. In June 2024 the High Court declared the abolition illegal, restoring the quotas and sparking fresh demonstrations coordinated under the banner "Students Against Discrimination".

Escalation

Protests intensified in mid-July 2024 after remarks by the prime minister were perceived as insulting to demonstrators. Clashes with police and ruling-party supporters left hundreds dead; the government imposed curfews, shut down the internet and deployed the army. On 21 July the Supreme Court scaled back the quotas, reserving 93 per cent of posts for merit-based recruitment, but protests continued with a one-point demand for Hasina's resignation following the killings.

Outcome

On 5 August 2024, as protesters marched on Dhaka, Sheikh Hasina resigned and fled to India. An interim government led by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus was sworn in on 8 August. The movement is widely regarded as the first successful student-led revolution in Bangladesh's history.
