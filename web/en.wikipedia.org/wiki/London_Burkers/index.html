London Burkers

The London Burkers were a gang of body snatchers active in London in the early 19th century, named for William Burke of the Edinburgh Burke and Hare murders. The London gang went beyond grave robbery: in 1831 they were implicated in the murder of an Italian street boy, Carlo Ferrari, whose body they attempted to sell to anatomists — a case that provoked public outrage and helped bring about the Anatomy Act 1832.

Background

London's anatomy schools depended on a steady supply of cadavers, but the law allowed only the bodies of executed criminals. "Resurrection men" filled the gap by exhuming fresh corpses, and the prices paid for "subjects" — especially children, sold by size — created an incentive for murder, as in Edinburgh.

The Italian boy case

On 5 November 1831 Bishop, Williams, and May tried to sell the body of a boy of about 14 at King's College School of Anatomy. Porter staff noticed the body was unusually fresh and had a wound on the forehead, and called the police. The victim was identified as Carlo Ferrari, an Italian street performer who exhibited white mice and played music for money. Evidence suggested he had been drugged with laudanum-laced rum and drowned head-first in a well in the garden of 3 Nova Scotia Gardens, Bethnal Green.

Trial and executions

John Bishop and Thomas Williams were tried at the Old Bailey on 2–3 December 1831, convicted, and hanged at Newgate on 5 December before a crowd estimated in the tens of thousands; their bodies were sent for dissection. James May's sentence was commuted to transportation after doubt about his involvement in the murder itself. Bishop confessed to participating in the theft of many bodies but admitted only to the Ferrari killing and two other murders.

Legacy

The case, following the earlier conviction of Bishop and Williams for the abduction of another boy, intensified the public panic over "burking" and accelerated passage of the Anatomy Act 1832, which gave anatomists lawful access to unclaimed bodies and ended the market that had sustained the trade.
