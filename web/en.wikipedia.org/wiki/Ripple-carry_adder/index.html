Ripple-carry adder

A ripple-carry adder (RCA) is a digital circuit that adds two binary numbers by cascading full adders, one per bit position, so that the carry output of each stage feeds the carry input of the next higher stage. The carry signal thus "ripples" through the chain.

Construction

An n-bit ripple-carry adder uses n full adders. The least significant full adder may be replaced by a half adder if there is no incoming carry. Each full adder computes a sum bit and a carry-out from its two operand bits and the carry-in.

Performance

The design is simple and uses few gates, but its worst-case delay grows linearly with the number of bits, because the most significant sum bit cannot settle until the carry has propagated through all lower stages. Faster alternatives include the carry-lookahead adder, carry-select adder, and carry-save adder.
