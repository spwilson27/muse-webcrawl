Flash (comics)

"The Fastest Man Alive" — the Flash is the name shared by several DC Comics superheroes with super-speed.

The Flash is the name of several superheroes in DC Comics, all possessing super-speed and a connection to the extradimensional energy known as the Speed Force. The character is nicknamed the "Scarlet Speedster" and "the Fastest Man Alive", and is one of DC's most enduring figures, central to the Justice League and to the concept of the DC Multiverse.

Jay Garrick

The original Flash, college student Jay Garrick, debuted in Flash Comics #1 (January 1940), created by writer Gardner Fox and artist Harry Lampert. After inhaling hard-water vapours in a laboratory accident, Garrick gained super-speed and fought crime in a costume with a winged helmet modelled on the god Mercury. He was a founding member of the Justice Society of America.

Barry Allen

In Showcase #4 (1956), editor Julius Schwartz, writer Robert Kanigher and artist Carmine Infantino revived the character as police scientist Barry Allen, who gained his powers when lightning struck a shelf of chemicals. The story launched the Silver Age of Comic Books. Barry's The Flash #123 (1961), "Flash of Two Worlds", revealed Garrick's world as the parallel Earth-Two, founding the DC Multiverse. Barry died heroically in Crisis on Infinite Earths (1985) and remained dead for 23 years before returning in Final Crisis (2008); he has since headlined the 2014–2023 television series The Flash and the 2023 film.

Wally West and Bart Allen

Barry's nephew Wally West, introduced in 1959 as Kid Flash, succeeded him from 1986 and held the role for over two decades, anchoring the Justice League. Barry's grandson Bart Allen, formerly Impulse and Kid Flash, briefly became the fourth Flash. Other speedsters include Jesse Quick and the villainous Reverse-Flash, Eobard Thawne.

Powers

The Flashes run at many times the speed of light, vibrate through solid matter, create vortexes, throw lightning, heal rapidly, and — by running fast enough — travel through time and between parallel universes, a device behind major storylines such as Flashpoint (2011).
