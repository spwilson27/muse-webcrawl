In symplectic geometry, a Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold (X, ω) is a category whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds of X and whose morphisms are given by Floer cohomology groups between pairs of Lagrangians. It is named after Kenji Fukaya, who introduced the concept in 1993 in connection with Morse homotopy theory.

Definition

More precisely, the Fukaya category is an A-infinity category: compositions of morphisms are defined not just by a binary product but by an infinite family of higher operations μk counting pseudoholomorphic polygons whose vertices lie at intersection points of the Lagrangians and whose edges lie along them. The morphism space between two Lagrangians L0 and L1 is (a suitable completion of) the Floer cochain complex CF*(L0, L1), and the A-infinity relations follow from the ways in which the moduli spaces of such polygons degenerate.

Technical complications — including transversality, orientations, grading and the possible obstruction of Floer theory by holomorphic discs of positive symplectic area — were addressed in the foundational work of Fukaya, Oh, Ohta and Ono, and in alternative frameworks due to Seidel and others. Variants include the infinitesimally wrapped and wrapped Fukaya categories, in which morphisms are computed after wrapping Lagrangians at infinity using a Hamiltonian flow.

Homological mirror symmetry

The Fukaya category is one side of Maxim Kontsevich's homological mirror symmetry conjecture (1994), which predicts an equivalence between the derived Fukaya category of a symplectic manifold and the derived category of coherent sheaves on a mirror complex manifold. This conjecture has been proved in a number of cases, beginning with elliptic curves and extending to many toric and Calabi–Yau examples, notably through work of Seidel, Auroux, Abouzaid and Sheridan.
