Hungarian language

Hungarian (endonym: magyar nyelv) is a Ugric language of the Uralic language family, spoken in Hungary and parts of several neighbouring countries. It is the official language of Hungary and one of the 24 official languages of the European Union. Outside Hungary, it is spoken by Hungarian communities in Romania (Transylvania), Slovakia, Serbia (Vojvodina), Ukraine, Croatia and Austria.

Classification

Hungarian is a member of the Uralic family, distantly related to Finnish and Estonian, and more closely to the Ob-Ugric languages Khanty and Mansi of western Siberia. It is not an Indo-European language, which makes it unusual among the languages of Central Europe.

Grammar

Hungarian is an agglutinative language with extensive suffixation: grammatical relations are expressed by suffixes rather than prepositions, and it has around 18 grammatical cases. It features vowel harmony, a definite/indefinite verb conjugation distinction, and typically places stress on the first syllable.

Phonology and writing

Hungarian distinguishes long and short vowels and consonants. Its Latin-based alphabet has 44 letters, including digraphs such as sz, zs, gy, ny and ty, and the distinctive vowels ö, ü, ő and ű.

History

The earliest written Hungarian text is the Funeral Sermon and Prayer (c. 1192–1195). During the Reform Era, a language reform modernised the vocabulary, and Hungarian replaced Latin as the official language in 1844.
