Rhinosinusitis

Rhinosinusitis is inflammation of the mucous membranes lining the nose and the paranasal sinuses. Because rhinitis and sinusitis almost always occur together, the combined term is preferred in modern medical usage over "sinusitis" alone.

Classification

Rhinosinusitis is classified by duration: acute (less than four weeks), subacute (four to twelve weeks), and chronic (more than twelve weeks). Recurrent acute rhinosinusitis involves multiple discrete episodes within a year with complete resolution between them.

Symptoms and causes

Cardinal symptoms include nasal blockage or congestion, nasal discharge, facial pain or pressure, and reduction or loss of the sense of smell. Acute cases are usually viral, following a common cold, though a minority become bacterial. Chronic rhinosinusitis is a multifactorial inflammatory condition, sometimes associated with nasal polyps, allergy, or anatomical variation.

Treatment

Acute viral disease is managed supportively with saline irrigation, analgesia, and intranasal corticosteroids. Antibiotics are reserved for presentations suggesting bacterial infection. Chronic disease may require prolonged topical steroids, and endoscopic sinus surgery is considered when medical therapy fails.
