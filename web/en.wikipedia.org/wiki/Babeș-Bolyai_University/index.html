Babeș-Bolyai University (Romanian: Universitatea Babeș-Bolyai, Hungarian: Babeș-Bolyai Tudományegyetem) is a public research university in Cluj-Napoca, Romania. With around 50,000 students it is the largest university in the country. It is a multicultural institution offering study lines in Romanian, Hungarian and German.

History

The university traces its origins to the Jesuit Academia Claudiopolitana founded in 1581 by Stephen Báthory. In 1919, following Transylvania's union with Romania, a Romanian-language university was founded, named after King Ferdinand I; in 1945 a Hungarian-language university, named after the mathematician János Bolyai, was established alongside it. In 1959 the two were merged into a single institution, renamed after Bolyai and the Romanian scientist Victor Babeș.

Organisation

The university comprises more than twenty faculties, including mathematics and computer science, physics, chemistry, law, economics, history, psychology, theology (in several confessional variants) and theatre and film. It consistently ranks among Romania's top universities and is a member of the Guild of European Research-Intensive Universities.
