Anamorphic widescreen

ApplicationDVD-Video, digital television, streaming
Common aspect ratios16:9 stored in a 4:3 or 1.85:1 frame

Anamorphic widescreen is a format that stores a widescreen image by horizontally squeezing it into a frame of narrower aspect ratio. On playback, the display or player stretches the image back to its intended proportions. The technique borrows its name from anamorphic lenses in cinematography, such as those used by CinemaScope.

On DVD

DVD-Video stores pictures in a 720×480 (NTSC) or 720×576 (PAL) raster regardless of aspect ratio. A film flagged as anamorphic widescreen has its 16:9 image squeezed into that raster, using all available vertical resolution; a widescreen television stretches it for display, while a 4:3 television letterboxes or pans and scans it. This gives an anamorphic disc better resolution than a letterboxed non-anamorphic one.

Pixel aspect ratio

In digital video the same idea is expressed through a non-square pixel aspect ratio: the stored pixels are wider than they are tall, and the display aspect ratio is the product of the storage ratio and the pixel ratio. Modern high-definition formats generally use square pixels, but anamorphic encoding remains relevant in standard-definition archives and broadcasting.
