Alexander Andreyevich Baranov (14 February 1746 – 28 April 1819), sometimes spelled Baranof, was a Russian merchant and coloniser who served as the chief manager of the Russian-American Company and the first governor of Russian America from 1799 to 1818. Under his administration, Russian settlements in Alaska were consolidated and expanded.

Early career

Born in Kargopol, Baranov became a fur trader in Siberia before sailing to Alaska in 1790 to manage the Shelikhov-Golikov Company's operations. Shipwrecked en route on Unimak Island, he reached Kodiak Island in 1791 and took charge of the colony.

Governor of Russian America

Baranov founded and relocated settlements including Pavlovskaya (Kodiak) and, in 1799, New Archangel (now Sitka). After Tlingit forces destroyed the Sitka post in 1802, he retook it in 1804 following the Battle of Sitka. He extended Russian activity down the Pacific coast, founding Fort Ross in California in 1812 as an agricultural outpost, and pursued trade with Hawaii, Spanish California and China, centred on the valuable sea-otter fur trade.

Legacy

Baranov's rule was energetic and often harsh toward Alaska Native peoples, whose labour sustained the fur trade; his era also saw the depletion of sea otter populations. Retiring in 1818, he died at sea the following year while returning to Russia. Baranov Island and the city of Baranof in Alaska are named after him.
