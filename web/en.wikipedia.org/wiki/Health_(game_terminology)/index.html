Health (game terminology)

Health is a game mechanic used in tabletop games, role-playing games and video games to represent the amount of damage a character, unit or object can withstand before being defeated, incapacitated or destroyed. Health is typically quantified by hit points (HP) — a numerical value that decreases as the entity takes damage and may be restored by healing effects, items or rest.

Hit points

The hit point concept originated in tabletop wargaming and was popularised by Dungeons & Dragons (1974), in which a character's hit points abstractly represent a combination of physical toughness, luck, skill and divine favour rather than literal wounds. When a character's hit points fall to zero, the character dies or is knocked unconscious, depending on the game system.

Presentation

In video games, a player's remaining health is commonly shown as a health bar — a horizontal meter that depletes as damage is taken — or as a numeric value, a row of hearts or icons, or screen effects such as red vignetting. Some games dispense with a visible meter in favour of regenerating health, in which health recovers automatically after a period without taking damage; this system became widespread in first-person shooters during the 2000s.

Restoration

Health can usually be restored through pickups such as health packs and food, healing spells and abilities, resting at safe locations, or drinking potions. In many games, a distinction is made between health and related resources such as mana (used to cast spells) and stamina (used for exertion).
