Divide and choose

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


"I cut, you choose" applied to cake

Divide and choose (also cut and choose or I cut, you choose) is a two-party proportional fair division method for allocating a divisible resource, such as a cake, between two people with different preferences. One person ("the cutter") cuts the resource into two pieces; the other person ("the chooser") chooses first. The procedure guarantees each participant a share they value at least as much as half the whole: the cutter ensures the pieces are equal in their own estimation, and the chooser takes the piece they prefer.

Properties

Divide and choose is both proportional and envy-free for two participants: neither party envies the other's share. It is also strategyproof for the cutter if they cut equally by their own measure. The method has been used since ancient times for disputes over land and other divisible goods, and it generalizes imperfectly to more than two parties, for which more complex fair-cake-cutting procedures such as the Steinhaus - Banach - Knaster "last diminisher" procedure are used.

Applications

Beyond cake cutting, divide and choose appears in the settlement of divorces and inheritance disputes, in treaty negotiations, and famously in the Law of the Sea conventions, where one country divides a seabed area and another chooses its portion.
