Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot (1796–1832) was a French military engineer and physicist who founded the science of thermodynamics. His only book, Reflections on the Motive Power of Fire (1824), analysed steam engines and introduced the idealised Carnot cycle.

Legacy

Carnot showed that a heat engine's maximum efficiency depends only on the temperatures between which it operates — a result ignored in his lifetime, then used by Clausius and Kelvin to formulate the second law and the concept of entropy. He died of cholera at 36, and much of his work was burned for fear of infection. He was the son of Lazare Carnot, the 'organiser of victory' of the French Revolutionary Wars.
