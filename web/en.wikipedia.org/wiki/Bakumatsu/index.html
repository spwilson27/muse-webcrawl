Bakumatsu

A warship of the final years of the Tokugawa shogunate

Bakumatsu (幕末, "end of the bakufu") was the final years of the Edo period of Japan, when the Tokugawa shogunate came to its end. It is generally dated between 1853 and 1868, spanning the arrival of Commodore Matthew Perry's Black Ships, the opening of Japan to foreign trade, and the collapse of the shogunate in the Boshin War.

Background

For over two centuries the Tokugawa shogunate had enforced a policy of relative isolation (sakoku). Perry's arrival at Uraga in 1853 and the subsequent Convention of Kanagawa (1854) and Harris Treaty (1858) opened Japanese ports to the United States and later to European powers. The unequal treaties, economic disruption from foreign trade, and resentment over the shogunate's handling of the foreign crisis destabilised the regime.

Conflict and restoration

The era saw the rise of the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement, political assassinations such as that of Ii Naosuke in the Sakuradamon Incident of 1860, and armed clashes including the bombardments of Kagoshima and Shimonoseki. The domains of Satsuma and Chōshū, historically rivals, allied against the shogunate. In 1867 the last shōgun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, returned governing power to Emperor Meiji (Taisei Hōkan), but fighting continued in the Boshin War of 1868–1869, ending with the fall of the Republic of Ezo at Hakodate. The Meiji Restoration followed, rapidly modernising Japan.
