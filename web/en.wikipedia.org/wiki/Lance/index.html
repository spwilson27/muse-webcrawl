Lance

TypePolearm (cavalry spear)
Used byHeavy cavalry: knights, lancers, uhlans, cataphracts
Typical length3–4.5 m (medieval war lance)
In serviceAntiquity – early 20th century

A lance is a long thrusting spear designed to be used by a mounted warrior or cavalryman (lancer). In ancient and medieval warfare, couched under the arm and levelled at the gallop, it delivered the momentum of horse and rider to a single point, making the heavy-cavalry charge the decisive shock weapon of its era.

History

Long cavalry spears were used by Assyrian, Macedonian (the xyston of Alexander's Companion cavalry), Sarmatian, and late-Roman and Byzantine cataphracts (the kontos). In medieval Europe the couched lance, used with stirrups and a high-cantled saddle, became the signature weapon of the knight from the 11th century onward. The medieval war lance grew heavy and specialized, with a vamplate protecting the hand; lighter versions persisted in Eastern Europe. Polish hussars wielded exceptionally long hollow lances into the 17th century. Lance-armed regiments — lancers and uhlans — survived into the First World War, and lances were still carried on ceremonial and some operational occasions into the 20th century.

Tournaments

In jousting, specialized blunted or coronel-tipped lances were used, often designed to shatter spectacularly on impact. Modern tent-pegging and mounted skill-at-arms retain lance techniques.
