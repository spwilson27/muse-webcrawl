Joe Shuster

Joseph Shuster (July 10, 1914 – July 30, 1992) was a Canadian-American comic book artist best known for co-creating the DC Comics character Superman with writer Jerry Siegel. Born in Toronto and raised in Cleveland, Ohio, Shuster defined Superman's visual appearance, costume, and supporting cast imagery in Action Comics #1 (1938).

Career

Shuster and Jerry Siegel worked together from high school in Cleveland, producing science-fiction fanzines and strips such as Doctor Occult and Slam Bradley for National Publications. After years of rejection, they sold Superman in 1938 for $130, signing away rights — a deal that led to decades of legal disputes.

Nearly blind by the late 1940s, Shuster left comics, later drawing the underground Nights of Horror series under pseudonyms. Public campaigns by Neal Adams and others in the 1970s won Siegel and Shuster restored credit, pensions, and bylines from DC Comics.

Legacy

Shuster was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame in 1992. The Joe Shuster Awards, established 2005, honour Canadian comic creators.
