Sven Hedin

Sven Anders Hedin (19 February 1865 – 26 November 1952) was a Swedish geographer, topographer, explorer, photographer, travel writer, and illustrator of his own works. He led four expeditions to Central Asia between 1893 and 1935, mapping and researching the Pamir Mountains, the Taklamakan Desert, Tibet, the ancient Silk Road, and Lake Lop Nur, and published extensive scientific and popular accounts of the region.

Expeditions

Hedin's travels made him one of the most celebrated explorers of his age. He discovered the source regions of the Brahmaputra, Indus, and Sutlej rivers, the ruins of desert cities such as Lou-lan in the Taklamakan, and evidence that Lop Nur was a "wandering lake". His maps of previously uncharted territory and his multi-volume expedition reports remained fundamental to Central Asian geography for decades. He was ennobled in Sweden and elected to the Swedish Academy in 1913.

Political views and controversy

Hedin was a conservative Swedish nationalist whose admiration for Germany persisted through both World Wars; he maintained personal contacts with leading Nazis and wrote approvingly of the Third Reich, which severely damaged his reputation after 1945. Historians continue to debate his legacy, separating the scientific value of his exploration from his political judgments.
