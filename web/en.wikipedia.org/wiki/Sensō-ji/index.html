Sensō-ji

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sensō-ji (金龍山浅草寺, Kinryū-zan Sensō-ji) is an ancient Buddhist temple in Asakusa, Tokyo, Japan. It is Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645 AD, and one of its most significant. Dedicated to Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion, it is the most widely visited spiritual site in the world, receiving over 30 million visitors annually.

History

According to legend, in 628 AD two fishermen brothers, Hinokuma Hamanari and Hinokuma Takenari, found a statue of Kannon in their net in the Sumida River. Their village chief, Haji no Nakatomo, recognised its sanctity and enshrined it in his house, which became a temple in 645. The temple grew under the patronage of the Tokugawa shogunate. Most of its buildings were destroyed in the air raids of 1945 and rebuilt afterwards; the five-story pagoda was rebuilt in 1973.

Grounds

Visitors enter through the famous Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate), hung with a giant red paper lantern (chōchin) bearing the characters for "wind god and thunder god" — actually Fūjin-Raijin-mon. Beyond it runs Nakamise-dōri, a shopping street of stalls said to date from the early 18th century, leading to the Hōzōmon gate, the main hall (rebuilt in 1958), and the five-story pagoda.

Events

The temple's largest festival is the Sanja Matsuri, held each May in honour of the three founders, drawing nearly two million people. Other events include the Hagoita-ichi market in December and Setsubun celebrations in February.
