Foreign direct investment

[Image: a foreign-owned car factory under construction, cranes over a new industrial plant]

Foreign direct investment (FDI) is Investment by a firm or individual in one country into business interests in another, involving a lasting interest and significant influence or control — conventionally ownership of at least 10% of voting stock, the International Monetary Fund/OECD threshold. It is the vehicle by which multinational corporations build factories, acquire companies and organise global supply chains, distinct from portfolio flows into securities.

Scale and effects

Global FDI flows ran at roughly US$1.3 trillion in 2023 (UNCTAD), concentrated among advanced economies and China, though volatile and distorted by conduit financial centres. Host countries seek FDI for capital, jobs, technology and export access, offering incentives that critics call a race to the bottom; home countries debate offshoring. Empirical research finds FDI raises host-country productivity through spillovers when local firms and skills can absorb it. Screening of inward FDI on national-security grounds (CFIUS in the US, EU and UK regimes) has expanded with US–China rivalry, reshaping flows toward 'friend-shoring'.
