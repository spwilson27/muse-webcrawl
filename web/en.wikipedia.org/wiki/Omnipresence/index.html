Omnipresence

Omnipresence is the property of being present everywhere at the same time. It is one of the classical attributes of God in monotheistic theology, alongside omnipotence (all-powerful), omniscience (all-knowing), and omnibenevolence (all-good). The term derives from the Latin omni- ("all") and praesens ("present").

In theology

In Christianity, Judaism, and Islam, God is understood to be present in all places simultaneously, though theologians distinguish between God's essential presence in creation and special modes of presence, such as in the Incarnation in Christianity or in sacred places. Scriptural passages commonly cited include Psalm 139: "Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence?" Hindu thought likewise describes Brahman and deities such as Vishnu as all-pervading.

Philosophical questions

Philosophers of religion debate how a non-physical being can be present at every location, whether omnipresence implies occupying space, and how it relates to doctrines such as pantheism (God is identical with the universe) and panentheism (the universe exists within God). Some traditions instead teach a more limited divine presence mediated through agents or manifestations.
