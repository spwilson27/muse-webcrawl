Solage

Solage (fl. late 14th century) was a French composer of the ars subtilior style, active in the later Middle Ages. Almost nothing is known of his life; his music survives principally in the Chantilly Codex (Musée Condé, MS 564), the most important source of ars subtilior repertory, which preserves ten works attributed to him — the largest group by any composer in the manuscript besides Senleches.

Music

Solage's works are mostly three-voice virelais, ballades, and rondeaux in the intricate rhythmic notation characteristic of the ars subtilior — a mannered, experimental style cultivated at courts in France and northern Italy around 1370–1420. Characteristic pieces include:

  • Fumeux fume par fumée — a famous rondeau about smokers ("smoky men smoke through smoke"), full of low notes and rhythmic puzzles, possibly written for a circle of tobacco-like herb smokers at court.
  • Le Basile — a virelai honouring a basilisk, or possibly a patron's name-day.
  • Pluseurs gens voy and Calextone, que fut dame d'Arouse — the latter a ballade lamenting a deceased lady.
  • Corps femenin, En l'amoureux vergier, Hélas! je voy mon cuer, Joieux de cuer, and S'aincy estoit.

Identity

The name "Solage" may be a pseudonym or a pun; scholars have suggested connections to the court of Jean, duc de Berry, one of the great patrons of the era. His rondeau Fumeux fume is among the most performed and recorded of all ars subtilior pieces, frequently cited as an emblem of the style's extravagant complexity.
