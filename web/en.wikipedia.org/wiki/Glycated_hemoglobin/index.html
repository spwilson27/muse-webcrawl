Glycated hemoglobin

Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) is a form of hemoglobin that is chemically linked to a sugar. Glucose in the blood reacts non-enzymatically with hemoglobin A to form HbA1c, and the amount present reflects average blood glucose over the preceding two to three months, the lifespan of red blood cells.

HbA1c is used to test for the presence of diabetes and to monitor glucose control in people who have it. Results are reported as a percentage (DCCT units) or in mmol/mol (IFCC units). A value of 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) or above is a diagnostic criterion for diabetes.

Levels can be misleading in conditions affecting red blood cell turnover, such as anemia, hemoglobinopathies or pregnancy.
