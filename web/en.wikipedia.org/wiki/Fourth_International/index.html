The Fourth International (FI) is a political international of Trotskyist organisations founded in Paris in 1938 by Leon Trotsky and his supporters, who had been expelled from the Soviet-led Communist International (Comintern, the "Third International"). Trotsky argued the Comintern was irreformably dominated by the Stalinist bureaucracy, and that a new international was needed to lead the world working class.

History

The founding congress, held near Paris in September 1938 with about 30 delegates, adopted Trotsky's Transitional Program ("The Death Agony of Capitalism and the Tasks of the Fourth International"). After Trotsky's assassination in 1940 and the Second World War, the International struggled to maintain unity; a major split in 1953 divided it into rival bodies (the International Secretariat and the International Committee), which partially reunified in 1963 as the United Secretariat. Numerous other organisations have since claimed the Fourth International's mantle, making Trotskyism a byword for both principled persistence and sectarian fragmentation.
