Overview

Bulawayo is Zimbabwe's second-largest city and its industrial and railway hub, in the southwest of the country in Matabeleland. The name derives from the Ndebele word uBulawayo, associated with the royal kraal of Lobengula, last king of the Ndebele (Matabele) kingdom.

History

The modern city was founded in 1893 after British South Africa Company forces occupied Lobengula's capital during the First Matabele War; Cecil Rhodes ordered the new town built beside the royal kraal. Bulawayo became a major railway junction (the Rhodesia Railways) and manufacturing centre. The Natural History Museum, Khami Ruins (a UNESCO site) nearby, and Matobo Hills National Park are regional landmarks.
