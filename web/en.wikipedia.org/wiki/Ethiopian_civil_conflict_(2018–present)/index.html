Ethiopian civil conflict (2018–present)

Ethiopian civil conflict
Date2018 – present
LocationEthiopia (chiefly Tigray, Amhara, Oromia, Afar regions)
Principal partiesFederal government of Ethiopia; TPLF (2020–2022); Fano militias; Oromo Liberation Army; Eritrean forces (2020–2022)

The Ethiopian civil conflict is a series of overlapping armed conflicts in Ethiopia that escalated after 2018, when Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed took office and began political reforms. Its deadliest phase was the Tigray War (November 2020 – November 2022) between the federal government, allied with Eritrea and regional forces, and the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF). Estimates of total war-related deaths, including from famine and lack of medical care, run into the hundreds of thousands, making it among the deadliest conflicts of the twenty-first century.

Background

From 1991 to 2018 Ethiopia was governed by the EPRDF coalition, dominated by the TPLF, under an ethnic-federal constitution. Abiy's rise ended TPLF dominance; relations between the federal government and Tigray's regional government deteriorated over disputed elections and constitutional questions until federal forces and Eritrean troops intervened in Tigray in November 2020.

Tigray War and peace agreement

The war involved massacres of civilians, widespread sexual violence, and a de facto blockade of Tigray that produced famine conditions. In November 2022 the parties signed the Pretoria Agreement, a permanent cessation of hostilities mediated by the African Union, providing for TPLF disarmament and restoration of federal authority and services.

Other theatres

Conflict continued elsewhere: the Oromo Liberation Army insurgency in Oromia region, and, from 2023, an insurgency by Fano militias in Amhara region — former federal allies during the Tigray War — prompting a state of emergency and sustained federal offensives. Border tensions around the disputed al-Fashaga area and the fallout of the war in neighboring Sudan further strained the region.
