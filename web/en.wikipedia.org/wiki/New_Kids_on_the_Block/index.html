New Kids on the Block (also known as NKOTB) is an American boy band formed in Boston, Massachusetts in 1984. The group — Donnie Wahlberg, Jordan Knight, Jonathan Knight, Joey McIntyre and Danny Wood — achieved massive teen popularity in the late 1980s and early 1990s and reunited in 2008.

Rise to fame

Assembled by producer Maurice Starr, who had earlier created New Edition, the group released its self-titled debut in 1986 to little success. The 1988 album Hangin' Tough broke through with singles such as 'You Got It (The Right Stuff)' and 'I'll Be Loving You (Forever)', making them one of the biggest pop acts in the world.

Their 1990 album Step by Step and its title track continued their success, and the group became a merchandising phenomenon with dolls, lunchboxes and a Saturday-morning cartoon.

Decline and reunion

Popularity waned in the early 1990s amid changing musical tastes, and the group split in 1994. Members pursued solo and acting careers, notably Donnie Wahlberg in film and television.

The quintet reunited in 2008 with the album The Block and has since toured extensively, including package tours with other acts of their era such as Backstreet Boys, Boyz II Men and Paula Abdul.
