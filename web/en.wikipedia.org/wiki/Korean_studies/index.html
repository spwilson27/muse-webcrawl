Korean studies

Hangul, the Korean alphabet, central to Korean studies

Korean studies (Korean: 한국학, Hangukhak) is an academic discipline concerned with the study of Korea, its language, history, literature, culture, society and politics. It forms part of the broader field of East Asian studies and area studies, and is taught at universities in Korea and around the world, often in dedicated institutes.

Scope

Korean studies encompasses Korean linguistics and the Korean language, including hangul and the historical use of hanja (Chinese characters); Korean history from the Three Kingdoms through the Joseon dynasty to the modern divided peninsula; classical and modern Korean literature; Korean philosophy and religion, including Confucianism, Buddhism and shamanism; and contemporary politics, economics and society of both South Korea and North Korea.

History of the field

Modern Korean studies in the West developed after the Second World War and expanded during and after the Korean War, driven by strategic interest in the peninsula. In South Korea, institutions such as the Academy of Korean Studies (founded 1978) and the Korea Foundation (1991) promote research and support Korean studies programmes abroad. The field grew further with global interest in the Korean Wave (Hallyu) in the 2000s.

Institutions and journals

Major scholarly associations include the Association for Asian Studies and the Association for Korean Studies in Europe. Leading English-language journals include the Journal of Korean Studies, Korean Studies, Acta Koreana and The Review of Korean Studies.
