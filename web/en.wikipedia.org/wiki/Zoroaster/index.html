Zoroaster, also known as Zarathustra, was an Iranian religious reformer and prophet who founded Zoroastrianism, among the earliest known monotheistic faiths. A priest (zaotar) by training, he proclaimed Ahura Mazda — the 'Wise Lord' — as the one uncreated God, rejecting the old gods (daevas) as demons, and taught that humans must actively choose truth over the lie. His hymns, the Gathas, composed in an archaic Avestan closely related to Vedic Sanskrit, are the only texts attributed directly to him.

Life and dating

Almost everything about Zoroaster's life is uncertain. Greek sources placed him absurdly early; Zoroastrian tradition dates him to '258 years before Alexander', suggesting the 7th–6th century BC, while linguistic evidence for the Gathas points several centuries earlier, perhaps 1200–1000 BC, in the steppes of Central Asia. Tradition tells of his laughing at birth, his revelation at thirty on a riverbank, persecution, and eventual conversion of King Vishtaspa. Nietzsche borrowed his name for the prophet of Thus Spoke Zarathustra precisely because, as he saw it, the inventor of good-and-evil dualism should be the first to unmask it.
