Division (military)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The NATO map symbol for a friendly infantry division

A division is a large military unit or formation, usually consisting of between 6,000 and 25,000 soldiers. In most armies, a division is composed of several regiments or brigades; in turn, several divisions typically make up a corps.

While the division was originally created as a combined arms unit capable of independent operations during the Napoleonic Wars, in modern times, it remains the smallest formation that combines units from various specialisms to achieve a balanced, self-sustaining force for sustained operations. A division is generally commanded by a major general.

History

The modern division originated in the French army under Napoleon Bonaparte, whose divisions could march separately and fight together, and in cultivating the system at corps level. The division became the standard large formation during the 19th and 20th centuries; in the World Wars, infantry divisions typically fielded 10,000-20,000 men, with armoured and airborne variants. In many modern armies, brigades have supplanted divisions as the primary deployable formations, with divisions serving as headquarters and force-generation structures.

Types

  • Armoured (tank) division
  • Airborne division
  • Mountain division
