Long Island City (LIC) is a neighborhood in the westernmost portion of the borough of Queens in New York City. It is bordered by Astoria to the north, the East River to the west, Newtown Creek and Brooklyn to the south, and Sunnyside to the east. Long Island City is a major commercial and manufacturing district and has one of the highest concentrations of art galleries, art institutions, and studio space of any neighborhood in New York City.

History

Long Island City was incorporated as a city in 1870 from the merger of the village of Astoria and several hamlets in the town of Newtown. It remained an independent city until 1898, when it was consolidated into the City of New York as part of the borough of Queens. Its first and only mayor was Patrick "Battle-Axe" Gleason.

The neighborhood's waterfront location made it a center for industry in the 19th and 20th centuries, with factories, rail yards, and warehouses lining the East River. The Long Island Rail Road's Hunters Point Avenue station and the Queensboro Bridge (opened 1909) tied the area closely to Manhattan.

Modern development

Since the late 20th century Long Island City has undergone rapid gentrification and high-rise residential development, particularly around Court Square and the Hunters Point waterfront. The Citicorp Building (One Court Square), completed in 1990, was for many years the tallest building in New York City outside Manhattan. Gantry Plaza State Park preserves the neighborhood's industrial gantries along the riverfront, and the restored Pepsi-Cola sign in Hunters Point is a designated New York City landmark.

The area is home to cultural institutions including MoMA PS1, the Noguchi Museum, and Socrates Sculpture Park. Silvercup Studios, one of the largest film and television production facilities on the East Coast, is also located in the neighborhood.

Transportation

Long Island City is served by several New York City Subway lines, including the 7, E, G, and M trains, as well as the Long Island Rail Road and the NYC Ferry's Astoria route. The Queensboro Bridge and the Queens–Midtown Tunnel connect it to Manhattan.
