William Lilly


Born 11 May 1602, Diseworth, Leicestershire, England
Died 9 June 1681 (aged 79)
Known for Christian Astrology (1647); Merlinus Anglicus almanacs

William Lilly (11 May 1602 – 9 June 1681) was an English astrologer, the most famous practitioner of astrology in 17th-century England and author of the first comprehensive astrological textbook in English.

Life

Lilly was born in Leicestershire and educated at Ashby-de-la-Zouch grammar school. In 1620 he came to London as a servant, later inheriting money through marriage, which allowed him to study astrology. From 1644 he published the popular annual almanac Merlinus Anglicus Junior, and in 1647 his Christian Astrology, a detailed manual of horary astrology that remains in print today.

Civil War fame

During the English Civil War, Lilly's almanacs and prophecies supported the Parliamentarian cause and reached a huge readership; he was consulted by figures on both sides, including Charles I's circle. His Monarchy or No Monarchy (1651) contained woodcut "hieroglyphics" later interpreted as depicting the Plague and the Great Fire of London of 1666; he was examined by a parliamentary committee about the fire but cleared of involvement.

Later life

In later years Lilly practised medicine as well as astrology from his home at Hersham, Surrey, and took the astrologer John Booker and Elias Ashmole among his pupils and patrons. His autobiography, published posthumously, is a vivid source for the period.
