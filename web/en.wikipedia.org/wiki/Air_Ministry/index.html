Air Ministry

Formed: 1918
Dissolved: 1964 (merged into the Ministry of Defence)
Minister: Secretary of State for Air

The Air Ministry was the British government department responsible for the affairs of the Royal Air Force between 1918 and 1964. Its political head was the Secretary of State for Air, and its governing body was the Air Council.

History

The Air Ministry was created during the First World War, following the Smuts Report of 1917, which recommended the creation of an independent air service. The Air Force Act of 1917 and the formation of the RAF on 1 April 1918 put military aviation under a single ministry, ending the rivalry between the Royal Flying Corps and the Royal Naval Air Service. In its early years the ministry, under Winston Churchill (who briefly held both War and Air portfolios) and then others, had to defend the RAF's independence against the older services.

During the Second World War the Air Ministry oversaw the RAF's expansion and operations, while aircraft production was hived off to the separate Ministry of Aircraft Production (1940–1946). The ministry was also responsible for civil aviation until a separate ministry took over after the war. In 1964 the Air Ministry, the Admiralty, and the War Office were merged into a unified Ministry of Defence.

Specifications and "Air Ministry" usage

The term "Air Ministry" also appears in the many technical specifications (A.M. specifications and publications such as Air Publications) issued by the ministry for aircraft, engines, and equipment.
