Eastern Abenaki language

Eastern Abenaki (also Eastern Abnaki) is an extinct language of the Eastern Algonquian subgroup of the Algonquian language family, formerly spoken by the Eastern Abenaki peoples of what is now Maine and adjacent parts of New Hampshire and New Brunswick. It is closely related to Western Abenaki, spoken in Quebec and Vermont, and some scholars treat the two as dialects of a single Abenaki language.

Dialects

Recorded dialects include Penobscot (spoken by the Penobscot Nation along the Penobscot River), Kennebec (Caniba), Androscoggin, and Pigwacket. The Penobscot dialect is the best documented, thanks to the work of linguist Frank Siebert, who recorded the language from its last fluent speakers in the twentieth century, and to the Penobscot dictionary project undertaken by the Penobscot Nation.

History

Eastern Abenaki was written down as early as the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries by French missionaries, most notably Sébastien Rale, who compiled a dictionary of the Kennebec dialect. Epidemics, warfare and colonial displacement drove speakers westward to Quebec or into English-speaking society, and the language declined through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The last native speaker of Penobscot, Madeline Shay, died in the 1990s, though tribal language programmes continue to teach and revive the language from the extensive documentation.
