The 1888–89 FA Cup was the eighteenth season of the Football Association Challenge Cup, England's oldest football competition. It was the first FA Cup season to be played alongside the newly formed Football League, and it was won by Preston North End, who defeated Wolverhampton Wanderers 3–0 in the final at the Kennington Oval on 30 March 1889.

Background

The 1888–89 season marked the beginning of the Football League, founded by twelve clubs including Preston North End, Aston Villa, and Wolverhampton Wanderers. Preston, managed on the field by the pioneering William Sudell, proceeded through the season unbeaten in both league and cup — a feat that earned the team the nickname "The Invincibles" and the first league and cup double in English football history.

Final

In the final, played before a crowd of roughly 25,000 at the Oval, goals from Jimmy Ross, Fred Dewhurst, and Sam Thomson gave Preston a comfortable 3–0 victory over Wolves. Preston completed the entire cup run without conceding a single goal, a defensive record that underlined their dominance of the early professional game.

Legacy

Preston's double-winning "Invincibles" side remains one of the most celebrated teams of the Victorian era, and the 1888–89 campaign is frequently cited as the foundation season of organised league football in England.
