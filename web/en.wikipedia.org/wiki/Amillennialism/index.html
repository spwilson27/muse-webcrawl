Amillennialism, in Christian eschatology, involves the rejection of the belief that Jesus Christ will have a literal, thousand-year-long, physical reign on Earth. The prefix "a-" (no) combined with "millennium" is somewhat misleading, as amillennialists do not deny a millennium altogether; rather, they interpret the "thousand years" of Revelation 20 symbolically as the present church age, during which Christ reigns spiritually from heaven and through his church, and Satan is "bound" in the sense of being unable to deceive the nations from receiving the gospel.

Beliefs

Amillennialists hold that the kingdom of God was inaugurated at Christ's first coming, that the first resurrection of Revelation 20 is the spiritual regeneration of believers (or their reign with Christ in heaven after death), and that the millennium will culminate in Christ's single, visible second coming, followed by the general resurrection, the final judgment, and the new heavens and new earth. There is no separate future earthly kingdom distinct from the eternal state, and typically no sharp distinction between Israel and the church in God's redemptive plan.

History

The view is associated above all with Augustine of Hippo, whose City of God shaped Western eschatology for over a millennium. It became the standard teaching of the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches and of the magisterial Reformers, and remains common among Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, and many other traditions. The term "amillennialism" itself is a later label, popularized in the 20th century as the position was contrasted with premillennialism and postmillennialism.
