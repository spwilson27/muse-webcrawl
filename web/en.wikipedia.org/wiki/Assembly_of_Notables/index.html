Assembly of Notables

Country: France

Type: Consultative assembly of appointed notables

Notable meetings: 1787, 1788

An Assembly of Notables (French: Assemblée des notables) was a group of high-ranking nobles, ecclesiastics and state functionaries in France, convened by the king to consult on matters of state. Unlike the Estates General, its members were appointed by the king rather than elected, and it had no legislative authority.

History

French kings summoned such assemblies from the Middle Ages onward, including notable gatherings in 1583, 1596–97, 1617 and 1626–27. The institution is best known for its final incarnations: in February 1787 Louis XVI convened 144 notables at Versailles to approve finance minister Calonne's reform programme, including a universal land tax. The notables refused to approve the reforms without wider consultation, demanding the Estates General — a refusal that marked a decisive step toward the French Revolution.

The 1787–88 assemblies

After Calonne's dismissal, his successor Loménie de Brienne again faced refusal from a second assembly in November 1788 on the question of how the Estates General should be composed. The Assembly of Notables thereby helped open, but could not resolve, the fiscal and constitutional crisis of the Ancien Régime.
