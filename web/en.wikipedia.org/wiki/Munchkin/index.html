Munchkin

The Munchkins are fictional inhabitants of Munchkin Country, the Eastern region in the Land of Oz. They first appear in the 1900 novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, in which they are described as short in stature, and wearing blue because blue is the favourite colour of their region. In the novel, the Munchkins free Dorothy Gale, whose house has landed on and killed the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing them from her domination. Their name has entered common usage for a short person, a child, or anything of diminutive size.

In the Oz books

In Baum's original novel, the Munchkins are described as being about as tall as Dorothy (who is not very tall herself), wearing pointed blue hats with little bells, and blue clothing. The wealthy Munchkin Boq hosts Dorothy at his home before she continues down the yellow brick road to the Emerald City. Munchkin Country is the eastern quadrant of Oz; its ruler before her death was the Wicked Witch of the East. The region appears throughout Baum's fourteen Oz books and in the works of his successors.

In the 1939 film

In Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer's 1939 film The Wizard of Oz, the Munchkins appear in a celebrated musical sequence set in Munchkinland, where they sing "Follow the Yellow Brick Road", "We Represent the Lollipop Guild", "We Welcome You to Munchkinland" and "Ding-Dong! The Witch Is Dead". The roles were played by a large troupe of adult little people, mostly members of the Singer Midgets company recruited from Europe, together with some child performers. The film's Munchkins cemented the characters in popular culture.

Other uses of the term

The word "munchkin" has developed several derivative meanings. It is an affectionate term for a small child. In role-playing games, a "munchkin" is a player who prioritises power and winning over role-playing; the humorous card game Munchkin (2001), designed by Steve Jackson, parodies this style of play. The Munchkin is also a breed of cat characterised by very short legs caused by a genetic mutation, named after the fictional people. "Munchkin" is additionally used in computing and business slang for various small tools and procedures.
