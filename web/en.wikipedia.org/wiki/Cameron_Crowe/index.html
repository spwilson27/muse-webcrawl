Cameron Crowe

BornJuly 13, 1957, Palm Springs, California, U.S.
OccupationsFilm director, screenwriter, producer, journalist

Cameron Bruce Crowe (born July 13, 1957) is an American filmmaker and journalist. He began his career as a teenage writer for Rolling Stone magazine, touring with rock bands of the 1970s, before becoming a screenwriter and director known for warmly humanistic films steeped in rock music.

Early career in journalism

Having skipped several grades, Crowe started contributing to Rolling Stone while still in high school, interviewing acts such as Led Zeppelin, the Eagles and Lynyrd Skynyrd. His experiences on the road later became the basis of his semi-autobiographical film Almost Famous.

Film career

Crowe wrote the screenplay for Fast Times at Ridgemont High (1982), based on his undercover book about high-school life. He made his directorial debut with Say Anything... (1989), followed by Singles (1992) and the hit Jerry Maguire (1996), which earned five Academy Award nominations. Almost Famous (2000) won him the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. Later films include Vanilla Sky (2001), Elizabethtown (2005), We Bought a Zoo (2011) and Aloha (2015).

Style

Crowe's films are noted for their heartfelt tone, classic-rock soundtracks and memorable dialogue, including the famous "You had me at hello" and "Show me the money" lines from Jerry Maguire.
