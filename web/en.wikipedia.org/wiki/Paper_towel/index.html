Paper towel

A paper towel is an absorbent, disposable towel made from paper instead of cloth, used for cleaning up spills, drying hands, and other household tasks. Paper towels are typically sold as perforated sheets on a roll, or as folded sheets for dispensers in commercial restrooms, and are usually single-use.

History

In 1907, Arthur Scott of the Scott Paper Company in Philadelphia created disposable paper towels after a shipment of toilet paper proved too thick to use; inspired by a teacher who gave soft paper to students to avoid sharing a germy cloth towel during a cold epidemic, he marketed paper "Sani-Towels" for restrooms. In 1919, William E. Corbin and colleagues at the Brown Company developed the folded Nibroc towel. Kitchen rolls for household use became common from the 1930s onward, and brands such as Bounty, Brawny, and Viva became household staples after mid-century.

Manufacture and properties

Paper towels are made from wood pulp, often virgin fibre for strength and absorbency, sometimes blended with recycled fibre. Resins may be added for wet strength, and sheets are often embossed with patterns that increase absorbency and create air pockets. Products vary in ply (one- or two-ply), sheet size (including "select-a-size" perforations), and texture.

Environmental considerations

Because they are disposable, contaminated with food and cleaning chemicals, and made to degrade in water, paper towels are generally not recyclable and contribute to landfill waste. Studies of hand drying have compared their hygiene and environmental impact with electric air dryers and cloth towels, with results depending on assumptions about energy and laundering.
