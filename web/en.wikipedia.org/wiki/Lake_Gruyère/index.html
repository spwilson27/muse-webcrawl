Lake Gruyère (French: Lac de la Gruyère) is an artificial lake in the canton of Fribourg, Switzerland, formed by damming the Saane (Sarine) river. Created in 1948 with the completion of the Rossens Dam, it is one of the largest artificial lakes in Switzerland.

Description

The reservoir stretches for about 13.5 km through the Gruyère region between the towns of Bulle and Fribourg. The arch dam at Rossens, around 83 m high, impounds the water for hydroelectric generation. Within the lake lies the Île d'Ogoz, an island crowned by the ruins of a medieval castle and chapel; when the water level is low in spring, the island can be reached on foot across a gravel spit. The construction of the A12 motorway across the lake on the Pont de la Madeleine viaduct is a prominent modern landmark.

Surroundings

Villages around the lake include Rossens, Pont-la-Ville, Morlon and Corbières. The shores are a mixture of pasture, forest and small harbours, and the lake is used for sailing, rowing and fishing (perch, pike and trout). Walking trails follow much of the shoreline, and the region is known for Gruyère cheese production in the surrounding alpine pastures.
