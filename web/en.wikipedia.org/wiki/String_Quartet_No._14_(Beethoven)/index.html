String Quartet No. 14 (Beethoven)


Key: C♯ minor
Opus: 131
Composed: 1825–1826
Dedication: Baron Joseph von Stutterheim

The String Quartet No. 14 in C♯ minor, Op. 131, by Ludwig van Beethoven was completed in 1826 and is the last of the three quartets commissioned by Prince Nikolai Galitzin. It is regarded by many as the greatest of Beethoven's late quartets; Beethoven himself is reported to have considered it his finest quartet.

Structure

The quartet consists of seven movements played without pause (attacca), lasting about 40 minutes:

  1. Adagio ma non troppo e molto espressivo — a slow fugue in C♯ minor.
  2. Allegro molto vivace (D major).
  3. Allegro moderato — a short recitative-like bridge.
  4. Andante ma non troppo e molto cantabile — a set of variations in A major.
  5. Presto (E major) — a scherzo-like movement.
  6. Adagio quasi un poco andante (G♯ minor) — a brief, sorrowful interlude.
  7. Allegro (C♯ minor) — a dramatic sonata-form finale.

Reception

The work's continuous span, fugal opening, and emotional range made it a touchstone for later composers. Schubert, on hearing it days before his death in 1828, is said to have exclaimed, "After this, what is left for us to write?" Wagner wrote of the first movement as "surely the saddest thing ever said in notes". T. S. Eliot's late poem cycle Four Quartets was partly inspired by Beethoven's late quartets, and the Op. 131 in particular.
