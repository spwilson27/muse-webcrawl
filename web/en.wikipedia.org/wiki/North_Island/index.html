North Island

The North Island (Māori: Te Ika-a-Māui, "the fish of Māui") is one of the two main islands of New Zealand, separated from the larger but less populous South Island by Cook Strait. With an area of about 113,700 km² it is the world's 14th-largest island and home to roughly three-quarters of New Zealand's population.

Geography

The island contains the country's largest city, Auckland, and its capital, Wellington, at the southern tip. The centre is dominated by the Volcanic Plateau, with active volcanoes including Mount Ruapehu, Mount Tongariro, and Mount Taranaki, and the geothermal zones around Rotorua and Lake Taupō, New Zealand's largest lake.

Demographics and economy

Major centres include Hamilton, Tauranga, Palmerston North, and Napier–Hastings. The island's economy mixes intensive dairy and horticulture in Waikato and the Bay of Plenty with the government, finance, and creative industries of Wellington and Auckland.
