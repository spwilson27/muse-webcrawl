The Saar Protectorate (German: Saarprotektorat) was a short-lived French protectorate in the Saar Basin, established after the Second World War and lasting from 1947 to 1956. It was separated from the French occupation zone of Germany, economically integrated with France, but was not formally annexed and did not form part of the French Republic.

History

After 1945 France sought to detach the coal-rich Saar from Germany, echoing the League of Nations administration of the territory between 1920 and 1935. A constitution adopted in December 1947 made the Saar autonomous under a French high commissioner, with its own government under Minister-President Johannes Hoffmann, its own flag, anthem and citizenship, and a customs and currency union with France. The protectorate issued Saar franc coins and stamps, fielded its own football team — which played in the qualifiers for the 1954 World Cup — and sent an independent team to the 1952 Helsinki Olympics.

Referendum and return to Germany

Under the 1954 Paris Agreements, France and West Germany agreed on a "European Statute" for the Saar, to be approved by referendum. On 23 October 1955, Saar voters rejected the statute by about 67%, a result widely read as a vote for reunion with the Federal Republic. Negotiations followed, and under the Saar Treaty of 1956 the territory acceded to West Germany on 1 January 1957 as the state of Saarland — the so-called "little reunification" (kleine Wiedervereinigung) — while economic integration with France was phased out by 1959.
