Illegal drug trade

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The illegal drug trade, or drug trafficking, is a global illicit black market dedicated to the cultivation, manufacture, distribution and sale of prohibited drugs. Most jurisdictions prohibit trade in certain types of drugs through drug prohibition laws. The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime has estimated the trade to be worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually, making it one of the largest illicit markets in the world.

History

International drug control began with the 1909 Shanghai Opium Commission and the Hague International Opium Convention of 1912, prompted by the opium trade in China. The modern framework rests on the UN Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs (1961), the Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) and the 1988 Vienna Convention against illicit traffic. Production is geographically concentrated: opium poppies in Afghanistan and Myanmar, coca in Colombia, Peru and Bolivia, and synthetic drugs manufactured in many regions.

Organisation and effects

Trade is conducted by a spectrum of actors from peasant cultivators to drug cartels — such as Mexico's Sinaloa and Jalisco New Generation cartels and Colombia's former Medellín and Cali cartels — and smaller distribution networks. Profits drive corruption, money laundering and violence, notably in Mexico's drug war since 2006. Consumers face risks from adulteration and potency variation, a factor in overdose epidemics involving heroin and synthetic opioids such as fentanyl.

Responses and debate

Governments pursue interdiction, eradication, precursor control and demand reduction, while critics argue that prohibition itself sustains prices and violence and advocate harm reduction, decriminalisation or regulated legalisation, citing Portugal's 2001 decriminalisation and legal cannabis markets in parts of the Americas as alternatives.
