Joe Paterno

Joseph Vincent Paterno (December 21, 1926 – January 22, 2012), nicknamed "JoePa", was an American college football player and coach, best known as the head coach of the Penn State Nittany Lions from 1966 to 2011. With 409 victories he is the most victorious coach in NCAA FBS history, and his teams won two national championships (1982 and 1986). His career ended amid the Penn State child sex abuse scandal.

Paterno coaching at Beaver Stadium.

Career

Paterno played quarterback at Brown University and joined Penn State as an assistant coach in 1950, becoming head coach in 1966. Over 46 seasons his teams went undefeated five times, won 24 bowl games, and were known for plain uniforms, academic emphasis and the "Success with Honor" ethos. He and his wife Sue were major benefactors of the university, notably funding the Paterno Library.

Scandal and death

In November 2011, Paterno's former assistant Jerry Sandusky was charged with child sexual abuse. Paterno, who had reported an allegation about Sandusky to his superiors in 2002, was dismissed by the university's board of trustees on November 9, 2011. He died of lung cancer ten weeks later, on January 22, 2012. The NCAA vacated 112 of his wins following the Freeh Report, but the victories were restored in 2015 as part of a legal settlement.

Legacy

Paterno's legacy remains contested: long celebrated as a model of integrity in college sports, his reputation was profoundly damaged by the scandal and by findings that he and other officials failed to act decisively on reports about Sandusky. His statue outside Beaver Stadium was removed in 2012.
