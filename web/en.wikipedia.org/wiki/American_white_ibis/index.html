American white ibis

Scientific name: Eudocimus albus
Family: Threskiornithidae
Range: Southeastern US, Caribbean, northern South America
Conservation: Least Concern

The American white ibis (Eudocimus albus) is a species of wading bird in the ibis family, found from the southeastern United States through the Caribbean to northern South America. Adults are almost entirely white with black wingtips visible in flight, a long downcurved red-orange bill and matching red-orange legs; immature birds are mottled brown and white. It is one of the most numerous wading birds in Florida and along the Gulf Coast.

Habitat and feeding

White ibises inhabit shallow wetlands — marshes, mangroves, flooded pastures, mudflats and lawns — where they forage in flocks, probing soft mud and shallow water with their sensitive bills for crayfish, crabs, insects, snails and small fish. They are highly nomadic, tracking rainfall and water levels, and flocks commute daily between feeding grounds and communal roosts in long V-formations or lines.

Breeding

Breeding colonies form in trees or shrubs over water, often in mixed heronries with herons and egrets. The female builds a platform nest of sticks gathered by the male and lays two to five pale blue-green eggs; both parents incubate and feed the chicks. Populations fluctuate with wetland conditions, and the species has shifted its diet and range in response to changes in south Florida's water management and the spread of invasive prey such as apple snails. The white ibis is the mascot of the University of Miami, chosen for the bird's reputed courage in hurricanes.
