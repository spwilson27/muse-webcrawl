Mazu (Chinese: 媽祖; also written Ma Zhu in some romanisations, and known in older Western sources as Matsu) is a Chinese sea goddess in Chinese folk religion, Chinese Buddhism and Taoism, and the deified form of the Fujianese shamaness Lin Moniang, who lived on Meizhou Island in the late tenth century. She is venerated as the protector of sailors, fishermen and travellers by sea.

Legend

According to tradition, Lin Moniang was born in 960 on Meizhou Island off Putian, Fujian, was a gifted swimmer and shamaness, and died in 987 while rescuing — in person or in spirit — members of her family during a storm at sea. After her death she was reported to appear in sailors' visions to save ships in distress, and temples to her spread along the Chinese coast.

Cult and titles

Successive dynasties granted the goddess ever higher titles: under the Song and Yuan she was promoted as a consort and princess of heaven, under the Ming she became Tianfei ("Princess of Heaven"), and under the Qing she was raised to Tianhou ("Empress of Heaven"). Her cult was carried by Fujianese and Cantonese emigrants to Taiwan — where she is one of the most important deities, honoured by great pilgrimages such as the Dajia Mazu pilgrimage — and throughout maritime Southeast Asia.

Recognition

In 2009, Mazu belief and customs were inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. There are thousands of Mazu temples worldwide, and her birthday on the 23rd day of the third lunar month is a major festival in Fujian, Taiwan, Macau and overseas Chinese communities.
