Cache la Poudre River

The Cache la Poudre River, also known locally as the Poudre River, is a river in the U.S. state of Colorado. It rises in Rocky Mountain National Park in the Front Range, flows eastward through the steep Poudre Canyon and the city of Fort Collins, and joins the South Platte River east of Greeley. It is about 126 miles (203 km) long and is Colorado's only nationally designated Wild and Scenic River.

Name

The French name means "hide the powder". According to tradition, in the 1820s French fur trappers caught in a severe snowstorm were forced to bury ("cache") part of their gunpowder along the riverbank to lighten their loads, giving the river its name.

Course and use

From its headwaters on the Continental Divide, the river drops through Roosevelt National Forest, where Colorado State Highway 14 (the Cache la Poudre–North Park Scenic Byway) follows the canyon. The river is heavily used for recreation — whitewater rafting, kayaking, and fly fishing for trout — and for irrigation and municipal water supply on the plains; its water rights have been contested since the 1870s, and the famous 1874 dispute between upstream and downstream irrigators (near present-day Fort Collins and Greeley) helped shape Colorado's prior-appropriation water law. Some 76 miles of the river were designated a National Wild and Scenic River in 1986, and in 2009 the Cache la Poudre River Corridor National Heritage Area was established.

Flooding

The river flooded Fort Collins severely in 1864 (contributing to the relocation of the army post that became the city) and in July 1997 a flash flood on its tributary Spring Creek killed five people. The High Park Fire of 2012 in the watershed caused ash and sediment problems in the river for several seasons afterward.
