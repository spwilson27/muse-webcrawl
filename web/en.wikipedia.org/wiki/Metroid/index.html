Metroid

Metroid is a Japanese science-fiction action-adventure game franchise created by Nintendo. The series follows bounty hunter Samus Aran as she battles the Space Pirates and their attempts to exploit the parasitic Metroid creatures. Combining platforming, shooting, and open-ended exploration of interconnected environments, the series — together with Castlevania — gave its name to the "Metroidvania" genre.

Gameplay

The 2D games feature side-scrolling exploration of a large, non-linear world: areas are gated by abilities (such as the Morph Ball, Missiles, and the Grapple Beam) acquired as power-ups, encouraging backtracking. Metroid Prime (2002) translated the formula into first-person 3D, with an emphasis on scanning and atmosphere. At the end of the first game, players famously discovered that Samus was a woman — one of gaming's earliest prominent female protagonists.

History

The original Metroid was released in 1986 for the Famicom Disk System and NES, produced by Gunpei Yokoi and directed by Satoru Okada and Masao Yamamoto, with music by Hirokazu Tanaka. Metroid II: Return of Samus (1991, Game Boy) and the acclaimed Super Metroid (1994, SNES) followed. After an eight-year gap, the series returned with Metroid Fusion and Metroid Prime (both 2002). Later entries include the Prime trilogy by Retro Studios, Metroid: Other M (2010), Metroid: Samus Returns (2017), and Metroid Dread (2021), which concluded the original storyline to critical acclaim.
