The Aldwych Theatre is a West End theatre on Aldwych in the City of Westminster, London. It was built in 1905 as part of a pair with the neighbouring Novello Theatre (originally the Waldorf Theatre), to designs by W. G. R. Sprague, and forms part of the grand Edwardian redevelopment of the Aldwych–Kingsway area. It is a Grade II* listed building. It should not be confused with the nearby Aldwych tube station.

History

The theatre's first manager, Seymour Hicks, opened it with his musical Bluebell in Fairyland. From 1906 to 1910 it was famous for the Aldwych farces' predecessors — a series of successful farces and musical comedies — and in the 1920s and 1930s Ben Travers's celebrated Aldwych farces, written for the company of Tom Walls and Ralph Lynn, defined the genre. After the Second World War the theatre hosted serious drama, including early London productions of Tennessee Williams and the first English-language productions of several European plays.

Royal Shakespeare Company era

From 1960 to 1982 the Aldwych served as the London home of the Royal Shakespeare Company, where Peter Hall and his successors premiered productions transferring from Stratford-upon-Avon and created landmarks such as The Wars of the Roses and the premiere of Peter Shaffer's The Royal Hunt of the Sun. In later decades the theatre has hosted long-running musicals and plays, including Fame, Dancing in the Dark seasons, Top Hat and, from 2018, Tina: The Tina Turner Musical.
