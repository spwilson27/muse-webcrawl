Akshaya Patra Foundation

The Akshaya Patra Foundation is an Indian non-profit organisation that operates the world's largest NGO-run school lunch programme, implementing the Government of India's PM POSHAN (Mid Day Meal) Programme. Headquartered in Bengaluru, the foundation serves freshly cooked midday meals to over two million children in government and government-aided schools across India each school day.

History

The foundation began in 2000 by feeding 1,500 children in five schools in Bengaluru, inspired by ISKCON's food-relief tradition and the vision of A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada that no child should be deprived of education because of hunger. Partnering with the Karnataka state government and later other states, it scaled rapidly using large centralised kitchens, some capable of cooking for 100,000 children, supplemented by decentralised kitchens run by women's self-help groups in remote areas.

Operations

Centralised kitchens use steam-based cooking, mechanised roti machines, and customised delivery vehicles with route optimisation to deliver meals within strict food-safety timelines. Menus are designed with nutritionists to meet prescribed calorie and protein norms and to suit regional tastes. The foundation also runs programmes such as breakfast initiatives, digital education support and scholarship schemes, and has sister organisations fundraising in the United States and the United Kingdom.

Recognition

Akshaya Patra's model has been studied by universities including Harvard Business School and recognised with awards such as the Nikkei Asia Prize and the Gandhi Peace Prize (awarded to ISKCON in 2021 in part for the programme's work). In 2019 the foundation served its cumulative three-billionth meal, commemorated at an event attended by the Prime Minister of India.
