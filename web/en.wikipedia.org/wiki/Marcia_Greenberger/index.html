Marcia Greenberger

Marcia D. Greenberger is an American women's rights attorney. She is the co-founder and co-president emerita of the National Women's Law Center (NWLC) in Washington, D.C.

Greenberger received her B.A. and her law degree from the University of Pennsylvania. After practicing tax law, she joined the Center for Law and Social Policy, where in 1972 she founded the Women's Rights Project. In 1981 the project expanded into the independent National Women's Law Center, which she led as co-president, alongside Nancy Duff Campbell, for more than four decades.

Work

Under Greenberger's leadership the NWLC became a leading American legal advocacy organization on issues including Title IX enforcement in education and athletics, pay equity, reproductive rights, child care, and women's health. She has testified frequently before Congress and litigated and supervised litigation in federal courts on sex discrimination. Greenberger retired as co-president in 2017, when Fatima Goss Graves became the Center's president and CEO, and has received numerous honors for her contributions to women's legal rights.
