World Trade Organization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an intergovernmental organisation that regulates and facilitates international trade. It is the world's largest international economic organisation, with around 166 member states representing over 98% of global trade. Established on 1 January 1995 under the Marrakesh Agreement concluding the Uruguay Round of negotiations, it succeeded the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT, 1947) and is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.

Functions

The WTO administers trade agreements covering goods (GATT), services (GATS) and intellectual property (TRIPS); provides a forum for negotiating further liberalisation; reviews members' trade policies; and operates a dispute-settlement system whose panels and (formerly) Appellate Body adjudicate complaints. Core principles include non-discrimination (most-favoured-nation and national treatment), reciprocity, transparency, and special treatment for developing countries.

Controversies

The organisation became a focal point of anti-globalisation protest (notably Seattle 1999). Negotiating rounds have stalled since the Doha Round (launched 2001); the Appellate Body ceased functioning in 2019 after the United States blocked judicial appointments, prompting interim arbitration arrangements among some members; and great-power trade conflicts, subsidies and national-security exceptions have strained the rules-based system the WTO embodies.
