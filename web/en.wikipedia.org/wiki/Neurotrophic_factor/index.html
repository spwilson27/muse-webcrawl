Neurotrophic factor

Neurotrophic factors (NTFs) are a family of biomolecules — nearly all of them peptides or small proteins — that support the growth, survival, and differentiation of both developing and mature neurons. Most neurotrophic factors belong to one of three families: the neurotrophins, the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family ligands, and the neuropoietic cytokines.

Neurotrophins

The neurotrophins include nerve growth factor (NGF), the first to be discovered, characterised by Rita Levi-Montalcini and Stanley Cohen; brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF); neurotrophin-3; and neurotrophin-4. They signal through Trk receptor tyrosine kinases and the p75 neurotrophin receptor.

Function

During development, target tissues secrete limited amounts of neurotrophic factors, and only neurons receiving sufficient trophic support survive — a mechanism that matches neuron numbers to their targets. In the adult nervous system the factors maintain neuronal function, support synaptic plasticity, and respond to injury. Reduced trophic support has been implicated in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, and therapeutic delivery of factors such as GDNF has been investigated.
