HDR10+
HDR10+ adds dynamic metadata to HDR video
Developed bySamsung, Amazon Video, Panasonic
AnnouncedApril 2017
TypeHigh dynamic range video format

HDR10+ is a high dynamic range (HDR) video format announced in April 2017 by Samsung and Amazon Video, and developed in partnership with Panasonic. It builds on the open HDR10 format by adding dynamic metadata, generated using Samsung's proprietary tone-mapping technology, which allows brightness levels to be adjusted on a scene-by-scene or even frame-by-frame basis rather than using a single static curve for an entire programme.

Technical details

Like HDR10, HDR10+ uses the PQ (perceptual quantizer) transfer function, 10-bit colour depth and the Rec. 2020 colour space, supporting peak brightness of up to 10,000 nits in the mastering specification. Its dynamic metadata is carried in the video stream and remains backwards compatible: a display that does not support HDR10+ simply reads the stream as standard HDR10. The format is standardised as SMPTE ST 2094-40 and is promoted by the HDR10+ Technologies licensing body.

Adoption

HDR10+ was positioned as a royalty-bearing but low-cost alternative to the proprietary Dolby Vision format. It is supported by Samsung and Panasonic televisions, a range of Ultra HD Blu-ray titles, and streaming services including Amazon Prime Video. A variant, HDR10+ Adaptive, adjusts the picture according to ambient room lighting using the television's light sensor.
