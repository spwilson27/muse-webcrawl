Tipitaka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tipiṭaka (Pali for "three baskets"), also known as the Pali Canon, is the traditional scriptural collection of Theravada Buddhism and the most complete surviving early Buddhist canon. Preserved in the Pali language, it is composed of three divisions, or "baskets" (piṭaka), and contains many thousands of discourses attributed to the Buddha and his disciples.

The three baskets

The Vinaya Piṭaka contains the monastic disciplinary code and the rules governing the community of monks and nuns. The Sutta Piṭaka contains the discourses, arranged into five collections (nikāya), including the Dhammapada and the long, medium-length and connected discourses. The Abhidhamma Piṭaka contains systematic philosophical and psychological analyses of the teachings.

History

The texts were transmitted orally for centuries before being written down, traditionally in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BC. In the 19th century the Burmese king Mindon had the entire canon inscribed on 729 marble slabs at Kuthodaw Pagoda in Mandalay, sometimes called "the world's largest book". The Pali Text Society, founded in 1881, produced the standard roman-script edition and many English translations.
