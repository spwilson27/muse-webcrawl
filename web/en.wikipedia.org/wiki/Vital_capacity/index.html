Vital capacity

Vital capacity (VC) is the maximum amount of air a person can expel from the lungs after a maximum inhalation. It equals the sum of the inspiratory reserve volume, tidal volume, and expiratory reserve volume, and is approximately 80% of total lung capacity.

Measurement

Vital capacity is measured with spirometry. Forced vital capacity (FVC) is measured during a forceful exhalation and, together with FEV1, helps distinguish obstructive from restrictive lung diseases. Typical adult values are roughly 3–5 litres, varying with height, age, sex and fitness.

Clinical significance

Reduced vital capacity occurs in restrictive disorders such as pulmonary fibrosis and in neuromuscular weakness. John Hutchinson invented the spirometer in the 1840s and introduced the term "vital capacity", believing it predicted longevity — an association modern studies partly support as a marker of overall health.
