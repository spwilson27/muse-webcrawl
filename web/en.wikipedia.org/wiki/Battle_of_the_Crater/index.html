The Battle of the Crater took place during the American Civil War on 30 July 1864, between the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia, commanded by Robert E. Lee, and the Union Army of the Potomac, commanded by George G. Meade under the overall direction of Ulysses S. Grant. Union soldiers dug a mine beneath the Confederate lines east of Petersburg, Virginia, and filled it with gunpowder; its explosion blew a huge crater in the Confederate defences, but the following assault degenerated into a costly failure.

The Crater at Petersburg, the scar left by the mine explosion of 30 July 1864.

The mine

The idea came from Lieutenant Colonel Henry Pleasants of the 48th Pennsylvania Infantry, many of whose men were coal miners. His regiment dug a tunnel over 500 feet (150 m) long beneath the Confederate salient, ending in a gallery packed with 8,000 pounds (3,600 kg) of gunpowder. Brigadier General Edward Ferrero's division of United States Colored Troops trained specifically to lead the assault around the crater's edges.

The assault

At the last moment, Meade and Grant replaced Ferrero's trained division with an unprepared white division under Brigadier General James Ledlie, fearing the political consequences if Black troops were sacrificed in a failed attack. When the mine exploded at 4:44 a.m. on 30 July, it killed some 300 Confederate soldiers instantly and blasted a crater roughly 170 feet long and 30 feet deep. But Ledlie's men, lacking ladders and instruction, charged into the crater itself and became trapped. Ferrero's USCT division went in later and was also caught. Confederate brigades under William Mahone counter-attacked and sealed the breach by early afternoon. Union casualties were about 3,800, Confederate about 1,500; many Black Union soldiers were killed after surrendering.

Aftermath

The battle was a severe Union setback that prolonged the siege of Petersburg by eight months. Burnside was relieved of command and later resigned; a court of inquiry censured several officers. The crater remains preserved today within Petersburg National Battlefield.
