Nihon-shiki romanization

Nihon-shiki (Japanese: 日本式, "Japan style") is a system for romanising the Japanese language, invented by the physicist Aikitsu Tanakadate in 1885. Unlike the later Hepburn system, Nihon-shiki follows the kana syllabary strictly, preserving the regular structure of the kana tables rather than approximating English pronunciation.

Features

Thus し is written si (not shi), ち is ti, つ is tu, ふ is hu, じ is zi, and づ and ぢ are du and di, keeping a one-to-one correspondence with kana. Long vowels are marked with a circumflex (e.g. ô).

Use

Nihon-shiki was a source for the later Kunrei-shiki system adopted by the Japanese government in 1937 and for ISO 3602. It is rarely used today outside linguistic contexts, Hepburn being dominant internationally and Kunrei-shiki in Japanese schools, but Tanakadate's design remains the most regular of the major systems.
