Nandan Mohan Nilekani (born 2 June 1955) is an Indian entrepreneur, technocrat, and author. He co-founded Infosys and served as its chief executive officer from 2002 to 2007; he returned to the company as non-executive chairman in August 2017. He was the founding chairman of the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI), in the rank of a cabinet minister, which created Aadhaar, the world's largest biometric identity programme.

Early life and career

Nilekani was born in Bangalore and educated at Bishop Cotton Boys' School and St Joseph's; he graduated in electrical engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay in 1978. He began his career at Patni Computer Systems in Mumbai, where he met N. R. Narayana Murthy. In 1981 he and six colleagues co-founded Infosys with initial capital of US$250; it grew into one of India's largest IT services companies. He succeeded Murthy as CEO in 2002, presiding over rapid global expansion, and served as co-chairman from 2007 to 2009.

Public service

In July 2009, Nilekani was invited by Prime Minister Manmohan Singh to head the newly created UIDAI. Under his leadership, the Aadhaar programme enrolled over a billion residents, issuing each a 12-digit unique identity number linked to biometric data, and became foundational digital public infrastructure for banking, welfare delivery, and later the India Stack (including UPI payments).

Nilekani has also chaired government committees on IT and digital payments, co-founded the EkStep Foundation for free learning tools, and mentors open digital-network initiatives such as the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) and the beckn protocol. He contested the 2014 Indian general election from Bangalore South as a Congress candidate and lost.

Writing and philanthropy

He is the author of Imagining India: The Idea of a Renewed Nation (2008) and co-author, with Viral Shah, of Rebooting India (2015), and with Tanuj Bhojwani, of The Art of Bitfulness (2022). Thomas Friedman credited Nilekani's phrase "the world is flat" as inspiration for his best-selling book of the same name. With his wife Rohini Nilekani, he is a major philanthropist, has signed the Giving Pledge (2017), and supports causes including education, water, and civil society through Rohini Nilekani Philanthropies and Arghyam.
