Minkowski's theorem

In mathematics, Minkowski's theorem is the statement that every convex set in Rn which is symmetric with respect to the origin and which has volume greater than 2n times the volume of the fundamental cell of a lattice contains a non-zero lattice point in addition to the origin. The theorem was proved by Hermann Minkowski in 1889 and became the foundation of the geometry of numbers, a branch of number theory he created.

Statement and proof idea

More precisely, let L be a lattice in Rn with determinant d(L), and let S be a measurable set symmetric about the origin (S = −S) and convex. If vol(S) > 2n d(L), then S contains a point of L other than 0. The standard proof considers the scaled set (1/2)S; because its volume exceeds d(L), two of its translates by lattice points must overlap, and the difference of the two overlapping points, combined with symmetry and convexity, produces the desired lattice point in S.

Applications

Minkowski's theorem and its refinements, such as Minkowski's theorem on linear forms, are used to prove results in Diophantine approximation and algebraic number theory, including the finiteness of the class number, Dirichlet's unit theorem, and Lagrange's four-square theorem (via the geometry of numbers). It is also a key tool in the theory of sphere packings and lattice-based cryptanalysis.
