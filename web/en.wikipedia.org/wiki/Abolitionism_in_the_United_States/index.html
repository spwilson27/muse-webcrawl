Abolitionism in the United States

Abolitionism in the United States was the movement to end slavery, active from the colonial era until the ratification of the Thirteenth Amendment in 1865 abolished slavery nationwide.


Period: c. 1688 – 1865
Key figures: William Lloyd Garrison, Frederick Douglass, Harriet Tubman, Harriet Beecher Stowe

History

Early opposition came from Quakers and Enlightenment thinkers; northern states abolished slavery gradually after the Revolution. From the 1830s William Lloyd Garrison's The Liberator and the American Anti-Slavery Society demanded immediate emancipation. Formerly enslaved speakers and writers such as Frederick Douglass and Sojourner Truth, the Underground Railroad, and Harriet Beecher Stowe's Uncle Tom's Cabin (1852) galvanised opinion, while John Brown's 1859 raid on Harpers Ferry heighted tensions.

Achievement

The Civil War made emancipation a Union war aim: the Emancipation Proclamation (1863) freed enslaved people in rebellious states, Black soldiers served in large numbers, and the Thirteenth Amendment completed abolition in December 1865.
