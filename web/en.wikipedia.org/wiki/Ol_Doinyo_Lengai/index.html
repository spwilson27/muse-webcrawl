Ol Doinyo Lengai is an active volcano in the Gregory Rift of northern Tanzania, south of Lake Natron. Its name means "Mountain of God" in the Maasai language, and it remains sacred to the Maasai people. It is the only volcano in the world known to erupt natrocarbonatite lava — a carbonate lava so fluid and cool (around 500–600 °C, glowing only faintly at night) that it flows almost like water, appears black in daylight, and weathers within days to a white, powdery surface.

Geology and eruptions

The cone, about 2,962 metres high, has two craters; activity is centred in the northern crater, where small cones (hornitos) extrude carbonatite flows. Larger explosive episodes, involving silicate ash, interrupted the quiet effusive activity in 1917, 1940, 1966 and dramatically in 2007–2008, when a new phase of explosive eruptions displaced nearby Maasai communities and temporarily closed the mountain to climbers. The 1960 eruption was famously visited by geologists who first documented the extraordinary lava chemistry; subsequent sampling confirmed the lava's composition of nyerereite and gregoryite, minerals named for Julius Nyerere and John Walter Gregory.

Setting

Ol Doinyo Lengai overlooks the soda flats of Lake Natron, a major breeding site for lesser flamingos, and forms part of the geologically young volcanic province of the East African Rift that includes the nearby highlands of Ngorongoro. The steep, ash-covered cone is a popular but demanding climb, usually made overnight to reach the summit at dawn.
