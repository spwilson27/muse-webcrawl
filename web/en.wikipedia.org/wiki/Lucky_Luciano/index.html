Charles "Lucky" Luciano (born Salvatore Lucania; 24 November 1897 – 26 January 1962) was an Italian-born American crime boss who operated mainly in the United States. He is considered the father of modern organised crime in the United States for establishing the Commission and restructuring the Italian-American Mafia after the Castellammarese War.

Early life

Born in Sicily, Lucania emigrated with his family to New York City in 1906, settling on the Lower East Side. He was drawn into crime as a teenager, joining the Five Points Gang, and during Prohibition built a lucrative bootlegging empire in partnership with Jewish gangsters Meyer Lansky and Bugsy Siegel and Italian associates such as Frank Costello and Vito Genovese. He earned the nickname "Lucky" in part after surviving a savage beating and throat-slashing in 1929.

Rise to power

Working for boss Joe Masseria, Luciano concluded that the old-guard "Mustache Petes" were holding back the organisation. In 1931 he arranged the murder of Masseria, then of Salvatore Maranzano, ending the Castellammarese War. Luciano then abolished the title of capo di tutti capi and created the Commission — a governing board of the Five Families of New York together with representatives from other cities — and divided New York's rackets among the Five Families.

Conviction and deportation

In 1936 special prosecutor Thomas E. Dewey convicted Luciano of running a prostitution empire; he was sentenced to 30 to 50 years. From prison, Luciano — through acting boss Vito Genovese and later Frank Costello — maintained influence. During the Second World War he cooperated with US naval intelligence ("Operation Underworld"), and in 1946 his sentence was commuted on condition of deportation to Italy, where he lived for the rest of his life, remaining a major figure in the international narcotics trade. He died of a heart attack at Naples airport in 1962.

Legacy

Luciano's organisational model — criminal enterprise structured like a corporation, multi-ethnic cooperation, and arbitration through the Commission — shaped the American Mafia for the rest of the twentieth century. Time magazine named him among the most influential businessmen of the century.
