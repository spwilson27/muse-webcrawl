Water hyacinth

Scientific namePontederia crassipes (syn. Eichhornia crassipes)
FamilyPontederiaceae
Native rangeAmazon basin, South America

Water hyacinth (Pontederia crassipes, commonly cited as Eichhornia crassipes) is a free-floating perennial aquatic plant native to the Amazon basin. It has glossy, rounded leaves on spongy, bulbous stalks and showy lavender flowers with a yellow eye. Introduced around the world as an ornamental, it has become one of the world's most troublesome invasive aquatic weeds.

Invasion and impacts

Under favourable conditions water hyacinth is among the fastest-growing plants known, doubling its population in a matter of weeks. Dense mats choke lakes, rivers and canals, blocking navigation and fishing, reducing oxygen for aquatic life, impeding hydroelectric intakes and increasing water loss by evapotranspiration. Major infestations have affected Lake Victoria in Africa, waterways in the southern United States, and wetlands across Asia and Australia.

Control and uses

Control combines mechanical harvesting, herbicides and biological control with host-specific weevils (Neochetina species) and other insects, which have achieved notable successes. The plant also has beneficial uses: it absorbs nutrients and heavy metals and is used in wastewater treatment, biogas production, animal fodder, compost and woven handicrafts.
