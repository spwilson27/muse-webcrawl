Gospel of Mark

The Gospel of Mark is the second of the four canonical gospels and one of the three synoptic gospels of the New Testament. It narrates the ministry of Jesus from his baptism by John the Baptist to his crucifixion, burial and the empty tomb. Most scholars date it to around AD 70, making it likely the earliest of the four gospels, and hold that Matthew and Luke used it as a source (Markan priority). Church tradition attributes it to John Mark, companion of Peter; its author is formally anonymous.

Contents

Mark begins abruptly with Jesus' baptism and temptation — there is no birth narrative — and proceeds in breathless succession ("immediately" is a favourite word) through exorcisms, healings, controversies and parables in Galilee. From chapter 8 the narrative turns toward Jerusalem: Peter's confession, the predictions of the Passion, the Transfiguration, teachings on discipleship, the entry into Jerusalem, the Last Supper, Gethsemane, the trial and the crucifixion. Mark emphasises Jesus as the suffering Son of God and the "Messianic secret" — Jesus' repeated commands to silence about his identity.

The ending

The earliest manuscripts end at 16:8 with the women fleeing the empty tomb in fear, "and they said nothing to anyone". The familiar "longer ending" (16:9–20), including appearances of the risen Jesus and the command to preach to all creation, is regarded by most scholars as a later addition; a "shorter ending" also circulates in some manuscripts.

Authorship and sources

Papias (early 2nd century) reports that Mark was Peter's interpreter and wrote accurately, though not in order. The gospel's rough Greek, explanations of Aramaic phrases and Jewish customs, and Latinisms suggest composition for a Gentile audience, possibly in Rome. Under the two-source hypothesis, Mark and a lost sayings source (Q) underlie Matthew and Luke.
