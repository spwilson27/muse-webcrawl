Allied invasion of Italy

Date3–16 September 1943 (mainland landings)
LocationSalerno, Calabria and Taranto, Italy
ResultAllied victory; Italian armistice; German occupation of northern and central Italy
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom, United States, Canada vs. Germany (and Italy until 8 September)
CommandersHarold Alexander, Mark Clark, Bernard Montgomery vs. Albert Kesselring, Heinrich von Vietinghoff

The Allied invasion of Italy was the Allied amphibious landing on mainland Italy during the Second World War, beginning on 3 September 1943. Following the conquest of Sicily, the invasion aimed to knock Italy out of the war, divert German forces from France and the Eastern Front, and gain airfields within range of southern Germany.

Operations

The operation comprised three landings: Operation Baytown (British Eighth Army across the Strait of Messina into Calabria, 3 September), Operation Slapstick (an airborne-naval seizure of Taranto, 9 September), and the main effort, Operation Avalanche — the US Fifth Army's landing at Salerno on 9 September, which met fierce German counterattacks and was nearly driven into the sea before naval gunfire and reinforcements secured the beachhead.

Consequences

Italy signed the Armistice of Cassibile, announced on 8 September 1943; Germany responded with Operation Achse, disarming Italian forces and occupying the peninsula north of the Allied beachheads. The invasion opened the long, grinding Italian Campaign, in which Allied forces fought their way north through successive German defensive lines — including the Gustav Line at Monte Cassino — until May 1945.
