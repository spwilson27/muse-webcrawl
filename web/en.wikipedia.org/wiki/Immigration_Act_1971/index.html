Immigration Act 1971

Immigration Act 1971
Act of Parliament (United Kingdom)
Citation: 1971 c. 77
Royal assent: 28 October 1971
Commenced: 1 January 1973

The Immigration Act 1971 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that, as amended, remains the main statute governing immigration control in the United Kingdom. It came into force on 1 January 1973, replacing the Commonwealth Immigrants Acts of 1962 and 1968 and the Immigration Appeals Act 1969.

Provisions

The Act introduced the concept of patriality: patrials — British citizens and certain Commonwealth citizens with a parent born in the UK — had the right of abode, while non-patrials required leave to enter or remain. It unified controls on Commonwealth and foreign nationals, gave immigration officers powers of examination and refusal, and provided for deportation.

Later law

The British Nationality Act 1981 replaced patriality with citizenship categories, but the 1971 Act's framework of leave, conditions, removal and offences still structures UK immigration law, heavily amended by later Acts.
