Hard rock (or heavy rock) is a loosely defined subgenre of rock music characterised by a heavy use of aggressive vocals, distorted electric guitars, bass guitar and drums, sometimes accompanied by keyboards. It began in the mid-1960s with the garage, blues rock and psychedelic rock movements, and reached mass popularity in the 1970s.

Characteristics

Hard rock is rooted in blues structures but played louder, faster and with thicker guitar tones built on power chords and prominent riffs. Songs often feature extended guitar solos, emphatic choruses designed for arena performance, and lyrics concerning rebellion, romance and fantasy. The boundary with heavy metal is porous: bands such as Led Zeppelin, Deep Purple and Black Sabbath are claimed by both traditions, with "hard rock" usually denoting the more blues- and melody-centred side of the spectrum.

History

Precursors in the 1960s included the Kinks' "You Really Got Me" (1964), the Who, Cream, the Jimi Hendrix Experience and Jeff Beck. Around 1969–1970 the classic hard rock sound crystallised in Led Zeppelin's first albums, Deep Purple's In Rock, and the work of Free, Uriah Heep and Grand Funk Railroad. Through the 1970s Aerosmith, Kiss, AC/DC, Thin Lizzy, Rainbow and Queen carried the style to stadium scale, while Van Halen (from 1978) revitalised guitar technique. In the 1980s hard rock merged with glam metal in the Los Angeles scene (Mötley Crüe, Guns N' Roses), before the rise of grunge and alternative rock in the early 1990s pushed it from the mainstream. Hard rock acts remain among the highest-grossing live performers in the world.
