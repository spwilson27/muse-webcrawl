De Havilland Canada Dash 8

Dash 8 / Q-Series
Role: Turboprop regional airliner
Manufacturer: de Havilland Canada; Bombardier
First flight: 20 June 1983
Status: In service

The De Havilland Canada Dash 8, also marketed as the DHC-8 and later as the Q Series, is a family of twin-engine, medium-range turboprop regional airliners developed by de Havilland Canada and produced for many years by Bombardier Aerospace. Introduced in 1984, the type became one of the most successful regional turboprops in the world.

Variants

The family grew through four main series: the Series 100 (37–40 seats), the Series 200 with more powerful engines, the stretched Series 300 (50–56 seats), and the further stretched, faster Series 400, marketed as the Q400 (68–90 seats), which introduced an active noise and vibration suppression system — hence the "Q" for "quiet". The Q400 cruises at about 667 km/h (414 mph), unusually fast for a turboprop, and is widely used by airlines such as Air Canada Express, Flybe (historically), Horizon Air and SpiceJet.

Production history

Bombardier sold the Dash 8 programme in 2019 to Longview Aviation Capital, which revived the de Havilland Canada name; Q400 production at Downsview, Toronto was paused in 2021 after more than 1,200 Dash 8s of all variants had been delivered. The type also serves in military, coast guard, firefighting and special-mission roles.
