Stanley Kubrick

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Star Gate sequence from 2001: A Space Odyssey: a corridor of streaming coloured light reflected in an astronaut's visor]

Stanley Kubrick (26 July 1928 – 7 March 1999) was an American film director, producer, screenwriter and photographer. Widely considered one of the greatest filmmakers of all time, his films — nearly all adaptations of novels or short stories — cover a wide range of genres and are noted for their innovative cinematography, extensive set design, dark humour, realistic attention to detail and expressive use of music.

From the war film Paths of Glory (1957) and the epic Spartacus (1960), Kubrick's reputation rests on a string of singular works: Dr. Strangelove (1964), 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), A Clockwork Orange (1971), Barry Lyndon (1975), The Shining (1980) and Full Metal Jacket (1987). A notorious perfectionist based in England, he died shortly after completing Eyes Wide Shut (1999).
