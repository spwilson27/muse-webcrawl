Tanna is an island in Tafea Province, the southernmost province of Vanuatu in the South Pacific. It is about 40 km long and 19 km wide, with the administrative centre Isangel on the west coast and the main town Lenakel nearby. Tanna is famous for Mount Yasur, one of the world's most accessible active volcanoes, which has been in near-continuous eruption for centuries and can be viewed from its crater rim.

History and culture

The island was sighted by Captain James Cook in 1774, who was guided to it by the glow of Mount Yasur and named the anchorage Port Resolution. Tanna became a focus of Presbyterian missionary activity in the 19th century; the missionary John Gibson Paton wrote a famous account of his years there. In the 20th century the island gave rise to notable cargo movements: the John Frum movement, centred on the village of Lamakara near Sulphur Bay, expects the return of the figure John Frum with cargo and maintains an associated movement that reveres Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, as a divine figure connected with the island — a belief that drew international attention after the prince's death in 2021.

Geography and life

Most Tannese live in traditional villages, speaking five indigenous languages (including North Tanna, Lenakel, Whitesands, Southwest Tanna and Kwamera) alongside Bislama, and many follow kastom — traditional custom — including the villages of the "Kastom" people who reject modern goods. Coffee, copra, kava and vegetables are the main cash products; coffee from Tanna is exported under its own brand. Giant banyan trees, blue holes and black-sand beaches draw visitors, alongside volcano tours. Tanna was devastated by Cyclone Pam in March 2015, which destroyed most housing and crops, but rebuilt with its social structures largely intact.
