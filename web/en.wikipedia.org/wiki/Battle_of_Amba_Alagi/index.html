Battle of Amba Alagi

The name Battle of Amba Alagi refers to several engagements fought around the mountain of Amba Alagi in northern Ethiopia, most notably in 1895 during the First Italo-Ethiopian War and in 1941 during the East African campaign of the Second World War.

LocationAmba Alagi, Tigray, Ethiopia
Battles1895 (First Italo-Ethiopian War); 1941 (Second World War)

1895

In December 1895, Ethiopian forces of Emperor Menelik II, including Ras Makonnen's army, attacked the Italian fort at Amba Alagi held by Major Pietro Toselli. Hopelessly outnumbered and refused permission to retreat until too late, Toselli and some 2,000 Italian and askari troops were killed. The defeat shocked Italy, triggered the fall of Prime Minister Crispi's government after the subsequent disaster at Adwa in March 1896, and secured Ethiopian independence.

1941

In May 1941, during the liberation of Italian East Africa, a multinational Allied force — British, South African, Indian, and Ethiopian patriot units — besieged the mountain stronghold held by Italian forces under Prince Amedeo, Duke of Aosta, the Italian viceroy. After fierce fighting and dwindling water, Amedeo surrendered with the honours of war on 19 May 1941, a major step toward the restoration of Emperor Haile Selassie. See also Italo-Ethiopian wars.
