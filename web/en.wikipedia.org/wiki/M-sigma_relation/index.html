M–sigma relation

The M–sigma relation is an empirical correlation between the stellar velocity dispersion (sigma) of a galaxy bulge and the mass (M) of the supermassive black hole at its centre. It is one of the tightest known correlations between black holes and their host galaxies.

M–sigma relation
Field: Astrophysics
Discovered: 2000
Key quantities: Black hole mass, bulge velocity dispersion

Discovery

The relation was reported independently in 2000 by Karl Gebhardt and collaborators and by Laura Ferrarese and David Merritt. It implies that black hole growth and galaxy formation are closely linked, probably through feedback processes.

Form

The relation is usually written as a power law: the black hole mass scales roughly as the fourth power of the velocity dispersion, M ∝ sigma^4, with relatively small intrinsic scatter.
