The Philadelphia Eagles are a professional American football team based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the NFC East division and play their home games at Lincoln Financial Field. The franchise was established in 1933, replacing the bankrupt Frankford Yellow Jackets, and took its name from the eagle symbol of the National Recovery Administration.

History

The Eagles won three NFL championships in the pre-Super Bowl era (1948, 1949 and 1960) — the 1960 team, led by quarterback Norm Van Brocklin and linebacker Chuck Bednarik, famously handed Vince Lombardi's Green Bay Packers their only postseason defeat. During the Second World War the Eagles temporarily merged with the Pittsburgh Steelers as the "Steagles" in 1943 because of player shortages.

The team reached Super Bowl XV in the 1980 season under coach Dick Vermeil, losing to the Oakland Raiders, and Super Bowl XXXIX in 2004 under Andy Reid, losing to the New England Patriots. In the 2017 season, under head coach Doug Pederson and with backup quarterback Nick Foles starting after an injury to Carson Wentz, the Eagles defeated the Patriots 41–33 in Super Bowl LII to win their first Super Bowl title; the game featured the famous "Philly Special" trick play. Foles was named the game's MVP.

Under head coach Nick Sirianni the Eagles returned to the Super Bowl in the 2022 season, losing Super Bowl LVII to the Kansas City Chiefs, and then defeated the Chiefs 40–22 in Super Bowl LIX in February 2025 to win their second Super Bowl championship, with quarterback Jalen Hurts named MVP.

Rivalries and culture

The Eagles have intense rivalries with the Dallas Cowboys, New York Giants and Washington Commanders. The fan base is known for its loyalty and intensity; the fight song "Fly, Eagles Fly", the E-A-G-L-E-S chant and mascot Swoop are staples of home games.
