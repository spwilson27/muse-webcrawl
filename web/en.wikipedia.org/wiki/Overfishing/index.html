Overfishing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Overfishing is the removal of fish and other marine animals from a body of water at rates too high for the species to replenish themselves, depleting stocks and disrupting ecosystems. By the UN Food and Agriculture Organization's assessments, roughly a third of assessed fish stocks are now fished beyond sustainable limits — up from about a tenth in the 1970s.

Causes and consequences

Drivers include industrial fishing technology (sonar, factory trawlers, vast nets), subsidies that keep excess fleets at sea, open-access 'tragedy of the commons' dynamics, and illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing. Consequences include collapsed fisheries — most famously Newfoundland's cod, closed in 1992 — 'fishing down the food web' to smaller species, bycatch of turtles, seabirds and dolphins, and habitat damage from bottom trawling.

Management

Remedies include science-based catch quotas, marine reserves, gear restrictions, individual transferable quotas, and international agreements for high-seas stocks; where well enforced, stocks such as some US and Icelandic fisheries have rebuilt. Aquaculture now supplies more than half of fish eaten by humans, easing some pressure while creating its own environmental demands.
