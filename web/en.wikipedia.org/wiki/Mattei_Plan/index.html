Mattei Plan
Italian development initiative for Africa
Announced: 2023–2024 under Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni

The Mattei Plan (Italian: Piano Mattei) is a strategic initiative of the Italian government aimed at building a new partnership with African countries based on cooperation in energy, development, agriculture, health, education and water resources. Named after Enrico Mattei, the founder of the energy company Eni who advocated fairer resource partnerships in the 1950s, the plan was presented by Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, including at the Italy–Africa Summit held in Rome in January 2024.

Background

Enrico Mattei, the post-war head of Eni, built a reputation for offering oil-producing countries more favourable terms than the dominant Western oil majors, an approach sometimes described as the "Mattei formula." The modern plan invokes that legacy in seeking a relationship presented as equal and non-predatory.

Objectives and projects

The plan involves pilot cooperation with a group of African nations including Algeria, Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Mozambique, the Republic of the Congo, Angola, Ivory Coast and Morocco. Projects span energy security and renewables, vocational training, agricultural development and infrastructure, and are linked in Italian policy discourse to addressing the root causes of migration across the Mediterranean.

Reception

African leaders welcomed dialogue but some observers, including African Union officials, noted limited consultation in the plan's preparation and called for concrete follow-through. Critics in Italy questioned its funding scale and the balance between energy interests and development goals.
