AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is a royalty-free image file format that stores still and animated images compressed with the AV1 video codec in a HEIF container. Developed by the Alliance for Open Media, AVIF offers substantially better compression than JPEG at equivalent quality, and supports features JPEG lacks, including high dynamic range, wide colour gamut, 10- and 12-bit colour depth, alpha transparency, film grain synthesis and image sequences.

Adoption

Support arrived in Chrome and Firefox in 2021 and in Safari in 2022, making AVIF usable across all major browsers. Streaming and image-heavy services such as Netflix, which helped develop the format, use AVIF to reduce bandwidth. Encoding is more computationally expensive than JPEG, though encoder speed has improved considerably with the libaom and SVT-AV1 encoders.

Comparison

AVIF competes with JPEG XL and WebP. It typically produces smaller files than WebP for photographs and gains AV1's mature tooling, but JPEG XL offers lossless JPEG recompression and progressive decoding advantages that AVIF lacks.
