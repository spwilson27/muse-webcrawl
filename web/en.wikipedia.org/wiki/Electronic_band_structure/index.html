Electronic band structure

Electronic band structure
FieldSolid-state physics, condensed matter
Key conceptsValence band, conduction band, band gap, Fermi level

In solid-state physics, the electronic band structure (or simply band structure) of a solid describes the range of energy levels that electrons may have within it (energy bands), as well as the ranges they may not have (band gaps).

Origin

Because electrons in a crystal move in a periodic potential, Bloch's theorem shows their wavefunctions are modulated plane waves; solving the Schrödinger equation across the Brillouin zone yields allowed bands separated by gaps. Models range from the nearly free electron and tight-binding approximations to modern density functional theory calculations.

Classification of solids

Band filling determines electrical behaviour: conductors have partially filled bands or overlapping bands; semiconductors have a small band gap (e.g., silicon ~1.1 eV); insulators have a large gap preventing thermal excitation across it.

Features

Important quantities include direct vs. indirect gaps, effective mass near band extrema, density of states, and the Fermi surface in metals. Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) measures band structures directly.
