The FIS Nordic World Ski Championships are a biennial sporting event organised by the International Ski Federation (FIS) covering the Nordic disciplines of cross-country skiing, ski jumping and Nordic combined. They have been held in various formats since 1925 and, in their modern form, take place every two years in odd-numbered years, avoiding the Winter Olympic years.

History

The first championships were held in Janské Lázně, Czechoslovakia, in 1925. Early editions were held annually and were sometimes part of the Winter Olympics, with Olympic results counting as world championship results until 1984. Women's events were added progressively, beginning with women's cross-country skiing in 1954 and women's ski jumping in 2009.

Events

The modern programme includes individual and team cross-country races (including sprint and skiathlon formats), ski jumping on normal and large hills (including a mixed team event), and Nordic combined individual and team competitions.

Medal history

Norway is by far the most successful nation in the history of the championships, dominating the overall medal table, followed by Finland, Sweden, Russia/the Soviet Union and Germany.
