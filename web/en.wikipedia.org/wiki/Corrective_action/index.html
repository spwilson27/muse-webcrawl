Corrective action

A corrective action is a change implemented to address a weakness identified in a management system or to eliminate the cause of a detected nonconformity or other undesirable situation, in order to prevent its recurrence. The term is central to quality management standards such as ISO 9001 and to safety and regulatory regimes.

Overview

Field: Quality management

Related: Preventive action, CAPA, root cause analysis

Process

A typical corrective-action process involves identifying and documenting the nonconformity, investigating its root cause (using tools such as the five whys or fishbone diagrams), determining and implementing action to eliminate that cause, and reviewing the effectiveness of the action taken. Corrective action differs from a simple correction, which fixes the immediate problem without addressing its cause, and from preventive action, which addresses potential problems before they occur.

CAPA systems

In regulated industries such as pharmaceuticals and medical devices, formal Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) systems are required by regulators such as the US Food and Drug Administration, and deficiencies in CAPA are among the most common findings in regulatory inspections.
