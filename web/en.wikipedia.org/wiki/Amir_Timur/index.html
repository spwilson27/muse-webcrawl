Overview

Timur (1336–1405), historically known as Amir Timur and commonly as Tamerlane in English, was a Turco-Mongol conqueror who founded the Timurid Empire in and around modern-day Iran, Iraq and Central Asia, becoming the most powerful ruler in the Muslim world of his time. Between 1382 and 1405 his armies — undefeated in battle — conquered Persia, Mesopotamia, the Caucasus, much of Anatolia (crushing the Ottomans at Ankara in 1402 and capturing Sultan Bayezid I) and northern India (sacking Delhi in 1398).

Rule and legacy

Timur ruled from Samarkand, which he adorned with the spoils and craftsmen of his conquests, founding a Persianate renaissance. His campaigns killed millions — estimates vary enormously — and pyramids of skulls marked cities that resisted, as at Isfahan and Baghdad; yet within his empire, trade and the arts flourished. He died at Otrar in 1405 preparing to invade Ming China. His tomb, the Gur-e-Amir in Samarkand, bears the famous (likely legendary) curse on disturbers; its exhumation by Soviet anthropologists in June 1941 is folklore-linked to the German invasion days later. Timur's descendant Babur founded the Mughal Empire.
