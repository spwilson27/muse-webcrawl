Achernar is the brightest star in the constellation Eridanus and the ninth-brightest star in the night sky. It has the Bayer designation Alpha Eridani (α Eridani). Its name derives from the Arabic ākhir an-nahr, "the end of the river," reflecting its position at the southern terminus of the celestial river Eridanus.

Achernar is one of the flattest stars known, owing to its extremely rapid rotation.

Properties

Achernar is a binary system: the primary is a hot, blue main-sequence Be star, and the companion is a smaller A-type star in an orbit of roughly 7–15 years. The primary rotates exceptionally fast — near its critical breakup velocity — which has distorted it into a pronounced oblate spheroid. Interferometric observations by the Very Large Telescope in 2003 showed its equatorial diameter to be more than 50% greater than its polar diameter, the most extreme flattening then measured for any star. Its equatorial regions are correspondingly cooler and dimmer than its poles (gravity darkening).

Observability

With a declination of about −57°, Achernar is a far-southern star, circumpolar for much of the southern temperate zone and never visible from latitudes north of about +33°. Because of precession, it lay farther south in antiquity and was not routinely visible to classical Mediterranean astronomers.
