Computerworld

Computerworld is an American digital magazine and website about information technology and business technology, historically published as a weekly print newspaper for IT professionals. It was founded in 1967 by Patrick J. McGovern, and was one of the flagship publications of International Data Group (IDG). At its peak, Computerworld had editions in dozens of countries around the world, many published by local IDG subsidiaries or licensees.

History

The first issue appeared on 21 June 1967. Computerworld targeted IT management and enterprise computing, covering mainframes, minicomputers, and later PCs, networking, and the internet, and was noted for industry reporting and investigative stories. In 2014, the US print edition ceased and Computerworld became a digital-only publication. Following the sale of IDG's media business, the brand came under Foundry (formerly IDG Communications).

Content and awards

The publication produces news, analysis, and research for technology decision-makers, and is known for programmes such as the "Best Places to Work in IT" list and the Computerworld Honors Program, which recognised innovative uses of technology. Sister publications included InfoWorld, Network World, CIO, and PC World.
