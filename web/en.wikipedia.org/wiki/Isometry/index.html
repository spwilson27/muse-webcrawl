Isometry

Isometry

Field: Geometry, functional analysis

Examples: Translations, rotations, reflections

Defining property: Preserves distances

In mathematics, an isometry is a distance-preserving transformation between metric spaces, usually assumed to be bijective. Isometries are the formalization of the intuitive notion of a rigid motion or congruence: applying an isometry changes a figure's position or orientation but not its size or shape.

Examples and classification

In the Euclidean plane, every isometry is a translation, rotation, reflection, or glide reflection. In Euclidean space of any dimension, isometries form the Euclidean group E(n), generated by translations and orthogonal transformations. The Mazur–Ulam theorem states that every surjective isometry between real normed spaces is affine.

Isometries in other contexts

In Riemannian geometry, an isometry is a diffeomorphism preserving the metric tensor; the isometry group of a Riemannian manifold is a Lie group. In functional analysis, linear isometries (such as unitary operators on Hilbert spaces) preserve inner products and norms, and play a central role in operator theory.
