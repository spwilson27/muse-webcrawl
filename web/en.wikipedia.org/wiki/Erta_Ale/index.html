Erta Ale

Erta Ale (or Ertale or Irta'ale) is a continuously active basaltic shield volcano in the Afar Region of northeastern Ethiopia, in the Danakil Depression. It is famed for one of the world's few persistent lava lakes, present since at least 1906 and possibly 1873. Its name means "smoking mountain" in the Afar language.

Geology

Erta Ale lies on the Erta Ale Range along the rifting axis where the African Plate is splitting — part of the East African Rift system descending to 125 m below sea level in the Danakil Depression, one of Earth's hottest places. The broad 50 km-wide shield has a summit caldera containing pit craters; the lava lake churns with fountaining and migrating crustal plates resembling miniature plate tectonics.

Eruptions and access

A major fissure eruption in January 2017 drained and refilled the lake and sent lava flows down the flanks. Reaching Erta Ale requires a trek, usually at night, from the Danakil with Afar guides and, historically, armed escorts due to regional insecurity; a 2012 attack killed tourists and scientists.
