Yuri Tsolakovich Oganessian (born 14 April 1933) is a Russian nuclear physicist of Armenian descent, regarded as the world's leading researcher on superheavy chemical elements. As scientific leader of the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research (JINR) in Dubna, Russia, he led or co-led the teams that synthesised elements 114 through 118, completing the seventh period of the periodic table.

Career

Oganessian graduated from the Moscow Engineering Physics Institute in 1956 and joined JINR, working under Georgy Flyorov. In the 1970s he pioneered the "cold fusion" method of producing new elements by bombarding lead and bismuth targets with medium-mass ions. Later, his laboratory developed the "hot fusion" approach using calcium-48 beams on actinide targets, in collaboration with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, which produced flerovium, moscovium, livermorium, tennessine, and oganesson between the late 1990s and 2010s.

Honours

In 2016, the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry named element 118 oganesson (symbol Og) in his honour, making him only the second person — after Glenn T. Seaborg — to have an element named after them during their lifetime. Oganessian is a full member of the Russian Academy of Sciences and has received numerous state and international awards.
