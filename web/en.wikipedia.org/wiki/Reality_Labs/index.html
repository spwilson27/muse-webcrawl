Reality Labs is a business and research unit of Meta Platforms (formerly Facebook, Inc.) that produces virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) hardware and software, including virtual reality headsets such as Quest, and metaverse platforms such as Horizon Worlds. It was formerly known as Oculus VR and later Facebook Reality Labs.

Reality Labs is the division of Meta charged with realising Mark Zuckerberg's vision of the metaverse, an immersive digital environment in which people can work, play and socialise through avatars. The division has operated at very large losses — tens of billions of dollars since 2020 — which Meta has attributed to long-term research and development investment.

History

Oculus VR was founded in 2012 by Palmer Luckey, Brendan Iribe and others, and launched the Oculus Rift through a successful Kickstarter campaign. Facebook acquired Oculus in 2014 for approximately US$2 billion. The hardware line was progressively rebranded: the Oculus Quest (2019) and Quest 2 (2020) became standalone best-sellers, and following Facebook's corporate rebrand to Meta in October 2021 the headsets became Meta Quest, with Quest 3 released in 2023.

In partnership with EssilorLuxottica, Reality Labs also produces Ray-Ban smart glasses, whose second generation (2023) added a camera, speakers and Meta AI integration and became a commercial success. Research projects include the Orion AR glasses prototype demonstrated in 2024, wrist-based neural interfaces, and Codec Avatars photorealistic telepresence.

Financial performance

Meta reports Reality Labs as a separate segment. Annual operating losses grew from about $6.6 billion in 2020 to more than $16 billion in 2023 and roughly $17.7 billion in 2024, making it one of the largest sustained corporate R&D bets in history. Meta executives have repeatedly stated the division is a long-term investment expected to become profitable as the metaverse ecosystem matures.
