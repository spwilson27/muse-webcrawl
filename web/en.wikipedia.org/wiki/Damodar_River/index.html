The Damodar River is a river flowing across the Indian states of Jharkhand and West Bengal. Rich in mineral resources, its valley is home to one of India's largest industrial belts and coalfields. The river was historically notorious for devastating floods in the plains of Bengal, earning it the name the "River of Sorrows" (or "Sorrow of Bengal").

Course

The Damodar rises on the Chota Nagpur Plateau near Chandwa in the Latehar district of Jharkhand, at an elevation of about 610 metres. It flows generally east-southeast for about 592 kilometres, passing the coal-mining cities of the Jharia and Raniganj fields, before joining the Hooghly River near the plains of West Bengal, not far from Kolkata.

Floods and the Damodar Valley Corporation

Catastrophic floods — notably in 1943 — devastated the lower valley. Modelled partly on the Tennessee Valley Authority, the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) was established in 1948 as independent India's first multipurpose river-valley project. Dams at Tilaiya (1953), Konar (1955), Maithon (1957) and Panchet (1959) provide flood moderation, irrigation, hydroelectric power and industrial water supply, though severe flooding has not been entirely eliminated.

Economy and environment

The Damodar valley, sometimes called the "Ruhr of India", contains major deposits of coal and mica and hosts steel plants at Bokaro and Durgapur, thermal power stations and heavy industry. This concentration has made the river one of the most polluted in India, with coal washery effluents and industrial discharge major problems, and large stretches described as biologically degraded.
