DVD-R

DVD-R is a DVD (Digital Versatile Disc) recordable format, developed by Pioneer in 1997 and released in the same year. It is supported by the DVD Forum. A DVD-R typically has a storage capacity of 4.7 GB. Newer dual-layer DVD-R DL discs hold 8.5 GB.

Data is written to the disc by a laser in the writer drive that alters the colour (optical properties) of an organic dye layer; the change is permanent, so DVD-R is a write once read many (WORM) medium.

Technical specifications

The original DVD-R discs had a capacity of 3.95 GB; the standard was soon expanded to 4.7 GB. DVD-R uses a wobble groove and land pre-pits for addressing, and a recording wavelength of about 650 nm (red laser). Writing speeds grew from 1× (1.385 MB/s) to 16× and beyond.

DVD-R for General and for Authoring

Two variants existed: "DVD-R for Authoring", intended for professional mastering and able to write Cutting Master Format data, and "DVD-R for General", cheaper and copy-protected in a way that prevents bit-for-bit duplication of CSS-encrypted commercial DVDs.

Comparison with rival formats

DVD-R's rival write-once format was DVD+R, promoted by the DVD+RW Alliance. DVD-R generally achieved wider compatibility with standalone DVD players, while DVD+R offered features such as lossless linking and bitsetting (book type) control. Dual-format drives eventually made the distinction moot for most users.
