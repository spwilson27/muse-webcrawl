The chimpanzee (Pan troglodytes), also simply known as the chimp, is a species of great ape native to the forests and savannahs of tropical Africa. Together with the bonobo, it is humans' closest living relative, sharing about 98.8% of DNA. It has a robust build, long arms, and a coat of coarse black hair, with bare face, fingers, palms, and soles.

Behaviour and society

Chimpanzees live in fission–fusion communities of some 15 to 150 individuals, led by an alpha male and structured by complex alliances, grooming networks, and dominance hierarchies. They are highly social, communicate with a rich repertoire of calls, gestures, and facial expressions, and maintain lifelong social bonds. Group hunting of monkeys, territorial patrols, and occasional lethal intergroup aggression have been documented since Jane Goodall's long-term research at Gombe Stream National Park, begun in 1960.

Intelligence and tool use

Chimps are accomplished tool users: they fish for termites with prepared sticks, crack nuts with stone hammers and anvils, use leaf sponges for drinking, and employ medicinal plants. They demonstrate problem-solving, planning, numerical abilities, and — in enculturated individuals — comprehension of human language symbols, though claims about their production of language remain debated.

Diet and ecology

Omnivorous frugivores, chimpanzees feed primarily on fruit, leaves, seeds, blossoms, and insects, supplemented by hunted mammals. They build sleeping nests in trees each night. Populations have declined sharply due to habitat loss, bushmeat hunting, and disease (including Ebola); all four subspecies are listed as Endangered or Critically Endangered.

Relation to humans

The chimpanzee and bonobo lineages diverged from the human lineage roughly 6–7 million years ago. Chimpanzees were used extensively in biomedical and behavioural research in the 20th century; many countries have since banned or ended invasive chimp research, and Ham the chimp famously flew on a suborbital NASA test flight in 1961.
