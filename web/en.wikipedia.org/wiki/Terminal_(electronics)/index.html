Terminal (electronics)

A terminal is the point at which a conductor from an electrical component, device or network comes to an end, providing a point of connection to external circuits. Terminals may be posts, lugs, pins, sockets, screw clamps or solder tags, and are commonly labelled by polarity (+/−) or function (input, output, ground).

Types

Batteries have two terminals (anode and cathode connections). Semiconductor devices expose terminals named by function — gate, source and drain in a field-effect transistor; base, collector and emitter in a bipolar transistor. Terminal blocks join wires in control panels; crimp terminals and spade connectors terminate stranded cable; ring terminals bolt to studs in automotive and power wiring.

Considerations

Good terminals minimise contact resistance, resist corrosion and loosening, and provide insulation against short circuits. Terminal design is standardised in many domains, from banana plugs and binding posts in laboratories to the blade terminals of car batteries.
