Artificial heart

A total artificial heart replaces both native ventricles

An artificial heart is a device that replaces the heart. Artificial hearts are typically used to bridge the time to heart transplantation, or to permanently replace the heart in the case that a heart transplant is impossible. A total artificial heart (TAH) replaces both ventricles and all four heart valves, and is distinct from a ventricular assist device (VAD), which supports only one ventricle.

History

The first artificial heart to be successfully implanted in a human was the Jarvik-7, designed by a team led by Robert Jarvik. On 2 December 1982, surgeon William DeVries implanted it into retired dentist Barney Clark at the University of Utah; Clark survived 112 days supported by the device. Earlier, in 1969, Denton Cooley had implanted an experimental device in Haskell Karp as a bridge to transplant.

Modern devices

The SynCardia temporary Total Artificial Heart (descended from the Jarvik-7) is the most widely used TAH, serving mainly as a bridge to transplantation. The Carmat bioprosthetic heart, first implanted in 2013, uses bovine tissue and embedded sensors. Development continues on fully implantable devices with transcutaneous power, aiming at smaller, quieter and longer-lived pumps that would reduce stroke and infection risk.
