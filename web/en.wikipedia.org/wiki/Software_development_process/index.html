Software development process

A software development process is a systematic approach used in software engineering to design, develop, test, deploy and maintain software. Structured processes — often collectively described as the software development life cycle (SDLC) — divide work into distinct but overlapping activities so that projects can be planned, estimated and controlled.

Core activities

Whatever methodology is followed, software development generally includes: requirements elicitation and analysis, specifying what the system must do; system and software design, defining architecture and components; implementation (coding); verification and testing, checking the product against requirements; deployment, releasing the product to users; and maintenance, correcting defects and adapting the system to change. Supporting disciplines include configuration management, quality assurance, documentation and project management.

Methodologies

The classical waterfall model, associated with Winston W. Royce's 1970 paper, executes phases largely in sequence and works best when requirements are stable. The spiral model of Barry Boehm emphasizes iterative cycles driven by risk analysis. From the 1990s onward, agile software development — crystallized in the 2001 Agile Manifesto — favoured short iterations, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change; frameworks include Scrum, Extreme Programming (XP) and Kanban. DevOps extends agile principles into operations, emphasizing automation, continuous integration and continuous delivery. Other approaches include the V-model, prototyping, iterative and incremental development, and the Rational Unified Process.

Standards

The life cycle is formalized in standards such as ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207, which defines processes for the life cycle of software, and capability maturity frameworks such as CMMI assess the maturity of an organization's processes.
