Sarah Margaret Fuller Ossoli (23 May 1810 – 19 July 1850), better known as Margaret Fuller, was an American journalist, editor, critic, translator and advocate of women's rights associated with the transcendentalist movement. She was the first American female war correspondent, and her book Woman in the Nineteenth Century (1845) is considered the first major feminist work in the United States.

Early life and education

Fuller received a rigorous classical education from her father, Timothy Fuller, a lawyer and congressman, learning Latin and Greek as a child. Largely self-educated thereafter, she became one of the best-read people in New England and moved in the circle of Ralph Waldo Emerson and the transcendentalists.

Conversations and The Dial

From 1839 to 1844 Fuller conducted her famous "Conversations" in Boston — discussion classes for women on literature, philosophy and the arts — intended to give women the intellectual education denied them elsewhere. She edited the transcendentalist journal The Dial for its first two years (1840–42), and in 1843 published the travel narrative Summer on the Lakes.

Woman in the Nineteenth Century

An expanded version of her 1843 essay "The Great Lawsuit", Woman in the Nineteenth Century (1845) argued for the full intellectual and spiritual equality of women and their right to self-development. It became a foundational text of American feminism.

Journalism and Italy

In 1844 Horace Greeley hired Fuller as literary critic of the New-York Tribune, making her one of the first women to work on the staff of a major American newspaper and, effectively, the first full-time American female book reviewer. In 1846 she travelled to Europe as the Tribune's foreign correspondent. In Italy she met the Italian nobleman Giovanni Angelo Ossoli, with whom she had a son, and supported the Roman Republic of 1849, reporting on the revolution and working in a hospital during the French siege of Rome.

Death

Returning to America in 1850, Fuller, Ossoli and their child drowned when their ship, the Elizabeth, wrecked in a hurricane off Fire Island, New York, within sight of shore. Her manuscript history of the Roman Republic was lost with her.
