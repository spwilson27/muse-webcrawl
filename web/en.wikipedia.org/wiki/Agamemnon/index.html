In Greek mythology, Agamemnon was a king of Mycenae who commanded the Achaean forces in the Trojan War. Son of Atreus and brother of Menelaus, he led the Greek expedition to recover Helen of Sparta, and on his victorious return was murdered by his wife Clytemnestra and her lover Aegisthus.

Myth

To obtain favourable winds for the fleet becalmed at Aulis, Agamemnon sacrificed his daughter Iphigenia to Artemis — an act Clytemnestra never forgave. In Homer's Iliad his quarrel with Achilles over the captive Briseis drives the poem's plot. After the war he returned to Mycenae with the captured Trojan prophetess Cassandra and was killed in his bath, in Aeschylus' Agamemnon (458 BC), the first play of the Oresteia trilogy, ensnared in a robe or net by Clytemnestra. His son Orestes later avenged him.

Historical echoes

Heinrich Schliemann's excavations at Mycenae in 1876 uncovered a gold death mask he triumphantly — but wrongly, by several centuries — identified as the face of Agamemnon. Hittite records mention a ruler "Akagamunaš", possibly a memory of a Mycenaean king of the Late Bronze Age.
