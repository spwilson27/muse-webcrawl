Saab 9000

The Saab 9000 is an automobile produced by the Swedish company Saab from 1984 to 1998. Representing the company's first entry into the executive car market, it was developed as part of the "Type Four" collaboration with Fiat's marques; the 9000 shared its platform and body structure with the Fiat Croma, Lancia Thema, and Alfa Romeo 164, though with substantial Saab-specific engineering for safety and handling.

Development and design

Designed under Björn Envall with input from Giorgetto Giugiaro's Italdesign for the shared platform, the 9000 was launched as a five-door hatchback in 1984 with a turbocharged 2.0-litre engine. Despite sharing doors and the basic shell with the Fiat Croma, only a small fraction of parts were interchangeable, as Saab substantially reinforced the structure — a fact demonstrated when the 9000 performed markedly better in crash tests. A four-door saloon, the 9000 CD, followed in 1988, and a facelifted CS range in 1991.

Engines and performance

Engines ranged from a 2.0-litre naturally aspirated four to the 2.3-litre turbo "Aero" producing 225 PS, one of the most powerful front-wheel-drive cars of its era, famous for Saab's Trionic engine management system and strong mid-range overtaking performance. In 1986, Saab set endurance records at Talladega Speedway in the "Long Run", covering 100,000 km at an average speed above 213 km/h with standard production cars. Total production reached 503,087 cars before the 9000 was replaced by the Saab 9-5 in 1997–98.
