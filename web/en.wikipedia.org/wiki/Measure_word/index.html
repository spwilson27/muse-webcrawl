Measure word

In linguistics, measure words are words (or grammatical morphemes) that are used in combination with a numeral to indicate an amount of something represented by a noun. Measure words are also called count words in some contexts, and are closely related to classifiers, which categorise the counted noun. They occur in many languages, notably East and Southeast Asian languages such as Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai, and Malay, as well as in constructions in English such as "three head of cattle" or "a loaf of bread."

Types

Two broad kinds are distinguished. Count-classifiers (or sortal classifiers) individuate the noun and depend on its inherent properties — shape, animacy, function and so on. Measure words proper (mensural classifiers) denote a unit of quantity, such as a container, a standard measure, or a group: "a cup of flour," "a herd of elephants."

In Chinese

Modern Chinese requires a measure word between a numeral or demonstrative and a noun. The generic measure word 个 (gè) can substitute for many specific ones in colloquial speech. Different varieties and registers maintain large inventories of specialised measure words chosen according to the shape and nature of the referent.
