Filter (signal processing)

In signal processing, a filter is a device or process that removes some unwanted components or features from a signal. Most often this means removing selected frequencies: a low-pass filter allows low frequencies to pass while attenuating high ones, while high-pass, band-pass, and band-stop (notch) filters select other ranges.

Analog and digital filters

Filters may be implemented in analog electronics, using circuits of resistors, capacitors, inductors, or active components such as operational amplifiers, or digitally, as algorithms operating on sampled data. Digital filters are classified as finite impulse response (FIR) or infinite impulse response (IIR); classic design families include Butterworth, Chebyshev, and elliptic filters, each trading passband flatness, transition steepness, and phase response.

Applications

Filtering is ubiquitous: radio receivers select one station from the spectrum; audio equalizers and crossovers shape sound; imaging software smooths or sharpens pictures; control and communications systems remove noise and interference; and statistical filtering — from moving averages to the Kalman filter — extracts signals from noisy measurements. A filter is characterized by its transfer function or impulse response, its frequency response, and its order.
