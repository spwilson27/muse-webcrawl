Frans Post

Frans Janszoon Post (17 November 1612 – 17 February 1680) was a Dutch Golden Age painter. He was the first European artist to paint landscapes of the Americas, having accompanied Johan Maurits, Prince of Nassau-Siegen, to Dutch Brazil in 1637.

Life

Post was born in Haarlem into a family of artists; his father was a glass painter and his brother Pieter Post became a noted architect. He joined the Haarlem Guild of St. Luke and, at the invitation of Johan Maurits, travelled to Brazil in 1637 as part of the count's scientific and artistic expedition, which also included the painter Albert Eckhout and the naturalists Willem Piso and Georg Marcgraf.

Brazilian landscapes

Post spent seven years in Brazil (1637–1644), producing drawings and oil sketches of the landscapes, sugar plantations, settlements and flora of the Dutch colony in Pernambuco. On his return to Haarlem he worked this material into finished paintings for the rest of his career; about 140 of his Brazilian landscapes survive, along with later imaginary tropical scenes painted for the European market. A group of his works was presented by Johan Maurits to Louis XIV of France in 1679, and several are now in the Louvre.

Style and legacy

Post's Brazilian paintings combine Dutch landscape conventions — high horizons, repoussoir framing and carefully observed skies — with exotic New World subject matter: palm trees, sugar mills, Brazilian wildlife and distant views of Olinda and Recife. They remain an important visual record of colonial Brazil and are held by museums including the Rijksmuseum and the Mauritshuis.
