The Belgrade Fortress (Serbian: Београдска тврђава, Beogradska tvrđava) is a historic fortification complex at the confluence of the River Sava and the Danube, in an urban area of modern Belgrade, Serbia. Consisting of the old citadel (Upper and Lower Town) and Kalemegdan Park on the former fortress field, it represents the core of the old city and the most visited tourist attraction in Belgrade. Owing to its strategic position it was fought over in some 115 wars and razed or rebuilt around 40 times over two millennia.

History

The site was first fortified by the Celtic tribe of the Scordisci in the 3rd century BC, whose settlement was called Singidunum. The Romans developed it into a legionary camp on the Danube frontier. The fortress was rebuilt by the Byzantine emperor Justinian I in the 6th century, passed through Bulgarian, Hungarian and Serbian hands, and was greatly strengthened by Despot Stefan Lazarević in the early 15th century, when Belgrade became the capital of the Serbian Despotate.

The Ottoman Empire captured the fortress in 1521 under Suleiman the Magnificent, and it remained largely Ottoman for three centuries, though the Habsburgs took and refortified it three times (1688–90, 1717–39 and 1789–91); the Austrian periods left much of the present Baroque bastioned trace. The Ottomans finally handed the keys of the fortress to Prince Mihailo Obrenović in 1867, a symbolic end of Ottoman rule in Serbia.

Features

Notable structures include the Despot's Gate and Castellan Tower, the Nebojša Tower by the river port, the Roman Well (actually an Austrian-era cistern), the Military Museum, the Victor Monument (Pobednik, 1928) overlooking the confluence, and the Sahat Tower clock gate. Kalemegdan Park, laid out from the late 19th century on the fortress field, is Belgrade's most famous park; Belgrade Zoo, founded in 1936, stands within the Lower Town.
