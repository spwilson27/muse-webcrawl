Incident at the Gaoping Tombs

The Incident at the Gaoping Tombs (高平陵之變) was a coup d'état that took place on February 5, 249, in the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period of China. The regent Sima Yi, who had long been sidelined by his co-regent Cao Shuang, seized control of the capital Luoyang while Cao Shuang accompanied the emperor Cao Fang on a visit to the Gaoping Tombs. The coup ended the Cao clan's effective grip on power and paved the way for the Sima family's eventual founding of the Jin dynasty.

Background

After the death of the emperor Cao Rui in 239, Cao Shuang and Sima Yi were appointed co-regents for the young emperor Cao Fang. Cao Shuang gradually monopolised power, promoted his supporters, and marginalised Sima Yi, who withdrew from court life claiming illness. Cao Shuang's faction became notorious for corruption, and his failed campaign against Shu Han at the Battle of Xingshi in 244 damaged his prestige.

The coup

On February 5, 249, Cao Fang left Luoyang with Cao Shuang and his brothers to pay respects at the Gaoping Tombs, the burial site of the first Wei emperor, Cao Pi. Sima Yi, having feigned sickness, suddenly acted: his sons and troops occupied the armoury and the gates of Luoyang, and the empress dowager was induced to issue an edict dismissing Cao Shuang. Sima Yi sent a message to Cao Fang demanding Cao Shuang's removal, while promising — through the minister Jiang Ji as guarantor — that Cao Shuang would merely lose his offices and keep his wealth and status.

Cao Shuang hesitated. His adviser Huan Fan, who had escaped the city to join him, urged him to take the emperor to Xuchang and call on the empire's armies to resist. Instead, Cao Shuang chose to surrender, reportedly saying that even without power he could still live the life of a rich man. Sima Yi accepted the surrender, then arrested Cao Shuang, his brothers, and his supporters on charges of treason.

Aftermath

Within days, Cao Shuang, Cao Xi, Cao Xun, He Yan, Deng Yang, Ding Mi, Bi Gui, Li Sheng, and Huan Fan — who had surrendered after escorting his family back — were executed along with their families. The pro-Cao grand tutor's faction was destroyed, and power in Wei passed firmly into the hands of the Sima clan. Sima Yi died in 251; his sons Sima Shi and Sima Zhao consolidated the family's control, and his grandson Sima Yan founded the Jin dynasty in 266, receiving the abdication of the last Wei emperor.
