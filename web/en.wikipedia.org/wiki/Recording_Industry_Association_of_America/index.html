The Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) is a trade organization that represents the music recording industry in the United States. Its members are record labels and distributors, which the RIAA says create, manufacture and/or distribute approximately 85% of all legally recorded music produced and sold in the United States. It is headquartered in Washington, D.C.

Functions

The RIAA was formed in 1952, originally to administer a uniform technical standard for vinyl records — the RIAA equalization curve. It is best known to the public for its certification awards: Gold and Platinum programmes launched in 1976 (and a Diamond award, introduced in 1999, for ten million units) honour albums and singles reaching specified sales and streaming thresholds. Its Latin programme, Los Premios De Oro y Platino, began in 2000.

The association lobbies on copyright and related policy, and it has been a central combatant in disputes over music piracy, from the Napster litigation of 2000–2001 to controversial lawsuits against individual file-sharers in the 2000s and later campaigns against stream-ripping sites and unauthorised AI uses of recordings. It works with technology partners on content identification and licensing infrastructure and publishes industry revenue statistics.

Structure

The three largest members — Universal Music Group, Sony Music Entertainment and Warner Music Group — dominate the association along with their constituent labels. Mitch Glazier has served as chairman and CEO since 2019.
