Fubini–Study metric

In mathematics, the Fubini–Study metric is a Kähler metric on complex projective space CPn endowed with a Hermitian form. This metric was originally described in 1904 and 1905 by Guido Fubini and Eduard Study. It is, up to constant rescaling, the unique Kähler metric on projective space that is invariant under the unitary group U(n+1), the isometry group of the space.

Construction

Complex projective space is the quotient of Cn+1 minus the origin by the action of nonzero complex scalar multiplication. The standard Hermitian inner product on Cn+1 descends to this quotient; in homogeneous coordinates the associated Kähler potential can be written as the logarithm of the Hermitian norm, log( |Z0|2 + … + |Zn|2 ), from which the metric is obtained as its complex Hessian. In the case n = 1, the Fubini–Study metric on the Riemann sphere is (up to scale) the standard round metric.

Properties and applications

The Fubini–Study metric is a Kähler–Einstein metric of positive Einstein constant, with constant holomorphic sectional curvature. In quantum mechanics, the same metric measures distances between quantum states: for states in a Hilbert space, the Fubini–Study metric on the projective space of rays gives the natural notion of statistical distance between states, closely related to the fidelity and to the quantum geometric tensor. It also plays a central role in algebraic geometry as the model metric on projective space, in the theory of Kähler–Einstein metrics, and in geometric approaches to quantum information.
