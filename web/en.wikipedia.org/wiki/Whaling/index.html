Whaling
Definition:Hunting of whales for meat, oil, baleen and other products
Peak:20th century industrial whaling (~3 million whales killed, 1900–1999)
Regulation:International Whaling Commission (1946); commercial moratorium from 1986
Current whaling states:Norway, Iceland, Japan (commercial); aboriginal subsistence hunts elsewhere

Whaling is the hunting of whales for their usable products, including meat, blubber (rendered into oil), baleen and ambergris. Practised in some form for thousands of years, it became an industrial global enterprise in the 19th and 20th centuries that drove several whale species to the brink of extinction.

History

Basque whalers pioneered commercial whaling in the Bay of Biscay by the 11th–12th centuries and carried it to Newfoundland. Dutch, British and American fleets followed; Nantucket and New Bedford sperm-whaling ships ranged worldwide, supplying oil for lamps and machinery, spermaceti for candles and baleen for corsets. Petroleum (from 1859) and spring steel eroded these markets.

Industrial whaling

The explosive harpoon cannon (Svend Foyn, 1860s–70s), steam catcher boats and later factory ships opened the fast rorquals — blue, fin and sei whales — to hunting, above all in the Antarctic. Roughly three million whales were killed in the 20th century; the blue whale population collapsed by over 90%.

Regulation and moratorium

The International Whaling Commission, founded in 1946, failed for decades to restrain catches, but in 1982 voted a moratorium on commercial whaling effective 1986, alongside sanctuaries in the Indian and Southern Oceans. Since then some whale populations have partially recovered, though many remain depleted.

Modern whaling debates

Japan conducted "scientific" whaling under permit until withdrawing from the IWC in 2019 and resuming commercial whaling in its own waters; Norway and Iceland have continued commercial hunts under objection or reservation. Aboriginal subsistence whaling by Inuit and other Indigenous peoples is separately permitted. Conservation groups continue to campaign against all commercial whaling.
