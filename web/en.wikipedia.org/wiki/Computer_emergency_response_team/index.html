Computer emergency response team

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A CERT operations centre coordinating incident response

A computer emergency response team (CERT) is an expert group that handles computer security incidents. Alternative names for such groups include computer security incident response team (CSIRT), computer incident response team (CIRT), and computer emergency readiness team. CERTs receive reports of security breaches and vulnerabilities, analyse them, coordinate responses among affected parties, issue alerts and advisories, and work to prevent future incidents.

History

The first CERT, the CERT Coordination Center (CERT/CC), was established at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh in November 1988, in direct response to the Morris worm, the first major Internet worm, which had exposed the absence of any coordinated mechanism for responding to network emergencies. The centre was created with funding from DARPA and set the template for the many national, governmental, academic, and corporate teams that followed. Because CERT is a registered trademark of Carnegie Mellon, many later teams adopted the CSIRT name instead.

Functions

  • Incident handling — triage, analysis, and coordination of responses to intrusions, malware outbreaks, and denial-of-service attacks.
  • Vulnerability coordination — receiving reports from researchers, notifying vendors, and managing coordinated disclosure timelines.
  • Alerts and advisories — publishing technical guidance for system administrators and the public.
  • Artefact analysis — examining malware and forensic evidence.
  • Awareness and training — promoting secure practices and running exercises.

Organization and cooperation

Teams exist at many levels: national CERTs (such as US-CERT within the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, JPCERT/CC in Japan, and the national teams coordinated in Europe through TF-CSIRT), sectoral teams for industries like banking and energy, and internal CSIRTs inside large companies and universities. The Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams (FIRST), founded in 1990, links hundreds of teams worldwide and sets standards for trusted information sharing. A closely related function is the product security incident response team (PSIRT), which handles vulnerabilities in a vendor's own products.
