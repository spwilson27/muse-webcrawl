Canon of Sherlock Holmes

The Canon of Sherlock Holmes traditionally consists of the 56 short stories and four novels written by the British author Sir Arthur Conan Doyle featuring the fictional detective Sherlock Holmes and his friend Dr. John Watson. The term "Canon" is used by Holmes enthusiasts (Sherlockians) to distinguish Doyle's original works from later pastiches and adaptations by other writers.

The works

The novels are A Study in Scarlet (1887), The Sign of the Four (1890), The Hound of the Baskervilles (1901–02) and The Valley of Fear (1914–15). The short stories appeared first in The Strand Magazine and were collected in five volumes: The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes (1892), The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes (1894), The Return of Sherlock Holmes (1905), His Last Bow (1917) and The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes (1927).

The Great Game

Devotees treat the stories as Watson's factual accounts in a playful scholarship known as the Great Game, reconciling inconsistencies in dates and details. Doyle's five additional Holmes-related pieces, such as the short piece "How Watson Learned the Trick", are usually classed as apocrypha rather than Canon.
