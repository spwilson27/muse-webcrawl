Writers' room
FieldTelevision production

A writers' room is the collaborative workspace — physical or virtual — in which a team of television writers, led by a showrunner, breaks stories, outlines episodes and writes and rewrites scripts for a series. The writers' room model is a defining feature of American television production, especially for serialised drama and multi-camera and single-camera comedy.

Process

Writers collectively pitch story ideas, "break" episodes into acts and scenes on whiteboards or index cards, and are then assigned scripts to draft individually. Drafts are revised by the room and the showrunner, whose voice ultimately unifies the series. Staff writers progress through ranks from staff writer to story editor, producer and co-executive producer, alongside the writing role historically combined with producing duties.

Debates

The structure and size of writers' rooms — including the rise of short-order seasons and "mini rooms" in the streaming era — were central issues in the 2023 Writers Guild of America strike, which resulted in agreements on minimum staffing for rooms on high-budget streaming series.
