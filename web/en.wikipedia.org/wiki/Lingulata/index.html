Lingulata

Lingulata is a class of brachiopods, among the oldest of all brachiopods, having existed since the Cambrian period (about 538 million years ago). They are also among the longest-lived brachiopod genera known, including the living genus Lingula, famous as a "living fossil" because its shell form has changed remarkably little over hundreds of millions of years.

Characteristics

Unlike most brachiopods, whose shells are calcareous, lingulates have shells composed of calcium phosphate interlayered with organic material (chitin). They are inarticulate brachiopods: their two valves are held together by muscles rather than by a hinge with teeth and sockets. Many lingulates burrow in sediment using a long, muscular pedicle.

Evolution

Lingulates survived all of the Big Five mass extinctions, and their shells are abundant in Paleozoic fossil assemblages, where they help date marine sedimentary rocks.
