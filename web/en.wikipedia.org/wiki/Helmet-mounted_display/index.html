Helmet-mounted display

A helmet-mounted display (HMD) is a display device, worn on a helmet, that presents flight, sensor and weapon information directly in the user's field of view. HMDs are a key element of modern military aircraft cockpits, replacing or supplementing the head-up display (HUD) by projecting imagery onto the helmet visor or a small monocle, so the pilot can view data while looking in any direction — "off-boresight".

An HMD keeps flight and targeting symbology in view wherever the pilot looks.

Technology

An HMD combines a miniature display (CRT, LCD or OLED) with optics that collimate the image so it appears at optical infinity, overlaid on the outside view. A helmet tracker — magnetic, optical or inertial — measures the head's position and orientation many times per second, enabling the system to slave sensors and weapons to the pilot's line of sight, display imagery from night-vision or infrared sensors, and cue targets by simply looking at them. Modern systems integrate night vision directly into the display rather than using separate goggles.

Examples

  • JHMCS (Joint Helmet Mounted Cueing System): used on F-15, F-16 and F/A-18 aircraft to cue high off-boresight missiles such as the AIM-9X.
  • F-35 HMDS: the Lightning II has no HUD; its helmet is the primary flight display and fuses imagery from the jet's distributed aperture cameras, giving a virtual view "through" the airframe.
  • Striker II (BAE Systems): integrated night vision and colour display for Eurofighter and other types.
  • Apache IHADSS: an early monocular system that slaves the helicopter's gun and sensors to the crew's gaze.

Challenges

HMDs impose demanding engineering trade-offs: weight and centre of gravity affect pilot fatigue and ejection safety; latency and tracker accuracy matter for stability of the symbology; and extensive custom fitting and calibration are required for each user.
