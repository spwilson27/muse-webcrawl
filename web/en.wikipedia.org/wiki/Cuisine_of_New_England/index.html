Cuisine of New England

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cuisine of New England is an American cuisine originating from the six states of New England: Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont. It is characterised by extensive use of seafood and dairy products, a legacy of English colonial cooking adapted to local ingredients such as corn, beans, squash, cranberries and maple syrup — several of them first learned from Native American peoples.

Traditional dishes

Signature dishes include New England clam chowder, a cream-based soup with potatoes, onions and quahog clams (Rhode Island maintains a clear-broth variant and Manhattan's tomato version is a rival); the clambake of clams, lobster and corn cooked on hot stones; Boston baked beans, sweetened with molasses, which gave Boston the nickname "Beantown"; boiled dinner of corned beef and cabbage; brown bread steamed in a can; and Johnny cakes, a cornmeal flatbread. Maine lobster, served simply steamed or as a lobster roll dressed with butter or mayonnaise, is a regional emblem, as are fried clams, credited to Lawrence "Chubby" Woodman of Essex, Massachusetts (1916).

Sweets and produce

Maple syrup, tapped from sugar maples especially in Vermont, flavours many dishes. Cranberries from the bogs of Cape Cod and apple cider and apple pie (traditionally served with cheddar cheese in Vermont) feature prominently. Desserts include Indian pudding, Boston cream pie — the official Massachusetts state dessert — and whoopie pies, claimed by both Maine and Pennsylvania. Coffee milk is the official state drink of Rhode Island, and Moxie is a distinctive regional soft drink.
