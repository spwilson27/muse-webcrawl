1729 is the natural number following 1728 and preceding 1730. It is known as the Hardy–Ramanujan number after a famous anecdote of the British mathematician G. H. Hardy regarding the Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan, and is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two positive cubes in two different ways.

The Hardy–Ramanujan anecdote

Hardy recalled visiting the ailing Ramanujan in hospital at Putney: "I remember once going to see him when he was ill at Putney. I had ridden in taxi cab number 1729 and remarked that the number seemed to me rather a dull one, and that I hoped it was not an unfavourable omen. 'No,' he replied, 'it is a very interesting number; it is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways.'" The two ways are 1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³.

Properties

Numbers that are the smallest expressible as the sum of two cubes in n distinct ways are called taxicab numbers after this story; 1729 is Ta(2). The number is also a Carmichael number (the third, after 561 and 1105), factoring as 7 × 13 × 19, and it appears in connection with Fermat's near-miss identities popularised by the anecdote. It is the third Carmichael number and the first absolute Euler pseudoprime.

In popular culture

The number recurs as an in-joke in works by mathematicians and in popular media — including repeated appearances in the animated series Futurama, whose writers included several mathematics graduates, and in the play and film about Ramanujan's life.
