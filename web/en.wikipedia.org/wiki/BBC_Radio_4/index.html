BBC Radio 4

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BBC Radio 4 is a British national radio station owned and operated by the BBC, broadcasting mainly speech programming — news, current affairs, drama, comedy, science, history and the arts. It launched on 30 September 1967, replacing the BBC Home Service, and is available on FM, long wave, DAB and online throughout the United Kingdom.

Programming

Radio 4's flagship programmes include the Today programme, the morning news and current affairs show that sets much of the national political agenda; The World at One and PM; and the evening Six O'Clock News. The station is famous for its drama output, including long-running soap opera The Archers (broadcast since 1951), and for comedy such as I'm Sorry I Haven't a Clue and The News Quiz. Other long-standing features include Desert Island Discs, Woman's Hour, Gardeners' Question Time, The Shipping Forecast and the annual Reith Lectures.

History

The station grew out of the wartime and post-war Home Service. Its long-wave frequency, inherited from earlier national programmes, carried Test Match Special cricket coverage and the parliamentary programme for many years. Plans in the 1990s to convert the long-wave service to a rolling news station provoked public outcry and were abandoned; instead BBC Radio 5 Live took that role in 1994.

Audience and character

Radio 4 reaches around ten million listeners a week and is often described as the intellectual heart of BBC radio, with a schedule built around a mixed "stream" of programmes rather than separate blocks. It broadcasts no advertising and is funded by the television licence fee. A sister station, BBC Radio 4 Extra, carries archive drama and comedy.
