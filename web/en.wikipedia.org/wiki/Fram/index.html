Fram

TypeThree-masted schooner, polar exploration vessel

Fram ("Forward") is a Norwegian polar exploration ship, famous as the vessel used in expeditions to both polar regions by Fridtjof Nansen, Otto Sverdrup, and Roald Amundsen between 1893 and 1912. Designed by shipbuilder Colin Archer with a specially rounded hull, she was built to be frozen into the Arctic pack ice and to rise with the pressure rather than be crushed.

Expeditions

On Nansen's expedition of 1893–1896, Fram drifted across the Arctic Ocean frozen in the ice, demonstrating the transpolar drift current; Nansen and Hjalmar Johansen left the ship to reach a record farthest north of 86°14′. Under Sverdrup (1898–1902), the ship charted much of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago. Amundsen used Fram to reach Antarctica in 1911, landing the party that became first to the South Pole in December 1911.

Fram is said to have sailed farther north and farther south than any other wooden ship. She is preserved at the Fram Museum on Bygdøy in Oslo, in a building constructed around her in 1935.
