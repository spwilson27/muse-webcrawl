Housefly

Scientific classification
Order:Diptera
Family:Muscidae
Genus:Musca
Species:M. domestica

The housefly (Musca domestica) is a fly of the suborder Cyclorrhapha and the most common fly species found in houses worldwide. Adults are 6 to 7 mm long with a grey thorax marked by four longitudinal dark stripes, red compound eyes and a single pair of membranous wings. Having originated in the Middle East, the species spread around the world as a commensal of humans.

Life cycle

Female houseflies lay batches of about 75 to 150 eggs in decaying organic matter such as manure, garbage and food waste; a female may lay up to several hundred eggs in her lifetime. The white, legless larvae (maggots) feed for three to eight days before pupating in a dry puparium. Adults emerge after a few days and typically live for two to four weeks, feeding on liquid and semi-liquid food, which they liquefy with regurgitated digestive juices and saliva.

Relation to humans

Houseflies are significant mechanical vectors of disease: their bodies and feeding habits can spread pathogens responsible for typhoid, cholera, dysentery, salmonellosis and parasitic worms, contaminating food and surfaces in homes and farms. Control relies on sanitation, waste management, screens, traps and insecticides, though resistance is widespread. Conversely, houseflies are used in research, as food for captive animals, and in forensic entomology, and their larvae are farmed industrially as animal feed protein.
