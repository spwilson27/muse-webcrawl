Extended Euclidean algorithm

In arithmetic and computer programming, the extended Euclidean algorithm is an extension of the Euclidean algorithm that, besides computing the greatest common divisor (gcd) of integers a and b, also finds integers x and y — Bézout coefficients — such that ax + by = gcd(a, b), as guaranteed by Bézout's identity.

The algorithm proceeds alongside the ordinary Euclidean algorithm, tracking the coefficients expressing each remainder as a combination of a and b. Its most important applications include computing modular multiplicative inverses (essential in cryptography such as RSA), solving linear Diophantine equations, and computing continued fractions.

The method generalises to other Euclidean domains, including polynomials.
