East African Legislative Assembly

TypeLegislative organ of the East African Community
Established30 November 2001
SeatArusha, Tanzania
CompositionElected members from each partner state plus ex-officio members

The East African Legislative Assembly (EALA) is the legislative organ of the East African Community (EAC). It was inaugurated on 30 November 2001 in Arusha, Tanzania, where it is headquartered. The Assembly enacts laws for the Community, approves its budget, and provides oversight of the EAC's organs and institutions.

Composition

The Assembly consists of members elected by the national assemblies of the partner states — nine elected members from each state — together with ex-officio members including the ministers responsible for East African affairs from each partner state, the Secretary General of the EAC and the Counsel to the Community. As the Community has grown from its original three members (Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda) to include Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Somalia, the Assembly has expanded correspondingly. Members serve five-year terms. The Assembly is headed by a Speaker elected from among its members.

Functions

Under the Treaty for the Establishment of the East African Community, EALA's functions include liaising with national assemblies, debating and approving the Community budget, considering annual reports and audit reports, and passing bills on matters within the Community's competence. Bills passed by the Assembly require the assent of the Heads of State to become Acts of the Community.

History

EALA revives a tradition of regional legislatures in East Africa stretching back to the Central Legislative Assembly of the colonial-era East African High Commission and the Legislative Assembly of the first East African Community (1967–1977). Successive assemblies since 2001 are known as the First through Fifth Assemblies; elections to the Assembly are indirect, a feature that has prompted recurring debate about moving to direct election by universal suffrage.
