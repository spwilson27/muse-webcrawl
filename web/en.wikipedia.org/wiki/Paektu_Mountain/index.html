Paektu Mountain

Paektu Mountain (Korean: 백두산), also known as Changbai Mountain in Chinese, is an active stratovolcano on the border between North Korea and China. At 2,744 metres (9,003 ft) it is the highest mountain on the Korean Peninsula and in northeastern China. Its summit caldera contains a large crater lake, Heaven Lake (Cheonji). The mountain is sacred in Korean culture and figures prominently in the national mythology of both Korean states.

Geology

Paektu is a stratovolcano built up over millions of years. Its most famous eruption, the so-called Millennium Eruption of 946 CE, was one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the last 5,000 years, ejecting tens of cubic kilometres of material and depositing ash as far away as northern Japan. The eruption created the present summit caldera, roughly 5 km wide, which filled with water to form Heaven Lake. Minor activity in the early 2000s, including increased gas emissions and ground deformation, prompted renewed monitoring and scientific cooperation between China and North Korea.

Cultural significance

In Korean mythology, Paektu is the legendary birthplace of Dangun, the mythical founder of the first Korean kingdom, Gojoseon. North Korean state ideology claims the mountain as the birthplace of Kim Jong-il and the site of Kim Il-sung's guerrilla base during the anti-Japanese struggle; the mountain appears on North Korea's national emblem. South Koreans also revere it, and it is mentioned in South Korea's national anthem.
