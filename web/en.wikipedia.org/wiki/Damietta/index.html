Damietta

Damietta (Arabic: دمياط, romanized: Dumyāṭ; Coptic: ⲧⲁⲙⲓⲁϯ, Tamiat) is a port city in northeastern Egypt, capital of the Damietta Governorate. It stands on the Damietta branch of the Nile Delta, about 15 kilometres (9 mi) from the Mediterranean Sea and some 200 kilometres (120 mi) north of Cairo. The modern city has a population of roughly 300,000, and the governorate about 1.5 million. Damietta gives its name to Domiati cheese, Egypt’s best-known white brined cheese.

History

Known in antiquity as Tamiat, the city rose to prominence in the medieval period as Egypt’s principal port for trade with Syria and the eastern Mediterranean. Its strategic position made it a repeated target of the Crusades: it was besieged and captured by the forces of the Fifth Crusade in 1218–1219, an occupation accompanied by a famous preaching mission of Francis of Assisi to Sultan al-Kamil, before being returned by treaty in 1221. In 1249 Louis IX of France seized Damietta at the start of the Seventh Crusade, but after his defeat and capture at Mansurah the following year the city was ransomed back. The Mamluk sultan Baybars subsequently razed the old city and rebuilt it inland with stronger defences, diverting the harbour. Nevertheless Damietta remained an important port, and in the modern era a new deep-water port was constructed west of the city in the 1980s; it is also a terminal for liquefied natural gas exports.

Economy

Damietta is nationally famous for its furniture industry — workshops in the city produce a majority of Egypt’s wooden furniture — and also for boatbuilding and fishing, sweets, textiles, and the manufacture of Domiati cheese. The New Damietta development and the port’s container and LNG terminals are major employers in the governorate.
