Sofia rules

The Sofia rules are a set of rules for chess tournaments designed to discourage players from agreeing short draws. They were first introduced at the M-Tel Masters tournament in Sofia, Bulgaria, in 2005, at the initiative of the organiser Silvio Danailov.

The rules

Under the Sofia rules, players are not permitted to offer or agree to a draw directly with each other. A draw may only be claimed — through the arbiter — in cases of threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, perpetual check, or a theoretically drawn position, and in some implementations the arbiter must agree that the position offers no winning chances. The intention is to force players to fight on the board and make games more attractive to spectators and sponsors.

Adoption and reception

The rules were subsequently adopted, in varying forms, by a number of elite tournaments, and FIDE has incorporated related anti-draw measures in some of its events, such as prohibiting draw offers before a specified move number. Players have had mixed reactions: many welcome decisive games, though critics note the rules can force play in genuinely dead positions and that the "arbiter-approved stalemate positions" exception still leaves loopholes. Short pre-arranged draws have largely disappeared from top events where the rules apply.
