Daphne

Daphne (Greek: Δάφνη, "laurel") is a figure in Greek mythology, a naiad — a female nymph associated with fountains, wells, springs and streams — best known for her role in the myth of Apollo and Daphne.

Mythology

Daphne was the daughter of the river god Peneus in Thessaly (in other versions, of Ladon). The god Apollo, struck by one of Eros's golden arrows and inflamed with love, pursued the unwilling Daphne, who had been struck by a leaden arrow that caused aversion and who had vowed perpetual virginity. As Apollo was about to overtake her, Daphne prayed to her father (or, in Ovid's version in the Metamorphoses, to Gaia) to save her, and she was transformed into a laurel tree. Apollo, grieving, declared the laurel his sacred tree and wore its leaves as a wreath — the origin of the laurel crowns awarded to victors and poets.

In art and literature

The myth has inspired countless works of art, most famously Gian Lorenzo Bernini's sculpture Apollo and Daphne (1622–1625) in the Galleria Borghese, Rome, which captures the moment of transformation, as well as operas by Jacopo Peri and Richard Strauss.
