Sluice

A sluice gate on a drainage channel.

A sluice (from the Latin exclusa, a gate) is a water channel containing one or more artificial barriers — most commonly a vertically sliding gate — used to control water flow and water levels. It is often used in water treatment plants, in mining (for washing ore), in milling, in irrigation, and in flood control. A sluice also traditionally describes a channel through which ship traffic passes, as in a canal lock gate, and the term is applied to the long wooden troughs used in placer mining.

Operation

Sluice gates may be manually operated, historically by a rack-and-pinion or screw jack, or driven by hydraulic or electric actuators. By raising or lowering the gate, operators regulate the discharge under the gate; the flow depends on the opening and on the upstream water depth. A series of sluices along a polder drainage system keeps water levels stable by discharging to a river or sea at low tide.

In mining

In placer mining a sluice box is an inclined trough with riffles across its bottom. Water carries gravel through the box; heavy particles such as gold settle behind the riffles while lighter material is washed away. Roman engineers used extensive sluicing, including the "hushing" technique of releasing stored water to strip overburden, famously at Las Médulas in Spain.

Flood and irrigation control

Coastal sluices discharge inland drainage to the sea when the tide is low and close automatically against tidal flooding. The Boston Barrier and other tidal barriers incorporate sluices to regulate river flow. In irrigation systems, sluices distribute water from main canals into field channels proportionally to entitlement.
