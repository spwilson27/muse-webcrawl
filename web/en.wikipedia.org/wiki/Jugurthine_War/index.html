Jugurthine War

Date112–105 BCE
LocationNumidia, North Africa
ResultRoman victory; Jugurtha captured
BelligerentsRoman Republic vs Kingdom of Numidia

The Jugurthine War (112–105 BCE) was an armed conflict between the Roman Republic and Jugurtha, king of Numidia in North Africa. Though Rome prevailed, the war exposed corruption and incompetence in the Roman aristocracy and launched the careers of Gaius Marius and Sulla. Our main source is Sallust's monograph Bellum Jugurthinum.

Background

Numidia had been Rome's ally under Masinissa and Micipsa. After Micipsa's death in 118 BCE, his adopted son Jugurtha eliminated his co-heirs Hiempsal and Adherbal — the latter murdered at Cirta despite Roman intervention — and bribed senators to delay Roman retaliation, prompting his famous remark that Rome was a city for sale.

Course of the war

Early Roman commanders performed poorly or were allegedly bribed; a Roman army even surrendered under the yoke in 110 BCE. The scandal led to the Mamilian Commission prosecuting corrupt nobles. Quintus Caecilius Metellus restored discipline from 109 BCE, then his legate Gaius Marius was elected consul and took command, enrolling landless volunteers and campaigning deep into Numidia. Marius's quaestor Sulla finally negotiated Jugurtha's betrayal and capture by his father-in-law Bocchus of Mauretania in 105 BCE.

Aftermath

Jugurtha was displayed in Marius's triumph and executed in the Tullianum in 104 BCE. Numidia was partitioned between Bocchus and a remaining Numidian king. The war accelerated the rise of popularis politics and the professionalisation of the Roman army.
