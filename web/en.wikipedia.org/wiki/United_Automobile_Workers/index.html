The United Automobile, Aerospace and Agricultural Implement Workers of America, better known as the United Auto Workers (UAW), is an American labor union that primarily represents workers in the United States (and formerly in Canada). Founded in 1935 under the American Federation of Labor and soon part of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), the UAW became one of the most powerful unions in American history.

History

The union's breakthrough came with the Flint sit-down strike of 1936–37, in which workers occupied General Motors plants in Flint, Michigan, and forced GM to recognize the UAW. Ford Motor Company held out until 1941, after the violent "Battle of the Overpass" in 1937 exposed anti-union tactics. Under Walter Reuther, president from 1946 until his death in 1970, the UAW won landmark contracts including the 1950 "Treaty of Detroit" with GM, which traded long-term labour peace for rising wages, pensions, and cost-of-living adjustments. The union was also a major supporter of the American civil rights movement and organized beyond the auto industry into aerospace, agriculture, higher education, healthcare, and gaming.

Recent history

Membership declined from a peak of about 1.5 million in 1979 as the American auto industry contracted. In 2023, under newly elected president Shawn Fain, the UAW conducted an unprecedented simultaneous "stand-up strike" against all three of Detroit's major automakers — General Motors, Ford, and Stellantis — winning record wage increases and other concessions. In 2024 the union won a landmark election at Volkswagen's Chattanooga plant, its first successful organizing of a foreign-owned auto plant in the South.
