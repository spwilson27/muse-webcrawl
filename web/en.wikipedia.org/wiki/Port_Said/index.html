Port Said

Port Said is a city in northeastern Egypt, situated on the Mediterranean Sea at the northern entrance of the Suez Canal. It is the capital of the Port Said Governorate and one of Egypt's most important ports.

History

Port Said was founded in 1859 during the construction of the Suez Canal and named after Said Pasha, the Khedive of Egypt who granted Ferdinand de Lesseps the concession to build the canal. The city grew around the canal works, its population drawn from Egyptian laborers and foreign workers. It was heavily damaged during the Suez Crisis of 1956, when British and French forces attacked Egypt after the canal's nationalization, and again during the wars of 1967 and 1973, after which many residents were evacuated; the city was rebuilt after the canal reopened in 1975.

Economy and landmarks

The city is a duty-free port and a center for fishing, shipping services, and industries including chemicals and food processing. Its twin city, Port Fuad, lies across the canal on the Asian side. Landmarks include the Suez Canal Authority building with its green dome, the Port Said Lighthouse (built in 1869, among the first reinforced concrete structures in the world), and the city's distinctive colonial-era arcaded buildings.
