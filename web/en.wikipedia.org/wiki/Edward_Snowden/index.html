Edward Snowden

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Edward Joseph Snowden (born 21 June 1983) is an American former intelligence contractor and whistleblower who in 2013 copied and leaked highly classified information from the National Security Agency (NSA), where he had worked as a systems administrator for a contractor (Booz Allen Hamilton, and earlier Dell) supporting NSA programmes. The disclosures, published from June 2013 by The Guardian and The Washington Post (journalists Glenn Greenwald, Laura Poitras and Barton Gellman), revealed global surveillance programmes including PRISM (compelled cooperation of US internet companies), bulk collection of Americans' telephone metadata under § 215 of the Patriot Act, and the tapping of allied leaders' communications (including Chancellor Merkel's phone).

Charged under the Espionage Act, Snowden fled to Hong Kong and then Moscow, where his passport was revoked in transit; Russia granted him asylum and, eventually, permanent residence (2020) and citizenship (2022), while he accepted the presidency of the Freedom of the Press Foundation. The leaks triggered a global debate on mass surveillance, the USA FREEDOM Act (2015, ending bulk telephony-metadata collection), court rulings (the Second Circuit held the § 215 programme unauthorised in 2015), industry-wide encryption defaults, and enduring argument over whether he is a whistleblower who performed a public service or a defector who damaged legitimate intelligence. No pardon has been granted, and he remains in Russia.
