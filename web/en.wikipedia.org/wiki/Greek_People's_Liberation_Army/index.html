Greek People's Liberation Army

The Greek People's Liberation Army (Greek: Ελληνικός Λαϊκός Απελευθερωτικός Στρατός, ELAS) was the military arm of the National Liberation Front (EAM), the largest resistance organisation in Greece during the Axis occupation of the Second World War. Dominated by the Communist Party of Greece (KKE), ELAS grew into a force of tens of thousands of fighters and effectively liberated much of rural Greece before the occupying forces withdrew.

Origins and growth

ELAS was formally established in December 1942, building on guerrilla bands organised by EAM since 1941. Its military commander was Stefanos Sarafis, a former army officer, while Aris Velouchiotis, its best-known field leader, served as chief kapetanios. From bases in the mountains of central Greece — the "Free Greece" administered by EAM — ELAS fought Italian and later German forces, took part alongside rival resistance groups in operations such as the blowing of the Gorgopotamos viaduct in November 1942, and progressively destroyed or absorbed competing guerrilla organisations, notably in clashes with EDES.

Liberation and the Dekemvriana

By the autumn of 1944 ELAS controlled most of the country outside Athens. Following the Caserta Agreement, ELAS forces were to come under the government of national unity led by Georgios Papandreou, but disputes over disarmament led to the resignation of EAM ministers and, in December 1944, to the Dekemvriana: street fighting in Athens between ELAS and government forces backed by British troops.

Disbandment

Under the Varkiza Agreement of February 1945, ELAS was demobilised and surrendered most of its weapons. Many of its former fighters were subsequently persecuted in the "White Terror", and significant numbers later joined the Democratic Army of Greece in the Greek Civil War (1946–1949).
