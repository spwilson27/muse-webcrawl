Merseyside derby

The "friendly derby" between Everton and Liverpool is England's longest-running top-flight derby.

The Merseyside derby is the football rivalry between Everton and Liverpool, the two clubs of Liverpool, England. Contested since 1894, it is the longest-running top-flight derby in England, the clubs having met in the top division every season since 1962–63. It is sometimes called the friendly derby, because unlike many rivalries the fanbases are not segregated by geography, religion or politics, and families are often split between blue and red.

Origins

The rivalry was born of a split: Everton played at Anfield until 1892, when a dispute over rent with the ground's owner, John Houlding, led Everton to move across Stanley Park to Goodison Park — and Houlding founded Liverpool F.C. to fill the vacant Anfield. The stadiums stand less than a mile apart, separated by Stanley Park. The first derby was played on 13 October 1894 at Goodison Park, Everton winning 3–0.

Character

For decades supporters mixed freely at derbies, and the fixture became a symbol of civic unity — most famously after the Hillsborough disaster of 1989, when Everton fans stood with Liverpool in grief and in the campaign for justice, symbolised by a chain of scarves stretching between the two grounds. On the pitch, however, the derby has produced more red cards than any other Premier League fixture. Everton dominated the early meetings; Liverpool's superiority grew from the 1970s onward, and the 1980s saw all-Merseyside FA Cup finals in 1986 and 1989, both won by Liverpool. Famous matches include the 4–4 FA Cup draw of 1991 and Liverpool's last-minute winners in the Klopp era. Everton's move to a new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock in 2025 ends the Stanley Park era, with the final Goodison derby played in February 2025.
