Parkway

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A parkway is a type of road designed for scenic, recreational driving. The term has several related meanings: in the United States it often denotes a landscaped, limited-access highway from which trucks and other commercial vehicles are prohibited; in other contexts it may refer to a wide boulevard with a central landscaped median, or to a route through a park.

History

The concept emerged in 19th-century landscape design. Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux designed parkways in Brooklyn, such as Eastern Parkway and Ocean Parkway, as landscaped corridors linking parks. In the early 20th century, parkways became prestige motoring routes: the Bronx River Parkway in Westchester County, New York (completed 1925), is often considered the first limited-access parkway in the United States. Under Robert Moses, a network of parkways was built on Long Island and around New York City, with low stone-arch bridges that excluded buses and trucks.

Federally administered scenic parkways built by the National Park Service include the Blue Ridge Parkway and the Natchez Trace Parkway, designed for leisurely driving at modest speeds through protected landscapes.

Characteristics

  • Landscaped medians and roadside plantings
  • Limited access via interchanges rather than at-grade intersections (on US parkways)
  • Prohibition of commercial vehicles and trucks
  • Curving, terrain-following alignments and scenic overlooks
  • Low bridge clearances in some historic examples

Other uses of the term

In British usage a "parkway" may appear in place names and, since the later 20th century, in "parkway stations" — railway stations with large car parks intended for drivers (e.g. Bristol Parkway). In some US cities, "parkway" is the locally customary name for a broad residential boulevard.

Notable parkways

  • Blue Ridge Parkway (Virginia and North Carolina)
  • Bronx River Parkway (New York)
  • Garden State Parkway (New Jersey)
  • Natchez Trace Parkway (Mississippi, Alabama, Tennessee)
  • Merritt Parkway (Connecticut)
  • Arroyo Seco Parkway (California)
