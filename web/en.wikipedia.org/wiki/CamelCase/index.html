CamelCase

CamelCase (also camel case, camelCase, or medial capitals) is the practice of writing phrases without spaces or punctuation and with capitalized words joined together. The format indicates the first word starting with either case, then the following words having an initial uppercase letter. Common examples include iPhone, eBay, and YouTube. The name comes from the resemblance of the internal capital letters to the humps of a camel.

Variants

Two variants are widely recognized. Upper camel case (also called Pascal case) capitalizes the first letter of every word, including the first: BackgroundColor. Lower camel case leaves the first word in lowercase: backgroundColor. Style guides for programming languages prescribe one or the other for particular kinds of identifiers — for example, Pascal case for class names and lower camel case for variables and methods in Java and C#.

History

Medial capitals occur occasionally in older proper names (such as MacDonald and FitzGerald) and in chemical formulas. Their systematic use in computing grew with programming languages that forbade spaces in identifiers, notably in the Smalltalk community at Xerox PARC in the 1970s, and spread through Pascal, Java, and .NET conventions. Brands adopted camel case widely in the 1990s technology boom. The term "camel case" itself entered general usage through computing journalism in the 1990s.

Use in other domains

CamelCase is used in trademarks and product names (PowerPoint, MasterCard), in wiki software — the original WikiWikiWeb used CamelCase "WikiWords" as automatic hyperlinks — and in hashtags on social media, where capitalizing each word (#CamelCase) improves readability and accessibility for screen readers. Related conventions include snake_case, which uses underscores, and kebab-case, which uses hyphens.
