The Daisenryō Kofun (Japanese: 大仙陵古墳) is a megalithic keyhole-shaped burial mound (kofun) in Sakai, Osaka Prefecture, Japan. It is traditionally regarded as the tomb of Emperor Nintoku, the 16th emperor of Japan, and is considered one of the largest tombs in the world by area — comparable in scale to the Great Pyramid of Giza and the Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor.

Description

The mound itself is about 486 metres long and up to 35 metres high, surrounded by three moats; the whole precinct covers roughly 46 hectares. The keyhole shape — a square front joined to a circular rear — is characteristic of the largest imperial kofun of the 5th century. Originally the mound was covered with fukiishi paving stones and adorned with thousands of haniwa clay figures, including figures of houses, warriors and horses excavated from the site.

History and administration

Construction is dated to the mid-5th century and is estimated to have taken many years and an enormous labour force. The tomb is administered by the Imperial Household Agency as an imperial mausoleum, which restricts archaeological excavation; the interior has never been scientifically excavated, so the identity of the occupant is unverified. In 2019 the mound was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Mozu-Furuichi Kofun Group.
