Hendrick Avercamp

Hendrick Avercamp (1585 – 15 May 1634) was a Dutch painter of the Dutch Golden Age, celebrated for his winter landscapes crowded with figures skating, sledding, and playing on frozen canals and rivers. Born in Amsterdam and deaf — contemporary records call him "de Stomme van Kampen", "the Mute of Kampen" — he spent most of his life in Kampen, where he was baptised and later buried.

Work

Avercamp trained in Amsterdam, possibly under Pieter Isaacsz. His mature paintings, such as Winter Landscape with Skaters (c. 1608) and A Winter Scene with Skaters near a Castle, present high-horizon panoramic views filled with small, carefully observed figures of all social classes enjoying the ice during the Little Ice Age's severe winters. His style combines the Flemish tradition of Pieter Bruegel the Elder with a delicate, silvery palette and precise detail.

Legacy

He was among the first Dutch artists to specialise in winter scenes, a genre that became hugely popular in the seventeenth-century Netherlands. His works are held by the Rijksmuseum, the Mauritshuis, the National Gallery in London, and many other museums.
