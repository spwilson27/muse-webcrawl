Mushroom poisoning
CausesIngestion of toxic wild mushrooms
Notable toxinsAmatoxins, orellanine, muscarine
TreatmentSupportive care; silibinin; liver transplant

Mushroom poisoning is poisoning resulting from the ingestion of toxic substances present in certain mushrooms. Symptoms can range from mild gastrointestinal discomfort to organ failure and death. Most fatal cases are caused by amatoxin-containing species such as the death cap (Amanita phalloides).

Toxins

Amatoxins inhibit RNA polymerase II, causing liver and kidney failure after a deceptive latent period. Orellanine, found in some Cortinarius species, damages the kidneys. Other toxin groups include muscarine, ibotenic acid, and gyromitrin.

Diagnosis and treatment

There is no reliable field test distinguishing edible from poisonous mushrooms. Treatment is mainly supportive, with activated charcoal, intravenous silibinin for amatoxin poisoning, and liver transplantation in the most severe cases.
