Specific gravity

Specific gravity, also called relative density, is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of a given reference material. For liquids and solids the reference is usually water at 4 °C, at which its density is 999.97 kg/m³ (approximately 1 g/cm³); for gases the reference is air at room temperature. Being a ratio of like quantities, specific gravity is dimensionless.

A hydrometer measures the specific gravity of liquids such as battery acid and brewing wort.

Measurement

Specific gravity is commonly measured with a hydrometer, a pycnometer (specific-gravity bottle), or an oscillating U-tube digital density meter. Because density varies with temperature, measurements are reported at specified temperatures for both sample and reference, written e.g. SG 20/20.

Uses

  • Urinalysis: urine specific gravity (typically 1.005–1.030) indicates kidney concentrating ability and hydration.
  • Brewing and winemaking: "original gravity" and "final gravity" of wort determine alcohol content; values around 1.040–1.090 are typical.
  • Batteries: the SG of lead–acid electrolyte (≈1.265 fully charged) indicates state of charge.
  • Gemology and geology: mineral SG (quartz 2.65, gold 19.3) aids identification.
  • Petroleum: API gravity is a related inverse scale derived from specific gravity.

Relation to density

If a substance's specific gravity is less than 1 relative to water, it floats; greater than 1, it sinks. Precise relative-density measurements historically provided some of the most accurate determinations of absolute density and of atomic weights.
