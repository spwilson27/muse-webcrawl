Neurulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Neurulation refers to the folding process in vertebrate embryos, which includes the transformation of the neural plate into the neural tube. The embryo at this stage is termed the neurula. The process begins when the embryonic ectoderm is induced to form the central nervous system by signals from the underlying notochord and paraxial mesoderm. Neurulation is one of the key early events of organogenesis: the neural tube will give rise to the brain and spinal cord, while cells at its dorsal edge, the neural crest, migrate away to form much of the peripheral nervous system and many other structures.

Primary neurulation

In primary neurulation — which forms the brain and most of the spinal cord in mammals, birds and amphibians — the ectoderm thickens into a flat neural plate. The edges of the plate, the neural folds, elevate as the plate bends at hinge points (a medial hinge point above the notochord and paired dorsolateral hinge points), forming a neural groove. The folds converge and fuse in the midline, converting the groove into a hollow tube that sinks beneath the surface ectoderm. Closure does not occur simultaneously along the whole body: in humans it begins around day 22 near the future neck and "zips" both rostrally and caudally, with the anterior (cranial) neuropore closing around day 24–25 and the posterior neuropore around day 26–28.

Secondary neurulation

In secondary neurulation, which forms the most caudal spinal cord (in humans, the sacral and coccygeal levels), the neural tube arises not by folding but from a solid mass of cells, the caudal eminence, which cavitates — developing a central lumen that joins the tube formed by primary neurulation.

Molecular control

Neural induction depends on inhibition of BMP signalling by factors secreted from the organiser (noggin, chordin, follistatin), allowing ectoderm to adopt its default neural fate. Planar cell polarity signalling drives convergent extension, the narrowing and lengthening of the plate without which the folds cannot meet; mutations in PCP genes such as VANGL1/2 cause severe closure defects. The transcription factor Pax3, Shh signalling from the notochord and folate-dependent metabolism all influence closure.

Clinical significance

Failure of neural tube closure produces neural tube defects: failure at the cranial end causes anencephaly, and failure at the caudal end causes spina bifida. Such defects occur in roughly 1 in 1,000 pregnancies worldwide; periconceptional supplementation with folic acid substantially reduces their incidence, which is why many countries fortify flour with folic acid and advise women of childbearing age to take supplements.
