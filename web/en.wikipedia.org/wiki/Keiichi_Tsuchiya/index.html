Keiichi Tsuchiya

Keiichi Tsuchiya (土屋圭市, Tsuchiya Keiichi, born 30 January 1956) is a Japanese retired professional racing driver. He is known as the Drift King (ドリキン, Dorikin) for his role in popularising drifting as a motorsport, and for his mastery of the Toyota AE86 Sprinter Trueno. He is also recognised for his trademark green racing overalls.

Career

Tsuchiya began as an illegal street racer on mountain passes (touge) before entering professional racing in 1977 in the Fuji Freshman series. He competed in the Japanese Touring Car Championship, NASCAR-sanctioned races in Japan, and the 24 Hours of Le Mans, where in 1999 his team finished second overall and won its class in a Toyota GT-One.

Drifting

Tsuchiya's drifting demonstrations, notably in the 1987 video Pluspy, helped inspire the first organised drifting competitions, which developed into the D1 Grand Prix, for which he served as a judge and adviser. He acted as stunt coordinator and technical adviser for the film The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift and made a cameo appearance.

Later activities

After retiring from professional racing in 2003, Tsuchiya remained active as a team executive, television presenter on programs such as Best Motoring, and ambassador for drifting worldwide.
