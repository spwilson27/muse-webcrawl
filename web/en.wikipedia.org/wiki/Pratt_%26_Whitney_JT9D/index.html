Pratt & Whitney JT9D

TypeHigh-bypass turbofan
ManufacturerPratt & Whitney
Major applicationsBoeing 747, 767; Airbus A300; DC-10

A JT9D on the wing of a Boeing 747

The Pratt & Whitney JT9D is a high-bypass turbofan engine developed for the Boeing 747, entering service in 1970 as the first high-bypass turbofan to power a wide-body airliner. It was later used on the Boeing 767, Airbus A300 and A310, and McDonnell Douglas DC-10.

Design

The JT9D introduced technologies that defined modern airliner engines: a single-stage fan of wide-chord blades, a bypass ratio around 5:1, and thrust ratings growing from roughly 43,500 lbf to over 56,000 lbf in later variants. Development difficulties during the 747 programme, including ovalisation of the engine casing under load, were resolved with structural stiffening.
