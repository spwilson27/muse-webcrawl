The Agusan Marsh is a large freshwater wetland in the province of Agusan del Sur on the island of Mindanao in the Philippines. Fed by the Agusan River and its tributaries, the marsh acts as a natural flood-retention basin for the Agusan River basin, absorbing floodwaters during the rainy season. It is one of the most ecologically significant wetlands in the Philippines and was declared a wildlife sanctuary; it is also listed as a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Ecology

The marsh is a mosaic of freshwater swamp forest, sago palm stands, floating vegetation and open lakes. It supports a rich variety of wildlife, including migratory and resident waterbirds, the Philippine crocodile and the saltwater crocodile, and numerous species of fish important to local livelihoods. Endangered species recorded in the area include the Philippine eagle in surrounding forests and various endemic birds.

People

The marsh is the ancestral domain of the Manobo people, some of whom famously live in floating houses that rise and fall with the water level — entire communities of houses built on rafts of bamboo and logs tethered in the lakes. Fishing, gathering and small-scale farming in the marsh support these communities.

Conservation

Threats to the marsh include conversion of surrounding land to plantations, logging, and hunting. The Agusan Marsh Wildlife Sanctuary was proclaimed in 1996, and conservation programmes work with the Manobo communities on sustainable management.
