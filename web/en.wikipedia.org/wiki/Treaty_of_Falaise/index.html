Treaty of Falaise

SignedDecember 1174
LocationFalaise, Normandy
PartiesHenry II of England, William the Lion of Scotland

The Treaty of Falaise was an agreement made in December 1174 between the captive William I, King of Scots (William the Lion), and Henry II, King of England.

Background

William had invaded northern England in support of the Revolt of 1173–74 against Henry II, led by Henry's own sons. On 13 July 1174, William was surprised and captured at the Battle of Alnwick by a party of English knights while besieging Alnwick Castle, and was taken in chains to Newcastle, then to Northampton, and finally to Falaise in Normandy, where Henry was based.

Terms

In return for his freedom, William was forced to acknowledge Henry as his feudal superior, surrender key Scottish castles — Roxburgh, Berwick, Jedburgh, Edinburgh, and Stirling — to English garrisons at Scottish expense, and subject the Scottish Church to the English Church. The people of Scotland were made subject to a tax to defray the cost of the occupation of the castles.

Aftermath

The treaty humiliated Scotland and remained in force for fifteen years. In 1189, Richard I of England, needing money for the Third Crusade, agreed to terminate the treaty in return for a payment of 10,000 marks under the Quitclaim of Canterbury, restoring Scottish independence in practice.
