Seattle Slew (February 15, 1974 – May 7, 2002) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse who won the Triple Crown in 1977 — the tenth of thirteen horses to achieve the feat and the only one to do so while undefeated. Purchased for only $17,500 as a yearling, a modest price for a champion, he became one of the most successful sires of the late 20th century.

Racing career

Owned by Karen and Mickey Taylor and Jim and Sally Hill, and trained by Billy Turner, Seattle Slew was voted champion two-year-old of 1976. At three he won the Wood Memorial and then the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness Stakes and the Belmont Stakes in succession. His only defeats at four came in the Swaps Stakes (fourth) and the Paterson Handicap (second); he closed his career with a famous victory over fellow Triple Crown winner Affirmed in the 1978 Marlboro Cup and a win in the Stuyvesant Handicap carrying 134 pounds.

Stud career and legacy

At stud, notably at Three Chimneys Farm in Kentucky, Seattle Slew sired more than 100 stakes winners, including A.P. Indy, himself a leading sire and grandsire of Tapit, ensuring Seattle Slew a lasting influence on the breed. He died in his sleep on 7 May 2002, exactly 25 years after his Kentucky Derby victory, and was ranked ninth in Blood-Horse magazine's list of the top 100 American racehorses of the 20th century.
