Mark Kac (1914–1984) was a Polish-American mathematician whose main interests were probability theory and statistical mechanics. He is famous for his 1966 paper "Can One Hear the Shape of a Drum?"

Life

Kac studied at Lwów under Hugo Steinhaus and emigrated to the United States in 1938, joining Cornell University in 1939. He later moved to Rockefeller University (1961) and the University of Southern California (1981). His autobiography, Enigmas of Chance, appeared posthumously.

Work

With Paul Erdős he helped found probabilistic number theory, proving the Erdős–Kac theorem on the distribution of prime factors. His formula relating Brownian motion to the heat equation — the Feynman–Kac formula — underlies much of mathematical physics and mathematical finance. He also contributed to the study of phase transitions and random matrices.
