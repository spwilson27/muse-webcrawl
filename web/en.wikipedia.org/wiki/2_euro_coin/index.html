The 2 euro coin (€2) is the highest-value euro coin, worth two euros. It has been issued since the euro was introduced in 1999 (entering general circulation in 2002) and is minted by the member states of the eurozone as well as several microstates with monetary agreements with the European Union. Like all euro coins, it has a common European side and a national side chosen by the issuing country.

Design

The coin is bimetallic: its centre is made of nickel brass and its outer ring of copper-nickel, giving it a distinctive two-colour appearance, and at 8.5 grams it is the heaviest euro coin in circulation. The common side, designed by Belgian artist Luc Luycx, shows a map of Europe and the denomination. The original map design was updated in 2007 to show the enlarged European Union. The national sides carry designs unique to each country — portraits of monarchs, national emblems, artworks and landmarks.

The edge of the coin bears lettering that varies by country, such as the repeated "2 **" pattern or national mottos, both as an identification aid and a security feature.

Commemorative issues

The €2 denomination is the standard denomination for commemorative coins issued by eurozone states. Each country may normally issue two commemorative €2 coins per year, which are legal tender throughout the eurozone. Notable joint issues include coins marking the 50th anniversary of the Treaty of Rome (2007), ten years of Economic and Monetary Union (2009), ten years of euro cash (2012), and 35 years of the Erasmus programme (2022). Some commemoratives, such as certain Monaco issues, are highly prized by collectors.
