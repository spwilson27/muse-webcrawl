R.E.M. was an American rock band formed in Athens, Georgia, in 1980 by drummer Bill Berry, guitarist Peter Buck, bassist Mike Mills and lead vocalist Michael Stipe. Pioneers of alternative rock, they rose from the U.S. college-radio underground to become one of the world's biggest bands, releasing fifteen studio albums before amicably disbanding in 2011. Worldwide sales are estimated at more than 90 million records.

History

The band's debut single "Radio Free Europe" (1981) and album Murmur (1983), on I.R.S. Records, established their signature sound — Buck's arpeggiated Rickenbacker guitar, Mills's melodic bass and backing vocals, and Stipe's mumbled, enigmatic lyrics. Through the 1980s, albums such as Reckoning (1984), Lifes Rich Pageant (1986) and Document (1987, with the hit "The One I Love") built a mass audience. Signing to Warner Bros., they achieved global superstardom with Out of Time (1991) — containing "Losing My Religion" and "Shiny Happy People" — and the critically revered Automatic for the People (1992), with "Everybody Hurts" and "Man on the Moon".

Berry suffered a brain aneurysm on tour in 1995 and left the band amicably in 1997; R.E.M. continued as a trio with albums including Up (1998), Reveal (2001) and Accelerate (2008). After Collapse into Now (2011) they announced their split that September. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2007, and all four members performed together publicly for the first time in 17 years at the Songwriters Hall of Fame in June 2024.

Legacy

R.E.M.'s success demonstrated that an independent-minded band could reach arenas without abandoning its sound, paving the way for the 1990s alternative boom; acts from Nirvana and Radiohead to The National cite them as influences.
