4th Armored Division (United States)

Shoulder patch of the 4th Armored Division — a red, yellow, and blue triangle with the numeral 4.

The 4th Armored Division was an armored division of the United States Army that earned distinction fighting in the European Theater of World War II. Activated on 15 April 1941 at Pine Camp, New York, it became one of the most celebrated American armored formations of the war, serving as the spearhead of General George S. Patton's Third Army.

World War II

The division landed in Normandy on 11 July 1944 and led the breakout of Operation Cobra, racing across Brittany and then France. Under commanders such as Major General John Shirley Wood — and later John J. Gaffey and Hugh Gaffey's successors — its combat commands (CCA, CCB, CCR) became famous for speed and improvisation. In September 1944 it fought the tank battle of Arracourt, blunting German counterattacks around Lorraine. In December 1944 the division attacked north through hard winter fighting, and on 26 December its Combat Command R reached Bastogne, helping relieve the besieged 101st Airborne during the Battle of the Bulge. In spring 1945 the division crossed the Rhine, liberated the Ohrdruf concentration camp — the first Nazi camp liberated by American troops — and drove into Czechoslovakia by VE Day.

The division famously had no official nickname; a postwar contest produced "Breakthrough Division," though soldiers preferred the line that a name was unnecessary: "They shall be known by their deeds alone."

Later service

Reactivated during the Cold War, the 4th Armored Division served in West Germany from 1957 until 1971, headquartered at Göppingen, before its colors were transferred to the 1st Armored Division.
