Fort Zeelandia (Paramaribo)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Zeelandia is a fortress in Paramaribo, Suriname, on the left bank of the Suriname River. Originally built by English settlers in 1651 as Fort Willoughby, it was captured by the Dutch in 1667 and renamed for the province of Zeeland. The pentagonal brick fort became the nucleus around which Paramaribo grew and served as a barracks and, later, a prison.

History

The fort changed hands several times between Dutch and British forces in the 17th and 18th centuries. In the 20th century it was used as a prison and police station. In December 1982, during the military regime of Dési Bouterse, fifteen opponents of the government were murdered at the fort in the "December murders," a defining event in modern Surinamese history.

Today

Fort Zeelandia now houses the Suriname Museum (Stichting Surinaams Museum), with exhibitions on the country's history, and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of the Historic Inner City of Paramaribo.
