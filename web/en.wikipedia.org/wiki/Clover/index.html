Clover

Clover is the common name for plants of the genus Trifolium ("having three leaves"), a group of about 300 species in the legume family Fabaceae. Clovers are small annual or perennial herbs recognisable by their usually trifoliate leaves and dense heads of white, pink, or red pea-like flowers.

Agriculture

Like other legumes, clovers host symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules that fix atmospheric nitrogen, enriching the soil. White and red clover are among the world's most important forage legumes, sown in pastures for grazing and hay, and used as green manure and cover crops. Clover-rich pasture supports honey production — clover is a premier nectar plant for bees — but some clovers contain compounds that can cause bloat in cattle or, in the case of red clover's formononetin, reduced fertility in ewes.

Culture

The four-leaf clover, a rare mutation of the normally three-leafed plant, is a traditional symbol of good luck. The shamrock, a symbol of Ireland associated with Saint Patrick, is generally identified as a young clover (usually T. dubium or white clover). Clover also figures in lawns, where attitudes have shifted from treating it as a weed to valuing it as a low-input, pollinator-friendly ground cover.
