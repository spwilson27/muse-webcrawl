Citadel of Erbil

The Citadel of Erbil (Kurdish: قەڵای هەولێر) is a fortified settlement on top of an inhabited tell in the historic centre of Erbil, in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. It is claimed to be among the oldest continuously inhabited sites in the world.

Erbil Citadel
Location: Erbil, Kurdistan Region, Iraq
Area: c. 11 hectares
Height of tell: 25–30 m
UNESCO World Heritage: 2014

History

The mound accumulated through occupation possibly stretching back to the Neolithic / Ubaid period, with Erbil (ancient Arbela) attested in Sumerian and Assyrian sources. The citadel figured in the Battle of Gaugamela's aftermath (Arbela, 331 BC) and passed through Assyrian, Median, Persian, Arab, Ottoman and other hands. Its single main gate, great mosque, hammam and courtyard houses reflect mainly Ottoman-era fabric.

Conservation

Residents were relocated in the 2000s for restoration by the High Commission for Erbil Citadel Revitalization with UNESCO support. It was inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2014.
