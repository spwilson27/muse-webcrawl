Jahangirnagar University (Bengali: জাহাঙ্গীরনগর বিশ্ববিদ্যালয়) is a public research university in Bangladesh, located in Savar, about 32 kilometres (20 mi) northwest of Dhaka. It is one of the leading universities in the country and is notable for being a fully residential university, with halls of residence for its students spread across a large, green campus.

History

The university was established in 1970 under the Jahangirnagar Muslim University Ordinance, and classes began on 4 January 1971 with four departments: Economics, Geography, Mathematics, and Statistics. It was formally inaugurated on 12 January 1971 by its first chancellor, Rear Admiral S. M. Ahsan, then Governor of East Pakistan. The word "Muslim" was dropped from its name in 1973, when it became simply Jahangirnagar University.

The name derives from the Mughal-era name of Dhaka, Jahangirnagar, given to the city by Islam Khan I in honour of the emperor Jahangir. Although the university stands outside the historic city, the name was chosen to preserve that heritage.

Campus

The campus covers roughly 697.56 acres of undulating land beside the Dhaka–Aricha highway, near the National Martyrs' Memorial at Savar. It is known for its lakes, which attract migratory birds in winter, and for its abundant greenery. Landmarks include the Shahid Minar (martyr monument), which is among the tallest in Bangladesh, an open-air amphitheatre, the central library, and numerous sculptures and murals.

Academics

The university is organised into faculties including Mathematical and Physical Sciences, Biological Sciences, Social Sciences, Arts and Humanities, Business Studies, and Law, together with institutes such as the Institute of Information Technology, the Institute of Business Administration, and the Institute of Remote Sensing and GIS. It offers undergraduate, postgraduate, MPhil and PhD programmes.

Student life

As a fully residential university, students live in halls on campus. The university has a lively cultural scene, with theatre groups, debating societies and an annual cultural week. The campus is also an informal bird sanctuary, and bird fairs are held during the winter migration season.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include economists, playwrights and actors such as Selim Al Deen, a leading figure in Bengali theatre who taught in the Department of Drama and Dramatics, and numerous academics, civil servants and public figures in Bangladesh.
