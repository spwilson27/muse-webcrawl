Jain cosmology

Jain cosmology is the description of the shape and functioning of the universe and its constituents (such as living beings, matter, space, time and principles of motion) according to Jainism. Jain cosmology holds that the universe is eternal and uncreated: it has always existed, was not made by any creator deity, and its basic constituents — souls (jīva) and matter (pudgala) among them — are uncreated and indestructible, changing only in their forms or states.

Shape of the universe

The universe (loka) is described as having the shape of a man standing with legs apart and arms resting on his waist. It is narrow at the top, widens in the middle region, becomes narrow again, and widens toward the bottom. The inhabited universe is surrounded by an uninhabited space (aloka) in which no souls, matter, or principles of motion exist. The whole universe is traditionally reckoned to be 14 rajju high.

Three realms

  • Urdhva Loka, the upper world — the heavens, inhabited by celestial beings (devas) in a series of layered abodes; at its very top lies the Siddhashila.
  • Madhya Loka, the middle world — the realm of humans and animals, consisting of concentric ring-shaped continents and oceans around the central continent Jambudvipa, with Mount Meru at its centre.
  • Adho Loka, the lower world — the hells, a series of seven regions inhabited by hellish beings (nārakī) suffering torments according to their karma.

Siddhashila

At the apex of the universe, above the highest heaven, is the Siddhashila, a crescent-shaped region where liberated souls (siddhas) reside eternally in bliss, free of body, birth and death. Souls rise to it upon attaining moksha, having exhausted all karma.

Time

Time (kāla) in Jain cosmology is eternal and is conceived as a wheel (kālacakra) with twelve spokes, endlessly rotating through ascending and descending half-cycles — utsarpinī, an era of increasing prosperity and happiness, and avasarpinī, an era of declining fortunes. Each half-cycle is divided into six eras (ārā). The present age is within an avasarpinī in its fifth era. Twenty-four tirthankaras appear in each half-cycle in the regions of Bharata and Airavata.

Constituents of reality

Jain metaphysics describes six eternal substances (dravya): souls (jīva), matter (pudgala), space (ākāśa), the medium of motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma) and time (kāla). Living beings are classified by their number of senses, from one-sensed beings such as plants and elemental bodies up to five-sensed beings such as humans, animals, gods and hell-beings.
