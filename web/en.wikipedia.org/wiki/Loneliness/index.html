Loneliness

Loneliness is an unpleasant emotional response to perceived or actual social isolation. Loneliness is often described as the discrepancy between the social connections a person has and the connections they would like to have. It is also described as social pain — a psychological mechanism to motivate the individual to seek social connections. Loneliness is distinct from solitude: people can feel lonely even in the company of others, and others can be content while alone.

Types and causes

Researchers distinguish emotional loneliness (the absence of an intimate attachment figure), social loneliness (the absence of an engaging social network), and existential loneliness. Causes and risk factors include bereavement, divorce, moving home, retirement, chronic illness, disability, and life transitions such as starting university. Some studies suggest a partially heritable component to susceptibility.

Health effects

Chronic loneliness is associated with increased risk of cardiovascular disease, depression, anxiety, cognitive decline and dementia, weakened immune responses, and premature mortality; its effect on mortality has been compared in size to that of smoking and obesity. These findings have prompted public health responses, including the appointment of a UK Minister for Loneliness in 2018 and a 2023 advisory by the US Surgeon General on the "epidemic of loneliness and isolation".

Prevalence

Surveys in many countries find that a substantial minority of adults report feeling lonely at least sometimes, with young adults and the very old among the loneliest groups. The COVID-19 pandemic's lockdowns increased reported loneliness worldwide.

Interventions

Approaches shown to help include cognitive behavioural therapy addressing maladaptive social cognition, social prescribing, volunteering and community groups, befriending services, and interventions that increase meaningful social contact rather than contact alone.
