Nevşehirli Damat Ibrahim Pasha

Nevşehirli Damat Ibrahim Pasha (1666 – 1 October 1730) was an Ottoman statesman who served as Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire from 1718 to 1730 under Sultan Ahmed III. His tenure corresponds to the Tulip Period, an era of relative peace, cultural flowering and opening toward Europe.

Grand vizierate

Ibrahim Pasha married Fatma Sultan, the sultan's daughter — hence the epithet Damat ("son-in-law"). He pursued a policy of peace after the Treaty of Passarowitz (1718), promoted diplomacy with European states, encouraged printing (supporting İbrahim Müteferrika's press), and patronised architecture, gardens and the tulip culture that gave the period its name.

Downfall

Public discontent over taxation, extravagance and the war with Persia erupted in the Patrona Halil rebellion of 1730. Ahmed III was deposed, and Ibrahim Pasha was handed over to the rebels and executed.
