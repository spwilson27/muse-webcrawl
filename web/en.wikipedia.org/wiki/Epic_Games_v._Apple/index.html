CourtU.S. District Court for the Northern District of California
DecidedSeptember 10, 2021
JudgeYvonne Gonzalez Rogers
Full nameEpic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.

Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc. was a lawsuit brought by Epic Games against Apple in August 2020 in the United States District Court for the Northern District of California, related to Apple's practices in the iOS App Store. Epic Games specifically challenged Apple's restrictions on apps from having other in-app purchasing methods outside of the one offered by the App Store, from which Apple takes a 30% revenue cut.

Background

On August 13, 2020, Epic updated its game Fortnite on iOS to include a direct payment option that bypassed Apple's in-app purchase system and offered players a discount. Apple removed Fortnite from the App Store the same day for violating store guidelines; Epic immediately filed suit, together with a public-relations campaign including the "Nineteen Eighty-Fortnite" video parodying Apple's famous "1984" advertisement. Apple later terminated Epic's developer account. Epic sought injunctive relief to return to the store under its own payment terms; the court granted temporary protection for Epic's Unreal Engine accounts but did not order Fortnite's restoration.

Trial and ruling

A bench trial was held in May 2021 before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, featuring testimony from Epic CEO Tim Sweeney and Apple CEO Tim Cook. On September 10, 2021, the court ruled largely in Apple's favour on the federal antitrust claims, finding that Epic had not proven Apple was a monopolist in the relevant market of digital mobile gaming transactions — though the court described Apple's conduct as borderline and found the market power concerns real. However, the court ruled for Epic under California's Unfair Competition Law: Apple's anti-steering provisions, which prevented developers from informing users of other payment options, were anticompetitive, and the court issued a permanent injunction against them.

Aftermath

Both sides appealed. The Ninth Circuit largely affirmed the judgment in April 2023, and in January 2024 the U.S. Supreme Court declined to hear either appeal, leaving the ruling in effect. Apple implemented changes allowing developers to link to external payment options, while charging commissions on those purchases — an approach Epic challenged as malicious compliance; in 2025 the district court found Apple had willfully violated the injunction and barred it from charging such commissions on external purchases in the United States, referring the matter for possible criminal contempt proceedings. Fortnite returned to the U.S. iOS App Store in May 2025. The case became a landmark in debates over app-store regulation worldwide, parallel to enforcement under the European Union's Digital Markets Act.
