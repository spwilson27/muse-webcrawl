Henry Hallam

Henry Hallam (9 July 1777 – 21 January 1859) was an English historian. The author of View of the State of Europe during the Middle Ages (1818), Constitutional History of England (1827), and Introduction to the Literature of Europe in the Fifteenth, Sixteenth, and Seventeenth Centuries (1837–39), he was one of the most respected Whig historians of the nineteenth century, noted for his judicial temper and learning.

Life and work

Hallam was educated at Eton and Christ Church, Oxford, and was called to the bar, though he soon devoted himself to historical writing. His Middle Ages surveyed European society from the fall of Rome to the opening of the sixteenth century; his Constitutional History treated English politics from the accession of Henry VII to the death of George II from a Whig standpoint, emphasising the growth of liberty under law; and his Literature of Europe was among the first comprehensive English histories of European literature and learning.

His eldest son, Arthur Henry Hallam, who died young in 1833, was the close friend of Alfred Tennyson and the subject of Tennyson's great elegy In Memoriam A.H.H.
