Tau Day


Tau equals the circumference divided by the radius

Tau Day is an unofficial holiday celebrated on 28 June (6/28 in month/day notation) by proponents of the mathematical constant tau (τ), defined as the ratio of a circle's circumference to its radius, equal to 2π ≈ 6.283185… Advocates argue that τ is the more natural circle constant than π, since a full turn is τ radians and many formulas simplify when expressed with τ.

Background

The case for tau was made by Bob Palais in his 2001 essay "π Is Wrong!" and popularised by Michael Hartl's Tau Manifesto, first published on 28 June 2010, which fixed the date of the celebration. Palais proposed the "three-legged" symbol later adopted as τ, while Hartl championed the Greek letter tau, partly because of its similarity to "turn" and the Chinese concept of the Tao.

Celebration

Fans mark Tau Day by eating twice as much pie as on Pi Day (14 March), a joke central to the manifesto's slogan that "on Tau Day you get to eat two pies." Mathematics communicators, schools and online communities post essays, videos and songs about circle constants. The day complements rather than replaces Pi Day in the view of many celebrants, and the π–τ debate is a recurring light-hearted feature of popular mathematics writing.

Use of tau

The constant appears in some textbooks, programming languages and calculators: Python's math module includes math.tau, and the symbol is used in a number of physics and engineering contexts, though π remains dominant in formal literature.
