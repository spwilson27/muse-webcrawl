Saclay

Saclay is a commune in the Essonne department in Île-de-France in northern France, about 19 km southwest of Paris. It sits on the Saclay plateau, a fertile agricultural plateau between the valleys of the Yvette and the Bièvre, and is best known as a major centre of French science and technology.

Science and education

The Commissariat à l'énergie atomique (CEA) established its Saclay research centre here in the early 1950s; the site hosted early French nuclear reactors and remains one of the country's largest research campuses, covering fundamental physics, life sciences and energy research. Since the 2010s the plateau has been developed as the Paris-Saclay cluster, bringing together the University of Paris-Saclay — ranked among the world's leading research universities — and grandes écoles including École Polytechnique (at nearby Palaiseau), CentraleSupélec, ENS Paris-Saclay and AgroParisTech, alongside corporate research laboratories. The development, one of France's flagship grands projets, aims to create a "French Silicon Valley" while preserving much of the plateau's farmland under a protection zone.

Geography

The plateau is drained by a celebrated network of ponds and channels built in the 17th century under Louis XIV to supply the fountains of Versailles, including the Étang de Saclay. The commune's population is roughly 4,000, though the wider Paris-Saclay campus hosts tens of thousands of students and researchers.
