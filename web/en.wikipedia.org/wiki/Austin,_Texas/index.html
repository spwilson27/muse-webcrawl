Austin, Texas

Austin is the capital city of the U.S. state of Texas and the seat of Travis County. Located in Central Texas on the Colorado River at the edge of the Texas Hill Country, it is one of the fastest-growing large cities in the United States, with a population of about 980,000 in the city and over 2.4 million in its metropolitan area. Austin is known as a centre for technology and business, as the "Live Music Capital of the World", and for its unofficial slogan "Keep Austin Weird".

History

The village of Waterloo was chosen in 1839 as the capital of the Republic of Texas and renamed for Stephen F. Austin, the republic's secretary of state. It remained the state capital after Texas joined the Union in 1845. The present Texas State Capitol, completed in 1888 in sunset-red granite, is taller than the U.S. Capitol. The University of Texas at Austin was founded in 1883 and grew into one of the largest public universities in the country. In the later twentieth century the city developed a major technology sector — nicknamed "Silicon Hills" — attracting firms such as IBM, Dell (founded in Austin in 1984), and later Tesla, Oracle and many startups.

Culture

Austin's music scene, built on hundreds of live venues, is celebrated by the long-running PBS series Austin City Limits (from 1974) and the festivals South by Southwest (SXSW) and Austin City Limits Music Festival. The city is also known for the Mexican free-tailed bat colony under the Congress Avenue Bridge, for Barton Springs Pool, and for a food culture ranging from barbecue and breakfast tacos to a prominent food-truck scene.

Economy and government

Government, education and technology anchor the economy. As state capital, Austin hosts the Texas Legislature and state agencies; the University of Texas at Austin is among the largest employers. Rapid growth has brought challenges including housing affordability, traffic and water management.
