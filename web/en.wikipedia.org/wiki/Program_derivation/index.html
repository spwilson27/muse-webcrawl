Program derivation

In computer science, program derivation is the construction of a program together with a proof of its correctness, developed hand in hand. Starting from a formal specification, the programmer applies correctness-preserving transformation steps — refinement steps — gradually turning the specification into efficient executable code. The approach is associated with Edsger W. Dijkstra and is sometimes called the "calculational" style of programming.

Approach

Program derivation typically works within a framework of predicate transformer semantics, using weakest preconditions to calculate the conditions under which program statements establish their postconditions. Guards and loop invariants are derived rather than guessed: the invariant is often obtained by weakening the postcondition, for example by replacing a constant with a variable.

Related work

The method is closely related to formal methods, program refinement, and program correctness. Tools and languages such as Dafny and the refinement calculus of Ralph-Johan Back and others support derivation mechanically.
