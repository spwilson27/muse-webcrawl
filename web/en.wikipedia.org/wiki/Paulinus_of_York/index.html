Paulinus (died 10 October 644) was a Roman missionary and the first Bishop of York. A member of the Gregorian mission sent from Rome by Pope Gregory the Great, he came to England in 601 as part of the second group of missionaries. He accompanied Æthelburh of Kent when she married King Edwin of Northumbria in 625, and succeeded in converting Edwin and large numbers of the Northumbrian people to Christianity.

Life

Paulinus was probably an Italian monk sent by Gregory in 601 to reinforce Augustine's mission in Kent. Bede describes him as a tall man with a slight stoop and black hair. Little is recorded of his first two decades in England, during which he presumably worked in Kent.

Mission to Northumbria

In 625 Paulinus was consecrated bishop and travelled north with the Kentish princess Æthelburh, who was betrothed to the pagan King Edwin on condition that she could practise her faith and that Paulinus could preach. After deliberation — including the famous council scene retold by Bede, in which a councillor compared human life to a sparrow flying through a fire-lit hall — Edwin accepted baptism at York on Easter Day 627, in a small wooden church on the site of the later York Minster. Paulinus baptised crowds of Northumbrians in the rivers Swale, Glen, and Trent, and founded churches, including one at Lincoln where he consecrated Honorius as Archbishop of Canterbury in 627 or 628. In 631 he received the pallium from Pope Honorius I, intending him to be archbishop of York.

Fall and later life

Edwin was killed at the Battle of Hatfield Chase in 633 by Cadwallon of Gwynedd and Penda of Mercia. Paulinus fled by sea to Kent with Queen Æthelburh and Edwin's children, leaving the deacon James behind to sustain the mission at York. In Kent, Paulinus became Bishop of Rochester, where he died on 10 October 644. He was canonised, with his feast day on the date of his death.
