The Theatre Royal, Drury Lane is a West End theatre in Covent Garden, London. The site has housed a theatre since 1663, making it London's oldest theatre site in continuous use; the present building, designed by Benjamin Dean Wyatt, opened in 1812 and seats roughly two thousand.

History

The first theatre was built under Thomas Killigrew with a royal patent from Charles II after the Restoration. Successive buildings were designed by Christopher Wren (1674) and Henry Holland (1794). The theatre is associated with David Garrick, who managed it from 1747 and made it the centre of Shakespeare production; with the actor-manager era of Richard Brinsley Sheridan, owner when the Holland building burned down in 1809 (Sheridan is said to have sat drinking wine watching the fire, remarking on his right to enjoy the blaze at his own fireside); and with Victorian spectacle under Augustus Harris. In the twentieth century it hosted Ivor Novello's operettas and then long runs of American musicals, including Oklahoma! and My Fair Lady, and later Miss Saigon.

Ghosts

Drury Lane is reputedly one of the most haunted theatres in the world, most famously by the "Man in Grey", an eighteenth-century figure in a tricorn hat seen in the upper circle, regarded as a good omen for productions; a skeleton with a dagger in its ribs, said to be his, was reportedly found bricked up in a wall during nineteenth-century alterations.
