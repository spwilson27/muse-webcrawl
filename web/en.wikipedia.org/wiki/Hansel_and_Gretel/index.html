"Hansel and Gretel" (German: Hänsel und Gretel) is a German fairy tale collected by the Brothers Grimm and published in 1812 in Grimm's Fairy Tales. It tells of a brother and sister abandoned in a forest who fall into the hands of a cannibalistic witch living in a house made of bread and cake.

Plot

During a famine, a woodcutter's wife persuades him to abandon his children in the forest. Hansel, overhearing, marks the first trail with pebbles and leads Gretel home; on the second attempt his breadcrumb trail is eaten by birds and the children are lost. They find a house of bread with sugar windows, whose owner, an old woman, lures them inside and imprisons Hansel in a cage to fatten him, enslaving Gretel. When the witch prepares her oven, Gretel tricks her into leaning in and pushes her to her death. The children escape with the witch's treasure and return home to their father, the stepmother having died.

Analysis and adaptations

Folklorists classify the tale as Aarne–Thompson type 327A. Its famine setting has been linked to the Great Famine of 1315–1317. In successive editions Wilhelm Grimm softened elements, turning the children's mother into a stepmother. The tale inspired Engelbert Humperdinck's opera Hänsel und Gretel (1893) and numerous films, ballets and retellings.
