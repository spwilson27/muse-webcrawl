Spectrochemical series

The spectrochemical series is an empirically derived list of ligands ordered by the size of the crystal field splitting (Δ) they produce in coordination complexes. It is used in crystal field theory and ligand field theory to predict the absorption spectra, magnetic properties, and spin states of transition-metal complexes.

The series

A common arrangement of ligands from weakest field (small Δ, "weak-field" ligands) to strongest field (large Δ) is:

I− < Br− < S2− < SCN− < Cl− < F− < OH− < oxalate < H2O < NCS− < EDTA4− < NH3 < ethylenediamine < bipyridine < phenanthroline < NO2− < PPh3 < CN− < CO < NO+

Interpretation

Weak-field ligands favour high-spin configurations because the splitting is smaller than the electron pairing energy, whereas strong-field ligands such as cyanide and carbon monoxide produce large splittings and low-spin complexes. The ordering reflects a mixture of σ-donor strength and π-bonding effects: halides act as π donors and reduce Δ, while CO and CN− are strong π acceptors that increase it.

A related ordering exists for metal ions, whose field strength increases roughly with oxidation state and down a group: Mn2+ < Ni2+ < Co2+ < Fe2+ < V2+ < Fe3+ < Co3+ < Mn4+ < Mo3+ < Rh3+ < Ru3+ < Pd4+ < Ir3+ < Pt4+.

The series was developed from absorption-spectroscopy studies of complexes, notably the work of Ryōgo Tsuchida in the 1930s, and remains a standard qualitative tool in inorganic chemistry, complemented by the nephelauxetic series, which orders ligands by covalency effects.
