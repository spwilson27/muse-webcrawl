Cosmos: A Personal Voyage

Cosmos: A Personal Voyage is a thirteen-part 1980–81 television documentary series written by Carl Sagan, Ann Druyan and Steven Soter, with Sagan as presenter. Produced by KCET in Los Angeles and broadcast on PBS, it covered a wide range of scientific subjects, including the origin of life and a perspective of humanity's place in the universe.

Content

Using devices such as the "Spaceship of the Imagination" and the Cosmic Calendar — which compresses the 13.8-billion-year history of the universe into a single year — the series surveyed astronomy, evolution, the history of science and the dangers of nuclear war. Episodes include "The Shores of the Cosmic Ocean", "Harmony of the Worlds" (on Kepler), "The Persistence of Memory" (on the brain) and "Who Speaks for Earth?". The accompanying music featured works by composers including Vangelis.

Impact

Cosmos won Emmy and Peabody awards, became the most widely watched series in the history of American public television at the time, and has been seen by hundreds of millions of viewers in dozens of countries. Sagan's companion book Cosmos (1980) was a bestseller. The series was followed decades later by the sequel series hosted by Neil deGrasse Tyson: Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey (2014) and Cosmos: Possible Worlds (2020).
