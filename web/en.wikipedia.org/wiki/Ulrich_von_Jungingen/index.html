Ulrich von Jungingen (c. 1360 – 15 July 1410) was the 26th Grand Master of the Teutonic Order, serving from 1407 until his death in battle at Grunwald (Tannenberg) at the head of the Order's army — the greatest defeat in the Order's history.

Career in the Order

Ulrich came to Prussia around 1383, following his elder brother Konrad. He rose through the Order's offices — Komtur of Balga, then of Mewe — and on Konrad's death in 1407 was elected Grand Master. Where Konrad had kept peace with Poland and Lithuania, Ulrich pursued confrontation: he purchased the Neumark question into crisis, seized the Dobrzyń Land, and answered Polish protests with war in 1409.

Grunwald and death

In the summer of 1410 the combined armies of Władysław II Jagiełło of Poland and Grand Duke Vytautas of Lithuania invaded Prussia. On 15 July 1410, near the villages of Grunwald and Tannenberg, Ulrich declined to attack the enemy in their positions and waited in the heat; when battle joined, the Lithuanian wing's real or feigned flight drew off part of his army, and the Polish heavy cavalry broke the Order's line. Ulrich refused to flee and was killed leading a charge of sixteen banners. Jagiełło had his body honourably returned to Marienburg.

Aftermath

Some 200 brother-knights died with him, decapitating the Order. Henry von Plauen saved Marienburg, but the Peace of Thorn and the ensuing indemnity began the Order's long decline in Prussia.
