Peerage of England

The Peerage of England comprises all peerages created by the sovereigns of England before the Act of Union 1707 united England and Scotland into Great Britain. Peers of England thereafter sat in the House of Lords of Great Britain, and later of the United Kingdom.

Ranks

The peerage is divided into five ranks, in descending order: duke, marquess, earl, viscount, and baron. The earliest English dukedom was created in 1337 for Edward, the Black Prince, as Duke of Cornwall; the marquessate followed in 1385. Most English peerages are hereditary and descend in the male line, though some were created with special remainders.

History

The medieval peerage grew out of the barons summoned by writ to the king's councils. Creations by letters patent became standard from the fourteenth century. After 1707 no further English peerages were created; new creations were in the Peerage of Great Britain and, from 1801, of the United Kingdom. Hereditary peers no longer automatically sit in the House of Lords since the House of Lords Act 1999, though 92 remain elected from among them.
