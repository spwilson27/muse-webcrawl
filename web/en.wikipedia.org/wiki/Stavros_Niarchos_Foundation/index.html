The Stavros Niarchos Foundation (SNF; Greek: Ίδρυμα Σταύρος Νιάρχος) is a private international philanthropic organization established in 1996 from the estate of Greek shipping magnate Stavros Niarchos (1909–1996). It makes grants worldwide in the areas of arts and culture, education, health and sports, and social welfare, and is one of the world's largest private foundations. Since its founding it has committed several billion dollars through thousands of grants in well over 100 countries.

Major projects

The foundation's best-known project is the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Cultural Center (SNFCC) in Kallithea, Athens, designed by architect Renzo Piano and completed in 2016 at a cost of roughly €600 million, funded entirely by the foundation and then donated to the Greek state. Built on a landscaped artificial hill above the Faliro Bay, it houses the National Library of Greece and the Greek National Opera, surrounded by the Stavros Niarchos Park.

In response to the Greek government-debt crisis, the foundation funded extensive social welfare programs, including food aid, youth unemployment initiatives, and support for public hospitals. International projects include the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Library in New York (the renovated central circulating branch of the New York Public Library, opened 2021), major support for Johns Hopkins University — including the Stavros Niarchos Foundation Agora Institute — and extensive COVID-19 relief grants exceeding $150 million. The SNF Dialogues and the annual SNF Nostos conference in Athens convene discussions on democracy, health, and technology.
