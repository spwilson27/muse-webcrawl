The Arctic char or Arctic charr (Salvelinus alpinus) is a cold-water fish in the family Salmonidae, native to alpine lakes and coastal waters of the circumpolar North. It is the most northerly distributed freshwater fish and the only fish found in many high-Arctic lakes. No other freshwater fish is found as far north. Both anadromous (sea-run) and landlocked populations occur, the latter often relicts stranded in deep, cold lakes after the retreat of the glaciers.

Description

Arctic char are highly variable in colour and size. Sea-run fish are silvery with pale spots on a darker back; at spawning time, especially males, they turn brilliant orange-red on the belly and flanks. Anadromous individuals may reach 85 cm and 15 kg, while landlocked dwarf forms may mature at under 20 g. The species feeds on zooplankton, insect larvae, molluscs, and smaller fish, and individual lakes often host distinct morphs specialized for different diets and depths.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges across northern North America, Greenland, Iceland, Scandinavia, Siberia, and deep alpine lakes of central Europe, as well as isolated relict populations in Ireland and Britain, where it is known as the Windermere charr and related local forms. It requires cold, well-oxygenated water and is highly sensitive to warming, acidification, and competition from introduced species.

Relationship to humans

Arctic char is an important subsistence and commercial fish for northern Indigenous peoples and is raised in aquaculture, notably in Iceland and Canada, where its flavour — intermediate between salmon and trout — commands a premium. Many southern landlocked populations are considered vulnerable and are protected.
