Ferrari S.p.A. is an Italian luxury sports car manufacturer based in Maranello, Italy. Founded by Enzo Ferrari in 1939 from the Alfa Romeo racing division as Auto Avio Costruzioni, the company built its first car bearing the Ferrari name in 1947. Ferrari is the most successful racing team in history through its Scuderia Ferrari division — the only team to have competed in every Formula One World Championship season since 1950 — and its road cars are globally recognized symbols of speed, luxury, and wealth. The company's famous symbol is the prancing horse (il Cavallino Rampante) on a yellow shield.

History

Origins and early years

Enzo Ferrari, a former Alfa Romeo driver, founded Scuderia Ferrari in 1929 as a racing team running Alfa Romeo cars. After leaving Alfa in 1939 he established Auto Avio Costruzioni, which built one car (the Tipo 815) before wartime restrictions intervened. The first true Ferrari, the 125 S with a 1.5-litre V12 designed by Gioacchino Colombo, debuted in 1947. Ferrari soon established the pattern of selling exclusive road cars to finance racing, winning the Mille Miglia in 1948 and Le Mans in 1949.

Growth and the Fiat era

Through the 1950s and 1960s, models such as the 250 series — including the 250 GTO, now among the most valuable cars in the world — cemented Ferrari's reputation. In 1963 Henry Ford II attempted to buy Ferrari; Enzo famously walked out of negotiations, prompting Ford's GT40 programme. In 1969 Fiat acquired a 50% stake, increased to 90% after Enzo's death in 1988. The last car Enzo personally approved was the F40 (1987), a raw twin-turbocharged supercar built for the company's 40th anniversary.

Modern era

Under long-serving chairman Luca di Montezemolo (1991–2014), Ferrari's Formula One team returned to dominance with Michael Schumacher, winning five consecutive drivers' titles (2000–2004); road-car production and profitability also expanded. In 2015–16 Fiat Chrysler Automobiles spun Ferrari off as an independent public company (ticker RACE). Recent models include the hybrid hypercar LaFerrari (2013), the SF90 Stradale plug-in hybrid, and the Purosangue (2022), the brand's first four-door, four-seat production car. Ferrari deliberately limits production to preserve exclusivity, yet has grown output to over 13,000 cars a year.

Motorsport

Scuderia Ferrari is Formula One's oldest and most successful team, holding records for constructors' championships (16), drivers' championships (15), and race wins. Its drivers have included Juan Manuel Fangio, Niki Lauda, Gilles Villeneuve, and Michael Schumacher. Ferrari also competes at the top level of endurance racing: after a 50-year absence it returned to the premier class of the 24 Hours of Le Mans with the 499P hypercar, winning the race in 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Brand and business

Ferrari consistently ranks among the world's strongest luxury brands, licensing its name to merchandise, theme parks (Ferrari World Abu Dhabi, Ferrari Land in Spain), and the Ferrari Owners' clubs network. The prancing horse emblem originated with First World War flying ace Francesco Baracca, whose mother suggested Enzo adopt it; the yellow background is the colour of Modena, near Maranello.
