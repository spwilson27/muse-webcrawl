The Isère (French pronunciation: [izɛʁ]) is a river in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-eastern France, a left-bank tributary of the Rhône. It rises in the Alps on the border with Italy and flows generally west for 286 kilometres, passing Albertville, Grenoble and Romans-sur-Isère before joining the Rhône a few kilometres north of Valence. It gives its name to the French department of Isère.

Course

The Isère takes its source from glaciers below the Col de l'Iseran and the Grande Aiguille Rousse in the Vanoise massif, near the Italian frontier above Val-d'Isère. It flows down the Tarentaise valley through Bourg-Saint-Maurice and Moûtiers — a valley famous for its ski resorts — receives the Doron de Bozel, and turns west at Albertville (host of the 1992 Winter Olympics), where the Arly joins it. Below Albertville it runs through the Combe de Savoie, is joined by the Arc from the Maurienne valley, then enters the broad Grésivaudan valley between the Chartreuse and Belledonne ranges and flows through Grenoble, the principal city on its banks. Downstream it passes Romans-sur-Isère and Tain-l'Hermitage country before its confluence with the Rhône near Pont-de-l'Isère in the Drôme.

Hydrology and use

The river has a nivo-glacial regime, with high flows in late spring and early summer from snowmelt and glacier melt. It is heavily developed for hydroelectricity, notably the Tignes (Chevril) dam near its source and the Grand'Maison scheme, and its gravel terraces and canals support irrigation in the lower valley. The Isère is notorious for floods at Grenoble — the great flood of 1859 reshaped the city's defences — and its name for the river's sudden violence survives in the local expression serpent et dragon for the Isère and its tributary the Drac. At Grenoble the river hosted the rowing and other water sports venues associated with the 1968 Winter Olympics era development of the city, and an artificial slalom course at Bourg-Saint-Maurice on the upper river is a leading international canoe-slalom venue, hosting world championships.
