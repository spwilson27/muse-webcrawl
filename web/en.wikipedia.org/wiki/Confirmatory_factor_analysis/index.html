Confirmatory factor analysis

A CFA path diagram: latent factors (ovals) measured by observed indicators (rectangles)

In statistics, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) is a special form of factor analysis, most commonly used in social science research. It is used to test whether measures of a construct are consistent with a researcher's pre-specified understanding of the nature of that construct (or factor). In contrast to exploratory factor analysis (EFA), in which the structure is derived from the data, the researcher using CFA specifies in advance which observed variables load on which latent factors, and tests whether the data fit this hypothesised measurement model.

The method was developed principally by the Swedish statistician Karl Gustav Jöreskog in the late 1960s and implemented in the LISREL software, becoming a central component of structural equation modeling (SEM).

The model

For each observed indicator x, the measurement model states x = λξ + δ, where ξ is the latent factor, λ the factor loading, and δ measurement error. In matrix form, x = Λxξ + δ. The model implies a covariance matrix Σ(θ) that can be compared with the observed sample covariance matrix. Identification requires fixing the scale of each latent variable, for example by fixing one loading per factor to 1 or fixing the factor variance to 1.

Estimation and fit

Parameters are usually estimated by maximum likelihood, with robust corrections or weighted least squares (WLSMV) used for ordinal indicators. Because the chi-square test of exact fit is highly sensitive to sample size, approximate fit indices are routinely reported, including:

  • CFI and TLI — comparative fit indices; values ≥ .95 are conventionally taken as good fit.
  • RMSEA — root mean square error of approximation; values ≤ .06 suggest close fit.
  • SRMR — standardised root mean square residual; values ≤ .08 are desirable.

Competing models can be compared by chi-square difference tests (for nested models) or information criteria such as AIC and BIC. Modification indices suggest parameters whose freeing would most improve fit, though purely data-driven respecification risks overfitting.

Applications

CFA is widely used in psychology and the social sciences to validate questionnaires and tests — for example, to verify that items of a personality inventory measure the intended dimensions — and to test measurement invariance across groups or over time (configural, metric and scalar invariance) before comparing latent means. It is implemented in software such as Mplus, LISREL, AMOS, Stata, and the open-source R package lavaan.
