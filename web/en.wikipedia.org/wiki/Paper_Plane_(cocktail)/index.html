Paper Plane (cocktail)

TypeCocktail
Base spiritBourbon whiskey
ServedStraight up, in a coupe
IngredientsEqual parts bourbon, Aperol, Amaro Nonino, fresh lemon juice (typically ¾ oz / 22 ml each)
Created2008, by Sam Ross

The Paper Plane is a modern cocktail made with equal parts bourbon, Aperol, Amaro Nonino and fresh lemon juice, shaken with ice and strained into a coupe glass. It was created in 2008 by Australian bartender Sam Ross for the opening of The Violet Hour in Chicago, and it has since become one of the most widely served contemporary classics.

History

Ross developed the drink as a variation on the Last Word, another equal-parts cocktail, taking its name from the M.I.A. song "Paper Planes", which was popular at the time. The original recipe used Campari, but Ross substituted the gentler, less bitter Aperol as the drink spread. Amaro Nonino, a grape-based Italian amaro with orange and tamarind notes, is considered essential to its balance of bitter, sweet, sour and herbal flavours. The drink is sometimes garnished with a miniature paper plane.
