Retinoscopy

Retinoscopy (also called skiascopy) is a technique to obtain an objective measurement of the refractive error of a patient's eyes. The examiner uses a retinoscope to shine light into the patient's eye and observes the reflection (reflex) off the patient's retina. While moving the streak or spot of light across the pupil the examiner observes the relative movement of the reflex, then uses trial lenses to "neutralize" the reflex.

Technique

Retinoscopy is performed in a dimly lit room, with the patient fixating on a distant target. A moving reflex that travels in the same direction as the light ("with" movement) indicates the eye is farsighted, myopic by less than the working distance, or emmetropic; movement in the opposite direction ("against" movement) indicates greater myopia. Plus or minus lenses are added until the reflex fills the pupil and no movement is seen (neutrality), at which point the lens power, adjusted for the working distance, gives the refractive error.

Uses

Retinoscopy is particularly valuable for measuring refractive error in patients who cannot cooperate with subjective refraction, such as young children and people with communication difficulties. It also reveals irregularities of the cornea and lens, and is used with cycloplegic drops to paralyse accommodation in children.
