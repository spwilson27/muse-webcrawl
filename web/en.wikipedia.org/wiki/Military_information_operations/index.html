Military information operations

Military information operations (often abbreviated IO) are the integrated employment of information-related capabilities — including electronic warfare, computer network operations, psychological operations, military deception and operations security — to influence, disrupt, corrupt or usurp the decision-making of adversaries while protecting one's own. The concept emerged in the 1990s as militaries recognised information itself as a domain and instrument of warfare.

Component capabilities

United States doctrine has historically grouped information operations around core capabilities: psychological operations (PSYOP), renamed military information support operations (MISO), which seek to influence foreign audiences; military deception, which misleads adversary decision-makers; operations security (OPSEC), which denies information to the enemy; electronic warfare, which contests the electromagnetic spectrum; and computer network operations, including attack, defence and exploitation. Supporting activities include public affairs, civil-military operations and combat camera.

Doctrinal development

The term grew out of command-and-control warfare concepts of the early 1990s and was formalised in US joint publications such as JP 3-13. In 2017 the US Army elevated "information operations" thinking into the broader concept of operations in the information environment, and other states — notably Russia, with its emphasis on "information confrontation", and China, with its "three warfares" — developed their own doctrines stressing media, psychological and legal influence. The rise of social media has blurred the line between military information operations and civilian information spaces, raising legal and ethical debates about targeting and blowback.
