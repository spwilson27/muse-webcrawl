Pterocarpus

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Fabales
Family: Fabaceae
Subfamily: Faboideae
Tribe: Dalbergieae
Genus: Pterocarpus Jacq.

Pterocarpus is a pantropical genus of trees in the family Fabaceae. The name derives from the Greek words pteron (wing) and karpos (fruit), referring to the unusual winged seed pods characteristic of the genus, which aid wind dispersal. Members of the genus are variously known as padauks, bloodwoods or rosewoods, and several species yield highly prized timber.

Species and distribution

The genus contains roughly 35–46 accepted species, distributed through the tropics of Africa, Asia and the Americas. Well-known species include:

  • Pterocarpus angolensis — muninga or kiaat, from southern Africa, whose wood exudes a dark red sap;
  • Pterocarpus indicus — narra, amboyna or Philippine mahogany, the national tree of the Philippines;
  • Pterocarpus marsupium — Indian kino tree, source of a traditional Ayurvedic remedy;
  • Pterocarpus santalinus — red sandalwood or red sanders, from southern India;
  • Pterocarpus soyauxii — African padauk, valued for its bright red-orange wood;
  • Pterocarpus macrocarpus — Burma padauk.

Uses

Pterocarpus timbers are dense, durable and richly coloured, ranging from golden yellow through orange-red to deep crimson, and are used for fine furniture, cabinetry, musical instruments, flooring and turnery. Several species produce kino, a red astringent resin used traditionally as a dye and in medicine. Because of heavy exploitation for the international timber trade, many species are threatened and are listed under CITES, which regulates their trade.

Ecology

Like most legumes, Pterocarpus species form root nodules with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, improving soil fertility. The trees are components of tropical dry forests, savannas and monsoon forests, and their flowers provide nectar for pollinating insects.
