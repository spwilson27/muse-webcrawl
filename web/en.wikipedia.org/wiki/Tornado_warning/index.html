Tornado warning

Issued byNational Weather Service (United States)
CriteriaTornado sighted or indicated by weather radar
RelatedTornado watch, Severe thunderstorm warning

A tornado warning is a severe weather statement issued by weather forecasting agencies to warn the public that severe thunderstorms with tornadoes are imminent or occurring. It can be issued after a tornado, funnel cloud and rotation in the clouds has been spotted by the public, storm chasers, emergency management or law enforcement, and/or indicated by Doppler weather radar. A tornado warning is the highest level of tornado alert used by the United States National Weather Service (NWS); it is more severe than a tornado watch.

Criteria

The National Weather Service issues a tornado warning when a tornado has been sighted or indicated by weather radar, or when a confirmed tornado is expected to develop. Warnings are also issued for severe thunderstorms capable of producing tornadoes. The typical warning polygon covers a portion of one or more counties and is generally in effect for 30 to 60 minutes, though particularly dangerous situation (PDS) warnings and tornado emergency declarations can be issued for confirmed, large and destructive tornadoes threatening populated areas.

Particularly dangerous situation

A particularly dangerous situation (PDS) tornado warning is an enhanced form of tornado warning issued by some National Weather Service offices when there is an exceptionally high confidence of a large, long-lived and violent tornado. PDS warnings were first used during the Super Outbreak of April 3, 1974, in experimental form, and became more commonly used in the 2000s.

Tornado emergency

A tornado emergency is an unofficial, enhanced wording used in tornado warnings when a confirmed, large and destructive tornado is expected to cause catastrophic damage in a populated area. The term was coined during the Bridge Creek–Moore tornado of May 3, 1999, when meteorologist David Andra at the National Weather Service office in Norman, Oklahoma, issued the first tornado emergency for the Oklahoma City metropolitan area.

Public response

When a tornado warning is issued, residents in the warned area are advised to take shelter immediately: go to the lowest floor of a sturdy building, to an interior room away from windows, such as a basement, storm cellar, bathroom or closet. Mobile home residents are advised to move to a nearby sturdy shelter. Local media, NOAA Weather Radio, wireless emergency alerts and outdoor warning sirens are used to disseminate warnings.
