Indo-Trinidadian and Tobagonian

Indo-Trinidadians and Tobagonians are nationals of Trinidad and Tobago of Indian ancestry, descended from indentured labourers brought from India by the British between 1845 and 1917. Together with Afro-Trinidadians they are one of the country's two largest ethnic groups, making up roughly a third or more of the population.

History

The first ship, the Fatel Razack, arrived on 30 May 1845 — a date commemorated as Indian Arrival Day. About 147,000 Indians came to Trinidad during the indenture period, most from the Bhojpuri-speaking regions of northern India. Labourers worked mainly on sugar estates; after indenture many acquired land, sometimes in lieu of return passage, and built villages across the island's sugar belt.

Culture

Indo-Trinidadian culture has shaped the national life of Trinidad and Tobago: Hindu, Muslim and Presbyterian traditions coexist; festivals such as Diwali, Hosay and Phagwah are public celebrations; and Indo-Trinidadian contributions include chutney and chutney soca music, tassa drumming, and foods such as doubles, roti and curry. Prominent Indo-Trinidadians include writer V. S. Naipaul and former prime ministers Basdeo Panday and Kamla Persad-Bissessar.
