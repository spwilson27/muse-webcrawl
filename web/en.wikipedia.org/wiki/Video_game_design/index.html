Video game design

Video game design is the process of designing the content and rules of video games in the pre-production stage and designing the gameplay, environment, storyline and characters in the production stage. Some common video game design subdisciplines are world design, level design, system design, content design, and user interface design. Within the video game industry, video game design is usually just referred to as "game design", which is a more general term elsewhere.

The video game designer is like the director of a film; the game designer is the visionary of the game and controls the artistic and technical elements of the game in fulfilment of their vision. However, with complex games, such as massively multiplayer online games or big-budget action or sports titles, designers may number in the dozens. In these cases, there are generally one or two principal designers and multiple junior designers who specify subsets or subsystems of the game.

Overview

As the industry has aged and embraced alternative production methodologies such as agile, the role of a principal game designer has begun to shift. In large companies, there may be separate designers for gameplay, mechanics, level layout, narrative, and more. Game design has traditionally been a hard-to-teach discipline because of its cross-functional nature.

Design process

Game design typically begins with a concept or idea, which is developed into a design document describing the game's mechanics, story, art style, and target audience. Designers then prototype gameplay systems, iterate through playtesting, and refine the design during production. Key concerns include player experience, difficulty balancing, reward structures, and the "game feel" of controls and feedback.

Level design

Level design involves creating stages, maps, and missions. Level designers use specialized editors to lay out terrain, place objects and enemies, and script events, shaping the pacing and challenge of the player's journey through the game.
