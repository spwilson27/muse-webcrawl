Steel wool

Steel wool, also known as iron wool, wire sponge, or wire wool, is a bundle of very fine and flexible sharp-edged steel filaments. It is used as an abrasive in finishing and repairing work for polishing wood or metal objects, cleaning household cookware, cleaning windows without a scratch, and sanding surfaces.

Description and manufacture

Steel wool is made from low-carbon steel wire that is drawn through a die with toothed edges, shaving off thin, ribbon-like filaments, which are collected and wound into pads or rolls. It is graded from the coarsest grades (No. 3 and 4) through medium grades to the finest (No. 0000, "four ought"), the finer grades being used for polishing and for work on delicate surfaces such as French polish and varnish.

Uses

Steel wool is commonly used by woodworkers, metalworkers and craftsmen for cleaning, smoothing and polishing. With soap it is sold as scouring pads for pots and pans. Pest controllers use it to block holes against rodents, since most pests cannot chew through it. Fine grades are also used by photographers and in light painting, since steel wool ignites readily and burns with a shower of sparks; it should never be used near flammable vapours or on live electrical terminals, as it is conductive and flammable.
