Chlorobenzene

Formula: C6H5Cl
Molar mass: 112.56 g/mol
Appearance: Colorless liquid
Boiling point: 131 °C

Chlorobenzene (abbreviated PhCl) is an aromatic organic compound with the chemical formula C6H5Cl. This colorless, flammable liquid is a common solvent and a widely used intermediate in the manufacture of other chemicals.

Production

Chlorobenzene is produced by chlorination of benzene in the presence of a catalytic amount of a Lewis acid such as ferric chloride, sulfur dichloride, or anhydrous aluminium chloride. It was first prepared and described in 1851.

Uses

Historically, chlorobenzene was used in the production of DDT via reaction with chloral, and as an intermediate for phenol manufacture by the Dow process (hydrolysis with aqueous sodium hydroxide at high temperature and pressure). Today its main applications are as an intermediate for herbicides, dyestuffs, rubber chemicals, and nitrochlorobenzenes, and as a high-boiling solvent.

Safety

Chlorobenzene is of moderate toxicity and is a persistent environmental pollutant; exposure mainly affects the central nervous system and the liver and kidneys.
