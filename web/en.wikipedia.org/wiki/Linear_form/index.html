Linear form

In mathematics, a linear form (also known as a linear functional or covector) is a linear map from a vector space to its field of scalars. In Euclidean space, linear forms can be represented as dot products with a fixed vector.

Definition

If V is a vector space over a field k, then a linear form f on V is a function satisfying f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y) and f(ax) = a f(x) for all x, y in V and all scalars a. The set of all linear forms on V forms a vector space called the dual space, denoted V*.

Examples

Integration against a fixed function gives a linear form on spaces of functions. In finite dimensions, every linear form on Rn has the form f(x) = a1x1 + ... + anxn for some coefficients ai. Linear forms are the rank-one case of multilinear and tensor concepts, and play a central role in differential geometry as differential one-forms.
