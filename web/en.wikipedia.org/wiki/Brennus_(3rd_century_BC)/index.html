Brennus (3rd century BC)

Brennus (died 279 BC) was a chieftain of the Gauls, one of the leaders of the great Gallic invasion of the Balkans in 279 BC. His army defeated the Macedonians, penetrated into central Greece, and attacked the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi. Parts of the invading force later crossed into Anatolia, where their descendants, the Galatians, settled. Brennus should not be confused with the earlier Brennus who led the Gauls that sacked Rome around 390 BC.

The invasion

Beginning around 281 BC, Gallic tribes moved south through the Balkans. In 279 BC the invasion divided into several columns; Brennus — together with Bolgios and others, according to later sources — commanded the force that advanced into Macedonia, where King Ptolemy Keraunos was defeated and killed. Brennus then led the main army south through Thermopylae, outflanking the Greek defenders, and on to Delphi, the richest sanctuary in Greece.

Delphi and death

Ancient accounts, including Pausanias and Justin, describe the attack on Delphi as repelled with divine assistance: storms, rockfalls from Mount Parnassus, and panic struck the Gauls, and the defenders counterattacked. Brennus was wounded; during the retreat he died of his wounds — the sources say he drank unmixed wine and took his own life to avoid capture, or died of his injuries. Whatever the truth of the miraculous details, the expedition failed to take Delphi and the surviving Gauls withdrew north. The Greeks commemorated the deliverance with the Soteria festival; the impress of the invasion persisted in art in the famous sculptures of dying Gauls.

Legacy

A related band under Leonnorius and Lutarius crossed into Asia Minor in 278–277 BC at the invitation of Nicomedes I of Bithynia, founding the Galatian settlement in central Anatolia. The name Brennus — possibly a title meaning "king" rather than a personal name — binds the two Gallic invasions of Greece and Rome together in classical memory.
