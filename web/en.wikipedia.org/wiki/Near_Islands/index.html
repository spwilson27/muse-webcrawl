Near Islands

The Near Islands (Russian: Ближние острова) are the westernmost group of islands in the Aleutian Islands chain of Alaska, United States. They are the Aleutian group closest to Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula, from which their name derives — they are "near" to Asia.

Geography

The largest islands in the group are Attu and Agattu. Between them lie smaller islands in the Semichi group, including Alaid, Nizki, and Shemya. The Near Islands are separated from the Rat Islands to the east by the Aleutian passes. The islands are volcanic in origin, treeless, and subject to the harsh maritime weather of the North Pacific and Bering Sea.

History

The islands were named by Russian explorers in the 18th century during the maritime fur trade, when Russian promyshlenniki reached the archipelago from Kamchatka. The Unangax̂ (Aleut) people had inhabited the islands for thousands of years before European contact.

During the Second World War, Attu became the only incorporated American territory invaded and occupied by enemy forces: Japanese troops landed in June 1942, and the Battle of Attu in May 1943 was among the bloodiest of the Pacific theatre. The island's village was never resettled. Shemya hosted a military air station during the Cold War.

Wildlife

The Near Islands form part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge. Agattu and other islands have been the focus of programs to eradicate introduced foxes, restoring habitat for seabirds and for the reintroduced Aleutian cackling goose.
