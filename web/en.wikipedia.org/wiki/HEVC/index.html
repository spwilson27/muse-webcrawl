High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC), also known as H.265 and MPEG-H Part 2, is a video compression standard designed as part of the MPEG-H project, and the successor to H.264/MPEG-4 AVC. It was developed jointly by the ISO/IEC Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG) and the ITU-T Video Coding Experts Group (VCEG) as ISO/IEC 23008-2 and ITU-T H.265. The first version was ratified in January 2013 and published in June 2013.

Features

HEVC offers substantially improved compression over H.264, typically delivering equivalent subjective quality at about half the bit rate. Its main innovations include coding tree units of up to 64×64 pixels (replacing 16×16 macroblocks), improved intra-picture prediction with 35 directional modes, more flexible motion compensation, improved entropy coding using context-adaptive binary arithmetic coding, and support for resolutions up to 8192×4320 (8K UHD) in its profiles.

Profiles and tiers

The standard defines profiles including Main, Main 10 (with 10-bit colour depth) and Main Still Picture, and two tiers (Main and High) across thirteen levels. Later versions added multiview, range-extension and scalable (SHVC) profiles.

Adoption and licensing

HEVC is used in Ultra HD Blu-ray, in many streaming services, in Apple's ecosystem (where it is the standard codec for photos and videos on iOS), in DVB broadcast standards and in the 4K broadcasts of numerous operators. Its adoption on the open web was slowed by its patent-licensing structure, which involved multiple patent pools (MPEG LA, HEVC Advance/Access Advance and Velos Media) as well as unaffiliated patent holders.
