An adventure game is a video game in which the player assumes the role of a protagonist in an interactive story driven by exploration and puzzle solving. Unlike action games, adventure games rely on narrative, character interaction and logic puzzles rather than reflexes or combat, and they rarely allow the player to die permanently or lose through poor timing.

History

The genre began with Colossal Cave Adventure (1976), a text-only game written by Will Crowther and expanded by Don Woods, which inspired the commercial text adventures of Infocom such as Zork (1980). Sierra On-Line introduced graphics with Mystery House (1980) and the King's Quest series, combining a parser with on-screen graphics.

In the late 1980s Lucasfilm Games (later LucasArts) replaced the text parser with point-and-click interfaces in games such as Maniac Mansion (1987), The Secret of Monkey Island (1990) and Day of the Tentacle (1993), while Sierra continued with series such as Space Quest and Leisure Suit Larry. The CD-ROM era produced the best-selling Myst (1993), a first-person pre-rendered adventure.

Decline and revival

Commercial adventure games declined in the late 1990s as action-oriented 3D titles dominated, but the genre survived through European developers, handheld platforms and Japanese visual novels, and revived in the 2000s and 2010s through episodic releases by Telltale Games (The Walking Dead, 2012), crowdfunded projects such as Broken Age, and acclaimed independent titles including Return of the Obra Dinn and Disco Elysium.

Characteristics

Adventure games typically feature inventory-based puzzles, dialogue trees, environmental storytelling and extensive written or voiced narrative. Challenges are mental rather than physical, and the pace allows the player to think indefinitely before acting.
