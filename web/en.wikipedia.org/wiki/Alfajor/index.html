Alfajor

Alfajor
TypeConfectionery / biscuit sandwich
RegionLatin America, Spain; origins in al-Andalus
Main ingredientsShortbread biscuits, dulce de leche (or jam/honey), chocolate or sugar coating

An alfajor (Spanish: [alfaˈxoɾ], plural alfajores) is a traditional confection found in Spain and throughout Latin America, consisting of two round sweet biscuits joined with a filling — most famously dulce de leche — and often coated in chocolate, meringue or powdered sugar.

Origins

The name derives from the Arabic al-fakhur ("the excellent"), and the sweet travelled from Moorish al-Andalus into Spanish baking, where in Andalusia it survives as a Christmas confection of flour, honey, almonds and spices. Spanish colonists brought the alfajor to the Americas, where it evolved most distinctively in the Río de la Plata region.

Regional varieties

In Argentina and Uruguay the classic alfajor pairs soft crumbly biscuits with a thick dulce de leche centre, dipped in dark or white chocolate. Argentina is among the world's largest consumers, with famous brands such as Havanna, founded in Mar del Plata in 1948. Other varieties include the Peruvian king kong of Lambayeque, cornflour (maicena) alfajores dusted with coconut, Chilean chilenitos, and fruit-filled versions with quince or membrillo paste.
