Erzurum Congress

Date: 23 July – 7 August 1919
Location: Erzurum, Ottoman Empire
Chairman: Mustafa Kemal Pasha

The Erzurum Congress (Turkish: Erzurum Kongresi) was an assembly of Turkish nationalist delegates held in Erzurum in eastern Anatolia from 23 July to 7 August 1919, during the opening phase of the Turkish War of Independence. Convened by regional Defence of Rights associations and chaired by Mustafa Kemal (later Atatürk), who had recently resigned his army commission, the congress is regarded as a foundational step in the creation of the Republic of Turkey.

Background

Following the Ottoman Empire's defeat in the First World War and the Armistice of Mudros (October 1918), Allied forces occupied parts of Anatolia, and Greek troops landed at Smyrna (İzmir) in May 1919. In response, local "Defence of Rights" societies formed to resist partition. Mustafa Kemal, sent to Samsun as an army inspector in May 1919, instead began organising national resistance, issuing the Amasya Circular, which declared that the nation's independence would be saved by the nation's own determination.

Resolutions

The roughly fifty delegates adopted resolutions that became core principles of the national movement: the territories inhabited by a Turkish-Muslim majority formed an indivisible whole and could not be partitioned; foreign occupation and interference would be resisted; if the Istanbul government proved unable to protect the country's independence, a provisional government would be formed; the national forces would be unified and the national will made supreme; and foreign protection or a mandate (such as the proposed American mandate) was rejected, though foreign aid without imperialist aims could be accepted. A nine-member Representative Committee (Heyet-i Temsiliye), headed by Mustafa Kemal, was elected to act as an executive for the movement.

Significance

The decisions of Erzurum were broadened and confirmed for the whole country by the Sivas Congress in September 1919, and their territorial principles were incorporated into the National Pact (Misak-ı Millî) adopted in 1920. The congress thus marked the shift from regional defence organisations to a unified national resistance with its own representative leadership.
