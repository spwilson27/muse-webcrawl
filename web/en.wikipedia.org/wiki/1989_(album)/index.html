1989 is the fifth studio album by the American singer-songwriter Taylor Swift. It was released on 27 October 2014 by Big Machine Records. Named after Swift's birth year, the album marked her deliberate transition from country music to pop, drawing on the synth-pop sound of the 1980s. Swift worked chiefly with producers Max Martin, Shellback and Jack Antonoff.

Background and release

After the country-pop crossover of Red (2012), Swift relocated to New York City and set out to make a cohesive pop record inspired by 1980s artists such as Peter Gabriel, Madonna and Annie Lennox. The album's cover, shot as a Polaroid-style photograph, reinforced the retro concept. 1989 sold 1.287 million copies in its first week in the United States — the fastest-selling album there in over a decade at the time — and it spent 11 weeks atop the Billboard 200.

Singles

The album produced seven singles, three of which reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100: "Shake It Off", "Blank Space" and "Bad Blood" (featuring Kendrick Lamar). Other singles included "Style", "Wildest Dreams", "Out of the Woods" and "New Romantics".

Reception and legacy

1989 won Album of the Year and Best Pop Vocal Album at the 58th Grammy Awards in 2016, making Swift the first woman to win Album of the Year twice as a lead artist. Critics praised its songwriting and production, and the album is widely credited with cementing Swift's status as a global pop star. The supporting 1989 World Tour became the highest-grossing tour of 2015. In 2023, Swift released a re-recorded version, 1989 (Taylor's Version), as part of her project to regain control of her master recordings; the re-recording included five previously unreleased "From the Vault" tracks, among them "Is It Over Now?", which topped the Hot 100.
