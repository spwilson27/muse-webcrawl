Twelfth Night

Twelfth Night, or What You Will is a romantic comedy by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written around 1601–1602 as a Twelfth Night entertainment for the close of the Christmas season. The play centres on the twins Viola and Sebastian, who are separated in a shipwreck; Viola, disguised as a young man called Cesario, falls into a tangle of mistaken identity and unrequited love in the court of Duke Orsino of Illyria.

Plot

Shipwrecked on the coast of Illyria and believing her twin brother Sebastian drowned, Viola disguises herself as the youth Cesario and enters the service of Duke Orsino. Orsino loves the Countess Olivia, who is in mourning and rejects his suit; sent to woo Olivia on Orsino's behalf, "Cesario" instead wins Olivia's love, while Viola herself falls for Orsino. Meanwhile Olivia's steward Malvolio is gulled by Sir Toby Belch, Maria and Sir Andrew Aguecheek into believing Olivia loves him, and is treated as a madman. Sebastian's arrival — alive and identical to "Cesario" — unravels the confusions: Sebastian marries Olivia, Viola reveals herself and marries Orsino, and Malvolio storms off vowing revenge.

Characters

  • Viola / Cesario — the shipwrecked heroine in male disguise
  • Orsino — Duke of Illyria, in love with Olivia
  • Olivia — a wealthy countess in mourning
  • Malvolio — Olivia's puritanical steward
  • Feste — Olivia's fool, singer of the play's famous songs, including "O Mistress Mine"
  • Sir Toby Belch and Sir Andrew Aguecheek — the comic revellers

Sources and legacy

Shakespeare drew on Italian novella traditions, notably Barnabe Riche's "Apolonius and Silla." The play, first published in the First Folio of 1623, remains one of his most performed comedies and has inspired adaptations from the film She's the Man to numerous musical versions.
