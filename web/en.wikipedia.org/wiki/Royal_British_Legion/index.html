The Royal British Legion (RBL), formerly the British Legion, is a British charity providing financial, social and emotional support to members and veterans of the British Armed Forces, their families and dependants. It is the United Kingdom's largest Armed Forces charity and custodian of national Remembrance.

History

The Legion was founded in 1921 by the merger of four ex-service organisations, championed by Field Marshal Douglas Haig, 1st Earl Haig, and Tom Lister. It received the "Royal" prefix in 1971 on its golden jubilee. Its founding principles included fighting for the rights of disabled and destitute veterans in the aftermath of the First World War.

Poppy Appeal and Remembrance

The Legion is best known for the annual Poppy Appeal, launched in 1921 under the influence of John McCrae's poem "In Flanders Fields": volunteers sell artificial remembrance poppies to raise funds in the weeks before Remembrance Sunday (the Sunday nearest 11 November, Armistice Day). The National Memorial Arboretum in Staffordshire is administered by the Legion.

Work

The Legion runs recovery centres (including Battle Back for wounded personnel), care homes, benefits advice and grants, and campaigns on issues from military compensation to veterans' mental health. Membership is open to all, not only veterans, organised through a network of local branches and clubs.
