Seven Factors of Awakening

In Buddhism, the Seven Factors of Awakening (Pali: satta bojjhaṅgā; Sanskrit: sapta bodhyanga) are qualities cultivated on the path to enlightenment (bodhi). They are among the thirty-seven bodhipakkhiyā dhammā, the "aids to awakening" enumerated in the earliest Buddhist texts. The Buddha described them as leading to knowledge and liberation when developed and cultivated.

Role in meditation

The factors are traditionally cultivated in sequence, beginning with mindfulness, which gives rise to investigation, energy, and joy, and culminating in tranquillity, concentration, and equanimity. Commentators group them into energising factors (investigation, energy, joy) and calming factors (tranquillity, concentration, equanimity), with mindfulness balancing the two: when the mind is sluggish the energising factors are emphasised, and when restless the calming factors.

Relation to the hindrances

The seven factors are presented as antidotes to the five hindrances (sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and remorse, and doubt), which obstruct meditation. Mindfulness is said to be "helpful in all cases."

Scriptural sources

The factors are expounded in the Bojjhaṅga Saṃyutta of the Saṃyutta Nikāya, including the well-known account in which the recitation of the factors was said to have aided the recovery of the elder monks Mahākassapa and Mahāmoggallāna, and of the Buddha himself. In Mahayana literature they appear in Sanskrit form as the sapta bodhyanga.
