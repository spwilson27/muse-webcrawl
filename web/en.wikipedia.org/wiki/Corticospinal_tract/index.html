Corticospinal tract

The corticospinal tract is a white matter motor pathway starting at the cerebral cortex and terminating in the spinal cord. It is involved in the voluntary control of movement, particularly fine, skilled movements of the limbs. It is also called the pyramidal tract, forming the medullary pyramids as it passes through the medulla oblongata.

Anatomy

The tract arises mainly from upper motor neurons in the primary motor cortex (Betz cells and other pyramidal neurons of layer V), descends through the posterior limb of the internal capsule, the cerebral peduncle and pons, and forms the pyramids of the medulla. At the pyramidal decussation, roughly 90% of fibres cross to the opposite side and descend as the lateral corticospinal tract; the remainder descend uncrossed as the anterior corticospinal tract, most crossing segmentally in the cord. Fibres terminate mainly on interneurons and some directly on lower motor neurons in the ventral horn.

Clinical significance

Damage produces upper motor neuron signs: weakness, spasticity, hyperreflexia and a positive Babinski sign. The tract is commonly affected in stroke (especially internal capsule infarcts), amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and multiple sclerosis.
