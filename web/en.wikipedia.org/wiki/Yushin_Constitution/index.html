Yushin Constitution

The Yushin Constitution (Korean: 유신 헌법, "Revitalising Reform Constitution") was the constitution of South Korea in force from 1972 to 1981. Promulgated under President Park Chung Hee, it established the Fourth Republic of Korea and concentrated sweeping powers in the presidency, inaugurating the authoritarian period known as the Yushin system.

Background

Park, who had held power since his 1961 military coup, narrowly won re-election in 1971 and then amended the constitution to allow a third term. On 17 October 1972 he proclaimed martial law, dissolved the National Assembly and announced "revitalising reforms", citing the need for national unity in the face of dialogue with North Korea and a changing international order. A referendum held under martial law on 21 November 1972 approved the new constitution, which took effect on 27 December 1972.

Provisions

The constitution removed presidential term limits and provided for the president to be elected indirectly by a government-dominated electoral college, the National Conference for Unification. The president could appoint one third of the National Assembly, dissolve the legislature, issue emergency decrees overriding civil liberties, and restrict the judiciary. Criticism of the constitution itself was criminalised, most notoriously under Emergency Decree No. 9 of 1975.

Opposition and end

The Yushin system provoked sustained protest by students, churches, journalists and opposition politicians such as Kim Young-sam and Kim Dae-jung. After Park's assassination on 26 October 1979 and Chun Doo-hwan's coup, a new constitution in 1980 established the Fifth Republic, formally ending the Yushin order. Historians generally regard the constitution as the legal framework of Park's dictatorship, and it remains a byword in Korean politics for authoritarian constitutional engineering.
