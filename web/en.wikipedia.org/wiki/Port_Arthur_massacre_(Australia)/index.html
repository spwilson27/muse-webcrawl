Port Arthur massacre (Australia)

The Port Arthur massacre was a mass shooting that took place on 28 April 1996 at Port Arthur, Tasmania, Australia. The gunman, Martin Bryant, killed 35 people and wounded 23 others at and around the Port Arthur Historic Site, a popular tourist attraction and former convict settlement. It remains the deadliest mass shooting in modern Australian history.

Events

Bryant began the attack at the Broad Arrow Café at the historic site, where he shot and killed many visitors and staff, before moving through the site and its surroundings, killing further victims including people at a nearby property and motorists. He then took a hostage and returned to a guest house, Seascape, where he held police at bay overnight; the building caught fire and Bryant was captured the following morning after emerging with burns.

Aftermath

Bryant pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 35 life sentences plus additional years without parole. He remains imprisoned in Tasmania.

Gun law reform

The massacre prompted a rapid and sweeping response from the Australian federal government under Prime Minister John Howard. Within weeks, the states and territories agreed to the National Firearms Agreement, which banned many semi-automatic rifles and shotguns, introduced uniform licensing and registration, and funded a large-scale buyback that collected and destroyed more than 650,000 firearms. The reforms are widely credited with a lasting reduction in firearm deaths in Australia.
