Distributor

An ignition distributor, a component of older petrol engines.

A distributor is a device in the ignition system of an internal combustion engine that sends voltage from the ignition coil to the spark plugs in the correct firing order. The term may also refer, in business, to a company that supplies goods to retailers, and in other contexts to a person or thing that distributes something. This article concerns the ignition component.

Operation

The distributor consists of a rotating arm, the rotor, mounted on top of a shaft driven by the camshaft. As the rotor turns, its tip passes close to contacts in the distributor cap, each connected by a spark plug wire to a spark plug. A breaker point or electronic switch interrupts the primary circuit of the coil at the precise moment, generating the high-voltage pulse that the rotor delivers to the correct cylinder. Many distributors also house mechanical and vacuum advance mechanisms that adjust ignition timing with engine speed and load.

Decline

From the 1980s onward, distributors were progressively replaced by distributorless ignition systems and coil-on-plug arrangements controlled by the engine management system, which offer more accurate timing and reduced maintenance. The business sense of the word is covered under distribution (marketing).
