Screening effect

In atomic physics, the screening effect (also called shielding) is the reduction of the effective nuclear charge experienced by an electron in a multi-electron atom, caused by the presence of other electrons between that electron and the nucleus. Because inner electrons repel an outer electron and partially cancel the attraction of the positively charged nucleus, the outer electron behaves as though the nuclear charge were Zeff, a value smaller than the true atomic number Z.

Consequences

Shielding explains trends in the periodic table: atomic radii increase down a group as new shells screen the valence electrons more strongly, ionisation energies vary across periods, and electrons in orbitals with greater penetration (s more than p more than d) are shielded less and held more tightly. Slater's rules give an empirical method for estimating the shielding constant S and hence Zeff = Z − S.
