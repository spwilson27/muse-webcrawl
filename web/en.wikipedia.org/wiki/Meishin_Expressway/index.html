Meishin Expressway

Route information

Length: 193.9 km (120.5 mi)

Existed: 1963–present

From: Komaki, Aichi

To: Nishinomiya, Hyōgo

Route number: E1

The Meishin Expressway (名神高速道路, Meishin Kōsoku-dōro) is an expressway in Japan, running from Komaki in Aichi Prefecture (near Nagoya) westward through Gifu, Shiga, Kyoto, and Osaka to Nishinomiya in Hyōgo Prefecture. Its name is formed from the characters for its endpoints, Nagoya (名古屋) and Kobe (神戸). Together with the Tōmei Expressway it forms the main road artery between Tokyo, Nagoya, and Osaka/Kobe.

History

The first section, between Amagasaki and Rittō, opened on 16 July 1963, making the Meishin Japan's first intercity expressway; the full route was completed in 1965, in time for growing traffic after the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and ahead of Expo '70 in Osaka. It is numbered E1 under Japan's expressway numbering system and forms part of Asian Highway 1 (AH1).

Route

The expressway passes Lake Biwa's eastern shore, intersects the Hokuriku Expressway at Maibara, crosses Kyoto, and connects with numerous other expressways including the Shin-Meishin Expressway, a newer parallel relief route opened in stages from 2008 to relieve congestion and provide redundancy after closures such as those during the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake.

Operation

The road is tolled and operated by Central Nippon Expressway Company (NEXCO Central) for its eastern portion and West Nippon Expressway Company (NEXCO West) for its western portion.
