Data scraping

Data scraping extracts information from human-readable output into structured form.

Data scraping is a technique in which a computer program extracts data from output generated by another program, most commonly human-readable content displayed by websites. Web scraping, the best-known form, involves fetching web pages and extracting information from their HTML, often converting unstructured content into structured records for a database or spreadsheet.

Techniques

Simple scrapers parse the raw HTML of a page with regular expressions or HTML parsers, selecting data by its position in the document structure. More sophisticated tools render pages in a headless browser so that content generated by JavaScript can be captured, and screen-scraping techniques extract data from terminal displays and legacy application interfaces. Where a site offers an API, structured data access through the API is generally more reliable than scraping.

Uses

Scraping is used for price comparison and monitoring, market research, lead generation, academic research, weather and travel data aggregation, and the collection of training data for machine-learning models.

Countermeasures and legality

Websites attempt to block automated scraping with CAPTCHAs, rate limits, IP blocking and honeypot links, while scrapers respond with rotating addresses and browser automation. The legal status of scraping is contested and varies by jurisdiction: it may implicate terms of service, copyright in databases, and computer-misuse laws such as the U.S. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, which the Supreme Court narrowed in Van Buren v. United States (2021) and which the Ninth Circuit addressed in the hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn litigation.
