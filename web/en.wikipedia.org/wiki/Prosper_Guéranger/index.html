Dom Prosper Louis Pascal Guéranger (4 April 1805 – 30 January 1875) was a French Benedictine monk and abbot who refounded monastic life in France after its suppression during the Revolution, restored the Abbey of Solesmes, and became the founding figure of the modern Liturgical Movement and of the restoration of Gregorian chant.

Life

Guéranger was born at Sablé-sur-Sarthe and ordained a priest in 1827, serving in Le Mans. Convinced that the revival of Catholicism in France depended on restoring its monastic and liturgical traditions, in 1833 he purchased the former priory of Solesmes — the last building of a monastery suppressed in the Revolution — and settled there with a small community. In 1837 he travelled to Rome, made his solemn profession at St Paul's Outside the Walls, and received from Pope Gregory XVI a brief erecting Solesmes as an abbey and motherhouse of a French Benedictine congregation, with himself as first abbot and superior general.

Work and influence

Guéranger campaigned tirelessly against the Gallican and neo-Gallican diocesan liturgies that had multiplied in France, arguing for unity with the Roman rite in his Institutions liturgiques (1840–1851). His most popular work, L'Année liturgique (The Liturgical Year, begun 1841), a multi-volume commentary on the feasts and seasons of the Church year, taught generations of clergy and laity to follow the Church's prayer. He was also an ardent ultramontane, defending papal authority and supporting the definition of papal infallibility at the First Vatican Council (1870).

At Solesmes he set monks to work comparing chant manuscripts, laying the foundations for the scientific restoration of Gregorian chant later completed by Dom Joseph Pothier and Dom André Mocquereau, and eventually embodied in the Vatican Edition of the chant books. The congregation he founded spread internationally, and Solesmes remains a centre of chant scholarship and performance.
