The Armenian alphabet is the writing system used for the Armenian language. It was created around 405 AD by the monk and scholar Mesrop Mashtots, with the support of Catholicos Sahak Partev and King Vramshapuh, expressly so that the Bible and Christian literature could be translated into Armenian.

Letters and history

The original alphabet had 36 letters, closely matching the phonology of Classical Armenian (Grabar). Two letters, Օ (O) and Ֆ (F), were added in the Middle Ages, bringing the total to 38–39; Soviet-era orthographic reform in the 1920s altered spelling conventions in eastern Armenia, while the diaspora largely retained classical orthography. Each letter also has a traditional numerical value, used historically as Armenian numerals.

Use and significance

The alphabet remains a potent symbol of Armenian identity, celebrated in monuments such as the letter park near Oshakan, where Mashtots is buried. The first sentence translated into Armenian — from the Book of Proverbs, "To know wisdom and instruction..." — inaugurated a golden age of Armenian literature in the 5th century.
