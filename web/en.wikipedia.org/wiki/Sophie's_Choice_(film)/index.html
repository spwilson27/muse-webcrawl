Sophie's Choice (film)

Sophie's Choice is a 1982 American drama film directed by Alan J. Pakula, who adapted William Styron's 1979 novel of the same name. The film stars Meryl Streep as Sophie Zawistowska, a Polish immigrant to the United States with a dark and devastating secret, Kevin Kline in his feature-film debut as her mercurial lover Nathan Landau, and Peter Macon as Stingo, the young aspiring writer who narrates the story.

Plot

In 1947 Brooklyn, Stingo moves into a boarding house and befriends Sophie and Nathan, whose passionate but unstable relationship fascinates and eventually alarms him. Through flashbacks, Sophie's past is gradually revealed: she is a survivor of Auschwitz, where, upon arrival, a Nazi officer forced her to choose which of her two children would live and which would die — the impossible "choice" that haunts her and gives the work its title.

Reception

Streep's performance is widely regarded as one of the greatest in film history; she won the Academy Award for Best Actress. The film was nominated for four additional Academy Awards. The phrase "Sophie's choice" has entered English usage for an impossibly cruel dilemma between two equally horrendous alternatives.
