Cheboksary (Russian: Чебоксары; Chuvash: Шупашкар, Shupashkar) is the capital city of the Chuvash Republic, Russia, and an important port on the Volga River. It lies about 650 kilometres east of Moscow and has a population of roughly half a million people.

History

The settlement was first mentioned in chronicles in 1469, when Russian troops paused there on their way to the Khanate of Kazan. A fortress was established in 1555, and Cheboksary developed as a trading town on the Volga. In the twentieth century the city grew rapidly with Soviet industrialisation, and the construction of the nearby Cheboksary hydroelectric station, completed in the early 1980s, created a large reservoir and reshaped the waterfront, including the artificial Cheboksary Bay in the city centre.

Economy and culture

Cheboksary is a centre for electrical engineering — the region is known for the production of relays and power-industry equipment — as well as machinery, textiles, and brewing. The city is home to Chuvash State University, the Chuvash National Museum, and the Chuvash State Opera and Ballet Theatre. A colossal statue known as Mother Patroness (Annene Piresh), erected in 2003, overlooks the bay and has become a symbol of the city.
