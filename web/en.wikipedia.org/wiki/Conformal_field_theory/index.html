Conformal field theory

A conformal field theory (CFT) is a quantum field theory that is invariant under conformal transformations. In two dimensions, there is an infinite-dimensional algebra of local conformal transformations, making two-dimensional conformal field theories enormously powerful and comparatively well understood, while in higher dimensions conformal field theory is more restricted and the subject of the modern conformal bootstrap programme.

Applications

Conformal field theories have important applications in theoretical physics and in the study of critical phenomena in statistical mechanics. They describe the critical points of phase transitions (for example, the two-dimensional Ising model is a CFT), and outside statistical mechanics they are central to string theory: the worldsheet theory of a string is a two-dimensional CFT. Since the late 1990s, CFTs have also been a key ingredient of the AdS/CFT correspondence, in which a conformal field theory in d dimensions is dual to gravity in (d+1)-dimensional anti-de Sitter space.

Structure

The basic objects of a CFT are local operators characterized by scaling dimensions and spins; their correlation functions are strongly constrained by conformal symmetry, with two- and three-point functions fixed up to constants. The operator product expansion organizes the theory's data, and in two dimensions the Virasoro algebra, parameterized by the central charge, governs the symmetry. Rational conformal field theories, such as the minimal models, have only finitely many primary fields and are largely solvable.
