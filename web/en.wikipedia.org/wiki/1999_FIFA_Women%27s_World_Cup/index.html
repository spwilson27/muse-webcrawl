1999 FIFA Women's World Cup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1999 FIFA Women's World Cup was the third FIFA Women's World Cup, held in the United States from 19 June to 10 July 1999. It was won by the host nation, the United States, and is widely regarded as a landmark moment that transformed the popularity and visibility of women's football worldwide.

Tournament

The tournament expanded to 16 teams. The United States topped its group, beat Germany in the quarter-finals and Brazil in the semi-finals. Norway, the defending champions, were eliminated by China in the other semi-final, setting up a final between the United States and China at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena.

Final

Played before 90,185 spectators — then the largest crowd ever for a women's sporting event — the final finished 0–0 after extra time. Key moments included Kristine Lilly heading a Chinese shot off the goal line in extra time. In the penalty shoot-out, goalkeeper Briana Scurry saved Liu Ying's kick, and Brandi Chastain converted the winning penalty, celebrating by whipping off her jersey — an image that became one of the most famous in sports photography.

Legacy

The tournament drew total attendance of over 1.19 million and large television audiences, demonstrated the commercial viability of the women's game, and helped launch the first American professional women's league, the WUSA, in 2001. Sun Wen of China won the Golden Ball and shared the Golden Boot with Brazil's Sissi.
