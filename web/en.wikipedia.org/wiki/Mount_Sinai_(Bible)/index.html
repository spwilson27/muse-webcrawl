Mount Sinai (Bible)

Mount Sinai (Hebrew: הַר סִינַי, Har Sinai) is the mountain at which, according to the Hebrew Bible, God gave Moses the Ten Commandments during the Exodus of the Israelites from Egypt. In the biblical narrative the giving of the Law at Sinai is the foundational covenant event of ancient Israel. The Bible also calls the mountain Horeb ("the mountain of God"), particularly in Deuteronomy and in the account of Elijah's journey.

The biblical account

According to Exodus, three months after leaving Egypt the Israelites encamped at the foot of the mountain. Moses ascended Sinai amid thunder, lightning, smoke and the blast of a shofar, and received the tablets of the Law. While he was on the mountain for forty days, the people made and worshipped the golden calf, which Moses shattered along with the first tablets on his descent; he then ascended again and received a second set. The laws promulgated at Sinai, together with the construction of the Tabernacle, occupy much of Exodus, Leviticus and Numbers.

Location

The actual location of the biblical mountain has never been established with certainty. Since the early Christian centuries, tradition has identified it with Jebel Musa ("Mountain of Moses") in the southern Sinai Peninsula, at whose foot stands Saint Catherine's Monastery, founded in the 6th century. Numerous alternative sites have been proposed, including mountains elsewhere in the Sinai, in the Negev, in Edom and Midian (northwest Arabia), and Har Karkom in the Negev desert. Biblical geographical clues — such as the eleven days' journey from Horeb to Kadesh Barnea mentioned in Deuteronomy — have been used to argue for several of these candidates, but no consensus exists.

Religious significance

The revelation at Sinai is commemorated in Judaism by the festival of Shavuot. The mountain is revered in Christianity and is mentioned in the Quran as Tur Sina; in Islamic tradition Moses (Musa) is a major prophet, and the mountain is associated with God's speaking to him. Pilgrims and tourists continue to climb Jebel Musa, often by night, to watch sunrise from the summit.
