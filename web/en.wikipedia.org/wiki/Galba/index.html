Galba (Servius Galba Caesar Augustus; 24 December 3 BC – 15 January AD 69) was Roman emperor from AD 68 to 69. He was the first emperor in the Year of the Four Emperors and assumed the throne following the emperor Nero's suicide.

Born into the ancient patrician family of the Sulpicii, Galba rose through the cursus honorum under the Julio-Claudian dynasty, serving as consul in AD 33 and governing provinces including Hispania Tarraconensis, where he was governor for eight years before his revolt. When the revolts of Vindex and then the Senate's declaration of Nero as a public enemy opened the way, Galba marched on Rome and was recognised as emperor.

Reign

Galba's seven-month reign was marked by frugality that alienated the soldiers whose support he needed. He refused to pay the donative promised to the Praetorian Guard in his name, executed several of Nero's officials and some senators without trial, and quarrelled with Otho, the governor of Lusitania who had supported his revolt and expected to be named heir. Instead Galba adopted Lucius Calpurnius Piso Licinianus.

Fall and death

On 15 January AD 69, Otho bribed the Praetorians and was proclaimed emperor. Galba was carried to the Forum, where he was murdered by soldiers near the Lacus Curtius; Piso was killed the same day. Galba was the first of the four emperors of AD 69, a year of civil war that ended with Vespasian's victory.
