Debye function

In mathematics, the family of Debye functions is defined by

Dn(x) = (n / xn) ∫0x tn / (et − 1) dt

The functions are named in honor of Peter Debye, who came across this function (with n = 3) in 1912 when he investigated the heat capacity of solids. They are also sometimes called generalized polylogarithm functions, since they can be expressed in terms of the polylogarithm and the Bernoulli numbers.

Mathematical properties

Relation to other functions

The Debye functions can be expanded as a series involving Bernoulli numbers, and they are related to the Riemann zeta function through their limiting values. As x → ∞, Dn(x) behaves asymptotically like n! ζ(n+1) n / xn contributions vanishing, with Dn(∞) involving ζ(n + 1). For small x, Dn(x) approaches 1, with a series expansion derived from the Taylor series of t/(et − 1).

Derivative

The Debye functions of different orders are connected by simple recurrence relations through differentiation with respect to their argument, which follow directly from differentiating under the integral sign.

Special values

Particular values include Dn(0) = 1 for all n, and closed forms at infinity in terms of values of the zeta function, for example D3 at large argument producing the constant π4/15 that appears in low-temperature heat-capacity laws.

Applications

The Debye function of order 3 is central to the Debye model of the specific heat of solids: the molar internal energy and heat capacity of a crystalline solid at low temperature are expressed through D3, yielding the famous T3 law as the temperature approaches absolute zero. Related functions also appear in the theory of black-body radiation, in the thermodynamics of the ideal Bose gas, and in the calculation of thermal properties of phonon systems.
