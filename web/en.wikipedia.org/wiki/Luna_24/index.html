Luna 24

Luna 24 was a robotic probe of the Soviet Luna programme and the last Soviet mission to land on the Moon. Launched on 9 August 1976, it soft-landed on 18 August 1976 in Mare Crisium (the Sea of Crises) at a site not far from where the failed Luna 23 had come down in 1974.

Mission

Luna 24 carried an improved drill capable of penetrating roughly two metres into the lunar regolith. It obtained a sample of about 170 grams from a depth of up to 2 metres — the first deep core sample returned from the Moon. The ascent stage lifted off on 19 August 1976, and the sample capsule was recovered on Earth on 22 August 1976 in Siberia, west of Surgut.

Water in the sample

Analysis of the Luna 24 core reportedly found a small water content (around 0.1% by mass) increasing with depth, a result published in 1978 that attracted little attention at the time but was later cited in discussions of lunar water. The mission was the final flight of the Luna programme and the last sample return from the Moon until China's Chang'e 5 in 2020.
