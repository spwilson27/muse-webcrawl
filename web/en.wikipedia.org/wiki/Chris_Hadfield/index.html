Chris Hadfield

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chris Hadfield
Born29 August 1959, Sarnia, Canada
OccupationAstronaut (CSA), test pilot, author
MissionsSTS-74, STS-100, Soyuz TMA-07M (ISS commander)
Hadfield and his guitar on the ISS.

Chris Austin Hadfield (born 1959) is a Canadian retired astronaut, engineer and test pilot who became the first Canadian to walk in space and the first to command the International Space Station, and — through social media and a zero-gravity cover of David Bowie's "Space Oddity" — arguably the most publicly beloved astronaut of the station era.

Hadfield flew two Space Shuttle missions (1995, helping dock Atlantis to Mir on STS-74; 2001, installing Canadarm2 on his spacewalk flight) before launching on a Soyuz in December 2012 for a five-month ISS expedition, commanding it from March 2013. His videos — wringing a washcloth in weightlessness, making a sandwich, crying in space (you can't) — drew hundreds of millions of views.

Space Oddity

His spare, revised-astronaut version of "Space Oddity", filmed aboard the station and posted on his last day in orbit in May 2013, was the first music video shot in space; rights negotiations briefly took it offline before Bowie's blessing restored it. Hadfield retired that year to write bestsellers including An Astronaut's Guide to Life on Earth — remaining Canada's defining space figure.
