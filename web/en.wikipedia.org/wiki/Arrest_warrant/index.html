Arrest warrant

An arrest warrant is a warrant issued by a judge or magistrate authorising the arrest of a person suspected of a crime. In most common-law jurisdictions a warrant is granted on the basis of a sworn complaint or indictment establishing probable cause.

Procedure

The warrant names the person to be arrested and the offence charged. Officers may enter a dwelling to execute an arrest warrant under rules that vary by jurisdiction; a bench warrant, by contrast, is issued for a person who has failed to appear in court. International arrest warrants may be circulated through Interpol red notices.

Requirements

In the United States the Fourth Amendment requires warrants to be supported by oath and to particularly describe the person to be seized. A warrant may be quashed if it was obtained through material misrepresentation, following the Franks doctrine.
