The Invictus Games is an international multi-sport event for wounded, injured and sick servicemen and women, both serving personnel and veterans. The Games use the power of sport to inspire recovery, support rehabilitation and generate a wider understanding and respect for those who serve their country.

History

Prince Harry was inspired to create the Games after visiting the Warrior Games in the United States in 2013, and by the effect of sport on injured personnel he had served alongside in Afghanistan. The inaugural Games were held in London on 10–14 September 2014 at the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park, with more than 400 competitors from 13 nations. The name "Invictus" (Latin for unconquered) draws on William Ernest Henley's poem of the same name.

Editions

Subsequent Games were held in Orlando, Florida (2016), Toronto (2017), Sydney (2018), and The Hague (2022, postponed from 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic). Düsseldorf hosted in 2023, and the first winter edition, featuring sports such as alpine skiing and skeleton, was held in Vancouver and Whistler, Canada, in February 2025. Birmingham was announced as host for 2027.

Sports

Events have included athletics, archery, indoor rowing, powerlifting, road cycling, sitting volleyball, swimming, wheelchair basketball and wheelchair rugby, with classifications adapted for competitors' injuries and illnesses.
