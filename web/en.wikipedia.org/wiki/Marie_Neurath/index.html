Marie Neurath

Marie Neurath (born Marie Reidemeister, 27 May 1898 – 10 October 1986) was a German designer, author and educator, a central figure in the development of Isotype (the International System of Typographic Picture Education), the pictorial language of statistics founded by her husband Otto Neurath and artist Gerd Arntz in Vienna.

Work

Marie studied mathematics and physics at Göttingen, where she met Otto Neurath. At the Social and Economic Museum in Vienna she worked as a “transformer” — her own term for the role of mediating between the expert who supplies data, the artist who draws the symbols, and the public who reads them. After fleeing the Nazis, the Neuraths settled in Oxford, England, and founded the Isotype Institute in 1942.

Later career

After Otto’s death in 1945, Marie directed the institute, producing notable series of illustrated children’s science books and educational materials on housing, health and citizenship, and advising the government of the Western Region of Nigeria on visual education. She donated the Isotype archive to the University of Reading in 1971, where the Otto and Marie Neurath Isotype Collection remains a major resource for information design history.
