Kuujjuaq (Inuktitut: ᑰᔾᔪᐊᖅ, "the great river") is the largest community in Nunavik, the Inuit region of northern Quebec, Canada, and serves as its administrative capital. It lies on the west bank of the Koksoak River, about 50 kilometres upstream from Ungava Bay, and roughly 1,400 kilometres north of Montreal. The village is not connected to the provincial road network; it is accessible by air year-round and by boat during the short summer season.

History

The site was long a summer gathering and trading place for Inuit of the Ungava region. Moravian missionaries established a mission station nearby in the early 19th century. In 1830 the Hudson's Bay Company founded a trading post called Fort Chimo on the east bank of the river; "Chimo" derives from an Inuktitut greeting. During the Second World War the United States built an airbase at the site (Crystal 1) as part of the Crimson Route ferrying aircraft to Europe; the airfield later became Kuujjuaq Airport. The post office and settlement took the name Fort Chimo until 1980, when it was officially renamed Kuujjuaq.

Demographics and services

The population is predominantly Inuit, with a minority of non-Indigenous residents, many of whom work for regional organizations. Kuujjuaq hosts the headquarters of the Kativik Regional Government and other Nunavik-wide bodies, a hospital (the Ungava Tulattavik Health Centre), schools, and the regional airport, which is a hub for Air Inuit flights linking Nunavik's villages with Montreal and each other.

Climate

Kuujjuaq has a subarctic climate with long, very cold winters and short, cool summers. Temperatures below −40 °C are common in midwinter, while July highs average around 17 °C. Because of its latitude, daylight varies dramatically across the year, though it lies south of the Arctic Circle.
