The Celtic Football Club, commonly known as Celtic, is a Scottish professional football club based in Glasgow, Scotland, which plays in the Scottish Premiership. The club was founded in 1887 with the purpose of alleviating poverty in the immigrant Irish population in the East End of Glasgow. They played their first match in May 1888, a friendly match against Rangers which Celtic won 5–2. Celtic established themselves within Scottish football, winning six successive league titles during the first decade of the 20th century.

Celtic are one of only five clubs in the world to have won over 100 trophies in their history. The club has won the Scottish league championship 55 times, most recently in 2024–25, as well as the Scottish Cup a record 42 times and the Scottish League Cup 22 times.

European Cup 1967

The club's greatest season was 1966–67, when Celtic became the first British team to win the European Cup, defeating Inter Milan 2–1 in the final in Lisbon on 25 May 1967. Every member of that squad, who became known as the "Lisbon Lions", was born within 30 miles of Celtic Park. That season Celtic also won every competition they entered: the Scottish League, the Scottish Cup, the League Cup, the Glasgow Cup, and the European Cup.

Rivalry

Celtic's traditional rivals are Rangers; collectively, the two clubs are known as the "Old Firm". The rivalry carries religious and political undertones and is one of the most famous in world football. Celtic's home ground is Celtic Park, popularly known as "Parkhead", the largest football stadium in Scotland.

Nine in a row

Under manager Jock Stein, Celtic won nine consecutive league titles between 1966 and 1974. The feat was matched by Rangers in the 1990s, and Celtic achieved a second nine-in-a-row between 2012 and 2020, a run that also included an unprecedented run of domestic trebles.
