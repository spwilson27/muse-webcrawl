Reining

Reining is a western riding competition in which riders guide horses through a precise pattern of circles, spins and stops. Originating in the working movements of cattle horses, it is often described as a western form of dressage, and is recognised by the International Federation for Equestrian Sports (FEI); it was contested at the World Equestrian Games from 2002 to 2018.

The pattern

Each pattern includes large fast circles and small slow circles, flying lead changes, rollbacks over the hocks, 360-degree spins, and the signature sliding stop, in which the horse slides on its hindquarters while continuing to "walk" with its front legs. Horses are judged on smoothness, finesse, attitude and speed of execution. The sport is governed internationally by the National Reining Horse Association (NRHA), founded in 1966, and is most popular in North America, Italy and Germany, with Quarter Horses predominating.
