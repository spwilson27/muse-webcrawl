Lindsay Anderson

Lindsay Gordon Anderson (17 April 1923 – 30 August 1994) was a British feature-film, theatre and documentary director, film critic, and leading light of the Free Cinema movement and of the British New Wave. He is best known for the films This Sporting Life (1963), if.... (1968) — which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes — and O Lucky Man! (1973).

Born: 17 April 1923, Bangalore, British India
Died: 30 August 1994, Angoulême, France
Occupation: Film and theatre director, critic

Early life and criticism

Anderson was born in Bangalore, India, the son of a British army officer, and educated at Cheltenham College and Wadham College, Oxford. With Karel Reisz, Tony Richardson and others he co-founded the influential film journal Sequence in 1947, and later wrote for Sight and Sound. His 1956 manifesto for the Free Cinema programmes at the National Film Theatre championed a poetic, socially committed documentary cinema.

Film career

His documentary Thursday's Children (1954), about a school for deaf children, won an Academy Award. His feature debut This Sporting Life (1963), starring Richard Harris, was a key work of kitchen-sink realism. if.... (1968), a savage satire of English public-school life starring Malcolm McDowell as Mick Travis, became one of the defining films of its era; Anderson continued Travis's story in O Lucky Man! (1973) and Britannia Hospital (1982).

Later works include In Celebration (1975), The Whales of August (1987), with Bette Davis and Lillian Gish, and the television film Glory! Glory! (1989). In theatre he was a celebrated director at the Royal Court Theatre, particularly of the plays of David Storey.
