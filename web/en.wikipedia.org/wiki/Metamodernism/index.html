Metamodernism

Metamodernism is a proposed cultural, philosophical, and artistic movement that emerged in the early 21st century as a reaction to, and development beyond, postmodernism. The term is most closely associated with the Dutch philosophers Timotheus Vermeulen and Robin van den Akker, who introduced it in their 2010 essay "Notes on Metamodernism". Metamodernism is characterised by an oscillation between modernist enthusiasm and postmodernist irony: between sincerity and irony, hope and doubt, naivety and knowingness, empathy and apathy.

Etymology and origins

The prefix "meta-" is used not in the sense of "beyond" but in the Platonic sense of metaxy — a state of being in-between or among. Vermeulen and van den Akker argued that the dominant cultural sensibility of the 2000s could no longer be described as postmodern, pointing to a return of sincerity, affect, and grand narratives, albeit tempered by postmodern awareness of their constructedness.

Characteristics

Key features attributed to metamodernism include:

  • Informed naivety — a pragmatic idealism pursued in full knowledge of its possible failure.
  • Oscillation — a swinging between opposing poles, such as irony and enthusiasm, rather than a synthesis of them.
  • New sincerity — a renewed investment in authentic feeling and earnestness in art and culture.
  • Romanticism revived — an interest in affect, depth, and transcendence filtered through postmodern critique.

In the arts

Metamodern tendencies have been identified in film, television, literature, music, and architecture. Filmmakers such as Michel Gondry and Spike Jonze, and the broader aesthetic sometimes called "quirky" cinema, have been read as metamodern. In architecture, a return to ornament, history, and vernacular forms has been discussed in metamodern terms. The movement is sometimes situated within a wider family of proposed successors to postmodernism, alongside concepts such as post-postmodernism, digimodernism, and the New Sincerity.

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether metamodernism names a genuinely new structure of feeling or merely rebrands tendencies long present within postmodern culture. Others object that its proponents' examples are selective, and that "postmodernism" itself was never as monolithic as the metamodern account suggests.
