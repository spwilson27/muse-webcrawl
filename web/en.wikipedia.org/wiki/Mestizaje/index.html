Mestizaje

Mestizaje (Spanish: [mestiˈsaxe]) is a term used in Spanish America for the cultural and racial mixing of Europeans and Indigenous Americans, producing the mixed-ancestry population known as mestizos. In a broader sense it has come to denote the fusion of cultures — European, Indigenous and African — that characterises much of Latin America.

Colonial period

Mixing began with the Spanish conquest of the Americas in the 16th century. Colonial society developed an elaborate sistema de castas classifying people by ancestry, with peninsulares and criollos at the top and mestizos in an intermediate position. Nevertheless, mestizos steadily grew to form the majority population in most mainland colonies.

National ideology

After independence, and especially in the 20th century, mestizaje was recast as a founding national identity in countries such as Mexico, where philosopher José Vasconcelos's La raza cósmica (1925) celebrated mixture as the destiny of a "fifth race". Critics note that official mestizaje often masked continuing discrimination against Indigenous and Afro-descendant peoples, and Indigenous movements have challenged its assimilative assumptions.
