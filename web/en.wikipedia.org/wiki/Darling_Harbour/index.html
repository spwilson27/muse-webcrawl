Darling Harbour, a major recreational and entertainment precinct in Sydney.

Darling Harbour is a harbour adjacent to the city centre of Sydney, New South Wales, Australia, that is made up of a large recreational and pedestrian precinct situated on the western outskirts of the Sydney central business district. It was named after Lieutenant-General Ralph Darling, who was Governor of New South Wales from 1825 to 1831.

History

The area was originally known to the Gadigal people as Tumbalong. It became a working port and industrial district of wharves, factories and railway yards through the 19th and early 20th centuries. As port activity declined, the precinct was redeveloped as a pedestrian and tourist area; the redevelopment was a major Bicentennial project, officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1988.

Attractions

Darling Harbour contains numerous public facilities and attractions, including the Australian National Maritime Museum, the SEA LIFE Sydney Aquarium, the Wild Life Sydney Zoo, Madame Tussauds Sydney, the Powerhouse Museum, the ICC Sydney convention centre, and Tumbalong Park. The Pyrmont Bridge, a heritage-listed swing bridge, crosses Cockle Bay at its centre.

Transport

The precinct is served by light rail, monorail (until its closure in 2013), ferries and nearby railway stations at Town Hall and Central.
