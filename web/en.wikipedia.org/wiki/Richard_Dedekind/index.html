Julius Wilhelm Richard Dedekind (October 6, 1831 – February 12, 1916) was a German mathematician who made important contributions to number theory, abstract algebra (particularly ring theory), and the axiomatic foundations of arithmetic. He was the last student of Carl Friedrich Gauss and a close friend and editor of Bernhard Riemann and Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet.

Life

Dedekind was born and lived most of his life in Braunschweig. He entered the University of Göttingen in 1850 and attended Gauss's lectures; his 1852 doctoral dissertation, supervised by Gauss, concerned Eulerian integrals. After habilitating at Göttingen in 1854, he taught in Zürich from 1858 and in 1862 returned to Braunschweig as professor at the Technische Hochschule, where he remained for the rest of his career. He never married and lived with his sister Julie.

Work

While editing Dirichlet's lectures on number theory, Dedekind developed the theory of ideals to restore unique factorisation in rings of algebraic integers, generalising Ernst Kummer's "ideal numbers". Rings now known as Dedekind domains are named after him. In 1872 he published Stetigkeit und irrationale Zahlen ("Continuity and irrational numbers"), giving the construction of the real numbers via Dedekind cuts — partitions of the rationals into two sets — which placed analysis on a rigorous arithmetical footing.

In his 1888 monograph Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen? ("What are numbers and what should they be?") he gave an axiomatic foundation for the natural numbers, including a definition of infinite sets: a set is infinite if it can be put into one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself. Dedekind also edited the collected works of Riemann and coined several terms still standard in algebra.

Legacy

Dedekind's structural, set-theoretic approach profoundly influenced modern algebra and the work of Emmy Noether and David Hilbert. He died in Braunschweig in 1916.
