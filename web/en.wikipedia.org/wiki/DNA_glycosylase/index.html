DNA glycosylase

DNA glycosylases are a family of enzymes involved in base excision repair, the cellular pathway that removes damaged or inappropriate bases from DNA.

Mechanism

A DNA glycosylase scans the DNA, recognises a specific lesion — for example uracil arising from cytosine deamination, or 8-oxoguanine from oxidative damage — flips the base out of the helix, and cleaves the N-glycosidic bond, releasing the base and leaving an abasic (AP) site for downstream repair enzymes such as AP endonuclease and DNA polymerase β.

Examples

Well-known members include uracil-DNA glycosylase (UNG), 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase (OGG1), MutY homolog (MUTYH), and thymine-DNA glycosylase (TDG). Defects in these enzymes are linked to cancer predisposition and other diseases.
