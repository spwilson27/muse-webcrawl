Founded1930s, Acre, Brazil

SacramentDaime (ayahuasca)

Santo Daime is a syncretic spiritual practice and religion founded in the Brazilian state of Acre in the 1930s by Raimundo Irineu Serra, known as Mestre Irineu. It combines elements of Roman Catholicism, African animism, South American shamanism, and esotericism. The central ritual involves drinking a sacramental tea called Daime, a preparation of ayahuasca, which practitioners believe facilitates communion with the divine.

History

Irineu Serra, a rubber tapper of African descent, encountered the plant brew ayahuasca in the Amazon rainforest near the border with Peru and Bolivia. Following a series of visions, including one of a female deity identified with the Virgin Mary, he began holding ceremonies in the 1930s. After his death in 1971, the movement split into several branches, the most widespread being the line led by Sebastião Mota de Melo, whose community of Céu do Mapiá in Amazonas became a centre of the church.

Practice

Services, called trabalhos (works), involve singing hymns (hinários) while dancing in formation or sitting in meditation, often lasting many hours. Members wear white uniforms on formal occasions. The hymns are considered to have been received spiritually by the founders and elders of the church.

Legal status

In Brazil, religious use of ayahuasca was authorized following government studies in the 1980s. Santo Daime churches have since spread to Europe, North America, and elsewhere, and their legal status has been contested in several countries, with court decisions varying by jurisdiction.
