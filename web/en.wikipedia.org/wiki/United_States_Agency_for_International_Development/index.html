The United States Agency for International Development (USAID) was an independent agency of the United States government primarily responsible for administering civilian foreign aid and development assistance. Created by President John F. Kennedy in 1961 under the Foreign Assistance Act, it became one of the largest official aid agencies in the world, operating in more than 100 countries in areas including global health, agriculture and food security, disaster relief, education, water and sanitation, and the promotion of democracy and human rights.

History

USAID consolidated earlier aid programmes descending from the Marshall Plan and President Truman's Point Four Program. During the Cold War its work was closely tied to US foreign policy; later decades saw major initiatives in family planning, HIV/AIDS (as an implementer of PEPFAR from 2003), malaria (the President's Malaria Initiative), food security (Feed the Future), and humanitarian response through its Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance, a successor to the Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance. The agency worked largely by funding grants and contracts to NGOs, universities, private firms and partner governments.

2025 dismantling

In early 2025 the second Trump administration moved to dismantle USAID: most staff were placed on leave or dismissed, the great majority of its programmes were terminated, and its remaining functions were folded into the Department of State, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio announcing the agency's effective closure as an independent body by mid-2025. The move, led in part by the Department of Government Efficiency, prompted litigation and wide debate over the legality of abolishing an agency created by statute and over the consequences for global health and humanitarian relief.
