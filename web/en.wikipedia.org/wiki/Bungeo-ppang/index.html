Bungeo-ppang
Origin: Korea (via Japanese taiyaki)
Type: Filled pastry / street food
Main ingredients: Waffle batter, sweet red bean paste

Bungeo-ppang (Korean: 붕어빵; "crucian carp bread") is a popular Korean street food: a fish-shaped pastry made by pouring waffle-like batter into a fish-shaped mould and filling it, traditionally with sweetened red bean paste, then grilling both sides until crisp and golden.

History

The pastry derives from the Japanese taiyaki, introduced to Korea during the Japanese colonial period in the 1930s. It became a beloved winter snack sold from street stalls, and its price is watched so closely that a "bungeo-ppang index" is jokingly invoked in Korean discussions of inflation; maps and apps tracking seasonal stall locations have also appeared.

Variations

Modern fillings include custard cream (shu-kurimu), chocolate, cheese, sweet potato, matcha, and savoury options. Miniature versions and variants such as gyeongju-style breads are also common.
