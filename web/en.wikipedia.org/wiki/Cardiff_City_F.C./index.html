Cardiff City F.C.

Cardiff City Football Club (Welsh: Clwb Pêl-droed Dinas Caerdydd) is a professional association football club based in Cardiff, Wales. The club was founded in 1899 as Riverside A.F.C. and is the only club from outside England to have won the FA Cup, a feat it achieved in 1927.

History

The club was founded in 1899 by Bartley Wilson as Riverside A.F.C., a way of keeping players of the Riverside Cricket Club together during the winter months. In 1908 the club changed its name to Cardiff City after Cardiff was granted city status. The club joined the English Football League in 1920 and enjoyed its most successful era in the 1920s, finishing as First Division runners-up in 1923–24 and winning the FA Cup in 1927 after defeating Arsenal 1–0 in the final at Wembley Stadium, with Hughie Ferguson scoring the only goal. Cardiff remain the only team from outside England to lift the trophy.

After decades of decline, the club returned to the top flight in 2003 and moved to the Cardiff City Stadium in 2009, leaving Ninian Park, its home for 99 years. In 2012, under the ownership of Malaysian businessman Vincent Tan, the club controversially changed its home kit colour from blue to red and altered its crest. The change was reversed in January 2015 following supporter protests. Cardiff won promotion to the Premier League in 2013 and again in 2018, but were relegated after a single season on both occasions.

Stadium

The Cardiff City Stadium opened in 2009 with a capacity of 26,828, later expanded to around 33,280. It also hosts matches for the Wales national football team.

Honours

Cardiff's major honours include the FA Cup (1927), the FA Charity Shield (1927), and the Welsh Cup, which the club has won 22 times. They also reached the final of the League Cup in 2012, losing to Liverpool on penalties.
