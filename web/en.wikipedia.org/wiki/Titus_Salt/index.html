Sir Titus Salt, 1st Baronet (20 September 1803 – 29 December 1876) was an English manufacturer, politician and philanthropist in Bradford, West Riding of Yorkshire, England, who built Salt's Mill and the village of Saltaire. He was a leading figure in the worsted trade, making his fortune by successfully spinning alpaca wool, which had previously been considered unusable in power looms.

Business career

Salt entered his father's wool business and took over Daniel Salt and Son in 1833. In 1836 he acquired bales of alpaca fibre from Peru that other spinners had rejected; working with his technicians he developed machinery to spin the lustrous fibre, producing the fashionable fabric known as "Genappe" and other alpaca cloths. By 1848 he was Bradford's largest employer, operating five mills. He served as mayor of Bradford in 1848–49 and as its Liberal Member of Parliament from 1859 to 1861.

Saltaire

Concerned by the polluted, overcrowded conditions of Bradford, Salt built a vast new mill on the River Aire at Shipley, opened on his fiftieth birthday in 1853, and around it constructed the model village of Saltaire (named from Salt + Aire). The village eventually comprised more than 800 stone houses with running water and gas, a church, school, hospital, almshouses, public baths, a park, and an institute — though, characteristically for a strict Congregationalist teetotaller, no public house. Saltaire is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site (designated 2001).

Honours and legacy

Salt was created a baronet in 1869. His funeral in 1876 drew an estimated 100,000 mourners. A statue of him stands in Lister Park, Bradford. Salts Mill today houses galleries (including works by David Hockney), shops, and workspaces, and Saltaire remains one of the best-preserved examples of a Victorian industrial model village, comparable to Bournville and Port Sunlight.
