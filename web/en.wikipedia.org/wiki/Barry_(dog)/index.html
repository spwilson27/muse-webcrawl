Barry (dog)

Barry der Menschenretter (1800–1814), also known simply as Barry, was a famous St. Bernard rescue dog who worked at the Great St Bernard Hospice in the Swiss Alps. He is credited with saving the lives of more than forty people during his career.

Life and legend

Barry worked as a mountain rescue dog at the hospice on the Great St Bernard Pass, guiding travellers through snow and locating those buried by avalanches. Legend holds that he was killed by a delirious soldier he had rescued, who mistook him for a wolf; in fact, Barry retired to Bern in 1812 and died of old age in 1814.

After his death, his body was preserved and displayed at the Natural History Museum in Bern, where his mounted skin remains on exhibit — making him one of the most famous individual dogs of the pre-modern era.

Legacy

Barry became the archetype of the Alpine rescue dog, and the popular image of a St. Bernard carrying a small barrel of brandy around its neck is associated with him, although the hospice dogs never actually carried brandy. A monument to Barry stands at the entrance of the Cimetière des Chiens pet cemetery near Paris.
