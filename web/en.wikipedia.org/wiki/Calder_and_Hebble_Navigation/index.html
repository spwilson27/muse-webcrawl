Calder and Hebble Navigation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Location: West Yorkshire, England
Length: ~21 miles (34 km)
Status: Open for leisure navigation

The Calder and Hebble Navigation is a river and canal navigation in West Yorkshire, England, linking the Aire and Calder Navigation at Wakefield with the Rochdale Canal at Sowerby Bridge. It made the River Calder navigable upstream to Halifax's neighbouring town of Sowerby Bridge and incorporated reaches of the River Hebble's valley via the short Halifax Branch.

History

An Act of Parliament was obtained in 1758, and the surveyor John Smeaton designed the works; the navigation opened in stages through the 1760s and 1770s, reaching Sowerby Bridge in 1770. It carried coal, wool, grain and manufactured goods for the growing textile towns of the Calder valley. Unusually, its locks were built to a length of about 57 feet 6 inches, governed by the size of the existing river locks downstream, and boats designed for it—"Calder and Hebble keels"—were correspondingly short.

A distinctive feature is the use of a handspike (a shaped wooden lever) to operate the ground paddles of many locks, a survival found nowhere else on the English canal system.

Present day

Commercial traffic ended in the mid-20th century, but the navigation remains open and is popular with leisure boaters. At Salterhebble, three locks raise boats toward the Halifax Branch basin. The waterway is managed by the Canal & River Trust and forms part of the trans-Pennine cruising routes via the Rochdale and Huddersfield Narrow canals.
