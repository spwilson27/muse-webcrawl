Sleep terror

A sleep terror, also known as a night terror or pavor nocturnus, is a sleep disorder causing feelings of panic or dread that typically occur during the first hours of stage 3–4 non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep. The person may scream, thrash and appear awake yet be inconsolable and usually has no memory of the episode on waking.

Sleep terrors are most common in children aged roughly three to twelve, and usually resolve by adolescence. They differ from nightmares, which occur in REM sleep and are often remembered. Episodes can be triggered by fever, sleep deprivation and stress.

Treatment

Reassurance and ensuring safety during episodes are usually sufficient; regular sleep schedules and addressing triggers help reduce frequency. Medication is rarely needed. Persistent cases may warrant evaluation for other parasomnias such as sleepwalking.
