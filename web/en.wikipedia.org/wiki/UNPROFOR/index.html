UNPROFOR

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations Protection Force (UNPROFOR) was the first United Nations peacekeeping force in Croatia and in the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina during the Yugoslav Wars, established by Security Council Resolution 743 on 21 February 1992. At its peak it deployed some 39,000 personnel from dozens of countries, with headquarters in Zagreb and Sarajevo.

Criticised during its existence and studied ever since as a symbol of the limits of 1990s peacekeeping, UNPROFOR was mandated to protect “safe areas” (Srebrenica among them, declared in 1993) without the mandate or means to defend them by force; its lightly armed Dutch battalion at Srebrenica could not prevent the July 1995 massacre of more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys by the Bosnian Serb Army — the worst atrocity in Europe since 1945 and adjudicated genocide by the ICTY and the ICJ. The mission also secured Sarajevo airport and aid convoys under fire, at significant casualties. It was restructured in March 1995 and replaced in Bosnia by NATO's IFOR after the Dayton Agreement (December 1995). Its failures drove the Brahimi Report (2000) and the doctrine of robust protection-of-civilians mandates.
