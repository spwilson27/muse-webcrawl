Burning Spear

Winston Rodney OD (born 1 March 1945), better known by the stage name Burning Spear, is a Jamaican roots reggae singer-songwriter, musician, and bandleader. A Rastafarian, he is known for his messages of African self-determination, repatriation, and black pride. His stage name derives from the Mau Mau leader Jomo Kenyatta's nickname, "Burning Spear".

Early life

Rodney was born in Saint Ann's Bay, Saint Ann, Jamaica — the same town that produced Marcus Garvey and Bob Marley. Growing up, he was exposed to the music of Curtis Mayfield and James Brown on American R&B radio. Encouraged by Marley, he approached Clement "Coxsone" Dodd at Studio One in Kingston, where he auditioned and recorded his debut single, "Door Peep", in 1969.

Career

Burning Spear's first two albums, Burning Spear (1973) and Rocking Time (1974), were recorded for Studio One. His breakthrough came with Marcus Garvey (1975), released on Island Records, which brought him international attention and remains one of the defining albums of roots reggae. He subsequently recorded for EMI and for his own Burning Spear label, releasing albums such as Man in the Hills (1976), Hail H.I.M. (1980), Resistance (1985), and Jah Kingdom (1991).

Rodney won the Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album twice: for Calling Rastafari (1999) and Jah Is Real (2008). He is noted for his long, intense live performances with a full horn section, and for remaining committed to roots themes throughout his career. In 2007 he was awarded the Order of Distinction by the Jamaican government.

Musical style and themes

Burning Spear's music is characterized by deep, chanting vocals, heavy bass lines, nyabinghi-inspired percussion, and prominent horns. His lyrics focus on Marcus Garvey, African history, Rastafari spirituality, and the experiences of enslaved Africans, most famously on the song "Slavery Days".
