Martin B-57 Canberra

The Martin B-57 Canberra is a twin-engined jet-powered light bomber that was manufactured by the Glenn L. Martin Company and entered service with the United States Air Force in 1953. It was a licence-built version of the British English Electric Canberra, the first foreign warplane built under licence in the United States since the First World War.

Development and service

Selected in 1951 to replace the Douglas B-26 Invader in Korea, the B-57 was adapted by Martin with a two-man tandem cockpit, cartridge engine starters, and American avionics and armament. The first production aircraft flew in July 1953. B-57s flew night interdiction and bombing missions in the Vietnam War with the USAF and the Republic of Vietnam Air Force, operating from Bien Hoa and Phan Rang.

Variants

The reconnaissance RB-57 served in tactical and strategic roles; the greatly enlarged RB-57D and the long-wing RB-57F — with a wingspan of 122 feet — performed high-altitude reconnaissance. NASA still flies three WB-57F aircraft for atmospheric research and airborne science, among the longest-serving aircraft in American service.
