Broken beat

Stylistic originsDrum and bass, acid jazz, house, hip hop, jazz fusion, funk
Cultural originsLate 1990s, West London, England
Key figuresIG Culture, Bugz in the Attic, 4hero, Kaidi Tatham, Dego, Afronaught
Key labelCo-op

Broken beat is a genre of electronic music that emerged in West London in the late 1990s and early 2000s. It is characterised by syncopated, "broken" rhythms that stutter and off-set the four-to-the-floor pulse of house music, drawing on the shuffled grooves of jazz fusion, funk, hip hop and the breakbeat science of drum and bass, and typically layered with soulful vocals, jazz chords and live-sounding basslines.

History

The sound coalesced around West London producers and collectives including IG Culture (New Sector Movements), Orin "Afronaught" Walters, Dego of 4hero, Kaidi Tatham, and the Bugz in the Attic collective. The club night and label Co-op, held at venues including Plastic People in Shoreditch, became the genre's epicentre, promoting a community of producers who also remixed artists from across jazz, soul and electronic music. Record labels such as 2000 Black, Archive, Bitasweet and Main Squeeze documented the scene.

Influence

Broken beat influenced UK funky and aspects of dubstep's rhythmic experimentation, and it maintained strong connections with scenes in Japan, Germany, the United States and New Zealand. Although the term fell from common use by the late 2000s, many of its producers continued working in jazz, house and soul — including in the 2010s London jazz revival — and the music has been periodically rediscovered by new generations of DJs.
