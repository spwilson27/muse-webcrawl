Vector control

Vector control is any method to limit or eradicate animals, especially arthropods, that transmit disease pathogens. The most commonly targeted vectors are mosquitoes, which spread malaria, dengue, Zika, and other diseases; other targets include ticks, flies, fleas, and rodents.

Methods include habitat and environmental management (draining breeding sites), chemical control with insecticides and larvicides, biological control using natural predators or bacteria such as Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, personal protection such as insecticide-treated bed nets and repellents, and newer genetic approaches including the sterile insect technique and gene drives.

Public health role

Vector control is a central element of integrated vector management promoted by the World Health Organization. Insecticide-treated nets and indoor residual spraying have been credited with a large share of the decline in malaria cases since 2000, though insecticide resistance and outdoor transmission remain challenges.
