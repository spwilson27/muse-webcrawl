Federal Open Market Committee

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is the monetary-policy committee of the US Federal Reserve, consisting of the seven members of the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, the president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, and four of the remaining eleven Reserve Bank presidents serving one-year terms on a rotating basis. It holds eight regularly scheduled meetings a year in Washington, D.C.

The FOMC sets the target range for the federal funds rate (the overnight interbank rate) and, since 2008, has also directed large-scale asset purchases (Quantitative easing) and forward guidance; implementation is by the New York Fed's trading desk. Its outputs — the post-meeting statement, minutes after three weeks, quarterly economic projections (the “dot plot”), and the chair's press conferences — are among the most market-moving communications in the world economy. Voting is formally by majority; dissents are published (rarely more than a few), and the strong norm of consensus under modern chairs makes the committee a case study in committee decision-making under uncertainty, from the Volcker disinflation (1979–82) to the pandemic easing and the 2022–23 tightening cycle.
