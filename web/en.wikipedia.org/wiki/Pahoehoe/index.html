Pahoehoe

Pāhoehoe lava is basaltic lava with a smooth, billowy, undulating, or ropy surface. The term comes from Hawaiian pāhoehoe, meaning smooth, unbroken lava.

Formation

Pahoehoe forms from fluid, low-viscosity lava that flows gently; a flexible skin forms on the surface and wrinkles into rope-like folds as the molten interior continues to move. It contrasts with rough, blocky ʻaʻā lava. Pahoehoe flows commonly advance through lava tubes and produce features such as tumuli and lava inflations.

Occurrence

Pahoehoe is characteristic of shield volcanoes such as Kīlauea and Mauna Loa in Hawaii, and occurs at many other basaltic volcanoes worldwide.
