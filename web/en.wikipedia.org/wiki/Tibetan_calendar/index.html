Tibetan calendar

The Tibetan calendar is a lunisolar calendar used in Tibet and by Tibetan communities elsewhere. It is based on a cycle of twelve years, each named after an animal in a manner similar to the Chinese zodiac, combined with the five elements of wood, fire, earth, iron, and water, producing a sixty-year cycle. The Tibetan New Year is called Losar.

Structure

The calendar year normally consists of twelve lunar months, and a thirteenth month is added roughly every three years to keep the calendar in step with the solar year. Months begin on the new moon, and days of the month are counted from one to thirty, though particular numbered days are occasionally duplicated or omitted for astrological reasons.

Eras and history

The traditional Tibetan era counts years from 127 BC, the legendary accession of the first king of Tibet, Nyatri Tsenpo. The sexagenary cycle, known as rab byung, is commonly reckoned from 1027 AD, associated with the introduction of the Kalachakra system of timekeeping from India.

Festivals

Major dates fixed by the calendar include Losar (New Year), Saga Dawa, marking the birth, enlightenment, and death of the Buddha, and numerous monastery festivals such as Tshechu.
