Applicative voice

The applicative voice is a grammatical voice that promotes an oblique argument of a verb, such as a beneficiary, recipient, instrument, or location, to the status of a core object argument. Applicatives are typically marked by a verbal affix and are common in many languages, including Bantu languages, Austronesian languages, and many Indigenous languages of the Americas.

Field: Linguistics / Morphosyntax
Effect: Increases verb valency
Promoted roles: Beneficiary, recipient, instrument, locative

Function

In a basic clause, a benefactive phrase might appear as an optional prepositional phrase ("she baked a cake for the children"). In an applicative construction, the beneficiary becomes a direct object ("she baked-APPL the children a cake"), often allowing it to display object properties such as passivization or object agreement on the verb.

Types

Linguists distinguish benefactive applicatives, instrumental applicatives, locative applicatives, and circumstantial applicatives, among others. Some languages, such as Kinyarwanda, allow multiple applicative suffixes on a single verb.
