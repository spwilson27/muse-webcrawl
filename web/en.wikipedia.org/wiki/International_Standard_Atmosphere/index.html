International Standard Atmosphere

The ISA models how temperature, pressure, and density vary with altitude.

The International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) is a static atmospheric model of how the pressure, temperature, density, and viscosity of the Earth's atmosphere change over a wide range of altitudes or elevations. It has been established to provide a common reference for temperature and pressure, and consists of tables of values at various altitudes, plus formulas by which those values were derived. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) publishes the ISA as international standard ISO 2533.

Model

The ISA divides the atmosphere into layers with linear temperature distributions (lapse rates). At mean sea level it specifies:

QuantitySea-level value
Temperature15 °C (288.15 K)
Pressure1013.25 hPa (101325 Pa)
Density1.225 kg/m³

In the troposphere, temperature decreases at a lapse rate of 6.5 °C per kilometre up to the tropopause at 11 km, where temperature is constant at −56.5 °C through the lower stratosphere to 20 km, before increasing in the upper layers of the model. The ISA assumes a dry atmosphere of fixed composition and still air.

Uses

The model is used as the basis for calibrating altimeters and other flight instruments, for aircraft performance calculations and flight testing, and for standardizing engineering calculations involving the atmosphere. The essentially identical ICAO Standard Atmosphere is used in aviation worldwide; related standards include the U.S. Standard Atmosphere. Actual conditions that differ from ISA are commonly reported in aviation as deviations, e.g. "ISA+10" for a day ten degrees warmer than standard.
