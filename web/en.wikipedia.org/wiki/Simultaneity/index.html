Simultaneity

Simultaneity is the property of two events happening at the same time. In classical physics, simultaneity was regarded as absolute: if two events occurred at the same instant for one observer, they did so for all. In Albert Einstein's theory of special relativity this is no longer true — whether spatially separated events are simultaneous depends on the observer's state of motion, a phenomenon known as the relativity of simultaneity.

Relativity of simultaneity

Einstein illustrated the idea with a thought experiment involving lightning strikes at the two ends of a moving train. An observer on the embankment, equidistant from the two strike points, receives the light flashes at the same moment and judges the strikes simultaneous. An observer riding at the centre of the train moves toward one flash and away from the other, receives them at different times, and — correctly applying the rule that light travels at the same speed in every inertial frame — judges the strikes not to have been simultaneous. Both judgements are valid within their own frames; there is no frame-independent fact of the matter.

Closely related is the problem of synchronising distant clocks. Einstein proposed defining simultaneity operationally: clocks at different locations are synchronous if light signals exchanged between them take equal time in each direction, which presumes the isotropy of the one-way speed of light — the Einstein synchronisation convention.

Philosophical issues

The relativity of simultaneity raises questions about the nature of time and the present: if different observers can disagree about which distant events belong to "now", the idea of a single universal present moment is undermined. This has been used to argue for eternalism (the block-universe view of time), in debates involving the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and in discussions of free will and determinism. In everyday language and in fields such as music, theatre and computing, "simultaneous" continues to be used in its ordinary sense of events coinciding in time within a tolerance.
