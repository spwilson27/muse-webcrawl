History of central banking in the United States

The history of central banking in the United States encompasses the First and Second Banks of the United States and the Federal Reserve System. Alexander Hamilton championed the First Bank, chartered in 1791 for twenty years; its charter lapsed in 1811.

Second Bank and the Bank War

The Second Bank of the United States was chartered in 1816. President Andrew Jackson vetoed its recharter in 1832 and withdrew federal deposits, in the "Bank War," and the bank expired in 1836. There followed the Free Banking era and the National Banking Acts of the Civil War period, but no central bank.

The Federal Reserve

Recurrent financial panics, culminating in the Panic of 1907, led Congress to pass the Federal Reserve Act of 1913, creating twelve regional Federal Reserve Banks under a Board in Washington. The Fed's powers expanded through the Great Depression, the 1951 Treasury–Fed Accord, and later crises including 2008. See also Federal Reserve.
