Ettore Majorana (born 5 August 1906; disappeared 25 March 1938) was an Italian theoretical physicist who worked on neutrino masses and the nuclear forces. He vanished under unknown circumstances while travelling by ship from Palermo to Naples in 1938, and his fate remains one of the famous unsolved mysteries of twentieth-century science.

Work

Majorana joined Enrico Fermi's group in Rome — the "Via Panisperna boys" — in 1928. Fermi reportedly compared him to Galileo and Newton. In a small number of papers he derived the Majorana equation, an infinite-component relativistic wave equation; predicted, ahead of publication, the existence of the neutron from experimental results; and introduced exchange forces (Majorana forces) in nuclear physics. His 1937 paper on a symmetrical theory of electrons and positrons introduced what are now called Majorana fermions — particles that are their own antiparticles — a concept that later became central to neutrino physics and to searches for exotic quasiparticles in condensed matter.

Disappearance

In 1937 Majorana became a professor of theoretical physics at Naples. On 25 March 1938 he withdrew his savings, wrote letters suggesting an intention to disappear (and one the next day seemingly retracting it), and boarded a ferry from Palermo to Naples; he was never seen again. Hypotheses have included suicide, emigration to South America, and withdrawal to a monastery. An Italian investigation reopened in the 2010s concluded he had probably lived in Venezuela between 1955 and 1959, based on photographic evidence, though the case remains disputed.
