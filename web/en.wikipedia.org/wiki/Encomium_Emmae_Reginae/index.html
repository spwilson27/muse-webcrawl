The Encomium Emmae Reginae ("Encomium of Queen Emma"), also known as the Gesta Cnutonis Regis, is an 11th-century Latin encomium written in praise of Queen Emma of Normandy, wife successively of Æthelred the Unready and of Cnut the Great. It was composed around 1041–1042 by an anonymous monk of the abbey of Saint-Bertin in Flanders, sometimes called "the Encomiast".

Contents

The work is divided into three books. The first concerns Sweyn Forkbeard and his conquest of England; the second covers the reign of his son Cnut; the third treats Emma and her sons by Cnut, Harthacnut, and by Æthelred. Famously, the Encomiast omits Emma's first marriage to Æthelred entirely in the main narrative scheme, presenting her history as if it began with Cnut.

Purpose and value

Written shortly after Harthacnut's accession, the Encomium was a political work intended to legitimise Emma and her son's position and to reconcile English and Danish factions at court. Despite its bias, it is a crucial near-contemporary source for the Scandinavian conquest of England and for Cnut's reign. The text survives in a single decorated manuscript (British Library Additional MS 33241), whose frontispiece shows Emma receiving the book from its author while her sons look on.
