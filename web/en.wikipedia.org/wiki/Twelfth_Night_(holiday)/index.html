Twelfth Night (holiday)

Twelfth Night
Observed byChristians in many countries
Date5 or 6 January (eve of Epiphany)
SignificanceEnd of the Twelve Days of Christmas; eve of Epiphany
TraditionsTwelfth cake, wassailing, removing decorations, mummer plays

Twelfth Night is a Christian festival marking the coming of Epiphany, the manifestation of Christ to the Gentiles in the persons of the Magi. Depending on the method of counting the Twelve Days of Christmas, it falls on the evening of 5 January (the eve of Epiphany) or of 6 January itself. It traditionally concludes the Christmas season.

Customs

In early modern England Twelfth Night was one of the great feasts of the year, marked by feasting, masking and misrule under a Lord of Misrule or a "King of the Bean" chosen by finding a bean hidden in the Twelfth cake. A pea might also be baked in for a queen. The cake evolved into the French galette des rois and the king cakes of Louisiana Mardi Gras tradition. Other customs include wassailing orchards to bless the apple harvest, chalking the door with the initials of the Magi, and church services blessing water and chalk.

Superstitions and decline

Folk tradition held it unlucky to leave Christmas decorations up past Twelfth Night. The festival declined after the Reformation in some regions and after the Victorian shift of Christmas festivity toward Christmas Day itself, though it survives in folk customs, in Shakespeare's comedy Twelfth Night (written for a performance at the close of the season), and in observances across Europe and Latin America, where Epiphany (Día de los Reyes) remains a major gift-giving feast.
