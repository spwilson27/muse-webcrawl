Scrum (rugby union)

A scrum (short for scrummage) is a method of restarting play in rugby union involving players packing closely together with heads down and attempting to gain possession of the ball. It is awarded after a minor infringement such as a forward pass or knock-on, or when the ball becomes unplayable.

A scrum in a rugby union match.

Formation

Each team's eight forwards bind together in three rows: the front row (two props and a hooker), the second row (two locks) and the back row (two flankers and a number eight). The two packs engage under the referee's sequence of calls — "crouch, bind, set". The scrum-half of the team awarded the put-in rolls the ball into the tunnel between the front rows, and the hookers strike for it with their feet while the packs push against each other.

Laws and safety

The scrum must remain stationary and parallel to the goal lines until the ball is fed; collapsing, twisting ("wheeling" beyond 90 degrees) or standing up results in penalties. Because of the enormous forces involved — elite packs generate well over a tonne of force — front-row play is restricted to trained players, and uncontested scrums are used if suitable replacements are unavailable.

In rugby league

Scrums also exist in rugby league but are smaller, traditionally six forwards per side, and largely uncontested in modern play.
