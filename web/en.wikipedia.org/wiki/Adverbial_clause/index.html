Adverbial clause

An adverbial clause is a dependent clause that functions as an adverb within a sentence — that is, it modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or the sentence as a whole, typically indicating time, place, manner, cause, condition, purpose, result, or concession. Adverbial clauses are introduced by subordinating conjunctions such as when, because, although, if, and while.

Examples

In "When the meeting ended, we went home," the clause when the meeting ended is an adverbial clause of time. In "She stayed home because she was ill," the clause expresses cause. Unlike independent clauses, an adverbial clause cannot stand alone as a complete sentence.

Reduced adverbial clauses

Adverbial clauses can be reduced to phrases by omitting the subject and a form of be: "While walking to work, he saw an old friend" (from while he was walking). Reduced forms are common in formal and written English and must normally have the same understood subject as the main clause to avoid dangling modifiers.
