The Kuranda Scenic Railway is a tourist railway line in Far North Queensland, Australia, running from Cairns, over the Great Dividing Range, to the town of Kuranda on the Atherton Tableland. The 37-kilometre journey climbs 327 metres through the Barron Gorge National Park, passing through 15 hand-excavated tunnels and over 55 bridges, including the famous curved trestle bridge at Stoney Creek Falls, with a stop overlooking the Barron Falls.

History

The Cairns–Herberton railway was built to serve the tin fields of Herberton and to provide a reliable supply line to the tableland after miners faced starvation during wet seasons. Construction of the difficult Barron Gorge section began in 1886 under chief contractor John Robb, employing up to 1,500 workers, many of them Italian and Irish navvies. The work was extremely hazardous — tunnels were dug with hand drills and explosives, and at least 32 workers died in construction accidents, commemorated at the Redlynch cemetery. The line opened to Kuranda (Myola) in June 1891 and eventually reached Herberton in 1910.

The journey

Trains depart Cairns and Freshwater stations, crossing the coastal plain before ascending the range through dense rainforest of the Wet Tropics World Heritage Area. Highlights include Horseshoe Bend, the Stoney Creek Falls bridge, the Barron Falls station viewing platform, and Robb's Monument. The restored timber carriages, some over a century old, are hauled by 1720-class diesel locomotives painted with Buda-dji, an Aboriginal Dreaming story of the carpet snake. At Kuranda station, famous for its tropical gardens and signal box, passengers can visit the village's markets, the Butterfly Sanctuary and Birdworld.

Heritage

The line is recognised as a feat of 19th-century engineering and is listed on the Queensland Heritage Register. It is regularly paired with the Skyrail Rainforest Cableway, which opened in 1995, for round trips between Cairns and Kuranda.
