Racial profiling

Racial profiling or ethnic profiling is the act of suspecting, targeting or discriminating against a person on the basis of their ethnicity, religion or nationality, rather than on individual evidence or behaviour. In law enforcement, the term usually refers to the practice of using race or ethnicity as a criterion in deciding whether to stop, search, question or investigate a person.

The practice is controversial worldwide and is prohibited, restricted or officially discouraged in many jurisdictions, although civil-liberties organisations argue that it persists in forms such as disproportionate traffic stops, airport screening and immigration enforcement.

Definition and terminology

Racial profiling is generally defined as any police or security action initiated primarily on the basis of a person's race, ethnicity or national origin rather than on specific evidence of wrongdoing. Related concepts include institutional racism, "driving while Black" in the United States, and stop and search disparities in the United Kingdom.

United States

In the United States, racial profiling has been a major focus of debate about policing. Studies of traffic stops in several states have found that Black and Hispanic drivers are stopped and searched at higher rates than white drivers, while searches of minority drivers often yield contraband at similar or lower rates. Federal guidance issued in 2003 and expanded in 2014 prohibits federal law-enforcement officers from using race or ethnicity in routine decision-making, with exceptions for national-security and border contexts.

The issue gained renewed prominence after 2020, when protests following the murder of George Floyd drew attention to broader patterns of unequal treatment by police.

United Kingdom

In Britain, police stop-and-search powers under the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 and counter-terrorism legislation have repeatedly been shown in official statistics to fall disproportionately on Black and Asian people. The Macpherson Report (1999), following the murder of Stephen Lawrence, characterised the Metropolitan Police as institutionally racist and recommended reforms in recording and accountability.

Other countries

Similar controversies exist in Canada, France, Germany, Israel and elsewhere, involving identity checks, border controls and counter-terrorism policing. France's prohibition on collecting ethnic statistics complicates measurement, though researchers using observational methods have reported large disparities in identity checks in Paris.

Effectiveness debate

Defenders of behaviour-based screening argue that investigative resources should follow evidence rather than demographic traits. Critics of profiling argue it is both discriminatory and inefficient, because it diverts attention from behavioural indicators and alienates communities whose cooperation police need. Some security officials have at times defended limited use of demographic factors, particularly after terrorist attacks, but empirical research generally finds profiling less effective than random or behaviour-based screening.
