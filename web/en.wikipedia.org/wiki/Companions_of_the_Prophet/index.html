Companions of the Prophet

The Companions of the Prophet (Arabic: اَلصَّحَابَةُ, aṣ-ṣaḥāba, singular صَحَابِيٌّ, ṣaḥābī, "companion") were the disciples and followers of the Islamic prophet Muhammad who "saw or met the prophet during his lifetime, while being a Muslim and were physically in his presence". The Companions are regarded in Islam as the first and exemplary generation of the Muslim community, and their transmission of Muhammad's words and deeds (the Sunnah) forms the basis of the hadith literature.

Definition

Scholars of hadith generally define a Companion as any Muslim who met Muhammad, believed in his message, and died as a Muslim. The category includes men, women, and children, those who emigrated with him (the Muhajirun) and the Medinan helpers (the Ansar), as well as late converts in the final years of his life. The generation after them is known as the Tabi'un (Successors).

Prominent Companions

Among the most prominent Companions were the first four caliphs — Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali — collectively known in Sunni Islam as the Rashidun ("rightly guided"). Others include Talha ibn Ubayd Allah, Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Sa'id ibn Zayd, and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, who with the four caliphs form the ten Companions promised Paradise (al-ʿashara al-mubashshara) in a well-known hadith. Important female Companions include Muhammad's wives Khadija and Aisha, and his daughter Fatima. Companions such as Abu Hurayra, Anas ibn Malik, and Abdullah ibn Abbas were prolific narrators of hadith.

Role in Islamic tradition

Sunni Islam holds the Companions in the highest esteem, teaches that they were the best generation of Muslims, and regards all of them as upright ('udul) transmitters whose reports can be trusted; criticising them is generally condemned. In Shia Islam, the assessment is more differentiated: while many Companions are honoured, a number are criticised for opposing Ali and his descendants after Muhammad's death, and only those deemed loyal to the Prophet's household are fully esteemed.

The Companions played the central role in the early expansion of Islam, the compilation of the Qur'an under Uthman, and the administration of the early caliphate. The last Companions are reported to have died in the late 7th and early 8th centuries CE.
