Holden Monaro

The Holden Monaro is a sports coupé manufactured by Australian car maker Holden. The nameplate was used in two distinct eras: from 1968 to 1977, when the original Monaro was offered as a two-door version of Holden's full-size sedans, and from 2001 to 2006, when it was revived as a coupé based on the Commodore.

First generation (1968–1977)

Launched with the HK series in 1968, the Monaro quickly became an icon of Australian motoring. The performance flagship, the Monaro GTS 327, won the Bathurst 500 in 1968 with Bruce McPhee and Barry Mulholland. Successive HT, HG, HQ, HJ, HX and HZ-series cars carried the name, with the HQ GTS coupé among the most collectible. The Monaro also saw success in touring car racing at the hands of drivers including Colin Bond.

Revival (2001–2006)

The Monaro returned in 2001 as the V2 series, derived from the VX Commodore and styled from the 1998 Coupé concept car shown at the Sydney Motor Show. Powered by V6 and LS-series V8 engines, it formed the basis of export programmes: it was sold in the United States as the revived Pontiac GTO (2004–2006), in the United Kingdom as the Vauxhall Monaro, and in the Middle East as the Chevrolet Lumina SS Coupé. High-performance versions were built by Holden Special Vehicles (HSV) as the Coupé GTO and GTS.

Legacy

Production ended in 2006, though the last car was auctioned for charity in 2006 for a record sum. The Monaro remains one of the most recognisable Australian performance cars.
