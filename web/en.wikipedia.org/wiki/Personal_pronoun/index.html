Personal pronoun

Personal pronouns are pronouns that are associated primarily with a particular person, in the grammatical sense of first person (the speaker), second person (the addressee), or third person (another party). They may also vary in number (singular or plural), gender, and grammatical case. In English, the personal pronouns include I, me, we, us, you, he, him, she, her, it, they, and them, together with related possessive and reflexive forms.

Features

Personal pronouns typically inflect or are selected according to person, number, gender, and case. Historically, many languages distinguished singular and plural second-person pronouns along with a T–V distinction of formality (such as French tu versus vous), which modern English lost when thou fell out of general use. Some languages mark clusivity, distinguishing inclusive "we" (including the listener) from exclusive "we". Many personal pronouns are suppletive, with unrelated forms for different cases, as in English I and me.

In English

Modern English personal pronouns retain case distinctions in the subjective (I, he, she, we, they), objective (me, him, her, us, them), and possessive forms. Singular they is used for a referent of unknown or unspecified gender and as the pronoun of choice of some individuals. Usage of gendered third-person pronouns and neopronouns has been a topic of active public discussion in recent years.
