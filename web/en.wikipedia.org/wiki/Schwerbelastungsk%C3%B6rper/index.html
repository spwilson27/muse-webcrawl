The Schwerbelastungskörper ("heavy load-bearing body") is a massive concrete cylinder in the Tempelhof district of Berlin, Germany, built in 1941 by the Nazi regime to test whether Berlin's marshy, sandy soil could bear the weight of the gigantic triumphal arch planned for Adolf Hitler and Albert Speer's redesigned world capital, Germania.

Construction and results

The cylinder is about 21 metres in diameter, 14 metres above ground, and extends some 18 metres below the surface, weighing about 12,650 tonnes — simulating the pressure one pier of the planned arch would exert. Instruments inside an access shaft measured subsidence: the structure sank about 19 cm during the war years, far beyond the 6 cm considered acceptable, indicating the arch could not have been built without extensive ground preparation.

Postwar

Too massive to demolish safely amid surrounding housing, the block survived and was used after the war by the Technical University of Berlin for soil-mechanics experiments. It is now a protected historic monument with an information pavilion and viewing platform.
