Julian Alexander Kitchener-Fellowes, Baron Fellowes of West Stafford (born 17 August 1949), known professionally as Julian Fellowes, is an English actor, novelist, writer, presenter, film director and Conservative peer. He is best known as the creator and writer of the period drama Downton Abbey and the screenwriter of Gosford Park, for which he won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay.

Early life and acting career

Fellowes was born in Cairo, where his father was a diplomat, and educated at Ampleforth College and Magdalene College, Cambridge, before studying at the Webber Douglas Academy of Dramatic Art. As an actor he appeared in numerous television productions, including the BBC sitcom Monarch of the Glen and, famously, as Kilwillie.

Writing career

His screenplay for Robert Altman's Gosford Park (2001), a murder mystery set in a 1930s English country house, won the Oscar for Best Original Screenplay. He went on to write Vanity Fair (2004), The Young Victoria (2009) and the musical Mary Poppins stage adaptation.

Downton Abbey, which he created and wrote almost entirely alone, ran on ITV from 2010 to 2015 and became a worldwide phenomenon, followed by feature films in 2019 and 2022 (A New Era) and 2025 (The Grand Finale). He later created The Gilded Age for HBO and Belgravia (2020), the latter adapted from his own novel. His novels include Snobs (2004) and Past Imperfect (2008).

Peerage

Fellowes was created a life peer on 13 January 2011, taking the title Baron Fellowes of West Stafford, of West Stafford in the County of Dorset, and sits in the House of Lords as a Conservative.
