Troll Wall

The Troll Wall (Norwegian: Trollveggen) is a steep mountain wall in the Romsdalen valley of Rauma Municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. Rising roughly 1,100 metres from the valley floor to the summit ridge of Trolltindene, it is the tallest vertical rock face in Europe and a celebrated objective for big-wall climbing. The wall forms part of the Reinheimen National Park area and overlooks the river Rauma and the Trollstigen road.

Climbing history

The Troll Wall resisted climbers until 1965, when a Norwegian team and a British team raced to complete the first ascents by different routes. Its loose rock, severe weather and serious objective danger gave it a forbidding reputation. In the 1970s and 1980s the wall became a centre for BASE jumping; a record jump was made by Carl Boenisch in 1984, but jumping was banned by law in 1986 after a series of fatalities.

Geology

The wall is composed of gneiss, a hard metamorphic rock, broken by bands of weaker material that contribute to frequent rockfalls; a major fall in 1998 measurably altered the upper wall and several of its pinnacles.
