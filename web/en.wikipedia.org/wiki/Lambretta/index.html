Lambretta

Lambretta is a brand of motor scooters originally manufactured in Milan, Italy, by Innocenti. Launched in 1947, the Lambretta — named after the Lambrate district of Milan and the nearby Lambro river — became, alongside Piaggio's Vespa, the defining scooter of the post-war era, and an enduring symbol of Italian design and of British mod culture in the 1960s.

History

Ferdinando Innocenti, whose steel-tube company had been bombed during the Second World War, commissioned engineer Corradino D'Ascanio's early design work and then Pier Luigi Torre to develop a cheap, enclosed two-wheeler for a ruined country. The Model A appeared in October 1947. Unlike the Vespa, the Lambretta used a tubular steel frame with the engine mounted centrally ahead of the rear wheel and a shaft or chain drive, giving characteristic elongated bodywork.

Successive series — the open-frame early models, the enclosed LC and LD of the 1950s, the slimmer Li series (1958), the TV performance models, and the SX and GP (DL) ranges of the late 1960s — made Lambretta Innocenti's best-known product, built under licence in Spain, India, Brazil, Argentina and elsewhere. Italian production ended in 1971 as Innocenti's finances collapsed; in India, Scooters India Limited continued building the design as the Vijai and Lamby for decades.

Culture

In Britain, Lambrettas (with Vespas) became the transport of choice of the mods, often heavily chromed and fitted with extra mirrors and lights, a style revived in later scooter scenes. Classic Lambrettas are now restored and traded worldwide, and the name has been revived on new scooters in the 21st century.
