Sovereign (British coin)

The sovereign is a British gold coin with a nominal value of one pound sterling. Struck from 1817 as part of the Great Recoinage, it became the principal gold coin of the British Empire under the gold standard, and is still produced by the Royal Mint as a bullion and collector coin.

Specifications

The modern sovereign weighs 7.988 grams (0.256 troy ounces) of 22-carat gold, 1.52 mm thick and 22.05 mm in diameter, containing 0.2354 troy ounces of pure gold. Its classic reverse, designed by Benedetto Pistrucci in 1817, shows Saint George slaying the dragon; other reverses have marked royal jubilees and anniversaries.

History

Sovereigns circulated widely in the 19th and early 20th centuries and were struck at branch mints in Australia, Canada, India and South Africa as well as London. Britain left the gold standard in the First World War and sovereigns ceased to circulate domestically, though they remained in use in parts of the Middle East and as a store of value. The Royal Mint resumed regular production for investors in 1957 and continues annual issues. Because sovereigns are UK legal tender, they occupy a distinctive tax position for British investors.
