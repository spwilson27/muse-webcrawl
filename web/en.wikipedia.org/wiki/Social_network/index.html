Social network

A social network is a social structure made up of a set of social actors (such as individuals or organisations), sets of dyadic ties, and other social interactions between actors. The social network perspective provides a set of methods for analysing social structures and theories explaining patterns observed in them.

Social network
FieldSociology, network science
Key conceptsNodes, ties, centrality, clusters, homophily

Overview

Networks are modelled as graphs: nodes represent actors and edges represent relationships such as friendship, kinship, work, information flow or online connection. Measures include degree, betweenness and closeness centrality, clustering coefficient and structural holes. Classic findings include Granovetter's "strength of weak ties" (1973), Milgram's small-world experiments ("six degrees of separation") and Dunbar's number (~150 stable relationships).

Applications

Social network analysis is used in sociology, epidemiology (disease spread), organisational studies, marketing and intelligence. The term also informally refers to online social networking services such as Facebook, but in scholarship it denotes the structure itself, on- or offline.
