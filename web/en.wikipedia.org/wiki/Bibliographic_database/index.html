Bibliographic database

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A bibliographic database is a database of bibliographic records, an organised digital collection of references to published literature, including journal and newspaper articles, conference proceedings, reports, government and legal publications, patents, books and so on. In contrast to library catalogue entries, a large proportion of the bibliographic records in these databases describe articles and conference papers rather than complete monographs, and they generally contain rich subject descriptions in the form of keywords, subject classification terms or abstracts.

Features

A bibliographic database may be general in scope or specialised in a discipline. Records typically include author names, title, source publication, date, and identifiers such as the DOI, ISBN or ISSN, together with an abstract and controlled-vocabulary subject headings (for example MeSH terms in MEDLINE). Many databases also provide citation indexing — tracking which later works cite a given publication — enabling citation analysis and bibliometrics.

History

Bibliographic databases grew out of printed abstracting and indexing services such as Chemical Abstracts (1907) and Index Medicus (1879). Computerised versions emerged in the 1960s and 1970s with batch-produced magnetic tapes and online services such as DIALOG and MEDLINE (1971). The web era made databases such as PubMed freely available, and Google Scholar (2004) brought broad multidisciplinary searching to the general public.

Examples

  • General / multidisciplinary: Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar
  • Biomedicine: PubMed / MEDLINE, Embase
  • Subject-specific: Chemical Abstracts (SciFinder), PsycINFO, EconLit, Inspec, MathSciNet, ERIC
  • Preprints and open archives: arXiv, SSRN
