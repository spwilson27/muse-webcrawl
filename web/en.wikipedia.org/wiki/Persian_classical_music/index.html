Persian classical music

Persian classical music (Persian: موسیقی سنتی ایران, "traditional music of Iran") is the art music tradition of Iran and the Persian-speaking world, with roots extending back more than a thousand years. It is a modal, largely improvisational music centred on the dastgah system and the repertoire known as the radif.

The dastgah system

The repertoire is organised into twelve modal systems: seven dastgahs and five derivative avaz. Each dastgah is defined by a characteristic scale, melodic patterns, and a sequence of shorter melodies called gushehs. Performers improvise within these models, typically in a small ensemble or solo.

The radif

The radif is the canonical collection of several hundred gushehs, memorised by students through years of study with a master (ostad). The most influential version was compiled in the nineteenth century by Mirza Abdollah and transmitted through his students. In 2009 the radif was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Instruments and forms

Principal instruments include the tar and setar (long-necked lutes), the santur (hammered dulcimer), the kamancheh (spike fiddle), the ney (end-blown flute), and the daf and tonbak frame and goblet drums. Vocal music often sets classical Persian poetry by Rumi, Hafez, and Saadi. Common forms include the pishdaramad (overture), the rhythmic tasnif (ballad), and free improvisation (avaz).

Notable musicians

Twentieth-century masters include Abolhasan Saba, Hossein Alizadeh, Kayhan Kalhor, and the vocalist Mohammad Reza Shajarian, widely regarded as the greatest singer of the tradition.
