Heartland rock is a genre of rock music characterised by a straightforward, roots-oriented musical style and a lyrical concern with the lives of ordinary, working-class Americans — especially in the industrial Midwest and Rust Belt. Exemplified by Bruce Springsteen, Bob Seger, Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers and John Mellencamp, it flourished commercially from the mid-1970s through the 1980s.

Characteristics

Heartland rock drew on 1960s rock and roll, folk, country and rhythm and blues, deliberately avoiding the artifice of glam rock, prog and disco. Songs typically tell stories of factory workers, small towns, failing farms, young lovers and economic decline, delivered with a sense of earnestness and communal identification. Arrangements favour ringing guitars, piano and organ, harmonica, and big, sing-along choruses.

History

The genre crystallised in the 1970s with Springsteen's Born to Run (1975) and Seger's Night Moves (1976), and reached mainstream peaks in the 1980s — Springsteen's Born in the U.S.A. (1984) and Mellencamp's Scarecrow (1985) — often in explicit sympathy with struggling industrial and agricultural communities. Mellencamp co-founded the Farm Aid benefit concerts in 1985. As grunge and alternative rock rose in the 1990s, heartland rock faded from the charts, but its influence persists in Americana, roots rock and artists such as The Gaslight Anthem, Jason Isbell and The War on Drugs.
