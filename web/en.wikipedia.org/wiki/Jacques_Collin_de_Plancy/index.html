Jacques Collin de Plancy

Born28 January 1793, Plancy-l'Abbaye, France
Died13 January 1881, Paris, France
OccupationWriter, occultist, demonologist
Notable workDictionnaire Infernal

Jacques Auguste Simon Collin de Plancy (28 January 1793 – 13 January 1881) was a French writer, occultist and demonologist, best known for his Dictionnaire Infernal (Infernal Dictionary), an illustrated encyclopedia of demons, occult beings and superstitions first published in 1818.

Life

Collin de Plancy was born in Plancy-l'Abbaye in the Aube department. He worked in Paris as a printer, bookseller and publisher, and produced a large number of works on the occult, folklore, legends and the supernatural, often writing under pseudonyms. In his early career he wrote in a sceptical, rationalist vein, mocking superstition; later in life, after returning to the Catholic faith, he revised his works to conform to orthodox religious teaching.

Dictionnaire Infernal

The Dictionnaire Infernal went through six editions between 1818 and 1863. The celebrated sixth edition of 1863 included 69 engravings by the artist Luis de M. Breton depicting demons in fantastical forms; these illustrations, including figures such as Astaroth (depicted as a naked man with a dragon-like mount) and Belphegor, have become iconic images of demonology and are widely reproduced.

Other works

Among his many other publications are the Dictionnaire féodal (1819), collections of ghost stories and legends, biographies of saints, and works on witchcraft and the history of religion.
