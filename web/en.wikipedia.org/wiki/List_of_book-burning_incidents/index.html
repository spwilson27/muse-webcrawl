List of book-burning incidents

Book burning is the deliberate destruction by fire of books or other written materials, usually carried out as an act of censorship, religious persecution or political suppression. Notable incidents include the following.

Ancient world

DateIncident
213 BCQin Shi Huang, first emperor of China, ordered the burning of books and burying of scholars to suppress dissenting schools of thought.
48 BCThe Library of Alexandria was partially burned during Julius Caesar's siege of Alexandria; the extent of the loss is debated.
c. AD 302Diocletian ordered the burning of Christian scriptures during the Great Persecution.

Medieval and early modern

DateIncident
12th centuryBurning of Talmud copies in Paris (1242), after the Disputation of Paris.
1497Savonarola's "Bonfire of the Vanities" in Florence consumed books, art and luxury goods.
1520sPublic burnings of Martin Luther's works in the Low Countries and England; Lutherans burned canon law and papal bulls in response.
1559 onwardsThe Catholic Church's Index Librorum Prohibitorum led to widespread destruction of prohibited books.
1562Diego de Landa ordered the burning of Maya codices at Maní, Yucatán.

Modern era

DateIncident
10 May 1933Nazi book burnings: students and SA men burned some 25,000 "un-German" books in Berlin's Opernplatz and other university cities.
1953–54Amid McCarthyism, some American libraries removed and in a few cases burned works by alleged communists; President Eisenhower publicly urged "Don't join the book burners."
1966–76China's Cultural Revolution saw mass destruction of books and cultural artefacts by Red Guards.
1981Burning of the Jaffna Public Library in Sri Lanka destroyed some 97,000 books and manuscripts.
1992Serb forces shelled and burned the National Library of Bosnia in Sarajevo, destroying about 1.5 million volumes.
2003Looting and burning of the Iraq National Library and archives followed the fall of Baghdad.
2014The Islamic State burned books and manuscripts from the Mosul Central Library.
