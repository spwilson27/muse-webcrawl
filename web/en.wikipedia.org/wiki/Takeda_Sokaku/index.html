Takeda Sokaku (also written Takeda Sōkaku; 1859–1943) was a Japanese martial artist of the late Edo and early Shōwa periods and the reviver and systematizer of Daitō-ryū Aiki-jūjutsu. This article uses the unmacronized romanization of his name.

Biography

Born into a samurai family of the Aizu domain, Takeda trained in swordsmanship, spear, and grappling arts from childhood. After the fall of the Tokugawa shogunate he wandered Japan as an itinerant teacher and duellist, refining the techniques passed down within the Takeda clan. From the 1890s onward he taught seminars across Hokkaidō and northern Honshū, particularly to police and military personnel.

Teachings

Takeda's curriculum centred on aiki techniques — methods of unbalancing an opponent through timing, posture, and joint manipulation — together with weapons work. He issued teaching licences (kyōju dairi) to senior students, most famously Morihei Ueshiba, whose aikido later carried these principles worldwide.
