Entente Cordiale
Signed8 April 1904
LocationLondon, United Kingdom
PartiesUnited Kingdom; French Third Republic
TypeColonial settlement and diplomatic rapprochement

The Entente Cordiale (French for "cordial understanding") comprised a series of agreements signed on 8 April 1904 between the United Kingdom and the French Republic which saw a significant improvement in Anglo-French relations. It settled long-running colonial disputes between the two powers and ended nearly a millennium of intermittent Anglo-French conflict, though it was not a formal military alliance.

Terms

The agreements dealt with a string of overseas flashpoints. Britain recognised France's predominance in Morocco, while France recognised Britain's position in Egypt. Colonial boundaries were settled in West Africa (Nigeria and the Niger basin), in Siam, Madagascar, and the New Hebrides, and French fishing rights off Newfoundland were traded for compensation and territorial adjustments in Africa. The settlement removed the causes of friction that had brought the two countries close to war over the Fashoda Incident of 1898.

Significance

The Entente was welcomed by British foreign secretary Lord Lansdowne and French foreign minister Théophile Delcassé, and was facilitated by King Edward VII's popular state visit to Paris in 1903. Together with the Franco-Russian Alliance and the Anglo-Russian Convention of 1907, it formed the Triple Entente, the alignment that faced the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy in the First World War. Closer military conversations, including naval arrangements under which France concentrated its fleet in the Mediterranean and Britain in the North Sea, gradually deepened the understanding well beyond its original colonial scope.
