Screwball comedy

Screwball comedy is a subgenre of romantic comedy that became popular in American cinema during the Great Depression, beginning in the mid-1930s and flourishing until the late 1940s. It is characterised by fast-paced overlapping dialogue, witty repartee, farcical situations, eccentric characters, plots involving courtship and marriage, and a playful battle of the sexes in which the woman often dominates the man.

History

The genre took off with films such as It Happened One Night (1934) and Twentieth Century (1934), partly as a response to the Hays Code: physical innuendo was displaced by verbal sparring. Classics include Bringing Up Baby (1938), The Philadelphia Story (1940) and Preston Sturges's The Lady Eve (1941) and Sullivan's Travels (1941). Elements of screwball persist in later romantic comedies and television sitcoms.
