W. M. Keck Observatory

W. M. Keck Observatory
Location: Mauna Kea, Hawaii, US
Altitude: 4,145 m
Telescopes: Keck I and Keck II, 10 m each
First light: 1990 / 1996

The W. M. Keck Observatory is an astronomical observatory near the summit of Mauna Kea in Hawaii. Its two telescopes, Keck I and Keck II, each have 10-metre primary mirrors and were among the largest optical telescopes in the world at their completion.

Telescopes

Each primary mirror consists of 36 hexagonal segments that act together as a single piece of reflective glass, aligned by active optics. Keck I began scientific observations in 1993 and Keck II in 1996. The telescopes can work together as an interferometer. They are equipped with adaptive optics using laser guide stars to correct atmospheric distortion.

Funding and operation

The observatory is funded by the W. M. Keck Foundation (named for William Myron Keck, founder of Superior Oil) and operated by the California Association for Research in Astronomy, a partnership of the California Institute of Technology and the University of California, with NASA as a partner.

Discoveries

Keck instruments have been used to study the supermassive black hole at the Galactic Center, exoplanets, distant galaxies and supernovae used in measuring the accelerating expansion of the universe.
