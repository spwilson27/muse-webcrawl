Sea Dart, or GWS.30, was a Royal Navy surface-to-air missile system designed in the 1960s and entering service in 1973. It was carried by Type 42 destroyers and the aircraft carriers of the Invincible class, and by the sole Type 82 destroyer, HMS Bristol. The missile used a ramjet sustainer fed by a solid-fuel booster, giving it high speed and long range against high-altitude and sea-skimming aircraft alike, guided by semi-active radar homing with illumination from the ship's Type 909 radar.

Combat use

Sea Dart saw extensive combat in the Falklands War of 1982, where it was credited with several confirmed kills of Argentine aircraft, though it also suffered from limitations against very low-level targets and was involved in the friendly-fire loss of a British helicopter. It was used again in the 1991 Gulf War, when HMS Gloucester shot down an Iraqi Silkworm anti-ship missile — the first successful interception of a missile by a missile in combat. The system was retired in 2012 with the last Type 42s, replaced by Sea Viper (Aster) on the Type 45 destroyer.
