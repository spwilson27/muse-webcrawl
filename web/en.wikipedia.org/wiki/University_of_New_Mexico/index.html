The University of New Mexico (UNM; Spanish: Universidad de Nuevo México) is a public research university in Albuquerque, New Mexico, United States. Founded in 1889 by the New Mexico Territorial Legislature, it is the state's flagship university and its largest institution of higher education by enrollment. UNM is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is one of only a handful of Hispanic-Serving Institutions in the United States that also hold that top research classification.

History

The university was authorized by an act of the territorial legislature signed by Governor L. Bradford Prince on February 28, 1889, a generation before New Mexico statehood in 1912. Classes began in 1892. Under its third president, William George Tight (1901–1909), the campus adopted the distinctive Pueblo Revival architectural style that still defines it, beginning with the remodeling of Hodgin Hall. Tight's successor, David Ross Boyd, oversaw expansion in the 1910s. The Zimmerman Library, designed by John Gaw Meem and opened in 1938, became the campus centerpiece and a landmark of Southwestern regionalist architecture. After the Second World War, enrollment grew rapidly under the G.I. Bill; the medical school was established in 1964.

Academics

UNM offers more than 200 degree programs through colleges including Arts and Sciences, the Anderson School of Management, Education and Human Sciences, Engineering, Fine Arts, Law, and the Health Sciences Center, which operates the UNM Hospital — the state's only Level I trauma center — along with schools of medicine, nursing, pharmacy, and population health. The university is noted for programs in Latin American studies, anthropology and archaeology, Earth and planetary sciences, and photonics, drawing on nearby national laboratories at Sandia and Los Alamos.

Campus

The main campus occupies about 800 acres in central Albuquerque, east of downtown along Central Avenue (historic Route 66). Its Pueblo Revival and Spanish Revival buildings — many designed by John Gaw Meem — make it one of the most architecturally distinctive campuses in the United States. Landmarks include Zimmerman Library, the Duck Pond, and the Center of the Universe sculpture. Branch campuses operate in Gallup, Los Alamos, Taos, Valencia County, and Rio Rancho.

Athletics

UNM's teams are the Lobos ("wolves" in Spanish), competing in the Mountain West Conference. The men's basketball team plays at The Pit (Dreamstyle Arena), an underground arena renowned as one of the loudest in college basketball and host of the 1983 NCAA Final Four. The football team plays at University Stadium.

Notable alumni

Alumni include author Rudolfo Anaya, artist Agnes Pelton, physicist and Nobel laureate Murray Gell-Mann (associated through research), former U.S. Senator Pete Domenici, and astronaut Mike Mullane. Faculty have included novelist Tony Hillerman, who taught journalism at the university.
