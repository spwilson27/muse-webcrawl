John Howard Northrop

John Howard Northrop (5 July 1891 – 27 May 1987) was an American biochemist who, with James Batcheller Sumner and Wendell Meredith Stanley, won the 1946 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the preparation of enzymes and virus proteins in a pure form.

Life

Northrop was born in Yonkers, New York, into a family of scientists — his father was a zoologist at Columbia University and his mother a botanist. He earned his PhD in chemistry from Columbia in 1915 and spent most of his career at the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research.

Work on enzymes

At the Rockefeller Institute, Northrop succeeded in crystallizing the digestive enzymes pepsin (1930), trypsin, and chymotrypsin, proving that enzymes are proteins — work that complemented Sumner's crystallization of urease. During World War II he worked on chemical warfare research.

Later life

After retiring from the Rockefeller Institute, Northrop worked at the University of California, Berkeley, on bacteriophages. He died in Wickenburg, Arizona, in 1987.
