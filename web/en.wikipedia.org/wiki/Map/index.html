Map

A map is a symbolic depiction emphasising relationships between elements of some space, such as objects, regions, or themes. Many maps are static two-dimensional representations, though modern maps can be interactive, three-dimensional, or depict change over time. The science and art of map-making is cartography.

History

Maps are among the oldest forms of human graphic communication; prehistoric cave and rock depictions are sometimes interpreted as maps. Mesopotamian clay tablets, Egyptian drawings, and Greek scholarship — culminating in Ptolemy's Geography (2nd century CE) — laid the foundations of systematic cartography. Medieval European mappaemundi, Islamic and Chinese map traditions, and the portolan charts of Mediterranean sailors preceded the great expansion of map-making in the Age of Discovery. The Mercator projection (1569) became standard for navigation. Modern mapping uses aerial photography, satellite imagery, GPS and geographic information systems (GIS).

Projections and scale

Because the Earth is roughly spherical, any flat map distorts area, shape, distance or direction; map projections are mathematical compromises among these properties. Map scale relates distance on the map to distance on the ground.

Types

General reference maps show topography and political boundaries; thematic maps show the distribution of a variable such as population or climate; nautical and aeronautical charts serve navigation; topographic maps show elevation with contour lines; and road maps, cadastral maps, geological maps and weather maps each serve specialised purposes. Digital web maps have become the most widely used form.
