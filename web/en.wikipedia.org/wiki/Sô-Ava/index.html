Sô-Ava is a town, arrondissement, and commune in the Atlantique Department of southern Benin. The commune occupies a watery landscape at the mouth of the Sô River on Lake Nokoué and is notable for its lacustrine villages built on stilts.

Ganvié

The commune includes Ganvié, often called the largest lake village in Africa and sometimes nicknamed the "Venice of Africa". According to tradition, the village was founded by the Tofinu people, who settled on the lake to escape slave raiders, protected by religious prohibitions against attacking people on the water. Houses, shops, and a market stand on wooden stilts above the lake, and transport is by dugout canoe.

Geography and life

Residents depend heavily on fishing, including the traditional acadja brush-park method of attracting fish, as well as tourism. The commune faces challenges from seasonal flooding, water quality, and pressure on fish stocks in Lake Nokoué.
