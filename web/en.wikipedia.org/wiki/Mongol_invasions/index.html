The Mongol invasions and conquests took place during the 13th and 14th centuries, creating the largest contiguous empire in history, the Mongol Empire (1206–1368). After Genghis Khan united the nomadic tribes of the Mongolian plateau in 1206, Mongol armies conquered the Western Xia and Jin dynasties in northern China, destroyed the Khwarezmian Empire of Persia in 1219–1221, and overran the Kievan Rus' principalities and eastern Europe in 1236–1242.

Major campaigns

Under Ögedei, the Mongols completed the conquest of northern China and invaded Korea and Europe, winning the battles of Legnica and Mohi in 1241 before withdrawing on the khan's death. Hulagu's campaigns in the Middle East culminated in the sack of Baghdad in 1258, ending the Abbasid Caliphate. Kublai Khan conquered the Song dynasty, completing the conquest of China in 1279 and founding the Yuan dynasty.

Failed and resisted invasions

Not all campaigns succeeded. Two invasions of Japan (1274 and 1281) were wrecked by typhoons — the famed kamikaze. Expeditions to Vietnam and Java ended in withdrawal, and the Mamluks of Egypt halted the Mongol advance at Ain Jalut in 1260. India under the Delhi Sultanate repelled repeated Mongol attacks.

Consequences

The conquests caused immense destruction and loss of life in many regions, yet the resulting Pax Mongolica secured trans-Eurasian trade along the Silk Road, enabled the exchange of technologies — including gunpowder, printing and astronomical knowledge — and facilitated diplomatic contacts between Europe and China. After the death of Möngke in 1259 the empire fragmented into the Yuan dynasty, the Ilkhanate, the Chagatai Khanate and the Golden Horde.
