Horn & Hardart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Horn & Hardart was an American food services company best known for operating Automats — cafeterias in which customers bought freshly prepared food from coin-operated glass-and-chrome compartments — in New York City and Philadelphia. At its height it was the world's largest restaurant chain, serving hundreds of thousands of customers a day.

History

Joseph Horn and Frank Hardart opened their first lunchroom in Philadelphia in 1888, advertising New Orleans-style coffee brewed with chicory. In 1902 they opened the first Automat in Philadelphia, adapting an idea from Germany, and in 1912 a flagship Automat opened in Times Square, New York. The Automats became famous for fresh food, low prices, uniform quality, and gleaming Art Deco interiors with dolphin-headed coffee spouts.

The Automat system

Behind the wall of compartments, kitchen staff continuously restocked dishes as they were sold. Customers exchanged paper money for nickels at cashier booths, inserted coins, turned a knob, and lifted the glass door to retrieve their food. The system eliminated the need for waiters and tipping, and the restaurants were celebrated as democratic spaces where customers of every class ate side by side.

Decline

The chain declined after the Second World War as fast-food chains, suburbanisation, and inflation — which made the nickel-based pricing model unworkable — eroded its business. The retail shops were converted to Burger King franchises in the 1970s, and the last New York Automat, on Third Avenue and 42nd Street, closed in 1991.
