Aden Governorate

Aden Governorate (Arabic: محافظة عدن) is a governorate of Yemen on the southern coast of the Arabian Peninsula, at the eastern approach to the Red Sea. Its capital is the port city of Aden, one of the world's great natural harbours. The governorate is among the smallest in Yemen by area — about 1,100 square kilometres — but is densely populated, with a population estimated in the high hundreds of thousands.

Geography

The governorate occupies a volcanic peninsula and surrounding coastal plain around Aden harbour, near the Bab el-Mandeb strait. The older city sits within an extinct volcanic crater (Crater district), while other districts — including Ma'alla, Tawahi, Sheikh Othman, Mansoura, Dar Sad, Buraika and Khormaksar — spread across the isthmus and mainland shore. The climate is hot and arid.

History

Aden was a British possession from 1839 to 1967, serving as a coaling and refuelling station on the route to India; it formed the Colony of Aden, distinct from the surrounding protectorates. After independence it became the capital of South Yemen (the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen, 1967–1990), and then a governorate of the unified Republic of Yemen. During the Yemeni civil war that began in 2014–15, Aden was taken by Houthi forces and retaken by government-aligned troops backed by a Saudi-led coalition; it served as the seat of the internationally recognised government, and control has since been contested with the Southern Transitional Council, which seized the city in 2019.

Administration

The governorate is divided into eight districts. The port, the Aden refinery and free zone, and Aden International Airport are its principal economic assets.
