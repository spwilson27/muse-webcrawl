Explication de texte

Explication de texte is a French formalist method of literary analysis involving the close reading of a text, in which all of a passage's formal features — diction, syntax, tone, rhythm, imagery, structure — are examined in order to elucidate its meaning. For many years it was the cornerstone of French literary studies and remains a standard exercise in French secondary and higher education.

Close annotation of a passage is the starting point of the method.

Method

In a traditional explication, the candidate first situates the extract within its work, author and genre, then follows the movement of the text in the order in which it unfolds, commenting on how language produces meaning and effect. Unlike the commentaire composé, which reorganises the analysis around thematic axes, the explication de texte proceeds linearly, section by section, and attends equally to form and content — the two being treated as inseparable.

History

The method was institutionalised in French universities and lycées in the early twentieth century, strongly associated with the literary historian Gustave Lanson. It was later challenged by the nouvelle critique of the 1950s–60s, and famously attacked by Roland Barthes in the Picard–Barthes quarrel, yet it survived as a central exercise of the French baccalauréat and the agrégation.

Influence

Explication de texte parallels, and partly influenced, the Anglo-American practice of close reading developed by the New Critics, and its discipline of attending to the words on the page continues to shape literary pedagogy in the francophone world.
