Oroville Dam

Oroville Dam is an earthfill embankment dam on the Feather River east of the city of Oroville in Northern California. At 770 feet (235 m) high, it is the tallest dam in the United States. Completed in 1968 as the centrepiece of the California State Water Project, it impounds Lake Oroville, a reservoir of about 3.5 million acre-feet used for water supply, flood control, recreation and hydroelectric generation at the Edward Hyatt Power Plant, one of the largest underground powerhouses in the country.

2017 spillway crisis

In February 2017, following heavy storms, a large crater eroded into the dam's concrete main spillway. With the reservoir rising, water flowed over the never-before-used emergency spillway, whose unpaved hillside eroded badly, prompting the evacuation of some 188,000 people downstream on 12 February. The dam itself was never in danger of collapse, but repairs to both spillways cost roughly $1.1 billion and were completed in 2019. An independent forensic report attributed the failure to long-term design, construction and maintenance shortcomings.
