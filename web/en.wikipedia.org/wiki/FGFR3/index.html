FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3)
Gene location: chromosome 4p16.3 (human)
Family: Receptor tyrosine kinases
Associated conditions: Achondroplasia, thanatophoric dysplasia, hypochondroplasia

FGFR3

Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the FGFR3 gene on chromosome 4. It is a receptor tyrosine kinase that binds fibroblast growth factors and regulates cell growth and differentiation, particularly in cartilage and bone development, where its normal role is to restrain the growth of long bones.

Clinical significance

Activating mutations in FGFR3 cause several skeletal dysplasias. The commonest form of dwarfism, achondroplasia, results in nearly all cases from a single glycine-to-arginine substitution (Gly380Arg) in the receptor's transmembrane domain, usually arising as a new mutation. More severe mutations cause thanatophoric dysplasia, which is lethal in the newborn period, while milder ones cause hypochondroplasia. Related mutations are also implicated in Muenke syndrome (craniosynostosis) and in some skin disorders.

Cancer

FGFR3 alterations occur in cancer as well: a chromosomal translocation, t(4;14), activates the gene in a subset of multiple myeloma, activating point mutations are frequent in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer, and FGFR3 fusions are targets of approved kinase inhibitors such as erdafitinib in urothelial carcinoma.
