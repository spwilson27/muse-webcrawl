Okinawan martial arts are the fighting traditions of the island of Okinawa, the largest of the Ryukyu Islands in present-day Japan. The best known of these arts is karate, together with the weapons art of kobudō. They developed over centuries from indigenous Ryukyuan fighting methods combined with influences from Chinese martial arts, and were shaped by periods during which weapons were banned on the island.

History

Okinawa's position on trade routes between China, Japan and Southeast Asia exposed it to many fighting traditions. Chinese envoys, merchants and martial artists visited the Ryukyu Kingdom, and Okinawan students travelled to Fuzhou and elsewhere in China to study.

Local unarmed fighting was known as te (手, "hand"). Distinct regional styles developed around the towns of Shuri (Shuri-te), Naha (Naha-te) and Tomari (Tomari-te). In 1609 the Satsuma domain of Japan invaded the Ryukyus and reinforced restrictions on weapons, encouraging the continued development of empty-hand methods and the adaptation of farm implements as weapons in kobudō.

Modern development

In the early 20th century, masters such as Ankō Itosu introduced karate into Okinawan school physical education, and Gichin Funakoshi brought it to mainland Japan, where it became the basis of Shotokan. Other major systems include Chōjun Miyagi's Gōjū-ryū, Kanbun Uechi's Uechi-ryū, and Chōtoku Kyan's Shōrin-ryū lineages. After the Second World War, American servicemen stationed on Okinawa helped spread the arts worldwide.

Principal arts

  • Karate — unarmed striking art emphasising punches, kicks, knee and elbow strikes, and open-hand techniques, practised through kata (forms), kihon (basics) and kumite (sparring).
  • Okinawan kobudō — weapons systems using the bō (staff), sai, tonfa, nunchaku, kama and other implements.
  • Tegumi — traditional Okinawan wrestling/grappling, related to Okinawan sumo.
