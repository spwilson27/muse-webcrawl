Compilation album

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Compilation albums collect previously released tracks

A compilation album comprises tracks, which may be previously released or unreleased, usually from several separate recordings by either one performer or by several performers. If by one artist, then generally the tracks were not originally intended for release together as a single work, but may be collected together as a greatest hits album or box set. If from several performers, there may be a theme, topic, or genre which links the tracks, or they may have been intended for release as a single work — such as a tribute album. When the tracks are by the same recording artist, the album may be referred to as a retrospective album or an anthology.

Common types

  • Greatest hits and best-of albums — collections of an artist's most successful or popular songs, often including singles and chart hits.
  • Rarities compilations — B-sides, outtakes, demos, and other recordings not previously available on albums.
  • Box sets — comprehensive multi-disc collections covering an artist's career, frequently with extensive liner notes.
  • Theme compilations — albums gathering songs on a subject, mood, or activity, often by various artists.
  • Genre and era compilations — records collecting representative tracks of a style or period, such as the long-running Now That's What I Call Music! series of current chart hits.
  • Sampler albums — inexpensive releases issued by record labels to showcase several artists on their roster.
  • Tribute albums — recordings of one artist's songs performed by other musicians.
  • Soundtrack compilations — songs assembled from or inspired by a film or television production.

History

Compilation albums became a major part of the record industry with the growth of the long-playing record in the 1950s and 1960s, as labels repackaged singles and back-catalogue material. Budget labels built businesses on licensing tracks for compilations. In the CD era of the 1980s and 1990s, compilations and box sets were highly profitable, and themed multi-artist compilations frequently topped album charts. The rise of digital downloads and streaming playlists reduced the commercial role of the traditional compilation, although greatest-hits packages and curated playlists continue the same basic idea.

Chart eligibility

Various-artists compilations have at times been excluded from main album charts; in the United Kingdom, for example, they were moved to a separate compilations chart in 1989 because they were dominating the artist album chart.
