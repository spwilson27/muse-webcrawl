CapitalsToungoo, Pegu (Bago), Ava (Inwa)

The Toungoo dynasty (Burmese: တောင်ငူမင်းဆက်) ruled Burma (Myanmar) from the mid-16th century to the mid-18th century. At its peak under Bayinnaung, the First Toungoo Empire was the largest empire in the history of Southeast Asia, encompassing present-day Myanmar, much of Thailand, Laos, and parts of Cambodia, Vietnam and Yunnan.

Rise

The dynasty began in Toungoo (Taungoo), a small principality in the Sittaung valley. Mingyi Nyo founded the dynasty in 1510, refusing obedience to the declining Ava Kingdom. His son-in-law and successor Tabinshwehti (r. 1530–1550) reunified Burma, conquered the Mon kingdom of Hanthawaddy (Pegu), and made Pegu his capital — beginning what historians call the First Toungoo Empire.

Bayinnaung and the empire

Tabinshwehti's brother-in-law and general Bayinnaung (r. 1550–1581) succeeded after Tabinshwehti's assassination and built an enormous empire: he reconquered the rebellious provinces, took the Shan states, conquered Ayutthaya (Siam) in 1564 and again in 1569, subdued Lan Xang (Laos), and pushed into Manipur. His empire, sustained by Portuguese mercenaries and firearms, overstretched its resources; after his death his son Nanda Bayin faced revolts on all sides, and the empire disintegrated spectacularly in the 1590s (the empire's implosion is sometimes called the "Southeast Asian equivalent" of the fall of a superpower).

Restored Toungoo (Nyaungyan dynasty)

Bayinnaung's son Nyaungyan Min, based at Ava, rebuilt a smaller, more defensible kingdom in Upper Burma, and his son Anaukpetlun reunified the country down to Pegu by 1613. King Thalun (r. 1629–1648) consolidated the restoration, moved the capital back to Ava, conducted a census and land survey, and avoided foreign adventures. The "Restored Toungoo" kingdom endured for over a century in relative stability, though gradually weakened by succession disputes, Manipuri raids, and the growing power of the Mon south.

Fall

In 1740 the Mon of Lower Burma revolted and founded the Restored Hanthawaddy Kingdom. Hanthawaddy forces captured Ava in 1752, ending the Toungoo dynasty after 242 years. The Burmese resistance coalesced almost immediately around Alaungpaya, founder of the Konbaung dynasty.
