FAME Studios

FAME Studios (Florence Alabama Music Enterprises) is a historic recording studio in Muscle Shoals, Alabama, founded by Rick Hall in the early 1960s. It created the "Muscle Shoals sound" of soul, R&B and rock, anchored by the house band later known as the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section ("the Swampers").

FAME Studios
Location: Muscle Shoals, Alabama
Founder: Rick Hall
Founded: 1959/1961

History

Early hits included Percy Sledge's "When a Man Loves a Woman" (recorded nearby) era sessions, Wilson Pickett's "Mustang Sally" and "Land of 1000 Dances", and Aretha Franklin's breakthrough 1967 sessions including "I Never Loved a Man". Etta James, Clarence Carter, Duane Allman and many others recorded there; after the Swampers left in 1969 to found a rival studio, FAME continued with artists from the Osmonds to Gregg Allman.

Legacy

The studio still operates, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and was featured in the 2013 documentary Muscle Shoals.
