Leo V the Armenian

Leo V the Armenian (Greek: Λέων ὁ Ἀρμενίος; c. 775 – 25 December 820) was Byzantine emperor from 813 to 820. A successful general of Armenian descent, he came to the throne after the catastrophic Bulgarian wars of Nikephoros I and Michael I, defeated the Bulgar khan Krum's threat to Constantinople, and reintroduced iconoclasm before being assassinated in the palace chapel on Christmas Day 820.

Reign

Leo served as strategos of the Anatolic theme and was proclaimed emperor by his troops after Michael I's defeat at Versinikia in 813 opened the way for the Bulgar khan Krum to advance on Constantinople. Krum died suddenly in 814 while preparing a massive siege, and Leo consolidated the frontier with a 30-year peace treaty with Krum's successor Omurtag (815/816), recovering territory in Thrace and rebuilding the capital's defences.

Iconoclasm and assassination

Attributing the disasters of the iconophile emperors to God's displeasure, Leo appointed a commission under John the Grammarian to gather patristic arguments against icons, deposed Patriarch Nikephoros I in 815, and convened a synod that reinstated the iconoclast decrees of 754. His renewed persecution was generally milder than that of Constantine V, but it poisoned relations with the church. In 820 Leo arrested his old comrade Michael the Amorian for conspiracy; Michael's supporters struck first, assassinating Leo before the altar of the palace chapel of St Stephen during Christmas matins. Michael II succeeded him and founded the Amorian dynasty.
