Fluxus

Fluxus was an international avant-garde collective of artists, composers and designers founded in 1960 by Lithuanian-American artist George Maciunas. Known for experimental performance (“event scores”), humour, simplicity and anti-art attitudes, Fluxus blurred art and everyday life and influenced conceptual art, performance art and intermedia.

Fluxus
Years active: 1960s–1970s (ongoing influence)
Founder: George Maciunas
Artists: Yoko Ono, Nam June Paik, Joseph Beuys, Dick Higgins, Alison Knowles

History

Maciunas, inspired by John Cage’s experimental composition classes and Dada, organised Fluxus festivals in Europe in 1962 and published a manifesto in 1963 calling to “purge the world of bourgeois sickness” and promote living art. The New York Fluxus shop and cooperatives produced cheap multiples — Fluxkits and games — meant to bypass the commercial art market.

Works and ideas

Event scores were brief instructions anyone could perform, such as Yoko Ono’s pieces in Grapefruit. Nam June Paik pioneered video art within the movement. Fluxus emphasised process over product, collectivity, and the idea that anyone can make art.
