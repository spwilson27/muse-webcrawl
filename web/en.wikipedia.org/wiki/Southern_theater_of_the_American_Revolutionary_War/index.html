Southern theater of the American Revolutionary War

Southern theater, 1775–1783

Date1775–1783
LocationVirginia, the Carolinas, Georgia, East Florida
ResultAmerican–French victory; British surrender at Yorktown
Key commandersNathanael Greene, Daniel Morgan; Lord Cornwallis, Banastre Tarleton

The southern theater of the American Revolutionary War was the central area of operations in the second half of the American Revolutionary War, from 1778 to 1781. After the failure of northern campaigns, Britain adopted a "southern strategy", hoping Loyalist support in the Carolinas and Georgia would restore royal control.

The southern strategy

British forces captured Savannah in December 1778 and Charleston in May 1780, the worst American defeat of the war. A second American army was destroyed at the Battle of Camden in August 1780. However, Loyalist mobilisation fell short of expectations, and partisan warfare by militia leaders such as Francis Marion, Thomas Sumter, and Andrew Pickens kept the countryside contested.

The Greene campaign

Major General Nathanael Greene took command in December 1780 and split his army, leading to Daniel Morgan's victory at Cowpens in January 1781. Greene then conducted the "Race to the Dan", retreating through North Carolina before turning to fight at Guilford Court House in March 1781. Though a tactical British victory, the battle cost Cornwallis a quarter of his army.

Yorktown

Weakened, Cornwallis marched into Virginia, where he fortified Yorktown. A combined American and French army under George Washington and Rochambeau, supported by the French fleet's victory at the Chesapeake, besieged him; Cornwallis surrendered on 19 October 1781, effectively ending major fighting and leading to the Treaty of Paris (1783).
