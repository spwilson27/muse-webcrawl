Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals

The Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS) is an internationally agreed-upon standard managed by the United Nations that was created to replace the assortment of hazardous material classification and labelling schemes around the world with a single consistent system. Its core elements include standardized criteria for classifying hazards (physical, health and environmental) and standardized labelling elements, such as pictograms, signal words and hazard and precautionary statements.

The GHS was developed through a mandate from the 1992 United Nations Conference on Environment and Development in Rio de Janeiro, and is updated biennially by the UN Sub-Committee of Experts on the GHS. The system is published in the so-called "Purple Book". It has been implemented in many jurisdictions, including the European Union through the CLP Regulation and in the United States through revisions to the OSHA Hazard Communication Standard.

GHS hazard pictograms include nine symbols: flame, flame over circle, exploding bomb, corrosion, gas cylinder, skull and crossbones, exclamation mark, health hazard, and environment. Signal words are either "Danger" or "Warning", and hazard statements are assigned standardized H-codes. The system also underpins the structure of safety data sheets, which follow a fixed 16-section format.
