Secured transaction

A secured transaction gives the lender a security interest in the borrower's collateral.

A secured transaction is a loan or credit transaction in which the lender acquires a security interest in collateral owned by the borrower, and is entitled to seize and sell that collateral if the borrower defaults. Secured transactions are fundamental to modern commercial finance: by reducing the lender's risk, they make credit cheaper and more widely available, and they allow businesses to borrow against inventory, equipment, accounts receivable and other assets.

United States law: UCC Article 9

In the United States, secured transactions in personal property are governed principally by Article 9 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), adopted in some form by every state. Article 9 replaced a patchwork of older devices — chattel mortgages, conditional sales, trust receipts, factor's liens — with a single concept of the "security interest". Its central concepts include:

  • Attachment — the point at which a security interest becomes enforceable against the debtor, generally requiring value, debtor rights in the collateral, and an authenticated security agreement describing the collateral.
  • Perfection — steps, usually filing a financing statement (Form UCC-1) in a central public registry, that make the interest effective against third parties; some interests perfect automatically or by possession or control.
  • Priority — rules ranking competing claims to the same collateral; in general, the first to file or perfect wins, with special superpriority for purchase-money security interests (PMSI).
  • Default and enforcement — on default the secured party may take possession of the collateral without breach of the peace and dispose of it in a commercially reasonable manner, applying the proceeds to the debt.

Other systems

Other jurisdictions achieve similar results through different legal forms. In England and Wales, companies grant fixed and floating charges over their assets, registered at Companies House. Many civil-law jurisdictions historically restricted non-possessory security over movables, but reforms inspired by Article 9 and the UNCITRAL Model Law on Secured Transactions (2016), as well as regional instruments such as the OHADA uniform act, have spread notice-filing registries and functional security interests worldwide. Security over land is generally handled separately, through mortgage law.
