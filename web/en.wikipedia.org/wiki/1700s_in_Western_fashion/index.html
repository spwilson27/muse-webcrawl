1700s in Western fashion

Fashion in the 1700s in Western Europe and its colonies was characterised by the height of Baroque splendour at the court of Louis XIV of France, whose style set the tone for aristocratic dress across the continent. The decade saw the consolidation of the three-piece suit for men and the mantua for women.

Men's fashion

Men wore the justaucorps, a long, knee-length coat fitted at the waist and flaring over the hips, worn over a waistcoat (veste) and breeches, with a full-bottomed periwig early in the decade that gradually gave way to smaller wigs. The cravat, often of lace or fine muslin, replaced the falling band collar.

Women's fashion

The mantua, an open-fronted gown with a trained skirt looped up over a contrasting petticoat, dominated formal dress. The fontange, a tall headdress of lace and ribbon, remained fashionable early in the decade. Rich brocaded silks, often from Lyon or Spitalfields, displayed large-scale floral patterns.

Influence

French court fashion spread through fashion dolls, engravings, and the first fashion periodicals, while sumptuary display reinforced hierarchy across Europe and the colonial Americas.
