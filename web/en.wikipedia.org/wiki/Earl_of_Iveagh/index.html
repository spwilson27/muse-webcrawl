Earl of Iveagh (pronounced "Ivy") is a title in the Peerage of the United Kingdom, created in 1919 for the businessman and philanthropist Edward Guinness, 1st Baron Iveagh, head of the Guinness brewing dynasty. The title is named after Iveagh, a barony in County Down, Northern Ireland.

History

The Guinness family descends from Arthur Guinness, founder of the Guinness brewery at St James's Gate, Dublin, in 1759. Edward Guinness (1847–1927), great-grandson of the founder, was created a baronet in 1885, raised to the peerage as Baron Iveagh in 1891, created Viscount Iveagh in 1905, and finally advanced to the earldom in 1919, with the subsidiary title of Viscount Elveden, of Elveden in the County of Suffolk.

The first Earl was one of Ireland's richest men and a notable philanthropist: he founded the Iveagh Trust in Dublin to provide housing for the poor, funded the restoration of St Patrick's Cathedral, and gave Farmleigh House and Kenwood House to the nation. He was succeeded by his son Rupert Edward Cecil Lee Guinness, 2nd Earl of Iveagh (1874–1967), who was chairman of Guinness and a Member of Parliament, and whose wife Gwendolen, Countess of Iveagh, was also an MP. The 2nd Earl was followed by his grandson Benjamin Guinness, 3rd Earl (1937–1992), and then by his son Edward Guinness, 4th Earl (born 1969), a former member of the House of Lords until 1999.

Family seats and wealth

The family's principal seat is Elveden Hall in Suffolk, near Thetford, purchased by the 1st Earl in 1894 from the exiled Maharaja Duleep Singh. The Elveden estate, of some 22,500 acres, is one of the largest farming estates in England. In Ireland the family seat was Farmleigh, in Dublin's Phoenix Park, now the official Irish state guest house. The Guinness family has frequently appeared near the top of British and Irish rich lists, with wealth derived from the brewery (now part of Diageo) and extensive investments.

Earls of Iveagh (1919)

  • Edward Guinness, 1st Earl of Iveagh (1847–1927)
  • Rupert Edward Cecil Lee Guinness, 2nd Earl of Iveagh (1874–1967)
  • (Arthur) Benjamin Guinness, 3rd Earl of Iveagh (1937–1992)
  • Arthur Edward Rory Guinness, 4th Earl of Iveagh (born 1969)

The heir apparent is the present holder's son, Arthur Benjamin Geoffrey Guinness, Viscount Elveden (born 2003).
