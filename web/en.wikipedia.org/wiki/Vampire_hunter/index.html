Vampire hunter


A traditional vampire-hunting kit

A vampire hunter, or vampire slayer, is a person who specializes in finding and destroying vampires. Vampire hunters appear in folklore, in Gothic and horror fiction, and in modern fantasy across literature, film, television and video games.

Folkloric vampire hunters

In Balkan and Eastern European folklore, vampire hunters (in Serbian tradition sometimes called dhampirs when believed to be the offspring of a vampire and a human) were specialists engaged by communities who believed a corpse had become a vampire. Their methods included exhuming the suspected corpse, driving a stake through its heart, decapitation, and burning the remains. In some traditions the hunter was believed to be able to see the vampire in its grave or to identify it by the corpse's ruddy complexion and lack of decomposition.

The motif of the professional vampire hunter draws partly on historical vampire panics of the 18th century, such as the cases of Petar Blagojević and Arnold Paole in Serbia, which drew the attention of Austrian imperial authorities and, indirectly, of Enlightenment writers such as Dom Augustin Calmet.

In fiction

The most famous fictional vampire hunter is Professor Abraham Van Helsing in Bram Stoker's 1897 novel Dracula. Van Helsing is a Dutch physician and polymath who brings both scientific knowledge and folk remedies—garlic, crucifixes, consecrated wafers and wooden stakes—against Count Dracula. The character set the template for the scholarly, well-equipped hunter in later works.

Later examples include the title character of Buffy the Vampire Slayer (film 1992; television series 1997–2003), in which the "Slayer" is a chosen young woman endowed with supernatural strength, advised by a Watcher; Blade, the Marvel Comics half-vampire hunter; the Belmont clan of the Castlevania video game series; and the hunters of the tabletop and television franchise Supernatural. Vampire hunters are typically depicted carrying specialized weapons such as wooden or silver stakes, crossbows, holy water, ultraviolet light and blessed blades.

Weapons and tools

Folkloric and fictional accounts commonly arm the vampire hunter with: wooden stakes (often of ash, hawthorn or oak), garlic, crucifixes and other holy symbols, mirrors, holy water, consecrated hosts, running water (which vampires were said to be unable to cross), and, in modern works, firearms loaded with silver or wooden projectiles and ultraviolet lamps.
