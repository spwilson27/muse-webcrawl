San Gimignano

San Gimignano
Country: Italy
Region: Tuscany (province of Siena)
Population: about 7,700
UNESCO World Heritage Site: Historic Centre of San Gimignano (1990)

San Gimignano is a small walled medieval hill town in the province of Siena, Tuscany, north-central Italy. Known as the Town of Fine Towers, it is famous for its medieval architecture, unique in the preservation of about a dozen of its tower houses, which, with its hilltop setting and surrounding landscape, form "an unforgettable skyline". Within the walls, the well-preserved buildings include notable examples of both Romanesque and Gothic architecture, with outstanding frescoes. The "Historic Centre of San Gimignano" is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The town takes its name from Saint Geminianus, the bishop of Modena who is said to have saved it from the Huns. It grew prosperous as a stopping point on the Via Francigena pilgrimage route to Rome and from trade in saffron and Vernaccia wine. Wealthy families competed by building ever taller tower houses — at the peak there were some 72; plague in 1348, followed by submission to Florence, ended its prosperity and, paradoxically, preserved its medieval fabric, since little new building took place.

Main sights

Fourteen towers of varying heights survive, including the Torre Grossa (1311), at 54 metres the tallest, attached to the Palazzo Comunale. The Collegiate Church (Duomo), consecrated in 1148, contains celebrated fresco cycles by Benozzo Gozzoli, Domenico Ghirlandaio, and Taddeo di Bartolo. The town's white wine, Vernaccia di San Gimignano, was the first Italian wine to receive DOC status in 1966.
