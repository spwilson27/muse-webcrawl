Penedès

Penedès is a natural and historical region and a wine-producing area in Catalonia, Spain, lying between Barcelona and Tarragona, stretching from the Mediterranean coast inland to the prelittoral hills. It corresponds roughly to the comarques of Alt Penedès, Baix Penedès, and Garraf. The capital is Vilafranca del Penedès.

Wine

Penedès is one of Spain's most important wine regions, with a tradition going back to the Phoenicians and Romans. It is best known as the home of Cava, the Spanish sparkling wine made by the traditional method, centered on the town of Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, where producers such as Freixenet and Codorníu are based. The Penedès Denominació d'Origen covers still wines, notably whites from the native Xarel·lo, Macabeo, and Parellada grape varieties, as well as reds from Tempranillo, Cabernet Sauvignon, and others.

Geography

The region is divided into the coastal Baix Penedès, the central plain, and the higher Alt Penedès. The Foix river crosses it, and the landscape is dominated by vineyards, almond and olive groves, and large wine estates (masies).
