Sengaku-ji (泉岳寺) is a Sōtō Zen Buddhist temple in Takanawa, Minato, Tokyo, Japan. It is famous as the burial place of Asano Naganori and the Forty-seven Rōnin, whose graves are a popular site of pilgrimage and tourism.

History

The temple was founded in 1612 by Tokugawa Ieyasu near Edo Castle. After a fire destroyed the original building in 1641, it was rebuilt at its present site in Takanawa on the orders of Tokugawa Ietsuna, with the work carried out by five daimyō including the Asano clan.

The Forty-seven Rōnin

In 1701, Asano Naganori, the daimyō of Akō, attacked the court official Kira Yoshinaka within Edo Castle and was ordered to commit seppuku. He was buried at Sengaku-ji. In December 1702, forty-seven of his now masterless retainers (rōnin) avenged him by killing Kira, after which they too were ordered to commit seppuku and were buried beside their lord. Their graves remain the temple's chief attraction, and incense burns perpetually before them. The story, known as Chūshingura, became one of Japan's most famous tales of loyalty and revenge.

Festivals

The temple holds festivals on April and December weekends commemorating the rōnin, drawing large crowds of visitors who come to pay respects at the graves.
