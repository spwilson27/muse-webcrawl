The Qassam rocket (Arabic: صاروخ القسام Ṣārūkh al-Qassām; also written Kassam) is a family of steel artillery rockets developed and deployed by the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades, the military wing of Hamas, in the Gaza Strip. Named after the Brigades' eponym, Sheikh Izz ad-Din al-Qassam, the rockets were first fired in 2001.

Variants

The family includes four main models. The Qassam 1, first used in October 2001, had a range of about 3 km and carried a small warhead; the Qassam 2 followed in 2002 with a range of 8–9.6 km and an approximately 5–7 kg warhead; and the Qassam 3 and 4, developed in 2005, extended the range to about 10–17 km with warheads of 10–20 kg. The rockets are fabricated from water-pipe steel tubing, sheet metal fins, and solid propellant made from sugar and potassium nitrate fertiliser, with TNT or urea-nitrate warheads.

Use and countermeasures

Thousands of Qassams were fired from Gaza at Israeli towns near the border, notably Sderot and Ashkelon, killing and injuring civilians over the years. The rockets are highly inaccurate—unguided and unstable in flight—and their military effect has been largely psychological. Israel responded with border closures, targeted strikes on production workshops, and, from 2011, the Iron Dome air-defence system, which intercepts larger projectiles. Use of the rockets has been condemned as indiscriminate attacks on civilians.
