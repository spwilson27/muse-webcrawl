Linus Carl Pauling (1901–1994) was an American chemist, biochemist and peace activist, and one of the most influential scientists of the 20th century. He is one of only five people to win two Nobel Prizes, and the only person to win two unshared ones — Chemistry (1954) and Peace (1962).

Pauling's book The Nature of the Chemical Bond (1939) founded modern structural chemistry: he introduced concepts of resonance, hybridisation and the electronegativity scale. He discovered the alpha helix structure of proteins (1951) and identified sickle-cell anaemia as a molecular disease.

[Image: Linus Pauling holding a molecular model]

Peace work and later years

Pauling campaigned against nuclear weapons testing, helping win the 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty. Later he controversially championed high-dose vitamin C for colds and cancer; controlled trials have not supported the strongest claims, and his institute continues research on micronutrients.

The DNA race

Pauling proposed an incorrect triple-helix DNA model in 1953, shortly before Watson and Crick, aided by Rosalind Franklin's data, published the double helix — one of history's famous near-misses.
