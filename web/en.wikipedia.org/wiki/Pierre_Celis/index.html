Pierre Celis

Born21 March 1925, Hoegaarden, Belgium
Died9 April 2011, Tienen, Belgium
OccupationBrewer
Known forReviving the Belgian witbier style; Hoegaarden brewery

Pierre Celis (21 March 1925 – 9 April 2011) was a Belgian brewer who revived the witbier (white beer) style and founded the Hoegaarden brewery, single-handedly rescuing a beer style that had died out.

Reviving witbier

Celis grew up in Hoegaarden next door to the Tomsin brewery, the last witbier brewery in Belgium, and worked there as a young man. When Tomsin closed in 1957, witbier — a cloudy wheat beer brewed with coriander and dried Curaçao orange peel — ceased to be produced. Working as a milkman, Celis began brewing the style again from memory in 1965–66, founding the Brouwerij Celis in Hoegaarden. His Hoegaarden witbier became a major success and led a worldwide revival of the style.

Later career

After a fire in 1985, Celis accepted investment from Interbrew, which eventually took control of the Hoegaarden brand; Celis left and in 1992 emigrated to Austin, Texas, where he founded the Celis Brewery and introduced Celis White to the American craft-beer market. The Texas brewery closed in 2000, but the brand was later revived by his daughter Christine Celis. Pierre Celis died in 2011 and is regarded as one of the most influential figures in the craft beer renaissance.
