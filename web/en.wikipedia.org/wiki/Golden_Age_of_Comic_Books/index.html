The Golden Age of Comic Books describes an era of American comic books from the late 1930s to the early 1950s, during which the modern comic book was created, sales soared, and the superhero genre was born and came to dominate the medium. The period conventionally begins with the publication of Action Comics #1 in 1938, featuring the first appearance of Superman.

History

Comic books developed in the early 1930s as reprints of newspaper comic strips before publishers began commissioning original material. Superman, created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster for what became DC Comics, was an immediate sensation: a superhuman champion with a secret identity, cape and costume — the template for the genre. Batman (1939, Bob Kane and Bill Finger) and a flood of costumed heroes followed: the Flash, Green Lantern, Hawkman, Wonder Woman (1941, William Moulton Marston), and Captain America (1941, Joe Simon and Jack Kirby), whose first cover famously showed him punching Adolf Hitler.

During the Second World War, comics were hugely popular with children and servicemen; superheroes fought Axis villains, and sales of some titles exceeded a million copies per issue. Many publishers competed — Timely Comics (the future Marvel), Fawcett (whose Captain Marvel at times outsold Superman), Quality, MLJ (Archie) and others.

Post-war diversification and decline

After the war, superhero sales faded while other genres flourished: crime comics, romance comics, westerns, funny animals, teen humour (Archie) and — most controversially — horror and crime titles from EC Comics, whose Tales from the Crypt and literary ambitions set new standards. Psychiatrist Fredric Wertham's book Seduction of the Innocent (1954) blamed comics for juvenile delinquency, prompting US Senate hearings and the industry's self-censorship body, the Comics Code Authority (1954). EC's horror and crime lines were destroyed, many publishers folded, and the superhero genre entered a lull until its revival in 1956 with the new Flash — the start of the Silver Age.

Legacy

The Golden Age created most of the enduring icons of American comics and the industrial model of work-for-hire superhero publishing. Original issues are among the most valuable collectibles in the world; a copy of Action Comics #1 has sold for several million dollars. The era's characters anchor today's comics, film and television franchises.
