Walter Rauschenbusch

Born: 4 October 1861, Rochester, New York
Died: 25 July 1918 (aged 56), Kingston, New York
Occupation: Baptist pastor, theologian
Known for: Social Gospel movement

Walter Rauschenbusch (4 October 1861 – 25 July 1918) was an American Baptist pastor and theologian, the leading intellectual figure of the Social Gospel movement in the United States. His books, especially Christianity and the Social Crisis (1907) and A Theology for the Social Gospel (1917), argued that Christianity must address social and economic injustice and not only individual salvation.

Life

Born in Rochester, New York, to German immigrant parents, Rauschenbusch was educated in Germany and at the University of Rochester and Rochester Theological Seminary. In 1886 he became pastor of the Second German Baptist Church in New York City, serving the immigrant neighbourhood of Hell's Kitchen. There, confronting poverty, unemployment, and child mortality among his congregation, he moved beyond purely evangelical preaching toward structural reform. A period of deafness ended his preaching career; from 1897 (formally 1902) he taught church history at Rochester Theological Seminary, where his writing flourished.

Thought

Rauschenbusch taught that sin was social as well as personal, embedded in what he called the "kingdom of evil" — exploitative institutions and inherited inequities — and that the Kingdom of God required the Christianisation of the social order: fair wages, labour rights, the abolition of child labour, and democratic control of industry. He historicised Jesus as a social prophet whose message had been obscured by individualistic theology. His work influenced later movements, including Martin Luther King Jr.'s social Christianity, and remains a reference point for Christian social ethics, though critics questioned his optimism about progress, which the First World War gravely damaged.
