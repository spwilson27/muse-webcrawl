Medial geniculate nucleus

The medial geniculate nucleus (MGN), also called the medial geniculate body, is the part of the auditory thalamus that relays information between the inferior colliculus of the midbrain and the primary auditory cortex. It forms a small bump on the posteroinferior surface of the thalamus, just medial to its visual counterpart, the lateral geniculate nucleus. The MGN is the final subcortical processing station of the ascending auditory pathway.

Divisions

  • Ventral division (MGv) – the "lemniscal" core: sharply tonotopically organized, receives input from the central nucleus of the inferior colliculus, and projects to layer III/IV of primary auditory cortex (A1), preserving frequency information for sound identification.
  • Dorsal division (MGd) – "non-lemniscal": broadly tuned neurons responsive to complex sounds; projects to secondary (belt) auditory areas and is involved in multisensory integration.
  • Medial (magnocellular) division (MGm) – receives auditory, somatosensory, and vestibular inputs and projects widely, including to the amygdala—an important pathway for auditory fear conditioning—and to all auditory cortical areas.

Pathway

Auditory signals ascend from the cochlea through the cochlear nuclei, superior olivary complex, and lateral lemniscus to the inferior colliculus, then travel in the brachium of the inferior colliculus to the MGN. From the MGN, the acoustic radiations carry fibers through the sublenticular part of the internal capsule to Heschl's gyrus in the temporal lobe.

Function

Beyond simple relay, the MGN gates and modulates auditory information according to attention and arousal, under heavy descending (corticothalamic) feedback from the auditory cortex—feedback fibers outnumber ascending ones. The MGN's medial division participates in the rapid subcortical route by which conditioned sounds trigger defensive responses via the amygdala.
