Tennessee House of Representatives

Tennessee House of Representatives
Type: Lower house of the Tennessee General Assembly
Seats: 99
Term length: 2 years
Meeting place: Tennessee State Capitol, Nashville

The Tennessee House of Representatives is the lower house of the Tennessee General Assembly, the state legislature of Tennessee. It consists of 99 members elected from single-member districts across the state, each representing roughly equal populations following decennial redistricting.

Membership and leadership

Representatives serve two-year terms with no term limits. To qualify, a candidate must be at least 21 years old, a U.S. citizen, a resident of Tennessee for three years, and a resident of the county represented for one year preceding the election. The House is led by the Speaker of the House, elected by the membership, who appoints committee chairs and controls the legislative calendar; party caucuses elect majority and minority leaders.

Functions

Along with the Tennessee Senate, the House passes legislation and the state budget. Revenue bills conventionally originate in the House, and the House has the sole power to bring impeachment charges against state officials, who are then tried by the Senate. A majority of the 99 members constitutes a quorum.

Recent history

Republicans won control of the House in 2010 and subsequently held a supermajority. In April 2023 the chamber drew national attention when its Republican majority expelled two Democratic representatives, Justin Jones and Justin J. Pearson, for participating in a gun-control protest on the House floor following the Covenant School shooting in Nashville; both were promptly returned to the chamber by local appointing bodies and then re-elected in special elections.
