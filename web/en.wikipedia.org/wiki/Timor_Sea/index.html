Timor Sea

The Timor Sea (Indonesian: Laut Timor; Portuguese: Mar de Timor) is a relatively shallow sea in the eastern Indian Ocean, bounded by the island of Timor to the north, the Arafura Sea to the east, and northern Australia (the Kimberley region and Darwin) to the south.

Geography

The sea covers the Sahul Shelf; its deeper feature is the Timor Trough to the north. Islands include Ashmore and Cartier Islands and other Australian offshore territories. Rivers such as the Daly and Victoria flow into its gulfs, including the Joseph Bonaparte Gulf.

Resources and boundary

The seabed holds significant oil and gas reserves, including the Bayu-Undan and Greater Sunrise fields. The maritime boundary between Australia and Timor-Leste was long disputed in the so-called Timor Gap; a treaty signed in 2018, following compulsory conciliation under UNCLOS, established a permanent boundary and arrangements for sharing Greater Sunrise revenues.
