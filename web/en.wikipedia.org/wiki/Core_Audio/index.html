Core Audio is a family of application programming interfaces from Apple for handling audio in macOS and iOS operating systems. Introduced in its modern form with Mac OS X 10.3 Panther in 2003, it provides low-latency, multichannel audio processing, recording and playback, MIDI handling, and a plug-in architecture for software instruments and effects.

Architecture

Core Audio is layered. At the base, the Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL) presents audio devices uniformly and communicates with the audio server (coreaudiod on macOS). Above it sit services such as Audio Units — Apple's plug-in format for effects and instruments, used by applications like Logic Pro and GarageBand — Audio File Services and Audio File Stream Services for reading and writing formats including AAC, MP3, AIFF and WAV, and the AVAudioEngine and AVFoundation frameworks that provide higher-level access for most applications.

Features

Core Audio performs sample-rate conversion, mixing and format conversion transparently, supports multichannel and spatial audio, and enables inter-application audio routing through aggregate and multi-output devices configurable in the Audio MIDI Setup utility. Its low-latency design made the Mac and later the iPhone and iPad dominant platforms for professional music production and live performance software.
