Sigismund II Augustus (Polish: Zygmunt II August; 1520–1572) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1548 until his death in 1572, and the last male ruler of the Jagiellonian dynasty. His reign saw the Union of Lublin (1569), which transformed the personal union of Poland and Lithuania into the single Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, one of the largest and most populous states in 16th-century Europe.

Reign

The only son of Sigismund I the Old and Bona Sforza, he was crowned vivente rege (during his father's lifetime) in 1529. His secret marriage in 1547 to Barbara Radziwiłł, a Lithuanian noblewoman, caused a political storm with the nobility and his mother; Barbara was crowned in 1550 but died months later, possibly poisoned. Sigismund devoted himself thereafter to government: he navigated the Livonian War against Ivan the Terrible's Russia, annexing Livonia in 1561, and presided over a flourishing of Renaissance culture and religious toleration unusual in Reformation-era Europe.

His great constitutional achievement was the Union of Lublin of 1 July 1569, in which Lithuania and Poland were joined into a single Commonwealth with a common monarch and parliament (Sejm), though retaining separate administrations, armies and laws. He died childless at Knyszyn in 1572; with him the Jagiellonian dynasty ended, and Poland became an elective monarchy.
