The National Research Council Canada (NRC; French: Conseil national de recherches du Canada, CNRC) is the primary national agency of the Government of Canada dedicated to science and technology research and development. Founded in 1916, it is Canada's largest federal research and development organisation.

Activities

The NRC operates research centres across Canada covering fields such as aerospace, astronomy and astrophysics, biotechnology, construction, energy and mining, metrology, ocean science, and information and communications technologies. It maintains the Dominion Astrophysical Observatory in British Columbia and is a partner in international telescopes. Its Industrial Research Assistance Program (NRC IRAP), begun in 1947, provides advice and funding to small and medium-sized enterprises.

History

The Council was created during the First World War to advise the government on scientific matters and grew rapidly during the Second World War, contributing to radar and atomic energy research; its Montreal Laboratory participated in the early work that fed into the Manhattan Project, and Canada's first nuclear reactor, ZEEP, went critical at Chalk River in 1945 under NRC auspices. The NRC also publishes the Canadian national building code and maintains Canada's official time signal.
