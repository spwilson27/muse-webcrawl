Catalan Republic

The Catalan Republic (Catalan: República Catalana) is a term used for several historical proclamations of a Catalan state separate from Spain, and for the republic proclaimed in principle by the Catalan independence movement in 2017. The idea forms a recurring thread in Catalan political history, appearing in 1641, 1873, 1931, 1934, and 2017.

Historical proclamations

During the Reapers' War (the Catalan Revolt), the statesman Pau Claris proclaimed a Catalan Republic in January 1641 under the protection of France; within days the arrangement was recast as a principality with Louis XIII of France as Count of Barcelona. In 1873, during the First Spanish Republic, federalists briefly proclaimed a Catalan state within a Spanish federal republic. On 14 April 1931 Francesc Macià proclaimed the "Catalan Republic within the Iberian Federation" from the balcony of the palace in Barcelona's Plaça de Sant Jaume, hours before the Second Spanish Republic was proclaimed in Madrid; after negotiations, the proclamation was traded for the restored Generalitat de Catalunya, Catalonia's autonomous government. In October 1934 Lluís Companys proclaimed a "Catalan State within the Spanish Federal Republic" during a revolt against the conservative government in Madrid; it was suppressed within hours.

2017 proclamation

Following the independence referendum of 1 October 2017, which the Spanish Constitutional Court had declared illegal, the Parliament of Catalonia voted on 27 October 2017 to approve a declaration of independence proclaiming a Catalan Republic. No state recognised it, and the Spanish government responded by imposing direct rule under Article 155 of the Spanish Constitution, dissolving the parliament and calling regional elections. President Carles Puigdemont and several ministers left Spain, while others were prosecuted for rebellion and sedition.
