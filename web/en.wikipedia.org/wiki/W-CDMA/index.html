W-CDMA (UMTS)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


W-CDMA is the air interface of the UMTS 3G mobile standard.

W-CDMA (Wideband Code-Division Multiple Access) is an air interface standard found in 3G mobile telecommunications networks. It is the basis of UMTS (Universal Mobile Telecommunications System), and was developed by the 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP). W-CDMA supports conventional cellular voice, text and MMS services, as well as data transmission at rates initially up to 384 kbit/s and, with later releases, several Mbit/s.

Technical features

W-CDMA uses direct-sequence code division multiple access with a chip rate of 3.84 Mcps in a channel bandwidth of about 5 MHz — much wider than the 200 kHz channels of GSM or the 1.25 MHz channels of cdma2000, hence "wideband". It employs variable spreading factors and multi-code transmission to provide variable bit rates, allowing multiple services with different quality requirements to be multiplexed on a single connection. The radio access network uses Node B base stations controlled by Radio Network Controllers (RNCs).

Evolution

Subsequent 3GPP releases extended W-CDMA with HSPA (High Speed Packet Access) — HSDPA for the downlink and HSUPA for the uplink — raising peak data rates first to 14.4 Mbit/s and, with later enhancements such as dual-carrier operation and MIMO, substantially higher. The first commercial W-CDMA network was NTT DoCoMo's FOMA service in Japan, launched in 2001, followed by widespread deployment across Europe and the rest of the world.

Relationship to CDMA2000

W-CDMA is distinct from the competing cdma2000 family, despite both using code-division multiple access; the two are not compatible. The term "CDMA" in casual usage sometimes refers to W-CDMA-based UMTS networks and sometimes to cdma2000 networks, depending on the market.
