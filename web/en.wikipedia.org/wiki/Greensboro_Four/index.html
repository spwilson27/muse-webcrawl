Greensboro Four

The Greensboro Four were four African American college students who, on 1 February 1960, staged a sit-in at a segregated Woolworth's lunch counter in Greensboro, North Carolina. Their action helped to spark the Greensboro sit-ins and a nationwide wave of sit-ins that became a pivotal part of the civil rights movement.

The sit-in

The four — Ezell Blair Jr. (now Jibreel Khazan), Joseph McNeil, Franklin McCain and David Richmond — were freshmen at North Carolina Agricultural and Technical State University. Influenced by the nonviolent philosophy of Gandhi and by earlier sit-ins, they bought small items in the store, then sat at the "whites only" lunch counter and politely asked for coffee. They were refused service but remained seated until the store closed. They returned in growing numbers in the following days, joined by students from other local colleges, including Bennett College.

Impact

Within weeks, sit-ins had spread to dozens of cities across the South. The protests drew national media attention and economic pressure through boycotts of segregated stores. In July 1960, the Greensboro Woolworth quietly began serving Black customers at its lunch counter; chain-wide desegregation followed. The sit-in movement also led directly to the founding of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) in April 1960.

Legacy

A section of the original lunch counter and stools is preserved at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History, and the former Woolworth building in Greensboro now houses the International Civil Rights Center and Museum, opened on the fiftieth anniversary of the sit-in, 1 February 2010.
