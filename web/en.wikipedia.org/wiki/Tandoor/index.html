Tandoor

Tandoor
Type: Clay oven
Region: South Asia, Central Asia, Middle East
Temperature: Up to ~480 °C (900 °F)

A tandoor is a cylindrical clay or metal oven used in cooking and baking in parts of South Asia, Central and Western Asia, and the Caucasus. Food is cooked over intense heat from a charcoal or wood fire at the oven's base.

History

Clay ovens of this type are ancient; examples and related tannur ovens date back thousands of years in the Indus Valley and Mesopotamia. The tandoor spread across the Persian- and Turkic-influenced world and became central to Punjabi cooking.

Use

The oven reaches temperatures around 480 °C. Flatbreads such as naan and roti are slapped onto the hot inner walls; meats are threaded on long skewers lowered into the oven. The result is a characteristic charred, smoky flavour. Best-known dishes include tandoori chicken and chicken tikka.

Names and variants

Related ovens include the tannur, tonir (Armenia), tandyr (Central Asia), and tandur (Turkey). Modern restaurant tandoors may be gas- or electric-fired and steel-clad.
