Battle of Beersheba (1917)

Battle of Beersheba
Date31 October 1917
LocationBeersheba, Ottoman Palestine (now Israel)
Part ofSinai and Palestine Campaign, First World War
CombatantsBritish Empire (Egyptian Expeditionary Force) vs Ottoman Empire, German Empire
ResultBritish Empire victory

The Battle of Beersheba was fought on 31 October 1917, when the Egyptian Expeditionary Force under General Edmund Allenby attacked the Ottoman garrison at Beersheba (now Be'er Sheva) at the eastern end of the Gaza–Beersheba defensive line. The battle opened the Southern Palestine offensive that led to the capture of Jerusalem in December 1917.

The charge

After infantry assaults secured the western defences, the town and its vital wells remained at risk as dusk approached. General Harry Chauvel ordered the 4th and 12th Australian Light Horse regiments to charge across open ground. Armed only with rifles and bayonets — the light horsemen were mounted infantry, not cavalry — they galloped through artillery and machine-gun fire, overran the trenches, and captured the wells largely intact. The charge is one of the most famous mounted actions of the war and is often called the last successful great cavalry charge in history.

Aftermath

The fall of Beersheba outflanked the Gaza line, which the Ottomans abandoned within days. The battle is commemorated in Australia and Israel, and was dramatized in the 1987 film The Lighthorsemen.
