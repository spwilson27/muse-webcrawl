Foundry

A foundry is a factory that produces metal castings. Metals are cast into shapes by melting them into a liquid, pouring the metal into a mold, and removing the mold material after the metal has solidified as it cools. The most common metals processed are aluminium and cast iron. However, other metals, such as steel, magnesium, copper, tin, and zinc, can also be used to produce castings in a foundry.

Process

In metalworking, casting involves pouring liquid metal into a mold containing a hollow cavity of the desired shape, then allowing it to cool and solidify. Several steps are involved: making the pattern (a replica of the part), preparing the mold (often from sand bonded with clay or chemical binders), melting the metal in a furnace (cupola, electric arc, induction, or crucible), pouring, cooling, shakeout of the casting from the mold, and cleaning/fettling to remove excess material. Modern foundries may use lost-wax (investment) casting, die casting, or continuous casting depending on the product.

Types

Foundries are often classified by the metals they pour: ferrous foundries (iron and steel) and non-ferrous foundries (aluminium, copper alloys, zinc and others). A "captive" foundry serves a single parent manufacturer, while a "jobbing" foundry produces castings for many customers.
