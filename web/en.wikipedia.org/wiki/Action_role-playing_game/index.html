Action role-playing game

An action role-playing game (often abbreviated action RPG or ARPG) is a subgenre of role-playing video games that emphasizes real-time combat, in which the player has direct control over the character's actions, rather than turn-based or menu-based battles. Action RPGs typically combine character progression — experience points, levels, skills, and equipment — with fast-paced action gameplay.

Characteristics

Defining features of the genre include real-time combat demanding player reflexes and positioning, character statistics that grow through combat and questing, loot systems that reward players with progressively stronger equipment, and explorable worlds with non-player characters and quests. Substyles range from isometric "hack and slash" dungeon crawlers to third-person open-world games and "Soulslike" titles emphasizing deliberate, punishing combat.

History

Early examples appeared in the 1980s, including Dragon Slayer (1984) and The Legend of Zelda (1986), which blended action-adventure with RPG elements, and Nihon Falcom's Ys series. Diablo (1996) by Blizzard North crystallized the isometric point-and-click form with procedurally generated dungeons and loot. In the 2000s and 2010s, titles such as Kingdom Hearts, The Elder Scrolls series, Mass Effect, Dark Souls, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, and Diablo's successors expanded the genre's popularity worldwide.
