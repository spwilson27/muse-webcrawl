Poodle

Origin: Germany / France

Sizes: Standard, Miniature, Toy

Coat: Dense, curly, low-shedding

The Poodle is a breed of water dog that comes in three size varieties — Standard, Miniature and Toy. The breed's name comes from the German Pudel, from puddeln, "to splash in water", reflecting its origin as a water retriever of shot game. France claims the breed as its own — it is the French national dog, known there as the Caniche.

History

Poodles were bred in Germany as duck-hunting retrievers, and the traditional elaborate clip had a practical origin: shaved areas reduced drag in the water while tufts of hair were left over the joints and chest to protect them from cold. The smaller varieties were developed later and became fashionable companion and circus dogs; the Toy was bred down in size in the 18th–19th centuries.

Characteristics

Poodles are consistently ranked among the most intelligent of dog breeds and are highly trainable. Their tightly curled coat grows continuously and sheds very little, which makes them popular with some allergy sufferers, though the coat requires regular grooming and clipping. Recognised colours include black, white, brown, grey, apricot and red, always solid. Standards stand over 38 cm (15 in) at the shoulder; Miniatures are 28–38 cm and Toys under 28 cm.

Health

Standard Poodles are prone to certain hereditary conditions, including hip dysplasia, the skin disorder sebaceous adenitis, the blood-clotting disease von Willebrand's, and gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat). Lifespan is typically 10–18 years depending on size, with smaller varieties living longer.

Crossbreeds

Poodles are widely crossed with other breeds to produce so-called designer dogs, most famously the Labradoodle (with the Labrador Retriever) and the Goldendoodle and Cockapoo.
