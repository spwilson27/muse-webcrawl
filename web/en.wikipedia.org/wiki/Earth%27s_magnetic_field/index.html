Earth's magnetic field

Earth's magnetic field, also known as the geomagnetic field, is the magnetic field that extends from Earth's interior out into space, where it interacts with the solar wind. It approximates the field of a magnetic dipole tilted about 11 degrees from Earth's rotation axis, as if a giant bar magnet were placed inside the planet.

Origin

The field is generated by the geodynamo: convection of molten iron alloys in Earth's liquid outer core, organised by rotation through the Coriolis effect, sustains electric currents that produce the magnetic field.

Characteristics

Surface field strength ranges from about 25 to 65 microteslas. The field protects the surface from charged particles of the solar wind, guides compasses, and is used by many animals for navigation. It varies over time — the magnetic poles wander, and the field has reversed polarity many times in geological history, most recently about 780,000 years ago (the Brunhes–Matuyama reversal).

Magnetosphere

The field carves out the magnetosphere, a cavity in the solar wind containing the Van Allen radiation belts and producing auroras near the poles.
