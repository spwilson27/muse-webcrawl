Avalanche rescue

Equipment: Transceiver, probe, shovel
Survival: Chance falls steeply after ~15 minutes of burial

Avalanche rescue involves locating and extricating people buried by an avalanche. Because survival chances drop rapidly with burial time — asphyxiation is the leading cause of death — companion rescue by the victim's own party, using avalanche transceivers, probes and shovels, is the most effective means of saving lives.

Companion rescue

Survivors switch transceivers to search mode, follow the signal to the burial area, pinpoint the victim with a probe, then dig strategically downhill of the probe strike. Organised rescue by mountain rescue teams and search dogs supplements companion rescue.

Equipment and prevention

Standard safety equipment includes a transceiver, collapsible probe and metal shovel; airbag packs reduce burial depth. Rescue is supported by avalanche forecasting, terrain selection and training.
