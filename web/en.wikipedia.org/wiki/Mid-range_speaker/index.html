Mid-range speaker

A typical cone-type mid-range driver

A mid-range speaker is a loudspeaker driver that reproduces the mid frequencies of the audio spectrum, generally from about 300–500 Hz to 5,000 Hz. In a multi-way loudspeaker system, the incoming signal is divided by a crossover network between a woofer (low frequencies), the mid-range driver, and a tweeter (high frequencies). The midrange band is critical because it contains most of the energy of the human voice and many musical instruments, and the human ear is most sensitive to distortion in this range.

Design

Mid-range drivers are commonly cone drivers of 3 to 6.5 inches diameter, made of paper, polypropylene, Kevlar, metal or composite materials, or dome drivers of impregnated fabric or metal for the upper midrange. Dedicated midrange units are usually mounted in their own sealed sub-enclosure within the cabinet so that pressure from the woofer does not modulate the midrange cone. Key design goals include flat frequency response, low distortion, and controlled dispersion at the crossover points.

Applications

Three-way loudspeakers, studio monitors and high-fidelity systems commonly employ a dedicated midrange driver, whereas two-way designs assign the midrange duty to the woofer (a mid-woofer) crossed directly to a tweeter. Mid-range speakers are also used in public address systems and car audio installations.
