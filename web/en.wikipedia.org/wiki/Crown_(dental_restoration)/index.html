Crown (dental restoration)

Dental crown
MaterialsPorcelain, ceramic, zirconia, metal alloys, gold
PurposeRestore and protect a damaged tooth

In dentistry, a crown is a type of dental restoration that completely caps or encircles a tooth or dental implant. Crowns are used when a large cavity threatens the health of a tooth, after root canal treatment, to restore a broken tooth, or to improve appearance. They are cemented or bonded onto a prepared tooth, restoring its shape, size, strength, and appearance.

Materials

Crowns can be made of porcelain or other ceramics (prized for a natural appearance), zirconia, metal alloys, gold, or porcelain fused to metal. Traditionally crowns were fabricated in a dental laboratory from an impression, requiring at least two visits; CAD/CAM systems such as CEREC allow crowns to be designed, milled, and fitted in a single appointment.

Procedure

The tooth is reshaped under local anaesthetic, an impression or digital scan is taken, and a temporary crown is fitted while the permanent one is made. Lifespan varies with material and oral hygiene, commonly 5 to 15 years or longer. Partial crowns that cover only part of the tooth include onlays and three-quarter crowns.
