Abell catalogue

The Abell catalogue is a catalogue of rich clusters of galaxies compiled by the American astronomer George O. Abell. The original catalogue, published in 1958 from the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey, listed 2,712 clusters in the northern sky. A supplementary catalogue of southern clusters, completed with Harold Corwin and Ronald Olowin, was published in 1989, bringing the all-sky total to 4,073 clusters.

Contents

Abell selected clusters by richness and compactness, requiring at least 50 member galaxies within a defined radius (the "Abell radius", 1.5 h−1 Mpc) and within two magnitudes of the third-brightest member. Each cluster is assigned a richness class (0–5) and a distance class (1–7) based on the magnitude of its tenth-brightest galaxy.

Usage

Abell clusters are designated "ACO" or "Abell" followed by a number, such as Abell 1656 (the Coma Cluster) and Abell 1689. The catalogue remains a standard reference for studies of large-scale structure, cluster cosmology and follow-up observations in X-ray and radio astronomy, though modern surveys such as the Sloan Digital Sky Survey have produced far larger samples.
