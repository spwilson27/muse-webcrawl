x265 is a free and open-source software library and application for encoding video streams in the High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC/H.265) format. It is developed by MulticoreWare together with the open-source community under the umbrella of the VideLAN project, and is modelled on the earlier x264 encoder for H.264/AVC. x265 is widely used in tools such as HandBrake and FFmpeg and by streaming services to reduce the bitrate of high-resolution video.

Features

The encoder supports the Main, Main 10 and Main 12 profiles of HEVC with 8-, 10- and 12-bit colour depth, coding tree units up to 64×64 pixels, adaptive quantisation, multiple rate-control modes (constant rate factor, average bitrate, two-pass), frame-parallel and WPP (wavefront parallel processing) threading, and psychovisual optimisations inherited from x264 such as adaptive B-frames and mb-tree. Presets from "ultrafast" to "placebo" trade encoding speed against compression efficiency, in the tradition of x264's tuning system.

Performance and adoption

HEVC encoding with x265 typically achieves the same subjective quality as H.264 at roughly half the bitrate, at the cost of substantially greater computation. x265 has won multiple MSU video codec comparisons in the HEVC category and has been adopted for 4K and HDR streaming workflows. Because it is written in C++ with hand-optimised assembly for x86 SIMD instruction sets (and later ARM), it serves as a reference-quality software encoder against which hardware encoders are often compared.

Licensing

x265 is available under the GNU General Public License version 2, with commercial licences offered by MulticoreWare for use in proprietary products. Users must separately consider patent licences covering the HEVC standard itself, administered by patent pools such as Access Advance (formerly HEVC Advance) and MPEG LA.
