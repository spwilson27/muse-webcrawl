Dinosaur Ridge is a segment of the Dakota Hogback near Morrison, Colorado, in the foothills west of Denver. It is one of the world's most famous dinosaur fossil localities. The steeply tilted sedimentary rock layers along the ridge expose rocks of the Jurassic Morrison Formation and the Cretaceous Dakota Group, and the site preserves dinosaur bones, trackways, and other trace fossils.

Discoveries

In 1877, schoolteacher Arthur Lakes began excavating large dinosaur bones from the Morrison Formation near the ridge, in the opening episode of the "Bone Wars" between paleontologists Othniel Charles Marsh and Edward Drinker Cope. Material from the area included some of the first specimens of Stegosaurus, Apatosaurus, Diplodocus, and Allosaurus.

In the 1930s, road construction for Alameda Parkway exposed a broad bedding plane of Dakota Group sandstone covered with dinosaur footprints. The tracksite preserves hundreds of tracks, mostly of ornithopod dinosaurs resembling Iguanodon, together with theropod tracks, running roughly parallel — a pattern interpreted as a "dinosaur freeway" along the shore of the Cretaceous Western Interior Seaway.

Visitor site

The site is managed for public education by the Friends of Dinosaur Ridge, which operates the Dinosaur Ridge Discovery Center and the Exhibit Hall (Trek Through Time) in Morrison, and offers guided tours of the tracksite, a bone bed where visitors can touch real dinosaur bones in place, ripple marks, and plant fossils.
