Nepal, officially the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal, is a landlocked country in South Asia. It is mainly situated in the Himalayas, but also includes parts of the Indo-Gangetic Plain. It borders China to the north and India to the south, east and west, while Bangladesh is located within only a narrow corridor's distance. Nepal has a diverse geography, including fertile plains, subalpine forested hills, and eight of the world's ten tallest mountains, including Mount Everest, the highest point on Earth.

The capital and largest city is Kathmandu. Nepal is multi-ethnic, multilingual, multi-religious and multicultural, with Nepali as the official language. Its distinctive double-pennon flag is the only national flag in the world that is not rectangular.

History

The Kathmandu Valley's recorded history stretches back over two millennia. Modern Nepal was unified in the second half of the 18th century by Prithvi Narayan Shah, the Gorkha king who conquered the valley in 1768 and founded the Shah dynasty. Nepal was never colonised, though it ceded territory to British India after the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–16) and maintained close ties with Britain, supplying Gurkha soldiers to the British and Indian armies.

The country was ruled for over a century (1846–1951) by hereditary Rana prime ministers, with the king sidelined. After decades of political struggle, a decade-long civil war between the state and Maoist insurgents (1996–2006), and a popular movement in 2006, Nepal abolished its 240-year-old monarchy in 2008 and became a federal democratic republic, adopting a new constitution in 2015.

Geography

Nepal is roughly trapezoidal, about 800 km long and 200 km wide. Elevations range from under 60 metres in the southern Terai plains to 8,848.86 metres at Everest. Five major rivers cut through the range. The country is seismically active: a magnitude ~7.8 earthquake struck near Gorkha in April 2015, killing around 9,000 people and damaging historic sites in the Kathmandu Valley.

Politics

Nepal is a federal parliamentary republic divided into seven provinces. The president is head of state and the prime minister head of government. Its post-2008 politics have been characterised by frequent changes of government and coalition bargaining among parties including the Nepali Congress, the CPN-UML and the Maoist Centre.

Economy

Agriculture employs a large share of the population; remittances from Nepali workers abroad contribute a very large share of GDP. Tourism — trekking and mountaineering in regions such as Everest (Khumbu), Annapurna and Langtang, and cultural tourism in Kathmandu, Pokhara and Lumbini, the birthplace of the Buddha — is a major earner. Hydropower from Himalayan rivers is a long-standing development ambition.

Demographics and culture

Nepal recognises well over a hundred ethnic groups and languages, including Newari, Maithili, Bhojpuri, Tharu, Tamang and Magar. A large majority of the population is Hindu, with significant Buddhist, Muslim, Kirat and Christian minorities; Hindu and Buddhist traditions interweave visibly in the Kathmandu Valley's temples and festivals such as Dashain and Tihar. The national sport is volleyball; cricket is also hugely popular. Cuisine centres on dal bhat (lentils and rice) and momo dumplings.
