The 1972 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XX Olympiad, were held in Munich, West Germany, from 26 August to 11 September 1972. They are remembered both for extraordinary athletic achievements and for the Munich massacre, in which eleven members of the Israeli Olympic team were taken hostage and killed by the Palestinian militant group Black September.

Sporting highlights

American swimmer Mark Spitz won seven gold medals, each in world-record time — a feat unmatched until Michael Phelps in 2008. Soviet gymnast Olga Korbut captured worldwide fame, while the United States lost the men's basketball final to the Soviet Union 51–50 in a famously disputed finish, and the US team refused their silver medals. Finnish runner Lasse Virén won the 5,000 m and 10,000 m double, recovering from a fall in the longer race. Judo and handball returned to the programme, and archery returned after 52 years.

Munich massacre

In the early hours of 5 September, eight Black September gunmen scaled the Olympic Village fence and seized Israeli athletes in their apartments, killing two immediately. A botched rescue attempt at Fürstenfeldbruck airfield ended with all nine remaining hostages, five gunmen, and a West German police officer dead. Competition was suspended for a day; IOC president Avery Brundage declared "the Games must go on". The attack permanently changed Olympic security.

Medal table

The Soviet Union topped the table with 50 gold and 99 medals overall, ahead of the United States (33 gold, 94 total) and East Germany (20 gold, 66 total).
