The Hail Mary (Latin: Ave Maria) is a traditional Catholic prayer addressed to Mary, the mother of Jesus. It asks for Mary's intercession with God. The prayer is also used in other denominations of Christianity, especially in the Eastern Orthodox Church and Oriental Orthodoxy (in adapted forms), by some High Church Anglicans and Lutherans, and it forms the basis of the Rosary and the Angelus devotions.

Text

Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou amongst women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

The first half combines the greeting of the Archangel Gabriel at the Annunciation ("Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with thee", Luke 1:28) with Elizabeth's greeting at the Visitation ("Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb", Luke 1:42). The second half, a petition for intercession, was added by the Church over the later Middle Ages and received its standard form in the Catechism of the Council of Trent (1566).

History

The biblical greetings were used liturgically in the East from early centuries and appear in the Liturgy of St James. In the West the Ave Maria spread as a devotional formula from the 11th and 12th centuries, repeated in cycles that became associated with the rosary of 150 prayers paralleling the 150 psalms — the "poor person's Psalter". The name "Jesus" was added to the scriptural half in the 13th–14th centuries, and the concluding petition ("Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death") became widespread in the 15th and 16th centuries.

Use

The Hail Mary is the central prayer of the Rosary, repeated in decades of ten; it is prayed three times in the Angelus. Eastern Orthodox Christians use a similar rule of prayer addressed to the Theotokos, derived from the same Lucan verses, commonly rendered "O Theotokos and Virgin, rejoice..." In music, the Latin text has been set countless times — the settings of Schubert ("Ellens Gesang III", D. 839) and Gounod (the "Ave Maria" superimposed on Bach's C-major prelude) are among the most famous, alongside settings by Josquin, Victoria, Bruckner and many others.

Other uses of the name

In American football, a "Hail Mary pass" is a long forward pass thrown in desperation with only a small chance of success, named after the prayer's use by the Dallas Cowboys' Roger Staubach in a 1975 playoff game. The phrase has passed into general English for any last-ditch improbable attempt; "Hail Mary" has also been used as a military codename, notably for covert operations, in various contexts.
