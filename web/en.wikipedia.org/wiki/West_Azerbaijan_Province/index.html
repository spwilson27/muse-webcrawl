West Azerbaijan Province (Persian: استان آذربایجان غربی) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, in the northwest of the country. Its capital is the city of Urmia. The province lies west of Lake Urmia and borders Turkey, Iraq and Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan exclave, as well as the Iranian provinces of East Azerbaijan, Zanjan and Kurdistan.

Geography

The province is mountainous in its west, where the Zagros ranges form the Turkish and Iraqi frontiers, and opens eastward to the Urmia basin, a fertile plain around Lake Urmia, historically one of the world's largest salt lakes, which has shrunk dramatically since the 1990s owing to drought and water diversion. Major cities include Urmia, Khoy, Mahabad, Miandoab, Bukan and Salmas.

People and history

The population is mixed: Azerbaijanis predominate in the north and around Urmia city, while Kurds form the majority in much of the south (Mahabad, Bukan, Sardasht); there are also long-established Armenian and Assyrian Christian communities around Urmia. The region corresponds roughly to the southern part of historical Azerbaijan/Atropatene in the north and Mukri Kurdistan in the south, and in 1945–46 it saw both the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government and the Kurdish Republic of Mahabad, before Iranian control was restored.

Economy

Agriculture (apples, grapes, tobacco, sugar beet) dominates, alongside food processing, petrochemicals and cross-border trade with Turkey and Iraqi Kurdistan through crossings such as Bazargan and Piranshahr.
