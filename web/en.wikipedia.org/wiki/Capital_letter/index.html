Capital letter

Also calledUppercase, majuscule
ContrastLowercase (minuscule)

Capital letters, also called uppercase or majuscule letters, are the larger form of letters in scripts that have a two-case system, such as the Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic alphabets. The smaller form is the lowercase (minuscule) letter. In English, capital letters are used at the beginning of sentences, for proper nouns and some adjectives, for the pronoun "I", and in various other conventional contexts.

History

The terms "uppercase" and "lowercase" come from the printing trade, where individual pieces of metal type were stored in shallow drawers called cases: the majuscule types were kept in the upper case and the minuscule types in the lower case. Roman inscriptions used what are now called capital letters — the monumental capitals seen on monuments such as Trajan's Column. Lowercase forms developed later from cursive handwriting of the early medieval period, notably Carolingian minuscule.

Uses in English

Capitalisation in English marks the start of sentences, proper names of people, places, organisations, days and months, languages and nationalities, titles of works, and certain abbreviations and acronyms. Styles differ: "title case" capitalises the principal words of headings, while many modern style guides prefer "sentence case". German differs from English in capitalising all nouns.

Other scripts

Not all writing systems have case distinctions: Chinese, Arabic, Hebrew, and Georgian (in modern usage) are unicameral. Greek and Cyrillic have two cases closely paralleling Latin. Some letters change form substantially between cases, and some languages have special rules — for example, German ß traditionally had no capital form, while Turkish distinguishes dotted and dotless I in both cases.
