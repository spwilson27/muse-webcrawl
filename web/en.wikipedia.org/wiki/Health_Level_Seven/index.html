Health Level Seven

Health Level Seven (HL7) is a set of international standards for the transfer of clinical and administrative data between software applications used by various healthcare providers. The standards are produced by the Health Level Seven International standards organisation and are adopted by national standards bodies around the world. The name refers to the seventh level of the ISO OSI model — the application layer.

Standards

HL7 version 2

Version 2 (v2) is a messaging standard that allows the exchange of patient administration, orders, results, scheduling and billing information between systems such as electronic health records, laboratory systems and radiology systems. Messages are composed of segments (such as the patient identification segment, PID) containing fields separated by delimiter characters, historically transmitted over the Minimal Lower Layer Protocol (MLLP). First published in the late 1980s, v2 remains the most widely deployed healthcare interoperability standard in the world, although its many optional elements mean that implementations vary between vendors.

HL7 version 3 and CDA

Version 3, developed from the 1990s, introduced a model-driven methodology based on a Reference Information Model (RIM). Its best-known product is the Clinical Document Architecture (CDA), an XML-based standard for clinical documents such as discharge summaries.

FHIR

FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), first released in the 2010s, is a modern standard built on web technologies — RESTful APIs, JSON and XML — that breaks healthcare data into modular "resources" such as Patient, Observation and Medication. FHIR has been widely adopted and is mandated for patient data access in several jurisdictions.
