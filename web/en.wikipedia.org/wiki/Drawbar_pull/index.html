Drawbar pull

Drawbar pull is the pulling force exerted by a vehicle — such as a locomotive, tractor or truck — at its drawbar or coupling, available for hauling a load. In railway engineering it is a key measure of a locomotive's useful hauling capability, distinct from tractive effort, which is measured at the wheel rim or coupling before accounting for the locomotive's own rolling resistance.

Measurement

Drawbar pull is typically measured with a dynamometer car coupled behind the locomotive, which records the force in the coupler as the train moves. The available drawbar pull decreases as speed rises, because the power output of the engine is finite: force multiplied by speed equals power, less transmission and resistance losses.

Factors affecting drawbar pull

Important factors include engine power, gearing, adhesion between wheels and rail (or tyres and ground), the weight on the driving wheels, gradient, curvature and the rolling resistance of the vehicle itself. For agricultural tractors, drawbar pull is a standard test quantity measured in official tractor tests, reported alongside drawbar horsepower.
