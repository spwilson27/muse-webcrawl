Darley Arabian

The Darley Arabian (c. 1700 – 1730) was one of three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred horse breed, alongside the Byerley Turk and the Godolphin Arabian. An Arabian stallion bought in Aleppo, Syria, in 1704 and shipped to England, he stood at stud at Aldby Park in Yorkshire and became the most important of the three founders in terms of male-line descent: the overwhelming majority of modern Thoroughbreds — commonly estimated at 95% — trace their direct sire line to him, principally through his great-great-grandson Eclipse.

History

Thomas Darley, a merchant and British agent in Aleppo, purchased the colt — a bay Arabian of the Mannicka strain — from a Bedouin sheikh and sent him by ship to his father's estate in Yorkshire in 1704, reportedly in exchange for a shipment that included a gun and other goods. He stood at Aldby Park from about 1706 until his death in 1730, covering mostly his owner's mares rather than racing stock, yet his descendants transformed the breed. His son Flying Childers was the first truly great racehorse of the modern era, and the lineage through Bartlett's Childers and Squirt led to Eclipse (foaled 1764), the undefeated champion whose sire line dominates Thoroughbred pedigrees today.
