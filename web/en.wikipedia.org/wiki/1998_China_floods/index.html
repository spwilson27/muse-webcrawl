The 1998 China floods (1998年中國洪水) were a series of catastrophic floods in the summer of 1998 affecting the basins of the Yangtze River, the Songhua and Nen rivers in the northeast, and other major waterways—the worst flooding in China since 1954. The floods affected some 240 million people, left about 14.5 million homeless, and caused more than 4,000 deaths by most official counts, with total economic losses estimated between US$24 and 30 billion.

Course of the floods

Unusually heavy monsoon rains, amplified by the 1997–98 El Niño, brought the Yangtze a succession of eight flood peaks between June and August. The river reached its third-highest levels of the 20th century around Jiujiang and Wuhan; at Jiujiang a major dike breach in early August was sealed by soldiers and workers using sunken barges and rock under the direction of Premier Zhu Rongji. In the northeast, the Nen and Songhua rivers flooded the Daqing oil fields and threatened Harbin. Unlike in 1954, authorities mostly chose to hold the main dikes rather than open diversion areas, avoiding far larger casualties.

Response

China mobilized hundreds of thousands of soldiers of the People's Liberation Army—by many accounts the military's largest peacetime deployment—alongside millions of civilian laborers for dike defense and rescue. President Jiang Zemin personally directed anti-flood efforts from the Yangtze dikes in August. Some 14 million people were evacuated at the peak of the emergency.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted sweeping policy changes: a nationwide ban on logging in upper Yangtze forests, conversion of reclaimed lakeside farmland back to flood-retention use ("return farmland to lake"), accelerated dike reinforcement, and reinforcement of the case for the Three Gorges Dam, then under construction. Environmental scientists linked the severity of flooding to decades of deforestation and the loss of lakes and wetlands that once absorbed floodwaters.
