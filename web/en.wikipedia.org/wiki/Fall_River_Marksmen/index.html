The Fall River Marksmen were an American soccer club based in Fall River, Massachusetts. They were one of the most successful teams of the 1920s, winning the American Soccer League championship seven times and the National Challenge Cup (now the U.S. Open Cup) four times, including a league-and-cup double in 1924.

History

The club began as Fall River United in 1922 and was taken over by Sam Mark, who renamed it the Marksmen and built Mark's Stadium across the state line in Tiverton, Rhode Island, to allow Sunday games prohibited by Massachusetts blue laws. Under player-manager Harold Brittan and with stars such as Billy Gonsalves in later years, the Marksmen dominated the American Soccer League's first era.

The Soccer War and decline

In 1928 the club was drawn into the "Soccer War" between the American Soccer League and the United States Football Association. In 1930 the Marksmen won a treble of league, cup and Lewis Cup, but the effects of the Great Depression and the war devastated American professional soccer. In 1931 Sam Mark moved the club to New York, where it merged with the New York Soccer Club to become the New York Yankees soccer club, before briefly relocating to New Bedford as the New Bedford Whalers.

Honours

American Soccer League champions: 1923–24, 1924–25, 1925–26, 1928–29, fall 1929, 1930 (and, as part of its record, the 1927 title by some listings). National Challenge Cup winners: 1924, 1927, 1930, 1931. Lewis Cup winners: 1930.
