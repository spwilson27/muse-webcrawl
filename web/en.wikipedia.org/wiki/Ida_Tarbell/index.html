Ida Minerva Tarbell (November 5, 1857 - January 6, 1944) was an American writer and investigative journalist, a leading 'muckraker'. Her History of the Standard Oil Company, serialised in McClure's Magazine from 1902, exposed the practices of John D. Rockefeller's trust and contributed to the Supreme Court's 1911 breakup of Standard Oil.

Early life

Born in Erie County, Pennsylvania, in the heart of the oil region, where her father's business suffered from Standard Oil's practices, Tarbell studied biology at Allegheny College, one of the few women graduates of her era.

McClure's and Standard Oil

At McClure's Magazine she produced the 19-part Standard Oil history (1902-1904), a landmark of documented exposure that made her reputation; she also wrote best-selling works on Napoleon and Lincoln.

Later career

She co-owned and wrote for The American Magazine, lectured widely, served on government conferences under President Wilson, and wrote an autobiography, All in the Day's Work (1939). Historians credit Tarbell with helping to define investigative reporting's methods; her Lincoln scholarship is also notable.
