Hendrik Verwoerd

Born8 September 1901, Amsterdam, Netherlands
Died6 September 1966 (aged 64), Cape Town, South Africa
OfficePrime Minister of South Africa (1958–1966)

Hendrik Frensch Verwoerd (8 September 1901 – 6 September 1966) was a South African politician and scholar, widely regarded as the chief architect of the apartheid system. A member of the National Party, he served as Minister of Native Affairs from 1950 to 1958 under prime ministers D. F. Malan and J. G. Strijdom, and then as Prime Minister from 1958 until his assassination in 1966.

Early life and academic career

Verwoerd was born in Amsterdam and emigrated with his family to South Africa as a small child. He studied psychology and philosophy at Stellenbosch University, earning a doctorate with distinction, and pursued further study in Germany, Britain and the United States. Appointed professor at Stellenbosch at an unusually young age, he later edited the nationalist newspaper Die Transvaler, using it to advance Afrikaner nationalist politics and to oppose South Africa's participation in the Second World War on Britain's side.

Architect of apartheid

As Minister of Native Affairs he designed and implemented much of the machinery of grand apartheid: the Bantu Education Act, which subordinated black schooling, and the policy of "separate development" that assigned black South Africans to ethnically defined homelands (Bantustans). As Prime Minister he led South Africa to become a republic in 1961, following a whites-only referendum, and withdrew the country from the Commonwealth after international condemnation of the Sharpeville massacre of 1960. His government banned liberation movements, detained opponents under sweeping security laws, and imprisoned Nelson Mandela and other leaders after the Rivonia Trial.

Assassination

Verwoerd survived a 1960 assassination attempt by the farmer David Pratt. On 6 September 1966 he was stabbed to death in the House of Assembly in Cape Town by Dimitri Tsafendas, a parliamentary messenger. He was succeeded as prime minister by B. J. Vorster.

Legacy

After the end of apartheid in 1994, many monuments, place names and institutions honouring Verwoerd were removed or renamed, and he remains one of the most reviled figures of the era — remembered above all as the intellectual designer of a system declared a crime against humanity.
