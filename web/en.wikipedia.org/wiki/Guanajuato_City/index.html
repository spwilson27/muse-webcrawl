Guanajuato is a city in central Mexico and the capital of the state of the same name. Built in a narrow valley, it is famous for its labyrinth of alleys, its colourful hillside houses, its underground roadways in former river tunnels, and its rich colonial architecture.

History

The city grew from one of the richest silver-mining districts of New Spain after major silver deposits were discovered in the 1550s. In 1810, at the outbreak of the Mexican War of Independence, the insurgent army of Miguel Hidalgo captured the city's granary, the Alhóndiga de Granaditas, in one of the first battles of the war. The historic town and adjacent mines were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988.

Culture

Guanajuato hosts the Festival Internacional Cervantino, one of Latin America's most important arts festivals, founded in 1972. The city is the birthplace of artist Diego Rivera and is known for its Mummy Museum and the University of Guanajuato.
