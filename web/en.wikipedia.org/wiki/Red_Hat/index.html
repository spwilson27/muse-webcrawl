Red Hat

Image placeholder: Red Hat's corporate offices.

Red Hat, Inc. is an American multinational software company that provides open source software products to enterprises, best known for Red Hat Enterprise Linux. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Raleigh, North Carolina, Red Hat became a subsidiary of IBM in 2019 in a deal valued at approximately $34 billion, then the largest software acquisition in history.

History

The company grew out of the early Linux movement: Marc Ewing created the Red Hat Linux distribution while Bob Young's ACC Corporation distributed it, and the two businesses merged in 1995. Red Hat pioneered the subscription business model for open source software, selling support, training and certification around freely available code. Its 1999 stock market debut was one of the landmark IPOs of the dot-com era.

Products

Major products include Red Hat Enterprise Linux, the OpenShift container platform built on Kubernetes, Ansible automation software, and a broad portfolio of middleware derived from its 2006 acquisition of JBoss. The community Fedora Project, sponsored by Red Hat, serves as an upstream testbed for technologies that later enter its commercial products.
