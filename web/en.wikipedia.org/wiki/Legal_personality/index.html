Legal personality

Incorporation confers legal personality on a company

Legal personality is a legal concept denoting the capacity of an entity to hold rights and obligations under the law — to own property, enter contracts, sue and be sued in its own name. Entities possessing legal personality are called legal persons. Human beings are "natural persons" whose personality generally begins at birth and ends at death, while "juristic" or "artificial persons" such as corporations, states, and certain associations acquire personality through a legal act such as incorporation or legislation.

Natural and juristic persons

Every legal system distinguishes natural persons from juristic persons. A company's separate legal personality — affirmed in English law by Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] — means the company is distinct from its shareholders, who are shielded by limited liability. Partnerships vary by jurisdiction: in Scots law a firm has separate personality, while in English law a traditional partnership does not. Unincorporated associations generally lack personality, contracting instead through their members.

International legal personality

In public international law, states are the paradigmatic legal persons. The International Court of Justice held in the Reparations for Injuries advisory opinion (1949) that the United Nations also possesses objective international personality, enabling it to bring claims and conclude treaties. The extent to which individuals, insurgent groups, and multinational corporations hold international personality remains debated.

Extensions and controversies

Some jurisdictions have extended legal personality beyond humans and organisations: New Zealand's Te Awa Tupua Act 2017 made the Whanganui River a legal person, and Indian courts have accorded similar status to the Ganges and Yamuna rivers and to Hindu deities in property disputes. Debates continue over personhood for great apes, other animals, ecosystems, and artificial intelligences, contrasted with historical systems — such as Roman law and slavery — that denied full personality to some human beings.
