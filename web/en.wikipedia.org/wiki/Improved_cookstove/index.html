Improved cookstove

An improved cookstove is a biomass-fuelled cooking stove designed to burn wood, charcoal, dung or crop residues more efficiently and with less smoke than a traditional open fire or three-stone hearth. Around two billion people rely on solid fuels for cooking, and household air pollution from traditional stoves is a major cause of respiratory disease, particularly among women and children.

Designs

Designs include the rocket stove, which uses an insulated L-shaped combustion chamber to achieve high temperatures and more complete combustion; mud and clay stoves built in place; portable metal stoves; and stoves with chimneys that vent smoke from the kitchen. Some advanced models use fan-assisted gasification to approach the cleanliness of gas cooking.

Impact and distribution

Governments and development organisations have promoted improved cookstoves since the 1970s and 1980s, notably in China, India and East Africa. Benefits sought include reduced fuel use and deforestation, time savings, lower emissions and better health, though field studies have found that real-world health gains depend heavily on consistent use and on how completely households switch from traditional fires.
