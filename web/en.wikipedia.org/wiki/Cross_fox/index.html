Cross fox

SpeciesVulpes vulpes (red fox)
TypeColour morph

The cross fox is named for the dark stripe down its back crossed by a band across the shoulders.

The cross fox is a colour morph of the red fox (Vulpes vulpes), partially melanistic and intermediate in colour between the typical red fox and the fully black silver fox. It takes its name from the long dark stripe running down its back that intersects a second stripe across the shoulders, forming a cross.

Description and distribution

Cross foxes have a reddish coat overlaid with black or dark brown along the spine, shoulders, throat and underside. They occur naturally in the northern parts of the red fox's range, especially in Canada and Alaska, where they historically accounted for a substantial share of trapped foxes. In the fur trade of the early 20th century, cross fox pelts were valued between ordinary red and silver pelts.

The morph is produced by the same genetic locus responsible for silver and black colouration in farmed foxes, and cross foxes appeared among the animals bred on Prince Edward Island and elsewhere during the silver fox farming boom.
