Malalai of Maiwand

Malalai of Maiwand (Pashto: د ميوند ملاله; c. 1861 – 27 July 1880), also known as Malala or Malalai Noorzai, is a national folk heroine of Afghanistan. She is said to have rallied Afghan troops at the Battle of Maiwand during the Second Anglo-Afghan War, and her name has become a byword for courage among Pashtuns.

Legend

According to the traditional account, Malalai was a young woman from the village of Khig near Maiwand, present at the battle on 27 July 1880 as a water-carrier and attendant tending the wounded, following an army that included her father and her fiancé. When the Afghan standard fell and the line wavered before the British-Indian force, she raised her veil as a banner and cried out a famous landai (couplet): "Young love! If you do not fall in the Battle of Maiwand, by God, someone is saving you as a symbol of shame!" The Afghan fighters rallied, and Ayub Khan's army won a famous victory over Brigadier-General Burrows' brigade. Malalai herself was struck down and killed.

Legacy

Historians debate the details of the story, which rests on oral tradition rather than contemporary records, but its cultural significance is undisputed. Malalai is buried near Maiwand, and schools, hospitals, and institutions across Afghanistan bear her name. The Pakistani activist Malala Yousafzai was named after her. In Pashto poetry and folklore she is the archetype of the heroine who shames cowardice and embodies national honour.
