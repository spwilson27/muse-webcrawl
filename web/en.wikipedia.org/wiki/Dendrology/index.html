Dendrology

Dendrology (from Ancient Greek déndron, "tree"; and -logía, "study of") or xylology is the science and study of woody plants (trees, shrubs, and lianas), specifically, their taxonomic classifications. There is no sharp boundary between plant taxonomy and dendrology; woody plants not only belong to many different plant families, but these families may consist of both woody and non-woody members. Some families include only a few woody species.

Scope

Dendrology, as a discipline of industrial forestry, tends to focus on identification of economically useful woody plants, their taxonomic interrelationships and silvicultural characteristics. As an academic course of study, dendrology includes all woody plants, native and non-native, that occur in a region. A related discipline is the study of sylvics, which focuses on the autecology of genera and species.

Methods

Identification of trees relies on characteristics of leaves, bark, buds, flowers, fruit and growth habit, often summarised in dichotomous keys. Dendrologists maintain collections in arboreta and herbaria for reference. The closely related field of dendrochronology dates woody material by analysing patterns of annual growth rings.
