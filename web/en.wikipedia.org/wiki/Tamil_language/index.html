Tamil (தமிழ், Tamiḻ) is a Dravidian language natively spoken by the Tamil people of South Asia. It is the official language of the Indian state of Tamil Nadu and the union territory of Puducherry, and an official language of Sri Lanka and Singapore, with large diaspora communities in Malaysia, South Africa, Canada, and elsewhere. In 2004 the Government of India declared Tamil the first "classical language" of India.

History

Tamil has the longest continuous literary tradition of any Dravidian language. The earliest records are short inscriptions in a variant of Brahmi script from roughly the 3rd–2nd century BCE, and the oldest extensive text is the Tolkāppiyam, a work on grammar and poetics. The classical Sangam literature (c. 300 BCE–300 CE) comprises over 2,000 poems. Tamil's literary history is conventionally divided into Old Tamil (to c. 700 CE), Middle Tamil (700–1600), and Modern Tamil (from 1600).

Features

Tamil is an agglutinative language with a subject–object–verb word order. It distinguishes a formal literary register (centamiḻ) from colloquial spoken varieties (koṭuntamiḻ), a diglossia that remains strong today. The traditional phoneme inventory lacks aspirated and voiced stops as independent phonemes, though loanwords have expanded usage. The Tamil script, derived from southern Brahmi, is an abugida of 12 vowels, 18 consonants, and 216 combined characters plus special signs.

Literature and influence

Medieval Tamil literature includes the devotional hymns of the Shaiva Nayanars and Vaishnava Alvars, the Tirukkural of Tiruvalluvar, and Kamban's 12th-century Ramavataram. Tamil was the liturgical and scholarly language of much of South and Southeast Asia in the early medieval period, leaving inscriptions as far afield as Indonesia. In the modern era, figures such as Subramania Bharati reshaped Tamil poetry, and Tamil cinema and media sustain the language's global reach.
