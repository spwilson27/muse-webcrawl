Blood in the Water match

The 1956 Olympic water polo semi-final between Hungary and the USSR

The "Blood in the Water" match (Hungarian: melbourne-i vérfürdő, "the Melbourne blood bath") was a water polo match between Hungary and the USSR at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics, held on 6 December 1956. The match took place against the backdrop of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, which had been crushed by Soviet forces only weeks earlier.

Background

On 23 October 1956, a student demonstration in Budapest escalated into a nationwide uprising against the Soviet-backed government. The revolt was briefly successful, but on 4 November Soviet tanks returned and crushed the resistance at a cost of thousands of Hungarian lives. The Hungarian Olympic team, travelling to Melbourne aboard a ship, learned of the defeat en route; several athletes later defected rather than return home.

The match

Hungary, the defending Olympic champions, dominated the semi-final, leading 4–0 late in the game. The contest was unusually physical, with players trading blows above and below the surface. With about a minute remaining, Soviet player Valentin Prokopov punched Hungary's Ervin Zádor, opening a cut above his eye. As Zádor climbed from the pool bleeding, the largely pro-Hungarian crowd erupted, and officials ended the match early, awarding victory to Hungary. Photographs of Zádor's bloodied face gave the match its enduring name.

Aftermath

Hungary went on to defeat Yugoslavia in the final and win the gold medal. Zádor was among the Hungarian athletes who defected to the West after the Games. The match remains the most famous in water polo history and was the subject of the 2006 documentary Freedom's Fury.
