The Shafi'i school (Arabic: الشافعية) is one of the four major schools (madhhab) of Islamic jurisprudence within Sunni Islam. It was founded by the Arab scholar Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i, whose treatise al-Risala systematised the theory of Islamic law (usul al-fiqh).

Principles

Shafi'i jurisprudence ranks the Qur'an and the Sunnah as the primary sources of law, followed by consensus (ijma) and analogical reasoning (qiyas). Al-Shafi'i rejected unrestricted juristic preference and local practice as independent sources, insisting on authenticated hadith. His approach set the framework later adopted, with variations, by the other schools.

Distribution

The school predominates in Lower Egypt, parts of the Levant and Hijaz, Yemen, the Swahili coast of East Africa, Kurdistan, the Caucasus and maritime Southeast Asia, where it is the traditional school of Malaysia, Indonesia and Brunei.
