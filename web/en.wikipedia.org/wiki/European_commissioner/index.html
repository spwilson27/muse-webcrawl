European commissioner

A European commissioner is a member of the European Commission, the executive branch of the European Union. There is one commissioner per member state — 27 since the withdrawal of the United Kingdom — including the president of the Commission and the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who is one of its vice-presidents. Together the commissioners form the College of Commissioners, which takes decisions collectively, normally by consensus.

Appointment

Commissioners are nominated by the government of each member state in consultation with the president-elect of the Commission, who assigns each a portfolio (such as trade, competition, agriculture or the internal market) and structures the College, including vice-presidents and, since 2019, executive vice-presidents. The proposed College must be approved as a body by the European Parliament, whose committees hold individual confirmation hearings of each commissioner-designate; candidates have occasionally been rejected or withdrawn following these hearings. The European Council then appoints the Commission by qualified majority for a five-year term, renewable once.

Role and independence

Although nominated by national governments, commissioners are required by the treaties to be completely independent: they "shall neither seek nor take instructions from any Government or other institution, body, office or entity". Each commissioner heads a Directorate-General (DG) of the Commission's civil service and is responsible for proposing legislation and implementing policy in their area. The Commission as a whole is answerable to the European Parliament, which can force its collective resignation by a motion of censure; individual commissioners can be required to resign by the president, a power strengthened by the Treaty of Lisbon.

History

The term derives from the members of the High Authority of the European Coal and Steel Community (1952). Under the Treaty of Rome there were initially nine commissioners, two from each of the larger states and one from each smaller state. The prospect of reducing the College to fewer than one member per state was debated from the Treaty of Nice onwards, but after the first Irish referendum on the Lisbon Treaty the European Council agreed in 2009 that the Commission would continue to include one national of each member state. A number of commissioners have left office early through scandal, most notoriously the entire Santer Commission, which resigned in 1999.

Conditions of service

Commissioners take an oath before the Court of Justice to respect the treaties and to be independent. They are paid from the EU budget, are subject to a code of conduct restricting outside activities, and after leaving office are subject to a "cooling-off" period during which lobbying activities must be notified and may be restricted.
