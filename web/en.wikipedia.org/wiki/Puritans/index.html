Puritans

The Puritans were English Protestants of the 16th and 17th centuries who sought to purify the Church of England of Roman Catholic practices they believed had been retained after the English Reformation. They emphasised preaching, scripture, disciplined personal piety and, in many cases, Calvinist theology.

History

Persecuted under Elizabeth I, James I and Charles I to varying degrees, some Puritans emigrated — famously the Pilgrims to Plymouth (1620) and the larger Puritan migration to Massachusetts Bay in the 1630s. Puritans were central to the Parliamentarian cause in the English Civil War and to the Commonwealth under Oliver Cromwell.

Legacy

After the Restoration of 1660, Puritanism fed into English Nonconformist traditions. In New England, Puritan values of literacy, congregational self-government and a work ethic left a lasting mark on American culture.
