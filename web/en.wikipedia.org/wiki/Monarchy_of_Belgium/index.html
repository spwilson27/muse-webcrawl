Monarchy of Belgium

The monarchy of Belgium is the constitutional monarchy of the Kingdom of Belgium. The monarch is titled King of the Belgians — a designation that emphasises the monarch's link to the Belgian people rather than to the territory. The current king is Philippe, who ascended the throne on 21 July 2013 following the abdication of his father, Albert II.

History

Belgium became independent from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands in the revolution of 1830, and the National Congress chose Leopold of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha as the first King of the Belgians; he took the oath on 21 July 1831, now Belgium's national day. His successors included Leopold II, under whom Belgium acquired the Congo Free State as his personal colony, and Albert I, who led the army during the First World War. The monarchy was shaken by the "Royal Question" over Leopold III's conduct during the Second World War, which led to his abdication in 1951 in favour of Baudouin. Albert II reigned from 1993 until his abdication in 2013.

Constitutional role

Under the Belgian Constitution, the king is head of state and commander-in-chief of the armed forces, appoints and dismisses ministers, and sanctions and promulgates laws. In practice these powers are exercised on ministerial advice, and the king's politically significant role lies largely in government formation, where he appoints the formateur after elections. The king's person is constitutionally inviolable and his acts require a minister's countersignature.
