Narasimha

Narasimha (Sanskrit: नरसिंह, "man-lion"), also rendered Narasimha Avatar, is the fourth avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu. Depicted as half man and half lion, he incarnated to destroy the demon king Hiranyakashipu and protect his devotee Prahlada, thereby restoring dharma.

The legend

Hiranyakashipu had obtained from Brahma a boon that he could be killed by neither man nor beast, neither indoors nor outdoors, neither by day nor by night, and by no weapon. Blaspheming against Vishnu, he persecuted his own son Prahlada, a steadfast devotee. Challenged to show whether Vishnu was present even in a pillar, the king smashed a column — from which Vishnu burst forth as Narasimha at twilight (neither day nor night), placed the demon across his lap on the threshold (neither inside nor outside) and tore him apart with his claws (no weapon). The story is told in the Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana and Mahabharata.

Worship and iconography

Narasimha is worshipped across India, especially in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, at temples such as Ahobilam, Simhachalam and Yadadri, and as the family deity of many Vaishnava communities. Iconography shows him either alone (Kevala), with Lakshmi on his lap (Lakshmi-Narasimha), or in yoga posture (Yoga-Narasimha). In Hampi, a colossal monolithic statue of Lakshmi Narasimha (1528) is among the largest sculptures of Vijayanagara.
