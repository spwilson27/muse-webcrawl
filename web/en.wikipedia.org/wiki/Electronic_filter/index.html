Electronic filter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Electronic filters are electrical circuits that remove unwanted frequency components from a signal, enhance wanted ones, or both. They may be passive or active, analog or digital.

Classification by frequency response

  • Low-pass filters pass low frequencies and attenuate high ones.
  • High-pass filters pass high frequencies and attenuate low ones.
  • Band-pass filters pass a band of frequencies and attenuate those outside it.
  • Band-stop (notch) filters attenuate a specific band and pass frequencies outside it.

Design

Passive filters use networks of resistors, inductors and capacitors; active filters add amplifying devices such as operational amplifiers, which allow gain and eliminate the need for bulky inductors. Classic polynomial approximations to the ideal filter response include the Butterworth (maximally flat), Chebyshev (steeper roll-off with ripple), and elliptic (steepest, with ripple in both bands) designs.

History

Filter theory grew out of telephone engineering in the early twentieth century, notably the work of George Ashley Campbell at AT&T and Otto Zobel, who developed the m-derived filter. Modern filter design draws on network synthesis theory and is also implemented digitally as FIR and IIR filters in signal processors.
