Russian Federation

The Russian Federation, commonly known as Russia, is a country spanning Eastern Europe and North Asia. It is the largest country in the world by area, covering more than 17 million square kilometres across eleven time zones, and has a population of roughly 144 million. Its capital and largest city is Moscow.

Geography

Russia stretches from the Baltic Sea to the Pacific Ocean, bordering fourteen countries. Its landscapes range from tundra and vast Siberian taiga forests to steppe and the Caucasus mountains. Major rivers include the Volga, Ob, Yenisei, and Lena; Lake Baikal is the world's deepest freshwater lake.

History

The East Slavic state of Kievan Rus' emerged in the ninth century; its successor principalities were overrun by the Mongols in the thirteenth century, after which Moscow rose to prominence, uniting the Russian lands and expanding into an empire under the tsars. The Russian Empire fell in the revolutions of 1917, and the Russian SFSR became the largest republic of the Soviet Union. The modern Russian Federation emerged on 25 December 1991 following the dissolution of the USSR.

Government and economy

Russia is constitutionally a federal semi-presidential republic, with a bicameral Federal Assembly. It is one of the world's largest producers of oil and natural gas, and its economy also rests on metals, agriculture, and arms exports. Russia is a permanent member of the United Nations Security Council and a nuclear-weapon state.
