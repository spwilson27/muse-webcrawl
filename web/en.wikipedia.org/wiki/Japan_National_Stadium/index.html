The Japan National Stadium (Japanese: 国立競技場, Kokuritsu Kyōgijō), officially the National Stadium, is a multi-purpose stadium in Shinjuku, Tokyo, built on the site of the former 1964 Olympic National Stadium. Designed by architect Kengo Kuma, it served as the main stadium for the 2020 Summer Olympics and Paralympics (held in 2021), hosting the opening and closing ceremonies and athletics events. Its design is noted for its extensive use of timber and greenery, intended to harmonise with the gardens of the Meiji Shrine nearby.

Development and design controversy

The stadium replaced the 1958 National Stadium, venue of the 1964 Tokyo Olympics. An international competition in 2012 selected a futuristic design by Zaha Hadid, but the project drew mounting criticism over its scale and cost, projected at more than ¥250 billion. In July 2015 Prime Minister Shinzo Abe scrapped the Hadid design and ordered a restart — an extraordinary step so close to the Games. A new competition selected Kengo Kuma's lower, timber-latticed scheme, proposed with Toyo Ito's team among the runners-up. Construction ran from December 2016 to November 2019.

Design and features

Kuma's design wraps the bowl in deep, layered eaves of steel and wood — timber sourced from all 47 prefectures of Japan — with planting on the terraces to blend the stadium into the green Outer Garden of Meiji Shrine. The roof partially covers the stands; the seating bowl is steep, in earthy "mosaic" colours intended to evoke dappled forest light. Capacity was about 68,000 for the Olympics. The first sporting event was the Emperor's Cup football final on 1 January 2020.

Tokyo 2020 and beyond

Because of the COVID-19 pandemic, the Olympic and Paralympic ceremonies and athletics took place in 2021 almost entirely without spectators. The stadium has since hosted Japan national football matches, the Emperor's Cup and J.League Cup finals, athletics meetings and concerts. A 2015 plan to keep the stadium in public hands led to negotiations over privatisation and naming rights to offset its running costs.
