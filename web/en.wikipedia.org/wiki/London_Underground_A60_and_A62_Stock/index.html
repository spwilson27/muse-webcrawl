London Underground A60 and A62 stock

A60 and A62 stock
In service: 1961–2012
Manufacturer: Cravens, Sheffield
Lines served: Metropolitan, East London, Hammersmith & City, Circle
Replaced by: S stock

The London Underground A60 and A62 stock, commonly referred to as A stock, was a type of sub-surface London Underground train built by Cravens of Sheffield in two batches, 1960–62 and 1961–63. It served the Metropolitan line for over 50 years and was, until its withdrawal in 2012, the oldest stock in regular service on the Underground, and also the widest and (at the end) among the most spacious.

A stock at work on the Metropolitan line, its home for five decades.

Design

A stock was built for the electrification and modernisation of the Metropolitan line to Amersham and Chesham. Trains were formed of four-car units coupled into eight-car trains, with distinctive flared body sides to maximise the loading gauge, luggage racks for long-distance commuters, and a design top speed of 70 mph — the fastest on the Underground network. The A62 batch differed slightly in details such as signalling equipment.

Service and withdrawal

Besides Metropolitan line services, A stock worked the East London line (until its closure for conversion to the Overground in 2007) and occasionally the Hammersmith & City and Circle lines. Refurbishment in the 1990s gave the trains new interiors and unpainted aluminium exteriors. They were replaced by S8 stock, with the last scheduled A stock passenger service running on 26 September 2012; a special farewell railtour ran in September 2012. One unit has been preserved by the London Transport Museum.
