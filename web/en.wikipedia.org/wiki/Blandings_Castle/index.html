Blandings Castle is a fictional country house in Shropshire, England, the setting for a series of comic novels and short stories by P. G. Wodehouse. It is the home of the absent-minded Clarence Threepwood, 9th Earl of Emsworth, whose placid devotion to his prize pig, the Empress of Blandings, is continually disturbed by domineering sisters, lovestruck nephews, impostors, and purloined objects.

The castle and its household

Wodehouse described Blandings as a large Tudor house with famous gardens, a lake, and extensive grounds near the village of Blandings Parva. The household includes Lord Emsworth's brother, the Hon. Galahad Threepwood, a sprightly bachelor writing scandalous memoirs; the butler Beach; and a rotation of formidable aunts, chief among them Lady Constance Keeble, who attempts to impose order. Plots frequently turn on the Empress of Blandings, a black Berkshire sow who repeatedly wins the Fat Pigs class at the Shropshire Agricultural Show and is repeatedly stolen, hidden, or fed whisky by rival factions.

The Blandings books

The series began with Something Fresh (1915) and continued for sixty years, through Summer Lightning (1929), Heavy Weather (1933), Uncle Fred in the Springtime (1939), Pigs Have Wings (1952), and Galahad at Blandings (1965), to the unfinished Sunset at Blandings, published posthumously in 1977. Blandings is, with the Jeeves stories, Wodehouse's best-known creation, and has been adapted several times for radio and television, including the BBC series Blandings (2013–2014).
