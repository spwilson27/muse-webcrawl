Jacqueline Pascal

Jacqueline Pascal (4 October 1625 – 4 October 1661) was a French poet, child prodigy, and nun, the younger sister of the philosopher and mathematician Blaise Pascal. She became a central figure in the Jansenist community of Port-Royal-des-Champs.

Early life

Born in Clermont-Ferrand to Étienne Pascal, Jacqueline displayed a remarkable gift for verse as a child and was presented at the court of Louis XIII, where her poetry charmed Cardinal Richelieu. The family moved to Rouen, where the young Blaise's scientific interests and Jacqueline's religious vocation both deepened.

Port-Royal

Drawn to the austere spirituality of Port-Royal, Jacqueline entered the convent in 1652 as Sister Jacqueline de Sainte-Euphémie, overcoming her father's initial opposition. She wrote poetry, letters, and a rule for the education of children in the convent's schools. During the persecution of the Jansenists she courageously defended the nuns but, under pressure in 1661, signed the formulary condemning Jansenius — an act that caused her great anguish. She died on her 36th birthday, shortly before her brother, whose devotion to her memory shaped the final year of his life.
