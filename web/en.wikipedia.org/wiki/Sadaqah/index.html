Sadaqah

Sadaqah (Arabic: صدقة, "charity", "benevolence", plural ṣadaqāt) in Islam is voluntary charity given out of compassion, love, friendship, religious duty or generosity. It differs from zakat, which is obligatory almsgiving calculated on wealth and one of the Five Pillars of Islam.

Meaning and scope

The word derives from the root meaning truthfulness and sincerity. In Qur'anic and hadith usage, sadaqah is broadly conceived: besides money and food, acts such as smiling at another, removing harm from a road, helping the blind, or speaking a kind word count as sadaqah. Famous hadith state that "every good deed is a charity" and that even half a date given sincerely can shield the giver.

Sadaqah jariyah

Sadaqah jariyah is ongoing charity whose benefit continues after the giver's death — such as digging a well, planting a tree, building a mosque or school, or spreading useful knowledge. It is one of three things, along with beneficial knowledge and a righteous child who prays for them, said to continue benefiting a person after death.

Practice

Sadaqah may be given at any time, to Muslims and non-Muslims in need, and is especially emphasised during Ramadan and times of hardship. Islamic teaching stresses giving discreetly, without humiliating the recipient or seeking praise.
