Moria (Middle-earth)

Moria, in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium, is the name given in later ages to Khazad-dûm, the vast underground kingdom of the Dwarves beneath the Misty Mountains. "Moria" is Sindarin for "the Black Pit" or "Black Chasm," a name applied after its fall; the Dwarves themselves called it Khazad-dûm, "the Delving of the Dwarves."

History

Founded by Durin the Deathless in the First Age, Khazad-dûm was the greatest of dwarf-realms, growing rich on mining mithril, the precious silvery metal found only there. It prospered in friendship with the Elves of Eregion in the Second Age. In the Third Age, however, the miners delved too deep and awoke a Balrog — "Durin's Bane" — which slew Kings Durin VI and Náin I and drove the Dwarves out (T.A. 1980–81). Thereafter Moria was a place of darkness, infested with Orcs; Balin's later colonising expedition (T.A. 2989) was annihilated, as the Fellowship discovered in the Chamber of Mazarbul.

The Fellowship's passage

In The Fellowship of the Ring, the Company enters through the Doors of Durin ("Speak, friend, and enter"), crosses the great halls, and on the Bridge of Khazad-dûm Gandalf confronts the Balrog; both fall into the abyss. After the War of the Ring, Dwarves under Durin VII are prophesied eventually to reoccupy their ancient home.
