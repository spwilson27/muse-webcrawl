Smartwatch

A smartwatch is a wearable computer in the form of a wristwatch. Modern smartwatches provide a touchscreen interface for daily use, while an associated smartphone app provides management and telemetry, such as long-term biomonitoring. Early models could perform basic tasks such as calculations, digital time telling, translations and game-playing; modern smartwatches have more general functionality closer to smartphones, including mobile apps, media playback, and phone calls.

Features

Smartwatches commonly pair with a smartphone by Bluetooth to mirror notifications, calls and messages. Most include sensors for fitness and health tracking: accelerometers, heart-rate monitors, GPS, blood-oxygen (SpO2) sensors, electrocardiogram (ECG) functions, skin temperature sensors and sleep tracking. Many support contactless payments, voice assistants, and cellular connectivity that lets them operate independently of a phone.

History

Early digital watches with computing features appeared in the 1970s and 1980s (such as the Pulsar NL C01 and Seiko's RC series). The category expanded in the early 2010s with the Pebble watch, funded by a record Kickstarter campaign, followed by Samsung's Galaxy Gear (2013) and Google's Android Wear platform (2014). The Apple Watch, released in 2015, became the market-leading line. Dedicated sports watches from manufacturers such as Garmin and Suunto emphasise battery life and advanced training metrics.

Health research

Because they are worn continuously, smartwatches are used in large-scale health studies, for example to detect irregular heart rhythms such as atrial fibrillation. Regulators have cleared specific watch features for ECG and irregular-rhythm notifications in many countries, though the devices are not substitutes for medical diagnosis.
