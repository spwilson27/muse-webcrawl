Barabati Fort is a ruined fortress in Cuttack, Odisha, India, standing on the bank of the Mahanadi River. It was the seat of power in medieval Odisha and today is one of the city's principal historical landmarks, adjacent to the Barabati Stadium cricket ground.

History

The origins of fortification at the site are traced to the early medieval period; tradition attributes an early fort to the Somavamshi/Kesari rulers, while the stone fort as it took shape is generally credited to the Eastern Ganga dynasty, particularly the fourteenth-century king Anangabhima Deva III or his successors, who made Cuttack (Kataka) their capital. Cuttack's name itself is derived from Kataka, meaning a military camp or capital. The fort later passed through Afghan, Mughal and Maratha hands, and was stormed by the British in 1803 during the Second Anglo-Maratha War, after which Odisha came under East India Company rule.

Description

The fort was protected by a wide moat fed from the Mahanadi and by massive laterite walls. Most of the structure was dismantled in the nineteenth century, its stone reused for embankments and public works. What survives chiefly is the arched entrance gateway, parts of the moat and the earthen mound of the citadel area, alongside foundations and scattered remains maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India. Within the fort precinct in Maratha times stood palaces and the famous Barabati mosque structures nearby.
