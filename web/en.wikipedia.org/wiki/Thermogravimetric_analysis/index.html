Thermogravimetric analysis
Acronym: TGA
Measures: Mass change vs. temperature or time

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) is a method of thermal analysis in which the mass of a sample is measured over time as the temperature changes. This measurement provides information about physical phenomena such as phase transitions, absorption and desorption, as well as chemical phenomena including chemisorption, thermal decomposition, and solid-gas reactions.

Instrumentation

A TGA instrument consists of a precision balance supporting a sample pan inside a programmable furnace. The atmosphere around the sample is controlled — it may be inert (nitrogen or argon) or reactive (air or oxygen). Mass is recorded continuously as temperature is ramped, held, or cycled, producing a thermogravimetric curve.

Applications

TGA is widely used in materials science and quality control to determine the composition of polymers and composites (for example filler and carbon black content), thermal stability, moisture and solvent content, and decomposition kinetics. Coupled instruments (TGA-MS or TGA-FTIR) analyse the evolved gases to identify decomposition products.

Derivative thermogravimetry

The derivative of the mass curve (DTG) is often plotted alongside the TGA curve; its peaks mark the temperatures of maximum rate of mass loss, helping to resolve overlapping decomposition steps.
