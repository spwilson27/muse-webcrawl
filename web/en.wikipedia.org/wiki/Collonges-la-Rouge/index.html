Collonges-la-Rouge

Collonges-la-Rouge (Occitan: Colonjas) is a commune in the Corrèze department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of south-central France. It is famous for its buildings constructed almost entirely of red sandstone, which give the village its name and appearance. It is a member of Les Plus Beaux Villages de France (The Most Beautiful Villages of France) association — indeed, the association was founded here in 1982 by the village's then mayor, Charles Ceyrac.

History

Monks of Charroux Abbey founded a priory at Collonges in the 8th century, and the village grew along the pilgrimage routes. It prospered under the Viscounts of Turenne, whose officials built manors and turreted houses in the local red sandstone in the 16th and 17th centuries. After the phylloxera crisis destroyed its vineyards in the 19th century the village declined, before being revived by tourism in the 20th century.

Sights

Notable buildings include the church of Saint-Pierre, begun in the 11th century with a distinctive two-naved interior; the Maison de la Sirène, with its carved mermaid; the covered market hall with its old grain measures; and numerous manor houses such as the Castel de Vassinhac. The red colour of the sandstone comes from its iron oxide content.

Population

The commune has only a few hundred permanent residents, but receives large numbers of visitors in the summer months.
