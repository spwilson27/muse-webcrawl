Zakynthos (Greek: Ζάκυνθος), also known by its Italian name Zante, is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea. It is the third largest of the Ionian Islands, with an area of about 406 square kilometres, and is noted for its fertile plains, olive groves, and the cliffs of its western coast.

Navagio Beach, one of the most photographed beaches in Greece.

Geography

The island has two distinct zones: the fertile southeastern plain, and the mountainous, cliff-bound west. Its best-known landmark is Navagio Beach, where the wreck of the freighter Panagiotis rests on white sand enclosed by limestone cliffs. The surrounding sea forms the National Marine Park of Zakynthos, established to protect the endangered loggerhead sea turtle (Caretta caretta), which nests on the beaches of Laganas Bay.

History

Named in myth after Zakynthos, son of Dardanus, the island was settled in antiquity and mentioned by Homer. It later passed through Venetian rule (1484–1797), during which it flourished culturally and earned the nickname Fior di Levante ("Flower of the Levant"). A devastating earthquake in 1953 destroyed most of the island's buildings, which were rebuilt in traditional style. Zakynthos is the birthplace of Greece's national poet, Dionysios Solomos, author of the "Hymn to Liberty".

Economy

Agriculture — olives, citrus, and the small Zante currant — was historically central, but tourism is now the dominant industry, drawing visitors to resorts such as Laganas and Tsilivi.
