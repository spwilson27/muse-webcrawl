Lithosphere

The lithosphere is the rigid outer layer of the Earth, consisting of the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle. It is broken into large slabs called tectonic plates, whose slow movements over the weaker, ductile asthenosphere drive plate tectonics.

Types

Oceanic lithosphere

Oceanic lithosphere consists of mafic crust a few kilometres thick overlying ultramafic mantle. It thickens as it ages and moves away from mid-ocean ridges, and it is continuously created at spreading centres and recycled into the mantle at subduction zones.

Continental lithosphere

Continental lithosphere has a thicker, less dense crust and may persist for billions of years beneath cratons. It is typically 40–280 km thick.

Movement

The lithosphere's rigidity distinguishes it from the asthenosphere below, which, although solid, can flow over geological time. Plate boundaries are the sites of most earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain building.
