Piper Aircraft

Piper Aircraft, headquartered in Vero Beach, Florida

Piper Aircraft, Inc. is an American manufacturer of general aviation aircraft, headquartered at Vero Beach Regional Airport in Florida. Founded in 1927 as the Taylor Brothers Aircraft Manufacturing Company and reorganised under William T. Piper in 1937, it is — with Cessna and Beechcraft — historically one of the "Big Three" of light aircraft manufacturing.

History

The company's first great success was the Piper J-3 Cub, the fabric-covered tandem-seat trainer produced from 1938 and built in huge numbers for civilian and military (L-4 Grasshopper) use during the Second World War. Postwar models expanded the range: the PA-28 Cherokee family, introduced in 1961, became one of the best-selling light aircraft series ever; other notable lines include the PA-18 Super Cub, the PA-23 Aztec and PA-30 Twin Comanche twins, the PA-31 Navajo, the PA-46 Malibu/Matrix/M600 pressurised singles and turboprops, and the Seminole and Seneca trainers.

Piper suffered through the general aviation downturn and product-liability crisis of the 1980s and 1990s, passing through bankruptcy and several owners. It was acquired in 2009 by Imprimis, an investment firm owned by the government of Brunei, and has since focused on trainer fleets and the M-class single-engine line, including the M600 SLS turboprop.

Present day

Piper remains one of the largest employers in Indian River County, Florida, manufacturing singles, twins and turboprops, and supplying pilot-training fleets to flight schools and universities around the world.
