Standard operating procedure

A standard operating procedure (SOP) is a set of step-by-step instructions compiled by an organization to help workers carry out routine operations. SOPs aim to achieve efficiency, quality output and uniformity of performance, while reducing miscommunication and failure to comply with industry regulations.

Purpose

SOPs describe both technical and administrative aspects of an organisation's recurring work. They are central to quality-management systems such as ISO 9000 and to regulated industries including pharmaceuticals (under Good Manufacturing Practice), clinical research (Good Clinical Practice), aviation, healthcare and the military, where the term also applies to standing orders for routine operations.

Development and use

An effective SOP is concise, clearly written and typically includes: the purpose and scope; definitions; roles and responsibilities; required materials and equipment; the procedure itself in ordered steps; and references. SOPs require periodic review and revision, version control, staff training and documented acknowledgement that staff have read and understood current versions.

Clinical research SOPs are required by ICH Good Clinical Practice guidelines so that trials are conducted consistently across sites and countries. In healthcare, SOPs and closely related clinical protocols standardise care tasks such as infection control and drug administration.
