Data assimilation is a mathematical discipline that seeks to combine observational data with the output of a numerical model of a real system, taking the errors in both into account, in order to obtain the best possible estimate of the system's state. It is best known from numerical weather prediction, where observations from satellites, aircraft, ships, buoys and ground stations are fused with a short-range forecast to produce the "analysis" from which each new forecast is launched.

Concept

Neither observations nor models are perfect: observations are sparse, indirect and noisy, while models are approximate and their initial conditions incomplete. Data assimilation formalises the compromise, typically in Bayesian terms — the model's previous forecast (the "background") provides a prior estimate, and the observations update it in proportion to their respective uncertainties. The cycle of forecast and analysis repeats throughout the lifetime of an operational system.

Methods

Early operational schemes used statistical interpolation (optimal interpolation). Modern methods fall into two broad families: sequential methods derived from the Kalman filter, including ensemble Kalman filters that represent uncertainty with an ensemble of model runs; and variational methods, notably 4D-Var, which adjust the model's trajectory over a time window to fit all observations within it. Hybrid ensemble-variational systems are used at leading centres such as ECMWF.

Applications

Beyond weather forecasting, data assimilation is used in ocean state estimation, land-surface and hydrological forecasting, air-quality modelling, ionospheric studies and reservoir engineering, and in reanalysis projects that produce consistent historical records of the climate system.
