Saudi Aramco, officially the Saudi Arabian Oil Company, is the national oil company of Saudi Arabia and one of the largest companies in the world by revenue. It manages the kingdom's hydrocarbon reserves — among the largest proven crude reserves on Earth — including Ghawar, the world's biggest conventional oil field, and is frequently the world's most profitable company.

History

Aramco began in 1933 when Saudi Arabia granted a concession to Standard Oil of California; oil was struck in commercial quantities at Dammam in 1938. The Arabian-American Oil Company (Aramco) name dates to 1944. The Saudi government bought in progressively and completed full nationalisation by 1980; the present company was established by royal decree in 1988.

In December 2019 Aramco listed about 1.5% of its shares on the Saudi Tadawul exchange in the largest IPO in history, valuing the company at roughly $1.7 trillion. A further stake was transferred to the Public Investment Fund, and a secondary offering followed in 2024.

Operations

The company produces on the order of 9–10 million barrels of crude per day, operates refineries and chemical ventures worldwide, and is a central instrument of Saudi economic policy, including OPEC+ production decisions. In 2019 drone and missile attacks on its Abqaiq and Khurais facilities temporarily halved output, underlining its global importance.
