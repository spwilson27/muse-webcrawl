Latin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Latin inscription carved in stone from ancient Rome, with classical capital letters]
A classical Latin inscription.

Latin is a classical language belonging to the Italic branch of the Indo-European languages. Originally spoken by the Latins in the region around Rome, Italy, it became the dominant language of the Roman Republic and Empire. Through the Middle Ages and into the modern era Latin remained the language of scholarship, law, medicine and the Roman Catholic Church in Western Europe, and it is the direct ancestor of the Romance languages — Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Romanian and others.

History

Classical Latin, the literary standard of authors such as Cicero, Virgil and Caesar, was codified in the 1st century BC. The spoken vernacular, Vulgar Latin, diverged into the Romance languages after Rome's fall. Medieval and Renaissance Latin continued as Europe's scholarly lingua franca; Newton and Linnaeus still published in it.

Grammar and influence

Latin is a highly inflected language: nouns decline in cases, verbs conjugate for person, tense, mood and voice, and word order is flexible. It has contributed an enormous vocabulary to English, directly and via French, and supplies the scientific names of organisms in binomial nomenclature.

Today

Latin is an official language of Vatican City and survives in mottos, legal phrases, medical terminology and education, though it has no native speakers as a community language.
