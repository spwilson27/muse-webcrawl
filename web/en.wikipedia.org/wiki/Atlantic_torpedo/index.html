Atlantic torpedo
Scientific nameTetronarce nobiliana
FamilyTorpedinidae
SizeUp to 1.8 m, 90 kg
RangeAtlantic Ocean, Mediterranean

The Atlantic torpedo (Tetronarce nobiliana, formerly Torpedo nobiliana) is a species of electric ray in the family Torpedinidae. It is the largest known electric ray, capable of producing an electric discharge of up to about 220 volts, which it uses to stun prey and deter predators.

Distribution and habitat

It occurs on both sides of the Atlantic Ocean — from Nova Scotia to Brazil in the west, and from Scotland to West Africa, including the Mediterranean Sea, in the east. Young fish are mainly benthic over sandy or muddy bottoms, while large adults are often pelagic and wide-ranging, occurring from shallow water down to about 800 m.

Biology

The Atlantic torpedo has a nearly circular, flabby disc and a short, muscular tail bearing two dorsal fins and a large caudal fin. Kidney-shaped electric organs, derived from muscle tissue, occupy much of the disc. It is aplacental viviparous, giving birth to up to 60 pups. Its diet consists mainly of bony fishes and small sharks, seized after being stunned and engulfed whole.

Human interaction

Its shock, though rarely fatal, can knock a person unconscious; divers are advised not to handle it. It is not commercially fished but is taken as bycatch and is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN.
