Mangosteen

Mangosteen
Scientific nameGarcinia mangostana
FamilyClusiaceae
OriginSoutheast Asia (Sunda Islands region)

The mangosteen (Garcinia mangostana), sometimes called the purple mangosteen, is a tropical evergreen tree and its fruit, believed to have originated in Maritime Southeast Asia. The fruit is widely celebrated in Southeast Asia as the "queen of fruits", a customary counterpart to the durian, the "king of fruits".

Fruit

The fruit is round, about the size of a tangerine, with a thick, deep-purple rind (exocarp) that encloses fragrant white segmented flesh (aril). The soft flesh is sweet, tangy, juicy and slightly fibrous, with a flavour often compared to a mixture of peach, strawberry, lychee and citrus. It is eaten fresh, usually by scoring and squeezing open the rind.

Cultivation

The tree grows slowly, is highly sensitive to cold, and requires the humid equatorial tropics. It is cultivated throughout Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines, and increasingly in parts of the Caribbean and Central America. Because of plant pest concerns, fresh imports were long restricted in some countries.
