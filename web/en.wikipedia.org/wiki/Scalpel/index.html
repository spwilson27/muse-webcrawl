Scalpel

A scalpel, or a lancet, or bistoury, is a small and extremely sharp bladed instrument used for surgery, anatomical dissection, podiatry and various arts and crafts (called a hobby knife). Scalpels may be single-use disposable or reusable. Reusable scalpels can have permanently attached but replaceable blades, or, more commonly, removable single-use blades.

Scalpels consist of two parts, a blade and a handle. The handles are often reusable, with the blades being replaceable. In medical applications, each blade is only used once (sometimes just for a single small cut).

Design and materials

Scalpel blades are usually made of hardened and tempered steel, stainless steel, or high-carbon steel; in addition, titanium, ceramic, diamond and even obsidian knives are not uncommon. Obsidian, for instance, produces an edge far thinner than steel — about 3 nanometres — and is used in some delicate surgeries, though the blades are fragile. Laser scalpels and ultrasonic scalpels are alternatives that cut using light or high-frequency vibration.

Blades are identified by a numbering system: common surgical blades include the No. 10 (a broad curved edge, used for skin incisions), No. 11 (a pointed tip for stab incisions and drains), No. 12 (a hooked blade), and No. 15 (a small curved blade for precise work). Handles are commonly numbered too; the No. 3 handle takes the 10–15 blade family and the No. 4 handle takes the larger 20-series blades.

Use and safety

In surgery the scalpel makes the primary incision in skin or tissue, after which scissors, cautery or blunt dissection may be preferred to limit damage. Because scalpel injuries are a significant occupational hazard in medicine, safety-engineered scalpels with retractable or shielded blades and hands-free passing techniques (such as use of a kidney dish or "neutral zone") have been widely adopted, and many institutions have moved to single-use disposable scalpels entirely.

History

Sharp stone and obsidian blades were used for ritual and surgical cutting in prehistoric times. Metal surgical knives are described in the Hippocratic writings and in the Sushruta Samhita of ancient India. The modern interchangeable blade was patented in 1915 by Morgan Parker, whose two-piece blade-and-handle design, commercialised by Bard-Parker, remains the standard pattern today.
