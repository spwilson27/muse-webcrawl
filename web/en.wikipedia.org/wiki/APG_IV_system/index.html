APG IV system

The APG IV system of flowering plant classification is the fourth version of a modern, mostly molecular-based system of plant taxonomy for flowering plants (angiosperms), developed by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG). It was published in 2016, seven years after its predecessor, the APG III system, in the Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society.

Details

The APG IV system recognises 64 orders and 416 families of flowering plants. Compared with APG III, a number of new orders were recognised, including Boraginales, Dilleniales, Icacinales, Metteniusales and Vahliales, and several families were recircumscribed. The system continues the APG philosophy of classifying plants according to monophyletic groups based on molecular phylogenetic evidence, while retaining a placement for taxa whose position remained uncertain.

Classification

The broad structure of the system follows earlier APG versions: a list of orders grouped into informal clades such as magnoliids, monocots, commelinids, fabids and malvids. Formally named clades above the level of order were used sparingly, and the system remains a list of orders and families rather than a fully ranked hierarchy. Several families that in APG III had been kept separate for convenience were merged, such as the inclusion of Xanthorrhoeaceae within Asphodelaceae and the recognition of a broadly circumscribed Primulaceae.
