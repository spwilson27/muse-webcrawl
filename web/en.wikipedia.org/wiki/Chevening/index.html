Chevening is a village and civil parish in the Sevenoaks District of Kent, England, best known for Chevening House, a large country house that serves as the official country residence of the United Kingdom's Foreign Secretary.

The house

The present house, built around 1630 and attributed to Inigo Jones, is one of the earliest examples of classical Palladian-influenced architecture in England. It was the seat of the Stanhope family, Earls Stanhope, for nearly 250 years. James Stanhope, the last Earl, bequeathed the house and estate to the nation in 1959 under the Chevening Estate Act, to be used by a person nominated by the Prime Minister — in practice usually the Foreign Secretary.

Use

Chevening hosts foreign dignitaries, diplomatic conferences, and cabinet gatherings. Its 3,500-acre estate includes a lake, gardens, and a maze. The name is also lent to the UK government's Chevening Scholarship programme for international students, administered by the Foreign Office.
