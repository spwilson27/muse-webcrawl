James Whale

James Whale (22 July 1889 – 29 May 1957) was an English film director, theatre director and artist. He is best remembered for his four classic horror films for Universal Pictures: Frankenstein (1931), The Old Dark House (1932), The Invisible Man (1933) and Bride of Frankenstein (1935), which helped define the horror genre in early sound cinema. Whale was openly gay in an era when few in Hollywood were, a fact central to later studies of his life and work.

Early life and theatre

Whale was born in Dudley in the Black Country, the sixth child of a blast-furnace worker. He studied art and worked as a cartoonist before enlisting in the army during the First World War; captured by the Germans in 1917, he staged plays in a prisoner-of-war camp. After the war he became an actor and stage director in London. His production of R. C. Sherriff's anti-war play Journey's End (1928) was a major success in London and New York and led to an invitation to Hollywood to direct the film version (1930).

Universal horror films

At Universal, Whale directed the war drama Waterloo Bridge (1931) before turning to horror. Frankenstein (1931), starring Boris Karloff as the Monster and Colin Clive as Henry Frankenstein, was a sensation and established Whale's reputation. He followed it with the comedy-thriller The Old Dark House (1932) and the special-effects showcase The Invisible Man (1933), with Claude Rains. Bride of Frankenstein (1935), widely considered his masterpiece, blended horror, pathos and camp wit. His other notable films include the musical Show Boat (1936) and The Man in the Iron Mask (1939).

Later life and death

Whale's career declined in the late 1930s, and he retired from directing in the early 1940s, devoting himself to painting and travel. After a series of strokes, he drowned in the swimming pool of his Pacific Palisades home in May 1957; a suicide note was later revealed. His final years were dramatised in the film Gods and Monsters (1998), in which he was played by Ian McKellen.
