Third Order of Saint Francis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St Francis founded orders for friars, nuns, and lay penitents

The Third Order of Saint Francis is an order of the Franciscan family founded by St Francis of Assisi for laypeople and secular clergy who wished to live according to the Franciscan spirit while remaining in the world. Tradition dates its origin to 1221, when Francis is said to have given a rule to the "Brothers and Sisters of Penance"; the first formal rule was approved by Pope Nicholas IV in 1289 (Supra montem).

Branches

The Third Order developed two principal forms. The Secular Franciscan Order (Ordo Franciscanus Saecularis) comprises lay men and women, and diocesan priests, who live in their homes and workplaces under a rule of prayer, simplicity, and service; its present rule dates from 1978. The Third Order Regular, by contrast, consists of religious congregations of men and women living in community under vows, many founded in the nineteenth century for teaching, nursing, and missionary work.

Notable members

Historical tertiaries include St Elizabeth of Hungary, St Louis IX of France, and, by tradition, Christopher Columbus and Dante Alighieri. In the Anglican Communion, a Third Order of the Society of Saint Francis continues the same pattern of dispersed, rule-based lay life.
