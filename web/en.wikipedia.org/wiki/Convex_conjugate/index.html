Convex conjugate

In mathematics, the convex conjugate (also called the Legendre–Fenchel transformation) of a function assigns to it the function whose graph encodes its supporting hyperplanes. It is the generalisation of the Legendre transformation to non-differentiable functions.

A convex function reconstructed from its tangent lines.

Definition

f*(x*) = supx ( ⟨x*, x⟩ − f(x) )

The conjugate f* is always convex and lower semicontinuous, being a supremum of affine functions.

Properties

  • Biconjugation: f** is the convex hull ("convex envelope") of f; for closed convex f, f** = f (Fenchel–Moreau theorem).
  • Fenchel–Young inequality: f(x) + f*(x*) ≥ ⟨x*, x⟩.
  • Conjugation reverses order: f ≤ g implies f* ≥ g*.

Examples

The conjugate of f(x) = |x| is the indicator of [−1, 1]; the conjugate of a quadratic ½xᵀAx is ½x*ᵀA⁻¹x*.

Applications

Convex conjugates underpin duality in optimisation, the relation between Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, and large-deviation rate functions in probability.
