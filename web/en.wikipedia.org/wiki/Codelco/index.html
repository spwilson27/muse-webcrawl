Codelco (Spanish: Corporación Nacional del Cobre de Chile, the National Copper Corporation of Chile) is a Chilean state-owned mining company and one of the world's largest producers of copper. Formed in 1976 to operate the large copper mines nationalized under President Salvador Allende in 1971, Codelco has historically accounted for around a tenth of world copper production and a major share of Chile's export earnings.

Operations

Codelco's divisions include some of the most famous copper mines in the world: Chuquicamata, for decades the largest open-pit copper mine on Earth; El Teniente, the world's largest underground copper mine; Andina; Radomiro Tomic; Gabriela Mistral; Ministro Hales; and El Salvador. Besides copper cathode and concentrates, the company is a significant producer of molybdenum. Its headquarters are in Santiago.

Challenges

The company's mature mines suffer from declining ore grades, rising costs, and large debts accumulated to fund "structural projects" — including the conversion of Chuquicamata to an underground mine — intended to extend their lives for decades. Codelco's production fell to its lowest levels in about a quarter-century in the early 2020s, prompting management changes and debate over the company's governance and finances. Under a 2023 agreement, Codelco also took a leading role in Chile's national lithium strategy, negotiating state participation in the lithium operations of the Salar de Atacama.
