Shropshire (abbreviated Salop) is a ceremonial county in the West Midlands of England, on the border with Wales. It is one of England's most rural and sparsely populated counties. The county town is Shrewsbury, and the largest settlement is the new town of Telford.

Geography

The River Severn, Britain's longest river, flows through the county. The Shropshire Hills, including the Long Mynd, Stiperstones, and the Wrekin, form an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in the south. The Ironbridge Gorge, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, was a cradle of the Industrial Revolution.

History

The area was part of the Welsh Marches, contested between the English and Welsh. The world's first iron bridge was built across the Severn at Ironbridge in 1779. Shropshire was the birthplace of the naturalist Charles Darwin, born in Shrewsbury in 1809.
