Nikolaus Ludwig, Imperial Count von Zinzendorf und Pottendorf (26 May 1700 – 9 May 1760) was a German religious and social reformer, bishop of the Moravian Church, and founder of the Herrnhut community.

Nikolaus Zinzendorf

Nikolaus Ludwig von Zinzendorf (1700–1760) was a Saxon nobleman and Pietist theologian who renewed the ancient Unitas Fratrum as the Moravian Church and pioneered Protestant world missions.

Life and Herrnhut

Born in Dresden into a pious Lutheran noble family, Zinzendorf was educated at Halle under August Hermann Francke and at Wittenberg, and entered Saxon state service. In 1722 he bought the estate of Berthelsdorf in Upper Lusatia and offered refuge there to persecuted Protestant exiles from Moravia, who founded the village of Herrnhut ("the Lord's watch") on his land. After initial divisions, the community was united in 1727 in a remarkable outpouring remembered as the Moravian Pentecost, and began a prayer watch that continued around the clock for a hundred years.

Missions and exile

Zinzendorf turned the small community outward: from 1732 Herrnhut sent missionaries to enslaved Africans in the Danish West Indies, to Greenland, North and South America, South Africa and elsewhere — beginning the modern Protestant missionary movement decades before William Carey. Ordained a Lutheran pastor and consecrated a bishop of the Moravian Church in 1737, he endured a decade of banishment from Saxony (1736–1747) and travelled widely, founding congregations such as Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. His emphasis on heartfelt "religion of the heart", ecumenical fellowship in small groups, and devotion to Christ's wounds influenced John Wesley and early Methodism, though the two later quarrelled over doctrine.

Legacy

Zinzendorf died at Herrnhut in 1760, leaving a church with missions on several continents and a model of communal, missionary Christianity that shaped evangelical Protestantism worldwide.
