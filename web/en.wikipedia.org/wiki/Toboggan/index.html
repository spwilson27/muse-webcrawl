Toboggan

A toboggan is a simple sled traditionally made of wood and used for carrying people or goods down snow-covered slopes. Its defining feature is a flat bottom of parallel boards with the front end curled upward, originally built without the metal runners used on other sleds.

History

The word comes from Canadian French tabaganne, itself borrowed from an Algonquian word such as Mi'kmaq topaĝan or Abenaki dabôgan, meaning a sled. Indigenous peoples of northern North America used toboggans of birch bark, moose hide, and wood to haul supplies over snow; the design was adopted by French-Canadian voyageurs and fur traders.

Design and use

A recreational toboggan is typically about 1.5 to 2 metres long, made of thin hardwood slats held together by crossbars, with the front rolled into a distinctive curl. Riders sit or kneel in a line, steering with their feet or by shifting weight. Tobogganing became a popular winter pastime in Canada and the northern United States in the 19th century, and purpose-built toboggan runs and chutes were constructed at resorts and city parks.

Racing

Competitive tobogganing survives notably at the U.S. National Toboggan Championships in Camden, Maine. Related but distinct Olympic sliding sports — bobsleigh, luge, and skeleton — use runners rather than the flat-bottomed toboggan design.
