Pergo

Pergo is a brand of laminate flooring and the name associated with the Swedish company that invented and popularised the product. The name derives from Perstorp, the Swedish chemicals company within which the flooring was developed, combined with a suffix suggesting forward motion. Pergo became so closely identified with laminate flooring that the name is sometimes used generically for the category.

History

Laminate flooring was developed in Sweden in the late 1970s by the Perstorp company, which had produced high-pressure decorative laminates since the 1920s. A team at Perstorp applied the laminate concept to flooring, and the Pergo product was launched around 1979–1980. The flooring consists of a clear wear layer over a photographic image of wood (or another surface), bonded to a high-density fibreboard core with a balancing backing layer.

Pergo flooring was introduced to the European market in the 1980s and to North America in the 1990s, where it helped create the laminate flooring category in home improvement retail. Glue-free click-lock installation systems later simplified fitting. Pergo's operations changed hands over the years: the brand and business were acquired by the Belgian group Unilin, part of Mohawk Industries, in the early 2010s.

Product

Modern Pergo ranges include laminate and rigid vinyl flooring in wood and stone effects, marketed on durability, scratch and water resistance, and ease of installation as a floating floor over underlay.
