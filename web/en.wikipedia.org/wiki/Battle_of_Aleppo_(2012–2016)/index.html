Battle of Aleppo (2012–2016)

The Battle of Aleppo was a major military confrontation in Aleppo, Syria's largest city and pre-war commercial capital, during the Syrian civil war. Fighting began on 19 July 2012 and lasted more than four years, making it one of the longest sieges and urban battles of the modern era. Syrian government forces, supported from 2015 by Russian air power and allied militias including Hezbollah, fought a shifting array of rebel groups — among them the Free Syrian Army, the al-Nusra Front and Ahrar al-Sham — while Kurdish forces held the Sheikh Maqsoud district. The battle ended in December 2016 with the evacuation of the remaining rebels and the government's recapture of the entire city.

Course of the battle

Rebel fighters entered Aleppo in July 2012 and seized much of the east of the city, while the government held the west. For years the front line through the Old City barely moved, while both sides shelled and bombed each other's districts; the medieval Citadel and the Great Mosque's minaret were among the landmarks destroyed. In 2013–2014 fighting also raged around Aleppo's airport and in the northern countryside. The Russian intervention from September 2015 shifted the balance: in February 2016 government forces cut the rebels' supply corridor toward Turkey, and in the summer and autumn of 2016 they encircled eastern Aleppo, subjecting its estimated 250,000–300,000 residents to siege and intensive bombardment. A final offensive in November–December 2016 collapsed the enclave; under a ceasefire deal, tens of thousands of fighters and civilians were evacuated by bus to Idlib province and the western countryside, and on 22 December 2016 the Syrian army declared full control of the city.

Human cost and legacy

Estimates of deaths in Aleppo during the war run to more than 30,000, and much of the eastern city was left in ruins; the Old City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, suffered extensive damage. The siege drew comparisons with Stalingrad, Sarajevo and Grozny, and its images — including that of the wounded five-year-old Omran Daqneesh in August 2016 — became emblematic of the Syrian war's toll on civilians. Reconstruction has proceeded slowly and unevenly since 2016.
