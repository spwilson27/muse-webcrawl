John Ashbery

John Lawrence Ashbery (28 July 1927 – 3 September 2017) was an American poet and art critic. He published more than twenty volumes of poetry and won nearly every major American award for poetry, including the Pulitzer Prize, the National Book Award, and the National Book Critics Circle Award, all in 1976 for Self-Portrait in a Convex Mirror.

Life

Ashbery was born in Rochester, New York, and grew up on a farm in Sodus. He attended Harvard College, where he was classmates with Frank O'Hara, Kenneth Koch, and Robert Bly. After graduating in 1949, he moved to New York, where he became associated with the New York School of poets.

Work

Ashbery's poetry is known for its elusive, shifting surfaces, mixing high and low diction, and its resistance to paraphrase. Early collections include Some Trees (1956), chosen by W. H. Auden for the Yale Series of Younger Poets, and The Tennis Court Oath (1962). He spent much of the 1960s in Paris as art critic for the New York Herald Tribune.

Later life

Ashbery taught at Brooklyn College and Bard College. He was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Letters and served as Poet Laureate of New York State. He died in Hudson, New York, in 2017.
