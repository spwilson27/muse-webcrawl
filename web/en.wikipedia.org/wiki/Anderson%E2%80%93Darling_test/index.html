Anderson–Darling test

Field: Statistics
Purpose: Goodness-of-fit test
Published by: T. W. Anderson and D. A. Darling (1952–1954)

The Anderson–Darling test is a statistical test of whether a given sample of data is drawn from a specified probability distribution. It is a modification of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test that gives more weight to the tails of the distribution, making it especially sensitive to departures from the hypothesised distribution in the extremes — a property that makes it one of the most powerful everyday tests of normality.

The statistic

The test compares the empirical cumulative distribution function of the ordered sample with the cumulative distribution function of the reference distribution, integrating the squared difference weighted by the inverse variance of the reference distribution. The resulting statistic, usually written A², is compared with critical values that depend on the distribution being tested and on whether its parameters were estimated from the data; adjusted statistics and tables for the normal, exponential, Weibull, logistic and other distributions were developed by Stephens and others.

Use

The Anderson–Darling test is widely used to check the normality assumption underlying parametric procedures, and to test fit to other continuous distributions in reliability engineering and hydrology. A two-sample (k-sample) version tests whether several samples come from a common distribution. Because it weights the tails heavily, it is generally preferred to the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test when tail behaviour matters most.
