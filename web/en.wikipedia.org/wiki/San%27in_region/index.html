The San'in region (山陰地方, San'in Chihō) is an area in the southwestern part of Honshu, the main island of Japan. It lies along the northern shore of the Chūgoku region, facing the Sea of Japan. The name means "shady side of the mountains," referring to its position on the northern, shaded side of the Chūgoku Mountains, in contrast to the sunnier San'yō region on the southern, Pacific-facing side.

Geography

The San'in region comprises Tottori and Shimane prefectures, together with the northern portions of Yamaguchi and Hyōgo prefectures. Its climate is marked by heavy winter snowfall, as moist air from the Sea of Japan rises over the mountains — the region lies within Japan's yukiguni ("snow country"). The coastline features the Tottori Sand Dunes, the largest sand dunes in Japan, and scenic rias and lagoons such as Lake Shinji and Lake Nakaumi.

History and culture

The ancient provinces of the region — Izumo, Iwami, Hōki, Inaba and Tajima among them — correspond roughly to the modern prefectures. Izumo was a major centre of early Japanese mythology; Izumo-taisha, one of Japan's oldest and most important Shinto shrines, stands there and is dedicated to Ōkuninushi, the deity of nation-building and matchmaking. Matsue retains a fine original castle, and the Iwami Ginzan silver mine in Shimane is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy and transport

San'in is among the least densely populated and least industrialised parts of Japan. Fishing — especially snow crab (matsuba-gani) — agriculture and tourism are important. The JR San'in Main Line runs along the coast, but the region has no Shinkansen service and limited expressway coverage, making it comparatively remote from Japan's major metropolitan centres.
