The Berlin U-Bahn is a rapid transit system in Berlin, Germany, and one of the main components of the city's public transport network, alongside the S-Bahn. Opened in 1902, it is one of the oldest underground railways in the world. Today the network has nine lines (U1 to U9), about 155 kilometres of track, and 175 stations, and is operated by the Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe (BVG).

History

The first line, largely elevated, opened in 1902 between Warschauer Brücke and Zoologischer Garten, built by Siemens & Halske. The network expanded rapidly before the First World War into two loading gauges: the small-profile (Kleinprofil) lines U1–U4 and the later large-profile (Großprofil) lines. After the Second World War the system was divided between East and West Berlin: two lines passed entirely through the East but were operated from the West, stopping at sealed "ghost stations" (Geisterbahnhöfe) in the eastern sector between 1961 and 1989. Following German reunification in 1990, the network was reintegrated and extended, most recently with the U5 extension to Berlin Hauptbahnhof in 2020.

Network

The lines are: U1 (Warschauer Straße – Uhlandstraße), U2 (Pankow – Ruhleben), U3 (Warschauer Straße – Krumme Lanke), U4 (Nollendorfplatz – Innsbrucker Platz), U5 (Hauptbahnhof – Hönow), U6 (Alt-Tegel – Alt-Mariendorf), U7 (Rathaus Spandau – Rudow), U8 (Wittenau – Hermannstraße), and U9 (Osloer Straße – Rathaus Steglitz). Trains are painted in the BVG's characteristic yellow; services run through the night at weekends.
