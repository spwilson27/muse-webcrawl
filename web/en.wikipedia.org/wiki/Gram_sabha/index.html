Gram sabha

A gram sabha meeting brings together all registered voters of a village panchayat area.

A gram sabha (Hindi: ग्राम सभा, "village assembly") is the general body of all persons registered on the electoral rolls of a village within the area of a Gram Panchayat in India. It is the foundational institution of the Panchayati Raj system of rural local self-government, functioning as a form of direct democracy at the village level: rather than being an elected body itself, the gram sabha comprises the entire electorate of the village, and the elected panchayat is accountable to it.

Constitutional basis

The gram sabha was given constitutional status by the 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992, which inserted Part IX into the Constitution of India. Article 243(b) defines the gram sabha, and Article 243A provides that it may exercise such powers and perform such functions at the village level as the legislature of a state may by law provide. State Panchayati Raj Acts therefore determine the detailed functions, meeting schedules and quorum — commonly requiring at least two to four meetings per year, with prescribed quorum (often one-tenth of members, with sub-quotas for women and Scheduled Caste/Scheduled Tribe members).

Functions

Typical functions assigned to gram sabhas include:

  • Approving the village development plan, annual budget and works proposed by the Gram Panchayat.
  • Selecting or approving lists of beneficiaries for welfare schemes such as housing (PMAY-G), pensions and subsidised food.
  • Conducting social audits of development works, notably of employment-guarantee works under MGNREGA, whose guidelines mandate gram sabha approval of the shelf of works and social audit of expenditure.
  • Reviewing the accounts and activities of the panchayat and questioning its elected members and functionaries.
  • Managing or consenting to the use of village common resources; in Scheduled Areas, the PESA Act, 1996 gives gram sabhas special rights, including consultation before land acquisition and ownership of minor forest produce.

Working and challenges

Meetings are convened and presided over by the sarpanch (panchayat president) or, in their absence, the deputy. Observers frequently note low attendance, domination by village elites or officials, and low participation of women as persistent challenges, alongside efforts — such as reserved meeting days, mahila (women's) sabhas, and ward sabhas for sub-village areas — to make the institution more inclusive. Supporters of the system regard the gram sabha as the closest Indian equivalent of a town meeting and the cornerstone of democratic decentralisation envisioned by Mahatma Gandhi's concept of Gram Swaraj (village self-rule).
