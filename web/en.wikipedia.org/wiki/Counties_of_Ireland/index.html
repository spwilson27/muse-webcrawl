Counties of Ireland

The counties of Ireland (Irish: contaetha na hÉireann) are historic administrative divisions of the island of Ireland. The system of 32 "traditional" counties was completed under English rule between the thirteenth and the early seventeenth centuries, generally on the basis of earlier Gaelic lordships and Anglo-Norman shires. Since the partition of Ireland in 1921, 26 counties lie in the Republic of Ireland and six in Northern Ireland, part of the United Kingdom. Although local government has been reorganised in both jurisdictions — the traditional counties no longer correspond exactly to administrative units — they remain deeply embedded in Irish identity, sport (notably the GAA county teams), postal addresses, tourism and cultural life.

History

The shiring of Ireland began after the Anglo-Norman invasion of 1169: Dublin, Meath and other early counties were created in the thirteenth century, and the process advanced with English control. Counties such as Wicklow (1606) were formed during the Tudor conquest, and County Londonderry was created in 1613 from Coleraine and part of Tyrone. The county boundaries fixed by the early 1600s remained essentially unchanged for nearly three centuries.

The 32 counties

Grouped by the four historic provinces:

  • Connacht: Galway, Leitrim, Mayo, Roscommon, Sligo.
  • Leinster: Carlow, Dublin, Kildare, Kilkenny, Laois, Longford, Louth, Meath, Offaly, Westmeath, Wexford, Wicklow.
  • Munster: Clare, Cork, Kerry, Limerick, Tipperary, Waterford.
  • Ulster: Antrim, Armagh, Cavan, Derry/Londonderry, Donegal, Down, Fermanagh, Monaghan, Tyrone.

Modern administration

In the Republic of Ireland, the Local Government Reform Act 2014 reduced the county-level authorities: Tipperary's two ridings were reunited, and the cities of Limerick and Waterford were merged with their counties, leaving 31 local authorities, three of them (Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown, Fingal and South Dublin) subdividing historic County Dublin. In Northern Ireland, the 26 district councils created in 1973 abolished the six county councils, and an 11-council system has operated since 2014; the traditional counties are retained for purposes such as lieutenancy. Two counties — Derry/Londonderry — are subject to a well-known naming dispute.

Identity

County allegiance is expressed most visibly in Gaelic games, where the All-Ireland championships are contested by county teams, and in county colours, songs and rivalries. The largest county by area is Cork and the smallest is Louth; by population, Dublin is first and Leitrim last.
