Kalaupapa is a small unincorporated community on the island of Molokai in Hawaii, United States. It occupies a flat peninsula at the base of some of the tallest sea cliffs in the world, cut off from the rest of Molokai by steep terrain. The village lies within Kalawao County, the smallest county in the United States, which is administered by the Hawaii State Department of Health.

History

From 1866, under a law signed by King Kamehameha V, Native Hawaiians and others afflicted with leprosy (Hansen's disease) were forcibly exiled to the Kalaupapa peninsula. Over the following century more than 8,000 people were sent there. The settlement is associated with the work of Saint Damien (Father Damien de Veuster), who arrived in 1873 and cared for the patients until his own death from the disease in 1889, and with Saint Marianne Cope and Brother Joseph Dutton.

Present day

The isolation policy ended in 1969, but a small number of former patients chose to remain in Kalaupapa for life. The Kalaupapa National Historical Park, established in 1980, preserves the settlement and its story. Access is restricted: visitors must obtain a permit and reach the peninsula by trail, mule ride, or small aircraft.
