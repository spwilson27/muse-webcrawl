Reserve team

In sports, a reserve team is a team composed of players under contract to a specific team but who do not normally play in matches for the primary team. Reserve teams often include back-up players, those recovering from injury, younger players who need more experience and game time, and players being prepared for promotion to the first team.

Association football

In association football, reserve teams may compete in dedicated reserve leagues, in under-23 or under-21 development competitions, or — in several European countries — directly within the senior league pyramid. In Spain, reserve teams such as Real Madrid Castilla and FC Barcelona Atlètic play in the professional divisions but cannot be promoted to the same division as their parent club. In Germany, reserve ("II") teams historically played as high as the third tier. In England, the old Central League and the Football Combination gave way to the Professional Development League and Premier League 2 for under-21 sides.

Other sports

Similar structures exist in other sports: North American ice hockey and baseball rely on affiliated farm teams in minor leagues rather than club-run reserves; rugby and Australian rules football clubs often field reserve-grade sides; and in motorsport, reserve drivers support the race drivers of a Formula One team.
