Cedar Revolution

The Cedar Revolution was a chain of demonstrations in Lebanon, mainly in Beirut, triggered by the assassination of former prime minister Rafic Hariri on 14 February 2005. The movement demanded the withdrawal of Syrian troops from Lebanon, which Syria had occupied since 1976, and the establishment of an international tribunal to investigate the assassination.

Events

Mass protests filled Martyrs' Square in Beirut; a rally on 14 March 2005 drew an estimated one million people. Counter-demonstrations were organised by pro-Syrian parties including Hezbollah. Under domestic and international pressure — including UN Security Council Resolution 1559 — Syria completed the withdrawal of its forces on 26–27 April 2005, ending nearly three decades of military presence.

Aftermath

The movement took its name from the cedar, Lebanon's national symbol. It led to the formation of the anti-Syrian March 14 Alliance and the pro-Syrian March 8 Alliance, alignments that shaped Lebanese politics for years afterward. The Special Tribunal for Lebanon was later established to prosecute those responsible for Hariri's killing.
