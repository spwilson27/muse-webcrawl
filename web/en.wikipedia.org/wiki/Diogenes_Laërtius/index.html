Diogenes Laërtius

Diogenes Laërtius (fl. 3rd century AD) was a biographer of the Greek philosophers. Nothing is definitively known about his life; his surname may derive from the town of Laerte in Cilicia or the Roman family name Laërtius. He is the author of Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers.

Lives of Eminent Philosophers

The work, in ten books, gives biographies and doxographies of some 80 philosophers from Thales to Epicurus, organised by Ionian and Italian schools. Despite its digressions and uneven reliability, it is an indispensable source, preserving otherwise lost material — including three letters and the will of Epicurus, and the sayings of Diogenes the Cynic, with whom he is often confused.

Reception

The Lives were translated into Latin in the Renaissance and shaped modern knowledge of ancient philosophy; Montaigne wished there had been a dozen Laërtiuses.
