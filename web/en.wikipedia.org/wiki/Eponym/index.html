Eponym

DefinitionA person, place, or thing after which something is named
Related termsToponym, namesake

An eponym is a person, a place, or a thing after which a particular person, place, or thing is named, or believed to be named. The adjective terms derived from eponym include eponymous and eponymic.

Examples

Famous eponyms include the sandwich, named after John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich; Alzheimer's disease, named after Alois Alzheimer; and the city of Alexandria, named after Alexander the Great. In the ancient world, Athens was said to be named for the goddess Athena, and Romulus was the legendary eponym of Rome.

Usage

Eponyms are common in medicine, science, geography, and commerce. Stigler's law of eponymy observes, ironically, that no scientific discovery is named after its original discoverer.
