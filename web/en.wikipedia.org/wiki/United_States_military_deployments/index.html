United States military deployments

United States military deployments refers to the stationing of United States Armed Forces personnel and assets around the world. The United States maintains the largest network of overseas military bases of any country, with personnel deployed in more than 80 countries and territories.

Overview

As of the early 2020s, roughly 170,000–200,000 active-duty US service members are stationed outside the United States at any given time, excluding those in active combat zones. The largest concentrations of personnel are in Japan, Germany, and South Korea, reflecting treaty commitments dating to the Second World War and the Korean War.

History

Large-scale overseas deployments became a permanent feature of American policy after 1945, when the United States retained bases won or established during the war and added new ones under Cold War containment strategy. Deployments peaked during the Korean and Vietnam Wars and again during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars of the 2000s–2010s.

Regional distribution

  • East Asia and Pacific: Japan (the largest single-country presence), South Korea, Guam, Australia.
  • Europe: Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, Spain, and rotational forces in Eastern Europe.
  • Middle East: Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, and naval forces of the Fifth Fleet.
  • Africa: Djibouti (Camp Lemonnier) and smaller cooperative security locations.
