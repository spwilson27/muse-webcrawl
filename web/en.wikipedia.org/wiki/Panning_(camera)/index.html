Panning (camera)

Panning is a photographic technique in which the camera is moved horizontally (or vertically) to follow a moving subject while the shutter is open. When performed correctly, the subject remains relatively sharp while the background becomes streaked with motion blur, conveying a strong sense of speed and movement.

Panning is closely related to motion blur techniques and is widely used in sports photography, motorsport, wildlife photography, and photojournalism.

Technique

To pan, the photographer selects a relatively slow shutter speed — often between 1/15 and 1/125 of a second depending on the subject's speed — and tracks the subject smoothly through the viewfinder, pressing the shutter while continuing the movement in a follow-through motion. The camera's movement should match the subject's angular speed as closely as possible; any mismatch produces blur in the subject itself.

Key elements of successful panning include:

  • A stable stance and smooth rotation from the hips rather than the arms alone.
  • Pre-focusing or using continuous autofocus on the subject's path.
  • Choosing a shutter speed slow enough to blur the background but fast enough to keep the subject acceptably sharp.
  • Continuing the pan after the shutter closes, much like a golfer's follow-through.

Applications

Panning is a staple of motorsport photography, where it keeps racing cars or motorcycles sharp against blurred grandstands and barriers. It is also used for cyclists, runners, aircraft in flight, and birds. In cinematography, panning shots serve a different but related purpose: the camera rotates on a fixed axis to follow action or reveal a scene.
