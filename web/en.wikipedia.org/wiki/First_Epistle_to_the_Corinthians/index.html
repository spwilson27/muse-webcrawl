First Epistle to the Corinthians

The First Epistle to the Corinthians (Ancient Greek: Α΄ Ἐπιστολὴ πρὸς Κορινθίους), often called First Corinthians and abbreviated 1 Cor., is a Pauline epistle of the New Testament of the Christian Bible. The epistle is attributed to Paul the Apostle and a co-author, Sosthenes, and is addressed to the Christian church in Corinth, Greece.

Background

Paul founded the church in Corinth during his second missionary journey (c. 50–52 AD). Writing from Ephesus around 53–55 AD, he responded to reports of problems in the congregation and to questions the Corinthians had sent him. The letter reflects a community struggling with factionalism, sexual ethics, lawsuits among believers, and disputes about food offered to idols, worship practices, and spiritual gifts.

Contents

The letter contains several famous passages: the teaching on the resurrection of the dead (chapter 15), the institution narrative of the Lord's Supper (chapter 11), the discussion of speaking in tongues and prophecy (chapter 14), and the "hymn to love" in chapter 13 — "Love is patient, love is kind..." — frequently read at weddings. Paul insists on the unity of the church as the body of Christ and closes with greetings and a final exhortation.
