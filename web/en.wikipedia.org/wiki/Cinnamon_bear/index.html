Cinnamon bear

A cinnamon bear is a colour phase of the American black bear (Ursus americanus), formally the subspecies Ursus americanus cinnamomum. Its coat ranges from light reddish-brown — resembling cinnamon — to dark chocolate, with lighter shades most common in the dry mountain regions of the western United States and Canada.

Distribution and biology

Cinnamon-coloured bears occur mainly in the Rocky Mountains, the Great Basin, and parts of the Pacific Northwest and southwestern Canada; black-phase animals predominate in the humid east. The colour difference is thought to help mimic grizzly bears or suit lighter, more open habitats. Like other black bears, cinnamon bears are omnivores, eating grasses, berries, nuts, insects, and carrion.

Confusion with grizzlies

Light-coloured cinnamon bears are sometimes mistaken for grizzly bears, but they lack the grizzly's shoulder hump and dished facial profile and have taller, more pointed ears and smaller claws.
