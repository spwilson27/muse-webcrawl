Postal address

An address is a collection of information, presented in a mostly fixed format, used to give the location of a building, apartment, or other structure, or a plot of land, generally using political boundaries and street names as references, along with other identifiers such as house or apartment numbers and organization names. A postal address is the form of an address used for delivering mail, usually including a postal code (such as a ZIP Code in the United States or a postcode in the United Kingdom).

Formats by country

Address formats differ substantially between countries. Many countries order addresses from the most specific element (the recipient) to the most general (the country), while others, such as Japan and Hungary, traditionally place the largest unit first. Common elements include the addressee's name, house number and street name, district or neighbourhood, town or city, state or prefecture, postal code, and country name for international mail. The Universal Postal Union publishes guidelines for international addressing.

Standards and data

Standardised address formats are important for mail automation, geocoding, and emergency services. Postal authorities maintain official address databases, and standards such as the UPU's S42 define address components for data processing.
