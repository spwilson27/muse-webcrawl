Wind turbine

Wind turbine
FunctionConverts wind energy to electricity
Typical formThree-bladed horizontal-axis rotor on a tower
Largest unitsOffshore turbines exceeding 15 MW
[Image: A row of white three-bladed wind turbines turning on a coastal headland]

A wind turbine is a device that converts the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy. Most modern turbines are horizontal-axis machines with three slender blades on a tall tower, which yaw to face the wind; a generator in the nacelle produces electricity stepped up for the grid.

Development

Windmills ground grain and pumped water for centuries — the simple machines of their gearing long predating electricity — but wind power for electricity dates from the late 19th century. Modern utility turbines grew rapidly from the 1970s energy crises, and offshore wind farms now use the largest rotating machines ever built, each rated above 15 megawatts. Wind is among the cheapest sources of new electricity and a pillar of sustainability strategies.
