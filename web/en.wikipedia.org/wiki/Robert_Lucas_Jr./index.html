Robert Emerson Lucas Jr. (September 15, 1937 – May 15, 2023) was an American economist at the University of Chicago, known for his work on the theory of rational expectations, which he developed and applied to macroeconomics. He was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1995 "for having developed and applied the hypothesis of rational expectations, and thereby having transformed macroeconomic analysis and deepened our understanding of economic policy".

Contributions

Lucas's 1972 paper "Expectations and the Neutrality of Money" introduced the Lucas islands model, showing how monetary policy could have real effects only when it surprised agents who could not distinguish local from aggregate price changes. His 1976 paper "Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique" — the Lucas critique — argued that the parameters of econometric models are not invariant to policy, undermining traditional Keynesian policy evaluation and reshaping macroeconomic methodology.

With Leonard Rapping, Lucas modelled labour supply over the business cycle; his presidential address on economic development highlighted human capital accumulation (the Uzawa–Lucas model) and asked why capital does not flow from rich to poor countries (the Lucas paradox). His work was central to the new classical macroeconomics of the 1970s and to subsequent real business cycle and dynamic general equilibrium modelling.

Career and life

Lucas earned his BA in history (1959) and PhD in economics (1964) from the University of Chicago, taught at Carnegie Mellon (1963–74), and returned to Chicago in 1975, where he remained, becoming John Dewey Distinguished Service Professor. He served as president of the Econometric Society and the American Economic Association. He died in Chicago in 2023.
