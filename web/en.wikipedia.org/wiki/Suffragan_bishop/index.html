Suffragan bishop

A suffragan bishop is a bishop who assists and is subordinate to a diocesan bishop, or, in another usage, a diocesan bishop who is subordinate to a metropolitan archbishop. The word relates to the bishops' right of suffrage — their vote in electing and supporting the metropolitan in the councils of an ecclesiastical province.

In the Roman Catholic Church

In Catholic usage, a suffragan diocese is a diocese in an ecclesiastical province other than the metropolitan archdiocese itself, and its bishop is a suffragan of the metropolitan archbishop. Separately, bishops who assist a diocesan bishop within his own diocese are called auxiliary bishops; they hold titular sees and exercise ministry delegated by the diocesan bishop.

In the Church of England

In the Church of England, suffragan bishops are full-time assistant bishops appointed to help the diocesan bishop, usually taking their titles from towns within the diocese (for example, the Bishop of Stepney in the Diocese of London). Their office was formalised by the Suffragan Bishops Act 1534 under Henry VIII, which named 26 towns whose titles could be used, and by the Suffragans Nomination Act 1888. Under "area schemes", many suffragans exercise delegated oversight of a defined episcopal area. Since 2014 the Church has also appointed provincial episcopal visitors — sometimes called "flying bishops" — as suffragans serving parishes across provinces.

Other traditions

The Episcopal Church in the United States uses "bishop suffragan" for an assisting bishop without right of succession, in contrast to a bishop coadjutor, who succeeds automatically. Similar assisting roles exist in other Anglican provinces and in some Lutheran and Methodist churches.
