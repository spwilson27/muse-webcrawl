Omniscience

Omniscience is the capacity to know everything. In Abrahamic religions, this is a property often attributed to God. Along with omnipotence and omnibenevolence, omniscience is one of the classical divine attributes discussed in theology and the philosophy of religion.

Definitions

Omniscience is commonly defined as knowledge of all true propositions: a being is omniscient if, for every proposition, if it is true, the being knows it. Philosophers distinguish this from weaker notions such as knowing everything knowable, and debate whether future contingent events — including the free choices of creatures — can be known in advance.

Omniscience and free will

A long-standing philosophical problem asks whether divine omniscience is compatible with human free will. If God infallibly knows what a person will do, critics argue, the person cannot do otherwise. Responses range from Boethius's view that God exists outside time and "sees" all events eternally, to open theism, which holds that the future is partly open and not a settled object of knowledge.

In other traditions

Concepts of complete knowledge appear in other religions as well: in Jainism, kevala jnana is the absolute omniscience attained by liberated souls; in Buddhism, the Buddha is described as possessing profound knowledge, though the scope of "omniscience" is debated. In literature, an "omniscient narrator" is a storytelling voice that knows the thoughts and events beyond any single character's perspective.
