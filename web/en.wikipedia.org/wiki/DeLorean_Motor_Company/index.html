The DeLorean Motor Company (DMC) was an American automobile manufacturer formed by automobile industry executive John DeLorean in 1975. It is remembered for the single model it produced—the stainless steel DeLorean DMC-12 sports car featuring gull-wing doors—and for the brief, troubled life of the company, which went into bankruptcy in 1982.

History

John DeLorean, formerly a vice president at General Motors, founded the company with the ambition of building an "ethical" sports car emphasizing safety, longevity, and fuel efficiency. After considering sites in Puerto Rico and the Republic of Ireland, DeLorean accepted a package of incentives from the British government to build the factory in Dunmurry, a suburb of Belfast, Northern Ireland, where unemployment was high.

Construction of the factory began in 1978, and production of the DMC-12 started in January 1981. Early cars suffered from poor build quality, which required expensive rectification work at quality assurance centers in the United States. Sales were hurt by the car's high price, modest performance, a recession in the US market, and the lack of an established dealer support network.

Collapse

By 1982 the company was in severe financial difficulty. The British government refused further funding, and DMC went into receivership in October 1982. On October 19, 1982, John DeLorean was arrested in Los Angeles on drug trafficking charges in an FBI sting operation; he was later acquitted, arguing entrapment, but the company could not be saved. Roughly 9,000 cars had been built when production ended in early 1983.

Later use of the name

In 1995, Liverpool-born mechanic Stephen Wynne founded a new company in Humble, Texas, also named the DeLorean Motor Company, which acquired the leftover parts inventory and the rights to the stylized "DMC" logo trademark. The Texas company is not related to the original, but supplies parts and service to the existing fleet of cars and has assembled a small number of cars from new and original parts.
