Mithras

Mithras is the god central to the Mithraic mysteries, a mystery religion practised in the Roman Empire from about the 1st to the 4th century AD. He is almost always depicted in the tauroctony scene, slaying a bull within a cave, wearing a Phrygian cap.

Origins

The name derives from the Iranian god Mithra (Mitra), associated with covenant and light, though the Roman cult's relationship to Iranian worship is debated. Roman Mithraism was especially popular among soldiers, and its temples, called mithraea, were built underground or cave-like.

Iconography

Besides the bull-slaying, recurring figures include the torchbearers Cautes and Cautopates, Sol and Luna, a dog, snake, scorpion, and raven around the scene, linked by scholars to constellations.
