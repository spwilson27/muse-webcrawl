Practical effect

A practical effect is a special effect produced physically, without computer-generated imagery or other post-production techniques. Such effects are created on set during filming and include mechanical props, animatronics, prosthetic makeup, pyrotechnics, rain and wind machines, miniatures, matte paintings, and in-camera tricks such as forced perspective.

Techniques

Practical effects span many crafts: creature shops build animatronic puppets and suits; makeup departments create prosthetic appliances; model units photograph miniatures at high frame rates so that they appear full-scale; and pyrotechnicians stage explosions and bullet hits using squibs and air mortars. Because the effect exists in the real world in front of the camera, it interacts naturally with lighting, actors and the set, which many filmmakers consider more convincing than digital substitutes.

History

Practical effects were the only option for most of film history, from the trick photography of Georges Méliès through the model work of 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) and Star Wars (1977) and the animatronics of Jurassic Park (1993). Although CGI became dominant in the 1990s and 2000s, practical effects remain widely used, often combined with digital work, and have enjoyed a deliberate revival in productions such as Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) and the later Star Wars films.
