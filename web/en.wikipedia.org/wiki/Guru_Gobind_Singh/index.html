Guru Gobind Singh (22 December 1666 – 7 October 1708), born Gobind Rai, was the tenth and last human Sikh Guru. A warrior, poet and philosopher, he succeeded his father Guru Tegh Bahadur at the age of nine after the latter was executed by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb. He founded the Khalsa, the community of initiated Sikhs, in 1699, introduced the Five Ks (articles of faith worn by Khalsa Sikhs), and before his death declared the Sikh scripture, the Guru Granth Sahib, to be the eternal Guru of the Sikhs.

Early life

Gobind Singh was born in Patna, in present-day Bihar, the only son of Guru Tegh Bahadur and Mata Gujri. His family returned to Punjab and settled at Anandpur, in the Shivalik foothills. After his father's martyrdom in Delhi in 1675, the young Gobind Rai was installed as Guru. He was educated in Persian, Sanskrit, Punjabi and martial arts, and became an accomplished poet and archer.

Founding of the Khalsa

On Vaisakhi, 30 March 1699, at Anandpur Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh called a great assembly and, according to Sikh tradition, demanded a head from the congregation five times. Five volunteers — the Panj Pyare, the Five Beloved Ones — were initiated with amrit (sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword) into the new order of the Khalsa, taking the name Singh ("lion") for men and Kaur ("princess") for women. The Khalsa adopted the Five Ks: kesh (uncut hair), kangha (wooden comb), kara (steel bracelet), kachera (cotton undergarment) and kirpan (ceremonial sword).

Wars and later life

The Guru fought a series of battles against the hill rajas and Mughal forces, including the battles of Anandpur and Chamkaur (1704), where his two elder sons fell; his two younger sons were executed at Sirhind. In 1705 he wrote the Zafarnama, a letter of moral reproach, to Aurangzeb. After Aurangzeb's death he briefly cooperated with his successor Bahadur Shah, but was assassinated at Nanded in 1708 by a Pathan sent, tradition holds, by the governor of Sirhind. Before dying he conferred Guruship on the Guru Granth Sahib.

Writings

His compositions, including the Jaap Sahib, Akal Ustat and Bachittar Natak, are collected in the Dasam Granth. He is also credited with finalising the text of the Guru Granth Sahib by adding the hymns of his father.
