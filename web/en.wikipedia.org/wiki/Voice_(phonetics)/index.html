Voice (phonetics)

In phonetics, voice or voicing is the vibration of the vocal folds during the articulation of a sound. Sounds produced with such vibration are voiced (e.g. [z], [d], [v] and all vowels); sounds produced without it are voiceless (e.g. [s], [t], [f]).

Mechanism

Voicing results when air from the lungs passes through the nearly closed glottis, setting the vocal folds into periodic vibration. Voice onset time — the timing of voicing relative to the release of a consonant — is a key cue distinguishing stop consonants such as /b/ and /p/ in many languages.

Phonology

Voicing is a distinctive feature in many languages: English contrasts voiced and voiceless obstruent pairs (pat/bat, sip/zip). Some languages, such as French, have fully voiced stops, while others show only partial voicing; final devoicing neutralises the contrast in languages like German and Russian.
