Tree of life (Book of Mormon)

The tree of life is a central symbol in the Book of Mormon, appearing in a vision recounted in the First Book of Nephi. The prophet Lehi dreams of a tree bearing white fruit "desirable to make one happy," and his son Nephi later seeks and receives an interpretation of the vision.

The vision

In 1 Nephi 8, Lehi describes a dark and dreary wilderness, a tree whose fruit was white and sweet above all, a rod of iron running beside a river of water, and a strait and narrow path leading to the tree. Multitudes press forward along the path, but many are lost in a mist of darkness or are drawn away to a "great and spacious building" filled with people mocking those who partake of the fruit. Some of Lehi's family — Sariah, Sam and Nephi — come and eat; Laman and Lemuel refuse.

Interpretation

In 1 Nephi 11–15, Nephi is shown that the tree represents the love of God, and its fruit is associated with the condescension of God and the life of Jesus Christ. The rod of iron is interpreted as the word of God, the river as filthiness (hell), and the great and spacious building as the pride of the world. The vision is widely taught in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints as an allegory of discipleship and enduring to the end.

Cultural significance

The tree of life vision is one of the most frequently illustrated scenes in Latter-day Saint art, and scholars have compared it both with the tree of life in Genesis and Revelation and with purported ancient Near Eastern and Mesoamerican symbols.
