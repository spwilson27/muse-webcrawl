Triptan

Triptans are a family of tryptamine-based drugs used as abortive medication in the treatment of migraine and cluster headaches. The first clinically available triptan, sumatriptan, was introduced in 1991 by Glaxo.

Mechanism

Triptans are selective agonists of serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. Their effects include constriction of dilated cranial blood vessels, inhibition of trigeminal nerve activation, and suppression of the release of vasoactive neuropeptides such as CGRP.

Members

The class includes sumatriptan, rizatriptan, zolmitriptan, eletriptan, naratriptan, almotriptan, and frovatriptan, differing mainly in speed of onset, half-life, and recurrence rates. They are available as tablets, nasal sprays, and injections.

Safety

Because of their vasoconstrictive action, triptans are contraindicated in patients with coronary artery disease and uncontrolled hypertension. Combined use with certain other serotonergic drugs carries a small risk of serotonin syndrome. Common side effects include tingling, flushing, and a sensation of chest tightness.
