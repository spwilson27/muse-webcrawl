Córdoba is the capital city of the province of Córdoba, Argentina. Located in central Argentina, in the foothills of the Sierras Chicas on the Suquía River, it is the second most populous city in Argentina after Buenos Aires, with roughly 1.5 million inhabitants in the city proper. Córdoba is a major cultural, educational, industrial and tourist centre.

History

The city was founded on 6 July 1573 by Jerónimo Luis de Cabrera, who named it after Córdoba, Spain. In 1613 the Jesuits founded the National University of Córdoba, one of the oldest universities in the Americas, which earned the city its nickname La Docta. The Jesuit Block and Estancias of Córdoba were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000. Córdoba played a key role in Argentine history: it was a centre of the 1918 university reform movement that reshaped higher education across Latin America, and of the Cordobazo, a major popular uprising in 1969 against the military dictatorship.

Geography and climate

Córdoba lies about 700 km (435 mi) northwest of Buenos Aires. It has a humid subtropical climate bordering on a temperate highland climate, with hot summers, mild winters and most rainfall concentrated in the summer months.

Economy and culture

The metropolitan area hosts automotive, aeronautical and technology industries, as well as a growing software sector. The city is known for its colonial architecture in the historic centre, its many churches — which gave Córdoba its other nickname, "the city of bells" — and for the cuarteto music genre. Talleres, Belgrano and Instituto are its principal football clubs.
