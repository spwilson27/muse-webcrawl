Vltava

Length: 430 km (267 mi)
Source: Šumava (Bohemian Forest)
Mouth: Elbe at Mělník
Basin: Czech Republic
German name: Moldau

The Vltava (German: Moldau) is the longest river in the Czech Republic, running 430 kilometres from the Šumava mountains in the south, through Český Krumlov, České Budějovice and Prague, to join the Elbe at Mělník. It is the country's national river, celebrated in music and literature, and a central feature of the Bohemian landscape.

Course

The Vltava is formed by the confluence of the Warm Vltava (Teplá Vltava) and Cold Vltava (Studená Vltava) in the Šumava near the German border. It flows southeast past the Lipno reservoir, then turns north at Vyšší Brod, winding through the deep meanders of Český Krumlov and on to České Budějovice. From there it runs north through a series of reservoirs — the "Vltava Cascade" including Orlík and Slapy — built for hydroelectric power and flood control, before entering Prague, where seventeen bridges cross it, including the Charles Bridge. North of the capital at Mělník it joins the Elbe, despite being the longer and fuller river at the confluence.

History and culture

The river was a historic transport artery for timber rafting and for salt and goods between Bohemia and the Danube region. Its valley is lined with castles and chateaux. In Prague the river has flooded catastrophically several times, most recently in 2002, when waters inundated parts of the Old Town and the metro.

In art, the Vltava is immortalised by Bedřich Smetana's symphonic poem Vltava (1874), the second movement of Má vlast, which traces the river from its two springs through forests, a peasant wedding, moonlight and the St. John's Rapids to Prague and beyond. The melody's resemblance to the folk song "La Mantovana" has often been noted.

Use and navigation

Dams on the middle course generate power and provide drinking water and recreation; the section through Prague hosts rowing, sightseeing cruises and the annual Navalis festivities. Locks maintain a navigable channel from Mělník to Prague and upstream toward České Budějovice.
