Pons Aelius

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pons Aelius (Latin for "Aelian Bridge"), also known as Newcastle Roman Fort, was a small fort and civil settlement in Roman Britain on the site of what is now Newcastle upon Tyne, England. The name derives from the Roman bridge over the River Tyne at the site; Aelius was the family name of the emperor Hadrian, by tradition credited with building the bridge around AD 122, when Hadrian's Wall was begun.

History

The fort stood at the northern end of the bridge, on a hill overlooking the river where the later Norman castle — the "new castle" that gave the city its name — was built in 1080. It guarded the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall, whose curtain began (or ended) at the fort before later extension to Wallsend (Segedunum). The fort is estimated to have covered a modest area on Castle Garth and housed a garrison of perhaps 480 men, including a detachment associated in inscriptions with the Legio VI Victrix. A civilian settlement, or vicus, grew up around the bridgehead along the road south toward Chester-le-Street (Concangis).

Little of the fort survives visibly today: the site is buried beneath the medieval castle and the later city centre, and much of what is known comes from antiquarian reports and rescue excavations. Finds and inscriptions from Pons Aelius are held by the Great North Museum in Newcastle.
