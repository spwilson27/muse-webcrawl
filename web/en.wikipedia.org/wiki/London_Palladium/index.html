The London Palladium is a Grade II* listed West End theatre in Soho, London, located on Argyll Street. With 2,286 seats it is one of the largest theatres in London and perhaps the most famous variety venue in Britain, long regarded as the pinnacle of a variety performer's career.

The Palladium's famous façade on Argyll Street.

History

The theatre opened on Boxing Day 1910, built for Walter Gibbons on the site of Hengler's Circus and an ice rink, to designs by Frank Matcham. It became famous for variety and pantomime. From 1955 to 1967 ITV's Sunday Night at the London Palladium, hosted for much of its run by Bruce Forsyth, made it a national institution; it has also hosted the Royal Variety Performance more often than any other venue, and legendary bills from Judy Garland and Frank Sinatra to the Beatles' 1963 appearance that helped spark Beatlemania.

Musicals and recent years

The Palladium has housed major musicals including The King and I (with Yul Brynner), Andrew Lloyd Webber's Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat, Chitty Chitty Bang Bang (whose run featured a record-breakingly expensive stage car), The Wizard of Oz and the annual Christmas pantomime revived in 2016. Since 2000 it has been owned by Andrew Lloyd Webber's LW Theatres group.
