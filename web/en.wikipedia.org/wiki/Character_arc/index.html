Character arc

A character arc is the transformation or inner journey of a character over the course of a story. As events unfold, the character's traits, beliefs or worldview change — often progressively, from a state of ignorance, weakness or falsehood toward knowledge, strength or truth. If a character's arc traces a fall from grace or a worsening of character, it is sometimes called a negative character arc.

Character arc
DomainNarrative, fiction writing, screenwriting
TypesPositive (change), negative (fall), flat
Related conceptsHero's journey, bildungsroman, character development

Types

Positive change arc

The most common arc: the protagonist begins with a flawed belief — the "lie" they believe, in K.M. Weiland's terminology — and the plot forces them to confront it and adopt a healthier truth, emerging changed for the better. Ebenezer Scrooge in A Christmas Carol is a classic example.

Negative arc

The character moves from a better state to a worse one: corruption, disillusionment or destruction. Michael Corleone's descent in The Godfather and Walter White's transformation in Breaking Bad are frequently cited examples.

Flat arc

The protagonist already embodies the story's truth and does not change; instead, their steadfastness changes the world around them. Many adventure heroes, such as Sherlock Holmes or Captain America in some portrayals, follow flat arcs.

Relationship to plot

Character arcs are closely tied to plot structure: in the classical three-act structure, external challenges escalate in step with the protagonist's internal change, culminating in a climax where the character's transformation is tested. The hero's journey described by Joseph Campbell provides a mythic template in which departure, initiation and return map onto inner change.
