Baaba Maal (born 12 November 1953) is a Senegalese singer and guitarist born in Podor, on the Senegal River. He is one of the best-known musicians of the Pulaar-speaking peoples of northern Senegal and has been a prominent ambassador for West African music worldwide. Singing primarily in Pulaar, he blends traditional Fula music with reggae, salsa, and modern pop influences.

Early life

Maal was born in Podor, a town on the banks of the Senegal River. Although his family were fishermen rather than members of the griot caste traditionally associated with music, he learned to play the hoddu (a traditional lute) and guitar. He studied in Dakar and later in Paris, where he formed the band Daande Lenol ("The Voice of the People") with his friend Mansour Seck, a blind guitarist.

Career

Maal rose to international prominence in the late 1980s and 1990s with albums such as Baayo (1991), Lam Toro (1992), and Firin' in Fouta (1994), the latter nominated for a Grammy Award. His album Nomad Soul (1998) continued his fusion of traditional and contemporary sounds. He collaborated with artists including Peter Gabriel and Brian Eno, and contributed to the soundtrack of the film Black Panther (2018).

Activism

Beyond music, Maal is a development activist. He founded the Nann-K Trust and has worked with organisations such as Oxfam and the United Nations Development Programme on issues including education, women's rights, and climate change in the Sahel region.
