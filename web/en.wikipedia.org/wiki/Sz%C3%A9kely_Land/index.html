Székely Land (Hungarian: Székelyföld; Romanian: Ținutul Secuiesc) is a historic and ethnographic region in eastern Transylvania, in present-day Romania. It is the traditional homeland of the Székelys, a Hungarian-speaking ethnic group, and corresponds roughly to the present-day counties of Harghita, Covasna, and parts of Mureș.

History

During the Kingdom of Hungary, the Székely Land was organised into autonomous "seats" (Hungarian: székek), self-governing districts whose inhabitants held collective privileges in exchange for military service guarding the eastern frontier of Transylvania. After the Treaty of Trianon in 1920, the region became part of Romania. Between 1952 and 1968, a Magyar Autonomous Region existed in the area under Romania's communist government.

Autonomy movement

Since the 1990s, Székely political organisations, including the Székely National Council, have campaigned for territorial autonomy within Romania, a demand Bucharest has consistently rejected. The region is known for its distinctive wooden Székely gates, fortified churches, and Hungarian-language cultural life.
