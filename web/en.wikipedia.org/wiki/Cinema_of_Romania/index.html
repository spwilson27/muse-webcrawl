Cinema of Romania

The cinema of Romania is the art of motion-picture making within the nation of Romania or by Romanian filmmakers abroad. Film production in Romania dates to the late 1890s, and the industry is best known internationally for the Romanian New Wave of the 2000s, a movement of realist, minimalist films that won many major international awards.

Early cinema and the communist era

The first film screenings in Romania took place in 1896 in Bucharest. The first Romanian feature film, The War of Independence (1912), dramatized the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78. After the Second World War, the film industry was nationalized under the communist regime, and production centred on the Buftea studios near Bucharest. Directors such as Liviu Ciulei, whose The Forest of the Hanged (1965) won the Best Director prize at Cannes, achieved recognition despite censorship.

Romanian New Wave

After a period of decline in the 1990s, Romanian cinema revived spectacularly in the 2000s. Cristi Puiu's The Death of Mr. Lazarescu (2005) won the Un Certain Regard prize at Cannes, and Cristian Mungiu's 4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days (2007), about an illegal abortion in communist Romania, won the Palme d'Or. Other notable directors of the movement include Corneliu Porumboiu (12:08 East of Bucharest, 2006; Police, Adjective, 2009), Radu Muntean, Cătălin Mitulescu, and Radu Jude, whose Bad Luck Banging or Loony Porn won the Golden Bear at Berlin in 2021.

The New Wave is characterized by long takes, naturalistic acting, fixed cameras, dark humour, and themes drawn from everyday life in late communist and post-communist Romania.
