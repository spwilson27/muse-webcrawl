Bardon Hill

Bardon Hill is a hill near Coalville in Leicestershire, England, rising to 278 metres (912 ft) above sea level. It is the highest point in Leicestershire and within the National Forest, and its summit area is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its geology and heathland.

Geology

The hill is formed of Precambrian volcanic rocks of the Charnian Supergroup, among the oldest rocks in England. Bardon Quarry, on the hill's flanks, has extracted hard rock aggregate since the 19th century and remains one of Britain's largest quarries.

History and access

The summit bears a trig point and a toposcope, with wide views across the Midlands. The hill is crossed by public footpaths and lies close to the Ivanhoe Way long-distance trail. Bardon Tower, a folly on the slopes, dates from the 19th century.
