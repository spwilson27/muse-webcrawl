Paddington is an area in the City of Westminster, in central London, England. Historically a parish in Middlesex, it is best known for Paddington station, a major London railway terminus, for St Mary's Hospital, and as the setting associated with the fictional Paddington Bear. The district lies between Marylebone to the east, Bayswater to the west, and Hyde Park to the south.

History

The name Paddington derives from an Anglo-Saxon personal name, Padda, with the elements -ing and tūn suggesting "the farmstead of Padda's people". The village grew along two Roman roads, corresponding to today's Edgware Road and the Bayswater Road. Paddington remained a small settlement until the late 18th century, when the Paddington branch of the Grand Union Canal opened in 1801, making the Paddington Basin a hub for freight transshipment between canal and road.

The arrival of the Great Western Railway transformed the area. A temporary station opened in 1838, and Isambard Kingdom Brunel's permanent terminus — the present Paddington station, with its celebrated three-arched trainshed — opened in 1854. Grand hotels, terraces of stucco houses, and institutions followed, including St Mary's Hospital (founded 1845), where Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928 and where several royal children have been born.

Regeneration

In the later 20th century parts of Paddington became run-down, but from the 1990s the Paddington Basin and Paddington Central developments brought offices, housing, restaurants, and moorings to the canalside, along with landmarks such as the Rolling Bridge and the Fan Bridge. Paddington station now also serves the Heathrow Express and the Elizabeth line, which opened through the district in 2022.

Paddington Bear

Michael Bond's fictional bear from "darkest Peru" — found at Paddington station with a tag reading "Please look after this bear" — first appeared in A Bear Called Paddington in 1958. A statue of Paddington Bear stands on the station concourse, and the character remains one of the best-known associations of the district worldwide.
