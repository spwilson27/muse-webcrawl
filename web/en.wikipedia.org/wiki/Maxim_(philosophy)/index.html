Maxim (philosophy)

[Image: Immanuel Kant, who gave the maxim a central role in moral philosophy]
Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)

A maxim is a principle or rule of conduct. In moral philosophy the term is especially associated with Immanuel Kant, who used it to mean a subjective principle of action — a personal rule that an individual adopts and upon which they act. The concept is central to Kant's categorical imperative and to the test of whether an action is morally permissible.

Kant's definition

In the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785), Kant defines a maxim as "the subjective principle of volition" — the rule in accordance with which an agent actually acts, as opposed to the objective principle (a practical law) that reason would command every rational being to follow. Maxims ordinarily have the form: "In circumstances of kind C, I will do action A in order to achieve end E."

"Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it become a universal law." — Kant, Groundwork, 4:421

On Kant's account, an action has moral worth and is permissible only if the maxim on which the agent acts could consistently be willed as a universal law for all rational agents. Maxims that generate a contradiction in conception (such as false promising, which would undermine the practice of promising if universalised) or a contradiction in the will are forbidden.

Formulation and criticism

Philosophers have debated how maxims are to be individuated: the same action can be described by indefinitely many maxims, and the outcome of the universalisation test can depend on how generally or specifically the maxim is stated. Commentators such as Onora O'Neill and Christine Korsgaard have defended principled ways of identifying the maxim "on which" an agent acts, based on the agent's own intended plan and reasons. The notion also appears in discussions of moral particularism and of rule-consequentialism, where subjective principles of conduct are evaluated by their general acceptance.

Other uses

More generally, "maxim" can denote any succinct statement of a general truth or rule of conduct — a sense related to the literary aphorism and to collections such as La Rochefoucauld's Maximes (1665). In law, maxims of equity are established principles applied by courts of equity.
