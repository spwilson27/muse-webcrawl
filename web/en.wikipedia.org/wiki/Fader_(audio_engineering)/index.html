Fader (audio engineering)

Channel faders on a mixing console

A fader is a device for gradually increasing or decreasing the level of an audio signal. The term also refers to the function itself. Faders are found on mixing consoles, DJ mixers, audio interfaces and in digital audio workstation software.

Types

The most common form is the linear (sliding) fader, a potentiometer whose knob travels along a straight track, giving the engineer a visual indication of relative levels across channels. Rotary controls used for the same purpose are usually called knobs or pots rather than faders. On DJ mixers a crossfader blends between two sources as it moves horizontally, and its response curve can often be adjusted for scratching or smooth mixing.

In modern consoles faders may be motorised, physically moving to recall stored positions when scenes or automation are loaded. The underlying gain change may be performed by a voltage-controlled amplifier (VCA), in which case groups of channels can be controlled together by VCA groups, or entirely in software in a digital console.

Fades

To fade in or fade out a signal is to raise or lower its level gradually; fades are used at the beginnings and ends of recordings and between items in broadcasting. Fader scales are typically marked in decibels, with finer resolution near the top of the travel (around unity gain) where most mixing adjustments are made.
