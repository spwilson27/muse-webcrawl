Triclabendazole

Triclabendazole, sold under the brand name Egaten among others, is a benzimidazole anthelmintic medication used to treat liver fluke infections, principally fascioliasis caused by Fasciola hepatica and Fasciola gigantica. Unusually for a benzimidazole, it is highly specific to flukes and is effective against both immature and adult stages of the parasite. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical use

Triclabendazole is the drug of choice for fascioliasis in humans; a course consists of one or two doses of 10 mg/kg taken with food. It has also been used against paragonimiasis (lung fluke). Because human fascioliasis is a neglected tropical disease, the manufacturer Novartis has donated the drug to the WHO for distribution in endemic countries for many years. The drug was long used in veterinary medicine against sheep and cattle liver fluke before its approval for human use in the United States in 2019.

Mechanism and resistance

Like other benzimidazoles, triclabendazole and its active sulfoxide metabolite bind parasite β-tubulin, disrupting microtubule formation, damaging the fluke's tegument and impairing its movement and feeding. Resistance in livestock flukes has been reported since the 1990s in Australia and Europe and is a growing veterinary concern.

Side effects

Side effects are usually mild and transient, including abdominal pain, nausea, headache and dizziness; pain may result from dying flukes obstructing the biliary tract.
