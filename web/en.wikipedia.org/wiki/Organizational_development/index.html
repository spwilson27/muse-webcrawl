Organizational development

An organisational development practitioner facilitating a group intervention.

Organizational development (OD) is the study and implementation of practices, systems and techniques that affect organisational change, the goal of which is to modify a group's / organisation's performance and/or culture. The organisational changes are typically initiated by the group's stakeholders. OD emerged from human relations studies in the 1930s and was shaped by Kurt Lewin's work on group dynamics and action research.

History

Kurt Lewin is widely considered the founding father of OD, introducing concepts such as group dynamics, force field analysis and the "unfreeze–change–refreeze" model of change. In the post-war decades, sensitivity training (T-groups), survey feedback and socio-technical systems work at the Tavistock Institute expanded the field. Later movements such as appreciative inquiry, large-group interventions and dialogic OD broadened its methods.

Core values and methods

Traditional OD is characterised by humanistic values: participation, openness, trust and the development of people as well as performance. Common interventions include team building, coaching, process consultation, survey feedback, conflict management and strategic planning. The OD practitioner, whether internal or an external consultant, typically works as a facilitator rather than an expert prescribing solutions.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the measurability of OD outcomes, its reliance on managerial agendas, and the transferability of its largely Western, humanistic assumptions to other cultures and organisational forms.
