Bingara is a small town on the Gwydir River in the New England region of New South Wales, Australia. It is the administrative centre of Gwydir Shire and lies about 160 kilometres north of Tamworth and 140 kilometres west of Inverell. The name is said to derive from an Aboriginal word meaning "creek".

History

The area was the country of the Kamilaroi people. European settlement followed exploration of the Gwydir valley in the 1830s, and the town grew after gold and diamonds were discovered nearby in the 1850s and in the 1870s–80s respectively; the Bingara diamond field was among Australia's early diamond sources, though stones were small. The town was also affected by the region's darker frontier history: the Myall Creek massacre of 1838, in which 28 Aboriginal people were murdered, occurred at Myall Creek station about 25 km east of Bingara and is now commemorated by a memorial of national significance.

Attractions

Bingara is known for its restored Art Deco Roxy Theatre and Peter's Café, built by Greek immigrants in the 1930s and now a museum celebrating Greek migration to rural Australia. The town promotes itself through its orange trees planted along its streets as a living memorial to its war dead — the fruit is ceremonially harvested each year. Copeton Dam on the Gwydir River, upstream to the east, provides irrigation and recreation. Farming in the district centres on cattle, sheep, and cropping.
