Transcaucasia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transcaucasia, also known as the South Caucasus, is a geographical region on the border of Eastern Europe and Western Asia, lying south of the main ridge of the Caucasus Mountains between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea. It comprises the modern republics of Georgia, Armenia, and Azerbaijan. The name Transcaucasia reflects the perspective of Imperial Russia — the land "beyond the Caucasus" as seen from the north — and is now often replaced by the neutral term South Caucasus.

Geography

The region is bounded to the north by the Greater Caucasus range, which forms the frontier with Russia (the North Caucasus), and to the south by Turkey and Iran. Between the Greater and Lesser Caucasus ranges lie the lowlands of the Kura–Aras basin and the Colchis plain, watered by rivers such as the Kura and the Rioni. The climate ranges from humid subtropical on the Georgian Black Sea coast to continental and semi-arid in the east. The region is seismically active and rich in oil and gas in its eastern (Azerbaijani) sector and in the Caspian basin.

History

Transcaucasia was home to some of the world's earliest Christian states — Armenia adopted Christianity traditionally in 301 AD and Georgia in the 4th century — and its kingdoms stood successively within the orbits of Persia, Rome and Byzantium, the Arab Caliphate, the Seljuks, the Mongols, the Ottoman Empire and Safavid Iran. Russia annexed the region piecemeal between 1801 and 1829.

After the Russian Revolution, the three peoples briefly formed the Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic (1918) before declaring separate independence. All three were absorbed into the Soviet Union, from 1922 to 1936 as the Transcaucasian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic, then as separate union republics. Since the Soviet collapse in 1991 the independent states have been marked by conflicts over Nagorno-Karabakh, Abkhazia and South Ossetia, by competing pipeline routes carrying Caspian energy westward, and by divergent geopolitical orientations toward Russia, the European Union and Turkey.
