'Nduja

'Nduja (pronounced roughly "en-DOO-yah") is a spicy, spreadable pork salume from Calabria in southern Italy, particularly associated with the town of Spilinga in the province of Vibo Valentia. It is made from pork fat and meat from various cuts, heavily seasoned with roasted Calabrian chilli peppers, stuffed into a casing (traditionally the large intestine casing called the orba), and cured until soft enough to spread.

History

The name derives from the French andouille, a type of sausage introduced to southern Italy during Angevin or Napoleonic-era French influence. Like many peasant foods, 'nduja made use of less prized parts of the pig, preserved with salt, smoke, and chilli — chillies themselves having arrived from the Americas and become central to Calabrian cooking. For most of its history it was a humble local product little known outside Calabria.

Characteristics and use

'Nduja is characterised by its deep red colour, soft paste-like texture, and pronounced heat — chillies may constitute a quarter to a third of the mixture. It melts readily, so it is spread on grilled bread (bruschetta), stirred into pasta sauces, used as a pizza topping, folded into eggs, or added to soups and stews as a flavouring. Since the 2010s it has become fashionable internationally, appearing in British and American restaurants and supermarkets, a trend that has boosted production in Calabria while prompting discussion of quality standards and protection of the traditional product.
