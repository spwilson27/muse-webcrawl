The Baptism of Christ (Verrocchio)


The Baptism of Christ, c. 1472–1475, Uffizi

The Baptism of Christ is a painting finished around 1475 in the studio of the Italian Renaissance painter Andrea del Verrocchio and generally attributed to him and his pupil Leonardo da Vinci. It depicts the Baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist as recorded in the Gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke. The painting is housed in the Uffizi Gallery in Florence.

Subject and composition

John the Baptist pours water over the head of Jesus as the two stand in the River Jordan, watched by two kneeling angels. Above, the hands of God the Father and the dove of the Holy Spirit emerge from a golden radiance. The picture was commissioned for the church of San Salvi in Florence, where it remained until it was transferred to the Uffizi.

Leonardo's contribution

Most of the painting was executed by Verrocchio and his workshop, largely in tempera. One of the angels — the kneeling figure on the left holding Jesus's drapery — together with parts of the landscape, was painted in oils by the young Leonardo, then an apprentice in Verrocchio's studio. According to Vasari, the angel's beauty so surpassed the master's work that Verrocchio resolved never to paint again — a story now regarded as legend, though the contrast in handling between the tempera figures and Leonardo's oil-painted passages remains striking.

Significance

The painting is a key document of Leonardo's early career and of the workshop system of Renaissance Florence. Scientific examination, including X-radiographs, has helped art historians distinguish the hands of Verrocchio, Leonardo, and possibly other assistants such as Lorenzo di Credi.
