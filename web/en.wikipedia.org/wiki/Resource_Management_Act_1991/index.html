Resource Management Act 1991

The Resource Management Act 1991 (RMA) was the main statute governing the use of land, air and water in New Zealand. It was described as the world's first legislatively-based integrated environmental management statute based on the concept of sustainability. Passed by the New Zealand Parliament in 1991 under the Fourth Labour Government, the Act replaced a large number of earlier statutes relating to town planning, water and soil conservation, and pollution control.

The stated purpose of the Act, set out in section 5, was "to promote the sustainable management of natural and physical resources." In practice much decision-making under the RMA was devolved to local government — regional councils and territorial authorities — which prepared regional and district plans and decided applications for resource consents for activities such as subdivision, land use, water takes and discharges.

History and reform

The RMA became a frequent subject of political debate, with critics arguing that its processes were slow and costly, particularly for housing and infrastructure. Successive governments amended it many times. In 2023 the Fifth Labour Government repealed the Act and replaced it with the Natural and Built Environment Act and the Spatial Planning Act; following a change of government later that year, those replacement Acts were themselves repealed and the RMA restored, pending further reform legislation.

Key features

Important concepts under the Act include effects-based planning, the hierarchy of national policy statements, national environmental standards, and the consideration of the principles of the Treaty of Waitangi in resource management decisions.
