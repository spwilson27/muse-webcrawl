Kura (Caspian Sea)

The Kura (Georgian: მტკვარი, Mtkvari; Azerbaijani: Kür; Turkish: Kura) is an east-flowing river south of the Greater Caucasus Mountains which drains the southern slopes of the Greater Caucasus into the Caspian Sea. At 1,515 kilometres (941 mi), it is the longest river of the South Caucasus, flowing through Turkey, Georgia and Azerbaijan, where it meets its main tributary, the Aras, and empties into the Caspian Sea near Neftçala.

Course

The Kura rises in northeastern Turkey, in the Kars region, and enters Georgia, where it flows east through the city of Tbilisi, whose historic centre straddles its gorge. It then crosses into Azerbaijan, passes the Mingachevir Reservoir — the largest body of water in the country — and is joined by the Aras near Sabirabad before reaching the Caspian Sea in a broad delta.

History

The river is the Cyrus of classical antiquity (Greek Κῦρος). Human settlement along its banks goes back tens of thousands of years, and its floodplain in Azerbaijan was a centre of early agriculture and of the ancient kingdoms of the region. The Georgian name Mtkvari is usually explained as "good water" or connected with a root meaning "gnawing" the rock of its canyon-like upper course.

Use and ecology

The Kura is heavily used for irrigation and hydroelectric power, especially in Azerbaijan, where the Mingachevir and Shamkir dams regulate its flow. Interbasin transfers and damming have reduced its discharge to the Caspian Sea, and pollution from industry and agriculture along the middle course is a recognised environmental problem. The delta and downstream wetlands remain important for migratory birds and for sturgeon spawning.
