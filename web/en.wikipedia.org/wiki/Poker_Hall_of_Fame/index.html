The Poker Hall of Fame is the hall of fame of professional poker in the United States. Founded in Las Vegas in 1979 by Benny Binion, the owner of Binion's Horseshoe, which also created the World Series of Poker (WSOP), it honors poker players who have played consistently well against top competition and stood the test of time, as well as non-players who have contributed to the growth of poker. Since 2004 the Hall of Fame has been owned and administered as part of the World Series of Poker.

History

The first inductee, in 1979, was Johnny Moss, voted in by a group that included many of the game's early legends. Early inductees included "Wild Bill" Hickok, Edmond Hoyle and other historical figures in the first years, after which induction focused on living and recently deceased professional players and contributors. Traditionally one or two people are inducted each year, announced during the WSOP. In 2020, Huck Seed was inducted as the sole member of his class; recent classes have continued the single-inductee format.

Requirements

Nominees for players generally must have: played poker against acknowledged top competition; played for high stakes; played consistently well, gaining the respect of peers; and stood the test of time. Players must be at least 40 years old at the time of nomination. Non-player nominees must have contributed to the overall growth of the game of poker, with indelible positive and lasting results. The public may submit nominations, after which a panel of existing Hall of Famers and media members votes on the finalists.
