Christmas in Sweden

Christmas in Sweden (Swedish: jul) is centred on Christmas Eve, 24 December, when families gather for the main meal and the exchange of gifts. The Advent season begins four Sundays before Christmas, with Advent candles and calendars marking the countdown.

Traditions

On 13 December, Saint Lucy's Day (Sankta Lucia) is celebrated with processions of candle-bearing children led by a girl wearing a crown of candles, singing Lucia songs and serving saffron buns (lussekatter) and coffee.

The Christmas meal, the julbord, is a smörgåsbord typically including pickled herring, cured salmon, Christmas ham (julskinka), meatballs, sausages, ribs, boiled potatoes, red cabbage, and the casserole known as Janssons frestelse ("Jansson's temptation"), accompanied by julmust, a spiced soft drink, and sometimes glögg (mulled wine).

A distinctive national tradition at 3 p.m. on Christmas Eve is watching the 1958 Walt Disney special From All of Us to All of You ("Kalle Anka och hans vänner önskar God Jul" — "Donald Duck and his friends wish you a Merry Christmas"), which has been broadcast on Swedish television since 1960 and draws a large share of the population each year.

Gifts are traditionally brought by the Jultomten (Christmas gnome), often a disguised family member, and distributed after the meal. The Christmas season formally ends on 13 January, Saint Knut's Day (tjugondag Knut), when trees are taken down in a julgransplundring ("Christmas tree plundering") party.
