Ōkubo Toshimichi

Ōkubo Toshimichi (26 September 1830 – 14 May 1878) was a Japanese statesman from Satsuma Domain and one of the "Three Great Nobles of the Restoration" who brought about the Meiji Restoration. As Home Minister he became the most powerful figure in the early Meiji government.

Career

A low-ranking Satsuma samurai, Ōkubo aligned with Saigō Takamori to overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate in 1868. He pushed through the abolition of the feudal domains in 1871 and travelled with the Iwakura Mission to study Western institutions. In the 1873 debate over invading Korea he prevailed over Saigō, arguing Japan must modernise first — a split that led to Saigō's resignation and eventually the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, which Ōkubo's government crushed.

Assassination

As Home Minister, Ōkubo promoted industry, infrastructure, and a strong centralised police. On 14 May 1878 he was assassinated in Tokyo by disgruntled former samurai who listed his authoritarian rule among their grievances. His legacy as a determined, ruthless moderniser remains debated in Japan.
