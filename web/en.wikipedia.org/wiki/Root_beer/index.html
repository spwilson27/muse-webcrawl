Root beer is a sweet North American soft drink traditionally made using the root bark of the sassafras tree or the vine of sarsaparilla as its primary flavour. Root beer is usually caffeine-free, sweet and carbonated, with a thick foamy head when poured.

History

Root beer descends from Indigenous and colonial American "small beers" brewed from roots and barks. Philadelphia pharmacist Charles Elmer Hires introduced the first commercial brand at the 1876 Centennial Exposition, marketing his root tea — renamed root beer to appeal to miners — as a wholesome temperance drink. During Prohibition, root beer boomed as a legal alternative to beer. Major brands include A&W (founded 1919, famous for drive-in restaurants and the root beer float), Barq's (unusually caffeinated), Mug, Dad's and IBC.

Flavour and regulation

In 1960 the US FDA banned safrole, the aromatic oil of sassafras root bark, as a carcinogen; modern root beers use safrole-free sassafras extract or artificial flavours, combined with wintergreen, vanilla, liquorice, anise, molasses and other spices in proprietary blends.
