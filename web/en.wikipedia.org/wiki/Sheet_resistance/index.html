Sheet resistance

Sheet resistance is the resistance of a square piece of a thin material with uniform thickness. It applies to two-dimensional systems such as thin films and semiconductor layers. Sheet resistance is an important property for materials with resistivity that is inversely proportional to layer thickness, and is measured in units of ohms per square (Ω/□, or Ω/sq). The concept is widely used in the semiconductor industry to characterise diffused, implanted, and deposited layers, as well as transparent conductive films used in displays and solar cells.

Sheet resistance is commonly measured with a four-point probe.

Calculation

For a rectangular sheet of resistivity ρ, length L, width W, and thickness t, the resistance along the length is

R = ρL / (W t)

If the section is square (L = W), the resistance reduces to

R_s = ρ / t

which depends only on resistivity and thickness, not on the size of the square. For arbitrary shapes, the resistance between opposite edges can be computed by counting the number of "squares" along the current path. Non-uniform doping profiles are handled by defining sheet resistance through the integral of conductivity through the layer: 1/R_s = ∫ σ(x) dx.

Measurement

The standard technique is the four-point probe method, in which current is passed through two outer contacts and the voltage measured between two inner contacts, eliminating contact resistance from the measurement. For an infinite sheet with equally spaced probes, R_s = (π/ln 2)(V/I). Alternative techniques include the van der Pauw method for arbitrarily shaped samples and non-contact eddy-current measurements used in wafer production.

Applications

Sheet resistance figures in the design of integrated-circuit resistors, polysilicon lines, transparent conductors such as indium tin oxide (ITO), thin-film photovoltaics, electromagnetic shielding, and printed electronics.
