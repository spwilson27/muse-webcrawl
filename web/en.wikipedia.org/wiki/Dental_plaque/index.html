Dental plaque

Dental plaque is a biofilm of microorganisms, mainly bacteria, embedded in a matrix of salivary polymers and bacterial extracellular products, that grows on the surfaces of the teeth. It appears as a pale yellow or greyish film and forms continuously. If not removed by brushing and flossing, plaque mineralises into calculus (tartar).

Formation

Within hours of cleaning, an acquired pellicle of salivary proteins coats the enamel; bacteria such as Streptococcus mutans and other oral streptococci then colonise the surface and multiply, maturing into a complex community in which anaerobic species become more prominent, particularly below the gumline (subgingival plaque).

Health effects

Acids produced by plaque bacteria fermenting sugars cause demineralisation of enamel and are the principal cause of dental caries (cavities). Plaque at the gum margin causes gingivitis and, if untreated, can progress to periodontitis, with loss of the tissues supporting the teeth.

Control

Regular tooth brushing (typically twice daily with fluoride toothpaste), interdental cleaning with floss or interdental brushes, and professional cleaning control plaque. Disclosing tablets, which stain plaque, are used to check cleaning effectiveness.
