Parietal cell

Parietal cells (also known as oxyntic cells) are epithelial cells in the stomach that secrete hydrochloric acid (HCl) and intrinsic factor. These cells are located in the gastric glands found in the lining of the fundus and body regions of the stomach. They secrete intrinsic factor, which is required for vitamin B12 absorption.

Acid secretion

The parietal cell secretes acid via the H+/K+-ATPase ("proton pump") in the membrane of deep invaginations called canaliculi, producing gastric juice at about pH 1.5–2. Secretion is stimulated by three signals acting together: histamine (from ECL cells, via H2 receptors), gastrin (from G cells, via CCK2 receptors) and acetylcholine (from vagal nerve endings, via M3 receptors)—the basis for drug classes that block each pathway.

Clinical significance

Proton pump inhibitors (such as omeprazole) irreversibly block the acid pump and are mainstays of treatment for peptic ulcer disease and gastro-oesophageal reflux. Autoimmune destruction of parietal cells in atrophic gastritis causes loss of acid and intrinsic factor, leading to vitamin B12 deficiency and pernicious anaemia. Excess acid production driven by gastrin-secreting tumours causes Zollinger–Ellison syndrome.
