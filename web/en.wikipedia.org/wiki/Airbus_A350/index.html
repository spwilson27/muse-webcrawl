Airbus A350

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An Airbus A350 with its distinctive black cockpit mask taking off]
An A350 departing.

The Airbus A350 is a long-range, wide-body twin-engine airliner developed and produced by Airbus, built largely from carbon-fibre composites and designed to compete with the Boeing 777 and 787. Launched as a clean-sheet design in 2006 (after an earlier A330-based concept was rejected by customers), it first flew in 2013 and entered service with Qatar Airways in 2015. Variants are the A350-900 and the stretched A350-1000, plus the ultra-long-range -900ULR used by Singapore Airlines for the world\'s longest flights (Singapore–New York, over 18 hours).

Design

The A350\'s composite fuselage (wider than the 787\'s, enabling 9-abreast seating) and Rolls-Royce Trent XWB engines — the most efficient large turbofans in service by Airbus/RR claims — give strong economics. The cockpit shares Airbus\'s fly-by-wire philosophy and commonality with the A330neo.

Operations

Over 600 A350s have been delivered (mid-2020s). Cathay Pacific, Singapore Airlines, Qatar Airways and Lufthansa are major operators; the -1000 competes head-on with the 777-300ER and delayed 777X. An A350F freighter is in development for mid-decade entry into service.

Notable flights

Singapore Airlines\' A350-900ULR services to New York (JFK and Newark) are the world\'s longest scheduled passenger flights by distance (about 15,300 km), flown with a premium-heavy two-class layout and crew-rest facilities.
