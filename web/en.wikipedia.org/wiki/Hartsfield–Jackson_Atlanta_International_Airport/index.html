Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (IATA: ATL) is the primary international airport serving Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Located about 10 miles (16 km) south of downtown Atlanta, it is the world's busiest airport by passenger traffic in nearly every year since 1998, and also leads in total aircraft movements. It is the largest hub of Delta Air Lines, whose headquarters and main technical operations centre are adjacent to the airport.

History

The site began in 1925 as a racetrack leased by the city and developed as Candler Field, named for former Atlanta mayor and Coca-Cola magnate Asa Candler. It was renamed for mayor William B. Hartsfield in 1971; in 2003 the name of former mayor Maynard Jackson was added. The current midfield terminal complex opened in 1980, and the Maynard H. Jackson Jr. International Terminal opened in 2012.

Facilities

The airport has five parallel east–west runways and two terminals (Domestic and International) linked to seven concourses (T and A–F) by an underground automated people mover, the Plane Train, and a pedestrian walkway famous for its art installations. Around three-quarters of its passengers are connecting travellers, a product of its hub-and-spoke role and its location within a two-hour flight of some 80% of the US population.
