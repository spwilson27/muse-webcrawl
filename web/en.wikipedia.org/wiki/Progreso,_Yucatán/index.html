Progreso is a port city in the Mexican state of Yucatán, situated on the Gulf of Mexico about 30 kilometres (19 mi) north of the state capital, Mérida. It is the chief port of Yucatán and the seat of Progreso Municipality.

History

Progreso was founded in 1871 to serve as a modern deep-water port for the export of henequen (sisal) fibre, the region's principal cash crop, replacing the older port of Sisal. Its pier, extended over the years to accommodate larger ships, became one of the longest in the world: the 1937–1941 extension reaches some 6.5 kilometres (4 mi) into the shallow Gulf, ending in a viaduct and terminal where cruise ships now dock.

Economy and tourism

Besides cargo operations and a fishing fleet, Progreso is a regular port of call for Gulf of Mexico cruise ships, whose passengers visit the beach, the malecón, and nearby attractions including Mérida and the Maya ruins of Chichen Itza, Uxmal and Dzibilchaltún. The town is also a popular weekend and holiday beach destination for residents of Mérida, and the surrounding coastal communities are home to many seasonal residents from the United States and Canada.

Geography

The city lies on a narrow barrier strip between the Gulf and a lagoon system (the ría), within a region of limestone karst noted for its cenotes. Its climate is hot and semi-arid, with a rainy season in summer and occasional exposure to hurricanes crossing the Yucatán Peninsula.
