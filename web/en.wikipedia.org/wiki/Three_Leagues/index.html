Three Leagues

The Three Leagues (German: Drei Bünde; Romansh: Trais Lias; Italian: Tre Leghe) was the federation of three regional leagues in the high Alpine valleys of what is now the Swiss canton of Graubünden (Grisons) between 1471 and 1799. The federation formed the Free State of the Three Leagues (Freistaat der Drei Bünde), an associate of the Old Swiss Confederacy.

The three leagues

The component leagues were the League of God's House (Gotteshausbund, founded 1367, centred on Chur and its bishop's subjects), the Grey League (Grauer Bund, 1395/1424, in the upper Rhine valleys), and the League of the Ten Jurisdictions (Zehngerichtenbund, 1436, in the Prättigau and Davos region). The three united formally in 1471 and concluded a common federal charter in 1524 (the Bundesbrief), headquartered at Chur. The Ilanz Articles of 1524 and 1526 curbed episcopal and feudal power and gave the communes remarkable democratic rights.

History

The Free State governed subject territories to the south — the Valtellina, Chiavenna, and Bormio (acquired 1512) — and stood allied with the Swiss cantons while retaining its own foreign policy. During the Thirty Years' War, the Valtellina became a battleground between France, Spain, and Austria in the episode known as the Bündner Wirren (Grisons troubles). In 1799, during the French Revolutionary Wars, the Three Leagues were incorporated into the Helvetic Republic, and in 1803 their lands became the canton of Graubünden in the restored Swiss Confederation. The canton's name — "Grey Leagues" — and its trilingual German, Romansh, and Italian character preserve the federation's heritage.
