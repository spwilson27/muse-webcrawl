Constitutional Court of Georgia

The Constitutional Court of Georgia (Georgian: საქართველოს საკონსტიტუციო სასამართლო) is the constitutional court of Georgia, exercising constitutional review of legal acts. Established under the 1995 Constitution, the court is seated in Batumi. It consists of nine judges appointed for ten-year terms: three each by the President, the Parliament, and the Supreme Court of Georgia.

Jurisdiction

The court rules on the constitutionality of laws, normative acts of the President and Parliament, and international treaties; resolves disputes over competence between state bodies; reviews the constitutionality of political parties; and hears constitutional claims of individuals who allege that their constitutional rights have been violated by a normative act. Its decisions are final and binding.

History

The court began operating in 1996 following the adoption of Georgia's post-Soviet constitution in 1995. In 2007 its seat was moved from Tbilisi to Batumi, the capital of the Adjara autonomous republic, making it one of the few high state bodies located outside the capital. Constitutional reforms in 2017–2018 altered aspects of its appointment procedure and jurisdiction.
