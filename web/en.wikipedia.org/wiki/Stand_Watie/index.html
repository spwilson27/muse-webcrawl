Stand Watie

Stand Watie (Cherokee: ᏕᎦᏔᏍᏙᏗ, Degadoga, "he stands [firm]"; 12 December 1806 – 9 September 1871), also known as Standhope Uwatie, was a Cherokee leader and the only Native American to attain the rank of brigadier general in the Confederate States Army during the American Civil War. He was the last Confederate general to surrender his forces.

Brigadier General Stand Watie

Early life and Removal

Watie was born in the Cherokee Nation in present-day Georgia, educated at mission schools, and became a planter and newspaper editor. He was one of the signers of the Treaty of New Echota (1835), which ceded Cherokee land east of the Mississippi and led to the forced removal known as the Trail of Tears. The treaty split the Cherokee deeply: several of Watie's relatives who had signed were assassinated, attempts were made on Watie himself, and the feud between the Watie and Ross factions lasted for years in Indian Territory.

Civil War

When the Civil War began, Watie raised the Cherokee Mounted Rifles for the Confederacy and became its colonel. He fought at Pea Ridge in 1862 and built a reputation in guerrilla-style operations in Indian Territory, capturing a Union steamboat, the J. R. Williams, and winning a notable victory at the Second Battle of Cabin Creek in 1864, where his men captured a large Federal wagon train. Promoted to brigadier general in May 1864, he surrendered his command at Doaksville on 23 June 1865, the last Confederate general to lay down arms.

Later life

After the war Watie returned to the Cherokee Nation and his plantation, and died in 1871. He remains a significant and debated figure in Cherokee and Civil War history.
