Kurdish–Turkish conflict

PeriodArmed phase since 1984
Principal partiesRepublic of Turkey; Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK)
RegionSouth-eastern Turkey; northern Iraq and Syria
DeathsMore than 40,000 (estimates vary)

The Kurdish–Turkish conflict is the long-running conflict between the Turkish state and Kurdish groups seeking greater rights, autonomy or independence for Turkey's Kurdish population, concentrated in the country's south-east. Its armed phase is generally dated from 1984, when the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), founded by Abdullah Öcalan in 1978, launched attacks on Turkish security forces.

Background

Kurds form Turkey's largest ethnic minority, and the early republic pursued assimilationist policies that denied Kurdish identity, language and names — policies that fuelled earlier revolts in the 1920s and 1930s, including the Sheikh Said rebellion and the Dersim rebellion. Kurdish language and cultural restrictions were tightened again after the 1980 military coup.

Course of the conflict

Through the 1980s and 1990s the war in the south-east saw village evacuations, states of emergency and heavy civilian displacement; Öcalan was captured in Kenya in 1999 and imprisoned on İmralı island. Ceasefires and a peace process in 2013–2015 raised hopes of a settlement, but the process collapsed in 2015 amid the Syrian civil war, and fighting resumed, extending to Turkish operations against Kurdish-led forces in northern Syria. In 2025, following a call by Öcalan, the PKK announced a ceasefire and its dissolution — a development whose implementation remains an evolving process.
