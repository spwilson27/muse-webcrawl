Dysmenorrhea

Dysmenorrhea, also known as period pain or painful periods, is pain during menstruation. It usually begins around the time bleeding starts and typically lasts less than three days. The pain is usually in the pelvis or lower abdomen and may be accompanied by back pain, diarrhoea, nausea, headache and fatigue. Dysmenorrhea is one of the most common gynaecological complaints and a leading cause of school and work absence among young women.

Types

Primary dysmenorrhea

Primary dysmenorrhea is recurrent menstrual pain without underlying pelvic disease. It typically starts within a year or two of menarche and is caused by prostaglandins released from the endometrium, which trigger strong uterine contractions and reduced blood flow to the uterine muscle.

Secondary dysmenorrhea

Secondary dysmenorrhea is pain caused by a disorder of the reproductive organs, such as endometriosis, adenomyosis, uterine fibroids or pelvic inflammatory disease. It tends to begin later in life and may worsen over time; investigation is aimed at the underlying cause.

Treatment

First-line treatment is usually NSAIDs (such as ibuprofen or naproxen), which inhibit prostaglandin synthesis, or hormonal contraceptives, which thin the endometrium. Heat applied to the abdomen, exercise and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) may also help. Severe or refractory cases warrant investigation for secondary causes.
