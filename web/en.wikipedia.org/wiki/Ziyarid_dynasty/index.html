Ziyarid dynasty

Ziyarids
Capital: Isfahan, Ray, Gorgan
Period: 930–1090
Religion: Islam, Zoroastrian roots
Founder: Mardavij

The Ziyarid dynasty (Persian: زیاریان) was an Iranian dynasty of Gilaki origin that ruled Tabaristan and parts of northern Persia from 930 to 1090. At its height under its founder it briefly controlled much of western Iran before contracting to the Caspian provinces of Gorgan and Mazandaran.

History

The dynasty was founded by Mardavij ibn Ziyar, a Gilaki soldier of fortune who rose in the service of the Alavids and Samanids, seized northern Iran, took Isfahan and Hamadan, and planned to revive the Persian empire before his assassination in 935. His brother Vushmgir succeeded him but lost the western territories to the Buyids, retaining Gorgan and Tabaristan, often as a Samanid or Buyid vassal.

Qabus and cultural flourishing

The most celebrated Ziyarid ruler was Shams al-Ma'ali Qabus (r. 978–1012), a noted patron of learning: the scholar al-Biruni dedicated his Chronology of Ancient Nations to him and resided at his court in Gorgan. Qabus built the great brick Gonbad-e Qabus tower in 1006, which still stands and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. His grandson Kaykavus authored the Persian mirror for princes, the Qabus-nama.

Decline

Through the 11th century the Ziyarids became vassals successively of the Ghaznavids and the Seljuks, clinging to the mountainous parts of Tabaristan. The last known Ziyarid ruler, Gilanshah, was killed around 1090, and the dynasty's remaining territory was absorbed amid the upheavals of the Seljuk succession and the rise of the Nizari Ismailis in the Alborz.
