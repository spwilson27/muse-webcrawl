Metrization theorem
Field: Topology
Famous cases: Urysohn, Nagata–Smirnov, Bing
Question: When is a space metrizable?

A metrization theorem in topology gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a topological space to be metrizable, that is, homeomorphic to a metric space. Metric spaces have many convenient properties — they are Hausdorff, first countable, and paracompact — so characterising which abstract spaces admit a metric is a central problem of general topology.

Urysohn metrization theorem

The first major result, proved by Pavel Urysohn in the 1920s, states that every second-countable regular Hausdorff space is metrizable. Second countability is a strong hypothesis, however, and many natural metrizable spaces (such as large discrete spaces) are not second countable.

Nagata–Smirnov theorem

The Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem, proved independently by Jun-iti Nagata and Yurii Smirnov around 1950, gives a complete characterisation: a space is metrizable if and only if it is regular and Hausdorff and has a σ-locally finite base (a base expressible as a countable union of locally finite families of open sets).

Bing metrization theorem

The Bing metrization theorem, proved by R. H. Bing in 1951, states that a space is metrizable if and only if it is regular and Hausdorff and has a σ-discrete base. It is slightly more awkward to prove but convenient in applications. Together, these theorems answer the metrization problem and remain standard results in topology textbooks.
