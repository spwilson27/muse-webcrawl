Air changes per hour

Air changes per hour (ACH or ACPH) is a measure of ventilation: the number of times the total volume of air in a room or building is replaced (or treated) within one hour. It is calculated by dividing the volumetric flow rate of air supplied to (or exhausted from) the space by the volume of the space.

Typical values and standards

Building codes and standards such as those of ASHRAE specify minimum ventilation rates, often expressed in ACH or per-person flow rates. Ordinary offices may require roughly 2–4 ACH of total supply air, while hospital operating rooms are commonly designed for around 20 ACH, and airborne infection isolation rooms require at least 12 ACH in new construction under US healthcare guidelines. Laboratories and cleanrooms can require much higher rates. In homes, natural infiltration typically provides about 0.3–1 ACH.

Measurement and related concepts

ACH can be measured by tracer-gas decay tests or calculated from measured supply airflow. Only the fraction of air that is outdoor (fresh) air counts toward dilution of indoor contaminants; "equivalent ACH" is used to credit air cleaning such as HEPA filtration or upper-room ultraviolet germicidal irradiation, a concept that gained prominence during the COVID-19 pandemic. ACH should not be confused with the number of times air passes through a recirculating filter alone without fresh-air exchange.
