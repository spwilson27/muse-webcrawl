China Railway High-speed (CRH; Chinese: 中国铁路高速) is the high-speed rail service system of China State Railway Group, introduced in 2007. CRH trains run on the world's largest high-speed network, which grew from the first dedicated line — the Beijing–Tianjin intercity railway, opened for the 2008 Olympics — to tens of thousands of kilometres linking nearly all major Chinese cities.

Development

China pursued high-speed rail through a strategy of importing foreign technology and then localising and extending it. Early CRH trainsets were derived from Japanese Shinkansen (E2 series), German ICE 3, Swedish Regina, and French Alstom designs, built in joint ventures with Chinese manufacturers. The indigenously designed Fuxing ("Rejuvenation") trains, launched in 2017 with a standard operating speed of 350 km/h on flagship routes such as Beijing–Shanghai, marked a shift to Chinese standards and intellectual property.

Services and network

CRH services are categorised by letter prefixes, including G (high-speed trains on dedicated lines), D (electric multiple unit express), and C (intercity). The network includes the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway (1,318 km), the Beijing–Guangzhou line — among the world's longest — and extensive coverage of the Yangtze and Pearl River deltas. Annual ridership runs into the billions of passenger journeys.

Safety and criticism

The rapid expansion attracted criticism over cost and the debt of the railway operator. The 2011 Wenzhou train collision, which killed 40 people, led to a national safety review, temporary speed reductions, and reforms of signalling and management. Speeds on many lines were subsequently restored and raised with the Fuxing generation.
