In the United Kingdom, a Chartered Engineer (CEng) is a professional engineer registered with the Engineering Council, the UK regulatory body for the engineering profession. Chartered Engineers are characterised by their ability to develop appropriate solutions to engineering problems using new or existing technologies, through innovation, creativity and change. The title is protected in the UK, and registrants are entitled to use the post-nominal letters CEng.

Requirements

Registration normally requires an accredited integrated Master's degree (MEng) or a Bachelor's degree plus further learning to Master's level, a period of initial professional development in a position of engineering responsibility, demonstration of the competences and commitments set out in the UK Standard for Professional Engineering Competence (UK-SPEC), and a professional review, including an interview, administered by a licensed professional engineering institution such as the IET, IMechE, ICE or RAeS. Registrants must maintain continuing professional development.

Related titles

The Engineering Council also registers Incorporated Engineer (IEng) and Engineering Technician (EngTech), with different educational and competence benchmarks. The CEng qualification is broadly aligned with the European Engineer (EUR ING) title administered by FEANI, and similar "chartered" titles exist in other Commonwealth countries, including Chartered Professional Engineer (CPEng) in Australia and New Zealand.
