Quttinirpaaq National Park is a Canadian national park located on the northeastern corner of Ellesmere Island in the Qikiqtani Region of Nunavut. It is the second-largest national park in Canada, after Wood Buffalo National Park, and the most northerly park in the country, lying only about 800 km from the North Pole.

The name Quttinirpaaq is Inuktitut for "top of the world". The park was established in 1988 as the Ellesmere Island National Park Reserve and received its current name and full national park status in 2000–2001.

Geography

The park protects a polar desert landscape of rugged mountains, ice caps, glaciers, and deep fiords. The Grant Land Mountains and the United States Range run through the park; Barbeau Peak, at 2,616 m the highest mountain in Nunavut, rises within it. Lake Hazen, one of the largest lakes by volume in the Canadian Arctic, lies in the park's interior, and the Tanquary Fiord and other fiords cut into its northern coast. Much of the terrain is covered by the northern Ellesmere ice shelves and permanent ice.

Wildlife

Despite the harsh climate, the park supports populations of muskoxen, Peary caribou, Arctic wolves, Arctic foxes, Arctic hares, lemmings, and polar bears, along with migratory birds in the summer. The thermal oasis around Lake Hazen supports richer vegetation than the surrounding high Arctic desert.

Access

Quttinirpaaq is among the most remote and least-visited national parks in Canada, receiving only a handful of visitors most years. Access is by charter aircraft, typically from Resolute Bay via the warden station at Tanquary Fiord, or by ski-equipped aircraft to Lake Hazen.
