The Cadillac Fleetwood is a model of luxury automobile manufactured by Cadillac, the luxury division of General Motors, across much of the 20th century. The name honours Fleetwood, Pennsylvania, home of the Fleetwood Metal Body Company, a coachbuilder acquired by Fisher Body and then General Motors.

Fleetwood began as a designation for Cadillac bodies built by Fleetwood, became a distinct model line, and from 1977 to 1996 named Cadillac's flagship full-size sedan.

History

Fleetwood-bodied Cadillacs of the 1920s and 1930s were among the most prestigious American cars. After periods as a trim and series designation — including the Sixty Special Fleetwood and the Eldorado and limousine lines — Fleetwood became a standalone rear-wheel-drive flagship in 1985's downsized range and, from 1993, the large Fleetwood Brougham on the D-body platform.

Final generation

The 1993–1996 Fleetwood was one of the largest production sedans ever built in the United States and, from 1994, used the 5.7-litre LT1 V8 shared with the Corvette. Production ended in 1996 when GM closed the Arlington plant line for full-size rear-wheel-drive cars.

Legacy

The Fleetwood name remains associated with traditional American full-size luxury; surviving examples are popular with collectors and in funeral and livery service.
