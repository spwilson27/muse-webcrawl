Batman (military)

A batman is a soldier assigned as a personal servant to a commissioned officer in the British Army and in other armies historically following British practice. The term is unrelated to the DC Comics superhero; it derives from the French bât, a packsaddle, via the "bat horse" that carried an officer's baggage — the soldier who tended it became the officer's batman.

Duties

A batman's duties included looking after the officer's uniform, kit and horse or quarters, cooking, valeting, carrying messages and acting as a bodyguard in battle. In the trenches of the First World War, batmen were often chosen for trustworthiness and shared much of their officer's danger. The Royal Navy equivalent was a steward, and the RAF used the same term for officers' servants.

Decline

The institution faded after the Second World War as armies professionalised; a variant, the "batman/driver", survived longest in some Commonwealth forces. In modern British service the role persists mainly in ceremonial and household contexts as the orderly or officer's attendant.
