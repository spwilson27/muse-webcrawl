LZ 129 Hindenburg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Hindenburg airship burning and falling at Lakehurst in 1937, famous news photograph]
The Hindenburg disaster, 6 May 1937.

LZ 129 Hindenburg was a German commercial passenger-carrying rigid airship, the lead ship of her class and, with her sister Graf Zeppelin II, the largest aircraft ever flown by volume. Launched in March 1936, she made 63 flights, including regular transatlantic services to the United States and Brazil, before being destroyed by fire while landing at Naval Air Station Lakehurst, New Jersey, on 6 May 1937 — a disaster filmed and broadcast worldwide that ended the era of passenger airships.

Design and service

Hindenburg was 245 metres long, held 200,000 m³ of hydrogen in 16 gas cells (helium being unavailable to Germany under US export restrictions), and carried 50–70 passengers in ocean-liner comfort: lounges, a dining room, cabins, and even a smoking room pressurised for safety. Four Daimler-Benz diesel engines gave a cruising speed of about 122 km/h.

The disaster

Arriving late and high after thunderstorms, the airship made a sharp turn toward the mooring mast; at 19:25 a fire erupted near the stern and the ship fell in 34 seconds, captured in Sam Shere\'s famous photograph and Herbert Morrison\'s anguished radio report (\'Oh, the humanity!\'). Of 97 aboard, 35 died, plus one member of the ground crew.

Cause and legacy

The official inquiries blamed a hydrogen leak ignited by an electrostatic discharge (the \'incendiary paint\' theory, blaming the dope coating, remains a minority view). Public confidence in airships collapsed; Graf Zeppelin II never carried fare-paying passengers, and both ships were scrapped in 1940.
