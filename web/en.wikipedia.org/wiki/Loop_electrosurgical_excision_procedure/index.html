Loop electrosurgical excision procedure

A wire loop electrode of the type used in LEEP

The loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP), also known as large loop excision of the transformation zone (LLETZ) outside North America, is a treatment for high-grade cervical dysplasia (cervical intraepithelial neoplasia) discovered on colposcopic examination. It removes the abnormal tissue of the cervix using a thin wire loop heated by electric current, which cuts and coagulates at the same time.

Procedure

LEEP is usually performed as an outpatient procedure under local anaesthetic. Through a colposcope, the clinician passes the electrified wire loop across the transformation zone of the cervix, excising a shallow cone or disc of tissue that is sent for histological examination, so the procedure is both diagnostic and therapeutic. Bleeding is controlled by ball cautery and haemostatic paste.

Benefits and risks

Compared with cold-knife conisation, LEEP is quicker, cheaper, requires less anaesthesia, and preserves a specimen with limited thermal artefact at its margins. Risks include bleeding, infection, cervical stenosis, and a modest increase in the risk of preterm birth in later pregnancies, which rises with the depth and volume of tissue excised. Most women are advised to have follow-up cytology and HPV testing to confirm the lesion has been fully treated.
