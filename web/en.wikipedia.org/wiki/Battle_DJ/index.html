Battle DJ

A battle DJ, also known as a turntablist in competitive contexts, is a disc jockey who competes against other DJs in structured battles, performing short, highly technical routines using turntablism techniques such as scratching, beat juggling, and body tricks. DJ battles are a central institution of hip hop culture and of the wider turntablist scene, with competitors judged on technical skill, originality, musicality and stage presence, typically in head-to-head elimination rounds lasting between 90 seconds and a few minutes per performer.

History

Competitive DJing grew out of the Bronx party scene of the 1970s, where DJs such as Grand Wizard Theodore, Grandmaster Flash and Afrika Bambaataa developed scratching and quick-mix techniques, and where sound-system-style clashes and MC-led crowd judgements rewarded showmanship. Formal contests emerged in the 1980s, including the New Music Seminar DJ Battle for World Supremacy and, from 1985, the DMC World DJ Championships founded by Tony Prince, whose winners — including DJ Cheese, Chad Jackson, Rock Steady DJs, the X-Ecutioners' members, Qbert, Mix Master Mike, Craze, A-Trak and DJ Kentaro — defined successive eras of technique.

Techniques

Core battle techniques include the baby scratch, chirp, flare, crab and transform scratches; beat juggling, in which two copies of the same record are manipulated to rearrange a drum break into new patterns; needle-dropping; and "body tricks" performed with elbows, chin, or behind the back. Routines are typically pre-planned and rehearsed like concert pieces, recorded in advance on "battle records" pressed with skipless scratch samples. Since the 2000s many battles permit digital vinyl systems (DVS) such as Serato Scratch Live and controllers, while some categories preserve all-vinyl formats.

Competitions and culture

The DMC Championships remain the best-known battle, with solo, team and "supremacy" (head-to-head) formats in many countries. Red Bull 3Style (and its successor events) emphasised party-rocking and genre variety, while the International DJ Association (IDA) and online battles expanded participation. Battle DJs formed influential crews such as the Invisibl Skratch Piklz, the Beat Junkies and the X-Ecutioners, and the format spread worldwide, with strong scenes in Japan, France, the UK and the Philippines.
