The European Train Control System (ETCS) is the signalling and control component of the European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS). It is designed to replace the many incompatible safety systems currently used by European railways, allowing interoperability and continuous speed supervision across national borders.

How it works

ETCS continuously calculates a speed profile for each train based on trackside data and the train's characteristics, and applies the brakes automatically if the driver exceeds the permitted speed. In Level 1, data is transmitted intermittently from balises in the track. In Level 2, movement authorities are transmitted continuously by radio (GSM-R) from a Radio Block Centre, and lineside signals become optional. Level 3, still under development, uses moving block principles with train integrity confirmed on board.

Deployment

ETCS is mandatory for new high-speed and corridor lines in the European Union and has been adopted by numerous countries outside Europe, including China, India, Australia and Saudi Arabia. Deployment programmes have repeatedly been affected by cost, versioning between specification baselines, and the complexity of fitting large legacy fleets.
