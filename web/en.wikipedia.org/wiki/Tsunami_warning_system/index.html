Tsunami warning system

Tsunami warning system
Components: Seismic networks, DART buoys, tide gauges, warning centres
Key centres: PTWC, national agencies

A tsunami warning system is a system used to detect tsunamis in advance and issue warnings to prevent loss of life and damage to property. It is made up of two equally important components: a network of sensors to detect tsunamis, and a communications infrastructure to issue timely alarms permitting evacuation of coastal areas.

Detection

Warning centres monitor seismic networks to detect undersea earthquakes capable of generating tsunamis. The DART (Deep-ocean Assessment and Reporting of Tsunamis) system, developed by the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, uses bottom pressure recorders on the ocean floor coupled to surface buoys that relay data by satellite; a passing tsunami produces a distinctive pressure signature. Coastal tide gauges provide confirmation as waves approach land.

Warning centres

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center (PTWC) in Hawaii, established in 1949 after the 1946 Aleutian Islands tsunami, serves the Pacific basin. After the devastating 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, an Indian Ocean warning system was built under UNESCO coordination, with regional service providers in India, Indonesia and Australia. Japan operates one of the world's most advanced national systems through the Japan Meteorological Agency.
