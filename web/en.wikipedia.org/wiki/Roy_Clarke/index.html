Roy Clarke

Roy Clarke (born 28 January 1930) is an English comedy writer best known as the creator of Last of the Summer Wine, the world's longest-running sitcom, which he wrote from 1973 to 2010.

Career

Born in Austerfield, Yorkshire, Clarke worked as a teacher, policeman and salesman before writing full-time. He created Open All Hours (1973–1985) and its sequel Still Open All Hours, and Keeping Up Appearances (1990–1995), all major BBC successes. Unusually, he was the sole writer of every episode of his principal series.

He was appointed OBE in 2001 and received a BAFTA lifetime achievement recognition for his contribution to comedy.
