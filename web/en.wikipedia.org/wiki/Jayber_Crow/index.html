Jayber Crow

Jayber Crow is a 2000 novel by American writer Wendell Berry, part of his Port William cycle of fiction set in a fictional small Kentucky farming community. The novel won the 2001 Lannan Literary Award for Fiction and is among Berry's best-known works.

Plot

The novel is narrated in the first person by Jonah "Jayber" Crow, born in 1914, who after a childhood journey to an orphanage and a stint in seminary school wanders back to Port William in 1937 and becomes the town barber — a listening post from which he observes the community across nearly six decades. Jayber narrates the loves, losses, and changes of the town's families, including his own unspoken lifelong love for Mattie Chatham, and laments the decline of agrarian life under industrial agriculture and the "two economies" he sees dividing his world.

Themes

Like much of Berry's fiction, Jayber Crow explores membership in a place, the virtues and sorrows of fidelity, the impact of war and mechanisation on rural communities, and the spiritual dimensions of work and belonging. It connects to Berry's other Port William novels and stories, including Hannah Coulter and The Memory of Old Jack.
