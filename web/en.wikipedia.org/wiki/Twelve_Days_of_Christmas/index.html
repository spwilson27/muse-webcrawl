The Twelve Days of Christmas

"The Twelve Days of Christmas" is an English Christmas carol in the form of a cumulative song, in which a series of increasingly numerous gifts are given on each of the twelve days of Christmas (25 December to 5 January). The song, published in England in 1780 without music as a children's rhyme and memory-and-forfeits game, is thought to have French origins. Its melody was standardised by the composer Frederic Austin in 1909, whose arrangement also introduced the drawn-out "five gold rings."

The gifts

Each verse adds a gift: a partridge in a pear tree; two turtle doves; three French hens; four calling birds; five gold rings; six geese a-laying; seven swans a-swimming; eight maids a-milking; nine ladies dancing; ten lords a-leaping; eleven pipers piping; and twelve drummers drumming. Sung in full, the song delivers 364 gifts in total. Early versions differ in detail — the "four calling birds" originally were "colly" (black) birds — and a popular but historically unsupported claim holds that the song encodes Catholic doctrine as a secret catechism.

Christmas Price Index

Since 1984 the US bank PNC has published a tongue-in-cheek "Christmas Price Index," calculating the annual cost of buying all the song's gifts; the figure is widely reported as a piece of seasonal financial journalism.
