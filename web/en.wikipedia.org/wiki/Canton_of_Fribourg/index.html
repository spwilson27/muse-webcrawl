The canton of Fribourg (French: Canton de Fribourg; German: Kanton Freiburg) is one of the 26 cantons forming the Swiss Confederation. It is located in western Switzerland, astride the language border between French- and German-speaking Switzerland, and is officially bilingual. Its capital and largest city is Fribourg.

Geography

The canton lies on the Swiss Plateau, stretching from the shores of Lake Neuchâtel and Lake Murten in the north to the Prealps in the south, where it borders the cantons of Vaud and Bern. The Sarine (Saane) river flows through it, cutting the deep gorge around which the old town of Fribourg was built. The district of Gruyère, in the south, gives its name to the famous cheese.

History

Fribourg was founded in 1157 by Duke Berthold IV of Zähringen and joined the Swiss Confederation in 1481, one of the earliest city cantons admitted after the Burgundian Wars. It remained predominantly Catholic during the Reformation and was long governed by a patrician oligarchy. The canton briefly belonged to the Helvetic Republic before regaining its status in the Act of Mediation of 1803.

Economy and culture

Agriculture — especially dairy farming and cheese-making (Gruyère AOP and Vacherin Fribourgeois) — remains important alongside food processing, machinery, and services. The University of Fribourg, founded in 1889, is Switzerland's only bilingual university.
