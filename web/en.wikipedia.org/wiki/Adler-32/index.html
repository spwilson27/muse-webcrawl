Adler-32

Adler-32 is a checksum algorithm invented by Mark Adler in 1995, used notably in the zlib compression library and the PNG-related data formats.

Algorithm

Two 16-bit sums are maintained modulo 65521 (the largest prime below 216): A is the sum of all bytes plus 1, and B is the sum of running A values. The checksum is (B × 65536) + A. It is fast to compute in software but, for short messages, weaker than CRC-32 at detecting errors; a head-and-tail weakness can allow collisions in small inputs.

Uses

Adler-32 serves as the checksum in zlib streams (RFC 1950) and in rsync-like comparisons in modified form.
