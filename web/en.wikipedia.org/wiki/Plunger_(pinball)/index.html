Plunger (pinball)

In a pinball machine, the plunger is the spring-loaded rod at the front-right of the cabinet used to launch the ball into play. The player pulls the plunger back against its spring, compressing it, and releases it; the plunger strikes the ball resting in the shooter lane, sending it up the lane and around onto the playfield.

Skill shots and design

Because the launch strength depends on how far the plunger is pulled, experienced players use it for the "skill shot": many games award bonuses for launching the ball with just enough force to reach a particular lane, rollover, or target at the top of the playfield. The shooter lane is often marked with a printed scale to help judge pull distance. Some modern machines replace the mechanical plunger with a launch button or an automatic kicker, though traditional plungers remain standard on most games. The plunger tip is usually capped with rubber to strike the ball cleanly, and the rod passes through the cabinet front with a barrel spring visible outside.

History

Spring plungers appeared on the bagatelle-derived countertop games of the early 1930s, replacing cue sticks and direct hand launches, and have been a defining control of pinball ever since, alongside the flipper (introduced in 1947).
