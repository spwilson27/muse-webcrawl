Wallacea

Wallacea is a biogeographical designation for a group of mainly Indonesian islands separated by deep-water straits from both the Australian and Asian continental shelves. Wallacea sits between the Sunda Shelf to the west and the Sahul Shelf (Australia and New Guinea) to the east.

Geography

The region includes Sulawesi, the largest island in Wallacea, together with Lombok, Flores, Sumba, Timor, Halmahera, Buru, Seram, and many smaller islands. The total land area is roughly 350,000 km². Because the surrounding straits remained deep even during glacial maxima, when sea levels fell and exposed the continental shelves, Wallacean islands were never connected by land to either Asia or Australia.

Biota

As a result, Wallacea has a distinctive fauna with high endemism, mixing some Asian and Australasian elements that crossed the water barriers by chance dispersal. Notable endemics include the anoa (dwarf buffalo), the babirusa, Sulawesi's crested macaques, and many marsupial cuscuses near the eastern edge. The region is named after Alfred Russel Wallace, who identified the faunal discontinuity marked by the Wallace Line to the west; the Lydekker Line marks the eastern boundary.

Wallacea is recognised as a biodiversity hotspot, harbouring thousands of endemic plant and animal species, though its forests face pressure from logging, agriculture, and mining.
