Kansas–Nebraska Act

Enacted30 May 1854
SponsorSenator Stephen A. Douglas
EffectCreated Kansas and Nebraska territories; repealed the Missouri Compromise line
Consequence"Bleeding Kansas"; founding of the Republican Party

The Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 was a United States territorial organising act that created the territories of Kansas and Nebraska. Championed by Senator Stephen A. Douglas of Illinois, it allowed settlers in those territories to decide by popular sovereignty whether slavery would be permitted — effectively repealing the Missouri Compromise of 1820, which had banned slavery north of latitude 36°30′.

Background and passage

Douglas wanted the territories organised to facilitate a transcontinental railroad through Chicago and to advance his presidential ambitions, and southern support required opening the area to slavery. The bill passed after bitter debate and was signed by President Franklin Pierce on 30 May 1854, detonating the national controversy over slavery's expansion.

Consequences

Pro- and anti-slavery settlers flooded into Kansas, producing rival governments and years of violence known as Bleeding Kansas. Nationally, the act destroyed the Whig Party, split the Democrats, and prompted the founding of the anti-slavery Republican Party in 1854 — a realignment that set the stage for the American Civil War.
