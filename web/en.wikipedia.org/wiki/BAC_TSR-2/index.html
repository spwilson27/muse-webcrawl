The British Aircraft Corporation TSR-2 was a cancelled Cold War strike and reconnaissance aircraft developed for the Royal Air Force (RAF) in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Its name stood for "Tactical Strike and Reconnaissance, Mach 2". Designed to penetrate a well-defended front line at low level and very high speed to deliver nuclear or conventional weapons, it was among the most advanced aircraft of its day, with a sophisticated terrain-following navigation and attack system.

Development and cancellation

The requirement, General Operational Requirement 339, sought a successor to the Canberra light bomber able to operate from short or damaged runways, fly at Mach 2 at altitude and at low level for long ranges, and perform all-weather reconnaissance. The British Aircraft Corporation (BAC), formed from English Electric and Vickers, received the development contract in 1960. The first prototype, XR219, flew on 27 September 1964 with test pilot Roland Beamont at the controls.

The project was plagued by rising costs, management problems arising from the forced merger of airframe and engine companies, and inter-service politics; the Royal Navy's rival carrier aircraft, the Blackburn Buccaneer, was promoted as a cheaper alternative. On 6 April 1965, Chancellor of the Exchequer James Callaghan announced the TSR-2's cancellation in the Budget speech, arguing the American General Dynamics F-111 would deliver the capability at half the cost. The airframes and jigs were ordered destroyed; only two airframes survive in museums. The cancellation remains one of the most controversial decisions in British aviation history — and, ironically, the F-111 variant ordered for the RAF was itself later cancelled, leaving the role to be filled by the Buccaneer and eventually the Panavia Tornado.

Design

The TSR-2 was a large two-seat aircraft with a small delta wing, blown flaps for short-field performance, and two Bristol Olympus turbojets (a development of the engine family later used in Concorde). Its Ferranti navigation/attack system, sideways-looking radar and reconnaissance pack were intended to give it an autonomous all-weather capability unmatched at the time.
