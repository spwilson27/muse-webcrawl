Haryana is a state in northern India, formed on 1 November 1966 when the Hindi-speaking areas of the former Punjab state were separated. It surrounds the national capital, Delhi, on three sides and shares its capital, Chandigarh, with Punjab. The state has 22 districts and an economy notable for agriculture, automobiles and information technology.

History

The region is identified with the legendary kingdom of the Mahabharata; the plain of Kurukshetra in northern Haryana is where tradition places the great battle and the discourse of the Bhagavad Gita. Haryana saw the three Battles of Panipat (1526, 1556, 1761), which successively reshaped Indian history, and it was a major theatre of the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

Economy

Haryana is one of India's wealthiest states by per-capita income. It is a leading producer of wheat, rice, cotton and milk, and a centre of the automobile industry: Maruti Suzuki's plants at Gurugram and Manesar anchor a large supplier cluster. Gurugram (formerly Gurgaon) has grown into a major hub for IT, finance and corporate headquarters, while the state's proximity to Delhi drives rapid urbanisation along its eastern districts.
