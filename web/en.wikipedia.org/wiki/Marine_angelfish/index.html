Marine angelfishes are perciform fishes of the family Pomacanthidae, found on shallow reefs in the tropical Atlantic, Indian, and Pacific Oceans. The family contains about 86 species in seven genera, many of them brilliantly coloured. They should not be confused with the freshwater angelfish (a cichlid), to which they are not closely related.

Description

Marine angelfish have deep, laterally compressed bodies, small mouths with brush-like teeth, and a characteristic sharp spine at the lower corner of the preopercle — the feature that gives the family its scientific name (poma, lid; akantha, thorn). The largest species, such as the grey angelfish, reach 60 cm, while the pygmy angelfish of the genus Centropyge are under 10 cm. In many species the juveniles are coloured entirely differently from the adults — emperor angelfish juveniles, for instance, are dark blue with concentric white rings.

Behaviour and diet

Angelfish inhabit coral and rocky reefs, generally in water less than 50 m deep. Most feed on sponges, tunicates, algae, and zooplankton; the pygmy angelfish are mainly herbivorous. Larger species are often territorial and may form long-term pairs or harems. The swallowtail angelfish (Genicanthus) are planktivores and are sexually dimorphic.

Aquarium keeping

Many species are prized in the marine aquarium trade for their colour, but larger angelfish are notoriously difficult: they require large tanks, may nip at corals and clam mantles, and depend on sponges in their diet. Pygmy angelfish are hardier and popular with hobbyists.
