Moulay Rashid ibn Sharif (Arabic: مولاي الرشيد بن الشريف), sometimes called Tafiletta, was Sultan of Morocco from 1666 until his death in 1672. He is regarded as the founder of the effective rule of the Alaouite dynasty, which continues to reign in Morocco today.

Rashid was a sharif — a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad through the line of Hasan ibn Ali — from the oasis region of Tafilalt in south-eastern Morocco. After the death of his father, Moulay Sharif, a succession struggle broke out among his sons. Rashid defeated and killed his brother, Moulay Mohammed, at the Battle of Angad in 1664 and consolidated control of eastern Morocco.

Reign

In 1666 Rashid captured Fes, ending the rule of the Dilaite zawiya there, and was proclaimed sultan. Over the following years he campaigned across the country, subduing the northern tribes and taking Marrakesh in 1669. By the end of his reign he had reunified most of Morocco under Alaouite authority for the first time, laying the foundations on which his half-brother and successor, Moulay Ismail, would build.

Rashid died on 9 April 1672 in Marrakesh after falling from his horse. He was succeeded by Moulay Ismail, whose long reign (1672–1727) completed the consolidation of the Alaouite state.
