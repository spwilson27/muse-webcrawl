Rugby

Highest governing bodyWorld Rugby (rugby union); Rugby League International Federation (rugby league)
First played19th century, England
CharacteristicsContact team sport; oval ball; running with the ball in hand
PresenceWorldwide; Rugby World Cup every four years

Rugby is a family of contact team sports that originated in England in the 19th century. The two main forms are rugby union and rugby league, which split in 1895 over the issue of payments to players. Both forms are played with an oval ball on a rectangular field and involve running with the ball in hand and tackling opponents.

History

According to legend, the game began at Rugby School in Warwickshire in 1823, when pupil William Webb Ellis picked up the ball during a football match and ran with it. The first written rules were drawn up at Rugby School in 1845. The Rugby Football Union (RFU) was founded in 1871. In 1895, a dispute over compensation for working-class players led northern clubs to form the Northern Rugby Football Union, which became rugby league, with modified rules including 13 players per side instead of 15 and the play-the-ball restart instead of contested scrums and rucks.

Gameplay

In both codes, the objective is to score more points than the opposition. Points are scored by grounding the ball beyond the opponent's goal line for a try, and by kicking the ball between the goal posts and over the crossbar for goals, penalty goals and drop goals. The ball may be passed backwards or kicked forwards, but never passed forwards by hand. Play is continuous, with tackles used to stop the ball carrier's progress.

Rugby union

Rugby union is played between two teams of 15 players, with set pieces including scrums and line-outs. It is governed internationally by World Rugby. Its premier tournament is the Rugby World Cup, first held in 1987 and won by New Zealand. Major annual competitions include the Six Nations Championship in Europe and The Rugby Championship in the southern hemisphere. Rugby union was historically an amateur sport but became openly professional in 1995.

Rugby league

Rugby league is played with 13 players per side and is faster-paced, with a simpler scoring structure and a limit of six tackles before possession changes hands. It is especially popular in northern England, Australia and New Zealand. Its major club competitions include the Super League in Europe and the National Rugby League (NRL) in Australasia.
