Neapolitan cuisine (Italian: cucina napoletana) is the traditional food of Naples and the Campania region of southern Italy, with roots in ancient Greek and Roman cooking and later Spanish and French influences. It is famous worldwide as the birthplace of pizza and for its pasta dishes, seafood, and pastries.

Signature dishes

The best-known creation is Neapolitan pizza, a soft, thin-centred flatbread baked very briefly in a wood-fired oven, codified by the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (founded 1984) and inscribed by UNESCO in 2017 as Intangible Cultural Heritage. Classic versions include the Margherita (tomato, mozzarella, basil) and the Marinara (tomato, garlic, oregano). Pasta dishes include spaghetti alle vongole, spaghetti alla puttanesca and the slow-cooked ragù napoletano served on Sundays. Street food includes fried pizza, frittatine di pasta and cuoppo (paper cones of fried seafood).

Pastries and coffee

Famous sweets include the shell-shaped sfogliatella, rum-soaked babà, pastiera (a ricotta and wheat-berry tart for Easter) and struffoli. Naples is also noted for its strong espresso culture and for limoncello from Sorrento lemons.

History

Naples' position as a port and capital of the Kingdom of Naples brought together peasant traditions (cucina povera) and aristocratic banquet cooking. Tomatoes and pasta from nearby Gragnano became staples from the 18th century, and emigration spread Neapolitan dishes around the world in the 19th and 20th centuries.
