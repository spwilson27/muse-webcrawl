Louis XIII

Louis XIII (27 September 1601 – 14 May 1643), called the Just, was King of France from 1610 until his death, succeeding his assassinated father Henry IV. His reign, guided by Cardinal Richelieu, strengthened royal absolutism and made France the dominant power in Europe.

Louis XIII
Reign: 14 May 1610 – 14 May 1643
Born: Fontainebleau, 1601
Died: Paris, 1643
House: Bourbon
Spouse: Anne of Austria
Chief minister: Cardinal Richelieu

Reign

After the regency of his mother Marie de' Medici, Louis asserted personal rule in 1617. With Richelieu as chief minister from 1624, the crown suppressed Huguenot political power (Siege of La Rochelle, 1628), curbed the nobility, and intervened against the Habsburgs in the Thirty Years' War.

Family and succession

His marriage to Anne of Austria produced the future Louis XIV, born in 1638 after 23 years without an heir. Louis died months after Richelieu.
