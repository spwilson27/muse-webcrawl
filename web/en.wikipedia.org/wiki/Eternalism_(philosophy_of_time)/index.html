Eternalism (philosophy of time)

Eternalism is a philosophical approach to the ontological nature of time, which takes the view that all existence in time is equally real, as opposed to presentism or the growing block universe theory of time, in which at least the future is held not to be real. Some forms of eternalism give time a similar ontology to that of space, as a dimension, with different times being as real as different places; such theories are often called block time or the block universe, because they describe spacetime as a single unchanging four-dimensional "block".

Arguments

Eternalism is commonly motivated by the theory of special relativity: because simultaneity is relative to the observer's frame of reference, there is no single, privileged "now" slicing the universe into past and future. What is future for one observer may already be past for another, which suggests that all events must be equally real. The philosopher Hilary Putnam and others developed this reasoning into the Rietdijk–Putnam argument.

Supporters also point to the apparent "passage" of time as an illusion produced by the asymmetry of entropy and the structure of memory: observers at each moment remember earlier moments and not later ones, creating the impression of flow within a static block.

Criticism

Critics object that eternalism conflicts with the experience of temporal passage and with ideas of free will and genuine change. Presentists argue that only the present exists and that relativity can be accommodated by a privileged frame or by revising the interpretation of the physics. The debate remains central to the philosophy of space and time.
