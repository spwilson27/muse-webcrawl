Antonio Cánovas del Castillo

Antonio Cánovas del Castillo (8 February 1828 – 8 August 1897) was a Spanish politician and historian, chief architect of the Bourbon Restoration of 1874 and the dominant political figure of late 19th-century Spain. He served six times as Prime Minister of Spain.

Career

Born in Málaga, Cánovas entered politics under the Liberal Union, drafted the Manzanares Manifesto of 1854, and held ministerial posts under Isabella II. After the revolution of 1868 he organised the Alfonsist movement that prepared the restoration of the monarchy in the person of Alfonso XII, proclaimed king in December 1874 following the pronunciamiento of General Martínez Campos.

The Restoration system

Cánovas designed the constitutional settlement of 1876: a conservative constitution, a two-party system in which his Conservative Party alternated in power with the Liberals of Sagasta (the turno pacífico), and elections managed through local bosses (caciques). The system brought decades of stability at the cost of genuine democracy. Cánovas also directed policy during the Cuban insurrection of 1895, supporting the harsh reconcentration policy of General Weyler.

Assassination

On 8 August 1897, while at the spa of Santa Águeda in Mondragón, Cánovas was shot dead by the Italian anarchist Michele Angiolillo, who claimed to avenge anarchists tortured at Montjuïc. He is remembered as one of the most influential — and controversial — statesmen of modern Spain.
