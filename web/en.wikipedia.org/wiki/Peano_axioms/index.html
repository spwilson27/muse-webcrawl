Peano axioms

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Illustration of the successor idea: a chain of numbers 0, 1, 2, 3 each pointing to the next]

The Peano axioms, also called the Peano postulates, are axioms for the natural numbers presented by the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano in 1889. They define the naturals by a starting element (0, or 1 in Peano's original), a successor operation, and the principle of induction.

In modern form the axioms say: 0 is a natural number; every natural number has a unique successor; 0 is no number's successor; different numbers have different successors; and any property true of 0 and inherited by successors is true of all natural numbers.

Significance

The axioms turned arithmetic into a formal system that could itself be studied mathematically — a key step toward formalism, logic and eventually Gödel's incompleteness theorems, which showed any consistent system strong enough to contain Peano arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency.

Richard Dedekind had published an equivalent characterisation in 1888; Peano's simpler notation is why his name attached to the axioms.
