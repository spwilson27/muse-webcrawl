Agilent Technologies, Inc. is an American public research, development and manufacturing company established in 1999 as a spin-off from Hewlett-Packard. Headquartered in Santa Clara, California, Agilent provides analytical instruments, software, services and consumables for laboratories worldwide, serving the life sciences, diagnostics, and applied chemical markets.

History

Agilent was created when Hewlett-Packard separated its test and measurement, chemical analysis, and medical products businesses from its computing and printing operations. The name "Agilent" was chosen to suggest agility. Its initial public offering in November 1999 was then the largest in Silicon Valley history. The company's roots lie in the original Hewlett-Packard founded in 1939, whose first product was an audio oscillator.

In 2014, Agilent spun off its electronic measurement business as Keysight Technologies, leaving Agilent focused on life sciences, diagnostics, and applied markets. Agilent has since grown through acquisitions, including the Danish cancer diagnostics company Dako and the bioprocessing firm BioTek Instruments.

Products and markets

Agilent's products include gas and liquid chromatographs, mass spectrometers, atomic and molecular spectroscopy instruments, vacuum systems, microarrays, and laboratory informatics software. Its instruments are used in pharmaceutical development, clinical diagnostics, food safety testing, environmental analysis, and academic research. The company operates in more than 100 countries and employs around 18,000 people.
