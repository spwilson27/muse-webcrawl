ZANU–PF

The Zimbabwe African National Union – Patriotic Front (ZANU–PF) is a political party that has governed Zimbabwe continuously since independence in 1980. It was formed in 1987 by the merger of Robert Mugabe's ZANU with Joshua Nkomo's ZAPU, and its military wing ZANLA fought in the Rhodesian Bush War.

History

ZANU split from ZAPU in 1963 and, based among the Shona majority, waged guerrilla war from Mozambique against Ian Smith's Rhodesian government. At the Lancaster House settlement ZANU won the 1980 election and Mugabe became prime minister. Relations with ZAPU collapsed into the Gukurahundi violence in Matabeleland (1983–87), ended by the Unity Accord that created ZANU–PF.

Rule and controversies

The party oversaw early gains in education and health, but from 2000 its fast-track land reform, economic mismanagement and contested elections under the opposition MDC brought hyperinflation, sanctions and international isolation. In November 2017 the military intervened, Mugabe was removed as party leader and resigned the presidency; Emmerson Mnangagwa succeeded him, promising reform under the slogan of a “new dispensation”, and ZANU–PF retained power in the 2018 and 2023 elections, whose conduct opposition groups disputed.

Organisation

ZANU–PF is led by a President and First Secretary, a Politburo and Central Committee, with Women's and Youth Leagues and strong ties to the security services and liberation-war veterans.
