Generative artificial intelligence (generative AI, GenAI) is a subset of artificial intelligence that uses generative models to produce text, images, videos, audio, or other media in response to prompts. Generative AI systems learn the patterns and structures of their training data and use them to generate new data with similar characteristics.

History

Early generative systems included rule-based programs such as ELIZA (1966) and statistical language models. The deep learning era brought variational autoencoders (2013), generative adversarial networks (2014), and the transformer architecture (2017), which underlies large language models such as the GPT series. Diffusion models became prominent for image generation around 2020–2022. The public release of ChatGPT in November 2022 triggered widespread adoption and investment, followed by systems such as GPT-4, DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and video generators.

Modalities

  • Text: large language models generating prose, code, and dialogue.
  • Images: text-to-image models using diffusion or autoregressive methods.
  • Audio and music: speech synthesis and music generation.
  • Video: text-to-video and video editing models.
  • Science: generating molecular structures, protein designs, and materials.

Concerns and regulation

Generative AI has raised concerns about misinformation and deepfakes, copyright and training-data provenance, bias, job displacement, academic integrity, and the environmental cost of training large models. Governments have responded with measures such as the European Union's AI Act, and litigation over training data is ongoing in several jurisdictions.
