Who Framed Roger Rabbit is a 1988 American live-action/animated fantasy comedy film directed by Robert Zemeckis, produced by Steven Spielberg's Amblin Entertainment and released by Touchstone Pictures. It is loosely based on Gary K. Wolf's 1981 novel Who Censored Roger Rabbit?. The film uniquely combined live action with hand-drawn animation, bringing together cartoon characters from competing studios — including Mickey Mouse and Bugs Bunny in a famous shared scene — for the first and only time.

Plot

In a 1947 Hollywood where cartoon characters ("toons") live alongside humans, down-on-his-luck private detective Eddie Valiant (Bob Hoskins) is hired to investigate rumours about Jessica Rabbit, the wife of toon star Roger Rabbit. When studio executive Marvin Acme is murdered and Roger is framed, Valiant must overcome his hatred of toons to uncover a conspiracy by the sinister Judge Doom (Christopher Lloyd) to destroy Toontown for a freeway project.

Production and legacy

The animation, directed by Richard Williams, was drawn by hand to interact convincingly with live actors through complex camera moves and realistic lighting and shadows. The film was a critical and commercial success, won four Academy Awards, revived interest in American animation — helping ignite the Disney Renaissance — and remains a landmark of visual effects craftsmanship.
