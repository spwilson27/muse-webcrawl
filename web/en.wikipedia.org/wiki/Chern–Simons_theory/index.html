Chern–Simons theory is a three-dimensional topological quantum field theory introduced into physics by Edward Witten in 1989. Its action is built from the Chern–Simons form, a secondary characteristic class constructed by Shiing-Shen Chern and James Simons. Because the action depends only on a connection and not on any metric, the theory's observables are topological invariants of three-manifolds and links.

The action

For a gauge group G with connection A on a closed oriented 3-manifold M, the Chern–Simons action is proportional to the integral over M of tr(A ∧ dA + (2/3) A ∧ A ∧ A). Gauge invariance of the quantum theory requires the coupling, the "level" k, to be an integer. The classical equations of motion state that the connection is flat.

Knot invariants

Witten showed that expectation values of Wilson loop operators in Chern–Simons theory with gauge group SU(2) reproduce the Jones polynomial of knots and links, giving knot theory a physical origin and earning Witten the Fields Medal in 1990. The theory also yields invariants of 3-manifolds related to the work of Reshetikhin and Turaev.

Applications

Chern–Simons theory describes the fractional quantum Hall effect, appears in string theory and the AdS/CFT correspondence, and underlies proposals for topological quantum computation, where its anyonic excitations would store information nonlocally.
