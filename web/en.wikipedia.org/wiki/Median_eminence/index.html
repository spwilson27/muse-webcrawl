Median eminence


The median eminence at the base of the hypothalamus

The median eminence is generally defined as the portion of the ventral hypothalamus from which the infundibulum (pituitary stalk) arises. It is one of the circumventricular organs of the brain — regions where the blood–brain barrier is absent — and forms a crucial interface between the hypothalamus and the anterior pituitary gland.

Function

Neurosecretory neurons of the hypothalamus release releasing and inhibiting hormones — including TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH, somatostatin, and dopamine — at the median eminence. There they enter the fenestrated capillaries of the hypophyseal portal system, which carries them directly to the anterior pituitary, where they regulate the secretion of pituitary hormones. Magnocellular axons pass through the internal zone of the median eminence on their way to the posterior pituitary.

Anatomy

The median eminence lies in the tuber cinereum of the hypothalamic floor, continuous with the infundibulum. Its external zone contains the nerve terminals and portal capillary loops; its blood supply derives from the superior hypophyseal arteries. Specialised glial cells called tanycytes line its ventricular surface and help regulate hormone release and barrier properties.
