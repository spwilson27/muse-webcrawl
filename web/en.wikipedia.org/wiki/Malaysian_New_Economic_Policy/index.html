The Malaysian New Economic Policy (NEP; Malay: Dasar Ekonomi Baru, DEB) was an economic restructuring programme launched by the government of Malaysia in 1971, following the racial riots of 13 May 1969. Its twin objectives were the eradication of poverty irrespective of race, and the "restructuring of society" to eliminate the identification of race with economic function — in practice by raising the economic position of the Bumiputera majority (ethnic Malays and other indigenous peoples) through affirmative action in education, employment, and corporate ownership. Although the NEP formally ran until 1990, its successor policies and its core preferences have continued in modified form ever since.

Objectives and targets

The NEP set two prongs and a set of twenty-year targets. Poverty, which affected about half of peninsular households in 1970, was to be sharply reduced for all communities. Restructuring aimed to raise Bumiputera ownership of share capital from about 2.4% in 1970 to 30% by 1990, with 40% held by other Malaysians and 30% by foreigners; employment patterns in modern sectors were likewise to reflect the racial composition of the population, and preferential quotas applied to university admissions, civil service and military recruitment, government contracts, licences, and subsidised credit. Implementing instruments included trust agencies such as MARA and Permodalan Nasional Berhad (PNB), created to hold and build Bumiputera equity collectively.

Outcomes

Under the NEP Malaysia achieved rapid growth, averaging around 6–7% per year, and official poverty fell dramatically, from roughly 49% of households in 1970 to about 15–17% by 1990, alongside the creation of a substantial Malay middle and professional class. Measured Bumiputera corporate equity rose to around 19–20% by 1990 on official methodology — short of the 30% target, whose measurement remains disputed. Critics point to patronage and rent-seeking in the allocation of contracts and equity, benefits skewed toward well-connected elites, and the emigration or disaffection of many non-Bumiputera Malaysians; defenders credit the policy with preventing a recurrence of 1969-style violence and with building national cohesion alongside growth.

Successors

The NEP was succeeded in 1991 by the National Development Policy under Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad, which softened some ethnic targets in favour of growth, and later by the National Vision Policy and various transformation programmes. The underlying system of Bumiputera preferences, grounded in Article 153 of the Federal Constitution, remains a central and contested feature of Malaysian politics and economic life.
