Miꞌkmaq

Miꞌkmaq is an orthographic rendering of the name of the Mi'kmaq people, an Indigenous people of the Atlantic Provinces of Canada and the northeastern United States, using the modifier letter turned comma (ꞌ, U+A78C in its capital-adjacent form appearing here as part of the spelling) rather than an apostrophe to mark the glottal element of the name.

Orthography

The Mi'kmaq language has been written in several systems over the centuries, including the Mi'kmaq hieroglyphic writing adapted in the 17th century, the Rand orthography, the Francis–Smith orthography and the Listuguj (Lexicon) orthography. In Francis–Smith spelling the name of the people is written Mi'kmaq, where the apostrophe marks a schwa-like reduced vowel; some modern renderings substitute the saltillo-like letter ꞌ for the apostrophe, producing Miꞌkmaq. The singular form is Mi'kmaw (Miꞌkmaw), and the language itself is called Mi'kmawi'simk.

The people

The Mi'kmaq (Miꞌkmaq) are an Eastern Algonquian people whose homeland, Mi'kma'ki (Miꞌkmaꞌki), spans present-day Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, parts of Newfoundland and Quebec's Gaspé Peninsula, and northern Maine. They concluded Peace and Friendship Treaties with the British Crown in the 18th century and retain recognised treaty rights in Canada.
