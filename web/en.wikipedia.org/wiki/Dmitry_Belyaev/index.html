Dmitry Belyaev

Dmitry Konstantinovich Belyaev (Russian: Дмитрий Константинович Беляев; 17 July 1917 – 14 November 1985) was a Soviet geneticist and academician who directed the Institute of Cytology and Genetics in Novosibirsk from 1959 until his death.

Fox domestication experiment

Belyaev is best known for the long-running farm-fox experiment, begun in 1952, in which silver foxes (Vulpes vulpes) were bred solely for tameness. Within a few generations the selected foxes began to show traits associated with domestication — floppy ears, piebald coats, curled tails, shorter snouts, and changes in breeding season — lending support to his hypothesis of destabilizing selection and the idea of a domestication syndrome driven by selection on behaviour and the neural crest / endocrine system.

The experiment continues today under his successor Lyudmila Trut and is regarded as one of the longest-running experiments in evolutionary biology.
