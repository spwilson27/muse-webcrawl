"For What It's Worth" is a song written by Stephen Stills and performed by the American–Canadian rock group Buffalo Springfield. Released as a single in early 1967, it reached No. 7 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became one of the defining protest songs of the 1960s.

Background

Stills wrote the song in response to the Sunset Strip curfew riots of November 1966 in Los Angeles, when police clashed with young people protesting a curfew and the planned demolition of the Pandora's Box nightclub. Although written about a local confrontation, its refrain — "stop, children, what's that sound / everybody look what's going down" — gave it a universal resonance, and it was widely adopted as an anthem of the counterculture and opposition to the Vietnam War.

Legacy

The song was added to the band's debut album on later pressings and was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. It has been covered by many artists and frequently appears in films about the 1960s, including Forrest Gump. See also Buffalo Springfield.
