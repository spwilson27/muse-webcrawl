Robert Catesby

Robert Catesby (c. 1572 – 8 November 1605) was the leader of a group of English Catholics who planned the failed Gunpowder Plot of 1605. Born in Warwickshire, Catesby was educated at Oxford but left before taking his degree to avoid swearing the Oath of Supremacy. Persecuted for his Catholic faith and fined for recusancy, he became increasingly radicalised and conceived the plan to blow up the House of Lords during the State Opening of Parliament, killing King James I and much of the Protestant aristocracy, in the hope of restoring a Catholic monarch to the throne.

Early life

Catesby was the only surviving son of Sir William Catesby of Lapworth in Warwickshire and Anne Throckmorton. His family had suffered heavily for its Catholicism: his father was imprisoned several times for recusancy, and his mother came from the staunchly Catholic Throckmorton family. Educated at Gloucester Hall, Oxford (later Worcester College), Catesby left without a degree rather than take the oath required of graduates.

Conspiracy

In 1601 Catesby took part in the Essex Rebellion, for which he was wounded and fined 4,000 marks — a ruinous sum that forced him to sell the Chastleton estate. Disillusioned with James I's failure to improve the lot of Catholics, by early 1604 he had devised the gunpowder scheme and recruited Thomas Wintour, John Wright, Thomas Percy and Guy Fawkes, later joined by others including his cousin Francis Tresham.

Death

After the plot was discovered on 5 November 1605, Catesby fled London with several companions. At Holbeche House in Staffordshire on 8 November, he was shot and killed by pursuers led by the Sheriff of Worcestershire. His body was later exhumed and his head displayed in London.
