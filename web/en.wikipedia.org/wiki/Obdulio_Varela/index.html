Obdulio Jacinto Muiños Varela (20 September 1917 – 2 August 1996) was a Uruguayan football player. He was the captain of the Uruguay national team that won the 1950 FIFA World Cup after beating Brazil in the decisive final round match popularly known as the Maracanazo. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest captains in football history and was nicknamed El Negro Jefe ("The Black Chief").

Career

Varela played most of his club career with Peñarol, winning numerous Uruguayan league titles. As a powerful centre-half he was the emotional leader of the national side. At the 1950 World Cup in Brazil, before the decisive match against the hosts at the Maracanã, he famously rallied his team-mates against the intimidating home crowd, reportedly telling them: "Don't think about all those people; don't look up. The game is played down below."

When Brazil took the lead, Varela famously slowed the game down by disputing the goal with the referee, cooling the atmosphere; Uruguay equalised and then scored the winner through Alcides Ghiggia, silencing nearly 200,000 spectators. Varela also played at the 1954 World Cup, where Uruguay finished fourth.

Legacy

Varela remains a national hero in Uruguay, symbolising the garra charrúa — the fighting spirit of Uruguayan football. Despite his fame he lived modestly and declined much of the money offered to him after 1950.
