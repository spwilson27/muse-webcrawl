Brecon

Brecon (Welsh: Aberhonddu)
County: Powys, Wales
Population: about 8,000

Brecon (Welsh: Aberhonddu) is a market and cathedral town in Powys, Mid Wales. It lies at the confluence of the River Honddu and the River Usk, at the northern edge of the Brecon Beacons National Park (Bannau Brycheiniog). Historically the county town of Brecknockshire, Brecon is known for its cathedral, its military associations, and as a centre for walking and outdoor pursuits.

History

The town grew up around a Norman castle founded by Bernard de Neufmarché in the late 11th century, near the site of the earlier Welsh kingdom of Brycheiniog. The Benedictine priory church became Brecon Cathedral when the Diocese of Swansea and Brecon was created in 1923. During the 19th century the Brecknock and Abergavenny Canal linked Brecon to the industrial valleys of South Wales.

Military connections

Brecon has long been a garrison town: the barracks in the town house the Regimental Museum of the Royal Welsh, and the surrounding countryside, including the Sennybridge training area, is used extensively by the British Army, notably for infantry training and selection courses in the Beacons.

Culture and geography

The Brecon Jazz Festival, founded in 1984, brought the town international attention. Pen y Fan, the highest peak in South Wales at 886 m, rises a few miles south of the town. The Monmouthshire and Brecon Canal ends in a basin at the town centre.
