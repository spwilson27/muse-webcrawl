A constructible polygon is a regular polygon that can be constructed with compass and straightedge. Equivalently, a regular n-gon is constructible if and only if the central angle 2π/n is constructible, i.e. cos(2π/n) can be expressed using integers and square roots.

Conditions for constructibility

In 1796, the 19-year-old Carl Friedrich Gauss proved that a regular 17-gon is constructible, the first advance in two millennia. He then derived a general condition, completed by Pierre Wantzel in 1837:

A regular n-gon is constructible with compass and straightedge if and only if n is the product of a power of 2 and any number of distinct Fermat primes — primes of the form 22k + 1.

The known Fermat primes are 3, 5, 17, 257, and 65537. Hence the constructible regular polygons with odd numbers of sides begin: triangle, pentagon, 15-gon, 17-gon, 51-gon, 85-gon, 255-gon, 257-gon, ...

Known constructions

Explicit constructions exist for the triangle and square (known to Euclid), the pentagon (Euclid), the 17-gon (Gauss), the 257-gon (Richelot, 1832), and the 65537-gon (Hermes, whose manuscript fills a suitcase-sized box).
