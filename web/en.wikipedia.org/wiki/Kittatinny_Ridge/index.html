Kittatinny Ridge

The Kittatinny Ridge, or Kittatinny Mountain, is a long ridge in the Ridge-and-Valley province of the Appalachian Mountains, running roughly 185 miles from southern New York through northwestern New Jersey into Pennsylvania, where it continues as Blue Mountain.

Geography

Formed of resistant Shawangunk conglomerate and sandstone, the ridge reaches 1,803 feet at High Point, the highest elevation in New Jersey. The Delaware River cuts through it at the Delaware Water Gap. Much of the ridge is protected within the Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area, Stokes State Forest and other public lands, and the Appalachian Trail follows its crest.

Ecology

The ridge is a major migration corridor for raptors, monitored at hawk watches such as Hawk Mountain in Pennsylvania.
