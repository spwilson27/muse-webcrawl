The Ringstrasse (German: Ringstraße, lit. "ring road") is a grand circular boulevard in Vienna, Austria, encircling the Innere Stadt (First District, the historic old town). Built in the second half of the 19th century on the site of the demolished city fortifications, it is lined with many of Vienna's most famous monumental buildings and is regarded as one of the great urban planning achievements of its era.

History

On 20 December 1857, Emperor Franz Joseph I issued his famous decree beginning with the words "Es ist Mein Wille" ("It is my will"), ordering the demolition of the city walls and moats and the construction of a broad boulevard in their place. The freed land was sold through a city expansion fund to finance the monumental public buildings erected along the new street.

An urban planning competition was held, and the winning designs were combined into a plan that combined parkland, gardens and representative architecture. The Ringstrasse was officially opened by the Emperor on 1 May 1865, although construction of its great buildings continued for decades.

Architecture

The buildings along the Ring were designed in the historicist style known as Ringstraßenstil, in which each institution received a facade evoking the era associated with its purpose: neo-Gothic for the Votive Church and the Rathaus (city hall), neo-Renaissance for the University and the Academy of Fine Arts, and neoclassicism for the Parliament, with its statue of Pallas Athena.

Notable buildings along the Ring include the Vienna State Opera (opened 1869), the Hofburg palace complex, the Kunsthistorisches Museum and Naturhistorisches Museum facing each other across Maria-Theresien-Platz, the Burgtheater, the Vienna Stock Exchange, and the Palais Ephrussi. Broad green spaces such as the Burggarten, Volksgarten and Stadtpark break up the built frontage.

Sections

The ring is made up of individually named segments, including Stubenring, Parkring, Schubertring, Kärntner Ring, Opernring, Burgring, Dr.-Karl-Renner-Ring, Universitätsring and Schottenring. Trams circle the ring on lines that historically formed a defining feature of the cityscape; since 2009 through services have run as the "Ring Tram" services of lines 1 and 2.
