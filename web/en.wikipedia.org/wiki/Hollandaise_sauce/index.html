Hollandaise sauce

[Image: Hollandaise sauce being spooned over eggs Benedict]

Overview

Hollandaise sauce is an emulsion of egg yolk, melted butter and lemon juice (or a white-wine or vinegar reduction), seasoned with salt and sometimes cayenne. It is one of the five mother sauces in French cuisine. Despite the name, the sauce is French; the name may honour Holland, source of fine butter, and similar butter sauces appear in Dutch cooking.

Preparation and uses

Hollandaise is made by whisking yolks with the acid over gentle heat until thickened, then slowly incorporating warm melted butter. It breaks easily if overheated. It is famous as the sauce of eggs Benedict and accompanies asparagus, fish and vegetables; its derivatives include béarnaise (with tarragon and shallot), maltaise (blood orange) and choron.
