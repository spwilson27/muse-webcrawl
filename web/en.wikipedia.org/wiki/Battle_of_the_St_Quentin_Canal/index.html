Battle of the St Quentin Canal

The Battle of the St Quentin Canal was a pivotal battle of the Hundred Days Offensive of the First World War, fought from 29 September to 10 October 1918. British, Australian and American forces under General Sir Henry Rawlinson breached the formidable German Hindenburg Line defences along the St Quentin Canal in northern France — a breakthrough widely regarded as one of the finest Allied achievements of the war.

Background

The St Quentin Canal formed the backbone of the Hindenburg Line in this sector: a deep cutting with steep banks, the canal tunnel at Bellicourt, and dense belts of wire, concrete pillboxes and machine-gun positions. The British Fourth Army's plan, largely devised by Australian Corps commander Lieutenant-General John Monash, assigned the assault to two American divisions stiffened by Australian troops, supported by tanks and a massive artillery barrage.

The battle

The preliminary bombardment began on 26 September. In dense fog on 29 September the American 27th and 30th Divisions attacked; the 30th broke through at Bellicourt while the 27th was held up by un-cleared strongpoints. Australian divisions then passed through to exploit the breach. A famous episode saw men of the British 46th (North Midland) Division, using lifebelts and rafts, storm the canal cutting itself and seize the Riqueval Bridge intact before German engineers could blow it.

Aftermath

The breach of the Hindenburg Line shattered German hopes of holding a strong defensive position into 1919. German forces fell back toward the Selle and the Sambre, and within six weeks the Armistice of 11 November 1918 ended the war.
