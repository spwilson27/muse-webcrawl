Somerset is a ceremonial county in South West England, bordered by Gloucestershire and Bristol to the north-east, Wiltshire to the east, Dorset to the south-east and Devon to the south-west, with a coastline on the Bristol Channel. Its county town is Taunton and its best-known city is Bath, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its Roman baths and Georgian architecture.

Geography

The county's landscape ranges from the Mendip Hills and Quantock Hills to the low-lying, flood-prone Somerset Levels and Moors, and part of Exmoor in the west. Major rivers include the Avon, Parrett, Brue and Tone. The Bristol Channel coast experiences one of the largest tidal ranges in the world.

History

Somerset contains some of Britain's earliest evidence of human occupation, including Cheddar Gorge, as well as Neolithic trackways such as the Sweet Track in the Levels. The Romans exploited the lead mines of the Mendips and built the baths at Bath (Aquae Sulis). In the Anglo-Saxon period Somerset formed part of the kingdom of Wessex; King Alfred sheltered in the marshes at Athelney before defeating the Danes in 878. The county saw the last pitched battle on English soil, Sedgemoor, in 1685, during the Monmouth Rebellion. Historically the county was also associated with coal mining in the Somerset Coalfield and with the woollen cloth trade.

Economy and culture

Somerset is strongly associated with cider and farmhouse Cheddar cheese, which originated in the village of Cheddar. Agriculture and tourism remain important, alongside the Hinkley Point nuclear power stations on the coast. The county hosts the Glastonbury Festival near Pilton, one of the world's largest performing-arts festivals. The ceremonial county includes the unitary authorities of Somerset, North Somerset, and Bath and North East Somerset.
