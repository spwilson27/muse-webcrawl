Lloyds Banking Group plc is a British financial institution formed through the acquisition of HBOS by Lloyds TSB in 2009. It is one of the United Kingdom's largest financial services organisations, with around 27 million customers, and operates mainly in the UK under brands including Lloyds Bank, Halifax, Bank of Scotland, and Scottish Widows. Its history traces to the founding of Lloyds Bank in Birmingham in 1765 and of the Bank of Scotland in 1695.

History

Lloyds Bank grew from Taylors and Lloyds, a Birmingham private bank of 1765, expanding through hundreds of acquisitions into one of Britain's "Big Four" clearing banks. HBOS was itself formed in 2001 from Halifax and Bank of Scotland. During the 2008 financial crisis, Lloyds TSB agreed a government-brokered takeover of the stricken HBOS; the combined group required a £20.3 billion state recapitalisation, leaving the UK government with a 43% stake. Competition concerns led to the divestment of over 600 branches as TSB Bank in 2013–2014.

Return to private ownership

Under chief executive António Horta-Osório (2011–2021), the group repaid its state support and refocused on UK retail and commercial banking; the government sold its final shares in May 2017, returning Lloyds fully to private ownership. The group was heavily affected by the payment protection insurance (PPI) mis-selling scandal, for which it set aside more than £20 billion in compensation — the largest share of any UK bank.

Operations

Lloyds is the UK's largest mortgage lender and operates the largest retail branch and digital banking networks in the country, including the Halifax, the UK's biggest savings and mortgage brand, and Scottish Widows in pensions and insurance. Charlie Nunn became chief executive in 2021.
