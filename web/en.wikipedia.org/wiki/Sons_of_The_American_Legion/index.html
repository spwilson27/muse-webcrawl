Sons of The American Legion

The Sons of The American Legion (SAL) is an organisation of male descendants of veterans of the United States Armed Forces, affiliated with The American Legion. Founded in 1932, the SAL is a program of the Legion, not a separate corporation, and its members support the Legion's aims of service to veterans, their families, and their communities. The organisation claims over 360,000 members organised into squadrons attached to local Legion posts across the United States and abroad.

History

The Sons of The American Legion was authorised by the Legion's 14th National Convention in Portland, Oregon, in September 1932, and the first squadrons were chartered soon afterward. The idea grew from junior organisations that had formed informally in the 1920s to involve veterans' sons in Legion activities. Membership grew substantially after the Second World War as the children of the new generation of veterans came of age.

Eligibility and structure

Membership is open to all male descendants — including sons, grandsons, great-grandsons, and adopted and step-descendants — of members of The American Legion, and of veterans who died in service or after honourable discharge and who would have been eligible for Legion membership. Members join a squadron chartered through a local post; squadrons are grouped into detachments at the state level and into the national organisation, which holds an annual National Convention alongside the Legion's.

Activities

Squadrons raise funds for veterans' causes, the American Legion Child Welfare Foundation, scholarships, and community programs. The SAL is historically the largest contributor to the Child Welfare Foundation and supports programs such as Boys State, American Legion Baseball, flag education and retirement ceremonies, and assistance at veterans' hospitals. Together with the Legion and the American Legion Auxiliary, the SAL forms part of the "Legion Family."
