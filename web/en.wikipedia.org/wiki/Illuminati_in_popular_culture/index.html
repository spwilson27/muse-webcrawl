Illuminati in popular culture

The Illuminati — referring principally to the Bavarian Illuminati, a secret society founded in 1776 by Adam Weishaupt — have become a staple of popular culture, appearing in literature, film, television, music, games and internet conspiracy theories. In fiction, the name is commonly applied to any shadowy cabal secretly controlling world events, a usage popularised by Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson's satirical Illuminatus! trilogy (1975) and by Dan Brown's novel Angels & Demons (2000).

Literature

Beyond The Illuminatus! Trilogy and Brown's novels, the Illuminati appear in Umberto Eco's Foucault's Pendulum (1988), which satirises conspiracy thinking, and in numerous thrillers and alternate-history works. In Marvel Comics, the Illuminati are a secret council of superheroes.

Film and television

The society features in films such as Lara Croft: Tomb Raider (2001), where it serves as the antagonist organisation, and Angels & Demons (2009). Television series including Gargoyles and various documentaries and drama series have used Illuminati-like organisations as plot devices.

Music and internet culture

Since the 1990s, and especially with the rise of social media, references to the Illuminati have proliferated in hip hop and pop music, where the term is often used ironically or as a marketing hook. Musicians such as Jay-Z, Beyoncé and Madonna have been jokingly or seriously accused by online conspiracy communities of membership, and Illuminati imagery — the Eye of Providence, pyramids, triangles — has become a widespread internet meme.

Video games

The Illuminati are a playable faction in the Deus Ex series and appear in The Secret World, Assassin's Creed lore and many other titles as hidden manipulators of history.
