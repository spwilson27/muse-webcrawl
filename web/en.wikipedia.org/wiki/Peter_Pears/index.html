Peter Pears

BornPeter Neville Luard Pears, June 22, 1910, Farnham, Surrey, England
DiedApril 3, 1986, Aldeburgh, Suffolk, England
OccupationOperatic tenor

Sir Peter Neville Luard Pears (June 22, 1910 – April 3, 1986) was an English tenor, the lifelong professional and personal partner of the composer Benjamin Britten. Britten wrote many of his greatest vocal works with Pears's distinctive high, light voice in mind, and Pears created the leading tenor roles in almost all of Britten's operas.

Career

Pears studied at Hertford College, Oxford, and the Royal College of Music, and sang with the BBC Singers and the New English Singers before meeting Britten in 1937. The two spent the war years in North America, giving recitals, and returned to England in 1942. Pears created the title role of Peter Grimes in 1945, and subsequently the roles of the Male Chorus in The Rape of Lucretia, Albert Herring, Captain Vere in Billy Budd, the Madwoman in Curlew River, and Aschenbach in Death in Venice (1973).

Legacy

With Britten, Pears co-founded the English Opera Group and the Aldeburgh Festival in 1948, as well as the Britten–Pears School for Advanced Musical Studies at Snape Maltings. His many recordings with Britten, including Schubert and Schumann song cycles and Britten's War Requiem — in which he sang at the 1962 premiere — document one of the most significant composer–performer partnerships of the twentieth century. He was knighted in 1978.
