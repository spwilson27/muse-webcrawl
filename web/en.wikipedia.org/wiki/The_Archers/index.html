The Archers

The Archers is a British radio drama programme on BBC Radio 4 — the world's longest-running radio soap opera and the world's longest-running drama overall, having broadcast its first episode on 1 January 1951. It is set in the fictional village of Ambridge in the fictional county of Borsetshire, in the English Midlands.

History

The programme was devised by Godfrey Baseley and was originally commissioned as part of an effort to disseminate agricultural information to farmers following the Second World War. The pilot episodes aired on the BBC Midlands Home Service during Whitsun week 1950, and the serial went national on New Year's Day 1951. At its peak in the 1950s the programme attracted some 20 million listeners.

The story centres on the Archer family at Brookfield Farm and the residents of Ambridge, including the Aldridges, Grundys, Carters and Snells. Episodes run for around thirteen minutes and are first broadcast at 19:02, with a repeat the following afternoon (except Saturdays) and an omnibus edition on Sunday mornings.

Setting and characters

Ambridge is modelled loosely on the village of Inkberrow in Worcestershire. Regular locations include Brookfield Farm, Bridge Farm (run organically by members of the Archer family), Home Farm, the village pub The Bull, the village shop and post office, and St Stephen's Church.

The programme's theme tune is "Barwick Green", a maypole gavotte by Arthur Wood, played in the omnibus edition by Sidney Torch and his orchestra.

Cultural impact

The Archers has become a fixture of British cultural life, notable for tackling contemporary rural and social issues, and for famous storylines such as the death of Grace Archer in a fire in 1955, broadcast on the night commercial television launched in Britain. In 2010–2011 the "Nigel fell" storyline and in 2016 the coercive-control storyline involving Helen and Rob Titchener drew widespread public attention.
