Socialization

Field: Sociology, psychology

Socialization (British spelling: socialisation) is the lifelong process through which individuals learn and internalise the norms, values, behaviours and social skills of their society, enabling them to function as members of it. It converts a biological infant into a social person.

Types and agents

Primary socialisation occurs in childhood within the family; secondary socialisation follows through schools, peer groups, workplaces and media. Anticipatory and re-socialisation occur when people prepare for, or are radically retrained into, new roles — as in total institutions described by Erving Goffman.

Theories

George Herbert Mead explained the self emerging through play and the "generalized other"; Talcott Parsons stressed value internalisation; Durkheim treated education as society's methodical socialisation of the young; Norbert Elias historicised manners and self-restraint. Critics note socialisation can reproduce inequality of class, gender and race.
