Blenheim (Māori: Waiharakeke) is the most populous town in the Marlborough region of the South Island of New Zealand. Lying on the Wairau Plain near the head of the Ōpaoa River, it is the centre of New Zealand's largest wine-growing area and is noted for some of the highest sunshine hours in the country.

History

The Wairau Plain was settled by Māori for centuries, and the nearby Wairau Bar is one of New Zealand's earliest known settlement sites. European settlement began in the 1840s; the 1843 Wairau Affray, a violent clash over land purchases, occurred nearby. The town was named after the Battle of Blenheim (1704) and became the seat of the Marlborough provincial government. Originally a service town for sheep stations and flax milling, its economy was transformed from the 1970s by viticulture.

Wine

Marlborough's stony, free-draining soils and long sunny autumns proved ideal for wine grapes, above all Sauvignon blanc, which accounts for the great majority of the region's plantings and most of New Zealand's wine exports. Dozens of wineries are based around Blenheim, and wine tourism is a major local industry.

Other features

Nearby Woodbourne is home to RNZAF Base Woodbourne. The Omaka Aviation Heritage Centre, founded with filmmaker Peter Jackson's support, displays First World War aircraft. Blenheim is served by State Highway 1 and the Main North railway line, with ferry connections at Picton about 28 km north.
