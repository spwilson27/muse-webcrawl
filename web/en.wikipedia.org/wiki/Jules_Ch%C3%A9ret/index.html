Jules Chéret

Born31 May 1836, Paris, France
Died23 September 1932, Nice, France
OccupationPainter, lithographer
Known forFather of the modern poster

Jules Chéret (1836–1932) was a French painter and lithographer who became known as the father of the modern poster. His colorful, dynamic advertisements transformed the visual landscape of Belle Époque Paris.

Early career

Apprenticed to a lithographer at thirteen, Chéret studied drawing in Paris and spent several years in London, where he designed perfume labels and posters. Influenced by British printing techniques, he returned to France and founded his own printing firm in 1866.

The advertising poster

Chéret perfected a three-stone lithographic process that allowed cheap mass production of vivid color posters. His advertisements for cabarets, theaters, products and entertainments — often featuring joyful, dancing women dubbed chérettes — made poster art a defining feature of Parisian street life and strongly influenced artists such as Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec.

Later work and honors

In his later years Chéret turned increasingly to murals and easel painting in Nice, where a museum of fine arts bearing his name now stands. He was made a member of the Legion of Honour and lived to the age of 96.
