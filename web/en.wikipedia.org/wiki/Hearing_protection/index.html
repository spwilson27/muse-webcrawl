Hearing protection

Hearing protection refers to devices and measures used to reduce the level of sound reaching the inner ear in order to prevent noise-induced hearing loss and tinnitus. Hearing protection is used in noisy workplaces, shooting sports, motorsport, music venues and aviation, and ranges from simple disposable earplugs to electronically active earmuffs.

Types

  • Earplugs fit into the ear canal. They may be disposable foam plugs rolled and inserted to expand, pre-moulded triple-flange plugs, or custom-moulded silicone plugs made from impressions of the user's ears.
  • Earmuffs cover the whole ear with cushioned cups connected by a headband, providing consistent attenuation and easy fitting.
  • Semi-insert (canal cap) protectors use soft tips held against the canal opening by a light band, common in intermittent noise.
  • Electronic and level-dependent protectors pass through or amplify quiet sounds such as speech while compressing or blocking loud impulses, popular for shooting and military use.
  • Double protection — plugs plus muffs — is recommended for extremely high noise, such as around jet engines.

Ratings

In the United States, protectors carry a Noise Reduction Rating (NRR) derived under ANSI standards; in Europe the Single Number Rating (SNR) and HML values are used under EN 352. Real-world attenuation is usually lower than laboratory ratings, so regulators such as OSHA recommend derating, and fit-testing systems can measure an individual user's personal attenuation rating.

Regulation

Occupational hearing conservation programs, such as those required by the U.S. Occupational Safety and Health Administration at an 8-hour time-weighted average of 85 dBA, require employers to provide hearing protection, training and audiometric testing. The hierarchy of controls treats personal protective equipment as the last line of defence after engineering and administrative noise controls.
