Ipperwash Provincial Park

Ipperwash Provincial Park is a former provincial park on the shore of Lake Huron in Lambton County, Ontario, Canada. Established in 1936, the park was noted for its long sandy beach and rare dune ecosystems, and became the site of the Ipperwash Crisis of 1995.

History

The land was part of territory of the Chippewas of Kettle and Stony Point First Nation. During the Second World War the federal government appropriated the adjacent Stony Point reserve under the War Measures Act to build Camp Ipperwash, promising its return after the war — a promise not fulfilled for decades.

Ipperwash Crisis

In September 1995, Stony Point protesters occupied the park, asserting it contained sacred burial grounds. On 6 September, Ontario Provincial Police officers confronted the occupiers, and acting sergeant Kenneth Deane shot and killed unarmed protester Dudley George. The subsequent Ipperwash Inquiry (2004–2007), led by Justice Sidney Linden, examined the events and recommended reforms.

Return of the land

The park was closed and title to the land was ultimately transferred to the Chippewas of Kettle and Stony Point First Nation under a settlement agreement, with the former park lands managed by the First Nation; the Camp Ipperwash lands were also returned in stages.
