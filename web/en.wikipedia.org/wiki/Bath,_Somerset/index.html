Bath is a city in Somerset, England, known for and named after its Roman-built baths. It lies in the valley of the River Avon, 97 miles (156 km) west of London and 11 miles (18 km) southeast of Bristol. The city became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, principally for its Roman remains and Georgian architecture, and was inscribed again in 2021 as one of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.

History

The Romans founded Aquae Sulis around AD 60–70, building a temple to the goddess Sulis Minerva and an extensive bathing complex over Britain's only hot springs. In the 18th century, under the influence of dandy Beau Nash and architects John Wood the Elder and Younger, Bath became England's fashionable spa resort, acquiring its honey-coloured Bath stone landmarks: Queen Square, the Circus, the Pump Room and the sweeping Royal Crescent. Jane Austen lived in Bath from 1801 to 1806 and set parts of Northanger Abbey and Persuasion there.

Architecture and culture

Notable buildings include Bath Abbey, Pulteney Bridge (one of few bridges lined with shops), the Assembly Rooms, and the Thermae Bath Spa, which reopened the hot springs to bathers in 2006. The city hosts the University of Bath and Bath Spa University, the Bath Festival, and Bath Rugby at the Recreation Ground. Tourism is the major industry, drawing millions of visitors each year.
