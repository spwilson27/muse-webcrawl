Music of Japan


Gagaku, Japan's ancient court music ensemble.

The music of Japan includes a wide array of distinct genres, both traditional and modern. Traditional Japanese music is broadly of two types: court music (gagaku), among the oldest continuously performed orchestral traditions in the world, and theatrical forms such as noh and kabuki music. Folk music (min'yō) accompanies work, ritual and festivals in the regions.

Traditional instruments

  • Koto — a 13-string zither, Japan's national instrument.
  • Shamisen — a three-string lute central to kabuki, bunraku and folk song.
  • Shakuhachi — an end-blown bamboo flute associated with Zen monks.
  • Biwa — a short-necked lute used for narrative storytelling.
  • Taiko — large drums performed solo and in festival ensembles.

Modern music

After the Meiji Restoration, Western classical music and later jazz, rock and pop were absorbed. J-pop dominates the domestic market — one of the largest music markets in the world — alongside enka ballads, anime songs, Vocaloid productions, and influential electronic and noise scenes. Karaoke, invented in Japan, became a global entertainment format.
