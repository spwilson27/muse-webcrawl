Great East Window

The Great East Window of York Minster in England is the largest single stained-glass window in the country, roughly the size of a tennis court, filling the east wall of the cathedral's Lady Chapel. It was created between 1405 and 1408 by the Coventry glazier John Thornton, who was contracted to design, paint and complete the window within three years for £56.

Iconography and restoration

The window's panels depict scenes from the Bible framed by its beginning and end: episodes from Genesis in the lower registers and the Apocalypse (Revelation) above, with figures of saints, kings and bishops in the tracery lights, overseen by the donor, Bishop Walter de Skirlaw. Between 2011 and 2018 the window underwent a major conservation program in which all 311 panels were removed, cleaned and releaded, and protective external glazing was installed — the largest single stained-glass conservation project in the United Kingdom for a generation.
