Khutbah (Arabic: خطبة, khuṭba; Persian: خطبه, xotbe; Turkish: hutbe) is the Islamic sermon delivered formally during the weekly Friday congregational prayer (Jumu'ah) and on the two Eid festivals. Mentioning the term also covers sermons given on other occasions, such as weddings or special supplications. The person who delivers the khutbah is called the khatib, a role usually performed by the mosque's imam.

Friday khutbah

The Friday khutbah precedes the noon prayer and is considered an integral part of the service; attendance at it is obligatory for those attending Jumu'ah, and worshippers should remain silent and listen while it is delivered. The khatib traditionally ascends the minbar (pulpit), greets the congregation, and delivers the sermon in two parts, sitting briefly between them — a practice traced to the Prophet Muhammad. Essential elements include praise of God, blessings on the Prophet, recitation of Quran verses, exhortation to God-consciousness (taqwa), and supplications for the community.

Historical and political role

In classical Islamic states, the Friday khutbah carried political weight: mentioning the ruler's name in the sermon, together with striking coinage (sikka), was a recognised sign of sovereignty. Usurpers and new dynasties therefore prized having the khutbah read in their name, and omitting a ruler's name signalled rebellion. Sermons could also be occasions for announcing important news and decrees to the public.

Modern practice

Traditionally delivered in Arabic, the khutbah in many countries is now given partly or wholly in the local vernacular, sometimes with the formal Arabic elements retained at the beginning and end. In Turkey, the Directorate of Religious Affairs (Diyanet) prepares centrally written sermons distributed to mosques nationwide. Debates in Muslim communities in the West concern language, length, the relevance of topics, and, in some reformist circles, women delivering the khutbah in mixed congregations.

Eid and other sermons

For the Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha prayers, the order is reversed: prayer is performed first and the khutbah follows, and listening to it is recommended rather than obligatory. Special khutbahs are also delivered during prayers for rain (istisqa), eclipses, and at the marriage sermon (khutbat al-nikah).
