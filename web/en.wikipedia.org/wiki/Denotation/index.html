Denotation

In linguistics and philosophy, denotation is the literal or primary meaning of a word or expression — the thing it directly refers to — as opposed to its connotation, the ideas and feelings it suggests. The word "snake," for example, denotes a legless reptile but may connote danger or deceit.

In semantics

The denotation of a term is sometimes identified with its extension: the set of things to which it applies. Philosophers from John Stuart Mill to Bertrand Russell have analyzed how denotation relates to meaning, reference and truth.
