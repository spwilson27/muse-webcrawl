Fraser Valley

The Fraser Valley is the section of the Fraser River basin in southwestern British Columbia, Canada, downstream of the Fraser Canyon and upstream of the river's mouth at the Strait of Georgia. It is one of the most fertile agricultural regions in Canada and among the fastest-growing parts of British Columbia.

Geography

The valley proper stretches from Hope in the east to the suburbs of Metro Vancouver in the west, a broad flat floodplain flanked by the Coast Mountains to the north and the Cascade Mountains to the south. Principal communities include Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Langley, Mission and Hope. The Fraser Valley Regional District administers the eastern portion. Mount Cheam and the Cheam Range dominate the skyline near Chilliwack, and the Sumas Prairie — a drained lakebed — is among the most productive dairy and berry farmland in the country.

Climate and hazards

The valley has a mild oceanic climate with warm, dry summers, though in summer it can be considerably hotter than the coast. Atmospheric rivers can bring heavy flooding, most catastrophically in November 2021, when the Sumas Prairie flooded after a dyke breach, forcing mass evacuations and the death of hundreds of thousands of farm animals. Air pollution also accumulates in the valley under temperature inversions.

People

The valley is the traditional territory of the Stó:lō peoples. Since settlement it has become notably diverse, with one of Canada's largest South Asian communities in Abbotsford and a significant Dutch-descended farming population. Agriculture — dairy, poultry, berries and greenhouse production — remains central to the regional identity.
