Positive Peace Index

The Positive Peace Index (PPI) is a composite measure of the attitudes, institutions and structures that sustain peaceful societies, produced by the Institute for Economics and Peace (IEP) alongside the Global Peace Index. Whereas the Global Peace Index measures "negative peace" — the absence of violence and the fear of violence — the PPI operationalises sociologist Johan Galtung's concept of positive peace: the presence of the conditions associated with resilient, peaceful and prosperous societies.

Methodology

The index is built from 24 indicators grouped into eight "Pillars of Positive Peace":

  • Well-functioning government
  • Sound business environment
  • Equitable distribution of resources
  • Acceptance of the rights of others
  • Good relations with neighbours
  • Free flow of information
  • High levels of human capital
  • Low levels of corruption

Indicators are drawn from sources such as the World Bank, the Economist Intelligence Unit and the United Nations. Each country receives a score on a 1–5 scale for each pillar, and the overall score is a weighted average of the pillars.

Findings

High Positive Peace is statistically associated with stronger GDP growth, greater resilience to shocks, lower inflation volatility, higher ecological sustainability and better outcomes on measures such as the Global Peace Index itself. The IEP reports that countries with stronger Positive Peace tend to recover faster from crises, and that deteriorations in Positive Peace can precede deteriorations in negative peace. Nordic countries such as Denmark, Finland, Norway and Sweden, together with countries such as Switzerland, New Zealand and Canada, consistently rank among the highest in the index.
