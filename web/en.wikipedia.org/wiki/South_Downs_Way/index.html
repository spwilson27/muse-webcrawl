South Downs Way

South Downs Way
Length: 160 km (99 mi)
Location: South East England
Trailheads: Eastbourne — Winchester
Use: Hiking, cycling, horse riding

The South Downs Way is a long-distance footpath and bridleway in southern England, a National Trail running for about 160 kilometres (99 miles) along the chalk escarpment of the South Downs from Eastbourne in East Sussex to Winchester in Hampshire.

Route

The trail follows ancient tracks along the northern crest of the Downs, passing the Seven Sisters cliffs, Beachy Head, Devil's Dyke, the Long Man of Wilmington, Chanctonbury Ring, and Old Winchester Hill. Because it is a bridleway along most of its length, it is open to walkers, cyclists, and horse riders. A short variant diverts to the coast at Seven Sisters, while the main route turns inland at Alfriston.

History

The route follows ridgeway tracks used for thousands of years by travellers avoiding the wet, wooded Weald below. It was proposed as a long-distance path in 1963 and officially opened in 1972, making it one of Britain's earlier National Trails. It now lies almost entirely within the South Downs National Park, established in 2010.

Records

End-to-end completions typically take eight to nine days on foot. Trail runners contest fastest known times, and an annual overnight event, the South Downs Way 100, is a popular ultramarathon.
