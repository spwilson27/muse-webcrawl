Jabiru

KingdomAnimalia
ClassAves
OrderCiconiiformes
FamilyCiconiidae (storks)
GenusJabiru
SpeciesJ. mycteria

The jabiru (Jabiru mycteria) is a large stork found in the Americas from Mexico to Argentina, except west of the Andes. It is the tallest flying bird in South America and the second-tallest in the Americas after the greater rhea (which is flightless relative comparisons aside, among storks it is the largest in the Americas), and it has one of the largest wingspans of any land bird. The name comes from a Tupi–Guaraní word meaning "swollen neck".

Description

Adults stand 122–140 cm (48–55 in) tall with a wingspan of about 2.3–2.8 m (7.5–9 ft) and can weigh around 8 kg (18 lb), males being larger than females. The plumage is entirely white, while the head and upper neck are featherless and black, with a broad red band at the base of the neck. The massive bill, up to 30 cm long, is black and slightly upturned. The sexes are similar in appearance, differing in eye colour: males have dark eyes and females yellow eyes.

Distribution and habitat

The jabiru inhabits wetlands, marshes, flooded savannas and the edges of rivers and ponds, including the Pantanal of Brazil and the Llanos of Venezuela. It is generally uncommon and widespread, with the largest populations in Brazil and Paraguay. It is a vagrant to the United States, occasionally recorded in Texas and elsewhere along the Gulf Coast.

Behaviour and ecology

Jabirus feed on fish, molluscs, crustaceans, amphibians, reptiles and small mammals, probing in shallow water and seizing prey with the bill. They are usually seen singly, in pairs or in family groups, and sometimes gather at drying pools or with other waterbirds. Pairs build a large stick nest in a tall tree, enlarging and reusing it in successive years; both parents incubate the clutch of two to five eggs and feed the young.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though populations declined in parts of Central America owing to wetland drainage and persecution; it is protected in several countries and is a flagship species of the Pantanal.
