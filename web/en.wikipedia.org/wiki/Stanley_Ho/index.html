Stanley Ho

Born: 25 November 1921, Hong Kong
Died: 26 May 2020 (aged 98), Hong Kong
Occupation: Businessman
Known for: Founder of SJM Holdings; "King of Gambling" of Macau
Children: 17, including Pansy Ho, Daisy Ho, Maisy Ho, Josie Ho and Lawrence Ho

Stanley Ho Hung-sun (Chinese: 何鴻燊; 25 November 1921 – 26 May 2020) was a Hong Kong and Macau businessman, investor and philanthropist. Nicknamed the "King of Gambling", he built a casino empire in Macau and for four decades held the government-granted monopoly on the territory's casino industry. Through his flagship company Sociedade de Turismo e Diversões de Macau (STDM) and its listed arm SJM Holdings, Ho's interests extended across gaming, tourism, shipping, real estate, air transport and finance in Hong Kong, Macau, mainland China, Portugal, Vietnam, the Philippines and elsewhere.

Early life

Ho was born into the wealthy Ho Tung clan of Hong Kong, of Dutch-Jewish, English and Chinese ancestry; he was a great-nephew of Robert Hotung, then one of Hong Kong's richest men. His family lost its fortune in the stock market crash of the 1930s, and his father fled Hong Kong after bankruptcy. Ho excelled at the University of Hong Kong, where he was the first student from a poor background to win a scholarship, but the Japanese invasion of 1941 interrupted his studies. He fled to Macau, then neutral Portuguese territory, where he traded goods and reportedly made his first fortune within a few years.

The Macau monopoly

In 1961 Ho and partners including Henry Fok, Yip Hon and Teddy Yip won the Macau government concession for casino gambling, outbidding the incumbent local syndicate. STDM modernised the ferry link to Hong Kong with high-speed hydrofoils and built landmark properties including the Hotel Lisboa (1970) and later the Grand Lisboa (2008), whose lotus-flower tower became a symbol of the city. The monopoly lasted until 2002, when the Macau government liberalised the industry and admitted international operators; even so, SJM remained one of the largest operators.

Later years and legacy

Ho was a standing committee member of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference and played a prominent role in the negotiations leading to Macau's handover to China in 1999. He retired as chairman of SJM in 2018. His vast family — he publicly acknowledged four wives and seventeen children — became the subject of a highly public inheritance dispute after his death in 2020. Elements of his collection of Chinese antiquities, including the controversial bronze horse head from the Old Summer Palace, were donated to the Chinese state.
