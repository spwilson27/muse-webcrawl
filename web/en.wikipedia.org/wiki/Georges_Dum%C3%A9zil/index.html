Georges Dumézil

Born4 March 1898, Paris, France
Died11 October 1986, Paris, France
OccupationPhilologist, historian of religion
Known forTrifunctional hypothesis

Georges Dumézil (4 March 1898 – 11 October 1986) was a French philologist and historian of religion best known for his comparative studies of Indo-European mythology and society. He proposed the trifunctional hypothesis, according to which Proto-Indo-European ideology was organised around three functions: sovereignty (sacral and juridical), martial force, and fertility or prosperity.

Career

Educated at the École Normale Supérieure, Dumézil taught in Istanbul, Uppsala, and Paris. He held the chair of Indo-European Civilisation at the Collège de France from 1949 to 1968 and was elected to the Académie française in 1978.

Work

His major works, including Mythe et épopée (1968–1973), compared mythic structures across Indo-Iranian, Roman, Germanic, Celtic, and other traditions. His ideas influenced historians and anthropologists, though aspects of the trifunctional model remain debated.
