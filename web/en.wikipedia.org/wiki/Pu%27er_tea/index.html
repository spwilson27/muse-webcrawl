Pu'er tea
TypeFermented tea
OriginYunnan, China
Main ingredientCamellia sinensis var. assamica leaves

Pu'er tea (Chinese: 普洱茶, pǔ'ěr chá) is a variety of fermented tea traditionally produced in Yunnan Province, China. Unlike most teas, pu'er undergoes microbial fermentation and oxidation after the leaves are dried and rolled, a process that can continue for years and gives the tea a dark colour and earthy flavour.

Production

Pu'er is made from the large-leaf assamica variety of the tea plant. Two main styles exist: raw (shēng) pu'er, which ages naturally over years, and ripe (shú) pu'er, developed in the 1970s, in which a wet-piling process accelerates fermentation over weeks. The tea is often compressed into cakes, bricks or discs for storage and transport.

Culture and trade

Pu'er has been traded along the Tea Horse Road for centuries and is prized by collectors; aged cakes can command high prices. The city of Pu'er in Yunnan gives the tea its name.
