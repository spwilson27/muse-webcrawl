School uniform

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A school uniform is a uniform worn by students primarily for school or other educational institutions. They are common in primary and secondary schools in many countries, and are typically set out by a school's dress code or uniform policy, specifying garments, colours, and standards of appearance.

History

Uniform dress for pupils is documented as early as the 16th century in England, where charity schools such as Christ's Hospital dressed students in distinctive blue coats. The practice spread through British public schools and the British Empire, and standardized uniforms became widespread in the 19th and 20th centuries. Japan adopted Western-style school uniforms in the Meiji era, evolving into styles such as the girls' sailor uniform and the boys' gakuran.

Around the world

School uniforms are near-universal in the United Kingdom, Ireland, and many Commonwealth countries, and are standard in much of East and Southeast Asia, Latin America, and parts of Africa. In the United States they are common in private and parochial schools and in a minority of public schools, though strict dress codes are more widespread. Many European countries, including France and Germany, have historically not required uniforms in state schools.

Debate

Supporters argue uniforms promote school pride and discipline, reduce visible socioeconomic differences, simplify morning routines, and may improve safety. Critics argue they restrict self-expression, impose costs on families when specific garments must be bought, and have uncertain effects on behaviour and achievement. Research findings on academic outcomes are mixed, and uniform policies remain a recurring subject of public debate.
