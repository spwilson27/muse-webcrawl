Will H. Hays

William Harrison Hays Sr. (November 5, 1879 – March 7, 1954) was an American Republican politician and the first president of the Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America (MPPDA, 1922–1945), the office that gave Hollywood's censorship code its name — the Hays Code.

Career

Hays chaired the Republican National Committee (1918–1921), managed Warren G. Harding's successful 1920 campaign, and served as US Postmaster General (1921–22), introducing airmail improvements and famously dismissing inefficiency. He resigned to head the new film-industry association amid scandals such as the Arbuckle trials.

The Hays Office

Hays issued the 1927 "Don'ts and Be Carefuls" and adopted the Motion Picture Production Code in 1930; strict enforcement from 1934 under Joseph Breen shaped American film content for three decades. He retired in 1945 and published his memoirs in 1955.
