An Australian region tropical cyclone is a tropical cyclone that forms in the waters around Australia and its neighbouring regions, within the basin bounded by 90°E and 160°E and south of the equator. Responsibility for monitoring and naming cyclones in this basin is shared between the Australian Bureau of Meteorology, Indonesia's meteorological agency in Jakarta, and Papua New Guinea's National Weather Service in Port Moresby.

Season and notable cyclones

The Australian cyclone season runs from 1 November to 30 April, driven by warm ocean waters and the monsoon trough; on average about ten cyclones form in the region each season, of which roughly half reach severe intensity (category 3 or higher). The most notorious Australian cyclone is Cyclone Tracy, which devastated Darwin on Christmas Day 1974. Cyclone Mahina (1899), which struck Bathurst Bay, Queensland, produced one of the largest storm surges ever recorded and remains the deadliest cyclone in Australian history; more recent severe landfalls include Cyclone Larry (2006) and Cyclone Yasi (2011) in Queensland.
