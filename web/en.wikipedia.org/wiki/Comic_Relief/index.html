Comic Relief

Founded: 1985
Founders: Richard Curtis, Lenny Henry
Type: Charitable organization
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom

Comic Relief is a British charity, founded in 1985 by the comedy scriptwriter Richard Curtis and comedian Lenny Henry in response to the famine in Ethiopia. The highlight of Comic Relief's appeal is Red Nose Day, a biennial telethon event that has become a fixture of British popular culture, in which people wear red plastic noses and take part in fundraising activities.

History

Comic Relief was launched on Christmas Day 1985 on BBC television. Its first Red Nose Day, held on 5 February 1988 with Lenny Henry hosting from the Sudan, raised £15 million. The telethons combine comedy sketches and special episodes of popular shows with documentary appeals from people supported by funded projects in the UK and Africa.

The charity's sister campaign, Sport Relief, launched in 2002, alternated with Red Nose Day and raised money through sporting challenges. Comic Relief expanded to the United States in 2015 with Red Nose Day USA.

Fundraising and impact

Red Nose Day telethons have collectively raised well over £1 billion for charitable causes. Money raised funds projects addressing poverty, homelessness, mental health, malaria, education, and access to vaccines, among many other issues, in the United Kingdom and internationally.

Criticism

Comic Relief has faced scrutiny over the imagery used in its appeals; in 2019, criticism of celebrity-fronted films from Africa as promoting a "white saviour" narrative led the charity to change its approach and stop sending celebrities abroad for appeal films, investing instead in local filmmakers.
