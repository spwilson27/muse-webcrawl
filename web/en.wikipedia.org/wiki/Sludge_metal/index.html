Sludge metal (also known as sludge or sludgecore) is a subgenre of heavy metal music that originated in the late 1980s by combining elements of doom metal and hardcore punk. It typically features slow tempos, heavy and abrasive guitars, shouted or screamed vocals, and a bleak, misanthropic atmosphere, often with lyrics addressing suffering, drug abuse and social decay.

Characteristics

Sludge metal generally eschews the fast tempos of most metal in favour of slow, grinding riffs, though passages of hardcore speed do occur. The guitar sound is typically thick, down-tuned and heavily distorted with abundant feedback. Vocalists commonly scream or shout in a manner derived from hardcore punk. The genre is strongly associated with the New Orleans scene — sometimes called NOLA metal — including bands such as Eyehategod, Crowbar, Down and Acid Bath.

History

The Melvins are widely credited as pioneers, slowing hardcore punk down to a crawl in the mid-1980s. Black Flag's album My War (1984) was another key influence with its slow, heavy second side. In the 1990s the Louisiana scene codified the genre, while bands elsewhere — Neurosis in California, for example — fused sludge with post-metal. Sludge has since crossed over with stoner metal, doom metal and post-metal, and acts such as Mastodon and Baroness began in sludge before evolving toward more progressive sounds.
