Construction management

Construction managers coordinate cost, schedule, quality and safety on site.

Construction management (CM) is a professional service that uses specialised project management techniques and software to oversee the planning, design, construction and closeout of a construction project, controlling its schedule, cost, quality and safety on behalf of the owner. A construction manager may act as the owner's adviser ("agency CM") or hold the trade contracts at risk for a guaranteed maximum price ("CM-at-risk").

Functions

The construction manager's responsibilities typically include defining the project programme and budget, cost estimating, value engineering, procurement and negotiation of contracts, scheduling (often using the critical path method), coordinating contractors and subcontractors, administering contracts and change orders, quality control, and managing site safety. Construction managers commonly hold degrees in construction management, architecture, engineering or a related field, and professional certification such as the Certified Construction Manager (CCM) credential administered in the United States.

CM is one of several project delivery methods, alongside design–bid–build, design–build and integrated project delivery; it is widely used for large commercial, institutional and infrastructure projects.
