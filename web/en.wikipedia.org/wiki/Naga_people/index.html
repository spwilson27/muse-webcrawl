Naga people

The Naga people are a conglomeration of Tibeto-Burman tribal communities native to north-eastern India and north-western Myanmar. They number roughly two million in India, concentrated in the state of Nagaland, with significant populations in Manipur, Arunachal Pradesh, and Assam, and in Myanmar's Sagaing Region. The Nagas comprise numerous major tribes — among them the Angami, Ao, Sema (Sumi), Lotha, Konyak, Phom, Sangtam, Chakhesang, Zeliang, Rengma, and Tangkhul — each with its own language, customs, and traditional territory, speaking languages of the Sal branch of Tibeto-Burman.

History

Naga society was historically village-based, often organised in fortified hilltop settlements, and included practices such as headhunting, which was suppressed during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The British annexed the Naga Hills progressively after the 1830s, administering them as an excluded area largely left to indirect rule. American Baptist missionaries, arriving in the 1870s, converted most Nagas to Christianity; today the population is overwhelmingly Baptist and English serves as a lingua franca alongside Nagamese, a creole based on Assamese.

At Indian independence, the Naga National Council under Angami Zapu Phizo declared Naga independence on 14 August 1947 and resisted incorporation into India, beginning one of Asia's longest-running insurgencies. Nagaland became a state of India in 1963. Armed movements, later led by the National Socialist Council of Nagaland (NSCN), continued for decades; a ceasefire with the largest faction has held since 1997, and a framework agreement was signed in 2015, though a final political settlement has remained elusive.

Culture

Naga culture is celebrated each December at the Hornbill Festival at Kisama Heritage Village near Kohima, bringing together all the major tribes in displays of dance, song, dress, and cuisine. Traditional institutions include the morung (youth dormitory), elaborate wood carving and beadwork, shawls whose patterns encode tribal and social identity, and a cuisine featuring rice, smoked meats, and fermented foods, including the intensely hot raja mircha (king chilli).
