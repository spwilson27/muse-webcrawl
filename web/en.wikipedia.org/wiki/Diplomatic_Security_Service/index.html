Diplomatic Security Service

The Diplomatic Security Service (DSS) is the law enforcement and security arm of the United States Department of State, within the Bureau of Diplomatic Security. Its special agents protect U.S. diplomatic missions and personnel abroad, the Secretary of State and visiting foreign dignitaries at home, and investigate passport and visa fraud, making the DSS one of the most widely deployed American law enforcement agencies, with offices in most U.S. embassies and consulates worldwide.

The service traces its lineage to 1916, when Secretary of State Robert Lansing created a small intelligence and security office during the First World War. It was reshaped by the Omnibus Diplomatic Security and Antiterrorism Act of 1986, passed in response to deadly attacks on U.S. facilities, which formally established the Bureau of Diplomatic Security and the DSS in their modern form.

DSS special agents conduct criminal investigations — notably document fraud, human smuggling connected to visas, and fugitive returns — and run protective operations, including the security details for major events such as UN General Assembly week. The service also administers the Rewards for Justice Program, offering rewards for information against international terrorism.
