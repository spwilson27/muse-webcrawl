Division (mathematics)

Division is one of the four basic operations of arithmetic, the others being addition, subtraction, and multiplication. At an elementary level, the division of two natural numbers is the process of calculating the number of times one number is contained within another. For example, if 20 apples are divided evenly between 5 people, each person receives 4 apples.

20 ÷ 5 = 4: twenty apples divided into five equal groups of four.

Notation

Division is often shown using the obelus symbol ÷, the solidus (slash) /, or the fraction bar. In the expression a ÷ b = c, a is called the dividend, b the divisor, and c the quotient. Division by zero is undefined for real numbers.

Euclidean division

For integers, division may leave a remainder. The Euclidean division theorem states that given integers a and b ≠ 0, there exist unique integers q and r with a = bq + r and 0 ≤ r < |b|.

Division of rational and real numbers

Division of rational numbers produces another rational number when the divisor is nonzero. For real numbers, division is defined as multiplication by the reciprocal: a/b = a · b−1.

Abstract algebra

In abstract algebra, division is generalized through the notion of inverse elements in groups, fields, and division rings. A division algebra is an algebra in which division by any nonzero element is possible.
