Bislett Stadium

LocationFrogner, Oslo, Norway
Opened1922 (original); 2005 (new stadium)
Capacity15,400
EventsBislett Games; 1952 Winter Olympics speed skating

Bislett Stadium (Norwegian: Bislett stadion) is a sports stadium in the Frogner district of Oslo, Norway. It is one of the most famous athletics venues in the world, long known as a mecca for world records in speed skating and track and field.

History

The original Bislett Stadium was built on the site of a former brickyard and opened for sport in 1922. Speed skating was held there from the earliest years, and the stadium hosted the speed skating events of the 1952 Winter Olympics. Over the decades, skaters set numerous world records on its ice, and the venue was nicknamed "the record factory".

The Bislett Games, an annual athletics meeting founded in 1965, made Bislett famous in summer sport as well; dozens of world records in running events were set on its distinctive six-lane track. The old stadium was demolished in 2004 and a new stadium, preserving the old one's footprint and famous curved shape, opened in 2005.

Events

The stadium hosts the Bislett Games, part of the Diamond League, and home matches of Norwegian football clubs. It is also used for concerts and community sport.
