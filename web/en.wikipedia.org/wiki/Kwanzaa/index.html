Kwanzaa

Kwanzaa is an annual celebration of African-American culture, held from 26 December to 1 January. It was created by Maulana Karenga, a professor of Africana studies, in 1966 in the aftermath of the Watts riots in Los Angeles. Kwanzaa derives its name from the Swahili phrase matunda ya kwanza, meaning "first fruits", referring to traditional African harvest festivals.

The seven principles

Each of the seven days is dedicated to one of the Nguzo Saba (seven principles):

  • Umoja (unity)
  • Kujichagulia (self-determination)
  • Ujima (collective work and responsibility)
  • Ujamaa (cooperative economics)
  • Nia (purpose)
  • Kuumba (creativity)
  • Imani (faith)

Symbols and observance

Celebrations centre on the kinara, a candle holder with seven candles (three red, one black, three green) representing the colours of the Pan-African flag. Other symbols include the mkeka (mat), mazao (crops), muhindi (ears of corn), the unity cup (kikombe cha umoja), and zawadi (gifts). The sixth day features the karamu feast. Kwanzaa is a cultural rather than religious holiday and is frequently observed alongside Christmas or other faith traditions.
