Jan van Goyen

Jan Josephsj. van Goyen (13 January 1596 – 27 April 1656) was a Dutch Golden Age landscape painter. His rapid, tonal style of river and seascape painting, usually in a restricted palette of browns and greys, defined early seventeenth-century Dutch landscape art.

Career

Van Goyen trained with Esaias van de Velde and travelled widely in the Netherlands and Germany, producing thousands of paintings and sketches. From the early 1630s he developed the monochrome "tonal phase" of Dutch landscape, with broad skies and low horizons.

Financial troubles and legacy

Despite enormous productivity — some 1,200 paintings and 1,000 drawings — van Goyen lost money in tulip speculation and real-estate ventures, dying in debt. His influence on painters such as Jan Steen (his son-in-law) and Salomon van Ruysdael was considerable.
