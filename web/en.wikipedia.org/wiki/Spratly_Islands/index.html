Spratly Islands

The Spratly Islands are a disputed archipelago in the South China Sea, comprising over 100 small islands, reefs, atolls, and cays spread over roughly 425,000 square kilometres of sea between the Philippines, Malaysia, and Vietnam. The islands have a total natural land area of less than 2 square kilometres, the largest being Itu Aba (Taiping Island) at about 46 hectares. They are claimed in whole or in part by China, Taiwan, Vietnam, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Brunei.

Geography and features

The archipelago lies near major shipping lanes and productive fishing grounds, and the surrounding seabed is believed to hold oil and natural gas reserves. Many features are submerged at high tide; under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, only naturally formed land above water at high tide can generate territorial seas.

Occupation and construction

Several claimants maintain garrisons and structures on features they occupy: Vietnam, the Philippines, China, Taiwan, and Malaysia all administer groups of features. Beginning around 2013–2014, China undertook large-scale land reclamation at seven reefs, including Fiery Cross, Subi, and Mischief Reefs, building runways, harbors, and military installations totaling thousands of acres of artificial land, drawing international criticism.

Legal dispute

In 2016, an arbitral tribunal in Philippines v. China, constituted under UNCLOS, ruled that China's "nine-dash line" claim to historic rights had no legal basis and that none of the Spratly features generates an exclusive economic zone; China rejected the ruling and did not participate. The dispute remains a major flashpoint in Southeast Asian geopolitics, with frequent confrontations between coast guards and maritime militias, especially around Second Thomas Shoal.
