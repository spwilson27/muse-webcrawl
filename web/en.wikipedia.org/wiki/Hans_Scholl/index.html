Hans Fritz Scholl (22 September 1918 – 22 February 1943) was a German medical student and anti-Nazi political activist. With his younger sister Sophie Scholl, he was a founding member of the White Rose, a non-violent resistance group of Munich students that distributed leaflets calling for opposition to the Nazi regime. Arrested in February 1943, he was tried by the People's Court and executed the same day.

Early life

Hans Scholl was born in Ingersheim, the second of six children of Robert Scholl, a liberal-minded mayor and tax consultant, and Magdalene Scholl. As a teenager he enthusiastically joined the Hitler Youth against his father's wishes, but his independent youth-group activities brought him into conflict with the authorities; in 1937 he was briefly arrested for involvement in a banned youth organisation. He began studying medicine at the University of Munich in 1939 and served as a medic on the Eastern and Western Fronts during semester breaks, experiences that deepened his disgust with the regime and exposed him to Russian culture and prisoners of war.

The White Rose

In the summer of 1942, Scholl and Alexander Schmorell wrote the first four White Rose leaflets, mailing them to academics and professionals in Munich; the group later included Sophie Scholl, Christoph Probst, Willi Graf, and professor Kurt Huber. In early 1943 the group produced two further leaflets, the sixth prompted by the German defeat at Stalingrad and addressed "Fellow Students!", urging revolt.

Arrest and execution

On 18 February 1943, Hans and Sophie scattered copies of the sixth leaflet from a balcony in the University of Munich's main building. Seen by a caretaker, they were arrested by the Gestapo; a draft of a further leaflet in Christoph Probst's handwriting was found on Hans. On 22 February, Roland Freisler's People's Court convicted Hans and Sophie Scholl and Christoph Probst of treason after a show trial lasting a few hours; all three were guillotined the same afternoon at Stadelheim Prison. Scholl's last words are reported as "Long live freedom!"

Legacy

The Scholl siblings became the most famous symbols of German resistance. Their story was smuggled out of Germany, and the sixth leaflet was dropped over Germany by the Allies in millions of copies. Schools, streets, and the Geschwister-Scholl-Preis literary prize commemorate them, and the university square in Munich is named Geschwister-Scholl-Platz.
