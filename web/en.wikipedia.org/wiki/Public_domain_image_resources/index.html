Public domain image resources

Public domain image resources are collections of images that are free of copyright restrictions — because copyright has expired, was never applicable, or was waived — and can therefore be used by anyone for any purpose, including commercially, without permission.

Sources

Major sources include the works of the United States federal government (generally public domain by statute), images released by agencies such as NASA and NOAA, digitized collections of libraries and museums including the Library of Congress, the Smithsonian Institution (via Smithsonian Open Access), and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and aggregators such as Wikimedia Commons, which hosts millions of freely usable media files with individual licensing information.

Cautions

"Public domain" status varies by jurisdiction: a work free of copyright in one country may remain protected in another. Images may also implicate trademark, privacy, or publicity rights even when the copyright has lapsed, and the copyright status of photographs of older artworks can be disputed. Users are advised to check the license statement of each repository and image.

Dedications

Creators can proactively release their own work using tools such as Creative Commons CC0, a public-domain dedication that waives rights as completely as local law allows.
