The Markale massacres were two mortar attacks on civilians at the Markale open-air market in Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia and Herzegovina, during the Siege of Sarajevo in the Bosnian War. The attacks, on 5 February 1994 and 28 August 1995, together killed more than one hundred people and wounded more than two hundred. Investigations and subsequent war-crimes trials attributed the shells to Bosnian Serb (Army of Republika Srpska) positions around the city.

First attack (5 February 1994)

Around midday on 5 February 1994, a 120 mm mortar shell landed among shoppers at the crowded Markale market in the old town of Sarajevo, killing 68 people and wounding 144. The attack was one of the deadliest single incidents of the siege. In its aftermath, NATO issued an ultimatum demanding that Bosnian Serb heavy weapons be withdrawn from around Sarajevo or placed under UN control, threatening air strikes; a ceasefire and heavy-weapons exclusion zone followed.

Second attack (28 August 1995)

On 28 August 1995, a further mortar shell struck near the market and the National Library area, killing 43 people and wounding 84. A UN investigation concluded the shell had been fired from Bosnian Serb positions. The attack was the immediate catalyst for NATO's Operation Deliberate Force, a sustained bombing campaign against Bosnian Serb targets beginning on 30 August 1995, which, combined with Croat and Bosniak ground offensives, helped bring the parties to the negotiating table and the Dayton Agreement that ended the war.

Responsibility and trials

Bosnian Serb officials at the time contested responsibility, claiming the attacks were staged. However, trials before the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY), including those of generals Stanislav Galić and Dragomir Milošević, commanders of the Sarajevo-Romanija Corps, established that the corps conducted a campaign of shelling and sniping against Sarajevo's civilian population; both were convicted. Crater analyses and UNPROFOR investigations supported the conclusion that the Markale shells came from Bosnian Serb-held territory.
