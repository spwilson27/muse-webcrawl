Molde

Country: Norway
County: Møre og Romsdal
District: Romsdal
Area (town): approx. 9 km²
Nickname: "Town of Roses"

Molde (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈmɔ̂ɫdə]) is a town and municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. It is located in the traditional district of Romsdal, on the Romsdal Peninsula, surrounding the Fannefjord and Moldefjord. The town is the administrative centre of the municipality and of Møre og Romsdal county, and a commercial and educational hub for the region, with a population of roughly 21,000 in the town itself and about 33,000 in the municipality.

History

Molde grew from a medieval trading post and ferry landing and received town privileges in 1742. In the late 19th century it became a popular tourist destination, visited by royalty and served by the Hurtigruten coastal steamers; the Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany holidayed there regularly. The town was largely destroyed by fire in January 1940 and then bombed during the German invasion in April–May 1940, when King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government fled through Molde; much of the town was rebuilt after the Second World War.

Geography and sights

Molde is known as the "Town of Roses" for its mild climate and gardens, which allow roses to bloom unusually far north. Its best-known landmark is the Varden viewpoint, 407 metres above the town, offering a panorama said to include 222 mountain peaks. The Romsdal Museum, one of Norway's largest folk museums, and the modernist Molde Cathedral (1957) are notable attractions. The Atlantic Ocean Road passes through the neighbouring area, and the trollstigen mountain road is within reach.

Culture and sport

Molde hosts the Moldejazz festival, one of Europe's oldest jazz festivals, held annually since 1961. The local football club, Molde FK, plays at the Aker Stadion and has won the Norwegian Eliteserien several times, first in 2011, and regularly competes in European competition. The town is served by Molde Airport, Årø, and is a port of call for the Hurtigruten and Havila coastal voyages.
