Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk (Russian: Леди Макбет Мценского уезда) is an opera in four acts and nine scenes by Dmitri Shostakovich, Op. 29, with a libretto by Alexander Preis and the composer, based on Nikolai Leskov's 1865 novella. It tells the story of Katerina Izmailova, a bored merchant's wife in provincial Russia who falls in love with her husband's worker Sergey and is driven to murder her father-in-law and husband, before being betrayed, convicted and drowning herself on the way to Siberia.

Initial success and denunciation

The opera was an immediate triumph at its January 1934 Leningrad premiere and was staged worldwide, with Shostakovich hailed as a genius. In January 1936, however, Stalin attended a Moscow performance and left displeased; days later Pravda published the unsigned editorial "Muddle Instead of Music", condemning the opera as coarse, formalist and "leftist chaos". The work was banned in the Soviet Union for nearly thirty years, and the episode — coming at the start of the Great Terror — haunted the composer for the rest of his life.

Revision and rehabilitation

Shostakovich later revised the opera as Katerina Izmailova (Op. 114, premiered 1962), softening its language and reworking parts of the score. Mstislav Rostropovich's 1978 recording restored the original version, which is now the form usually performed; the opera is regarded as one of the great operatic works of the 20th century.
