Gilgai

A gilgai is a small ephemeral lake formed in a depression in clay-rich soil, found in Australia and similar semi-arid regions. The name comes from an Aboriginal Australian language (Kamilaroi or Wiradjuri) word meaning a small waterhole. Gilgais are also used more broadly for the microrelief pattern of mounds and depressions produced by shrink–swell clays.

Formation

Gilgai relief forms in vertisols — soils rich in swelling clays such as montmorillonite. Seasonal wetting and drying causes the soil to expand and contract, heaving up mounds (puffs) between seasonally water-filled depressions. The repeated churning of the soil profile is called argillipedoturbation.

Ecology and land use

Gilgai depressions hold water after rain and provide important wetland habitat for frogs, birds, and invertebrates in otherwise dry landscapes. The uneven surface complicates farming and road building, and gilgai country is often used for grazing rather than cropping.
