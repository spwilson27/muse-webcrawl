Whiskeytown

Whiskeytown was an American alternative country band formed in Raleigh, North Carolina, in 1994. Fronted by singer-songwriter Ryan Adams, the band became one of the defining acts of the alt-country movement of the late 1990s.

History

Adams formed Whiskeytown with violinist Caitlin Cary, guitarist Phil Wandscher, bassist Steve Grothmann and drummer Skillet Gilmore, among a rotating cast of members. The band signed to the independent label Bloodshot Records and released its debut album, Faithless Street, in 1995.

Their second album, Strangers Almanac (1997), released on the major label Outpost, is widely regarded as a landmark of alternative country, blending country-rock with Adams's melancholic songwriting. Internal conflict, heavy touring and Adams's growing solo ambitions led to the band's dissolution; their final album, Pneumonia, recorded in 1999, was released in 2001 after the band had effectively broken up.

Legacy

Whiskeytown's recordings influenced a generation of Americana and indie-rock acts, and both Adams and Cary went on to solo careers. Strangers Almanac in particular is frequently cited among the essential albums of 1990s roots rock.
