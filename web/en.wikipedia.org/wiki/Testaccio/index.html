Testaccio (Italian pronunciation: [teˈstattʃo]) is the 20th rione (administrative district) of Rome, Italy, identified by the initials R. XX. It takes its name from Monte Testaccio, an artificial hill composed almost entirely of fragments of broken ancient Roman amphorae, and lies south of the Aventine Hill between the Tiber River and the Aurelian Walls.

History

In antiquity the area was the landing place for goods shipped up the Tiber to Rome's river port, the Emporium, and was filled with warehouses (horrea). Olive oil imported from Hispania Baetica (modern Andalusia) arrived in large Dressel 20 amphorae that could not be reused once emptied — the oil seeped into the clay — so the vessels were smashed and stacked in a systematically managed dump. Over roughly the 1st to 3rd centuries AD this accumulated into Monte Testaccio, about 35 metres high, made of an estimated 53 million amphora fragments.

The modern neighbourhood developed after Italian unification, when the area was zoned for industry and working-class housing. Slaughterhouses (the former Mattatoio), a gasometer, and markets shaped its strongly working-class identity.

Today

Testaccio is known for its authentic Roman cuisine — rooted in the quinto quarto ("fifth quarter") offal dishes of the slaughterhouse workers — its covered market, the Nuovo Mercato Testaccio, and cultural venues such as the Mattatoio contemporary art complex and the Città dell'Altra Economia. Landmarks include the Protestant Cemetery, where the poets Keats and Shelley are buried, and the Pyramid of Cestius at the district's edge. The local football club's fans helped give the rione a reputation as the heartland of AS Roma support.
