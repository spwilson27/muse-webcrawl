Ancient Greek comedy

Ancient Greek comedy was a dramatic genre performed at festivals in Athens such as the Dionysia and Lenaia from the 5th century BC. Alongside tragedy and the satyr play, it was one of the three main forms of Greek theatre.

PeriodsOld, Middle and New Comedy
Main authorsAristophanes, Menander

Old Comedy

Old Comedy, exemplified by Aristophanes (The Clouds, Lysistrata, The Frogs), was topical, fantastical and personally satirical, with a chorus that addressed the audience in the parabasis. Cratinus and Eupolis were his main rivals. Only Aristophanes' eleven plays survive complete.

Middle and New Comedy

Middle Comedy (c. 400–323 BC) moved toward mythological burlesque and stock situations as political satire waned; it survives mainly in fragments. New Comedy, typified by Menander's Dyskolos, focused on domestic life, young lovers, clever slaves and mistaken identity, influencing Roman comedy through Plautus and Terence and, through them, European drama and sitcoms.

Structure

Plays typically combined prologue, parodos (chorus entry), agon (contest), episodes and exodos, blending dialogue, song, dance, obscenity and social criticism.
