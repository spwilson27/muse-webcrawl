Old Town Square

Old Town Square (Czech: Staroměstské náměstí) is a historic square in the Old Town quarter of Prague, the capital of the Czech Republic. Founded in the 12th century as a marketplace at the crossroads of European trade routes, it is one of the city's most visited landmarks.

Landmarks

The square is dominated by the Church of Our Lady before Týn with its twin Gothic towers, the Baroque Church of St. Nicholas, and the Old Town Hall, whose tower carries the Prague astronomical clock (Orloj), first installed in 1410 and the oldest astronomical clock still operating. The Jan Hus Memorial, unveiled in 1915, stands at the square's centre.

History

The square witnessed major events of Bohemian history, including the execution of 27 leaders of the Bohemian Revolt in 1621 and mass gatherings during the Velvet Revolution of 1989. Today it hosts Christmas and Easter markets and is ringed by buildings in Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, and Baroque styles.
