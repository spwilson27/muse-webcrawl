Sustainable marketing

Sustainable marketing (also called green marketing when focused on environmental claims) is the process of creating, communicating, and delivering value to customers in such a way that both natural and human capital are preserved or enhanced throughout. It extends conventional marketing by requiring that products, pricing, distribution, and promotion account for long-term ecological and social impacts, not merely immediate sales.

Ecolabels are a common sustainable-marketing tool

Practices

Sustainable marketing includes designing products for durability, reuse, and recyclability; truthful environmental labelling and certification (such as Energy Star or Fairtrade); take-back and product-as-a-service models; and supply-chain transparency. It overlaps with social marketing and corporate social responsibility, and is often summarised through the "triple bottom line" of people, planet, and profit.

Criticism

The field's central controversy is greenwashing — making misleading or unsubstantiated environmental claims. Regulators including the US Federal Trade Commission (Green Guides) and the European Union have tightened rules on claims such as "carbon neutral" and "eco-friendly". Scholars also note a persistent attitude–behaviour gap: consumers report willingness to buy sustainable products but often prioritise price and convenience at the till.
