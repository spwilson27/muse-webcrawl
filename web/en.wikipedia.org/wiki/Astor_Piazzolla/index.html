Astor Piazzolla

Astor Pantaleón Piazzolla
Born: 11 March 1921, Mar del Plata, Argentina
Died: 4 July 1992, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Occupation: Composer, bandoneon player
Genre: Nuevo tango

Astor Pantaleón Piazzolla (March 11, 1921 – July 4, 1992) was an Argentine tango composer, bandoneon player, and arranger. His oeuvre revolutionized the traditional tango into a new style termed nuevo tango, incorporating elements from jazz and classical music. A virtuoso bandoneonist, he regularly performed his own compositions with a variety of ensembles.

In 1992, American music critic Stephen Holden described Piazzolla as "the world's foremost composer of tango music".

Biography

Piazzolla was born in Mar del Plata, Argentina, in 1921, the only child of Italian immigrant parents. In 1925 his family moved to Greenwich Village in New York City, where he was exposed to jazz and the music of Bach. He began playing the bandoneon at the age of eight, and in 1934 met the tango singer Carlos Gardel, who invited the young prodigy to join him on tour.

Returning to Argentina, Piazzolla played in the orchestra of Aníbal Troilo, one of the great traditional tango bandleaders, before forming his own groups. A grant in 1953 took him to Paris to study composition with Nadia Boulanger, who famously urged him to embrace the tango rather than suppress it in favour of classical forms.

Nuevo tango

Back in Buenos Aires, Piazzolla formed ensembles including the Octeto Buenos Aires and later his celebrated Quinteto, blending tango rhythm and melody with the counterpoint, fugue, extended harmony, and improvisation he had absorbed from classical music and jazz. Traditionalists at first condemned his music — "In Argentina everything can change — except the tango" — but his compositions, including Adiós Nonino, written in 1959 on the death of his father, eventually gained worldwide recognition.

Later work and legacy

In the 1970s and 1980s Piazzolla toured internationally and collaborated with musicians such as the jazz saxophonist Gerry Mulligan and the Kronos Quartet. His best-known pieces include Libertango (1974), Oblivion (1982), and the suite The Four Seasons of Buenos Aires, modelled on Vivaldi. He died in Buenos Aires in 1992, leaving more than 3,000 compositions and a transformed musical genre.
