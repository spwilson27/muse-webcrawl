Education Amendments of 1972

Enacted bythe 92nd United States Congress
EffectiveJune 23, 1972
Public lawPub. L. 92–318
Statutes at Large86 Stat. 235

The Education Amendments of 1972 (Public Law 92–318, 86 Stat. 235), enacted June 23, 1972, are a United States federal law that amended the Higher Education Act of 1965 and other education statutes. The amendments are best known for Title IX, which prohibits sex-based discrimination in any education program or activity receiving federal financial assistance.

Title IX

Title IX states: "No person in the United States shall, on the basis of sex, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any education program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance." Sponsored principally by Senator Birch Bayh of Indiana and Representative Patsy Mink of Hawaii, Title IX has had its most visible impact on school athletics, dramatically expanding opportunities for girls and women in sports, though it applies to all aspects of education, including admissions, employment, and protection from sexual harassment.

Other provisions

The 1972 amendments also:

  • Created the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant program, later renamed the Pell Grant, providing need-based aid to undergraduate students.
  • Established the Fund for the Improvement of Postsecondary Education (FIPSE).
  • Created the National Institute of Education.
  • Amended Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Title VII regarding discrimination.
  • Reauthorized and restructured federal student loan and work-study programs.

Legacy

The amendments reshaped American higher education finance and civil rights enforcement in schools. Regulations implementing Title IX were issued in 1975, and the law's athletics requirements were later clarified by policy interpretations and court decisions, including the Supreme Court's rulings on institutional liability for harassment.
