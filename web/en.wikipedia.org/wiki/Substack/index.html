Substack

Substack is an American online platform that provides publishing, payment, analytics, and design infrastructure for subscription newsletters, blogs, and podcasts. Founded in 2017, it enables writers to send digital newsletters directly to subscribers' email inboxes and to charge for subscriptions, taking a percentage (typically 10 percent) of paid subscription revenue in addition to payment-processing fees.

History

Substack was founded in 2017 by Chris Best (a co-founder of the messaging app Kik), journalist Hamish McKenzie, and developer Jairaj Sethi, and launched through the Y Combinator accelerator. It grew rapidly during 2020–2021 as prominent journalists and authors left traditional media outlets to publish independently, attracted by the ability to keep most subscription income. The company raised substantial venture funding and later added podcasting, video, a reader app, and a social-networking feature called Notes (launched in 2023), positioning itself increasingly as a subscription network rather than a pure email tool.

Model and features

Publications on Substack may be free, paid, or a mix (with some posts paywalled). Writers control their mailing lists and can export subscribers' email addresses, a portability feature Substack markets as writer independence. The platform supplies hosting, a web archive of posts, comment sections, recommendation tools that cross-promote publications, and payments via Stripe.

Criticism

Substack has been criticized over its content-moderation stance, particularly its decision to allow some controversial and extremist publications while banning a small number of pro-Nazi newsletters in late 2023 after public pressure. It has also faced scrutiny over advance payments used in its "Substack Pro" program to recruit established writers, and over whether the subscription model is viable for most writers, since earnings are heavily concentrated among a small number of top publications.
