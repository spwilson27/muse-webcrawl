LocationKodiak Island, Alaska, United States
Area1,946,579 acres (7,878 km²)

The Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge is a United States National Wildlife Refuge in the Kodiak Archipelago of south-western Alaska, covering roughly two-thirds of Kodiak Island as well as parts of Uganik Island, Ban Island, the Red Peaks area on Afognak Island and other nearby lands. It was established in 1941 to protect the habitat of the Kodiak bear, and was greatly expanded by the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act of 1980.

Geography and habitat

The refuge spans rugged glaciated mountains, tundra, wetlands, spruce forest and hundreds of kilometres of coastline. There are no roads within the refuge; access is by boat, floatplane or on foot. Its rivers and lakes — including the Karluk and Ayakulik river systems — support all five species of Pacific salmon.

Wildlife

The refuge is home to an estimated 3,000 Kodiak bears, the largest brown bears in the world, which gather at salmon streams in summer. Other mammals include Sitka black-tailed deer, mountain goat, red fox, river otter and tundra vole. Around 250 species of birds have been recorded, including more than a million wintering seabirds and waterfowl, and about 600 nesting pairs of bald eagles.
