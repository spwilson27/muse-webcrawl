San Agustin Church (Filipino: Simbahan ng San Agustin) is a Roman Catholic church in Intramuros, the walled city of Manila, Philippines. Administered by the Order of Saint Augustine, it is the oldest stone church in the country still standing, completed in 1607.

History

Earlier structures of bamboo and nipa were destroyed by fire. The present church, built of adobe stone between 1587 and 1607 to designs associated with Juan Macias, has survived earthquakes, the British occupation of Manila, and the Second World War — it was the only one of Intramuros' seven churches left standing after the Battle of Manila in 1945. The adjoining monastery now houses the Museo San Agustin.

Architecture

The church is noted for its Baroque facade, its trompe-l'œil ceiling paintings, and the tomb of conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi in a side chapel. In 1993 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as one of the Baroque Churches of the Philippines.
