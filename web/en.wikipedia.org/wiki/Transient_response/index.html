Transient response

In electrical engineering and mechanical engineering, a transient response is the response of a system to a change from an equilibrium or steady state. It is the part of the system's output that decays toward zero (or toward the new steady state) after a disturbance, as opposed to the steady-state response that persists.

Characterisation

For a second-order system such as an RLC circuit or a mass–spring–damper, the transient is described by parameters including rise time, peak time, overshoot, and settling time, all governed by the damping ratio and natural frequency. Depending on damping, the response may be overdamped (slow, non-oscillatory), critically damped (fastest non-oscillatory return), or underdamped (decaying oscillations).

Analysis

Transient behaviour is analysed using time-domain step and impulse responses, Laplace transforms, and state-space methods, and it is a central topic in control theory, circuit design, and structural dynamics.
