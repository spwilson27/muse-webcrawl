Noshaq (also spelled Nowshak) is the highest mountain in Afghanistan and the second-highest independent summit of the Hindu Kush range after Tirich Mir. Its summit, at 7,492 metres, lies on the border between Afghanistan's Badakhshan Province and Pakistan.

Climbing

Noshaq was first climbed in 1960 by a Japanese expedition via the normal route from the Qadzi Deh Glacier on the Afghan side. It became a popular objective for expeditions approaching through the Wakhan Corridor. Climbing activity was interrupted for decades by conflict in Afghanistan; ascents resumed in the 2000s, including the first Afghan ascent in 2009. The mountain is notable as the highest peak climbed by Afghan nationals and has been used to promote tourism and mountaineering in the country.

Geography

The peak rises above the Wakhan Corridor, the narrow panhandle of northeastern Afghanistan. Its main summit is accompanied by several subsidiary peaks, including Noshaq East, Central and West.
