Deutsche Bundespost

The Deutsche Bundespost was the federal state-owned postal and telecommunications authority of the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany). Founded in 1947 as the Deutsche Post and renamed in 1950, it operated the country's post, telephone, and later broadcasting networks as a division of the federal Ministry for Post and Telecommunications.

At its peak the Bundespost was one of West Germany's largest employers, with around half a million staff. Its yellow livery and post horn emblem were ubiquitous features of German life. In East Germany, the corresponding authority was the Deutsche Post der DDR, which merged with the Bundespost after reunification in 1990.

As part of postal reform, the Bundespost was dissolved on 1 January 1995 and split into three successor companies: Deutsche Post AG (mail and logistics), Deutsche Telekom AG (telecommunications), and Postbank (postal banking), which were subsequently privatised.
