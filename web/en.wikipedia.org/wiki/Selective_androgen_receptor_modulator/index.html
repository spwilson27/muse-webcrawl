Selective androgen receptor modulator

Selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs) are a class of drugs that bind to the androgen receptor with tissue-selective effects — aiming to produce the anabolic benefits of androgens (increased muscle and bone mass) with fewer of the unwanted effects of conventional anabolic steroids on the prostate, skin, and cardiovascular system. Analogous in concept to SERMs such as tamoxifen for the estrogen receptor, most SARMs are nonsteroidal small molecules.

Development and intended uses

SARMs emerged from pharmaceutical research in the 1990s and 2000s into treatments for muscle wasting (cachexia) in cancer and chronic disease, sarcopenia and frailty in the elderly, osteoporosis, and male hypogonadism. Compounds that reached clinical trials include enobosarm (ostarine, GTx-024), ligandrol (LGD-4033), and andarine (S-4). Despite encouraging early results on lean body mass, none had secured approval from the U.S. FDA as of the mid-2020s; enobosarm was investigated in Phase 3 trials, including in combination with weight-loss drugs to preserve muscle.

Misuse and regulation

SARMs are widely sold online as unapproved "supplements" and misused by bodybuilders and athletes. The World Anti-Doping Agency has prohibited them since 2008, and numerous athletes have been sanctioned after positive tests, sometimes attributed to contaminated products. Health authorities warn that unregulated SARM products may be mislabelled and have been associated with liver injury, suppressed natural testosterone, and cardiovascular risks; their long-term safety is unknown.
