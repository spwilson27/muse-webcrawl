3D projection

3D projection
FieldComputer graphics, descriptive geometry
Main typesPerspective, orthographic, oblique, axonometric

A 3D projection (or graphical projection) is a design technique used to display a three-dimensional object on a two-dimensional surface, such as a screen or sheet of paper. Projections are the foundation of technical drawing and of the rendering pipeline in computer graphics.

Perspective projection

In perspective projection, parallel lines converge toward vanishing points and distant objects appear smaller, mimicking human vision and photography. It is defined by the position of the centre of projection (the virtual camera) and is described mathematically by a projection matrix dividing by depth — the basis of the view frustum in real-time 3D graphics.

Parallel projection

In parallel (orthographic and oblique) projections, projectors are parallel and size does not diminish with distance, preserving measurements. Orthographic views (front, top, side) dominate engineering drawing; axonometric variants such as isometric projection are common in games and illustration. Oblique projections keep one face undistorted while extruding depth at an angle.

Weak perspective

Weak perspective approximates full perspective by scaling each object by its average depth — historically used in early 3D games and still useful in computer vision.
