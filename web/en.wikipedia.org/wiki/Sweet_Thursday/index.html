Sweet Thursday is a 1954 novel by American author John Steinbeck. It is the sequel to Cannery Row (1945), returning to the sardine-canning district of Monterey, California, and its cast of drifters, and was written partly with the stage in mind.

Plot

After the war, the Row has changed: most of the canneries are closed, old friends are gone, and Doc — the marine biologist at the heart of the earlier novel — returns from wartime service lonely and restless. Mack and the boys of the Palace Flophouse decide that what Doc needs is a wife, and the new girl in town, Suzy, a waitress with a past at the Bear Flag Restaurant, seems the obvious candidate. Their elaborate matchmaking schemes misfire repeatedly before the reluctant pair finally come together, with Doc's research into octopus behaviour mirroring his own emotional thaw.

Adaptations

Steinbeck hoped the book would be adapted into a musical; Rodgers and Hammerstein turned the material into the 1955 Broadway musical Pipe Dream, which was a notable commercial failure despite its pedigree. Sweet Thursday remains among Steinbeck's warmest and most comic works.
