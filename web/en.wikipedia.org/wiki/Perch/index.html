Perch

Perch
Family: Percidae
Genus: Perca
Species include European perch, yellow perch, Balkhash perch

Perch is a common name for freshwater fish of the genus Perca, belonging to the family Percidae of the order Perciformes. The name derives from Greek perke and Latin perca, both meaning "perch". Perch are carnivorous fish most commonly found in small ponds, lakes, streams and rivers.

Species

Most authorities recognize three species within the perch genus:

  • The European perch (Perca fluviatilis) is found in Europe and northern Asia.
  • The yellow perch (Perca flavescens), smaller and paler than the European perch, is found in North America.
  • The Balkhash perch (Perca schrenkii) is found in Kazakhstan, in and around Lake Balkhash and the Alakol lakes.

Anatomy and habits

Perch have a long, round body. Their paired pectoral and pelvic fins are close together, and the large first dorsal fin is spiny. The body is covered with rough ctenoid scales. Perch feed on smaller fish, shellfish and insect larvae, and are themselves a popular food and game fish; attempts have been made to rear them artificially.

As food and sport

Perch are prized freshwater game fish, especially in ice fishing. The flesh is firm, white and mild-flavoured, and perch fillets are widely served fried. Yellow perch support important commercial fisheries in the Great Lakes region.
