Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem

Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
Field: Combinatorics
Proved by: Paul Erdős, Chao Ko, Richard Rado (1961)
Conjectured: 1938

The Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem is a theorem in extremal combinatorics concerning intersecting families of finite sets. It states that if a family of k-element subsets of an n-element set is pairwise intersecting (any two sets share at least one element) and n ≥ 2k, then the family has at most C(n−1, k−1) members — the size of the family of all k-sets containing a fixed element.

History and proofs

Erdős, Ko and Rado proved the theorem in 1961, having discovered it in 1938. Several proofs are now known, including Gyula Katona's elegant "circle method" (1972), in which the sets are arranged around a circle, and algebraic proofs using linear algebra methods. The result launched much of modern extremal set theory, with analogues proved for vector spaces, permutations and other structures.
