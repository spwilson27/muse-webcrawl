Poker is a family of comparing card games in which players wager over which hand is best according to a game's rules. Players bet in rounds, may bluff, and win the accumulated pot either by holding the best hand at showdown or by inducing all opponents to fold. Poker originated in the United States in the early 19th century, likely developing from the French game poque and the Persian game as-nas, and spread via Mississippi riverboats and the American Civil War.

A game of Texas hold 'em in progress.

Variants

The main families are draw poker (such as five-card draw), stud poker (seven-card stud), and community card games, of which Texas hold 'em — dominant since the 2000s — and Omaha are the most popular. Each variant has its own betting structure: fixed limit, pot limit, or no limit.

Modern popularity

The World Series of Poker, founded in 1970 in Las Vegas, and televised coverage using hole-card cameras drove the "poker boom" of 2003–2006, amplified by Chris Moneymaker's 2003 WSOP Main Event win after qualifying online. Online poker grew rapidly before the US Department of Justice's 2011 "Black Friday" indictments curtailed the market. Game-theoretic optimal (GTO) strategy and solver software have transformed elite play in the 2010s and 2020s.
