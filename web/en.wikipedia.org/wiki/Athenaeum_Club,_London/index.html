Athenaeum Club, London

Athenaeum Club
Founded: 1824
Location: 107 Pall Mall, London
Type: Private members' club
Clubhouse architect: Decimus Burton

The Athenaeum is a private members' club in London, founded in 1824. Its clubhouse is located at 107 Pall Mall, on the corner of Waterloo Place, in a Neoclassical building designed by Decimus Burton and distinguished by a gilded statue of Pallas Athene, the Greek goddess of wisdom, above the entrance and a frieze copied from the Parthenon.

History

The club was founded by John Wilson Croker and others as a meeting place for men distinguished in science, literature and the arts, together with patrons of those pursuits. It quickly became famous for its library, one of the finest private collections in London. Its early membership included artists, writers and scientists of the first rank, and the club has counted many Nobel laureates among its members.

Membership

Election is by proposal and balloting, and membership is traditionally drawn from the senior ranks of the universities, the church, the law, medicine, the civil service and the arts. The club's Rule II allows the committee to elect distinguished individuals directly. Women were admitted as full members in 2002, following a vote of the membership.

The clubhouse

Burton's building, completed in 1830, contains drawing rooms, a dining room, a library spread over several floors, and one of London's most admired club staircases. A famous anecdote holds that Michael Faraday and Humphry Davy, both members, contributed to the club's early scientific conversation; the Athenaeum remains closely associated with intellectual life in the capital.
