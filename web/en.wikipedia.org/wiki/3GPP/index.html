The 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) is an umbrella term for a number of standards organizations which develop protocols for mobile telecommunications. It was established in December 1998 to produce globally applicable technical specifications for third-generation (3G) mobile systems based on evolved GSM core networks.

Scope

3GPP's work expanded far beyond 3G. It developed the specifications for UMTS and HSPA, then LTE (marketed as 4G), LTE-Advanced, and subsequently the 5G standards known as 5G NR (New Radio) together with the 5G core network. Its specifications are organized into numbered "releases," each adding new features; Release 15 introduced standalone 5G NR, while later releases added enhancements such as 5G-Advanced.

Organizational partners

3GPP unites seven regional telecommunications standard development organizations as Organizational Partners: ETSI (Europe), ATIS (North America), CCSA (China), ARIB and TTC (Japan), TTA (South Korea), and TSDSI (India). Market Representation Partners, such as the GSMA and the 5G Automotive Association, provide market advice.

Structure

Technical work is carried out in three Technical Specification Groups (TSGs): Radio Access Network (RAN), Service and System Aspects (SA), and Core Network and Terminals (CT), each subdivided into working groups. Companies participate through membership in one of the Organizational Partners, and specifications are transposed into standards by those partners.
