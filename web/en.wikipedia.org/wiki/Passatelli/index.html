Passatelli

TypePasta
Place of originItaly
RegionEmilia-Romagna (and parts of Marche and Umbria)
Main ingredientsBreadcrumbs, Parmesan, eggs, nutmeg, lemon zest

Passatelli are a traditional Italian pasta from the Emilia-Romagna region, made from a dough of breadcrumbs, grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese and eggs, flavoured with nutmeg and lemon zest. The dough is pressed through a special perforated iron (ferro per passatelli) — today often a potato ricer with large holes — producing short, thick, slightly rough strands.

Preparation and serving

Passatelli were born as a peasant dish to use up stale bread. Classically they are cooked and served in brodo — in a rich capon or beef broth — as a festive first course in Romagna. In coastal areas of Romagna and the Marche they are also paired with fish broth or seafood sauces, and modern versions are sometimes served dry with vegetable or meat sauces.

Characteristics

Unlike egg pasta, the dough contains no flour beyond the crumbs, giving a tender, delicate texture that must be handled gently so the strands do not disintegrate in the broth. The dish is protected and promoted by local gastronomic associations as part of Romagna's culinary heritage.
