Framingham Risk Score

The Framingham Risk Score is a sex-specific algorithm used to estimate the 10-year cardiovascular risk of an individual. It was developed from data collected in the Framingham Heart Study, a long-term cohort study begun in 1948 in Framingham, Massachusetts.

Inputs and output

The score uses age, sex, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure (and whether it is treated), smoking status, and diabetes. Points for each factor sum to an estimated percentage risk of a coronary heart disease event or broader cardiovascular event over the following ten years. Versions include the 1998 Wilson score for hard coronary events and the 2008 D'Agostino general cardiovascular risk profile.

Clinical use

Guidelines have used the score to guide decisions about statin therapy, aspirin and blood-pressure treatment, stratifying patients into low (<10%), intermediate (10–20%) and high (>20%) risk. It tends to overestimate risk in some contemporary and non-US populations and underestimate it in others, prompting alternatives such as QRISK, SCORE and the ACC/AHA pooled cohort equations.
