Allspice, also known as Jamaica pepper, pimenta or pimento, is the dried unripe berry of Pimenta dioica, a midcanopy tree native to the Greater Antilles, southern Mexico and Central America, now cultivated in many warm parts of the world. English settlers named it in the early 17th century because its flavour seemed to combine cinnamon, nutmeg and cloves.

Production and use

The berries are picked green and traditionally dried in the sun until they turn dark brown and rattle. Jamaica has historically been the principal exporter and its berries are prized for high volatile-oil content (the oil is rich in eugenol). Allspice is essential to Jamaican jerk seasoning and Caribbean cooking generally; it also appears in Middle Eastern spice mixes, Scandinavian baking and pickling, British Christmas pudding and commercial sauces.

History

Christopher Columbus encountered allspice on Jamaica during his second voyage. Early Spanish exporters confused it with black pepper (Piper nigrum), whence "pimenta". The tree resists transplantation of production: attempts to grow it widely elsewhere often failed because the species is dioecious and its seeds pass best through birds.
