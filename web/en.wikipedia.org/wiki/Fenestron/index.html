Fenestron

A Fenestron (from the Occitan word for "little window") is a ducted fan that replaces the conventional tail rotor of a helicopter. Set into a circular housing within the vertical tail fin, the fan's multiple blades provide the anti-torque thrust needed to counteract the main rotor while being shielded from ground strikes and foreign-object damage.

Development

The concept was developed by Sud Aviation in France in the 1960s and first flown on the second prototype of the Aérospatiale Gazelle in 1968. It became a signature feature of helicopters from Aérospatiale and its successors Eurocopter and Airbus Helicopters, appearing on types such as the Dauphin, EC120 Colibri, EC130 and H145.

Characteristics

Advantages include improved safety for ground personnel, reduced noise, and protection of the rotor in confined-area operations. Disadvantages are greater weight, higher construction cost and increased power demand in some flight regimes. Unlike a conventional tail rotor, the Fenestron's many short blades (typically 8–18) are unevenly spaced on some models to spread noise across frequencies, making the helicopter noticeably quieter.
