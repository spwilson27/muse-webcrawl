Mestranol

Mestranol, sold under brand names such as Enovid (in combination) and others, is an estrogen medication used in birth control pills and previously in menopausal hormone therapy. It is the 3-methyl ether of ethinylestradiol and acts as a prodrug: mestranol itself binds only weakly to the estrogen receptor and is converted in the liver to ethinylestradiol.

Chemical formulaC21H26O2
Molar mass310.44 g/mol
ClassEstrogen; estrogen ether

History

Mestranol was the estrogen component of Enovid, the first combined oral contraceptive, approved in the United States in 1960, and of many subsequent pills such as Norinyl and Ortho-Novum. Because conversion to ethinylestradiol is incomplete and variable, mestranol is slightly less potent by weight, and most modern formulations use ethinylestradiol directly. Combined contraceptives containing mestranol have largely been discontinued, surviving in a few products such as Norinyl 1+50.
