Demographics of Iran

Iran's population pyramid reflects the large youth cohort born in the 1980s

The population of Iran is estimated at roughly 89–91 million, making it one of the most populous countries in the Middle East. Iran's population grew dramatically in the twentieth century, from about 10 million in 1900 to over 50 million by the time of the 1979 revolution and roughly 80 million by the 2016 census.

Ethnic and linguistic composition

Persians form the largest ethnic group, and Persian (Farsi) is the official language. Significant minorities include Azerbaijanis, Kurds, Lurs, Arabs, Baloch, Turkmen and others, each with their own languages spoken alongside Persian. Roughly three-quarters of Iranians live in urban areas; the largest city is the capital, Tehran, with around 9 million residents in the city proper and some 15 million in its metropolitan area.

Religion and fertility

The overwhelming majority of Iranians are Muslims, mostly Twelver Shia — Iran is the world's largest Shia-majority country — with a Sunni minority and small recognized communities of Christians, Jews and Zoroastrians. After rapid growth in the 1980s, one of the most successful family-planning programs in the world brought fertility down sharply; the total fertility rate fell below replacement level by the early 2000s and has remained low, prompting government concern about population aging.
