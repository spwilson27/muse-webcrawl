TransUnion

TransUnion is an American consumer credit reporting agency headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. It is one of the three largest credit bureaus in the United States, alongside Equifax and Experian, collecting and aggregating information on more than one billion individual consumers in over thirty countries.

History

TransUnion was formed in 1968 as the holding company for the Union Tank Car Company, a railcar leasing business. In 1969 it acquired the Credit Bureau of Cook County and began expanding nationally. The Marmon Group controlled the company for decades before selling it in 2010; it later passed through ownership by Advent International and Goldman Sachs, and in 2015 TransUnion held an initial public offering on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker TRU.

Products and services

The company sells credit reports, credit scores, and analytics to lenders, insurers, employers, and landlords, and markets identity protection and credit monitoring products directly to consumers, including the Credit Karma business it partially supplies with data. Its VantageScore product, developed jointly with Equifax and Experian in 2006, competes with the FICO score. Like other bureaus, TransUnion operates under the U.S. Fair Credit Reporting Act, which entitles consumers to free copies of their reports and a means to dispute errors.

Incidents

In March 2022 the company's South African unit suffered a data breach in which attackers accessed personal information of millions of South Africans. TransUnion has also faced regulatory actions and class-action litigation over inaccurate reporting, including disputes concerning consumers wrongly flagged on watch lists.
