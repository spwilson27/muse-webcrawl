Blues shouter

A blues shouter is a blues singer, often male, capable of singing unamplified — or with only modest amplification — over the sound of a jazz or rhythm-and-blues orchestra. The style flourished in the 1940s and 1950s, bridging big-band swing and rhythm and blues, and it strongly influenced rock and roll.

Style and performers

Shouters combined the melodic phrasing of blues with the rhythmic drive of swing bands, delivering lyrics with volume, grit and call-and-response interplay with horn sections. The archetype was Big Joe Turner, whose career ran from Kansas City boogie-woogie ("Roll 'Em Pete", 1938) to "Shake, Rattle and Roll" (1954), a hit that helped launch rock and roll. Other celebrated shouters include Wynonie Harris ("Good Rockin' Tonight"), Jimmy Witherspoon, Jimmy Rushing of the Count Basie Orchestra, Eddie "Cleanhead" Vinson and Roy Brown. The style underlies jump blues and the work of early rock vocalists.

Legacy

As small-combo R&B and electric blues replaced the big bands, and microphones made raw volume unnecessary, shouting receded, but its phrasing and repertoire shaped soul, rock and roll and modern blues singing.
