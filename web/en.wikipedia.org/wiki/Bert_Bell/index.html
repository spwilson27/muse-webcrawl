De Benneville "Bert" Bell (25 February 1895 – 11 October 1959) was an American sports executive who co-founded the Philadelphia Eagles of the National Football League (NFL) and served as the league's commissioner from 1946 until his death in 1959. As commissioner he is credited with professionalising the NFL: he introduced the college draft system (originally as Eagles owner, in 1936, to promote competitive balance), engineered the league's merger with the All-America Football Conference, established revenue-sharing and television policies, and coined the phrase "on any given Sunday, any team in our league can beat any other team."

Early life and the Eagles

Bell was born into a wealthy Philadelphia family and played quarterback at the University of Pennsylvania. In 1933, with Lud Wray, he bought the NFL's Frankford Yellow Jackets franchise rights and founded the Philadelphia Eagles. Struggling financially, Bell proposed the NFL draft in 1935 to restrain player salaries and help weaker teams; the first draft was held in 1936. He later sold his Eagles interest and became co-owner of the Pittsburgh Steelers with Art Rooney.

Commissioner

Bell succeeded Elmer Layden as commissioner in 1946. He negotiated the 1949 peace with the rival AAFC, recognised the players' association, instituted sudden-death overtime for championship games (first used in the famous 1958 NFL Championship Game), and managed the league's first national television contracts while blacking out home games to protect attendance. He also imposed a strict anti-gambling regime following the 1946 championship game betting scandal.

Death and legacy

Bell died of a heart attack on 11 October 1959 while attending an Eagles–Steelers game at Franklin Field in Philadelphia, between the two teams he had owned. He was succeeded as commissioner by Pete Rozelle in 1960 and was inducted as a charter member of the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1963.
