Venipuncture

Venipuncture (also spelled venepuncture) is the process of obtaining intravenous access in order to collect a sample of venous blood or to administer intravenous therapy. It is one of the most common invasive procedures in health care and is performed by phlebotomists, nurses, paramedics and physicians.

Procedure

The usual site is a vein in the antecubital fossa at the elbow — typically the median cubital vein — or on the back of the hand. A tourniquet is applied above the site to make the vein prominent, the skin is disinfected, and a sterile needle is inserted at a shallow angle. Blood is drawn into evacuated tubes in a prescribed order of draw to avoid additive cross-contamination, or into a syringe. After collection the tourniquet is released, the needle withdrawn, and pressure applied with gauze to prevent a haematoma.

Uses

Venipuncture provides samples for laboratory analysis — full blood counts, biochemistry, blood cultures, coagulation studies and blood typing — and is the first step in blood donation, therapeutic phlebotomy for conditions such as haemochromatosis, and establishing intravenous access.

Complications

Complications are usually minor: bruising, pain, vasovagal fainting and small haematomas. Rare complications include nerve injury, arterial puncture and infection.
