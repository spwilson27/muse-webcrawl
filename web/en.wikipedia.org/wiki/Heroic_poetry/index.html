Heroic poetry

Heroic poetry is a genre of narrative poetry celebrating the deeds of heroes and legendary figures, often connected with a people's traditional history and heroic age. Emerging from oral tradition, heroic poems were composed and performed for audiences in courts and halls, preserving the exploits of warriors such as Achilles, Beowulf, Roland, Siegfried and the Cid.

Characteristics

Heroic poetry typically features an elevated style, formulaic diction, catalogues, boasts and flyting, single combats, and themes of honour, loyalty, vengeance, fate and the winning of fame (kleos) in the face of death. Many works were originally sung to musical accompaniment and show the marks of oral-formulaic composition studied by Milman Parry and Albert Lord.

Examples

Major traditions include the Greek epics Iliad and Odyssey; the Old English Beowulf; the German Nibelungenlied; the French Song of Roland; the Spanish Cantar de mio Cid; the Persian Shahnameh; the Indian Mahabharata and Ramayana; the Finnish Kalevala; and the Serbo-Croatian heroic songs sung to the gusle. Shorter heroic lays, such as the Old High German Hildebrandslied, stand alongside the great epics.

Influence

Heroic poetry shaped national literatures and later literary epic, from Virgil's Aeneid to Milton's Paradise Lost, and continues to influence modern fantasy literature and popular culture.
