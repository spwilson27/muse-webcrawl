Funeral potatoes

Funeral potatoes are a traditional potato hot dish (casserole) associated with the American Midwest and Mountain West, and especially with members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Utah and Idaho. The dish is commonly served at gatherings after funerals—where Relief Society members traditionally provide a meal for the bereaved family—as well as at potlucks and holiday dinners.

Composition

The casserole typically combines diced or shredded potatoes (often frozen hash browns) with a canned cream soup, sour cream, grated cheese and butter, topped with crushed cornflakes or potato chips that bake to a crisp crust. Regional and family variations add onions, use different cheeses, or substitute breadcrumbs for the topping. The dish is calorie-dense comfort food designed to feed a crowd and transport well.

Cultural recognition

Funeral potatoes have become an emblem of Utah food culture: during the 2002 Salt Lake City Winter Olympics, a commemorative pin depicting a bowl of funeral potatoes was one of the most sought-after souvenirs. The dish is sometimes called "Mormon potatoes" and appears in community cookbooks throughout the Intermountain West.
