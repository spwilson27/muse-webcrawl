Sustainability appraisal

A sustainability appraisal (SA) is a systematic process for evaluating the social, economic and environmental effects of a plan, programme or policy, used especially in land-use planning in the United Kingdom. Its purpose is to test policy options against sustainability objectives and to improve the plan before adoption. Sustainability appraisal incorporates the requirements of strategic environmental assessment (SEA).

Legal basis

In England, sustainability appraisal of local development plans became a statutory requirement under the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004, and the process is designed simultaneously to satisfy the European SEA Directive (2001/42/EC), which requires environmental assessment of certain plans and programmes. Similar arrangements apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Process

The appraisal typically proceeds through stages: (1) a scoping stage, establishing the context, baseline data and a framework of sustainability objectives and indicators; (2) assessment of the plan's options and draft policies against those objectives, predicting significant effects; (3) preparation of a sustainability report published alongside the draft plan for consultation; (4) consideration of responses and changes before examination and adoption; and (5) monitoring of the plan's actual effects during implementation, so that unintended consequences can be identified.

Criticism

Practitioners have criticised sustainability appraisal for sometimes becoming a procedural, box-ticking exercise, for the difficulty of weighting economic against environmental and social effects, and for appraisals that rationalise decisions rather than shape them. Supporters argue it remains a useful structured way to integrate sustainability into plan-making.
