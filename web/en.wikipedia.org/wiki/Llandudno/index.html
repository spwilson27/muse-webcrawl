Llandudno

Llandudno (Welsh pronunciation: [ɬanˈdɪdnɔ]) is a seaside resort town and community in Conwy County Borough, Wales, located on the Creuddyn peninsula between the Great Orme and Little Orme headlands, which protrude into the Irish Sea. With a population of around 20,000, it is Wales's largest seaside resort, often styled the "Queen of the Welsh Resorts".

History

The town takes its name from Saint Tudno, a 6th-century saint who founded a church on the Great Orme. Copper was mined on the Great Orme from the Bronze Age, and its mines are among the largest prehistoric copper mines in the world. Modern Llandudno was developed from 1849 onwards by the Mostyn family as a planned Victorian resort, with a wide promenade curving around Llandudno Bay between the two Ormes.

The Llandudno Pier, opened in 1877 and extending 700 metres (2,295 ft) into the sea, is the longest pier in Wales. The town has strong associations with Lewis Carroll: the family of Alice Liddell, the inspiration for Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, holidayed here, and the town has a White Rabbit statue and an Alice trail.

Attractions

The Great Orme is served by the Great Orme Tramway, one of Britain's few cable-hauled street tramways, and by a cable car. Other attractions include Venue Cymru theatre, the north-shore and west-shore beaches, Happy Valley gardens, and a resident herd of Kashmiri goats, descendants of a herd presented to Queen Victoria, which roam the Great Orme and famously wandered the empty town during the COVID-19 lockdown of 2020.
