An attending physician, also known simply as an attending, is a physician in the United States and Canada who has completed residency and fellowship training and practices independently in their specialty. In teaching hospitals, the attending is the doctor who is ultimately responsible for a patient's care and who supervises and teaches residents, fellows and medical students.

Role

The attending physician holds final responsibility for diagnosis, treatment plans and discharge decisions, reviews and co-signs trainees' notes and orders, and performs or supervises procedures. The term reflects the older practice of the patient being "attended" by the senior doctor, assisted by house staff. In inpatient services, attending-led teaching rounds are a central part of graduate medical education.

Related terms

The closest equivalent in the United Kingdom and much of the Commonwealth is the consultant. A hospital-based attending who specialises in inpatient care is called a hospitalist. The "attending of record" is a formal designation in hospital documentation and billing, indicating the physician accountable for an episode of care.
