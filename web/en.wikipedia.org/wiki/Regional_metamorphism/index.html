Regional metamorphism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Banded gneiss, a high-grade product of regional metamorphism

Regional metamorphism is a type of metamorphism that affects large volumes of the Earth's crust over areas of thousands of square kilometres. It is associated with mountain-building (orogenic) events at convergent plate boundaries, where rocks are subjected to elevated temperatures and directed pressures during burial, deformation and crustal thickening. Regional metamorphism produces most of the metamorphic rocks exposed in the continental shields and in the crystalline cores of mountain ranges.

Causes

During an orogeny, sediments and volcanic rocks on a continental margin are buried to depths of tens of kilometres, folded and faulted. Heat from the Earth's interior, thickened radiogenic crust and intruding magmas raises temperatures, while tectonic compression imposes a directed stress (deviatoric stress) that deforms the rocks and aligns their minerals. Fluids released by dehydration reactions assist chemical changes and the growth of new minerals.

Products

The hallmark of regional metamorphism is the development of foliation — slaty cleavage, schistosity and gneissic banding — as platy and elongate minerals recrystallise perpendicular to the maximum compressive stress. A typical progressive sequence for a shale protolith with increasing metamorphic grade is slate → phyllite → schist → gneiss, and at the highest grades partial melting (anatexis) produces migmatite. Sandstones become quartzite and limestones become marble, which typically lack strong foliation because of their monomineralic composition.

Metamorphic facies and gradients

Regional terranes are described in terms of metamorphic facies — characteristic mineral assemblages formed under particular pressure–temperature conditions — and by their apparent thermal gradient. Barrow's zones in the Scottish Highlands, mapped by George Barrow in 1893, defined the classic Barrovian sequence (chlorite, biotite, garnet, staurolite, kyanite, sillimanite zones) characteristic of medium-pressure regional metamorphism. Low-pressure, high-temperature (Buchan) metamorphism and high-pressure (blueschist and eclogite facies) metamorphism in subduction zones represent contrasting pressure–temperature regimes.
