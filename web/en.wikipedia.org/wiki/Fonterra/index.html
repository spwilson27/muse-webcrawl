Fonterra

TypeCooperative
IndustryDairy
Founded16 October 2001
HeadquartersAuckland, New Zealand

Fonterra Co-operative Group Limited is a New Zealand multinational publicly traded dairy co-operative owned by around 9,000 New Zealand farmers. The company is responsible for approximately 30% of the world's dairy exports and has revenue exceeding NZ$23 billion, making it New Zealand's largest company.

Fonterra was established in October 2001 following the merger of the country's two largest dairy co-operatives, New Zealand Dairy Group and Kiwi Co-operative Dairies, together with the New Zealand Dairy Board. The merger was enabled by the Dairy Industry Restructuring Act 2001, which exempted the combined entity from normal competition law restrictions.

Operations

Fonterra collects milk from its shareholder farms across New Zealand and processes it into products including whole milk powder, butter, cheese, and specialised nutritional ingredients. Its consumer brands include Anchor, Mainland, and Anmum. The co-operative sells products in more than 130 countries, with major operations in Australia, China, Latin America, and Southeast Asia.

Controversies

In 2008, Fonterra held a 43% stake in the Chinese dairy company Sanlu, which was at the centre of the melamine-contaminated infant formula scandal. Fonterra has also faced criticism over the environmental impact of intensive dairy farming in New Zealand, and scrutiny of its capital structure, which led to the launch of the Fonterra Shareholders' Fund in 2012, allowing public investment in units linked to the co-operative's dividends.
