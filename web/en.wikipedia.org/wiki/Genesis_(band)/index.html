Genesis (band)

Genesis are an English rock band formed at Charterhouse School in Godalming, Surrey, in 1967. The band's classic line-up of Peter Gabriel, Steve Hackett, Mike Rutherford, Tony Banks, and Phil Collins developed a theatrical progressive rock style in the 1970s. After Gabriel's departure in 1975 and Hackett's in 1977, the remaining trio of Banks, Rutherford, and Collins (as lead singer) moved toward accessible pop rock and achieved major commercial success in the 1980s and early 1990s. Genesis have sold an estimated 100–150 million albums worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2010.

History

Genesis formed from two Charterhouse school bands, with songwriter-producer Jonathan King signing them and naming the group. After the overlooked debut From Genesis to Revelation (1969), they developed their progressive style on Trespass (1970), Nursery Cryme (1971), Foxtrot (1972), Selling England by the Pound (1973), and the concept album The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway (1974), with Gabriel's elaborate costumes making their concerts famous.

Collins replaced Gabriel as frontman for A Trick of the Tail (1976). As a trio from 1978, Genesis shifted style with ...And Then There Were Three..., Duke (1980), Abacab (1981), Genesis (1983), and Invisible Touch (1986), whose title track became their only U.S. number-one single. We Can't Dance (1991) was Collins's last studio album with the band before his 1996 departure; Ray Wilson fronted the less successful Calling All Stations (1997). The Banks–Rutherford–Collins trio reunited for tours in 2007 and again for The Last Domino? tour (2021–2022), which Collins, limited by ill health, performed seated.

Legacy

Genesis are regarded as pioneers of progressive rock and neo-prog, and their members' solo careers — Gabriel's and Collins's in particular — made them among the few acts with multiple members selling over 100 million records each. The band's 1986–87 Invisible Touch Tour was at the time the largest world tour by a rock group.
