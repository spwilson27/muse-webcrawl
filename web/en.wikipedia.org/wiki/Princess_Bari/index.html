Princess Bari (Korean: 바리공주, "the Abandoned Princess") is a goddess of Korean mythology and the central figure of the narrative shamanic song Baridegi (also known as the Song of Princess Bari), one of the best-known myths in the Korean shamanic tradition. She is worshipped as the deity who guides the souls of the dead to the afterlife, and her myth is sung during funeral rites, especially the jinogi-gut performed to escort the deceased's spirit.

The myth

In the best-known versions, a king and queen who longed for a son were blessed instead with seven daughters. Enraged at the birth of a seventh girl, the king ordered the infant abandoned — bari derives from the Korean verb meaning "to abandon" — and she was set adrift in a stone box on a stream. Rescued and raised by an elderly couple (in some versions blessed by the Buddha), Bari learned of her origins and returned to the palace, where her parents had fallen mortally ill. The only cure was the medicinal water of the Western Heaven. Where her sisters refused, Bari disguised herself as a man and undertook the journey to the underworld, enduring years of servitude to the guardian of the water, bearing him seven sons, and finally winning the elixir. Returning, she found her parents already dead, but revived them with the water and the flowers of resurrection.

Worship and significance

Offered the reward of a kingdom, Bari refused and asked instead to become the goddess who guides the dead, ferrying souls across to paradise. Because she crossed between the worlds of the living and the dead, she is considered the mythic ancestress and divine patroness of shamans (mudang), and her story is interpreted as an archetype of filial piety, endurance, and the redemptive journey. The tale exists in more than a hundred recorded regional variants and remains a staple of Korean oral literature, modern retellings, theatre, and children's books.
