Bayesian nonparametric statistics

Bayesian nonparametric statistics is the branch of Bayesian statistics in which the model is placed on an infinite-dimensional parameter space — most commonly the space of all probability distributions — rather than being described by a fixed finite set of parameters. "Nonparametric" here does not mean parameter-free; it means the effective number of parameters can grow with the amount of data.

Priors on distribution spaces

The foundational object is the Dirichlet process, introduced by Thomas Ferguson in 1973: a stochastic process whose sample paths are probability distributions, governed by a base distribution and a concentration parameter. A draw from a Dirichlet process is discrete, which makes it natural for clustering via its "Chinese restaurant process" representation. Other widely used priors include the Pólya tree, Dirichlet process mixtures for density estimation, the Indian buffet process for latent feature models, and Gaussian processes, which place priors directly on function spaces and underpin Bayesian nonparametric regression and classification.

Methods and computation

Posterior computation typically uses Markov chain Monte Carlo methods such as Gibbs sampling, exploiting conjugacy where available, or variational approximations for large data sets. Posterior consistency and contraction rates — whether the posterior concentrates around the true distribution as data accumulate — form a major theoretical research area.

Applications

Bayesian nonparametric methods are used for flexible density estimation, clustering without fixing the number of clusters, topic modelling, survival analysis (for example with beta and neutral-to-the-right processes), and reinforcement learning. Software packages in R and Python have made Dirichlet process mixture models in particular routine in applied work.
