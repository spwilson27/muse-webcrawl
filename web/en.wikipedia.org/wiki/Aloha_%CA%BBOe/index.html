Aloha ʻOe

ComposerLiliʻuokalani
Writtenc. 1878
GenreHawaiian song

"Aloha ʻOe" ("Farewell to Thee") is the most famous song composed by Liliʻuokalani, the last reigning monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii. Written around 1878, it has become a symbol of Hawaii and is sung at farewells, graduations, and public gatherings.

Composition

According to the queen's account, the song was inspired by a farewell embrace she witnessed between a young Hawaiian girl and an officer during a visit to a ranch on Oahu. The melody of the chorus resembles the earlier song "The Lone Rock by the Sea". Liliʻuokalani, a prolific composer, wrote the lyrics in Hawaiian.

Lyrics and legacy

The chorus begins "Aloha ʻoe, aloha ʻoe / E ke onaona noho i ka lipo" ("Farewell to thee, farewell to thee / Thou charming one who dwells in the shaded bowers"). The song has been recorded by numerous artists and remains a standard of Hawaiian music.
