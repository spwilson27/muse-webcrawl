Barotrauma

CausePressure difference between gas spaces in the body and the environment
SitesMiddle ear, sinuses, lungs, teeth, gastrointestinal tract
ContextsDiving, aviation, mechanical ventilation, explosions

Barotrauma is physical damage to body tissues caused by a difference in pressure between a gas space inside, or in contact with, the body and the surrounding gas or liquid. The damage results when the pressure difference causes the gas space to expand or contract beyond the tissue's ability to accommodate it.

Types

Common forms include middle-ear barotrauma ("ear squeeze"), the most frequent injury in divers and air travellers, when pressure in the middle ear fails to equalise through the Eustachian tube; sinus barotrauma; and pulmonary barotrauma, in which expanding gas over-distends the lungs during ascent while breath-holding, potentially causing pneumothorax or arterial gas embolism. Barotrauma can also affect teeth (barodontalgia), the gastrointestinal tract, and — in medicine — the lungs of mechanically ventilated patients exposed to excessive airway pressures.

In diving

Diving barotrauma occurs during descent (a "squeeze" of air spaces such as mask, ears and sinuses) or ascent (over-expansion). Divers are trained to equalise pressure early and often using techniques such as the Valsalva manoeuvre, and never to hold their breath while ascending on compressed gas.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention centres on slow pressure changes, equalisation, and avoiding diving or flying with congested airways. Treatment ranges from rest and decongestants for mild ear barotrauma to recompression in a hyperbaric chamber for arterial gas embolism, and emergency decompression for pneumothorax.
