Host family

A host family is a family that provides accommodation, meals, and care in their home to a guest — most commonly a foreign exchange student, an au pair, a language-study student on a homestay, or a volunteer or visiting scholar — for a period ranging from a few weeks to an academic year.

Exchange students and homestays

In student exchange programs such as those run by AFS Intercultural Programs, Rotary Youth Exchange, and EF, host families are typically volunteers who receive no payment, hosting from a wish to share their culture. Language schools and study-abroad organizations also arrange paid homestays, in which the student pays the family through an agency for a room and meals ("full board" or "half board"). Living with a host family is intended to immerse the guest in everyday language and customs, complementing classroom study.

Selection and responsibilities

Placement organizations screen host families through interviews, home visits, criminal background checks, and references, and provide orientation and a local coordinator for both family and student. Host families generally provide a bed, meals, a place to study, and inclusion in family life; students are expected to follow house rules, attend school, and participate in family activities. Programs designate procedures for changing placements if the match fails. Related arrangements include host families for international athletes, foster care, and wartime evacuee billeting programs such as Britain's Kindertransport and evacuation schemes.
