Zero Dark Thirty

Zero Dark Thirty is a 2012 American political action thriller film directed by Kathryn Bigelow and written by Mark Boal. It dramatises the decade-long manhunt for al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden after the September 11 attacks, culminating in his death in the May 2011 raid on his compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan.

Plot

CIA analyst Maya Harris, recruited straight out of high school, spends her career fixated on finding bin Laden. After years of dead ends, detainee interrogations under the CIA's "enhanced interrogation" programme, and painstaking analysis, she follows the trail of bin Laden's courier, Abu Ahmed al-Kuwaiti, to a large compound in Abbottabad. Maya's certainty that bin Laden is hiding there persuades the CIA to mount Operation Neptune Spear by SEAL Team Six, who kill him in the night raid that gives the film its title — "zero dark thirty" being military slang for the small hours of the morning.

Reception and controversy

The film was a critical success, winning the Academy Award for Best Sound Editing and receiving nominations including Best Picture and Best Actress for Jessica Chastain, who won the Golden Globe for her performance. It also provoked intense controversy over its depiction of torture: several US senators and commentators charged that the film implied torture produced the key intelligence leading to bin Laden, while the filmmakers maintained it portrayed a range of intelligence work. The film also drew scrutiny over the access Boal and Bigelow were given to classified details of the operation.
