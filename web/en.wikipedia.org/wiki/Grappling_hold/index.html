Grappling hold

A grappling hold, commonly called simply a hold, is any specific technique consisting of gripping or controlling an opponent, used in many forms of grappling, martial arts, and combat sports. Holds are employed to control an opponent's movement, set up throws, gain a positional advantage, or force a submission, and they form a core element of arts such as judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, wrestling, sambo, and mixed martial arts.

Categories

  • Clinch holds — applied while standing, such as the collar tie and bear hug, to control posture and set up takedowns.
  • Pinning holds — holding an opponent to the ground, as in judo's osaekomi techniques, scoring points or exhausting the opponent.
  • Submission holds — techniques intended to force a tap-out, divided into joint locks, which hyperextend or rotate a joint (armlocks, leglocks, wristlocks), and chokeholds or strangles, which compress the neck's air passages or carotid arteries (for example the rear naked choke and triangle choke).
  • Escapes and counters — techniques for freeing oneself from an opponent's hold.

Safety

Submission holds can cause serious injury or, in the case of strangles held too long, unconsciousness and death; they are therefore practised under supervision with a convention of tapping out to signal surrender. Rules in sport grappling prohibit certain dangerous holds, and law-enforcement use of some neck restraints is restricted in many jurisdictions.
