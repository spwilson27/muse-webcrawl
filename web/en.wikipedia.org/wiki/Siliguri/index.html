Siliguri is a major city in the Indian state of West Bengal, situated on the banks of the Mahananda River at the foothills of the Himalayas. It straddles the Darjeeling and Jalpaiguri districts and is the largest city of North Bengal. Siliguri is widely known as the "Gateway of Northeast India" because the narrow Siliguri Corridor — the so-called "Chicken's Neck" — near the city is the only land connection between mainland India and its eight northeastern states.

Geography and strategic importance

The city lies in the Terai region, a flat alluvial plain south of the outer Himalayas. Its location is of exceptional strategic significance: the Siliguri Corridor, roughly 20–22 km wide at its narrowest, connects the rest of India with the Northeast and is bordered by Nepal, Bhutan, and Bangladesh. Siliguri also serves as the transit point for traffic to the hill towns of Darjeeling, Kalimpong, and Gangtok in Sikkim.

Economy and transport

Siliguri is a major commercial and trading hub, historically built on the "three T's" — tea, timber, and tourism — the produce of the surrounding Dooars and Terai regions. It is one of India's most important transport nodes: New Jalpaiguri Junction (NJP), just south of the city, is among the busiest railway stations in eastern India, and Bagdogra Airport is the principal airport for the region. National Highway 27 and the Asian Highway network pass through the city.

Demographics

The city is ethnically and linguistically diverse, with Bengali, Nepali, Hindi, Marwari, Bihari, and other communities. Hinduism is the majority religion, with significant Muslim, Buddhist, and Christian minorities.
