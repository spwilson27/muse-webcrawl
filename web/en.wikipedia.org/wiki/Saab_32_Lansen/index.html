The Saab 32 Lansen (Swedish for "the Lance") is a two-seat, transonic military aircraft designed and manufactured by Saab for the Swedish Air Force. It was Sweden's first aircraft capable of exceeding the speed of sound in a shallow dive, and it served in attack (A 32A), all-weather fighter (J 32B) and reconnaissance (S 32C) versions.

Development

Designed from the late 1940s to replace Saab's piston-engined attack aircraft, the Lansen used a swept wing developed with the help of captured German research and was powered by a licence-built Rolls-Royce Avon engine, the Svenska Flygmotor RM5 and later RM6 series. Development proceeded through extensive wind-tunnel work and a scaled test aircraft, the Saab 210 "Lilldraken", in related programmes. The prototype first flew in November 1952.

Service

The attack version entered service in 1956, armed with cannon, rockets, bombs and anti-ship missiles, including the early Rb 04. The J 32B fighter variant, with a more powerful engine and radar, defended Swedish airspace through the 1960s before being replaced in the interceptor role by the Saab 35 Draken. Reconnaissance and, later, electronic warfare and target-towing Lansen variants soldiered on long after the frontline versions, and the last examples were retired in 1997 — among the longest-serving Swedish combat aircraft.
