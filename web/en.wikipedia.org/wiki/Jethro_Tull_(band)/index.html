Jethro Tull are a British rock band formed in Blackpool, England, in 1967. Initially playing blues rock and jazz fusion, the band developed a signature sound incorporating folk, classical music and hard rock, fronted by vocalist, flautist and guitarist Ian Anderson, famous for playing the flute while standing on one leg. The band are named after the 18th-century agriculturalist Jethro Tull.

History

The band's second album, Stand Up (1969), reached number one in the UK. Their breakthrough came with Aqualung (1971), whose title track and "Locomotive Breath" became rock-radio staples, followed by the single-song concept album Thick as a Brick (1972), which topped the US charts. Later successes included A Passion Play (1973), War Child (1974) and Songs from the Wood (1977).

In 1989 the band controversially won the first Grammy Award for Best Hard Rock/Metal Performance for Crest of a Knave (1987), beating favourites Metallica — an upset still cited in Grammy lore. Jethro Tull have sold an estimated 60 million albums worldwide. After Anderson ended the band in 2012, he revived the name from 2017 and released new studio albums including The Zealot Gene (2022), the group's first in nearly two decades.

Style

Anderson's flute and acoustic guitar, Martin Barre's electric guitar work, complex time signatures, and literate, often wry lyrics — touching on religion, English folklore and social observation — define the band's sound, bridging progressive rock and folk rock.
