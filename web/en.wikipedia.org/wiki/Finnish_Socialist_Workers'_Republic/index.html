Finnish Socialist Workers' Republic

The Finnish Socialist Workers' Republic (Finnish: Suomen sosialistinen työväentasavalta), more commonly referred to as Red Finland, was a short-lived socialist state established in Finland during the Finnish Civil War of 1918. It controlled the industrial south of the country, including Helsinki, from late January to late April or early May 1918, when it was defeated by the White forces of the Senate of Finland, supported by the German Empire.

Background

Finland declared independence from Russia on 6 December 1917, following the October Revolution. Deep social tensions remained: the Social Democratic Party had won a parliamentary majority in 1916 but lost it in the 1917 elections, while landless rural labourers and industrial workers demanded reform. Russian troops remained stationed in the country, and both the socialist Red Guards and the bourgeois White Guards armed themselves through late 1917.

Establishment and government

On 27–28 January 1918 the Reds seized Helsinki and the major towns of southern Finland. A revolutionary government, the Finnish People's Delegation, was formed under Kullervo Manner, and the Senate of Finland fled to Vaasa in the west. The People's Delegation proclaimed a programme of democratic and socialist reforms and negotiated a treaty of friendship with Soviet Russia, signed in Petrograd on 1 March 1918, under which Russia ceded territory in Karelia and the Pechenga area in principle, though the transfers were never fully realised.

Red Finland's territory covered the most industrialised and populous parts of the country, but it suffered from shortages of food, arms and trained officers, and its Red Guards could not break the White front in the north.

Defeat

The Whites under General C. G. E. Mannerheim, reinforced in April 1918 by the German Baltic Sea Division, which landed at Hanko and captured Helsinki on 12–13 April, steadily crushed the Red state. The People's Delegation fled to Vyborg and then to Soviet Russia. The last Red strongholds surrendered in early May 1918. Tens of thousands of Red prisoners were held in prison camps, where many died of hunger and disease, and thousands were executed during and after the war.

Legacy

Red Finland remains a painful episode in Finnish history. Its leaders established the Communist Party of Finland in Moscow in August 1918. Finland instead developed as a parliamentary republic, and the social reforms demanded by the Reds — including land reform and the eight-hour working day — were partly enacted by the victors in the years after the war.
