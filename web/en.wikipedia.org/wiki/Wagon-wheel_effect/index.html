Wagon-wheel effect

The wagon-wheel effect (also called the stagecoach-wheel effect) is an optical illusion in which a spoked wheel appears to rotate differently from its true rotation — slowly, backwards, or not at all — when viewed on film or video, or under strobe lighting.

Cause

Film and video sample motion at a fixed frame rate. If a wheel rotates by slightly less than the angle between spokes between frames, the brain matches each spoke to the nearest corresponding spoke in the next frame, so the wheel appears to turn backwards (temporal aliasing, related to the Nyquist limit). Similar effects occur under flickering light such as mains-frequency fluorescent lighting.

Human vision debate

Reports of the effect in continuous light have fuelled debate about whether human vision samples the world in discrete "frames"; most researchers attribute continuous-light cases to other mechanisms, such as attentional rivalry between motion interpretations.

The effect is also seen with aircraft propellers and helicopter rotors in videos, sometimes appearing to hover nearly stationary.
