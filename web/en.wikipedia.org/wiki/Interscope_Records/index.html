Interscope Records is an American record label based in Santa Monica, California, owned by Universal Music Group through its Interscope Geffen A&M imprint. Founded in late 1990 by Jimmy Iovine and Ted Field as a $20 million joint venture with Atlantic Records, it differed from most labels by letting A&R staff control decisions and signing artists quickly.

Interscope's headquarters in Santa Monica, California.

History

Interscope achieved early success with Gerardo's "Rico Suave" and Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch in 1991. Its distribution of Death Row Records releases by Dr. Dre (The Chronic, 1992), Snoop Dogg and Tupac Shakur made it the defining label of 1990s West Coast hip hop, but also drew controversy over gangsta rap lyrics; Time Warner divested its stake in 1995. Seagram acquired Interscope in 1996 for its MCA/Universal group, and after acquiring Geffen Records and A&M Records the three were merged in 1999 under the Interscope Geffen A&M umbrella.

In the 2000s Interscope remained home to Eminem (via Shady/Aftermath), 50 Cent, Gwen Stefani and No Doubt, The Black Eyed Peas, and Lady Gaga. Its joint ventures have included Aftermath Entertainment (Dr. Dre), Shady Records (Eminem), will.i.am Music Group, and Top Dawg Entertainment partnerships; later signings and deals brought Billie Eilish, Kendrick Lamar, Lana Del Rey, Olivia Rodrigo, Selena Gomez, and Imagine Dragons to the roster. Jimmy Iovine left in 2014 to join Apple after co-founding Beats Electronics, which had been acquired by Apple for $3 billion; John Janick became chairman and CEO of the label group.

Artists

Notable current and former Interscope artists include Dr. Dre, Eminem, Lady Gaga, Billie Eilish, Kendrick Lamar (via affiliation), Lana Del Rey, Maroon 5, OneRepublic, Imagine Dragons, U2 (US), Madonna, Blackpink, Juice Wrld, DaBaby, and Playboi Carti, among many others.
