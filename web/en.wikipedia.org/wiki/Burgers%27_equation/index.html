Burgers' equation

Burgers' equation
Field: Fluid dynamics, partial differential equations
Named after: Johannes Martinus Burgers
Related: Navier–Stokes equations, KdV equation

Burgers' equation is a fundamental partial differential equation named after the Dutch physicist Johannes Martinus Burgers (1895–1981), who introduced it in 1948 as a simplified model of fluid flow and turbulence. In its viscous form it reads ∂u/∂t + u ∂u/∂x = ν ∂²u/∂x², combining nonlinear convection with diffusion.

Behaviour

Without the viscous term, the equation is the inviscid Burgers' equation, whose solutions develop discontinuities — shock waves — in finite time, even from smooth initial data, because faster-moving parts of the profile overtake slower ones. The viscous term smooths these steepening gradients, producing stationary shock profiles of finite width.

Exact solution

Burgers' equation is one of the few nonlinear PDEs solvable exactly: the Cole–Hopf transformation, u = −2ν (∂φ/∂x)/φ, converts it into the linear heat equation for φ, a result published by Eberhard Hopf (1950) and Julian Cole (1951).

Applications

The equation serves as a model problem for turbulence theory, traffic flow, nonlinear acoustics, gas dynamics, and as a testbed for numerical methods for convection-dominated flows. Related forms, including stochastic and multidimensional versions, remain active research topics. See also nonlinear PDEs generally.
