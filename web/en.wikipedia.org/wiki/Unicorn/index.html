Unicorn

The unicorn is a legendary creature that has been described since antiquity as a beast with a single large, pointed, spiraling horn projecting from its forehead. It was depicted in ancient art of the Indus Valley Civilization and was mentioned by ancient Greeks in their accounts of natural history, not as mythology but as accounts of distant India. In European literature and art, the unicorn has for the last thousand years or so been depicted as a white horse-like or goat-like animal with a long straight horn with spiraling grooves, cloven hooves, and sometimes a goat's beard.

History and symbolism

The Greek physician Ctesias (c. 400 BC) gave one of the earliest accounts, describing a fleet wild ass of India with a single horn whose filings were said to be an antidote to poison. In the Middle Ages the unicorn's horn (the "alicorn") was credited with purifying poisoned water and healing sickness, and "unicorn horn" — often narwhal tusks — commanded high prices. Medieval bestiaries described the unicorn as fierce and untameable, capturable only by a virgin, in whose lap it would lay its head; this became an allegory in Christian art for the Incarnation. The famed Unicorn Tapestries (c. 1500) depict its hunt and captivity.

Heraldry and modern culture

The unicorn appears in the royal arms of Scotland, where it is the national animal, and supports the shield in the royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom opposite the English lion. In modern usage, "unicorn" also denotes a privately held startup company valued at over US$1 billion, a term coined in 2013 by venture capitalist Aileen Lee.
