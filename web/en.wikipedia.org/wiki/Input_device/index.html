Input device

In computing, an input device is a piece of equipment used to provide data and control signals to an information processing system, such as a computer. Examples include keyboards, computer mice, scanners, cameras, joysticks, and microphones. Input devices are the means by which users interact with machines; their counterparts, output devices, convey results back to the user.

Categories

Input devices can be classified by modality — text, pointing, audio, video, or motion — and by whether input is discrete (key presses) or continuous (mouse position, sampled many times per second). Pointing devices include the mouse, trackball, touchpad, and touchscreen; imaging devices include digital cameras and scanners; sensors such as accelerometers and gyroscopes provide motion input in mobile devices.

History

Early computers received input through punched cards and paper tape. Teletypes and then video terminals made keyboards the standard text input, while the mouse, developed in the 1960s, enabled direct manipulation in graphical user interfaces. Voice recognition and gesture input have since broadened the range.
