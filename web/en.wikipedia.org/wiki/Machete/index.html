Machete

A machete (Spanish pronunciation: [maˈtʃete]) is a broad blade used either as an agricultural implement similar to an axe, or in combat like a long-bladed knife. The blade is typically 30 to 45 centimetres (12 to 18 in) long and usually under 3 millimetres (1⁄8 in) thick.

Use in agriculture

In tropical and subtropical countries the machete is an everyday tool used to cut through rainforest undergrowth, harvest sugar cane, open coconuts and clear brush. Regional variants include the bolo of the Philippines, the panga of East Africa, the parang of Malaysia and the golok of Indonesia.

Use in conflict

Because of its ubiquity, the machete has frequently been pressed into service as a weapon, from 19th-century Caribbean and Latin American warfare to the Rwandan genocide of 1994, in which machetes were widely used.
