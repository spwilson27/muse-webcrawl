Haematoxylin

Haematoxylin (also spelled hematoxylin) is a naturally occurring chemical compound extracted from the heartwood of the logwood tree (Haematoxylum campechianum). With the formula C16H14O6, it has been used historically as a dye and remains one of the most important stains in histology.

Use in histology

Haematoxylin itself is colourless; its oxidation product, haematein, is the active colorant. In combination with a metallic mordant such as aluminium or iron salts, haematein forms a positively charged complex that binds to basophilic structures, staining cell nuclei blue-purple. It is most famously paired with eosin in the H&E stain, the standard stain of diagnostic pathology. Common formulations include Harris's, Mayer's, and Ehrlich's haematoxylins.

Other uses

Before the development of synthetic dyes, logwood extracts were an important source of black and dark dyes for textiles and leather, and haematoxylin was used in inks. Shortages of logwood during the First World War prompted research into synthetic alternatives and standardisation of supply.
