Edmond Louis Antoine Huot de Goncourt (May 26, 1822 – July 16, 1896) was a French writer, literary and art critic, art historian and collector, who together with his younger brother Jules (1830–1870) formed one of the most famous literary partnerships of the nineteenth century. He is the founder of the Académie Goncourt, whose Prix Goncourt, established under his will, remains France's most prestigious literary prize.

Life and work

Born in Nancy into a family of minor nobility, Edmond and his brother Jules inherited enough wealth to live independently and devoted themselves entirely to literature and art. They began with art history — notably studies of eighteenth-century French art, L'Art du XVIIIe siècle — and collected paintings, drawings and Japanese art, helping to popularise Japonisme in France. Together the brothers wrote historical studies of women of the Revolution and the Directory, plays, and a series of novels in a meticulously observed naturalist style that anticipated Émile Zola: Sœur Philomène (1861), Renée Mauperin (1864), Germinie Lacerteux (1865) — the story of a servant's double life, considered a landmark of realism — Manette Salomon (1867), and Madame Gervaisais (1869).

From 1851 the brothers kept a joint diary, the Journal des Goncourt, a vast, candid record of Parisian literary and artistic life that Edmond continued alone after Jules's death in 1870 and which was published (in expurgated form) from 1887 onward. After his brother's death Edmond wrote further novels alone, including La Fille Élisa (1877) and Les Frères Zemganno (1879), as well as monographs on Hokusai and Utamaro.

The Académie Goncourt

By his will, Edmond left his estate to fund an academy of ten writers that would award an annual prize for "the best work of imagination in prose published during the year." The Académie Goncourt was formally constituted in 1903 after litigation by his heirs, and the first Prix Goncourt was awarded that year. Winners have included Marcel Proust (1919) and Simone de Beauvoir (1954); the prize, though worth only a nominal sum, virtually guarantees bestseller status.
