Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument preserves the site of the Battle of the Little Bighorn, fought on 25–26 June 1876 along the Little Bighorn River in south-central Montana, near the present-day town of Crow Agency. In the battle — often called "Custer's Last Stand" — the U.S. Army's 7th Cavalry under Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer was defeated by combined Lakota Sioux, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho forces; Custer and all 210 men of his immediate command were killed.

Site and memorials

The monument covers 765 acres in two main units: the Custer Battlefield, including Last Stand Hill, and the Reno-Benteen Battlefield about four miles south, where Major Marcus Reno and Captain Frederick Benteen's commands were besieged. White marble markers show where individual cavalry soldiers fell; in 1999 red granite markers began to be placed for Lakota and Cheyenne warriors who died in the battle. The 7th Cavalry Memorial obelisk on Last Stand Hill was erected in 1881, and Custer National Cemetery lies adjacent to the visitor centre.

History of the monument

The battlefield was first protected in 1879 as a national cemetery. For most of its history it was administered as Custer Battlefield National Monument by the War Department and then the National Park Service. In December 1991 Congress renamed it Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument and authorised an Indian Memorial to honour the Native American participants and fallen. The memorial — a circular earthwork and sculpture titled Peace Through Unity — was dedicated on 25 June 2003, the battle's 127th anniversary.

Visiting

The monument includes a visitor centre and museum, a 4.5-mile tour road connecting the two battlefield units, ranger-led talks in summer, and the national cemetery, where veterans from many wars are buried alongside the 7th Cavalry dead reinterred in 1881.
