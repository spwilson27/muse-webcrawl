Walter Bright (born March 10, 1957) is an American computer programmer known for being the designer of the D programming language and for developing the first native compiler for the C++ programming language. His company, Digital Mars, produces compilers for C, C++ and D.

Career

Bright graduated from the California Institute of Technology in 1979 with a degree in mechanical engineering and worked briefly in the aircraft industry before turning to software. As a student he co-developed Empire, a pioneering computer wargame, on the PDP-10; its later versions influenced the strategy game genre.

In the 1980s Bright wrote the Datalight C compiler and then Zortech C++, generally recognised as the first C++ compiler to generate native code rather than translating to C. The compiler line passed through Symantec before Bright resumed independent development under the name Digital Mars. In 1999 he began designing the D language, which combines the power of C++ with productivity features such as garbage collection, design by contract and compile-time function execution. Andrei Alexandrescu joined as co-designer of D2. Bright also wrote the reference D compiler (DMD).
