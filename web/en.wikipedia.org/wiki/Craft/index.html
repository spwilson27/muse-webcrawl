Craft

Image: A craftsperson working at a pottery wheel (placeholder)

A craft or trade is a pastime or profession that requires particular skills and knowledge of skilled work. In a historical sense, particularly the Middle Ages and earlier, the term is usually applied to people occupied in small-scale production of goods, or their maintenance — for example by tinkers. The traditional term craftsman (or craftsperson) describes a person who is skilled in a craft.

Historically, crafts were organised through guilds, which controlled training through apprenticeships and regulated quality and prices. Craftspeople typically worked in workshops, producing goods by hand or with the assistance of hand tools and simple machinery. The Industrial Revolution shifted much production to factories, but craft production survived in areas where individual skill, customisation or small volume mattered most.

Types of craft

Crafts are commonly grouped by the material worked: textiles (weaving, knitting, embroidery), wood (carpentry, joinery, woodturning), metal (blacksmithing, jewellery), clay (pottery, ceramics), glass (glassblowing, stained glass), paper and leather. The Arts and Crafts movement of the late 19th century, associated with William Morris, championed traditional craftsmanship against industrial mass production and influenced design throughout the 20th century.

Studio craft and contemporary craft

In the modern era, "studio craft" refers to craft practised by independent artists in their own workshops, producing functional or decorative objects that are sold through galleries, craft fairs and shops. Craft also exists as a popular hobby and educational activity ("arts and crafts"). Bodies such as the American Craft Council and the Crafts Council in the United Kingdom promote craft practice and exhibiting. Contemporary usage also extends the word to skilled production in new media, as in "craft beer" and craft software.
