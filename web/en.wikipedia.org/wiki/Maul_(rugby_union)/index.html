Maul (rugby union)

In rugby union, a maul is a phase of play that occurs when a ball-carrier is held by one or more opponents, and one or more of the ball-carrier's team-mates bind onto the ball-carrier, all remaining on their feet and moving towards a goal line. The ball is not on the ground. A maul is distinct from a ruck, in which the ball is on the ground, and from a tackle, in which the ball-carrier has been brought down.

Formation and laws

A maul begins when the required players bind together around the ball-carrier. Players joining a maul must do so from behind the offside line — the hindmost foot of their own players in the maul — and must bind onto it rather than charging in from the side. The maul ends successfully when the ball emerges, is grounded over the goal line, or a player carrying the ball breaks free; it ends unsuccessfully if the ball becomes unplayable, the maul collapses illegally, or it remains stationary. If a maul forms directly from a kick (other than a kick-off or restart), the team that did not take possession may be awarded a scrum if the ball becomes unplayable — the so-called "use it or lose it" provision.

The driving maul

From lineouts, attacking teams frequently form a rolling or driving maul: the catcher is protected by forwards binding in front, and the group marches towards the try line, the ball transferred hand to hand to the back. It is one of the most effective weapons in the game for forward packs, used to score "pushover" tries and to draw penalties. Defending teams may legally pull down a maul only in certain age-grade variants; in senior rugby, deliberately collapsing a maul is a penalty offence.
