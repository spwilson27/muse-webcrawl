Ceredigion (Welsh: [kɛrɛˈdɪɡjɔn]) is a county in the west of Wales, corresponding largely to the historic county of Cardiganshire. It borders the sea of Cardigan Bay to the west, Gwynedd to the north, Powys to the east, and Carmarthenshire and Pembrokeshire to the south.

Geography

The Cambrian Mountains dominate the sparsely populated east, including Plynlimon, source of the Severn and Wye. The main towns are Aberystwyth, Cardigan, Lampeter and Aberaeron. Rivers include the Teifi, Aeron, Ystwyth and Rheidol.

History and culture

Ceredigion takes its name from Ceredig, a son of Cunedda, in whose kingdom the territory formed. It is one of the most Welsh-speaking parts of Wales and a heartland of Welsh culture: Aberystwyth hosts the National Library of Wales and one of the oldest university colleges in Wales, while Lampeter's university was founded in 1822. The county returns one Member of Parliament to Westminster and a Member of the Senedd in Cardiff.

Economy

Farming (especially dairy and livestock), tourism and the public sector dominate employment; there is little heavy industry, and the county has among the lowest average wages in the UK.
