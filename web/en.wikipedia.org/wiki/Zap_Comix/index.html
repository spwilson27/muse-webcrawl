Zap Comix

Zap Comix is an American underground comic book created by Robert Crumb, first published in San Francisco in early 1968. Widely regarded as the founding title of the underground comix movement, Zap rejected Comics Code restrictions with anarchic humour, psychedelic imagery and explicit treatments of sex, drugs and politics. From its second issue it became an anthology of the movement's core artists—S. Clay Wilson, Robert Williams, Spain Rodriguez, Gilbert Shelton, Victor Moscoso and Rick Griffin—who styled themselves the "Zap crew". Sixteen issues appeared between 1968 and 2014.

History

Crumb drew the first Zap in 1967; when the original pages were stolen, he redrew the material and published a replacement (later issued as Zap #0), while the first-printed version became issue #1, sold from a baby carriage on Haight Street in February 1968. Issue #4 (1969) provoked obscenity prosecutions of sellers in New York, and the series became a test case for comics' free-speech status. Published irregularly, later issues appeared from Last Gasp; the final issue, #16, was published in 2014 by Fantagraphics, which also issued a complete boxed collection.

Content and style

Zap introduced or popularised characters including Crumb's Mr. Natural, Fritz the Cat (elsewhere), Wilson's the Checkered Demon and Captain Pissgums, and Shelton's Wonder Wart-Hog and Fabulous Furry Freak Brothers (in related titles). Its jam sessions, in which the seven artists drew over each other's pages, epitomised the movement's collective, counter-cultural spirit.

Legacy

Zap is credited with launching the underground comix boom, demonstrating that comics could be an adult, auteur-driven art form, and paving the way for alternative comics. The complete Zap has been exhibited in museums and is preserved in library special collections.
