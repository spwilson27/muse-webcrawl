Aventura (band)

Aventura is an American bachata group formed in the Bronx, New York City, in 1993 by Anthony "Romeo" Santos, Lenny Santos, Max Santos and Henry Santos, cousins of Dominican descent. They are credited with modernising bachata — a guitar-driven music of the Dominican Republic long marginalised as rural and low-class — by fusing it with R&B, hip hop and pop, and are widely known as the "Kings of Bachata Moderna".

History

The group began as Los Tinellers, performing for schoolmates in the Bronx, and released a first album in 1995 with little success. Renamed Aventura, they broke through with We Broke the Rules (2002), whose single "Obsesión" — featuring Judy Santos — topped charts across Europe and Latin America and held number one in Italy for sixteen weeks. Subsequent albums Love & Hate (2003), God's Project (2005), the record-breaking live set K.O.B. Live (2006) and The Last (2009) made Aventura the best-selling bachata act in history; among their signatures are "Un Beso", "Los Infieles", "El Perdedor", "Por un Segundo" and "Dile al Amor". In 2009 they performed at the White House for President Barack Obama.

Hiatus and reunions

In 2011 the group went on hiatus as Romeo Santos launched an enormously successful solo career and Henry Santos also recorded solo. Aventura reunited for a series of sold-out shows at Yankee Stadium's neighbouring venues in 2016, released the single "Inmortal" on Romeo's album Utopía in 2019, and undertook the Cerrando Ciclos reunion tour in 2024.

Style and influence

Aventura retained bachata's lead requinto guitar and rhythm guitar interplay while adding electric-guitar effects, R&B vocal runs and Spanglish lyrics about romance and urban life. Their success opened mainstream US and European markets to bachata and influenced a generation of urban-Latin artists.
