Wakame

Scientific nameUndaria pinnatifida
FamilyAlariaceae
Native rangeCold temperate coasts of NW Pacific (Japan, Korea, China)

Wakame (Undaria pinnatifida) is a species of kelp native to cold, temperate coasts of the northwest Pacific Ocean. As an edible seaweed, it has a subtly sweet, yet distinctive and strong flavour and texture. It is most often served in soups and salads. Wakame has long been farmed in Japan and Korea, where it is an ingredient of miso soup and seaweed salads.

Dried wakame expands greatly when rehydrated in water.

Nutrition

Wakame is rich in iodine, calcium, iron and B vitamins, and is a source of fucoxanthin and omega-3 fatty acids in the form of EPA. It is low in calories and has been studied for potential effects on blood pressure and metabolism.

As an invasive species

Outside its native range, wakame is a significant invasive species, listed among the world's 100 worst invasive alien species. It has spread via ship ballast and aquaculture equipment to New Zealand, Australia, Europe and the Americas, where it colonises harbours and can displace native kelps.
