Cranial cavity

The cranial cavity, also known as the intracranial space, is the space within the skull that accommodates the brain. It is formed by eight cranial bones: the frontal, occipital, sphenoid and ethmoid bones and the paired parietal and temporal bones.

Contents and structure

Besides the brain, the cavity contains the meninges, the proximal parts of the cranial nerves, blood vessels and cerebrospinal fluid. In adults its capacity is roughly 1,300–1,500 ml. The floor of the cavity is divided into the anterior, middle and posterior cranial fossae.

Clinical significance

Because the skull is rigid, increases in intracranial volume from bleeding, tumour or oedema raise intracranial pressure, which can compress the brain and cause herniation — a life-threatening condition treated by measures ranging from head elevation to decompressive craniectomy.
