Tilt–shift photography

Tilt–shift photography is the use of camera movements that change the orientation or position of the lens with respect to the image plane. Tilting the lens plane relative to the sensor applies the Scheimpflug principle, placing the plane of focus at an angle; shifting the lens parallel to the sensor corrects converging vertical lines, especially in architectural photography.

Technique

True tilt–shift requires a special lens that can tilt and shift, or a view camera with flexible bellows. Tilting controls which parts of the scene are sharp: a small tilt can bring an entire landscape into focus at a wide aperture, while tilting the opposite way produces a very shallow band of focus.

Miniature faking

The selective-focus effect of tilt lenses has popularized "miniature faking", in which real scenes shot from above with a narrow strip of focus appear to be photographs of miniature scale models. The effect can also be simulated in software and became a popular filter in early smartphone photo applications.
