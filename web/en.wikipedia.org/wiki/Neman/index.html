The Neman, also called the Nemunas, Nyoman, and (in its lower course) the Memel, is a European river rising in central Belarus and flowing through Lithuania before draining into the Curonian Lagoon of the Baltic Sea. At 937 kilometres (582 mi) in length and with a drainage basin of about 98,000 km², it is the longest river in Lithuania and one of the major rivers of the Baltic catchment.

Course

The Neman rises in the Minsk Upland south of Minsk in Belarus, where two small headwater streams join. It flows westward past Stolbtsy and then north and west through Grodno, crossing into Lithuania, where it passes Druskininkai, Alytus, Prienai, and Kaunas — at Kaunas it is joined by its largest tributary, the Neris. From Kaunas it flows westward to Jurbarkas and then forms the border between Lithuania and Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast, passing the town of Neman (Ragnit), before dividing into a delta of distributaries, principally the Rusnė and Gilija, that enter the Curonian Lagoon near Rusnė island.

History and culture

The river has long been a trade route and a political frontier. In 1807 Napoleon and Alexander I of Russia met on a raft in the middle of the river near Tilsit to sign the Treaties of Tilsit. In German literature the river (as the Memel) is celebrated in the song "Lied der Deutschen" ("From the Meuse to the Memel"), and the region north of the river was historically known as Lithuania Minor. Adam Mickiewicz invoked the Niemen (Polish name) in his epic Pan Tadeusz.

Use and environment

The Kaunas Hydroelectric Power Plant, completed in 1960, is the largest source of renewable electricity in Lithuania. The river is used for navigation, fishing and tourism, and the Neman Delta Regional Park protects wetlands of international importance for migratory birds.
