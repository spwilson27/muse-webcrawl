Sikhote-Alin

The Sikhote-Alin (Russian: Сихотэ-Алинь) is a mountain range in the Russian Far East, extending about 900 kilometres (560 mi) to the northeast of the Russian Pacific seaport of Vladivostok. It spans Primorsky Krai and Khabarovsk Krai. The highest summits are Tordoki Yani (2,090 m), Ko Mountain (2,003 m), and Anik Mountain (1,933 m).

Geography and nature

Although Sikhote-Alin is a temperate zone, species typical of the northern taiga coexist there with tropical species, including the Amur leopard, Siberian tiger, Asiatic black bear, and Amur goshawk. The region avoided large glaciation during the last ice age, which allowed a unique ecosystem to form, mixing taiga and subtropical flora and fauna. Major rivers draining the range include the Amur, Ussuri, and Tumen.

The range is home to the Sikhote-Alin Nature Reserve, founded in 1935, and in 2001 UNESCO listed the Central Sikhote-Alin as a World Heritage Site, citing the importance of its temperate forests and endangered species such as the Siberian tiger and Blakiston's fish owl. In 2018 the listing was extended to include the Bikin River valley.

History

The range was first explored by Russian expeditions in the early 20th century, including those led by Vladimir Arsenyev, whose books (notably Dersu Uzala) described the region and its indigenous Nanai guide. In the 1910s–1930s the area was mapped in detail by Arsenyev and other explorers.

Meteorite

On 12 February 1947, one of the largest meteorite falls in recorded history occurred in the Sikhote-Alin mountains. The Sikhote-Alin meteorite exploded in the atmosphere during daylight, showering iron fragments over an area of about 1.3 square kilometres and creating numerous craters. It is one of the very few iron meteorite falls observed with a large strewn field.
