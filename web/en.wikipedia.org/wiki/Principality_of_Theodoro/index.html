Principality of Theodoro

Existed: early 14th century – 1475
Capital: Theodoro (Mangup)
Region: Southwestern Crimea
Ruling family: Gabras (Alexios dynasty)

The Principality of Theodoro, also known as Gothia, was a small Greek principality in the southwestern Crimea that existed from the early 14th century until its conquest by the Ottoman Empire in 1475. It was one of the last remnants of the Byzantine world, surviving Constantinople's fall by more than two decades.

History

The principality emerged as the Byzantine themes in Crimea fragmented after 1204. Its capital was the fortress-city of Theodoro, identified with Mangup, dramatically sited on a flat-topped mountain. Its population was a mixture of Greeks, Crimean Goths, Alans, Armenians, Jews and Tatars, and its rulers, of the Gabras family, styled themselves "Lord of the city of Theodoro and the Maritime Region". The state maintained close ties with the Empire of Trebizond and, later, with Moscow.

Fall

Theodoro survived under pressure from the Genoese colonies and the Crimean Khanate, but in 1475 an Ottoman expedition under Gedik Ahmed Pasha besieged Mangup for several months and stormed it, ending the principality. The last prince was executed in Constantinople. Gothic language and identity in the region nevertheless lingered into the 18th century.
