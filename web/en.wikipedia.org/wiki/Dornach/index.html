Dornach is a municipality in the district of Dorneck in the canton of Solothurn in Switzerland, situated about 10 kilometres southeast of Basel on the river Birs. It is best known for the Battle of Dornach (1499) and as the home of the Goetheanum, the world centre of the anthroposophical movement.


The second Goetheanum in Dornach, designed by Rudolf Steiner.

History

On 22 July 1499, Dornach was the site of the final battle of the Swabian War, in which Swiss Confederate forces defeated the army of Maximilian I, effectively securing Swiss independence from the Holy Roman Empire. A battle monument and ossuary chapel commemorate the event.

Goetheanum

In 1913 Rudolf Steiner chose Dornach as the seat of the Anthroposophical Society. The first Goetheanum, an elaborate wooden building, was destroyed by fire on New Year's Eve 1922/23; its replacement, one of the earliest large buildings in sculptural cast concrete, was completed in 1928 and remains a major centre for anthroposophy, eurythmy, and Waldorf education, surrounded by Steiner-designed houses.
