Milcom

Milcom is a common abbreviation for military communications, the field encompassing all aspects of the transmission of information by armed forces — voice, data, imagery and signalling — across radio, satellite, wired and networked systems. The term is also used in hobbyist and professional contexts for the monitoring and study of military radio traffic.

Overview

Military communications provide command and control: the ability of commanders to direct forces, coordinate fires and logistics, and receive intelligence. Modern milcom systems include high-frequency (HF) skywave networks, VHF/UHF line-of-sight tactical radios, satellite communications (SATCOM) such as the US MUOS and Wideband Global SATCOM constellations, and IP-based battlefield networks. Encrypted waveforms, frequency hopping and low-probability-of-intercept techniques protect traffic from interception and jamming.

History

From semaphore and heliograph through field telegraph in the American Civil War, wireless in the First World War, and the integrated systems of the Second World War (such as the SCR radio series), communications have repeatedly transformed warfare. The Cold War added airborne command posts and nuclear command-and-control networks.

Related uses

"MILCOM" is also the name of the IEEE Military Communications Conference, held annually since 1982, and of a long-running column on military monitoring in radio hobbyist publications. Radio enthusiasts use the term for monitoring military aviation and HF utility stations, a legal pastime in many jurisdictions for unencrypted transmissions.
