Mikoyan MiG-29

The Mikoyan MiG-29 (NATO reporting name: Fulcrum) is a twin-engine air-superiority fighter aircraft designed in the Soviet Union by the Mikoyan design bureau. Developed in the 1970s as a counterpart to American fighters such as the F-15 and F-16, it entered service with the Soviet Air Forces in 1983.

Design

The MiG-29 combines a wide lifting-body fuselage with two Klimov RD-33 engines, giving excellent manoeuvrability and short-field performance. Early versions carried a Phazotron radar, an infrared search-and-track sensor, and a helmet-mounted sight paired with the high off-boresight R-73 missile — a combination that impressed Western pilots after the Cold War.

Service

More than 1,600 MiG-29s were built and exported widely. Upgraded variants include the MiG-29M, the carrier-based MiG-29K, and the substantially redesigned MiG-35. The type has seen combat in conflicts from the Gulf War to the Russo-Ukrainian war.
