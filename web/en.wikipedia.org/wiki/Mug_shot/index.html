Mug shot

A mug shot or mugshot is a photographic portrait of a person from the waist up, taken after their arrest during the police booking process. The subject typically poses against a height chart, sometimes holding a placard bearing their name, date and case number. The standard format comprises two views — one front-facing and one in profile — a convention established in the 19th century so that offenders could be identified from either angle.

History

Police forces began photographing criminals within years of photography's invention in 1839. In the 1870s and 1880s the Paris police official Alphonse Bertillon standardised the practice as part of his system of criminal identification, and rogues' galleries of booking photographs became a fixture of detective work in Europe and the United States. The word "mug" is 18th-century slang for the face.

Modern practice and controversies

In the United States, mug shots are generally public records and are widely published by news media and commercial websites; this has drawn criticism, since a mug shot is taken at arrest — before any conviction — and its publication can stigmatise people who are later acquitted or never charged. Some jurisdictions and agencies have restricted the release of booking photographs, and several states have passed laws requiring commercial sites to remove them. Mug shots of public figures and celebrities, from Al Capone to modern pop stars, have become iconic images in their own right.
