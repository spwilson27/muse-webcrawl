F. O. Matthiessen

Born19 February 1902, Pasadena, California
Died1 April 1950, Boston, Massachusetts
OccupationLiterary critic, historian

Francis Otto Matthiessen (19 February 1902 – 1 April 1950) was an American educator, scholar and literary critic at Harvard University, best known for his book American Renaissance: Art and Expression in the Age of Emerson and Whitman (1941), which shaped the study of 19th-century American literature for generations.

Life

Educated at Yale and Harvard, and a Rhodes Scholar at Oxford, Matthiessen joined the Harvard faculty in 1929. He was a committed Christian socialist, active in teachers' union politics and in campaigns for social justice, positions that brought him under suspicion during the early Cold War.

Work and legacy

American Renaissance focused on Emerson, Thoreau, Hawthorne, Melville and Whitman, arguing that their writing formed a coherent national literature. Matthiessen's criticism fused close reading with social and historical analysis. Suffering from depression, he died by suicide in 1950. A visiting professorship at Harvard bears his name, and an LGBTQ literary award, the F. O. Matthiessen Award, was later established in his honour.
