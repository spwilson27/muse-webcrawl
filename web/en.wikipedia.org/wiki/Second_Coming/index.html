Second Coming
Also calledSecond Advent, Parousia
TypeChristian eschatological belief
Key textsMatthew 24; 1 Thessalonians 4; Revelation 19
RelatedLast Judgment, Resurrection, Millennialism

The Second Coming (sometimes called the Second Advent or Parousia) is the Christian belief that Jesus Christ will return to Earth after his ascension to heaven, to fulfil the remaining prophecies of his Messianic office, judge the living and the dead, and establish the Kingdom of God in its fullness. The doctrine is affirmed in the historic creeds: the Nicene Creed states that Christ "will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead, and his kingdom will have no end."

Biblical basis

The New Testament repeatedly speaks of Christ's return. In the Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24–25, Mark 13, Luke 21), Jesus describes signs preceding his coming, emphasizing that the exact day and hour are unknown. The apostle Paul wrote of the "day of the Lord" and of believers being caught up to meet Christ at his coming (1 Thessalonians 4), while the Book of Revelation depicts Christ returning as a conquering king (Revelation 19). The term parousia, Greek for "presence" or "arrival," is used in the New Testament for this event and also carried connotations of a royal visit in the Hellenistic world.

Interpretations

Christians differ over the timing and nature of the Second Coming in relation to other end-time events. Premillennialism holds that Christ will return before a literal thousand-year reign (the Millennium); within it, dispensationalists commonly expect a rapture of the church before a period of tribulation. Postmillennialism expects Christ to return after a golden age of Christian influence. Amillennialism interprets the Millennium symbolically as the present church age, with Christ returning at its end. Preterism holds that many prophecies were fulfilled in the first century, particularly in the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70; partial preterists still expect a future visible return.

Other traditions

Most Christian denominations — Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant — affirm a future Second Coming, though they differ in detail. Jehovah's Witnesses teach that Christ's invisible presence (parousia) began in 1914. In Islam, Jesus (Isa) is also expected to return before the Day of Judgment, in conjunction with the Mahdi. The Baháʼí Faith interprets the expected return as fulfilled in its founders. Because the date is declared unknown, attempts to predict it — such as the Millerite "Great Disappointment" of 1844 — have repeatedly failed, and mainstream churches discourage date-setting.

In culture

The phrase "the Second Coming" also titles W. B. Yeats's famous 1919 poem, which begins "Turning and turning in the widening gyre" and introduced phrases such as "things fall apart; the centre cannot hold" into common usage.
