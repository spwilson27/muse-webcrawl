Voyage of the James Caird

The voyage of the James Caird was a journey of 1,300 kilometres (800 mi) from Elephant Island in the South Shetland Islands through the Southern Ocean to South Georgia, undertaken by Sir Ernest Shackleton and five companions in a 6.9-metre ship's boat in April–May 1916. It is considered one of the greatest feats of small-boat navigation in history.

Background

After their ship Endurance was crushed by Antarctic ice, Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition reached Elephant Island. With no prospect of rescue there, Shackleton chose to sail to the whaling stations of South Georgia, strengthening the whaler James Caird with a deck of timber and canvas.

The voyage

Shackleton, Frank Worsley (navigator), Tom Crean, Timothy McCarthy, John Vincent, and Harry McNish set out on 24 April 1916. Worsley navigated by rare glimpses of the sun through storms, and after 16 days they landed at King Haakon Bay on South Georgia's uninhabited south coast on 10 May. Shackleton, Worsley, and Crean then crossed the island's unmapped glaciers on foot to reach the whaling station at Stromness, enabling the rescue of the men on Elephant Island, all of whom survived.
