Bashert

Bashert (Yiddish: באַשערט) is a Yiddish word meaning "destined" or "fated". In Jewish tradition it is most commonly used for a divinely ordained spouse or soulmate — one's bashert is the person one is destined to marry. By extension, the word describes any event that seems meant to be, often rendered in English as "it was bashert" ("it was meant to be").

Origins and belief

The concept draws on rabbinic teachings that marriages are arranged in heaven. The Talmud (Sotah 2a) relates that forty days before a child is formed, a heavenly voice proclaims the match: "The daughter of so-and-so [is destined] for so-and-so." The Midrash similarly teaches that God occupies Himself with matchmaking. The destined partner is called one's zivug.

Usage

In contemporary usage, especially among Ashkenazi Jews and in the Orthodox dating world (shidduchim), finding one's bashert is the goal of courtship. The term also appears in popular culture, including the musical Fiddler on the Roof and the song "Bashana Haba'ah" is sometimes confused with it. More broadly, Yiddish speakers apply bashert to coincidences and fortunate turns of fate.
