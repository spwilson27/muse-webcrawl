The April Fair (Spanish: Feria de Abril) is a week-long festival held each spring in Seville, Andalusia, Spain. Beginning on a Saturday night about two weeks after Holy Week, the fair transforms the Los Remedios district into an ephemeral city of canvas marquees (casetas), fairground rides and lantern-lit streets, attracting well over a million visitors.

History

The fair began in 1847 as a livestock market proposed by two city councillors, the Basque José María de Ybarra and the Catalan Narciso Bonaplata. Queen Isabella II sanctioned its creation, and the first fair was held on 18 April 1847 at the Prado de San Sebastián. Over time the commercial livestock function faded and the event became principally a social festival of dancing, music and food.

The fairgrounds

The Real de la Feria covers a large site beside the Guadalquivir river, organised into streets named after famous bullfighters. Its ornate entrance gate (the portada) is newly designed each year and lit on the opening night. Adjacent lies the Calle del Infierno ("Hell Street"), a large funfair.

Casetas

More than a thousand casetas — striped canvas pavilions — line the streets. Most are private, run by families, groups of friends, companies or clubs, and entry is by invitation; a handful of municipal and district casetas are open to the public. Inside, visitors drink fino sherry and rebujito (sherry with lemonade), eat tapas, and dance sevillanas, the folk dances closely associated with the fair.

Customs

Women traditionally wear colourful flamenco dresses (trajes de flamenca), and horsemen and horse-drawn carriages parade through the grounds in the early afternoon. The fair week also features a major season of bullfighting at the Maestranza bullring. The fair ends with a fireworks display at midnight on the closing Sunday.
