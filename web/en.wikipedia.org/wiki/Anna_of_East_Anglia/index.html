Anna (died 654) was King of East Anglia from the early 640s until his death. A member of the Wuffingas dynasty and son of Eni, he was praised by Bede as a devout Christian ruler who gave sanctuary to the exiled Cenwalh of Wessex, converting him to Christianity.

Reign

Anna's daughters became notable saints and abbesses: Seaxburh of Ely, Æthelthryth (founder of Ely Abbey), Æthelburh and Wihtburh. Anna fought the expanding power of Penda of Mercia; he was defeated and exiled in 651, and on his return was killed with his son Jurmin at the Battle of Bulcamp in 654, fighting Penda. He was succeeded by his brother Æthelhere.

Legacy

Anna was buried at Blythburgh in Suffolk and was later regarded locally as a saint and martyr, though never formally canonised.
