Las Posadas

Las Posadas (Spanish for "The Inns") is a religious festival celebrated in Mexico and parts of Central America and the United States between December 16 and December 24. The nine nights of celebration, a novena, commemorate the journey of Mary and Joseph from Nazareth to Bethlehem and their search for shelter (posada) before the birth of Jesus.

Celebration

Each evening, a procession — often led by children carrying statues or images of Mary and Joseph, sometimes with a child dressed as an angel — makes its way through the neighbourhood carrying candles and singing. At a designated house the group divides: those outside, playing the pilgrims, sing a traditional song asking for lodging, while those inside respond as the innkeepers, at first refusing, until finally the doors are opened and the pilgrims are welcomed in. The gathering then celebrates with prayer, food, and drink such as ponche (hot fruit punch) and tamales, and frequently with the breaking of a piñata — traditionally a seven-pointed star representing the seven deadly sins. On the final night, Christmas Eve, the celebration culminates with the laying of the baby Jesus figure in the Nativity scene and Midnight Mass (Misa de Gallo).

History

The tradition was introduced in the 16th century by Spanish missionaries in New Spain; Augustinian friars are generally credited with organising the nine nights of festivities in 1586 at Acolman, near Mexico City, in part to supplant indigenous Aztec winter celebrations. From Mexico the custom spread through Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Colombia, and other parts of Latin America, and is also observed by Hispanic communities in the United States.
