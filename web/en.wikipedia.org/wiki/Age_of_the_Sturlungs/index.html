Age of the Sturlungs

The Age of the Sturlungs (Icelandic: Sturlungaöld) was a period of Icelandic history in the mid-thirteenth century (roughly 1220–1262) marked by violent feuding among the island's great chieftain families, named after the Sturlungar, the most powerful of them.

Background and conflicts

By the 1200s power in the Icelandic Commonwealth had concentrated in a handful of families — the Sturlungs, Ásbirningar, Haukdælir, Oddaverjar, Vatnsfirðingar, and Svínfellingar — whose rivalry, encouraged by the King of Norway's ambitions, erupted into open warfare. Major episodes include the battle of Örlygsstaðir (1238), the burning at Flugumýri (1253), and the battle of Haugsnes (1246), the bloodiest in Icelandic history.

Outcome

The period is chronicled in the Sturlunga saga compilation, much of it written by Sturla Þórðarson, nephew of the chieftain and author Snorri Sturluson, who was murdered in 1241. Exhausted by civil war, Icelandic chieftains swore allegiance to King Haakon IV of Norway in 1262–1264, ending the Commonwealth.
