Billie Jean King (née Moffitt, born 1943) is an American former world No. 1 tennis player and one of the most influential figures in the history of sport. She won 39 Grand Slam titles across singles, doubles and mixed doubles, including a record 20 titles at Wimbledon in all events — a mark she shares in Wimbledon history — and 12 singles majors. Off the court she led the campaign that made the US Open the first major to offer equal prize money in 1973, founded the Women's Tennis Association the same year, and became the public face of women's professional sport.

Battle of the Sexes and legacy

In September 1973, before a huge television audience at the Houston Astrodome, the 29-year-old King defeated 55-year-old former men's champion Bobby Riggs in the "Battle of the Sexes", a straight-sets victory that became a cultural landmark for women's sport and the women's movement. She later founded World TeamTennis and the Women's Sports Foundation, and in 2006 the US Open's home — the USTA National Tennis Center, whose showpiece is Arthur Ashe Stadium — was renamed the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in her honour. In 2020 the Fed Cup, the women's world team championship, was renamed the Billie Jean King Cup.
