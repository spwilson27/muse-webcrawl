Printer Command Language, more commonly referred to as PCL, is a page description language (PDL) developed by Hewlett-Packard as a printer protocol, and a de facto standard of the industry. PCL was first introduced in 1984 for the early inkjet printer, has since been released in six major versions, and has become widely used on HP LaserJet and other printers.

Overview

PCL commands are embedded escape sequences inserted into the print job at the system level before printing. A PCL driver on the host computer converts document data into PCL instructions, which the printer's formatter interprets to render text and raster/vector graphics. Compared with PostScript, PCL relies more on the printer's built-in fonts and processing, historically making it faster on lower-cost hardware but less portable for complex graphics.

Versions

  • PCL 1 and 2 (1984): basic printing and terminal emulation.
  • PCL 3 (1984): introduced with the first inkjet printers; added bitmapped fonts and improved quality.
  • PCL 4 (1985): added macros, composite fonts, and portrait/landscape control for the LaserJet II.
  • PCL 5 (1990): added scalable Intellifont fonts and HP-GL/2 vector graphics.
  • PCL 5e / 5c: enhanced resolution and color support.
  • PCL 6 (1995, "PCL XL"): a ground-up redesign with an object-oriented, binary-encoded protocol ("PCL 6 Enhanced") alongside the text-based "PCL 6 Standard".
