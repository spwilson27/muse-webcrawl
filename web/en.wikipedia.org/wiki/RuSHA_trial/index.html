RuSHA trial

Defendants in the dock at Nuremberg

The RuSHA trial (officially The United States of America vs. Ulrich Greifelt, et al.) was the eighth of the twelve "subsequent Nuremberg trials" held by United States authorities after the Second World War. It was held from 20 October 1947 to 10 March 1948.

Background

The fourteen defendants were officials of three Nazi organisations concerned with racial policy: the SS Race and Settlement Main Office (RuSHA), which screened racial suitability and supervised Germanization; the office of the Reich Commissioner for the Consolidation of German Nationhood, headed by Heinrich Himmler; and the Lebensborn programme. They were charged with crimes against humanity and war crimes including the kidnapping of "racially valuable" foreign children, forced abortions and deportations, the expulsion of populations from occupied territories, and the persecution and murder of Jews and others.

Verdict

Thirteen defendants were convicted; one was acquitted. Ulrich Greifelt received life imprisonment, several others sentences of 10 to 25 years, and the Lebensborn officials were convicted only of membership in a criminal organisation, receiving short sentences. The tribunal ruled that Lebensborn, as such, was principally a welfare institution despite its role in the transfer of kidnapped children.
