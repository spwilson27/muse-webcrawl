Sattva

The three guṇas: sattva, rajas and tamas.

Sattva (Sanskrit: सत्त्व) is one of the three guṇas, or "qualities", in Samkhya philosophy in Indian philosophy. The other two are rajas (passion and activity) and tamas (darkness and inertia). Sattva is the quality of balance, harmony, goodness, purity, light, serenity and constructive calm, and is associated with clarity of knowledge and freedom from agitation.

Meaning

The word derives from the Sanskrit sat, meaning "being", "reality" or "truth". In Samkhya, all of material nature (prakṛti) is composed of the three guṇas in varying proportions, and the character of any person or thing reflects the predominance of one over the others.

In the Bhagavad Gita

The fourteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita describes sattva as binding the soul through attachment to knowledge and happiness, even as it is the purest of the three qualities. Foods, actions, knowledge and persons are classified in the Gita's later chapters as sattvic, rajasic or tamasic according to these qualities; sattvic food, for example, is said to be fresh, wholesome and pleasing, promoting life, strength and joy.

In Ayurveda and yoga

In Ayurvedic and yogic traditions, diets and lifestyles that cultivate sattva — fresh vegetarian food, meditation, cleanliness and calm routine — are recommended for spiritual practice and mental clarity.
