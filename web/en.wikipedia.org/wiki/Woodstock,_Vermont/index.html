Woodstock is a town in and the county seat (shire town) of Windsor County, Vermont, United States. Situated on the Ottauquechee River in east-central Vermont, it is celebrated for its well-preserved Federal and Greek Revival architecture, covered bridges and surrounding pastoral hills, and is often described as one of the prettiest small towns in America.

History

Chartered in 1761 by New Hampshire governor Benning Wentworth, Woodstock grew prosperous in the early nineteenth century as a centre of law, publishing and manufacturing. Wealth from local industry built the elegant houses around the village green. In the twentieth century the Rockefeller family connection—Laurance Rockefeller married Mary French, whose family had built the Woodstock Inn—helped shape the town's conservation ethos; the Marsh-Billings-Rockefeller National Historical Park, Vermont's only national park unit, preserves a model nineteenth-century managed forest and farm.

Attractions

Points of interest include the Billings Farm & Museum, the Middle Covered Bridge (1869), the Woodstock Inn & Resort, and nearby Mount Tom trails. The town hosts the annual Wassail Weekend and book and film festivals.
