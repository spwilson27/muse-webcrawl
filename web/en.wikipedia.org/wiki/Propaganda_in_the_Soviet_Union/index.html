Propaganda in the Soviet Union

Propaganda in the Soviet Union was the practice of the Communist Party and state to promote the official ideology of Marxism–Leninism and to mobilize the population for state goals. It was pervasive, extending through newspapers, radio, film, posters, education, and mass organizations, and was coordinated by the Party's Department for Agitation and Propaganda (Agitprop).

Methods and themes

Soviet propaganda celebrated industrialization and collectivization, the achievements of workers and collective farmers, the Red Army, and the leadership of the Party and, under Stalin, a cult of personality around the leader. Artistic production was channelled through socialist realism. Propaganda also targeted foreign audiences through the Comintern, friendship societies, and publications, while domestic media were subject to strict censorship by Glavlit.

Institutions

Newspapers such as Pravda and Izvestia, the TASS news agency, and state film studios were the main channels. Agitprop trains and ships in the revolutionary period carried print, theatre, and film to remote areas.
