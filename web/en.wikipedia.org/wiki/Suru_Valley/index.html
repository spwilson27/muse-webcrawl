Suru Valley

The Suru Valley beneath the Nun Kun massif

The Suru Valley is a valley in the Kargil district of Ladakh, in northern India. It is drained by the Suru River, a tributary of the Indus, which rises at the Pensila pass near the Zanskar range and flows north-west past Kargil to join the Indus near Marol.

Geography

The valley stretches for roughly 140 kilometres from the Pensila to Kargil. Its lower reaches are among the most fertile in Ladakh, supporting barley, wheat, and apricot orchards despite the high altitude and short growing season. The valley is dominated by the twin peaks of Nun (7,135 m) and Kun (7,077 m), the highest mountains in Ladakh accessible from the Srinagar–Leh highway corridor.

People and culture

The principal town is Sankoo, a market centre often described as the valley's bowl of flowers because of its relatively lush meadows. The population is predominantly Balti and Purki, mostly Shia Muslim, in contrast to the Buddhist majority of the neighbouring Zanskar valley. Villages such as Panikhar, Tangole, and Parkachik serve as bases for trekking and mountaineering expeditions to Nun Kun and the Pensi La.
