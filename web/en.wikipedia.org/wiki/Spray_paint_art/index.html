Spray paint art

Spray paint art is an art form using spray paint on non-traditional surfaces such as glossy poster board or cardboard, rather than canvas. Distinct from both graffiti and spray painting as a coating technique, it is typically performed as street performance: the artist creates a complete work, usually a landscape, cityscape or cosmic scene, in a matter of minutes before an audience, using stencils, found objects as masks, sponges, newspaper and palette knives to texture and scrape the paint.

History

The form is credited to Ruben Sadot Fernandez, known as Sodot, who began developing the technique in Mexico City in the early 1980s and taught it to other street artists. From Mexico it spread through tourist districts and boardwalks worldwide, notably in the United States, Europe and Australia, where performers attract crowds and sell finished pieces on the spot. The advent of online video platforms in the 2000s and 2010s vastly expanded its audience, with time-compressed process videos becoming a popular genre.

Technique

Because enamel or lacquer spray paints dry within seconds, artists work in reverse layering — dark backgrounds and cosmic mists first, then planets formed by spraying over circular lids or plates used as masks, with details added by crumpled newspaper for texture, torn sponge for foliage, and a palette knife scraped across wet paint for mountains, waves and reflections. A final glossy clear coat gives the signature luminous finish. The speed and apparent improvisation are central to the performance's appeal.
