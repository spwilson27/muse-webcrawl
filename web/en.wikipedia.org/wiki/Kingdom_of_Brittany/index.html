Kingdom of Brittany

The Kingdom of Brittany was a short-lived vassal state of the Frankish Empire, and for a time broadly independent, in the early Middle Ages. It emerged in the mid-9th century when the Breton leader Erispoe defeated the West Frankish king Charles the Bald at the Battle of Jengland in 851 and was recognised with a royal title.

History

Brittany had been settled by Britons migrating from Britain in the early medieval period and was organised into small kingdoms such as Domnonée, Cornouaille, and Broërec. Under Nominoe (died 851), the Bretons revolted against Frankish overlordship. His son Erispoe secured recognition after Jengland. The kingdom reached its greatest extent under Salomon (857–874), who brought much of western Brittany, and at times the Cotentin and Avranchin, under Breton rule and was recognised as king by Charles the Bald.

Decline

After Salomon's murder in 874, civil war and devastating Viking invasions weakened the kingdom. Alain Barbetorte restored Breton rule in the 10th century but took the lesser title of duke, and Brittany thereafter developed as the Duchy of Brittany within the orbit of the French crown.
