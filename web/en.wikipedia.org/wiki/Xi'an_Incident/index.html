The Xi'an Incident (Chinese: 西安事變) was a political crisis that took place in Xi'an, China, in December 1936. Chiang Kai-shek, the leader of the Republic of China, was detained by his subordinate generals Zhang Xueliang and Yang Hucheng, who demanded an end to the civil war against the Chinese Communist Party and active resistance against Imperial Japan instead.

Background

Following the Japanese invasion of Manchuria in 1931, Chiang's Nationalist government pursued a policy of first suppressing the Communists before confronting Japan. Zhang Xueliang, the "Young Marshal" whose Northeastern Army had been driven from Manchuria, was ordered to attack Communist forces in Shaanxi but grew increasingly sympathetic to a united front against Japan.

The incident

Chiang flew to Xi'an on 4 December 1936 to pressure Zhang and Yang into renewed offensives against the Communists. In the early hours of 12 December, Zhang's bodyguards stormed Chiang's quarters at the Huaqing Pool resort; Chiang fled in his nightclothes and was captured on a mountainside. He was held for two weeks while negotiations, involving Communist representative Zhou Enlai, took place.

Outcome

Chiang was released on 25 December after verbally agreeing to halt attacks on the Communists and to lead national resistance against Japan. The incident led to the Second United Front between the Kuomintang and the Communists during the Second Sino-Japanese War. Zhang Xueliang accompanied Chiang back to Nanjing, where he was court-martialled and placed under house arrest for more than fifty years.
