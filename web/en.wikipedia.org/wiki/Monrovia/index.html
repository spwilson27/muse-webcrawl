Monrovia is the capital and largest city of Liberia, a West African coastal city situated on the Cape Mesurado peninsula at the mouth of the Mesurado River on the Atlantic Ocean. Founded in 1822 by the American Colonization Society as a settlement for freed African-American slaves, it was named in honour of U.S. President James Monroe, a prominent supporter of the colonisation project — making it one of the few world capitals named after a foreign leader.

History

The first settlers, arriving aboard the ship Elizabeth via Sherbro Island, established Christopolis on Cape Mesurado in 1822; the settlement was renamed Monrovia two years later and became the nucleus of the independent Republic of Liberia in 1847. The city grew slowly as an Americo-Liberian administrative and commercial centre, with its Freeport later developed by American forces during the Second World War.

Monrovia suffered catastrophic damage during Liberia's civil wars (1989–2003), when sieges and fighting destroyed infrastructure and displaced much of the population. In 2014–2016 the city was a centre of the West African Ebola epidemic, with the West Point township a focus of quarantine measures. Post-war reconstruction has been gradual, with chronic shortages of electricity and piped water.

Geography and economy

The city spreads over the Mesurado peninsula and along the Saint Paul River. The Freeport of Monrovia is the country's principal port and the hub of a large shipping registry — Liberia's flag of convenience is one of the largest in the world. Monrovia's economy is dominated by government, trade, and services, and the city hosts the University of Liberia and the John F. Kennedy Medical Center.
