Blodeuwedd

Welsh mythology
Created by: Gwydion and Math
Spouse: Lleu Llaw Gyffes
Transformed into: An owl

Blodeuwedd (Welsh: "Flower-Faced") is a woman in Welsh mythology, made from flowers by the magicians Math and Gwydion in the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi to be the wife of Lleu Llaw Gyffes, thereby circumventing the curse of his mother Arianrhod that he should never have a human wife.

Story

Gwydion created her from the blossoms of oak, broom and meadowsweet. Blodeuwedd later fell in love with Gronw Pebr, a neighbouring lord, and the lovers plotted Lleu's death. Because Lleu could be killed only under elaborate conditions — neither indoors nor outdoors, neither on horseback nor on foot, by a spear a year in the making — Blodeuwedd coaxed the secret from him and staged the scene by a riverbank.

Transformation

Lleu, struck by Gronw's spear, turned into an eagle and was restored by Gwydion. Gronw was killed by Lleu in turn. As punishment, Gwydion transformed Blodeuwedd into an owl, condemning her to shun daylight and be mobbed by other birds — an origin story for the owl's habits in Welsh tradition.

Legacy

Her tale has inspired poetry, novels and Saunders Lewis's Welsh verse drama Blodeuwedd (1948).
