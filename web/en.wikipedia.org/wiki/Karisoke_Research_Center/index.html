Karisoke Research Center

The Karisoke Research Center is a research station in the Virunga Mountains of Rwanda, founded by primatologist Dian Fossey on 24 September 1967 to study and protect the endangered mountain gorilla. Named by combining the nearby mountains Karisimbi and Visoke, Karisoke is the longest-running field site dedicated to gorillas.

History

Fossey established the camp between Mount Karisimbi and Mount Visoke in what is now Volcanoes National Park. From Karisoke she habituated gorilla groups, campaigned against poaching, and recorded behaviour that transformed scientific understanding of the species, later described in her book Gorillas in the Mist (1983). Fossey was murdered at the camp in 1985 and is buried there beside her beloved gorilla Digit. The original cabins were destroyed during the Rwandan civil war of the 1990s, and the programme was rebuilt at new facilities on the park boundary.

Work today

Operated by the Dian Fossey Gorilla Fund, Karisoke now employs hundreds of staff, runs daily anti-poaching patrols and gorilla monitoring, and supports conservation, education and research in Rwanda. Data from Karisoke underpin much of what is known about mountain gorilla social behaviour, demography and health.
