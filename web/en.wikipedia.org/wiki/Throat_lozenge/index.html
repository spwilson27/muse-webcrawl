Throat lozenge

A throat lozenge, also known as a cough drop, cough sweet, troche, cachou, or pastille, is a small, typically medicated tablet intended to be dissolved slowly in the mouth to temporarily stop coughs and lubricate and soothe irritated tissues of the throat, usually due to a sore throat or the common cold. Cough tablets have diamond (lozenge) shapes, from which the name derives.

Ingredients

Many lozenges contain menthol, eucalyptol, benzocaine, or other demulcents and mild local anaesthetics; some contain dextromethorphan as a cough suppressant, or antiseptics such as amylmetacresol and dichlorobenzyl alcohol. Sugar-free varieties use sweeteners such as isomalt or sorbitol.

History

Candies to soothe the throat date back to 1000 BC; Smith Brothers cough drops, introduced in the United States in 1847, were among the first mass-marketed brands, followed by products such as Fisherman's Friend (1865) and Ricola.
