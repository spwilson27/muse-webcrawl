Cross

The Latin cross, the most common form of the Christian cross symbol.

A cross is a geometrical figure consisting of two lines or bars, usually perpendicular to each other, dividing one or both of the lines in half. The lines usually run vertically and horizontally. A cross of oblique lines, in the shape of the Latin letter X, is termed a saltire in heraldic terminology.

History

The cross-shaped sign is one of the most ancient human symbols, appearing in prehistoric petroglyphs and artefacts around the world, with meanings that included the sun, the four elements, and the division of the world into four cardinal points. In the ancient Near East and Egypt, related symbols such as the ankh represented life.

Religious significance

The cross is most widely known as the principal symbol of Christianity, commemorating the crucifixion of Jesus of Nazareth. The Christian cross, in variants including the Latin cross and the Greek cross (with arms of equal length), is used in worship, church architecture, jewellery, and funerary monuments, and appears in the flags and heraldry of many countries and regions. Crosses also feature in other traditions, including the Hindu and Jain swastika, and in pre-Christian European religions.

Other uses

Beyond religion, crosses serve as marks of location (the red cross of the humanitarian movement), as symbols in mathematics and writing (the dagger † and double dagger ‡), as navigational and cartographic signs, and in phrases such as "to bear one's cross", meaning to endure a burden.
