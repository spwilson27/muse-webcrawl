The Monument, London

Location: Fish Street Hill, City of London
Designed by: Christopher Wren and Robert Hooke
Built: 1671–1677
Height: 61.5 m (202 ft)

The Monument is a Doric column in London, England, standing near the northern end of London Bridge. Designed by Sir Christopher Wren and Robert Hooke and built between 1671 and 1677, it commemorates the Great Fire of London of 1666 and celebrates the city's rebuilding.

Description

The fluted Portland stone column is 202 feet (61.5 m) tall — its height exactly the distance from its base to the bakery in Pudding Lane where the fire started. It is topped by a gilded urn of fire, and its pedestal carries allegorical sculpture by Caius Gabriel Cibber and Latin inscriptions describing the fire and the restoration. Inside, a spiral staircase of 311 steps climbs to a viewing platform, which was enclosed in mesh in the nineteenth century.

History

The Monument was conceived not only as a memorial but as a scientific instrument: its central shaft contains an underground laboratory and was designed for gravity and pendulum experiments and as a zenith telescope, though passing traffic made precision work impossible. It remains a Grade I listed building and a popular visitor attraction, giving its name to Monument station on the London Underground.
