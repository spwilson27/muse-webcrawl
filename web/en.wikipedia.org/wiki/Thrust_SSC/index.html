Thrust SSC

Thrust SSC is a British jet-propelled car developed by Richard Noble, Ron Ayers, Jeremy Bliss, and Glyn Bowsher, and driven by Andy Green. On 15 October 1997, at Black Rock Desert in Nevada, it set the world land speed record of 1,228 km/h (763 mph) and became the first car to break the sound barrier officially, producing a sonic boom. Its predecessor was Thrust2, which had held the record from 1983; earlier British record cars were driven by men such as Malcolm Campbell at venues including Bonneville Speedway.

Design

The car is 16.5 m long and weighs about 10.5 tonnes. Two Rolls-Royce Spey engines — of the type used in the RAF's Phantom jets — are mounted on either side of the narrow body, producing a combined thrust of about 50,000 lbf. It rides on solid aluminium wheels, and is steered with rear-wheel steering controlled through a handlebar-like yoke in the cockpit. Ron Ayers led the aerodynamic design, using computational fluid dynamics to keep the car stable and on the ground through the transonic regime.

Record runs

After testing in Jordan's Al-Jafr desert, the team moved to Black Rock Desert, Nevada. On 25 September 1997 Thrust SSC set a record of 714 mph; on 15 October 1997, in two runs within the required hour, it averaged 763.035 mph (Mach 1.02), the first officially supersonic land speed record, eclipsing the mark set days short of fifty years after Chuck Yeager's first supersonic flight. The record still stands. Thrust SSC is now displayed at the Coventry Transport Museum alongside Thrust2.
