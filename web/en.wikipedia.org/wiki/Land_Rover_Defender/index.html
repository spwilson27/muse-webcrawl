The Land Rover Defender is a series of British four-wheel-drive off-road vehicles produced by Land Rover. The name was introduced in 1990 for the vehicles previously known simply as the Land Rover Ninety and One Ten, descended from the original Land Rover Series models launched in 1948.

The classic Defender, built on a separate chassis with a boxy aluminium body and permanent four-wheel drive, became an icon of rugged utility, serving farmers, militaries, aid agencies and explorers worldwide. Production of the classic model ended at Solihull in January 2016 after 68 years of continuous Series/Defender production. An all-new unibody Defender (L663) was launched in 2020, offered in 90, 110 and 130 body styles.

History

The coil-sprung Land Rover One Ten arrived in 1983 and the Ninety in 1984, modernising the leaf-sprung Series III. The Defender name was adopted in 1990 to distinguish the vehicles from the new Discovery. Engines over the years included Rover petrol and diesel units, the Td5, and Ford-derived Duratorq diesels in the final "Puma" models.

The 2020 Defender

Revealed at the 2019 Frankfurt Motor Show, the new Defender uses an aluminium monocoque, independent air suspension and modern petrol, diesel and plug-in hybrid powertrains, while retaining strong off-road geometry, wading ability and a functional, washable interior with optional jump seat.

Use

Defenders have served with the British Army (as the Wolf), police and fire services, the International Red Cross, and on expeditions including early overland crossings of Africa; they remain a staple of rural life in much of Britain and the Commonwealth.
