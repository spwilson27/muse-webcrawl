Capital ship

Capital ship is a term for the most important warships in a navy — the vessels around which a fleet is built and whose loss would be a major strategic blow. In the age of sail, capital ships were the ships of the line; in the early 20th century they were battleships and battlecruisers; after the Second World War, the role passed to aircraft carriers, and in some usage to ballistic missile submarines.

History of the concept

The term came into use in the sailing era, when the largest ships — those fit to stand in the line of battle — determined naval power. The launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906 redefined the category around all-big-gun battleships. Interwar naval treaties, notably the Washington Naval Treaty of 1922, explicitly classified capital ships by displacement and gun calibre and limited their numbers.

Modern usage

Since 1945, fleet carriers have been the capital ships of major navies, with nuclear-powered attack and ballistic-missile submarines sometimes counted in the category. The designation is strategic rather than a formal classification: it denotes the units a navy would risk least and protect most.
