A combined authority is a type of local government institution in England, established by two or more local authorities to work jointly across a functional economic area, typically a city region. Combined authorities receive devolved powers and funding from central government — covering transport, economic development, housing, skills, and sometimes policing and health — under negotiated "devolution deals".

Combined authorities cover most of England's major city regions.

History

The Greater Manchester Combined Authority, created in 2011, was the first. The Cities and Local Government Devolution Act 2016 allowed combined authorities to be headed by directly elected "metro mayors"; the first mayoral elections took place in May 2017 in six areas including the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, Tees Valley, and the West of England. Subsequent deals extended the model to areas including South Yorkshire, West Yorkshire, the North East, and (as a combined county authority under the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023) the East Midlands.

Powers and governance

Mayoral combined authorities typically control local transport (including bus franchising), strategic planning, housing investment funds, and the adult education budget; some mayors also serve as Police and Crime Commissioner. Members are drawn from the constituent councils, and the mayor is directly elected by residents. The 2024 English Devolution White Paper proposed extending devolution across England and simplifying structures into "strategic authorities".
