DVB-T2

DVB-T2 is an abbreviation for "Digital Video Broadcasting — Second Generation Terrestrial"; it is the extension of the television standard DVB-T, issued by the consortium DVB. DVB-T2 is designed for the broadcast of television and radio over terrestrial transmitters using OFDM modulation with concatenated channel coding. Compared with DVB-T, it offers substantially improved robustness and an increase in usable data rate of at least 50 percent for equivalent reception conditions, enabling more channels and widespread high-definition television over terrestrial networks.

Development

The DVB Project began work on a successor to DVB-T in 2006, setting commercial requirements for higher capacity and improved performance in portable and mobile reception. The standard was ratified by the DVB Steering Board in June 2008 and published by ETSI as EN 302 755 in September 2009. The United Kingdom became the first country to deploy DVB-T2 services, launching with the Freeview HD platform in December 2009 ahead of the 2010 FIFA World Cup.

Technical features

DVB-T2 employs several techniques absent from its predecessor:

  • Modulation: OFDM with constellations up to 256-QAM, and rotated constellations that improve robustness by spreading signal components.
  • Forward error correction: LDPC coding combined with BCH coding, replacing the convolutional and Reed–Solomon codes of DVB-T.
  • FFT sizes: options from 1K to 32K subcarriers, with a wider range of guard intervals.
  • Physical layer pipes (PLPs): separate logical channels within one RF channel, each with its own modulation and coding, allowing services to be protected to different degrees.
  • Multiple input modes: support for multiple PLPs and, in later profiles, MIMO-related techniques and Time Frequency Slicing (TFS).

Adoption

DVB-T2 has been adopted across much of Europe, Africa, and Asia as countries complete digital switchover or upgrade from DVB-T and MPEG-2. Germany replaced its DVB-T network with DVB-T2 HD using HEVC encoding between 2016 and 2019, and Italy, Spain, and many African states have followed similar paths. A "T2-Lite" profile was defined for mobile and portable reception at reduced bit rates.
