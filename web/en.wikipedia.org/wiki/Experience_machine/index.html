Experience machine

The experience machine is a thought experiment proposed by the philosopher Robert Nozick in his 1974 book Anarchy, State, and Utopia. Nozick asks us to imagine a machine that could give us any experiences we desire: superduper neuropsychologists stimulate the brain so that one thinks and feels one is writing a great novel, making a friend, or reading an interesting book, all while floating in a tank with electrodes attached.

Purpose

Nozick posed the thought experiment against hedonism — the view that pleasure (and the avoidance of pain) is all that matters. If pleasurable experience were all we valued, we should have no reason to refuse to plug in for life. Nozick argued that most of us would hesitate, because we also value actually doing things, actually being a certain kind of person, and contact with reality itself: "What does matter to us in addition to our experiences?"

Responses

Some philosophers argue reluctance to plug in reflects status quo bias rather than a genuine insight about value; others propose variations (such as entering the machine unknowingly) to test this. The idea has influenced debates over virtual reality, video games, and the philosophy of well-being, and is often compared with the "brain in a vat" scenario and the film The Matrix.

See also: Robert Nozick, Hedonism, Anarchy, State, and Utopia.
