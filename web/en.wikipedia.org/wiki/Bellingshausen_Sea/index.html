Bellingshausen Sea

The Bellingshausen Sea is an area of the Southern Ocean along the west side of the Antarctic Peninsula, between Alexander Island and Thurston Island. It is named after Admiral Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen, the Russian explorer who sailed the area in 1821 during the first expedition to sight the Antarctic mainland.

Geography

The sea stretches along the coasts of Ellsworth Land and the English Coast, bordered by the Amundsen Sea to the west. Peter I Island, discovered by Bellingshausen's expedition, lies within it. Much of the sea is covered by sea ice for much of the year, and icebergs calved from Antarctic ice shelves drift through its waters.

Climate and research

The Bellingshausen Sea region of West Antarctica has experienced significant warming and glacial retreat, making it an important focus of climate research. Its marine ecosystem supports krill, seals, penguins and whales, and it is visited by research vessels studying ocean circulation and ice-shelf melting.
