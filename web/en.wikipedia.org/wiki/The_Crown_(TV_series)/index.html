The Crown (TV series)

The Crown is a historical drama television series about the reign of Queen Elizabeth II, created and principally written by Peter Morgan for Netflix. Produced by Left Bank Pictures and Sony Pictures Television, it premiered on 4 November 2016 and ran for six seasons, concluding in December 2023. Each pair of seasons covers roughly a decade of the Queen's life, with the principal roles recast as the characters age. At the time of its launch it was among the most expensive television series ever made.

Claire Foy portrayed the young Queen Elizabeth II in the first two seasons.

Premise and seasons

The series begins with Elizabeth's marriage to Prince Philip in 1947 and her accession in 1952, and follows the political and personal events of the reign: the Suez Crisis, the Profumo affair, the Aberfan disaster, the Apollo 11 audience, the miners' strike, the Falklands War, the marriages and divorces of her children, the death of Diana, Princess of Wales (covered in season 6), and the early relationship of Prince William and Catherine Middleton, ending around 2005. Each episode typically interweaves a public event with a private or family drama.

Cast

Three casts portray the royal family across the decades. Claire Foy's Elizabeth, Matt Smith's Philip, and Vanessa Kirby's Princess Margaret anchor seasons 1–2; Olivia Colman, Tobias Menzies, and Helena Bonham Carter lead seasons 3–4, which add Josh O'Connor as Prince Charles and Emma Corrin as Diana; Imelda Staunton, Jonathan Pryce, Lesley Manville, Dominic West, and Elizabeth Debicki head seasons 5–6. John Lithgow's Winston Churchill in season 1 won particular acclaim.

Reception and accuracy

The Crown won wide critical praise for its writing, performances, and lavish production, receiving 21 Primetime Emmy Awards from 69 nominations, including Outstanding Drama Series for season 4, and sweeping all seven drama categories at the 2021 Emmys. Its blend of fact and invention has been repeatedly debated: historians and former officials have criticised fabricated conversations and storylines presented with documentary realism, and in 2022 Netflix added a disclaimer describing the series as a fictionalised dramatisation after public pressure, including from Dame Judi Dench.
