Ecotourism

Ecotourism
FocusNature, conservation, local communities
Term popularised1980s (Héctor Ceballos-Lascuráin)
Leading destinationsCosta Rica, Galápagos, Kenya, Borneo

Ecotourism is a form of tourism marketed as 'responsible' travel (using what proponents say is ecological and ethical) to natural areas, conserving the environment, and improving the well-being of the local people. The intent of ecotourism is to offer tourists insight into the impact of human beings on the environment and to foster a greater appreciation of natural habitats. Ecotourism aims at minimal environmental impact on the areas visited.

Besides reducing environmental impact, ecotourism also aims to provide an effective economic incentive to conserve nature and enhance the bio-cultural diversity of threatened species. Successful ecotourism programmes explicitly channel revenue to conservation and to local communities, as in Costa Rica's national parks system and gorilla trekking in Rwanda and Uganda, where permit fees fund park protection.

Principles

Definitions by bodies such as The International Ecotourism Society stress minimising physical, social, and psychological impacts; building environmental and cultural awareness; providing direct financial benefits for conservation and local people; and raising sensitivity to host countries' political, environmental, and social climates.

Criticism

Critics argue that much marketed 'ecotourism' amounts to greenwashing: long-haul flights create large carbon footprints, wildlife can be disturbed, profits may leak to foreign operators, and fragile sites can be degraded by visitor pressure. Certification schemes attempt to distinguish genuine projects, with mixed success.
