XM Satellite Radio

XM Satellite Radio (XM) was one of the three satellite radio (SDARS) and online radio services in the United States and Canada, operated by Sirius XM Holdings. It provided pay-for-service radio with channels for music, sports, talk, comedy and entertainment, received via geostationary satellites and a network of terrestrial repeaters.

History

The company was founded in 1988 as the American Mobile Satellite Corporation and won one of two FCC licences for satellite digital audio radio service in 1997. Its satellites, named "Rock" and "Roll", were launched in 2001, and the service began broadcasting in September 2001, rolling out nationally by November. XM grew through deals with automakers — notably General Motors, which factory-installed XM receivers — and reached several million subscribers by the mid-2000s, competing with rival Sirius Satellite Radio, which famously signed Howard Stern.

Merger

In February 2007 XM and Sirius announced a merger; after lengthy regulatory review it closed in July 2008, forming Sirius XM Radio. The two programme line-ups were progressively unified, the separate XM branding was retired in 2011, and former XM channels were carried within the SiriusXM service. XM's satellites broadcast in the S band around 2.3 GHz, using a signal compatible with later combined receivers.

Programming

XM offered more than 150 channels, including commercial-free music channels, Major League Baseball play-by-play (for which it held exclusive satellite rights from 2005), NHL hockey, talk personalities such as Opie and Anthony, and the Bob Dylan-hosted Theme Time Radio Hour.
