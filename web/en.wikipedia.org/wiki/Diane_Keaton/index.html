Diane Keaton

Diane Keaton (born Diane Hall, January 5, 1946) is an American actress. She has received numerous accolades, including an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award and two Golden Globe Awards. Known for her distinctive style and comic timing, she became a major star in the 1970s.

Career

Keaton rose to prominence with her role as Kay Adams-Corleone in Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather (1972) and its sequels. Her collaboration with Woody Allen, including Play It Again, Sam, Sleeper, Love and Death and Manhattan, culminated in Annie Hall (1977), for which she won the Academy Award for Best Actress; the film also made her menswear-inspired look a fashion landmark. Later successes include Reds (1981), Marvin's Room (1996), Something's Gotta Give (2003) and the Book Club films.

Other work

Keaton is also a photographer, author and real-estate developer with a noted interest in architectural preservation and design, publishing books on California architecture and her own homes.
