The Squad Designated Marksman Rifle (SDM-R) is a designated marksman rifle used by the United States Army. It is a heavily modified M16 rifle intended to extend the effective range and accuracy of an infantry squad by providing precision fire at ranges beyond those of standard-issue carbines, out to about 600–800 metres.

Background

The SDM-R grew out of the U.S. Army's designated marksman programme, which sought to place a specially trained and equipped rifleman in every infantry squad. The 3rd Infantry Division requested accurised M16-type rifles for its designated marksmen, and the Army Marksmanship Unit (AMU) at Fort Benning designed and built the rifles to meet that requirement.

Design

The SDM-R uses a 20-inch (510 mm) 1:8-inch twist free-floated match barrel, a Daniel Defense rail handguard, a two-stage match trigger, and a fixed A2-style buttstock. It is fitted with a telescopic sight — originally the Trijicon ACOG 4× or a Leupold variable-power optic on later builds — and Harris bipod. Ammunition is the Mk 262 Mod 1 cartridge with a 77-grain Open-Tipped Match bullet, which offers improved long-range performance from the 5.56×45mm platform.

Role

Unlike a sniper, the squad designated marksman remains an integral member of the rifle squad, fighting in the same manner as other riflemen while providing more accurate, longer-ranged fire against point targets. The SDM-R concept influenced later designated marksman programmes, including the adoption of 7.62mm systems such as the M110 and the HK417-based rifles.
