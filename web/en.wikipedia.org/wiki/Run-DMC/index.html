Run-DMC was an American hip hop group formed in Hollis, Queens, New York City, in 1983 by Joseph Simmons, Darryl McDaniels and Jason Mizell. They are regarded as one of the most influential acts in the history of hip hop culture and one of the most famous hip hop acts of the 1980s. Together with the Beastie Boys, LL Cool J and Public Enemy, they pioneered new school hip hop.

Career

The group's debut single "It's Like That" (1983) introduced a stripped-down sound built on drum machines and aggressive, traded-off rhymes. Their self-titled debut album (1984) was the first rap album to be certified gold. King of Rock (1985) followed, and Raising Hell (1986) became the first platinum and multi-platinum hip hop album; it included "Walk This Way", a collaboration with Aerosmith that revived that band's career and broke hip hop decisively into the rock mainstream. The group's endorsement deal with Adidas, celebrated in "My Adidas", was a landmark in music merchandising.

Jam Master Jay was murdered in 2002, and the group disbanded shortly afterwards. Run-DMC were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009, only the second hip hop act so honoured.

Legacy

Run-DMC are credited with moving hip hop from disco-influenced party music to a harder, rock-influenced sound, and with establishing the album as hip hop's central format. Their fashion — Adidas sneakers worn without laces, Kangol hats and leather fedoras — became an enduring visual identity for the genre.
