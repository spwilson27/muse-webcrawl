Şanlıurfa

Şanlıurfa (often shortened to Urfa) is a city in southeastern Turkey and the capital of Şanlıurfa Province, with a population of over 2 million in its province. Known in antiquity as Edessa, it lies on a plain beneath a rocky ridge crowned by a citadel.

History

Urfa is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities of Upper Mesopotamia, near the Neolithic site of Göbekli Tepe. As Edessa it was a frontier city between Rome and Parthia, an early centre of Syriac Christianity, and later a Crusader state (the County of Edessa, 1098–1144). The honorific prefix "Şanlı" ("glorious") was added in 1984 to commemorate local resistance during the Turkish War of Independence.

Religious significance

Tradition identifies Urfa as the birthplace of Abraham (İbrahim), and the Balıklıgöl — the sacred fish pools beside the Halil-ür Rahman Mosque — draws pilgrims who believe the fish live where Abraham was cast into fire that turned to water. The city's covered bazaars and stone architecture reflect its Ottoman heritage.
