Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corporation was a United States tobacco company and a subsidiary of the multinational British American Tobacco (BAT), which acquired it in 1927. Founded in Winston, North Carolina, in 1894 by George T. Brown and his brother-in-law Robert L. Williamson, it later relocated its headquarters to Louisville, Kentucky.

History and brands

Brown & Williamson marketed cigarette brands including Kool, Lucky Strike (in the US), Pall Mall, Viceroy, Raleigh and Barclay. In 2004 its US operations were merged with R. J. Reynolds to form Reynolds American, in which BAT held a controlling interest.

Controversy

The company became notorious in the 1990s when its former head of research, Jeffrey Wigand, disclosed that executives had known nicotine was addictive and that cigarettes were manipulated to enhance nicotine delivery. Wigand's story was dramatised in the 1999 film The Insider. Internal documents known as the "Brown & Williamson documents" were a key part of the litigation leading to the 1998 Tobacco Master Settlement Agreement.
