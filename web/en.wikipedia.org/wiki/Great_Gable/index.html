Great Gable

Elevation899 m (2,949 ft)
Prominence425 m
LocationLake District, Cumbria, England
ListingWainwright, Hewitt, Nuttall

Great Gable is a mountain in the Lake District, Cumbria, England, rising to 899 metres (2,949 ft) above sea level. It is named for its appearance as a great dome-shaped gable end when seen from Wast Water to the southwest. Great Gable is a celebrated fell, popular with walkers and climbers, and forms the centerpiece of the view depicted in the Lake District National Park's emblematic scenery.

Geography

The fell stands at the head of several valleys: Wasdale to the southwest, Ennerdale to the northwest, and Borrowdale via the Sty Head pass to the northeast. Its crags — including the Great Napes traversed by the climbers' Napes Needle — were a birthplace of British rock climbing in the Victorian era.

Remembrance

On the summit plateau stands a memorial tablet placed by members of the Fell and Rock Climbing Club commemorating club members who died in the First World War. Each year on Remembrance Sunday a service is held at the summit, attended by hundreds of walkers. In 1924 the club gave a large tract of surrounding fells to the National Trust as a memorial.

Ascents

Popular routes ascend from Wasdale Head, from Seathwaite in Borrowdale via Sty Head, and from Honister Pass. Alfred Wainwright described Great Gable as one of the finest of the Lakeland fells.
