Lo Shu square

The Lo Shu square (洛書, "Luo River writing") is a 3×3 magic square from ancient China, and the oldest known example of a normal magic square. In its standard form the numbers 1 to 9 are arranged so that every row, column and diagonal sums to 15, the square's magic constant.

Legend

According to legend, the pattern was seen on the shell of a tortoise emerging from the Luo River during the reign of the mythical Emperor Yu, who used it in governing and flood control. The square — often depicted as patterns of black and white dots, with even numbers as black dots and odd numbers as white — was paired with the Hetu ("River Map") diagram in Chinese cosmology.

Significance

The Lo Shu is unique (up to rotations and reflections) among 3×3 normal magic squares. It became deeply embedded in Chinese thought: its arrangement underlies the nine halls of the Mingtang temple, feng shui compass layouts, the bagua-derived "nine palaces" diagrams, and traditional Chinese medical and astrological schemes. The number 15 also matches the number of days in each of the 24 solar terms of the Chinese calendar's cycle.
