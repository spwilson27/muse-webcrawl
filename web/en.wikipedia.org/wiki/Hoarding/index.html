Hoarding

Hoarding is the persistent accumulation of possessions and difficulty discarding them, regardless of their actual value. In its clinical form, hoarding disorder, the behaviour causes significant distress or impairment and clutters living areas to the point that they cannot be used for their intended purposes.

Forms

Hoarding behaviour occurs across several domains:

  • Object hoarding — the most common form, involving household items, newspapers, clothing, and containers.
  • Animal hoarding — keeping large numbers of animals without providing adequate care.
  • Digital hoarding — accumulating files, emails, and media without organizing or deleting them.
  • Economic hoarding — buying and stockpiling goods, for example during shortages or in anticipation of price rises; in economics, "hoarding" also refers to holding money or commodities out of circulation.

Psychology

Hoarding disorder was classified as a distinct diagnosis in the DSM-5 (2013), having previously been considered a symptom of obsessive–compulsive disorder. It is associated with strong emotional attachment to objects, indecisiveness, and beliefs about wastefulness. Onset is often in adolescence, with severity increasing with age. Treatment typically involves specialized cognitive behavioural therapy focused on sorting, decision-making, and gradual exposure to discarding.

Cultural aspects

Famous historical cases include the Collyer brothers of Harlem, found dead among more than 100 tons of accumulated material in 1947. Hoarding has been widely depicted in reality television programmes, which researchers note may both raise awareness and sensationalize the condition.
