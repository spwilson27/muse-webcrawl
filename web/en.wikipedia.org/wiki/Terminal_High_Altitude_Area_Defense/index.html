Terminal High Altitude Area Defense

Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD), formerly Theater High Altitude Area Defense, is an American anti-ballistic missile defence system designed to intercept short-, medium- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles in their terminal phase, both inside and just outside the atmosphere (endo- and exo-atmospheric intercepts), using hit-to-kill kinetic impactors.

Components

A THAAD battery comprises truck-mounted launchers carrying eight interceptors each, the AN/TPY-2 X-band radar, a fire-control and communications unit, and support equipment. The interceptor separates its kill vehicle in flight, which homes on the target with an infrared seeker and destroys it by collision.

History and deployment

Developed by Lockheed Martin after the 1991 Gulf War exposed the limits of Patriot against Scud missiles, THAAD entered service in 2008. Batteries have been deployed to Guam, South Korea (2017, provoking a diplomatic dispute with China over the radar), Israel, Romania, and Middle Eastern purchasers including the UAE and Saudi Arabia. THAAD forms the upper tier of a layered defence above Patriot.
