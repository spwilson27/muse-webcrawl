Feliks Dzierżyński

Feliks Dzierżyński
Born: 11 September 1877, Dzerzhinovo, Russian Empire (now Belarus)
Died: 20 July 1926, Moscow, Soviet Union
Known for: Founding and leading the Cheka

Feliks Edmundovich Dzierżyński (Polish: Feliks Dzierżyński; 1877–1926), nicknamed "Iron Felix", was a Polish-born Bolshevik revolutionary who founded and led the Cheka, the first Soviet secret police organisation, and directed the Red Terror during the Russian Civil War.

Early life and revolutionary activity

Born into a Polish noble family on an estate near Ivyanets in present-day Belarus, Dzierżyński was expelled from school and joined the Lithuanian Social Democratic movement as a teenager, co-founding the Social Democracy of the Kingdom of Poland and Lithuania with Rosa Luxemburg. He spent roughly eleven years in tsarist prisons and Siberian exile, escaping several times, and was freed by the February Revolution of 1917.

The Cheka

In December 1917 Lenin appointed him head of the newly created All-Russian Extraordinary Commission — the Cheka — charged with combating counter-revolution and sabotage. Under his direction the Cheka became the instrument of the Red Terror, conducting mass arrests and executions. When the Cheka was reorganised as the GPU (later OGPU) in 1922, Dzierżyński remained in charge. He also served as Commissar for Internal Affairs and for Transport, and from 1924 chaired the Supreme Council of the National Economy (Vesenkha), promoting industrial planning within the New Economic Policy.

Death and legacy

Dzierżyński died of a heart attack on 20 July 1926, shortly after delivering a speech to the Central Committee. His statue famously stood in Lubyanka Square in Moscow until it was toppled in August 1991; a large statue in Warsaw was likewise removed in 1989. He remains a deeply controversial figure: revered in Soviet tradition as an incorruptible defender of the revolution, and condemned by critics as an architect of mass repression.
