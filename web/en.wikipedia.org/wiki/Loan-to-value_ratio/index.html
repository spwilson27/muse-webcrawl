Loan-to-value ratio

Image placeholder: loan amount versus appraised value

The loan-to-value (LTV) ratio is a financial term used by lenders to express the ratio of a loan to the value of an asset purchased. In real estate, the term is commonly used by banks and building societies to represent the ratio of the first mortgage line as a percentage of the total appraised value of real property. For instance, if someone borrows $130,000 to purchase a house worth $150,000, the LTV ratio is $130,000 ÷ $150,000 = 87%.

LTV ratio = Mortgage amount ÷ Appraised property value

Risk assessment

Loan-to-value is one of the key risk factors that lenders assess when qualifying borrowers for a mortgage. The risk of default is always at the forefront of lending decisions, and the likelihood of a lender absorbing a loss increases as the amount of equity decreases. Hence, as the LTV ratio of a loan increases, the qualification guidelines for certain mortgage programs become more strict. Lenders can require borrowers with high LTV ratios to purchase mortgage insurance, which protects the lender from loss if the borrower defaults, thereby increasing the cost of the loan.

Low LTV ratios (below 80%) tend to carry lower interest rates, because the lender's position is better protected by the borrower's equity. Conversely, borrowers with LTV ratios above 100% — where the loan exceeds the property's value — are said to be in negative equity or "underwater", a condition associated with sharply higher default rates.

Combined loan-to-value

Where more than one loan is secured on the same property — for example a first mortgage plus a home equity line of credit — lenders use the combined loan-to-value (CLTV) ratio, the sum of all secured loans divided by the property value.

Policy use

Regulators in many countries impose LTV limits as a macroprudential tool to restrain house-price booms and protect banking stability. Central banks in countries such as Ireland, New Zealand and Canada have at various times capped the proportion of new lending permitted above specified LTV thresholds.
