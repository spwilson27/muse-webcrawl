Archie (search engine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of a telnet session querying an Archie server for a filename]

Archie is a tool for indexing FTP archives, allowing users to find specific files. It is considered the first Internet search engine — the first tool that let people search the contents of the network rather than merely browse it.

Archie was created in 1990 by Alan Emtage, a student at McGill University in Montreal, with Bill Heelan and J. Peter Deutsch. Archie servers periodically contacted known anonymous FTP sites, downloaded their directory listings, and built a searchable database of filenames; users queried it by telnet, email or early clients. The name derives from 'archive' (minus the v), and inspired the era's naming style — the Gopher search tools Veronica and Jughead followed the comic-book joke.

Archie indexed only filenames, not contents, and the rise of the World Wide Web brought web search engines that indexed the text of pages themselves. Archie servers dwindled through the late 1990s, though at least one, in Poland, kept the service alive for decades as a working museum of the early Internet.
