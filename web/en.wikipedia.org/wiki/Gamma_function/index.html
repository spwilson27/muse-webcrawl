Gamma function

In mathematics, the gamma function (represented by Γ, the capital letter gamma from the Greek alphabet) is one commonly used extension of the factorial function to complex numbers. Derived by Leonhard Euler in 1729, the gamma function is defined for all complex numbers except the non-positive integers, and satisfies Γ(n) = (n − 1)! for positive integers n.

Γ(z) = ∫0∞ tz−1 e−t dt

Definition and properties

The gamma function satisfies the functional equation Γ(z + 1) = zΓ(z), which generalizes the factorial recurrence. Euler's reflection formula states Γ(1 − z)Γ(z) = π / sin(πz), from which Γ(1/2) = √π. The function has simple poles at 0, −1, −2, … and no zeros. The Bohr–Mollerup theorem characterizes Γ as the unique log-convex function satisfying Γ(1) = 1 and the recurrence.

History

Euler introduced the function in correspondence with Christian Goldbach in 1729 while seeking to interpolate the factorial. Daniel Bernoulli independently proposed a product formula. The notation Γ(z) is due to Adrien-Marie Legendre. Karl Weierstrass and others later gave infinite-product representations.

Applications

The gamma function appears throughout analysis, probability, and combinatorics: in the gamma and chi-squared distributions, in volumes of n-dimensional spheres, in analytic number theory through its role in the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function, and in physics in dimensional regularization and statistical mechanics.
