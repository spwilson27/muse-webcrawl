Let It Be is the twelfth and final studio album by the English rock band the Beatles. It was released on 8 May 1970, almost a month after the group's break-up, in tandem with the documentary film of the same name. Recorded largely during January 1969 as part of the "Get Back" project intended to return the band to its live roots, the sessions were marked by tension and were shelved for over a year before producer Phil Spector reworked the tapes.

Background

Paul McCartney conceived the project as a back-to-basics album rehearsed and performed without studio trickery. The band rehearsed at Twickenham Film Studios and then at Apple Corps headquarters on Savile Row, culminating in an unannounced rooftop concert on 30 January 1969 — the Beatles' final live performance. Keyboardist Billy Preston joined the sessions at George Harrison's invitation.

Release and reception

Singles from the album included "Get Back", "Let It Be", and "The Long and Winding Road". Spector's orchestral overdubs, especially on "The Long and Winding Road", were controversial; McCartney later oversaw the stripped-down remix album Let It Be... Naked (2003). In 2021 Peter Jackson's documentary series The Beatles: Get Back drew on the original session footage.
