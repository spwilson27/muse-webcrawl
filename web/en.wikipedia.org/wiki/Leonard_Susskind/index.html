Susskind lecturing at Stanford.

Leonard Susskind (born 20 May 1940) is an American theoretical physicist, the Felix Bloch Professor at Stanford University and a founding director of the Stanford Institute for Theoretical Physics. He is considered one of the fathers of String theory — his 1970 work gave the dual resonance model its string interpretation — and he co-proposed the Holographic principle (independently developing Gerard 't Hooft's idea), fought and settled the 'black hole war' over information with Stephen Hawking (the subject of his 2008 book), and co-created the ER = EPR conjecture linking wormholes and Quantum entanglement.

Susskind began as a plumber in the Bronx (his father's trade) before switching to physics at City College and Cornell (PhD 1965). His later career includes popularising the string landscape and anthropic reasoning for the Cosmological constant, and the celebrated 'Theoretical Minimum' lecture series and books bringing modern physics to general readers.

Black hole complementarity

Susskind's complementarity principle (1993, with 't Hooft and Thorlacius) proposed that information both falls into a black hole and reflects from its horizon — contradictory descriptions that no single observer can compare — an idea matured by holography into the modern view. His 2004–08 exchange with Hawking ended with Hawking's concession on their famous bet.
