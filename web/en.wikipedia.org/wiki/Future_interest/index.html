Future interest

In property law, a future interest is a legal right to property ownership that does not include the right to present possession or enjoyment of the property. Future interests are created on the formation of a defeasible estate; that is, an estate with a condition or event triggering transfer of possession. A common example is the landlord's reversion after a lease ends.

Types

Future interests held by the transferor (grantor) include:

  • Reversion — the interest remaining in a grantor who transfers a lesser estate than they own, such as a life estate or a term of years.
  • Possibility of reverter — following a fee simple determinable, where the estate ends automatically on the occurrence of a stated event.
  • Right of entry (power of termination) — following a fee simple subject to condition subsequent, where the grantor must act to reclaim the property.

Future interests held by third parties (transferees) are remainders — which may be vested or contingent — and executory interests, which cut short a preceding estate or follow a fee simple subject to an executory limitation.

Rules and policy

Because future interests can cloud title and restrain the use of land for generations, the common law developed limiting doctrines, most famously the rule against perpetuities, which generally voids interests that might vest more than a life in being plus 21 years after their creation. Future interests are generally transferable, devisable and inheritable, though historically contingent remainders faced restrictions.
