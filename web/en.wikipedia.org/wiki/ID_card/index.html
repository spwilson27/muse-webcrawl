Identity document

An identity document (also called an ID or id card, identity card, citizen card, national identification card, or passport card) is any document that may be used to prove a person's identity. If issued in a small, standard credit-card size form, it is usually called an identity card (IC, ID card, citizen card), or passport card. Some countries issue formal identity documents as national identification cards that may be compulsory or non-compulsory, while others may require identity verification using regional identification or informal documents.

A generic national identity card in the standard ID-1 (credit card) format.

Information included

Identity documents typically include a photograph of the holder, full name, date of birth, address, a unique personal identification number, and an expiry date. Modern cards increasingly incorporate biometric data such as fingerprints or facial-recognition templates stored on an embedded chip, and machine-readable zones conforming to ICAO standards.

National identity cards

Many countries, particularly in Europe, Asia, Africa, and Latin America, issue national identity cards to citizens, often compulsory above a certain age. Examples include the German Personalausweis, the French carte nationale d'identité, the Spanish Documento Nacional de Identidad, and India's Aadhaar system. In countries without national cards, such as the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia, driving licences and passports commonly serve as de facto identification.

Uses

ID cards are used for domestic identification, voting, access to government services and banking, age verification, and in some regions — such as the European Union — as travel documents between member states in place of a passport.

Privacy and security debates

Compulsory identity cards have been debated on civil-liberties grounds, with critics raising concerns about surveillance and data security, while supporters cite fraud reduction and administrative efficiency. Security features on modern cards include holograms, guilloche printing, laser engraving, and public-key infrastructure for electronic authentication.
