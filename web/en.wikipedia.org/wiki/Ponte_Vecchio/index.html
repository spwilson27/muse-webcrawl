Ponte Vecchio

Ponte Vecchio
Crosses: Arno
Locale: Florence, Italy
Design: Closed-spandrel segmental arch bridge
Material: Stone

The Ponte Vecchio ("Old Bridge") is a medieval stone closed-spandrel segmental arch bridge over the Arno in Florence, Italy, noted for the shops built along it — as was once common on great bridges. Butchers, tanners, and farmers initially occupied the shops; the present tenants are jewellers, art dealers, and souvenir sellers.

The bridge spans the Arno at its narrowest point where it is believed that a bridge was first built in Roman times, originally in wood. It was destroyed by a flood in 1117 and reconstructed in stone, then swept away again in 1333. The bridge was rebuilt in 1345 and is attributed to Taddeo Gaddi or Neri di Fioravanti.

Vasari Corridor

In 1565 the Vasari Corridor was built above the bridge by Giorgio Vasari for Cosimo I de' Medici, connecting the Palazzo Vecchio to the Palazzo Pitti and allowing the Grand Duke to pass freely and safely between his residence and the seat of government.

Second World War

During the German retreat in August 1944, the Ponte Vecchio was the only bridge across the Arno in Florence not destroyed; access to it was blocked by demolishing the buildings at both ends, which were later rebuilt.
