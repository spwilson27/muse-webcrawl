Fort Lauderdale is a coastal city located in the U.S. state of Florida, 30 miles north of Miami along the Atlantic Ocean. It is the county seat of and most populous city in Broward County. With canals that wind throughout the city, Fort Lauderdale has earned the nickname "Venice of America".

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Tequesta people. The city is named after a series of forts built by the United States during the Second Seminole War, the first of which was built in 1838 under Major William Lauderdale. The city was incorporated in 1911 and grew rapidly during the Florida land boom of the 1920s.

Tourism and economy

Fort Lauderdale is a major yachting center, with more than 50,000 registered yachts and 100 marinas. It is a principal city of the Miami metropolitan area and a popular tourist destination, long famous as a spring break center and home to Port Everglades, one of the busiest cruise ports in the world.

Geography

The city is traversed by the New River and an extensive canal system, and lies within the Atlantic coastal plain with an average elevation of about 6 feet above sea level.
