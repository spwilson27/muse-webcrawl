Industrial heritage

Industrial heritage consists of the physical remains of the history of technology and industry, such as manufacturing and mining sites, power stations, transport infrastructure, workshops and workers' housing, together with associated intangible social and cultural records. It is studied through the discipline of industrial archaeology.

Recognition

Interest in preserving industrial sites grew in the mid-20th century as deindustrialisation threatened factories, mines and canals. The International Committee for the Conservation of the Industrial Heritage (TICCIH), founded in 1973, promotes the field, and its 2003 Nizhny Tagil Charter set out principles for industrial heritage conservation. The European Route of Industrial Heritage (ERIH) links major sites across Europe.

Examples

Numerous industrial sites are UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including Ironbridge Gorge in England, the Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex in Germany, the Blaenavon Industrial Landscape in Wales, and the salt works of Salins-les-Bains and the Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans in France. Preserved sites are often reused as museums, housing or cultural venues.
