Necessity and sufficiency

In logic and mathematics, a condition is necessary for a statement if the statement cannot be true without it, and sufficient if the condition alone guarantees the statement. A condition can be both (necessary and sufficient, "if and only if"), either, or neither.

Examples

Being a mammal is necessary but not sufficient for being human; being a father is sufficient but not necessary for being a parent. Oxygen is necessary for fire but not sufficient. The concepts underpin definitions, causal analysis and legal tests.
