NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt

AIDS Memorial Quilt
Conceived: 1985, Cleve Jones
First display: 1987, National Mall
Panels: more than 50,000
Weight: about 54 tons

The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt, often abbreviated to the AIDS Quilt, is an enormous memorial quilt and community folk-art project commemorating people who have died of AIDS-related causes. It is considered the largest piece of community folk art in the world.

History

San Francisco activist Cleve Jones conceived the quilt in 1985; the first panels were made in 1987 and 1,920 panels were displayed on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. in October 1987, covering an area larger than a football field. The quilt returned to Washington several times, most completely in 1996, when it covered the entire Mall.

Panels and custody

Each panel measures three by six feet — approximately the size of a grave — and is made by friends, lovers and families of the person commemorated. In 2019 the NAMES Project Foundation transferred stewardship to the National AIDS Memorial, and the quilt's archives went to the Library of Congress. See also Cleve Jones.
