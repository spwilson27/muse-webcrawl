Sasol Limited is a South African multinational integrated energy and chemical company, headquartered in Sandton, Johannesburg. Founded in 1950 as the South African Coal, Oil and Gas Corporation, it pioneered the commercial application of Fischer–Tropsch technology to convert coal and natural gas into liquid fuels and chemicals — a technology developed in the 1920s in Germany and scaled up in South Africa to exploit the country's abundant coal in the absence of significant oil reserves.

History

Sasol was established by the South African government in 1950, and its first plant, Sasol One at Sasolburg in the Free State, began producing synthetic fuel in 1955. Strategic importance grew under apartheid-era oil embargoes: the much larger Sasol Two and Sasol Three complexes at Secunda, Mpumalanga, opened in 1980 and 1982, greatly expanding synthetic fuel output. The company was privatised and listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange in 1979, later adding a New York listing.

From the 1990s Sasol internationalised, building gas-to-liquids (GTL) plants in Qatar (Oryx GTL) and Nigeria (Escravos GTL), and acquiring chemical businesses abroad, notably in Germany and the United States, including a large ethane cracker complex at Lake Charles, Louisiana.

Operations

Sasol's South African operations convert coal mined at Secunda into petrol, diesel and a wide range of chemicals — solvents, polymers, waxes, fertilisers and explosives components. It supplies a substantial share of South Africa's liquid fuel. Its chemicals business markets products in more than 100 countries, with major operations in South Africa, the United States, Germany and elsewhere.

Environmental record

Coal-to-liquids production is highly carbon-intensive: Sasol's Secunda complex is frequently cited as one of the world's largest single sources of greenhouse gas emissions, and the company is South Africa's second-largest emitter after the utility Eskom. Sasol has faced sustained criticism from environmental groups and litigation over air pollution in the Vaal Triangle and Mpumalanga highveld, and has announced plans to reduce emissions, repurpose Secunda towards gas and renewable feedstocks, and reach net zero by 2050, though the pace of transition remains contested.
