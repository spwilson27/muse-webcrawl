Think break

Labelled placeholder: a classroom "think break" in progress.

A think break is a behaviour management and reflective technique used in classrooms, particularly in primary and early secondary education in Australia, in which a student who has been disruptive is given a short period of structured quiet time to calm down, reflect on their behaviour, and plan how to rejoin the class activity. It is generally presented as a restorative, non-punitive alternative to older "time out" practices.

Description

In a typical implementation, the teacher briefly names the behaviour of concern and directs the student to a designated quiet spot, in or near the classroom, for an agreed short period — often a few minutes. During the think break the student is expected to consider three questions: what happened, who was affected, and what they can do differently when they return. The teacher then welcomes the student back without further discussion unless the behaviour recurs.

Rationale

Advocates describe the think break as consistent with restorative practices and trauma-informed teaching: the pause interrupts escalation, preserves the student's membership of the class community, and frames the conversation around repairing harm rather than punishment. Critics note that if used mechanically or for long periods it can function as simple exclusion under a friendlier name.
