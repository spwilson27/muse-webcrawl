Mass murder

Mass murder is the killing of a number of people at the same time or over a relatively short period of time and in close temporal and geographic proximity. Definitions vary: the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation has defined mass murder as an incident in which four or more people are killed (excluding the perpetrator), while researchers and statutes sometimes use thresholds of three or more victims. Mass murder is distinguished from serial murder, in which killings occur in separate episodes with a cooling-off period between them, and from spree killing, a term that has largely been retired in favour of a continuum definition.

Causes and characteristics

Mass murders are committed by individuals or small groups, commonly with firearms but also with explosives, vehicles, blades, or arson. Perpetrators include disgruntled employees, students, ideologically motivated extremists, and people killing their own families (familicide). Criminologists note that many perpetrators are suicidal, plan their attacks for extended periods, and seek notoriety; research on media "contagion" suggests that intensive coverage of such events can inspire imitations, leading to guidelines in some newsrooms about naming and depicting attackers.

By organisations and states

The term is also applied to massacres and atrocities committed by organised groups and governments, although the mass killing of civilians by states is more often discussed under headings such as genocide, crimes against humanity, and democide.
