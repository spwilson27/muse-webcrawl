Fréchet distribution

Parametersα > 0 shape; scale s > 0; location m
Supportx > m

CDFe−((x−m)/s)−α

The Fréchet distribution, also known as the inverse Weibull distribution, is a special case of the generalized extreme value distribution. It has a probability density function

f(x; α, s, m) = (α/s) ((x − m)/s)−1−α exp(−((x − m)/s)−α)

for x > m, where α > 0 is the shape parameter, s > 0 is the scale parameter, and m is the location parameter (the minimum value). It is named after the French mathematician Maurice Fréchet, who introduced it in a 1927 paper on the asymptotic distribution of the largest value of a random sample.

Properties

The Fréchet distribution arises in extreme value theory as one of the three possible limiting distributions for the maximum of a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables, alongside the Gumbel distribution and the Weibull distribution. It applies when the parent distribution has a heavy, polynomially decaying tail.

The distribution is heavy-tailed: its moments exist only up to a certain order. The mean exists only when α > 1, and the variance exists only when α > 2. The cumulative distribution function of the single-parameter form (with s = 1 and m = 0) is exp(−x−α) for x > 0.

Applications

The Fréchet distribution is used in hydrology to model extreme events such as maximum one-day rainfall and river discharges, in finance to model extreme losses, and in reliability analysis. In multivariate statistics, Fréchet bounds describe the best-possible bounds on a joint cumulative distribution function given only the marginal distributions.
