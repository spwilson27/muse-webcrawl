Carl Van Doren

Carl Clinton Van Doren (September 10, 1885 – July 18, 1950) was an American literary critic and biographer. He won the Pulitzer Prize for Biography in 1939 for his life of Benjamin Franklin.

Life and career

Born in Hope, Illinois, Van Doren studied at the University of Illinois and Columbia University, where he taught English from 1911 to 1934. He served as literary editor of The Nation and of The Century Magazine, and was a founder of the Literary Guild book club. His brother was the poet and critic Mark Van Doren.

Work

Van Doren wrote widely on American literature, including The American Novel (1921) and editions of Franklin's writings. His Benjamin Franklin (1938) remains a landmark scholarly biography. His popular history Secret History of the American Revolution (1941) drew on newly opened British archives to recount the treason of Benedict Arnold. He also appeared as an expert panelist on the radio quiz show Information Please.
