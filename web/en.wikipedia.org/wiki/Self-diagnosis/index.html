Self-diagnosis

Self-diagnosis is the process of diagnosing, or identifying, one's own medical condition without the direct involvement of a healthcare professional. People may self-diagnose using personal experience, books, symptom checkers, online resources, or at-home tests.

Advantages and risks

Self-diagnosis can help people recognize minor, self-limiting conditions, decide when to seek care, and participate more actively in managing chronic conditions, for example through home blood-pressure or blood-glucose monitoring. However, it carries risks: laypeople may misinterpret symptoms, miss serious illnesses that share symptoms with benign ones, or experience unnecessary anxiety — sometimes called cyberchondria when driven by internet searching. Confirmation bias and the prevalence of unreliable health information online add to these risks.

In mental health

Self-diagnosis of mental health conditions has become more common with the spread of information and personal accounts on social media. Clinicians and researchers debate its effects: it can validate experiences and lead people to professional help, but informal labels may also be inaccurate or applied without the differential assessment used in formal diagnosis.

Regulation

Some jurisdictions restrict the marketing of direct-to-consumer diagnostic tests and symptom-checker software, which may be regulated as medical devices.
