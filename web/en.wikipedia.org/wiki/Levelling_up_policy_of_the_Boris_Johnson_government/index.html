Levelling up was the flagship domestic policy agenda of the British government of Boris Johnson, aimed at reducing regional economic and social inequalities across the United Kingdom, particularly between London and the South East and the North of England, the Midlands, and the devolved nations. The phrase became a central slogan of the Conservative Party's successful 2019 general election campaign.

Background

The United Kingdom has some of the widest regional disparities in productivity, pay, health, and life expectancy among developed countries. The 2019 manifesto promised to "level up" opportunity, building on the votes of former Labour strongholds in the so-called "Red Wall" that the Conservatives captured at that election. A dedicated ministry was created in September 2021 under Michael Gove, the Department for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities.

The White Paper and missions

The February 2022 White Paper set out twelve "missions" to be achieved by 2030, covering living standards, research and development spending outside the South East, transport connectivity, digital connectivity, education, skills, health, well-being, pride in place, housing, crime, and local leadership. It also promised further devolution deals for English counties and metro areas. Funding instruments included the Levelling Up Fund (£4.8 billion), the Towns Fund, and the UK Shared Prosperity Fund.

Criticism and aftermath

Critics, including the Institute for Fiscal Studies and the National Audit Office, questioned whether the funding matched the rhetoric, whether competitive bidding wasted local resources, and whether the missions could be met. After Johnson's resignation in 2022, his successors kept the department but gradually dropped the slogan; in 2024 the incoming Labour government renamed the department and retired the phrase.
