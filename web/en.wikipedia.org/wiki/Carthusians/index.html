Carthusians

The Carthusians (Latin: Ordo Cartusiensis), also known as the Order of Saint Bruno, are a Latin enclosed religious order of the Catholic Church, founded by Saint Bruno of Cologne in 1084. The order combines eremitical and cenobitic life: monks live mostly in solitary cells within a communal monastery called a charterhouse.

History

Bruno, a noted scholar and chancellor of Reims, withdrew with six companions to the remote Chartreuse Mountains near Grenoble, France, where Bishop Hugh granted them land. The mother house, La Grande Chartreuse, remains the head of the order. The customs of the order were written down by Guigo I in the Consuetudines Cartusiae (1120s). The motto of the order is Stat crux dum volvitur orbis — "The cross stands while the world turns."

Life

Carthusian monks spend most of the day alone in their cells in prayer, study, and manual work, gathering for the night office, Mass, and on Sundays and feasts for a common meal and recreation. They observe silence, abstain perpetually from meat, and fast regularly. Carthusian nuns follow a similar life. Lay brothers support the community's material needs.

Today

The order maintains about twenty charterhouses worldwide. The monks of La Grande Chartreuse produce the famous Chartreuse liqueur, whose sales fund the order. The 2005 documentary Into Great Silence brought the order's life to wide attention.
