Ferranti

Ferranti was a British electrical engineering firm that built the world's first commercially available general-purpose computer.

Ferranti was a British electrical engineering and equipment firm founded in 1885 by Sebastian Ziani de Ferranti. Over more than a century it manufactured power generation and distribution equipment, domestic appliances, military electronics and semiconductors, and it holds a distinguished place in computing history: working with the University of Manchester, Ferranti delivered the Ferranti Mark 1 in 1951, the first commercially available general-purpose electronic computer.

History

Ferranti pioneered alternating-current power systems in Britain, building the Deptford power station (1891), then the largest in the world, which supplied London with high-voltage AC power. The company produced transformers, meters, radios, televisions and the famous Ferranti electrical clocks and fires from its Hollinwood and Moston works in Manchester.

Computing

After the Second World War Ferranti commercialised the Manchester university computers, selling the Mark 1 and its successors (Pegasus, Mercury, Atlas — built with the university and among the fastest machines of the early 1960s). Ferranti also built computers in Canada, where Ferranti-Packard produced the FP6000, ancestor of the ICT 1900 series. The company's computer interests were sold to International Computers and Tabulators (ICT) in 1963.

Semiconductors and defence

Ferranti was an early British semiconductor manufacturer, and in 1977 launched the Ferranti F100-L military microprocessor. Its defence electronics — radar for aircraft such as the Tornado, inertial navigation, and laser systems — became the core business. A disastrous 1987 acquisition of the American firm International Signal & Control exposed the company to a massive fraud; losses mounted and Ferranti went into receivership in 1993, its defence businesses largely passing to GEC-Marconi.

Legacy

The Ferranti name survives in museums and in the firm's archives at the Museum of Science and Industry in Manchester. Sebastian de Ferranti is remembered as one of the founders of the modern electricity supply industry.
