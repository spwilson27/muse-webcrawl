The Gene Ontology (GO) is a major bioinformatics initiative to unify the representation of gene and gene-product attributes across all species. It provides a controlled vocabulary of terms describing gene functions, organized into three aspects: molecular function (activities of a gene product), biological process (larger processes the product contributes to), and cellular component (where in the cell it acts).

Structure

GO terms are connected in a directed acyclic graph by relations such as is_a and part_of, so a term may have several parents. Each term has a unique identifier (e.g. GO:0008150 for "biological_process"). Gene products are linked to terms by annotations, each supported by an evidence code describing how the association was made — experimentally, computationally, or by curator inference.

Uses

The best-known application is GO enrichment analysis: given a set of genes from an experiment (such as differentially expressed genes), statistical tests identify GO terms over-represented in the set, helping interpret genomics data. GO annotations underpin databases such as UniProt-GOA and are a standard layer in genome browsers and functional-genomics pipelines.

History

The Gene Ontology Consortium was formed in 1998 by the model-organism databases for yeast, fly and mouse, and has since expanded to most major genome resources. The ontology is continuously revised as biological knowledge grows.
