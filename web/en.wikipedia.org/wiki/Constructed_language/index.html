Constructed language
ExamplesEsperanto, Klingon, Quenya, Toki Pona, Interlingua

A constructed language (often shortened to conlang) is a language whose phonology, grammar, and vocabulary have been consciously devised by an individual or group, rather than having evolved naturally. Constructed languages are created for many purposes: facilitating international communication, enriching works of fiction, linguistic experimentation, or artistic pleasure.

Categories

Constructed languages are commonly divided by purpose. International auxiliary languages such as Esperanto (1887), Ido, and Interlingua aim to serve as neutral second languages. Artistic languages are devised for creative works: J. R. R. Tolkien's Quenya and Sindarin for Middle-earth, Klingon for Star Trek, and Dothraki for Game of Thrones. Engineered languages such as Logban test hypotheses about language and thought, while minimalist languages like Toki Pona (2001) explore expression with a tiny vocabulary.

A priori and a posteriori

Conlangs are also classified by source: a posteriori languages draw vocabulary and grammar from existing natural languages, whereas a priori languages invent them from first principles.

Community

Esperanto is the most widely spoken constructed language, with estimates ranging from tens of thousands to two million speakers and a small number of native speakers. Language invention, once a solitary pursuit, is now supported by an active online community of "conlangers".
