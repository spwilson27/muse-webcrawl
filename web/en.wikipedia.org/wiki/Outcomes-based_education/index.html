Outcomes-based education

Abbreviation: OBE

Key advocate: William Spady

Origins: Mastery learning (Bloom); US reforms 1980s–90s

Related: Competency-based education; backward design

Outcomes-based education (OBE) is an educational approach that organises curriculum, instruction and assessment around the demonstrable outcomes students are expected to achieve by the end of a course or programme, rather than around the content covered or time served. Its best-known formulation is that of William G. Spady, who defined outcomes as "clear learning results that we want students to demonstrate at the end of significant learning experiences."

Principles

Spady's model rests on designing down from culminating outcomes, holding high expectations for all students, and expanding opportunity and time for success rather than letting the calendar dictate learning — ideas descended from Benjamin Bloom's mastery learning and the criterion-referenced measurement tradition. Curricula are built by "backward design": identify outcomes, decide how they will be evidenced, then plan teaching.

Implementation

OBE influenced state standards movements in the United States in the early 1990s and was adopted, with varying results, in national reforms in South Africa (Curriculum 2005), Australia, Hong Kong and the Philippines. Versions of outcomes-based accreditation now govern much of higher and professional education, notably engineering under the Washington Accord and medical education standards.

Criticism

Opponents have criticised early OBE schemes for vague or values-laden outcomes, heavy assessment bureaucracy, and for reducing education to measurable performance; supporters reply that clarity about goals and alignment of teaching with assessment are indispensable. South Africa's abandonment of its first OBE curriculum is frequently cited as a cautionary case of over-ambitious, under-resourced implementation.
