Group of Eight

The Group of Eight (G8) was an intergovernmental political forum from 1997 until 2014, consisting of Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States, together with representation from the European Union. It evolved from the Group of Six (G6) founded in 1975 and the Group of Seven (G7) formed the following year, and it held annual summits of heads of government dealing with economic policy, security, energy, and development. In March 2014, following Russia's annexation of Crimea, the other members suspended Russia and resumed meeting as the G7; the G8 format has not met since.

History

The forum began with the 1975 Rambouillet summit convened by French president Valéry Giscard d'Estaing and West German chancellor Helmut Schmidt, bringing together six countries to coordinate responses to the oil crisis and the breakdown of the Bretton Woods system. Canada joined in 1976, creating the G7, and the European Community/EU began participating soon after. After the Cold War the group sought to integrate Russia: Russian leaders attended as guests from 1991, Russia joined fully in 1997–98 (the 1998 Birmingham summit was the first full G8), though the finance ministers' process continued in G7 format since Russia was not admitted to that track.

Activities

G8 summits addressed macroeconomic coordination, trade, debt relief for developing countries (the 2005 Gleneagles summit paired debt cancellation with aid commitments), climate change, food security, and global health (the Global Fund grew out of the 2000–01 summits). The summits also attracted mass protest, most famously at Genoa in 2001, where a protester was killed, and at Gleneagles in 2005 alongside the Make Poverty History campaign. From the late 2000s the larger G20 increasingly took over global economic coordination, eclipsing the G8's role.

Suspension of Russia

In response to Russia's annexation of Crimea in March 2014, the seven other members suspended Russia's participation, cancelled the planned Sochi summit, and met instead in Brussels as the G7 — the format that has continued since, with further summits addressing Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and subsequent sanctions coordination.
