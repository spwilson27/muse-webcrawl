Isosceles trapezoid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An isosceles trapezoid with its axis of symmetry

In Euclidean geometry, an isosceles trapezoid (isosceles trapezium in British English) is a convex quadrilateral with a line of symmetry bisecting one pair of opposite sides. It is a special case of a trapezoid: its two non-parallel sides (legs) are equal in length, its base angles are equal in pairs, and its opposite angles are supplementary.

Characterisations

A trapezoid is isosceles if and only if any of the following equivalent conditions hold: the legs are congruent; the base angles are congruent; the diagonals are congruent; or the figure is a cyclic quadrilateral (its vertices lie on a single circle). The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are equal and divide each other in the same ratio. Rectangles and squares qualify as isosceles trapezoids under the inclusive definition of trapezoid used by some authors; under the exclusive definition (exactly one pair of parallel sides) they do not.

Properties

If the bases have lengths a and b (with a > b) and the legs length c, the height is h = √(c² − ((a−b)/2)²), and the area is K = ((a+b)/2) · h — the average of the bases times the height, as for any trapezoid. The circumradius can be computed from the sides, and the diagonals have length p = √(c² + ab). The symmetry axis passes through the midpoints of the two bases.

Special cases

An isosceles trapezoid with three equal sides is sometimes called a trilateral trapezoid. A crossed isosceles trapezoid (with the legs crossing like a bow tie) is a complex quadrilateral also known as a crossed trapezium.
