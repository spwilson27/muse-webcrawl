Clarithromycin

Clarithromycin, sold under the brand name Biaxin among others, is an antibiotic used to treat various bacterial infections. It belongs to the macrolide class of antibiotics and works by stopping the growth of bacteria. Clarithromycin is used to treat respiratory tract infections such as pneumonia, bronchitis, and infections of the sinuses, ears, skin, and throat.

Medical uses

Clarithromycin is primarily used to treat respiratory tract infections caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, and Moraxella catarrhalis. It is also used in combination therapy to treat Helicobacter pylori infections that cause stomach ulcers, and to prevent and treat Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) infections in people with HIV.

Mechanism of action

Clarithromycin works by binding to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome, thereby inhibiting the translation of proteins. By inhibiting protein synthesis, it prevents bacteria from growing and multiplying.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, headaches, and diarrhea. More serious side effects may include allergic reactions, liver problems, and abnormal heart rhythms. Clarithromycin should not be used with certain other medications due to its inhibition of the CYP3A4 enzyme.

History

Clarithromycin was developed by researchers at the Japanese pharmaceutical company Taisho Pharmaceutical in the 1970s by modifying the structure of erythromycin. It was approved for medical use in the 1990s and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
