Conformal geometry

A conformal map distorts a grid while preserving the angles at which curves meet

In mathematics, conformal geometry is the study of the set of angle-preserving (conformal) transformations on a space. In a real two-dimensional space, conformal geometry is precisely the geometry of Riemann surfaces. In more than two dimensions, conformal geometry may refer either to the study of conformal transformations of (possibly curved) "flat" spaces (such as Euclidean spaces or spheres), or to the study of conformal manifolds, which are Riemannian manifolds with a class of angle-equivalent metrics rather than a single metric.

A conformal structure on a smooth manifold is an equivalence class of Riemannian metrics in which two metrics are equivalent if they differ by multiplication by a positive smooth function: g′ = e2ωg. Angles between tangent vectors are well defined for such a class, but lengths and volumes are not. The group of conformal transformations of the n-sphere is the Lorentz group of (n+2)-dimensional Minkowski-type space, a fact underlying the conformal compactification methods used in general relativity and the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Conformal manifolds and invariants

Key objects of the theory include the Weyl tensor, which vanishes exactly for locally conformally flat manifolds in dimension ≥ 4 (and measures conformal curvature in general), and the Cotton tensor in dimension 3. The tractor calculus and ambient metric construction of Fefferman and Graham provide tools for constructing conformal invariants — quantities unchanged under conformal rescaling — such as Q-curvature. The Yamabe problem, solved through the work of Yamabe, Trudinger, Aubin and Schoen, asks for a metric of constant scalar curvature within a given conformal class.

Higher dimensions and physics

In dimension two the local conformal group is infinite-dimensional, described by holomorphic functions, and Liouville's theorem shows that in dimension ≥ 3 all conformal maps between domains are restrictions of Möbius transformations. Conformal geometry is central to conformal field theory, twistor theory, and the study of Einstein manifolds; conformal infinity (as in the Penrose compactification) is used to analyse the asymptotic structure of spacetimes.
