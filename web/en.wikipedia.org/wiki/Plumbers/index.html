White House Plumbers

The White House Plumbers, sometimes called simply the Plumbers, were a covert special investigations unit established in the Nixon White House in 1971 to stop the leaking of classified information to the press — to "plug leaks". Led by Egil "Bud" Krogh and David Young, and operationally driven by E. Howard Hunt and G. Gordon Liddy, the unit broke into the office of Daniel Ellsberg's psychiatrist in 1971 and went on to supply the nucleus of the Watergate burglars whose arrest in June 1972 precipitated the Watergate scandal and the resignation of President Richard Nixon.

Formation

After the New York Times began publishing the Pentagon Papers in June 1971, Nixon demanded action against leaks. On the advice of John Ehrlichman, a Special Investigations Unit was created in Room 16 of the Executive Office Building; a sign on the door advertising David Young's name with the word "Plumber" — a family-trade joke — gave the group its lasting nickname. Its members included former CIA officer E. Howard Hunt and former FBI agent G. Gordon Liddy.

Operations

The Plumbers' first major operation targeted Daniel Ellsberg, the RAND analyst who had leaked the Pentagon Papers: on 3 September 1971 Hunt and Liddy supervised a break-in by Bernard Barker, Eugenio Martínez and Felipe de Diego at the Beverly Hills office of Ellsberg's psychiatrist, Dr Lewis Fielding, seeking material to discredit Ellsberg. They found little, and the operation later destroyed the government's espionage case against Ellsberg when it was exposed. The unit also investigated other leaks and discussed schemes ranging from firebombing the Brookings Institution to assassinating columnist Jack Anderson — plans that were not carried out.

From Plumbers to Watergate

In 1972 Liddy, financed by the Committee to Re-elect the President, developed the "Gemstone" plans for political espionage. Hunt and Liddy directed the two break-ins at the Democratic National Committee headquarters in the Watergate complex; the second, on 17 June 1972, ended with the arrest of five burglars, including Barker, Martínez and James McCord. The cover-up that followed unraveled through 1973–74: Krogh pleaded guilty over the Fielding break-in, Hunt and Liddy were convicted, and Nixon resigned on 9 August 1974.

In popular culture

The unit's name inspired the 2023 HBO miniseries White House Plumbers, dramatising Hunt and Liddy's partnership. The episode remains the classic American example of a secret White House operation escaping legal control.
