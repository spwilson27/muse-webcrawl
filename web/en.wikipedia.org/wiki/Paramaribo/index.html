Paramaribo is the capital and largest city of Suriname, located on the banks of the Suriname River in the Paramaribo District, about 15 kilometres inland from the Atlantic coast. Home to roughly half of the country's population, the city is noted for its well-preserved Dutch colonial wooden architecture, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2002, and for one of the most ethnically diverse populations in the Americas.

History

The name derives from an Indigenous village called Parmurbo. Dutch traders established a post at the site in the early seventeenth century; the settlement changed hands between the Dutch and English during the colonial wars, and under the Treaty of Breda (1667) Suriname became definitively Dutch — Britain receiving New Amsterdam (New York) in exchange. Fort Zeelandia guarded the river approach. Paramaribo remained the capital through Suriname's independence in 1975.

The city

The historic inner city features white-painted wooden buildings on brick foundations, a distinctive Dutch colonial style adapted to the tropics. Landmarks include the Presidential Palace on Independence Square (Onafhankelijkheidsplein), the wooden Saint Peter and Paul Cathedral — among the largest wooden structures in the Americas — Fort Zeelandia, and the Neveh Shalom Synagogue standing beside the Keizerstraat Mosque, a frequently cited symbol of Suriname's religious tolerance. The city's population includes people of Creole, Hindustani (Indian), Javanese, Maroon, Chinese, Indigenous, and European descent, and its markets and festivals reflect that mixture.
