The Surfers Paradise Street Circuit is a temporary street circuit on the Gold Coast of Queensland, Australia. The circuit uses public roads in the beachside suburb of Surfers Paradise, running past high-rise towers and along the esplanade beside the Pacific Ocean.

Supercars racing between the towers of Surfers Paradise

History

The circuit opened in 1991 as the home of the Gold Coast Indy 300, a round of the CART/Champ Car series that became one of that championship's most popular events. After the American open-wheel era ended following 2008, the event transitioned to Australian touring cars, with A1 Grand Prix and later the Supercars Championship headlining. Since 2010 the Supercars have raced there in an endurance format, currently known as the Gold Coast 500, featuring two 250 km races.

Layout

The original 4.47 km layout was shortened in 2010 to 2.984 km. The track is notorious for its unforgiving concrete walls, aggressive kerbs at the beach chicane, and heavy braking zones, which produce frequent safety-car periods and are regarded as one of the toughest tests in Supercars racing.
