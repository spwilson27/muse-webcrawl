The UEFA European Championship, commonly known as the Euros, is the primary association football competition contested by the senior men's national teams of the members of the Union of European Football Associations (UEFA), determining the continental champion of Europe. Held every four years since 1960, in the even-numbered year between World Cups, it was originally called the European Nations' Cup.

The idea for a European championship was proposed by French Football Federation secretary-general Henri Delaunay in the 1920s, and the trophy is named in his honour. The first tournament, in 1960, featured four teams in the finals; the field grew to eight in 1980, sixteen in 1996, and twenty-four from 2016.

Format

All UEFA member associations enter a qualifying competition; the host nation(s) qualify automatically. The finals comprise a group stage followed by a single-elimination knockout stage culminating in the final. The tournament is one of the most-watched sporting events in the world.
