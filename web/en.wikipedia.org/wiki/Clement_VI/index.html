Pope Clement VI (Latin: Clemens VI; born Pierre Roger; c. 1291 – 6 December 1352) reigned from 1342 until his death during the Avignon Papacy. A Benedictine scholar and former archbishop of Rouen, he was the most cultivated of the Avignon popes — and the pope of the Black Death.

Pontificate

Clement enlarged the papal palace at Avignon into a sumptuous court. In 1348 he purchased Avignon outright from Joanna of Naples. His diplomacy sought to mediate the Hundred Years' War and to negotiate reunion with the Byzantine Church.

The Black Death

When plague reached Avignon in 1348, Clement issued bulls protecting Jews against pogrom rumors, consecrated the Rhône so corpses could be buried in it, and granted remissions to those who nursed the sick. He survived the plague by isolating himself between two great fires, as his physician Guy de Chauliac advised.

Legacy

He declared 1350 a jubilee year, drawing vast pilgrimages to Rome. Petrarch, who knew the court, both praised his learning and satirized Avignon's luxury.
