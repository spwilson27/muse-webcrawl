The Sound of Music is a 1965 American musical drama film produced and directed by Robert Wise, starring Julie Andrews and Christopher Plummer. It is an adaptation of the 1959 stage musical by Rodgers and Hammerstein, itself based on the memoir of Maria von Trapp.

Plot

Maria, a free-spirited young postulant at an abbey in Salzburg, becomes governess to the seven children of the widowed Captain Georg von Trapp. She brings music and joy into the household, falls in love with the Captain, and marries him. After the Anschluss of 1938, the family escapes Nazi-occupied Austria over the mountains.

Reception

The film won five Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director, and became one of the highest-grossing films of all time when adjusted for inflation. Songs include "Edelweiss", "My Favorite Things", "Do-Re-Mi", "Climb Ev'ry Mountain" and the title song, filmed in the meadows above Werfen, Austria.
