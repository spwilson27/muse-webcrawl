College admissions

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Applicants typically submit applications in the final year of secondary school.

College admissions is the process through which students enter tertiary education at universities and colleges. Admissions processes vary widely between countries and institutions, ranging from systems based almost entirely on national examination results to "holistic" review that considers grades, essays, recommendations, extracurricular activities, and personal background.

By country

United States

In the United States, most colleges use a holistic review process. Applicants submit transcripts, standardized test scores (SAT or ACT, increasingly optional), personal essays, letters of recommendation, and lists of extracurricular activities. Application platforms such as the Common Application streamline submission to many schools. Decision plans include early action, early decision (binding), regular decision, and rolling admissions. Highly selective institutions admit only a small fraction of applicants; acceptance rates at the most competitive colleges have fallen below 5 percent.

United Kingdom

Applications to British universities are made through UCAS, with applicants limited to five course choices and writing a single personal statement. Admission depends largely on predicted and achieved grades in A-levels or equivalent qualifications, with interviews at Oxford and Cambridge.

Other systems

Many countries, including China, South Korea, India, and Japan, center admission on high-stakes national entrance examinations such as the gaokao, the College Scholastic Ability Test, and the JEE.

Issues

Debates about college admissions include the role of affirmative action (curtailed in the United States by the Supreme Court's 2023 decisions in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard), legacy preferences for children of alumni, athletic recruitment, access for low-income and first-generation students, and the growth of a private admissions consulting industry. The 2019 Varsity Blues scandal exposed bribery schemes to secure admission to elite universities.
