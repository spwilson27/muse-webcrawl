The Grisons (German: Graubünden; Romansh: Grischun; Italian: Grigioni) is the largest and easternmost canton of Switzerland. It is the only trilingual canton, with German, Romansh and Italian all having official status.

Geography

The canton is entirely mountainous, covering the upper valleys of the Rhine and the Inn and the Italian-speaking valleys descending toward Lombardy. It contains over 600 lakes and numerous glaciers, and includes parts of the Swiss National Park. Famous resorts in the canton include Davos, St. Moritz and Klosters, and it is crossed by scenic railways such as the Bernina and Albula lines, inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

The region corresponds largely to the Roman province of Raetia. In the late Middle Ages its communes organized themselves into the Three Leagues — the League of God's House, the Grey League (from which the name Graubünden, "Grey League", derives) and the League of the Ten Jurisdictions — a free state associated with the Swiss Confederacy. The Grisons were a battleground during the Thirty Years' War (the Bündner Wirren). The canton joined the Swiss Confederation in 1803.

Languages and culture

Romansh, a Romance language descended from the Latin of Raetia, survives mainly in the Surselva, Engadine and Val Müstair and is a Swiss national language. The canton is also known for its distinctive Engadine houses, capuns and other regional cuisine, and strong traditions of communal democracy.
