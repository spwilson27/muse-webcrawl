Constituency

[Hero image placeholder: map of parliamentary constituencies in a nation]

A constituency is a group of voters in a specified area who elect a representative to a legislative body. In many countries the term is used interchangeably with electoral district. Constituencies may be single-member or multi-member districts, and their boundaries are typically reviewed periodically to maintain roughly equal populations, a process vulnerable to manipulation through gerrymandering.

Terminology

In the United Kingdom, each Member of Parliament represents a parliamentary constituency, while devolved assemblies and local councils have their own constituency and ward divisions. In Canada, federal constituencies are officially "electoral districts" but commonly called "ridings". In India, Lok Sabha and state assembly constituencies are delimited by the Delimitation Commission. In Germany, the Bundestag constituency (Wahlkreis) elects one member directly, balanced by party-list seats. In Australia, federal constituencies are known as "divisions".

Constituent

An individual voter within a constituency is a constituent. Elected representatives are expected to serve the interests of their constituents, including through constituency casework — handling individual grievances about public services — which in many democracies consumes a large share of a legislator's time.

Redistricting

Because populations shift, constituencies must be redrawn from time to time. Independent boundary commissions in countries such as the UK, Canada and Australia conduct reviews intended to equalise electorates and follow community ties, whereas in the United States redistricting is largely controlled by state legislatures and has frequently been litigated.
