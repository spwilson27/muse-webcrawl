University of Huddersfield

University
Founded: 1825 (as Huddersfield Scientific and Mechanic Institute); university status 1992
Location: Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, England
Students: about 20,000

The University of Huddersfield is a public research university in Huddersfield, West Yorkshire, England. It has been a university since 1992, but its origins lie in a series of institutions dating back to 1825.

History

The university traces its history to the Huddersfield Scientific and Mechanic Institute of 1825 and the Young Men's Mental Improvement Society of 1841, which became a Mechanics' Institution offering technical education for the textile town's workers. Successor bodies included Huddersfield Technical College and, from 1970, Huddersfield Polytechnic. It gained university status under the Further and Higher Education Act 1992.

Academics

Teaching is organized into schools including Applied Sciences, Arts and Humanities, Business, Education and Professional Development, and Computing and Engineering. The university is noted for precision engineering — home to research in metrology and the £30 million MIAMI facilities for ion beam research — and for requiring most courses to include professional work placements ("sandwich years").

Campus and people

The Queensgate campus sits beside the Huddersfield Narrow Canal in the town centre. Actor Sir Patrick Stewart served as chancellor from 2004 to 2015; the university awards honorary doctorates at ceremonies in St Paul's Hall, a former church.
