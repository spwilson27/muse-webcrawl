Internal conflict in Myanmar

The internal conflict in Myanmar refers to a series of ongoing insurgencies that have been waged in Myanmar since 1948, when the country, then known as Burma, gained independence from the United Kingdom. The conflict has largely been ethnic-based, with several ethnic armed groups fighting the central government for self-determination. It is often described as the world's longest ongoing civil war.

Background

At independence, disputes over federalism and autonomy for ethnic minorities such as the Karen, Kachin, Shan and Mon were left unresolved. The Panglong Agreement of 1947 had promised autonomy to several frontier areas, but its terms were never fully implemented, and armed resistance began almost immediately.

Major groups

Armed organisations have included the Karen National Union, the Kachin Independence Army, the Shan State Army and, historically, the Communist Party of Burma. In 2015 several groups signed the Nationwide Ceasefire Agreement, though fighting continued in many regions.

Since 2021

Following the military coup of February 2021, conflict escalated sharply as new resistance forces, including People's Defence Forces aligned with the National Unity Government, fought alongside established ethnic armed organisations against the junta.
