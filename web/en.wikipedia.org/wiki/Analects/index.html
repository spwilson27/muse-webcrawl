The Analects (Chinese: Lunyu, 'Selected Sayings') is a collection of sayings and ideas attributed to Confucius and his contemporaries, compiled and edited by his followers over succeeding generations. It is the most revered text of Confucianism and one of the Four Books canonised by Zhu Xi: for two thousand years its short dialogues and aphorisms ('Learning without thought is labour lost; thought without learning is perilous') were memorised by every literate East Asian schoolchild.

Content

The Analects present the Master teaching benevolence, ritual, filial piety and the rectification of names — let a ruler be a ruler, a father a father — through concrete exchanges rather than systematic treatise. Its portrait of Confucius (delighted in learning, grief-stricken for his favourite disciple Yan Hui, wryly aware of his political failure) shaped the ideal of the junzi, the exemplary person.
