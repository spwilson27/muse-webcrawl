Caribbean monk seal

Conservation status: Extinct (c. 1952)
Scientific name: Neomonachus tropicalis (Gray, 1850)

The Caribbean monk seal (Neomonachus tropicalis), also known as the West Indian monk seal, was a species of monk seal native to the Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of Mexico. It is the only seal species known to have gone extinct through human activity, and was officially declared extinct in 2008.

Description and habitat

The seals reached about 2.4 metres in length and weighed around 200 kilograms, with brown or greyish fur. They hauled out on sandy beaches and rocky islets, feeding on fish, lobsters, and octopuses in shallow lagoons and coral reefs.

Extinction

First recorded by Columbus in 1494, the seals were hunted for oil, meat, and hides from the colonial era onward, and later killed by fishermen who saw them as competitors. The last confirmed sighting was of a small colony at Serranilla Bank in 1952. Despite later aerial surveys, no evidence of survival was found, and the species was listed as extinct after a five-year review by NOAA in 2008.
