Yield (finance)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Yield
Yield expresses an investment's income return relative to its price or value
DefinitionIncome return on an investment, expressed as a percentage
ExamplesDividend yield, bond yield, rental yield, earnings yield

In finance, the yield is the income return on an investment, such as the interest or dividends received, expressed as an annual percentage based on the investment's cost, current market value, or face value. Yield is distinct from total return because it excludes capital gains and losses: it measures only the cash income an asset generates relative to its price.

Types of yield

Bond yields

  • Coupon yield (nominal yield): the annual coupon payment divided by the bond's face value. It is fixed at issue.
  • Current yield: the annual coupon payment divided by the bond's current market price.
  • Yield to maturity: the internal rate of return if the bond is held until maturity, accounting for coupon reinvestment and the difference between price and par.
  • Yield to call and yield to worst: variants for callable bonds.

Equity yields

  • Dividend yield: annual dividends per share divided by the share price.
  • Earnings yield: earnings per share divided by price — the inverse of the price/earnings ratio.

Property yield

For real estate, yield is typically annual rental income divided by the property's value; net yields deduct operating costs, taxes and vacancies.

Interpretation

Yield generally moves inversely with price: when a bond's price rises, its current yield and yield to maturity fall. Investors often compare yields across assets as a rough gauge of relative value and risk, though a high yield may signal market scepticism about the issuer's ability to keep paying, as with "junk" bonds or so-called dividend traps.
