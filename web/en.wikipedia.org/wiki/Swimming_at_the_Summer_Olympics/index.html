Swimming at the Summer Olympics

Swimming has been a sport at every modern Summer Olympic Games, held since the first Games in Athens in 1896. It is one of the most popular sports at the Games, along with athletics, gymnastics and cycling. Swimming is governed internationally by World Aquatics (formerly FINA).

History

At the 1896 Games, swimming events were held in the open sea in the Bay of Zea; only freestyle events were contested. Backstroke was added in 1900 and breaststroke in 1904. Women's swimming events were first held at the 1912 Stockholm Games. Butterfly, developed as a variant of breaststroke, became a separate stroke with its own events from 1956. The programme grew over the twentieth century to include individual medley, relay events and, from 2008, the 10 km open-water marathon swim.

Events

The modern programme consists of 35 pool events — 17 for men, 17 for women and one mixed relay — held in a 50-metre pool: freestyle (50 m, 100 m, 200 m, 400 m, 800 m, 1500 m), backstroke, breaststroke and butterfly (100 m and 200 m each), the 200 m and 400 m individual medley, the 4 × 100 m and 4 × 200 m freestyle relays, the 4 × 100 m medley relay and the mixed 4 × 100 m medley relay, plus men's and women's 10 km marathon swims.

Successful nations and swimmers

The United States is by far the most successful nation in Olympic swimming history, followed by Australia. Michael Phelps is the most decorated Olympian of all time, with 28 medals, including 23 golds, won between 2004 and 2016. Other multiple champions include Mark Spitz, Ian Thorpe, Katie Ledecky and Kristin Otto.
