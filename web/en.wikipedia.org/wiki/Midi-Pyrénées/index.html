Midi-Pyrénées
Country: France
Capital: Toulouse
Area: 45,348 km²
Status: Former region (1970–2015)

Midi-Pyrénées (French pronunciation: [midi piʁene]; Occitan: Miègjorn-Pirenèus) was a former administrative region of France. From 1970 until 2015 it was the largest region of metropolitan France by area, larger than the Netherlands or Denmark. On 1 January 2016 it merged with Languedoc-Roussillon to form the new region of Occitanie.

Geography

The region stretched from the Pyrenees along the Spanish border in the south to the Massif Central in the north-east. It comprised eight departments: Ariège, Aveyron, Haute-Garonne, Gers, Lot, Hautes-Pyrénées, Tarn and Tarn-et-Garonne. Its capital and largest city was Toulouse, France's fourth-largest city and a major centre of the European aerospace industry.

History

Midi-Pyrénées was created as a modern administrative region in the second half of the 20th century from parts of the historic provinces of Languedoc, Gascony, Guyenne, Foix and Roussillon. None of the historic provinces corresponded exactly to the modern region, and the name "Midi-Pyrénées" was a geographical coinage — midi being a colloquial French term for the south, combined with the Pyrenees range.

Economy and culture

The region's economy rested on agriculture (including foie gras, Roquefort cheese and Armagnac brandy), tourism in the Pyrenean ski resorts and pilgrimage centre of Lourdes, and the aerospace cluster around Toulouse, home to Airbus assembly lines. Occitan was historically spoken across much of the region, alongside Gascon dialects.
