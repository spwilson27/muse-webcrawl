The Battle of Bạch Đằng of 938 was a naval battle on the Bạch Đằng River in what is now northern Vietnam, in which the Vietnamese leader Ngô Quyền defeated an invasion fleet of the Southern Han dynasty, ending roughly a millennium of Chinese domination of Vietnam.

Background

After the death of the Khúc-era leader Dương Đình Nghệ, Ngô Quyền moved to confront a Southern Han fleet sent to reassert control. He prepared the battlefield by driving iron-tipped wooden stakes into the riverbed, concealed beneath the water at high tide.

The battle

At high tide, small Vietnamese boats attacked and then feigned retreat, drawing the larger Southern Han warships over the stakes. When the tide ebbed, the Han ships were stranded and pierced by the stakes, and Ngô Quyền's forces attacked in force. The Han commander, Liu Hongcao, was killed.

Significance

The victory is traditionally regarded as the founding moment of an independent Vietnamese state; Ngô Quyền proclaimed himself king in 939. The same stake tactic was repeated at Bạch Đằng in 1288.
