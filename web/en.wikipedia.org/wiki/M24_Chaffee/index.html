The M24 Chaffee was an American light tank used during the Second World War and the Korean War. Named for General Adna R. Chaffee Jr., a founder of the US armoured force, it replaced the inadequate M5 Stuart, giving reconnaissance units a much more powerful 75 mm gun in a modern hull with torsion-bar suspension and sloped armour.

Development and service

Production began in April 1944 at Cadillac and Massey-Harris plants; 4,731 were built before production ended in August 1945. The M24 reached front-line units late in 1944 and saw its heaviest fighting during the Battle of the Bulge. It was well liked for its mobility and firepower, though its thin armour left it vulnerable to German anti-tank weapons.

Later use

M24s formed the first US tank units committed in Korea in 1950, where they fared poorly against North Korean T-34-85s. Large numbers were exported to allied nations; France used them in Indochina and Algeria, and upgraded derivatives and chassis variants — including self-propelled guns and the Norwegian NM-116 — served for decades.
