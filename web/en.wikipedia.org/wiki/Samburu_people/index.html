Samburu people

RegionsSamburu District, Rift Valley, Kenya
LanguageSamburu (a Maa language)
ReligionTraditional monotheism (Nkai), Christianity
Related groupsMaasai, Turkana, Rendille

The Samburu are a Nilotic people of north-central Kenya, closely related to the Maasai, who speak the Maa language. The Samburu speak their own dialect, also called Samburu, and are traditionally semi-nomadic pastoralists who herd mainly cattle, along with sheep, goats and, in drier areas, camels.

Culture and society

Samburu society is organised around age sets. Boys are circumcised and initiated into the moran (warrior) age grade in ceremonies held roughly every seven years; moran traditionally protect the community and its herds, wear their hair in long ochred braids, and are expected to show endurance and stoicism. Elders govern through councils and conduct rituals, marriages and the blessing of cattle.

Women wear elaborate bead necklaces and ornaments, build the low dome-shaped houses of mud, dung and hides, and carry responsibility for children, milking and household life. The traditional diet centres on milk and meat, historically supplemented by blood drawn from cattle.

Land and livelihoods

The Samburu inhabit the lowlands of the Great Rift Valley around Mount Nyiru and the Ndoto Mountains. Their pastoralist economy depends on mobility between wet- and dry-season grazing, a pattern increasingly constrained by land subdivision, group ranch conversion, conservation areas and recurrent drought. Many Samburu now combine herding with small-scale farming, wage work and tourism-related employment, while cultural tourism in northern Kenya showcases Samburu dress, dance and beadwork.

Beliefs

Traditional Samburu religion is monotheistic, centred on the god Nkai, associated with the sky and rain. Ritual specialists known as loiboni bless, divine and mediate for the community. Christianity has spread through missions and schooling, and many Samburu combine elements of both traditions.
