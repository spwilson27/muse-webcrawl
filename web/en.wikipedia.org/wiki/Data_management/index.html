GoalDelivering data as a reliable organisational asset

Data management comprises all the disciplines related to handling data as a valuable resource: the policies, practices and technologies by which organisations collect, store, organise, protect, quality-assure and deliver data throughout its lifecycle. Its aim is to ensure that the right people can obtain accurate, consistent and appropriately secured data at the right time, while complying with legal and regulatory obligations.

Disciplines

The professional association DAMA International's Body of Knowledge (DMBOK) organises the field around knowledge areas including data governance, data architecture, data modelling and design, data storage and operations, data security, data integration and interoperability, document and content management, reference and master data management, data warehousing and business intelligence, metadata management and data quality management.

Governance and quality

Data governance establishes accountability — owners and stewards for particular data domains — together with standards and decision rights. Data quality work addresses accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness and validity, typically through profiling, cleansing and monitoring. Poor data management is costly: duplicated customer records, inconsistent product codes and untracked spreadsheets undermine reporting, analytics and regulatory compliance.

Modern developments

The growth of big data, cloud storage and data lakes, and later lakehouse architectures, shifted management challenges toward scale and variety, while privacy laws such as the GDPR added duties concerning personal data, retention and deletion. Concepts like FAIR data (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) guide research-data management in the sciences.
