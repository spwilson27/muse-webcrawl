The Bears–Lions rivalry is a National Football League (NFL) rivalry between the Chicago Bears and the Detroit Lions. It is one of the longest-running annual series in the NFL, dating to 1930, when the Lions were based in Portsmouth, Ohio, as the Portsmouth Spartans.

History

The Spartans moved to Detroit and became the Lions in 1934. The two teams have met twice every season since then as members of the same division, first the NFL Western Division and later the NFC Central and NFC North. The Bears generally dominated the series in the 1930s and 1940s and again under coach Mike Ditka in the 1980s, while the Lions enjoyed strong stretches in the 1950s, when they won three NFL championships.

Notable games

Memorable meetings include the 1932 NFL playoff game, played indoors at Chicago Stadium because of severe weather, which the Bears won 9–0; the finish led directly to rule changes including the legalisation of the forward pass from anywhere behind the line of scrimmage. In 1980 the Bears won a Thanksgiving game in Detroit on an overtime kickoff return by Dave Williams.
