UV mapping

UV mapping is the 3D modelling process of projecting a 2D image onto a 3D model's surface for texture mapping. The letters "U" and "V" denote the axes of the 2D texture because "X", "Y", and "Z" are already used to denote the axes of the 3D object in model space, while "W" (in addition to XYZ) is used in calculating quaternion rotations, a common operation in computer graphics.

A checkered texture applied to a cube and sphere via their UV layouts; distortion in the layout appears as stretching of the checks on the model.

Process

UV texture space is a unit square (or rectangle) in which each vertex of the model's polygons is assigned a two-dimensional coordinate. A texture image is then "wrapped" onto the model so that the point of the texture at each (U, V) coordinate appears at the corresponding place on the surface. The process of laying out the polygons flat in texture space is called unwrapping; seams are placed where the surface is cut, and the layout should minimize stretching and make efficient use of the texture area.

Techniques

Simple projections—planar, cylindrical, and spherical—suit regular shapes but distort organic forms. Modern 3D software offers interactive unwrapping tools and automatic algorithms that flatten meshes while optimizing for low distortion, often visualized with a checkerboard test pattern. Artists may also "paint" directly on the 3D model, with the software projecting strokes through the UV layout into the texture.

Applications beyond colour

The same UV coordinates address many kinds of maps: normal maps, bump and displacement maps, roughness and metalness maps in physically based rendering, lightmaps that bake illumination, and decal placement. Models may carry several UV sets for different purposes, and repeating (tiling) textures exploit coordinates outside the 0–1 range.
