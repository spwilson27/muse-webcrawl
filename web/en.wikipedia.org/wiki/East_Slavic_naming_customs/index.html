East Slavic naming customs

East Slavic naming customs are the traditional ways of identifying a person in Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, and some other East Slavic cultures. A full name traditionally consists of three parts: a given name, a patronymic, and a family name (surname).

Structure

The patronymic is derived from the father's given name: sons receive the suffix -ovich/-evich (Russian) and daughters -ovna/-evna. For example, Ivan Petrovich Sokolov is Ivan, son of Pyotr, of the Sokolov family. Addressing a person by given name plus patronymic is the standard polite form.

Surnames

Common surname suffixes include -ov/-ev, -in, -sky, and -enko (Ukrainian). Female forms of surnames often add an -a. Diminutives of given names (e.g., Sasha for Alexander) are widely used informally.
