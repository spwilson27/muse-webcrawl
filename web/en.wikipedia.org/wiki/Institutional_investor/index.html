Institutional investor

An institutional investor is an entity that pools money to purchase securities, real property, and other investment assets or originate loans. Institutional investors include commercial banks, central banks, credit unions, government-linked companies, insurers, pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, charities, hedge funds, REITs, investment advisors, endowments, and mutual funds.

Role in markets

Institutional investors are the major force in financial markets, accounting for the majority of trading volume on large exchanges. Because of their size, they can often negotiate lower fees and gain access to investments unavailable to retail investors, such as private equity and hedge funds.

Regulation

Institutional investors are generally subject to lighter regulation than retail investors on the theory that they are more knowledgeable and better able to protect themselves, though pension funds and insurers face extensive prudential rules.

Activism

Large institutions increasingly engage in shareholder activism, voting on corporate governance matters and pressing companies on executive pay, strategy and environmental and social policies.
