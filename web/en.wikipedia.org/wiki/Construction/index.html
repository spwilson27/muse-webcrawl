Construction

Construction
SectorsBuildings, infrastructure (civil engineering), industrial
Share of GDPRoughly 13% of world output (incl. supply chain)
Key professionsArchitects, structural engineers, contractors, surveyors

Construction is the process of creating buildings and infrastructure, and the industry that carries it out — one of the largest sectors of the world economy. It spans residential and commercial buildings, heavy civil works (roads, bridges, dams, tunnels), and industrial facilities, and proceeds through stages of planning, design, financing, procurement, building, and handover to operation and maintenance.

Process

A construction project typically begins with a feasibility study and design by architects and engineers, expressed in drawings and, increasingly, in building information models (BIM). After permits and procurement — often by competitive tender — a general contractor manages site works and specialist subcontractors (groundworks, structure, mechanical and electrical services, finishes). Work is inspected against building codes and contracts; modern methods of construction shift work off-site into factories (prefabrication, modular construction) for speed and quality.

Materials and methods

Structures are built from building materials — concrete, steel, timber, masonry and glass — chosen for strength, cost, durability and environmental impact. Concrete, produced from cement, aggregate and water, is the most consumed material on Earth after water; companies such as US Gypsum supply interior products like gypsum wallboard. Formwork, cranes, scaffolding and earthmoving plant are characteristic site equipment.

Industry characteristics

Construction is fragmented, labour-intensive and cyclical, with many small firms and a few global contractors. It has historically low productivity growth and high accident rates, driving regulation on site safety, training and insurance. Sustainability pressures are rising: buildings and construction account for roughly a third of global energy-related emissions when materials and operations are combined, prompting interest in low-carbon cement, mass timber and retrofit of existing stock — including the reuse of site water and, in arid regions, desalinated water for concrete and dust control.

History

Construction is as old as settled life: Neolithic villages, Egyptian pyramids, Roman concrete (opus caementicium) and Gothic cathedrals all demanded sophisticated organisation. The Industrial Revolution brought iron, steel and the professionalisation of engineering; the twentieth century added reinforced concrete frames, tower cranes and prefabrication.
