Veterinary medicine

Veterinary medicine is the branch of medicine concerned with the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of disease, disorder and injury in animals. Its practitioners, veterinarians (vets), care for companion animals, livestock, zoo and laboratory animals, and wildlife, supported by veterinary nurses and technicians.

Scope

Beyond clinical care, veterinary medicine includes surgery, dentistry, epidemiology, food safety inspection, control of zoonotic diseases transmissible to humans, and advice on animal welfare and husbandry. The "One Health" approach recognises that human, animal and environmental health are interdependent. The profession's formal training dates from the first veterinary school, founded at Lyon in 1761 by Claude Bourgelat, largely in response to cattle plague.

Practice

Modern vets qualify through university degrees of five or more years and are licensed by national regulators. Practice ranges from first-opinion small-animal clinics to referral hospitals with advanced imaging and surgery, farm and equine practice, and government and research roles. Specialisation in fields such as cardiology, oncology and orthopaedics is increasingly common.
