Jan Oort

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Black-and-white portrait photograph of Jan Oort at a desk with star charts]

Jan Hendrik Oort (28 April 1900 – 5 November 1992) was a Dutch astronomer who made fundamental contributions to understanding the structure of the Milky Way. He is remembered for the Oort constants describing galactic rotation near the Sun, for early evidence of dark matter, and for the Oort cloud, the postulated spherical shell of icy bodies surrounding the Solar System.

Galactic astronomy

In the 1920s Oort helped establish that the Milky Way rotates differentially and worked out the Sun's position and motion within it. In 1932 he found that the visible matter near the Sun could not account for the observed motions of stars — an early indication of what is now called dark matter.

Oort cloud and radio astronomy

In 1950 Oort proposed that long-period comets originate in a vast distant cloud of icy planetesimals left over from the formation of the Solar System. He was also a pioneer of radio astronomy in the Netherlands, and the Dutch radio observatories at Westerbork and the later LOFAR array descend from that work.
