The Desert War is a name commonly given to the desert phase of the North African campaign of the Second World War, fought between the Axis forces — principally Erwin Rommel's Afrika Korps and Italian armies — and the British Commonwealth Eighth Army between 1940 and 1943. The campaign swung back and forth across Libya and Egypt as each side outran its supply lines along the single coastal road.

Course of the campaign

Italy invaded Egypt from Libya in September 1940 but was routed by Operation Compass. German forces under Rommel arrived in early 1941 and drove the British back toward Egypt, besieging Tobruk. After the seesaw battles of 1941–42 — including the fall of Tobruk in June 1942 — the Axis advance was halted at the First Battle of El Alamein. Under Bernard Montgomery, the Eighth Army won the decisive Second Battle of El Alamein (October–November 1942) and pursued the Axis westward; following the Allied landings in Morocco and Algeria (Operation Torch), Axis forces in Tunisia surrendered in May 1943.

Character of the fighting

The open, featureless terrain made the Desert War unusually mobile and tank-heavy, dominated by logistics: water, fuel and ports constrained every offensive. Long Range Desert Group and SAS raids, Malta-based interdiction of Axis shipping, and signals intelligence (Ultra) all played outsized roles.
