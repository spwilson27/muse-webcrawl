Generation Alpha

Generation Alpha (often shortened to Gen Alpha) is the demographic cohort succeeding Generation Z and preceding Generation Beta. Researchers and popular media generally use the early 2010s as starting birth years and the mid-2020s as ending birth years. Generation Alpha is the first generation to be born entirely in the 21st century and the third millennium.

The term was coined by Australian demographer and social researcher Mark McCrindle. Generation Alpha is notable for being the first generation largely born into a world of smartphones, tablet computers, streaming media and voice assistants, and its members were young children during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Naming

The name Generation Alpha follows the convention of naming generations after Greek letters, begun by social researchers after Generation X, Y (Millennials) and Z exhausted the Latin alphabet. McCrindle has stated that the name reflects a generation that will be shaped by a new era of technology and global change.

Date and age range definitions

Definitions vary by source. McCrindle defines Generation Alpha as those born from 2010 to 2024. Other commentators use 2010–2025 or 2013–2025. The succeeding cohort has been dubbed Generation Beta, generally proposed to begin in 2025.

Demographics

By the mid-2020s, roughly 2.5 billion Generation Alpha births were projected worldwide, with more than two million born each week at the generation's peak birth rate. Many members are the children of Millennials, and to a lesser extent of older Generation Z parents.

Technology

Generation Alpha has been described as "digital natives" to an even greater degree than Generation Z. Touchscreen devices, video-sharing platforms such as YouTube, and short-form video were commonplace in their early childhood. Educational technology and remote learning became prominent during the COVID-19 pandemic school closures.
