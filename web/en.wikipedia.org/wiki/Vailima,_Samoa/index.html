Vailima, Samoa

Vailima
Country: Samoa
Island: Upolu, near Apia
Known for: Robert Louis Stevenson's home

Vailima is a village and estate situated about six kilometres south of Apia, the capital of Samoa, on the island of Upolu. The name means "five waters", from the streams crossing the estate. Vailima is best known as the final home of the Scottish author Robert Louis Stevenson, whose five-bedroom wooden villa there has been preserved as a museum.

Stevenson's Vailima

Stevenson, seeking a climate beneficial to his poor health, bought about 300 acres at Vailima in 1890 and built a large five-bedroom house, an unusual mansion for the South Pacific at the time. He lived there with his wife Fanny and extended family, writing works including The Beach of Falesá and much of Weir of Hermiston. Known to Samoans as Tusitala ("teller of tales"), he took an active interest in local politics, opposing colonial mismanagement. He died at Vailima on 3 December 1894 and was buried on nearby Mount Vaea, above the estate, according to his wishes, where his tomb bears his famous "Requiem" epitaph.

The estate today

After passing through use as the residence of Samoa's colonial administrators and head of state, the villa was restored in the 1990s with American philanthropic support and reopened as the Robert Louis Stevenson Museum. The village is also home to Samoa's national brewery, which produces Vailima beer, one of the country's best-known brands.
