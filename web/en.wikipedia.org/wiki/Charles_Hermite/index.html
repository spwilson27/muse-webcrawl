Charles Hermite

Charles Hermite (24 December 1822 – 14 January 1901) was a French mathematician who worked on number theory, quadratic forms, invariant theory, orthogonal polynomials and elliptic functions. He is best remembered for proving in 1873 that the number e is transcendental, the first such result for a number that was not constructed for that purpose.

Life

Hermite was born in Dieuze, Lorraine. He studied at the Collège de France and the École Polytechnique, and taught at the École Polytechnique, the École Normale Supérieure and the Faculty of Sciences in Paris, where he held a chair for many years. His students included Henri Poincaré. He was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1856.

Work

Hermite solved the general quintic equation using elliptic modular functions (1858). His name attaches to Hermite polynomials, Hermitian matrices and forms, Hermite interpolation and the Hermite normal form. His 1873 proof that e is a transcendental number was extended by Ferdinand von Lindemann to show the transcendence of π. Hermite also corresponded extensively with Thomas Stieltjes, and their published letters are a notable record of nineteenth-century analysis.
