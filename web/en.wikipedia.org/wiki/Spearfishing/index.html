Spearfishing

Spearfishing is fishing using handheld implements that are similar to a spear or a trident, combined with the activity of hunting fish underwater using a spear or similar devices. Historically it was done with sharpened sticks and harpoons; modern spearfishers use elastic-powered spearguns or compressed-gas pneumatic guns while freediving, snorkelling, or scuba diving. Spearfishing may be done for sport or commercially, and is considered among the most selective and least environmentally damaging fishing methods because the fisher targets individual fish, producing virtually no bycatch.

A freediving spearfisher stalking reef fish.

History

Spearfishing with barbed poles (harpoons) was widespread in ancient times; Palaeolithic cave art and finds such as the Azilian harpoons document the practice. Civilisations used tridents: the Greek god Poseidon and Roman Neptune are depicted with one. The modern sport developed in the Mediterranean in the 1930s–1950s, and the world championships have been held by CMAS since 1954.

Equipment

Equipment includes spearguns (band-powered or pneumatic), pole spears and Hawaiian slings, along with mask, snorkel, fins, wetsuit, weight belt, dive knife, and a float or floatline to retain speared fish and mark the diver's position. Spear tips range from single barbed points to tridents and slip-tips for large pelagic fish.

Techniques

Freediving spearfishers use techniques such as ambush at the surface, diving to structure and waiting (the Mediterranean agguato), and burrowing/flashers to attract pelagic species. Shallow-water blackout is a recognised hazard of the breath-hold diving involved.

Regulation and controversy

Many jurisdictions prohibit spearfishing on scuba, restrict it to designated species and seasons, or ban it in marine reserves. Because spearfishers selectively take the largest individuals of reef species, some ecologists argue for size and species limits; supporters counter that the method's selectivity and lack of bycatch make it sustainable relative to net and line fishing.
