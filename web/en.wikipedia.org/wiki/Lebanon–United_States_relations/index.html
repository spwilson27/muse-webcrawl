Lebanon–United States relations

Lebanon–United States relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of Lebanon and the United States of America. Formal diplomatic relations date to 1944, following Lebanon's independence from the French Mandate, though American missionaries, educators, and merchants had been active in the Levant since the 19th century—the American University of Beirut was founded in 1866.

History

The United States supported Lebanon's independence and landed Marines in 1958 under the Eisenhower Doctrine at the request of President Camille Chamoun. During the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), US involvement deepened: American peacekeepers deployed with the Multinational Force in 1982–84, and the 1983 Beirut barracks bombing killed 241 US servicemen, the deadliest single-day loss for the Marine Corps since Iwo Jima.

Contemporary relations

Since the Cedar Revolution of 2005 and Syria's withdrawal, the US has provided substantial economic and military assistance, particularly to the Lebanese Armed Forces, while designating Hezbollah as a terrorist organization and imposing related sanctions. A large Lebanese-American diaspora and continuing educational ties remain central to the relationship.
