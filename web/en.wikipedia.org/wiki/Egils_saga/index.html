Egils saga

Egils saga (Old Norse: Egils saga Skalla-Grímssonar) is an Icelandic saga believed to have been written by Snorri Sturluson (1179–1241), probably between 1220 and 1240. It is one of the most celebrated of the Icelanders' sagas and tells the life story of the farmer, Viking, and skald Egill Skallagrímsson, spanning several generations of his family, from his grandfather Kveldúlfr in Norway to Egill's old age in Iceland.

Summary

The saga opens with Egill's grandfather Kveldúlfr ("Evening Wolf"), who falls foul of King Harald Fairhair of Norway. Kveldúlfr's son Þórólfr is killed through the king's suspicion, and the family emigrates to Iceland, where Skalla-Grímr settles at Borg. The main part of the saga concerns Egill, an ugly and formidable figure who composes his first poem at the age of three, kills a boy in a ball game at seven, and becomes a great Viking warrior and poet. His long feud with King Eric Bloodaxe culminates in the famous episode in which, captured in York, Egill composes the praise poem Höfuðlausn ("Head-Ransom") overnight to save his own life. The saga also contains the lament Sonatorrek ("Loss of Sons"), composed after the deaths of Egill's sons, which is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Old Norse poetry.

Themes and significance

The saga portrays the conflict between Icelandic settlers and the Norwegian monarchy, the workings of honour and feud, and the character of the poet-hero, at once violent, cunning, miserly, and capable of profound grief. Its poetry, including dozens of stanzas attributed to Egill, makes it a major source for skaldic verse.
