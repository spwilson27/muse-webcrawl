Dynamic stochastic general equilibrium

Dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (abbreviated as DSGE) modelling is a macroeconomic method often employed by monetary and fiscal authorities for policy analysis, explaining historical time-series data, and forecasting. DSGE econometric modelling applies general equilibrium theory and microeconomic principles in a tractable form to capture macroeconomic phenomena.

DSGE
Field: Macroeconomics
Related: New Keynesian economics, Real business-cycle theory

Structure

Models typically consist of households, firms, a government and a central bank. Households maximise utility, firms maximise profits, and monetary policy follows a rule such as the Taylor rule. Stochastic shocks to technology, preferences or policy drive fluctuations.

Schools

Real business-cycle models emphasising technology shocks were proposed by Finn Kydland and Edward Prescott. New Keynesian DSGE models incorporate monopolistic competition and sticky prices, following Christiano, Eichenbaum, Evans, Galí and Gertler.

Criticism

After the 2008 financial crisis critics argued DSGE models failed to predict the crisis, relied on representative agents, and neglected financial frictions. Heterogeneous-agent New Keynesian (HANK) models are a response.
