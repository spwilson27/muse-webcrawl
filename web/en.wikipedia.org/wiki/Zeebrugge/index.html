Zeebrugge (Dutch: Zeebrugge, "Bruges-on-Sea") is a village and seaport in the municipality of Bruges, in the province of West Flanders, Belgium. Situated on the North Sea coast, it serves as the outer port of Bruges, to which it is connected by the Baudouin Canal, and is one of Belgium's most important ports for roll-on/roll-off traffic, liquefied natural gas, and containerised cargo, as well as a major fishing port and seaside resort.

History

The modern port was constructed at the end of the nineteenth century, when Bruges sought to revive its medieval maritime trade. A new harbour was excavated on the coast and linked to the city by an 8-mile canal; the port was inaugurated in 1907 by King Leopold II. During the First World War the German navy used Zeebrugge and Ostend as bases for U-boats threatening Allied shipping in the English Channel.

The Zeebrugge Raid

On 23 April 1918 (St George's Day), the Royal Navy mounted the celebrated Zeebrugge Raid, an attempt to block the canal entrance by sinking concrete-filled blockships. Under heavy fire, the cruiser HMS Vindictive landed storming parties on the harbour mole while old submarines were exploded against the viaduct. The blockage was only partial and temporary, but the raid became a famous episode of British naval daring, with eight Victoria Crosses awarded.

Herald of Free Enterprise disaster

On 6 March 1987 the roll-on/roll-off ferry Herald of Free Enterprise, having sailed from Zeebrugge with her bow doors open, capsized in shallow water minutes after leaving port, with the loss of 193 lives. The disaster led to major reforms in ferry safety and corporate accountability in the United Kingdom.

The modern port

Today Zeebrugge handles tens of millions of tonnes of cargo annually. It is a principal European hub for the import and export of new cars, a landing point for liquefied natural gas at the Fluxys terminal, and a base for ferries and cruises. The village retains a fishing harbour, a marina and a sandy beach resort area.
