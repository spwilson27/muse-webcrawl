Mystras

Location: Laconia, Peloponnese, Greece
Founded: 1249
Status: Archaeological site; UNESCO World Heritage Site (1989)

Mystras or Mistras is a fortified town and archaeological site in Laconia, Peloponnese, Greece. Situated on Mt. Taygetus near Sparta, it served as the capital of the Byzantine Despotate of the Morea in the 14th and 15th centuries and became a major centre of late Byzantine learning and culture.

History

The fortress was built in 1249 by the Frankish prince William II of Villehardouin. After the Byzantines recovered the area in 1262, a town grew around the castle; from 1349 it was ruled by despots of the imperial Palaiologos family. The philosopher George Gemistos Plethon taught there, influencing the Italian Renaissance. Mystras fell to the Ottoman Turks in 1460. The last Byzantine emperor, Constantine XI Palaiologos, had been Despot of the Morea and was crowned in the cathedral of Mystras in 1449.

The site

The ruins include the castle, the palace of the despots, churches such as the Metropolis of St Demetrios, the monasteries of the Pantanassa and Peribleptos with their frescoes, mansions and houses. The Pantanassa monastery remains inhabited by nuns. Mystras was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1989.
