Diego Velázquez

Diego Rodríguez de Silva y Velázquez
Born: baptised 6 June 1599, Seville, Spain
Died: 6 August 1660, Madrid
Known for: Painting; Las Meninas
Movement: Baroque, Spanish Golden Age

Diego Velázquez (1599–1660) was a Spanish painter, the leading artist at the court of King Philip IV and a central figure of the Spanish Golden Age. He is renowned for portraits and complex court scenes rendered with striking naturalism.

Early life

Born in Seville, Velázquez trained under Francisco Pacheco. Early works — bodegones such as Old Woman Frying Eggs — combined everyday subjects with Caravaggesque chiaroscuro.

Court painter

Moving to Madrid in 1623, he became Philip IV's painter and rose through court offices. Two trips to Italy deepened his handling of light and color. Masterpieces include Las Meninas (1656), The Surrender of Breda, Rokeby Venus, and penetrating portraits of Pope Innocent X and of his assistant Juan de Pareja.

Legacy

Velázquez profoundly influenced later artists including Manet, Picasso, Dalí and Francis Bacon. Most of his major works are in the Museo del Prado, Madrid. See Las Meninas.
