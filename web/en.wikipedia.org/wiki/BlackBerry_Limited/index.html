BlackBerry Limited (formerly Research In Motion, or RIM) is a Canadian software company specializing in cybersecurity and the Internet of Things. Founded in 1984 in Waterloo, Ontario, by Mike Lazaridis and Douglas Fregin, it became famous for the BlackBerry line of smartphones, whose secure email and physical keyboards made them the dominant mobile devices for business and government in the 2000s.

Rise of the smartphone business

RIM began with wireless data technology and pagers; its two-way pager with thumb keyboard led to the first BlackBerry devices around 1999–2002, offering push email through the company's network operations centres. BlackBerry's share of the smartphone market peaked around 2009–2010 at roughly 20% globally and over 40% in the United States. The ubiquity of the devices earned them the nickname "CrackBerry."

Decline and transformation

The iPhone (2007) and Android handsets rapidly eroded BlackBerry's position, as consumers favoured touchscreens and rich app ecosystems. The BlackBerry 10 platform launched in 2013 failed to reverse the decline. Under CEO John Chen (from 2013) the company exited hardware manufacturing, licensed the BlackBerry brand for phones, and pivoted to software: enterprise mobility management, the QNX real-time operating system widely used in automotive systems, and endpoint security, strengthened by the 2019 acquisition of Cylance for US$1.4 billion.
