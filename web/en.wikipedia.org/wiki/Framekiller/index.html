Framekiller

A framekiller (or framebuster) is a technique used by websites and web applications to prevent their content from being displayed within a frame or iframe on another website. The technique is typically implemented with JavaScript that compares the current window with the topmost window; if the page is not the top-level document, the script forces navigation so that the page becomes the top-level browsing context (for example, by assigning top.location to the page's own URL).

Purpose

Websites use framekillers to prevent unauthorized framing of their content by third parties, to protect layout and advertising, and to defend against clickjacking attacks, in which a malicious site overlays invisible framed content to trick users into clicking. Site owners may also wish to ensure that users see the page in its intended context, including its own navigation and address bar.

Countermeasures and standards

Framebusting scripts can themselves be defeated by framing pages, leading to an arms race of techniques. More robust protections are provided by HTTP response headers: the older X-Frame-Options header and the frame-ancestors directive of Content Security Policy allow servers to declare whether and by whom a page may be framed, and browsers enforce these declarations regardless of scripts.
