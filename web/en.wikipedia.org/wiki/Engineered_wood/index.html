Engineered wood

Engineered wood, also called mass timber, composite wood, human-made wood or manufactured board, includes a range of derivative wood products manufactured by binding or fixing the strands, particles, fibres, veneers or boards of wood together with adhesives, or other methods of fixation, to form composite material.

Plywood, the best-known engineered wood, is built from cross-laminated veneers.

Products

Major products include plywood, oriented strand board (OSB), particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), laminated veneer lumber (LVL), glued laminated timber (glulam) and cross-laminated timber (CLT). Engineered products are designed to meet specific performance requirements, can be made in dimensions larger than any single tree, and make efficient use of fast-growing or lower-grade timber.

Advantages and concerns

Advantages include dimensional stability, consistent strength and the ability to span long distances in timber construction, which has enabled a new generation of tall wooden buildings. Concerns include the formaldehyde emissions of some resin binders, moisture sensitivity of certain boards, and end-of-life recycling.
