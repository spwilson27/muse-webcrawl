Fernand Point

Fernand Point
Born: 25 February 1897, Louhans, France
Died: 4 March 1955, Vienne, France
Occupation: Chef, restaurateur
Known for: La Pyramide; founder of modern French haute cuisine

Fernand Point (25 February 1897 – 4 March 1955) was a French chef and restaurateur widely regarded as one of the founders of modern French cuisine. At his restaurant La Pyramide in Vienne, south of Lyon, he trained a generation of chefs who would go on to define nouvelle cuisine, including Paul Bocuse, Alain Chapel, and the Troisgros brothers.

Early life

Point was born in Louhans, in the Saône-et-Loire department, into a family of hoteliers and restaurateurs. His grandmother and mother were accomplished cooks, and he began his apprenticeship at the age of ten, working in the kitchens of leading hotels on the French Riviera and in Paris, including the Hôtel Bristol.

La Pyramide

In 1923 Point took over his father's restaurant in Vienne, renaming it La Pyramide. The restaurant became one of the most celebrated in France, earning three stars in the Michelin Guide — among the first restaurants to hold the guide's highest rating. Unusually tall and heavy, Point was a commanding presence in his kitchen, known for insisting on the freshest ingredients, generous portions, and sauces built on butter and cream, exemplified by dishes such as gratin of crayfish tails in champagne sauce.

Philosophy and legacy

Point summarised his approach in the posthumously published notebook Ma Gastronomie, which recorded aphorisms such as "Butter! Give me butter! Always butter!" and his belief that a cook should begin each day as if starting anew. His protégés carried his methods across France and beyond, and La Pyramide remained a pilgrimage site for chefs long after his death in 1955. His widow, Mado Point, continued to run the restaurant until 1986.
