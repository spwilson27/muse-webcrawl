Ninety East Ridge

LocationEastern Indian Ocean
Lengthapprox. 5,000 km (3,100 mi)
OrientationRoughly along the 90°E meridian

The Ninety East Ridge (also rendered Ninetyeast Ridge) is a mid-ocean ridge on the Indian Ocean floor, running approximately 5,000 kilometres (3,100 mi) along the 90th meridian east, from the Bay of Bengal southwards toward the Southeast Indian Ridge. It is one of the longest straight features on the Earth's surface and entirely aseismic.

Formation

The ridge is interpreted as the track left by the Kerguelen hotspot as the Indian Plate moved rapidly northwards during the Late Cretaceous and early Cenozoic. Drilling by the Deep Sea Drilling Project and Ocean Drilling Program recovered basalts whose ages decrease from about 82 million years in the north to about 38 million years in the south, supporting the hotspot-track model; at the time of eruption the basalts lay at or above sea level and have since subsided into deep water.

Geology

The ridge divides the Indian Ocean into the Wharton Basin to the east and the Central Indian Basin to the west. It is offset in places by fracture zones related to changes in the direction of plate motion and was partially overprinted by the effects of the collision of India with Asia.
