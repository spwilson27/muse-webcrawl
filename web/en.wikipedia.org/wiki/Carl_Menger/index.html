Carl Menger

Carl Menger von Wolfensgrün (28 February 1840 – 26 February 1921) was an Austrian economist, founder of the Austrian school of economics and a co-originator of the marginal utility theory of value.

Born28 February 1840, Neu-Sandez, Austrian Empire (now Poland)
Died26 February 1921, Vienna
SchoolAustrian school
Notable workPrinciples of Economics (1871)

Life

Menger studied law at Prague and Vienna, worked as a journalist and in the Austrian civil service, and tutored Crown Prince Rudolf. He became professor of political economy at the University of Vienna in 1873.

Contributions

In Grundsätze der Volkswirtschaftslehre (Principles of Economics, 1871) Menger explained value subjectively: goods are valuable because they satisfy human needs, with price determined at the margin. Simultaneously but independently, William Stanley Jevons and Léon Walras made similar breakthroughs — the marginal revolution. Menger also developed an account of the spontaneous origin of money and classified goods by order. His Methodenstreit (methods dispute) with the German historical school under Gustav von Schmoller defined the Austrian school's deductive method.

Legacy

His students and successors included Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk, Friedrich von Wieser and, in the next generation, Ludwig von Mises and Friedrich Hayek. See Austrian school.
