Chimayó, New Mexico

El Santuario de Chimayó

Chimayó is a census-designated place in Rio Arriba and Santa Fe counties in the U.S. state of New Mexico. The population was about 3,000 at recent censuses. The name derives from a Tewa word for a local landmark, the hill of Tsi Mayoh. Chimayó is situated in a valley in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, roughly 28 miles north of Santa Fe.

El Santuario de Chimayó

The community is best known for El Santuario de Chimayó, a Roman Catholic church built in the early 19th century, around 1813–1816, by Bernardo Abeyta. The shrine is famous for a small room containing "holy dirt" (tierra bendita) believed by many pilgrims to have healing properties. Each year during Holy Week, tens of thousands of pilgrims walk to the sanctuary, some traveling on foot from Santa Fe or even Albuquerque, making it one of the most important Catholic pilgrimage sites in the United States. The sanctuary was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1970.

Weaving tradition

Chimayó is also renowned for its Hispanic weaving tradition, dating to the Spanish colonial period. The Ortega and Trujillo families have produced handwoven blankets, rugs, and garments in the Rio Grande style for generations, and their shops remain landmarks on the road to the sanctuary.

Chimayó chile

The area gives its name to the Chimayó chile, a landrace variety of chile pepper prized in New Mexican cuisine for its complex, earthy flavor.
