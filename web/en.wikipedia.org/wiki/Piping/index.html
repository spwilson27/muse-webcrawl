Piping

Within industry, piping is a system of pipes used to convey fluids (liquids and gases) from one location to another. The engineering discipline of piping design studies the efficient transport of fluid. Piping systems include the pipes themselves, fittings (elbows, tees, reducers), flanges, valves, supports, and instruments, and are documented in piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs).

Materials and standards

Pipes are manufactured from steel, cast iron, copper, stainless steel, and plastics such as PVC and HDPE, chosen for pressure, temperature, corrosion, and cost. Design codes such as the ASME B31 series govern process piping (B31.3), power piping (B31.1), and pipeline transportation systems. Sizes are specified by nominal pipe size (NPS) and wall thickness schedule.

Analysis

Piping engineers perform stress analysis to verify that thermal expansion, weight, pressure, and occasional loads such as water hammer and seismic events do not overstress the system, and they design flexibility — loops, bends, and expansion joints — to absorb movement. Fabricated piping is assembled in spools, welded or bolted on site, and pressure-tested before commissioning.
