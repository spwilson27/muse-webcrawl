Verse of Wilaya
SurahAl-Ma'idah (5)
Verse55
TopicWilaya (guardianship/authority)

The Verse of Wilaya (Arabic: آية الولاية) is verse 55 of Al-Ma'idah, the fifth chapter (surah) of the Quran. The verse is a central proof-text in Shia Islam for the doctrine of wilaya — the guardianship and authority of Ali ibn Abi Talib and the Imams after the Prophet Muhammad — while Sunni commentators read it more generally as concerning the believers' mutual alliance and loyalty.

"Your guardian (wali) is only Allah and His Messenger and those who believe — those who establish prayer and give zakat while they are bowing [in prayer]." — Quran 5:55

Interpretation

The Arabic word wali carries a range of meanings, including guardian, protector, master, patron and friend. Shia exegesis holds that the verse restricts true guardianship to God, the Prophet, and a specific believer identified by the description of one who gives charity while bowing in prayer. According to widely transmitted reports in both Shia and Sunni sources, the occasion of revelation was Ali ibn Abi Talib giving his ring to a beggar while in the state of ruku (bowing) during prayer. On this basis Shia theologians argue the verse designates Ali as the Prophet's successor in authority.

Sunni commentators generally interpret wali here as ally or supporter, understanding the verse as commending the believers collectively and encouraging charity, though many Sunni collections also preserve the report about Ali's ring. The debate over the verse is closely linked to other texts cited in the succession question, such as the hadith of Ghadir Khumm and the Verse of Ikmal al-Din (Quran 5:3).
