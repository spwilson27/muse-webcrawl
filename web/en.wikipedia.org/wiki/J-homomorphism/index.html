J-homomorphism

In mathematics, the J-homomorphism is a mapping from the homotopy groups of the special orthogonal groups to the homotopy groups of spheres. It was defined by Heinz Hopf in 1935, and extended by Daniel Quillen and others.

Definition

The J-homomorphism is a group homomorphism from the stable homotopy groups of the orthogonal group to the stable homotopy groups of spheres. It is constructed from the action of orthogonal transformations on spheres by suspension.

Image

The image of the J-homomorphism is a well-understood part of the stable homotopy groups of spheres, related to Bernoulli numbers and the Adams e-invariant. Frank Adams conjectured, and Quillen and Dennis Sullivan proved, a precise description of its image.
