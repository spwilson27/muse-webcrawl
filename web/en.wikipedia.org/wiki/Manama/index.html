Manama

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Manama (Arabic: al-Manāma) is the capital and largest city of Bahrain, with an approximate population of 297,000 people as of 2024, roughly a quarter of the country's population. Long an important trading centre in the Persian Gulf, Manama is home to a very diverse population.

History

Manama was mentioned in Islamic chronicles from at least 1345. It was taken by the Portuguese in 1521 and by the Persians in 1602, and has been ruled by the Al Khalifa dynasty since 1783. It was declared a free port in 1958 and became the capital of independent Bahrain in 1971.

Economy

Historically based on pearling, fishing and trade, Manama's economy shifted with the discovery of oil in Bahrain in 1932. Today the city is a major financial centre for the Gulf region, with a large banking and Islamic finance sector, aluminium and tourism also contributing. Landmarks include the Bahrain World Trade Center and the Bahrain Financial Harbour.

Geography

The city lies on the northeastern coast of Bahrain Island, on generally flat, arid terrain, with much of the modern city built on reclaimed land.
