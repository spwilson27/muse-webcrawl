The Rank Organisation was a British entertainment conglomerate founded in 1937 by J. Arthur Rank. For much of the 20th century it was the largest film company in Britain, controlling production, distribution and exhibition, and famous for its trademark of a muscular man striking a gong, filmed with athlete Bombardier Billy Wells.

Film business

Rank owned Pinewood Studios and Denham Studios, the Odeon cinema chain, and production companies behind films such as the works of Powell and Pressburger (The Archers), David Lean's Great Expectations and Oliver Twist, and the Carry On series in its early years. Its Charm School trained contract actors including Dirk Bogarde, Diana Dors and Christopher Lee.

Diversification and decline

From the 1950s Rank diversified into photocopiers through the joint venture Rank Xerox, into leisure (Rank Leisure, Butlin's-related holdings and Hard Rock Cafe interests) and other businesses, while film production declined. The film and cinema assets were sold off in the 1990s, and the remaining group was absorbed into the modern Rank Group, which focuses on gambling, in 1996.
