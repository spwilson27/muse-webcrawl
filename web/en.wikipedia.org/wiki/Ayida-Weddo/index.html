Ayida-Weddo (also spelled Ayida-Wedo, Aida-Wedo, and Ayida Wedo) is a loa (spirit) in Haitian Vodou and related African diasporic traditions, originating in the Vodun of the Fon and Yoruba peoples of West Africa. She is the rainbow serpent, consort of Damballa, the primordial serpent creator loa, and together they symbolize the unity of creation, fertility, and cosmic order.

Role and attributes

Ayida-Weddo embodies the rainbow that arches across the sky after rain, and she is associated with fertility, rain, wind, water, and the treasures of the earth. In Vodou cosmology she and Damballa are among the most ancient and pure of the loa, sometimes depicted as two serpents twined together or as a single serpent arching across the heavens. Her colours are those of the rainbow, and offerings to her include white foods, eggs, white chickens, and iridescent cloth.

Because of her primordial nature she is considered, like Damballa, to be beyond direct possession speech: in ritual, those possessed by the serpent loa may writhe on the ground like snakes rather than speak. Her Catholic counterpart in Haitian syncretism is sometimes Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception.

In culture

Ayida-Weddo appears in modern fantasy and popular culture, notably as "Aida Wedo" in works inspired by Vodou mythology, and the rainbow-serpent motif connects her to wider West African traditions of the serpent deity Da (Dan) among the Fon of Dahomey, from whose religion Haitian Vodou largely derives.
