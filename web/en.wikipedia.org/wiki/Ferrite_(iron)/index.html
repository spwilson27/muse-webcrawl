Ferrite (iron)

Crystal structureBody-centred cubic (BCC)
Symbolα-Fe, δ-Fe
Max carbon solubility0.0218 wt% at 727 °C

Ferrite, also known as α-iron (alpha iron), is a solid solution of carbon in iron with a body-centred cubic crystal structure. It is the soft, ductile, magnetic phase that forms the matrix of most mild steels and cast irons at room temperature, and its name derives from the Latin ferrum, iron.

Properties

Pure ferrite is soft and ductile, with a tensile strength of roughly 280 MPa. Its BCC lattice has few closely spaced slip pathways for carbon atoms, so it dissolves very little carbon — at most about 0.022 weight percent at 727 °C, falling to nearly zero at room temperature. Ferrite is ferromagnetic below its Curie temperature of about 770 °C. The high-temperature form, δ-ferrite, exists between 1,394 °C and iron's melting point.

Role in steels

In slowly cooled carbon steels, ferrite forms on cooling from austenite, alone or interleaved with cementite in the lamellar aggregate called pearlite. The proportion and grain size of ferrite largely determine a steel's strength and ductility, so controlling ferrite formation is central to steel heat treatment and alloy design.
