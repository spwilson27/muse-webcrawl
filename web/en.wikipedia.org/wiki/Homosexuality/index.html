Homosexuality

Homosexuality is romantic attraction, sexual attraction, or sexual behaviour between people of the same sex or gender. As a sexual orientation, it is an enduring pattern of emotional, romantic and/or sexual attraction to persons of the same sex. Men who are homosexual are typically called gay, and women lesbian.

History

Same-sex relations are documented in many ancient societies, including Greece, Rome, China and Japan, though social attitudes varied widely. The word "homosexual" was coined in 1868 by Karl-Maria Kertbeny. In the 19th and 20th centuries homosexuality was widely criminalised and classified as a mental disorder; the American Psychiatric Association removed it from the DSM in 1973.

Science

Major medical and psychological organisations hold that homosexuality is a normal variation of human sexuality. Research has found no simple single cause; biological factors (including genetics and prenatal influences) and environmental factors are thought to interact. Same-sex behaviour has been observed in over 1,500 animal species.

Society and law

Attitudes and legal status vary greatly worldwide. As of the 2020s, marriage between same-sex couples is legal in more than 35 countries, while in others same-sex activity remains criminalised, in some cases severely. The modern LGBT rights movement grew from the mid-20th century, with the 1969 Stonewall riots a landmark event.

Demographics

Surveys in Western countries typically find that between 2% and 10% of adults identify as lesbian, gay or bisexual, with younger cohorts reporting higher proportions.
