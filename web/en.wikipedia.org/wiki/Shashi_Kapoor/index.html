Shashi Kapoor

Born: Balbir Raj Kapoor, 18 March 1938, Calcutta, British India
Died: 4 December 2017, Mumbai, India
Occupation: Actor, film producer
Spouse: Jennifer Kendal (m. 1958; died 1984)

Shashi Kapoor (born Balbir Raj Kapoor; 18 March 1938 – 4 December 2017) was an Indian actor and film producer celebrated for his work in Hindi cinema and in international English-language films. The youngest son of Prithviraj Kapoor and brother of Raj Kapoor and Shammi Kapoor, he was a leading romantic star of the 1960s and 1970s and a member of the Kapoor film dynasty.

Career

After child roles and work in his father's Prithvi Theatre, Kapoor made his adult debut in Dharmputra (1961). His popular hits include Jab Jab Phool Khile (1965), Deewaar (1975) — in which his line "Mere paas maa hai" became iconic — Kabhi Kabhie (1976), Trishul (1978), Satyam Shivam Sundaram (1978) and Namak Halaal (1982). Unusually for a Bollywood star of his era, he built a parallel career in Merchant Ivory productions such as Shakespeare Wallah (1965), Bombay Talkie (1970) and Heat and Dust (1983), as well as films like Sammy and Rosie Get Laid (1987).

Producer and theatre

Through his company Film-Valas he produced acclaimed parallel-cinema works including Junoon (1978), Kalyug (1981) and 36 Chowringhee Lane (1981), and directed Ajooba (1991). With his wife Jennifer Kendal he founded Mumbai's Prithvi Theatre in 1978, fulfilling his father's dream of a permanent home for Hindi theatre.

Honours

Kapoor received three National Film Awards, the Padma Bhushan in 2011, and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award, Indian cinema's highest honour, in 2014. He died in Mumbai in 2017 at the age of 79.
