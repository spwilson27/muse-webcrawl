Locally compact group

A locally compact group is a topological group whose underlying topology is locally compact and Hausdorff. Because the group operations are continuous, local compactness at the identity element implies local compactness everywhere. Locally compact groups form the natural setting for much of modern harmonic analysis, since they carry a Haar measure.

Examples

The real numbers and the circle group under addition/multiplication are locally compact, as are all Lie groups and all discrete groups. The group of p-adic integers and the general linear group over the p-adic numbers are totally disconnected, compact or locally compact examples of importance in number theory. Matrix groups over the real or complex numbers, and adele groups, are also locally compact.

Haar measure

Every locally compact group possesses a left-invariant regular Borel measure, the Haar measure, unique up to multiplication by a positive constant (shown by Alfréd Haar in 1933 and generalised by André Weil). Left and right Haar measures may differ; the ratio is measured by the modular function, and groups for which they coincide are called unimodular. Haar measure permits integration of functions on the group, and so underlies Fourier analysis, representation theory, and the study of unitary representations of groups such as those arising in physics and number theory.

Structure theory

The solution of Hilbert's fifth problem by Gleason, Montgomery, and Zippin showed that a locally euclidean topological group is necessarily a Lie group. General locally compact groups can be approximated by Lie groups in a precise sense (the Gleason–Yamabe theorem), making them a well-behaved enlargement of the class of Lie groups.
