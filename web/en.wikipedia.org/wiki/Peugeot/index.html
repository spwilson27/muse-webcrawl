Peugeot

Peugeot (UK: /ˈpɜːʒoʊ/, US: /p(j)uːˈʒoʊ/, French: [pøʒo]) is a French automobile brand owned by Stellantis. The Peugeot family business, founded in 1810, made coffee mills and bicycles before Armand Peugeot produced the company's first steam-powered car in 1889 and petrol cars under licence from Daimler soon after. Peugeot is one of the oldest car brands in the world and is recognised by its lion emblem, first registered as a trademark in 1858.

History

The Peugeot family of Valentigney, in the Doubs, began manufacturing steel products — saw blades, springs, coffee and pepper grinders — in 1810. Bicycles followed in 1882, and Armand Peugeot exhibited a steam tricycle built with Léon Serpollet at the 1889 Paris World's Fair. The first petrol Peugeot (1890) used a Panhard-built Daimler engine. The Automobiles Peugeot company was split off from the family firm in 1896.[1]

Peugeot cars won early motor races, including the 1913 Indianapolis 500 under Jules Goux — the first European victory there. In 1976 Peugeot SA (PSA) took over Citroën, forming PSA Peugeot Citroën; it later acquired Chrysler Europe (1978) and Opel/Vauxhall from General Motors (2017). In January 2021 PSA merged with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles to form Stellantis, within which Peugeot is the group's largest-selling mainstream brand. Best-selling modern models include the 208, 2008, 308, 3008, and 508; the lion badge was redesigned as a shield emblem in 2021.

Motorsport

Peugeot Sport has won the World Rally Championship manufacturers' title five times (1985–86 with the 205 Turbo 16, 2000–02 with the 206 WRC), the Le Mans 24 Hours (1992, 1993, 2009), and the Dakar Rally multiple times.
