HOMFLY polynomial

In the mathematical field of knot theory, the HOMFLY polynomial (also called the HOMFLYPT polynomial) is a two-variable knot polynomial P(L; a, z) that generalises both the Alexander polynomial and the Jones polynomial. It is named from the initials of its independent discoverers: Jim Hoste, Adrian Ocneanu, Kenneth Millett, Peter Freyd, W. B. R. Lickorish and K. M. Yetter, together with the later independent work of Józef Przytycki and Paweł Traczyk ("PT").

Definition

The HOMFLY polynomial is defined by the skein relation a·P(L+) − a−1·P(L−) = z·P(L0), applied to triples of link diagrams differing at one crossing, together with the normalisation P(unknot) = 1. Setting a = 1 recovers the Conway form of the Alexander polynomial, while the substitution a = t−1, z = t1/2 − t−1/2 yields the Jones polynomial.

Properties

Like the Jones polynomial, the HOMFLY polynomial detects chirality in many cases (it distinguishes a knot from its mirror image more often than the Alexander polynomial), but it does not distinguish mutant knots. Its discovery, coming immediately after Vaughan Jones's 1984 breakthrough, helped launch the modern era of quantum knot invariants; it can be interpreted through the representation theory of quantum groups and, physically, through Chern–Simons theory.
