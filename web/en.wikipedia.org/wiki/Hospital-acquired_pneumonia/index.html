Hospital-acquired pneumonia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP), or nosocomial pneumonia, is pneumonia that occurs 48 hours or more after admission to a hospital and was not present at the time of admission. Ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) is the subtype that develops more than 48 hours after endotracheal intubation. HAP is one of the most common healthcare-associated infections and an important cause of mortality in hospitalised patients.

Causes

HAP is most often caused by bacteria rather than viruses. Common pathogens include aerobic Gram-negative bacilli such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Escherichia coli, and Klebsiella pneumoniae, as well as Gram-positive cocci such as Staphylococcus aureus, including methicillin-resistant strains (MRSA). Aspiration of oropharyngeal secretions colonised by these organisms is the main route by which bacteria reach the lungs.

Risk factors

Risk is increased by mechanical ventilation, prolonged hospital stay, impaired consciousness, supine positioning, nasogastric tubes, recent abdominal or thoracic surgery, and therapy that raises gastric pH. Patients in intensive care units are at greatest risk.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on a new or progressive infiltrate on chest imaging together with clinical signs such as fever, purulent secretions, leukocytosis, and worsening oxygenation, ideally supported by respiratory cultures. Treatment is empiric antibiotic therapy started promptly and chosen according to local resistance patterns and the patient's risk factors for multidrug-resistant organisms, then narrowed when culture results return.

Prevention

Preventive bundles include elevating the head of the bed, daily interruption of sedation, oral care, hand hygiene, early mobilisation, and minimising the duration of intubation.
