Early Middle Ages

Early Middle Ages
Period: c. 5th–10th century CE
Preceded by: Late antiquity
Followed by: High Middle Ages

The Early Middle Ages (or early medieval period), sometimes called the Dark Ages, was the period in European history lasting from the 5th to the 10th century, following the fall of the Western Roman Empire and preceding the High Middle Ages.

History

Population, urbanisation and trade declined in the West as Germanic kingdoms — Franks, Visigoths, Ostrogoths, Anglo-Saxons — succeeded Rome, while the Byzantine Empire persisted in the East. The spread of Islam from the 7th century reshaped the Mediterranean. Under Charlemagne, crowned emperor in 800, the Carolingian Empire briefly united much of western Europe and sponsored a cultural revival.

Society and culture

Monasticism preserved learning; feudal relationships and manorial agriculture developed, though historians debate their timing. Viking, Magyar and Saracen raids marked the 9th–10th centuries. Modern scholarship rejects a simple 'dark age', stressing continuity and regional diversity. See also Middle Ages and Carolingian Empire.
