Benedetto "Bettino" Craxi (24 February 1934 – 19 January 2000) was an Italian politician and statesman, leader of the Italian Socialist Party (PSI) from 1976 to 1993 and Prime Minister of Italy from 1983 to 1987 — the first Socialist to hold the office and one of the longest-serving postwar premiers.

Prime Minister

Craxi's governments pursued economic stabilisation, cutting inflation and curbing the automatic wage-indexation system (scala mobile). In foreign policy he asserted Italian independence, most famously in the 1985 Sigonella crisis, when he refused to hand over Palestinian hijackers of the Achille Lauro to the United States.

Downfall

The Tangentopoli corruption investigations of the early 1990s engulfed the PSI; Craxi was convicted in absentia of corruption and illegal party financing. Facing arrest, he fled to Tunisia in 1994 and died in exile in Hammamet in 2000. His legacy remains deeply contested in Italian politics.
