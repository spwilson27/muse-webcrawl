The Avenue des Champs-Élysées is a famous avenue in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, running 1.9 kilometres between the Place de la Concorde and the Place Charles de Gaulle, where the Arc de Triomphe stands. Its name refers to the Elysian Fields, the paradise for dead heroes in Greek mythology.

History

The avenue began as fields and market gardens until Marie de' Medici extended the Tuileries gardens' axis in 1616. Landscape architect André Le Nôtre lengthened the promenade in 1667. It became fashionable in the 19th century and was reshaped during Haussmann's renovation of Paris with wide pavements, gas lamps and chestnut trees.

Events and landmarks

The Champs-Élysées hosts the annual Bastille Day military parade, the largest regular military parade in Western Europe, and is the traditional finishing line of the Tour de France. It witnessed celebrations at the Liberation of Paris in 1944 and France's World Cup victories. Landmarks include the Grand Palais, the Petit Palais, the Théâtre Marigny and luxury flagship stores; the lower part of the avenue passes through gardens while the upper part is lined with shops, cinemas and cafés.

Reputation

Long described as "the most beautiful avenue in the world", it is one of the most expensive strips of real estate in Europe and a symbol of Parisian elegance and national celebration.
