Northampton is the county seat of Hampshire County, Massachusetts, United States. Situated on the western bank of the Connecticut River in the Pioneer Valley, the city is known for its vibrant downtown, its cultural life, and as the home of Smith College, one of the largest women's colleges in the country. It is nicknamed "Paradise City", a name popularised in the 19th century by the Swedish singer Jenny Lind, who called it "the Paradise of America".

History

The area was inhabited by the Pocumtuc people, who called the place Norwottuck, meaning "in the midst of the river". English settlers purchased the land in 1653 and established the town in 1654, naming it after Northampton in England. The preacher Jonathan Edwards, a central figure of the First Great Awakening, served the town's church from 1727 to 1750.

In the 19th century Northampton was a centre of abolitionist and utopian activity. The Northampton Association of Education and Industry, a commune founded in 1842, attracted members including the abolitionist Sojourner Truth, who lived in the Florence section of the city. Former U.S. president Calvin Coolidge served as the city's mayor and later kept his law practice and home there.

Education

Smith College, founded in 1871, is the city's largest employer and anchors its cultural life, with the Smith College Museum of Art and the Botanic Garden of Smith College. The city is also part of the Five College Consortium with Amherst College, Hampshire College, Mount Holyoke College and the University of Massachusetts Amherst.

Culture

Northampton is noted for its music venues, including the Iron Horse Music Hall and the Calvin Theatre, its many independent shops and restaurants, and large annual events such as the Paradise City Arts Festival. The city has a long-standing reputation as a centre of LGBTQ+ life in New England.
