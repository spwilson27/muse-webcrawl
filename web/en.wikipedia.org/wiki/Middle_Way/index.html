Middle Way

Middle Way
Pali: Majjhimāpaṭipadā
Tradition: Buddhism
First taught: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta

The Middle Way (Pali: Majjhimāpaṭipadā) is a central concept in Buddhism describing the path between the extremes of sensual indulgence and self-mortification. According to tradition, the Buddha discovered it after abandoning years of severe asceticism, and it was the subject of his first sermon at Sarnath.

Meaning

In the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, the Middle Way is identified with the Noble Eightfold Path, which "gives vision, gives knowledge, and leads to calm, to insight, to awakening". In Mahayana philosophy, particularly the Madhyamaka school of Nagarjuna, "middle way" also names the doctrine that things are empty of inherent existence, avoiding the extremes of eternalism and nihilism.

Related uses

The term has been adopted more broadly, including in politics — for example the 14th Dalai Lama's "Middle Way Approach" seeking genuine autonomy for Tibet within China.
