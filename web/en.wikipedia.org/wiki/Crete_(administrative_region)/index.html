Crete (administrative region)

Crete is the largest island of Greece and one of its thirteen administrative regions

Crete (Greek: Κρήτη, Kríti) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece and coextensive with the island of Crete, the largest and most populous Greek island, and the fifth largest island in the Mediterranean Sea. The regional capital is Heraklion. The region includes the island of Crete and a number of surrounding islets, among them Gavdos, the southernmost point of Europe.

Geography

Crete stretches about 260 km from east to west and is dominated by three mountain ranges: the White Mountains (Lefka Ori), Mount Ida (Psiloritis), and the Dikti Mountains. The island's south coast is rugged, cut by gorges such as the Samaria Gorge, while the north coast holds the main cities: Heraklion, Chania, Rethymno, and Agios Nikolaos. The Mesara plain in the south is a major agricultural area.

History

Crete was the centre of the Minoan civilisation (c. 3000–1100 BC), Europe's earliest advanced civilisation, whose palaces at Knossos, Phaistos, and Malia are major archaeological sites. The island subsequently passed through Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, and Venetian rule — the Venetian period (1204–1669) ended with the Ottoman conquest in the Cretan War. After a period as an autonomous Cretan State under international oversight from 1898, Crete united with Greece in 1913. During the Second World War it was the scene of the Battle of Crete in 1941.

Administration and economy

The region is divided into four regional units corresponding to the former prefectures. Tourism and agriculture — olive oil, wine, cheese, and greenhouse vegetables — dominate the economy. Crete has international airports at Heraklion and Chania and major ports linking it to Piraeus and the Aegean islands.
