Byzantine Greek

Era: c. 330–1453 AD
Region: Eastern Mediterranean
Family: Hellenic, Attic–Ionic
Developed into: Modern Greek

Byzantine Greek, also known as Medieval Greek, is the stage of the Greek language spoken in the Byzantine Empire between late antiquity and the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople in 1453. It developed from Koine Greek and in turn gave rise to Modern Greek.

Characteristics

Byzantine Greek existed in a state of diglossia: an archaizing written language modelled on Attic Greek was used for literature and administration, while the spoken vernacular steadily lost features such as the dative case, the optative mood and vowel-length distinctions, and developed a stress accent replacing the ancient pitch accent.

Literature

The language is attested in chronicles, hagiography, legal texts and vernacular romances such as the epic of Digenes Akritas.
