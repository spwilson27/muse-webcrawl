61 Cygni

ConstellationCygnus
Distance11.4 light-years
Spectral typeK5 V + K7 V
Proper motionAbout 5 arcseconds per year

61 Cygni is a binary star system in the constellation Cygnus, consisting of a pair of K-type dwarf stars orbiting each other with a period of about 659 years. It lies about 11.4 light-years from the Sun and is just visible to the naked eye from dark locations.

Historical significance

The system is famous for its very large proper motion — Giuseppe Piazzi dubbed it the "Flying Star" in 1804. In 1838 Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel measured its parallax, obtaining the first reliable distance to a star other than the Sun, a landmark result establishing the scale of the stellar universe.

Planetary claims

Twentieth-century astrometric studies claimed several unseen companions around the stars, but these were later attributed to observational error. Both stars are magnetically active flare stars, and the system remains a benchmark for stellar distance measurements.
