House Lannister is a fictional great house in George R. R. Martin's fantasy series A Song of Ice and Fire and its television adaptation Game of Thrones. The Lannisters rule the Westerlands from Casterly Rock and are among the richest and most powerful families of the Seven Kingdoms of Westeros, their wealth derived from the gold mines of their lands. Their official motto is "Hear Me Roar!", though they are better known by the unofficial saying "A Lannister always pays his debts."

Background

House Lannister descends, in legend, from Lann the Clever, a trickster who supposedly winkled Casterly Rock from House Casterly. Historically the Lannisters were kings of the Rock until they submitted to Aegon the Conqueror. In the generation before the novels, Lord Tywin Lannister rebuilt the house's prestige after the weak rule of his father Tytos, ruthlessly destroying the rebellious Houses Reyne and Tarbeck — an event immortalised in the song "The Rains of Castamere".

In the narrative

Tywin's children are Queen Cersei, wife of King Robert Baratheon; her twin brother Jaime, the "Kingslayer", a knight of the Kingsguard; and Tyrion, a dwarf of sharp wit despised by his father. The secret incest between Cersei and Jaime — and the true parentage of Cersei's children — triggers the War of the Five Kings after Robert's death. Lannister characters are among the series' most prominent point-of-view figures, notable for their moral ambiguity.

Reception

The Lannisters drew wide acclaim in the television adaptation, with Peter Dinklage's Tyrion winning multiple Emmy Awards. Critics have compared the family to the historical Borgias and Lancaster dynasty.
