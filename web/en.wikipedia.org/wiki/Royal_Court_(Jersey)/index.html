Royal Court (Jersey)

The Royal Court is the principal court of the Bailiwick of Jersey, a Crown Dependency in the Channel Islands. It sits in the Royal Court House on the Royal Square in St Helier. The court has a history stretching back to the medieval period, and its procedure reflects Jersey's unique legal system, based substantially on Norman customary law.

Composition

The Royal Court is presided over by the Bailiff or Deputy Bailiff of Jersey. In its most historic form, the court consists of the Bailiff sitting with twelve Jurats, elected lay members who act as judges of fact. Since the late 20th century many matters are heard by a single judge (the Bailiff or a Commissioner) sitting alone, or with Jurats in cases involving questions of fact, damages or criminal sentencing. Commissioners are appointed to assist with the civil workload.

Divisions and jurisdiction

The court sits in several divisions. The Samedi Division handles much of the ordinary civil business; the Héritage Division deals with matters concerning real property (immovable property); the Family Division handles matrimonial and family matters; and probate functions are exercised by the Probate Division. The Royal Court also exercises criminal jurisdiction over serious offences, sitting with Jurats as the Inferior Number (Bailiff plus two Jurats) or Superior Number (Bailiff plus five or more Jurats). Youth and lesser criminal matters fall to the Magistrate's Court.

History

The institution derives from the court of the Duke of Normandy's officers in the islands. The office of Bailiff dates from the 13th century, and the Jurats are recorded from the same period. Following constitutional reforms in 2005 (the Clive Committee reforms), the Bailiff's role as president of the States Assembly and civic head was retained, while the judicial function remained centred on the Royal Court. Judgments of the Royal Court may be appealed to the Court of Appeal of Jersey and ultimately to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London.
