Steve Hart

Stephen Hart (13 February 1859 – 28 June 1880) was an Australian bushranger, a member of the Kelly gang led by Ned Kelly.

Born13 February 1859, Wangaratta, Victoria, Australia
Died28 June 1880 (aged 21), Glenrowan, Victoria, Australia
OccupationBushranger
Known forMember of the Kelly gang

Life

Hart was born in Wangaratta, Victoria, to Irish immigrant parents. A skilled horseman, he worked as a jockey and was convicted of horse stealing in his teens, serving time in Beechworth Gaol. After his release he associated with Ned and Dan Kelly and Joe Byrne, and when the four were outlawed following the Stringybark Creek killings of three policemen in October 1878, Hart became the fourth member of the Kelly gang.

Hart took part in the gang's celebrated raids on Euroa (December 1878) and Jerilderie (February 1879), where the gang held up banks and, at Jerilderie, Ned Kelly dictated his famous letter. Known as the most flamboyant of the gang, Hart was reported to have ridden about in a gold sash and to have disguised himself in women's clothing during the gang's years on the run.

Death

On 28 June 1880 Hart died, along with Dan Kelly and Joe Byrne, during the gang's last stand at Glenrowan, where the gang had taken hostages at the Glenrowan Inn while wearing homemade suits of plate armour. The inn was set on fire by police; Hart's charred body was recovered from the ruins. Ned Kelly, wounded and captured, was hanged in Melbourne later that year.
