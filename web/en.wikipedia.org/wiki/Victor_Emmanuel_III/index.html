Victor Emmanuel III

Victor Emmanuel III (1869–1947) was King of Italy from 1900 until his abdication in May 1946. His nearly 46-year reign encompassed the First and Second World Wars, the rise of Fascism under Mussolini, and Italy's colonial wars.

Reign

Succeeding his assassinated father, he supported Italy's entry into WWI in 1915. In 1922, fearing civil war, he refused to declare a state of siege against Mussolini's March on Rome and appointed him prime minister — a decision that enabled two decades of Fascist rule. He accepted the imperial titles of Emperor of Ethiopia (1936) and King of Albania (1939).

Downfall

In July 1943 he dismissed and arrested Mussolini, appointing Pietro Badoglio. After the September 1943 armistice he fled Rome for Brindisi. Tainted by his Fascist association, he abdicated in favour of his son Umberto II in 1946, weeks before the referendum that abolished the monarchy; he died in exile in Egypt.
