Astrakhan Khanate

The khanate controlled the Volga delta and its fisheries and trade routes.

The Astrakhan Khanate was a Tatar state that emerged from the disintegration of the Golden Horde in the 1460s, centred on the city of Khadzhi-Tarkhan near the mouth of the Volga, close to modern Astrakhan. It was the smallest and weakest of the successor khanates, which also included Kazan, Crimea and Sibir.

History

The khanate was founded around 1466 by Mahmud bin Küchük of the Great Horde. Its economy rested on the Volga–Caspian trade, salt, and the rich sturgeon and caviar fisheries of the delta, but it lacked the agricultural base and manpower of its neighbours and was repeatedly dominated by the Nogai Horde and the Crimean Khanate, whose candidates often occupied its throne.

Russian conquest

After conquering Kazan in 1552, Ivan IV of Russia moved against Astrakhan. A Russian expedition installed a client khan, Dervish Ali, in 1554; when he turned against Moscow, Russian forces returned in 1556, deposed him and annexed the khanate outright. The conquest gave Russia control of the entire Volga and direct trade access to the Caspian Sea and Persia.
