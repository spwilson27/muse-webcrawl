Folkwang Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Museum Folkwang is a major art museum in Essen, Germany, holding collections of 19th- and 20th-century painting and sculpture, photography, prints, and ancient and non-European art. Its name derives from Folkvangr, the meadow of the Norse goddess Freyja, and it has long been regarded as one of Germany's leading modern art museums.

History

The museum was founded by the art patron Karl Ernst Osthaus, who opened his collection in Hagen in 1902 and made it a centre of the European avant-garde, acquiring works by Van Gogh, Gauguin, Matisse, Kirchner and other modernists when they were still controversial. After Osthaus's death in 1921, the collection was sold to the city of Essen, where it merged with the municipal art museum and opened as the Museum Folkwang in 1929.

Nazi period and postwar rebuilding

In 1937 the Nazi regime confiscated more than 1,400 works from the museum as "degenerate art", devastating the collection. The building was destroyed in wartime bombing. After the war the museum was rebuilt and systematically re-acquired modern art; a new building opened in 1960.

Collection and building

Holdings include French and German modernism — Cézanne, Monet, Renoir, Kandinsky, Marc, Beckmann and Rothko among many others — together with an internationally important photographic collection (established as a department in 1978) and antiquities. In 2010, for Essen's year as European Capital of Culture, a new building designed by David Chipperfield opened on the old site, integrating the surviving 1960 structure with light-filled glass courtyards.
