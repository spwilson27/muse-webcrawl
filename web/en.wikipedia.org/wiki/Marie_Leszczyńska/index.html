Marie Leszczyńska (Polish: Maria Karolina Zofia Felicja Leszczyńska; 23 June 1703 – 24 June 1768) was Queen of France as the wife of King Louis XV. The daughter of the deposed Polish king Stanisław Leszczyński, she reigned as queen consort for nearly 43 years — the longest tenure of any queen of France — though she exercised little political influence after the early years of the marriage.

Early life and marriage

Marie was born at Trzebnica in Silesia during her father's turbulent career: Stanisław had been placed on the Polish throne by Charles XII of Sweden in 1704 and deposed in 1709, after which the family lived in exile in Sweden, the Palatinate, and finally Wissembourg in Alsace, in modest circumstances. When the engagement of the young Louis XV to the Spanish infanta Mariana Victoria was broken off in 1725, Marie — seven years older than the king and chosen partly because she was old enough to bear children immediately and because her match carried no awkward political entanglements — was selected from a list of candidates. They married by proxy at Strasbourg in August 1725 and in person at Fontainebleau on 5 September 1725.

Life as queen

The marriage was initially a success: Marie bore ten children between 1727 and 1737, including the dauphin Louis. Pious, charitable and fond of music and painting, she maintained a cultured circle at Versailles and supported Polish exiles and musicians. After the birth of their last child, and as Louis XV took a succession of official mistresses — most famously Madame de Pompadour and Madame du Barry — the queen withdrew into private devotion and family life, conducting herself with widely respected dignity. Her father, made Duke of Lorraine for life in 1737, kept close ties with her court at Lunéville.

Death

Marie died at Versailles on 24 June 1768, a day after her 65th birthday, and was buried at Saint-Denis. Her grandson became Louis XVI.
