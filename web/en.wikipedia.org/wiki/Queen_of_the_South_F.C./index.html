Queen of the South Football Club is a Scottish professional football club based in Dumfries, Dumfries and Galloway. Founded in 1919, the club takes its name from the nickname given to Dumfries by the poet David Dunbar, who described the town as the "Queen of the South". Its supporters and players are known as the Doonhamers, from the local name for people from Dumfries. The club plays home matches at Palmerston Park.

History

Queen of the South was formed in March 1919 by the merger of several local sides, including the 5th Kirkcudbrightshire Rifle Volunteers team. The club joined the Scottish Football League in 1923 and won promotion to the top division in 1933. The club's golden era came in the 1950s under manager Jimmy McKinnell, when Queens regularly finished in the upper half of the First Division and reached the Scottish Cup semi-finals; goalkeeper Roy Henderson and inside-forward Bobby Black were among the era's notable players, and Ivor Broadis also played for the club.

2008 Scottish Cup run

The club's most celebrated modern achievement came in 2008, when, as a second-tier side, Queens reached the Scottish Cup Final for the first time. In the semi-final they beat Aberdeen 4–3 in a match widely regarded as a classic. In the final at Hampden Park they lost 3–2 to Rangers after rallying from two goals down. The run qualified the club for the UEFA Cup—the only European campaign in its history—where it was narrowly eliminated by Danish side FC Nordsjælland.

Later years and honours

Queens won the Scottish Challenge Cup in 2002–03 and again in 2012–13, the latter season also bringing the Second Division championship as part of a league and cup double. The club celebrated its centenary in 2019. Notable former players include all-time top scorer Jim Patterson and Hughie Gallacher, who began his senior career in Dumfries before fame with Newcastle United.
