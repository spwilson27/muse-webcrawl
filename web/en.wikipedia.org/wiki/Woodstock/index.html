The Woodstock Music and Art Fair, commonly referred to simply as Woodstock, was a music festival held from August 15 to 18, 1969, on Max Yasgur's dairy farm in Bethel, New York, about 95 kilometres southwest of the town of Woodstock. Billed as "An Aquarian Exposition: 3 Days of Peace & Music", it attracted more than 400,000 people and became a defining moment of the 1960s counterculture.

Background and organisation

The festival was created by John Roberts, Joel Rosenman, Artie Kornfeld and Michael Lang, financed through their company Woodstock Ventures. After the town of Wallkill revoked their permit in July, dairy farmer Max Yasgur offered his 600-acre farm in Bethel. Tickets cost $18 in advance, but fences and ticket booths were never completed and the event became effectively free; roads clogged for miles, and Governor Nelson Rockefeller considered sending the National Guard before supplies were airlifted in by Army helicopters.

The music

Thirty-two acts performed through rain and mud. Richie Havens opened on Friday; Saturday ran from Quill through Santana, Creedence Clearwater Revival, Janis Joplin, The Who and Jefferson Airplane into Sunday morning. Sunday featured Joe Cocker's famous set and a storm delay, and Jimi Hendrix closed on Monday morning before a much-reduced crowd with his celebrated rendition of "The Star-Spangled Banner". Other performers included Joan Baez, The Band, Blood, Sweat & Tears, Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young, Ravi Shankar and Sly and the Family Stone.

Legacy

Despite shortages of food, water and sanitation, the festival remained famously peaceful — "three days of peace and music" with remarkably little violence. The 1970 documentary film Woodstock won an Academy Award and fixed the event's place in cultural memory. Anniversary festivals followed in 1979, 1989, 1994 and the ill-fated Woodstock '99, while a planned 50th-anniversary event in 2019 was cancelled. The original site is now home to the Bethel Woods Center for the Arts and a museum, and "Woodstock" endures as shorthand for the idealism of the 1960s generation.
