The Eurasian beaver (Castor fiber) is a species of beaver widespread across Europe and northern Asia. It is the largest living rodent in Eurasia, typically weighing 11–30 kg, and one of only two extant beaver species, the other being the North American beaver (Castor canadensis), from which it differs in skull shape, fur and chromosome number (48 versus 40).

Behaviour and ecology

Eurasian beavers are semi-aquatic, nocturnal herbivores living in family groups. They fell trees with continuously growing incisors to build dams and lodges, creating ponds and wetlands that support rich biodiversity—a classic keystone and ecosystem-engineering role. They communicate and scent-mark with castoreum, and store branches underwater as winter food caches.

Decline and recovery

Hunted for fur, meat and castoreum, the species was reduced by about 1900 to roughly 1,200 animals in eight isolated relict populations. Twentieth-century protection and extensive reintroductions across Europe—including, controversially and later officially, to Britain—restored it to well over a million animals. It was assessed as Least Concern in 2016, though conflicts over flooding of farmland and forestry require active management.
