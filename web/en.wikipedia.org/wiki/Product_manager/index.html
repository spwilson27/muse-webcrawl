Product manager

A product manager (PM) is a professional role responsible for the strategy, roadmap and feature definition of a product or product line. Often described as the "voice of the customer" within a company, the product manager sits at the intersection of business, technology and user experience, deciding what should be built and why, while designers and engineers determine how.

Responsibilities

Typical duties include market and competitor research, gathering and prioritising customer requirements, defining a product vision and roadmap, writing product requirements and user stories, setting pricing and positioning (in some organisations), coordinating launches with marketing and sales, and analysing product metrics. The role has responsibility without direct authority over the engineering team in most structures.

History

The role traces to Procter & Gamble's 1931 "brand man" memo by Neil McElroy, which proposed dedicated managers for each brand. In technology, the modern product management function developed at companies such as Microsoft (where PMs originated in the 1980s) and was popularised in Silicon Valley; related roles include programme manager, product owner in Scrum (a distinct, team-level role), and growth product manager. Product marketing management focuses more on outbound positioning and go-to-market activities.
