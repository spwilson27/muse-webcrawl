Law of Brazil

The law of Brazil is based on the civil law tradition, deriving primarily from Portuguese law, itself influenced by Roman law, the Napoleonic Code, and German legal scholarship. The supreme law is the Federal Constitution, promulgated on October 5, 1988, following the end of military rule. It establishes Brazil as a federal presidential republic with separation of powers among the executive, legislative, and judicial branches.

Structure

Brazil's legal system is codified. Major codes include the Civil Code (2002), the Penal Code (1940), the Code of Civil Procedure (2015), and the Code of Criminal Procedure (1941), supplemented by special legislation such as the Consumer Defence Code (1990) and the Statute of the Child and Adolescent. The Union legislates on most private and criminal law; states and municipalities have limited supplementary competence.

Judiciary

The judiciary is headed by the Supreme Federal Court (Supremo Tribunal Federal), which guards the Constitution, and the Superior Court of Justice, which harmonises federal law. Specialised branches include labour, electoral, and military courts. The Public Prosecutor's Office (Ministério Público) is an independent institution charged with defending the legal order and collective rights. Legal education and admission to practice are regulated by the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB), which administers a national bar examination.
