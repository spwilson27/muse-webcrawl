Pemmican War

The Red River Colony, focus of the Pemmican War.

The Pemmican War was a series of violent confrontations between the Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) and the North West Company (NWC) in the Red River Colony and the Athabasca country between roughly 1812 and 1820. The conflict, driven by competition in the fur trade and by the struggle over pemmican—the dried buffalo-meat provision essential to the fur brigades—culminated in the Battle of Seven Oaks in 1816 and ended with the forced merger of the two companies in 1821.

Background

In 1811 the Earl of Selkirk, a major HBC shareholder, obtained a grant of the District of Assiniboia and founded the Red River Settlement in 1812. The colony sat astride the NWC's supply routes and in the heart of the country where the Métis and First Nations hunters produced pemmican for the fur trade. In January 1814 the colony's governor, Miles Macdonell, issued the Pemmican Proclamation, banning the export of pemmican and other provisions from Assiniboia in order to feed the settlers. The Nor'Westers and their Métis allies saw the ban as a direct threat to their trade.

Escalation and Seven Oaks

Harassment, arrests, and seizures of provisions followed on both sides, and in 1815 most settlers were driven out before being restored by Colin Robertson. On 19 June 1816, a party of Métis led by Cuthbert Grant clashed with Governor Robert Semple and his men at Seven Oaks; Semple and about twenty of his party were killed, against one or two Métis dead. The killings—described by one side as a battle and the other as a massacre—became the defining episode of the war.

Aftermath

Selkirk seized the NWC headquarters at Fort William in 1816, prosecutions followed in the Canadian courts with mixed results, and fighting and seizures continued in the Athabasca region. The costly rivalry finally led the British government to compel a union of the companies in 1821, after which the merged Hudson's Bay Company held a monopoly over Rupert's Land.
