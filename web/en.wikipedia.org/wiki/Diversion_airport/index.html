Diversion airport

Diversion airport
SynonymAlternate airport; alternate aerodrome
Used whenWeather, medical emergency, technical problems, destination closure
RequirementsSuitable runway, weather above minima, handling capability

A diversion airport, also called an alternate airport or alternate aerodrome, is an airport to which a flight diverts when it cannot — or should not — land at its intended destination. Diversions occur because of bad weather at the destination, medical emergencies on board, mechanical problems, security incidents, unruly passengers, or runway closures.

Planning

Flight plans designate one or more alternates, chosen for suitable weather (forecast above prescribed minima at the estimated time of arrival), adequate runway length and rescue facilities, and, for passenger flights, ground-handling arrangements. Regulations require aircraft to carry enough fuel to reach the destination, fly onward to the alternate, and still hold a final reserve. For long overwater routes, ETOPS planning additionally requires that an adequate diversion airport always be within a specified flying time on one engine.

Examples

Remote airports can acquire outsized roles as diversion fields: Gander International Airport in Newfoundland famously hosted 38 diverted airliners and about 6,600 passengers during Operation Yellow Ribbon on 11 September 2001, while airports such as Shannon, Keflavík, Terceira, and Anchorage serve as traditional North Atlantic and Pacific alternates.
