Magdeburg rights

Magdeburg rights (German: Magdeburger Recht) were a set of town privileges first developed by Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor (936–973), and based on the Flemish law of merchants. Named after the city of Magdeburg, they became one of the most influential systems of municipal law in Central and Eastern Europe.

Content

The law granted towns self-government under their own councils, regulated property, trade, inheritance and criminal matters, and limited the interference of feudal lords. Merchants' disputes could be judged according to the customs of trade rather than local arbitrary power.

Spread

Magdeburg law was adopted — often in local variants such as the Środa Śląska (Neumarkt) and Chełmno (Kulm) laws — by hundreds of towns across modern Germany, Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, Czechia and Slovakia, including Kraków, Vilnius and Kyiv.
