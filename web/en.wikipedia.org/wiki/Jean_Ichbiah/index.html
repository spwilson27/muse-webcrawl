Jean Ichbiah

Jean David Ichbiah
Born: 25 March 1940, Paris, France
Died: 26 January 2007 (aged 66), Boston, Massachusetts, US
Known for: Chief designer of the Ada programming language

Jean David Ichbiah (25 March 1940 – 26 January 2007) was a French computer scientist best known as the chief designer of the Ada programming language, developed for the United States Department of Defense in the late 1970s.

Career

Ichbiah worked at CII (Compagnie internationale pour l'informatique) and its successor companies in France, where his team at CII-Honeywell Bull won the international competition to design a common high-order language for the US armed services. The resulting language, named Ada after Ada Lovelace, was standardised in 1980 and became Ada 83. Ichbiah later founded the company Alsys, which produced Ada compilers and development tools, and after its acquisition by Thomson-CSF he continued in technology ventures in the United States, including work on keyboard and text-input technology at Textware Solutions.

Ada

Ada was designed for reliability in large, long-lived embedded systems, with strong typing, modularity through packages, exception handling and facilities for concurrent tasks. Ichbiah's design, codenamed "Green", was selected in 1979 over competing proposals. He received the Legion of Honour and other distinctions for the work.
