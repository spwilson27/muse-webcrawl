Calvin Klein

Calvin Richard Klein (born November 19, 1942) is an American fashion designer who launched the company that later became Calvin Klein Inc. in 1968. The brand became famous for minimalist design, designer jeans, underwear and provocative advertising.

Early life

Klein was born in the Bronx, New York, to a Jewish family. He attended the High School of Art and Design and the Fashion Institute of Technology, then worked in cloak-and-suit houses before designing his own line.

Career

In 1968, with childhood friend Barry K. Schwartz, Klein founded Calvin Klein Limited, a coat shop in the York Hotel. Orders from Bonwit Teller and coverage in Vogue followed. In the 1970s his jeans — with the CK logo on the back pocket — helped create the designer-jeans boom; controversial ads featuring Brooke Shields in 1980 made the brand notorious.

In the 1990s, stark black-and-white underwear and fragrance campaigns (Obsession, Eternity, CK One) with models such as Mark Wahlberg and Kate Moss defined the brand's image. Klein sold the company to Phillips-Van Heusen (PVH) in 2003.

Personal life and legacy

Klein's minimalist aesthetic — clean lines, neutral colours, modern tailoring — influenced American sportswear. He has received multiple Coty and CFDA awards.
