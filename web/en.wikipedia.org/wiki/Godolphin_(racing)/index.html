Godolphin (racing)

Godolphin is the global thoroughbred horse racing and breeding operation founded by Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, ruler of Dubai. Named after the Godolphin Arabian, one of the three foundation sires of the Thoroughbred breed, the stable was established in 1992 and is among the most successful in the history of the sport, with runners campaigning in the famous royal blue silks across Europe, North America, Australia, Japan and the Middle East.

History

Godolphin's first major international success came in 1994, when Balanchine won the Oaks at Epsom and the Irish Derby. The following year Lammtarra, trained by Saeed bin Suroor, won the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes and the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe. Dubai Millennium, winner of the 2000 Dubai World Cup, became a flagship horse and influential sire. Other outstanding runners in the blue silks have included Daylami, Fantastic Light, Street Cry, Dubawi, Thunder Snow — the only horse to win the Dubai World Cup twice (2018 and 2019) — and in later years stars such as Pinatubo, Modern Games, Rebel's Romance and Notable Speech.

Organisation

The operation winters in Dubai, where the climate allows horses to be trained through the European off-season, before shipping them to campaigns around the world. Principal trainers have included Saeed bin Suroor and Charlie Appleby in Britain and Dubai, and James Cummings in Australia. Godolphin's breeding arm, Darley, stands stallions at studs in Ireland, England, Kentucky, Australia and Japan. Godolphin has been champion owner at the British flat racing championships many times and has won hundreds of Group 1 races worldwide, including Classics in Britain, France and Ireland and the Melbourne Cup (with Cross Counter in 2018).

Controversies

In 2013 the stable was embroiled in a doping scandal when trainer Mahmood Al Zarooni was banned for eight years after anabolic steroids were found in horses at the Moulton Paddocks yard in Newmarket; the British Horseracing Authority investigation led to reforms in Godolphin's management and Godolphin's horses were temporarily locked out of racing in Britain.
