Asset and liability management

Asset and liability management (often shortened to ALM) is the practice by which financial institutions — principally banks, insurance companies and pension funds — manage the risks arising from mismatches between their assets and liabilities. It addresses liquidity risk, interest-rate risk in the banking book, and currency risk, aiming to keep the institution solvent and able to meet obligations as they fall due.

Techniques

Core tools include gap analysis of maturities and repricing dates, duration and convexity measures of interest-rate sensitivity, cash-flow matching and dedication strategies, scenario analysis and stress testing, and earnings-at-risk or economic-value-of-equity models. Derivatives such as interest-rate swaps are used to hedge residual exposures. Governance typically rests with an asset-liability committee (ALCO).

Regulation

Regulatory frameworks including Basel III require banks to hold sufficient liquid assets (the Liquidity Coverage Ratio) and stable funding (the Net Stable Funding Ratio), embedding ALM within prudential supervision. Failures of ALM — notably maturity transformation with long-term fixed-rate assets funded by short-term deposits — contributed to crises such as the US savings-and-loan collapse of the 1980s and the 2023 failure of Silicon Valley Bank.
