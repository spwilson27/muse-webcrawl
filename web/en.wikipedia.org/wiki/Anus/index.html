Anus

The anus is the external opening at the end of the digestive tract through which faeces leave the body. In humans it is located at the end of the anal canal, which follows the rectum, and its opening and closing are controlled by two sphincter muscles: the internal anal sphincter, under involuntary control, and the external anal sphincter, under voluntary control.

Anatomy and function

The anal canal is about 2.5 to 4 cm long in adults. During defecation, intra-abdominal pressure rises and the sphincters relax to allow the passage of faeces. The anus is lined partly by skin-like tissue containing numerous sensory nerve endings. Anal columns and the dentate line mark the transition between the lining of the rectum and that of the anal canal.

Development and other animals

In embryonic development, animals are divided into protostomes, in which the first opening formed becomes the mouth, and deuterostomes — including humans — in which it becomes the anus. Most bilaterian animals possess an anus; some groups, such as certain parasites and cnidarians, lack one entirely. Conditions affecting the human anus include haemorrhoids, anal fissures, fistulas and anal cancer.
