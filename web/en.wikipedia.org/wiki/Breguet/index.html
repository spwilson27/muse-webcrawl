Breguet

Breguet is a Swiss manufacturer of luxury watches, clocks, and high-jewellery pieces, founded in Paris in 1775 by the Neuchâtel-born watchmaker Abraham-Louis Breguet (1747–1823). Among the oldest continuously operating watch brands in the world, it is celebrated for inventions including the tourbillon, the gong spring for repeating watches, and the first wristwatch. Breguet has been part of the Swatch Group since 1999, with manufacturing in L'Abbaye, in Switzerland's Vallée de Joux.

Abraham-Louis Breguet

Breguet established his workshop on the Quai de l'Horloge in Paris and quickly won royal patronage. His innovations included the self-winding perpétuelle watches (c. 1780), the gong spring (1783), the pare-chute shock-protection system (1790), the tourbillon escapement (patented 1801), and the Breguet overcoil hairspring. He created the first wristwatch in 1810–1812 for Caroline Murat, Queen of Naples, and the celebrated No. 160 "Marie-Antoinette" pocket watch, commissioned in 1783 and completed after his death, is considered among the most complicated and storied timepieces ever made.

House style

Breguet watches are identified by engine-turned (guilloché) dials, coin-edge cases, blued open-tipped "Breguet hands", Roman numerals, and secret signatures. The brand's archives — a register of every watch sold since the late 18th century — survive and are used to authenticate historic pieces.

Later history

The company passed through the founder's family and, in the 20th century, produced noted aviation chronographs such as the Type XX for the French Navy. After the quartz crisis it was revived as a high-end mechanical brand by the Chaumet group, then by Investcorp, before its acquisition by the Swatch Group in 1999 under Nicolas G. Hayek, who championed traditional hand-finishing. Breguet also became a major patron of classical music and of the restoration of monuments including parts of Versailles.
