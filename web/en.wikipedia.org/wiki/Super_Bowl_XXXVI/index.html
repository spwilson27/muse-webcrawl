Super Bowl XXXVI

Super Bowl XXXVI was an American football game played on February 3, 2002, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans, to determine the champion of the National Football League (NFL) for the 2001 season. The American Football Conference (AFC) champion New England Patriots defeated the National Football Conference (NFC) champion St. Louis Rams, 20–17, in one of the greatest upsets in Super Bowl history.

Background

The Rams, nicknamed "The Greatest Show on Turf," entered with a 14–2 record and the league's highest-scoring offense. New England had lost starting quarterback Drew Bledsoe to injury early in the season; second-year backup Tom Brady kept the job and led the Patriots to an 11–5 record. The game, originally scheduled for January 27, was pushed back a week after the September 11 attacks reshaped the NFL schedule, and it carried a heavily patriotic theme.

Game summary

New England's defense disrupted the Rams' timing all game. Ty Law returned a Kurt Warner interception 47 yards for a touchdown, and the Patriots led 17–3 entering the fourth quarter. Warner rallied St. Louis with two late touchdowns to tie the game at 17 with 1:30 remaining. With no timeouts left, Brady drove New England 53 yards in the final minute — prompting commentator John Madden to marvel on air — and Adam Vinatieri kicked a 48-yard field goal as time expired. Brady was named Super Bowl MVP, beginning the Patriots dynasty.

Entertainment

Mariah Carey sang the national anthem, and U2 performed the halftime show, scrolling the names of the victims of the September 11 attacks on a backdrop during "Where the Streets Have No Name" — a performance frequently ranked among the greatest in Super Bowl history.
