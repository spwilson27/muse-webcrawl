Provinces of Finland


The provinces of Finland

The provinces of Finland (Finnish: läänit, singular lääni; Swedish: län) were the administrative divisions of Finland from their modern establishment until 31 December 2009, when they were abolished as part of a major reform of regional administration. The provinces were replaced by regional state administrative agencies and the system of regions (maakunta).

History

The provincial division has its roots in the period of Swedish rule. Between 1831 and 1867, the Russian Grand Duchy of Finland was divided into eight provinces. In 1947 and 1960, Lapland Province and Central Finland Province were separated from their parent provinces. A major reform in 1997 reduced the number of provinces from twelve to six, but the reform was widely criticized and in 2009 the government decided to abolish the provinces entirely.

Administration

Each province was headed by a governor (maaherra) appointed by the President of Finland. Provincial state offices administered functions such as education, health, social services and emergency services. The autonomous province of Åland had its own government and parliament, the Lagting.

After abolition, most provincial functions were transferred to the Regional State Administrative Agencies (AVI) and the Centres for Economic Development, Transport and the Environment (ELY Centres) from 1 January 2010.
