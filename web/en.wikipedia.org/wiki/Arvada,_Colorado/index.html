Arvada is a home rule municipality in Jefferson and Adams counties in Colorado, United States, and a suburb northwest of Denver. The population was 124,402 at the 2020 census, making it one of the larger cities in the Denver metropolitan area.

History

The area was settled during the Colorado gold rush era; gold was found in Ralston Creek in 1850, and farming followed as irrigation ditches were dug. The town was named for Hiram Arvada Haskin, a relative of an early settler, and was incorporated in 1904. Arvada grew rapidly after the Second World War as a residential suburb.

Community

Olde Town Arvada, the historic downtown, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and hosts shops and festivals. The Arvada Center for the Arts and Humanities is one of the largest multidisciplinary arts centres in the region. The G Line commuter rail connects Arvada with downtown Denver.
