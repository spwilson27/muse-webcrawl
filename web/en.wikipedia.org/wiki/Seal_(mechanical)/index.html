Seal (mechanical)


Function: Prevent leakage between components
Common types: Gaskets, O-rings, mechanical seals, labyrinth seals

A seal is a device that helps join systems or mechanisms together by preventing leakage (for example in a pumping system), containing pressure, or excluding contamination. A static seal, such as a gasket or O-ring, sits between two surfaces that do not move relative to one another, while a dynamic seal separates surfaces in relative motion, such as a rotating shaft passing through a pump casing. The seal is usually a flat material, a shaped ring, or an engineered assembly; when a seal occupies a groove it may be termed packing.

Types

  • Gasket — a shaped sheet or ring placed between two flanges to fill irregularities under compression.
  • O-ring — a torus-shaped elastomer loop seated in a groove, used in both static and dynamic duties.
  • Mechanical seal — a precision assembly of lapped rotating and stationary faces used on pump and compressor shafts.
  • Labyrinth seal — a non-contacting maze of fins used on turbines and gas compressors.
  • Lip seal — a flexible lip riding on a shaft, common in automotive and hydraulic applications.
  • Hermetic seal — a fused or welded airtight closure, as in glass-to-metal seals.

Seal performance depends on materials compatibility, surface finish, pressure, temperature, and installation; seal failure can cause everything from minor fluid loss to catastrophic equipment damage, making seals a critical element of mechanical design and maintenance.
