Tactical ballistic missile

A tactical ballistic missile (TBM), also called a battlefield range ballistic missile (BRBM), is a ballistic missile designed for short-range battlefield use, typically with a range of roughly 150 to 300 kilometres. Tactical ballistic missiles are usually mobile and carry conventional high-explosive warheads, though some types were designed to deliver chemical or nuclear warheads.

Characteristics

TBMs fill the gap between conventional rocket artillery and longer-range theatre ballistic missiles. They are intended to strike targets immediately behind enemy lines, such as command posts, supply dumps, airfields, and troop concentrations. Because of their relatively low cost per round and the absence of a pilot, they can be used for massed strikes, though their accuracy varies greatly by type.

Examples

Well-known types include the Soviet/Russian OTR-21 Tochka and Iskander, the American MGM-140 ATACMS fired from MLRS and HIMARS launchers, and various Scud-derived systems exported widely during and after the Cold War.
