The People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN; Vietnamese: Quân đội nhân dân Việt Nam) is the military force of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam and the armed wing of the ruling Communist Party of Vietnam. The PAVN is part of the People's Armed Forces and includes the Ground Force, the Navy, the Air Force, the Border Guard, the Coast Guard and the Cyber Operations Command.

History

The PAVN was founded on 22 December 1944 as the Armed Propaganda Unit for National Liberation, a 34-soldier unit led by Võ Nguyên Giáp under the direction of Hồ Chí Minh. It grew during the First Indochina War, defeating French Union forces at the Battle of Điện Biên Phủ in 1954.

During the Vietnam War, the PAVN fought against South Vietnam and the United States and its allies, operating both conventional forces and supporting operations along the Hồ Chí Minh trail. After the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, Vietnam was reunified. The PAVN later fought in the Cambodian–Vietnamese War (1978–1989) and the Sino-Vietnamese War of 1979 against China.

Organisation

The Central Military Commission of the Communist Party is the highest party organ on military matters, while the Ministry of National Defence is the governmental organ of command. The PAVN is organised into military regions and corps, and maintains large reserve and militia forces. Since the 1990s it has also become heavily involved in economic activities, running enterprises such as the telecommunications group Viettel.

Doctrine

Vietnamese defence policy rests on the "people's war" doctrine and the so-called "three nos": no military alliances, no foreign military bases on Vietnamese territory, and no reliance on one country against another.
