The Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC) is a collegiate athletic conference in the United States, a member of NCAA Division I, with football in the Football Bowl Subdivision. Founded in 1953 in Greensboro, North Carolina, by seven universities formerly in the Southern Conference, it expanded repeatedly.

Members

Long-standing members include Duke, North Carolina, NC State, Wake Forest, Clemson, Georgia Tech, Virginia and Florida State. Later additions included Miami, Virginia Tech, Boston College, Pittsburgh, Syracuse, Louisville and Notre Dame (in most sports). In 2024, California, Stanford and SMU joined, extending the conference coast-to-coast despite its name.

Sports and reputation

The ACC is especially renowned for men's basketball, with Duke and North Carolina among the sport's most successful programmes, and has won national championships in football, notably Clemson and Florida State.
