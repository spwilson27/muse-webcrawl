Compact executive car

[Image: BMW 3 Series, a typical compact executive car]

A compact executive car, also known as a compact luxury car, is a premium car larger than a premium compact and smaller than an executive car. It is roughly equivalent to the D-segment in the European vehicle classification system, and to the "compact" size class for luxury vehicles in North America.

The segment is dominated by German models — the BMW 3 Series, Mercedes-Benz C-Class and Audi A4/A5 — which have defined the class since the 1970s and 1980s. Other entrants include the Jaguar XE, Alfa Romeo Giulia, Volvo S60, Lexus IS, Infiniti Q50, Acura TLX, Cadillac CT4 and Tesla Model 3. Cars in the class are usually offered as four-door saloons, with estate (wagon), coupé and convertible variants, rear-wheel drive or all-wheel drive layouts, and an emphasis on handling, refinement and interior quality.

History

Early compact luxury cars included the Lancia Beta and the BMW 02 series; the BMW 3 Series, launched in 1975, established the template of a compact, driver-focused premium saloon. The Mercedes-Benz 190E (W201) of 1982 was developed at great cost to compete in the segment, and Audi's 80/A4 line completed the German trio. From the 2010s, electrified entrants such as the Tesla Model 3 and Polestar 2 reshaped the class, and compact executive crossover SUVs based on the same platforms became increasingly popular.
