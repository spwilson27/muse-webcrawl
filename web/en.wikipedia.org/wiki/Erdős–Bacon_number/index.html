Erdős–Bacon number

A person's Erdős–Bacon number is the sum of their Erdős number — the collaboration distance from mathematician Paul Erdős in academic publications — and their Bacon number — the acting-collaboration distance from actor Kevin Bacon in films. The combined number is a playful measure of how small the worlds of mathematics and cinema can be: a low Erdős–Bacon number requires a person to have both published mathematical research and acted in films with short chains to both namesakes.

Definition

By convention, Erdős himself has Erdős number 0 and Kevin Bacon has Bacon number 0. A person who has co-authored a paper with a co-author of Erdős has Erdős number 2, and so on; the same rule applies to film co-appearances for the Bacon number. The Erdős–Bacon number is simply the sum of the two.

Notable holders

Very few people have a finite Erdős–Bacon number, because it demands a genuine foothold in both academia and professional acting. Among the lowest known are mathematician and actor Danica McKellar, and the physicist-actor Brian Greene is often cited in discussions of the combined metric. The mathematician Daniel Kleitman achieved a notably low sum (3), having appeared in Good Will Hunting, for which he was also a mathematics consultant. Erdős himself acted in a documentary and is sometimes credited with a Bacon number, giving him a finite combined number as well.

Related concepts

The Erdős–Bacon number belongs to a family of "small world" measures popularised by the six degrees of separation idea. Extensions include the Erdős–Bacon–Sabbath number, which adds the music-collaboration distance from the band Black Sabbath.
