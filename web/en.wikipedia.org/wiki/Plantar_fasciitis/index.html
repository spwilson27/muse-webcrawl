Plantar fasciitis

Plantar fasciitis is a disorder of the plantar fascia, which is the connective tissue that supports the arch of the foot. It results in pain in the heel and bottom of the foot that is usually most severe with the first steps of the day or following a period of rest. Pain is also frequently brought on by bending the foot and toes up towards the shin.

Causes and risk factors

The cause of plantar fasciitis is poorly understood. Risk factors include overuse such as from long periods of standing, an increase in exercise, obesity, and inward rolling of the foot. It is associated with running and is one of the most common causes of heel pain, affecting roughly 10% of people at some point in their lives.

Treatment

About 90% of cases resolve with time and conservative treatment, including rest, stretching exercises, orthotics, and medication for pain. In persistent cases, extracorporeal shockwave therapy or surgery may be considered.
