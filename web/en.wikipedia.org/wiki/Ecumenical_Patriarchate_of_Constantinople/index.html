Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople

Ecumenical Patriarchate
Seat: Fener, Istanbul, Turkey
Current Patriarch: Bartholomew I (since 1991)
Tradition: Eastern Orthodox

The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople is one of the fifteen to seventeen autocephalous churches of Eastern Orthodox Christianity, traditionally founded by Saint Andrew. Its head, the Ecumenical Patriarch, is considered primus inter pares among Orthodox bishops.

History

Constantinople, capital of the Byzantine Empire, rose in ecclesiastical rank after 381 and 451, rivalling Rome. After the East–West Schism of 1054 and the Ottoman conquest of 1453, the Patriarchate continued under Ottoman rule, with wide responsibilities for Orthodox Christians. Since 1923 it has been based in Istanbul under Turkish law, now with a much-reduced local flock but global diaspora jurisdiction.

Role today

Patriarch Bartholomew I is noted for interfaith dialogue and environmental advocacy. Disputes include the 2019 grant of autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, which led Moscow to break communion. See also Eastern Orthodox Church.
