Ludwig Alois Friedrich Ritter von Köchel (1800–1877) was an Austrian musicologist, writer, composer, botanist, and publisher, best known for compiling the first comprehensive chronological catalogue of the works of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, published in 1862. The "K." numbers from his catalogue remain the standard way of identifying Mozart's compositions.

Life

Köchel studied law in Vienna and became tutor to the sons of Archduke Charles of Austria, a post that brought him a knighthood and financial independence. He travelled widely, pursuing botanical and geological research in North Africa and Europe, and published works on the flora of the Danube region and on mineralogy.

The Mozart catalogue

Devoting his later years to Mozart, Köchel assembled the Chronologisch-thematisches Verzeichnis sämtlicher Tonwerke Wolfgang Amade Mozarts (1862), listing 626 works in chronological order — hence designations such as K. 626 for the Requiem. Later editions revised the numbering (notably the sixth edition of 1964), but the original K. numbers remain in common use. Köchel also funded an early complete edition of Mozart's works published by Breitkopf & Härtel.
