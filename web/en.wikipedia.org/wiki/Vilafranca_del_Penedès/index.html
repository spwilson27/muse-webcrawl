Vilafranca del Penedès is a municipality in the province of Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain. It is the capital of the comarca of Alt Penedès. The town lies in the Penedès wine-growing region, about 50 kilometres southwest of Barcelona, and is historically associated with the trade in wine and the production of cava.

Castellers performing during the Festa Major

History

The town developed in the 12th century on the Via Augusta road as a market settlement on the frontier of Christian Catalonia. Its medieval churches, notably the basilica of Santa Maria, and palaces such as the Palau Reial reflect its early importance. In the modern era its fortunes were tied to viticulture; the phylloxera crisis of the late 19th century hit the region hard before recovery through replanting on resistant rootstock.

Culture

Vilafranca is famous in Catalonia for its castellers — the tradition of building human towers. The local team, the Castellers de Vilafranca, founded in 1948, is among the most successful colles, renowned for completing the most difficult tower structures. The town's Festa Major, held around the feast of Saint Felix (30 August), is considered one of the most traditional festivals in Catalonia and features castells, folk dances and processions. The town also hosts a wine museum, VINSEUM, devoted to the culture of wine in the Penedès.
