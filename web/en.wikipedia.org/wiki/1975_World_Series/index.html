The 1975 World Series was the championship series of Major League Baseball's 1975 season, played between the National League champion Cincinnati Reds — the "Big Red Machine" — and the American League champion Boston Red Sox. The Reds won in seven games, capturing their first World Series title since 1940. The series is widely ranked among the greatest ever played.

Game 6

The series is immortalised by Game 6 at Fenway Park, considered one of the finest baseball games ever played. Boston, facing elimination, rallied from a 6–3 deficit on Bernie Carbo's pinch-hit three-run homer in the eighth inning, and in the twelfth inning catcher Carlton Fisk hit a towering drive off the left-field foul pole — waving his arms to keep the ball fair in one of the most replayed images in sports history — for a 7–6 Red Sox victory that forced a deciding game.

Game 7 and outcome

In Game 7, Boston led 3–0 before Cincinnati rallied; Joe Morgan's two-out RBI single in the ninth inning gave the Reds a 4–3 lead they held to win the championship. Series MVP Pete Rose batted .370. Boston's defeat extended the Red Sox' championship drought, often attributed to the "Curse of the Bambino", which lasted until 2004.

Legacy

The dramatic series, broadcast nationally, is credited with reviving popular interest in baseball in the United States and influencing television coverage of the sport — Game 6's reaction shots of Fisk pioneered techniques that became standard in sports broadcasting.
