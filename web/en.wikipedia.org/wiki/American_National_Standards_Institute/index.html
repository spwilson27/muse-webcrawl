The American National Standards Institute (ANSI) is a private nonprofit organisation that oversees the development of voluntary consensus standards for products, services, processes, systems and personnel in the United States. ANSI also coordinates U.S. standards with international standards so that American products can be used worldwide, and it is the official U.S. representative to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).

History

ANSI was founded in 1918 as the American Engineering Standards Committee by five engineering societies and three government agencies, to stop the proliferation of incompatible factory standards. It was reorganised as the American Standards Association in 1928, became the United States of America Standards Institute in 1966, and took its present name in 1969.

Role

ANSI does not itself write standards. Instead it accredits standards-developing organisations (such as ASME, ASTM International, IEEE and UL) whose procedures meet ANSI's requirements for openness, balance, consensus and due process; standards approved under these rules are designated American National Standards. Well over 10,000 ANSI-accredited standards are in force, covering everything from screw threads and safety colours to the ANSI C programming language standard (X3J11) and paper sizes. ANSI also accredits conformity-assessment bodies and operates, with ASQ, the ANSI-ASQ National Accreditation Board.
