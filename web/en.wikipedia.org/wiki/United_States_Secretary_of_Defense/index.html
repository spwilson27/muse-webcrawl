United States Secretary of Defense

SecDef
Reports to: President of the United States
Appointed by: President, with Senate advice and consent
First holder: James Forrestal (1947)

The United States Secretary of Defense (SecDef) is the head of the United States Department of Defense, the executive department of the U.S. Armed Forces, and is a high-ranking member of the federal cabinet. The secretary is the principal assistant to the president in all matters relating to the Department of Defense and exercises authority, direction, and control over the department.

Role

Subject to the direction of the president, the secretary has authority over the military departments and the combatant commands, whose commanders report through the secretary to the president in the operational chain of command. The secretary is sixth in the presidential line of succession.

History

The position was created by the National Security Act of 1947, which unified the armed services under the National Military Establishment, renamed the Department of Defense in 1949. James Forrestal, previously Secretary of the Navy, was the first secretary. Notable holders include George C. Marshall, Robert McNamara, Donald Rumsfeld (who served under two presidents), Robert Gates, and Lloyd Austin.

Eligibility

By law the secretary must be a civilian who has not been on active duty as a commissioned officer within the preceding seven years; Congress has occasionally waived this requirement by statute, as for George Marshall, James Mattis, and Lloyd Austin.
