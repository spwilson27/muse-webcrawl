Bailey Whitfield "Whit" Diffie (born 5 June 1944) is an American cryptographer and one of the pioneers of public-key cryptography, along with Martin Hellman and Ralph Merkle. With Hellman he published the landmark 1976 paper "New Directions in Cryptography", which introduced the concept of public-key cryptography and the Diffie–Hellman key exchange protocol, allowing two parties to establish a shared secret over an insecure channel.

Early life and career

Diffie studied mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, graduating in 1965, and worked at the MITRE Corporation and as a graduate student at Stanford, where he met Martin Hellman. Their work at Stanford in the mid-1970s laid the foundations of modern cryptography and brought them into conflict with the U.S. National Security Agency over export controls and the proposed weakening of the Data Encryption Standard's key length, which Diffie publicly criticised.

Diffie later worked at Northern Telecom and, from 1991, at Sun Microsystems, where he was a distinguished engineer and chief security officer. He has been a prominent advocate for privacy and against government restrictions on civilian cryptography, testifying before the U.S. Congress during the "Crypto Wars" of the 1990s. In 2010 he joined the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) as vice president for information security.

Honours

Diffie shared the 2015 ACM Turing Award with Martin Hellman "for fundamental contributions to modern cryptography". He is also a foreign member of the Royal Society and has received the Marconi Prize.
