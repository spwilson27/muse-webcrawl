Total variation denoising

Total variation denoising, also known as total variation regularization or the ROF model, is a technique in signal and image processing for removing noise while preserving sharp edges. It was introduced by Leonid Rudin, Stanley Osher, and Emad Fatemi in 1992.

Formulation

Given a noisy signal, the method seeks a reconstruction u that minimises an energy combining a data-fidelity term (the squared difference from the observed signal) with the total variation of u — the integral of the magnitude of its gradient — weighted by a regularization parameter λ. Unlike smoothing filters, which penalise the squared gradient and blur edges, the L1-type total variation penalty tolerates large jumps at isolated points, so piecewise-smooth signals are recovered with their discontinuities intact. In one dimension the related taut-string and pool-adjacent-violators algorithms give exact, fast solutions; in two dimensions iterative algorithms such as Chambolle's projection method are used.

Applications

Total variation methods are used in image restoration, medical imaging, compressed sensing and seismic processing. A known artifact is "staircasing", in which smooth ramps are approximated by flat steps; higher-order variants address this.
