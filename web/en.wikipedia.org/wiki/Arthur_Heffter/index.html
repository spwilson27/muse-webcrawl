Arthur Heffter (1871–1925)

Arthur Heffter (15 June 1871 – 20 July 1925) was a German chemist and pharmacologist, remembered as the first chairman of the German Pharmacological Society and as a pioneer in the study of plant alkaloids and psychedelic compounds. He is often called the father of German experimental pharmacology.

Mescaline

Heffter's most famous work concerned the peyote cactus (Lophophora williamsii). In 1897, through systematic fractionation and self-experimentation, he isolated the principal psychoactive alkaloid of peyote and named it mescaline. His Heffter technique of bioassay-guided isolation — testing fractions on himself to track the active principle — became a model for natural-product pharmacology.

Career

Heffter studied chemistry at Freiburg and Leipzig, worked under Rudolf Boehm, and became professor of pharmacology at Leipzig in 1908 and in Berlin in 1917. He edited the influential Handbuch der experimentellen Pharmakologie (Handbook of Experimental Pharmacology), founded in 1920, which long remained a standard reference.
