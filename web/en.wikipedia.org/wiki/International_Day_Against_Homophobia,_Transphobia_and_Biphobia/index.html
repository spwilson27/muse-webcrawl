International Day Against Homophobia, Transphobia and Biphobia

The day is marked annually on 17 May in more than 130 countries.

The International Day Against Homophobia, Biphobia and Transphobia (IDAHOBIT) is observed on 17 May to coordinate international events that raise awareness of LGBT rights violations and stimulate interest in LGBT rights work worldwide.

History

The date of 17 May was chosen to commemorate the World Health Organization's decision, on 17 May 1990, to remove homosexuality from the International Classification of Diseases. The day was founded in 2004 by the French academic and activist Louis-Georges Tin, initially as the International Day Against Homophobia (IDAHO). The first observance took place on 17 May 2005, with events in around 40 countries. Transphobia was added to the name in 2009, and biphobia in 2015, reflecting the day's widening scope.

Observance

By the 2010s, the day was being marked in more than 130 countries, including many where homosexuality remains criminalized. Events range from rallies, marches, and cultural festivals to reports on the state of LGBT rights, school programmes, and statements by governments and international bodies. Several governments and the European Parliament have officially recognized the day. Commemorations frequently focus on an annual theme, developed by an international committee together with local organizations.
