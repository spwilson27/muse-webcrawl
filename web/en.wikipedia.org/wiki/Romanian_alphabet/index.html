Romanian alphabet

The Romanian alphabet is a modification of the Latin alphabet used for writing the Romanian language. It consists of 31 letters: the 26 letters of the basic Latin alphabet plus five modified letters bearing diacritics — Ă, Â, Î, Ș and Ț.

Diacritics

  • Ă ă — represents the schwa /ə/.
  • Â â and Î î — both represent the close central unrounded vowel /ɨ/; Â is used inside words and Î at the beginning or end, following the 1993 orthographic reform.
  • Ș ș — s with comma below, representing /ʃ/ ("sh").
  • Ț ț — t with comma below, representing /ts/.

The comma-below forms of Ș and Ț are the correct characters; cedilla forms (Ş, Ţ) were long used as substitutes because of limited font and encoding support, particularly in older versions of Windows.

History

Romanian was originally written in a Romanian Cyrillic alphabet. A transitional alphabet mixing Cyrillic and Latin letters was used in the mid-19th century before the Latin-based alphabet was officially adopted in 1860 in Wallachia and 1862 in Moldavia. Spelling reforms followed in 1904, 1932, 1953 — when â was abolished in favour of î under Soviet influence — and 1993, when â was restored within words.

Foreign letters

K, Q, W and Y occur only in foreign names and recent loanwords, but are counted as part of the alphabet for official purposes.
