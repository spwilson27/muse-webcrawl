Max Berg (17 April 1870 – 22 January 1947) was a German architect and urban planner, best known as the designer of the Centennial Hall (Jahrhunderthalle) in Breslau (now Wrocław, Poland), one of the pioneering works of modern architecture in reinforced concrete.

Career

Berg studied architecture at the Technische Hochschule in Charlottenburg (Berlin) under Carl Schäfer, an advocate of Gothic revival. After work in Frankfurt am Main, where he contributed to the design of the Festhalle, he became city building councillor (Stadtbaurat) of Breslau in 1909, a post he held until 1925.

Centennial Hall

The Centennial Hall, built in 1911–1913 to commemorate the centenary of the Battle of Leipzig and to host the Centennial Exhibition, was at the time the largest reinforced-concrete structure in the world: its ribbed dome spans 65 metres, exceeding the Pantheon in Rome. Berg conceived it as a "cathedral of democracy", a great communal hall for the modern city. In 2006 the hall was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Berg's other Breslau works include schools, the Messehof exhibition buildings and contributions to the 1913 garden suburb projects.

Urban planning

As city architect, Berg produced bold plans for Breslau's expansion, including a 1920s competition scheme proposing high-rise office towers in the city centre — remarkably early examples of skyscraper planning in Germany.
