UNITA

The National Union for the Total Independence of Angola (Portuguese: União Nacional para a Independência Total de Angola), better known by its acronym UNITA, is an Angolan political party and former armed movement. Founded in 1966 by Jonas Savimbi, UNITA fought against Portuguese colonial rule and then, after independence in 1975, against the MPLA government in the long Angolan Civil War (1975–2002).

History

Savimbi, who had earlier been foreign minister of the FNLA-aligned revolutionary government in exile, broke away to found UNITA in Angola's central highlands, drawing support principally from the Ovimbundu people, the country's largest ethnic group. After Portuguese rule ended, the Alvor Agreement collapsed and civil war broke out between the MPLA — backed by the Soviet Union and Cuba — and UNITA, supported by the United States and apartheid South Africa. UNITA financed its insurgency substantially through diamonds from territories under its control ("blood diamonds"), prompting UN sanctions in the 1990s.

From war to opposition politics

Peace accords at Bicesse (1991) and Lusaka (1994) both broke down, and war resumed after UNITA rejected the results of the 1992 elections. The conflict ended only after Savimbi was killed by government troops in February 2002; a ceasefire followed within weeks. UNITA then demilitarised and reconstituted itself as a civilian political party under leaders including Isaías Samakuva and, from 2019, Adalberto Costa Júnior. It remains the principal opposition party in the National Assembly, and in 2022 its electoral alliance won its strongest-ever share of the vote, though the MPLA retained power amid disputed results.
