Nakuru is a city in the Rift Valley region of Kenya, lying about 160 km (100 mi) northwest of Nairobi. It is the capital and largest city of Nakuru County and one of Kenya's fastest-growing urban centres; in 2021 it was elevated to city status, becoming Kenya's fourth city after Nairobi, Mombasa, and Kisumu. The name is derived from a Maasai word meaning "dusty" or "smoky place".

Lake Nakuru, at the city's edge, is famed for the flamingos that feed on its algae.

History

Nakuru was founded in 1904 as a station on the Uganda Railway and grew as an agricultural service centre for the surrounding settler farming region, the "White Highlands". After Kenyan independence in 1963 it expanded rapidly as an administrative and commercial hub. The city experienced serious violence during the post-election crisis of 2007–08. Rapid growth has brought infrastructure strain, and since 2020 rising Rift Valley lake levels have flooded parts of the Lake Nakuru shoreline.

Geography and attractions

The city sits at about 1,850 m above sea level on the floor of the Great Rift Valley. Its best-known attraction is Lake Nakuru National Park, a UNESCO-listed site famous for lesser and greater flamingos and for its sanctuary for black and white rhinoceroses. Nearby sites include the Menengai Crater, one of the world's largest volcanic calderas, and the Hyrax Hill prehistoric site.

Economy

Nakuru is a centre for agriculture — dairy, wheat, maize, pyrethrum, and floriculture — along with food processing, manufacturing, and tourism. It is a major transport node on the northern corridor highway and the standard-gauge and metre-gauge railways.
