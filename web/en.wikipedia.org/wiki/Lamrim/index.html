Lamrim

Lamrim (Tibetan: lam-rim, 'stages of the path') is a Tibetan Buddhist textual genre presenting the stages in the complete path to enlightenment as taught by the Buddha, arranged as a graduated course of meditation.

TraditionTibetan Buddhism
OriginAtisha, 11th century
Key textTsongkhapa's Lamrim Chenmo

History

The genre derives from Atisha's 11th-century A Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment (Bodhipathapradipa), which organised the teachings for practitioners of small, medium and great capacity. Gampopa and later Je Tsongkhapa developed the form; Tsongkhapa's Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path (Lamrim Chenmo, 1402) became the definitive version in the Gelug school, and lamrim texts exist in all Tibetan schools.

Content

Typical lamrim outlines reliance on a spiritual teacher, the preciousness and impermanence of human life, death, karma, refuge, the sufferings of samsara, bodhicitta, the six perfections, calm abiding (shamatha) and special insight (vipashyana) into emptiness. Lamrim remains the basis of systematic study and retreat in Tibetan Buddhism worldwide.
