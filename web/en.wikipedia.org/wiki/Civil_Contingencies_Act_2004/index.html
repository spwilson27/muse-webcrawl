Civil Contingencies Act 2004

The Civil Contingencies Act 2004 (c. 36) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that establishes a legislative framework for civil protection in the United Kingdom. It places duties on local responders to assess risks and plan for emergencies, and confers wide emergency regulation-making powers on the government.

Part 1: Local arrangements

Part 1 defines "Category 1 responders" (such as local authorities, police, fire and ambulance services, and NHS bodies) and "Category 2 responders" (such as utilities and transport operators), requiring risk assessment, emergency planning, business continuity management, and public communication.

Part 2: Emergency powers

Part 2 allows senior ministers to make emergency regulations if an emergency has occurred or is about to occur that threatens serious damage to human welfare, the environment, or national security. Regulations lapse after seven days unless approved by Parliament, and cannot amend the Human Rights Act 1998 or Part 2 of the Act itself.
