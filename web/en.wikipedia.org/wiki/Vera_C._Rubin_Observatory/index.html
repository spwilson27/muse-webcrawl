Vera C. Rubin Observatory

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory, formerly known as the Large Synoptic Survey Telescope (LSST), is an astronomical observatory on Cerro Pachón in northern Chile. It houses an 8.4-metre wide-field survey telescope with the largest digital camera ever built for astronomy — a 3.2-gigapixel camera roughly the size of a small car. The observatory is named for astronomer Vera Rubin, whose work on galaxy rotation curves provided key evidence for dark matter.

Legacy Survey of Space and Time

Its flagship project is the ten-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), which will image the entire visible southern sky every few nights, accumulating tens of petabytes of data and cataloguing tens of billions of stars and galaxies. The survey will track near-Earth asteroids, supernovae, variable stars and other transient phenomena, and probe dark matter and dark energy through weak gravitational lensing and large-scale structure.

History

The project was the top-ranked ground-based initiative in the United States' 2010 decadal survey of astronomy. Construction began in 2015, first images were released in 2025, and the full survey is scheduled to run for a decade. Data alerts for changing objects are issued publicly within minutes of each observation.
