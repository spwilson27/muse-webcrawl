Galvanic series

Galvanic series
Subject: Electrochemistry, corrosion
Medium commonly used: Seawater
Order: Most noble (cathodic) to most active (anodic)

The galvanic series (or electropotential series) determines the nobility of metals and semi-metals. When two metals are submerged in an electrolyte, while also electrically connected by some external conductor, the less noble (base) metal will experience galvanic corrosion. The rate of corrosion is determined by the electrolyte, the difference in nobility, and the relative areas of the anode and cathode.


Galvanic series in seawater

Galvanic series in seawater

The following is the galvanic series for stagnant (that is, low-oxygen) seawater. The surface of most metals will have an oxide film which behaves differently, so the ordering can shift in other environments.

PositionMetal
Most noble (cathodic)Platinum, gold, graphite, titanium, silver
Stainless steels (passive), nickel alloys, copper, brass, bronze
Tin, lead, carbon steel, iron
Aluminium alloys, cadmium
Least noble (anodic)Zinc, magnesium and its alloys

Use

Engineers use the galvanic series to select compatible metals in marine and plumbing applications, to design cathodic protection systems, and to predict which member of a bimetallic couple will corrode preferentially. Metals close together in the series generally can be joined with low risk of galvanic corrosion.
