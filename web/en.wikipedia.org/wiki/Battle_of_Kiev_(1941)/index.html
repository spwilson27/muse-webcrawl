Battle of Kiev (1941)

The Battle of Kiev of 1941, often called the Kiev encirclement, was fought from late August to 26 September 1941 during Operation Barbarossa, the German invasion of the Soviet Union. German panzer groups closed a vast pocket east of Kiev (Kyiv), encircling and destroying the bulk of the Soviet Southwestern Front — one of the largest encirclements in military history.

Background

In August 1941, Adolf Hitler ordered Army Group Centre's panzer forces diverted south to destroy the Soviet forces in Ukraine rather than continuing directly on Moscow. Guderian's Panzer Group 2 drove south from the Gomel area while Kleist's Panzer Group 1 broke out of its Dnieper bridgehead and drove north; the two pincers met near Lokhvitsa on 16 September, trapping four Soviet armies — the 5th, 21st, 26th and 37th — in the pocket around Kiev.

The encirclement

Stalin forbade a timely withdrawal, and General Kirponos's repeated requests to retreat were refused until it was too late. Kiev itself fell on 19 September after fierce fighting and devastating explosions set by Soviet demolition teams. Within the pocket, German infantry divisions compressed the encircled forces; breakout attempts mostly failed, and Kirponos was killed on 20 September trying to escape. Organised resistance ended on 26 September.

Aftermath

The Germans claimed some 665,000 prisoners, a figure broadly consistent with Soviet losses of around 700,000 men killed, wounded or captured — the Red Army's worst defeat of the war. Yet the diversion consumed weeks of good campaigning weather, and the ensuing drive on Moscow began late, in the autumn mud and cold. Ukraine's occupation that followed was marked by atrocities including the Babi Yar massacre of Kiev's Jews on 29–30 September 1941.
