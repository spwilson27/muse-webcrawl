The Eastern Iranian languages are a subgroup of the Iranian languages, once spoken across a vast area of Central Asia and the Eurasian steppe, from the Caucasus to western China. They are distinguished from the Western Iranian languages (such as Persian, Kurdish and Balochi) by a series of phonological and morphological innovations. Most Eastern Iranian languages are extinct; the survivors are concentrated in Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Pakistan and the Caucasus.

History

The earliest attested Eastern Iranian language is Avestan, the language of the Zoroastrian scriptures. In the middle period, the group included Sogdian, the lingua franca of the Silk Road; Bactrian, written in Greek script in what is now northern Afghanistan; Khotanese, from the Tarim Basin; Chorasmian; and the steppe languages of the Scythians, Sarmatians and Alans, whose only living descendant is Ossetian in the Caucasus.

Living languages

The largest living Eastern Iranian language is Pashto, spoken by tens of millions in Afghanistan and Pakistan. Other survivors include Ossetian, the Pamir languages of the Wakhan corridor and Badakhshan (such as Shughni, Wakhi and Sarikoli), Yaghnobi — a direct descendant of Sogdian spoken in Tajikistan — and Ormuri and Parachi in small pockets. Many of the smaller languages are endangered.
