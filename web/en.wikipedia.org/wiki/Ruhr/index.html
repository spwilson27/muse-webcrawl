The Ruhr (German: Ruhrgebiet, also Ruhrpott) is a polycentric urban area in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, and one of the largest metropolitan regions in Europe, with a population of roughly five million. Bounded by the rivers Ruhr to the south, Rhine to the west and Lippe to the north, it grew into Germany's industrial heartland in the 19th and 20th centuries on the basis of coal mining and steel production.

Industrial history

Coal had been dug along the Ruhr valley for centuries, but deep mining spread northward from the 1850s, and firms such as Krupp (Essen) and Thyssen built vast steel empires; the region's canals, the port of Duisburg (the world's largest inland port) and dense railways served the trade. Polish-speaking migrants, the Ruhrpolen, formed a large part of the early workforce. The Ruhr was occupied by French and Belgian troops in 1923 over reparations defaults, provoking passive resistance. In the Second World War its armaments industry made it a prime target of Allied bombing, including the famous Dambusters raid on the Möhne and Eder dams in 1943.

Postwar change

After 1945 the Ruhr's coal and steel fuelled the West German economic miracle under the European Coal and Steel Community — a body partly designed to bind the region's heavy industry into European integration. From the coal crisis of 1958 and the steel crisis of the 1970s, employment in mining collapsed; the last German hard-coal mine, Prosper-Haniel in Bottrop, closed in 2018. The region has since restructured toward services, logistics, technology and universities — the Ruhr University Bochum, founded 1962, was the first new university in postwar West Germany — while former collieries such as the Zollverein in Essen (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) became cultural landmarks. The Ruhr was European Capital of Culture in 2010 ("Ruhr.2010").

Geography and cities

The region has no single centre: Dortmund, Essen, Duisburg and Bochum are its largest cities, joined by Gelsenkirchen, Oberhausen, Hagen, Hamm and others, administered regionally through the Regionalverband Ruhr. The Emscher river, long an open industrial sewer, has been the subject of a decades-long renaturation project.
