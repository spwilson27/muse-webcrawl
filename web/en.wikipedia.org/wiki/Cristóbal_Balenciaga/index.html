Cristóbal Balenciaga

Cristóbal Balenciaga Eizaguirre (21 January 1895 – 23 March 1972) was a Spanish couturier and the founder of the fashion house Balenciaga. Revered by contemporaries such as Christian Dior, who called him "the master of us all", he was famed for his sculptural silhouettes and uncompromising craftsmanship.

Life and career

Born in Getaria in the Basque Country, Balenciaga opened dressmaking establishments in San Sebastián and Madrid before fleeing the Spanish Civil War and establishing his Paris house in 1937. His designs — including the barrel line, the baby doll dress, the balloon dress, and the sack dress — reshaped postwar fashion by moving away from the hourglass silhouette.

Legacy

Balenciaga closed his house in 1968 and died in 1972. The brand was later revived and became one of the world's leading luxury labels. The Balenciaga Museum in his hometown of Getaria preserves his work.
