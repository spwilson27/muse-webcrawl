Brasenose College, Oxford

Brasenose College (BNC), officially King's Hall and College of Brasenose, is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England. It was founded in 1509 by the lawyer Sir Richard Sutton and William Smyth, Bishop of Lincoln. The college stands on the site of the medieval Brasenose Hall, one of Oxford's academic halls, and is located on the west side of Radcliffe Square.

Name

The name "Brasenose" is traditionally explained by a brass or bronze door knocker in the shape of a nose that adorned the original hall. The original 12th-century knocker, said to have been taken to Stamford, Lincolnshire, when students migrated there in 1333–34, was purchased by the college in 1890 and is displayed in the college hall. An alternative scholarly suggestion derives the name from the Latin brasina (brewhouse), but the knocker explanation remains the college's traditional account.

History

William Smyth and Richard Sutton, both from the northwest of England, endowed the college in the early 16th century. Brasenose developed strong links with Lancashire and Cheshire, which persisted in its scholarships for centuries. The Old Quad was built in the 17th century, the chapel in the 1650s, and further additions followed in the 19th and 20th centuries, including buildings on the High Street designed by T. G. Jackson. Women were first admitted in 1974.

People and traditions

Notable alumni include the writers John Buchan and William Golding, the Arctic explorer and statesman Fridtjof Nansen (a visiting figure), the former British prime minister David Cameron, the actor and comedian Michael Palin, and the Australian prime minister Malcolm Turnbull. The college has a traditional rivalry with neighbouring Lincoln College, reenacted each Ascension Day in a ceremony involving ivy beer. Its boat club, founded in 1815, is regarded as one of the oldest in the world, and Brasenose crews have been Head of the River in the Oxford bumps races many times.
