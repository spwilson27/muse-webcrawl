Ardea, Lazio

The historic centre of Ardea, in the Metropolitan City of Rome

Ardea is a town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Rome, Lazio, Italy, about 35 km south of Rome and 3 km from the Tyrrhenian coast. It stands on an ancient site of the same name, once the capital of the Rutuli.

Ancient history

Ancient Ardea was one of the oldest cities of Latium, a centre of the Rutuli whose king Turnus, in Virgil's Aeneid, is the rival of Aeneas. Colonised by Rome, it retained importance into the Republic, and remains of its walls and temples survive. Excavations have revealed Iron Age and archaic remains, including rich tombs in the surrounding area.

Modern town

The medieval borgo grew around a castle on the rocky acropolis. In the 20th century the Pontine Marshes reclamation and Roman suburban growth expanded the comune, which includes the coastal resort of Tor San Lorenzo and planned settlements such as Colle Romito. The town is noted for its art colony history and the museum devoted to the sculptor Giacomo Manzù, who lived nearby.
