The Port of Gdynia is a Polish seaport on the western shore of the Gulf of Gdańsk, on the Baltic Sea, in the city of Gdynia. Founded in the 1920s by the newly independent Second Polish Republic, it transformed a small fishing village into a modern port city and was, by the late 1930s, among the largest and most modern ports on the Baltic. Today it is one of Poland's largest ports, a major centre for container and roll-on/roll-off traffic and for grain and other bulk cargoes, and a base for the Polish Navy.

History

After the First World War, the Treaty of Versailles gave Poland access to the sea through the "Polish Corridor" but left the region's chief port, Danzig (Gdańsk), a Free City outside direct Polish control. Determined to have a port of its own, the Polish parliament voted in September 1922 to build a harbour at Gdynia; construction, directed by the engineer Tadeusz Wenda, began with a temporary pier that opened in 1923, followed by massive works through the interwar years, championed by minister Eugeniusz Kwiatkowski. By 1938 Gdynia had surpassed Danzig in turnover and handled the bulk of Poland's sea trade, notably coal exports. The city built around it grew from about 1,300 inhabitants in 1921 to some 120,000 by 1939. The port was fought over in September 1939, occupied and renamed Gotenhafen during the German occupation, and heavily damaged in 1945; it was rebuilt in the post-war decades as a principal port of communist Poland, with major shipyards, and played its part in the Solidarity-era history of the Tricity (Gdańsk–Gdynia–Sopot) coast.

Facilities and operations

The port occupies a large enclosed basin system protected by breakwaters, with terminals operated by several companies: container terminals (BCT and GCT), a bulk terminal handling coal, grain, and feedstuffs, ro-ro terminals serving ferry lines to Sweden (Gdynia–Karlskrona), and general cargo and offshore-energy facilities. Regular ferry and feeder services connect Gdynia with Scandinavia and with hub ports in northern Europe. The port's hinterland connections have been improved by the A1 motorway and rail links, and expansion plans include a new outer port (Port Zewnętrzny) on reclaimed land to accommodate larger vessels and offshore wind installation work.
