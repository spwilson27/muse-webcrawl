Jacques Brel

Jacques Romain Georges Brel

Born: April 8, 1929, Schaerbeek, Brussels, Belgium

Died: October 9, 1978, Bobigny, Paris, France

Occupation: Singer-songwriter, actor, director

Genres: Chanson, French pop

Signature songs: "Ne me quitte pas," "Amsterdam," "Quand on n'a que l'amour"

Jacques Romain Georges Brel (April 8, 1929 – October 9, 1978) was a Belgian singer-songwriter whose impassioned songs, delivered with theatrical intensity, made him one of the most influential figures of French-language chanson. Though he wrote mostly in French (and occasionally in Dutch), his work has been translated and recorded by artists around the world.

Life and career

Born in Schaerbeek, Brussels, to a cardboard-manufacturing family, Brel left his father's factory in 1953 for Paris, where Jacques Canetti signed him to Philips. Early success came slowly, but by the late 1950s songs such as "Quand on n'a que l'amour" (1957) and "Ne me quitte pas" (1959) — one of the most covered French songs ever written — established his reputation. Through the 1960s he toured relentlessly, performing songs like "Amsterdam," "Les Bourgeois," "La chanson des vieux amants," and "Bruxelles," renowned for concerts in which he seemed to live each song to exhaustion.

Later years

At the peak of his fame Brel retired from touring in 1967 to act and direct in theater and film, including a long-running stage production of L'Homme de la Mancha (Man of La Mancha), in which he played Don Quixote. An avid sailor and pilot, he spent his last years voyaging in the Pacific and settled on Hiva Oa in the Marquesas Islands, near the grave of Paul Gauguin. Diagnosed with lung cancer, he returned to France for treatment and died in Bobigny in 1978, aged 49; he is buried on Hiva Oa.

Legacy

Brel's songs have been adapted into English as "If You Go Away" ("Ne me quitte pas"), "Seasons in the Sun" ("Le Moribond," a worldwide hit for Terry Jacks), and "Amsterdam," covered by David Bowie and Scott Walker, among many others. The 1968 off-Broadway revue Jacques Brel Is Alive and Well and Living in Paris introduced him to American audiences. He remains a towering influence on singer-songwriters and is regarded in Belgium and France as one of the greatest artists of the twentieth century.
