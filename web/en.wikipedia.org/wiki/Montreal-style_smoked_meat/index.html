Montreal-style smoked meat

Montreal-style smoked meat, or simply smoked meat (French: smoked meat or viande fumée), is a type of kosher-style deli meat product made by salting and curing beef brisket with spices. The brisket is allowed to absorb the flavours over a week, then hot-smoked to cook through, and finally steamed to completion. It is a quintessential dish of Montreal, Quebec, traditionally served in Jewish delicatessens as a thick-cut sandwich on rye bread with mustard.

Preparation

Montreal smoked meat is made from the brisket, cured in a dry rub of cracked black pepper, coriander, garlic, mustard seed, and other spices, with little or no sugar. After curing (traditionally longer than for New York pastrami), it is smoked for several hours and then steamed until tender. At the deli counter it is hand-sliced to order and typically ordered by fat content: lean, medium, medium-fat, fat, or speck (the fatty trimmings).

History

The dish arrived with Jewish immigrants from Romania and Eastern Europe in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Its origins are variously credited to butchers and deli owners such as Herman Rees Roth, Benjamin Kravitz (founder of Bens De Luxe Delicatessen, 1908), and Aaron Sanft. Famous establishments include Schwartz's (the Montreal Hebrew Delicatessen, founded 1928), the Main Deli, Reuben's, and Dunn's. Montreal smoked meat is often compared and contrasted with New York pastrami — smoked meat uses a dry cure and the whole brisket, whereas pastrami traditionally uses the navel cut and a wet brine — and it is a classic accompaniment to Montreal-style bagels and poutine.
