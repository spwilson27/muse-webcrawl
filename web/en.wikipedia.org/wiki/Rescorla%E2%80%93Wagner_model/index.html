Rescorla–Wagner model

FieldPsychology, learning theory
Proposed1972
AuthorsRobert A. Rescorla, Allan R. Wagner

Classical conditioning

The Rescorla–Wagner model is a model of classical conditioning proposed by Robert A. Rescorla and Allan R. Wagner in 1972. It attempts to describe learning in terms of changes in the strength of associations between stimuli, driven by the discrepancy between what an animal expects and what actually occurs.

The model

On each trial, the change in associative strength of a conditioned stimulus is proportional to the difference between the maximum strength supportable by the unconditioned stimulus and the total strength of all stimuli present. Learning therefore proceeds quickly at first and slows as the outcome becomes fully predicted — learning is driven by surprise.

Phenomena explained

The model accounts for several important phenomena that earlier theories could not, including blocking (a previously conditioned stimulus prevents learning about a redundant new stimulus), overshadowing, and conditioned inhibition. It remains one of the most influential formal theories of associative learning, though later models such as the Pearce–Hall model addressed its limitations.
