Krio language

Krio is an English-based creole language that is the de facto national language and lingua franca of Sierra Leone. It is the native language of the Sierra Leone Creole people (the Krio), descendants of freed African-American, Afro-Caribbean and liberated African slaves who settled in Freetown from the late 18th century onward. Although the Krio form only a small minority of the population, their language is understood by the great majority of Sierra Leoneans and serves as the main medium of informal inter-ethnic communication across the country.

History and origins

Krio developed in Freetown, founded in 1792 as a settlement for freed slaves. Its principal settler groups — the "Black Poor" from London, the Nova Scotian Settlers, the Jamaican Maroons, and tens of thousands of Liberated Africans recaptured from slave ships — brought varieties of English-based pidgin and creole from the Atlantic world. The language's lexifier is English, from which most vocabulary derives, but its grammar and a substantial part of its lexicon reflect West African languages such as Yoruba and Igbo, as well as Portuguese elements surviving from earlier trade jargons on the coast.

Characteristics

Krio is closely related to the other Atlantic English creoles, including Nigerian Pidgin, Cameroonian Pidgin, Ghanaian Pidgin, and the Gullah language of the American Southeast, with which it shares many features and much vocabulary. It has an established (though not official) orthography and a growing literature, including translations of Shakespeare and the Bible. Example vocabulary includes kushɛ ("hello", from Yoruba), sabi ("to know"), and pikin ("child"). Despite its wide use, English remains Sierra Leone's sole official language, and Krio has historically carried a stigma in formal education, a situation that has gradually improved.
