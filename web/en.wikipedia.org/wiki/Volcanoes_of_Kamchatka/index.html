Volcanoes of Kamchatka

The Volcanoes of Kamchatka are a group of volcanoes on the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East, included on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The peninsula, part of the Pacific Ring of Fire, contains one of the world's highest densities of active volcanoes — around 29–30 active volcanoes among some 300 in total — together with a wide variety of associated volcanic features.

Geography and activity

Kamchatka's volcanoes result from the subduction of the Pacific Plate beneath the Okhotsk Plate. Two main volcanic belts run along the peninsula: the older Central Kamchatka Depression belt, including Klyuchevskaya Sopka (about 4,750 m, Eurasia's highest active volcano), Shiveluch, Bezymianny, and Tolbachik; and the Eastern Volcanic Zone, including Koryaksky, Avachinsky, Zhupanovsky, Karymsky, and Mutnovsky.

Major eruptions include Bezymianny's 1956 directed blast (similar in style to Mount St. Helens in 1980), the 1975–76 Great Fissure Eruption of Tolbachik, and continued activity at Shiveluch and Klyuchevskoy. In April 2023 a major eruption of Shiveluch deposited deep ash on nearby settlements.

Protected areas

The World Heritage site, inscribed in 1996 and extended in 2001, comprises several protected areas, among them Kronotsky Nature Reserve, Bystrinsky Nature Park, Nalychevo Nature Park, and the Southern Kamchatka Sanctuary, famed for geysers (the Valley of Geysers), caldera lakes such as Kurile Lake, brown bears, and Steller's sea eagles.
