Strickland in 2018.

Donna Theo Strickland (born 27 May 1959) is a Canadian optical physicist and pioneer in the field of pulsed lasers. She shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics with her former supervisor Gérard Mourou 'for their method of generating high-intensity, ultra-short optical pulses' — chirped pulse amplification (CPA) — and with Arthur Ashkin, honoured for optical tweezers. She was the third woman ever to win the Physics Nobel, and the first in 55 years (after Marie Curie and Maria Goeppert Mayer).

CPA (1985, Strickland's first scientific paper, written as a Rochester PhD student) stretches a laser pulse in time, amplifies it safely, then recompresses it — raising attainable intensities by orders of magnitude without destroying the amplifier. The technique underlies the world's most powerful lasers (petawatt class), precision eye surgery (LASIK's femtosecond lasers), micromachining, and attosecond science, and Strickland has spent her career at the University of Waterloo developing ultrafast laser systems.

Recognition

The Nobel drew attention because Strickland was still an associate professor and lacked even a Wikipedia article the morning of the announcement — a case study in the visibility gap for women scientists that she discussed with characteristic humour. She used the attention to advocate for women and underrepresented groups in physics.
