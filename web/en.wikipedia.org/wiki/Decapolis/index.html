Overview

The Decapolis was a group of ten cities on the eastern frontier of the Roman Empire in the southeastern Levant, in modern Jordan, Syria and Israel. The cities — Hellenistic in culture and largely Greek-speaking, founded or refounded under Alexander's successors and reorganised by Pompey in 63 BC — formed a league bound by trade, coinage and culture, on the edge of the Nabataean and Jewish worlds.

History and remains

The Decapolis is mentioned in the Gospels as a region of Jesus's ministry and of the Gerasene demoniac. Its cities prospered spectacularly under Rome: Gerasa (Jerash) and Gadara (Umm Qais) are among the best-preserved Roman cities anywhere; Scythopolis (Beit She'an) was the largest, and Philadelphia is modern Amman. The league faded after the cities were assigned to provinces (Arabia, Syria, Palaestina) in the 2nd century, though "Decapolis" long survived as a regional name. Earthquakes, especially in 749, ended their classical urban life.
