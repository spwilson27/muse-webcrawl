The Middle Paleolithic (or Middle Palaeolithic) is the second subdivision of the Paleolithic or Old Stone Age, spanning roughly 300,000 to 50,000–40,000 years ago in Europe, western Asia and Africa. It is defined by the dominance of prepared-core flake-tool industries and is associated principally with the Neanderthals in Europe and the Levant and with early anatomically modern humans in Africa and Arabia.

Technology

The hallmark of the period is the Levallois technique, in which a stone core is carefully shaped so that a flake of predetermined size and form can be struck from it; the resulting flakes were retouched into scrapers, points and denticulates. In Europe these industries are grouped as the Mousterian. Tools were increasingly hafted, and wooden spears — such as the Schöningen spears (now usually dated to the end of the Lower Paleolithic/transition) — attest to sophisticated woodworking. Regular use of fire, pigments such as ochre, and in some regions shell beads and other ornaments are documented.

Behaviour

Middle Paleolithic hominins hunted large game, occupied caves and open-air sites, and buried at least some of their dead, as at Shanidar, La Chapelle-aux-Saints and Qafzeh. The extent to which Neanderthals possessed fully modern language and symbolic behaviour remains debated, though evidence of personal ornaments and pigment use has grown.

End of the period

The Middle Paleolithic gave way to the Upper Paleolithic — marked by blade technologies, bone and antler tools and abundant art — as anatomically modern humans dispersed across Eurasia after about 50,000 years ago and Neanderthals disappeared by around 40,000 years ago. In Africa the broadly equivalent period is called the Middle Stone Age.
