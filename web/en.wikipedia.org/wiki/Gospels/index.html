Gospel

The gospel, or gospels, are the four canonical accounts of the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus in the New Testament: the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. The word "gospel" derives from the Old English gōdspel, "good news", translating the Greek euangelion.

The first three are known as the Synoptic Gospels because they share much of the same material and structure, while John's gospel differs markedly in style and content. Most scholars date Mark to around AD 70 and the others to the following two decades. The gospels were written as proclamations of the Christian message rather than as modern biographies, blending historical recollection with theological interpretation.

Besides the four canonical gospels, many non-canonical or "apocryphal" gospels survive, such as the Gospel of Thomas and the Gospel of Peter, which were excluded from the canon as the New Testament took shape in the early centuries of the church.

In Christian liturgy, readings from the gospels hold a place of honour, and the term "gospel" is also used more broadly for the central message of Christianity and for a genre of music, gospel music.
