History of Naples

The history of Naples spans more than 2,800 years, from its founding as a Greek colony to its role today as the capital of Italy's Campania region. One of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited cities, Naples has been a Roman resort, a Byzantine duchy, the capital of the Norman, Hohenstaufen, Angevin, and Spanish-ruled Kingdom of Naples, and — briefly restored under the Bourbons — the largest city of pre-unification Italy.

Greek and Roman Naples

Greek settlers from Cumae founded Neapolis ("new city") around the 6th century BC beside the older settlement of Parthenope. Under Rome — which took the city in the 4th century BC — Naples retained its Greek language and culture and became a fashionable retreat; Virgil wrote the Georgics there and was buried nearby. Vesuvius' eruption in AD 79 devastated neighbouring Pompeii and Herculaneum but spared Naples.

Medieval capital

After Rome's fall, Naples became a largely autonomous Byzantine duchy, resisting Lombard and Saracen pressure. The Norman Roger II incorporated it into the Kingdom of Sicily in 1137. Frederick II founded its university in 1224 — the world's oldest state university. When Charles of Anjou took the kingdom in 1266, Naples became its mainland capital; under the Angevins and then the Aragonese (from 1442) it flourished as a Mediterranean court city.

Spanish, Bourbon, and modern Naples

From 1504 Naples was ruled by Spanish viceroys for two centuries; it was one of Europe's largest cities, though plagued by revolt (Masaniello, 1647) and plague (1656, which killed perhaps half the population). Austrian rule (1707–34) gave way to the Bourbon Kingdom of Naples under Charles VII, who built the San Carlo opera house (1737) and the palace at Caserta. After Napoleonic interludes and restoration, Garibaldi's expedition brought Naples into the new Kingdom of Italy in 1860–61. The city suffered heavy bombing and rose against German occupation in the Four Days of Naples (1943). Post-war decades brought overcrowding and Camorra crime, but also major urban renewal from the 1990s; the historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1995).
