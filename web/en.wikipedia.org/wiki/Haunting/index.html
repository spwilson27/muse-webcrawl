Haunting

A haunting is the purported recurrence of supernatural phenomena such as apparitions, unexplained sounds, moving objects, or sensed presences, attributed to the spirit of a dead person or other entity at a particular location. Tales of hauntings appear in folklore worldwide and in literature from antiquity, including Pliny the Younger's account of a haunted house in Athens.

Classification

Parapsychologists have proposed categories such as "residual" hauntings, in which events repeat like recordings with no apparent intelligence, and "intelligent" hauntings, in which a presence appears to interact with witnesses. Poltergeist cases, often centred on an adolescent, form another commonly reported category.

Explanations

Sceptical investigators attribute reported hauntings to natural causes including carbon monoxide poisoning, infrasound, electromagnetic fields, sleep paralysis, suggestion, and misperception of ordinary sounds and shadows. Hauntings remain a staple of popular culture, from ghost stories and Gothic novels to films and television, and "haunted house" attractions are common entertainment.
