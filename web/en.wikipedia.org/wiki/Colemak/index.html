Colemak

Colemak
Type: Keyboard layout
Released: 2006
Designer: Shai Coleman
Based on: QWERTY

Colemak is an alternative keyboard layout for Latin-script alphabets, designed by Shai Coleman and released in 2006. It is intended to be more efficient and comfortable than the standard QWERTY layout while being easier for QWERTY typists to learn than the older Dvorak layout.

Design

Colemak changes the positions of 17 keys relative to QWERTY, placing the most frequent letters of English on the home row so that most typing occurs there. It keeps many common shortcut keys—such as Z, X, C, and V—in their QWERTY positions, preserving familiar copy, cut, paste, and undo commands. The Caps Lock key is replaced by a second Backspace in the standard layout.

Ergonomics

Advocates argue that Colemak reduces finger travel and awkward same-finger bigrams compared with QWERTY, and that its metrics compare favourably with Dvorak for English text. Empirical studies of alternative layouts generally find modest speed gains after retraining, with comfort benefits varying between individuals.

Support

Colemak is included as an option in several operating systems and can be installed on others; it is also supported by many programmable keyboards. Variants such as Colemak-DH modify the layout further to reduce central column reaches.
