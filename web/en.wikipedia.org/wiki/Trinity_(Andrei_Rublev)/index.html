Trinity (Andrei Rublev)

The Trinity (Russian: Троица, Troitsa), also called The Hospitality of Abraham, is an icon created by the Russian painter Andrei Rublev in the early fifteenth century. It is considered the most famous work of Russian painting and one of the supreme achievements of Orthodox Christian art. The icon depicts the three angels who visited Abraham at the Oak of Mamre (Genesis 18), interpreted by the Church as a prefiguration and image of the Holy Trinity — Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Composition

The three angels sit around a table bearing a chalice with the head of a sacrificial calf, a type of the Eucharist. Behind them stand a house (the dwelling of Abraham), a tree (the Oak of Mamre) and a mountain. The figures' bowed heads and interlocking gazes form a circular composition often read as an image of divine unity and mutual love; the subtle reverse perspective and restrained palette create its famously serene atmosphere.

History

Tradition dates the icon to about 1411 or to 1425–27, painted for the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius in connection with its new Trinity Cathedral, where it hung in the iconostasis. After centuries darkened by candle smoke and oil, cleaning in 1904–05 revealed its original colours. Since 1929 it has been held by the State Tretyakov Gallery in Moscow. In 2023 the icon was controversially transferred to the Russian Orthodox Church and placed in the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour before being moved to a chapel at the Trinity Lavra, over the objections of restorers concerned for its fragile state.
