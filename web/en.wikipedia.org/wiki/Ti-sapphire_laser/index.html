Ti-sapphire laser

Ti-sapphire lasers (also known as Ti:Al2O3 lasers or titanium-sapphire lasers) are tunable lasers which emit red and near-infrared light in the range from about 650 to 1100 nanometres. The lasing medium is a crystal of sapphire (aluminium oxide) doped with titanium ions. They are mainly used in scientific research because of their ability to be tuned over a wide wavelength range and to generate ultrashort pulses.

History

The titanium-sapphire laser was invented by Peter Moulton in June 1982 at MIT Lincoln Laboratory. It later displaced the dye lasers that had previously dominated tunable and ultrashort-pulse applications, offering a wider tuning range and far easier operation.

Properties

Ti:sapphire has an exceptionally broad gain bandwidth, supporting pulse durations of only a few femtoseconds when mode-locked, typically by the Kerr-lens mechanism. The crystals are usually pumped by a frequency-doubled Nd:YAG or similar laser emitting green light near 532 nm. Common applications include spectroscopy, multiphoton microscopy, frequency-comb generation, and seeding high-power chirped-pulse amplification systems.
