Sąjūdis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Reform Movement of Lithuania (Lithuanian: Lietuvos Persitvarkymo Sąjūdis), known simply as Sąjūdis ("the Movement"), was a political movement and organisation that led the struggle for Lithuanian independence from the Soviet Union in the late 1980s and early 1990s, culminating in the restoration of the State of Lithuania on 11 March 1990.

Formation and growth

Sąjūdis was founded on 3 June 1988 at a meeting in the Lithuanian Academy of Sciences in Vilnius, which elected a 35-member initiative group including intellectuals such as Vytautas Landsbergis (who became its chairman), Romualdas Ozolas, and Kazimira Prunskienė. Riding Mikhail Gorbachev's policies of glasnost and perestroika, it grew rapidly into a mass movement with hundreds of local groups and its own publications, including the newspaper Atgimimas ("Rebirth"). Initially calling for greater autonomy, after its first congress in October 1988 it moved openly toward full independence.

Road to independence

Sąjūdis organised the great Baltic Way demonstration of 23 August 1989, in which some two million people formed a human chain across Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, and exposed the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. In the February 1990 elections to the Supreme Soviet of the Lithuanian SSR, Sąjūdis-backed candidates won a majority, and on 11 March 1990 the new parliament proclaimed the re-establishment of an independent Lithuanian state — the first Soviet republic to do so. Sąjūdis supporters withstood the Soviet January Events of 1991 and the August coup, after which independence was internationally recognised. The movement dissolved in 1993, its members dispersing into new political parties.
