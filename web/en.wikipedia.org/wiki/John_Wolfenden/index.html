John Frederick Wolfenden, Baron Wolfenden (26 June 1906 – 18 January 1985) was a British educationalist and administrator. He is best remembered as chairman of the committee whose 1957 report recommended the decriminalisation of homosexual acts between consenting adults in private in Britain.

Education and schools

Wolfenden was educated at Queen's College, Oxford, where he gained a first in classics and became a fellow and tutor at Magdalen College. He was headmaster of Uppingham School from 1934 and of Shrewsbury School from 1944 to 1950, then became Vice-Chancellor of the University of Reading, a post he held until 1963.

The Wolfenden Report

In 1954 Wolfenden was appointed to chair the Committee on Homosexual Offences and Prostitution. Its report, published in September 1957, concluded that the criminal law should not "intervene in the private lives of citizens" and recommended that homosexual behaviour between consenting adults (over 21) in private no longer be an offence. The recommendation was controversial and was not enacted until the Sexual Offences Act 1967, but the "Wolfenden principle" — a boundary between private morality and the law — became a lasting reference point in British public debate.

Later life

Wolfenden later served as a director of the British Museum and chaired various public bodies. He was made a life peer in 1973 as Baron Wolfenden of Westcott. His son Jeremy Wolfenden was a journalist and Soviet affairs correspondent.
