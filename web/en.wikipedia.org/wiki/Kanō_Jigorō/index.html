Kanō Jigorō

Kanō Jigorō (嘉納 治五郎, 10 December 1860 – 4 May 1938) was a Japanese educator and martial artist, best known as the founder of judo. In 1882 he established the Kodokan dojo in Tokyo and systematised the techniques of traditional jujutsu into a modern discipline governed by the principles of seiryoku zen'yō (maximum efficient use of energy) and jita kyōei (mutual welfare and benefit). He was also the first Japanese member of the International Olympic Committee.

Early life

Kanō was born in Mikage (now part of Kobe), the third son of a sake-brewing family. Physically slight as a youth, he began studying jujutsu in Tokyo under Fukuda Hachinosuke of the Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū and later under Iikubo Tsunetoshi of the Kitō-ryū, whose throwing techniques strongly influenced judo.

Founding of judo

In 1882, aged 21, Kanō rented rooms at the Eishō-ji temple in Tokyo and founded the Kodokan ("place for teaching the way"). He removed dangerous techniques from jujutsu, emphasised randori (free practice) and kata, and framed judo as a method of physical, mental and moral education rather than mere combat. Judo spread worldwide through his students, and men's judo became an Olympic sport at Tokyo 1964.

Education and public life

Kanō served as principal of the Tokyo Higher Normal School for over two decades, founded schools, promoted physical education in Japan, and served on the IOC from 1909 until his death. He died of pneumonia aboard ship while returning from an IOC meeting in Cairo in 1938.
