Majjhima Nikaya

The Majjhima Nikāya ("Middle-length Collection") is a Buddhist scripture, the second of the five nikāyas (collections) in the Sutta Piṭaka of the Pali Canon, the scriptural canon of Theravada Buddhism. It contains 152 discourses (suttas) attributed to the Buddha and his chief disciples, of medium length compared with the longer discourses of the Dīgha Nikāya and the shorter ones of the Saṃyutta and Aṅguttara Nikāyas.

Contents

The discourses are organised into three divisions of fifty (paṇṇāsas), each subdivided into groups of ten (vaggas). They cover the full range of the Buddha's teaching — ethics, meditation, wisdom and monastic life — in dialogues with monks, Brahmins, ascetics of other schools, lay followers and rulers. Famous discourses include the Mūlapariyāya Sutta, the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta on the foundations of mindfulness, the Ānāpānasati Sutta on mindfulness of breathing, the Madhupiṇḍika Sutta, the Raṭṭhapāla Sutta, and the Aṅgulimāla Sutta telling of the converted brigand Aṅgulimāla.

Parallels and translations

Many Majjhima discourses have parallels in the Chinese Madhyama Āgama (Taishō 26), preserved by the Sarvāstivāda school. The collection has been translated into English in full by Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli and Bhikkhu Bodhi as The Middle Length Discourses of the Buddha, a widely used edition.
