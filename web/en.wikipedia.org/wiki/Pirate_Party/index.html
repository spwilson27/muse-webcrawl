Pirate Party

Pirate Party is a label adopted by political parties around the world inspired by the Swedish Piratpartiet, founded in 2006 by Rick Falkvinge. Pirate parties advocate reform of copyright and patent law, increased government transparency, protection of privacy, freedom of information, and direct democracy.

History and spread

The Swedish party gained prominence after the trial of The Pirate Bay and won two seats in the European Parliament in 2009. Pirate parties were subsequently founded in dozens of countries, coordinated from 2010 by Pirate Parties International. The German Pirate Party won seats in several state parliaments in 2011–2012, and the Icelandic Pirate Party became a major force after the financial crisis, briefly leading opinion polls and joining Reykjavík's city government. Czech Pirates entered parliament in 2017 and later joined a national governing coalition.

Policies

Core policies include shortening copyright terms, legalising non-commercial file sharing, opposing mass surveillance, and promoting open government and participatory decision-making, sometimes through "liquid democracy" software. While electoral success has varied, the movement influenced debates on digital rights across Europe and beyond.
