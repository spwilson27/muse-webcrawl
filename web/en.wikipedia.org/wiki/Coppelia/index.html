Coppélia

ChoreographerArthur Saint-Léon
ComposerLéo Delibes
LibrettoCharles Nuitter, after E. T. A. Hoffmann
Premiere25 May 1870, Théâtre Impérial de l'Opéra, Paris
GenreComic ballet in three acts

Coppélia, full title Coppélia, ou La Fille aux yeux d'émail ("Coppélia, or The Girl with the Enamel Eyes"), is a comic ballet in three acts originally choreographed by Arthur Saint-Léon to music by Léo Delibes, with libretto by Charles Nuitter. It premiered on 25 May 1870 at the Théâtre Impérial de l'Opéra in Paris. Based loosely on E. T. A. Hoffmann's story "The Sandman" (1816), it tells of the doll-maker Dr Coppélius, his life-sized dancing doll Coppélia, and the lovers Swanilda and Franz.

Synopsis

In a village square, Swanilda is jealous because her fiancé Franz keeps flirting with a beautiful girl reading on the balcony of Dr Coppélius's house — in fact the doll Coppélia. During the festival of the new bell, Swanilda and her friends slip into the workshop, discover the doll and a room of automatons, and wind them up for a romp; when Coppélius returns, Swanilda hides and later pretends to be the doll come to life in order to rescue Franz, whom the doctor has drugged in the hope of transferring his life-force into Coppélia. In the final act the lovers are reconciled and married amid the Festival of the Bell dances, and Coppélius is compensated for his broken doll.

Music and choreography

Delibes's score, with its Hungarian czárdás, mazurka, and the famous waltz and "Dance of the Hours", is regarded as a landmark of ballet music that influenced Tchaikovsky. The original Swanilda was the 16-year-old Giuseppina Bozzacchi, who died during the Franco-Prussian War siege of Paris shortly after the premiere; the role of Franz was originally danced en travesti. Modern productions generally follow the version revised by Marius Petipa in St Petersburg (1884). Coppélia remains one of the most frequently performed classical ballets.
