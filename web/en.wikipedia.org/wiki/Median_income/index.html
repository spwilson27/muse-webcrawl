Median income

Median household income is a common measure of the typical standard of living

Median income is the income amount that divides a population into two equal groups, half having an income above that amount and half below. It is widely used by governments and economists as a measure of the typical income of a household, family, or individual within a country, region, or demographic group, because unlike mean income it is not skewed by a small number of very high incomes.

Definitions

Median income may be reported for households, families, or individuals, and may be expressed before or after taxes and transfers. For international comparisons, statisticians often use median equivalised disposable income, which adjusts household income for household size using an equivalence scale and converts currencies at purchasing power parity. The OECD and the Luxembourg Income Study publish standardised cross-country estimates.

Interpretation

Because the median tracks the middle of the distribution, it is a central indicator in debates about living standards and the "middle class". In many developed countries, median income growth has lagged productivity and mean income growth since the late 20th century, a pattern associated with rising income inequality: when high incomes grow faster than middle incomes, the mean rises faster than the median. In the United States, the Census Bureau reports median household income annually; it was roughly $78,000 in 2023.

By country

Countries with the highest median disposable incomes typically include Luxembourg, the United States, Switzerland, Norway, Canada, Australia, and the Nordic countries, though rankings vary with the equivalence scale and data source used.
