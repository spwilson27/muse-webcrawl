Charles and Ray Eames

Charles Eames (1907–1978) and Ray Kaiser Eames (1912–1988) were American husband-and-wife designers who made major contributions to modern architecture, furniture, industrial design, film and graphic design. Working from the Eames Office in Venice, California, they produced some of the most iconic furniture of the 20th century, including the Eames Lounge Chair and the molded plywood and fibreglass chairs manufactured by Herman Miller.

Early lives

Charles Eames was born in St. Louis, Missouri, studied architecture briefly at Washington University, and headed the department of industrial design at the Cranbrook Academy of Art in Michigan. There he collaborated with Eero Saarinen on molded plywood designs that won the Museum of Modern Art's 1940 Organic Design in Home Furnishings competition. Ray Kaiser, born in Sacramento, California, studied abstract painting with Hans Hofmann in New York and was a founding member of the American Abstract Artists group before enrolling at Cranbrook, where she met Charles. They married in 1941 and moved to California.

Furniture

In Los Angeles the Eameses experimented with molding plywood in their apartment with a homemade machine nicknamed the "Kazam!" During the Second World War they produced plywood splints and stretchers for the U.S. Navy, which financed the development of their molding techniques. After the war they designed the LCW (Lounge Chair Wood) and DCW chairs, described by Time magazine as the chair of the century, followed by the La Chaise (1948), the fibreglass shell chairs (1950), the Wire Chair, the Aluminum Group, and the Eames Lounge Chair and Ottoman (1956), made of rosewood plywood and leather.

Architecture

As part of the Arts & Architecture magazine Case Study House programme, they designed the Eames House (Case Study House No. 8, 1949) in Pacific Palisades, built from off-the-shelf steel and industrial components and filled with folk art, textiles and plants; it is considered a masterpiece of post-war residential architecture. The neighbouring Entenza House (Case Study House No. 9) was designed with Eero Saarinen.

Films and exhibitions

The Eames Office made more than 125 short films, including Toccata for Toy Trains (1957) and the celebrated Powers of Ten (1977), which zooms from the edge of the known universe to a subatomic particle. For the 1959 American National Exhibition in Moscow they created Glimpses of the USA, projected on seven giant screens. They also designed exhibitions such as Mathematica: A World of Numbers... and Beyond (1961) for IBM.

Legacy

Charles died in 1978 and Ray exactly ten years later, in 1988. The Eames Office continues under their descendants. Their work is held in major museum collections, and their furniture remains in production, making them among the most influential designers of the modern era.
