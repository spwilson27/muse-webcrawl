Competency-based learning

Competency-based learning or competency-based education is a framework for teaching, learning and assessment that focuses on students demonstrating that they have learned the knowledge and skills (competencies) expected of them as they progress through their education. Students advance upon demonstrated mastery of a competency rather than by time spent in class, and pacing is typically personalised: learners who need more time or different instruction receive it, while those who master material quickly move ahead.

Key elements

Commonly cited design principles include: explicit, measurable learning objectives; meaningful, performance-based assessments that require learners to apply knowledge; timely and differentiated support based on individual needs; evaluation of evidence of learning rather than seat time; and a culture of equity in which all students are held to the same high expectations. Competency-based approaches descend from earlier outcome-based education and mastery learning movements, including Benjamin Bloom's work on mastery learning in the 1960s.

Implementation

Competency-based systems have been adopted by school districts such as those in New Hampshire and Maine in the United States — where state policy shifted from Carnegie-unit credit to proficiency-based diplomas — and by higher-education institutions such as Western Governors University, founded in 1997, which awards degrees entirely on the basis of competency assessments. In medical and vocational education, competency frameworks define the observable abilities a graduate must possess.

Criticism

Supporters argue that the model ends "social promotion" and makes expectations transparent. Critics contend that defining competencies can narrow the curriculum to what is easily measured, that assessment quality varies widely, and that implementation often founders on grading traditions, timetabling and the difficulty of tracking many individual learning pathways.
