Cultural policy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Placeholder image

Cultural policy is the area of public policy concerned with the arts, heritage and culture. It covers government support for museums, libraries, theatres, orchestras, broadcasting and the creative industries; the preservation of historic buildings and intangible heritage; language policy; and the promotion of national culture abroad. Cultural policy is implemented by ministries of culture, arts councils and local authorities, and studied as an academic field drawing on sociology, political science and cultural studies.

Models

Analysts distinguish several models: the facilitator model (the state supports culture indirectly through tax incentives, as in the United States); the patron model (arm's-length arts councils distribute public funds, as in the United Kingdom); the architect model (a ministry directly plans and funds cultural life, as in France, where André Malraux became the first Minister of Cultural Affairs in 1959); and the engineer model (culture directed to ideological ends in authoritarian states).

Debates

Recurring questions include artistic freedom versus public accountability, elitist versus popular definitions of culture, the economic value of the creative industries, cultural diversity and the "cultural exception" in trade law, and the protection of heritage in war. UNESCO's 2005 Convention on the Diversity of Cultural Expressions is a key international instrument.
