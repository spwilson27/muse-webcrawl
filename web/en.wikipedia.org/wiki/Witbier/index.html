Witbier

Witbier (Dutch, literally "white beer") is a Belgian-style ale brewed with a large proportion of wheat relative to malted barley. Belgian witbiers are typically spiced with coriander and orange peel (often Curaçao orange), giving a refreshing, lightly spicy and citrusy character.

History

Wheat beers were brewed for centuries in the region around Hoegaarden and Leuven in Brabant. The style nearly died out in the 1950s when the last witbier brewery in Hoegaarden closed, but was revived in 1966 by milkman Pierre Celis, who founded the Hoegaarden brewery. The style later spread worldwide, exemplified by brands such as Blue Moon in the United States.

Characteristics

Witbier is pale and cloudy — "white" — because of suspended wheat proteins and yeast. Alcohol content is typically 4.5–5.5%. It is traditionally served in a hexagonal tumbler, sometimes garnished with a slice of orange.

Related styles

German Weissbier differs in using at least 50% malted wheat and no spices, with flavour from yeast alone; the tart Berliner Weisse and Leipzig Gose are other historic German wheat styles.
