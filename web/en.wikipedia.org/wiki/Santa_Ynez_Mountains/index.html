Santa Ynez Mountains

The Santa Ynez Mountains are a mountain range in the Transverse Ranges of Southern California, running roughly east–west through Santa Barbara and Ventura counties, immediately north of the city of Santa Barbara.

Geography

The range extends about 80 km from Point Arguello area toward the Santa Clara River valley. Peaks include Broadcast Peak and Santa Ynez Peak (about 1,310 m). The southern face rises steeply from the coastal plain — a classic fault-block front — while the northern slopes descend into the Santa Ynez Valley. Much of the range lies within Los Padres National Forest.

Geology and climate

The mountains consist mainly of uplifted sedimentary rocks, especially sandstone, giving them their characteristic rocky ridges. The chaparral-covered slopes have a Mediterranean climate with hot dry summers. The range's south-facing slopes shelter Santa Barbara from cold north winds, contributing to its mild climate, but also channel the hot "sundowner" winds that have fuelled wildfires such as the Thomas and Cave fires.

Recreation

East Camino Cielo road runs along the crest, giving access to hiking trails, rock climbing, paragliding launch sites and panoramic views of the Channel Islands.
