El Lahun (also written el-Lahun or Illahun) is an archaeological site in Egypt, located near the modern village of el-Lahun in the Faiyum Governorate, at the entrance to the Faiyum Oasis. It is best known for the pyramid of the 12th Dynasty pharaoh Senusret II and for the nearby planned town of Kahun, which housed the workers and officials associated with the pyramid and has yielded an exceptional archive of Middle Kingdom papyri.

Pyramid of Senusret II

The pyramid of Senusret II (reigned c. 1897–1878 BC) was built of mudbrick around a core of limestone pillars and walls, originally cased in white limestone. Unusually, the entrance was placed on the south side rather than the north, concealed beneath the floor of a princess's tomb, a measure intended to frustrate tomb robbers. Within the pyramid complex, excavator Flinders Petrie discovered a spectacular hoard of jewellery and cosmetic objects in the tomb of Princess Sithathoriunet, among the finest Middle Kingdom treasures ever found.

Kahun

The town of Kahun, laid out on a regular grid plan, is one of the earliest known examples of a planned settlement. It contained large mansions for officials on one side and much smaller, uniform houses for workers on the other, providing rare evidence for social organisation in ancient Egypt. Finds from the town include tools, furniture, household objects, and the Kahun Papyri — documents dealing with mathematics, medicine, veterinary practice, law, and administration — which form one of the most important sources for everyday life in the Middle Kingdom.

Excavation history

The site was excavated by Flinders Petrie in 1889–1890 and again in 1913–1914. Petrie's work at Lahun and Kahun helped establish his reputation for meticulous recording of mundane artefacts, and the finds are now divided mainly between the Egyptian Museum in Cairo and the Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology in London.
