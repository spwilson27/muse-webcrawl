Australia Act 1986

The Australia Act 1986 is the short title of a pair of enactments — one of the Parliament of Australia and one of the Parliament of the United Kingdom — that severed the remaining constitutional links between Australia and the United Kingdom. The Acts ended the power of the British Parliament to legislate for Australia, removed appeals from Australian state courts to the Privy Council in London, and made the Australian states fully sovereign within the federal system. Both Acts commenced on 3 March 1986.

Background

Australia had been legally independent in most respects since the adoption of the Statute of Westminster 1931 (adopted by Australia in 1942, retroactive to 1939). But anomalies remained: the UK Parliament retained theoretical power to legislate for the Australian states, state laws could be struck down under the Colonial Laws Validity Act 1865, and appeals lay from state supreme courts to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council — even after High Court appeals had been abolished in stages (1968–1975). The 1975 constitutional crisis had also exposed uncertainties about the role of the monarch's representatives.

Provisions

The Acts provided that no future UK Act would extend to Australia or its states; that state parliaments could legislate free of the Colonial Laws Validity Act and repugnant-to-imperial-law doctrine; that state governors would thereafter deal directly with the monarch (advised by state premiers, not British ministers) on state matters; and that no appeal could be taken from any Australian court to the Privy Council. Because the Commonwealth Parliament lacked clear power to legislate for the states' constitutions, the Australian Act was passed at the request of all state parliaments under section 51(xxxviii) of the Constitution.

Significance

The Australia Act is generally regarded as the final step in Australia's constitutional independence from Britain — the point at which Australia became, in law, fully sovereign — while remaining a constitutional monarchy sharing the same sovereign. Queen Elizabeth II travelled to Canberra to sign the proclamation bringing the Australian Act into force.
