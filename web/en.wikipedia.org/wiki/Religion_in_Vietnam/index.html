Religion in Vietnam

Religion in Vietnam has historically been shaped by the "three teachings" (tam giáo) of Mahayana Buddhism, Taoism and Confucianism, blended with indigenous ancestor veneration. A large proportion of Vietnamese describe themselves as non-religious, yet folk religious practices are pervasive.

Major traditions

Buddhism is the largest organised religion, followed by Catholicism, which arrived with French missionaries and accounts for roughly seven per cent of the population. Protestantism is smaller but growing, particularly among ethnic minorities. Vietnam is also the homeland of two distinctive religions: Caodaism, a syncretic faith founded in the 1920s, and Hòa Hảo Buddhism, founded in 1939 in the Mekong Delta.

Islam and Hinduism

Small Muslim and Hindu communities exist among the Cham people of the south-central coast, including a distinctive Cham Bani tradition.

State policy

Vietnam is officially an atheist state under its ruling Communist Party, though freedom of belief is guaranteed by the constitution; religious organisations must register with the government, and regulation of religion remains a sensitive matter.
