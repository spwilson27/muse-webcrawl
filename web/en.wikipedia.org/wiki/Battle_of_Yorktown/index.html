Siege of Yorktown

The Siege of Yorktown, also known as the Battle of Yorktown and the Surrender at Yorktown, was the decisive final battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought from 28 September to 19 October 1781 at Yorktown, Virginia. A combined American and French army under George Washington and the Comte de Rochambeau, supported by a French fleet under the Comte de Grasse, trapped the British army of Lord Cornwallis, which surrendered on 19 October 1781.

Background

Cornwallis, after campaigning in the southern states, fortified Yorktown on the York River as a deep-water port. Washington, learning that de Grasse's fleet was sailing for the Chesapeake, marched the allied armies from New York to Virginia. The French naval victory at the Battle of the Chesapeake (5 September) sealed Cornwallis off from relief by sea.

The siege

Formal siege parallels were dug in the European manner under the direction of French engineers. On the night of 14 October, American troops under Alexander Hamilton stormed Redoubt No. 10 while French troops took Redoubt No. 9, allowing the allies to complete their second parallel and bombard the town at point-blank range. A failed breakout attempt across the York River on 16 October ended British hopes.

Surrender and aftermath

Cornwallis, pleading illness, sent Brigadier Charles O'Hara to surrender the army's sword — legend holds that the British bands played "The World Turn'd Upside Down". Some 8,000 British and German soldiers became prisoners. The defeat broke British political will to continue the war; peace negotiations followed, and the Treaty of Paris (1783) recognised American independence.
