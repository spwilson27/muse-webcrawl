Vijayadashami

Also calledDussehra, Dashera, Dashain (Nepal)
TypeHindu festival
ObservancesRamlila performances, burning of Ravana effigies, processions, worship of tools and books
DateAshvin Shukla Dashami (September–October)

Vijayadashami, more commonly known as Dussehra, is a major Hindu festival celebrated every year at the end of the nine nights of Navaratri, on the tenth day (dashami) of the Hindu month of Ashvin. It commemorates the victory of good over evil: in northern and western India, the god Rama's defeat of the demon king Ravana as told in the Ramayana; in eastern and southern traditions, the goddess Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura.

Celebrations

  • In North India, open-air Ramlila plays dramatize Rama's life for weeks, culminating in the burning of huge effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Meghnada filled with fireworks.
  • In West Bengal, Vijayadashami marks the immersion (visarjan) of Durga idols, closing the Durga Puja festival.
  • In Mysuru, Karnataka, the festival is the state celebration (Naada Habba), famous for the royal procession (Jamboo Savari) with decorated elephants.
  • In South India, families display dolls (golu), worship books and tools (Ayudha Puja), and begin children's formal education (Vidyarambham) on this auspicious day.
  • In Nepal, Dashain is the longest and most important festival of the year.

The day is also associated with the Pandavas of the Mahabharata, said to have retrieved their weapons from a shami tree on Vijayadashami after their exile; exchanging shami (apta) leaves as symbols of gold remains a Maharashtrian custom.
