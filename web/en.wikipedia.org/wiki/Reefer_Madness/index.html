Reefer Madness


Reefer Madness (1936), directed by Louis J. Gasnier; also released as Tell Your Children.

Reefer Madness is a 1936 American exploitation film directed by Louis J. Gasnier concerning the fictitious dangers of cannabis use. Originally financed by a church group under the title Tell Your Children, the film was intended as anti-drug propaganda for parents. Producer Dwain Esper bought the rights, added salacious material, and distributed it on the exploitation circuit.

Plot

High school students Bill Harper and Mary Lane are lured by pushers Mae and Jack to an apartment where they are induced to smoke marijuana. A series of increasingly melodramatic events follows—hallucinations, a hit-and-run accident, manslaughter, suicide, attempted rape, and madness—framed by a high school principal's lecture warning parents of the drug's menace.

Reception and cult status

Considered lost for decades, the film was rediscovered in the early 1970s when a print was found in the Library of Congress. Keith Stroup, founder of NORML, obtained and redistributed it as an unintentional comedy; its hysterical, overwrought depiction of marijuana made it a cult favourite and a staple of midnight-movie screenings. It is now in the public domain and is frequently cited as one of the most famous propaganda films ever made. It was later adapted into a stage musical and a 2005 television film.
