Diary of Samuel Pepys

The diary of Samuel Pepys is the diary kept by the English naval administrator and Member of Parliament Samuel Pepys from 1 January 1660 to 31 May 1669. Written in a form of shorthand, it provides an unrivalled first-hand account of Restoration London — its politics, theatre, music, food and domestic life — and of great events including the Second Anglo-Dutch War, the Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of London in 1666.

Content

Pepys began the diary at the age of 26 and wrote almost daily, candidly recording his career at the Navy Office, his ambitions and quarrels, his marriage to Elizabeth, his infidelities, his love of music and books, and minutiae such as meals and new clothes. He stopped in 1669 fearing that writing by candlelight was destroying his eyesight. The diary runs to roughly 1.25 million words.

Publication

Pepys bequeathed the six shorthand volumes to Magdalene College, Cambridge, with his library. A first selection, deciphered by John Smith, was published in 1825; Victorian editions were expurgated. The complete, unabridged text appeared only in the Latham and Matthews edition of 1970–83. The diary has since become a classic of English literature and a popular subject for daily-reading projects and blogs.
