Vinho Verde

Vinho Verde (Portuguese for "green wine", referring to its youth rather than its colour) is a Portuguese wine from the historic Minho province in the far north of the country. The Vinho Verde Denominação de Origem Controlada (DOC) is the largest Portuguese DOC and one of the largest in Europe, covering a lush, rainy and verdant region between the Douro and Minho rivers.

Characteristics

Vinho Verde wines are bottled young and typically light, fresh, low in alcohol (often 8.5–11%) and lightly effervescent, with bright acidity and floral and citrus aromas. Most are white, made from grapes such as Alvarinho, Loureiro, Arinto, Avesso, Azal and Trajadura, though red and rosé versions also exist; the red is traditionally drunk locally from earthenware bowls with sardines. Historically the slight sparkle came from malolactic fermentation in bottle; today carbon dioxide is usually added.

Region and production

The DOC is divided into nine subregions, including Monção e Melgaço, famed for Alvarinho wines with more body and ageing potential. Vines were traditionally trained high on trees, fences and pergolas (enforcado) to allow other crops beneath and to lift grapes away from the damp ground; modern vineyards increasingly use conventional trellising. Vinho Verde is a major export, popular as an inexpensive summer wine in the United States and northern Europe.
