Katyusha (song)

"Katyusha" (Russian: Катюша) is a famous Soviet song composed by Matvey Blanter with lyrics by Mikhail Isakovsky, first performed in 1938. Its narrator, a young woman named Katyusha (a diminutive of Ekaterina), sings from a riverbank to her beloved, a soldier serving far away on the frontier. During the Second World War it became one of the most beloved songs of Soviet soldiers and remains a fixture of Victory Day commemorations.

History

The song premiered in Moscow in November 1938, performed by Valentina Batishcheva with the State Jazz Orchestra. Its popularity surged after the German invasion of 1941. Soviet troops nicknamed their BM-13 multiple rocket launchers "Katyusha" after the song — according to one story, factory workers chalked the name on the first launchers — and the name has passed into military vocabulary worldwide. Italian partisans adapted the melody as "Fischia il vento", and the tune spread across Europe and Asia; it was for decades one of the best-known Russian melodies in China.

Lyrics

The opening verse describes apple and pear trees in blossom and morning mist over the river, as Katyusha climbs the steep bank singing a song for her beloved, "the one she loves, the one whose letters she keeps". The melody is simple, lyrical and readily singable, part of a body of pre-war Soviet popular song that blended folk idioms with mass-song style.
