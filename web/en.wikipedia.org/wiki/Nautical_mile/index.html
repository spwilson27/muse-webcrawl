Nautical mile

A nautical mile is a unit of length used in air, marine, and space navigation, and for the definition of territorial waters. It is defined internationally as exactly 1,852 metres (about 6,076 feet; 1.151 miles). The nautical mile is not an SI unit, but it is accepted for use with the SI.

A nautical mile corresponds to one minute of arc of latitude.

Definition and origin

Historically, the nautical mile was defined as one minute of arc along a meridian of the Earth — that is, one sixtieth of a degree of latitude. This made it directly convenient for navigation with charts: a distance measured in nautical miles could be converted to angular differences in position. Because the Earth is not a perfect sphere, the length of a minute of latitude varies slightly with latitude, and the value of exactly 1,852 metres was adopted internationally in 1929.

Use

The nautical mile is the standard unit of distance in maritime and aviation navigation worldwide. Speed at sea and in the air is measured in knots, where one knot equals one nautical mile per hour. Territorial seas are defined in international law (UNCLOS) as extending up to 12 nautical miles from the baseline of a coastal state, and exclusive economic zones extend up to 200 nautical miles.

Related units

A cable length is one tenth of a nautical mile. The sea mile and geographical mile are related historical units based on the Earth's circumference.
