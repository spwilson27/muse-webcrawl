Coppa Florio

Coppa Florio
Country: Italy (Sicily)
First race: 1900
Last race: 1929

The Coppa Florio was a car race first held in Italy in 1900. It was renamed in 1905 when Vincenzo Florio offered the initial 50,000 Italian lire and a cup designed by Polak, a Parisian jeweller, as the prizes. The winner's cup was named the Coppa Florio (Florio Cup).

History

The race was created by Vincenzo Florio, a wealthy Sicilian businessman and motoring enthusiast. It was originally staged as the Coppa Brescia, held on a course of the province of Brescia (Brescia-Cremona-Mantua-Brescia). From 1906 the Coppa Florio shared the Madonie circuit in Sicily with the Targa Florio, also founded by Florio, running over several laps of the mountainous course. In 1914 the race moved to Sicily permanently.

The event was contested by many of the leading racing drivers and marques of the pre-war and 1920s eras, including Fiat, Alfa Romeo, Bugatti and Mercedes. The last Coppa Florio races were held in the late 1920s, with a final edition in 1929. In the 1970s the name was briefly revived for a round of the World Sportscar Championship at Pergusa.
