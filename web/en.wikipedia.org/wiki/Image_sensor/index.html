Image sensor

Image sensor
TypeElectronic component
Main technologiesCMOS (active pixel), CCD
FunctionConverts light into electrical signals forming an image

An image sensor is a sensor that detects and conveys information used to form an image. It converts the variable attenuation of light waves striking its surface into electrical signals, pixel by pixel. Image sensors are the heart of digital cameras, smartphones, video cameras, medical endoscopes, scanners and many scientific instruments.

How it works

The sensing elements are photodiodes arranged in a two-dimensional array, usually covered by a colour filter array (most often the Bayer pattern of red, green and blue filters) and a grid of microlenses. Incoming photons generate electrons in proportion to the light intensity; the accumulated charge is measured and digitised to give each pixel's brightness, and demosaicing reconstructs a full-colour image.

Technologies

CCD (charge-coupled device) sensors, invented in 1969, shift charge across the chip to a single readout amplifier, historically giving uniform, low-noise images. CMOS (complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor) sensors give each pixel its own amplifier, allowing faster readout, lower power consumption and on-chip processing; continuous improvements — backside illumination, stacked designs — made CMOS dominant in nearly all applications by the 2010s. Sensors are described by their optical format (e.g. "full-frame" 36 × 24 mm, APS-C, 1-inch, 1/2.3-inch) and pixel pitch, which trade off against noise, dynamic range and low-light performance.

Applications

Beyond consumer photography, image sensors are used in astronomy, microscopy, machine vision, automotive cameras, security systems and satellite Earth observation.
