Flamingo Las Vegas is a casino hotel on the Las Vegas Strip in Paradise, Nevada. It is owned and operated by Caesars Entertainment. The Flamingo originally opened on 26 December 1946 and is the oldest continuously operating resort on the Strip; its opening, financed in part by mobster Benjamin "Bugsy" Siegel, is widely regarded as a foundational moment in the history of modern Las Vegas.

History

The Flamingo was begun by Hollywood reporter and nightclub owner Billy Wilkerson; when he ran short of money, Siegel and his associates took over the project. The resort opened prematurely on 26 December 1946 to rain and poor attendance, and lost money until its grand reopening in March 1947. Siegel was murdered in June 1947. Under later owners the Flamingo prospered as a mid-market Strip resort; it was bought by Kirk Kerkorian in 1967 — his first Las Vegas acquisition — and later by Hilton, which operated it as the Flamingo Hilton for many years. The original low-rise buildings were progressively replaced by hotel towers, the last of the original 1946 structure being demolished in 1993.

Features

The resort keeps its tropical theme, including a 15-acre pool complex and a wildlife habitat in its courtyard gardens that is home to a flock of live Chilean flamingos, along with pelicans, parrots and koi ponds — a rare free attraction on the Strip. Its central Strip location, opposite Caesars Palace, keeps it among the most recognisable properties in Las Vegas, and its famous pink neon signage has been repeatedly restored and reinterpreted over the decades.
