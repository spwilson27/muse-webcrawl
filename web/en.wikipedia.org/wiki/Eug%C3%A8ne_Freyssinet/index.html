Eugène Freyssinet

Eugène Freyssinet (13 July 1879 – 8 June 1962) was a French structural and civil engineer, regarded as the father of prestressed concrete. His inventions transformed bridge and building construction worldwide in the twentieth century.

Born13 July 1879, Objat, France
Died8 June 1962, Saint-Martin-Vésubie, France
OccupationCivil engineer
Known forPrestressed concrete

Early work

Trained at the École Polytechnique and the École des Ponts et Chaussées, Freyssinet built pioneering reinforced-concrete bridges in central France, including the Pont du Veurdre and the great arch bridge at Plougastel (Pont Albert-Louppe), then the longest concrete arch span in the world.

Prestressing

In 1928 Freyssinet patented the technique of prestressing concrete with high-strength steel wires tensioned before loading, overcoming concrete's weakness in tension and the effects of creep that had defeated earlier attempts. He went on to develop anchorage systems and founded a company to license his methods, which were applied to bridges such as the Luzancy Bridge and to structures from hangars at Orly to offshore works.
