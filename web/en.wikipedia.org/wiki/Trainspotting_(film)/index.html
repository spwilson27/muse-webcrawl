Trainspotting (1996)
Directed by: Danny Boyle
Screenplay by: John Hodge
Based on: Trainspotting by Irvine Welsh
Starring: Ewan McGregor, Ewen Bremner, Jonny Lee Miller, Kevin McKidd, Robert Carlyle, Kelly Macdonald

Country: United Kingdom

Trainspotting is a 1996 British black comedy-drama film directed by Danny Boyle and adapted by John Hodge from Irvine Welsh's 1993 novel of the same name. The film follows a group of heroin addicts in an economically depressed area of Edinburgh and their passage through life, and stars Ewan McGregor as Mark Renton, alongside Ewen Bremner, Jonny Lee Miller, Kevin McKidd, Robert Carlyle, and Kelly Macdonald in her debut role.

The film's iconic opening chase through Edinburgh.

Plot

Mark Renton, a young unemployed heroin addict in Edinburgh, drifts through life with his friends: the hapless Spud, the James Bond-obsessed Sick Boy, the clean-living Tommy, and the violent, drink-fuelled Begbie, who uses no heroin but is the most dangerous of the group. Renton's attempts to quit — intercut with his sardonic voiceover monologue beginning "Choose life" — repeatedly fail amid squalor, including the notorious "worst toilet in Scotland" scene and a horrifying hallucination involving a dead baby. After Tommy, drawn into heroin after a breakup, dies of AIDS-related toxoplasmosis, Renton moves to London and finds work, only for his friends to follow. A final heroin deal in London yields £16,000; Renton seizes his chance, steals the money while the others sleep, and walks away resolving to "choose life" — leaving a share for Spud, the only friend who never harmed him.

Production and release

The film was produced by Andrew Macdonald for Figment Films, from the team behind Shallow Grave (1994). Despite its subject matter, the film was marketed energetically, with distinctive posters and a bestselling soundtrack mixing Britpop and electronic music — including Iggy Pop's "Lust for Life", Lou Reed's "Perfect Day", and Underworld's "Born Slippy .NUXX" — which became emblematic of 1990s British culture. Released in February 1996, it was a major commercial and critical success, frequently ranked among the greatest British films: the British Film Institute placed it tenth in its list of the top 100 British films of the 20th century.

Themes and controversy

On release, some critics and politicians accused the film of glamourising drug use, while others praised its energetic style and its refusal to moralise. The title refers to the obsessive, ostensibly pointless hobby of trainspotting, a metaphor for addiction; in the film an old drunk at a disused station is revealed as Begbie's father. The novel's episodic structure and Scots dialect were reshaped into a tighter first-person narrative centred on Renton.

Sequel

A sequel, T2 Trainspotting, directed by Boyle and reuniting the principal cast, was released in 2017, loosely adapting Welsh's novel Porno and revisiting the characters twenty years on.
