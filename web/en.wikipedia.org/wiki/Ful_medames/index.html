Ful medames

Ful medames (Egyptian Arabic: فول مدمس‎), or simply fūl, is a stew of cooked fava beans served with olive oil, cumin and optionally parsley, garlic, onion and lemon juice. It is a staple food in Egypt, often eaten at breakfast, and is considered one of the country's national dishes.


Ful medames served with garnishes

History

Fava beans have been cultivated in Egypt since antiquity, and it has been suggested that ful was eaten in pharaonic times, though firm evidence is later. The name medames may derive from a Coptic word meaning "buried", referring to the traditional method of cooking: the pot of beans was buried in the embers of a fire or in the hot sand of a bathhouse furnace and left to cook slowly overnight in a vessel called a qidra.

Preparation and serving

Dried fava beans are soaked and then simmered for many hours until tender and creamy; canned beans are a common shortcut. The cooked beans are seasoned and mashed to varying degrees, then topped with oil, chopped tomato, onion, parsley, hard-boiled egg, or tahini. Ful is eaten with baladi flatbread and is widely sold by street vendors and dedicated ful carts throughout Egypt. Variants are found across the Levant, Sudan, Ethiopia (as part of special ful dishes) and the wider Arab world.
