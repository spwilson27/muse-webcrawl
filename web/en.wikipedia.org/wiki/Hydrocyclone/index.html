Hydrocyclone

A hydrocyclone (often shortened to cyclone) is a device to classify, separate or sort particles in a liquid suspension based on the ratio of their centripetal force to fluid resistance. It is a centrifugal separator with no moving parts, widely used in mineral processing, drilling mud cleaning, wastewater treatment and the separation of oil and water.

Operation

The suspension enters tangentially into a cylindrical section, creating a strong vortex. Coarser or denser particles are thrown outward to the wall and spiral downward through the conical section to the underflow outlet (apex), while finer or lighter particles are carried by an inner upward vortex to the overflow through the vortex finder. The particle size at which a particle has an equal probability of reporting to either stream is the cut size (d50), adjustable via inlet pressure, geometry and the apex and vortex-finder diameters.

Applications

Hydrocyclones are standard in grinding circuits for closed-circuit classification with ball mills; in clusters ("cyclone packs") they dewater and deslime ores. Smaller versions ("minicyclones") de-oil produced water offshore, and cyclones remove sand from irrigation and well water. In paper making they act as centrifugal cleaners removing contaminants from pulp.
