Ephydridae

Ephydridae (shore fly, sometimes brine fly) is a family of flies in the order Diptera, containing about 2,000 described species in nearly 200 genera worldwide. They are commonly found in wet habitats around the edges of ponds, streams, salt marshes, and beaches.

Biology

Adult ephydrids are small, usually dark flies, typically 1–6 mm long. The larvae are aquatic or semi-aquatic and display remarkable tolerance of extreme environments: some live in hot springs, highly alkaline or saline lakes (such as the brine flies of Mono Lake and the Great Salt Lake), and even in pools of crude petroleum, as with the petroleum fly Helaeomyia petrolei. Larvae feed on algae and bacteria skimmed from the water surface or scraped from submerged surfaces.

Ecology and significance

Shore flies form an important food source for migratory birds at saline lakes, where they can occur in enormous numbers. A few species, such as Hydrellia species, are pests of rice and other aquatic crops because their larvae mine leaves, while others are used in biological control of invasive aquatic weeds.

Classification

The family is divided into several subfamilies, including Ephydrinae, Gymnomyzinae, Ilytheinae, and Psilopinae. Genera include Ephydra, Hydrellia, Ochthera (whose adults prey on other insects), and Scatella, a common greenhouse pest.
