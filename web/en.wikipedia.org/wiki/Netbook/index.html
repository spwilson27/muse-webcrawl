Netbook

A netbook is a small, lightweight and inexpensive laptop computer suited for general computing and accessing web-based applications. The category flourished between roughly 2007 and 2013. The term was popularized by Intel and was famously applied to the ASUS Eee PC, launched in 2007, which followed the nonprofit OLPC XO-1 in demonstrating demand for very small, cheap laptops.

Characteristics

Netbooks typically had screens of 7 to 10.2 inches, low-power processors such as the Intel Atom, small solid-state drives or modest hard disks, and ran lightweight operating systems — Linux distributions initially, then Windows XP and Windows 7 Starter. They omitted optical drives and sacrificed performance for portability, battery life and prices often under US$400.

Decline

Sales fell sharply after 2010 as tablets such as the iPad and increasingly thin conventional laptops (ultrabooks) eroded the niche; major manufacturers discontinued netbook lines by 2013. The category is seen as a forerunner of later Chromebooks and low-cost cloud-focused laptops.
