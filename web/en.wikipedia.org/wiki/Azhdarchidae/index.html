Azhdarchidae (from Persian azhdar, a dragon-like creature of Persian mythology) is a family of pterodactyloid pterosaurs of the Late Cretaceous, characterised by very long, stiffened necks, toothless beaks, long limbs and proportionally small bodies. The family includes the largest flying animals known: Quetzalcoatlus northropi of North America and Hatzegopteryx thambema of Romania had wingspans estimated at 10–11 metres, and stood as tall as a giraffe when on the ground.

Anatomy and lifestyle

Azhdarchids had elongated cervical vertebrae but few of them, giving a stiff, giraffe-like neck; long, toothless jaws; and hindlimbs well suited to walking. Trackways and functional studies suggest they were competent terrestrial quadrupeds. A widely supported hypothesis pictures them as terrestrial stalkers — giant analogues of storks or hornbills that strode through floodplains and picked up small animals, eggs and carrion, rather than as aerial fishers; their fossils are mostly found in inland deposits.

Distribution and extinction

Azhdarchid fossils occur worldwide in Late Cretaceous rocks — Central Asia (where Azhdarcho was named by Lev Nesov in 1984), North and South America, Europe, Africa and possibly Antarctica. They were among the last surviving pterosaurs and perished in the Cretaceous–Palaeogene extinction event 66 million years ago, along with the non-avian dinosaurs.
