Covering fire


Fire and movement

Covering fire is a military tactic in which one element of a force fires on an enemy position to suppress it and protect friendly troops who are moving, typically to assault or withdraw. It is a central element of fire and movement (or fire and manoeuvre), in which units alternate between firing and moving so that the enemy is kept under fire throughout.

Suppression

The purpose of covering fire is usually suppression rather than destruction: even inaccurate fire forces defenders to keep their heads down, degrading their ability to aim at the moving element. Effective covering fire requires coordination — the moving element must complete its move before the firing element runs low on ammunition or must itself begin to move.

Variants

At higher levels of command, artillery, mortars, and air support may provide covering fire for manoeuvre formations. The United States Army historically drilled the technique at fire-team level as part of battle drills, and similar tactics date to the fire-and-movement methods developed during the First World War.
