The Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso (LNGS; Gran Sasso National Laboratory) is a particle physics laboratory in Italy operated by the INFN. It is the largest underground research centre in the world, with about 180,000 square metres of space in halls excavated inside the Gran Sasso massif in the Apennines, beside the tunnel of the A24 motorway between L'Aquila and Teramo.

Shielding and experiments

The rock above the laboratory reduces the flux of cosmic-ray muons by about a factor of a million, making it an ideal site for experiments that must look for extremely rare events. Major experiments have included:

  • Borexino – measured neutrinos from the Sun, including the first direct spectroscopy of pp neutrinos.
  • OPERA – observed the appearance of tau neutrinos in a muon neutrino beam sent 730 km from CERN; it also featured in the 2011 "faster-than-light neutrino" episode, traced to a faulty cable connection.
  • Dark matter searches – including XENON, DarkSide and CRESST detectors seeking weakly interacting massive particles.
  • CUORE and GERDA/LEGEND – searching for neutrinoless double-beta decay.

History

The laboratory was proposed by physicist Antonino Zichichi in 1979 and built during the 1980s, taking advantage of the excavation of the Gran Sasso highway tunnel. It hosts about a thousand scientists from around the world.
