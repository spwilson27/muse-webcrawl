Sasanian Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Rock relief of Shapur I's triumph over Roman emperors at Naqsh-e Rostam]

The Sasanian Empire (AD 224–651), the last pre-Islamic Persian empire, was founded by Ardashir I, who overthrew the Parthians, and endured for over four centuries as Rome's — and then Byzantium's — great eastern rival. The Sasanians revived Iranian kingship under the title shahanshah (King of Kings), made Zoroastrianism a state religion, and built a centralised state whose court culture, art, law and administrative practice deeply influenced the Islamic world that succeeded it. Its last king, Yazdegerd III, was killed in 651 as Arab armies completed the conquest.

Rome's rival

Shapur I (r. 240–270) humbled Rome repeatedly, capturing the emperor Valerian alive in 260 — a triumph carved in rock reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam. A long equilibrium of war and diplomacy followed: Khosrow I (r. 531–579) reformed taxation and the army, sacked Antioch yet also made the 'Eternal Peace' (532), and his court received Greek philosophers and Indian chess. Khosrow II (r. 590–628) conquered Syria, Egypt and Anatolia and took Jerusalem (614) and the True Cross, before Heraclius' counter-campaigns and a palace coup ended his reign.

Fall

The catastrophic war of 602–628, followed by civil war that put a dozen rulers on the throne in four years, left the empire defenceless when Arab armies, inspired by Islam, advanced in the 630s. The Sasanian field army was destroyed at al-Qadisiyyah (636) and again at Nahavand (642); Ctesiphon fell, and Yazdegerd III fled east until murdered at Merv in 651. Zoroastrian culture, the Middle Persian language and Sasanian models of kingship passed into Islamic Iran.
