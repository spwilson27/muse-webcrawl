WireGuard

WireGuard is a communication protocol and free and open-source software that implements encrypted virtual private networks (VPNs), and was designed with the goals of ease of use, high speed performance, and low attack surface. It was initially developed by Jason A. Donenfeld and first released in 2016. WireGuard was merged into the Linux kernel in version 5.6, released in March 2020.

Design

WireGuard uses modern cryptographic primitives, including the Noise protocol framework, Curve25519 for key exchange, ChaCha20 for symmetric encryption, Poly1305 for message authentication, BLAKE2s for hashing, and HKDF for key derivation. Its codebase is deliberately small — around 4,000 lines in the kernel implementation — making it easier to audit than older VPN protocols such as IPsec and OpenVPN.

Operation

WireGuard works by adding a network interface that can be configured with standard networking tools. Peers are identified by short public keys, and traffic is routed according to a list of allowed IP addresses associated with each key ("cryptokey routing"). The protocol is connectionless and silent to unauthenticated packets, which reduces its visibility to scanning.

Adoption

WireGuard has been adopted by commercial VPN providers and is supported on Linux, Windows, macOS, BSD, iOS and Android. It has received praise for its performance and simplicity, though it has also been criticised for logging and privacy design choices relating to IP address assignment.
