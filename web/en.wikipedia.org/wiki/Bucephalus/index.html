Bucephalus or Bucephalas (Ancient Greek: Βουκεφάλας, from βοῦς "ox" and κεφαλή "head", meaning "ox-head") was the horse of Alexander the Great, and one of the most famous horses of classical antiquity. Ancient accounts describe Bucephalus as a huge black stallion with a white star on his brow. He carried Alexander through most of his campaigns from the taming around 344 BC until his death in 326 BC after the Battle of the Hydaspes in India; Alexander founded the city of Bucephala (Alexandria Bucephalous) on the site in his honour.

The taming

According to Plutarch, the horse was offered to Philip II of Macedon by the Thessalian horse dealer Philonicus for the enormous price of thirteen talents, but no one could ride him — he reared and shied at everyone. The young Alexander, watching, noticed that the horse was afraid of his own shadow, turned him toward the sun so the shadow fell behind him, calmed him and mounted him. Philip, delighted, told his son to seek a kingdom worthy of himself, for Macedon was too small. The story became one of the canonical legends of Alexander's youth.

Death and legacy

Ancient sources disagree about the cause of death: Arrian and Plutarch report that Bucephalus died of old age and exertion (at about thirty) after the Hydaspes, while the Alexander Romance tradition has him killed in the battle. The city Alexander founded at the place of his death, Bucephala, has been tentatively identified with modern Jalalpur Sharif in Pakistan's Punjab province.
