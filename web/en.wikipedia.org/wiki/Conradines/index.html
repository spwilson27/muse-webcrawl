Conradines

The Conradines rose to prominence in Franconia during the dissolution of the Carolingian empire.

The Conradines or Conradiner (German: Konradiner) were a dynasty of Frankish counts and dukes in the late 9th and early 10th centuries, named after Conrad the Elder. From their heartlands in the Lahngau and Hesse they rose to control the Duchy of Franconia and, briefly, the kingship of East Francia, before being displaced by the Ottonians.

Rise

The family's documented rise began under Louis the Younger in the late 9th century. Conrad the Elder and his brothers Gebhard and Rudolf contested control of Franconia and Lotharingia with the rival Babenberg (Popponid) counts in the bloody Babenberg feud, which ended in 906 with the execution of Adalbert of Babenberg and Conrad's own death in battle the same year; his son Conrad the Younger emerged as the dominant Franconian magnate.

Kingship and fall

In 911, on the death of Louis the Child, the last East Frankish Carolingian, the nobles elected Conrad I ("the Younger") as king — the first non-Carolingian to hold the East Frankish throne. His reign (911–918) was beset by Magyar invasions and rebellious stem dukes; dying, he is said to have designated his former rival Henry the Fowler of Saxony as successor. Conrad's brother Eberhard of Franconia initially supported Henry and Otto I but joined the great revolt of 939 and was killed at the Battle of Andernach, ending Conradine power in Franconia. Later branches of the family held counties in Swabia (the Udo line, ancestors of the counts of the Wetterau) and are implicated in the ancestry of the Salian dynasty through Conrad the Red, duke of Lotharingia.

Legacy

Although their kingship lasted only seven years, the Conradines mark the transition from Carolingian to Ottonian rule in Germany: their election in 911 is often treated as the starting point of the medieval German kingdom.
