Dickin Medal

The PDSA Dickin Medal is the highest award that any animal can receive whilst serving in military conflict. It is recognised worldwide as the animals' Victoria Cross. Instituted in 1943 in the United Kingdom by Maria Dickin, founder of the People's Dispensary for Sick Animals (PDSA), it is a bronze medallion bearing the words "For Gallantry" and "We Also Serve", within a laurel wreath, carried on a ribbon of green, dark brown, and pale blue stripes representing water, earth, and air.

History

Maria Dickin founded the award after being moved by the bravery of animals in the Second World War. Between 1943 and 1949 it was awarded 54 times — to 32 homing pigeons, 18 dogs, 3 horses, and 1 cat — recognising acts such as pigeons flying vital messages through enemy fire, dogs detecting mines and locating survivors of bombing, and Simon, the ship's cat of HMS Amethyst, who continued his duties despite wounds sustained in the Yangtze Incident of 1949. Famous recipients include the pigeon G.I. Joe, credited with saving the lives of Allied troops in Italy, and the dog Rob, who made over 20 parachute jumps with the SAS.

Revival and later recipients

The award was revived in 2000. Notable modern recipients include the spaniels Buster and Theo, the explosive-detection dog Treo, and the Belgian Malinois Diesel, killed during the 2015 Saint-Denis raid in Paris. In 2014 an honorary Dickin Medal was awarded — backdated — to all animals that served in the First World War, represented by the warhorse Warrior. In 2020 the medal was awarded to the mine-detection giant pouched rat Magawa, the first rat recipient, for clearing landmines in Cambodia.

Related awards

The PDSA also awards the Gold Medal, instituted in 2001 as the animal equivalent of the George Cross for civilian animals, and the PDSA Order of Merit, launched in 2014 to recognise animals displaying outstanding devotion. As of the early 2020s the Dickin Medal has been awarded more than 70 times.
