Battle of Kandahar (1880)

The Battle of Kandahar, fought on 1 September 1880, was the decisive final battle of the Second Anglo-Afghan War. A British-Indian force under Lieutenant-General Sir Frederick Roberts defeated the army of Ayub Khan outside Kandahar, Afghanistan, following Roberts's celebrated march from Kabul to relieve the city.

Background

On 27 July 1880 Ayub Khan destroyed a British brigade at the Battle of Maiwand and besieged Kandahar. Roberts was detached from Kabul with about 10,000 men and marched some 313 miles (504 km) in about three weeks through difficult country — the famous "Kabul to Kandahar" march — arriving on 31 August.

The battle

On 1 September Roberts attacked Ayub Khan's entrenched camp at Baba Wali, north-west of the city. After a reconnaissance, the British-Indian infantry, supported by artillery, turned the Afghan left, storming the villages of Gundi Mulla Sahibdad and Pir Paimal before rolling up the main position. Ayub Khan's army broke and fled, abandoning its camp, guns and standards; Afghan losses were heavy, while British casualties were about 250 killed and wounded in total for the day (approximate).

Aftermath

The victory, with Abdur Rahman Khan recognised as Emir, effectively ended the Second Anglo-Afghan War. Roberts became a national hero in Britain. Kandahar was evacuated by the British in 1881 as Britain settled for influence over Afghan foreign policy rather than occupation.
