Spadix (zoology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In zoology, a spadix is a specialised anatomical structure found in certain male cephalopods and other animals. In cephalopods such as the nautilus and some octopuses, the spadix is a reproductive organ formed from modified arms, used to transfer spermatophores to the female during mating.

In cephalopods

In the paper nautilus (Argonauta) and related forms, a detachable hectocotylus arm carries the spermatophore; in the chambered nautilus, four small arms fuse into a conical spadix. The structure was historically puzzling to naturalists, and the detached arms were once described as parasitic worms.

Other uses

The term is also used for spadix-like accessory structures in other invertebrate groups. (For the botanical structure of the same name — a spike of flowers on a fleshy axis, typical of the arum family — see the article on the inflorescence.)
