Chromatic semitone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A chromatic semitone is a semitone (half step) between two notes that share the same letter name and staff position, one of which is altered by an accidental — for example, from C to C♯, or from E to E♭. It contrasts with a diatonic semitone, which occurs between notes of different letter names, such as E to F or C to D♭.

Tuning

In twelve-tone equal temperament, chromatic and diatonic semitones are the same size, 100 cents. In historical and just intonation systems, however, they differ: in Pythagorean tuning the chromatic semitone (apotome) is larger than the diatonic semitone (limma), and in quarter-comma meantone the chromatic semitone is smaller. These differences matter for the spelling and tuning of music in meantone and well-tempered systems.

Notation and use

Chromatic semitones are the building blocks of chromatic scales and chromatic voice leading, and augmented unisons are spelled as chromatic semitones. Recognizing the distinction is important in music theory for correct notation: a melodic move from C to D♭ forms a diatonic semitone (a minor second between different staff positions), while C to C♯ forms an augmented unison.
