Práxedes Mateo Sagasta

Born: 21 July 1825, Torrecilla en Cameros, Spain
Died: 5 January 1903 (aged 77), Madrid, Spain
Office: Prime Minister of Spain (multiple terms, 1871–1902)
Party: Liberal Party

Práxedes Mateo Sagasta Escolar (21 July 1825 – 5 January 1903) was a Spanish civil engineer and politician who served as Prime Minister on eight occasions between 1870 and 1902, always in charge of the Liberal Party. He was a key figure of the Restoration period, alternating in power with Antonio Cánovas del Castillo of the Conservative Party in the managed two-party system known as the turno pacífico.

Trained as an engineer, Sagasta entered politics with the Progressive Party and took part in the Revolution of 1868 that deposed Isabella II. He held ministerial posts and twice headed governments during the turbulent years that followed, including under King Amadeo I and the First Spanish Republic, before going into exile after the Bourbon Restoration of 1874.

The Liberal leader

Sagasta founded the Liberal Party and, first in opposition and then in government from 1881, became Cánovas's great counterpart. His long government of 1885–1890 (the "Long Parliament") enacted major reforms including universal male suffrage (1890), freedom of association, and trial by jury. The Pact of El Pardo (1885), by which the parties agreed to alternate power on the death of Alfonso XII to protect the regency, institutionalised the turno system.

Sagasta presided over the government during the Spanish–American War of 1898, in which Spain lost Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Philippines. He returned for a final term in 1901–1902 and died in Madrid in January 1903.
