Dragon Ball (Japanese: ドラゴンボール) is a Japanese media franchise created by Akira Toriyama in 1984. The original manga, serialised in Weekly Shōnen Jump from 1984 to 1995 and collected in 42 tankōbon volumes, follows the monkey-tailed boy Son Goku from childhood to adulthood as he trains in martial arts and searches for the seven magical Dragon Balls, which when gathered summon a wish-granting dragon, Shenron. Initially a gag-inflected adventure loosely based on the Chinese novel Journey to the West, the series progressively shifted toward spectacular martial-arts battles, becoming one of the most influential shōnen manga of all time, with more than 160 million copies sold in Japan alone and around 300 million worldwide including international sales.

Story

Goku, secretly a member of the alien Saiyan warrior race sent to Earth as an infant, is raised by the kindly Son Gohan and befriends the inventor Bulma, the shape-shifter Oolong, the bandit Yamcha and the lecherous master Muten Roshi, under whom he trains alongside Krillin. Later arcs pit an adult Goku and his allies — including his son Gohan, the Saiyan prince Vegeta, Piccolo and Future Trunks — against escalating cosmic threats: the Saiyan invasion, the tyrant Frieza on planet Namek, the androids and the bio-engineered Cell, and the ancient monster Majin Buu. Signature elements include the Kamehameha energy wave, fusion, and the golden-haired Super Saiyan transformation.

Adaptations

Toei Animation produced the anime series Dragon Ball (1986–1989), Dragon Ball Z (1989–1996) and the sequel Dragon Ball GT (1996–1997, not based on Toriyama's manga), plus more than twenty animated feature films. The franchise returned with Dragon Ball Super (2015–2018), plotted by Toriyama, and films including Battle of Gods (2013), Broly (2018) and Super Hero (2022). The English-language broadcast of Dragon Ball Z on Cartoon Network's Toonami block in the late 1990s was instrumental in popularising anime in North America.

Legacy

Dragon Ball is widely credited with shaping modern battle manga: creators of Naruto, One Piece and Bleach cite Toriyama as a formative influence. Its video games, trading cards and merchandise have generated tens of billions of dollars, and Goku has become a global pop-cultural icon, honoured as an ambassador of the Tokyo 2020 Olympics. Toriyama died in March 2024, prompting tributes worldwide, including from governments and fellow artists.
