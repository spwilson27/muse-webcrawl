Dobruja

Dobruja (Romanian: Dobrogea; Bulgarian: Добруджа, Dobrudzha) is a historical region in Eastern Europe, shared today by Romania and Bulgaria. It lies between the lower Danube River and the Black Sea, and includes the Danube Delta in its north. It is divided into Northern Dobruja, belonging to Romania, and Southern Dobruja, belonging to Bulgaria.

Geography

The region covers about 23,100 km². Northern Dobruja (about 15,570 km²) comprises the Romanian counties of Constanța and Tulcea; its main city is the Black Sea port of Constanța, the largest city of the region. Southern Dobruja (about 7,570 km²) corresponds roughly to the Bulgarian provinces of Dobrich and Silistra. The terrain is mostly a low, rolling plateau, with the Măcin Mountains in the northwest reaching 467 m at Țuțuiatu Peak.

History

In antiquity the region was inhabited by Getae and Dacians, with Greek colonies such as Histria, Tomis and Callatis on the coast; it was later ruled by Rome as part of Moesia Inferior and then Scythia Minor. In the Middle Ages it passed between the Byzantine Empire, the Bulgarian empires and other powers. In the fourteenth century it was the core of the semi-independent Despotate of Dobruja under Balik and Dobrotitsa, from whose name the region's name is usually derived.

The Ottomans controlled Dobruja from the early fifteenth century until the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, after which the Treaty of Berlin awarded Northern Dobruja to Romania and Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria. The south was ceded to Romania by the Treaty of Bucharest (1913) after the Second Balkan War, returned to Bulgaria under German pressure, regained by Romania in 1919, and finally restored to Bulgaria by the Treaty of Craiova (1940), which involved a population exchange between the two states.

Population

Dobruja is ethnically diverse: Romanians form the majority in the north, Bulgarians in the south, with significant minorities of Turks, Tatars (especially in the Romanian part), Roma, Lipovan Russians (in the Danube Delta), Greeks, Armenians and others — a legacy of its position at a crossroads of empires.
