Mozambique is divided into ten provinces (províncias) and one capital city, Maputo, which holds provincial status, making eleven first-level divisions in all. From north to south they are Niassa, Cabo Delgado, Nampula, Zambézia, Tete, Manica, Sofala, Inhambane, Gaza, Maputo Province and the city of Maputo. The provinces are in turn subdivided into 154 districts, and below them into municipalities and administrative posts (postos administrativos) and localities. Provincial governors were appointed by the president of the republic until constitutional reforms took effect at the elections of 2019, after which governors are elected—the candidate heading the list that wins the provincial assembly election becomes governor—while a secretary of state in each province represents the central government.

History

The provincial map descends from the districts of Portuguese Mozambique, reorganised several times during the colonial period. At independence in 1975, FRELIMO retained and renamed several units (Lourenço Marques became Maputo, Vila Cabral became Lichinga, Porto Amélia became Pemba) and in 1978–79 adjusted boundaries, splitting, among other changes, the old Gaza and Manica e Sofala administrations into the present provinces. Zambézia and Nampula, the two most populous provinces, dominate national politics together with the capital region, and the provinces differ sharply in wealth: the south (Maputo city and province) is markedly richer than the rural north and centre, a divide with roots in colonial investment patterns and the civil war of 1977–1992.

Governance

Each province has an elected provincial assembly and, since 2020, an elected governor heading the provincial executive council; simultaneously, a Secretary of State in the province, appointed by the president, oversees the organs of central state administration, a dual structure negotiated as part of the 2018–2019 peace and decentralisation agreements with RENAMO. Provinces have limited revenue-raising powers and depend heavily on transfers from the national budget distributed by a formula weighted toward population and poverty.
