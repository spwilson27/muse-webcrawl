Coronation Cup

The Coronation Cup is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to horses aged four years or older. It is run at Epsom Downs Racecourse over a distance of 1 mile, 4 furlongs and 6 yards (2,420 metres), on the same undulating course as the Derby and the Oaks. It is traditionally scheduled on the Friday of the Derby meeting in late May or early June, alongside the Oaks.

History

The race was established in 1902 to commemorate the coronation of King Edward VII, which was postponed from June to August of that year because of the king's illness. It was intended as a championship contest for the best older staying horses, complementing the classic generation tested in the Derby. The event was not run during the world wars, when racing at Epsom was suspended and its substitute fixtures held at other courses such as Newmarket.

Several winners have also won the Derby or other championships: Pretty Polly won in 1905 and 1906; the wartime and post-war eras produced dual-purpose stars such as Aureole (1954), owned by Queen Elizabeth II, and the brilliant Mill Reef (1972). More recent notable winners include the globetrotter Singspiel (1997), Yeats (2005), the filly Pride, Highland Reel (2017), and Cracksman (2018). St Nicholas Abbey won the race three times (2011, 2012, 2013), the race record. In 2020 the race was run at Newmarket behind closed doors owing to the COVID-19 pandemic, and in 2023 it was won by Emily Upjohn.

Records

The most successful jockeys in the race's history include Lester Piggott, with nine victories. Leading trainers by wins include André Fabre and Aidan O'Brien, whose multiple successes reflect the race's attraction for top European middle-distance horses as a stepping stone to the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes at Ascot and the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe in the autumn.
