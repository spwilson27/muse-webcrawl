The Grand Lisboa is a 47-storey, 258-metre (846 ft) hotel and casino complex in Sé, Macau, China. Owned by Sociedade de Jogos de Macau (SJM Holdings), it is the tallest building in Macau and one of the most recognisable landmarks of the territory's skyline. Its distinctive golden tower, shaped like a blooming lotus flower rising from a domed podium, has become a symbol of Macau's gaming industry.

History

The casino portion of Grand Lisboa opened on 11 February 2007, replacing the function of the original Casino Lisboa, which stands across the street and was opened in 1970 by Stanley Ho. The hotel tower opened on 17 December 2008. The complex was developed by SJM, the company founded by Ho that held Macau's casino monopoly until the market was liberalised in 2002.

Design

Designed by Hong Kong architects Dennis Lau and Ng Chun Man, the tower's form was inspired by a lotus flower, the floral emblem of Macau, as well as by the headdress of a Brazilian carnival dancer — a nod to Macau's Portuguese heritage. The eight-storey podium is shaped like a Fabergé egg and houses the casino floors. The building's gold-tinted glass facade makes it visible across much of the Macau Peninsula.

Facilities

The hotel contains around 430 rooms and suites. Its restaurants include The Eight, which has held three Michelin stars, and Robuchon au Dôme, located at the top of the tower. The casino offers several hundred gaming tables and slot machines across multiple floors. The lobby displays the "Star of Stanley Ho", a 218.08-carat cushion-shaped diamond named after the casino magnate, considered among the largest cushion-shaped diamonds in the world.
