Literary society

A literary society is an organisation devoted to the discussion, promotion and enjoyment of literature. Such societies range from informal reading circles and book clubs to national academies charged with honouring a country's writers and language. Historically, literary societies have been important centres of intellectual exchange, education and literary production, particularly before the spread of universities and the mass press.

History

Literary societies flourished in the 18th and 19th centuries. In Britain, clubs such as The Club (founded 1764 by Samuel Johnson and Joshua Reynolds) brought together writers, artists and thinkers. In the early American republic, college literary societies — such as the American Whig and Cliosophic societies at Princeton (1765/1770) and the literary societies of many other colleges — were the principal arena of student intellectual life, maintaining libraries, debating, and publishing journals. Many American fraternities grew out of these societies.

Women's literary societies and reading groups played an important role in female education and the suffrage movement in the 19th century. In the colonial and postcolonial world, literary societies promoted vernacular languages and national literatures.

Types

  • College literary societies — student debating and literary organisations; some survive as independent societies or evolved into fraternities.
  • Author societies — devoted to a single writer's life and work, e.g. the Jane Austen Society (1940), the Dickens Fellowship (1902), and numerous Shakespeare societies.
  • National academies — e.g. the Académie française (1635), the Royal Society of Literature (1820), and the American Academy of Arts and Letters (1898).
  • Regional and language societies — promoting the literature of a nation, language or region, such as the Welsh and Gaelic literary societies.
  • Book clubs and reading groups — informal modern descendants of the literary society.

Activities

Typical activities include lectures, debates, readings, publication of journals and proceedings, awarding of literary prizes, maintenance of libraries and archives, and the erection of memorials to writers.
