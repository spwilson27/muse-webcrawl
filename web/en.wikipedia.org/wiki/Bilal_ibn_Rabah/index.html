Bilal ibn Rabah

Born: c. 580, Mecca
Died: c. 640, Damascus
Known for: First muezzin; companion of Muhammad
Title: Sayyid al-Mu'azzin

Bilal ibn Rabah (Arabic: بلال بن رباح; c. 580–640), also known as Bilal al-Habashi, was an Ethiopian-born companion of the Prophet Muhammad and the first muezzin in Islam, chosen for the beauty and strength of his voice to call the faithful to prayer.

Early life and conversion

Born in Mecca to enslaved parents — his mother Hamamah was an Abyssinian princess enslaved in war — Bilal was enslaved to Umayya ibn Khalaf. On embracing Islam he was tortured, including by being laid on hot sand with a heavy stone on his chest, but is remembered for repeating "Ahad, Ahad" ("One, One"). Abu Bakr purchased and freed him.

Career

Bilal emigrated to Medina, fought at Badr and other battles, and gave the first adhan (call to prayer). After Muhammad's death he is said to have been unable to bear giving the call in Medina and moved to Syria, where he died in Damascus around 640 and is buried at Bab al-Saghir cemetery.

Legacy

Bilal is a symbol of Islam's opposition to racism and of devotion under persecution, honoured by Muslims worldwide and especially revered in African Muslim traditions. See Muezzin and Sahabah.
