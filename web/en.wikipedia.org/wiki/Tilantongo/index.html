Tilantongo

Tilantongo is an archaeological site and pre-Columbian Mixtec city-state in the Mixteca Alta region of the modern Mexican state of Oaxaca, near the modern community of Santiago Tilantongo. In the Mixtec language it was known as Ñuu Tnoo, "Black Town", after the colour of its temple. Tilantongo was one of the most important Mixtec kingdoms of the Postclassic period (c. 900–1521 AD).

History

Occupation of the site goes back to earlier periods, but Tilantongo rose to prominence in the Postclassic under its famous ruler Lord 8 Deer "Jaguar Claw" (1063–1115), who was born at Tilantongo and, according to the Mixtec screenfold manuscripts (codices) such as the Codex Zouche-Nuttall and Codex Bodley, unified much of the Mixteca through conquest and marriage alliance before being sacrificed after his capture by rivals. The codices preserve extensive dynastic genealogies of Tilantongo's rulers reaching back centuries, making the site central to Mixtec historical tradition.

The site

The archaeological zone occupies a hilltop with plazas, a ballcourt, elite residences and temples, including the Temple of Heaven (Temple of the Sky), associated with the royal necropolis. Alfonso Caso conducted important excavations and studies linking the site to the personages of the codices in the twentieth century. Finds include a celebrated mosaic skull and other fine objects in tombs near Tilantongo and neighbouring sites. Today the ruins lie within the municipality of Santiago Tilantongo in the Nochixtlán district of Oaxaca.
