Cuthbert of Lindisfarne (c. 634 – 20 March 687) was an Anglo-Saxon saint of the early Northumbrian church in the Celtic tradition. He was a monk, bishop and hermit, associated with the monasteries of Melrose and Lindisfarne in the Kingdom of Northumbria, and was one of the most important medieval saints of England, with a widespread cult both before and after the Reformation.

Life

Cuthbert entered Melrose Abbey as a youth, reportedly after a vision of St Aidan's soul ascending to heaven on the night Aidan died in 651. He became prior of Melrose, helped found the monastery at Ripon, and after the Synod of Whitby (664) accepted Roman practices. He moved to Lindisfarne, becoming prior and later a hermit on Inner Farne Island. In 685 he was made Bishop of Lindisfarne but, after a short episcopate, returned to his hermitage, where he died in 687.

Cult and relics

Eleven years after his death, his coffin was opened and his body was found incorrupt, which greatly promoted his cult. His remains were carried by the monks of Lindisfarne fleeing Viking raids in 875, wandering for years before settling at Chester-le-Street and finally Durham in 995. His shrine at Durham Cathedral became a major pilgrimage site; his relics there include his pectoral cross and the treasures associated with his coffin. The Lindisfarne Gospels were made in his honour. The coffin-reliquary of Saint Cuthbert is one of the most important surviving objects from Anglo-Saxon England.
