Master craftsman

A master craftsman is a person who has reached the highest level of qualification in a skilled trade or craft. Historically, the title derives from the guild system of medieval and early modern Europe, in which a craftsman progressed from apprentice to journeyman and finally to master, a rank that entitled him to own a workshop, sell his wares and train apprentices of his own. In several countries, notably Germany, the master craftsman qualification (Meister) remains a formal, state-recognised credential.

History

From the 12th century onwards, craft guilds in European towns regulated training and quality. A boy was apprenticed for several years, then travelled as a journeyman (the Wanderjahre in German-speaking lands), and finally submitted a "masterpiece" — a work demonstrating complete command of his craft — for judgement by the guild's masters. If accepted and able to pay the required fees, he became a master, with the right to open a workshop and take apprentices. Master craftsmen formed an influential urban class, often sitting on town councils.

Modern qualifications

In Germany, the Meisterprüfung (master craftsman examination), administered by chambers of crafts (Handwerkskammern), remains compulsory for owning a business in many trades, from electricians and plumbers to bakers and hairdressers; the qualification combines advanced technical skill with pedagogy, business management and law, and ranks in the European Qualifications Framework alongside a bachelor's degree. Similar master-level credentials exist in Austria, Switzerland, France (maître artisan) and Japan (takumi traditions), while in English-speaking countries the term is used more loosely for a highly experienced craftsperson or for licensed "master" grades in trades such as plumbing and electrical work.
