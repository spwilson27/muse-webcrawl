The West Kennet Long Barrow, also known as South Long Barrow, is a chambered long barrow located near the village of Avebury in the English county of Wiltshire. Built around 3650 BC in the Early Neolithic period, it is one of the largest and best-preserved long barrows in Britain, and one of the most visited.

Description

The trapezoidal earthen mound is about 100 metres long and up to 25 metres wide. At its eastern end, behind a facade of large sarsen stones, a passage about 13 metres long opens into five burial chambers. Excavations in the 1850s and 1950s recovered the disarticulated remains of at least 46 individuals, along with pottery, flint tools and beads; the bones showed signs of disease and injury typical of early farming communities.

History

The tomb was used for over a thousand years before being deliberately filled in and blocked around 2000 BC. Antiquarians noted it from the 17th century onward; it is now in the care of English Heritage and forms part of the Avebury section of the Stonehenge and Avebury World Heritage Site, near Silbury Hill.
