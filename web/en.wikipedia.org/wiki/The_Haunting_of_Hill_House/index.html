The Haunting of Hill House is a 1959 gothic horror novel by American author Shirley Jackson. A finalist for the National Book Award, it is widely regarded as one of the finest literary ghost stories of the twentieth century.

Plot

Dr. John Montague, an investigator of the supernatural, rents the eighty-year-old Hill House — a mansion built with deliberately disorienting architecture and a history of violent deaths — and invites several guests with past paranormal experiences to stay there, including the shy Eleanor Vance and the bohemian Theodora. As the house produces cold spots, banging doors, spectral writing, and a malevolent presence that seems to single Eleanor out, her grip on reality deteriorates. The novel famously leaves ambiguous whether the haunting is real or a projection of Eleanor's unravelled mind, and ends in tragedy as she is sent away.

Adaptations and legacy

The novel was adapted as Robert Wise's acclaimed film The Haunting (1963), again in 1999, and loosely reimagined as the 2018 Netflix series created by Mike Flanagan. Stephen King and others have praised its opening paragraph as among the greatest in horror fiction.
