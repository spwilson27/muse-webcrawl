Blenheim Palace is a country house in Woodstock, Oxfordshire, England. It is the seat of the Dukes of Marlborough and the only non-royal, non-episcopal country house in England to hold the title of palace. Named after the 1704 Battle of Blenheim, it was built as a gift from Queen Anne and a grateful nation to John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough, for his victory over the French and Bavarians in the War of the Spanish Succession.

Architecture

Designed in the rare English Baroque style by Sir John Vanbrugh with Nicholas Hawksmoor, the palace was built between 1705 and 1722 amid bitter disputes over cost and design between Vanbrugh and Sarah, Duchess of Marlborough, who eventually dismissed him. The vast park was later landscaped by Capability Brown, who created the great lake in the 1760s.

History

Blenheim is the birthplace and ancestral home of Sir Winston Churchill, who was born there in 1874 and is buried nearby at Bladon. The palace, its park and gardens are open to the public and form a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1987 as a masterpiece of English Baroque architecture and landscape design. The state rooms — including the Great Hall and the Saloon — are noted for their acoustics during concerts as much as for their paintings and tapestries.
