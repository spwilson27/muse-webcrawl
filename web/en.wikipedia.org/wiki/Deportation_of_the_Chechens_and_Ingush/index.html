The deportation of the Chechens and Ingush, codenamed Operation Lentil (Russian: Чечевица, Chechevitsa), was the Soviet forced transfer of the whole of the Vainakh (Chechen and Ingush) populations of the North Caucasus to Central Asia on 23 February 1944, during the Second World War. The expulsion was ordered by NKVD chief Lavrentiy Beria and approved by Joseph Stalin, as part of a Soviet forced settlement program and population transfer that affected several million members of ethnic minorities in the USSR between the 1930s and the 1950s.

The deportation was officially justified by accusations that the Chechens and Ingush had collaborated en masse with Nazi Germany, although German forces had occupied only a small part of the region briefly in 1942 and many Chechens and Ingush served in the Red Army. The Checheno-Ingush Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was dissolved and its territory divided among neighbouring regions.

Background

The Chechens and Ingush, collectively known as the Vainakh peoples, are indigenous peoples of the North Caucasus who had resisted Russian conquest in the 19th-century Caucasian War. Soviet collectivisation and anti-religious campaigns provoked repeated uprisings in the 1920s and 1930s, most notably the insurgency led by Hasan Israilov from 1940.

The deportation

On 23 February 1944 — Red Army Day — NKVD troops surrounded villages across the republic. Residents were given minutes to pack and were loaded onto trains, many in unheated freight and cattle wagons, for journeys lasting weeks to the Kazakh and Kirghiz Soviet Socialist Republics. Those unable to travel, including hospital patients and inhabitants of remote mountain villages, were in some cases killed; the most infamous incident was the Khaibakh massacre, in which hundreds of villagers were allegedly burned alive in a stable.

Exile and return

In exile the deportees lived as "special settlers" under NKVD supervision, barred from leaving their assigned settlements. Hunger, cold, and disease caused very high mortality in the first years. After Stalin's death and Nikita Khrushchev's 1956 Secret Speech, the Checheno-Ingush ASSR was restored on 9 January 1957, and the surviving Chechens and Ingush were permitted to return home — where they found their houses and land occupied by settlers, seeding later conflicts, including the Prigorodny district dispute between Ingush and Ossetians.

Recognition

In 2004 the European Parliament recognised the deportation as an act of genocide. The event remains central to Chechen and Ingush collective memory and is commemorated annually on 23 February.
