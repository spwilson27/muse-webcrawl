Erebuni Fortress

LocationArin Berd hill, Yerevan, Armenia
Founded782 BC
FounderArgishti I of Urartu
MaterialStone, mud-brick

Erebuni Fortress (Armenian: Էրեբունի ամրոց) is an Urartian fortified city founded by King Argishti I in 782 BC, located on the Arin Berd hill in present-day Yerevan, the capital of Armenia. Erebuni is regarded as the birthplace of Yerevan: the city's name is traditionally derived from "Erebuni", and Yerevan officially celebrated its 2,750th anniversary in 1968, counting from the fortress's foundation.

History

A cuneiform inscription found at the site records that Argishti I, son of Menua, built the stronghold in the year of his accession campaign, settling it with thousands of captives from conquered lands. Erebuni served as a major administrative and military centre of the kingdom of Urartu at the northern frontier, guarding the Ararat plain and storing tribute and harvests. Successive kings Sarduri II and Rusa II maintained and enlarged the complex.

After Urartu's decline, the site continued in use into the Achaemenid Persian period; excavations have found Achaemenid-era structures and objects such as silver rhyta. The city eventually shifted toward the nearby site that developed into historic Yerevan.

Archaeology and architecture

Excavations began in the 1950s after a farmer found the foundation inscription stone in 1950. The fortress occupies a roughly triangular plateau with massive stone foundation walls that supported mud-brick superstructures. Notable structures include a palace complex, storerooms for grain, wine and oil, and a temple dedicated to the god Haldi, later converted; wall paintings in vivid colours — among the few surviving Urartian frescoes — were uncovered and are preserved in museums. Finds include cuneiform inscriptions, bronze helmets and shields, pottery, and jewellery.

The Erebuni Museum, opened at the foot of the hill in 1968, displays material from the fortress and from the related Urartian site of Teishebaini (Karmir Blur). The fortress walls have been partly reconstructed and the site is a protected monument and a symbol of Yerevan.
