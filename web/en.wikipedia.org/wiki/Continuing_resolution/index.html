Continuing resolution

A continuing resolution (often abbreviated CR) is a type of appropriations legislation in the United States that appropriates money to specific federal government agencies, departments and programs for a defined period of time, typically at the previous year's funding level, when the formal appropriations bills for the fiscal year have not been enacted.

Purpose and effect

The United States federal fiscal year begins on 1 October. If Congress has not passed the twelve regular appropriations bills by that date — or by the expiry of a previous continuing resolution — federal agencies face a funding gap that can force a government shutdown. A continuing resolution prevents this by temporarily extending funding, usually prohibiting new programs or projects ("new starts") and sometimes including specific exceptions called anomalies.

History

Continuing resolutions have become a routine feature of the federal budget process; in many recent decades most fiscal years have begun under a CR, and some years have been funded entirely by a series of them, supplemented by omnibus appropriations acts.
