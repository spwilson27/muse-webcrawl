San Andrés Cholula is a municipality and town in the Mexican state of Puebla, immediately west of the state capital, Puebla de Zaragoza. Together with San Pedro Cholula it forms the modern city of Cholula, one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the Americas, founded around 500 BCE and sacred in pre-Hispanic times to the god Quetzalcóatl. The municipality has grown rapidly as a suburb of Puebla and hosts the campus of the Universidad de las Américas Puebla.

The Great Pyramid of Cholula, partly within San Andrés, is crowned by the colonial church of Nuestra Señora de los Remedios; the volcano Popocatépetl rises behind.

History and geography

Pre-Hispanic Cholula was a major religious and commercial centre; its Great Pyramid (Tlachihualtépetl), the largest pyramid by volume in the world, lies on the boundary between San Andrés and San Pedro. Under Spanish rule the two parishes developed as separate municipalities — San Andrés traditionally associated with the indigenous population and San Pedro with the Spanish town — a duality that persists in local identity, festivals, and competing claims to the pyramid and its churches.

The municipality comprises the municipal seat of San Andrés Cholula and localities such as San Bernardino Tlaxcalancingo, San Antonio Cacalotepec, San Rafael Comac, and Santa María Tonantzintla, the last famous for its exuberantly decorated indigenous Baroque church. Since the late twentieth century the area has urbanized heavily, with universities, shopping centres, and housing developments along the Puebla–Cholula corridor, while conflicts over land, development, and the proposed projects near the pyramid archaeological zone have periodically made it a focus of heritage-protection campaigns.

Culture

San Andrés shares in Cholula's intense calendar of Catholic festivals, in which dozens of neighbourhood churches maintain round-the-year celebrations with fireworks, processions, and floral decorations. Local specialties include the cemita sandwich and the ritual use of the pyramid hill during the spring equinox festival.
