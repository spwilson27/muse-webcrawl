Mithril

Mithril is a fictional metal found in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth writings. It is a silvery, precious metal, stronger than steel but much lighter, which does not tarnish or dim. The name combines the Sindarin elements mith ("grey") and ril ("glitter"); in Khuzdul it was called Zirakzigil, and in Quenya míre forms are also attested. The Dwarves valued it so greatly that they named it "true-silver".

In Tolkien's works

In The Lord of the Rings, mithril is mined only in Khazad-dûm (Moria), where the Dwarves' delving for it awakened the Balrog. The mail shirt given by Thorin Oakenshield to Bilbo Baggins in The Hobbit — later passed to Frodo — proves strong enough to turn a spear thrust and the knife of an Orc chieftain, saving Frodo's life. Gandalf remarks that its worth was greater than the entire Shire and everything in it.

In popular culture

Mithril has been widely adopted in fantasy role-playing games, video games, and literature as a archetypal magical metal, appearing in settings such as Dungeons & Dragons and numerous computer games under spellings including "mithril", "mithral", and "mythril".
