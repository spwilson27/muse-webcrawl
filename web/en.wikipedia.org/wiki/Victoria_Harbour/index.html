Victoria Harbour

Victoria Harbour is a natural landform harbour in Hong Kong separating Hong Kong Island in the south from the Kowloon Peninsula in the north. Known for its panoramic views of the city's skyline, it is a major tourist attraction and the historical heart of Hong Kong's development as a trading port. The harbour was named after Queen Victoria in 1843, during the period of British colonial rule.

Geography

The harbour covers roughly 41 square kilometres. Its deep, sheltered waters made it an ideal anchorage and were a principal reason for the establishment of the British colony. The eastern entrance is the Lei Yue Mun strait, and the western approaches open toward Lantau Island. Extensive land reclamation on both shores — including the Central and Wan Chai reclamations and West Kowloon — has narrowed the harbour considerably since the nineteenth century, prompting legal protection under the Protection of the Harbour Ordinance of 1997.

Transport

The Star Ferry has carried passengers across the harbour since 1888 and remains an icon of the city. Three road tunnels — the Cross-Harbour Tunnel (1972), the Eastern Harbour Crossing (1989), and the Western Harbour Crossing (1997) — supplement the ferries, along with several MTR rail crossings.

Events

The harbour hosts the New Year and Lunar New Year fireworks displays, the "A Symphony of Lights" nightly multimedia show along its shores, and annual dragon boat and harbour swimming races.
