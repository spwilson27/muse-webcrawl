Perfection of Wisdom

Perfection of Wisdom is a translation of the Sanskrit term Prajñāpāramitā, a central concept of Mahāyāna Buddhism referring to the perfected way of seeing the nature of reality — above all, the emptiness (śūnyatā) of all phenomena — and to the large body of Mahāyāna sutras that expound it. Prajñāpāramitā is the sixth of the six perfections (pāramitās) cultivated by a bodhisattva.

The Prajnaparamita sutras

The literature began to be composed in India around the 1st century BCE to 1st century CE. Its best-known texts include the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā (Perfection of Wisdom in 8,000 Lines), and the much shorter and hugely influential Heart Sutra and Diamond Sutra, which compress the teaching into paradoxical statements such as "form is emptiness, emptiness is form." Larger versions expand the text to 100,000 lines.

Teachings

The sutras teach that all dharmas (phenomena) lack intrinsic existence (svabhāva) and that even the Buddha, the path, and nirvana are empty — a point developed philosophically by Nāgārjuna and the Madhyamaka school. In East Asian Buddhism, Prajñāpāramitā was also personified as a goddess, "the mother of all Buddhas," and her texts were objects of devotion, copying, and recitation.
