Chevron Corporation

Industry: Oil and gas
Founded: September 10, 1879 (as Pacific Coast Oil Co.)
Headquarters: San Ramon, California, U.S.

Chevron Corporation is an American multinational energy corporation predominantly specializing in oil and gas. The second-largest direct descendant of Standard Oil, it is headquartered in San Ramon, California, and active in more than 180 countries.

History

Chevron traces its origins to the Pacific Coast Oil Company, founded in 1879 in San Francisco. It became part of John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil trust and, after the 1911 breakup of Standard Oil, operated as Standard Oil Company (California), or Socal. In 1984, Socal merged with Gulf Oil in what was then the largest merger in history, and the combined company adopted the name Chevron. In 2001 Chevron merged with Texaco to form ChevronTexaco, reverting to Chevron in 2005. In 2005 it also acquired Unocal Corporation.

Operations

Chevron is engaged in every aspect of the oil and natural gas industries, including hydrocarbon exploration and production, refining, marketing and transport, chemicals manufacturing and sales, and power generation. Its brands include Chevron, Texaco, and Caltex.

Environmental record

Chevron has faced significant litigation and criticism over environmental contamination, most notably the Lago Agrio oil field case in Ecuador involving Texaco's operations there, as well as disputes over emissions and oil spills in several countries.
