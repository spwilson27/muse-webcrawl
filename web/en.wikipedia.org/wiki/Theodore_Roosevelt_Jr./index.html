Theodore Roosevelt Jr.

Born13 September 1887, Oyster Bay, New York
Died12 July 1944, Normandy, France
ServiceUnited States Army
RankBrigadier general
AwardsMedal of Honor, Distinguished Service Cross, Silver Star, Purple Heart

Theodore Roosevelt Jr. (1887–1944) was an American soldier, businessman and public official, the eldest son of President Theodore Roosevelt. He served with distinction in both world wars and is remembered above all for leading troops ashore at Utah Beach on D-Day despite arthritis, a heart condition and the need for a cane. He received the Medal of Honor for his actions in Normandy and died in France a little more than a month after the landing.

Early life and First World War

Roosevelt grew up in the intense public atmosphere of the Roosevelt family at Sagamore Hill. Educated at Harvard, he entered business and banking but moved quickly toward public service. When the United States entered the First World War, he helped organize and train the 1st Division and served in France, where he was wounded and gassed. His wartime reputation for sharing danger with enlisted men followed him throughout his career.

After the war he was a founder of the American Legion and served as Assistant Secretary of the Navy under President Warren G. Harding. He later became Governor of Puerto Rico and then Governor-General of the Philippines under Herbert Hoover, posts that mixed reformist administration with the burdens of American colonial governance.

Politics and interwar years

Roosevelt’s political career was complicated by his famous name and by association, however unfair, with the Teapot Dome era through his Navy Department service; he was not implicated in corruption, but opponents used the period against him. He ran unsuccessfully for governor of New York in 1924, losing to Al Smith. With his wife Eleanor Butler Roosevelt he also wrote and travelled widely, cultivating the family image of vigorous public life.

Second World War

Returning to active duty before Pearl Harbor, Roosevelt commanded infantry in North Africa and Sicily. He was admired by soldiers for front-line visibility and blunt humour, though his informal style sometimes troubled superiors. For the Normandy invasion he repeatedly requested permission to land with the first wave of the 4th Infantry Division. Major General Raymond O. Barton finally approved, making Roosevelt the only general known to land with the initial assault on 6 June 1944.

At Utah Beach he discovered that the landing craft had come ashore south of the planned sector. His reported remark—“We’ll start the war from right here!”—became famous because it captured the decision to adapt and push inland from the actual beachhead. He moved among units under fire, redirected traffic, coordinated attacks on German positions and helped turn confusion into momentum.

Death and honours

Roosevelt died of a heart attack on 12 July 1944 near Méautis and was buried in Normandy; he later was reinterred at the Normandy American Cemetery beside his brother Quentin, who had been killed in the First World War. His Medal of Honor citation praised conspicuous gallantry, personal bravery and leadership at the risk of life above and beyond the call of duty. Historians continue to debate elements of his generalship, but few question his physical courage or the morale effect of his presence at Utah Beach.
