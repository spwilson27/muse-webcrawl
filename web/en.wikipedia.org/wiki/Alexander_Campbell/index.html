Alexander Campbell (12 September 1788 – 4 March 1866) was an Irish-born American minister and reformer who, with his father Thomas Campbell, led the Restoration Movement (also called the Stone–Campbell Movement), a effort to restore New Testament Christianity and Christian unity. The movement gave rise to the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), the Churches of Christ and related bodies.

Early life

Campbell was born near Ballymena, Ireland, the son of Thomas Campbell, a Seceder Presbyterian minister. He studied briefly at the University of Glasgow. In 1809 he emigrated to the United States, joining his father in western Pennsylvania, where Thomas had issued the Declaration and Address, a founding document of the movement.

Ministry and reforms

Rejecting creeds and denominational divisions, Campbell advocated believer's baptism by immersion, weekly communion and congregational independence, arguing that the Bible alone should guide the church. He edited the influential journals The Christian Baptist (1823–1830) and The Millennial Harbinger (1830–1866), published a translation of the New Testament (The Living Oracles, 1826), and became famous for public debates, including his 1837 debate with Catholic bishop John B. Purcell and his 1843 debate with Presbyterian minister Nathan Rice.

Bethany College

In 1840 Campbell founded Bethany College in Bethany, Virginia (now West Virginia), serving as its first president. He died there in 1866; his home and study are preserved as a historic site.
