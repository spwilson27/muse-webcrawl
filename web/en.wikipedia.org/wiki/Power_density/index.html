Power density

Power density is the amount of power (time rate of energy transfer) per unit volume. In energy transformation devices such as batteries, fuel cells, and engines, power density is a key figure of merit, usually expressed in watts per litre (W/L) or watts per cubic metre (W/m³). Its analogue per unit mass is called specific power. For electromagnetic radiation, power density may instead be expressed per unit area, as in the flux density of a radio signal or sunlight.

Applications

Power density determines how compact and light a power source can be. High power density is especially valuable in vehicles and portable electronics: supercapacitors have very high power density but low energy density, whereas lithium-ion batteries occupy a middle ground and fuels have high energy density. In computing, the power density of microprocessors — heat generated per unit area of chip — rose dramatically with scaling and became a central limit on clock frequencies in the mid-2000s, contributing to the shift toward multi-core processors.

Related quantities

Power density is often plotted against energy density on a Ragone chart to compare energy storage technologies. It must be distinguished from power-to-weight ratio, which expresses power per unit mass.
