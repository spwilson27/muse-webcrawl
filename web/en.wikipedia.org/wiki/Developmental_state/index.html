Developmental state

Developmental state is a term used by international political economy scholars to refer to state-led macroeconomic planning in East Asia in the late 20th century. In this model of capitalism, the state has more independent — or autonomous — political power, as well as more control over the economy. The term was coined by Chalmers Johnson in his 1982 study of Japan's MITI.

A developmental state is characterised by having strong state intervention, extensive regulation and planning, and a bureaucracy capable of guiding private industry toward national development goals such as export-led industrialisation. The model is associated with the rapid growth of Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Singapore — the "East Asian miracle" — and has been debated in relation to China's economic rise and to development policy in other regions.

Scholars such as Robert Wade, Alice Amsden, and Peter Evans ("embedded autonomy") analysed how state capacity and state–business relations produced rapid industrialisation, while critics questioned the model's transferability and its reliance on authoritarian governance in several cases.
