Hurdy-gurdy

The hurdy-gurdy is a stringed instrument that produces sound by a hand-crank-turned, rosined wheel rubbing against the strings. The wheel functions much like a violin bow, and single notes played on the instrument sound similar to those of a violin. Melodies are played on a keyboard that presses tangents against one or more strings to change their pitch.

History

The instrument descends from the medieval organistrum, a large instrument played by two people in monasteries. By the 18th century it enjoyed a vogue in the French court as the vielle à roue, while also becoming associated with street musicians and beggars elsewhere in Europe. It remains important in the folk musics of France, Hungary, Poland, Ukraine, and Spain.

Construction

A hurdy-gurdy typically has one or two melody (chanterelle) strings passing through the keybox, plus drone strings providing a constant accompaniment. A rhythmic buzzing bridge (the chien, or "dog") can be made to rattle by accelerating the crank, a technique known as coup de poignet.
