Arthur Michael Kleinman (born March 11, 1941) is an American psychiatrist and medical anthropologist. He is the Esther and Sidney Rabb Professor of Anthropology at Harvard University and a professor of medical anthropology and psychiatry at Harvard Medical School. Kleinman is widely regarded as one of the founders of modern medical anthropology, a field that examines how culture shapes the experience, interpretation, and treatment of illness.

Early life and education

Kleinman was born in New York City. He earned both his bachelor's degree and his medical degree at Stanford University. Initially trained as a psychiatrist, he developed an interest in cross-cultural psychiatry during clinical work and research in Taiwan and China, where he studied how patients understood and coped with conditions such as depression and neurasthenia.

Career

Before joining Harvard, Kleinman taught at the University of Washington, where he helped build one of the first programs in medical anthropology. At Harvard he directed the Department of Social Medicine and played a central role in the university's programs in global health and social medicine. He also chaired the Department of Anthropology and directed the Harvard University Asia Center.

Concepts and contributions

Kleinman drew a distinction between disease—the biomedical malfunction as understood by practitioners—and illness—the lived experience of suffering as understood by patients and families. His concept of explanatory models describes the notions that patients and healers hold about an episode of sickness: its causes, timing, pathophysiology, course, and appropriate treatment. Eliciting a patient's explanatory model, he argued, is essential to effective and humane clinical care.

His later work addressed social suffering, the moral dimensions of caregiving, and the experiences of family caregivers, themes developed in works including The Soul of Care (2019), a memoir of caring for his wife Joan, who had early-onset Alzheimer's disease.
