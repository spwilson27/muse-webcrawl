Independent state legislature theory

The independent state legislature theory (ISL) is a reading of the Elections Clause and Electors Clause of the U.S. Constitution holding that state legislatures have exclusive, largely unchecked power to regulate federal elections, free from state courts, governors and state constitutions.

Origins

The theory gained prominence after Bush v. Gore (2000), in a concurrence by Chief Justice Rehnquist, and was later advanced in challenges to state-court rulings on redistricting and election rules in the 2020 election cycle.

Moore v. Harper

In Moore v. Harper (2023), the Supreme Court rejected the maximal ISL theory 6–3, holding that the Elections Clause does not exempt state legislatures from ordinary state judicial review, while reserving a federal check if state courts transgress the ordinary bounds of judicial review.

Criticism and support

Supporters cite the text's assignment to "the Legislature thereof" and federalism. Critics, including many legal historians, argue the founding-era understanding included normal state constitutional constraints and warn the theory would destabilise election administration.
