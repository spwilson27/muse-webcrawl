Sir Thomas Bouch (25 February 1822 – 30 October 1880) was a British railway engineer, chiefly remembered for the Tay Bridge disaster, in which his first Tay Rail Bridge collapsed in a storm on 28 December 1879, killing all 75 people aboard a crossing train.

Career

Bouch specialised in economical railway construction, introducing the train ferry and roll-on/roll-off concepts in Scotland, and built many lines and viaducts including the St Albans and Durham routes. He was engaged to design bridges across the Tay and the Forth.

Tay Bridge

The first Tay Bridge, then the world's longest, opened in 1878 and Bouch was knighted in 1879. The Court of Inquiry found the bridge badly designed, badly constructed and badly maintained, with inadequate allowance for wind loading. Bouch's health broke; he died the following year, and his Forth Bridge design was abandoned in favour of the cantilever design of Fowler and Baker.

Legacy

The disaster transformed British bridge engineering, leading to explicit wind-pressure standards and more rigorous inspection.
