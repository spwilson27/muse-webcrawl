Nicosia (Greek: Λευκωσία, Lefkosia; Turkish: Lefkoşa) is the capital and largest city of Cyprus. It is located near the centre of the Mesaoria plain, on the banks of the Pedieos River. Nicosia is the world's last divided capital city: since 1974 its northern part has been the capital of the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus, separated from the south by the United Nations Buffer Zone, often called the Green Line.

History

The site has been inhabited since the Bronze Age. The ancient city of Ledra stood here, and the settlement became a Byzantine town before passing to the Lusignan kings of Cyprus in 1192. Under Venetian rule in the 16th century the city was ringed with its distinctive circular star-shaped walls, built in 1567 to defend against the Ottomans, who nevertheless conquered the city in 1570. Britain took control of Cyprus in 1878, and Nicosia became the capital of independent Cyprus in 1960. Intercommunal violence in 1963 led to the drawing of the Green Line through the city; the Turkish invasion of 1974 made the division permanent.

Landmarks

The old city within the Venetian walls contains the Selimiye Mosque (the former Gothic cathedral of St Sophia), the Büyük Han caravanserai, Famagusta Gate, and the Cyprus Museum. Ledra Street, once a symbol of division, reopened as a crossing point in 2008.

Economy

Nicosia is the financial and administrative centre of Cyprus, home to the island's major banks, the stock exchange, and most foreign embassies.
