Voltage spike

In electrical engineering, a voltage spike (also called a surge) is a transient event, typically lasting microseconds to milliseconds, in which the voltage in an electrical circuit rises well above its intended level. Voltage spikes can be caused by lightning strikes, power outages, tripped circuit breakers, short circuits, or power transitions in other large equipment on the same power line.

Causes

Lightning is the most dramatic natural source: a nearby strike can induce very large transients on power and signal lines. Man-made spikes commonly arise when inductive loads such as motors and transformers are switched off, collapsing magnetic fields and releasing stored energy as a brief high-voltage pulse.

Effects

Spikes can degrade or destroy semiconductor junctions, corrupt data, trip protective devices, and gradually weaken insulation. Repeated smaller transients shorten the service life of electronic equipment even when no immediate failure occurs.

Protection

Surge protectors and transient voltage suppression devices, such as metal-oxide varistors (MOVs) and TVS diodes, clamp excess voltage and divert the energy of a spike away from sensitive loads. Whole-building surge protective devices are installed at service entrances in lightning-prone areas.
