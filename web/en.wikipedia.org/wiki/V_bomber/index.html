V bomber

The V bombers were the Royal Air Force (RAF) aircraft during the 1950s and 1960s that comprised the United Kingdom's strategic nuclear strike force known officially as the V force or Bomber Command Main Force. The three models of strategic bomber, all named for words beginning with V, were the Vickers Valiant (first flown 1951), the Handley Page Victor (1952), and the Avro Vulcan (1952).

Development

Post-war requirements for a jet bomber able to deliver Britain's first atomic bombs produced three parallel designs. The Valiant, the most conventional, entered service first (1955) and dropped Britain's first hydrogen bomb in Operation Grapple (1957). The crescent-winged Victor and the delta-winged Vulcan followed, carrying weapons such as the Blue Danube and Yellow Sun bombs and later the Blue Steel stand-off missile.

Operational history

The V force stood at quick reaction alert through the height of the Cold War, later switching from high-altitude to low-level penetration as Soviet air defences improved. The Valiant was retired in 1965 after fatigue cracking. When the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines assumed the deterrent role in 1969, the Vulcan and Victor continued in conventional bombing, reconnaissance and — in the Victor's case, extensively — air-to-air refuelling roles. The Vulcan gained fame for the ultra-long-range Black Buck raids on Port Stanley during the 1982 Falklands War. The last Vulcans left bomber service in 1984; Victor tankers served until 1993.
