Direct comparison test

If each term of one series is bounded by the corresponding term of another, their convergence behaviour is linked.

In mathematics, the direct comparison test (or simply the comparison test) is a criterion for determining whether an infinite series converges or diverges, by comparing its terms with those of another series whose behaviour is already known.

Statement

Let Σan and Σbn be series with non-negative terms, and suppose that an ≤ bn for all sufficiently large n. Then:

  • If Σbn converges, then Σan also converges.
  • If Σan diverges, then Σbn also diverges.

An analogous version applies to improper integrals: if 0 ≤ f(x) ≤ g(x) on an interval, then convergence of ∫g implies convergence of ∫f, and divergence of ∫f implies divergence of ∫g.

Use and related tests

The test is typically used with standard benchmarks such as geometric series and p-series (for example, showing that Σ 1/(n2+1) converges by comparing it with Σ 1/n2). When a direct bound is awkward, the limit comparison test — based on the limit of an/bn — is often more convenient. The comparison test is one of the foundations of the analysis of convergence, alongside the ratio test, root test and integral test.
