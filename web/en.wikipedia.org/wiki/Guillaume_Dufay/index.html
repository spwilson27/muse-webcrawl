Guillaume Du Fay

Bornc. 1397, Beersel, Duchy of Brabant
Died27 November 1474, Cambrai, France
OccupationComposer, singer, priest
EraEarly Renaissance (Burgundian School)

Guillaume Du Fay (also Du Fayt, Du Fay; c. 1397 – 27 November 1474) was a Franco-Flemish composer of the early Renaissance. He was one of the most famous and influential composers in Europe during the mid-15th century, and his music bridged the late medieval and early Renaissance styles. Du Fay is often regarded as the leading composer of the Burgundian School, and his works helped define the international polyphonic style that would dominate European music for the following century.

Life

Du Fay was born in Beersel, near Brussels, around 1397, the illegitimate son of a priest named Marie. He was educated at Cambrai Cathedral, where he was a choirboy and probably studied with Richard Loqueville. He later became a priest. In the 1420s he travelled to Italy, serving the Malatesta family in Rimini and Pesaro. By 1428 he was a singer in the papal choir in Rome, a post he held for six years. He later served the court of the Duke of Savoy before returning to Cambrai, where he spent most of his later life as a canon of the cathedral. He died in Cambrai in 1474 and was buried in the cathedral; his tombstone was rediscovered in the 19th century.

Music

Du Fay composed in nearly all the forms of his time: masses, motets, Magnificats, hymns, antiphons, and secular chansons in French and Italian. His output includes six complete cyclic masses (or seven, counting the disputed Missa Ecce ancilla Domini), numerous mass movements, some 80 chansons, and more than a dozen isorhythmic motets. His best-known works include the Missa Se la face ay pale, the Missa L'homme armé (one of the earliest settings based on the famous L'homme armé tune), and the motet Nuper rosarum flores, written for the consecration of the Florence Cathedral in 1436, whose structure is sometimes linked to the proportions of Brunelleschi's dome.

Du Fay's style is characterised by clear textures, elegant melodic lines, and a blending of English, French, and Italian influences. His later works show a growing interest in the cyclic mass and the use of a cantus firmus to unify multi-movement compositions.

Legacy

Du Fay's reputation was immense in his lifetime and after his death; composers such as Johannes Ockeghem and Josquin des Prez were influenced by his music. He is credited with helping to establish the four-voice texture that became standard in Renaissance polyphony.
