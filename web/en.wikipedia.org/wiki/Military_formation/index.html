Military formation

A military formation is the arrangement of moving troops, vehicles, ships, or aircraft in a coordinated manner, or an organisational unit of an armed force. The term covers both tactical dispositions on the battlefield and standing units such as divisions and brigades.

Tactical formations

Historically, infantry fought in dense formations such as the phalanx, the Roman maniple and legion, pike blocks, and the line and column of the gunpowder era. Firepower and mechanisation dispersed battlefield formations in the 20th century, favouring smaller, flexible units, while parade-ground and marching formations survive for ceremony and movement. Common movement formations include column, line, echelon, wedge, and diamond.

Units as formations

In organisational usage, a formation is a military unit larger than a regiment — typically a brigade, division, corps, or army — composed of subordinate units and commanded by a general officer.
