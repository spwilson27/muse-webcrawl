Võro language

Native toSouthern Estonia
SpeakersAround 75,000
FamilyUralic → Finnic → South Estonian
Writing systemLatin

Võro (Võro: võro kiilʼ) is a language belonging to the Finnic branch of the Uralic languages. Traditionally considered a dialect of the South Estonian dialect group of the Estonian language, it is nowadays widely recognised as a distinct language with its own literary standard.

History and status

South Estonian literary language was in use from the 16th to the 19th century, when the standard based on North Estonian became dominant. A Võro language revival began in the late 1980s; the Võro Institute was founded in 1995 to promote the language, which is now taught in some schools and used in local media. Võro retains features lost in standard Estonian, such as vowel harmony, and has a distinctive glottal stop marked with an apostrophe.

Geography

Võro is spoken in the historic Võro County in south-eastern Estonia and the surrounding parishes. Related South Estonian varieties include Seto, Tartu, and Mulgi.
