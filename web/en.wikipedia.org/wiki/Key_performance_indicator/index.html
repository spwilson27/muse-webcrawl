Key performance indicator

A key performance indicator (KPI) is a type of performance measurement. KPIs evaluate the success of an organization or of a particular activity (such as projects, programs, products, and other initiatives) in which it engages. Choosing the right KPIs relies upon a good understanding of what is important to the organization.

Definition and characteristics

Often success is simply the repeated, periodic achievement of some levels of operational goal (e.g. zero defects, 10/10 customer satisfaction), and sometimes success is defined in terms of making progress toward strategic goals. Accordingly, KPIs are commonly defined in a way that is understandable, meaningful, and measurable. They are rarely defined in such a way that their fulfillment would be hampered by actions not under the control of the organization, or that lack an accountable owner.

A widely used guideline is that indicators should be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. KPIs may be leading (predictive of future results, such as pipeline activity) or lagging (reporting past outcomes, such as quarterly profit).

Examples

  • Finance: net profit margin, revenue growth, return on investment
  • Sales and marketing: customer acquisition cost, conversion rate, customer lifetime value, churn rate
  • Operations: defect rate, overall equipment effectiveness, on-time delivery
  • Human resources: employee turnover, time-to-fill vacancies, absenteeism
  • Digital products: monthly active users, bounce rate, net promoter score

Use in management frameworks

KPIs are central to management systems such as the balanced scorecard, which groups indicators across financial, customer, internal-process, and learning perspectives, and to objectives and key results (OKRs), in which "key results" function similarly. Critics warn that an excess of indicators dilutes attention, and that measures tied to incentives can be gamed — an instance of Goodhart's law, "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."
