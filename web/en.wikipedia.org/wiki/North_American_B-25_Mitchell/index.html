North American B-25 Mitchell

[Image: B-25 Mitchell bomber]

The North American B-25 Mitchell is an American medium bomber that was introduced in 1941 and named in honour of Brigadier General William "Billy" Mitchell, a pioneer of U.S. military aviation. Built by North American Aviation, nearly 10,000 B-25s were produced, more than any other American twin-engine combat aircraft of the Second World War.

Operational history

The B-25 served in every theatre of the war with the United States Army Air Forces and with Allied air forces including the Royal Air Force, Royal Australian Air Force, and, under Lend-Lease, the Soviet Union. It is best known for the Doolittle Raid of 18 April 1942, when sixteen B-25Bs launched from the carrier USS Hornet to bomb Tokyo in the first American air raid on the Japanese home islands. Gunship variants such as the B-25G and B-25H mounted a 75 mm cannon in the nose for anti-shipping strikes in the Pacific.

Design

The B-25 was a mid-wing, twin-tail monoplane powered by two Wright R-2600 Twin Cyclone radial engines, with a typical bomb load of about 3,000 lb (1,360 kg) and a crew of five or six. Later solid-nose "strafer" versions carried up to fourteen forward-firing .50-calibre machine guns.

Legacy

The type remained in service after the war as a trainer and transport (as the TB-25) into the late 1950s. Dozens of airworthy Mitchells survive with warbird operators, and the aircraft features prominently in the novel and film Catch-22.
