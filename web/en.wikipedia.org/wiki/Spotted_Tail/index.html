Spotted Tail (Lakota: Siŋté Glešká; c. 1823 – August 5, 1881) was a chief of the Brulé (Sicangu) Lakota people who rose to prominence as a warrior and became one of the most influential Native American diplomats of the nineteenth century. Pragmatic about the overwhelming power of the United States, he worked to secure the best possible terms for his people through negotiation rather than prolonged war.

Early life and rise

Spotted Tail was born along the White River in present-day South Dakota. He took his name from a raccoon-skin cap with the tail attached that he wore as a young man. He gained a reputation as a formidable warrior in fights against the Pawnee and Crow. After the Grattan massacre of 1854 and the subsequent punitive campaign by General William S. Harney, Spotted Tail was among the Brulé who surrendered and were imprisoned at Fort Leavenworth; the experience convinced him of the futility of open war with the United States.

Diplomacy

Spotted Tail was a leading figure in the negotiations that produced the Fort Laramie Treaty of 1868, although he insisted the government close the Bozeman Trail forts before signing. Thereafter he travelled repeatedly to Washington, met presidents including Ulysses S. Grant, and manoeuvred between federal officials and his own people's factions. During the Great Sioux War of 1876–77 he kept his band out of the fighting and helped persuade many northern Lakota to come in to the agencies. The United States government cultivated him as a counterweight to Red Cloud, designating him head chief of the Lakota agencies — a status many Lakota resented as an American invention.

Later life and death

Spotted Tail established his agency on Rosebud Creek in present-day South Dakota (the modern Rosebud Indian Reservation) and supported schooling for Lakota children while resisting efforts to fragment Lakota land. On August 5, 1881, he was shot and killed by Crow Dog, a fellow Brulé, in a dispute whose roots lay in tribal politics and personal grievances. The resulting federal prosecution, Ex parte Crow Dog (1883), reached the U.S. Supreme Court, which held that federal courts lacked jurisdiction over crimes by one Native American against another in Indian Country — a decision Congress answered with the Major Crimes Act of 1885. Spotted Tail's legacy is commemorated by Sinte Gleska University on the Rosebud Reservation, which bears his Lakota name.
