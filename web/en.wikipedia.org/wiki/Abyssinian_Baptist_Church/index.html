Abyssinian Baptist Church

The Abyssinian Baptist Church is a historic African American Baptist congregation in Harlem, New York City, founded in 1808. One of the oldest Black churches in the United States, it became a leading religious and political institution during the Harlem Renaissance and the civil rights era under pastors Adam Clayton Powell Sr. and his son, Adam Clayton Powell Jr.

History

The church was founded in 1808 by African American parishioners who withdrew from the First Baptist Church of New York in protest at segregated seating, together with Ethiopian merchant seamen; the name "Abyssinian" reflects the old name for Ethiopia. The congregation worshipped at several lower Manhattan sites before following Harlem's growing Black population north, completing its present church on West 138th Street in 1923.

The Powells

Adam Clayton Powell Sr. became pastor in 1908 and built the church into a centre of community life, education and civil rights advocacy. His son, Adam Clayton Powell Jr., succeeded him in 1937 and combined the pulpit with a political career, becoming in 1944 the first African American from New York elected to the U.S. House of Representatives, where he chaired the powerful Education and Labor Committee.

Later ministry

Under the Reverend Samuel DeWitt Proctor and, from 1989, the Reverend Calvin O. Butts III, the church remained prominent in Harlem civic life, founding the Abyssinian Development Corporation, which built housing and commercial projects in the neighbourhood. The church building is a New York City designated landmark and is known for its gospel choir and its popularity with visiting congregations and tourists.
