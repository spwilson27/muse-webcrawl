McMartin preschool trial

McMartin preschool trial
Date: 1983–1990
Location: Los Angeles, California
Defendants: Virginia McMartin, Ray Buckey and five others
Outcome: All charges dropped or acquittals; no convictions

The McMartin preschool trial was a day-care sex-abuse case in the 1980s, prosecuted by the Los Angeles County District Attorney. Members of the McMartin family, who operated a preschool in Manhattan Beach, California, were charged with numerous acts of sexual abuse of children in their care. The case became one of the longest and most expensive criminal trials in American history, running from arrests in 1984 to the final dismissal of charges in 1990, and ended with no convictions. It is now regarded as a central episode of the "satanic panic" and day-care abuse hysteria of the era.

Allegations and investigation

The case began in 1983 with an accusation by the mother of one pupil against teacher Ray Buckey. Police letters to parents and interviews of hundreds of children at the Children's Institute International produced escalating claims: ritual abuse, underground tunnels, animal sacrifice and other elaborate scenarios, for which no physical evidence was found. Prosecutors charged seven defendants with hundreds of counts involving dozens of children; charges against five were dropped in 1986 for insufficient evidence.

Trials

Ray Buckey and his mother Peggy McMartin Buckey were tried from 1987 to 1990; the jury acquitted on most counts and deadlocked on the remainder. A brief retrial of Buckey ended in another hung jury, and all charges were dismissed in 1990. The trials cost an estimated $15 million, then the most expensive in American criminal history.

Legacy

The case prompted lasting changes in how children are interviewed as witnesses, with research into suggestive questioning and protocols for forensic interviewing of minors, and it is frequently cited in discussions of moral panic, false memory and miscarriages of justice.
