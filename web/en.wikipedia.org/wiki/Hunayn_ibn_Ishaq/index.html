Hunayn ibn Ishaq

Born808, al-Hira
Died873, Baghdad
OccupationPhysician, translator, scholar
Known forTranslations of Galen and Greek medical works into Arabic

Hunayn ibn Ishaq (808–873), known in Latin as Johannitius, was an influential Arab Nestorian Christian physician, translator and scholar of the Abbasid era. Working at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad, he led the translation movement that rendered Greek scientific and medical texts into Syriac and Arabic.

Translations

Hunayn set new standards for accuracy, collating multiple Greek manuscripts rather than translating word for word. He and his school translated much of Galen, Hippocrates, Plato, Aristotle and Dioscorides. His own medical works include the Ten Treatises on the Eye, the earliest systematic textbook of ophthalmology, and the Questions on Medicine, a standard introduction used for centuries.

Legacy

Through Hunayn's translations, Greek medicine entered the Islamic world and, via later Latin versions, medieval Europe, shaping medical education for centuries.
