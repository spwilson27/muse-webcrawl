First Schleswig War

The First Schleswig War (German: Schleswig-Holsteinischer Krieg; Danish: Treårskrigen, "the Three Years' War") was a military conflict in southern Denmark and northern Germany rooted in the Schleswig-Holstein Question. It was waged from 1848 to 1852 by a provisional German-minded government of the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein, supported by the German Confederation and Prussia, against Denmark. It ended with a Danish victory and the international settlement of the London Protocol of 1852.


The Battle of Isted (Idstedt), 25 July 1850, the largest battle of the war.

The Schleswig-Holstein Question

Schleswig was a Danish fief with a mixed Danish- and German-speaking population; Holstein was a member of the German Confederation inhabited mainly by German speakers; both were ruled by the Danish king as duke. German national liberals claimed the duchies were inseparably united (the doctrine of the 1460 Treaty of Ribe, "up ewig ungedeelt" — forever undivided) and should join a German national state, while Danish "Eider Danes" wanted Schleswig incorporated into Denmark proper up to the Eider river. The succession question — the Danish royal line faced extinction in the male line, while the duchies followed Salic law — made the issue explosive; Lord Palmerston famously quipped that only three people had ever understood the Schleswig-Holstein question: one was dead, one mad, and he himself had forgotten it.

Course of the war

Revolution in March 1848 produced a provisional government in Kiel and a Danish national government in Copenhagen. Prussian and German Confederation troops intervened on the duchies' side (battle of Schleswig/Bau, April 1848), but great-power pressure — including Russian threats and British diplomacy — pushed Prussia into the Armistice of Malmö (August 1848), which Prussia concluded against the will of the Frankfurt Parliament. Fighting resumed in 1849 (battles of Kolding and Fredericia, a notable Danish victory). After a second armistice and the Treaty of Berlin (1850), Prussia withdrew; the duchies' army fought on alone and was beaten at Isted (Idstedt) on 25 July 1850, the largest battle in Scandinavian history to that date. Resistance ended with the surrender of Rendsburg in early 1852.

Settlement

The London Protocol of 8 May 1852, signed by the great powers, upheld the integrity of the Danish monarchy, confirmed Prince Christian of Glücksburg as heir to the whole monarchy, and forbade incorporating Schleswig more closely into Denmark than Holstein. The settlement lasted only until 1863, when Denmark's November Constitution violated it and triggered the Second Schleswig War of 1864.
