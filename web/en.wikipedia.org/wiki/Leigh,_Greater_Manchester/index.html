Leigh is a town in the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan, Greater Manchester, England, lying on low land northwest of the Bridgewater Canal, about 8 miles (13 km) southeast of Wigan and a similar distance west of Manchester city centre. Historically part of Lancashire, it was an important centre of cotton spinning and coal mining during the Industrial Revolution.

History

Leigh's name derives from the Old English leah, meaning a woodland clearing. The medieval parish of Leigh comprised the townships of Pennington, Bedford, and Westleigh. The town transformed in the 19th century: coal seams of the Manchester Coalfield were exploited by deep mines, and multi-storey cotton mills — among them the great mills along the canal — made Leigh one of Lancashire's principal textile towns. The Bridgewater Canal reached Leigh in 1795 and was later joined by the Leigh Branch of the Leeds and Liverpool Canal, linking the town to both the Mersey and the Pennines.

Landmarks and sport

Landmarks include the parish church of St Mary the Virgin, the obelisk in the market place, and Leigh Town Hall (1907). The town's rugby league club, Leigh Leopards (formerly Leigh Centurions), plays at Leigh Sports Village, which it shares with Manchester United Women and academy teams; the stadium opened in 2008. Leigh's last deep colliery, Parsonage Colliery, closed in 1992, and the last cotton mills followed, leaving a legacy of mill buildings, several converted to new uses. The town is represented in Parliament by the constituency of Leigh and Atherton.
