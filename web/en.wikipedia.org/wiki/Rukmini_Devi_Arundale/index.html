Rukmini Devi Arundale

Rukmini Devi Arundale
Born:29 February 1904, Madurai
Died:24 February 1986, Chennai
Known for:Bharatanatyam revival; Kalakshetra

Rukmini Devi Arundale (29 February 1904 – 24 February 1986) was an Indian theosophist, dancer and choreographer of the Indian classical dance form of Bharatanatyam, and an activist for animal welfare. She was the first woman in Indian history to be nominated as a member of the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of the Parliament of India. She is regarded as the person most responsible for the revival of Bharatanatyam from its earlier status as a temple dance performed by devadasis to a respected art form performed on the concert stage.

Early life and Theosophy

Rukmini Devi was born into a Brahmin family in Madurai. Her father, Nilakanta Sastri, was an engineer associated with the Theosophical Society at Adyar. She met the prominent theosophist George Arundale and married him in 1920, an inter-community marriage that caused considerable controversy at the time. She travelled widely with Annie Besant and worked for the Theosophical Society.

Bharatanatyam

Encouraged by Anna Pavlova, whom she met on a voyage, Rukmini Devi learned the temple dance form then called Sadhir, studying under Mylapore Gowri Amma and Pandanallur Meenakshi Sundaram Pillai. She gave her first public performance in 1935. In 1936 she founded Kalakshetra in Adyar, Chennai, an academy for dance and music built on the gurukulam model in a natural setting, which became a landmark institution of Indian culture.

Animal welfare and public life

She was a pioneering animal welfare activist, instrumental in the establishment of the Animal Welfare Board of India and in the passage of the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. She was awarded the Padma Bhushan in 1956 and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship in 1967.
