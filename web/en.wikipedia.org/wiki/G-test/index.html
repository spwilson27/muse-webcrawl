G-test

G-test
TypeStatistical hypothesis test
Based onLikelihood-ratio test
DistributionApproximately chi-squared (χ²)

In statistics, G-tests are likelihood-ratio or maximum likelihood statistical significance tests that are increasingly used in preference to chi-squared tests for goodness of fit and tests of independence in contingency tables. The test statistic is

G = 2 Σ Oi ln(Oi / Ei),

where Oi are the observed counts and Ei the counts expected under the null hypothesis. Under the null hypothesis, G is approximately distributed as a chi-squared distribution with the same degrees of freedom as the corresponding chi-squared test, so the two tests give similar results in large samples.

Properties

The G-test has the advantage of additivity: G values from nested sub-analyses sum to the total, which makes it natural for partitioning in multi-way tables and in information-theoretic contexts — G is proportional to the mutual information between variables in a contingency table. It is closely related to the log-likelihood ratio and to Kullback–Leibler divergence. For small samples, corrections such as the Williams correction improve the chi-squared approximation. The G-test is standard in fields such as ecology (following Sokal and Rohlf's Biometry) and in natural language processing.
