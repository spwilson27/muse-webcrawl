The African Footballer of the Year award, presented to the best African football player each year, has been conferred by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) since 1992. An earlier award of the same name was given by the magazine France Football from 1970 to 1994 and by the African Sports Journalists Union.

History

The France Football version was won by such players as Salif Keïta (the first winner, in 1970), Roger Milla, and Abedi Pele, who won three consecutive awards. When CAF took over the award in 1992, the first recipient was again Abedi Pele. The women's award, African Women's Footballer of the Year, was introduced in 2001.

Notable winners

Samuel Eto'o of Cameroon and Yaya Touré of Ivory Coast hold the record with four wins each; Touré won four in a row from 2011 to 2014. George Weah, who won the award three times, remains the only African player to have won the Ballon d'Or. Other multiple winners include Didier Drogba, Mohamed Salah, Sadio Mané, and Riyad Mahrez. The award is typically decided by votes from CAF technical committees, media representatives, and national team coaches and captains.
