Tommy Lucchese

Thomas Gaetano Lucchese (born Gaetano Lucchese; December 1, 1899 – July 13, 1967) was an Italian-American mobster and the boss of the New York crime family that now bears his name, the Lucchese crime family, from 1951 until his death.

Rise

Born in Palermo, Sicily, Lucchese immigrated to New York as a teenager. He lost parts of fingers in an industrial accident — a distinctive injury that led to the nickname "Tommy Three-Finger Brown," after a baseball pitcher, which he disliked. Through the 1920s he rose in the organization of Gaetano Reina and, after the Castellammarese War, served as underboss to Thomas Gagliano. When Gagliano died in 1951, Lucchese took over.

Boss

Lucchese ran his family with a low profile and a preference for legitimate fronts, building rackets in the garment industry, trucking, and labour unions. He was a member of the Mafia Commission and an ally of Carlo Gambino and Frank Costello. He attended the 1957 Apalachin meeting but avoided prosecution. He died of a brain tumour in 1967 and was succeeded by Carmine Tramunti and later Anthony Corallo.
