Third Macedonian War

The Third Macedonian War (171–168 BC) was the third of the Macedonian Wars fought between the Roman Republic and King Perseus of Macedon. It ended with the decisive Roman victory at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC, the abolition of the Antigonid monarchy, and the consolidation of Roman dominance over Greece and the eastern Mediterranean.

Background

Perseus, who succeeded his father Philip V in 179 BC, rebuilt Macedonian strength and influence, alarming Rome's Greek allies such as Eumenes II of Pergamum, whose speech to the Senate helped precipitate war. Early campaigns in Thessaly went poorly for Rome, and Perseus won a cavalry success at Callinicus in 171 BC, but he failed to exploit his advantages or secure Balkan and Greek allies.

Pydna and aftermath

In 168 BC the consul Lucius Aemilius Paullus took command and, on 22 June, destroyed the Macedonian army at Pydna, where the legion's flexibility overcame the phalanx once gaps opened in its line. Perseus was captured and paraded in Paullus's triumph in Rome. Macedonia was divided into four client republics, and a thousand Achaean hostages — including the historian Polybius — were deported to Italy.
