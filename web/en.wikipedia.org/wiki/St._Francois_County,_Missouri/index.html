St. Francois County, Missouri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

St. Francois County is a county in the Lead Belt region of south-eastern Missouri, United States. As of the 2020 census its population was about 66,900. Its county seat is Farmington.

Geography

The county lies at the eastern edge of the Ozark Plateau, adjoining the St. Francois Mountains. Communities include Farmington, Park Hills (formed in 1994 by the merger of Flat River, Elvins, Esther and Rivermines), Desloge, Bonne Terre and Leadington.

History and economy

Organised in 1821 and named for the St. Francis River (whose name was rendered in French), the county was at the heart of the Southeast Missouri Lead District; lead mining shaped its towns for two centuries. Bonne Terre is home to the Bonne Terre Mine, a flooded former lead mine now a noted scuba-diving site, and to a museum of the Missouri mining industry. St. Joe State Park occupies former mining land and is popular for off-road riding.
