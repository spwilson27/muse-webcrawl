Scion FR-S

The Scion FR-S is a 2+2 sports car sold in North America from 2012 to 2016 by Scion, Toyota's youth-oriented marque. It is one of three variants of the jointly developed Toyota 86 and Subaru BRZ, a front-engine, rear-wheel-drive coupé powered by a naturally aspirated 2.0-litre flat-four "boxer" engine producing 200 hp (149 kW).

When the Scion brand was discontinued in 2016, the car was rebadged as the Toyota 86 for the North American market from the 2017 model year.

Development

The car was developed jointly by Toyota and Subaru, with Subaru supplying the engine and manufacturing the cars at its Gunma plant. Toyota aimed to recreate the spirit of the AE86 Corolla Levin/Sprinter Trueno with a lightweight, low-centre-of-gravity sports car at an affordable price; the distribution of weight near 53:47 front:rear and a very low centre of gravity (460 mm) were central design goals.

Reception

The FR-S and its siblings were widely praised for their handling balance and steering feel, winning numerous car-of-the-year and comparison-test awards despite modest power.
