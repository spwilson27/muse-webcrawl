Ralf Rangnick

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ralf Rangnick (born 29 June 1958) is a German football coach and executive who is widely credited with popularising gegenpressing and modern pressing-based football in Germany. He has managed clubs including VfB Stuttgart, Hannover 96, Schalke 04, 1899 Hoffenheim and RB Leipzig, served as interim manager of Manchester United, and became head coach of the Austria national team.

Early career

Rangnick was born in Backnang, West Germany. His playing career was spent mostly at amateur and lower-league level, including a spell in England with Southwick while he was a student. He began coaching in the 1980s, influenced by the zonal marking and pressing systems of coaches such as Valeriy Lobanovskyi and Arrigo Sacchi.

Coaching career

Rangnick came to national prominence with SSV Ulm and VfB Stuttgart, and famously explained his back-four zonal system on German television in 1998, earning the nickname "the Professor". He won the DFB-Pokal with Schalke 04 in 2011. At Hoffenheim and later as sporting director and coach within the Red Bull football network, he built young, high-pressing teams and helped establish RB Leipzig as a Bundesliga force. His ideas influenced a generation of German coaches including Jürgen Klopp, Thomas Tuchel and Julian Nagelsmann.

Manchester United and Austria

In November 2021 Rangnick was appointed interim manager of Manchester United until the end of the season. In 2022 he took over as head coach of Austria, leading the team to qualification for Euro 2024, where they reached the round of 16.
