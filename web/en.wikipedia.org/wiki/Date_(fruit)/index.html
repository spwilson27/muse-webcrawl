Overview

Dates are the fruit of the date palm (Phoenix dactylifera), cultivated since ancient times in Mesopotamia and the Middle East for their intensely sweet fruit. Dates are oval-cylindrical, ripen through stages ending in the soft (khalal/rutab) and dried (tamr) phases, and are a staple food and major export across North Africa and the Middle East.

Varieties and use

Hundreds of cultivars exist; Medjool ("king of dates"), Deglet Noor, Ajwa and Sukkari are among the best known. Dates are eaten fresh and dried, made into syrup (dibs) and paste for sweets, and traditionally break the fast during Ramadan. Egypt is the largest producer, followed by Saudi Arabia and Iran.
