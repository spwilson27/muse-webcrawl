George V of Hanover

George V (German: Georg Friedrich Alexander Karl Ernst August; 27 May 1819 – 12 June 1878) was the last King of Hanover, reigning from 18 November 1851 until the kingdom's annexation by Prussia in 1866. He was the only child of King Ernest Augustus I and Duchess Frederica of Mecklenburg-Strelitz. Blind from an illness and an accident in his youth, George nevertheless succeeded his father, and his blindness caused a constitutional question as to whether he was capable of discharging the functions of a king.

Early life

George was born in Berlin, where his father, then Duke of Cumberland, was serving. He spent part of his childhood in Berlin and at Windsor. In 1837 his father became King of Hanover, ending the personal union between Hanover and the United Kingdom, since Hanoverian law barred a woman — Queen Victoria — from its throne. George became crown prince. He lost the sight of one eye in childhood through illness, and of the other in an accident in 1833, leaving him totally blind.

Reign

George succeeded on his father's death in 1851. Reversing his father's policies in some respects, he was nonetheless a conservative ruler who clashed repeatedly with the Hanoverian parliament (Landtag). His reign saw industrial development, including the expansion of railways, but also growing tension with Prussia. George refused demands to join a Prussian-led alliance, preferring neutrality and reliance on Austria.

Dethronement and exile

During the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, Hanover sided with Austria. After the Hanoverian army's capitulation following the Battle of Langensalza, Prussia annexed the kingdom outright. George never formally abdicated and maintained his claim in exile, first in Austria at Hietzing and Gmunden, and later in Paris. He founded the Guelphic Legion from Hanoverian exiles, which disbanded after 1870. The Prussian government sequestered his fortune, the so-called Guelph Fund, which Otto von Bismarck used to subsidise the press.

Death and succession

George died in Paris in 1878 and was buried in the royal vault at St George's Chapel, Windsor. His son Ernest Augustus continued to style himself Duke of Cumberland and heir to Hanover; a reconciliation between the Guelphs and Hohenzollerns came only in 1913 with the marriage of his grandson Ernest Augustus to Princess Victoria Louise of Prussia.
