Hazard rate

In survival analysis, the hazard rate (or hazard function) at time t is the instantaneous rate at which events occur given that no event has yet occurred — formally, the limit of the probability of an event in a small interval following t, conditional on survival to t, divided by the interval length. The related hazard ratio compares the hazard rates of two groups, such as a treatment and a control group in a clinical trial.

Interpretation

A hazard ratio of 1 indicates no difference between groups; a ratio below 1 for a treatment group suggests a lower instantaneous event rate, and above 1 a higher one. Unlike relative risk, the hazard ratio accounts for the timing of events over the whole follow-up period.

Models

The Cox proportional hazards model estimates hazard ratios under the assumption that they remain constant over time. The baseline hazard function connects to the survival function through the cumulative hazard, with survival equal to the exponential of the negative cumulative hazard.
