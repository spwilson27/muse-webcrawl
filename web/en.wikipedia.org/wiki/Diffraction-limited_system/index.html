Diffraction-limited system

In optics, a diffraction-limited system is one in which the resolution is limited principally by diffraction through the aperture, rather than by aberrations or imperfections of the optics. The angular resolution is set by the wavelength of light and the diameter of the aperture.

Angular resolution

For a circular aperture, the Rayleigh criterion gives θ ≈ 1.22 λ / D, where λ is the wavelength and D the aperture diameter. The image of a point source is an Airy pattern — a bright central disk surrounded by faint rings.

Applications

Large telescopes are designed to approach the diffraction limit, though in practice the atmosphere limits ground-based resolution unless adaptive optics is used. Space telescopes such as Hubble and James Webb operate close to diffraction-limited performance. Microscopes are likewise diffraction-limited, with resolution around 200 nm for visible light.
