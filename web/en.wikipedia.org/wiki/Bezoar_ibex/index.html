The bezoar ibex (Capra aegagrus aegagrus), also known as the Anatolian bezoar ibex, is a wild goat subspecies native to the mountainous regions of western Asia. It is the nominate subspecies of the wild goat and one of the ancestors of the domestic goat.

Description

Males are large, with long, scimitar-shaped horns that can exceed a metre, used in clashes during the rut. Females are smaller with shorter horns. Bezoar ibex are agile climbers of cliffs and rocky slopes, feeding on grasses, herbs and shrubs.

Distribution and name

Populations occur from Turkey and the Caucasus through Iraq to Iran. The name comes from the bezoar — a stony concretion sometimes found in its stomach — once prized in Persian and European medicine as an antidote to poison.
