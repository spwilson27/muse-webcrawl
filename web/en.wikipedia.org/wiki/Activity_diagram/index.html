In the Unified Modeling Language (UML), an activity diagram is a behavioural diagram that represents the flow of control and data among the activities of a system or process. Activity diagrams are essentially advanced flowcharts, extended to model concurrency, branching, merging and object flow, and are used in software engineering and business process modelling.

A UML activity diagram showing actions, a decision node, and parallel flows joined before the final node.

Notation and elements

Key elements include the initial node (filled circle) and activity final node; actions drawn as rounded rectangles; control flows (arrows); decision and merge nodes (diamonds) with guard conditions; fork and join nodes (bars) that split and synchronise concurrent flows; object nodes and pins for data; and partitions ("swimlanes") assigning activities to responsible actors or components. In UML 2, the semantics are based on token flow, inspired by Petri nets, giving the diagrams a precise execution meaning.

Uses

Activity diagrams are used to model use-case flows, algorithms, business workflows and system operations, especially where parallel behaviour matters. They are closely related to flowcharts and to state diagrams, differing in that they emphasise flows of activity rather than object states.
