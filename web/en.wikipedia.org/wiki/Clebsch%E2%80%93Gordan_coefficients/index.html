Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

In physics, Clebsch–Gordan (C-G) coefficients are numbers that arise in angular momentum coupling in quantum mechanics. They appear as the expansion coefficients of total angular momentum eigenstates in an uncoupled tensor product basis. In more mathematical terms, the CG coefficients are used in representation theory, particularly of compact Lie groups, to perform the explicit direct sum decomposition of the tensor product of two irreducible representations into irreducible representations.

Definition

When two angular momenta j1 and j2 are coupled to form a total angular momentum J with projection M, the coupled state is a linear combination of the uncoupled product states, with the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients as weights. The coefficients vanish unless M = m1 + m2 and the triangle condition |j1 − j2| ≤ J ≤ j1 + j2 holds.

Properties

The coefficients are real by convention and satisfy orthogonality relations that make the transformation between coupled and uncoupled bases unitary. They are closely related to Wigner's 3-j symbols and are tabulated in standard references. In particle physics they generalise to the isospin and flavour symmetries of the quark model.

History

The coefficients are named after the German mathematicians Alfred Clebsch and Paul Gordan, who encountered the equivalent problem in invariant theory in the 19th century.
