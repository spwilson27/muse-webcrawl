Nguni languages

The Nguni languages are a group of closely related Bantu languages spoken in southern Africa by the Nguni peoples. They include Zulu, Xhosa, Swazi (siSwati) and Ndebele, together with several smaller varieties. Tens of millions of people speak a Nguni language as a first language, principally in South Africa, Eswatini and Zimbabwe.

Characteristics

Nguni languages are tonal and agglutinative, with an elaborate system of noun classes marked by prefixes that govern agreement throughout the sentence. A notable feature of the Zunda subgroup (Zulu, Xhosa and Ndebele) is the use of click consonants, borrowed from neighbouring Khoisan languages; clicks are written with the letters c, q and x. Zulu and Xhosa are largely mutually intelligible, as are Swazi and the Tekela varieties.

Classification and status

Linguists divide the group into Zunda and Tekela branches. Zulu is South Africa's most widely spoken home language, and Xhosa the second; both, along with Swazi and Ndebele, are official languages of South Africa, while siSwati is also official in Eswatini.
