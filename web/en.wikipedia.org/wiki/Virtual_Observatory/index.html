Virtual Observatory

A virtual observatory (VO) is a collection of interoperating data archives, software tools, and standards that allows astronomers to access and analyse astronomical data seamlessly, as if all the world's telescope archives formed a single digital observatory. Rather than downloading data from individual observatory websites in incompatible formats, a researcher can query many archives at once through standard protocols, cross-match catalogues, overlay images from different telescopes and wavelengths, and analyse the results with common tools.

History

The concept emerged around 2000 as survey astronomy and large digital archives made data volumes explode. National projects were founded in the United States (the National Virtual Observatory, later the Virtual Astronomical Observatory), Europe (Euro-VO and the Astrophysical Virtual Observatory), the United Kingdom (AstroGrid), and many other countries. In 2002 these projects formed the International Virtual Observatory Alliance (IVOA) to coordinate the development of shared standards.

Standards and tools

The IVOA defines protocols and data formats that make the system work, including:

  • VOTable — an XML format for exchanging tabular catalogue data.
  • Cone search, SIAP and SSAP — simple protocols for querying catalogues, images, and spectra by sky position.
  • ADQL and TAP — a query language and table access protocol for database-style queries of large catalogues.
  • SAMP — a messaging protocol that lets applications such as Aladin, TOPCAT, and DS9 exchange data and selections in real time.

Well-known VO-enabled tools include the Aladin sky atlas, the TOPCAT table manipulation tool, and archive services operated by CDS in Strasbourg, ESA, NASA, and ESO. The VO infrastructure underlies modern archive science, including services built around the Gaia mission's billion-star catalogues.
