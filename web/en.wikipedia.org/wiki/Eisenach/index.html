Eisenach
Country: Germany
State: Thuringia

Eisenach is a town in Thuringia, Germany, with a population of about 42,000. It lies at the northwestern edge of the Thuringian Forest and is best known for the Wartburg castle above the town, where Martin Luther translated the New Testament into German, and as the birthplace of the composer Johann Sebastian Bach.

History

Eisenach grew up around 1180 beneath the Wartburg, founded around 1067 by the Ludowingian counts of Thuringia. In 1521–22, Luther, declared an outlaw after the Diet of Worms, was sheltered in the Wartburg by Frederick the Wise and there translated the New Testament in eleven weeks. Bach was born in Eisenach in 1685 and spent his first ten years there. In 1869 the Social Democratic Workers' Party of Germany — a forerunner of the SPD — was founded at a congress in Eisenach.

Industry

The town has a long automotive tradition: the Eisenacher Motorenwerk (EMW) built cars there from 1896, producing the Wartburg car in the GDR era. After reunification, Opel opened a major plant in Eisenach in 1992, and automotive suppliers remain key employers.

Sights

Principal sights include the Wartburg (a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999), the Bach House museum, the Luther House, St. George's Church, and one of the narrowest inhabited half-timbered houses in Germany. The town is also a gateway to the Rennsteig hiking trail.
