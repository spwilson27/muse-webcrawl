The naginata (なぎなた, 薙刀) is a Japanese polearm consisting of a wooden or metal shaft with a curved single-edged blade mounted on the end, similar in appearance to a European glaive. It was a common weapon of the samurai class, of warrior monks (sōhei), and of women of the samurai class (onna-musha), for whom it became an emblematic weapon.

History

The naginata emerged by the Kamakura period and was widely used in the warfare of the Nanbokuchō era, effective against cavalry and infantry alike. As battlefield tactics shifted to massed spearmen with long yari pikes in the Sengoku period, the naginata declined as a battlefield weapon but was retained in martial schools and adopted as the standard weapon taught to samurai women for defence of the home. Famous wielders in legend and history include Tomoe Gozen and the monk Benkei.

Modern practice

Traditional schools (koryū) such as Tendo-ryū and Jikishinkage-ryū preserve naginatajutsu. The modern sport, atarashii naginata, is governed by the All Japan Naginata Federation (founded 1955) and the International Naginata Federation; competitors wear armour (bōgu) and use bamboo-shafted practice weapons in kata and sparring (shiai). The sport is practised predominantly by women in Japan.
