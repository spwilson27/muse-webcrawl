Siachen conflict

Date: 1984 – present (ceasefire since 2003)
Location: Siachen Glacier, Karakoram, Kashmir region
Combatants: India and Pakistan
Result: India controls the glacier and Saltoro Ridge passes

The Siachen conflict, sometimes called the Siachen Glacier conflict, is a military standoff between India and Pakistan over the disputed Siachen Glacier region in the Karakoram mountains of Kashmir. It began on 13 April 1984 with India's Operation Meghdoot, which pre-empted a planned Pakistani operation and seized the strategic Saltoro Ridge passes of Bilafond La and Sia La. It is the highest battlefield on Earth, with posts above 6,000 metres (20,000 ft), and more soldiers have died from the extreme weather and terrain than from combat.

Background

The 1949 Karachi Agreement and the 1972 Simla Agreement delineated the Line of Control (LoC) in Kashmir only as far as map point NJ9842, beyond which the boundary was described as running "thence north to the glaciers." Both countries claimed the undefined triangle around the 76-kilometre-long Siachen Glacier. Pakistani-issued maps and mountaineering permits in the 1970s and early 1980s treated the glacier as Pakistani, prompting India's pre-emptive move in 1984.

Course of the conflict

After establishing its positions, India repelled repeated Pakistani attempts to dislodge it, including major attacks in 1987 and the battle for the post called Bana Top (previously Quaid Post), captured by Indian forces under Captain (later Major) Bana Singh in June 1987 — an action for which he received the Param Vir Chakra. Artillery duels and small-unit actions continued through the 1990s. A ceasefire along the Actual Ground Position Line (AGPL) took effect in November 2003 and has largely held, though the deployment continues.

Human and environmental cost

Temperatures fall below −50 °C, and avalanches, crevasses, frostbite, and altitude sickness have killed thousands of personnel. An avalanche at Pakistan's Gayari base in April 2012 buried 140 soldiers and civilians. The cost of supplying the glacier — by helicopter and porter — runs to millions of dollars per day for both sides, and military waste has made the glacier a byword for environmental damage in a fragile high-altitude ecosystem. Periodic talks on demilitarization, including the long-running Siachen dialogue within the India–Pakistan composite dialogue, have repeatedly stalled over authentication of current positions.
