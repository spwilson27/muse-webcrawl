Air Force Medal

Awarded for: Acts of courage, valour or devotion to duty whilst flying, though not in active operations against the enemy
Established: 3 June 1918
Country: United Kingdom

The Air Force Medal (AFM) was a British military decoration awarded to personnel of the Royal Air Force and other services, and to Commonwealth forces, for acts of courage, valour, or devotion to duty performed whilst flying, though not during active operations against the enemy. It was the other-ranks equivalent of the Air Force Cross (AFC), which was awarded to officers and warrant officers.

History

The medal was instituted on 3 June 1918, shortly after the formation of the RAF, alongside the Air Force Cross. Recipients were entitled to use the post-nominal letters "AFM". The medal is circular, bearing the effigy of the sovereign on the obverse and, on the reverse, a depiction of Hermes riding on a hawk; its ribbon has alternating red and white stripes set at an angle. Following the 1993 review of the British honours system, which removed distinctions of rank in gallantry awards, the Air Force Medal was discontinued and the Air Force Cross became open to all ranks.
