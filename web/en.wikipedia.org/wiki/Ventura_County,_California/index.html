Ventura County is a county located in the southern part of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, the population was 843,843. The county seat is Ventura, and the largest city is Oxnard. The county lies along the Pacific coast northwest of Los Angeles County, and is sometimes considered the northern part of the Greater Los Angeles area or, alternatively, part of California's Central Coast.

The Ventura County coastline near Point Mugu.

Geography

The county covers about 2,208 square miles, including portions of the Channel Islands of Anacapa and San Nicolas. The northern part of the county is mountainous, within the Los Padres National Forest, while the southern Oxnard Plain is one of the most fertile agricultural regions in California, known for strawberries, lemons, and avocados. Major cities include Oxnard, Ventura, Thousand Oaks, Simi Valley, and Camarillo.

History

The area was inhabited for thousands of years by the Chumash people. Spanish explorer Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo landed in 1542, and Mission San Buenaventura was founded in 1782. Ventura County was created in 1873 from the southern part of Santa Barbara County, taking its name from the mission town of San Buenaventura.

Economy

Agriculture remains a major industry, alongside the U.S. Navy facilities at Naval Base Ventura County (Port Hueneme and Point Mugu), the biotechnology company Amgen, headquartered in Thousand Oaks, and the outdoor clothing company Patagonia, headquartered in Ventura. The Ronald Reagan Presidential Library is located in Simi Valley.
