Vaccination policy

Vaccination policy is the health policy a government adopts in relation to vaccination. Vaccination policies have been developed over approximately two centuries since the invention of vaccination, initially to eradicate disease and later to manage outbreaks. Policies vary worldwide: some countries make certain vaccines compulsory, others merely recommend them, and most operate childhood immunisation schedules supported by public funding.

Goals

The central aim of vaccination policy is to achieve high coverage so that herd immunity protects those who cannot be vaccinated, and ultimately to eliminate or eradicate targeted diseases, as was achieved with smallpox in 1980 and, nearly, with poliomyelitis. Policies typically specify which vaccines are offered, at what ages, and whether exemptions (medical, religious or philosophical) are permitted.

Compulsion and controversy

Mandatory vaccination has been contested since the 19th century, when Britain's Vaccination Acts provoked organised opposition. Modern debates involve the balance between public health and individual liberty, school-entry requirements, and compensation schemes for rare vaccine injuries, such as the United States National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program established in 1986.
