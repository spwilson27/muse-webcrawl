Areac. 1,583,000 km²

The Sea of Okhotsk is a marginal sea of the western Pacific Ocean, lying between Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula in the east, the Kuril Islands in the southeast, Japan's island of Hokkaido in the south, the island of Sakhalin in the west, and the Siberian coast in the north and west. It is named after Okhotsk, the first Russian settlement in the Far East.

Geography and climate

Covering about 1.58 million square kilometres, the sea is mostly less than 1,500 metres deep, with a maximum depth of 3,374 metres in the Kuril Basin. It is the coldest sea in East Asia: much of it is covered by sea ice from roughly October/November to June, and the region produces the most southerly sea ice in the Northern Hemisphere, driven by cold Siberian winds and freshening from the Amur River.

Economy and ecology

The sea supports rich fisheries — pollock, salmon, herring and crab — for Russia and Japan, and its floor holds oil and gas reserves, particularly off Sakhalin. Its dense water formation ("the heart of the North Pacific") drives ventilation of the North Pacific intermediate waters, making its sea ice climatically significant.

History

Russian explorers reached the sea in the 1630s–40s, and it was later charted by Vitus Bering's expeditions. In the 19th century it was a major whaling ground for American ships.
