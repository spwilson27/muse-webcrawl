Nez Perce War

The Nez Perce War was an armed conflict in 1877 between several bands of the Nez Perce tribe of Native Americans and their allies, and the United States Army. Facing forced removal from their ancestral Wallowa Valley in Oregon to a reservation in Idaho, about 800 Nez Perce under leaders including Chief Joseph, Looking Glass, White Bird, Ollokot and Toohoolhoolzote instead fled toward Canada in one of the most famous fighting retreats in history.

Course

Over roughly four months the Nez Perce travelled some 1,170 miles (1,880 km) through Oregon, Idaho, Wyoming and Montana, repeatedly outmanoeuvring pursuing U.S. forces under General Oliver O. Howard and Colonel John Gibbon. Engagements included White Bird Canyon, the Clearwater, the Big Hole (where many Nez Perce non-combatants were killed), Camas Meadows, Canyon Creek and Cow Island. Colonel Nelson Miles intercepted them at the Bear Paw Mountains, only about 40 miles (64 km) from the Canadian border, and after a five-day siege Chief Joseph surrendered on 5 October 1877 with words remembered as "I will fight no more forever."

Aftermath

Despite promises, the surrendered Nez Perce were exiled to Kansas and Indian Territory, where many died, before survivors were allowed in 1885 to return to reservations in the Northwest. The war is commemorated by the Nez Perce National Historical Park, whose sites span four states.
