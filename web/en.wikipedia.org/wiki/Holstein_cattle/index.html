Holstein cattle

A Holstein cow, the world's highest-production dairy breed

Holstein cattle (often shortened to Holsteins in North America, or Holstein-Friesians in some countries) are a breed of dairy cattle originating from the Dutch provinces of North Holland and Friesland and the Schleswig-Holstein region of northern Germany. They are the world's highest-production dairy animals, and are known for their distinctive black-and-white (or occasionally red-and-white) markings. Holsteins dominate the global dairy industry, making up the large majority of dairy herds in countries such as the United States, Germany, and the Netherlands.

Characteristics

Holsteins are large cattle: mature cows typically stand about 1.45–1.65 m tall at the shoulder and weigh around 580–750 kg. The breed is renowned for very high milk yields — a managed Holstein cow can produce more than 10,000 kg of milk per lactation, and record individuals have exceeded 30,000 kg in a year — although the milk is lower in butterfat and protein percentages than that of breeds such as the Jersey. Selective breeding, especially in North America, has strongly favoured milk volume.

History

The breed was developed over roughly 2,000 years in the low countries of northwestern Europe from black cattle of the Batavians and white cattle of the Frisians. Dutch settlers brought the cattle to America in the 17th century, and organized breeding there in the 19th century led to the formation of herd books. In the 20th century, North American Holstein genetics were exported worldwide and largely displaced local Friesian strains, including in the Netherlands itself. In Britain and Ireland the breed is commonly called Holstein-Friesian, reflecting crosses between imported North American Holsteins and native Friesian stock.

Use

Holsteins are kept primarily for milk production; male calves and cull cows also enter the beef supply chain, and Holstein crosses are common in beef production. The red-and-white variant arises from a recessive gene and is registered separately in some herd books.
