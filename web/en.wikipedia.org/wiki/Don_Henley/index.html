Don Henley

Donald Hugh Henley (born July 22, 1947) is an American musician. He is a founding member of the rock band Eagles, for which he is the drummer and one of the lead vocalists, alongside Glenn Frey. Henley sang the lead vocals on Eagles hits such as "Witchy Woman", "Desperado", "Best of My Love", "One of These Nights", "Hotel California", "Life in the Fast Lane" and "The Long Run".

Early life

Henley was born in Gilmer, Texas, and grew up in the small town of Linden. He attended the University of North Texas before moving to Los Angeles in 1970, where his band Shiloh recorded an album produced by Kenny Rogers. Shortly afterwards he met Glenn Frey; both were hired as backing musicians for Linda Ronstadt, and in 1971 they founded the Eagles with Randy Meisner and Bernie Leadon.

Eagles

The Eagles became one of the best-selling bands of all time, with albums including Desperado, On the Border, One of These Nights, Hotel California and The Long Run. The band broke up in 1980 and reunited in 1994 for the Hell Freezes Over tour and album. Following Frey's death in 2016, Henley continued touring with the band.

Solo career

Henley's solo albums include I Can't Stand Still (1982), Building the Perfect Beast (1984) and The End of the Innocence (1989), producing hits such as "Dirty Laundry", "The Boys of Summer" and "The End of the Innocence". He has won several Grammy Awards as a solo artist and with the Eagles.

Activism

Henley co-founded the Walden Woods Project in 1990 to preserve land associated with Henry David Thoreau, and has been active in environmental and artists' rights causes.
