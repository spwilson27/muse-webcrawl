X-ray fluorescence
AcronymXRF
TypeSpectroscopy

X-ray fluorescence (XRF) is the emission of characteristic fluorescent X-rays from a material excited by high-energy X-rays or gamma rays. It is a non-destructive analytical technique for elemental analysis.

Principle

Primary radiation ejects inner-shell electrons; when outer electrons fill the vacancies, they emit X-rays at energies unique to each element. Detectors measure energy (EDXRF) or wavelength (WDXRF).

Applications

XRF is widely used in geology, mining, archaeology, environmental monitoring, metal alloy sorting and art analysis, including handheld instruments. See Auger electron spectroscopy.
