TypeInherited blood disorder — reduced or absent globin chain production

Overview

Thalassaemia (thalassemia) is a group of inherited blood disorders characterised by decreased production of one of the globin chains of haemoglobin, causing anaemia of varying severity. Alpha thalassaemia results from deletions affecting the α-globin genes (chromosome 16); beta thalassaemia from mutations in the β-globin gene (chromosome 11). The trait is common in populations from historically malaria-endemic regions, because carrier states protect against severe malaria.

Clinical spectrum

Severity ranges from silent carriage to beta thalassaemia major (Cooley's anaemia), which presents in infancy with severe anaemia, bone expansion and growth failure, historically fatal without treatment. Regular blood transfusions (with iron-chelation therapy to manage iron overload) and haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation are the mainstays; the gene therapy betibeglogene autotemcel (Zynteglo) and, in 2023, the CRISPR therapy exagamglogene autotemcel (Casgevy) — first approved for sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta thalassaemia — opened curative options. Carrier screening and prenatal diagnosis are established in Cyprus, Sardinia and other high-prevalence regions.
