The Falkirk Wheel is a rotating boat lift in Tamfourhill, Falkirk, central Scotland, connecting the Forth and Clyde Canal with the Union Canal. It is the world's only rotating boat lift of its kind and a landmark of modern Scottish engineering.

History

The two canals were once joined at Falkirk by a flight of 11 locks, which were dismantled in the 1930s. As part of the Millennium Link project to restore navigation between Glasgow and Edinburgh, a new connection was needed. Rather than rebuild the locks, engineers chose a rotating lift. The wheel was designed by Butterley Engineering and RMJM architects, and opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 24 May 2002 as part of her Golden Jubilee celebrations.

Operation

The wheel raises or lowers two axially opposed caissons (gondolas), each holding about 300 tonnes of water and boats, through 24 metres (79 ft); a short aqueduct and two further locks bridge the remaining 11 metres to the Union Canal level. Because the caissons always contain equal weights under Archimedes' principle, the rotation takes only about 22 kW and roughly four minutes. A system of ten hydraulic motors drives the central axle while ring gears keep the gondolas level.
