Intel Corporation is an American multinational technology company headquartered in Santa Clara, California. Founded in 1968 by semiconductor pioneers Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore (of Moore's law fame), Intel invented the first commercial microprocessor (the 4004, in 1971) and for decades dominated the market for PC and server CPUs through its x86 chips and the "Intel Inside" campaign. From the 2010s it faced manufacturing delays, the loss of Apple as a customer, and competition from AMD, Arm-based chips and Nvidia in AI, entering a difficult restructuring period in the mid-2020s. Its products are presented at intel.com.

History

Noyce and Moore left Fairchild Semiconductor to found Intel (from "Integrated Electronics"), with venture capitalist Arthur Rock. Early products were memory chips; the 8080 and then the 8086/8088 family became the standard PC architecture after IBM chose the 8088 for its 1981 personal computer — a design win that, with Microsoft's operating systems, created the "Wintel" era. The Pentium brand (1993) and the Core series (2006) made Intel the default choice in desktops, laptops and servers, and its "tick-tock" cadence of shrinking transistors each generation seemed unassailable.

Recent struggles

Intel's 10-nanometre manufacturing process slipped by years, allowing TSMC-built rivals to overtake it; Apple replaced Intel processors in Macs with its own silicon from 2020. CEO Pat Gelsinger (2021–2024) bet heavily on becoming a contract foundry (Intel Foundry) and new US and European plants, supported by the US CHIPS Act, but heavy losses in the foundry business led to his departure in December 2024 and to Lip-Bu Tan becoming CEO in 2025, alongside layoffs and asset sales. In 2025 the US government took an equity stake in Intel, and Nvidia agreed to invest in and partner with the company.

Products

Intel's current lines include Core and Core Ultra processors for PCs, Xeon for servers, Arc graphics, and networking and FPGA products (from its 2015 acquisition of Altera, later partly spun out again).
