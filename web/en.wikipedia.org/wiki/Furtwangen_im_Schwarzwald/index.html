Furtwangen im Schwarzwald is a small town in the Black Forest in the south-western German state of Baden-Württemberg, in the Schwarzwald-Baar district. Situated at between 850 and 1,150 metres above sea level, it is one of the highest towns in Germany. The river Breg, the longer of the two headstreams of the Danube, rises near Furtwangen. The town is historically a centre of Black Forest clockmaking and is home to the German Clock Museum (Deutsches Uhrenmuseum) and Furtwangen University.

History

Furtwangen was first mentioned in documents in 1175. For centuries part of Further Austria, it passed to the Grand Duchy of Baden in 1806. In the 18th and 19th centuries the town became a hub of the Black Forest clock industry: its clockmakers' school, founded in 1850 by Robert Gerwig — who also planned the Black Forest Railway — trained generations of watch- and clockmakers, and the collection he began grew into the German Clock Museum. Precision engineering and electronics firms later succeeded traditional clockmaking as the town's economic base.

Geography and climate

The town lies in a valley where the Breg begins, surrounded by forested heights of the central Black Forest. Its high elevation gives it a cool, wet climate with heavy winter snowfall; Furtwangen is a recognised climatic health resort and a centre for cross-country skiing and hiking.

Education and sights

Furtwangen University (Hochschule Furtwangen), founded as the clockmaking school, is one of Germany's oldest universities of applied sciences and is noted for computer science, engineering and media programmes. The German Clock Museum displays some 1,100 timepieces tracing the history of Black Forest clockmaking, including early wooden-wheel clocks and cuckoo clocks. Nearby is the Hexenloch Mill and the source of the Breg, marked as a Danube source.
