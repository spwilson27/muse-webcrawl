Dominique Jean Larrey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dominique Jean Larrey, Baron Larrey (8 July 1766 – 25 July 1842) was a French surgeon and military doctor in Napoleon's armies. He is regarded as the father of modern military medicine and emergency care: he invented the ambulance volante ("flying ambulance") for rapidly evacuating the wounded and introduced the principle of triage, treating casualties by urgency of need rather than by rank or nationality.

Career

Born in Beaudéan in the Pyrenees, Larrey trained in medicine at Toulouse and Paris and served as a naval surgeon before joining the Army of the Rhine in 1792. Observing that wounded men lay on the field until fighting ended, he organised light horse-drawn ambulance wagons with trained crews to collect and treat casualties during battle. He served through nearly all of Napoleon's campaigns, from Italy and Egypt to Russia and Waterloo, performing speed amputations and pioneering early surgery close to the front line.

Legacy

Napoleon, who called Larrey "the most virtuous man I have known", made him a baron and left him money in his will. Larrey treated enemy wounded as readily as French ones and was respected by opposing armies. His Mémoires de chirurgie militaire remain classics of military surgery, and his concepts — rapid evacuation, forward surgery, and priority by severity — underpin modern emergency medical services and battlefield medicine.
