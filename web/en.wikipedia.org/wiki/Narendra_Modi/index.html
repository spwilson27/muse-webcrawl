Narendra Damodardas Modi (born 17 September 1950) is an Indian politician who has served as the prime minister of India since 2014. Modi was the chief minister of Gujarat from 2001 to 2014 and is the member of parliament (MP) for Varanasi. He is a member of the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), a right-wing Hindu nationalist paramilitary volunteer organisation. He is the longest-serving prime minister from outside the Indian National Congress.

Early life and Gujarat

Modi was born in Vadnagar, Gujarat, to a family of grocers of the Modh Ghanchi caste, and as a youth helped at his father's tea stall — an origin story central to his political image. He joined the RSS as a pracharak (full-time worker), was assigned to the BJP in 1985, and organised campaigns including L. K. Advani's 1990 Rath Yatra. Appointed chief minister of Gujarat in October 2001, his tenure was overshadowed by the 2002 Gujarat riots, in which over a thousand people, mostly Muslims, were killed; critics accused his government of complicity or inaction, while a Supreme Court-appointed Special Investigation Team later found insufficient evidence to prosecute him. As chief minister he was re-elected in 2002, 2007 and 2012 on a platform of economic development — the "Gujarat model" — attracting major industrial investment such as Tata's Nano plant.

Prime minister

Modi led the BJP to a landslide in the 2014 general election — the party's first single-party parliamentary majority since 1984 — campaigning on development, anti-corruption and his monthly radio programme Mann Ki Baat. His government launched Swachh Bharat (sanitation), Make in India, the Goods and Services Tax (2017), the Jan Dhan financial-inclusion scheme, and the abrupt demonetisation of 2016, which withdrew 86 per cent of circulating currency by value and remains economically debated. Re-elected with a larger majority in 2019, his second term saw the abrogation of Jammu and Kashmir's special status (Article 370) in August 2019, the Citizenship (Amendment) Act protests, the Ram temple consecration at Ayodhya in January 2024, and the COVID-19 pandemic response. India under Modi held the G20 presidency in 2023 and became the world's most populous country. In the 2024 election the BJP lost its sole majority but returned to power at the head of the National Democratic Alliance, making Modi only the second Indian prime minister, after Jawaharlal Nehru, to win three consecutive terms.
