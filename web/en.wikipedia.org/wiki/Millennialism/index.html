Millennialism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The thousand-year reign (the "millennium") of Revelation chapter 20.

Millennialism (or chiliasm, from the Greek chilioi, "thousand") is a belief held by some religious denominations that a Golden Age or Paradise will occur on Earth prior to the final judgment, or that a thousand-year age of blessedness will follow the return of Christ. The concept derives primarily from chapter 20 of the Book of Revelation, which describes the binding of Satan and the reign of Christ with the resurrected saints for a thousand years.

Christian views

Christian positions on the millennium are conventionally divided into three camps:

  • Premillennialism — Christ will return before the millennium and literally reign on earth for a thousand years. This view was common in the early church and is widespread among evangelical and fundamentalist Christians today; its dispensational form includes a rapture of believers before a period of tribulation.
  • Postmillennialism — the millennium is a period of increasing Christian influence and righteousness on earth, at the end of which Christ returns. Prominent in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, it undergirded much reform activism.
  • Amillennialism — the thousand years are symbolic of the present church age; Christ's reign is spiritual and there will be no literal future millennium. This is the historic view of the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and many mainline Protestant churches, following Augustine of Hippo.

History

Millennial expectations were present in early Christianity (Papias, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus) and in Jewish apocalyptic literature. Augustine's spiritualised interpretation dominated Western theology for a millennium, but millennial movements repeatedly resurfaced, notably in the radical Reformation, in seventeenth-century England, and in the Millerite excitement of the 1840s in America, out of which Seventh-day Adventism and related movements grew. Jehovah's Witnesses hold a form of premillennial belief centred on Christ's invisible presence and a coming thousand-year reign.

Other traditions

Analogous expectations of a future world-transforming age appear in other religions: the Jewish messianic age, the coming of the Mahdi in Islam (especially Twelver Shi'ism), the Hindu Kali Yuga cycle and the future avatar Kalki, the Zoroastrian Frashokereti, and the Baháʼí concept of progressive revelation. Scholars group these, with secular analogues, under the broader heading of millenarianism.
