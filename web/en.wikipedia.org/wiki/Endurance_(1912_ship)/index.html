Endurance (1912 ship)

Launched: 17 December 1912, Sandefjord, Norway
Type: Barquentine (steam-assisted)
Tonnage: 350 gross register tons
Length: 144 ft (43.9 m)
Fate: Crushed by ice and sank, 21 November 1915

Endurance was the three-masted barquentine in which Sir Ernest Shackleton and a crew of 27 men sailed for the Antarctic on the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition of 1914–1917. Built in Sandefjord, Norway, and originally named Polaris, she was considered one of the strongest wooden ships ever built for polar work.

The expedition

Endurance left South Georgia on 5 December 1914 bound for the Weddell Sea, where Shackleton planned to land and cross the continent to the Ross Sea. Heavy pack ice slowed progress, and on 19 January 1915 the ship became beset — frozen fast — less than a day's sail from her destination. She drifted with the ice for ten months while pressure gradually crushed her hull.

On 27 October 1915 Shackleton ordered the ship abandoned; she sank on 21 November. The crew camped on the ice, then sailed in open lifeboats to Elephant Island. Shackleton and five men then made the celebrated 800-nautical-mile voyage in the lifeboat James Caird to South Georgia, and after crossing the island's mountains, organised the rescue of the remaining men on 30 August 1916 — all survived.

The wreck

The wreck of Endurance was found on 5 March 2022 by the Endurance22 expedition, lying at a depth of about 3,008 metres on the Weddell Sea floor, remarkably preserved in the cold, dark, wood-borer-free water. The discovery was announced on 9 March 2022, a century after Shackleton's burial on South Georgia.
