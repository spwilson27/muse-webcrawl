President of China

The president of China, officially the president of the People's Republic of China, is the head of state of the People's Republic of China. Under the 1982 constitution, the presidency is a largely ceremonial office with limited power, though in practice it has been held simultaneously by the general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party and the chairman of the Central Military Commission since 1993, making the officeholder the paramount leader of China. The incumbent is Xi Jinping, in office since 2013.

Powers

The president promulgates laws, appoints and removes the premier and other state officials on the decision of the National People's Congress (NPC), grants pardons, declares states of emergency and martial law, issues mobilization orders, and ratifies treaties and receives foreign ambassadors. Most powers are exercised in accordance with decisions of the NPC or its Standing Committee.

Selection and term

The president is formally elected by the NPC for a five-year term. Chinese citizens aged 45 or older who have the right to vote and stand for election are eligible. Between 1982 and 2018 the office was limited to two consecutive terms; the 2018 constitutional amendment removed term limits. There is also a vice president who assists the president and may act in their place.

History

The office was first established in 1954, held by Mao Zedong and then Liu Shaoqi, before being left vacant and abolished under the 1975 constitution. It was restored in 1982, with Li Xiannian the first to hold the revived office.
