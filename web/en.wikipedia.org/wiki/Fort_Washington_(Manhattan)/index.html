Fort Washington (Manhattan)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Washington was a fortified position on the highest point of Manhattan Island, New York, built by Continental Army troops during the American Revolutionary War. Situated near the northern tip of the island, the fort and its outworks were intended, together with Fort Lee across the Hudson River, to prevent British warships from sailing upriver.

Battle of Fort Washington

On 16 November 1776, British and Hessian forces under General William Howe assaulted the fort from three directions. The American garrison of roughly 3,000 men under Colonel Robert Magaw was overwhelmed and surrendered — one of the worst American defeats of the war. The British renamed the position Fort Knyphausen after the Hessian general Wilhelm von Knyphausen and held it for the remainder of the war.

Today

The site is preserved in part as Bennett Park in the Washington Heights neighbourhood, where an outline of stones marks the fort's location. The surrounding neighbourhood retains the name Fort Washington.
