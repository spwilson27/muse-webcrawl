Corona Borealis is a small constellation in the Northern Celestial Hemisphere. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its brightest stars form a semicircular arc, and the pattern is recognisably a crown or wreath; in Greek myth it was the crown of Ariadne of Crete, placed in the sky by Dionysus after he married her, her wedding diadem studded with jewels. Its southern counterpart is Corona Australis.

Notable features

The brightest star, Alphecca (also called Gemma), is an eclipsing binary about 75 light-years away. The constellation contains two remarkable variable stars: R Coronae Borealis, the prototype of a class of stars that fade dramatically at irregular intervals as clouds of carbon dust form around them, and T Coronae Borealis, the "Blaze Star", a recurrent nova that erupted in 1866 and 1946 and is anticipated to erupt again in the mid-2020s. Deep-sky contents are modest, but the distant Corona Borealis Galaxy Cluster and the Great Wall of galaxy clusters lie in this direction.

Mythology and other cultures

Besides the Ariadne story, the ring of stars has been read as a wreath, a broken dinner plate, an eagle's nest and a smoke hole of a sweat lodge by various cultures. In Welsh tradition it is Caer Arianrhod, the castle of the goddess Arianrhod; in Australian Aboriginal astronomy it is the boomerang-shaped Woomera.
