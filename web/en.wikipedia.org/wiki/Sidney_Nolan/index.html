Sir Sidney Robert Nolan (22 April 1917 – 28 November 1992) was one of Australia's most celebrated artists, best known for his series of paintings of the bushranger Ned Kelly. His stark, simplified depictions of the Australian landscape and its myths — including Kelly, the explorers Burke and Wills, and the shipwreck survivor Eliza Fraser — helped define a modern Australian visual identity.

Life and career

Born in Carlton, Melbourne, Nolan studied intermittently at the National Gallery of Victoria Art School and was closely associated with art patrons John and Sunday Reed at Heide, and with the Angry Penguins circle of modernist artists and writers. During the Second World War he served in the army and painted in the Wimmera region of Victoria.

His first Kelly series (1946–47), painted in enamel on composition board, presented the outlaw as a black silhouette of home-made armour against luminous outback landscapes. Nolan travelled extensively — to Central Australia, Antarctica, Africa, China, and Gallipoli — and from the mid-1950s was based mainly in Britain, where he also designed sets for ballet and opera, including for the Royal Opera House.

Nolan was knighted in 1981 and appointed to the Order of Merit in 1983. He died in London in 1992. Twenty-seven of his Kelly paintings were donated to the National Gallery of Australia in Canberra.
