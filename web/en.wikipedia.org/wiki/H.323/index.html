H.323 is a recommendation from the ITU Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) that defines the protocols to provide audio-visual communication sessions on any packet network. The H.323 standard addresses call signaling and control, multimedia transport and control, and point-to-point and multi-point conferences for terminals, gateways and multipoint control units.

History

First approved in 1996, H.323 was one of the earliest standards for voice over IP and videoconferencing, adapted from the H.320 ISDN videoconferencing standard for use on packet-switched networks. It was widely deployed in the late 1990s and early 2000s for corporate videoconferencing and early VoIP services. Although it has largely been displaced in new deployments by the Session Initiation Protocol (SIP), H.323 equipment remains in service in many installed videoconferencing systems.

Architecture

An H.323 system consists of four main components. Terminals are endpoints such as video phones and conference room systems. Gateways connect H.323 networks to other networks, such as the public switched telephone network. Gatekeepers provide address translation, admission control and bandwidth management for terminals in their zone. Multipoint control units (MCUs) support conferences between three or more endpoints.

Protocol stack

Call setup uses H.225.0 RAS (registration, admission and status) signaling to the gatekeeper and Q.931-derived call signaling between endpoints. H.245 negotiates media capabilities and opens logical channels. Media itself is carried by the Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP), using codecs such as G.711 and G.729 for audio and H.261, H.263 and H.264 for video. H.323 protocols are encoded using the ASN.1 Packed Encoding Rules, which makes them compact but considerably more complex to implement and debug than the text-based SIP.
