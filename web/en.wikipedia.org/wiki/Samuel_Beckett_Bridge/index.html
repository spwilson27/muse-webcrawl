Samuel Beckett Bridge

LocationDublin, Ireland — River Liffey
ArchitectSantiago Calatrava
Opened10 December 2009
TypeCable-stayed swing bridge
Length120 m (394 ft)

The Samuel Beckett Bridge is a cable-stayed swing bridge over the River Liffey in Dublin, Ireland. Designed by Spanish architect-engineer Santiago Calatrava, it links Sir John Rogerson's Quay on the south bank to Guild Street and North Wall Quay in the Docklands on the north bank. It is named after the Irish writer and Nobel laureate Samuel Beckett.

Design

The bridge's most striking feature is its single curved pylon, leaning forward over the river, from which 31 steel cables fan out to support the deck. The shape is intended to evoke a harp — the national emblem of Ireland — lying on its side, with the cables as its strings. The bridge carries four traffic lanes, cycle paths and footpaths. Unusually, the entire structure can swing open through 90 degrees on its central pier to allow ships to pass, although this function is rarely used.

Construction

The steel superstructure was fabricated by Hollandia in the Netherlands and transported by barge to Dublin in May 2009, a journey widely covered in Irish media. The project, managed by Dublin City Council and constructed with Graham Hollandia Joint Venture, cost approximately €60 million. It was opened by the Lord Mayor of Dublin on 10 December 2009.

Context

The bridge was Calatrava's second in Dublin, after the James Joyce Bridge upstream (2003), and formed part of the regeneration of the Dublin Docklands. It sits near the Convention Centre Dublin and has become one of the most recognisable modern landmarks of the city.
