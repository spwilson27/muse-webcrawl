The 1966 FIFA World Cup was the eighth FIFA World Cup, held in England from July 11 to 30, 1966. England defeated West Germany 4–2 after extra time in the final at Wembley Stadium, winning the country's only World Cup title. Geoff Hurst scored a hat-trick in the final, including the disputed third England goal that struck the crossbar and was judged to have crossed the line.

Tournament

Sixteen teams competed. Portugal, led by Eusébio, finished third in its debut; North Korea produced a famous upset by beating Italy and reaching the quarter-finals. Brazil, champions in 1958 and 1962, were eliminated in the group stage after physical treatment of Pelé became a major controversy. The Jules Rimet Trophy had been stolen before the tournament and recovered by a dog named Pickles.

Final and legacy

The final remains central to English sporting culture and to the England–Germany football rivalry. Hurst's second goal is still debated through the “Wembley goal” controversy, while his third, completed as spectators entered the field, supplied one of football commentary's most quoted lines.
