Eric Allman

Eric Paul Allman (born September 2, 1955) is an American software engineer, best known as the creator of sendmail and the syslog format.

Eric Paul Allman (born September 2, 1955) is an American computer programmer who developed sendmail and its precursor delivermail in the late 1970s and early 1980s while at the University of California, Berkeley. Sendmail became the dominant mail transfer agent on the Internet for decades, and Allman is regarded as one of the foundational figures of Internet email.

Early life and Berkeley

Allman was born in El Cerrito, California. He became involved with computing early and joined Berkeley as a student, working on the INGRES database project before turning to the problem of moving mail between the heterogeneous networks — ARPANET, UUCP and Berkeley's own Berknet — that coexisted on campus. He earned his bachelor's and master's degrees in computer science from UC Berkeley.

Sendmail

His first mail system, delivermail (1979), shipped with BSD Unix. For 4.1c BSD in 1983 he rewrote it as sendmail, a general-purpose internetwork mail router whose famously arcane configuration file allowed administrators to rewrite addresses and route mail between networks with different conventions. Sendmail was distributed free with BSD and became the standard SMTP server of the early Internet; at its height it handled the majority of the world's email. In 1998 Allman co-founded Sendmail, Inc. to commercialise the software.

Other work

Allman also created the syslog logging facility used throughout Unix-like systems, wrote the trek game distributed with BSD, and contributed to the tset terminal-handling utility. He later served as chief technology officer of Sendmail, Inc. and worked on email authentication standards. He is the author of the "Allman style" of C code indentation, the brace style used in the BSD source tree, in which opening braces sit on their own line aligned with the closing brace.

Personal life

Allman is openly gay and has spoken about being out in the technology industry since his student days. He lived for many years in Berkeley with his partner of more than three decades, Marshall Kirk McKusick, the BSD contributor.
