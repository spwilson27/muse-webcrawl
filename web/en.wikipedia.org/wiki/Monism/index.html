Monism

Monism is the metaphysical view that reality is fundamentally one: one substance (Spinoza's God-or-Nature), one kind of thing (materialism or idealism), or one whole (priority monism, on which the cosmos is the single basic object and parts are derivative). Ancient monists include Parmenides, for whom being is one and unchanging, and the atomists' critics; in Indian thought Advaita Vedanta's Brahman-without-a-second is a close cousin.

Forms

Substance monism (one token substance), attribute monism, and existence monism (only one object exists at all) are distinguished. Monism's appeal is unity and elegance; its burden is explaining the evident plurality and variety of the world it denies is fundamental.
