Euclidean space

Euclidean space
Field: Geometry, linear algebra
Notation: Rn or En
Structure: Real vector space with inner product
Named after: Euclid

Euclidean space is the fundamental space of geometry, intended to represent physical space. Originally, in Euclid's Elements, it was the three-dimensional space of Euclidean geometry, but in modern mathematics it refers to Euclidean spaces of any finite positive dimension. An n-dimensional Euclidean space can be defined as a real vector space equipped with an inner product—the dot product—which allows notions of distance and angle.

Definition

Using Cartesian coordinates, the Euclidean space of dimension n is identified with Rn, the set of n-tuples of real numbers. The distance between two points is given by the Pythagorean formula, and the geometry of the space is characterised by flatness: Euclid's parallel postulate holds, triangles have angles summing to 180 degrees, and circles have circumference 2πr.

History and generalisations

Euclid's synthetic axiomatic treatment dominated geometry for two millennia. In the 19th century the analytic definition via coordinates, vector spaces and inner products subsumed the classical theory, while the discovery of non-Euclidean geometries showed that Euclidean space is one geometry among many. Euclidean spaces remain foundational in physics (Newtonian space), calculus, and data analysis.
