Mast (sailing)

A mast is a tall, upright spar or pole on a sailing vessel, to which the sails are attached. A vessel's rig — the configuration of its masts, sails, and rigging — determines its sailing characteristics, and a ship may carry one or more masts. In a sailing vessel, the mast or masts provide the vertical support for the sails, transferring the wind's force into forward motion.

Types and names

On a traditional square-rigged ship, the masts are named by position from bow to stern: the foremast (forward), the mainmast (usually the tallest, amidships), and the mizzenmast (aft). Additional masts on larger vessels included the jiggermast. On fore-and-aft rigged vessels, masts may be named similarly, though a two-masted ketch has a mizzenmast forward of the rudder post while a yawl's mizzen is further aft.

Each mast is itself composed of sections on large sailing ships: the lower mast, the topmast, the topgallant mast, and (on some ships) the royal mast, built as separate spars joined at overlapping sections held by trestletrees and crosstrees.

Materials and construction

Historically, masts were made of wood — often fir or pine, prized for straightness and flexibility — and could reach enormous heights on clipper ships and warships. Modern masts are typically made of aluminium or carbon fibre, which offer lighter weight and higher stiffness for racing yachts. Masts are supported by standing rigging: stays running fore and aft and shrouds running to the sides.

Other uses of the term

The word "mast" is also used for the tall pole on a radio or television antenna (antenna mast), for a vertical pole on a windsurfing board, and for the upright structure on a forklift truck that raises and lowers its load.
