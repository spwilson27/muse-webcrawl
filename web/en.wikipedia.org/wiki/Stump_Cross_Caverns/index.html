Stump Cross Caverns

Stump Cross Caverns is a limestone cave system located in the Yorkshire Dales in northern England, between the villages of Pateley Bridge and Grassington in North Yorkshire. It is a popular show cave that has been open to the public since the 19th century, offering guided and self-guided tours through a series of chambers filled with stalactites, stalagmites, and other speleothems.

Geology

The caves were formed in Carboniferous limestone by the action of water dissolving the rock over hundreds of thousands of years. The system includes notable chambers such as the Wedding Cake, the Wolverine Cave, and a chamber containing a reindeer fossil thought to date from the last Ice Age. The caves lie at an altitude of around 440 metres (1,450 feet), making them one of the highest show caves in England.

History

The cave was discovered in 1860 by lead miners seeking new seams. Fossilised remains of animals including wolverine and reindeer have been found within the cave, providing evidence of fauna that lived in the area during the Pleistocene. The name is thought to derive from a boundary stone or "stump cross" that stood near the site.
