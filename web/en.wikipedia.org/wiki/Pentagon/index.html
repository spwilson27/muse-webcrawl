Location: Arlington County, Virginia, United States

The Pentagon

The Pentagon is the headquarters building of the United States Department of Defense, located in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. Named for its distinctive five-sided shape, it is one of the world's largest office buildings by floor area, with about 6.5 million square feet (600,000 m²), and employs roughly 23,000 military and civilian personnel. The word "Pentagon" is also commonly used as a metonym for the Department of Defense and its leadership.

Construction

The building was constructed on an accelerated schedule during the Second World War to consolidate War Department offices scattered around Washington. Ground was broken on 11 September 1941, and the building was dedicated on 15 January 1943. Its five concentric rings of offices are connected by ten radial corridors; despite its size, any point in the building can be reached on foot within about seven minutes. The design, led by architect George Bergstrom, answered the constraints of the site between Memorial Bridge and Arlington National Cemetery, and its low, sprawling form conserved steel needed for the war effort.

September 11 attacks

On 11 September 2001, exactly sixty years after construction began, hijacked American Airlines Flight 77 was flown into the western side of the Pentagon during the September 11 attacks, killing 125 people in the building and all 59 aboard the aircraft besides the hijackers. The damaged section had recently been renovated with blast-resistant features that limited casualties, and it was rebuilt within a year under the Phoenix Project.

Facilities

The building contains five above-ground floors plus mezzanine and basement levels, a five-acre central courtyard, and the Pentagon Memorial, dedicated in 2008, commemorating the victims of the 2001 attack. It is served by the Pentagon station of the Washington Metro and a major bus terminal.
