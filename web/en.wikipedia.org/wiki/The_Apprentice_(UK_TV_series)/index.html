The Apprentice is a British business-styled reality game show, first broadcast in 2005, in which a group of aspiring entrepreneurs compete in a series of business tasks for the chance to win an investment from businessman Lord Sugar (Alan Sugar), founder of Amstrad. It is the British version of the American format created by Mark Burnett and originally fronted by Donald Trump.

The boardroom, where candidates face Lord Sugar

Format

Around sixteen candidates, divided into two teams, complete weekly tasks ranging from buying and selling goods to designing advertising campaigns and launching new products. The losing team returns to the boardroom, where, after interrogation by Lord Sugar — assisted by his advisers, who have included Nick Hewer, Margaret Mountford, Karren Brady, Claude Littner and Tim Campbell — one candidate is dismissed with Sugar's catchphrase, "You're fired!"

Prize

In the first six series the winner received a six-figure job in one of Sugar's companies. From the seventh series (2011), the format changed: the winner receives a £250,000 investment and a 50/50 business partnership with Sugar. Spin-offs have included Young Apprentice, Celebrity Mastermind-style charity specials, and the companion discussion show You're Fired! on BBC Two.

Reception

The programme has been a long-running ratings success and winner of BAFTA awards, credited with popularising business television in Britain, though critics have questioned how far its edited confrontations reflect real business practice.
