Château de Valençay

The Château de Valençay is a château in the town of Valençay in the Indre department of central France, counted among the châteaux of the Loire Valley. Built in the 16th and 17th centuries in a blend of late Gothic, Renaissance, and classical styles, it is most famous for its association with the statesman Charles Maurice de Talleyrand.

History

The château was begun around 1540 by the d'Estampes family on the site of a medieval fortress. In 1803 Napoleon ordered his foreign minister Talleyrand to buy it as a place to receive foreign dignitaries in splendour. From 1808 to 1813 it was the gilded prison of the Spanish princes, including the future Ferdinand VII, detained there under the Treaty of Valençay. Talleyrand's celebrated chef Marie-Antoine Carême cooked at Valençay.

Grounds

The château stands in a large park with formal gardens, an orangery, and a small theatre built for Talleyrand in 1810. It is now owned by the department and the town and is open to the public, with rooms preserved in Empire style.
