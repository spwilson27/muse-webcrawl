The Severan dynasty was a dynasty of Roman emperors that ruled the Roman Empire between 193 and 235 AD. It was founded by the emperor Septimius Severus (r. 193–211) and included his sons Caracalla and Geta, the usurper Macrinus (who interrupted the line in 217–218), and the later emperors Elagabalus and Severus Alexander.

History

Septimius Severus, a senator from Leptis Magna in Africa, seized power in the civil wars of the Year of the Five Emperors (193) and founded a dynasty resting openly on military support. His reign saw the empire reach its greatest territorial extent with the annexation of northern Mesopotamia. Under Caracalla, the Constitutio Antoniniana (212) granted Roman citizenship to nearly all free inhabitants of the empire. The later Severan emperors were dominated by the women of the dynasty — Julia Domna, Julia Maesa, and Julia Mamaea — whose influence was a distinctive feature of the period.

End and significance

The assassination of Severus Alexander and his mother by mutinous troops in 235 ended the dynasty and opened the Crisis of the Third Century, half a century of civil war and fragmentation. The Severan age was nevertheless a period of legal flowering under the jurists Papinian, Ulpian, and Paul, and of extensive building in Rome and the provinces.
