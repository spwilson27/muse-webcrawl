The trimeric high-affinity IL-2 receptor bound to interleukin-2.

The interleukin-2 receptor (IL-2R) is a heteromeric protein complex found on the surface of certain immune cells, such as lymphocytes, that binds and responds to the cytokine interleukin-2 (IL-2). It regulates the proliferation and differentiation of T cells and is constitutively expressed at high levels on regulatory T cells.

Structure

The receptor consists of up to three chains: the alpha chain (CD25), the beta chain (CD122), and the common gamma chain (CD132, shared with other cytokine receptors). The βγ dimer forms a medium-affinity receptor; adding the α chain creates the high-affinity receptor that captures IL-2 at picomolar concentrations.

Signalling

IL-2 binding brings the cytoplasmic domains of the β and γ chains together, activating the Janus kinases JAK1 and JAK3 and phosphorylating STAT5, which drives transcription of genes involved in cell division and survival.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the γ chain cause X-linked severe combined immunodeficiency (X-SCID). Soluble CD25 is a biomarker in haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis and some lymphomas. The IL-2 pathway is targeted by drugs: basiliximab blocks CD25 in transplantation, high-dose IL-2 (aldesleukin) is used in some cancers, and low-dose IL-2 is trialled to expand regulatory T cells in autoimmunity.
