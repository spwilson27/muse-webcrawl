Gavi (wine)

TypeWhite wine, still and sparkling
RegionPiedmont, Italy
GrapeCortese
StatusDOCG (since 1998; DOC from 1974)

Gavi, or Cortese di Gavi, is an Italian white wine produced in a restricted area of the Province of Alessandria, in Piedmont, around the town of Gavi. Made entirely from the Cortese grape, it was granted DOC status in 1974 and elevated to DOCG in 1998, one of the most prestigious denominations of Piedmontese white wine.

Production area

The denomination covers vineyards in eleven communes, including Gavi, Novi Ligure, Serravalle Scrivia, Bosio and Tassarolo, in hills on the Ligurian Apennine foothills. Wines labelled Gavi di Gavi come from vineyards within the commune of Gavi itself. Still, frizzante and spumante versions are permitted, as well as a Riserva.

Characteristics

Gavi is typically pale straw in colour, with a delicate bouquet of white flowers, green apple, citrus and almond, and a fresh, dry, mineral palate with crisp acidity. It is traditionally paired with seafood, a legacy of the area's historic trade links with the Ligurian coast at Genoa.

History

Cortese has been grown in the Alessandria hills since at least the 17th century; a document of 1659 records the grape at a farm near Gavi. The wine gained international fame in the 20th century as a refined alternative to heavier Italian whites.
