Rosetta (spacecraft)

OperatorEuropean Space Agency (ESA)

Rosetta was a space probe built by the European Space Agency that performed the first rendezvous with, orbit of, and landing on a comet. Launched on 2 March 2004, it travelled for ten years through the inner Solar System before reaching comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko in August 2014. Its lander, Philae, touched down on the comet's nucleus on 12 November 2014 — the first soft landing on a comet nucleus in history.

Name

The mission was named after the Rosetta Stone, the ancient Egyptian stele whose parallel inscriptions allowed hieroglyphs to be deciphered; the spacecraft was likewise intended to unlock the secrets of the early Solar System preserved in comets. The lander was named for Philae, the Nile island where an obelisk bearing a bilingual inscription was found.

Journey

Rosetta was originally to visit comet 46P/Wirtanen, but a failure of an Ariane 5 launcher in December 2002 delayed the launch and the target was changed to 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko. Reaching the comet required four gravity assists — three from Earth and one from Mars — and a trajectory that carried the probe out beyond the orbit of Jupiter. En route it flew by the asteroids Šteins (2008) and Lutetia (2010). From June 2011 to January 2014 the spacecraft was placed in a deep-space hibernation to conserve power, waking on schedule on 20 January 2014.

At the comet

Rosetta arrived at 67P on 6 August 2014, revealing a nucleus shaped like two lobes joined by a narrow neck. On 12 November 2014 it released Philae, which descended to the surface at the site named Agilkia. Philae's anchoring harpoons failed to fire and the lander bounced twice before coming to rest in shadow, where its batteries ran down after about 57 hours of surface science; it briefly reawakened in June 2015. The lander was finally located in images in September 2016.

The orbiter escorted the comet through perihelion in August 2015 and studied the composition of its coma, finding among other results that the comet's water has a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio very different from Earth's oceans, and detecting molecular oxygen and the amino acid glycine. As the comet receded from the Sun and power dwindled, ESA ended the mission on 30 September 2016 by manoeuvring Rosetta into a controlled impact on the nucleus, in the Sais region of the comet's smaller lobe.
