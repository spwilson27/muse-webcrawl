Penne

TypePasta
Place of originItaly
Main ingredientsDurum wheat semolina flour, water
VariationsPenne lisce, penne rigate, pennette, mostaccioli

Penne (Italian: [ˈpenne]) is a type of pasta with cylinder-shaped pieces, their ends cut at an angle. Penne is the plural of the Italian penna (meaning "feather" but also, historically, "quill"), and its shape is intended to imitate a fountain pen's steel nib or a quill.

Description

The tubular shape holds sauces inside and out, and the diagonal cuts help scoop sauce. In Italy two main variants exist: penne lisce ("smooth penne") and penne rigate ("furrowed penne"), the latter with ridges along the surface that grip sauce. A smaller version is called pennette. In the United States, a related shape sold as mostaccioli is similar but typically lacks the angled cut.

The shape is comparatively recent: it was patented in 1865 by the Genoese pasta maker Giovanni Battista Capurro, whose machine cut the tubes diagonally without crushing them — previously, diagonal pasta cuts were made by hand with scissors.

Use

Penne is versatile, widely used in pasta bakes and in dishes such as penne all'arrabbiata (with a spicy tomato, garlic, and chilli sauce, a speciality of Lazio and Rome), penne alla vodka, and pasta salads. Its sturdy structure withstands oven baking well, making it common in dishes like baked penne with cheese and tomato.
