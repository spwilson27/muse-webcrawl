Douglas Engelbart

Douglas Carl Engelbart (January 30, 1925 – July 2, 2013) was an American engineer and inventor, and an early computer and Internet pioneer. He is best known as the inventor of the computer mouse and for his work at SRI International's Augmentation Research Center, where his team developed hypertext, networked computers, and precursors to the graphical user interface. His philosophy of using computers to augment human intellect shaped the field of human–computer interaction.

Career

After wartime service as a radar technician and an electrical engineering degree from Oregon State College, Engelbart earned a PhD from UC Berkeley in 1955. Inspired by Vannevar Bush's essay "As We May Think", he set out to build tools for augmenting collective intelligence. At SRI he founded the Augmentation Research Center and developed the oN-Line System (NLS).

The Mother of All Demos

On December 9, 1968, Engelbart presented a 90-minute live demonstration in San Francisco — later dubbed "The Mother of All Demos" — showcasing the mouse, hypertext links, multiple windows, video conferencing, collaborative editing and word processing, decades before these became commonplace.

Later life and honours

Engelbart's lab declined in the 1970s as researchers left for Xerox PARC. He later worked at Tymshare and McDonnell Douglas and founded the Bootstrap Institute to promote his vision of improving collective IQ. He received the Turing Award in 1997 and the National Medal of Technology in 2000.
