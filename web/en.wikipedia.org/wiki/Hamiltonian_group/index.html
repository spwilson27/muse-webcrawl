Hamiltonian group

In group theory, a branch of abstract algebra, a Dedekind group is a group all of whose subgroups are normal. All abelian groups are Dedekind groups. A non-abelian Dedekind group is called a Hamiltonian group, after William Rowan Hamilton, the inventor of quaternions.

The smallest example of a Hamiltonian group is the quaternion group Q8, the group of order 8 realized in the quaternions. Every Hamiltonian group is a direct product of the form Q8 × B × D, where B is an elementary abelian 2-group and D is a periodic abelian group all of whose elements have odd order.

History

Dedekind groups were named after Richard Dedekind, who investigated them in a paper of 1897, proving that they have the form described above. As a corollary, he proved that all abelian groups of order 8 are Dedekind groups. Hamilton had earlier presented the quaternions as a non-abelian example.

Properties

Every Hamiltonian group is nilpotent. The class of groups that are either abelian or Hamiltonian has sometimes been studied as the class of Dedekind groups. A finite group is a Dedekind group if and only if it is nilpotent and its Sylow subgroups are Dedekind.
