Carlos Gracie (14 September 1902 – 7 October 1994) was a Brazilian martial artist who, together with his younger brother Hélio Gracie, founded Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ), also known as Gracie jiu-jitsu. He is regarded as the patriarch of the Gracie family and one of the most influential figures in the development of modern grappling and mixed martial arts.

Early life

Carlos Gracie was born in Belém, in the northern Brazilian state of Pará, the eldest son of Gastão Gracie, a businessman descended from Scottish immigrants. As a teenager he studied judo and jiu-jitsu under Mitsuyo Maeda, a Japanese judoka who had settled in Belém and taught the Gracie brothers as a favour to their father, who had helped Maeda establish himself in Brazil.

Career

In 1925 Carlos opened the first Gracie academy in Rio de Janeiro, marketing the art as a complete system of self-defence suitable for smaller, weaker practitioners. To promote the school he issued open challenges and fought a series of no-holds-barred contests (vale tudo) against representatives of other styles, building the family's reputation. He later codified a diet, the Gracie Diet, which he promoted as part of the Gracie lifestyle.

Legacy

Carlos had 21 children, many of whom became prominent jiu-jitsu instructors and fighters. His descendants and those of his brother Hélio spread Brazilian jiu-jitsu worldwide, and the art became a foundational discipline of the Ultimate Fighting Championship and modern mixed martial arts.
