HMCS Sackville is a Canadian Flower-class corvette that served in the Royal Canadian Navy during the Second World War and is preserved as a museum ship in Halifax, Nova Scotia. She is the last surviving member of her class and serves as Canada's naval memorial, administered by the Canadian Naval Memorial Trust.

Service history

Sackville was built by the Saint John Dry Dock and Shipbuilding Co. and commissioned on 30 December 1941, named after the town of Sackville, New Brunswick. She spent most of the war escorting convoys across the North Atlantic in the Battle of the Atlantic, notably with escort group C-3 and later Mid-Ocean Escort Force groups. In August 1942, escorting convoy ON 115, she engaged three U-boats in a single night, damaging U-43 with depth charges and fighting gun duels with U-552 and U-704 on the surface. She was paid off on 20 July 1945.

Postwar and preservation

After the war the vessel served as a loop-laying and later oceanographic research ship for the Department of Marine and Fisheries. Recognising her historical importance, the Canadian Naval Memorial Trust restored her to her 1944 appearance — camouflage, armament and fittings — and she opened as a museum in Halifax in the 1980s. In 1988 she was designated a National Historic Site of Canada. Each May she hosts ceremonies for the Battle of the Atlantic commemorations, and wreaths from her stern are committed to the sea.
