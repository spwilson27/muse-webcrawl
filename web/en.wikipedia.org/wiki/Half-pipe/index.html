Half-pipe

A half-pipe is a structure used in gravity extreme sports such as skateboarding, snowboarding, freestyle skiing, BMX and aggressive inline skating. It consists of two concave ramps facing each other across a flat transition, resembling half of a pipe.

Structure

Each ramp has transitions (trannies) topped by vertical sections (vert) and coping at the lip. Skateboard half-pipes are typically about 3–4 m high; snow superpipes used in the Winter Olympics and X Games have walls about 6.7 m (22 ft) high.

History and competition

Half-pipes developed from empty swimming pools skated in 1970s California. Riders travel back and forth, performing aerial tricks above the lip. Halfpipe snowboarding became an Olympic event in 1998, and skateboarding vert competitions were a long-standing feature of the X Games.
