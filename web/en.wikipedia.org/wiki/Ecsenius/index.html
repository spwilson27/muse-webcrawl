Ecsenius

Ecsenius is a genus of combtooth blennies (family Blenniidae) found in the Pacific and Indian Oceans. It is the largest genus in the family, containing more than fifty recognised species. The genus was established by Richard McKay in 1972 and named for the Greek scholar and collector of fishes.

Description

Species of Ecsenius are small, bottom-dwelling fishes, most growing to only a few centimetres in length. Like other combtooth blennies, they have comb-like teeth used to scrape algae and detritus from rock and coral surfaces, and they lack scales. Many species are territorial, living in holes in dead coral or rock from which they dart out to feed and to which they retreat when threatened. A number of species mimic the venomous fang blennies of the genus Meiacanthus to gain protection from predators.

Species

Well-known species include the bicolor blenny (Ecsenius bicolor), the Midas blenny (Ecsenius midas), the striped blenny (Ecsenius nalolo), Ecsenius stigmatura and Ecsenius dilemma. Several species are popular in the marine aquarium trade because of their small size, algae-eating habits and bold behaviour. The genus is most diverse in the western Pacific, particularly around Indonesia, Papua New Guinea and the Great Barrier Reef.

Behaviour

Most species are herbivorous grazers, though some take zooplankton. Males guard eggs laid within their shelter holes. Their habit of perching upright on fins and watching their surroundings gives many species a characteristic, alert posture.
