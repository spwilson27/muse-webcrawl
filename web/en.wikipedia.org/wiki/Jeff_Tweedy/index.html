Jeffrey Scot Tweedy (born August 25, 1967) is an American musician, singer-songwriter and author, best known as the frontman of the rock band Wilco. He co-founded the influential alternative country band Uncle Tupelo with Jay Farrar in the late 1980s.

Early life

Tweedy was born in Belleville, Illinois, the youngest of four boys. His mother bought him his first guitar at age six, though he did not begin playing seriously until he was twelve, after a bicycle accident left him housebound for a summer. He formed the band The Plebes with Jay Farrar in high school, which evolved into Uncle Tupelo.

Career

Uncle Tupelo

Uncle Tupelo released four albums, including No Depression (1990), widely credited with launching the alternative country movement, before splitting in 1994 amid tensions between Tweedy and Farrar. Farrar went on to form Son Volt.

Wilco

Tweedy retained the remaining members of Uncle Tupelo to form Wilco. The band's albums include Being There (1996), Summerteeth (1999), the critically acclaimed Yankee Hotel Foxtrot (2002)—famously rejected by their label and streamed free online before becoming a commercial success—A Ghost Is Born (2004), which won the Grammy Award for Best Alternative Music Album, and Sky Blue Sky (2007). Tweedy also collaborated with Billy Bragg on the Mermaid Avenue albums setting Woody Guthrie lyrics to music.

Solo and other work

Tweedy has released solo albums including Together at Last (2017), WARM (2018) and Love Is the King (2020), and formed the band Tweedy with his son Spencer. His memoir Let's Go (So We Can Get Back) was published in 2018, followed by How to Write One Song (2020) and World Within a Song (2023). He has also produced albums for artists such as Mavis Staples.

Personal life

Tweedy has spoken openly about his struggles with chronic migraines, anxiety and past painkiller addiction, entering rehabilitation in 2004. He lives in Chicago with his wife, Susie, with whom he opened the Wilco Loft recording studio.
