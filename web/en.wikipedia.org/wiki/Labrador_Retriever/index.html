Labrador Retriever

The Labrador Retriever or simply Labrador is a British breed of retriever gun dog. It was developed in the United Kingdom from St. John's water dogs imported from the colony of Newfoundland (now part of Canada) and was named after the Labrador region of that colony. It is among the most commonly owned dog breeds in many countries, particularly in the Western world, and is widely kept as a pet, gun dog and assistance dog, and employed for scent detection.

History

The breed descends from the St. John's dogs of Newfoundland fishermen, which hauled nets and retrieved lines and fish from the icy North Atlantic. British sportsmen — among them the Earl of Malmesbury, the Duke of Buccleuch and Lord Home — imported them in the 19th century and standardised the breed as an unrivalled water retriever for shooting estates. The Kennel Club recognised the Labrador in 1903. Its popularity as a family pet exploded in the later 20th century; it topped the American Kennel Club's registration statistics for a record 31 consecutive years (1991–2022).

Characteristics

Labradors are medium-large, powerfully built dogs with a broad head, kind expression, otter-like tail and webbed feet suited to swimming. Temperament standards describe them as friendly, outgoing, eager to please and famously fond of food — a trait linked partly to a POMC gene variant common in the breed, which also predisposes them to obesity. Working lines are slighter and more energetic than show ("bench") lines.

Work and health

Beyond gundog work, Labradors' trainability and gentle nature make them the most common breed for guide dogs, assistance dogs and search-and-rescue, and their noses are used for narcotics, explosives and medical detection. Recognised health issues include hip and elbow dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy, exercise-induced collapse and hereditary myopathy; responsible breeders screen breeding stock. Gastric torsion and obesity-related disease are also concerns in the breed.
