Big Crunch

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Graph of the universe's scale factor over time rising, peaking, and collapsing back to zero]
The Big Crunch scenario: expansion reversed into collapse.

The Big Crunch is a hypothetical scenario for the ultimate fate of the universe, in which the expansion eventually reverses and the universe recollapses, ending in a state analogous to its beginning — all matter and energy crushed toward a singularity. It was a leading possibility when the universe's density seemed possibly above the critical value: a closed universe would expand, halt and contract, blueshifting the sky hotter until, in the final moments, galaxies, atoms and finally spacetime itself disintegrate — with speculative extensions (the Big Bounce) imagining the collapse seeding a new Big Bang, making the cosmos cyclic.

The 1998 discovery that expansion is accelerating, driven by Dark energy, largely ruled the scenario out in its simple form: the universe will instead dilute toward heat death. The Crunch survives if dark energy decays or reverses sign — not excluded in principle, and the subject of some recent (contested) analyses of supernova and DESI data — and in cyclic/ekpyrotic models where what looks like dark energy today ends in a turnaround in the far future.

Fate scenarios compared

The universe's destiny is an empirical question about the dark-energy equation of state: w = −1 exactly (cosmological constant) gives eternal acceleration and heat death; w < −1 gives the Big Rip (expansion tearing structures apart); evolving dark energy might give Crunch or Bounce. Current data are consistent with −1, and the scenarios' discriminating power lies decades of precision surveys ahead.
