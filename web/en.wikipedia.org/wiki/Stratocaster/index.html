The Fender Stratocaster, colloquially known as the Strat, is a model of electric guitar designed between 1952 and 1954 by Leo Fender, Bill Carson, George Fullerton and Freddie Tavares. It has been manufactured continuously by the Fender Musical Instruments Corporation since 1954 and is one of the most recognisable and widely copied electric guitar designs in the world.

Design

The Stratocaster introduced a contoured solid body with a double cutaway, three single-coil pickups, and a "synchronized tremolo" bridge that allows pitch bending. Its bolt-on maple neck, six-in-line headstock and comfort-contoured body were radical departures from the arch-top guitars of the era. A five-way pickup selector switch (standardised in 1977) gives access to pickup combinations including the distinctive out-of-phase "quack" tones.

Players and influence

Famous Stratocaster players include Buddy Holly, Jimi Hendrix, Eric Clapton, David Gilmour, Stevie Ray Vaughan, John Mayer and Yngwie Malmsteen. The guitar has been central to rock, blues, pop, country and many other genres.
