Sesquipedalianism

Sesquipedalianism is the practice of using long words, or a habit of speech and writing characterised by them. A person given to it is a sesquipedalianist, and an individual long word may itself be described as sesquipedalian.

Etymology

The term derives from the Latin sesquipedalis, "a foot and a half long", from sesqui- ("one and a half") and pes ("foot"). The Roman poet Horace used the phrase sesquipedalia verba — "words a foot and a half long" — mocking writers who used ponderous language in his Ars Poetica.

Usage and criticism

Sesquipedalian style is often associated with technical, academic and bureaucratic prose, and is frequently criticised as obscuring meaning where simpler words would serve. Deliberate sesquipedalianism is also used for comic effect, and competitive celebrations of very long words — such as pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis, among the longest words in English dictionaries — form a minor literary tradition.
