Maria Elisabeth Dickin CBE (22 September 1870 – 1 March 1951) was a British social reformer and animal welfare pioneer. In 1917 she founded the People's Dispensary for Sick Animals (PDSA), which provided free veterinary care for the animals of the poor, and in 1943 she instituted the Dickin Medal, often called "the animals' Victoria Cross", for animals displaying conspicuous gallantry in war.

Work

Shocked by the suffering of animals in London's East End, Dickin opened the first PDSA dispensary in Whitechapel in 1917. The charity grew rapidly, operating clinics, mobile dispensaries and animal hospitals across Britain. She was appointed OBE in 1929 and CBE in 1948.

Dickin Medal

The bronze Dickin Medal was awarded 75 times between 1943 and 1949 and again in later decades, to carrier pigeons, dogs, horses and one cat, Simon of HMS Amethyst.
