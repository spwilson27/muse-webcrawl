William I (German: Wilhelm I; 22 March 1797 – 9 March 1888) was King of Prussia from 1861 and the first German Emperor (Deutscher Kaiser) from the unification of Germany in 1871 until his death. Under his reign, and the chancellorship of Otto von Bismarck, Prussia won the wars against Denmark, Austria and France that created the German Empire.

Early life and regency

The second son of Frederick William III, William served as an officer in the Napoleonic Wars and later became associated with the conservative military establishment. During the revolutions of 1848 his hardline reputation forced him briefly into exile in England, earning him the liberal nickname "Prince of Grapeshot" after he directed the suppression of the Baden uprising in 1849. In 1858 he became regent for his incapacitated brother Frederick William IV, and succeeded him as king in 1861.

Unification of Germany

A constitutional conflict over army reform led William to appoint Bismarck as Minister President in 1862. Bismarck then directed the wars of unification: the Second Schleswig War (1864), the Austro-Prussian War (1866), and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71). On 18 January 1871, in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, William was proclaimed German Emperor — a title he accepted reluctantly, preferring "Emperor of Germany".

Later reign

William survived two assassination attempts in 1878, which Bismarck used to pass the Anti-Socialist Laws. Revered in his old age as the white-bearded hero of unification (Barbablanca, echoing the Barbarossa legend), he died in Berlin in March 1888; the "Year of the Three Emperors" saw him succeeded by his son Frederick III and then his grandson William II.
