James Gregory (mathematician)

BornNovember 1638, Drumoak, Aberdeenshire, Scotland
DiedOctober 1675, Edinburgh, Scotland
FieldsMathematics, astronomy
Known forReflecting telescope (Gregorian); infinite series; early calculus

James Gregory (November 1638 – October 1675), sometimes spelled Gregorie, was a Scottish mathematician and astronomer. He described the first practical reflecting telescope (the Gregorian telescope), discovered infinite series representations for trigonometric functions, and anticipated key results of calculus before Newton and Leibniz published their work.

Work

Gregory studied in Padua, Italy, where he wrote Vera Circuli et Hyperbolae Quadratura (1667), an attempt at proving that the areas of the circle and hyperbola could not be obtained by classical means. His Optica Promota (1663) described a reflecting telescope using a concave secondary mirror. In 1671 he discovered the series expansion for the arctangent — later generalised into the Gregory series for pi — and independently arrived at results closely related to the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Career

Gregory became the first Regius Professor of Mathematics at the University of St Andrews in 1668 and later took the mathematics chair at Edinburgh in 1674, dying the following year at the age of 36.
