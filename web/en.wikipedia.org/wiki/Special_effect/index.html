Special effect

Special effects (often abbreviated as SFX, SPFX, or simply FX) are illusions or visual tricks used in the theatre, film, television, video game, amusement, and simulator industries to simulate the imagined events in a story or virtual world. Traditionally, special effects are divided into mechanical (physical) effects, achieved during live-action shooting, and optical effects, achieved in post-production; since the 1990s, computer-generated imagery (CGI) has largely absorbed the latter category and is usually termed visual effects (VFX).

Mechanical effects

Physical effects include pyrotechnics, weather effects (rain, snow and wind machines), breakaway props, squibs simulating bullet hits, hydraulic gimbals, animatronics, and special makeup effects such as prosthetics. Miniature models — "bigatures" when very large — were long used for vehicles and environments, from the ships of silent epics to the models of 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) and Star Wars (1977).

Optical effects

Optical techniques include matte paintings, rear and front projection, the Schüfftan process, optical printing, double exposure, and stop-motion animation. Ray Harryhausen's "Dynamation" model animation defined the genre for a generation of fantasy films.

Digital era

The 1990s saw a transition: Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991) and Jurassic Park (1993) demonstrated photoreal CGI creatures and transformations, after which digital compositing and 3D animation displaced most optical methods. Modern productions combine practical and digital effects — "in-camera VFX" using LED volumes (popularised by The Mandalorian, 2019) has revived some optical-era ideas on set.

Safety

Special effects work has a history of serious accidents, driving strict licensing for pyrotechnicians and stunt coordination; the 1982 Twilight Zone: The Movie helicopter crash led to major reforms in US film-set safety practice.
