Pacific Legal Foundation

The Pacific Legal Foundation (PLF) is an American nonprofit public-interest law firm established in 1973 in Sacramento, California. It describes itself as defending individual liberty, private property rights, free enterprise and limited government, and it litigates — free of charge to its clients — in courts across the United States, including the Supreme Court, where it has won a notable series of property-rights and administrative-law cases.

History

PLF was founded in 1973 by staff members from Governor Ronald Reagan's administration, making it the oldest organisation in the conservative and libertarian public-interest legal movement. From its early work on California land-use and environmental regulation, it expanded into a national litigation practice with offices across the country.

Notable cases

The Foundation has argued and won numerous cases before the U.S. Supreme Court. Among the best known are Nollan v. California Coastal Commission (1987), which restricted government's ability to demand concessions in exchange for building permits; Sackett v. EPA (2012), which allowed landowners to challenge EPA compliance orders in court; Knick v. Township of Scott (2019), which opened federal courts to takings claims; and a second Sackett decision (2023) narrowing the federal government's definition of "waters of the United States" under the Clean Water Act. PLF also represented Abigail Fisher in her challenge to affirmative action at the University of Texas.

Positions and criticism

PLF's litigation commonly challenges environmental regulation, wetlands protection, rent control and occupational licensing, and defends school choice and equal-treatment claims. Supporters praise it as a defender of constitutional rights against regulatory overreach; critics in the environmental and labour movements describe it as an industry-aligned opponent of public-interest regulation.
