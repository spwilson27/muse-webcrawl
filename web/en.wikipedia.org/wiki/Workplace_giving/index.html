Workplace giving

Workplace giving is a form of charitable fundraising in which employees make donations to nonprofit organisations, most commonly through regular payroll deductions arranged by their employer. In the United States, workplace campaigns raise billions of dollars annually for charities, federated campaigns such as United Way historically having organised much of the activity. The largest single programme is the US federal government's Combined Federal Campaign.

Payroll deduction

The defining mechanism is the payroll gift: an employee authorises a fixed amount or percentage per pay period, and the employer deducts and forwards the accumulated funds to the designated charity or federation. Because donations are taken automatically, participation and retention tend to be higher than with one-off appeals, and donors give smaller amounts more frequently. In the United Kingdom the equivalent scheme, Payroll Giving (Give As You Earn, launched in 1987), is taken from pre-tax pay, so the donation receives immediate tax relief. In the US donations are made from post-tax pay but are generally tax-deductible for itemising taxpayers.

Forms and matching

Modern programmes extend beyond payroll deduction to employer matching gifts, in which a company matches employee donations — commonly dollar-for-dollar and sometimes more, up to a cap — volunteer grants that convert recorded volunteer hours into cash grants to the volunteer's charity, and one-off disaster-relief or seasonal appeals. Digital platforms have largely replaced paper pledge cards, allowing employees to research charities, give to any registered nonprofit, and carry a giving account between employers.

Combined Federal Campaign

The Combined Federal Campaign (CFC), established in 1961 by executive order of President John F. Kennedy, consolidated earlier competing federal fund drives into a single annual campaign through which federal civilian and military personnel can give by payroll deduction to thousands of vetted charities. It is overseen by the US Office of Personnel Management and is described as the largest workplace giving campaign in the world, raising hundreds of millions of dollars a year at its peak.

Criticism and trends

Traditional federated campaigns have been criticised for restricting donor choice, for administrative costs, and for workplace pressure during pledge drives. Participation rates in classic United Way-style campaigns have declined since the 1990s as donors shifted toward direct and online giving, prompting federations and employers to broaden charity choice and adopt year-round, employee-directed platforms.
