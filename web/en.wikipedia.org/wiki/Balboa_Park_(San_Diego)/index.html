Balboa Park is a 1,200-acre (490 ha) urban cultural park in San Diego, California. Named for the Spanish explorer Vasco Núñez de Balboa, it was reserved as parkland in 1868, making it one of the oldest public recreational sites in the United States. The park contains museums, gardens, theatres, and the San Diego Zoo.

History

City leaders reserved 1,400 acres as "City Park" in 1868; landscape planting began under horticulturist Kate Sessions, the "Mother of Balboa Park", in the 1890s. The park took its present form for the 1915–16 Panama–California Exposition, which produced the Spanish Colonial Revival ensemble along El Prado, including the California Building and Tower, designed by Bertram Goodhue. The 1935–36 California Pacific International Exposition added further buildings. Much of the park was used by the U.S. Navy during both world wars. The historic core was designated a National Historic Landmark District in 1977.

Attractions

Institutions include the San Diego Museum of Art, the Mingei International Museum, the San Diego Museum of Man (Museum of Us), the Fleet Science Center, the San Diego Natural History Museum, and the Old Globe Theatre, a replica of Shakespeare's theatre opened in 1935. The Botanical Building (1915), one of the largest lath structures in the world, fronts a lily pond. Seventeen gardens range from a Japanese friendship garden to desert and rose gardens. The San Diego Zoo, founded in 1916 from exposition animal exhibits, occupies 100 acres in the park's northwest.

Access

The park is free to enter and draws millions of visitors annually; December Nights, a two-day holiday festival, is among its largest events.
