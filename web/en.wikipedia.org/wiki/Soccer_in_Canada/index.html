Soccer in Canada

Soccer in Canada (called football in French, le soccer in Quebec usage varies) is governed by the Canadian Soccer Association, founded in 1912. The sport has a long history in the country: a Canadian club, Galt F.C., won the gold medal at the 1904 St. Louis Olympics, and Canada won the 1985 CONCACAF Championship, qualifying for its first World Cup in 1986.

Professional leagues

Three Canadian clubs — Toronto FC, CF Montréal and Vancouver Whitecaps FC — play in Major League Soccer; Toronto FC won the MLS Cup in 2017. The fully professional Canadian Premier League began play in 2019. On the women's side, the Northern Super League launched in 2025, giving Canada its first domestic professional women's league.

National teams

The women's national team has been among the world's best, winning gold at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics (held in 2021) and bronze at London 2012 and Rio 2016; Christine Sinclair retired as international football's all-time leading scorer. The men's team returned to the World Cup at Qatar 2022 after a 36-year absence, and Canada co-hosts the 2026 FIFA World Cup with the United States and Mexico.

Participation

Soccer is among the most-played team sports in Canada, particularly among children, with registered participation rivalling ice hockey — fuelled by the country's diverse immigrant communities.
