Internet art

Internet art
Other namesNet art, net.art, web art
Period1990s – present
Key venuesRhizome, Whitney Biennial, documenta X, ZKM

Internet art (also called net art) is a form of new media art distributed via the Internet. It typically exploits the interactive, hyperlinked and networked character of the medium, and emerged as an art movement in the mid-1990s alongside the popularisation of the World Wide Web. Works may take the form of websites, browser-based performances, software art, online interventions and telematic projects.

History

The term "net.art" became attached around 1995–97 to a loose international circle of artists including Vuk Ćosić, JODI (Joan Heemskerk and Dirk Paesmans), Olia Lialina, Alexei Shulgin and Heath Bunting, connected by mailing lists such as Nettime. Early works played with the browser itself — JODI's scrambled pages, Lialina's hypertext narrative My Boyfriend Came Back from the War (1996) — and institutions soon followed: net art appeared at documenta X (1997) and the Whitney Biennial (2000), while the platform Rhizome (founded 1996) became a central archive and community. Later generations addressed social media, surveillance, platform economies and post-internet aesthetics.

Characteristics

Internet art is typically process-based and immaterial, dependent on software, networks and user participation rather than on a fixed object. This raises distinctive problems of preservation: works decay as browsers, plug-ins and servers change, so museums employ emulation, migration and documentation strategies to keep historical net art accessible.
