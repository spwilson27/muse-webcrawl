Pomerol AOC

Region: Bordeaux, Right Bank

Main grape: Merlot

Notable estate: Château Pétrus

Pomerol is a French wine appellation (Appellation d'origine contrôlée, AOC) on the Right Bank of the Bordeaux wine region, near the town of Libourne. It is the smallest of Bordeaux's major appellations, at roughly 800 hectares, yet it produces some of the most sought-after and expensive wines in the world.

Geography and grapes

Pomerol's plateau is composed of gravel, sand and clay, including a distinctive subsoil of iron-rich clay known as crasse de fer; the finest estates sit on a band of blue clay. In sharp contrast to the Cabernet Sauvignon-dominated Left Bank, Pomerol wines are made primarily from Merlot, usually blended with Cabernet Franc. The Merlot gives Pomerol wines their characteristic plush, velvety texture, richness and early approachability, though top wines age for decades.

Estates

Unlike the Médoc, Pomerol has never been officially classified. Its most famous estate is Château Pétrus, made almost entirely from Merlot and among the most expensive wines in the world. Other leading properties include Le Pin — tiny in production and likewise extremely costly — Château Lafleur, Vieux Château Certan, Château L'Évangile, Château Trotanoy and Château La Conseillante. Many estates are small family holdings of only a few hectares, in keeping with the appellation's fragmented ownership.
