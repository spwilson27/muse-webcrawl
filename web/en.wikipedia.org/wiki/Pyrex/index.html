Pyrex

Product typeGlassware, cookware
Introduced1915
OriginatorCorning Glass Works

Pyrex (trademarked as PYREX and pyrex) is a brand introduced by Corning Inc. in 1915 for a line of clear, low-thermal-expansion borosilicate glass used for laboratory glassware and kitchenware. It was later expanded to include kitchenware products made of other materials, including tempered soda-lime glass.

History

Corning developed its borosilicate glass (originally sold as Nonex) for railroad lanterns and battery jars. Bessie Littleton, wife of Corning scientist Jesse Littleton, famously tested a cut-down battery jar as a cake pan, leading to the launch of Pyrex bakeware in 1915. The glass's resistance to thermal shock made it popular for both laboratory and domestic use.

Materials

European Pyrex products, made by Arc International, remain borosilicate. In the United States, kitchenware sold under the pyrex name since the late 1990s has generally been made of tempered soda-lime glass, which is more resistant to mechanical impact but less resistant to sudden temperature change.
