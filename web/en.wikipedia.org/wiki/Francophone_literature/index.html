Francophone literature is literature written in the French language by authors from countries and regions outside metropolitan France where French is spoken — including parts of Africa, the Caribbean, Canada, Belgium, Switzerland, the Middle East and Southeast Asia. The term francophone in this literary sense usually distinguishes these works from the literature of France itself.


French-language writing from across the francophone world

History

French-language writing outside France grew out of the colonial expansion of France and Belgium, the slave trade in the Caribbean, and older French settlement in North America. Early francophone literatures developed in Louisiana, Haiti, Quebec and the Maghreb. In the 20th century, writers from colonized societies began using French — often the language of their schooling — to describe their own cultures, histories and grievances, frequently reshaping the language in the process.

Négritude and after

The most influential movement was Négritude, launched in the 1930s in Paris by Aimé Césaire of Martinique, Léopold Sédar Senghor of Senegal and Léon Damas of French Guiana. It asserted the value of Black African culture and identity against colonial assimilation. Césaire's Cahier d'un retour au pays natal (1939) became a foundational text.

After independence in the 1950s and 1960s, francophone African fiction flourished with authors such as Ahmadou Kourouma (Les Soleils des indépendances), Mariama Bâ (Une si longue lettre) and Ousmane Sembène. In the Caribbean, Édouard Glissant developed the poetics of Antillanité and Relation, and Patrick Chamoiseau, Raphaël Confiant and Jean Bernabé articulated Créolité. Maghrebi writers such as Kateb Yacine, Assia Djebar and Tahar Ben Jelloun explored colonial memory, language and gender.

Littérature-monde

In 2007 a manifesto signed by 44 writers, "Pour une littérature-monde en français," declared the centre–periphery model dead and called for a single world literature in French. The debate renewed questions about whether "francophone" remains a useful category or a marginalizing one.
