Publius Licinius Valerianus (c. 193/200–260/264), known as Valerian, was Roman emperor from 253 to 260, ruling jointly with his son Gallienus, whom he left in the west while he campaigned in the east during the Crisis of the Third Century.

Reign

A senator proclaimed emperor by the Rhine legions in 253, Valerian restored order after civil war, but faced plague (the Plague of Cyprian), Gothic invasions and renewed war with Sasanian Persia under Shapur I. He issued edicts in 257–258 persecuting Christians, leading to the martyrdoms of Pope Sixtus II, Cyprian of Carthage and others.

Capture

In 260, after the Battle of Edessa, Valerian was captured by Shapur I — the only Roman emperor taken prisoner by a foreign enemy. Persian reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam depict his submission. Christian writers such as Lactantius claimed he was used as a footstool and after death flayed, though these accounts are hostile tradition; the date and circumstances of his death in captivity are uncertain.

Aftermath

His capture shocked the Roman world, ushering in the fragmentation Gallienus faced alone: the Gallic Empire in the west and Palmyra under Odaenathus defending the east.
