Carrier-based aircraft

A carrier-based aircraft (also carrier-capable aircraft or carrier-borne aircraft) is a naval aircraft designed for operations from aircraft carriers. Carrier aircraft must be sturdy enough to withstand the demanding conditions of carrier operations, fold their wings for storage, and be able to launch and recover in very short distances.

Design requirements

Key features include a reinforced airframe and landing gear, an arrestor hook for landing, corrosion protection against sea water, low approach speeds, and folding wings to fit hangar decks and elevators. Launch methods include catapult-assisted take-off (CATOBAR), short take-off (STOBAR), and vertical/short take-off (STOVL) configurations.

Types

  • Fighters and strike aircraft — e.g., F/A-18E/F Super Hornet, F-35B/C Lightning II, Rafale M, MiG-29K.
  • Airborne early warning — e.g., E-2 Hawkeye.
  • Electronic warfare — e.g., EA-18G Growler.
  • Helicopters — anti-submarine, utility, and search-and-rescue roles.
  • Carrier onboard delivery — e.g., C-2 Greyhound, CMV-22 Osprey.

History

Carrier aviation began in the 1910s with experimental take-offs from ships; the first true carriers appeared in the 1920s. Carrier aircraft became decisive in World War II battles such as Taranto, Pearl Harbor, Midway, and the Philippine Sea. The jet age brought angled decks, steam catapults, and mirror landing aids, transforming carrier operations.
