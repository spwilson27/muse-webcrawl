Christmas in Ukraine (Ukrainian: Різдво, Rizdvo) blends Orthodox and Greek Catholic liturgical observance with older folk customs. The centrepiece is the Christmas Eve supper, Sviata Vecheria ("Holy Supper"), a meatless meal of twelve dishes symbolising the twelve apostles, eaten after the first star appears in the evening sky.

Date

Historically, most Ukrainian churches followed the Julian calendar, celebrating Christmas on 7 January (25 December Julian). Following Russia's 2022 invasion and a broader shift away from Moscow-linked tradition, the Orthodox Church of Ukraine and the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church adopted the Revised Julian calendar in 2023, and Ukraine changed its public holiday to 25 December. Some parishes and the Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate) continue to observe 7 January.

Customs

Before supper, a sheaf of wheat called a didukh ("grandfather") is brought into the house to honour the ancestors and the harvest; straw may be scattered under the tablecloth, and an empty place is set for departed family members. The twelve dishes typically include kutia (sweet wheat berry pudding with honey, poppy seeds, and walnuts), borscht with mushroom dumplings (vushka), varenyky, fish, holubtsi, beans, and uzvar, a dried-fruit compote. Households fast during the day of Christmas Eve.

Carolling and vertep

Carol singers (koliadnyky) go from house to house singing koliadky, some of pre-Christian origin, carrying a large spinning star on a pole. The famous melody "Carol of the Bells" derives from Mykola Leontovych's arrangement of the Ukrainian New Year carol Shchedryk. A vertep — a portable puppet theatre or costumed nativity performance — enacts the Christmas story, often mixing religious scenes with comic folk characters. Malanka celebrations on New Year's Eve (13 January in the old calendar) extend the season with masquerades in western Ukraine.
