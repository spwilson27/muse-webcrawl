Midi-Pyrénées

Midi-Pyrénées
Country: France
Capital: Toulouse
Status: Former region (until 2016)
Now part of: Occitanie

Midi-Pyrénées (Occitan: Miègjorn-Pirenèus) was a former administrative region of southern France and, before the 2016 territorial reform, the largest region of metropolitan France by area. Its capital and largest city was Toulouse.

Geography

The region stretched from the Pyrenees along the Spanish border in the south to the Massif Central in the north-east, and comprised eight departments: Ariège, Aveyron, Gers, Haute-Garonne, Hautes-Pyrénées, Lot, Tarn and Tarn-et-Garonne. Landmarks include the pilgrimage town of Lourdes, the medieval fortress city of Carcassonne nearby (in neighbouring Languedoc), and the Pic du Midi de Bigorre observatory.

Merger

On 1 January 2016, Midi-Pyrénées merged with Languedoc-Roussillon to form the new region of Occitanie, with Toulouse remaining the regional capital. The area is known for aerospace industry (Airbus headquarters), cassoulet, and Armagnac.
