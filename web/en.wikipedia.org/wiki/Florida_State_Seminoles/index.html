The Florida State Seminoles are the athletic teams representing Florida State University (FSU) in Tallahassee, Florida. The Seminoles compete in NCAA Division I and are members of the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC), which FSU joined in 1991. The university fields 18 varsity teams in nine men's and ten women's sports.

History

Florida State's athletic program dates to the late 19th century, when the institution was the Florida State College. The Seminole nickname was adopted in 1947, chosen by students in honor of the Seminole Tribe of Florida, with whom the university maintains a formal relationship. The tribe has officially endorsed FSU's use of the name and imagery, including the portrayal of Chief Osceola by a student riding the Appaloosa horse Renegade at football games.

Football

The Florida State Seminoles football program is the school's most prominent team. Under head coach Bobby Bowden (1976–2009), the Seminoles became a national power, winning national championships in 1993 and 1999 and finishing in the top five of the AP poll for 14 consecutive seasons (1987–2000). Jimbo Fisher led the team to a third national championship in the 2013 season. The team plays at Doak Campbell Stadium.

Other sports

Florida State has won national championships in several other sports, including women's soccer, softball, men's track and field, and women's cross country. The baseball program, long led by Mike Martin, is among the most successful in college baseball by wins, and the women's soccer team has won multiple NCAA titles in the 21st century.

Rivalries

The Seminoles' chief rivals are the Florida Gators and the Miami Hurricanes. The annual football games against these in-state opponents are among the most watched in college football.
