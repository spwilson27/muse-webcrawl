Virtual reality headset

A virtual reality headset (or VR headset) is a head-mounted display that provides virtual reality for the wearer. VR headsets are widely used with video games but are also used in other applications, including simulators and trainers. They comprise a stereoscopic head-mounted display (providing separate images for each eye), stereo sound, and sensors for head-motion tracking, often with positionally tracked hand controllers.

Technology

Each eye views a small high-refresh-rate display (LCD or OLED) through lenses that create a wide field of view and the illusion of depth. Inertial measurement units track rotation at high speed, while inside-out cameras or external base stations track position in space ("6DoF"). Low motion-to-photon latency, ideally under 20 milliseconds, is critical to comfort; excessive latency and vergence–accommodation conflict can cause simulator sickness.

History and major products

Early devices included Ivan Sutherland's "Sword of Damocles" (1968) and 1990s systems such as Nintendo's Virtual Boy. The modern era began with the Oculus Rift Kickstarter (2012), followed by the HTC Vive and PlayStation VR (2016). Standalone headsets using mobile processors — Meta Quest (2019), Quest 2 (2020), and Quest 3 (2023) — brought VR to a mass market, while Apple's Vision Pro (2024) popularised mixed reality passthrough at the premium end.
