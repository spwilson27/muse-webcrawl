Lester Bowles Pearson (23 April 1897 – 27 December 1972) was a Canadian scholar, statesman, diplomat, soldier, and politician who served as the 14th prime minister of Canada from 1963 to 1968. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1957 for organizing the United Nations Emergency Force to resolve the Suez Canal Crisis, and is widely credited as a founder of the modern concept of peacekeeping.

Early life and diplomatic career

Pearson was born in Newtonbrook, Ontario (now part of Toronto), and studied at the University of Toronto and the University of Oxford. He served in the First World War as a medical orderly and later as a flying officer; it was during flight training that he acquired the nickname "Mike," by which he was known throughout his life. After the war he joined Canada's Department of External Affairs and rose to become one of the country's leading diplomats, serving as ambassador to the United States and as a central figure in the founding of both the United Nations and NATO.

Foreign minister and the Nobel Prize

Entering politics with the Liberal Party in 1948, Pearson became Secretary of State for External Affairs under Prime Minister Louis St. Laurent. During the Suez Crisis of 1956, Pearson proposed the creation of an armed neutral force to interpose between combatants while a settlement was negotiated — the first large-scale UN peacekeeping force. For this he received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1957, the first Canadian to win it individually.

Prime Minister

Pearson became Liberal leader in 1958 and prime minister in 1963, leading two successive minority governments. Despite lacking a parliamentary majority, his governments introduced landmark measures: the Canada Pension Plan, universal health care (Medicare), the Canada Student Loans Program, and the abolition of capital punishment for ordinary crimes. In 1965 his government adopted the red maple leaf as Canada's new national flag after a prolonged parliamentary debate. Pearson retired in 1968 and was succeeded by Pierre Trudeau. Toronto's international airport and numerous schools bear his name.
