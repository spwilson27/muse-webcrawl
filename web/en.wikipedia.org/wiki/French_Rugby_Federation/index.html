French Rugby Federation

Sport: Rugby union
Founded: 1919
Affiliation: World Rugby (since 1978, via IRFB membership from 1948)
Headquarters: Marcoussis, France

The French Rugby Federation (French: Fédération Française de Rugby, FFR) is the governing body for rugby union in France. It is responsible for the French national team, the development of the game at all levels, and the organisation of amateur and community rugby. Professional club rugby is administered in partnership with the Ligue Nationale de Rugby (LNR), which runs the Top 14 and Pro D2 competitions.

History

Rugby was administered in France from the late 19th century by the multi-sport Union des Sociétés Françaises de Sports Athlétiques (USFSA). The FFR was founded in 1919 and took over the running of the French championship. France was expelled from the Five Nations in 1931 amid concerns over professionalism and on-field violence, and was readmitted in 1939, though the championship did not resume until after the Second World War. France joined the International Rugby Football Board in 1948.

National teams

The FFR fields the France national team (Les Bleus), which competes in the Six Nations Championship and the Rugby World Cup, where France has reached the final three times (1987, 1999 and 2011). It also oversees the women's national team, under-20 and age-grade teams, and France's rugby sevens teams, whose men's side won the gold medal at the Paris 2024 Olympic Games.

Organisation

The federation is headquartered at the Centre National du Rugby in Marcoussis, south of Paris, which also serves as the national training base. The president of the FFR is elected by an assembly of club representatives. Bernard Laporte served as president from 2016 until 2023; Florian Grill was elected president in 2023. The federation counts several hundred thousand licensed players and more than a thousand affiliated clubs across metropolitan France and the overseas territories.
