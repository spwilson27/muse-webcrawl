Plug-in hybrid

A plug-in hybrid electric vehicle (PHEV) is a hybrid electric vehicle whose battery pack can be recharged by plugging a charging cable into an external electric power source, in addition to internally by its on-board internal combustion engine (ICE) powered generator. Most PHEVs are passenger cars, but there are also vans, trucks, buses, trains and military vehicles.

BatteryRechargeable, typically 5–25 kWh (cars)

Characteristics

PHEVs share the characteristics of both conventional hybrid electric vehicles, having an electric motor and an internal combustion engine, and of battery electric vehicles, having a plug to connect to the electrical grid. Their battery packs are substantially larger than those of conventional hybrids, giving typical electric-only ranges of 30 to 80 kilometres (20–50 mi) in modern models. When the battery is depleted, the vehicle operates as a conventional hybrid.

History

The first mass-produced plug-in hybrid was the Chevrolet Volt, launched in December 2010, followed by the plug-in version of the Toyota Prius in 2012 and the Mitsubishi Outlander P-HEV in 2013, which became one of the world's best-selling plug-in hybrids. China and Europe became the largest PHEV markets during the 2010s and 2020s, with models such as the BYD Qin and Song series selling in large numbers.

Operation modes

Most PHEVs can operate in charge-depleting mode (using stored grid electricity), charge-sustaining mode (maintaining battery level with the engine), or a blended mode. Series PHEVs, in which only the electric motor drives the wheels (sometimes called extended-range electric vehicles), include the Chevrolet Volt and BMW i3 with range extender.

Benefits and criticism

Supporters argue PHEVs allow most daily driving to be done electrically while retaining long-distance flexibility. Studies by organisations such as the International Council on Clean Transportation have criticised real-world fuel savings, finding that company-car PHEVs in Europe are driven electrically far less than official test figures assume, although private owners who charge regularly achieve much better results.
