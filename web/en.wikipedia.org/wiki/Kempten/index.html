Kempten (Swabian: Kempte) is a town in the Allgäu region of Bavaria, Germany, on the river Iller. With about 68,000 inhabitants it is one of the oldest towns in Germany, tracing its origins to the Celtic and Roman settlement of Cambodunum.

History

Cambodunum is mentioned by Strabo as a Celtic town and became an important Roman city; its remains are displayed in the Archaeological Park Cambodunum. Medieval Kempten developed as two rival towns: the abbey town ruled by the prince-abbots of Kempten Abbey and the free imperial city, which became Protestant. The two were united only in 1818 under Bavaria. This dual heritage is visible in the two town centres, with the baroque Residenz and St. Lorenz Basilica in the abbey town and the Gothic St. Mang church and town hall in the old imperial city.

Today

Kempten is the economic and educational centre of the Allgäu, known for dairy processing and machinery, and home to the Kempten University of Applied Sciences. The Allgäuer Festwoche, a major regional fair and festival, is held every August.
