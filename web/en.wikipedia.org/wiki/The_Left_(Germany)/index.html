The Left (Germany)

German name: Die Linke
Founded: 16 June 2007
Ideology: Democratic socialism, left-wing populism
Position: Left-wing to far-left
Colour: Red

The Left (German: Die Linke) is a democratic socialist political party in Germany. It was formed in 2007 through the merger of the Party of Democratic Socialism (PDS) — the successor party to East Germany's ruling SED — and the western-based Electoral Alternative for Labour and Social Justice (WASG), a breakaway from the Social Democrats.

History

The alliance of the two parties first contested the 2005 federal election as the Left Party, winning 8.7% of the vote. The formal merger in 2007 created a party with a strong base in the eastern states and a growing western presence, led initially by figures including Oskar Lafontaine and Gregor Gysi. The party advocates higher taxes on the wealthy, a stronger welfare state, withdrawal of German troops from foreign missions, and opposition to NATO in its current form.

Electoral performance

The Left has been represented in the Bundestag continuously since 2005 and has participated in several state governments, including leading the government of Thuringia under Bodo Ramelow. Its federal vote share declined in 2021, and in 2023 a prominent faction around Sahra Wagenknecht split away to form a new party, though The Left subsequently recovered in the 2025 federal election.
