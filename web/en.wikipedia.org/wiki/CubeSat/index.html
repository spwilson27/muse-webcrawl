A CubeSat is a class of small satellite built to a standard form factor based on cubes of 10 cm side. One CubeSat unit, "1U", has a volume of one litre and a mass of no more than about 2 kg; units are combined into larger spacecraft of 1.5U, 2U, 3U, 6U, 12U and beyond. The CubeSat standard was developed in 1999 by Jordi Puig-Suari of California Polytechnic State University (Cal Poly) and Bob Twiggs of Stanford University, with the aim of giving university students affordable access to space.

Design and deployment

The specification defines the satellite's dimensions, mass, structural rails and separation springs so that many CubeSats can be launched together as secondary payloads in a standard deployer — originally the Poly Picosatellite Orbital Deployer (P-POD). Commercial off-the-shelf components, standardised buses and "rideshare" launches have made CubeSats the most common type of satellite launched, used for education, technology demonstration, Earth observation, communications and science.

Notable missions

Thousands of CubeSats have been launched since the first in June 2003. Notable examples include Planet Labs' Dove satellites, which image the entire Earth daily from 3U CubeSats; the twin MarCO CubeSats, which flew to Mars with NASA's InSight lander in 2018 and relayed its landing telemetry — the first interplanetary CubeSats; and LightSail 2, a solar-sailing demonstration. CubeSat-based constellations have also raised concerns about orbital debris and light pollution.
