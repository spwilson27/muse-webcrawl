Klotz family

The Klotz family was a family of violin makers in Mittenwald, Germany, one of the principal centres of German violin making. Over several generations from the seventeenth century onward, members of the family produced instruments in the tradition of the Tyrolean and Cremonese schools.

History

The family's founding figure was Matthias Klotz (1653–1743), who is said to have studied in Italy, possibly with the Amati circle in Cremona, before returning to Mittenwald and establishing a workshop. His sons, including Georg, Sebastian and Aegidius Klotz, continued the trade; Sebastian and Aegidius are especially esteemed, with instruments modelled on Stainer and Amati patterns that are prized by players and collectors. The family tradition of lutherie in Mittenwald persisted into later centuries, and Klotz instruments remain in concert use.

Legacy

Mittenwald still hosts a violin-making school and a museum in which Klotz instruments feature prominently.
