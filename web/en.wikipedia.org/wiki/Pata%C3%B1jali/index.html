Patañjali

Patañjali
Era: c. 2nd century BCE – 4th century CE (debated)
Known for: Yoga Sūtras; Mahābhāṣya (attributed)

Patañjali was a sage of ancient India credited as the compiler of the Yoga Sūtras, the foundational text of the Yoga school of Hindu philosophy. He is also traditionally identified with the author of the Mahābhāṣya, a major commentary on Pāṇini's grammar, though modern scholars debate whether the grammarian and the yoga teacher were the same person, and proposed dates for him span from the 2nd century BCE to the 4th century CE.

The Yoga Sūtras

The Yoga Sūtras comprise 195 (or 196) aphorisms organized into four chapters (pāda): Samādhi (contemplation), Sādhana (practice), Vibhūti (powers), and Kaivalya (liberation). The text defines yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind and describes the eight-limbed (aṣṭāṅga) path: ethical restraints (yama), observances (niyama), posture (āsana), breath control (prāṇāyāma), withdrawal of the senses (pratyāhāra), concentration (dhāraṇā), meditation (dhyāna), and absorption (samādhi).

Legacy

After a period of relative obscurity, the Yoga Sūtras were revived in the 19th and 20th centuries and are now among the most translated and widely read of Indian texts, forming the philosophical reference point of much modern yoga practice. In Indian tradition Patañjali is sometimes depicted as half-serpent and regarded as an incarnation of the serpent Śeṣa.
