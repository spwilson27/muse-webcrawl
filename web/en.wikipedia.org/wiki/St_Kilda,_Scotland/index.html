St Kilda, Scotland

St Kilda Location: North Atlantic, Outer Hebrides, Scotland
Main island: Hirta
Highest point: Conachair (430 m)
Population: Evacuated 1930; no permanent residents
UNESCO: World Heritage Site (1986)

St Kilda (Scottish Gaelic: Hiort) is an isolated archipelago 64 kilometres west-northwest of North Uist in the North Atlantic, the remotest part of Scotland. The islands — Hirta, Soay, Boreray and Dun — are famed both for their dramatic sea cliffs and bird colonies and for the unique island community that lived there for centuries before its evacuation in 1930.

The restored cottages of Main Street, Village Bay, Hirta

The island community

St Kildans subsisted on seabirds (northern gannets, fulmars and puffins), soay sheep, and small-scale crofting, climbing barefoot on the cliffs to harvest birds and eggs. Life was communal, governed by a daily "parliament" of the island men. Disease, emigration and the hardships of the First World War shrank the population; in 1930 the remaining 36 islanders asked to be evacuated to the mainland.

Natural heritage

The archipelago hosts one of Europe's most important seabird colonies, including the world's largest colony of northern gannets (on Boreray and its sea stacks), and endemic subspecies including the St Kilda wren and St Kilda field mouse. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for both cultural and natural criteria — one of very few "mixed" sites.
