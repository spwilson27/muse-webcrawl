Cabinetry

FieldWoodworking / carpentry
MaterialsWood, plywood, MDF, laminates

Cabinetry is the craft and industry of building cabinets — storage furniture with doors and drawers, typically installed in kitchens, bathrooms, and offices. Cabinetry ranges from fully custom, furniture-grade work built in a workshop to mass-produced stock cabinets assembled from flat-packed components.

Cabinets are generally classified as stock, semi-custom, or custom, and by construction as framed (face-frame) or frameless (European-style).

Construction

Traditional cabinetry uses joinery such as dovetails, mortise and tenon, and dado joints. Modern production relies on panel materials — plywood, medium-density fibreboard, and particleboard — finished with veneer, laminate, paint, or thermofoil.

Frameless cabinets use thicker panels and concealed cup hinges, giving fuller access to the interior, while face-frame cabinets attach a hardwood frame to the front of the cabinet box.

History

Cabinet making emerged as a distinct trade from general carpentry in the 17th and 18th centuries, producing fine furniture for wealthy households. Industrialisation in the 20th century brought factory-made kitchen cabinets, and post-war housing booms standardised modular kitchen systems.

Installation

Installed cabinetry must be levelled and anchored to wall studs; kitchen work is planned around the 'work triangle' between sink, stove, and refrigerator, and countertops are fitted after the base cabinets are set.
