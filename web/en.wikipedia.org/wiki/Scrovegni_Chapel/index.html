The Scrovegni Chapel (Italian: Cappella degli Scrovegni), also known as the Arena Chapel, is a small church in Padua, Italy, adjacent to the Augustinian monastery of the Eremitani. It contains one of the most important fresco cycles in Western art, painted by Giotto and completed about 1305. The chapel was commissioned by the wealthy Paduan banker Enrico Scrovegni, traditionally said to have built it to atone for the sin of usury practised by his father, Reginaldo — whom Dante placed in the Seventh Circle of Hell in the Inferno.

The fresco cycle

Giotto painted the entire interior in fresco: some 37 scenes arranged in three tiers along the nave walls narrate the lives of the Virgin Mary and her parents Joachim and Anna, and the life, death and resurrection of Christ. Famous panels include The Kiss of Judas, The Lamentation, The Adoration of the Magi (whose comet-like Star of Bethlehem may reflect Halley's Comet, seen in 1301) and The Meeting at the Golden Gate. On the west wall is a monumental Last Judgment, in which Enrico Scrovegni himself appears presenting a model of the chapel. The ceiling is painted as a deep blue vault with stars and roundels of Christ, the Madonna and prophets.

Significance

The cycle is regarded as a turning point in European painting: Giotto's solid, emotionally expressive figures, modelled with light and shadow and set in coherent spaces, broke decisively with the flatter Byzantine manner. The chapel and frescoes were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021 as part of the serial site of Padua's 14th-century fresco cycles.

Conservation

Visitors enter through an air-locked, climate-controlled body ("equipped technological chamber") to protect the frescoes from humidity and pollution; visits are timed and limited.
