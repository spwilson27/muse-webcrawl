Metallocene

A metallocene is a compound typically consisting of two cyclopentadienyl anions (Cp, C5H5−) bound to a metal centre (M) in the oxidation state II, with the resulting general formula (C5H5)2M. The metal is "sandwiched" between the two parallel ring ligands. The first metallocene discovered was ferrocene in 1951, whose unexpected stability launched the modern era of organometallic chemistry.

Examples

Metallocenes are known across the transition metals: ferrocene (iron), cobaltocene, nickelocene, ruthenocene, and osmocene, among others. Bent metallocenes, in which additional ligands occupy the open wedge, include titanocene and zirconocene derivatives of great industrial importance.

Applications

Cationic group-4 metallocenes, activated by cocatalysts such as methylaluminoxane, are highly active single-site catalysts for olefin polymerisation. Metallocene-catalysed polyethylenes and polypropylenes offer controlled molecular weight distributions and comonomer incorporation, and are produced on a large commercial scale. Ferrocene derivatives are also used as fuel additives, redox standards in electrochemistry, and building blocks in medicinal chemistry.
