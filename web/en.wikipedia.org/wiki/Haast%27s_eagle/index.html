Haast's eagle

Haast's eagle (Hieraaetus moorei, formerly Harpagornis moorei) is an extinct species of eagle that lived in the South Island of New Zealand. It was the largest eagle known to have existed, with females weighing an estimated 10–15 kg (22–33 lb) and a wingspan of up to about 3 metres. A specialist predator of the flightless moa, it became extinct around 1400 AD, shortly after Māori settlers hunted its prey to extinction.

Description and ecology

First described by Julius von Haast in 1871 from bones found in a swamp at Glenmark, the eagle evolved from a much smaller ancestor closely related to the Australian little eagle, rapidly increasing in size under island conditions — an example of rapid evolution documented by DNA analysis. Its powerful legs and enormous talons, comparable to a tiger's claws, were adapted for striking and holding large prey; marks on moa pelvises show the eagle attacked from behind, crushing bones with a grip powerful enough to puncture internal organs.

Extinction and legend

Māori arrived in New Zealand around 1250–1300 and hunted moa intensively while burning the eagle's forest habitat. With its prey gone, Haast's eagle died out by about 1400. It may survive in Māori tradition as the pouākai, a giant bird said to be capable of carrying off people.
