Battle of Long Island

The Battle of Long Island, also known as the Battle of Brooklyn or Battle of Brooklyn Heights, was fought on 27 August 1776 in present-day Brooklyn, New York, during the American Revolutionary War. The first major battle of the war after the Declaration of Independence — and the largest battle fought in North America in the 18th century — it ended in a British victory, though General George Washington's skilful night-time evacuation across the East River saved the Continental Army.

Background

After evacuating Boston, Washington fortified New York, correctly expecting a British descent on the city. General Howe landed some 32,000 troops on Staten Island and, on 22 August 1776, ferried over 15,000 men to Gravesend Bay on Long Island. The Americans held the wooded ridge of the Heights of Guan across Brooklyn, guarding its passes — Gowanus, Flatbush, Bedford and the far eastern Jamaica Pass — with their main works behind them at Brooklyn Heights, facing the East River.

The battle

On the night of 26/27 August, Howe led 10,000 men on a flank march through the lightly guarded Jamaica Pass, guided by local Loyalists, and fell on the American rear at dawn while Hessian and British forces pinned the American line frontally at the other passes. The American forward line collapsed in confusion; the Maryland 400 under Lord Stirling made a celebrated stand at the Old Stone House on the Gowanus Road, repeatedly charging British troops to buy time for the retreat, at the cost of most of the detachment. Generals Sullivan and Stirling were captured. The survivors fell back into the Brooklyn Heights lines — and Howe, remembering Bunker Hill, chose to besiege rather than storm them.

The evacuation

On the night of 29/30 August, in rain and under cover of fog, Washington ferried his entire army — some 9,000 men with cannon, horses and stores — across the East River to Manhattan in small boats, many manned by John Glover's Marblehead mariners, without losing a man. It remains one of the most celebrated retreats in American military history and preserved the Continental Army to fight on; New York City, however, remained in British hands until 1783.
