Neurocognitive disorder

A neurocognitive disorder is a category of mental health disorders that primarily affect cognitive abilities including learning, memory, perception, and problem solving. The term was introduced in the DSM-5, which groups these conditions into delirium, major neurocognitive disorder (which largely replaced the term dementia), and mild neurocognitive disorder.

Classification

Neurocognitive disorders are classified by aetiology, including Alzheimer's disease, vascular disease, Lewy body disease, frontotemporal lobar degeneration, Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease, HIV infection, traumatic brain injury, prion disease and substance or medication use. Diagnosis requires evidence of significant cognitive decline from a previous level of performance in one or more cognitive domains, based on concern of the individual or an informant and documented impairment on testing.

Mild and major disorders

In mild neurocognitive disorder, cognitive deficits do not interfere with independence in everyday activities, though greater effort or compensatory strategies may be needed. In major neurocognitive disorder, the deficits are severe enough to interfere with independence, requiring assistance with complex instrumental activities of daily living.

Assessment and management

Assessment includes history taking, cognitive screening instruments such as the Mini–Mental State Examination and Montreal Cognitive Assessment, laboratory tests and neuroimaging to identify reversible causes. Management focuses on treating underlying causes where possible, managing symptoms, and supporting patients and carers.
