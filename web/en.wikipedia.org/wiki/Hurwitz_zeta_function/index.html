Hurwitz zeta function

In mathematics, the Hurwitz zeta function, named after Adolf Hurwitz, is a generalisation of the Riemann zeta function to a shifted argument. It is defined for complex s with Re(s) > 1 and real q with 0 < q ≤ 1 by the series ζ(s, q) = Σn=0∞ 1/(n + q)s. For q = 1 it reduces to the ordinary Riemann zeta function.

Analytic continuation

Like the Riemann zeta function, ζ(s, q) extends to a meromorphic function of s on the whole complex plane, with a single simple pole at s = 1 of residue 1. The values at negative integers are related to the Bernoulli polynomials: ζ(−n, q) = −Bn+1(q)/(n+1). A functional equation, the Hurwitz formula, relates ζ(1 − s, q) to values at s expressed through Fourier series in q.

Special values and uses

The derivative with respect to s at s = 0 is connected to the gamma function via Lerch's formula. Special cases include the polygamma functions, the Dirichlet L-functions (when q is rational, decomposing into characters), and Catalan's constant through ζ(2, 1/4). The Hurwitz zeta function appears in number theory (class number formulas), probability (limit laws for random variables), and mathematical physics.
