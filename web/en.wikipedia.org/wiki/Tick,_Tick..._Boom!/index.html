Tick, Tick... Boom!

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tick, Tick... Boom! (styled tick, tick... BOOM!) is a musical by Jonathan Larson, the composer of Rent. It tells the semi-autobiographical story of Jon, an aspiring composer in New York City who, on the eve of his 30th birthday in 1990, fears he has chosen the wrong career path while his friends move on to more settled lives. The work is noted for its rock-influenced score and its refrain-like ticking-clock motif, which gives the show its title.

History

Larson first performed the piece in the early 1990s as a solo "rock monologue" under titles including Boho Days and 30/90. After Larson's death in 1996, playwright David Auburn restructured the material into a three-actor musical, which premiered Off-Broadway in 2001 at the Jane Street Theatre, in a production directed by Scott Schwartz and starring Raúl Esparza as Jon. The show later toured and received numerous regional and international productions.

Plot and songs

Jon is torn between his girlfriend Susan, who wants to leave New York and start a family, and his best friend Michael, who has traded acting for a lucrative marketing career. As Jon waits to hear whether his musical workshop, Superbia, will be produced, he questions whether to keep pursuing his dream. Well-known songs from the score include "30/90", "Johnny Can't Decide", "Sunday" (a homage to Stephen Sondheim's Sunday in the Park with George), "Why", "Louder Than Words" and "Come to Your Senses", the latter later incorporated into the show from Larson's Superbia material.

Film adaptation

A film adaptation directed by Lin-Manuel Miranda in his directorial debut was released by Netflix in 2021, starring Andrew Garfield as Jonathan Larson. Garfield received widespread acclaim and an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor for his performance.
