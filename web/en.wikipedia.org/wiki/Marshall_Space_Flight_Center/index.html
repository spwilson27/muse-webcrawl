Marshall Space Flight Center

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marshall Space Flight Center
LocationHuntsville, Alabama (Redstone Arsenal)
AgencyNASA
Established1 July 1960
First directorWernher von Braun
SpecialityPropulsion and launch vehicles
Static test firing heritage at Marshall.

The George C. Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) is NASA's lead centre for rocket propulsion and launch vehicle development, located on the Redstone Arsenal at Huntsville, Alabama — "Rocket City". It was built around Wernher von Braun's Army rocket team, transferred to the new civilian agency in 1960, and under his directorship developed the Saturn rockets that carried Apollo to the Moon.

Marshall went on to manage the Space Shuttle's propulsion elements (main engines, external tank, solid rocket boosters), payload operations for the International Space Station, and the Space Launch System programme, whose core stages are assembled at the Michoud facility it manages in Louisiana and tested on Marshall's historic stands.

Heritage

The centre's test stands — including the towering Dynamic Structural Test Facility and the S-IC static test stand that shook Huntsville with full-duration F-1 firings — are national historic landmarks. The von Braun team's move from the Army's missile work (Redstone, Jupiter) to Saturn made Huntsville synonymous with American rocketry, a legacy the city still trades on.
