The Petersberg tasks are a set of military and security priorities defined by the Western European Union (WEU) in June 1992 at the Petersberg hotel near Bonn, Germany. They established the types of missions for which WEU member states' armed forces could be deployed, beyond the traditional collective defence of territory.

Content

According to the Petersberg Declaration, member states declared themselves ready to make available military units for:

  • humanitarian and rescue tasks;
  • peacekeeping tasks;
  • tasks of combat forces in crisis management, including peacemaking (peace enforcement).

Integration into EU law

The tasks were incorporated into the Treaty of Amsterdam (1997) as part of the European Union's Common Foreign and Security Policy, laying the foundation for the EU's later Common Security and Defence Policy. The Treaty of Lisbon (2009) expanded them to include joint disarmament operations, humanitarian and rescue tasks, military advice and assistance tasks, conflict prevention and peace-keeping tasks, tasks of combat forces in crisis management (including peace-making and post-conflict stabilisation), and support for third countries in combating terrorism. Petersberg-type missions underlie subsequent EU operations such as EUFOR Althea in Bosnia and the EU's training missions in Africa.
