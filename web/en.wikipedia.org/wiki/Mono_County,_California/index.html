Mono County, California

Mono County is a county located in the east central portion of the U.S. state of California, east of the Sierra Nevada between Yosemite National Park and the Nevada state line. As of the 2020 census, the population was 13,195, making it the fourth-least populous county in California. The county seat is Bridgeport. The county's only incorporated town is Mammoth Lakes, home to the Mammoth Mountain ski area.

History

Mono County was formed in 1861 from parts of Calaveras, Fresno, and Mariposa counties. Parts of the county were later given to Inyo County in 1866. The county takes its name from Mono Lake, which in turn derives from Monachi, a Yokuts term for the bands of Paiute (Numa) people who lived on both sides of the Sierra in the Mono Basin region. The county experienced a mining boom in the late 19th century, most famously at Bodie, now preserved as a ghost town in a state of "arrested decay" at Bodie State Historic Park.

Geography

The county spans the eastern escarpment of the Sierra Nevada and a portion of the western Great Basin. Notable features include Mono Lake, a saline soda lake known for its tufa towers; the Long Valley Caldera; Crowley Lake; Convict Lake; and June Lake. U.S. Route 395 is the county's principal highway. Portions of Yosemite National Park, Inyo National Forest, and the Ansel Adams and John Muir wildernesses lie within the county.

Communities

Communities include Mammoth Lakes, Bridgeport, Lee Vining, June Lake, Benton, Chalfant, Coleville, Walker, Crowley Lake, and the ghost town of Bodie.
