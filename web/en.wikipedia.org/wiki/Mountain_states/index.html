Mountain states

The Mountain states (also known as the Mountain West or the Interior West) form one of the nine geographic divisions of the United States recognised by the U.S. Census Bureau. The division comprises the states through which the Rocky Mountains run: Arizona, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, New Mexico, Utah and Wyoming.

Geography

The Mountain states are the most sparsely populated division in the United States, characterised by high elevations, arid and semi-arid climates, and landscapes that include the Rocky Mountains, the Great Basin, the Colorado Plateau and portions of the Great Plains. The region contains the highest state capitals in the country and several of the largest metropolitan areas of the interior West, including Phoenix, Denver, Las Vegas, Salt Lake City, Tucson, Albuquerque and Boise.

Terminology

The Census Bureau's Mountain division is a subdivision of the Western United States, alongside the Pacific division. The terms "Mountain West" and "Interior West" are also used more loosely in regional studies and politics; some definitions extend the region to include parts of neighbouring states. The Mountain Time Zone roughly corresponds to the division, although Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) does not observe daylight saving time.

Demographics and economy

Since the late 20th century the Mountain states have been among the fastest-growing parts of the United States, with growth concentrated in metropolitan areas. Historically dependent on mining, ranching, agriculture and federal land management — the federal government owns a large share of the land in most of these states — the regional economy has diversified into tourism, outdoor recreation, technology and services.
