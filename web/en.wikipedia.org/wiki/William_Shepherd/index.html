William Shepherd

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

William Shepherd
Born26 July 1949, Oak Ridge, Tennessee
OccupationUS Navy SEAL, astronaut
MissionsSTS-27, STS-41, STS-52, Expedition 1
Shepherd aboard Station.

William McMichael Shepherd (born 1949) is an American former Navy SEAL and NASA astronaut who commanded Expedition 1, the first crew of the International Space Station, in 2000–01 — the man who opened humanity's permanent presence in orbit. A non-pilot in a pilot-dominated corps, Shepherd was selected in 1984 in NASA's first group chosen partly for non-flying expertise, and flew three Shuttle missions before the station command.

Shepherd negotiated the young station's culture from orbit: insisting the crew be one crew (and taking the radio call sign 'Station'), running a famously disciplined ship with his Russian crewmates Gidzenko and Krikalev, and defusing early US–Russian management friction with SEAL-team pragmatism. His log entries — blunt, funny, operational — are a primary source for the station's founding months.

Recognition

Shepherd, Gidzenko and Krikalev received the Congressional Space Medal of Honor jointly (2003, in the aftermath of Columbia) — the Expedition 1 crew honoured as founders. Shepherd later managed the station programme's technical integration before retiring from NASA in 2002.
