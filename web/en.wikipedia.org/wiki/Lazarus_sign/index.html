Lazarus sign

The Lazarus sign is a reflex movement in brain-dead and brainstem-failure patients, where the arms flex and rise and may cross over the chest, resembling depictions of the biblical figure Lazarus being raised from the dead. It is a spinal reflex and does not indicate any recovery of brain function.

Mechanism

The movement is thought to arise from complex spinal cord reflex arcs that remain intact after brain death, often triggered by stimulation such as hypoxia, hypotension, or touching the patient's neck or chest during apnea testing or organ procurement. The reflex typically involves flexion of the arms at the elbows, adduction of the shoulders, and sometimes raising of the hands toward the neck or chin.

Significance

Although alarming to witnesses, the Lazarus sign is a recognized phenomenon compatible with the diagnosis of brain death, since it is generated below the level of the brain. Clinicians may warn families and staff in advance so the movement is not mistaken for purposeful activity or recovery. It was first described in the medical literature in the 1980s.
