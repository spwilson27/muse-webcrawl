Antonio Amador José de Nariño y Álvarez del Casal (9 April 1765 – 13 December 1823) was a Colombian political and military leader, journalist and ideologue of independence from Spain. He is honoured in Colombia's national anthem as the "forerunner" (el Precursor) of independence.

The Rights of Man

In 1793–94 Nariño secretly translated and printed the French Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen on his own press in Bogotá. Denounced to the colonial authorities, he was tried for sedition, his property confiscated, and sentenced to ten years' imprisonment in Africa; escaping in Cádiz, he sought British and French support for Spanish American independence before surrendering in 1797 and spending years in prison.

Independence wars

Freed amid the upheavals of 1810, Nariño became president of the free state of Cundinamarca and led its centralist cause in Colombia's first civil war against federalists. In 1813–14 he commanded a campaign to liberate the southern royalist stronghold of Pasto but was captured and spent four more years imprisoned in Cádiz. Released in 1820, he returned to serve as vice president of Gran Colombia under Simón Bolívar and helped draft its constitution, before retiring in declining health.
