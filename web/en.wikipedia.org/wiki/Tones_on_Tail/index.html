Tones on Tail were a British post-punk band formed in Northampton in 1981 by guitarist Daniel Ash and drummer Kevin Haskins, both formerly of the gothic rock band Bauhaus, together with bassist Glenn Campling, who had been Bauhaus's roadie and a graphic artist involved with the band's artwork. The group began as a side project during a Bauhaus hiatus and continued after Bauhaus split in 1983.

History

Taking their name from a phrase referring to the tails of musical notes, Tones on Tail released their first material in 1982: an eponymous EP on the 4AD label, followed by the single "There's Only One" on Situation Two. Their sound mixed the dark atmospheres of gothic rock with funk, dub, psychedelia and electronic experimentation, reflecting Ash's wide-ranging tastes.

In 1984 the band released their only studio album, Pop, on Beggars Banquet. The album included the track "Go!", which later became a dance-club and alternative-radio staple, especially after it was widely sampled and revived in the 1990s; the song reached the charts in the United States through its use in films, advertisements and reissues. Other notable tracks included "Lions", "War", "Performance" and "Happiness".

Tones on Tail disbanded in 1984. Ash and Haskins went on to form Love and Rockets with Bauhaus bassist David J, achieving commercial success, while Campling pursued design and other musical projects. In 1998, Ash and Haskins briefly revived Tones on Tail as the support act on a Bauhaus reunion tour (the "Resurrection Tour"), performing a short set of the band's songs.

Musical style

The band's music is characterised by Ash's processed guitar textures, dub-influenced bass lines, and electronic percussion. Songs range from abrasive post-punk to melodic, almost pop material, a restlessness often cited as a bridge between Bauhaus's gloom and the more eclectic rock of Love and Rockets.
