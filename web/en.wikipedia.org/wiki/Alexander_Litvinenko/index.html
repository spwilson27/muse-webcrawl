Alexander Valterovich Litvinenko (30 August 1962 – 23 November 2006) was a Russian officer of the KGB and its successor, the FSB, who became a prominent critic of the Russian government. After publicly accusing his superiors of ordering the assassination of oligarch Boris Berezovsky, he fled Russia and received asylum in the United Kingdom, where he wrote books alleging state involvement in terrorism, including claims about the 1999 Russian apartment bombings.

Poisoning and death

On 1 November 2006 Litvinenko fell ill after meeting two former Russian agents in London. He died three weeks later; tests identified the radioactive isotope polonium-210 in his body, making him the first known victim of lethal polonium poisoning. On his deathbed he accused Russian president Vladimir Putin of responsibility.

Inquiry

A British public inquiry chaired by Sir Robert Owen concluded in 2016 that Litvinenko was killed by Andrey Lugovoy and Dmitry Kovtun, that the operation was probably approved by the FSB leadership, and that President Putin "probably approved" it. The European Court of Human Rights likewise held Russia responsible in 2021. Russia has denied involvement.
