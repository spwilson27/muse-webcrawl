Paul Edward Ginsparg (born January 1, 1955) is an American physicist and information scientist. He is best known as the founder of arXiv, the open-access repository of electronic preprints that transformed scholarly communication in physics, mathematics, computer science, and related fields.

Early life and education

Ginsparg was born in Chicago, Illinois, and grew up in Syosset, New York, on Long Island. He studied physics at Harvard University, earning his bachelor's degree in 1977, and completed his doctorate at Cornell University in 1981, working on theoretical particle physics and lattice gauge theory.

Career

After his PhD, Ginsparg held positions at Harvard and then joined the theoretical division of Los Alamos National Laboratory in New Mexico. It was at Los Alamos, in August 1991, that he launched an automated e-mail server for sharing preprints in theoretical high-energy physics. The system, initially known as xxx.lanl.gov and later renamed arXiv.org, allowed researchers to submit and retrieve papers electronically, bypassing the slow postal distribution of printed preprints.

The archive grew rapidly beyond its original subfield, absorbing preprint collections from across physics and later mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, statistics, and economics. In 2001 Ginsparg moved to Cornell University, taking arXiv with him; it has since been operated by Cornell Tech and the Cornell University Library. Ginsparg holds appointments in physics and in information science at Cornell.

Recognition

Ginsparg received a MacArthur Fellowship in 2002, was named one of Time magazine's "Time 100" figures, and has received numerous awards recognising arXiv's impact on open scholarly communication, including the Paul Evan Peters Award. He has written extensively on scholarly communication, open access, and the future of scientific publishing.
