Film series

A film series is a collection of related films in succession that share the same fictional universe, or are marketed as a series. A series typically consists of a first film followed by sequels, though it may also include prequels, spin-offs, and remakes. The films in a series may be connected by characters, settings, or continuing plotlines.

History

Film series date to the earliest days of cinema, with serial films and continuing characters such as Fantômas in the 1910s. The James Bond series, begun with Dr. No in 1962, became the model of the long-running franchise series. In the modern era, series have become the dominant commercial model of the film industry, with the highest-grossing franchises — the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Star Wars, Harry Potter, and James Bond — earning tens of billions of dollars at the box office.

Terminology

Series of two films are sometimes called dilogies; three, trilogies (such as The Godfather and The Lord of the Rings); four, tetralogies. A series sharing a universe but made of overlapping sub-series is often termed a media franchise or cinematic universe.
