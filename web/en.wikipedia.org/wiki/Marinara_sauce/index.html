Marinara sauce is a tomato sauce usually made with tomatoes, garlic, herbs, onions and olive oil. It is used in Italian and Italian-American cuisine as a sauce for pasta and pizza, and as a base for many other dishes. In Italy, a salsa alla marinara is a tomato sauce with garlic and herbs but no onions, and it does not include meat.

Etymology and origin

Marinara means "sailor-style" or "seafaring" in Italian (alla marinara). Several folk explanations connect the name with the wives of Neapolitan sailors, who supposedly prepared the sauce for their husbands returning from sea, or with sailors themselves, who could make the sauce aboard ship because its ingredients kept well and it contained no perishable meat. Tomato sauces of this kind emerged in Naples after tomatoes arrived from the Americas in the 16th century; an early printed reference to a tomato sauce "in the Spanish style" appears in Antonio Latini's cookbook Lo scalco alla moderna (1692–1694).

Preparation

A basic marinara is prepared by gently cooking garlic (and, in Italian-American versions, chopped onion) in olive oil, adding crushed or puréed tomatoes, and simmering briefly — often only 20–30 minutes — with salt, basil and sometimes oregano, so that the sauce retains a fresh tomato flavour. The short cooking distinguishes marinara from long-simmered meat ragùs. It is served over pasta such as spaghetti, used on pizza, and as a dipping sauce for dishes like mozzarella sticks and calamari in Italian-American cooking.

Variations

Capers, olives and anchovies may be added (as in the related puttanesca), wine is sometimes included, and in the United States "marinara" is frequently used as a generic term for any smooth, meatless tomato sauce. Marinara forms the basis of dishes such as chicken or eggplant parmigiana and shrimp fra diavolo (with chilli).
