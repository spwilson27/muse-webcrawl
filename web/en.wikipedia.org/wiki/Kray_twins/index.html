Kray twins

Ronnie (left) and Reggie Kray

Ronald "Ronnie" Kray (24 October 1933 – 17 March 1995) and Reginald "Reggie" Kray (24 October 1933 – 1 October 2000) were identical twin brothers and English organised crime figures who led "the Firm," a gang at the centre of London's criminal underworld in the 1950s and 1960s. Mixing nightclub ownership and celebrity socialising with armed robbery, arson, protection rackets and murder, they became notorious public figures before their arrest in 1968 and conviction in 1969.

Early life

The twins were born in Haggerston, East London, and raised in Bethnal Green. Both became skilled amateur boxers in their teens before being called up for national service, which was marred by desertion and imprisonment. Dismissed from the army, they moved into crime, taking over a billiard hall and building a network of clubs in the East End and West End.

Criminal career

As club owners the Krays socialised with politicians and entertainers, cultivating an image of glamorous respectability. Behind it, the Firm ran protection rackets and committed murders, including those of George Cornell (shot by Ronnie in the Blind Beggar pub, Whitechapel, in 1966) and Jack "the Hat" McVitie (killed by Reggie in 1967). Ronnie was later certified insane and spent much of his sentence in Broadmoor Hospital.

Trial and imprisonment

Arrested in May 1968 by a team led by Detective Superintendent Leonard "Nipper" Read, the twins were tried at the Old Bailey in 1969 and sentenced to life imprisonment with a recommended minimum of 30 years. Ronnie died of a heart attack in Broadmoor in 1995; Reggie, released on compassionate grounds in August 2000, died of cancer weeks later.

In popular culture

The twins have been portrayed in films including The Krays (1990) and Legend (2015), and their story is a staple of London crime literature and tourism.
