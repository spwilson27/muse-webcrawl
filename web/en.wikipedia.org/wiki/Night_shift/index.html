Night shift

The night shift, also known as the graveyard shift, is a work shift that takes place during the night, typically between about 11 p.m. and 7 a.m., though hours vary by employer, industry and country. It is the third of the three shifts used in round-the-clock operations, after the day (first) and evening or swing (second) shifts.

Practices

Continuous operations such as hospitals, power plants, factories, emergency services, logistics and customer support rely on night shifts. Employees working nights often receive a shift differential — additional pay to compensate for the inconvenience and health burden. Rotating schedules may move workers between day and night duties, though permanent night work is common in some sectors.

Health effects

Working against the body's circadian rhythm disrupts sleep and hormonal cycles. Shift work sleep disorder is characterised by insomnia and excessive sleepiness in people who work non-traditional hours. Long-term night work has been associated with increased risks of cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, gastrointestinal disorders and some cancers; in 2007 the International Agency for Research on Cancer classified shift work involving circadian disruption as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A).

Alertness and performance reach their lowest point in the early morning hours, and accident risk is higher on night shifts, particularly towards the end of a run of consecutive nights. Several major industrial disasters, including the Exxon Valdez oil spill and the Three Mile Island accident, occurred during night hours and have been partly attributed to fatigue.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions regulate night work. The European Union's Working Time Directive defines night time and limits the average working hours of night workers, with special protections for health assessments. Labour law in various countries restricts night work by minors and, historically, by women in certain industries.
