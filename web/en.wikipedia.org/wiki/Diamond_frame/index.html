Diamond frame

A diamond frame is the most common type of bicycle frame, consisting of a main triangle formed by the top tube, down tube and seat tube, and a rear triangle formed by the seat stays and chain stays. The design derives from the safety bicycle of the 1880s, notably the Rover safety bicycle, and remains the dominant frame geometry.

Geometry

In its classic form the top tube runs roughly horizontally (or with a slight slope in "compact" geometry) from the head tube to the seat tube. The arrangement of thin-walled tubes in triangles gives an excellent strength-to-weight ratio. Traditional diamond frames require the rider to swing a leg over the top tube, which led to the development of step-through frames as an alternative.

Materials

Diamond frames have been built from steel, aluminium alloys, titanium and carbon fibre composites, each material affecting ride quality, weight and cost.
