Catriona (novel)

AuthorRobert Louis Stevenson
Published1893
GenreHistorical novel, adventure
Preceded byKidnapped (1886)

Catriona (1893) is a historical adventure novel by the Scottish author Robert Louis Stevenson. It is the sequel to Kidnapped (1886) and continues the story of David Balfour, picking up almost exactly where the earlier novel ends, in the aftermath of the Jacobite rising of 1745. In the United States it was published under the title David Balfour.

Plot

David Balfour, now come into his inheritance, seeks justice for James Stewart of the Glen, who is to be tried for the Appin Murder — the killing of the government agent Colin Campbell of Glenure. David's efforts to testify bring him into conflict with powerful interests, including Simon Fraser, Lord Lovat, and the Lord Advocate. He is kidnapped and held on the Bass Rock to keep him from the trial, which condemns James despite David's eventual testimony. Meanwhile David falls in love with Catriona Drummond, daughter of the disreputable James More Drummond (a son of Rob Roy), whose schemes repeatedly endanger them both. After adventures in the Netherlands, where David studies law at Leiden and shelters Catriona's reputation at great personal cost, the misunderstandings between the lovers are resolved and they marry.

Themes and reception

The novel interweaves David's coming of age and courtship with a sombre account of political justice in post-'45 Scotland. Though long overshadowed by Kidnapped, Catriona has been praised for its portrait of Edinburgh legal society, the pathos of the Appin trial, and the character of Catriona herself, one of Stevenson's most fully realised heroines.
