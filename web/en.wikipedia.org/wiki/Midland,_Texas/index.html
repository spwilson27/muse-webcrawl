Midland, Texas

Midland is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the county seat of Midland County, with part of the city extending into Martin County. Located on the southern edge of the Llano Estacado in West Texas, it had a population of about 132,500 at the 2020 census. Midland is the principal city of the Midland metropolitan area and, together with neighbouring Odessa, forms the Midland–Odessa combined statistical area.

History

Midland was founded in 1881 as Midway Station on the Texas and Pacific Railway, at the halfway point between Fort Worth and El Paso. It took its present name because it was the midway point and to avoid confusion with other "Midway" post offices. Ranching dominated the early economy.

The discovery of oil in the Permian Basin in the 1920s transformed the city; Midland became the administrative capital of the West Texas oil industry, hosting the offices of major oil companies even though much production centred on Odessa and the surrounding fields. Its skyline of downtown office towers earned it the nickname "The Tall City."

Economy

The economy remains centred on oil and natural gas, and Midland's fortunes rise and fall with energy prices. The Permian Basin's shale boom of the 2010s made the Midland area one of the fastest-growing metropolitan economies in the United States and drove low unemployment and rapid population growth.

Notable connections

Midland was the childhood home of President George W. Bush and the hometown of First Ladies Barbara and Laura Bush; George H. W. Bush also lived there early in his oil career. Midland International Air and Space Port serves the region, and the city is home to the Midland RockHounds minor-league baseball team.
