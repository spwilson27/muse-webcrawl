Dassault Falcon 8X

The Dassault Falcon 8X is an ultra-long-range business jet developed by Dassault Aviation as a derivative of the Falcon 7X. Announced at the European Business Aviation Convention & Exhibition (EBACE) in Geneva in May 2014, it made its first flight on 6 February 2015 and entered service in October 2016.

Design

The 8X features a fuselage stretched by about 1.1 metres compared with the 7X, providing the longest passenger cabin in the Falcon family and greater flexibility in cabin layouts. It is powered by three Pratt & Whitney Canada PW307D engines and offers a range of 6,450 nautical miles (11,945 km), linking city pairs such as Hong Kong–Paris and Los Angeles–Beijing nonstop. Fuel efficiency was improved through aerodynamic refinements and weight savings, and the wing was redesigned with additional fuel capacity.

Cockpit

The flight deck uses the EASy III avionics suite based on Honeywell Primus Epic, with optional dual head-up displays and the FalconEye combined vision system, the first in business aviation to blend synthetic and enhanced vision imagery.

Specifications

Typical accommodation is 12–16 passengers; maximum speed is Mach 0.90, service ceiling 51,000 ft, and maximum takeoff weight about 33,113 kg.
