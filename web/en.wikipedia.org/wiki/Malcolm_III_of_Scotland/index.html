Malcolm III, King of Scots.

Malcolm III (Middle Gaelic: Máel Coluim mac Donnchada; c. 1031 – 13 November 1093), nicknamed Canmore ("big head", or "great chief"), was King of Scotland from 1058 to 1093. He was the eldest son of Duncan I, whose death at the hands of Macbeth in 1040 sent the young Malcolm into exile.

Reign

With the support of Siward, Earl of Northumbria, Malcolm defeated Macbeth at the Battle of Lumphanan in 1057; Macbeth's stepson Lulach succeeded briefly before Malcolm killed him in 1058 and took the throne. His long reign saw repeated invasions of northern England — exploiting the Norman Conquest and the Harrying of the North — and the submission of Malcolm to William the Conqueror under the Treaty of Abernethy (1072).

Marriage and family

Malcolm first married Ingibiorg Finnsdottir, widow of Thorfinn Sigurdsson, Earl of Orkney. His second marriage, to Margaret of Wessex — a refugee from the Norman Conquest and later Saint Margaret of Scotland — brought Anglo-Saxon and continental influences to the Scottish court. Their sons Edward, Edmund, Ethelred, Edgar, Alexander and David all became kings or prominent figures; David I completed many of the reforms his parents began.

Death

Malcolm was killed at the Battle of Alnwick in 1093 during another invasion of Northumberland, along with his eldest son Edward; Margaret died days later at Edinburgh Castle. In Shakespeare's Macbeth, Malcolm appears as the rightful heir who defeats the tyrant with English support.
