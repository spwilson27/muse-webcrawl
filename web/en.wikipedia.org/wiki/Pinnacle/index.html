Pinnacle

Pinnacles crowning the buttresses of a Gothic cathedral.

A pinnacle is an upright ornamental element, typically a small turret or spire, placed on top of a buttress, parapet, tower or other part of a building. The word derives from the Latin pinnaculum, a little feather or wing. In common usage "pinnacle" also denotes the highest point of anything — the peak of a mountain or the culminating point of a career.

Architectural function

Pinnacles are characteristic of Gothic architecture, especially the Rayonnant and Flamboyant styles of France and the Decorated and Perpendicular styles of England. Beyond decoration they serve a structural purpose: their weight presses straight down on a buttress, counteracting the outward thrust of the vaults and flying buttresses behind it, and helping to hold the buttress masonry in compression. They are commonly crowned with a small spire (a spirelet), enriched with crockets — carved leaf-like projections — and finished with a finial.

Other uses of the term

Geologists use "pinnacle" for tall, thin spires of rock, such as limestone karst pinnacles and the erosional pinnacles of Bryce Canyon. In geology of coral reefs a pinnacle is a column-like coral growth rising from the lagoon floor. Figuratively, the word is a common metaphor for a summit or climax.
