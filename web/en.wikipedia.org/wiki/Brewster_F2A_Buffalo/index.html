The Brewster F2A Buffalo is an American fighter aircraft which saw limited service early in the Second World War. Designed and built by the Brewster Aeronautical Corporation, it was one of the first U.S. Navy monoplane fighters, serving aboard carriers and from land bases.

Design and development

The F2A was a mid-wing monoplane with retractable landing gear and an enclosed cockpit, powered by a Wright R-1820 Cyclone radial engine. It won a 1936 competition against the Grumman XF4F-1 and entered service in 1939. Successive variants added armour, self-sealing fuel tanks and more powerful engines, but the added weight progressively degraded performance.

Operational history

In U.S. service the Buffalo was soon relegated to training, although Marine Corps F2As fought at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, suffering heavy losses. British and Commonwealth squadrons used export Buffaloes in Malaya and Burma in 1941–42, where they were outclassed by Japanese fighters. By contrast, the Finnish Air Force achieved outstanding success with its Buffaloes against Soviet aircraft during the Continuation War, with several pilots becoming aces; Finnish pilots nicknamed the aircraft the "Taivaan helmi" ("Sky Pearl"). Finland's top Buffalo ace, Hans Wind, claimed 39 victories in the type.
