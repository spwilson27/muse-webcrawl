Scots Wha Hae

"Scots Wha Hae" (English: Scots, Who Have) is a patriotic song of Scotland written by Robert Burns in 1793. It is presented as the address of Robert the Bruce to his troops before the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314, and for centuries it has served as an unofficial national anthem of Scotland, as well as the party song of the Scottish National Party.

Background

Burns wrote the lyrics in a letter of 1793 to his publisher George Thomson, presenting them (with a wink) as an ancient composition he had collected. The tune, "Hey Tuttie Tatie", was traditionally said to have been played at Bannockburn. The song appeared in the politically charged atmosphere after the French Revolution: its call to resist tyranny and "Edward's chains" resonated both as history and as contemporary radicalism. Thomson was initially cautious about publishing it because of its Jacobin undertones.

Lyrics

Scots, wha hae wi' Wallace bled,
Scots, wham Bruce has aften led;
Welcome to your gory bed,
Or to victory!
Now's the day, and now's the hour;
See the front o' battle lour;
See approach proud Edward's power —
Chains and slavery!

The later verses swear that whoever would be a traitor knave, fill a coward's grave, or submit to be a slave shall flee, while those who for Scotland's king and law "freedom's sword will strongly draw, freeman stand or freeman fa', let him follow me!"

Usage

"Scots Wha Hae" has long been sung at Burns suppers, at gatherings of Scots abroad, and to close the annual conference of the Scottish National Party. In polls of Scotland's preferred national anthem it is frequently named alongside "Flower of Scotland" and "Scotland the Brave", though Scotland has no officially adopted anthem.
