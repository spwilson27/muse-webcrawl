Robert Sanderson Mulliken (June 7, 1896 – October 31, 1986) was an American physicist and chemist chiefly responsible for the early development of molecular orbital theory, for which he received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1966.

Work

From the 1920s Mulliken built the molecular-orbital (Hund–Mulliken) description of chemical bonding, assigning electrons to orbitals spread over whole molecules rather than localised between pairs of atoms, in competition with Linus Pauling's valence-bond approach. He introduced the orbital nomenclature (σ, π) still in use, the concepts of orbital electronegativity and charge-transfer complexes, and Mulliken population analysis for interpreting computed wavefunctions. During the Second World War he worked on the Chicago branch of the Manhattan Project's plutonium programme.

Legacy

Mulliken spent most of his career at the University of Chicago. His methods underpin modern computational chemistry, and the Nobel citation honoured his "fundamental work concerning chemical bonds and the electronic structure of molecules by the molecular orbital method".
