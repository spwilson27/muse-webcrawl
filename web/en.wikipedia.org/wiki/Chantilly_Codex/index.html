Chantilly Codex

The Chantilly Codex (Chantilly, Musée Condé MS 564) is one of the most important manuscripts of medieval music, containing examples of the late-fourteenth-century style known as ars subtilior. It preserves 112 pieces by 33 named and anonymous composers, dating roughly from 1350 to 1400, in the forms of the ballade, rondeau, virelai and isorhythmic motet.

Contents

The repertoire is principally French, with a small group of Italian pieces. Composers represented include Baude Cordier, Solage, Trebor (possibly Johan Robert), Guido, Grimace, Pierre des Molins and others associated with the courts of Avignon, Foix and Aragon. The music is rhythmically and notationally intricate — a hallmark of the ars subtilior — employing red notation, unusual mensuration signs and complex syncopation.

Notable folios

Two pieces by Baude Cordier were added at the front of the manuscript in decorative shapes: the rondeau Belle, bonne, sage is written in the shape of a heart, and Tout par compas suy composée in the shape of a circle. These are celebrated examples of "eye music", in which the visual form of the notation mirrors the subject of the song.

Provenance

The manuscript was probably copied in the early fifteenth century and was later in the library of the Bibliothèque du roi in Paris and in Florence, where it appears in a 1482 inventory of the Medici library, before coming to the Château de Chantilly as part of the collection of the duc d'Aumale.
