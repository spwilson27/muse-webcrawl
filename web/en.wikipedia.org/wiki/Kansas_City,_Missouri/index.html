Kansas City (often abbreviated KCMO) is the largest city in the U.S. state of Missouri by population and area. It lies at the confluence of the Kansas and Missouri rivers, directly across the state line from Kansas City, Kansas, with which it anchors the bi-state Kansas City metropolitan area. The city is famous for its contributions to jazz and barbecue, its stockyards heritage, and its many boulevards and fountains.

History

The city began as a river landing where fur trader François Chouteau set up a post in the 1820s. The Town of Kansas was incorporated in 1850 and renamed the City of Kansas in 1853. Its position as the eastern jumping-off point for the Santa Fe, California and Oregon trails, and later as a rail hub second only to Chicago, made it a booming cattle and distribution centre. Under political boss Tom Pendergast in the 1920s–30s, the wide-open city became a cradle of Kansas City jazz, home to Count Basie and Charlie Parker.

Culture and landmarks

Major institutions include the Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art, the National World War I Museum and Liberty Memorial, the American Jazz Museum in the historic 18th & Vine district, and the Kauffman Center for the Performing Arts. Kansas City's sports teams include the NFL's Chiefs and Major League Baseball's Royals, whose stadiums stand side by side at the Truman Sports Complex. The city is also a major logistics and rail centre, home to Hallmark Cards and the animal-health corridor.

Geography

The city spans bluffs and floodplains at the junction of the two rivers; its street grid gives way to a famed system of parks and boulevards designed under the City Beautiful movement by George Kessler.
