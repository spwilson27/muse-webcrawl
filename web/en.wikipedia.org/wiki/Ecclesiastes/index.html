Ecclesiastes (Greek: Ἐκκλησιαστής, Ekklēsiastēs; Hebrew: קֹהֶלֶת, Qōheleṯ, "Preacher" or "Teacher") is one of the Ketuvim ("Writings") of the Hebrew Bible and one of the "Wisdom" books of the Christian Old Testament. The title is a Latin transliteration of the Greek translation of the Hebrew word Qoheleth, the narrator's self-designation throughout the book.

Summary

The book is narrated largely in the voice of Qoheleth, presented as a son of David and king in Jerusalem, who recounts his search for meaning. Having pursued wisdom, pleasure, wealth, building projects and labour, he judges all of them hevel — traditionally translated "vanity", but more literally "breath" or "vapour": transient, elusive and beyond human control. Its famous refrain opens the book: "Vanity of vanities, says the Preacher, vanity of vanities! All is vanity."

Alongside its scepticism, the book repeatedly commends simple enjoyment — eating, drinking and finding satisfaction in one's work — as gifts of God. Chapter 3 contains the celebrated poem beginning "For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven", a passage adapted in the Pete Seeger song "Turn! Turn! Turn!". An epilogue, apparently in a second voice, concludes: "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man."

Authorship and date

Tradition attributes the book to Solomon, but linguistic evidence — Persian loanwords and Aramaic influences, and the absence of the text from earlier Hebrew — leads most modern scholars to date it to the Persian or early Hellenistic period, roughly between 450 and 200 BCE.

Themes and interpretation

Ecclesiastes is unusually sceptical for a biblical book: it questions whether wisdom is better than folly when death awaits both, doubts that justice prevails, and resists systematic theology. Interpreters have debated whether the book is pessimistic, whether its affirmations of joy are its true message, and how the pious epilogue relates to the body of the work. It is read in Judaism during the festival of Sukkot.
