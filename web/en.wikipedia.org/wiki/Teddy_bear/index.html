Teddy bear

A teddy bear is a stuffed toy in the form of a bear. Developed seemingly simultaneously by toymakers Morris Michtom in the United States and Richard Steiff in Germany in the early 20th century, the teddy bear takes its name from President Theodore Roosevelt, whose nickname was "Teddy". Teddy bears have become popular collector's items and comfort objects for children worldwide.

History

The name derives from an incident on a bear-hunting trip in Mississippi in November 1902, when Roosevelt refused to shoot a black bear that had been captured and tied to a tree, deeming it unsportsmanlike. The episode was depicted in a famous cartoon by Clifford Berryman in The Washington Post. Brooklyn shopkeeper Morris Michtom placed two stuffed bears in his window, advertised as "Teddy's bears" with the president's permission, and went on to found the Ideal Novelty and Toy Company.

Independently, the German firm Steiff produced a jointed stuffed bear (design 55 PB) designed by Richard Steiff in 1902, which was shown at the Leipzig Toy Fair in 1903 and ordered in quantity by an American buyer. Early teddy bears had mohair fur, shoe-button eyes, and elongated snouts resembling real bears; over time they became softer, rounder, and more infant-like in design.

Cultural role

Teddy bears serve as transitional comfort objects for children and appear widely in literature and media, including A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh, Michael Bond's Paddington Bear, and the character Rupert Bear. Antique Steiff and early American bears are highly sought by collectors, and specialist museums of teddy bears exist in several countries.
