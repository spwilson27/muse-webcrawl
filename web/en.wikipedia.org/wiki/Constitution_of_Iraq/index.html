Constitution of Iraq

Created: 15 October 2005 (ratified)
System: Federal parliamentary republic
Branches: Executive, legislative, judicial
Chambers: Council of Representatives (unicameral)

The Constitution of the Republic of Iraq is the fundamental law of Iraq. It was drafted by a committee of the National Assembly elected in January 2005 and approved by referendum on 15 October 2005, coming into effect following the December 2005 elections. It replaced the Law of Administration for the State of Iraq for the Transitional Period (2004).

Overview

The constitution establishes Iraq as a federal, parliamentary, democratic republic and declares Islam a foundational source of legislation while guaranteeing freedom of religion. Article 2 provides that no law may be enacted that contradicts the established provisions of Islam, the principles of democracy, or the rights and freedoms stipulated in the constitution. Arabic and Kurdish are the official languages.

Federalism

The constitution recognizes the Kurdistan Region as a federal region and provides a mechanism (Article 119) by which governorates may form additional regions. Contentious questions — notably the status of Kirkuk, addressed by Article 140, and the management of oil and gas revenues — were left partially unresolved and have remained sources of political dispute.

Rights

The constitution guarantees civil and political rights including equality before the law, freedom of expression, and a quota reserving 25% of seats in the Council of Representatives for women.
