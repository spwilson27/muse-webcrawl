The Taiheiki (太平記, "Chronicle of Great Peace") is a Japanese historical epic written in the late 14th century. Comprising forty volumes, it recounts the political turmoil and warfare of Japan from 1318 to 1367: the efforts of Emperor Go-Daigo to overthrow the Kamakura shogunate, the Kenmu Restoration, and the ensuing Nanbokuchō ("Northern and Southern Courts") conflict between rival imperial lines and the Ashikaga shogunate.

Contents

Despite its title, the work describes an age of near-constant war. It opens with the misrule and portents preceding Go-Daigo's uprising, narrates the fall of the Kamakura regime in 1333, the brief Kenmu Restoration, Ashikaga Takauji's rebellion, and decades of fighting between the Northern and Southern Courts. Among its most famous episodes are the loyalist heroics of Kusunoki Masashige, the death of Nitta Yoshisada, and the tragic end of the young Emperor Go-Murakami's cause. The narrative blends documented events with legend, Buddhist moralising, and Confucian political commentary.

Authorship and text

The authorship is uncertain; tradition attributes the work to the monk Kojima Hōshi, possibly with later editing by others. Written in a mixed Sino-Japanese style (wakan konkō-bun), the Taiheiki was widely recited by blind minstrels (Taiheiki-yomi) during the Muromachi and Edo periods, which helped fix its stories in popular memory.

Influence

Alongside The Tale of the Heike, the Taiheiki is a principal monument of the gunki monogatari (war tale) genre. Its portrayal of Kusunoki Masashige as the paragon of loyalty was revived in the Meiji era and again in the 1930s–40s, and its episodes have been adapted repeatedly for theatre, film, and the NHK Taiga drama Taiheiki (1991).
