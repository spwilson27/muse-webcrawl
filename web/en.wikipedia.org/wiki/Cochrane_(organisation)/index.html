Cochrane (organisation)

Cochrane, formerly the Cochrane Collaboration, is a British international charitable organisation formed to organise medical research findings in order to facilitate evidence-based choices about health interventions by clinicians, patients, and policy makers. It includes over 40,000 volunteer experts and publishes systematic reviews in the Cochrane Library, principally the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.

History

The organisation was founded in 1993 under the leadership of Iain Chalmers, inspired by Scottish epidemiologist Archie Cochrane, whose 1972 book Effectiveness and Efficiency argued that healthcare resources should be used to provide interventions shown by reliable research to be effective. The first Cochrane Centre opened in Oxford, named for Cochrane at his suggestion. Cochrane's logo depicts a forest plot from an early meta-analysis showing that corticosteroids given to women in premature labour reduced infant deaths—an intervention whose benefits had been obscured until trials were systematically combined.

Work

Cochrane review groups produce systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials and other evidence on the effects of healthcare interventions, following published protocols and rigorous methods including risk-of-bias assessment and GRADE ratings of evidence certainty. The reviews are widely used in clinical guidelines and health policy.

Funding and controversies

Cochrane is funded by governments, universities, foundations, and library subscription revenue, and it prohibits commercial sponsorship of individual reviews. The organisation has faced internal disputes, notably the 2018 expulsion of co-founder Peter Gøtzsche, which prompted several board resignations and debate about its governance.
