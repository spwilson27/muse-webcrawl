The Motorcycle Diaries (Spanish: Diarios de motocicleta) is a 2004 biographical road film directed by Walter Salles. It dramatises the 1952 motorcycle journey across South America by the young Ernesto Guevara (Gael García Bernal) and his friend Alberto Granado (Rodrigo de la Serna), an experience that awakened Guevara's political consciousness years before he became the revolutionary known as "Che".

Plot

The two friends set out from Buenos Aires on Granado's ageing Norton 500 motorcycle, "La Poderosa", intending to reach Venezuela. Travelling through Chile, Peru (including Machu Picchu and a leper colony at San Pablo on the Amazon), Colombia and Venezuela, Guevara witnesses poverty and exploitation that transform his outlook. The film ends as he departs, determined to fight for social change.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at the Sundance Film Festival and screened in competition at Cannes. It won the Academy Award for Best Original Song for Jorge Drexler's "Al otro lado del río" — the first Spanish-language song so honoured — and the BAFTA Award for Best Film Not in the English Language, among many other prizes. Critics praised its warmth, photography and performances.
