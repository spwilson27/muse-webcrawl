Mesothelium

The mesothelium is a membrane composed of simple squamous epithelial cells of mesodermal origin that forms the lining of several body cavities: the pleura (pleural cavity around the lungs), peritoneum (abdominopelvic cavity), mediastinum, and pericardium (around the heart). Mesothelial tissue also surrounds the male internal reproductive organs (tunica vaginalis) and covers the internal reproductive organs of women (tunica serosa uteri).

FunctionLubrication, protection, fluid transport, immune surveillance

Structure and function

Mesothelial cells form a continuous monolayer resting on a basement membrane. Their apical surfaces bear microvilli and secrete a lubricating serous fluid rich in glycosaminoglycans and surfactant-like material, allowing organs to slide against one another with minimal friction. The mesothelium also participates in fluid and solute transport, antigen presentation, inflammation and tissue repair, and the prevention and formation of adhesions.

Clinical significance

Mesothelioma is a malignant cancer of mesothelial cells, most commonly of the pleura, strongly associated with occupational exposure to asbestos. Other conditions involving the mesothelium include pleural effusion, peritonitis, and post-surgical adhesions. Reactive mesothelial hyperplasia can mimic malignancy in cytology specimens, posing a diagnostic challenge.
