Imperial fora
LocationRome, Italy
Built46 BC – AD 113

The Imperial fora (Fori Imperiali in Italian) are a series of monumental public squares constructed in Rome over a period of about 150 years, between 46 BC and AD 113, by successive rulers of the late Roman Republic and Roman Empire. They extended the original Roman Forum, which had become too small and crowded for the city's growing administrative, judicial and ceremonial needs.

The individual fora

Forum of Caesar

Julius Caesar built the first of the imperial fora, inaugurated in 46 BC, dominated by the Temple of Venus Genetrix, mythical ancestress of the Julian family. It provided additional space for law courts and public business.

Forum of Augustus

Augustus vowed his forum before the Battle of Philippi (42 BC); it was inaugurated in 2 BC around the Temple of Mars Ultor ("Mars the Avenger"), flanked by porticoes displaying statues of Rome's great men.

Forum of Peace

Vespasian built the Forum of Peace (Templum Pacis) in AD 71–75 to celebrate the conquest of Jerusalem; it housed treasures from the Temple in Jerusalem and a famous library and art collection, and its wall later carried the marble Forma Urbis Romae.

Forum of Nerva

Domitian began and Nerva dedicated (AD 97) a narrow forum linking the earlier complexes, centred on a temple of Minerva.

Forum of Trajan

Trajan's forum, dedicated in AD 112, was the largest and most splendid: a vast plaza, the Basilica Ulpia, libraries, the equestrian statue of Trajan, and Trajan's Column, whose spiral relief narrates the Dacian Wars. The adjacent Markets of Trajan housed offices and shops.

Later history

In the 1930s Mussolini cut the Via dei Fori Imperiali through the area for military parades, destroying neighbourhoods but exposing parts of the fora; the avenue remains a major archaeological zone of modern Rome.
