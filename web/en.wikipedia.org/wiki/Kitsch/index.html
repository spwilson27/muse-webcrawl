Kitsch

Kitsch is a term applied to art and design that is considered in poor taste because of excessive garishness or sentimentality, but sometimes appreciated in an ironic or knowing way. Originating in the Munich art markets of the 1860s-70s, from German kitschen, it came to denote mass-produced, sentimental, melodramatic work appealing to popular taste.

Critics such as Clement Greenberg in 'Avant-Garde and Kitsch' (1939) and Milan Kundera in fiction contrasted kitsch with authentic art, linking it to totalitarian aesthetics and denial of doubt. Later artists in pop art and camp deliberately embraced kitsch.
