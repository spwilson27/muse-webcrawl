HuffPost

HuffPost, formerly The Huffington Post, is an American progressive news website and online newspaper founded in 2005 by Arianna Huffington, Andrew Breitbart, Kenneth Lerer, and Jonah Peretti. It became known for combining original journalism, aggregation, and a large network of unpaid contributor blogs.

History

The site launched in May 2005 as The Huffington Post. AOL acquired it in 2011 for $315 million, with Huffington becoming president and editor-in-chief of the Huffington Post Media Group. In 2012 its national reporting staff won the Pulitzer Prize for National Reporting, the first for a commercially run digital news outlet. The publication was renamed HuffPost in 2017. Verizon sold it to BuzzFeed in 2020, and BuzzFeed's news operations later underwent significant restructuring.

Editions

HuffPost has published numerous international editions, including versions for Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Italy, Japan, and other markets, several in partnership with local media organizations.
