Gdańsk (German: Danzig) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of about 487,000, it is the capital of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and Poland's principal seaport. Together with Gdynia and Sopot it forms the Tricity metropolitan area.

History

First mentioned in 997, Gdańsk grew into a wealthy Hanseatic trading city, trading grain and amber. It passed between Polish, Teutonic Order and Prussian rule over the centuries and was twice a "Free City" (1807–1814 and 1920–1939). The first shots of the Second World War were fired at Westerplatte on 1 September 1939. The ruined city was rebuilt after 1945 as part of Poland, with its historic Main Town faithfully reconstructed.

In 1980 the Gdańsk Shipyard strike led by Lech Wałęsa produced the Solidarity trade union, the first independent union in the Soviet bloc, a turning point in the collapse of communism in Eastern Europe.

Landmarks

The Long Market (Długi Targ) with the Neptune Fountain, St Mary's Church — one of the largest brick churches in the world — the medieval port Crane (Żuraw), and the European Solidarity Centre are among its landmarks. The city is also known as the world's amber capital.
