The Mysterious Island (French: L'Île mystérieuse) is a novel by the French writer Jules Verne, published in 1875. It is one of Verne's best-known works and is considered a classic of the robinsonade genre. The novel serves as a sequel to Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea (1870) and, less directly, to In Search of the Castaways (1868), reuniting characters and resolving the mystery of Captain Nemo.

Plot summary

During the American Civil War, five Union prisoners — engineer Cyrus Smith (Cyrus Harding in some translations), journalist Gideon Spilett, sailor Pencroff (Pencroft), servant Neb, the young Herbert Brown, and Smith's dog Top — escape the siege of Richmond, Virginia, by balloon. A storm carries them across the Pacific to an uncharted volcanic island in the South Pacific, which they name Lincoln Island.

Using Smith's scientific knowledge, the castaways build a thriving colony: they make fire, pottery, bricks, iron tools, glass, a telegraph, and eventually a seaworthy vessel, the Bonaventure. A series of unexplained events — the miraculous survival of Smith after a fall into the sea, a crate of supplies washed ashore, the rescue of the marooned Ayrton from nearby Tabor Island — convinces them that a hidden benefactor inhabits the island.

The benefactor proves to be Captain Nemo, the dying commander of the Nautilus, hidden in a submarine grotto beneath the island. Nemo reveals his past as Prince Dakkar and dies aboard his vessel, which is sunk as his tomb. When the island's volcano erupts and destroys Lincoln Island, the colonists escape on a small rock remnant and are rescued by the ship Duncan.

Themes

The novel celebrates human ingenuity, science, and cooperation: the colonists recreate civilization from nothing, cataloguing the island's flora and fauna and systematically applying chemistry, physics, and engineering. It also deepens the character of Nemo, transforming the enigmatic anti-hero of the earlier novel into a penitent benefactor.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times, notably the 1961 film Mysterious Island with effects by Ray Harryhausen, the 1973 Franco-Spanish-Italian film and miniseries starring Omar Sharif as Nemo, and the 2012 film Journey 2: The Mysterious Island, loosely based on the book.
