Irish pound

ISO code: IEP
Symbol: £
Issued by: Central Bank of Ireland
Replaced by: Euro (1999/2002)

The Irish pound (Irish: punt Éireannach; ISO code IEP) was the currency of Ireland from the foundation of the Irish Free State until the adoption of the euro. For most of its history it was maintained at par with the pound sterling; the link was broken in 1979 when Ireland joined the European Monetary System and sterling did not. The currency sign was £, and the pound was subdivided into 100 pence after decimalisation in 1971.

History

The Free State introduced its own coinage in 1928 (as the Saorstát pound) and banknotes followed. The Currency Act 1927 established the currency's legal basis, and the Central Bank of Ireland, created in 1943, became the issuing authority. Banknotes of the later "Series C" featured portraits of Irish literary and historical figures, while coins after 1928 carried designs of animals and birds by Percy Metcalfe.

Replacement by the euro

Ireland was a founding member of the euro. The conversion rate was fixed at €1 = £0.787564 on 1 January 1999, and euro notes and coins entered circulation on 1 January 2002. Irish pound cash ceased to be legal tender on 9 February 2002, though the Central Bank continues to exchange it for euro indefinitely.
