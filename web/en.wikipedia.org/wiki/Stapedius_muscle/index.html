Stapedius muscle

The stapedius is the smallest named skeletal muscle in the human body, measuring just over one millimetre in length. It lies within the tympanic cavity of the middle ear and serves to stabilize the stapes, the smallest bone in the body, damping its vibrations in response to loud sounds.

Structure

The stapedius emerges from the pyramidal eminence, a small projection on the posterior wall of the tympanic cavity, and its tendon inserts into the neck of the stapes. It is supplied by the nerve to stapedius, a branch of the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII), reflecting its development from the second pharyngeal arch.

Function

When the stapedius contracts — reflexively, in response to loud noise, just before speaking (the acoustic or stapedial reflex) — it pulls the stapes posteriorly and stiffens the ossicular chain, reducing the transmission of low-frequency sound energy to the cochlea. Together with the tensor tympani, it helps protect the inner ear from acoustic trauma and improves the perception of speech in noisy environments.

Clinical significance

Paralysis of the stapedius, as may occur in Bell's palsy when the facial nerve lesion is proximal to the nerve to stapedius, causes hyperacusis — abnormal sensitivity to ordinary sounds. The stapedial reflex is measured clinically in acoustic reflex testing as part of audiological assessment.
