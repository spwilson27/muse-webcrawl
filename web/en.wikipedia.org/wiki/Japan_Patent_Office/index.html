The Japan Patent Office (JPO; Japanese: 特許庁, Tokkyochō) is a Japanese government agency under the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry. It administers Japan's industrial property system, examining and granting patents, utility model rights, design rights and trademark rights.

History

Japan's first patent law, the Patent Monopoly Ordinance, was promulgated in 1885 under the direction of Takahashi Korekiyo, who became the first head of the patent bureau. The JPO is one of the world's largest patent offices and, together with the United States Patent and Trademark Office and the European Patent Office, forms the "trilateral" offices; it is also a member of the IP5 group of the five largest offices.

Functions

The office employs roughly a thousand patent examiners and handles several hundred thousand patent applications annually. It publishes patent documents, maintains the J-PlatPat information platform, and cooperates internationally through the Patent Cooperation Treaty and work-sharing programmes such as the Patent Prosecution Highway.
