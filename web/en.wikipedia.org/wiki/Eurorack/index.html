Eurorack is a modular synthesizer format introduced by the German company Doepfer in 1995 with its A-100 system. It has become the most popular modular format, with modules produced by hundreds of manufacturers worldwide that interoperate in shared cases.

Specification

Eurorack modules are 3U high, with widths measured in horizontal pitch (1 HP = 0.2 inches). Signals are patched with 3.5 mm mono cables; audio and control voltages share the same connections, following roughly a 1 volt per octave standard for pitch. Power is supplied from a bus board, typically ±12 V with ribbon cables using 10- or 16-pin connectors.

Growth

Originally a niche format based on Doepfer's adaptation of industrial Eurocard rack standards, Eurorack grew rapidly in the 2010s as small and boutique manufacturers — including Mutable Instruments, Make Noise, Intellijel and many others — released innovative modules, and larger firms such as Moog and Roland entered the format. The ecosystem spans analogue oscillators and filters, digital effects, sequencers, samplers and complete voices, and a large do-it-yourself community publishes open-source designs.
