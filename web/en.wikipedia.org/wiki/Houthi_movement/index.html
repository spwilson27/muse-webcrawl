The Houthi movement, officially called Ansar Allah ("Supporters of God"), is a Zaydi Shia Islamist political and military organisation that emerged from Saada Governorate in northern Yemen in the 1990s. It takes its name from the al-Houthi family, whose religious scholars led a revival of Zaydism — the branch of Shia Islam historically dominant in Yemen's north — against perceived marginalisation by the Yemeni government and the spread of Salafism.

Origins and wars with the government

The movement grew out of the "Believing Youth" (al-Shabab al-Mo'min) organisation founded in the early 1990s to teach Zaydi doctrine. Under Hussein Badreddin al-Houthi, a former parliamentarian, it adopted an increasingly political and confrontational stance, popularising the slogan known as the sarkha. In 2004, President Ali Abdullah Saleh's government launched a military campaign against the group; Hussein al-Houthi was killed that September, but leadership passed to his brother Abdul-Malik al-Houthi, and six rounds of war (the Saada Wars) continued until a ceasefire in 2010.

Takeover of Sanaa and the civil war

Taking advantage of the chaos following Yemen's 2011 uprising, the Houthis expanded southward, captured the capital Sanaa in September 2014, and in early 2015 forced President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi to flee. In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition intervened militarily to restore the internationally recognised government, beginning a devastating war that produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises. The Houthis survived the campaign and consolidated control over Yemen's populous northwest, including the capital and the Red Sea port of Hodeidah. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 largely held thereafter at reduced intensity, though no comprehensive peace settlement was reached.

Red Sea attacks

Following the outbreak of the Gaza war in October 2023, the Houthis began launching missiles and drones at Israel and attacking commercial shipping in the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb strait, declaring they would target vessels linked to Israel until the blockade of Gaza ended. The attacks sharply reduced traffic through the Suez Canal route, forced major shipping lines to reroute around Africa, and prompted US- and UK-led air strikes on Houthi targets in Yemen from January 2024, as well as a US-led maritime coalition (Operation Prosperity Guardian) and an EU mission (Aspides) to protect shipping.

Beliefs and support

The movement's slogan — "God is Great, Death to America, Death to Israel, A Curse Upon the Jews, Victory to Islam" — is drawn on its banners, though leaders describe it as directed at governments' policies rather than peoples. Yemen's government and Saudi Arabia accuse Iran of supplying the Houthis with weapons and training as part of the Iran–Saudi proxy conflict; the Houthis and Iran acknowledge political support while the extent of material support and Iranian control remains disputed among analysts. The United States designated Ansar Allah a terrorist organisation in 2021 (revoked the same year for humanitarian reasons), re-designated it as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist entity in 2024, and as a Foreign Terrorist Organization in 2025.
