The World Scout Jamboree is a Scouting jamboree organized by the World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM), held every four years in a different country. It brings together tens of thousands of Scouts, generally aged 14 to 17, together with adult leaders and an International Service Team of volunteers, for around ten days of camping, outdoor activities, community service and cultural exchange.

History

The first World Scout Jamboree was held at Olympia in London in 1920, where Scouting's founder Robert Baden-Powell was acclaimed Chief Scout of the World. Subsequent jamborees were held in Denmark, Hungary, the Netherlands, France and elsewhere; the event was interrupted by the Second World War. The 1957 Jamboree at Sutton Park in England marked Scouting's golden jubilee. Notable recent editions include the 21st World Scout Jamboree in the United Kingdom in 2007, the centenary year of Scouting, which drew about 40,000 participants; the 24th in 2019, jointly hosted by Canada, Mexico and the United States at the Summit Bechtel Reserve in West Virginia; and the 25th in South Korea in 2023, which was disrupted by extreme heat and a typhoon evacuation. The 26th Jamboree is scheduled for Gdańsk, Poland, in 2027.

Programme

Participants live in sub-camps organised by contingent, and activities typically include water sports, climbing, global development workshops, faith and beliefs zones, and off-site community service. A central arena hosts opening and closing ceremonies. The word "jamboree" is said to have been chosen by Baden-Powell to mean a joyful gathering, and the event is intended to promote international understanding and peace.
