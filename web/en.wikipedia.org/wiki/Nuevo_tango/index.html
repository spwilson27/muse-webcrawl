Nuevo tango

Nuevo tango (Spanish for "new tango"), also called tango nuevo, is a style of tango music developed principally by the Argentine composer Ástor Piazzolla from the 1950s onward. It fused traditional tango with elements of jazz and classical music — extended harmonies, dissonance, counterpoint, and new instrumental forms — moving tango from the dance hall toward the concert stage.

Ástor Piazzolla

Piazzolla, a bandoneon virtuoso who had played in Aníbal Troilo's orchestra, studied in Paris with Nadia Boulanger, who encouraged him to embrace his tango roots. With ensembles such as the Octeto Buenos Aires (founded 1955) and his quintets, he composed landmark works including "Adiós Nonino" (1959), "Libertango" (1974), the Four Seasons of Buenos Aires, and the "tango operita" María de Buenos Aires (1968). Traditionalists initially denounced his music as "not tango", but it achieved worldwide acclaim.

Characteristics

Nuevo tango retains tango's characteristic rhythms and the bandoneon, violin, piano, electric guitar, and double bass line-up, while incorporating fugue, chromaticism, sudden tempo changes, and improvisational passages drawn from jazz. Later developments include electrotango groups such as Gotan Project, which combined nuevo tango with electronic music.

Dance

The term also describes a modern, improvisational style of tango dancing that developed from the 1980s–90s, analysing tango's structure and exploring open embraces and new combinations, distinct from the traditional milonguero style.
