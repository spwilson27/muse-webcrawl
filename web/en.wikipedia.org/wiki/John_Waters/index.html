John Samuel Waters Jr. (born 22 April 1946) is an American filmmaker, writer, actor and artist. Born and raised in Baltimore, Maryland, he rose to fame in the early 1970s with transgressive cult films he called his "Trash Trilogy": Pink Flamingos (1972), Female Trouble (1974) and Desperate Living (1977). William S. Burroughs dubbed him the "Pope of Trash".

Career

Waters's films feature his repertory company, the Dreamlanders, most famously the drag performer Divine. His work moved toward the mainstream with Hairspray (1988), which was adapted into a hit Broadway musical and a 2007 film remake, followed by Cry-Baby (1990), Serial Mom (1994), Pecker (1998) and A Dirty Shame (2004).

Beyond film, Waters is the author of books including Shock Value (1981), Crackpot (1986), Role Models (2010), Carsick (2014) and the novel Liarmouth (2022); he performs spoken-word shows, exhibits visual art, and is famous for his pencil moustache. In 2023 he received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. Several of his films have been selected for the National Film Registry.
