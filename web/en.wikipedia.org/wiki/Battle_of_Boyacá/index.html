The Battle of Boyacá (7 August 1819) was the decisive battle of Simón Bolívar's campaign to liberate New Granada (modern Colombia and Panama) from Spanish rule. The patriot victory at the bridge of Boyacá opened the road to Bogotá and secured the independence of Colombia, providing the base for the later liberation of Venezuela, Ecuador, Peru and Bolivia.

Background

In May–July 1819 Bolívar led his army on an audacious crossing of the flooded llanos and the icy Eastern Cordillera of the Andes at the Páramo de Pisba, an ordeal that cost many lives but achieved complete surprise. After hard fighting at Pantano de Vargas on 25 July, Bolívar manoeuvred to cut off the royalist army of Colonel José María Barreiro from the capital, Santa Fe de Bogotá.

The battle

On 7 August the royalists, some 2,700 strong, marched toward Bogotá and reached the bridge over the Teatinos River near Tunja. Bolívar's roughly 2,850 men got there first. The patriot vanguard under Santander seized the bridge while Bolívar struck the royalist main body; sandwiched and cut off from the crossing, the royalist force collapsed after about two hours. Barreiro and some 1,600 men were captured; royalist dead numbered around 100, against only 13 patriot dead.

Aftermath

The viceroy, Juan José de Sámano, fled Bogotá, which Bolívar entered unopposed on 10 August. Barreiro was later executed. The victory created the Republic of Colombia (Gran Colombia), proclaimed at the Congress of Angostura and confirmed at Cúcuta in 1821. 7 August is a national holiday in Colombia, and the bridge is preserved as a national monument.
