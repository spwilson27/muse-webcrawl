Emperor Wu of Liang

Emperor Wu of Liang (464 – 12 June 549), personal name Xiao Yan, was the founding emperor of the Liang dynasty of southern China, reigning from 502 to 549 — one of the longest reigns in Chinese history. Initially a general of the Southern Qi, he usurped the throne and established the Liang.

Reign

Emperor Wu's early reign was considered competent and prosperous, with attention to scholarship, law, and administration. He was a notable poet and patron of learning, and compiled literary anthologies. Devoutly Buddhist in his later years, he banned animal sacrifice, promoted vegetarianism at court, and several times "gave himself" to a monastery, obliging his officials to ransom him back with large donations — earning him the nickname "the Bodhisattva Emperor".

Fall

His credulity toward the rebel general Hou Jing proved fatal. Hou, welcomed after defecting from Northern Wei in 547, rebelled the following year, besieged the capital Jiankang, and starved the emperor to death in 549. The Hou Jing rebellion devastated the south and fatally weakened the Liang.
