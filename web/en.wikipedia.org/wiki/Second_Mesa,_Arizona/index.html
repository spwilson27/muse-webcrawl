Second Mesa and its Hopi villages

Second Mesa is a census-designated place (CDP) in Navajo and Coconino Counties in northeastern Arizona, United States, within the Hopi Reservation. It takes its name from the mesa — the middle of the three Hopi mesas, between First Mesa and Third Mesa — on and around which its villages stand, at roughly 5,700 feet above sea level along Arizona State Route 264.

Villages

Second Mesa comprises three Hopi villages occupying the top and flanks of the mesa: Shungopavi (Hopí: Songòopavi), one of the largest and oldest continuously inhabited Hopi settlements, traditionally the "mother village" of Second Mesa; Mishongnovi (Musangnuvi), on the steep northeastern side; and Sipaulovi (Sipàulovi), near the mesa's edge. After the Pueblo Revolt era and the Hopi return to the mesas around 1700, the villages were rebuilt in their present elevated, defensible positions.

History and culture

The Hopi have farmed the arid lands around the mesas for centuries, using dry-farming techniques in sandy fields and washes below the mesa to grow corn, beans, squash and melons. Each village is organised around clan-based ceremonial life centred on kivas, with an annual cycle of katsina dances and other ceremonies; some events are open to visitors under village rules, while others are closed, and photography and recording are generally prohibited.

The Hopi Cultural Center, with a museum, restaurant and motel, is located on Second Mesa along SR 264 and serves as the principal visitor facility on the reservation. Second Mesa is also known for its artists, particularly coiled pottery and basketry — the mesa's weavers produce distinctive coiled plaques and baskets — as well as silver overlay jewellery associated with Hopi craftspeople more broadly.

Geography and demographics

The CDP covers a broad area of mesa top and surrounding desert; nearly all residents are Native American, predominantly Hopi, and the Hopi language remains in daily use alongside English. The population was 962 at the 2010 census and approximately 900 at the 2020 census.
