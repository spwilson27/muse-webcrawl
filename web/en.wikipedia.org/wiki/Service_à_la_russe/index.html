Service à la russe

Service à la russe (French for "service in the Russian style") is a manner of dining that involves courses being brought to the table sequentially, with each course served to diners individually by waiters, rather than the older service à la française, in which a large array of dishes was placed on the table at once for guests to serve themselves.

History

The style became fashionable in France in the early 19th century; it is traditionally credited to the Russian ambassador Alexander Kurakin, who is said to have introduced it in Paris around 1810, and it was promoted by the chef Antonin Carême and later codified by Urbain Dubois and Georges Auguste Escoffier. During the later 19th century it displaced service à la française throughout Europe and became the basis of the modern restaurant and banquet service sequence: hors d'œuvres, soup, fish, entrée, roast, and so on, each plated or portioned by staff.

Characteristics

Under service à la russe, food is finished and portioned in the kitchen or at a side table, and waiting staff require greater skill in carving, filleting and plating. The table is set only with the items needed for the courses to come, in contrast to the lavish display pieces and full silver of the French style. The sequential format also fixed the modern structure of the formal multi-course menu.
