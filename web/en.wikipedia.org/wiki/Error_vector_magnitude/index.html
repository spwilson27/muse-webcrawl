Error vector magnitude

The error vector between the ideal and measured points on a constellation diagram (image placeholder)

The error vector magnitude or EVM (sometimes also called relative constellation error, RCE) is a measure used to quantify the performance of a digital radio transmitter or receiver. A signal sent by an ideal transmitter or received by a receiver would have all constellation points precisely at the ideal locations; however, impairments such as phase noise, low image rejection ratio, and non-linearity cause the actual constellation points to deviate from the ideal locations. EVM measures that deviation.

Definition

An error vector is a vector in the I–Q plane between the ideal constellation point and the point actually received. The EVM is the root mean square (RMS) value of the error vectors, normalised to the average power (or peak power) of the ideal constellation, and is usually expressed as a percentage or in decibels:

EVM (dB) = 10 log10 (Perror / Preference)

Applications

EVM is a standard compliance metric in wireless standards including Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), LTE and 5G NR, where higher-order modulation schemes such as 256-QAM and 1024-QAM require very low EVM values, typically a few percent. Because EVM aggregates many different impairments, it provides a single "figure of merit" for modulation quality, and it is related to the achievable bit error rate of the link.
