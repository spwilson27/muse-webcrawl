Bid–ask spread

The bid–ask spread (also bid–offer or bid/ask) is the difference between the prices quoted for an immediate sale (ask) and an immediate purchase (bid) for stocks, futures contracts, options, currency pairs, and many other financial instruments. The size of the spread is one measure of market liquidity and of transaction cost.

Example

If a stock is quoted at a bid of $10.00 and an ask of $10.05, the spread is $0.05. A buyer using a market order pays the ask; a seller receives the bid. Market makers profit from the spread in exchange for providing liquidity.

Types and determinants

Spreads widen with volatility, low trading volume, and information asymmetry, and narrow in heavily traded markets. Variants include the quoted spread, effective spread (versus the midpoint), and realised spread.
