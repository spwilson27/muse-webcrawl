BALCO scandal

The BALCO scandal was a scandal involving the use of banned performance-enhancing substances by professional athletes, centred on the Bay Area Laboratory Co-operative (BALCO), a California nutritional supplements company founded by Victor Conte. A federal investigation beginning in 2002 revealed that BALCO had supplied athletes with undetectable designer steroids, most famously tetrahydrogestrinone (THG), nicknamed "the clear".

Investigation

In September 2003 federal agents raided BALCO's offices in Burlingame, California. An anonymous coach — later revealed as Trevor Graham — had sent authorities a syringe of THG, enabling drug testers to develop a screen for it. Grand jury testimony by prominent athletes was reported in the press, implicating stars in baseball and track and field.

People implicated

Athletes linked to BALCO included baseball players Barry Bonds and Jason Giambi, and sprinters Marion Jones, Tim Montgomery and Dwain Chambers. Jones pleaded guilty in 2007 to lying to federal investigators and served prison time; Bonds was convicted of obstruction of justice (later reversed on appeal). Conte and BALCO vice-president James Valente pleaded guilty to distributing steroids and served prison sentences.

Aftermath

The scandal intensified pressure on professional leagues to strengthen drug testing, and led to congressional hearings on steroids in baseball.
