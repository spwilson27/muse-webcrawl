8K resolution

8K resolution refers to a horizontal display resolution of approximately 8,000 pixels. The most common 8K format is 7680 × 4320 pixels (8K UHD), which has four times the total pixel count of 4K and sixteen times that of Full HD 1080p. It is the highest resolution defined in the Rec. 2020 (UHDTV) standard.

History

Japanese broadcaster NHK began research into what it called Super Hi-Vision in the 1990s and demonstrated 8K systems from the early 2000s. The first 8K television, Sharp's LV-85001, went on sale in Japan in 2015. NHK launched BS8K, the first 8K broadcast channel, in December 2018, and 8K coverage featured prominently at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics.

Adoption

Consumer 8K televisions from major manufacturers appeared from 2018 onward, but adoption has remained limited by high prices, a scarcity of native 8K content, and the modest perceived benefit over 4K at typical screen sizes and viewing distances. 8K capture is meanwhile available in professional cinema cameras and in flagship smartphones, and the format is used in fields such as digital signage, planetariums and medical imaging.

Standards

8K UHD (7680 × 4320) uses a 16:9 aspect ratio. The 8K Association, founded in 2019, certifies consumer 8K televisions, while HDMI 2.1 provides the interface bandwidth needed for 8K at 60 Hz. Digital cinema also defines an 8K format of 8192 × 4320.
