Divje Babe flute

The Divje Babe flute is a cave bear femur pierced by spaced holes that was found at the Divje Babe I archaeological park near Cerkno in northwestern Slovenia in 1995. It has been suggested as the world's oldest known musical instrument — a Neanderthal flute — though this interpretation is disputed.

Divje Babe flute
Material: Cave bear femur
Age: c. 50,000–60,000 years (disputed)
Location: National Museum of Slovenia, Ljubljana

Discovery

Excavators led by Ivan Turk found the bone in a Mousterian layer attributed to Neanderthals. It is a juvenile cave bear femur with two complete holes and broken ends that may have held more holes.

Debate

Supporters argue the hole spacing matches a diatonic scale and experiments show a replica can play melodies. Critics, including researchers who studied carnivore gnawing, argue the holes were made by hyena or wolf teeth puncturing the bone, citing similar punctured bones elsewhere. The question remains unresolved and is a prominent case in debates about Neanderthal symbolic behaviour and the origin of music.
