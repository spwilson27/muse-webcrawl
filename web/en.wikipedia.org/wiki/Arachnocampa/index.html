Arachnocampa is a genus of fungus gnats in the family Keroplatidae whose larvae are bioluminescent and are commonly known as glowworms (not to be confused with the glowworm beetles of the Northern Hemisphere). The genus is found in Australia and New Zealand, where the larvae live in caves, grottoes, and sheltered forest banks, hanging from silken threads and using their light to lure flying insects into sticky snares.

The name Arachnocampa means "spider-worm" (from Greek arachne, spider, and kampe, caterpillar or worm), a reference to the larva's spider-like habit of fishing for prey with lines of silk hung with droplets of mucus.

Species

Five species are generally recognised:

  • Arachnocampa luminosa — New Zealand
  • Arachnocampa richardsae — New South Wales, Australia
  • Arachnocampa tasmaniensis — Tasmania, Australia
  • Arachnocampa buffaloensis — Victoria, Australia
  • Arachnocampa girraweenensis — Queensland, Australia

Life cycle and behaviour

The female lays eggs on cave walls or sheltered rock faces. On hatching, each larva builds a horizontal silk tube, or "nest", from which it suspends a set of vertical fishing lines studded with sticky mucus droplets. The larva's light organs, modified Malpighian tubules at the tip of the abdomen, emit a blue-green light (peak emission around 487 nm) produced by a luciferin–luciferase reaction unique to the genus. Small flying insects, drawn toward the light, become trapped on the lines and are reeled in and eaten.

Larvae are territorial and may shine more brightly when hungry or when disturbed. After several months the larva pupates, suspended from the ceiling, and the adult emerges as a short-lived fly that does not feed; adults live only a few days, mating and laying eggs before dying. Cannibalism between larvae and predation on adults by other larvae are common.

Tourism

Colonies of Arachnocampa luminosa in the Waitomo Caves of New Zealand's North Island are a major tourist attraction, as are the glowworm displays at Springbrook National Park in Queensland, Australia. Tours are typically conducted in darkness by boat or on foot, and artificial light and noise are restricted to protect the colonies.
