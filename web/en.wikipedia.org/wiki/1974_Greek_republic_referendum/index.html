1974 Greek republic referendum

Date8 December 1974
LocationGreece
QuestionMonarchy or republic?
ResultRepublic: 69.2%; Monarchy: 30.8%

The 1974 Greek republic referendum was held on 8 December 1974 to decide whether Greece would retain the monarchy or become a republic. Following the collapse of the military junta earlier that year, voters chose overwhelmingly for a republic.

Background

King Constantine II had fled Greece in 1967 after a failed counter-coup against the junta. The junta abolished the monarchy in 1973 in a disputed referendum. After democracy was restored under Konstantinos Karamanlis in 1974, a free referendum was promised.

Campaign and result

Karamanlis's government remained officially neutral. About 69.2% of voters supported a republic and 30.8% the monarchy. Greece was proclaimed a republic, and the result was incorporated into the Constitution of 1975.
