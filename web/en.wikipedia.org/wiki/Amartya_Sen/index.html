Amartya Sen (born 3 November 1933) is an Indian economist and philosopher whose work transformed welfare economics, development economics and the study of famine and Poverty. He received the 1998 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his contributions to welfare economics.

Contributions

In Poverty and Famines (1981) Sen showed that famines — including the Bengal famine of 1943, which he witnessed as a child — typically occur not from an absolute lack of food but from the collapse of people's entitlements: what they can command through production, work, trade and transfers. With Mahbub ul Haq he helped create the Human Development Index used by the UNDP as an alternative to GDP per capita. His capability approach judges development by the real freedoms people have to live lives they value — the theme of Development as Freedom (1999). Earlier work in social choice theory revisited Arrow's impossibility theorem and the liberal paradox. Sen has taught at Delhi, the LSE, Oxford, Harvard and Cambridge, and remains a leading public voice on inequality, democracy and public health.
