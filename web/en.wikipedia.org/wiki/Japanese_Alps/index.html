The Japanese Alps (日本アルプス, Nihon Arupusu) is a series of mountain ranges in central Honshu, Japan, forming the mountainous spine of the island. The name was coined by English mining engineer William Gowland and popularized by the missionary and mountaineer Walter Weston, often called the "father of Japanese mountaineering."

The three ranges

The Northern Alps (Hida Mountains) stretch along the borders of Nagano, Toyama, and Gifu prefectures and include Mount Hotaka and Mount Yari, with the high plateau of Kamikōchi a famous visitor destination. The Central Alps (Kiso Mountains), the smallest range, lie in Nagano and are crowned by Mount Kisokoma. The Southern Alps (Akaishi Mountains) contain Mount Kita, Japan's second-highest peak at 3,193 metres after Mount Fuji, and Mount Akaishi.

Significance

The ranges' heavy winter snowfall feeds major rivers and hydroelectric dams (including the Kurobe Dam), while their dramatic scenery and reliable snow made towns such as Hakuba and the Nagano region hosts of the 1998 Winter Olympics. Much of the area is protected within national parks and is a centre for hiking, climbing, and skiing.
