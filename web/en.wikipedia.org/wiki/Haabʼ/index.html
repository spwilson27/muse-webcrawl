The Haabʼ is the Maya civilisation's 365-day solar calendar, used alongside the 260-day Tzolkʼin ritual calendar. Together the two cycles form the 52-year Calendar Round. The Haabʼ approximates the tropical year but, having no leap-day correction, its New Year drifted slowly against the seasons over the centuries.

Structure

The Haabʼ consists of 18 months of 20 days each, numbered 0 to 19, totalling 360 days, followed by a five-day period called Wayebʼ (also spelled Uayeb), making 365 days. The months, in order, are:

Wayebʼ

The five closing days of Wayebʼ were regarded as an unlucky and dangerous time, when the portals between the mortal realm and the underworld dissolved. During Wayebʼ, Maya people stayed home, avoided washing and work, and performed rituals to ward off misfortune for the coming year.

Use

Dates were normally given as a combination of Tzolkʼin and Haabʼ positions, a pairing that repeats only every 52 Haabʼ years. The Haabʼ governed the agricultural and civic year, while the Long Count was used for historical dates spanning longer periods. The calendar was still in use in parts of highland Guatemala at the time of the Spanish conquest.
