Higher Education Opportunity Act

The Higher Education Opportunity Act (HEOA, Public Law 110-315) is a United States federal law enacted on August 14, 2008, which reauthorized the Higher Education Act of 1965. It was the first comprehensive reauthorization of the Act since 1998, following multiple temporary extensions.

Provisions

The Act addresses college affordability and transparency: it requires institutions to publish net price calculators, creates state and institutional watch lists for tuition increases, and mandates disclosure of textbook pricing. It expands Pell Grant year-round availability, reforms student loan programs, simplifies the FAFSA, strengthens accreditation and program-integrity rules, increases authorization for programs serving minority and low-income students, and requires campus emergency-notification and fire-safety reporting (incorporating provisions named for victims of the Virginia Tech shooting). It also addresses illegal peer-to-peer file sharing on campus networks.

Aftermath

Although the HEOA expired in 2013, the Higher Education Act programs have continued under extensions. Repeated congressional attempts at a further comprehensive reauthorization have stalled, making the 2008 Act the last full rewrite of the statute for many years.
