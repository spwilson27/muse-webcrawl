Heather

Heather is the common name for a group of low-growing evergreen shrubs of the heath family (Ericaceae), principally Calluna vulgaris (common heather or ling), the sole species of the genus Calluna, together with the many species of heaths in the genus Erica. Heather dominates large areas of moorland and heathland in Europe and Asia Minor, producing masses of purple, pink or white flowers in late summer.

Calluna vulgaris

Calluna vulgaris grows 20–50 cm tall in acidic, nutrient-poor soils, from Iceland and Scandinavia across Europe to Siberia and northwestern Africa, and has been introduced to North America and New Zealand, where it can be invasive. It is an important food plant for grazing animals, red grouse and the larvae of various moths, and supports the production of heather honey by beekeepers.

Uses

Heather has long been used for thatching, bedding, brooms (besoms), fuel, and basket making, and its shoots were historically used to flavour beer before the widespread use of hops. Heather is a traditional remedy in folk medicine, and heather honey is a prized, strongly flavoured product. Numerous cultivars of both Calluna and Erica are grown as ornamental garden plants for their foliage and flower colour, providing ground cover in acid soils.

Culture

In Scottish tradition, white heather is considered lucky and is worn in bridal bouquets; purple heather is associated with admiration and solitude. Heather is strongly associated with the landscapes of Scotland, where it blooms across the Highlands in August and September.

This article concerns the plants. "Heather" is also a common given name.
