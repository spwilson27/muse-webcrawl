Lushan Conference

The Lushan Conference was a meeting of the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party held in the resort town of Lushan, Jiangxi province, from July to August 1959. Convened to discuss the course of the Great Leap Forward, it became famous for the purge of Defence Minister Peng Dehuai after his criticism of the campaign.

Background

By mid-1959 the Great Leap Forward had produced severe economic dislocation and the beginnings of famine, though its scale was obscured by inflated production reports. On 14 July 1959 Peng Dehuai wrote Mao Zedong a private letter of opinion criticising the "petty-bourgeois fanaticism" of the Leap, the communal canteens and exaggerated output claims, while still endorsing Mao's leadership overall.

The purge

Mao circulated the letter to the conference, denounced Peng's views as a right-opportunist attack, and forced the leadership to choose sides. Peng was removed as defence minister, placed under house arrest, and replaced by Lin Biao; his associates Huang Kecheng, Zhang Wentian and Zhou Xiaozhou were also purged. Rather than moderating policy, the conference launched a nationwide campaign against "right opportunism" that intensified the Leap and deepened the ensuing famine. Peng was later persecuted during the Cultural Revolution and died in 1974; he was posthumously rehabilitated in 1978.
