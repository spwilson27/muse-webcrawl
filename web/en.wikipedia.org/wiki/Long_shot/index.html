Long shot

In photography, filmmaking and video production, a long shot (sometimes referred to as a full shot or wide shot) is a shot that typically shows the entire object or human figure and is usually intended to place it in some relation to its surroundings. A long shot is often used as an establishing shot at the start of a scene, showing the setting and the spatial relationships of the characters within it.

Types

An extreme long shot (or extreme wide shot) frames the subject from a great distance, making the figure small or even lost in the landscape; it is used to convey scale, isolation or grandeur, as in the desert vistas of Lawrence of Arabia. A conventional long shot shows a human figure in full, head to feet, with some space above and below. When the framing cuts off around the knees it shades into the "American shot" or three-quarter shot.

Usage

Directors use long shots to orient the audience geographically, to emphasise spectacle or environment over individual emotion, and as a contrast with close-ups. Because facial expressions are unreadable at this distance, meaning is carried by staging, costume, movement and setting. The term should not be confused with the long s, an archaic letterform.
