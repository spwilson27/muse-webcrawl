Erich Kästner

Emil Erich Kästner (23 February 1899 – 29 July 1974) was a German writer, poet, screenwriter and satirist, best known internationally for his children's books, including Emil and the Detectives (1929), Lottie and Lisa (1949), and The Flying Classroom (1933).

Life

Born in Dresden, Kästner served in the First World War, then studied in Leipzig and worked as a journalist and critic in Berlin during the Weimar Republic. His satirical novel Fabian (1931) captured the era's anxieties. Under the Nazi regime his books were burned in the May 1933 book burnings and he was banned from publishing, though he remained in Germany—an instance of "inner emigration"—and wrote the screenplay for the 1943 film Münchhausen under a pseudonym.

Works and legacy

After the war Kästner edited the children's magazine Pinguin and wrote the anti-war works The Animals' Conference and the autobiographical When I Was a Little Boy. Lottie and Lisa, about separated twin sisters, has been adapted repeatedly for film, most famously in Disney's The Parent Trap. He received numerous honours, including the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 1960.
