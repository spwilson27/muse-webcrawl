First impeachment of Donald Trump

The first impeachment of Donald Trump occurred in 2019–2020, when the United States House of Representatives impeached President Donald Trump on charges of abuse of power and obstruction of Congress, arising from the Trump–Ukraine scandal. The Senate acquitted him on both articles on 5 February 2020. Trump was the third president in American history to be impeached, after Andrew Johnson and Bill Clinton.

Background: the Trump–Ukraine scandal

In August 2019 an anonymous whistleblower in the intelligence community complained about a 25 July 2019 telephone call in which Trump had asked Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy to investigate former vice-president Joe Biden — then a leading candidate for the Democratic presidential nomination — and his son Hunter, as well as a discredited theory about Ukrainian interference in the 2016 election. Nearly $400 million in congressionally approved military aid to Ukraine had been withheld in the weeks before the call. House testimony by diplomats and officials described a pressure campaign conducted partly through Trump's lawyer Rudy Giuliani.

Inquiry and articles

Speaker Nancy Pelosi announced a formal impeachment inquiry on 24 September 2019. After closed-door depositions and public hearings before the House Intelligence and Judiciary Committees, the House adopted two articles of impeachment on 18 December 2019, almost entirely along party lines: abuse of power (230–197) and obstruction of Congress (229–198), the latter based on the administration's refusal to comply with subpoenas.

Senate trial and acquittal

The Senate trial, presided over by Chief Justice John Roberts, ran from 16 January to 5 February 2020. The Senate voted against calling witnesses, rejecting in particular testimony from former national security adviser John Bolton. On 5 February the Senate acquitted Trump: 48–52 on abuse of power, with Mitt Romney the sole Republican voting to convict, and 47–53 on obstruction. Trump thus remained in office; he was impeached a second time in January 2021 following the attack on the Capitol, a distinction no other president shares.
