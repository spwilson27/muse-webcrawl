Smart grid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A smart grid links producers and consumers with two-way communication

A smart grid is an electricity network that uses digital communications and information technology to monitor, control and optimise the generation, distribution and consumption of electric power. Unlike the traditional one-way grid, a smart grid supports two-way flows of both electricity and information.

Features

Key elements include smart meters and advanced metering infrastructure, which record consumption in near-real time; sensors and automated switches along distribution lines that can detect faults and reroute power ("self-healing"); demand response programmes that shift consumption away from peak periods; and the integration of distributed energy resources such as rooftop solar panels, wind turbines, battery storage and electric vehicles. Time-varying pricing can signal customers to use electricity when it is cheap and abundant.

History and deployment

The concept gained prominence in the 2000s as metering and communications costs fell. In the United States the Energy Independence and Security Act of 2007 set out federal smart grid policy, and large rollouts of smart meters followed in North America, Europe and China. National and regional programmes differ, but common goals include reliability, efficiency, decarbonisation and accommodating variable renewable generation.

Criticism and challenges

Concerns raised about smart grids include cybersecurity (a networked grid presents a larger attack surface), privacy of detailed consumption data, the cost of deployment, and questions over electromagnetic emissions and meter accuracy. Interoperability standards are an ongoing focus of industry bodies.
