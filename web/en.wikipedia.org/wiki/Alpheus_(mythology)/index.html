Alpheus (mythology)

[Image: Classical artwork of the river god Alpheus pursuing Arethusa]
Alpheus pursues the nymph Arethusa.

Alpheus (Ancient Greek: Ἀλφειός, Alpheiós) is, in Greek mythology, the divine personification of the Alpheus River (the modern Alfeios) in the Peloponnese, and one of the river gods counted among the sons of Oceanus and Tethys. He is chiefly known for his pursuit of the nymph Arethusa and for his association with the Olympic Games and the cleansing of the Augean stables.

The pursuit of Arethusa

According to the best-known myth, told in Ovid's Metamorphoses, Alpheus fell in love with Arethusa, a huntress nymph in the retinue of Artemis, after glimpsing her bathing in his waters in Arcadia. He pursued her relentlessly across Greece; exhausted, Arethusa prayed to Artemis, who transformed her into a spring on the island of Ortygia in Sicily. Undeterred, Alpheus dissolved into his river form, flowed beneath the sea, and mingled his waters with her spring — a myth that explained the freshwater spring rising in Syracuse's harbour and underlay the poetic idea that the Greek river travelled unmixed under the Mediterranean.

Other myths

Heracles, in performing his fifth labour, diverted the waters of the Alpheus and the neighbouring Peneus through the stables of King Augeas of Elis, cleansing in a single day the filth accumulated over thirty years. The river was also sacred at Olympia, where its god received worship alongside Zeus; Pausanias records altars and traditions connecting Alpheus with the sanctuary and with the nymph Ortygia.

In some accounts Alpheus also loved or was loved by Artemis, who is said to have smeared her face with mud to escape him, an aetiological tale connected with her cult title Alpheioa at Letrini.
