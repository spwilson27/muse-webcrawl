Title insurance

Title insurance is a form of indemnity insurance predominantly found in the United States that insures against financial loss from defects in title to real property and from the invalidity or unenforceability of mortgage loans.

How it works

Before a sale closes, a title search examines public records for the property's chain of title. The insurer then issues a policy — typically an owner's policy and a lender's policy in the standard ALTA forms — covering risks such as forged documents, undisclosed heirs, errors in records, and liens that the search missed. Unlike most insurance, the single premium covers past events rather than future risks.

Why it exists

The industry arose in the 19th century because US land registration (a recording system rather than a title-registration/Torrens system in most states) does not guarantee ownership; the 1868 Pennsylvania case Watson v. Muirhead highlighted buyers' exposure and led to the first title insurance company in Philadelphia in 1876. Critics note high premiums relative to claims paid, since the bulk of cost goes to search and prevention.
