Noosphere

The noosphere (from Greek νόος "mind" and σφαῖρα "sphere") is the sphere of human thought and cognition, conceived as a stage in the development of the Earth following the geosphere (inanimate matter) and the biosphere (biological life). The concept was developed in the 1920s in the work of the geochemist Vladimir Vernadsky, the philosopher Édouard Le Roy, and the Jesuit palaeontologist and theologian Pierre Teilhard de Chardin, who met in Paris and popularised the term.

Development of the concept

For Vernadsky, the noosphere was the new state of the biosphere transformed by human scientific thought and technology — the idea that humanity had become a geological force. Teilhard de Chardin, in The Phenomenon of Man (written in the 1930s, published posthumously in 1955), gave the concept a teleological and spiritual cast: as human minds interconnect across the planet, the noosphere converges toward an "Omega Point" of maximum complexity and consciousness.

Modern usage

The term was revived in discussions of the global communications network: commentators have described the internet, the World Wide Web, and collective online knowledge projects as realisations or precursors of the noosphere, and Marshall McLuhan's "global village" is a related idea. The word also lends its name to projects such as Princeton's noosphere.com and has influenced ecological and systems thinking about planetary-scale human cognition and the Anthropocene.
