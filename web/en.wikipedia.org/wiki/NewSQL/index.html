NewSQL

NewSQL is a class of relational database management systems that seek to provide the scalability of NoSQL systems for online transaction processing (OLTP) workloads while maintaining the ACID guarantees of a traditional database system. The term was coined by analyst Matthew Aslett of the 451 Group in 2011.

Characteristics

NewSQL systems typically feature a distributed, shared-nothing architecture that scales horizontally across commodity servers, a SQL interface, and support for strong consistency through consensus protocols such as Paxos or Raft. They arose in response to the limitations of conventional single-node RDBMS products at web scale and the compromises of early NoSQL stores, which relaxed consistency and abandoned SQL.

Examples

Notable NewSQL or NewSQL-like systems include Google Spanner and its open-source descendant CockroachDB, VoltDB, NuoDB, TiDB, YugabyteDB, and SingleStore (formerly MemSQL). Research prototypes such as Calvin and H-Store influenced several commercial products. Adoption has grown for globally distributed applications requiring both transactional integrity and horizontal scalability.
