Patricia Churchland

Patricia Smith Churchland (born 16 July 1943) is a Canadian-American philosopher noted for her work in the philosophy of mind and neurophilosophy. She is Professor Emerita at the University of California, San Diego (UCSD), where she taught from 1984 until her retirement. Together with her husband, the philosopher Paul Churchland, she is a leading proponent of eliminative materialism, the view that common-sense "folk psychology" will eventually be replaced by a mature neuroscience.

Churchland helped found neurophilosophy — the study of mind grounded in neuroscience.

Career

Churchland studied at the University of British Columbia, the University of Pittsburgh, and Oxford, where she took a BPhil in philosophy. She taught at the University of Manitoba from 1969 to 1984 before moving to UCSD. She has also held an adjunct position at the Salk Institute for Biological Studies, working alongside neuroscientists such as Terry Sejnowski, with whom she co-authored The Computational Brain (1992).

Philosophy

Her landmark book Neurophilosophy (1986) argued that understanding the mind requires understanding the brain, and that philosophy of mind, psychology, and neuroscience should be pursued as a single continuum. In later work, including Brain-Wise (2002), Braintrust (2011), and Conscience (2019), she developed a naturalistic account of morality, arguing that moral values are rooted in brain systems for attachment and care — involving neurochemicals such as oxytocin — shaped by evolution and social learning. She received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1991.
