Cartan subalgebra

A Cartan subalgebra is a maximal nilpotent self-normalizing subalgebra.

In mathematics, a Cartan subalgebra of a Lie algebra is a nilpotent subalgebra that is its own normalizer: it is maximal with respect to the property that adjoining any further element that normalizes it would leave the subalgebra unchanged. The notion was introduced in the work of Élie Cartan and is central to the structure and classification of Lie algebras.

Properties

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, any two Cartan subalgebras of a finite-dimensional Lie algebra are conjugate under the automorphism group, so their common dimension is an invariant called the rank. In a semisimple Lie algebra, a Cartan subalgebra is abelian, consists of semisimple elements, and is a maximal toral subalgebra.

Role in root systems

Choosing a Cartan subalgebra h of a semisimple Lie algebra g allows g to be decomposed into simultaneous eigenspaces for the adjoint action of h. The nonzero eigenvalues are the roots, and the resulting root system encodes the algebra’s structure, leading to the classification by Dynkin diagrams.

Examples

In the algebra of traceless 2×2 matrices, the diagonal matrices form a Cartan subalgebra. More generally, in matrix Lie algebras, suitable sets of diagonalizable commuting matrices provide standard examples.
