Ordinary (liturgy)

The ordinary, in Catholic liturgy, refers to the part of the Mass or of the canonical hours that is reasonably constant without regard to the date on which the service is performed. It is contrasted with the proper, which is that part of these liturgies that varies according to the date, either representing an observance within the liturgical year, or of a particular saint or significant event.

The ordinary of the Mass

The texts of the Mass ordinary that are sung by the choir or congregation are the Kyrie eleison, the Gloria in excelsis Deo, the Credo (the Nicene Creed), the Sanctus (with the Benedictus), and the Agnus Dei. In musical settings of the Mass, from medieval chant through the great polyphonic masses of the Renaissance to later works such as Bach's Mass in B minor, it is precisely these five ordinary texts that form the standard cycle, since their words never change. Some settings also include the Ite missa est or its replacement Benedicamus Domino.

The ordinary of the Office

The term also applies to the Divine Office: the fixed framework of psalmody, hymns, and prayers at each hour remains the ordinary, while antiphons, readings, and collects for particular feasts constitute the proper. Liturgical books accordingly divide material into the Ordinary, the Proper of Seasons (Temporale), and the Proper of Saints (Sanctorale).

Related usage

"Ordinary" in canon law also denotes a bishop or equivalent authority holding ordinary jurisdiction, and "Ordinary Time" is the name of the long liturgical season outside Advent, Christmas, Lent, and Easter — a separate usage derived from "ordered" (numbered) weeks.
