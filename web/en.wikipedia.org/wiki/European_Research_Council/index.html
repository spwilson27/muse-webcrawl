The European Research Council (ERC) is a public body for funding of scientific and technological research conducted within the European Union (EU). Established by the European Commission in 2007, it was the first pan-European funding body designed to support investigator-driven frontier research across all fields, with scientific excellence as the sole criterion for selection.

History

The idea of a European-level research council modelled on national research councils and the United States National Science Foundation circulated among European scientists for years before it was realised in the EU's Seventh Framework Programme (FP7). The ERC was formally established by a Commission decision in February 2007, and its first calls for proposals were launched that year. Greek photochemist Fotis Kafatos served as its first president. The ERC proved immediately popular: its first Starting Grant call attracted more than 9,000 applications for roughly 300 grants.

Under the Horizon 2020 programme (2014–2020) the ERC received a budget of about €13.1 billion, and under Horizon Europe (2021–2027) its budget rose to more than €16 billion, around a quarter of the programme's total.

Mission and structure

The ERC's mission is to encourage the highest-quality research in Europe through competitive funding and to support investigator-driven frontier research across all fields, on the basis of scientific excellence. It is overseen by an independent Scientific Council, composed of eminent scientists and scholars, which sets the funding strategy and selection methods. Day-to-day operations are run by the European Research Council Executive Agency (ERCEA) in Brussels. Jean-Pierre Bourguignon and Mauro Ferrari are among its former presidents; Maria Leptin took office as president in November 2021.

Grant schemes

  • Starting Grants — for promising early-career researchers, typically 2–7 years after the PhD, of up to €1.5 million over five years.
  • Consolidator Grants — for researchers 7–12 years after the PhD who are consolidating their independence, of up to €2 million.
  • Advanced Grants — for established research leaders with a track record of significant achievements, of up to €2.5 million.
  • Synergy Grants — for groups of two to four principal investigators tackling ambitious problems jointly, of up to €10 million over six years.
  • Proof of Concept Grants — small grants (around €150,000) enabling ERC grantees to explore the commercial or societal potential of their results.

Grants are portable between host institutions in EU member states and associated countries, and applications are evaluated by international peer-review panels organised by research domain. ERC grantees include numerous winners of major international prizes, among them Nobel Prize laureates whose prize-winning work was supported by ERC funding.

Criticism and debate

Discussion around the ERC has included the concentration of grants in a small number of wealthy western European countries and prestigious institutions, the administrative burden of applications given low success rates (often around 10–14 per cent), and the effects of the United Kingdom's departure from the EU on British researchers' eligibility, resolved for Horizon Europe when the UK associated to the programme.
