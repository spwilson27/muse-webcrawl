The Second Hellenic Republic (Greek: Βʹ Ελληνική Δημοκρατία) was the Greek state from the abolition of the monarchy in 1924 until its restoration in 1935. It followed the Greek defeat in the Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922), the abdication of King Constantine I, and the exile of King George II.

History

The National Assembly proclaimed the republic on 25 March 1924, and a referendum in April confirmed it. Alexandros Papanastasiou, the republic's chief architect, became prime minister, and Admiral Pavlos Kountouriotis served as its first president. A republican constitution was adopted in 1927, establishing a bicameral legislature with a Senate.

The republic's politics were unstable, with frequent changes of government, the dictatorship of Theodoros Pangalos (1925–1926), and military interventions. Its most stable period came under Eleftherios Venizelos (1928–1932), whose government pursued economic modernization, land reform, settlement of the 1.2 million refugees from Asia Minor, and rapprochement with Turkey, capped by the 1930 friendship treaty with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. The Great Depression and the default of 1932 undermined the government; political polarization between Venizelists and anti-Venizelists deepened after a failed Venizelist coup in March 1935.

Restoration

Following the suppression of the 1935 coup and the death of leading republicans in reprisals, Prime Minister Georgios Kondylis staged a plebiscite in November 1935 — widely regarded as rigged — that restored the monarchy, and King George II returned to Athens. The Second Republic was succeeded by the Kingdom of Greece and, soon after, the authoritarian 4th of August Regime of Ioannis Metaxas (1936).
