Tithe

Tithe
DefinitionOne tenth of income or produce given to a religious institution
EtymologyOld English teogoþa, "tenth"

A tithe (from Old English teogoþa, "tenth") is a one-tenth part of something, paid as a contribution to a religious organization or compulsory tax to government. Today, tithes are normally voluntary and paid in cash or cheques, whereas historically tithes were required and paid in kind, such as agricultural produce. Church tax in some European countries, connected with the Christian church, is a modern form of the tithe.

Ancient origins and Judaism

Tithing is mentioned in the Hebrew Bible: Abraham gives a tenth of the spoils to Melchizedek (Genesis 14), and Jacob vows a tenth of all he receives to God (Genesis 28). The Mosaic law prescribed several tithes (ma'aser): a tenth of produce and livestock given to the Levites, who in turn gave a tenth to the priests; a second tithe eaten by the owner in Jerusalem; and a poor tithe given in the third and sixth years of the seven-year cycle. These tithes supported the Temple service and the landless Levites and poor.

Christianity

Tithing was widespread in early and medieval Christianity, passing into canon law and then into the secular law of many European states, where it functioned as a major tax on agriculture, payable in crop sheaves (great tithes) or smaller produce (small tithes). Resistance to tithes contributed to peasant revolts, and reformers debated whether the practice was binding under the New Covenant. In England, tithes were largely commuted to money payments under the Tithe Act 1836. In the modern era, some churches — notably the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, which requires a tenth of members' income — and many evangelical congregations teach tithing as an obligation, while others treat it as voluntary giving.

Other traditions and modern church taxes

Analogous levies exist in other religions, such as zakat in Islam (a fixed proportion of savings) and dasvandh in Sikhism. In countries such as Germany, Austria, and the Scandinavian states, registered members of recognized churches pay a church tax collected by the state, a system descended from historical tithes.
