Charlton Comics was an American comic book publishing company that existed from 1945 to 1986, having begun in 1940 as a magazine publisher under different names. Based in Derby, Connecticut, Charlton published a wide variety of genres including crime, horror, romance, science fiction, war, Westerns, licensed titles, and superheroes. It was notable for its low-budget production model: it owned its own printing presses and paid among the lowest rates in the industry, but in return gave creators considerable freedom.

History

The company was founded by John Santangelo Sr. and attorney Ed Levy, who met while Santangelo was serving time in prison for copyright infringement related to song-lyric magazines. Their publishing ventures evolved through several imprints before adopting the name Charlton — reportedly after one of their sons. Charlton survived the mid-1950s collapse of many comics publishers in part because of its in-house printing operation, which cut costs dramatically.

Action Heroes

In the 1960s Charlton developed a line of superhero titles remembered as the "Action Heroes," largely under editor Dick Giordano. These included Captain Atom (co-created by Steve Ditko), Blue Beetle, The Question, Peacemaker, Nightshade, Judomaster, Peter Cannon, Thunderbolt, and Sarge Steel. After Charlton folded, DC Comics acquired the characters in 1983; they appeared prominently in Crisis on Infinite Earths (1985) and served as the templates for the characters of Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons's Watchmen (1986–87) after DC declined to let Moore use the originals directly.

Other publications

Charlton published licensed comics based on Hanna-Barbera cartoons (such as The Flintstones), the long-running horror anthologies Ghostly Tales and The Many Ghosts of Doctor Graves, and in the 1970s a notable line of black-and-white magazines. It was also a launching ground for talents including John Byrne, Denny O'Neil, and Mike Zeck. Declining sales led Charlton to cease publishing in 1986.
