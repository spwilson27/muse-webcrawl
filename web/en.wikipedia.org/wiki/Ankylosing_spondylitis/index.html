Ankylosing spondylitis

SpecialtyRheumatology
SymptomsChronic back pain and stiffness, improving with activity
OnsetUsually before age 45, often young adults
Risk factorsHLA-B27 gene variant, family history
TreatmentExercise, physiotherapy, NSAIDs, biologic drugs (TNF and IL-17 inhibitors)
[Image: X-ray showing a fused "bamboo spine" in advanced ankylosing spondylitis]

Ankylosing spondylitis (AS), now often classified within axial spondyloarthritis, is a chronic inflammatory arthritis that primarily affects the spine and the sacroiliac joints. Over time inflammation can cause vertebrae to fuse, producing a rigid spine.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features are back pain and stiffness that are worse in the morning and after rest but improve with exercise. Other manifestations include inflammation where tendons attach to bone (enthesitis), eye inflammation (uveitis), and associations with psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease.

Cause

The cause is not fully understood, but the genetic marker HLA-B27 is present in the large majority of people with the condition, though most carriers never develop it.

Management

There is no cure; regular exercise and physiotherapy are central, with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and, if needed, biologic therapies to control inflammation and slow progression.
