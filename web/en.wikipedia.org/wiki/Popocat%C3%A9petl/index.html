Popocatépetl


Popocatépetl erupting

Popocatépetl (Spanish pronunciation: [popokaˈtepetl]; Nahuatl: Popōcatepētl, "Smoking Hill") is an active stratovolcano in central Mexico, lying on the borders of the states of Puebla, Morelos, and Mexico, about 70 km southeast of Mexico City. At 5,426 metres (some surveys give 5,393 m) it is the second-highest peak in Mexico, after Pico de Orizaba. Its neighbour to the north is the dormant volcano Iztaccíhuatl; the two are the subject of the Aztec legend of Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl.

Activity

Popocatépetl has had more than 15 major eruptions since the arrival of the Spanish in 1519. After decades of dormancy, the volcano reawakened in 1994 and has been erupting intermittently ever since, with frequent ash emissions, incandescent ejecta, and occasional larger explosions that prompt alert-level changes and ashfall in Puebla and Mexico City. A permanent monitoring programme by CENAPRED tracks seismicity, deformation, and gas emissions, and an exclusion zone is maintained around the crater.

Geology and setting

The volcano is part of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Iztaccíhuatl–Popocatépetl National Park. Its symmetrical cone is capped by a steep-walled crater about 600 metres wide. Glaciers on the summit largely disappeared around 2001 because of increased activity.
