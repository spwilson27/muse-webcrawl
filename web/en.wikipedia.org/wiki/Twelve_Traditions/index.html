Twelve Traditions

The Twelve Traditions of Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) are a set of guiding principles for the governance and public relations of the fellowship, companion to the Twelve Steps of personal recovery. They were first published in 1946 in the AA magazine Grapevine by co-founder Bill Wilson and formally adopted at AA's first international convention in Cleveland in 1950.

AuthorBill Wilson
First published1946
Adopted1950, Cleveland

Principles

The Traditions emphasise common welfare first, a single ultimate authority (a loving God as expressed in group conscience), membership open to anyone with a desire to stop drinking, group autonomy, a single primary purpose (carrying the message to the alcoholic who still suffers), no endorsement of outside enterprises, self-support through members' contributions, remaining non-professional, no organisation beyond service boards, no opinion on outside issues, public relations based on attraction rather than promotion, and anonymity as the spiritual foundation.

Influence

Adapted by Narcotics Anonymous, Al-Anon, Gamblers Anonymous, Overeaters Anonymous and many other twelve-step fellowships, the Traditions are credited with keeping AA decentralised, non-commercial, and focused despite rapid growth.
