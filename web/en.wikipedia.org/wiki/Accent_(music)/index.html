Accent (music)

In music, an accent is an emphasis, stress, or stronger attack placed on a particular note or set of notes, or chord, either as a result of its context or specifically indicated by an accent mark. Accents contribute greatly to the expressive character of a performance.

Types of accent

A metric accent arises from the position of a note within the metre — the downbeat of a bar is naturally accented. A rhythmic accent falls on an off-beat or a long note in an unexpected place. A dynamic accent is produced by playing a note louder, and an agogic accent by holding it slightly longer. Syncopation works by placing accents where the listener does not expect them.

Notation

The most common accent mark is the horizontal wedge (>). Stronger degrees of emphasis are shown by the vertical wedge of the marcato mark, while the staccato dot and tenuto line indicate articulation rather than accent proper. Combinations of these signs give performers detailed instructions about attack and duration.
