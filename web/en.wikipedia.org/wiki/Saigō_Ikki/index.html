Saigō Ikki

Saigō Ikki (西郷一季, 1914–1944) was a Japanese judoka recognized as one of the standout practitioners of his generation in the early Shōwa era. A graduate of the Kodokan, he was noted for his powerful tachi-waza and his rivalry with other leading judoka of the 1930s, including Kimura Masahiko.

Career

Saigō rose through the Kodokan's ranks during the 1930s, when Japanese judo was dominated by a small circle of exceptionally strong competitors. He distinguished himself in the All-Japan Judo Championships and in the celebrated kōhaku (red-and-white) matches, and was regarded as one of the few men capable of contesting Masahiko Kimura, who later recalled the difficulty of facing him. His career was cut short by the Pacific War; he died in 1944 while in military service.

Legacy

Although less widely known outside Japan than some contemporaries, Saigō is remembered in judo histories as part of the remarkable pre-war generation whose members pushed the technical and competitive standards of the art, and his bouts are cited in Kimura's memoir My Judo.
