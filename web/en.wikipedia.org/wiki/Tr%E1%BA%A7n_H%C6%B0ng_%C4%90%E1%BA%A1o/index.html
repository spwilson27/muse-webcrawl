Trần Hưng Đạo

Trần Hưng Đạo (c. 1228–1300), born Prince Trần Quốc Tuấn, was a Vietnamese prince and military commander of Đại Việt during the Trần dynasty. He is celebrated as one of the greatest military commanders in Vietnamese history for leading Đại Việt to victory against three Mongol invasions launched by the Yuan dynasty of Kublai Khan between 1258 and 1288.

Mongol invasions

Trần Hưng Đạo commanded Vietnamese forces during the second (1285) and third (1287–88) Mongol invasions. His strategy combined guerrilla warfare, scorched-earth retreats — including the evacuation of the capital Thăng Long — and strikes against Mongol supply lines, exploiting the invaders' vulnerability to tropical disease and their dependence on supply fleets from China.

His most famous victory came at the Battle of Bạch Đằng in 1288, where he destroyed the Yuan supply fleet by planting iron-tipped stakes beneath the river's surface, impaling the ships as the tide fell — a tactic echoing Ngô Quyền's victory on the same river in 938.

Writings and legacy

He authored influential military texts, including the Proclamation to the Officers (Hịch tướng sĩ), a celebrated call to patriotic duty still studied in Vietnam. After his death he was deified; temples dedicated to him stand throughout Vietnam, and he is venerated as a saint (Đức Thánh Trần) in Vietnamese folk religion.
