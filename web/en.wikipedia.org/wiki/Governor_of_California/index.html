The governor of California is the head of government of the U.S. state of California. The governor is the commander-in-chief of the California National Guard and the California State Guard, and is responsible for preparing the state budget, signing or vetoing bills passed by the California State Legislature, and making appointments to state agencies, boards and the judiciary.

Election and term

Governors are elected by popular vote to four-year terms and, under term limits adopted in 1990, may serve no more than two terms. A governor must be a United States citizen and a registered voter in California. Uniquely among American governors, California's may be removed by recall election: voters have twice forced recall votes, removing Gray Davis in 2003 (replaced by Arnold Schwarzenegger), while Gavin Newsom survived a recall attempt in 2021.

Powers

The governor signs or vetoes legislation, including a line-item veto over appropriations; grants pardons, reprieves and commutations; calls special sessions of the Legislature; and delivers the annual State of the State address. The governor also serves as an ex officio regent of the University of California and a trustee of the California State University.

Notable governors

Prominent holders of the office include Hiram Johnson, the Progressive reformer; Earl Warren, later Chief Justice of the United States; Ronald Reagan, later President of the United States; and Jerry Brown, the state's longest-serving governor, who held the office in 1975–1983 and again in 2011–2019.
