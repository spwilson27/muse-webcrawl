Serpentine belt

A serpentine belt (also called a multi-vee, poly-v, or multi-rib belt) is a single, continuous drive belt used in modern automotive engines to drive multiple peripheral devices: the alternator, power steering pump, water pump (in some layouts), air conditioning compressor and sometimes other accessories.

Design

The belt is flat with multiple longitudinal ribs on its inner surface that mate with grooves in the pulleys. Its long path winds — or "serpentines" — between pulleys, driven by the crankshaft pulley, so that one belt can drive all accessories. An automatic spring-loaded tensioner pulley keeps the belt under the correct tension, and smooth idler pulleys route the belt, sometimes contacting its back (flat) side to increase wrap on grooved pulleys.

Advantages

Replacing the older arrangement of several individual V-belts with one serpentine belt allows a more compact engine accessory drive, lower cost, easier installation and better efficiency; the ribbed design can transmit more power in less space and is less prone to slipping or being thrown off by pulley misalignment.

Maintenance

Serpentine belts are commonly made from EPDM rubber, which tends to wear by gradually losing material rather than cracking, so condition is judged by rib-depth measurement as well as visible damage. Failure of the belt immediately disables all driven accessories, including the alternator and power steering, and if the water pump is belt-driven it can quickly cause engine overheating.
