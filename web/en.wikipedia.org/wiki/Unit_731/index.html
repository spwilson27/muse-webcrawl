Unit 731 was a covert biological and chemical warfare research and development unit of the Imperial Japanese Army that engaged in lethal human experimentation and biological warfare during the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) and World War II. Based at Pingfang, near Harbin in Japanese-occupied Manchuria, it was commanded by Lieutenant General Shirō Ishii and officially disguised as an epidemic prevention and water purification unit.

Activities

Prisoners — mostly Chinese civilians and prisoners of war, referred to by staff as "logs" (maruta) — were subjected to vivisection without anaesthesia, deliberate infection with plague, cholera, anthrax and other pathogens, weapons tests, frostbite experiments and other lethal procedures. None survived the experiments. The unit also conducted field attacks, dispersing plague-infected fleas and contaminating wells and food supplies in Chinese cities and farmland, causing large-scale epidemics.

Aftermath

As Soviet forces invaded Manchuria in August 1945, the unit destroyed its facilities and records and killed remaining prisoners. Unlike many Japanese war criminals, the unit's senior personnel were not prosecuted at the Tokyo Trials: United States authorities granted them immunity in exchange for their experimental data. Twelve members of related units were tried by the Soviet Union at Khabarovsk in 1949. Japan's acknowledgement of the unit's existence came only decades later, and the episode remains a source of tension in Japan–China relations. The Pingfang site is preserved as a museum.
