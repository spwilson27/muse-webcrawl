Starhawk (born Miriam Simos, June 17, 1951) is an American feminist and author. She is known as a theorist of feminist neopaganism and ecofeminism, and as a co-founder of the Reclaiming tradition of modern witchcraft. Her best-known book, The Spiral Dance: A Rebirth of the Ancient Religion of the Great Goddess (1979), became a foundational text of the Goddess movement and contemporary pagan practice in North America.

Starhawk has been active for decades in movements for peace, environmental protection, and social justice, and is a prominent trainer in nonviolent direct action. She also teaches permaculture and earth-based design and has written both nonfiction and fiction, including the utopian novel The Fifth Sacred Thing (1993).

Early life and education

Miriam Simos was born in Saint Paul, Minnesota, to Jack and Bertha Simos. Her father, a social worker, died when she was five; her mother was a professor of social work at the University of California, Los Angeles. Simos studied at UCLA, where she took a degree in fine arts and later a master's degree in psychology with a concentration in feminist therapy. While at UCLA she won a Samuel Goldwyn writing award for a novel manuscript. Drawn to the resurgent interest in goddess religion and witchcraft in the 1970s feminist movement, she studied with the Hungarian witchcraft teacher Zsuzsanna Budapest and took the craft name Starhawk.

The Spiral Dance and Reclaiming

Published in 1979, The Spiral Dance drew on Simos's work with Budapest's Dianic tradition and on the Feri tradition of Victor and Cora Anderson, combining ritual practice, goddess theology, feminism, and psychology. The book has remained in print through multiple anniversary editions and is credited with bringing the Goddess movement to a wide audience.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s Starhawk helped found the Reclaiming Collective in San Francisco, which developed into the Reclaiming tradition of witchcraft: an egalitarian, activist-oriented form of neopagan practice that links ritual with political engagement. Reclaiming communities became known for public rituals, including the annual Spiral Dance ritual in San Francisco held around Samhain, and for training in magic, ritual, and direct action.

Activism

Starhawk has participated in nonviolent direct actions against nuclear power and weapons, logging of old-growth forests, and corporate-led globalization. She was among the activists who organized around the 1999 protests against the World Trade Organization in Seattle, where Reclaiming-affiliated "pagan clusters" joined the affinity-group blockades. She has described her activism as an expression of earth-based spirituality and has written extensively on consensus decision-making, group facilitation, and movement strategy, notably in The Empowerment Manual (2011).

Earth Activist Training

Combining permaculture design with her spiritual and activist work, Starhawk co-founded Earth Activist Training, which offers courses in regenerative land design and social permaculture. She lives much of the time in western Sonoma County, California, where she manages land using permaculture principles. She has also written on food politics, including work connected with opposition to genetically modified crops and support for seed saving.
