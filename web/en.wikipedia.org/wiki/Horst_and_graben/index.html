Horst and graben

Horst and graben
Field: Structural geology
Cause: Extensional tectonics, normal faulting
Examples: Basin and Range Province, Rhine Graben, Death Valley

In geology, a horst is a raised block of the Earth's crust bounded by parallel normal faults, and a graben is the down-dropped block between two horsts. The words are German for "heap/mound" and "ditch/trench" respectively. Together, horst and graben structures form in regions of extensional tectonics where the crust is being pulled apart.

Formation

As crust stretches, blocks slip along normal faults. The block that drops relative to its neighbours forms a graben — often a rift valley with a flat floor — while the relatively uplifted block is a horst. Grabens are frequently bounded by horsts on both sides, though one side may instead grade into a half-graben.

Examples

The Basin and Range Province of the western United States is a vast horst-and-graben landscape; Death Valley is a graben flanked by horst ranges. In Europe, the Upper Rhine Graben lies between the Vosges and Black Forest horsts. The East African Rift contains prominent graben valleys hosting lakes and early hominin fossils.
