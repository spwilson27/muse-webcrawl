Poplar Forest

Location: Bedford County, Virginia, United States

Designed by: Thomas Jefferson

Built: 1806–1826

Poplar Forest is a plantation and retreat house in Bedford County, Virginia, designed and built by Thomas Jefferson. Begun in 1806, while Jefferson was President of the United States, it served as his private retreat from the public world of Monticello and is regarded as a masterpiece of his architectural work.

Design

The house is a striking exercise in geometry: a near-perfect octagon of brick, with a central square dining room lit by a large skylight. Jefferson drew on the ideas of Andrea Palladio and on French neoclassical models. The main floor contains four elongated octagonal chambers in the corners, and the house originally stood amid ornamental plantings arranged to Jefferson's designs, with two earthen mounds flanking the house.

History

Jefferson inherited the Poplar Forest plantation — some 4,800 acres worked by enslaved labourers — through his wife Martha Wayles Skelton from her father's estate. He visited regularly from 1809, increasingly so after his retirement, staying weeks at a time. In 1823 his granddaughter Ellen Randolph married Joseph Coolidge there; Jefferson gave the property to his grandson Francis Eppes in 1826, the year of his death. The house passed out of the family, suffered a fire in 1845 that destroyed its interior, and served for generations as a farmhouse.

Restoration

The nonprofit Corporation for Jefferson's Poplar Forest bought the property in 1984 and has conducted an award-winning restoration, using architectural archaeology to reconstruct Jefferson's design. Poplar Forest is a National Historic Landmark and is open to the public, with interpretation that includes the lives of the enslaved community whose labour sustained the plantation.
