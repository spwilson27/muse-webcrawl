An Comunn Gàidhealach (pronounced [əŋ ˈkʰomən̪ˠ ˈkɛːt̪əɫ̪əx]; literally "The Gaelic Association") is a Scottish charitable organisation formed in Oban in 1891 to preserve and promote the Scottish Gaelic language and culture. Modelled in part on the Welsh Eisteddfod movement and supported by figures including Lord Archibald Campbell, it became the central body of the Gaelic revival in Scotland.

The Mòd

The organisation's best-known activity is the Mòd, a competitive festival of Gaelic music, song, poetry, drama, and sport first held in 1892 and now known as the Royal National Mòd (after receiving royal patronage), staged each October in a different Scottish town. Local and provincial mòds feed into the national event, whose top honours include the Gold Medal and the Traditional Gold Medal for solo singing. An Comunn also supports Gaelic education, publishing, and youth work, and has branches and affiliated societies across Scotland and abroad.

History

Through the 20th century An Comunn campaigned — sometimes controversially, as it balanced cultural promotion with political neutrality — for Gaelic teaching in schools, broadcasting, and official recognition, goals advanced by the Gaelic Language (Scotland) Act 2005. Its publishing arm issued Gaelic books and the periodical literature that sustained the language's literary culture.
