Inishmaan

Inishmaan (Irish: Inis Meáin, "middle island") is the middle of the three Aran Islands in Galway Bay, County Galway, Ireland, lying between Inis Mór and Inisheer. It is the least visited and most traditional of the islands, with a population of around 160, and retains a strong Irish-speaking Gaeltacht culture.

History and sights

The island's monuments include the oval stone fort of Dún Chonchúir and Dún Fearbhaí, as well as Cill Cheannanach church. Inishmaan is closely associated with the playwright John Millington Synge, who spent parts of five summers there (1898–1902) learning Irish and gathering material; his cottage, Teach Synge, is preserved as a museum, and his experiences informed The Aran Islands and his plays. The island gives its name to Martin McDonagh's play The Cripple of Inishmaan (1996).

Economy

Fishing, small-scale farming and limited tourism sustain the community; the knitwear company Inis Meáin, founded on the island in the 1970s, exports luxury knitwear internationally. Ferries link the island to Rossaveal and the neighbouring islands.
