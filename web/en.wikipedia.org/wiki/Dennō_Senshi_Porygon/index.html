Dennō Senshi Porygon

"Dennō Senshi Porygon" (Japanese: でんのうせんしポリゴン, translated as "Cyber Soldier Porygon" or "Electric Soldier Porygon") is the 38th episode of the Pokémon anime's first season. It aired only once in Japan on December 16, 1997, on TV Tokyo, and became infamous after flashing red and blue strobe effects in the episode caused photosensitive epileptic seizures in a large number of Japanese viewers.

Plot

In the episode, Ash Ketchum and his friends learn that the Pokémon transfer system at the Pokémon Center is malfunctioning. They enter cyberspace with the help of Professor Akihabara to confront Team Rocket, who have stolen Pokémon using the virtual Pokémon Porygon. During a climactic scene, Pikachu uses a Thunderbolt attack on a group of "vaccine" missiles, producing a rapid strobe of flashing red and blue lights lasting several seconds.

Incident and aftermath

Approximately 685 viewers across Japan were taken to hospitals by ambulance after suffering symptoms including seizures, vomiting, blurred vision, and eye irritation. The incident, widely covered by the Japanese press, led to the episode being banned; it has never been rebroadcast or released on home media in any country, and the Pokémon anime went on a four-month hiatus. The event prompted broadcasters to adopt guidelines on flashing imagery (later known in the industry alongside the Harding test) and is referenced in guidelines for photosensitive epilepsy triggers in television and video games.

Legacy

The incident was parodied in episodes of The Simpsons and South Park. Porygon and its evolved forms were largely excluded from the anime afterward despite the flashing effect having been caused by Pikachu's attack rather than Porygon itself.
