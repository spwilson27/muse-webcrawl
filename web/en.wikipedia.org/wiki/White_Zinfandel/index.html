White Zinfandel

White Zinfandel is a type of rosé wine made from the red Zinfandel grape in California. Typically slightly sweet, pink in colour and low in alcohol, it is one of the most popular wine styles in the United States.

Origin

White Zinfandel was popularised in the 1970s by Sutter Home Winery in the Napa Valley. Winemaker Bob Trinchero, experimenting with a stuck fermentation of a dry rosé made from Zinfandel in 1975, found the resulting sweet, pink wine sold far better than the dry version. The style took off in the 1980s and at its peak accounted for a large share of all wine sold in the United States.

Production and character

The wine is made by crushing red Zinfandel grapes and giving the juice only brief contact with the skins — or by fermenting the free-run juice separately — so that little colour or tannin is extracted. Arresting the fermentation early leaves residual sugar, producing a light, fruity, off-dry wine with flavours of strawberry, watermelon and citrus.

Significance

Although often dismissed by wine connoisseurs, White Zinfandel is credited with saving California's old Zinfandel vines from being grubbed up in favour of more fashionable varieties: growers had a profitable market for the grapes, so the old vineyards survived to produce the serious red Zinfandels prized today.
