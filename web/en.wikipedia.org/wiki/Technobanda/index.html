Technobanda

Other namesTecnobanda
Stylistic originsBanda, grupero, cumbia, techno, disco
Cultural originsLate 1980s, Sinaloa, Mexico
Typical instrumentsElectric keyboards, drum machines, synthesisers, brass, electric bass, percussion

Technobanda (Spanish: tecnobanda) is a subgenre of regional Mexican music that emerged at the end of the 1980s, principally in the Mexican state of Sinaloa. It fuses the repertoire and style of traditional banda — ranchera, corrido, cumbia, and bolero songs — with electronic instruments, replacing parts of the traditional brass band with synthesisers, electric keyboards, drum machines, and electric bass. The result is a danceable sound associated with the quebradita dance craze of the early 1990s.

History

Traditional banda ensembles of a dozen or more brass and woodwind players were expensive to hire, which encouraged smaller groups to imitate the banda sound using electronic instruments. Bands from Sinaloa and the surrounding region began in the late 1980s to perform banda arrangements with synthesisers playing the parts of trumpets and clarinets, backed by drum machines and electric bass. The style spread rapidly through Mexico and the Mexican-American Southwest, closely tied to the quebradita, an acrobatic partner dance performed to fast banda and technobanda rhythms.

Notable technobanda acts include Banda Machos, Banda Maguey, Banda El Recodo de Cruz Lizárraga (which moved between traditional and techno styles), Los Bukis' offshoot projects, and groups such as Banda Vallarta Show and Mi Banda El Mexicano, whose hit "No bailes de caballito" became emblematic of the era. The movement peaked commercially in the early-to-mid 1990s and then waned as traditional banda and later banda hybrids regained dominance.

Characteristics

Technobanda preserves the song forms and much of the repertoire of banda sinaloense — polkas, rancheras, cumbias, and romantic ballads — but substitutes electronic timbres for acoustic ones. Synthesisers often carry the melodic lines, drum machines or electronic kits provide a steady dance beat, and vocals remain prominent. Performances frequently feature choreographed dancing and colourful matching stage outfits.
