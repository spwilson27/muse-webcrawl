City-state

A city-state is an independent sovereign city which serves as the centre of political, economic and cultural life over its contiguous territory. City-states were especially prominent in the ancient world — the Sumerian cities of Mesopotamia, the Greek poleis such as Athens and Sparta, and the Phoenician trading cities — and again in medieval Italy and Germany.

Historical examples

The earliest city-states arose in Sumer around the 4th millennium BC (Uruk, Ur, Lagash). The Greek polis combined the city with its agricultural hinterland and a citizen body; there were over a thousand of them. Medieval and Renaissance examples include Venice, Genoa, Florence and the free imperial cities of the Holy Roman Empire, as well as the merchant cities of the Hanseatic League.

Modern city-states

The most prominent surviving sovereign city-states are Singapore, Monaco and Vatican City. Entities sometimes described as city-states or quasi-city-states include Hong Kong and Dubai; the German states of Berlin, Hamburg and Bremen are city-states within a federation.

Characteristics

City-states tend to rely on trade rather than agriculture, to develop strong naval power where coastal, and to be vulnerable to absorption by larger territorial states — the fate of most historical examples.
