Panj Pyare

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Panj Pyare (Punjabi: ਪੰਜ ਪਿਆਰੇ, "the five beloved ones") is the name given to the first five Sikhs initiated into the Khalsa by Guru Gobind Singh at Anandpur Sahib on Vaisakhi, conventionally dated 1699, in a ceremony that founded the Khalsa Panth.

The first five

According to Sikh tradition, before a vast gathering Guru Gobind Singh drew his sword and asked for volunteers willing to give their heads. Five men stepped forward, one after another: Bhai Daya Singh (a Khatri from Lahore), Bhai Dharam Singh (a Jat from Hastinapur), Bhai Himmat Singh (a water-carrier from Jagannath Puri), Bhai Mohkam Singh (a calico-printer from Dwarka), and Bhai Sahib Singh (a barber from Bidar). Drawn from different regions and castes, they embodied the Khalsa ideal of equality. The Guru revealed them alive and splendidly dressed, administered to them amrit — sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword (khanda) — and gave them the surname Singh. He then knelt and received initiation from them in turn, making the Guru the disciple of his own disciples.

Role today

In modern Sikhism, any five initiated (amritdhari) Sikhs may constitute a Panj Pyare to conduct the Amrit Sanchar initiation ceremony, lead religious processions (nagar kirtans), and make collective decisions on religious matters in the presence of the Guru Granth Sahib. At major historical gurdwaras — above all the Akal Takht — formally constituted Panj Pyare hold particular authority. Their traditional colours of saffron and blue and their carrying of swords in processions recall the founding five.
