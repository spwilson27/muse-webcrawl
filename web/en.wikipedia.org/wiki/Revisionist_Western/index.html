Revisionist Western

The revisionist Western, also called the anti-Western or post-Western, is a subgenre of the Western film that subverts the traditional values, mythology, and conventions of the classical Western. Revisionist Westerns emerged prominently from the late 1960s onward, reflecting the cultural upheavals of the Vietnam War era and a more critical view of American frontier history.

Characteristics

Unlike the moral clarity of classical Westerns such as those of John Ford and John Wayne, revisionist films feature morally ambiguous protagonists, sympathetic portrayals of Native Americans, explicit violence, and a cynical view of westward expansion and the "Manifest Destiny" myth. Heroes are often ageing, compromised, or indistinguishable from villains.

Notable examples

Key revisionist Westerns include Sam Peckinpah's The Wild Bunch (1969), Robert Altman's McCabe & Mrs. Miller (1971), Sergio Leone's spaghetti Westerns, and Clint Eastwood's Unforgiven (1992), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture. Later examples include Dead Man (1995), The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford (2007), and the video game Red Dead Redemption 2, with related works sometimes termed "neo-Westerns".
