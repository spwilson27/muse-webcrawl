Weyl character formula

In mathematics, the Weyl character formula, introduced by Hermann Weyl, gives a closed expression for the character of an irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie algebra or compact Lie group in terms of its highest weight.

Statement

For an irreducible finite-dimensional representation with highest weight λ, the character is expressed as a quotient: the numerator is an alternating sum over the Weyl group of exponentials of the weights w(λ + ρ), and the denominator is the corresponding sum for λ = 0, where ρ is the weight equal to half the sum of the positive roots. The denominator can also be written as a product over positive roots, giving the Weyl denominator formula.

Consequences

Setting the group element to the identity yields the Weyl dimension formula, a product formula for the dimension of the representation. The character formula is a cornerstone of representation theory and has analogues for Kac–Moody algebras, where it is known as the Weyl–Kac character formula.
