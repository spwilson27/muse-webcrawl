Antwerp Province (Dutch: Provincie Antwerpen) is the northernmost province both of the Flemish Region of Belgium and of Belgium as a whole. It borders the Netherlands to the north and the Belgian provinces of Limburg, Flemish Brabant, and East Flanders. Its capital is the city of Antwerp, home to the Port of Antwerp-Bruges, one of Europe's largest seaports. With roughly 1.9 million inhabitants it is the most populous of Belgium's ten provinces.

Geography and subdivisions

The province is divided into three arrondissements — Antwerp, Mechelen, and Turnhout — comprising 69 municipalities. The south is crossed by the river Rupel and the Nete; the north-east, known as the Kempen (Campine), is a sandy, historically poor region of heath and pine forest that industrialised in the 20th century. Important towns besides Antwerp include Mechelen, Turnhout, Geel, Mol, Herentals, and Lier.

Economy

The economy is dominated by the port and its associated petrochemical cluster, one of the largest in the world, together with the Antwerp diamond trade, logistics, and the pharmaceutical and chemical plants around Geel and the Campine. During the Second World War the province was heavily contested, notably in 1944 when Antwerp's liberation left the port initially unusable because the Scheldt estuary remained in German hands until the Battle of the Scheldt.
