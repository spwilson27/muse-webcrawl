The Kumano Kodō (熊野古道) is a series of ancient pilgrimage routes that crisscross the Kii Peninsula, the largest peninsula of Honshū in Japan. These mountain trails lead to the Kumano Sanzan, the three grand shrines of Kumano: Hongū Taisha, Hayatama Taisha, and Nachi Taisha.

History

For more than a thousand years, people from all levels of society — retired emperors, aristocrats, samurai, and commoners — walked these routes to worship at Kumano, a region revered as a pure land on earth in both Shinto and Buddhist belief. Pilgrims were guided by yamabushi mountain ascetics, and the routes were lined with small ōji shrines offering rest and protection. The pilgrimage became so popular it was called the "pilgrimage of ants" for the streams of people on the trails.

Routes

The principal routes include the Nakahechi (from Tanabe through the mountains to Hongū), the Kohechi (linking Mount Kōya to Kumano), the Ōhechi (coastal route), the Iseji (from the Ise Grand Shrine), and the Ōmine Okugakemichi. In 2004 the Kumano Kodō was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, one of only two pilgrimage route networks so honoured worldwide, alongside the Camino de Santiago.
