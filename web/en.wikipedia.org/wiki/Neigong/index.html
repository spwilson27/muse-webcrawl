Neigong

Neigong
Chinese內功 (nèigōng), "internal skill/work"
TraditionsTaoism, Chinese martial arts (neijia)
ContrastWaigong (external exercise)

Neigong (Chinese: 內功; pinyin: nèigōng), also spelled nei kung or nei gung, is a system of coordinated body-posture and movement, breathing, and meditation practices used in Taoism and in the "internal" Chinese martial arts (neijia) such as tai chi, baguazhang and xingyiquan. It aims to develop internal strength and health by cultivating and circulating qi through the body's meridians and dantian centres, rather than relying on muscular force.

Practice

Typical neigong training includes standing meditation (zhan zhuang), slow coordinated movements with regulated breathing, silk-reeling exercises, bone-marrow washing sets, and seated meditation. In martial contexts it is said to produce whole-body power (jin) and resilience; in Taoist contexts it is preparatory to internal alchemy (neidan). Neigong is distinguished from waigong (external exercises such as weight training and calisthenics) and overlaps considerably with qigong, though it is often taught as a deeper, lineage-based curriculum.

History

The term and its methods developed within Taoist longevity practices and were adopted by the internal martial arts schools that emerged in the Ming and Qing dynasties; the modern popularization of neigong parallels that of qigong in the 20th century.
