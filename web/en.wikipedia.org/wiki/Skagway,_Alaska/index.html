Skagway, Alaska

Skagway is a municipality and first-class borough in Alaska on the Alaska Panhandle. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 1,240. It is situated at the northern end of the Lynn Canal, at the head of the Inside Passage, and is famous as a gateway to the Klondike during the gold rush of the late 1890s.

The name Skagway is derived from a Tlingit word, Shgagwèi, referring to the rough seas and strong north winds characteristic of the area.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Tlingit people. In 1896–97, following the discovery of gold in the Klondike region of Canada's Yukon Territory, Skagway boomed almost overnight as prospectors poured through on their way to the goldfields via the White Pass and Chilkoot trails. Captain William Moore, who had earlier surveyed the area, founded the townsite and built a sawmill and wharf.

At its peak in 1898, Skagway was the largest city in Alaska, with a population estimated between 8,000 and 10,000. It was notorious for lawlessness, dominated for a time by the confidence man Jefferson "Soapy" Smith, who was killed in a famous shootout on the Juneau wharf in July 1898. The completion of the White Pass and Yukon Route railway in 1900, running from Skagway over White Pass to Whitehorse in Yukon, secured the town's survival after the gold rush faded.

Geography and climate

Skagway lies in a narrow glacial valley at the head of Taiya Inlet, the northernmost arm of Lynn Canal. It is one of the driest places in the Alaska Panhandle owing to a rain shadow effect from the surrounding mountains, receiving far less precipitation than Juneau or Ketchikan. The Skagway River flows through the valley.

Economy and tourism

Today the economy is dominated by tourism. Skagway is a popular port of call for cruise ships travelling the Inside Passage, receiving over a million visitors in peak seasons. Much of the historic downtown is preserved as part of the Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park, administered jointly with units in Seattle. The White Pass and Yukon Route operates scenic narrow-gauge excursions over the pass during the summer. The port also handles ore and other cargo from Yukon.

Transportation

Skagway is connected by the Klondike Highway to the Alaska Highway via Whitehorse, making it one of only three Southeast Alaska communities accessible by road (with Haines and Hyder). It is served by the Alaska Marine Highway ferry system and by Skagway Airport.
