Link budget

A link budget is an accounting of all of the power gains and losses that a radio signal experiences between a transmitter and a receiver in a telecommunications system. By summing transmit power, antenna gains, path loss, and other losses in decibels, engineers predict the received power and hence whether the link will work reliably.

Components

A typical link budget includes transmitter output power, feeder cable losses, transmit antenna gain, free-space path loss (which grows with distance and frequency), atmospheric and rain attenuation, polarisation and pointing losses, receive antenna gain, and receiver sensitivity. The difference between the predicted received power and the sensitivity threshold is the link margin, which provides protection against fading and other impairments.

Applications

Link budgets are fundamental to the design of satellite communications, deep-space links, cellular networks, Wi-Fi installations, and optical fibre systems, where an analogous power budget is used. The Friis transmission equation provides the theoretical basis for the radio case.
