Grenfell Tower fire

On 14 June 2017, a high-rise fire broke out in the 24-storey Grenfell Tower block of flats in North Kensington, West London, England, at 00:54 BST and burned for 60 hours. Seventy-two people died, including two who later died in hospital, and more than 70 others were injured; 223 people escaped. It was the deadliest structural fire in the United Kingdom since the 1988 Piper Alpha oil-platform disaster, and the worst UK residential fire since the Second World War.

The fire

The fire was started by an electrical fault in a refrigerator on the fourth floor. Flames rapidly spread up the exterior of the building, largely due to the aluminium composite material (ACM) cladding with a polyethylene core installed during a 2015–2016 refurbishment, together with combustible insulation. The fire breached the building's compartmentation, filling the single staircase with smoke and making escape impossible for many residents, some of whom had been advised to "stay put" under the building's fire policy.

Causes and investigation

Inquiries found that the cladding system did not comply with building regulations and that the refurbishment had created a lethal combination of materials. Residents had raised fire safety concerns for years before the disaster, including through the Grenfell Action Group, which warned in 2016 that a serious fire was foreseeable. The London Fire Brigade's "stay put" advice was maintained for nearly two hours after the fire began.

Aftermath

A public inquiry chaired by Sir Martin Moore-Bick reported in two phases: Phase 1 (2019) examined the night of the fire; the Phase 2 report (September 2024) found decades of failures by governments, regulators, and the construction industry, describing "systematic dishonesty" among manufacturers of the products used. The disaster triggered a nationwide building safety crisis, with thousands of buildings found to have unsafe cladding, and led to the Building Safety Act 2022. The tower remained standing, covered, and the government announced in 2025 that it would be demolished over approximately two years.

Community response

The local community and volunteers organized extensive relief efforts in the aftermath. Monthly silent walks are held by survivors and bereaved families, and the slogan "Justice for Grenfell" became a rallying call. The ruins of the tower, lit green at night on anniversaries, serve as a memorial to the victims.
