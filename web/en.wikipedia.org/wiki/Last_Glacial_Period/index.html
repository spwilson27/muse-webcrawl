Last Glacial Period

The Last Glacial Period (LGP), popularly the last ice age, was the most recent period of widespread glaciation within the current ice age, lasting from about 115,000 to 11,700 years ago, ending with the start of the Holocene.

Climate and ice sheets

At the Last Glacial Maximum, the Laurentide and Cordilleran ice sheets covered much of North America — damming lakes such as Lake Agassiz — while the Fennoscandian sheet covered northern Europe and Siberia experienced cold, dry steppe. Global temperatures were roughly 4–7°C colder and sea levels about 120 metres lower, exposing land bridges including Beringia, by which humans reached the Americas.

End and effects

Deglaciation brought abrupt swings such as the Younger Dryas cold reversal (c. 12,900–11,700 BP). Megafauna including mammoths and woolly rhinoceroses became extinct near the period's end, and warming enabled the development of agriculture in the Holocene.
