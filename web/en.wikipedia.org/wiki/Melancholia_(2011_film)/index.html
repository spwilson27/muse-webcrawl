Melancholia is a 2011 science fiction drama film written and directed by Lars von Trier and starring Kirsten Dunst, Charlotte Gainsbourg, and Kiefer Sutherland. Its story revolves around two sisters during a wedding celebration and in the days after, as a rogue planet named Melancholia approaches Earth on a collision course. The film is the second entry in von Trier's unofficially titled "Depression Trilogy," following Antichrist (2009) and preceding Nymphomaniac (2013).

The film's overture, a slow-motion prologue of painterly tableaux, is set to the prelude of Wagner's Tristan und Isolde, which recurs throughout the score. The narrative is divided into two parts, "Justine" and "Claire," named for the two sisters.

Plot

Newlywed Justine, who suffers from severe depression, unravels during her lavish wedding reception at her sister Claire's estate. Later, as the planet Melancholia draws closer, roles reverse: the anxious Claire panics while Justine, calm and resigned, faces the end with tranquillity. The film closes with the collision of the two worlds.

Reception

The film premiered at the 2011 Cannes Film Festival, where Dunst won the award for Best Actress; the premiere was overshadowed by von Trier's controversial remarks at a press conference, after which the festival declared him persona non grata. Critics praised Dunst's performance, the cinematography, and the film's depiction of depression, and it has since been ranked among the finest films of the 2010s in several polls, including the BBC's 2016 list of the greatest films of the 21st century.
