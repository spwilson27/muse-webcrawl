Luxury vinyl tile

Luxury vinyl tile (LVT), and in plank form luxury vinyl plank (LVP), is a type of resilient flooring made from polyvinyl chloride (PVC) designed to imitate natural materials such as hardwood, stone, and ceramic tile using photographic printing and embossing. Since the 2010s it has become one of the fastest-growing categories in the flooring industry, used in both residential and commercial settings.

Construction

A typical LVT product is built in layers: a backing layer; a core of filled PVC (in rigid-core products, stone-plastic composite, SPC, or wood-plastic composite, WPC); a printed design layer reproducing the look of wood grain or stone; and a transparent wear layer, often with a UV-cured urethane finish, whose thickness (commonly 6–30 mil) determines durability.

Properties and installation

LVT is waterproof or highly water-resistant, relatively warm and quiet underfoot, and cheaper than the materials it imitates. Products are installed by glue-down, loose-lay, or interlocking "click" floating-floor methods, the latter popular for DIY renovation. Rigid-core click products can be laid over many existing floors with minimal subfloor preparation.

Criticism

As a PVC product, LVT raises environmental and health questions over plasticisers, indoor air emissions (largely mitigated in modern low-VOC products certified under standards such as FloorScore), and end-of-life recycling, which remains limited.
