Shares outstanding

Shares outstanding are all the shares of a corporation or financial asset that have been authorized, issued and purchased by investors and are held by them. They are distinguished from treasury shares, which are shares held by the corporation itself and carry no rights. Shares outstanding are a central input to market capitalization and earnings per share.

Basic and diluted figures

Companies report two measures. Basic shares outstanding counts the shares actually in investors' hands at a point in time. Diluted shares outstanding adds the effect of dilutive securities — stock options, warrants, restricted stock units and convertible bonds — computed under the treasury stock method. Over a reporting period, firms disclose a weighted-average share count to smooth changes caused by issuances and buybacks.

Relationship to market capitalization

Market capitalization equals the share price multiplied by shares outstanding. It differs from enterprise value, which also accounts for debt and cash. A company's "float" — the subset of outstanding shares freely available for public trading — is always less than or equal to the total outstanding.

Changes over time

The count rises when a company issues new stock (for example in a secondary offering, an acquisition paid in shares, or option exercises) and falls when it repurchases its own shares in a buyback or executes a reverse stock split. Stock splits increase the number of shares while reducing the price proportionally, leaving market capitalization unchanged.
