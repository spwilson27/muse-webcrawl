Kentucky Colonel

Kentucky Colonel is the highest title of honour bestowed by the Commonwealth of Kentucky. Commissions as Kentucky colonels are granted by the Governor to individuals for notable achievement, public service or contributions to the state and community.

History

The title traces to 1813, when Governor Isaac Shelby appointed Charles Stewart Todd as an aide-de-camp with the rank of colonel after the War of 1812. In the 19th century the colonelcy became largely honorary. Governor Ruby Laffoon commissioned hundreds in the 1930s and in 1936, with restaurateur Harland Sanders among the recipients — he later built the Kentucky Fried Chicken brand as "Colonel Sanders".

Honorable Order

The Honorable Order of Kentucky Colonels, founded in 1931, is a charitable organisation of commission holders supporting Kentucky causes. Colonels traditionally may be addressed as "Colonel" and are associated with customs such as the Kentucky Derby celebrations.
