Guerlain (French pronunciation: [ɡɛʁlɛ̃]) is a French perfume, cosmetics and skincare house, among the oldest in the world. Founded in Paris in 1828 by Pierre-François-Pascal Guerlain, it was run by the Guerlain family for five generations before its acquisition by the luxury group LVMH in 1994.

History

Pierre-François-Pascal Guerlain opened his first shop on the Rue de Rivoli, making custom fragrances for Parisian high society; he composed the Eau de Cologne Impériale for Empress Eugénie in 1853, presented in the famous bee bottle (flacon aux abeilles) that remains a house emblem. His son Aimé Guerlain created Jicky in 1889, often called the first modern perfume for its abstract construction and use of synthetics such as coumarin and vanillin. Jacques Guerlain, the third generation, composed enduring classics including L'Heure Bleue (1912), Mitsouko (1919), Vol de Nuit (1933) and Shalimar (1925), the archetypal oriental vanilla perfume. Jean-Paul Guerlain, the last family perfumer, created Vétiver and Habit Rouge before retiring in 2002.

Products and heritage

The house is known for the "Guerlinade", a signature accord of bergamot, rose, jasmine, iris, vanilla and tonka bean recurring across its creations, and for products such as Terracotta bronzing powder (1984) and the Orchidée Impériale and Abeille Royale skincare lines. Its flagship at 68 Avenue des Champs-Élysées, opened in 1914, is a Parisian landmark. Since 2008 the in-house perfumer has been Thierry Wasser, the first non-family perfumer in the role.
