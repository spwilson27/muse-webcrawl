The arts

The arts is a broad term encompassing the diverse range of human activities involving creative expression, storytelling and cultural participation. They are commonly divided into the visual arts (painting, drawing, sculpture, photography, architecture), the literary arts (poetry, fiction, drama), and the performing arts (music, theatre, dance, film), together with related forms such as the decorative and applied arts and newer media arts.

Overview

Art-making is among the oldest human behaviours: carved objects, cave paintings and musical instruments survive from the Palaeolithic. Across cultures the arts have served ritual, commemorative, political and commercial purposes as well as aesthetic ones. The modern Western concept of "fine art" crystallised in the 18th century, distinguishing painting, sculpture, architecture, music and poetry from crafts and popular entertainment — distinctions many contemporary artists and scholars have challenged.

Categories

Visual arts produce works appreciated primarily by sight. Literary arts work through written and spoken language. Performing arts are enacted before an audience and are often ephemeral. Culinary, media and digital arts are increasingly recognised categories. The arts are taught in schools and universities, supported by public funding bodies, museums, theatres and festivals, and collectively represent a major global economic sector. The phrase "the arts" is also used institutionally — in the names of funding councils, academies and qualifications — to denote the creative sector as a whole.
