Yekaterinburg

Yekaterinburg (Russian: Екатеринбург) is the fourth-largest city in Russia and the administrative centre of Sverdlovsk Oblast, located on the eastern side of the Ural Mountains at the boundary between Europe and Asia. Founded in 1723 as a fortified ironworks, it was named after Catherine I, wife of Peter the Great. Between 1924 and 1991 it was known as Sverdlovsk, after the Bolshevik leader Yakov Sverdlov.

History

The city was founded by Vasily Tatishchev and Georg Wilhelm de Gennin as a centre of metallurgy in the mineral-rich Urals. It gained grim fame as the place of execution of the last Russian Emperor, Nicholas II, and his family, who were shot by Bolsheviks in the Ipatiev House in July 1918; the Church on the Blood now stands on the site. During the Second World War, many factories and institutions were evacuated to Sverdlovsk from western Russia, making it a major industrial centre. Boris Yeltsin, later the first President of Russia, served as the region's party chief from 1976 to 1985.

Economy and transport

Yekaterinburg is a major industrial, financial and logistical hub on the Trans-Siberian Railway, with strengths in heavy machinery, metallurgy and defence production. It hosted matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup at the Central Stadium (Ekaterinburg Arena) and is served by Koltsovo International Airport. Landmarks include the Vysotsky skyscraper, the constructivist White Tower, and the Europe–Asia border obelisk outside the city.
