Hida Mountains


The Hida Mountains

The Hida Mountains (飛騨山脈, Hida Sanmyaku), or Northern Alps (北アルプス, Kita Arupusu), is a Japanese mountain range that stretches through the prefectures of Toyama, Nagano, and Gifu in central Honshu. Together with the Kiso Mountains (Central Alps) and the Akaishi Mountains (Southern Alps), they form the Japanese Alps.

Geography

The range runs roughly north–south for about 105 km. Its highest peaks include Mount Hotaka (3,190 m), Mount Yari (3,180 m), Mount Tate (3,015 m), Mount Tsurugi (2,999 m), and Mount Shirouma (2,932 m). The mountains are characterized by deep V-shaped valleys carved by rivers such as the Kurobe, whose gorge is crossed by the Kurobe Dam, one of Japan's tallest dams.

Tourism and access

Much of the range lies within Chūbu-Sangaku National Park. Popular destinations include Kamikōchi, the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route, and the historic Nakasendō-side valleys. The English missionary and mountaineer Walter Weston helped popularize the range in the West in the late 19th century and is commemorated as the "father of Japanese mountaineering."
