Cucurbita

Cucurbita is a genus of herbaceous fruits in the gourd family, Cucurbitaceae, native to the Americas. Five edible species are grown and consumed worldwide as squash, pumpkin or gourd, for their flesh and seeds.

Cucurbita

Family: Cucurbitaceae
Genus: Cucurbita L.

Species

The main domesticated species are C. pepo (pumpkin, zucchini/courgette, acorn squash), C. maxima (winter squash), C. moschata (butternut), C. argyrosperma and C. ficifolia. They were domesticated in the Americas thousands of years ago and spread globally after 1492.

Description

Plants are annual vines or bushes with large lobed leaves, tendrils, and unisexual yellow-orange flowers. Fruits are pepos with a hard rind. Many contain bitter cucurbitacins, bred out of edible cultivars.

Uses

Eaten cooked, seeds roasted as pepitas and pressed for oil; ornamental gourds and Halloween pumpkins are culturally prominent.
