Al-Askari Shrine

The Al-Askari Shrine (Arabic: مرقد الإمامين علي الهادي والحسن العسكري) is a Shia mosque and mausoleum complex in Samarra, Iraq. It contains the tombs of the tenth and eleventh Shia Imams, Ali al-Hadi and Hasan al-Askari, father of the hidden twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, whose disappearance (occultation) is also associated with the site. It is one of the most important centres of Shia pilgrimage.

History

The Imams lived and died in Samarra, the Abbasid garrison city (al-ʿAskar) from which their epithet derives. Shrines over their graves were built and rebuilt over the centuries, notably under the Buyids and Safavids; the golden dome and tilework were largely the work of the Qajar era in the 19th century.

2006 and 2007 bombings

On 22 February 2006, an attack attributed to al-Qaeda in Iraq destroyed the shrine's golden dome. The bombing — intended to provoke sectarian war — triggered widespread Shia–Sunni violence across Iraq, killing thousands and marking an escalation into civil conflict. In June 2007 a second attack destroyed the shrine's two minarets. The shrine was subsequently rebuilt under Iraqi government and UNESCO-supported efforts, with a new golden dome completed in the following years.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit Samarra, especially on the anniversaries of the Imams. Beneath the complex lies the sardab (cellar) where, according to Twelver belief, the twelfth Imam entered the occultation in 874 CE.
