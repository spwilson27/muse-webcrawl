Okinawan music

Okinawan music is the music associated with the Okinawa Islands and the wider Ryukyu archipelago of Japan. It is distinguished from mainland Japanese music by its use of the Ryukyuan pentatonic scale and its central instrument, the sanshin, a three-stringed lute covered with snakeskin.

Styles

Traditional Okinawan music is often divided into classical music (kotenteki ongaku), developed at the royal court of the Ryukyu Kingdom in Shuri, and folk music (min'yō), sung by common people and accompanied by sanshin, drums, and finger-whistling (yubi-bue). Folk songs accompany dances such as the eisa performed at Obon festivals.

The characteristic Ryukyu scale — with its raised fourth and lowered seventh relative to the major scale — gives Okinawan melodies their distinctive sound, heard in famous songs such as "Tinsagu nu Hana" and "Asadoya Yunta".

Modern music

In the twentieth century, Okinawan musicians blended tradition with popular styles. The group Champloose and singers such as Shoukichi Kina pioneered "Okinawan rock" and pop, while Rimi Natsukawa's rendition of "Nada Sōsō" became a nationwide hit. The island music scene remains influential in Japanese popular culture.
