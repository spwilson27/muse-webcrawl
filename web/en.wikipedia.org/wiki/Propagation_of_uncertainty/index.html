Propagation of uncertainty

Propagation of uncertainty (or propagation of error) is the effect of variables' uncertainties (or errors, more specifically random errors) on the uncertainty of a function based on them. When the variables are experimental measurements, they have uncertainties due to measurement limitations (e.g., instrument precision) which propagate through the combination of variables in the function.

Linear combinations

The uncertainty of a quantity f computed from measured quantities x, y, ... can be estimated using partial derivatives. For a function f(x1, ..., xn), assuming the errors are independent and uncorrelated, the standard deviation of f is:

σf² ≈ Σi (∂f/∂xi)² σxi²

This follows from a first-order Taylor series expansion of the function about the measured values, combined with the rules for the variance of a sum of random variables. When variables are correlated, covariance terms must be included:

σf² ≈ ΣiΣj (∂f/∂xi)(∂f/∂xj) σij

Simplified rules

For sums and differences, absolute errors add in quadrature. For products and quotients, relative (fractional) errors add in quadrature. For a quantity raised to a power n, the relative error is multiplied by |n|.

Non-linear combinations

When the function is strongly non-linear, or the uncertainties are large, the first-order approximation may be inadequate. Alternatives include higher-order Taylor expansions and numerical methods such as Monte Carlo simulation, in which random samples are drawn from the distributions of the input quantities and passed through the function to build up the distribution of the result. The international Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) provides a standard framework.
