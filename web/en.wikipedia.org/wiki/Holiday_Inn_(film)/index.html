Holiday Inn is a 1942 American musical film directed by Mark Sandrich and starring Bing Crosby and Fred Astaire, with Marjorie Reynolds and Virginia Dale in supporting roles. Based on an idea by Irving Berlin, who also wrote the songs, the film tells the story of a song-and-dance trio whose act breaks up when one member leaves to open a country inn that is open only on holidays.

The film is best remembered for introducing the song "White Christmas", sung by Crosby, which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song at the 15th Academy Awards and became one of the best-selling singles of all time. Berlin wrote twelve songs for the film, one to celebrate each major American holiday, including "Happy Holiday", "Easter Parade" and "Be Careful, It's My Heart".

Plot

Jim Hardy (Crosby), Ted Hanover (Astaire) and Lila Dixon (Dale) perform together as a popular song-and-dance act. On Christmas Eve, Jim plans to retire with Lila to a farm in Connecticut, but Lila leaves him for the womanising Ted. A year later, Jim has converted the farm into "Holiday Inn", an entertainment venue open only on public holidays. He hires singer Linda Mason (Reynolds), and the two fall in love, but Ted arrives after Lila leaves him and sets out to steal both Linda and the act. Jim's jealousy leads him to conceal Linda's talent from a Hollywood scout, but at the climax Ted dances with Linda in a film being shot at the inn; backstage, Jim finally wins her back.

Cast

Bing Crosby as Jim Hardy; Fred Astaire as Ted Hanover; Marjorie Reynolds as Linda Mason; Virginia Dale as Lila Dixon; Walter Abel as Danny Reid; Louise Beavers as Mamie; and Irving Bacon as Gus.

Music

All songs were written by Irving Berlin. "White Christmas" was originally written by Berlin in 1940 and was introduced on radio by Crosby on Christmas Day 1941, months before the film's release. The recording Crosby made for the film's soundtrack became a perennial best-seller. Astaire's celebrated solo number is the Fourth of July "firecracker dance" to "Let's Say It with Firecrackers". The Abraham Lincoln birthday sequence features Crosby and Reynolds performing "Abraham" in blackface, a minstrel-style number that is frequently cut from modern television broadcasts.

Legacy

The film's premise inspired the founding of the Holiday Inn chain of motels, founded in 1952 by Kemmons Wilson, who took the name from the film. A stage adaptation premiered on Broadway in 2016. The film was released by Paramount Pictures and has remained a seasonal favourite, though modern screenings often omit or re-edit the Lincoln's Birthday sequence.
