Monthly Review is an independent socialist magazine published 11 times a year in New York City. Founded in 1949 by economist Paul Sweezy and writer Leo Huberman, it describes itself as "an independent socialist magazine" and is the longest continuously published socialist magazine in the United States. Its associated publishing house, Monthly Review Press, has issued many influential works of Marxist political economy and Third World studies.

History

The first issue, in May 1949, opened with Albert Einstein's essay "Why Socialism?". During the McCarthy period the magazine and its editors came under pressure; Sweezy's refusal to answer questions about his associations before a New Hampshire investigation led to the landmark U.S. Supreme Court case Sweezy v. New Hampshire (1957), an important academic-freedom precedent. Huberman and Sweezy's 1966 book Monopoly Capital, written with Paul Baran (author of The Political Economy of Growth, 1957), shaped the Monthly Review school of Marxist economics, emphasising monopoly, surplus absorption, and imperialism.

Editors and contributors

After Huberman's death in 1968 and Sweezy's in 2004, the magazine was edited by Harry Magdoff together with, later, John Bellamy Foster, who became editor in 2000. Contributors over the decades have included W. E. B. Du Bois, Che Guevara, Fidel Castro, Joan Robinson, E. P. Thompson, Samir Amin, and Immanuel Wallerstein. The magazine publishes analysis of U.S. politics, political economy, ecology, and liberation movements around the world, and maintains the online magazine MR Online.
