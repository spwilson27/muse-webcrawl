Rösti (German: [ˈrøːʃti]) is a Swiss dish consisting mainly of potatoes, grated and fried into a flat cake with crisp, golden edges. Originally a farmers' breakfast from the canton of Bern, it is now eaten throughout Switzerland and is considered a national dish.

Preparation

Parboiled or raw potatoes are coarsely grated, pressed into a pan with butter or fat, and fried until crisp on both sides. Variations add cheese, bacon, onion, apple or herbs. Purists in Bern traditionally use previously boiled potatoes, while other cantons use raw.

Cultural significance

The dish gives its name to the Röstigraben ("rösti ditch"), the humorous metaphor for the cultural and linguistic divide between German-speaking and French-speaking Switzerland.
