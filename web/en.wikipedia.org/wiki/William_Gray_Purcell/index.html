William Gray Purcell (2 July 1880 – 11 April 1965) was an American architect associated with the Prairie School. With his partner George Grant Elmslie, in the firm Purcell & Elmslie, he designed some of the most accomplished Prairie-style houses, banks and public buildings in the American Midwest.

Early life

Purcell grew up in Oak Park, Illinois, amid the early works of Frank Lloyd Wright, and studied architecture at Cornell University. After working in Chicago, including briefly in Wright's orbit and with Louis Sullivan's former chief draftsman George Elmslie, he settled in Minneapolis.

Purcell & Elmslie

From 1907 Purcell practised in Minneapolis, formally partnering with Elmslie in 1910. The firm produced notable works including Purcell's own house in Minneapolis (1913), the Merchants National Bank in Winona, Minnesota, and numerous small-town banks, libraries and residences noted for their integrated ornament and horizontal lines.

Later life

Ill health forced Purcell to curtail practice in the 1920s; he moved to the West Coast and devoted much of his later life to writing and documenting the Prairie School's history, leaving extensive memoirs and archives.
