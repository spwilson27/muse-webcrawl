Roxburghe Club

The Roxburghe Club is a society of British bibliophiles founded in London in 1812. It is generally regarded as the first book club and the oldest society of bibliophiles in the world. Membership is limited to forty, and members are expected to produce scholarly editions of rare texts at their own expense for presentation to the club.

Founding

The club grew out of the celebrated sale, between May and July 1812, of the library of John Ker, 3rd Duke of Roxburghe, at which the Valdarfer Boccaccio of 1471 sold for the then-record price of £2,260 to the Marquess of Blandford. A group of collectors, including Earl Spencer and the bibliographer Thomas Frognall Dibdin, dined together on 17 June 1812 and resolved to meet annually on the anniversary; the gathering developed into a permanent club named in the Duke's honour.

Activities

Each member traditionally undertakes to print, at his or her own cost, an edition of some rare or curious work — frequently an unpublished manuscript from the member's own collection or a public archive — bound sumptuously and distributed only to members. Over two centuries the club has issued several hundred such volumes, many of lasting scholarly value. It still dines annually and admits members by election upon a vacancy.

Influence

The Roxburghe Club's model inspired many later bibliophile societies, including the Grolier Club in New York (1884), and its name is commemorated by the Roxburghe Club of San Francisco.
