Grass (1925 film)

The Bakhtiari migration: crossing the Karun River with herds of sheep and goats.

Grass: A Nation's Battle for Life is a 1925 documentary film directed by Merian C. Cooper and Ernest B. Schoedsack. It follows the Bakhtiari tribe of Persia (now Iran) as they make their seasonal migration to find grazing land ("grass") for their herds.

Production

Cooper and Schoedsack, accompanied by journalist Marguerite Harrison, travelled to Anatolia and Persia and attached themselves to the Bakhtiari under Haidar Khan. The film documents the tribe's arduous twice-yearly trek of some 48 days across the Zagros Mountains — including the crossing of the Karun River on inflated goatskin rafts and a bare-footed ascent over snow-covered passes at Zard Kuh — undertaken by roughly 50,000 people with half a million animals.

Reception and legacy

Distributed by Paramount Pictures, Grass was the second ethnographic documentary of its kind, following Robert Flaherty's Nanook of the North (1922). It launched the partnership of Cooper and Schoedsack, who went on to make Chang (1927) and King Kong (1933). In 1997, Grass was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". The Bakhtiari migration was later revisited in Anthony Howarth's documentary People of the Wind (1976).
