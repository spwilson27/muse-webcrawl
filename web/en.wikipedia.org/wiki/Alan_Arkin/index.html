Alan Wolf Arkin (26 March 1934 – 29 June 2023) was an American actor, director, musician and author whose career spanned seven decades. Known for his dry, understated comic style and his versatility in drama, he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for playing the heroin-addicted grandfather Edwin Hoover in Little Miss Sunshine (2006) and a Tony Award for his Broadway debut in Enter Laughing (1963).

Early life and career

Arkin was born in Brooklyn to Jewish parents and grew up in Los Angeles. He first found success as a folk singer with The Tarriers, whose version of "The Banana Boat Song" was a hit in 1957. He then became an early member of Chicago's Second City improvisational troupe before winning the Tony for Enter Laughing.

Film career

Arkin received an Academy Award nomination for his second film, the Cold War comedy The Russians Are Coming, the Russians Are Coming (1966), and again for The Heart Is a Lonely Hunter (1968). Leading roles followed in Catch-22 (1970) as Yossarian, The In-Laws (1979) opposite Peter Falk, and Wait Until Dark (1967), in which he chillingly played a psychopathic killer. Later generations knew him from Edward Scissorhands (1990), Glengarry Glen Ross (1992), Gattaca (1997), Little Miss Sunshine (2006), Argo (2012) — for which he was again Oscar-nominated — and the Netflix series The Kominsky Method (2018–2019), which brought him Golden Globe and Emmy nominations late in life.

He also directed films including Little Murders (1971) and wrote several books, among them the children's novel The Lemming Condition and memoirs.

Death

Arkin died of heart problems at his home in San Marcos, California, on 29 June 2023, aged 89. He was widely eulogised as one of the most respected character actors of his generation.
