Engineering ethics

The 1981 Hyatt Regency walkway collapse, a canonical ethics case study

Engineering ethics is the field of applied ethics that examines and sets the obligations of engineers to society, their clients, employers and the profession. Because engineering work directly affects public safety and welfare, professional societies codify these duties: the near-universal first canon is that engineers shall hold paramount the safety, health and welfare of the public.

Principles

Common obligations in professional codes (such as those of the NSPE, ASCE, IEEE and the UK's Royal Academy of Engineering) include:

  • Hold paramount public safety, health and welfare
  • Perform services only in areas of competence
  • Issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner
  • Act for each employer or client as a faithful agent, avoiding conflicts of interest
  • Conduct themselves honourably and avoid deceptive acts
  • Protect confidential information, while recognising that public safety overrides confidentiality

Case studies

Engineering ethics is commonly taught through disasters and near-disasters in which ethical failures — ignored warnings, falsified data, suppressed dissent — contributed to harm. Standard cases include the Challenger Space Shuttle disaster (1986), in which engineer Roger Boisjoly's warnings about O-ring performance in cold weather were overridden; the Hyatt Regency walkway collapse in Kansas City (1981), caused by an unreviewed design change; the Citicorp Center crisis (1978), a contrasting positive case in which William LeMessurier disclosed and fixed a dangerous flaw in his own design; the Ford Pinto fuel-tank controversy; and the Volkswagen emissions scandal (2015), in which software was deliberately designed to cheat emissions tests.

Whistleblowing

A recurring theme is the engineer's duty when an employer insists on an unsafe course: codes generally require escalation and, if the danger persists, notification of appropriate authorities — whistleblowing — despite the personal and legal risks involved.
