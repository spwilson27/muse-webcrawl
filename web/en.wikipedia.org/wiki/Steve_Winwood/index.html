Steve Winwood

Born: Stephen Lawrence Winwood, May 12, 1948, Birmingham, England

Genres: Blue-eyed soul, rock, blues rock, progressive rock, jazz

Instruments: Vocals, keyboards, guitar

Stephen Lawrence Winwood (born 12 May 1948) is an English musician and songwriter whose genres include blue-eyed soul, rhythm and blues, blues rock and pop rock. Though primarily a keyboard player and vocalist, Winwood also plays a wide variety of other instruments; on several of his solo albums he has played all instrumentation.

Winwood was an integral part of three seminal musical acts: the Spencer Davis Group, Traffic and Blind Faith. He found major commercial success as a solo artist in the 1980s with hits such as "Valerie", "Higher Love", and "Roll with It".

Career

Winwood joined the Spencer Davis Group at the age of 15, singing lead on hits including "Gimme Some Lovin'" and "I'm a Man". In 1967 he co-founded Traffic, whose albums blended rock, folk, and jazz. In 1969 he joined the short-lived supergroup Blind Faith with Eric Clapton, Ginger Baker, and Ric Grech, before reforming Traffic.

His solo album Back in the High Life (1986) won Grammy Awards including Record of the Year for "Higher Love". Winwood was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Traffic in 2004.
