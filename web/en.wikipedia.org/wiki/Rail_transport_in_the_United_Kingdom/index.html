Overview

Rail transport in the United Kingdom is the oldest railway system in the world: the Stockton and Darlington Railway opened in 1825 and the Liverpool and Manchester Railway in 1830. Railways were built by private companies in a Victorian mania, grouped into the "Big Four" in 1923, nationalised as British Rail in 1948, and privatised again in the 1990s into franchised operators over a renationalised infrastructure (Network Rail, from 2002).

Recent history

Passenger demand doubled from the mid-1990s to a pre-pandemic peak around 2019. Beeching's 1960s cuts closed a third of the network — still politically resonant. The Channel Tunnel (1994) and HS1 brought Eurostar services to St Pancras; Crossrail (the Elizabeth line) opened in 2022; the HS2 high-speed project was progressively curtailed in 2023 to the London–Birmingham section. Under the Passenger Railway Services (Public Ownership) Act 2024, franchises are being brought back into public hands as they expire, overseen by the planned Great British Railways — a reversal of the 1990s model whose outcome remains in progress.
