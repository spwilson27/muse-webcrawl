The thylacine (Thylacinus cynocephalus), also known as the Tasmanian tiger or Tasmanian wolf, is an extinct carnivorous marsupial that was native to the Australian mainland, New Guinea, and the island of Tasmania. The last known individual died at Hobart Zoo on 7 September 1936.

Description

A large dog-like marsupial with a stiff tail, a pouch in females (and a scrotal pouch in males) and 15–20 dark stripes across its sandy back, the thylacine is a striking example of convergent evolution with placental canids. Both sexes could open their jaws unusually wide.

Extinction

Thylacines vanished from the mainland about 2,000–3,600 years ago, probably under pressure from dingoes and humans. In Tasmania, government and private bounties, habitat loss and disease drove the decline; over 2,000 bounties were paid between 1888 and 1909.

Legacy

Unconfirmed sightings persist, the species is an emblem of Tasmania, and its genome, sequenced in 2017 from a preserved pup, has made it a flagship candidate—and cautionary case—in de-extinction debates.
