Rate law

The rate law (also known as the rate equation) for a chemical reaction is an expression that links the rate of the reaction to the concentrations or partial pressures of the reactants and constant parameters (normally rate coefficients and partial reaction orders). For a reaction aA + bB → products, the rate law commonly takes the form rate = k[A]x[B]y, where k is the rate constant and x and y are the partial orders of reaction.

Rate laws are determined experimentally and cannot in general be deduced from the stoichiometry of the balanced equation. The sum x + y is the overall order of the reaction. Differential and integrated rate laws underpin the quantitative study of chemical kinetics.
