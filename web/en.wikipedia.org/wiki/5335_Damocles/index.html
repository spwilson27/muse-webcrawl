5335 Damocles

The steeply tilted, comet-like orbit of Damocles.

5335 Damocles, provisional designation 1991 DA, is a centaur-like body on a retrograde, highly inclined orbit typical of dormant comets. It was discovered on 18 February 1991 by Australian astronomer Robert H. McNaught at Siding Spring Observatory, and named for Damocles, the courtier of Greek legend who sat beneath a sword suspended by a single hair.

Orbit

Damocles follows a Halley-type comet's path: an orbital period of about 41 years, a perihelion of about 1.58 AU inside the orbit of Mars, an aphelion beyond 22 AU, an eccentricity near 0.87, and an inclination of roughly 62° — travelled in the retrograde direction. Its Tisserand parameter with respect to Jupiter is below 2, characteristic of comets from the Oort cloud rather than asteroids. Bodies sharing such orbits are now called Damocloids after this prototype; they are thought to be inert, devolatilized Halley-family comets whose ice has been exhausted or sealed beneath a crust.

Physical characteristics

Damocles measures roughly 10 kilometers across. Spectroscopic observations show a very red, nearly featureless surface resembling D-type asteroids and inactive comet nuclei, consistent with a mantle of irradiated organic-rich material. Despite its comet-like orbit, no coma or tail has ever been observed from it.
