Wallsend

Wallsend
Borough: North Tyneside
County: Tyne and Wear, England
Population: approx. 44,000

Wallsend is a town in the Metropolitan Borough of North Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England, lying on the north bank of the River Tyne about 3.5 miles (5.6 km) east of Newcastle upon Tyne. It takes its name from its position at the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall.

History

The Romans built the fort of Segedunum at Wallsend, the eastern terminus of Hadrian's Wall, constructed from AD 122. The excavated fort and its museum are now a major visitor attraction. In the industrial era the town became a centre of coal mining and shipbuilding: the Swan Hunter shipyard, founded in Wallsend, built famous vessels including RMS Mauretania and HMS Ark Royal. Shipbuilding declined in the late 20th century, and the yard closed in the 2000s, though offshore engineering continues in the area.

Transport and culture

Wallsend is served by a station on the Tyne and Wear Metro, whose signs are displayed partly in Latin in honour of the town's Roman heritage — a feature that has attracted international attention. The town is also the birthplace of the musician Sting, who drew on his Wallsend upbringing in the musical The Last Ship.
