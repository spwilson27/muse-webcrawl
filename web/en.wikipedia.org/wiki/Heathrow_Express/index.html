Heathrow Express

The Heathrow Express is a high-speed airport rail link operating in London, England, connecting London Paddington station with Heathrow Airport. The service follows the Great Western Main Line from Paddington to Heathrow Junction and then a dedicated spur to the airport. Trains run non-stop to Heathrow Central (Terminals 2 and 3), with some continuing to Terminal 5; the journey time is about 15 minutes to Heathrow Central and a few minutes more to Terminal 5.

Heathrow Express trains operate between London Paddington and Heathrow Airport.

History

Construction of the Heathrow Express link began in 1993 as a joint project between the British Airports Authority (BAA) and British Rail. During construction of the tunnels in October 1994, a major collapse occurred at the airport station site using the New Austrian Tunnelling Method, causing a large crater and the collapse of a car park and office building; no one was injured, but the contractor Balfour Beatty was later fined for health and safety failures.

The service was officially launched on 23 June 1998 by Prime Minister Tony Blair. Initially an interim service operated to a temporary station, Heathrow Junction, from January 1998. A slower stopping counterpart, Heathrow Connect, was introduced in 2005; its services were absorbed into TfL Rail (later the Elizabeth line) in 2018.

Services

Heathrow Express trains depart Paddington every 15 minutes for most of the day. Services call at Heathrow Central for Terminals 2 and 3, with most continuing to Terminal 5. The service to Terminal 4 was suspended in 2020 and later withdrawn, with Terminal 4 passengers directed to the Elizabeth line and Piccadilly line. Trains convey standard and first-class ("Business First") accommodation, with luggage racks, power sockets and free Wi-Fi.

Rolling stock

From opening until 2020–21, the service was operated by fourteen Class 332 electric multiple units built by Siemens and CAF. They were replaced by a fleet of twelve Class 387 units transferred from Great Western Railway, maintained at Reading depot, and given a Heathrow Express interior refit.

Fares

The Heathrow Express has long been among the most expensive airport rail journeys in Europe per mile, though advance-purchase tickets, railcards and ticket offers have reduced prices for some passengers. It competes with the cheaper but slower Piccadilly line and the Elizabeth line for airport traffic.
