Ontology

BranchMetaphysics

Ontology is the philosophical study of being — what exists, and how the things that exist can be grouped and related. It is traditionally listed as a part of the major branch of Philosophy known as Metaphysics. Ontologists ask whether entities such as properties, numbers, fictional characters and possible objects genuinely exist, and what the most fundamental categories of reality are.

History

The term was coined in the early 17th century from the Greek on (being) and logos (study). Aristotle had already described the inquiry as the study of 'being qua being'. Medieval scholastics elaborated hierarchies of being, while Descartes and later Immanuel Kant reframed the questions. In the 20th century W. V. O. Quine made ontological commitment — what a theory says exists — a standard analytic tool with his criterion 'to be is to be the value of a bound variable'.

Questions

Typical questions include: What is existence? Do abstract objects exist independently of minds (realism) or not (nominalism)? What is an object, and how do parts relate to wholes (mereology)? Debates also cover social ontology — the existence of groups, institutions and money — and applied ontology in information science, where the word names formal specifications of concepts in a domain.
