Eduard Shevardnadze

Born25 January 1928, Mamati, Georgian SSR, Soviet Union
Died7 July 2014 (aged 86), Tbilisi, Georgia
OfficesMinister of Foreign Affairs of the USSR (1985–1990); President of Georgia (1995–2003)

Eduard Shevardnadze (25 January 1928 – 7 July 2014) was a Georgian politician who played a central role in ending the Cold War as the Soviet Union's foreign minister under Mikhail Gorbachev, and later served as president of independent Georgia from 1995 until his resignation in the Rose Revolution of 2003.

Soviet career

Shevardnadze rose through the Communist Party in Georgia, becoming the republic's interior minister and then, in 1972, first secretary of the Georgian Communist Party, where he campaigned against corruption. In 1985 Gorbachev appointed him foreign minister, a surprise choice given his lack of diplomatic experience. Together with Gorbachev he pursued the "new thinking" in foreign policy: the INF Treaty eliminating intermediate-range nuclear missiles (1987), the withdrawal of Soviet troops from Afghanistan, the peaceful reunification of Germany within NATO, and arms-control agreements with the United States. He resigned in December 1990, warning of a coming dictatorship.

President of Georgia

Return and rule

After the overthrow of President Zviad Gamsakhurdia, Shevardnadze returned to Georgia in 1992 to head the State Council. He took power amid civil war and the loss of Abkhazia and South Ossetia, survived assassination attempts, and was elected president in 1995 and again in 2000. His government stabilised the country and attracted Western support — Georgia joined the Council of Europe and promoted the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan oil pipeline — but was increasingly marred by corruption and economic stagnation.

Rose Revolution

Fraudulent parliamentary elections in November 2003 triggered mass protests led by Mikheil Saakashvili. On 23 November 2003, after demonstrators stormed parliament, Shevardnadze resigned. He lived quietly in Tbilisi until his death in 2014.
