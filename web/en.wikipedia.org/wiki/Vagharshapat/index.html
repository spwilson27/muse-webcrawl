Vagharshapat

Vagharshapat (Armenian: Վաղարշապատ) is a city in Armavir Province of Armenia, located about 18 km west of Yerevan. It is commonly known as Etchmiadzin, the name it officially bore from 1945 to 1995 and which remains in widespread use. The city is the seat of the Catholicos of All Armenians and the spiritual centre of the Armenian Apostolic Church.

History

One of the oldest settlements in Armenia, the site was founded as Vardkesavan and refounded as a capital by King Vagharsh I in the 2nd century AD. It served as the capital of the Kingdom of Armenia on several occasions. According to tradition, Saint Gregory the Illuminator founded Etchmiadzin Cathedral in 301–303 AD, making it one of the oldest cathedrals in the world, after converting King Tiridates III and Armenia to Christianity — Armenia being the first state to adopt Christianity as its state religion.

Landmarks

The city's churches — Etchmiadzin Cathedral, Saint Hripsime Church, Saint Gayane Church, and Shoghakat Church — together with the ruins of Zvartnots Cathedral nearby, are listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin, within the city, is the headquarters of the Armenian Church.
