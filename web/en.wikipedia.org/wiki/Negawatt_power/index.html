Negawatt power
ConceptPower saved through energy efficiency or reduced consumption
Coined byAmory Lovins (1989)

Negawatt power is a theoretical unit of power representing an amount of electrical power (measured in watts) saved. The concept was coined by physicist and energy analyst Amory Lovins of the Rocky Mountain Institute in a 1989 speech: a "negawatt" is a watt of generating capacity avoided through efficiency or conservation — in effect, energy not used.

Concept

Lovins argued that utilities could meet demand more cheaply by investing in end-use efficiency (better insulation, efficient lighting, motors and appliances) than by building new power stations, and that customers do not want electricity itself but the services it provides — light, heat, motion, refrigeration. The idea underpins demand-side management programmes, energy efficiency resource standards, and markets in which saved energy ("negawatts") is bid into capacity markets alongside generation, for example in the PJM and New England markets in the United States.

Criticism

Critics note measurement difficulties (baseline determination, rebound effects) and the free-rider problem in utility programmes, though efficiency is widely regarded as among the cheapest and cleanest energy "resources".
