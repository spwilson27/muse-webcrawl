Special move

In video games, a special move is an action available to a character that goes beyond the basic moveset, typically triggered by a specific input combination and producing an enhanced or unique effect. The term is most closely associated with fighting games, where special moves define a character's play style.

In fighting games

Special moves are usually performed with directional inputs combined with attack buttons — for example the quarter-circle motion plus punch that produces Ryu's Hadouken fireball in the Street Fighter series (1991's Street Fighter II popularized the convention). Special moves are commonly limited by execution difficulty, startup or recovery time, or resource meters; more powerful "super moves" typically consume a built gauge.

Other genres

Beyond fighters, the term is used for signature abilities in role-playing games, platformers, and beat-'em-ups, and more broadly for any character-specific technique that distinguishes one playable character from another.
