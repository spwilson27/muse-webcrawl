Russian Laundromat

The Russian Laundromat (Russian: Российский ландромат) was a scheme to move money out of Russia between 2010 and 2014, estimated at between $20 billion and $80 billion, through a network of global banks, many of them in Moldova and Latvia. It was uncovered by the Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project (OCCRP) together with Novaya Gazeta and other partners, and reporting began in 2014, with a major data release in 2017.

Mechanism

The scheme relied on fake loans: a Russian company would "lend" money to a shell company, and the debt would be guaranteed by another Russian company. When the borrower defaulted, a Moldovan judge would certify the debt, ordering payment from the guarantor. The money was then transferred through Moldovan and Latvian banks — notably Moldindconbank and Trasta Komercbanka — into accounts across Europe and beyond, acquiring an appearance of legitimacy.

Exposure and consequences

Journalistic investigations revealed that funds flowed to individuals and companies worldwide, and led to regulatory action: Trasta Komercbanka lost its licence in 2016, and Moldova's banking sector was overhauled amid the related Moldovan bank fraud scandal. Investigations in several countries examined the role of major Western banks in processing the transactions.
