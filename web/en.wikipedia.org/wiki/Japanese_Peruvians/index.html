Japanese Peruvians (Spanish: nipo-peruanos; Japanese: 日系ペルー人, Nikkei Perūjin) are Peruvian citizens of Japanese origin or ancestry. Peru was the first South American country to establish diplomatic relations with Japan (1873) and the first to accept Japanese immigrants, beginning in 1899.

History

The first 790 Japanese contract labourers arrived aboard the Sakura Maru in 1899 to work on coastal sugar plantations. Immigration continued until the 1930s, and the community moved into commerce and small business in Lima. During World War II, anti-Japanese riots struck in 1940, and some 1,800 Japanese Peruvians were deported to internment camps in the United States, many being refused return after the war — a grievance only partly redressed decades later.

The Fujimori era

In 1990 Alberto Fujimori, the son of Japanese immigrants, was elected President of Peru, becoming the first person of Japanese descent to lead a Latin American nation; he governed until 2000. His daughter Keiko Fujimori has been a major presidential contender in subsequent elections, keeping the Nikkei community at the centre of national politics.

Culture

The community sustains institutions such as the Asociación Peruano Japonesa, the Japanese-Peruvian Cultural Center in Lima, and Nikkei schools and newspapers. Japanese immigration also reshaped Peruvian cooking: Nikkei cuisine, including dishes like tiradito, blends Japanese technique with Peruvian ingredients and is celebrated worldwide.
