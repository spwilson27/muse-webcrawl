Mysticism is the pursuit and experience of direct, unmediated communion with ultimate reality — often described as union with God or the absolute — reported across the world's religions. Mystics such as Meister Eckhart, Teresa of Ávila, Ibn Arabi and the authors of the Upanishads describe states transcending ordinary subject–object awareness, frequently declared ineffable and paradoxical.

Study

William James's Varieties of Religious Experience (1902) catalogued mystical states by four marks — ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, passivity — and perennialists (Aldous Huxley) claimed a common core across traditions, while constructivists (Steven Katz) argue experience is always shaped by prior concepts. Mystical practice — meditation, contemplation, dhikr, hesychasm — is cultivated within most major traditions, sometimes eyed warily by their authorities.
