Henotheism

Henotheism is the worship of a single, supreme god while not denying the existence or worthiness of other deities. The term was coined by German philologist and orientalist Friedrich Max Müller, who studied Vedic religion.

Definition

Henotheism differs from monotheism, which denies other gods, and from polytheism without hierarchy. Related is monolatry — worshipping only one god while believing others exist. In Müller's account of the Rigveda, each hymned deity is treated in turn as supreme (sometimes termed kathenotheism).

Examples

The concept has been applied to Vedic Hinduism, ancient Egyptian religion focused on a city god, Greek devotion, and early Israelite religion before exclusive monotheism. Zoroastrian and some modern Hindu practice are often described in henotheistic terms.

Criticism

Scholars debate whether the term imposes a developmental scheme — polytheism evolving toward monotheism — on traditions that understand themselves differently.
