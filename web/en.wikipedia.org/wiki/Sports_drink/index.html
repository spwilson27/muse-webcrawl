Sports drink

Sports drinks, also known as electrolyte drinks, are functional beverages intended to help athletes replace water, electrolytes and energy lost through sweating before, during and after training or competition. A typical sports drink contains water, carbohydrates (usually sugars such as glucose, sucrose or maltodextrin at about 6–8% concentration) and electrolytes, principally sodium and potassium, sometimes with other minerals, flavourings and colours.

History

The first sports drink, Gatorade, was developed in 1965 by a team of researchers at the University of Florida for the Florida Gators American football team, giving the drink its name. Commercial competitors followed, including Powerade (Coca-Cola) and Lucozade Sport, and the category grew into a multi-billion-dollar global market marketed heavily through sports sponsorship.

Types and effectiveness

Drinks are classified by tonicity: isotonic drinks match the osmolality of body fluids and are the standard exercise formulation; hypotonic drinks are more dilute and emphasise rapid fluid replacement; hypertonic drinks are more concentrated and are generally used for post-exercise energy replenishment. Scientific reviews generally find that for exercise lasting less than about 60–90 minutes, water alone is sufficient for most people, while electrolyte-carbohydrate drinks confer benefits during prolonged endurance exercise. Sports drinks are distinguished from energy drinks, which contain caffeine and other stimulants and are not designed for hydration. Excessive consumption of plain water without electrolytes during endurance events can contribute to hyponatraemia, a risk sports drinks partly mitigate, though their sodium content is modest. Health bodies have also criticised the marketing of sugary sports drinks to children, linking them to obesity and dental erosion.
