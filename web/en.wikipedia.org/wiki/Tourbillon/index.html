Tourbillon

A tourbillon (French for "whirlwind") is a complication in a mechanical watch in which the escapement and balance wheel are mounted in a rotating cage. The rotation averages out the effects of gravity on the timekeeping rate when the watch remains in a fixed vertical position, as pocket watches typically did.

History

The mechanism was developed by the Swiss-born watchmaker Abraham-Louis Breguet and patented on 26 Messidor Year IX (14 June 1801). Breguet's early tourbillons were built into a small number of pocket chronometers. The device remained a rarity until the late twentieth century, when it was revived in wristwatches — the first wristwatch tourbillon was produced by Audemars Piguet in 1986 — and became a showcase of high watchmaking craftsmanship, often displayed through an aperture in the dial.

Variants and debate

Variants include multi-axis tourbillons, double and quadruple tourbillons, and the related karussel. Horologists debate the tourbillon's practical benefit in wristwatches, which constantly change orientation, but its difficulty of manufacture — a cage may weigh under half a gram and comprise dozens of parts — makes it a symbol of prestige, and tourbillon watches command very high prices.
