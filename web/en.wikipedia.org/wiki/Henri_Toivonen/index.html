Henri Pauli Toivonen (25 August 1956 – 2 May 1986) was a Finnish rally driver. He competed in the World Rally Championship (WRC) from 1975 to 1986 and won three world rallies. Toivonen, son of 1962 European Rally Champion Pauli Toivonen, was one of the fastest drivers of the Group B era. His death, along with his co-driver Sergio Cresto, in a crash at the 1986 Tour de Corse led directly to the banning of Group B cars.

Career

Toivonen began in circuit racing and karting before switching to rallying. He made his WRC debut at the 1975 1000 Lakes Rally. In 1980, driving a Talbot Sunbeam Lotus, he won the RAC Rally, becoming the youngest driver to win a WRC event at the time. He later drove for Opel (Ascona 400 and Manta 400) before joining Lancia in 1984, campaigning the 037 and then the four-wheel-drive Delta S4, with which he won the 1985 RAC Rally and the 1986 Monte Carlo Rally.

Death and legacy

On 2 May 1986, leading the Tour de Corse, Toivonen's Delta S4 left the road on a mountain stage, plunged into a ravine, and caught fire; he and Cresto were killed. Within hours, FISA president Jean-Marie Balestre announced the abolition of Group B. Toivonen is remembered as one of rallying's great natural talents, and his younger brother Harri also became a rally and racing driver.
