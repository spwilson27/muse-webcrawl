Gravity loss

In astrodynamics and aerospace engineering, gravity loss is a measure of the loss in the net performance of a rocket while it is thrusting, arising from the need to counter Earth's gravity. In essence, while a rocket flies vertically, part of its thrust is spent fighting gravity rather than accelerating the vehicle, so the vehicle's change in velocity (delta-v) is less than the ideal amount given by the rocket equation.

Description

Gravity losses depend on the launch profile: they accumulate at a rate of g sin θ per second, where θ is the angle of the thrust direction from the local horizontal. A vehicle that ascends vertically at high thrust suffers larger instantaneous gravity loss but for a shorter time, while a slow vehicle on a long, near-vertical climb accrues more total loss; in practice, launch vehicles execute a gravity turn to pitch over gradually so that more thrust goes into horizontal velocity. Typical gravity losses for orbital launches are around 1.5–2 km/s of delta-v.
