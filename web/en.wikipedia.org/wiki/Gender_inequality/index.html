Gender inequality

[Image placeholder: Gender inequality statistics]

Gender inequality is the social phenomenon in which women and men are not treated equally, arising from distinctions — real or perceived — in biology, psychology, or cultural norms. It is measured across dimensions such as income, education, health, political representation, and personal safety, and it affects both women and men, though women are disadvantaged in most global indices.

Measurement

The World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report and the UN Development Programme's Gender Inequality Index track progress across countries. Nordic countries consistently rank among the most equal, while the largest gaps appear in economic participation and political empowerment worldwide: women hold roughly a quarter of parliamentary seats globally and earn less than men in nearly every economy, with a global gender pay gap often estimated around 20%.

Domains

In education, girls' enrolment has reached parity in many countries but lags in parts of sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia; in some developed countries boys now lag in literacy and university enrolment. Health disparities include maternal mortality and unequal access to care, while men have lower life expectancy nearly everywhere. Unpaid care and domestic work falls disproportionately on women — a burden the ILO estimates at about three times men's contribution. Violence against women, child marriage, and discriminatory inheritance laws remain central concerns of the UN's Sustainable Development Goal 5 (gender equality).
