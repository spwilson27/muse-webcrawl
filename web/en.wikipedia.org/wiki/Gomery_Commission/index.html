Gomery Commission

The Gomery Commission, formally the Commission of Inquiry into the Sponsorship Program and Advertising Activities, was a Canadian federal commission of inquiry headed by Justice John Gomery, established on 19 February 2004 by Prime Minister Paul Martin to investigate the sponsorship scandal — the misuse of public funds in a federal program intended to promote Canadian unity in Quebec after the 1995 referendum.

Background

The sponsorship program, created in 1996–97, channeled hundreds of millions of dollars to pro-federalist advertising and events in Quebec. A 2002 internal audit and a damning 2004 report by Auditor General Sheila Fraser found that about $100 million had been paid to communications agencies with close Liberal Party ties for little or no work, with funds in some cases kicked back to the Liberal Party's Quebec wing.

Proceedings and reports

The commission heard testimony through 2005 from advertising executives, bureaucrats and politicians, including former prime minister Jean Chrétien and sitting prime minister Paul Martin — a rare appearance by a serving head of government before an inquiry. Gomery's Phase I report, Who Is Responsible?, released 1 November 2005, blamed Chrétien and his chief of staff Jean Pelletier for creating a program that bypassed normal controls, and found a network of kickbacks benefiting the Liberal Party; it cleared Martin of direct responsibility. The Phase II report, Restoring Accountability (February 2006), made wide-ranging recommendations on government accountability and the public service.

Political impact

The scandal devastated the Liberal Party: Martin's minority government fell on a non-confidence motion in November 2005, and the Conservatives under Stephen Harper won the January 2006 election, enacting the Federal Accountability Act as their first legislation. The inquiry also faced legal challenges, including a Federal Court ruling that some of Gomery's Phase I conclusions showed a reasonable apprehension of bias toward Chrétien; those findings were later set aside in part on appeal proceedings between Chrétien and the commission.
