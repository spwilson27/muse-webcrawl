Payback period

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The payback period in capital budgeting refers to the time required for the cash flows generated by an investment to repay its initial cost. The payback period is expressed in years and fractions of years; projects with shorter payback periods are generally preferred under this rule, since the initial outlay is recovered sooner and the investment's liquidity and risk profile are considered better.

Calculation

For an investment with equal annual cash inflows, the payback period is simply the initial investment divided by the annual cash inflow. For uneven cash flows, the payback period is found by accumulating cash inflows year by year until the cumulative total equals the initial cost, interpolating within the final year.

YearCash flowCumulative cash flow
0−$100,000−$100,000
1$30,000−$70,000
2$40,000−$30,000
3$50,000+$20,000

In this example the payback period is 2 + (30,000 / 50,000) = 2.6 years.

Discounted payback period

The basic method ignores the time value of money. The discounted payback period corrects this by discounting each cash flow at the project's cost of capital before accumulating; it answers the question of how long the project takes to break even in present-value terms. The discounted payback is always longer than the simple payback for a positive discount rate.

Advantages and limitations

Payback is simple to compute and understand, favours liquidity, and can serve as a crude screen against obsolescence and risk — surveys consistently show a majority of firms, especially smaller ones, use it alongside discounted cash flow methods. Its weaknesses are serious, however: it ignores cash flows received after the payback date (and therefore overall profitability), and in its simple form ignores the time value of money entirely. Arbitrary cutoff periods can cause firms to reject long-lived projects with high net present value.

Use in energy and other fields

Beyond corporate finance, payback period is widely used to evaluate energy-efficiency investments, insulation, solar panels and other household or industrial upgrades ("energy payback"), where the metric communicates how quickly savings cover the installation cost.
