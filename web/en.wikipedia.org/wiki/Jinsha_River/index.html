Jinsha River

The Jinsha River ("Gold Sands River") is the Chinese name for the upper course of the Yangtze River. It flows through western China in Qinghai, Tibet, Sichuan and Yunnan, joining the Min River at Yibin to form the Yangtze proper.

Course

The river begins as the Tongtian River in the Tanggula Mountains of the Tibetan Plateau and takes the name Jinsha after Batang. It runs roughly 2,300 km, carving deep gorges along the borders of Sichuan, Tibet and Yunnan, including Tiger Leaping Gorge, one of the deepest river canyons in the world.

Hydropower

The Jinsha's steep gradient has made it a major site for hydroelectric development. Large dams include Xiangjiaba, Xiluodu, Wudongde and Baihetan, several of which rank among the largest hydroelectric stations on Earth by installed capacity.

Name

"Jinsha" means "golden sands", referring to placer gold historically panned from the river's banks.
