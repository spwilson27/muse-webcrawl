Beersheba (Hebrew: בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, Be'er Sheva; Arabic: بئر السبع, Biʾr as-Sabʿ) is the largest city in the Negev desert of southern Israel. Often referred to as the "Capital of the Negev", it is the centre of the fourth-most populous metropolitan area in Israel and the eighth-most populous Israeli city, with a population of roughly 214,000.

History

The name derives from the Hebrew be'er ("well") and sheva ("seven" or "oath") — traditionally the "Well of the Oath" sworn by Abraham and Abimelech in the Book of Genesis. The nearby ancient mound of Tel Be'er Sheva, a fortified Israelite town of the Iron Age, is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Settlement in the area is documented from the Chalcolithic period (c. 4000 BCE).

The modern city was founded by the Ottoman Empire in 1900 as a planned administrative centre for the Bedouin of the Negev. During the First World War, the Battle of Beersheba (31 October 1917) saw the Australian Light Horse make one of history's last great cavalry charges, capturing the town from Ottoman forces. Under the British Mandate the town grew; it was allocated to the Arab state in the 1947 UN Partition Plan but was captured by Israel in October 1948 during Operation Yoav.

Modern city

Beersheba expanded rapidly with immigration in the 1950s and the large influx from the former Soviet Union in the 1990s. It is home to Ben-Gurion University of the Negev (founded 1969), the Soroka Medical Center, a growing cyber-security and technology park adjacent to the university, and the IDF's technology campus. The Old City preserves Ottoman-era buildings, the Turkish railway station, and the Negev Museum of Art.

Geography and climate

Beersheba lies on the northern edge of the Negev, about 115 km southeast of Tel Aviv and 120 km southwest of Jerusalem, at roughly 260 m above sea level. It has a hot semi-arid climate, with hot dry summers and mild winters.
