German occupation of Norway

The German occupation of Norway lasted from 9 April 1940, when Nazi Germany invaded in Operation Weserübung, until the German surrender in May 1945. King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government fled to London and led a government-in-exile, while the country was administered by a Reichskommissariat under Josef Terboven alongside a collaborationist government headed by Vidkun Quisling.

Invasion and administration

Germany attacked Norway to secure iron ore shipments from Sweden through the port of Narvik and to gain naval and air bases against Britain. After a two-month campaign, despite Allied assistance, organised Norwegian resistance on the mainland ended in June 1940. Quisling's Nasjonal Samling was the only permitted political party, and in February 1942 Quisling became minister-president of a puppet government, while real power remained with Terboven.

Resistance and repression

The Norwegian resistance movement, including the Milorg organisation, carried out sabotage such as the heavy water operations at Vemork that hindered the German nuclear programme. The occupation authorities deported Norwegian Jews — 772 were deported, of whom only a small number survived — and executed hostages in reprisals. Northern Norway suffered heavily when German forces retreating from Finland in 1944–45 burned Finnmark and northern Troms.

End of the occupation

Some 350,000 German troops were still in Norway at the war's end in May 1945 and surrendered without fighting. Quisling was tried and executed for treason in October 1945, and his surname became a byword for a traitor.
