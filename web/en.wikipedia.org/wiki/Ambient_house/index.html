Ambient house

Ambient house is a downtempo subgenre of house music that first emerged in the late 1980s, combining elements of acid house and ambient music. The genre grew out of the UK's rave scene and the "chill-out rooms" of clubs, where DJs played slower, atmospheric music for dancers to recover to. Tracks were characterised by synthesizer pads, sampled vocals, and slower tempos than typical house music.

History

The genre is closely associated with The Orb, whose 1989 sessions at Paul Oakenfold's Land of Oz club and the single "Little Fluffy Clouds" (1990) helped define the sound. The KLF's album Chill Out (1990) was another landmark, blending ambient textures with samples of trains, sheep, and Elvis Presley. Other key acts included The Future Sound of London, whose Lifeforms (1994) became a landmark of ambient electronica, as well as Mixmaster Morris, Sun Electric, and the artists of the German label Fax +49-69/450464.

Characteristics

Ambient house typically features four-on-the-floor rhythms softened or submerged beneath layers of synthesiser wash, nature sounds, spoken-word samples, and dub-influenced bass. It drew on Brian Eno's concept of ambient music as "as ignorable as it is interesting" while retaining the pulse of dance music.

Influence

The genre fed into the broader 1990s downtempo and trip hop movements and into "ambient techno" and IDM. Its legacy persists in chillwave, lo-fi house, and modern ambient electronic music, and The Orb and contemporaries continued to tour and record into the twenty-first century.
