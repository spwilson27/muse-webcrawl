Nowruz (Persian: نوروز, "new day") is the Persian New Year and the first day of spring, celebrated on the March equinox by more than 300 million people worldwide. With roots in the ancient Iranian religion of Zoroastrianism, it has been celebrated for over 3,000 years across Western and Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Black Sea basin, and the Balkans. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed Nowruz on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and the United Nations proclaimed 21 March the International Day of Nowruz.

Origins

Nowruz marks the vernal equinox, when day and night are of equal length, and begins the first month (Farvardin) of the Iranian solar calendar. Its mythology is associated with the legendary king Jamshid and with the Zoroastrian creator god Ahura Mazda. The festival symbolises rebirth, renewal, and the triumph of light and spring over winter darkness.

Customs

Preparations begin weeks ahead with khane tekani ("shaking the house"), a thorough spring cleaning. On the eve of the last Wednesday before Nowruz, people celebrate Chaharshanbe Suri, jumping over bonfires while chanting a wish for the fire to take away sickness and give back warmth and colour.

The centrepiece of the celebration is the Haft-Sin table, set with seven items beginning with the Persian letter s: sabzeh (sprouted wheat or lentils, rebirth), samanu (sweet pudding, affluence), senjed (oleaster fruit, love), sir (garlic, health), sib (apples, beauty), somaq (sumac, sunrise), and serkeh (vinegar, patience). The table often also holds a mirror, candles, painted eggs, a goldfish, and a book of poetry — commonly the Divan of Hafez, or the Quran in Muslim households.

The thirteen days of festivities end with Sizdah Bedar, the "nature day", when families picnic outdoors and throw the sprouted sabzeh into running water, carrying away the bad luck of the year.

Regional variants

Nowruz is a public holiday in Iran, Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, and parts of other countries. Kurdish communities celebrate it as Newroz, associated with the legend of Kawa the blacksmith. Afghan customs include the Haft-Mewa fruit compote and buzkashi tournaments.
