Limonene is a colourless liquid aliphatic hydrocarbon classified as a cyclic monoterpene. It is the major component of the oil of citrus fruit peels and takes its name from the lemon. The D-isomer, occurring more commonly in nature, has a strong smell of oranges and is used as a flavouring agent in food manufacturing and as a fragrance in perfumery and cleaning products.

Chemistry

Limonene is a chiral molecule with two enantiomers. D-limonene smells of oranges, while L-limonene, found in mint and other herbs, has a piney, turpentine-like odour; the racemic mixture is known as dipentene. In plants it is biosynthesised from geranyl pyrophosphate via cyclisation by limonene synthase. Limonene is a skin irritant and sensitiser in its oxidised form, and it is combustible.

Uses

Beyond flavour and fragrance, limonene serves as a botanical insecticide, an ingredient in adhesive and paint removers, and a renewable solvent derived from citrus-processing waste — for example, in degreasers and in the solvent used to dissolve polystyrene for recycling. It has also been investigated as a biofuel component and as a feedstock for bio-based polymers.
