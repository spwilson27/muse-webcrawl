Nelson's Column is a monument in Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, Central London, built to commemorate Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, who died at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. The Corinthian column, designed by William Railton and built between 1840 and 1843, is topped by a sandstone statue of Nelson sculpted by Edward Hodges Baily.

Description

The granite column rises about 46 metres, and with the 5.5-metre statue the monument stands 51.6 metres (169 feet 3 inches) tall. The capital is decorated with bronze acanthus leaves cast from captured French cannon, and the pedestal carries four bronze relief panels, also cast from captured guns, depicting Nelson's great battles: Cape St Vincent, the Nile, Copenhagen, and his death at Trafalgar. The four monumental bronze lions at the base, sculpted by Sir Edwin Landseer, were added in 1867.

History

The monument was funded largely by public subscription, with contributions from the government of the Russian tsar and others. It has become one of London's best-known landmarks and a focal point of public gatherings; the column was restored and cleaned in 2006 using scaffolding raised by specialist steeplejacks.
