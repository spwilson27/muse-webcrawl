Intonation (linguistics)

In linguistics, intonation is the variation in pitch used while speaking, serving to communicate phrasing, emphasis, questions versus statements, and attitude. It is a feature of spoken language distinct from word-level tone in tonal languages, though the two interact.

Functions

Intonation performs several overlapping functions. The attitudinal function conveys emotion and stance; the grammatical function distinguishes sentence types (a rising contour often marks yes–no questions in English) and clause boundaries; the accentual function places focus on new or contrasted information; and the discourse function signals topic structure and turn-taking in conversation.

Analysis

Phoneticians describe intonation using pitch contours over the utterance. Theories differ on the basic units: the British tradition analyses intonation with "tones" or tunes over tone units, while the autosegmental-metrical approach (used in the ToBI transcription system) decomposes contours into sequences of high and low target tones associated with stressed syllables and phrase edges.

Cross-linguistic variation

Languages differ in how they use pitch: some mark questions with final rises, others with falls or particles, and the overall pitch range and alignment of peaks vary systematically. Intonation also varies by dialect and sociolect, contributing to perceptions of accent.
