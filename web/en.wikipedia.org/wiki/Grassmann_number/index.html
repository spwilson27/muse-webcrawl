Grassmann number

Grassmann variables anticommute: θiθj = −θjθi, so θ² = 0.

In mathematical physics, a Grassmann number (also called an anticommuting number or supernumber) is an element of an algebra generated by quantities that anticommute. The concept is named after Hermann Grassmann, whose exterior algebra of the 1840s introduced such quantities.

Definition

A Grassmann algebra is generated by variables θ1, θ2, … satisfying θiθj = −θjθi for all i, j. Taking i = j gives θi² = 0: each generator is nilpotent. Any function of a single Grassmann variable therefore terminates: f(θ) = a + bθ, where a and b are ordinary (complex) numbers. With n generators, the algebra has dimension 2n, and any element splits into an even part (the "body", built from products of even numbers of generators together with commuting coefficients) and an odd part (the "soul").

Integration

Berezin integration defines an operation on Grassmann variables that behaves like both integration and differentiation: ∫ dθ = 0 and ∫ θ dθ = 1. This calculus makes Grassmann numbers indispensable in the path-integral formulation of fermionic quantum fields, since anticommuting variables reproduce the antisymmetry of fermionic states.

Applications

Grassmann numbers are central to supersymmetry, where superspace coordinates include fermionic directions, and to the BRST quantisation of gauge theories, where Faddeev–Popov ghost fields are Grassmann-valued. They also appear in the theory of differential forms, which form a Grassmann algebra, and in Berezin's formulation of analysis on supermanifolds.
