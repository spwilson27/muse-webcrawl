Undergarment

Undergarments, also called underwear or underclothes, are items of clothing worn beneath outer clothes, usually in direct contact with the skin. They protect outer garments from sweat and body oils, provide warmth, shape and support the body, and serve modesty and hygiene.

Types

Common undergarments include briefs, boxer shorts, undershirts and vests for men, and bras, panties, camisoles and slips for women, along with unisex items such as thermal long underwear ("long johns"), socks and hosiery. Foundation garments such as corsets, girdles and shapewear are designed to alter the body's silhouette. Some garments, including the Mormon temple garment and the Jewish tzitzit, have religious significance.

History

The earliest undergarment was the loincloth. In ancient Rome men and women wore a subligaculum and, for women, a breast band. Through the medieval and early modern periods, linen shirts, chemises and drawers served as washable layers beneath hard-to-clean outer clothing. Mass production in the 19th and 20th centuries introduced knitted cotton underwear, the union suit, the brassiere (patented by Mary Phelps Jacob in 1914) and elasticated briefs such as the Jockey briefs of 1935.

Materials

Cotton is the most common material for its absorbency and comfort; microfibre synthetics, silk, wool (for thermal wear) and elastane blends are also widely used.
