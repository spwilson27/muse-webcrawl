The Green Line in Nicosia

Green Line (Cyprus)

The Green Line (Greek: Πράσινη Γραμμή, Prasini Grammi; Turkish: Yeşil Hat) is the dividing line between the areas of Cyprus controlled by the Republic of Cyprus and those under the control of the self-declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus. Running for some 180 km across the island, it is patrolled by the United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP) within a buffer zone.

History

The line was first drawn at Christmas 1963, after intercommunal violence erupted between Greek Cypriots and Turkish Cypriots in Nicosia. Major-General Peter Young, commander of a British truce force, drew the line across a map of the capital with a green chinagraph pencil, separating the Greek and Turkish quarters of the city. After the events of 1974 — a Greek-backed coup and the subsequent Turkish military intervention — the line was extended across the whole island along the ceasefire lines, and the buffer zone became a de facto border.

The buffer zone

The buffer zone varies in width from a few metres in central Nicosia to several kilometres where it takes in farmland, and it contains abandoned villages, farms and the derelict Nicosia International Airport. Nicosia remains the last divided capital in Europe. Since 2003 a number of crossing points have opened, most notably at Ledra Street in 2008, allowing Cypriots and visitors to cross between the two sides.
