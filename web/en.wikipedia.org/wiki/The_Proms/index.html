The Proms, formally The Henry Wood Promenade Concerts presented by the BBC, is an eight-week summer season of daily orchestral classical music concerts and other events held annually in central London, principally at the Royal Albert Hall. Founded in 1895 by the impresario Robert Newman with conductor Henry Wood, the Proms were taken over by the BBC in 1927 and remain the world's largest classical music festival, typically comprising more than 70 concerts plus chamber recitals, late-night events, workshops and talks. The word "Proms" derives from "promenade concert," reflecting the tradition of audience members standing in the arena and gallery — the "Prommers" — whose inexpensive standing tickets remain a hallmark of the season.

History

Newman, manager of the Queen's Hall in Langham Place, aimed to build a broad audience for orchestral music by offering informal concerts with low-priced promenade tickets, refreshments and a relaxed atmosphere, and engaged the young Henry Wood to conduct. Wood used the platform to raise orchestral standards in Britain and to introduce British audiences to an extraordinary range of new and unfamiliar music — from Wagner, Brahms and Tchaikovsky to Debussy, Mahler, Schoenberg and Richard Strauss — as well as championing British composers. After Newman's finances collapsed, the BBC took over in 1927 under its music chief, and William Walton, Vaughan Williams and others benefited from the exposure. Queen's Hall was destroyed by an incendiary bomb in May 1941, and the Proms moved to the Royal Albert Hall, where they have remained.

Modern Proms

Directors of the Proms have included Sir Malcolm Sargent (chief conductor 1947–1966, indelibly associated with the festival), William Glock, Robert Ponsonby, John Drummond, Nicholas Kenyon and Roger Wright; the season is closely linked with the BBC Symphony Orchestra and other BBC ensembles, alongside visiting international orchestras and soloists. The Proms commission and premiere dozens of new works each season and have expanded to include jazz, world music, film and television soundtrack concerts, and relaxed and children's performances. Every concert is broadcast on BBC Radio 3, with many televised.

The Last Night

The season closes with the Last Night of the Proms, a famously exuberant concert whose second half traditionally features patriotic staples — Elgar's Pomp and Circumstance March No. 1 ("Land of Hope and Glory"), Arne's "Rule, Britannia!", Parry's "Jerusalem" and the national anthem — sung with flag-waving enthusiasm by the audience. Since 1996 the celebrations have extended to open-air "Proms in the Park" events across the United Kingdom linked by broadcast. The Last Night's rituals, and Wood's bust — garlanded with a laurel wreath by the Prommers each season — remain central symbols of the festival.
