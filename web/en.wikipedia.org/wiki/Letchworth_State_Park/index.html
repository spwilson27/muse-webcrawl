Letchworth State Park

Letchworth State Park is a 14,427-acre state park in western New York State, in Livingston and Wyoming counties. Nicknamed the "Grand Canyon of the East," it stretches roughly 17 miles along the Genesee River as it flows through a deep gorge with three major waterfalls.

LocationWestern New York, US
Area14,427 acres (58.4 km²)
RiverGenesee River
Established1906 (gift of William Pryor Letchworth)

Geography

The Genesee River runs north through a gorge with cliffs up to 600 feet high, passing the Upper, Middle and Lower Falls. The Middle Falls, near the Glen Iris Inn, is the largest of the three. The park's forests, trails and the Portageville entrance are popular for hiking, camping and whitewater rafting.

History

Industrialist William Pryor Letchworth bought land around the falls in the 1850s to protect it from development and bequeathed the core estate to New York State in 1906. The Mount Morris Dam, completed in 1952 just north of the park, controls flooding on the Genesee. The park was voted the top state park in the United States in a 2015 readers' poll.
