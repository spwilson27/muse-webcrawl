United States Department of Education

Formed: 17 October 1979
Headquarters: Lyndon Baines Johnson Building, Washington, D.C.
Head: Secretary of Education

The United States Department of Education is a cabinet-level department of the United States government. It began operating on May 4, 1980, having been created by the Department of Education Organization Act, championed by President Jimmy Carter, which split the former Department of Health, Education, and Welfare into the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services.

Functions

The department administers federal assistance to education, distributes financial aid to students through Federal Student Aid, enforces federal prohibitions on discrimination administered by the Office for Civil Rights, and collects data on America's schools through the National Center for Education Statistics. Major laws it administers include the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (as amended by the Every Student Succeeds Act), the Higher Education Act, and the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act.

Scale

Unlike education ministries in many countries, the department does not set curricula or operate schools; education in the United States is primarily a state and local responsibility. It is among the smallest Cabinet departments by number of employees, though its budget is large because of student loan and grant programs.

Criticism and abolition proposals

Since the Reagan administration, some politicians have proposed abolishing the department, arguing that education should be left entirely to the states; others defend its role in civil rights enforcement and student aid.
