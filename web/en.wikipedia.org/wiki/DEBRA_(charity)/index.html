DEBRA is a charity dedicated to people living with epidermolysis bullosa (EB), a group of genetic conditions that cause the skin to blister and tear at the slightest touch — hence the nickname "butterfly skin", and the description of affected children as "butterfly children". The name was originally an acronym for the Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa Research Association. DEBRA funds research, provides specialist healthcare and community support, and campaigns for better services.

History

DEBRA was founded in the United Kingdom in 1978 by Phyllis Hilton, whose daughter Debra had dystrophic EB, at a time when almost nothing was known about the condition. From a small parent support group it grew into a national charity funding pioneering research — including the identification of the genes responsible for EB — and supporting specialist EB clinical services at centres such as Great Ormond Street Hospital and St Thomas' Hospital in London. DEBRA of America (now debra of America) was founded in 1980, and DEBRA International coordinates a network of national groups in around 50 countries.

Activities

The charity funds research into gene, cell, protein and drug therapies aimed at treating and ultimately curing all forms of EB, and has supported drug-repurposing trials seeking to ease symptoms. It runs a network of charity shops in the UK, funds EB community support workers, provides grants and accessible holiday accommodation, and raises awareness of the condition through campaigns fronted by vice-presidents and patrons including football manager Gareth Southgate.

Epidermolysis bullosa

EB ranges from mild forms causing blistering of the hands and feet to severe forms that cause widespread wounds, fusion of fingers, anaemia, and a greatly increased risk of aggressive skin cancer in early adulthood. There is currently no cure; care focuses on wound management, pain relief and monitoring.
