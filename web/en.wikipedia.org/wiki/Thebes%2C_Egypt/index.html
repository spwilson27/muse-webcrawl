Thebes, Egypt


Ancient name: Waset
Location: Upper Egypt, around modern Luxor
Flourished: Middle and New Kingdoms
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site

Thebes (ancient Egyptian: Waset) was an ancient city of Egypt located along the Nile about 800 kilometres south of the Mediterranean. It rose to prominence as the capital of the New Kingdom and the centre of worship of the god Amun, and its ruins lie within and around the modern city of Luxor.

History

Thebes grew in importance during the Middle Kingdom and reached its zenith in the New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BC), when its temple estates amassed immense wealth. The city remained a religious capital long after political power moved north.

Archaeology

On the east bank stand the great temples of Karnak and Luxor; on the west bank lie the mortuary temples and royal tombs of the Valley of the Kings and Valley of the Queens, including the tomb of Tutankhamun. The whole area is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
