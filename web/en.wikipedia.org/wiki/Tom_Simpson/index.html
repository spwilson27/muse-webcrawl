Thomas Simpson (30 November 1937 – 13 July 1967) was one of Britain's most successful professional cyclists. He won the World Road Race Championship in 1965 and the three "Monument" classics of Milan–San Remo (1964), the Tour of Flanders (1961), and the Giro di Lombardia (1965). He died of heart failure during the 1967 Tour de France on Mont Ventoux; amphetamines were found in his bloodstream and jersey pocket, making his death a landmark in the history of doping in sport.

Career

Simpson was born in Haswell, County Durham, and grew up in Harworth, Nottinghamshire. He won a bronze medal in the team pursuit at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics and silver at the 1958 Empire Games, then turned professional in 1959, moving to Brittany in France. In 1962 he became the first British rider to wear the yellow jersey of the Tour de France, finishing sixth overall in Paris. His 1965 season brought the world championship in Lasarte, Spain, and the Giro di Lombardia, and he was voted BBC Sports Personality of the Year — still one of very few cyclists to win the award.

Death on Mont Ventoux

During the 13th stage of the 1967 Tour, on the barren upper slopes of Mont Ventoux in extreme heat, Simpson weaved across the road, collapsed, was helped back onto his bicycle, and collapsed again about a kilometre from the summit. He was flown to hospital in Avignon, where he died. His reported last words were "Put me back on my bike." The post-mortem found amphetamines and alcohol in his system; the official cause of death was heart failure from a combination of heat exhaustion, dehydration, alcohol, and amphetamines. A granite memorial stands near the spot, and cyclists still leave tributes there.

Legacy

Simpson's death prompted the International Olympic Committee and cycling authorities to begin formal drug testing and prohibited-substance lists. He remains one of Britain's greatest riders of his era, commemorated by memorials at Harworth, on Mont Ventoux, and at the National Cycling Centre, and by the biography Put Me Back on My Bike by William Fotheringham.
