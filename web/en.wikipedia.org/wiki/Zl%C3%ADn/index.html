Zlín

Zlín (Czech pronunciation: [zliːn]; German: Zlin) is a city in the Zlín Region of the Czech Republic, in the Moravian area of the country. From 1949 to 1990 it was called Gottwaldov, after the Czechoslovak leader Klement Gottwald. The city has a population of roughly 74,000 and lies on the Dřevnice River.

History

Zlín was first recorded in 1322 as a small craft and trading settlement. Its transformation began in 1894 when Tomáš Baťa founded a shoe factory there; the Baťa company grew into one of the world's largest footwear manufacturers and rebuilt Zlín as a model industrial garden city. Under Baťa, the population multiplied and the city acquired pioneering functionalist architecture in reinforced concrete, including the Baťa Skyscraper (Building 21, completed 1938), one of the tallest buildings in pre-war Czechoslovakia.

Modern city

After the communist era and the decline of the shoe industry, Zlín diversified into other manufacturing and services. It is home to Tomas Bata University (founded 2001). The city is noted for its functionalist urbanism, and since 1961 it has hosted the Zlín Film Festival, one of the world's oldest festivals of films for children and youth.
