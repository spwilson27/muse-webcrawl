David III of Tao

David III Kurapalates (Georgian: დავით III კურაპალატი; died 1001) was a Georgian prince of the Bagratid dynasty of Tao, a historic region of the Georgian–Armenian frontier, and a pivotal figure in the political unification of the Georgian lands in the late 10th century. Bearing the high Byzantine title of kouropalates, he was a powerful kingmaker whose support secured the imperial throne for Basil II during the Byzantine civil wars.

Reign

David inherited Tao from his father Adarnase V and expanded his power across southwestern Georgia, patronising the construction of great monasteries such as Oshki and Khakhuli. In 979 he sent 12,000 cavalry under John Tornike to crush the revolt of Bardas Skleros against the young emperor Basil II, a service rewarded with extensive territory and confirming David's status as the most powerful ruler in the Caucasus. Later, however, he backed the rebel Bardas Phokas (987–989); after the revolt failed he was forced to make the emperor his heir.

Death and aftermath

David died childless in 1001 — Georgian chronicles suggest poisoning — and under the terms of his submission his core Tao lands passed to Byzantium, while his Georgian possessions passed to his adopted heir, Bagrat III, the first king of a united Georgia. His former territories became the Byzantine theme of Iberia.
