Simon Peyton Jones (born 18 January 1958) is a British computer scientist who researches functional programming languages and their implementation. He is one of the principal designers of the Haskell programming language and the lead developer of the Glasgow Haskell Compiler (GHC).

Career

Peyton Jones studied electrical sciences at Trinity College, Cambridge. He taught at University College London and then at the University of Glasgow, where he led the development of GHC. In 1998 he moved to Microsoft Research Cambridge, remaining there for over two decades; he later joined Epic Games as an Engineering Fellow, working on the Verse programming language.

Contributions

His research covers lazy functional languages, type systems (including type classes and generalised algebraic data types), software transactional memory, and compiler technology. He co-authored the Haskell 98 language report and influential papers on implementing functional languages on stock hardware. He has also championed computing education in schools, chairing the Computing at School group in the UK. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2016.
