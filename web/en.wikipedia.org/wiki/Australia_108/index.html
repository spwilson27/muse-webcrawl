Australia 108 (previously stylised 108) is a residential supertall skyscraper in the Southbank precinct of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. Completed in 2020, it rises 316.7 metres (1,039 feet) over 100 floors, making it the tallest building in Australia by roof height and the first building in the Southern Hemisphere to have 100 floors. Its full height is exceeded in Australia only by the Q1 tower on the Gold Coast when measured to its spire.

Design

Designed by Fender Katsalidis Architects, the tower's sculptural form features a distinctive protruding section around the 83rd to 85th floors, known as the "Star Club," inspired by the geometry of the Commonwealth Star on the Australian flag. The Star Club houses resident amenities including pools, a gym, and lounge areas with cantilevered views over the city. The building contains more than 1,100 apartments.

History

The site, at 70 Southbank Boulevard, was previously planned for a 72-storey tower. Singapore-based developer Aspial Corporation acquired the site in 2013 and advanced the taller proposal, which received planning approval in 2014 despite debate over its shadows and proximity to the neighbouring Eureka Tower. Construction began in 2015 and proceeded in stages, with completion reached in 2020.

Records

On completion, Australia 108 became the tallest residential building in Australia by roof height and the second-tallest structure in the country overall after Q1's spire. Its 100 floors made it a landmark of the Melbourne skyline and a symbol of the city's high-density apartment boom of the 2010s.
