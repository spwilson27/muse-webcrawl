Paramount Television


Type: Division
Industry: Television production
Founded: 1967 (original); 2013 (revived)
Headquarters: Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Paramount Television is the television production division of Paramount Pictures, itself part of Paramount Global. The name has been used by several incarnations of the studio's television arm over more than half a century.

History

The original Paramount Television was established in 1967, growing out of Desilu Productions, which Paramount's then-parent Gulf+Western had acquired. The unit inherited a library that included series such as Star Trek and Mission: Impossible, and went on to produce long-running hits including Happy Days, Laverne & Shirley, Cheers, Frasier, Family Ties, and Taxi. For decades it was one of the dominant suppliers of programming to the American broadcast networks.

Following the 2005 split of Viacom and CBS Corporation, the television library and production operations largely passed to CBS, which folded them into what became CBS Television Studios (later CBS Studios). Paramount retained the name for film-derived projects.

Revival

In 2013 Paramount revived Paramount Television as a production banner focused on developing series based on the studio's film properties and original concepts. Its productions included Grease: Live, The Alienist, 13 Reasons Why (for Netflix), Jack Ryan (for Amazon Prime Video), and Station Eleven. In 2020 the unit was renamed Paramount Television Studios amid corporate restructuring.
