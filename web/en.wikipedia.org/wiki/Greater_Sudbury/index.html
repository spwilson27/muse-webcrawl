Greater Sudbury

Greater Sudbury (French: Grand Sudbury) is a city in Ontario, Canada. It is the largest city in Northern Ontario by population, with a population of 166,004 at the 2021 Canadian Census, and the largest by land area as well, at 3,228.35 square kilometres. Greater Sudbury was formed in 2001 by amalgamating the cities and towns of the former Regional Municipality of Sudbury with several previously unincorporated townships.

History

The Sudbury region takes its name from Sudbury, Suffolk, England, the birthplace of the wife of a Canadian Pacific Railway commissioner. Nickel ore was discovered during railway construction in 1883, and the city grew into one of the world's largest centres of nickel mining. The Sudbury Basin, the geological structure underlying the area, is the eroded remnant of a meteorite impact crater formed about 1.85 billion years ago, and is rich in nickel, copper, platinum, and other metals.

Geography

The city lies on the Canadian Shield and contains more than 300 lakes, including Lake Wanapitei, the largest lake in the world completely contained within the boundaries of a single city. Ramsey Lake, in the city's south end, held that distinction before amalgamation. Much of the landscape was blackened and denuded by decades of smelter emissions in the twentieth century; a major regreening program begun in the 1970s has since planted millions of trees and is regarded as a landmark in environmental restoration.

Economy

Mining remains the dominant industry, with major operations run by Vale and Glencore, but the economy has diversified into health care, education, government services, tourism, and film and television production. The city is home to Laurentian University, Cambrian College, Collège Boréal, the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory's successor laboratory SNOLAB, and Science North, whose snowflake-shaped building is a prominent landmark alongside the Big Nickel.

Demographics

Greater Sudbury has a large Franco-Ontarian community; roughly a quarter of residents report French as a mother tongue, and the city is known for its bilingual character. Indigenous peoples, principally Anishinaabe of the Atikameksheng Anishnawbek and Wahnapitae First Nations, have a long presence in the region.
