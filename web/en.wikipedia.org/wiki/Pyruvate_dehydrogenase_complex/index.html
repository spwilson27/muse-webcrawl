The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC) is a large multi-enzyme complex that catalyses the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA and carbon dioxide. It links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle, and in aerobic organisms it is essentially irreversible, committing the carbon of carbohydrates to oxidation or to fatty acid synthesis.

Schematic of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex core and its component enzymes

Components

The complex consists of multiple copies of three enzymes:

  • E1, pyruvate dehydrogenase (EC 1.2.4.1), a thiamine pyrophosphate-dependent enzyme that decarboxylates pyruvate.
  • E2, dihydrolipoyl transacetylase (EC 2.3.1.12), which forms the structural core of the complex and transfers the acetyl group to coenzyme A, using a swinging lipoamide arm.
  • E3, dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase (EC 1.8.1.4), an FAD-dependent enzyme that reoxidises the lipoamide, producing NADH.

In mammals the complex also contains an E3-binding protein and the regulatory enzymes pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase and pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatase. The mammalian complex is roughly 7.5 to 10 megadaltons in mass and about 50 nanometres in diameter, among the largest known enzyme assemblies.

Reaction

The overall reaction is:

pyruvate + CoA + NAD+ → acetyl-CoA + CO2 + NADH + H+

Five cofactors take part: thiamine pyrophosphate, lipoamide, coenzyme A, FAD, and NAD+. The related α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complexes share the same architecture and use the same E3 enzyme.

Regulation

Because its reaction is a committed step, the complex is tightly regulated. It is inactivated by phosphorylation of E1 by pyruvate dehydrogenase kinases, which are activated by high levels of NADH, acetyl-CoA, and ATP, and it is reactivated by pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatases, which are stimulated by Ca2+ and, in adipose tissue, by insulin.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of the complex, most often affecting the E1α subunit encoded on the X chromosome, causes congenital lactic acidosis and severe neurological impairment. Arsenite and other arsenicals inhibit the complex by binding its lipoamide cofactor.
