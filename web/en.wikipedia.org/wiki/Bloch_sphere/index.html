Bloch sphere

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In quantum mechanics, the Bloch sphere is a geometrical representation of the pure state space of a two-level quantum system, or qubit. It is named after the physicist Felix Bloch. Every pure state of a qubit corresponds to a unique point on the surface of a unit sphere, and mixed states correspond to points inside it.

Definition

A general qubit state can be written as |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + eiφ sin(θ/2)|1⟩, where θ and φ are the polar and azimuthal angles of the point on the sphere. The north pole represents |0⟩ and the south pole |1⟩; points on the equator represent equal superpositions of the two basis states.

Operations

Unitary operations on a single qubit appear on the Bloch sphere as rotations of the state vector. Measurement in the computational basis projects the vector onto the vertical axis, yielding |0⟩ with probability cos²(θ/2) and |1⟩ with probability sin²(θ/2).

Generalisation

The Bloch representation generalises to mixed states via the density matrix, and to higher-dimensional systems through generalised Bloch vectors, though these no longer map onto an ordinary sphere. See also Quantum computing.
