Gulch

A gulch is a deep, narrow ravine with a marked gradient, typically eroded by running water. The term is used mainly in the western United States and Canada. Gulches are similar to arroyos and gullies but generally larger, and they may contain a seasonal stream or be dry except during flash floods.

Gulches were significant sites during western gold rushes, as gold-bearing gravels accumulated in their beds; many mining camps and ghost towns in Montana, Colorado and California bear names derived from the gulches where gold was found. Because water can rise rapidly after distant rain, dry gulches are hazardous during storms.
