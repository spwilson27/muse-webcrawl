Mansion

A mansion is a large dwelling house. The word derives from the Latin word mansio, meaning 'dwelling', through Old French. Within the mansion, the word manse is used in some contexts for the house of a minister. The word itself derives from another Latin word, manere, meaning 'to dwell'. Although there is no precise measure, a mansion is generally a house of at least 5,000 square feet (460 m²) with substantial grounds and numerous reception rooms and bedrooms, and in the modern real-estate market the term often denotes a luxury residence well above local norms.

History

Large elite residences have existed since antiquity, from Roman villas to medieval castles and great country houses. In Renaissance Italy, palazzi served as urban mansions for noble families. The classical revival of the 17th and 18th centuries produced great English country houses such as Blenheim Palace and Holkham Hall. In the 19th century, industrial wealth produced mansions in styles including Second Empire, Queen Anne, and Beaux-Arts, exemplified by the "cottages" of Newport, Rhode Island, such as The Breakers (1895).

Modern usage

Today, in the United States and elsewhere, "mansion" is largely a marketing term of real-estate practice, denoting large, lavishly appointed single-family homes, often with amenities such as home theatres, pools, and staff quarters. Regional terms include villa, château, manor house, and stately home.
