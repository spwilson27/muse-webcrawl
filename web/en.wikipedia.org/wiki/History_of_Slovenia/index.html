History of Slovenia

The history of Slovenia traces the Slovene lands from Slavic settlement in the 6th century through centuries of Habsburg rule, incorporation into Yugoslavia in the 20th century, and independence in 1991, when Slovenia became a sovereign state for the first time.

Early history

Slavic tribes settled the eastern Alps and the Karst in the late 6th century. The principality of Carantania, formed in the 7th century, is regarded as an early Slavic state; its rulers were inaugurated in a rite conducted in Slovene that continued for centuries. From the 8th century the region was absorbed into the Frankish and later the Holy Roman Empire, and by the late Middle Ages most Slovene lands — Carniola, Styria, Carinthia and Gorizia — had passed to the Habsburgs, under whom they remained, apart from a brief Napoleonic interlude, until 1918. The Freising manuscripts (c. 1000) are the oldest surviving texts in Slovene, and Primož Trubar printed the first Slovene books in 1550.

The 19th century and Yugoslavia

The revolutions of 1848 produced the first Slovene national programme, "United Slovenia". After the First World War, most Slovenes joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia), while the Littoral Slovenes came under Italy. During the Second World War the territory was partitioned between Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy and Hungary; the Liberation Front organised resistance, and the Partisans emerged victorious. In 1945 Slovenia became a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia, gaining Trieste's hinterland but not the city itself.

Independence

Slovenia was Yugoslavia's most prosperous republic. In a December 1990 plebiscite nearly 89% voted for independence, declared on 25 June 1991. The brief Ten-Day War with the Yugoslav People's Army ended with the Brioni Agreement, and Slovenia gained international recognition in early 1992. It joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2007, the first of the post-2004 member states to do so.
