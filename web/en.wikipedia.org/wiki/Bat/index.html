Bat

Bats are mammals of the order Chiroptera. With their forelimbs adapted as wings, they are the only mammals capable of sustained flight. Bats are the second largest order of mammals after rodents, with more than 1,400 species — about 20% of all mammal species — found on every continent except Antarctica.

Biology

Bats range from the 2-gram bumblebee bat, among the smallest mammals, to flying foxes with 1.7 m wingspans. Traditionally divided into megabats (mostly fruit-eating) and microbats, most species are nocturnal insectivores that navigate and hunt by echolocation, emitting ultrasound and listening for echoes. Others eat fruit, nectar, fish, or blood (the three vampire bat species of Latin America). Wings are membranes stretched between greatly elongated fingers.

Ecology and humans

Bats pollinate agave, durian, and many tropical plants, disperse seeds, and consume vast quantities of pest insects. Threats include habitat loss, wind turbines, and white-nose syndrome, a fungal disease devastating North American cave bats. Bats are natural reservoirs of several viruses, including rabies and coronaviruses, though direct transmission to humans is rare. In culture they range from symbols of luck in China to creatures of horror in the West, capped by Batman and vampire lore. See also mammal.
