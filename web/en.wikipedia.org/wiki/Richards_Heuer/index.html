Richards Heuer

Richards J. Heuer Jr. (1935 – February 27, 2018) was an American intelligence analyst at the Central Intelligence Agency, best known for his book Psychology of Intelligence Analysis (1999) and for developing the structured analytic technique known as Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH), which is now taught across the United States Intelligence Community and allied services.

Career

Heuer joined the CIA in the 1950s and spent much of his career in analysis and methodology, working on problems including the detection of deception. He became a prominent internal advocate for applying cognitive psychology to intelligence work.

Psychology of Intelligence Analysis

His 1999 book, published by the CIA's Center for the Study of Intelligence and widely distributed, describes how mental models, confirmation bias and other cognitive limitations distort analysis, and argues that analysts should seek to disconfirm hypotheses rather than accumulate supporting evidence.

Analysis of Competing Hypotheses

ACH formalises this: the analyst lists competing hypotheses, evaluates each item of evidence for consistency with each hypothesis, and focuses on the evidence that discriminates between them, with the aim of identifying the least-disconfirmed explanation. Heuer later co-authored Structured Analytic Techniques for Intelligence Analysis (2010) with Randolph H. Pherson.
