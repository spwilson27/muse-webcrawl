Ashiq

An ashiq accompanying himself on the saz

An ashiq (Azerbaijani: aşıq; Turkish: âşık; Persian: عاشیق) is a singer-poet and folk musician in the cultures of Azerbaijan, Turkey, Iran and the wider Caucasus and Central Asia. Ashiqs accompany their songs on the saz, a long-necked lute, and perform a repertoire that combines lyrical love poetry, epic tales (dastan) and improvised verbal duels.

History

The ashiq tradition descends from the ozan, the minstrel-bards of the early Turkic peoples, and developed its distinctive form from around the 15th and 16th centuries. The word ashiq derives from the Arabic ʿāshiq, meaning "lover", reflecting the central role of both earthly and divine love in the poetry. Figures such as Shah Ismail I, who wrote poetry under the pen name Khatai, and the legendary Ashiq Qarib, helped shape the canon.

Performance

Ashiqs traditionally learned their art through apprenticeship to a master (usta), memorising a large repertoire of songs and epics in recognised melodic modes. A characteristic feature is the deyişmə, a sung contest of wit and riddles between two ashiqs. Performances take place at weddings, festivals and coffee houses.

Recognition

In 2009 the art of Azerbaijani ashiqs was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Famous ashiqs include Ashiq Alasgar, Ashiq Huseyn and, in the modern era, performers who have carried the tradition onto concert stages and recordings.
