Cycling infrastructure

Cycling infrastructure is all infrastructure cyclists are allowed to use. Bikeways include bike paths, bike lanes, cycle tracks, protected intersections, and shared-use paths. Good road design, man-made or natural, deliberately encourages cycling, including adequate parking facilities and systems such as bike-sharing.

Types

Bike lanes are marked portions of the roadway designated for cyclists. Cycle tracks are physically separated from motor traffic by curbs, bollards, or parked cars, and are associated with higher cycling rates and improved perceived safety. Bike paths are off-road routes for cyclists and often pedestrians. Bicycle boulevards are low-speed streets optimized for bicycle traffic. At intersections, treatments include bike boxes (advanced stop lines), dedicated signals, and protected intersection designs pioneered in the Netherlands.

Networks and planning

Advocates argue that coherent, connected networks — rather than isolated segments — are key to increasing cycling mode share, citing high-cycling cities such as Amsterdam, Copenhagen, and Utrecht, where cycling accounts for a large share of trips. Supporting infrastructure includes secure bicycle parking, racks on public transport, signage and wayfinding, and end-of-trip facilities.

Safety

Studies generally find that separated infrastructure reduces cyclist injuries, and that increases in cycling produce a "safety in numbers" effect. Debates continue over mandatory helmet laws, vehicular cycling approaches, and the allocation of road space.
