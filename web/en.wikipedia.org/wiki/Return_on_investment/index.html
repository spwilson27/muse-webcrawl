Return on investment

Return on investment (ROI) is a performance measure used to evaluate the efficiency or profitability of an investment, or to compare the efficiency of a number of different investments. In its simplest form it is the ratio of net profit (gain minus cost) to the cost of the investment, expressed as a percentage:

ROI = (Net profit ÷ Cost of investment) × 100

Because of its simplicity and versatility, ROI is widely used in business for appraising projects, marketing campaigns, equipment purchases and divisions, and by individuals for comparing assets such as shares and property.

Limitations

ROI in its basic form ignores the time value of money: a 50 per cent return earned over one year is treated the same as one earned over ten. It also depends heavily on what is counted as "profit" and "cost", which vary between organisations and can be manipulated. For comparisons over time, analysts use annualised returns or discounted measures such as net present value (NPV) and the internal rate of return (IRR). Related ratios include return on assets (ROA) and return on equity (ROE).

Uses

In marketing, ROI is used to judge campaign profitability; in capital budgeting, to rank projects subject to correction for scale and duration; and in social policy, "social return on investment" (SROI) attempts to quantify wider social and environmental value in monetary terms.
