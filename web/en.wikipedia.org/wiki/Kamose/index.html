Kamose

Kamose was the last king of the Theban Seventeenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt and a key figure in the wars that expelled the Hyksos from Egypt. He reigned for about three years, around 1555–1550 BC, succeeding Seqenenre Tao and preceding his brother Ahmose I, founder of the Eighteenth Dynasty.

War against the Hyksos

Two stelae erected by Kamose at Karnak record his decision, against the advice of his council, to attack the Hyksos king Apepi, who ruled northern Egypt from Avaris. Kamose campaigned northward down the Nile, captured a Hyksos messenger carrying a letter from Apepi to the king of Kush seeking an alliance against Thebes, and raided Hyksos territory. A third source, the Carnarvon Tablet, preserves a copy of the first stela's text.

Death and succession

Kamose died before completing the reconquest, and the war was finished by his brother Ahmose I, who captured Avaris and founded the New Kingdom. Kamose's modest pyramid tomb was at Dra Abu el-Naga; his coffin was found in 1857, containing a mummy in poor condition adorned with weapons, including a dagger now in Brussels and an axe in the Ashmolean Museum.
