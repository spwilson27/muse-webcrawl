Henry Hudson (c. 1565 – disappeared 23 June 1611) was an English sea explorer and navigator during the early 17th century, best known for his explorations of present-day Canada and parts of the north-eastern United States in search of a northern passage to Asia.

Voyages

In 1607 and 1608 Hudson sailed for the Muscovy Company seeking a north-east passage. In 1609, sailing for the Dutch East India Company aboard the Halve Maen, he explored the river that now bears his name and laid the foundation for Dutch colonisation of New Netherland. In 1610–11, aboard the Discovery for an English syndicate, he passed through Hudson Strait into Hudson Bay and wintered in James Bay.

Mutiny and death

In June 1611, after a winter of hardship, part of the crew mutinied. Hudson, his teenage son John, and seven loyal or sick men were set adrift in a small boat and never seen again. The bay, strait, river, and the city of Hudson, New York, are named after him.
