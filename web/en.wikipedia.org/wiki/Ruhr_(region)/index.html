The Ruhr (German: Ruhrgebiet, also Ruhrpott) is a polycentric urban area in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. With a population density of 2,800/km² and a population of over 5 million, it is the largest urban area in Germany and the third largest in the European Union. It consists of several large industrial cities bordered by the rivers Ruhr to the south, Rhine to the west and Lippe to the north. In the southwest it borders the Bergisches Land.

Geography

The urban area stretches from the Lower Rhine Basin eastward to the Westphalian Basin and southward to the hills of the Rhenish Massif. It is considered part of the larger Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region of more than 10 million people. The region takes its name from the Ruhr river, a right tributary of the Rhine.

History

During the 19th century, the Ruhr area grew into one of the world's great industrial regions, driven by its rich coal deposits and the steel industry that grew around them. Cities such as Essen (home of Krupp) and Dortmund expanded rapidly as migrants arrived from across Germany and Poland. After the Second World War the region lay in the British occupation zone and became the industrial heartland of West Germany's Wirtschaftswunder.

From the 1960s onward, the decline of coal mining and steel production forced a long structural transformation. Former industrial sites such as the Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex in Essen — now a UNESCO World Heritage Site — were converted to cultural uses, and the region developed universities, technology centres and service industries. In 2010 the Ruhr was a European Capital of Culture under the name RUHR.2010.
