Hadal zone

The hadal zone, also called the hadopelagic zone, is the deepest region of the ocean, lying within oceanic trenches from about 6,000 to 11,000 metres (20,000 to 36,000 feet) below sea level. Named after Hades, the Greek underworld, it is the least explored environment on Earth.

Conditions

Conditions in the hadal zone are extreme: complete darkness, temperatures close to freezing, and hydrostatic pressures exceeding 1,000 standard atmospheres in the deepest trenches, such as Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench. No sunlight reaches these depths, so ecosystems depend on organic matter sinking from above ("marine snow") and on chemosynthesis near geological features.

Life

Despite the pressure, hadal trenches support specialised fauna, including amphipods, snailfish (among the deepest-living fish), holothurians, and microbial communities. Adaptations include pressure-resistant proteins and membranes enriched in unsaturated fatty acids. Only a handful of crewed and uncrewed vehicles, from the bathyscaphe Trieste in 1960 to modern full-ocean-depth submersibles, have visited the zone.
