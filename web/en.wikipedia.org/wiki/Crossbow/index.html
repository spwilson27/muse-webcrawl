Crossbow

A crossbow is a ranged weapon using an elastic launching device consisting of a bow-like assembly called a prod, mounted horizontally on a main frame called a tiller, which is hand-held in a similar fashion to the stock of a long gun. Crossbows shoot arrow-like projectiles called bolts or quarrels.

Crossbow

Type: Ranged weapon
Origin: China, ancient Greece

History

Crossbows were invented in ancient China and independently known in Greece (the gastraphetes). In China, bronze crossbow locks date to the Warring States period, and crossbows were important weapons for Han armies. In medieval Europe the crossbow was a major military weapon, able to be used with little training and capable of piercing armour; the Church at times sought to restrict its use against Christians.

Mechanics

The string is drawn back — by hand, lever, windlass or cranequin — and held by a lock (nut and trigger), allowing the weapon to be kept loaded and aimed. This gives greater draw weight than a hand bow but a slower rate of shooting.

Modern use

Today crossbows are used for target shooting, hunting in some jurisdictions, and some military and law-enforcement roles.
