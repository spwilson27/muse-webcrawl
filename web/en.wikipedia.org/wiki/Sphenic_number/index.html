Sphenic number

In number theory, a sphenic number (from Greek sphēn, "wedge") is a positive integer that is the product of three distinct prime numbers. Equivalently, a sphenic number is a square-free integer with exactly three prime factors. The Möbius function takes the value −1 at every sphenic number, since μ(pqr) = (−1)3 = −1 for distinct primes p, q, r.

Examples

The smallest sphenic number is 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. The first few sphenic numbers are:

30, 42, 66, 70, 78, 102, 105, 110, 114, 130, 138, 154, 165, 170, 174, 182, 186, 190, 195, 222, 230, 231, 238, 246, 255, 258, 266, 273, 282, 285, 286, 290, …

Properties

  • Every sphenic number has exactly eight divisors: 1, the three primes, the three products of pairs of primes, and the number itself.
  • The divisor function satisfies d(n) = 23 = 8 for a sphenic number n — though the converse is false, since the same holds for products p3 and p7.
  • Consecutive sphenic numbers exist in runs; for example 130, 131 is not such a pair, but pairs such as (230, 231) and (606, 607)… more precisely, sphenic numbers occasionally occur consecutively, e.g. 645 = 3 × 5 × 43 and 646 = 2 × 17 × 19.
  • The count of sphenic numbers up to x grows asymptotically like (ln ln x)2 x / (2 ln x), by the Landau–Selberg theory of integers with a fixed number of prime factors.

Generalization

Sphenic numbers are the simplest case of integers with three prime factors counted without multiplicity. The concept generalises the earlier notions of semiprime (two prime factors) and almost-prime classes, and sphenic numbers appear in recreational number theory contexts such as the enumeration of square-free integers by their number of prime divisors.
