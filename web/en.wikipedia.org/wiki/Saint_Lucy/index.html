Saint Lucy

Saint Lucy (Italian: Lucia di Siracusa; 283–304), also called Saint Lucia, was a Christian martyr who died during the Diocletianic Persecution. A virgin martyr from Syracuse, Sicily, she is venerated in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran traditions. Her feast day, 13 December, is widely celebrated in Scandinavia as Saint Lucy's Day, a festival of light in the darkness of winter.

Saint Lucy, patron saint of the blind and of sight.

Life and legend

According to tradition, Lucy was born to noble and wealthy parents in Syracuse. Having consecrated her virginity to Christ, she distributed her dowry to the poor, angering the pagan suitor to whom she had been betrothed. Denounced as a Christian to the authorities during the persecution of Diocletian, she refused to renounce her faith and was executed in 304, traditionally by a sword thrust through the throat. Later accounts added episodes of torture, including the legend that her eyes were gouged out — hence her patronage of the blind and her depiction carrying her eyes on a dish.

Veneration

Lucy is one of the few female saints mentioned in the Canon of the Mass, and her cult spread rapidly through the early Church. She is the patron saint of Syracuse and of the blind, glaziers, and those suffering eye diseases. Her name derives from the Latin lux (light), which underlies her association with light returning after the winter solstice — in the old calendar, her feast fell on the shortest day of the year.
