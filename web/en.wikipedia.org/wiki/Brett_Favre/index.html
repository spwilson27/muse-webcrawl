Brett Favre

Brett Lorenzo Favre (born 10 October 1969) is an American former professional football quarterback who played in the National Football League (NFL) for 20 seasons, primarily with the Green Bay Packers. Famous for his durability, arm strength, and improvisational "gunslinger" style, Favre holds the NFL record of 297 consecutive regular-season starts (321 including playoffs) and was the first quarterback to reach 500 touchdown passes, 70,000 passing yards, and victories over all 32 NFL teams.

Career

Favre was drafted by the Atlanta Falcons in 1991 and traded to Green Bay in 1992, where he began his record streak that September. Under coaches Mike Holmgren and Mike Sherman he led the Packers to victory in Super Bowl XXXI over the New England Patriots and a return trip in Super Bowl XXXII, won three consecutive league MVP awards — a feat unmatched — and set then-records for passing touchdowns and yards. After announcing retirement in March 2008, he returned with the New York Jets for one season, then joined the division-rival Minnesota Vikings, leading them to the NFC Championship Game in 2009 before finally retiring after the 2010 season. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2016, and his number 4 was retired by the Packers.

Legacy and controversies

Favre's career is defined by longevity and toughness — he played through numerous injuries — along with a league-record interception total reflecting his risk-taking style. Memorable moments include the game the night after his father's death in December 2003, when he threw for 399 yards and four touchdowns. In later years Favre has faced public scrutiny, including an NFL fine in 2010 in an investigation involving messages to a Jets employee, and, in Mississippi, litigation and investigations over welfare funds directed to projects with which he was associated; Favre denied wrongdoing and repaid certain speaking fees.
