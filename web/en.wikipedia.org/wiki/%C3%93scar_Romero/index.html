Óscar Romero

Saint Óscar Arnulfo Romero y Galdámez (15 August 1917 – 24 March 1980) was a Salvadoran Roman Catholic prelate who served as the fourth Archbishop of San Salvador. A voice for the poor and victims of El Salvador's escalating violence, he denounced human rights abuses by the military regime in widely broadcast sermons. He was assassinated while celebrating Mass in 1980, at the outset of the Salvadoran Civil War. Pope Francis canonised him in 2018.

Early life and priesthood

Romero was born in Ciudad Barrios and trained for the priesthood in San Miguel, San Salvador, and Rome, where he was ordained in 1942. He served as a parish priest and diocesan administrator, becoming auxiliary bishop of San Salvador in 1970 and bishop of Santiago de María in 1974. Initially seen as conservative, his outlook was transformed by the repression he witnessed and by the 1977 murder of his friend, the Jesuit priest Rutilio Grande.

Archbishop

Appointed archbishop in February 1977, Romero used his Sunday sermons, broadcast nationwide by radio, to document killings, disappearances, and torture, and to demand an end to repression. Days before his death he appealed directly to soldiers to disobey orders to kill. On 24 March 1980, a gunman shot him as he celebrated Mass in the chapel of the Divina Providencia hospital. A UN truth commission later concluded the assassination was ordered by Major Roberto D'Aubuisson; no one was convicted.

Veneration

Romero was beatified in San Salvador in May 2015 and canonised by Pope Francis on 14 October 2018. He is honoured in the Anglican Communion and Lutheran churches as well, and a statue of him stands above the west door of Westminster Abbey among ten 20th-century martyrs.
