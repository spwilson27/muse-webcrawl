Patent slip

A patent slip, also called a marine railway, is an inclined plane extending from a shipyard into the water, fitted with a cradle on rails that can be winched ashore carrying a vessel, so the hull can be inspected, cleaned, and repaired out of the water. It is a smaller, cheaper alternative to a dry dock.

History

The device was patented in the early 19th century — the name reflects Thomas Morton's 1818 Scottish patent for a ship-hauling slip — and spread to shipyards and ports around the world. Vessels are floated over the submerged cradle, secured, and hauled up by steam or electric winches.

Use

Patent slips typically handle vessels of up to a few thousand tonnes and remain in use at smaller yards, fishing ports, and heritage sites, such as the patent slips at Nelson and Evans Bay in New Zealand.
