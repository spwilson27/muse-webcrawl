The Dúnedain (singular Dúnadan) are a fictional people in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. They are the descendants of the Númenóreans who survived the fall of Númenor and settled in Middle-earth, founding the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor. In Sindarin, Dúnedain means "Men of the West".

History

Led by Elendil and his sons Isildur and Anárion, the Faithful of Númenor escaped its destruction and established realms in exile. The northern kingdom of Arnor later fragmented and fell, and its Dúnedain dwindled into the secretive Rangers of the North, led by the Chieftains descended from Isildur — the last of whom, Aragorn, was crowned King of the Reunited Kingdom after the War of the Ring.

Characteristics

The Dúnedain were longer-lived, taller, and wiser than lesser Men, though their gifts waned over the centuries through intermarriage and decline. The Rangers protected the Shire and Bree-land unthanked for centuries.
