Education in Brazil

Education in Brazil is overseen by the federal Ministry of Education and delivered mainly by states and municipalities. Under the 1988 Constitution education is a right of all and a duty of the state and the family. Schooling is compulsory and free in public institutions from ages 4 to 17.

Structure

Basic education comprises early childhood education (creches and pre-school), ensino fundamental (primary and lower secondary, nine years, ages 6–14) and ensino médio (upper secondary, three years, ages 15–17). School quality is monitored through national assessments such as SAEB and the IDEB index. Access to public universities is highly competitive and is governed largely by entrance examinations — traditionally the vestibular, increasingly the national ENEM exam, whose scores feed the SISU allocation system. Affirmative-action laws reserve places in federal universities for public-school graduates, with sub-quotas by income and race.

Higher education

Brazil has large public federal and state universities, such as the University of São Paulo, which are free of charge, alongside a much larger private sector that enrols the majority of students. The country expanded access substantially in the 2000s and 2010s through programmes such as PROUNI scholarships and FIES student loans.

Challenges

Despite near-universal primary enrolment and a literacy rate above 90 percent, Brazilian students perform below OECD averages in reading, mathematics and science in the PISA assessments, and attainment varies sharply by region and income. Funding is distributed partly through the FUNDEB fund, which redistributes resources to poorer states and municipalities. Recurring debates concern teacher pay, secondary-school reform and the quality gap between public and private schools.
