Lakemba Mosque, officially the Imam Ali bin Abi Taleb Mosque, is one of the largest mosques in Australia, located in the suburb of Lakemba in southwestern Sydney, New South Wales. It is administered by the Lebanese Muslim Association (LMA), whose headquarters adjoin the mosque.

History

The Lebanese Muslim Association purchased land at Lakemba in the 1960s and first used a small house on the site for prayer before the purpose-built mosque was completed and opened in 1977. The mosque became the focal point of Sydney's large Lebanese and broader Muslim community, situated in a suburb where Muslims form a very large share of the population.

Community role

The mosque hosts daily prayers, Friday congregational prayers drawing thousands, and major gatherings for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, when worshippers fill the surrounding streets. It is frequently the mosque from which Australian Muslim community statements issue, and its representatives feature prominently in national media coverage of Muslim affairs. Lakemba's surrounding streets, especially Haldon Street, are known for Ramadan night markets during the fasting month.
