A wrecking ball is a heavy steel ball, usually suspended from a crane, that is used to demolish large buildings. It was most commonly in use during the 1950s and 1960s. Several wrecking companies claim to have invented the wrecking ball. The balls are made from forged steel, meaning the steel is not cast into a mould in a molten state; rather, it is formed under very high pressure while the steel is red hot to compress and strengthen it.

Use

The ball is swung by the crane operator against the structure to be demolished, or dropped onto it. The kinetic energy of a ball weighing several tonnes can crack masonry and concrete, but the method is comparatively imprecise and can throw debris unpredictably, so it requires skilled operators and wide exclusion zones. Wrecking balls are sometimes pear-shaped or spherical, with a thickened top through which the suspension chain passes.

Decline

With the rise of hydraulic excavators fitted with high-reach arms, shears and crushers, and the need for more controlled demolition and recycling of materials, wrecking ball use has sharply declined. It survives mostly as a symbol of demolition — for example in popular culture and music — though balls are still occasionally employed on heavy concrete structures such as piers, silos and foundations.
