Cirque du Soleil

Cirque du Soleil (French: [siʁk dy sɔlɛj], "Circus of the Sun" or "Sun Circus") is a Canadian entertainment company and the largest contemporary circus producer in the world. Located in the inner-city area of Saint-Michel, Montreal, it was founded in Baie-Saint-Paul on 16 June 1984 by former street performers Guy Laliberté and Gilles Ste-Croix.

History

Originating as a performing troupe called Les Échassiers ("The Stilt Walkers"), Cirque du Soleil toured Quebec in various forms between 1979 and 1983. Its first official production, Le Grand Tour du Cirque du Soleil, was staged in 1984 for the 450th anniversary celebrations of Jacques Cartier's arrival in Canada. The company reinvented the circus by combining circus arts with street entertainment, eliminating animal acts and emphasising human performance, elaborate costumes, original music, and theatrical storytelling.

Productions

Cirque du Soleil expanded rapidly through the 1990s and 2000s, growing from one production to dozens of resident and touring shows around the world, including Mystère, O, Ka, and Michael Jackson: One in Las Vegas, and touring productions such as Alegría, Quidam, Varekai, and Kooza. The shows have been seen by well over 150 million spectators worldwide. In 2020 the company filed for creditor protection during the COVID-19 pandemic and emerged under new ownership later that year.
