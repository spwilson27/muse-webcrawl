Nettle soup

Nettle soup is a traditional soup prepared from stinging nettles (Urtica dioica). It has been eaten in northern and eastern Europe for centuries, typically in spring when the young nettle shoots are at their most tender and nutritious. Cooking destroys the nettles' sting.

Preparation

Young nettle tops are gathered in spring, washed, and simmered, often with potatoes, leeks, onions, garlic and sometimes rice or barley, then blended or served brothy with cream or a poached egg. The soup has an earthy, spinach-like flavour.

Regional traditions

Nettle soup is a recognised part of regional cuisines in Ireland, Sweden (where it is called nässelsoppa and served with hard-boiled eggs), Ukraine, Russia and among Indigenous peoples of North America, where nettles were also valued as a spring tonic food rich in iron, calcium and vitamins A and C.
