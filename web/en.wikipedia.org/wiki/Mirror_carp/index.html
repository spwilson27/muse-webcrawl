Mirror carp

The mirror carp (Cyprinus carpio var.) is a variety of the common carp with a distinctive pattern of large, mirror-like scales. Unlike the common carp, which is fully covered in regular scales, the mirror carp has irregular and patchy scaling, with some areas of bare skin.

Genetics and history

Mirror carp were produced by selective breeding. The variation is controlled by two genes affecting scale coverage. They are said to have been bred by monks in Europe to make the fish easier to prepare for the table, as fewer scales need to be removed. The related leather carp has almost no scales.

Angling

Mirror carp are highly prized by coarse anglers in the United Kingdom and Europe. Individual fish are often recognisable by their unique scale patterns and are given names by anglers. They can grow very large, with specimens over 60 lb (27 kg) recorded. See also common carp and koi.
