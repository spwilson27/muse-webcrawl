Macau

Macau (also spelled Macao), officially the Macao Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China, is a city and special administrative region on the western shore of the Pearl River estuary in Guangdong Province, China. With a population of roughly 700,000 in an area of about 33 square kilometres, it is the most densely populated region in the world. Macau was a Portuguese colony from the mid-16th century until its transfer to China on 20 December 1999.

History

Portuguese traders settled in Macau in 1557, leasing the territory from Ming China and later administering it as a colony; it became a major entrepôt for trade between China, Japan and Europe, and the oldest European outpost in East Asia. Its importance declined after the founding of Hong Kong in the 1840s. Under the Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration of 1987, Portugal returned Macau to China in 1999, and Macau has since been governed under the "one country, two systems" principle, with a high degree of autonomy until at least 2049.

Economy

Macau's economy is dominated by tourism and gambling. Following the liberalisation of the casino industry in 2002, gaming revenue surpassed that of Las Vegas, making Macau the largest gambling centre in the world. The territory consists of the Macau Peninsula and the islands of Taipa and Coloane, largely joined by the reclaimed Cotai strip, home to many mega-resorts. The Historic Centre of Macao, with its blend of Portuguese and Chinese architecture including the Ruins of Saint Paul's, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Government and law

The Chief Executive is the head of government, chosen by an election committee and appointed by Beijing. The Legislative Assembly is partly directly elected and partly appointed or indirectly elected. Macau maintains its own legal system based on Portuguese civil law, its own currency, customs territory and immigration controls separate from mainland China.
