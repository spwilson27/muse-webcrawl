Benz Patent-Motorwagen
ManufacturerBenz & Cie., Mannheim
Production1886–1893 (25 built of the later models)
DesignerKarl Benz
Engine954 cc single-cylinder four-stroke petrol, 0.75 hp (later versions up to 3 hp)
Top speedc. 16 km/h (10 mph)

The Benz Patent-Motorwagen ("patent motorcar"), built in 1885 by Karl Benz and publicly unveiled in Mannheim on 3 July 1886, is widely regarded as the first practical automobile — the first vehicle designed from the outset to be propelled by an internal combustion engine, rather than a horse-drawn carriage with an engine added. Benz received the patent (DRP No. 37435) for his "vehicle powered by a gas engine" on 29 January 1886.

Design

The three-wheeled vehicle integrated engine, chassis and drive as a single unit. Its water-cooled single-cylinder four-stroke engine (954 cc) was mounted horizontally at the rear, with a large horizontal flywheel, surface carburettor, trembler-coil ignition and a belt-and-chain drive to the rear wheels; early output was about two-thirds of a horsepower. The tubular steel frame, rack-and-pinion steering of the single front wheel by a tiller, bench seat and differential were all purpose-built. Successive models (No. 2 and No. 3) added power and refinements; the Model 3, with wooden-spoke wheels, was the version exhibited at the 1889 Paris Exposition.

Bertha Benz's journey

In August 1888, without her husband's knowledge, Bertha Benz took the Patent-Motorwagen on a 106 km (66 mile) journey from Mannheim to Pforzheim with their sons Eugen and Richard — the first long-distance automobile trip. She bought fuel (ligroin) at a pharmacy in Wiesloch, cleared a blocked fuel line with a hatpin, insulated a wire with a garter, and had a cobbler nail leather onto the worn brake blocks, thereby inventing brake pads. The publicity proved the automobile's practicality and led to improved models and the first sales.

Legacy

Twenty-five Patent-Motorwagens were built between 1886 and 1893 before the four-wheeled Benz Velo succeeded it. The route of Bertha Benz's drive is commemorated today as the Bertha Benz Memorial Route in Baden-Württemberg.
