Thawte Consulting (pronounced "thought") is a certificate authority (CA) for X.509 certificates. It was founded in 1995 by Mark Shuttleworth in Cape Town, South Africa, and grew from a garage project into one of the first commercial certificate authorities outside the United States, issuing digital certificates used for SSL/TLS encryption on websites worldwide.

Thawte logo

History

Shuttleworth started Thawte out of his parents' garage as an ISP and web-server company before focusing on digital certificates. Because US export controls restricted strong cryptography, Thawte's position outside the United States helped it win customers in many countries. In December 1999, VeriSign acquired Thawte for about US$575 million in stock, making Shuttleworth briefly one of South Africa's richest men; he later flew to the International Space Station as a space tourist and founded the Ubuntu Linux project. VeriSign's authentication business, including Thawte, was sold to Symantec in 2010.

Later ownership

Following browser distrust of Symantec's certificate businesses in 2017, Symantec sold its Website Security division, including the Thawte brand, to DigiCert in 2018. Thawte continues to operate as a DigiCert brand issuing TLS/SSL and code-signing certificates.
