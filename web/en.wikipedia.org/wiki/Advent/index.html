Advent

Advent
Observed by: Many Christians
Season: Four Sundays before Christmas
Liturgical colour: Violet / purple (blue in some traditions)

Advent is a season observed in most Christian denominations as a time of expectant waiting and preparation for both the celebration of the Nativity of Christ at Christmas and the return of Christ at the Second Coming. The term derives from the Latin adventus, meaning "coming" or "arrival".

Dates and practice

In Western Christianity Advent begins on Advent Sunday, the fourth Sunday before Christmas (between 27 November and 3 December), and ends on Christmas Eve. Practices include Advent wreaths with candles, Advent calendars, daily devotionals, fasting or prayer in some traditions, and special music and readings focused on prophecy.

Eastern Christianity

In Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches, the equivalent is the Nativity Fast, a longer fasting period of 40 days beginning 15 November in many calendars.

History

Preparatory periods before Christmas developed between the 4th and 7th centuries in Spain, Gaul, and Rome, gradually standardising around four weeks in the West.
