Phidias or Pheidias (Ancient Greek: Φειδίας; c. 480 – 430 BCE) was an Athenian sculptor, painter and architect, generally regarded as one of the greatest artists of classical antiquity. His Statue of Zeus at Olympia was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and his colossal gold-and-ivory Athena Parthenos stood in the Parthenon he helped supervise on the Acropolis of Athens.

Career

Phidias was a friend and artistic adviser of the statesman Pericles, who entrusted him with oversight of the Acropolis building programme. His works in Athens included the bronze Athena Promachos, the Athena Lemnia, and the 11.5-metre Athena Parthenos (dedicated 438 BCE), built of ivory and over a tonne of gold. At Olympia his workshop — excavated in the 1950s, complete with a cup inscribed "I belong to Phidias" — produced the seated Zeus, some 12 metres high, whose majesty defined the god's image for centuries.

Fall and death

Attacked through his patron, Phidias was prosecuted at Athens — ancient sources disagree whether for embezzling the Parthenos's gold (of which he was acquitted, the gold being removable and weighed) or for impiety in carving portraits of himself and Pericles on Athena's shield. He died around 430 BCE, in prison at Athens or in exile at Olympia, according to varying accounts.

Legacy

No certain original by Phidias survives; his style is known through Roman copies, the Parthenon sculptures produced under his direction, and literary descriptions. The Parthenon marbles embody his hallmarks — naturalistic drapery clinging to the body ("wet drapery") and serene, idealised figures. The American sculptor Daniel Chester French modelled his seated Abraham Lincoln for the Lincoln Memorial consciously on Phidias's Zeus.
