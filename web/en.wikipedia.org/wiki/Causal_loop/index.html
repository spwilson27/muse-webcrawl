Causal loop

A causal loop is a paradox of time travel in which a later event is among the causes of an earlier event, which is in turn among the causes of the later event. The two events are linked in a self-consistent cycle, so that objects or information can exist without ever having been created — the bootstrap paradox.

TypeTemporal paradox
Also calledPredestination paradox, bootstrap paradox
Related conceptsClosed timelike curve, grandfather paradox

Bootstrap paradox

In the classic form, a time traveller takes an object or piece of information into the past, where it becomes the source of its own later existence: a book copied from an older copy brought from the future may never have an original author. Robert A. Heinlein explored such loops in stories including "—All You Zombies—", and they feature in films such as The Terminator and Predestination.

Physics and philosophy

General relativity permits solutions with closed timelike curves, such as the Gödel universe, though whether such curves can form physically is disputed. The Novikov self-consistency principle proposes that events on a closed timelike curve must be globally consistent, allowing loops but forbidding contradictions such as the grandfather paradox. Philosophers debate whether causal loops are metaphysically possible or merely consistent fictions.
