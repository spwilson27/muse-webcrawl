Proth number

A Proth number takes the form N = k·2n + 1.

In number theory, a Proth number is a natural number of the form N = k × 2n + 1, where k is an odd natural number and n is a positive integer such that 2n > k. They are named after the French mathematician François Proth (1852–1879).

Proth primes

A Proth number that is prime is called a Proth prime. Proth's theorem gives an efficient primality test: if N is a Proth number and there exists an integer a for which a(N−1)/2 ≡ −1 (mod N), then N is prime. This makes Proth numbers attractive targets for computer searches for large primes.

Examples

The first Proth numbers are 3, 5, 9, 13, 17, 25, 33, 41, 49, 57, 65, 81, 97, ... The Proth primes include 3, 5, 13, 17, 41, 97, 113, 193, 241, 257 and 337. Cullen numbers (n·2n + 1) and Fermat numbers are special cases or close relatives. When k = 1 and n is a power of 2, the number is a Fermat number.

Prime searches

Some of the largest known primes are Proth primes, found by projects such as PrimeGrid and the Seventeen or Bust effort to prove that 78557 is the smallest Sierpiński number. Searches use Proth's theorem together with fast modular arithmetic implementations such as LLR (based on Jean Pené's work).
