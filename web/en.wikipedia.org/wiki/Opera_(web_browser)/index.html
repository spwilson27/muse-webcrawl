Opera (web browser)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Opera (web browser)
Released10 April 1995
DeveloperOpera Software (Norway)
EnginePresto (until 2013); Blink (Chromium) since
[Image: The Opera logo: a red letter O with a cut-out on its right side]

Opera is a multi-platform web browser developed by Opera Software, a Norwegian company founded by Jon Stephenson von Tetzchner and Geir Ivarsøy out of a research project at Telenor. First released publicly in 1996 (developed from 1994), Opera is one of the oldest browsers still in production.

Opera pioneered features that later became universal: tabbed browsing (2000), a built-in pop-up blocker, page zoom, mouse gestures, a download manager, and 'speed dial' start pages. Its small size made it a favourite on early mobile phones (Opera Mini, 2005, compressed pages through proxy servers to slash data costs — transformative in Africa and Asia where bandwidth was dear) and on games consoles and embedded devices.

In 2013 Opera abandoned its own Presto engine for Chromium's Blink, shedding some distinctiveness for compatibility; a Chinese consortium acquired the browser business in 2016. Opera has since repositioned around built-in services — a free VPN, ad blocking, crypto wallet, and the AI-focused Opera One redesign — holding a small but durable global share, with particular strength in Africa and Asia.
