Microclimate

A microclimate is a local set of atmospheric conditions that differ from those in the surrounding areas, often slightly but sometimes substantially. The term may refer to areas as small as a few square metres or as large as many square kilometres.

Formation

Microclimates are shaped by topography, water bodies, vegetation and human-built structures. Slopes, valleys, forests and urban areas alter temperature, humidity, wind and frost patterns. Cities commonly form urban heat islands warmer than surrounding countryside.

Significance

Microclimates matter for agriculture, viticulture, gardening, ecology and architecture, since plants and animals may thrive in a sheltered local pocket within a broader climate zone that would otherwise be unsuitable.
