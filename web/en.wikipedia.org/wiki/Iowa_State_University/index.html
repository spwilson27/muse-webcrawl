Iowa State University of Science and Technology (Iowa State University, Iowa State, or ISU) is a public land-grant research university in Ames, Iowa. Founded in 1858 as the Iowa Agricultural College and Model Farm, it was one of the first institutions chosen as a land-grant university under the Morrill Act of 1862, with Iowa the first state to accept the act's terms. The university is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

History

Classes opened in 1869, and the college was coeducational from the start — unusual for the era. The first president was Adonijah Welch, and Mary B. Welch organized what became pioneering programs in domestic economy (home economics). Iowa State became a leader in agricultural research and extension; the federal Hatch Act experiment-station system and the Smith–Lever extension service both drew strongly on the Iowa model. Agricultural scientist George Washington Carver completed his bachelor's and master's degrees at Iowa State in the 1890s, becoming its first Black graduate and later a faculty member before leaving for Tuskegee.

During World War II the university contributed to the Manhattan Project through chemist Frank Spedding's Ames Project, which developed an economical process for producing pure uranium metal at what is now Ames National Laboratory, still operated by the university for the U.S. Department of Energy. In 1949–50, researchers at Iowa State, including John Vincent Atanasoff's earlier work (1937–1942) on the Atanasoff–Berry Computer — built at the university and recognized in a 1973 federal court decision as the first electronic digital computer — placed ISU near the origins of modern computing.

Academics

Iowa State comprises eight colleges, including colleges of Agriculture and Life Sciences, Engineering (one of the largest in the nation), Veterinary Medicine (the oldest public veterinary college in the U.S., founded 1879), Design, and Liberal Arts and Sciences. It operates the ISU Research Park and the BioCentury Research Farm, and its campus — centered on a large landscaped central lawn with the Campanile (1899) and Memorial Union — is regarded as one of the most attractive in the country.

Athletics

The Iowa State Cyclones compete in the Big 12 Conference. The nickname dates to 1895, when a team from the school "swept" Northwestern like a cyclone. The university is particularly known for wrestling, men's and women's basketball at Hilton Coliseum, and its annual football rivalry with the University of Iowa for the Cy-Hawk Trophy.
