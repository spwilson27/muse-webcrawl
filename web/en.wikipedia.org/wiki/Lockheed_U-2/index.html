The Lockheed U-2, nicknamed "Dragon Lady", is a single-engine, high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft operated by the United States Air Force and previously flown by the Central Intelligence Agency. Designed at Lockheed's Skunk Works under Clarence "Kelly" Johnson, it was built to fly above 70,000 feet, beyond the reach of Soviet interceptors and surface-to-air missiles of the 1950s, to conduct photographic and signals reconnaissance over the Soviet Union and other denied areas.

Development

Ordered in 1954 under intense Cold War pressure for intelligence on Soviet bomber and missile programmes, the U-2 mated glider-like 103-foot wings to a lightweight airframe powered by a Pratt & Whitney J57 engine. Its first flight took place at Groom Lake (Area 51) in August 1955. CIA pilots began operational overflights of the USSR in June 1956.

The 1960 incident

On 1 May 1960, CIA pilot Francis Gary Powers was shot down by an S-75 Dvina missile near Sverdlovsk during a deep-penetration mission, ending Eisenhower's planned Paris summit with Khrushchev in diplomatic humiliation and forcing Washington to acknowledge the programme. The US publicly denied the mission's espionage purpose until the Soviets produced the surviving pilot.

Service

Satellites partly displaced its strategic role, but the U-2's sensors, range, and flexibility kept it in service for decades: the Cuban Missile Crisis imagery of 1962 was gathered by U-2s, and upgraded U-2S aircraft with glass cockpits and new engines fly today from Beale Air Force Base, serving intelligence, scientific, and NASA high-altitude research missions. The later SR-71 Blackbird was designed to fly faster rather than merely higher.
