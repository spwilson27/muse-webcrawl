Julie Taymor (born 15 December 1952) is an American director and writer of theatre, opera, and film, known for visually inventive work drawing on puppetry, mask, and world theatre traditions, particularly from her studies in Indonesia and Japan.

Career

Taymor achieved international fame directing and designing the stage adaptation of Disney's The Lion King (1997), which premiered on Broadway and became one of the most successful musicals in history; she was the first woman to win the Tony Award for Best Direction of a Musical. Her earlier theatre work included Juan Darién and The Green Bird.

In film she directed Titus (1999), Frida (2002), which earned six Academy Award nominations, the Beatles musical Across the Universe (2007), The Tempest (2010), and The Glorias (2020). She also directed the troubled Broadway production Spider-Man: Turn Off the Dark before departing in 2011, and operas including Oedipus rex and The Magic Flute at the Metropolitan Opera.

Style

Her work blends avant-garde and commercial forms, using elaborate puppetry — developed with Michael Curry — masks, and projections to create a distinctive visual language.
