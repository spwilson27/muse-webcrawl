Karl Ehn

Karl Ehn (1 November 1884 – 29 July 1959) was an Austrian architect, a student of Otto Wagner, and the leading designer of municipal housing for the city of Vienna during the interwar period of "Red Vienna". As head of the technical division of the city's housing office, he was responsible for numerous large public housing estates (Gemeindebauten), most famously the monumental Karl Marx-Hof.

Career

Ehn studied at the Academy of Fine Arts Vienna under Otto Wagner and joined the Vienna municipal building department in 1908. After the First World War, the Social Democratic city government launched an ambitious housing programme funded by a dedicated housing tax, building tens of thousands of flats in large perimeter-block complexes with courtyards, laundries, baths, kindergartens and clinics. Ehn became the office's most prolific architect, designing or supervising a large share of these estates.

Karl Marx-Hof

His masterpiece, the Karl Marx-Hof in Heiligenstadt (built 1927–1930), stretches over a kilometre in length and originally contained some 1,300 apartments for about 5,000 people. With its fortress-like arches, towers and red-plastered façades, it became the emblem of Red Vienna. During the Austrian Civil War of February 1934, the complex was a stronghold of the Social Democratic Schutzbund and was shelled by government forces; it was renamed Heiligenstädter Hof under the Austrofascist regime and later restored to its original name.

Later life

After 1934 Ehn's role diminished under the authoritarian government, and he withdrew from major public work, continuing in quieter municipal service. He died in Vienna in 1959. His housing estates remain protected monuments and working-class residences, studied worldwide as exemplars of socially oriented urban design.
