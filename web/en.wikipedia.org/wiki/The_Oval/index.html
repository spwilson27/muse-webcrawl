The Oval, known for sponsorship reasons as the Kia Oval, is an international cricket ground in Kennington, in the London Borough of Lambeth, in south London. The Oval has been the home ground of Surrey County Cricket Club since it was opened in 1845. It was the first ground in England to host international Test cricket, in September 1880, and traditionally hosts the final Test match of the English summer.

History

The ground was laid out in 1844 on a former market garden leased from the Duchy of Cornwall; its oval shape — despite wide modern boundaries — derives from the surrounding street plan. The famous gasholders beside the ground, built in the 1850s, are Grade II listed and beloved landmarks. In 1868 the first Aboriginal cricket team to tour England played at The Oval; in 1870 the ground hosted the first ever international football match, England v Scotland; the first FA Cup Final was played there in 1872, and rugby internationals also followed.

The Ashes

The Oval is the birthplace of the Ashes: after Australia's first Test victory on English soil there in 1882, The Sporting Times published a mock obituary for English cricket, and the following winter Ivo Bligh's England team recovered "the ashes" in Australia. The ground has since hosted many famous concluding Ashes Tests, including Len Hutton's record 364 in 1938, Don Bradman's duck in his final innings in 1948, and England's celebrated series wins of 2005, 2009, and 2023. It also hosted the final of the 2017 Women's Cricket World Cup qualifying matches and the inaugural World Test Championship final of 2023, won by Australia over India.

The ground today

Recent redevelopment, including the OCS Stand and a planned expansion, has raised capacity to 27,500. The Pavilion, dating to 1898, remains among the most handsome in world cricket.
