12 Angry Men

12 Angry Men is a 1957 American courtroom drama film directed by Sidney Lumet in his directorial debut, adapted by Reginald Rose from his own 1954 teleplay. It tells the story of a jury of twelve men as they deliberate the conviction or acquittal of a teenager charged with murder on the basis of reasonable doubt. Starring Henry Fonda, who also produced the film, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made.

Plot

In a New York City courthouse, a jury retires to deliberate the case of an 18-year-old boy accused of stabbing his father to death. If found guilty, he faces a mandatory death sentence. Eleven jurors vote guilty almost at once; only Juror 8 (Henry Fonda) dissents, arguing that the evidence deserves discussion. Over the course of a sweltering afternoon in the jury room, Juror 8 methodically raises doubts: the supposed uniqueness of the murder weapon, the reliability of witnesses, and the plausibility of the defendant's alibi. One by one, the jurors change their votes until the verdict is unanimously not guilty.

Production

The film was shot in New York in under three weeks after extensive rehearsal. Lumet and cinematographer Boris Kaufman employed a visual strategy in which the camera gradually moves from above eye level to below it, and focal lengths lengthen as the deliberation proceeds, intensifying the room's claustrophobia. The picture was a box-office disappointment on release but received acclaim and three Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director and Best Adapted Screenplay.

Legacy

12 Angry Men has grown steadily in stature and is frequently ranked among the finest American films. In 2007 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". It is studied for its portrayal of consensus-building, prejudice and the principles of reasonable doubt, and the American Film Institute named it the second-greatest courtroom drama of all time.
