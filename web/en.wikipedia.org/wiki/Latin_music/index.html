Latin music

Latin music (Portuguese and Spanish: música latina) is a term used by the music industry to describe music originating from or influenced by Latin America, Spain, and Portugal, as well as music performed in Spanish or Portuguese more broadly. The category encompasses an enormous diversity of styles, from traditional folk forms to contemporary commercial genres, unified loosely by geography, language, and shared Iberian, Indigenous, and African musical inheritances.

History and origins

Latin music developed from the encounter of European (especially Spanish and Portuguese), Indigenous American, and West African musical traditions during and after colonisation. African rhythms and call-and-response practices, European harmony and instruments such as the guitar, and Indigenous instruments and melodies combined in different proportions across the region: Afro-Cuban traditions in the Caribbean, Andean folk music in the highlands, and Iberian-derived forms such as the corrido and ranchera in Mexico.

Major genres

Tropical and Caribbean

Cuban son, salsa, mambo, cha-cha-chá, bolero, Dominican merengue and bachata, and Puerto Rican bomba and plena form the Afro-Caribbean core of the category. Salsa, commercialised in New York in the 1970s around labels such as Fania Records, became one of the most globally successful Latin styles.

Mexican and regional

Mariachi, ranchera, norteño, banda, and corridos — including contemporary corridos tumbados — represent the Mexican regional tradition, which dominates the "Regional Mexican" charts in the United States.

South American

Brazilian music — samba, bossa nova, tropicália, forró, and funk carioca — is usually included despite the Portuguese language. Argentina contributed tango; the Andean and Southern Cone regions produced folk styles such as zamba and cueca; Colombia and Venezuela gave cumbia and vallenato, with cumbia spreading throughout Latin America.

Contemporary and urban

From the 1990s, Latin pop (associated with artists such as Ricky Martin, Shakira, and Enrique Iglesias) achieved global crossover success. Reggaeton, Latin trap, and the broader urbano movement originating in Puerto Rico and Panama became dominant in the streaming era, with artists such as Daddy Yankee, Bad Bunny, and Karol G achieving worldwide chart success; "Despacito" (2017) and Bad Bunny's Spanish-language albums marked milestones in the genre's global reach.

Industry and recognition

The Recording Academy in the United States has recognised Latin music in Grammy categories since the 1970s; the separate Latin Grammy Awards were established in 2000. Billboard maintains dedicated Latin charts, and Billboard's definition of Latin music for chart purposes has historically centred on recordings predominantly in Spanish. The category remains contested: critics note that it groups together radically different traditions on the basis of language and marketing rather than musical structure.
