Yalda Night

Yalda Night (Shab-e Yalda)
Observed byIran, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Kurdistan, diaspora communities
DateNight of the winter solstice (20 or 21 December)
SignificanceLongest night of the year; triumph of light over darkness

Yalda Night (Persian: شب یلدا, shab-e yalda), also known as Shab-e Chelleh, is an Iranian festival celebrated on the longest and darkest night of the year — the night of the winter solstice in the Northern Hemisphere, usually falling on 20 or 21 December. Families and friends gather, typically at the home of the eldest member, to stay up past midnight eating, drinking and reading poetry.

Origins

The festival has roots in ancient Iranian religion and the worship of Mithra, the god of light and covenant, whose birth the night was believed to mark. After the longest night, days grow longer, symbolising the victory of light and goodness over darkness and evil. The name "Yalda" derives from the Syriac word for "birth."

Customs

Signature foods are red fruits — watermelon and pomegranate — whose colour recalls the dawn and the glow of life after darkness. Nuts, dried fruits and sweets are served, and a central ritual is fal-e Hafez: bibliomancy using the Divan of the 14th-century poet Hafez, in which a randomly opened poem is read aloud and interpreted as an omen or wish for the future. Storytelling, music and reading from Ferdowsi's Shahnameh also feature.

Recognition

Yalda is celebrated across Greater Iran, including in Azerbaijan, Afghanistan, Tajikistan and Kurdish regions, and by the Iranian diaspora worldwide. In 2022 it was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, jointly for Iran and Afghanistan.
