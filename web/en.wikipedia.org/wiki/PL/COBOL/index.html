PL/COBOL

PL/COBOL was a teaching programming language in the PL/I family, designed as a simplified subset-style dialect that combined the data-processing style of COBOL with the structured-programming concepts of PL/I. Languages of this kind were created during the 1960s and 1970s, when PL/I was promoted as a single language intended to serve commercial, scientific, and systems programming alike, and simplified variants were used to introduce business-programming students to its facilities.

Background

PL/I was developed by IBM in the mid-1960s for the System/360. Because the full language was very large, educators and system designers produced reduced dialects aimed at particular communities. A COBOL-oriented dialect emphasized record structures, decimal arithmetic, and report-style input/output, while retaining PL/I's block structure, condition handling, and data types.

Features

In keeping with the PL/I family, such a dialect characteristically provided:

  • DECLARE statements with PICTURE-style and fixed-decimal data attributes familiar to COBOL programmers;
  • Structured control flow (DO groups, IF/THEN/ELSE, SELECT) instead of COBOL's paragraph-and-PERFORM style;
  • Record-oriented and stream input/output;
  • ON-condition exception handling in the PL/I manner.

Context

The experiment of merging COBOL-style data processing into PL/I reflected the era's goal of a universal programming language. In practice, commercial shops largely stayed with COBOL itself, while PL/I found its strongest use in systems and scientific work. Other members of the family included PL/Fortran for scientific users, PL/Multics written in and for the Multics system, and the microcomputer language PL/M.
