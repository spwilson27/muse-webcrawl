PL/Fortran

PL/Fortran was a member of the PL/I programming language family oriented toward scientific and engineering computation, in the tradition of Fortran. When IBM introduced PL/I in the mid-1960s as a single language meant to unify commercial and scientific programming, reduced or specially oriented dialects were proposed so that Fortran users could work in a familiar style while gaining PL/I's structured programming, data structures, and exception handling.

Background

Fortran, created in the 1950s, dominated scientific computing when PL/I appeared. PL/I deliberately incorporated Fortran-like array operations and mathematical functions, and teaching materials of the period often presented PL/I "for Fortran programmers." A Fortran-oriented subset or dialect emphasized:

  • Multi-dimensional arrays and implied-DO style iteration;
  • Floating-point and complex arithmetic;
  • Mathematical function libraries comparable to Fortran's;
  • Structured control constructs (DO groups, IF/THEN/ELSE, SELECT) not present in early Fortran;
  • PL/I's ON-condition handling for computational exceptions such as overflow and zerodivide.

Outcome

As with the other specialized PL/I dialects, PL/Fortran remained largely a teaching and transitional tool. Scientific users generally continued with Fortran itself, whose compilers were highly optimized and whose code libraries were extensive, while PL/I's full language was used in systems programming and by the Multics project.
