PL/I and Multics

PL/Multics refers to the use of the PL/I programming language in the development of the Multics (Multiplexed Information and Computing Service) operating system. Multics, developed jointly by MIT, Bell Labs, and General Electric beginning in 1964, was one of the first major operating systems written primarily in a high-level language, and its implementation language was PL/I—a landmark decision at a time when operating systems were almost universally written in assembly language.

EPL: the bootstrap subset

Because no PL/I compiler initially existed for the GE-645 computer chosen for Multics, the project first defined EPL (Early PL/I), a subset of the language, and implemented an EPL compiler written partly in assembly language. EPL was used to bootstrap the system; once Multics could host development, a full PL/I compiler (written largely by Douglas McIlroy and Robert Morris, among others) replaced it, and the system code was converted to standard PL/I.

Significance

Writing Multics in PL/I demonstrated that a large, secure, interactive operating system could be built in a high-level language, with benefits in reliability and maintainability. Multics introduced or popularized hierarchical file systems, dynamic linking, access control lists, and ring-based security—ideas that later influenced Unix, whose creators had worked on Multics at Bell Labs.

The Multics PL/I compiler became notable in its own right: it generated code for the segmented, paged GE/Honeywell hardware and supported the system's elaborate calling conventions across protection rings. PL/I's structured programming and string handling were credited with making the very large Multics codebase manageable by the standards of the era.

Aftermath

Multics ran in production from 1969 until 2000. Its PL/I heritage lingered in systems influenced by it, and the success of PL/I on Multics helped inspire later systems languages—including, indirectly, CPL, BCPL, and C, via the reaction of the Unix team against Multics' complexity.
