Aldhelm

Aldhelm (c. 639 – 25 May 709), also spelled Ealdhelm, was an Anglo-Saxon abbot of Malmesbury Abbey, first bishop of Sherborne, scholar, and poet — the first English writer whose Latin works survive in quantity. He was later venerated as a saint, with a feast day of 25 May.

Life and learning

Tradition links Aldhelm to the royal house of Wessex. He was educated first in the Irish-influenced school at Malmesbury and then at Canterbury under Archbishop Theodore and Abbot Hadrian, making him a product of the most advanced scholarship in the Anglo-Saxon world. As abbot of Malmesbury from about 675, he built churches and gathered a major library; in 705 he became the first bishop of the new diocese of Sherborne, carved out of the see of Winchester.

Works

Aldhelm's most famous work is De virginitate, a treatise praising virginity written first in elaborate Latin prose and then reworked as verse — addressed to the nuns of Barking Abbey. His Enigmata, a collection of one hundred Latin riddles modelled on Symphosius, influenced the later Old English riddles of the Exeter Book. His letters include an important early discussion of the date of Easter addressed to King Geraint of Dumnonia. Bede praised his learning, and William of Malmesbury recorded that Aldhelm would sing popular songs from a bridge to draw crowds whom he could then preach to — an early story of vernacular evangelism in England.
