John of Gaunt

John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster (6 March 1340 – 3 February 1399), was an English prince, military leader and statesman. The third surviving son of King Edward III, he became one of the richest and most powerful men of his age through his marriage to Blanche of Lancaster, and exercised great influence during the reigns of his father and his nephew Richard II. He was the founder of the royal House of Lancaster.

Early life

John was born at Ghent in Flanders — "Gaunt" being the English form of Ghent. He fought in France and Spain during the Hundred Years' War and married Blanche, heiress to the vast Lancastrian estates, in 1359, receiving the dukedom of Lancaster in 1362.

Power and unpopularity

During Edward III's declining years Gaunt effectively dominated the government, which made him deeply unpopular; his Savoy Palace in London was destroyed by a mob during the Peasants' Revolt of 1381. Through his second marriage, to Constance of Castile, he claimed the Castilian crown and campaigned in Spain in the 1380s, eventually renouncing the claim in return for a settlement.

Descendants

Gaunt's children included Henry IV, the first Lancastrian king of England, and, by his third wife Katherine Swynford, the Beaufort family, from whom the Tudors descended. His daughter Philippa became queen of Portugal and Catherine queen of Castile. Virtually every English monarch since Henry IV descends from him.
