Autogas

Autogas or LPG is liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) used as a fuel in internal combustion engines in vehicles as well as in stationary applications such as generators. It is a mixture of propane and butane. Autogas is widely used as a "green" fuel, as its use reduces CO2 exhaust emissions by around 15% compared to petrol, and produces considerably fewer particulates and nitrogen oxides. Globally, it is the third most popular automotive fuel after petrol and diesel, powering more than 27 million vehicles worldwide.

Use and infrastructure

Autogas is sold at dedicated filling stations and at many ordinary petrol stations in countries where it is popular. Leading markets include Turkey (where LPG has powered a large share of the passenger car fleet), South Korea, Poland, Italy, Australia, and the Netherlands. Vehicles may be manufactured as dedicated LPG or bi-fuel models, or converted from petrol cars by fitting a gas tank—commonly a toroidal tank in the spare-wheel well or a cylindrical tank in the boot—along with a vaporiser/reducer (or liquid injection system), injectors, and an electronic control unit.

Properties and performance

LPG has a high octane rating (around 108 RON for propane), which can permit efficient combustion, but its energy content per litre is lower than petrol's—roughly 26 MJ/L versus about 34 MJ/L—so volumetric fuel consumption is higher. Modern sequential vapour injection and liquid-phase injection systems largely eliminate the power loss associated with older mixer-type conversions. Because LPG burns cleanly and leaves few deposits, engine wear can be reduced, although valve seat recession can affect engines with soft valve seats unless hardened seats or additives are used.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include lower fuel cost in many markets (partly due to lower taxation), reduced CO2 and pollutant emissions, and an extensive existing distribution network. Disadvantages include the space taken by the tank, a sparser refuelling network in some countries, conversion costs, and restrictions on LPG vehicles in some tunnels and underground car parks.

Safety and regulation

Autogas systems are governed by regulations such as UNECE Regulation 67, covering tank strength, pressure relief valves, and crash safety. Tanks are substantially stronger than petrol tanks, and the fuel's narrow flammability limits and added odorant (ethyl mercaptan) aid leak detection.
