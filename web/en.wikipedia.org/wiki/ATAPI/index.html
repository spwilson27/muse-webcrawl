ATAPI

ATAPI (ATA Packet Interface) is a protocol used with the Parallel ATA (IDE) and Serial ATA standards so that a greater variety of devices can be connected to a computer than with the ATA command set alone. It carries SCSI commands and responses through the ATA interface.

Overview

ATA was originally designed for, and worked only with, hard disks and devices that could emulate them. The ATAPI working group, part of the committee that developed the ATA standard, introduced packet commands so that drives such as CD-ROM and DVD drives, tape drives, and large-capacity floppy drives like the Zip drive could be attached to the same interface.

Relation to ATA and SCSI

ATAPI devices speak a packet protocol derived from SCSI, encapsulated in ATA transfers. Under modern ATA standards (ATA/ATAPI-7 and later), the specification has been merged into a single document set, and Serial ATA (SATA) has largely succeeded the parallel interface in which ATAPI first appeared.
