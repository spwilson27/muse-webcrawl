Outreachy is a programme that provides paid, remote internships in free and open-source software to people subject to systemic bias and underrepresentation in the technology industry of their country. Interns work for three months with mentors from participating open-source communities on coding, documentation, design, research, and community projects.

History

The programme began in 2006 as the GNOME Foundation's Women's Summer Outreach Program, organised after the GNOME project received no applications from women for Google Summer of Code. Karen Sandler and Marina Zhurakhinskaya were central to its development. In 2013 it expanded beyond GNOME and beyond coding as the Free and Open Source Software Outreach Program for Women, and in 2015 it was renamed Outreachy and broadened its eligibility rules. The programme is organised under the fiscal sponsorship of the Software Freedom Conservancy, with funding from participating projects, companies, and foundations.

How it works

Outreachy runs two internship rounds a year. Applicants first complete a contribution period in which they make small contributions to a chosen community and work with its mentors; selected interns then receive a stipend (historically around US$7,000, plus a travel grant) for a full-time, remote three-month internship. Eligibility centres on being at least 18, not having participated before, and facing underrepresentation or systemic bias in their local tech industry; applicants generally must not be full-time students in the relevant hemisphere's school term or employed full-time in tech during the internship.

Impact

By the early 2020s Outreachy had supported more than a thousand interns across dozens of communities, including the Linux kernel, Mozilla, Wikimedia, Debian, and Git. Alumni studies reported by the programme show high rates of continued contribution to open source and employment in technology, and the programme is credited with demonstrating that structured outreach, mentorship, and a contribution-based application process can substantially diversify applicant pools compared with open calls alone.
