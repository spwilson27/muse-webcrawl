The Global Methane Pledge is an international initiative launched at the COP26 climate conference in Glasgow in November 2021 by the United States and the European Union. Participating countries commit to a collective goal of reducing global methane emissions by at least 30% from 2020 levels by 2030.

Rationale

Methane is a potent greenhouse gas responsible for roughly half a degree Celsius of observed warming. Because methane breaks down in the atmosphere within about a decade — far faster than carbon dioxide — cutting it is regarded as one of the fastest ways to slow near-term warming. The pledge targets the energy sector (oil and gas leaks and venting), agriculture (livestock and rice) and waste (landfill gas).

Participation and progress

More than 100 countries joined at launch, and participation grew to over 150 countries, covering about half of global anthropogenic methane emissions. Major emitters including China, India and Russia have not joined, although China released its own methane action plan in 2023. Participants are expected to develop national methane action plans; satellite monitoring initiatives such as MethaneSAT support verification.
