The pied avocet (Recurvirostra avosetta) is a large black and white wader in the avocet and stilt family, Recurvirostridae. It breeds in temperate Europe and across the Palearctic to Central Asia, and winters in Africa and southern Asia. It is a striking bird with bold black-and-white plumage, long bluish legs, and a long, thin bill that curves upward — the feature from which the genus of avocets takes its character.


A pied avocet; the upcurved bill is swept side to side through shallow water when feeding.

Description

The pied avocet is 42–45 cm long (excluding the bill) with a wingspan of about 77–80 cm. The plumage is white with a black cap and black patches on the wings and back; the legs are long and blue-grey, and the bill is black and distinctly upturned. The sexes are similar, though the male's bill is less strongly curved. Juveniles are browner where the adult is black.

Behaviour

Avocets breed in colonies on flat, open ground near shallow brackish or saline water — lagoons, salt pans, estuaries and lake shores — nesting in scrapes on the ground or among low vegetation. They feed by sweeping the bill from side to side through the water ("scything") to catch crustaceans, aquatic insects and other invertebrates, and they swim readily, upending like dabbling ducks.

Conservation and the RSPB emblem

The species was extinct as a breeding bird in Britain from the mid-19th century until 1947, when it returned to breed at Minsmere and Havergate Island in Suffolk — wartime flooding of the coast having recreated suitable habitat. Its recovery became the symbol of the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds: the avocet is the RSPB's logo. It is now protected and expanding in Britain and is listed as Least Concern globally, though it depends on managed coastal wetlands in many areas.

Taxonomy

The pied avocet was one of the many bird species originally described by Carl Linnaeus in his landmark 1758 10th edition of Systema Naturae, where it was given the binomial name Recurvirostra avosetta, which it still bears. It is one of four avocet species in the genus Recurvirostra.
