Vale of Tempe

The Vale of Tempe (Greek: Κοιλάδα των Τεμπών) is a gorge in Thessaly, Greece, cut by the Pineios River between Mount Olympus to the north and Mount Ossa to the south. About 10 kilometres long, it is the principal pass between Macedonia and Thessaly and has been a strategic route since antiquity.

Legend and history

In Greek mythology, Tempe was a favourite haunt of Apollo; here he was purified after slaying the serpent Python, and here he pursued the nymph Daphne, who was transformed into a laurel. Every nine years a procession from Delphi re-enacted Apollo's journey to gather laurel for the Pythian Games. Xerxes' Persians examined the pass in 480 BC, and it featured in campaigns of the Roman and later eras. The valley was famous in antiquity for its beauty, celebrated by poets from Virgil to Milton.

Modern era

The main road and railway between Athens and Thessaloniki pass through the gorge; the rail line was replaced by a tunnel in 2003. The Tempi train crash of February 2023, just south of the vale, was Greece's deadliest rail disaster. Shrines and a chapel of Agia Paraskevi, reached by a suspension bridge, draw pilgrims to a spring in the gorge.
