The MGM-140 Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS, pronounced "attack 'ems") is an American tactical ballistic missile manufactured by Lockheed Martin. It is fired from the tracked M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System and the wheeled M142 HIMARS, and is designed to attack high-value targets deep behind enemy lines.

Development and service

ATACMS entered service in 1991 and was used in combat during Operation Desert Storm, where it struck air defence sites, logistics, and artillery positions. It was used extensively again in the 2003 invasion of Iraq.

The system was transferred to Ukraine beginning in 2023, where longer-range variants supplied in 2024 were used against Russian airfields, air defences, and logistics hubs.

Variants

Early Block I missiles carried hundreds of M74 anti-personnel/anti-materiel submunitions over ranges of about 165 km. Later variants added GPS guidance, longer range (up to roughly 300 km), and unitary blast-fragmentation warheads, including a variant with an area-effects warhead intended to comply with cluster-munitions policy. The Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) is being developed as its successor.
