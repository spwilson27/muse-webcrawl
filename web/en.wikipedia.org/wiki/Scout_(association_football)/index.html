Scout (association football)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A scout in association football is a person employed by a football club to identify and evaluate players who might be signed, or to assess upcoming opponents. Talent scouts attend matches at every level — from schoolboy and grassroots football to professional reserve and international games — searching for players whose ability, character, and potential fit their club's needs and playing style.

Role

Scouts traditionally relied on personal judgement developed over years of watching football, compiling written reports on a player's technique, athleticism, tactical understanding, and temperament. Clubs structure scouting in hierarchies, from part-time regional spotters to chief scouts and heads of recruitment who compile shortlists for the manager and board. Youth scouts specialise in identifying children and teenagers for academies, a role now tightly regulated by rules on approaching young players and their families.

Opposition scouting

A second kind of scout — the opposition or tactical scout — studies a club's next opponents, analysing their formations, set pieces, and strengths and weaknesses so the coaching staff can prepare a match plan. This function has largely merged into the work of performance analysts.

Data and video scouting

Since the 2000s, scouting has been transformed by video platforms (such as Wyscout and Hudl) and event and tracking data from providers such as Opta and StatsBomb. Analysts can now screen thousands of players statistically before a scout watches them live, an approach popularised as "Moneyball"-style recruitment and used successfully by clubs such as FC Midtjylland and Brentford. Nevertheless, most clubs still insist that a player be watched in person before signing, to judge qualities that data cannot capture.
