Global nomad

A global nomad is a person who lives a mobile, location-independent lifestyle, moving between countries and cultures without a single fixed home base. The term, in use since the 1990s, overlaps with the later notion of the digital nomad, but places greater emphasis on cultural mobility and identity than on remote work technology alone.

Background

Global nomads are frequently "third culture kids" grown up — people raised outside their parents' home culture because of diplomatic, military, missionary, corporate, or academic postings — who continue a migratory pattern into adult life. Others adopt the lifestyle later, combining international careers, seasonal work, study, and travel. Research on the population describes characteristic strengths — adaptability, cross-cultural fluency, and ease with ambiguity — alongside recurring challenges, including unresolved grief at repeated departures, difficulty forming a stable identity, and a persistent sense of rootlessness or "homelessness" in both their passport country and their host countries.

Related concepts

The lifestyle intersects with expatriate communities, perpetual travellers, and backpacker culture, but global nomads are distinguished by the indefinite and habitual nature of their movement and by an identity organized around mobility itself. Some jurisdictions have responded with special nomad visas, while critics note that the term can obscure the privilege involved in frictionless border crossing.
