The Battle of Appomattox Court House, fought on the morning of 9 April 1865 at Appomattox Court House, Virginia, was one of the last battles of the American Civil War (1861–1865). It culminated in the surrender that afternoon of Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia to Union forces under General Ulysses S. Grant, effectively ending the war in Virginia.

Background

After the fall of Petersburg and Richmond in early April 1865, Lee's army retreated westward, harried by Union cavalry under Philip Sheridan and infantry of the Army of the Potomac and Army of the James. Lee hoped to reach supplies at Appomattox Station and then march south to join General Joseph E. Johnston's army in North Carolina. On 8 April, Union cavalry captured the supply trains at Appomattox Station and blocked the road west.

The battle

On the morning of 9 April, Confederate troops under Major General John B. Gordon attacked Sheridan's cavalry in a final attempt to break through. The attack initially succeeded, but Gordon's men crested a ridge to find two Union infantry corps drawn up in line of battle. Recognising that the army was surrounded and outnumbered, Lee concluded that "there is nothing left for me to do but to go and see General Grant."

The surrender

Lee and Grant met that afternoon in the parlor of the Wilmer McLean house in the village. Grant's terms were generous: officers and men could return home on parole, keeping their horses for spring planting. About 28,000 Confederate soldiers were paroled. Other Confederate armies surrendered over the following weeks, and the war ended by June 1865.

Legacy

The village is preserved today as the Appomattox Court House National Historical Park. The magnanimity of the surrender terms is often credited with aiding national reconciliation after the war.
