Tonsillitis

Tonsillitis is inflammation of the palatine tonsils, most often caused by viral infection, with bacterial infection — principally by group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus (Streptococcus pyogenes) — accounting for roughly 15–30% of cases in children and 5–15% in adults.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features are sore throat, painful swallowing (odynophagia), fever, enlarged red tonsils sometimes coated with white or yellow exudate, swollen tender cervical lymph nodes, and referred ear pain. Viral cases more often include cough, coryza and conjunctivitis; streptococcal cases classically lack cough. Scores such as the Centor criteria estimate the likelihood of streptococcal infection and guide testing and antibiotic use.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, supported where indicated by rapid antigen detection testing or throat culture. Most cases resolve within a week with rest, fluids and analgesia. Antibiotics (usually penicillin V) are reserved for likely or confirmed streptococcal disease, where they shorten symptoms modestly and prevent suppurative complications and rheumatic fever. Peritonsillar abscess requires drainage. Recurrent tonsillitis meeting frequency thresholds (e.g., the Paradise criteria) may justify tonsillectomy.

Complications

Local complications include peritonsillar and retropharyngeal abscess and airway obstruction; systemic sequelae of streptococcal infection include scarlet fever, acute rheumatic fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis.
