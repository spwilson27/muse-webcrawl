Gonzalo de Berceo

Gonzalo de Berceo (c. 1197 – before 1264) was a Castilian poet and cleric, the first Spanish-language author known by name. A secular priest associated with the monastery of San Millán de la Cogolla in La Rioja, he wrote religious verse in the mester de clerecía tradition — learned poetry in regular quatrains (cuaderna vía) intended for edification and for promoting the shrines in his care.

Works

His best-known work is the Milagros de Nuestra Señora ("Miracles of Our Lady", c. 1246–1252), a collection of twenty-five miracles of the Virgin Mary preceded by an allegorical introduction set in a flowery meadow. He also wrote lives of saints, including the Vida de San Millán de la Cogolla, the Vida de Santo Domingo de Silos, and the Vida de Santa Oria, as well as doctrinal and Marian poems such as the Loores de Nuestra Señora and the Sacrificio de la Misa.

Style and significance

Writing in a plain, accessible register of early Castilian — "the vernacular that people use to talk with their neighbours," as he put it — Berceo translated Latin sources while adding vivid local detail and dialogue. His poems, preserved in later manuscripts, are foundational documents of Spanish literature and of the history of the Spanish language; the Riojan monasteries of San Millán are traditionally associated with the earliest written Spanish, the Glosas Emilianenses.
