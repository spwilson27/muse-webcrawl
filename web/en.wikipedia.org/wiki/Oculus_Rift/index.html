The Oculus Rift is a discontinued line of virtual reality headsets developed and manufactured by Oculus VR, a virtual reality company founded by Palmer Luckey that is now known as Reality Labs, a division of Meta Platforms. The consumer version, commonly called the Rift CV1, was released on March 28, 2016.

History

The Rift began as a Kickstarter campaign in August 2012, which raised about US$2.4 million and is credited with reviving mainstream interest in virtual reality. Two pre-release development kits, DK1 (2013) and DK2 (2014), were sold to developers. In March 2014, Facebook acquired Oculus VR for about US$2 billion. The consumer Rift launched in 2016 bundled with an Xbox One controller, and the Oculus Touch motion controllers followed in December 2016.

Hardware

The CV1 uses a pair of PenTile OLED displays with a combined resolution of 2160×1200 (1080×1200 per eye) running at 90 Hz. Positional tracking is provided by an external infrared sensor using the Constellation tracking system. The headset includes integrated headphones and weighs about 470 grams. A later model, the Rift S (2019), co-designed with Lenovo, moved to inside-out tracking; the original Rift line was discontinued in 2021 as Meta shifted focus to the standalone Quest headsets.
