The 1999 Düzce earthquake occurred on 12 November 1999 at 18:57 local time in Düzce Province, northwestern Turkey. The magnitude 7.2 event struck only three months after the devastating İzmit earthquake of 17 August 1999, and is generally regarded as having been triggered by the stress changes caused by that earlier shock along the North Anatolian Fault.

Damage and casualties

The earthquake was centred on the Düzce segment of the North Anatolian Fault. It killed at least 710 people and injured around 2,678 others. Roughly 500 buildings collapsed in Düzce, Kaynaşlı and nearby settlements, many of them structures already weakened by the August earthquake. Around 2,000 buildings were damaged beyond repair. The town of Kaynaşlı was among the worst affected, with much of its centre destroyed.

Geological setting

The North Anatolian Fault is a right-lateral strike-slip fault running for more than 1,200 km across northern Turkey, accommodating the westward motion of the Anatolian Plate away from the Arabian Plate. The 1999 sequence continued a westward-migrating series of large earthquakes that began with the 1939 Erzincan earthquake. Seismologists noted that the Düzce rupture filled a gap between the 1944 Bolu–Gerede and 1967 Mudurnu Valley ruptures.

Aftermath

Tent cities were erected for displaced residents, and international rescue teams assisted in the search for survivors. The two 1999 earthquakes together prompted major reforms of Turkish building codes and the introduction of a compulsory earthquake insurance scheme (DASK) in 2000.
