National Treasure (Japan)

A National Treasure (国宝, kokuhō) is the most precious of Japan's Tangible Cultural Properties, as determined and designated by the Agency for Cultural Affairs, a special body of the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology. A Tangible Cultural Property is considered to be of historic or artistic value, classified either as "buildings and structures" or as "fine arts and crafts".

Each designated National Treasure must satisfy the following criteria: it must possess outstanding workmanship, a high significance for world cultural history, and particularly high value as a historical reference. The category of "National Treasures" includes some of the most ancient and significant works in Japan, such as the seventh-century Hōryū-ji temple buildings in Nara, said to be among the oldest wooden structures in the world.

History

The term "National Treasure" has been used in Japan since the nineteenth century. The system of designation developed from the Ancient Temples and Shrines Preservation Law of 1897 and was eventually folded into the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, enacted in 1950 after the fire that damaged the murals of Hōryū-ji's Golden Hall in 1949.

Categories

Designated items include castles, shrines, temples, residences, documents, sculptures, paintings, calligraphy, lacquerware, metalwork, swords and textiles. As of recent counts, more than a thousand items have been designated as National Treasures, alongside a larger body of Important Cultural Properties.
