Law enforcement in India is a primarily state subject under the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution of India, and crime prevention and detection are therefore the responsibility of the states and union territories. Nevertheless, the Constitution also empowers the central government to participate in police operations and organisation. Police services are provided by both the central government and state governments; large metropolitan cities have their own metropolitan police forces under separate commissioners.

At the national level, policing is supported by the Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs), central investigation agencies such as the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) and the National Investigation Agency (NIA), and central police organisations that provide training, research and logistical support. The Indian Police Service (IPS) is an All India Service that provides senior officers to both central and state forces.

History

Modern policing in India derives substantially from the Indian Police Act of 1861, enacted after the Indian Rebellion of 1857, which established a unified police force in each province under an inspector general. Reforms in the colonial period were driven by the Police Commission of 1860 and later commissions in 1902–03. After independence in 1947, the basic structure was retained, though successive committees — including the National Police Commission (1977–81) and the directives of the Supreme Court in Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006) — have pressed for greater operational autonomy and accountability.

Organisation

Central agencies

The Ministry of Home Affairs oversees the Central Armed Police Forces: the Border Security Force (BSF), Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP), Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB), Assam Rifles and the National Security Guard (NSG), a counter-terrorism unit. Central investigation and intelligence agencies include the CBI, the NIA, the Narcotics Control Bureau and the Intelligence Bureau.

State police

Each state has its own police force headed by a Director General of Police (DGP). State forces are organised into ranges, zones, districts and police stations; the station house officer is the pivotal figure in day-to-day policing and crime investigation. Several states have adopted a police commissionerate system in large cities, where commissioners exercise magisterial powers.

Recruitment and training

Officers enter through the Indian Police Service via the Union Public Service Commission's Civil Services Examination and are trained at the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy in Hyderabad. State services recruit deputy superintendents and constables through state commissions, with training at state police academies and district training schools.
