The history of Sudan extends from the ancient Kingdom of Kush, whose pharaohs ruled Egypt as its 25th Dynasty, through the Christian kingdoms of Nubia, Islamisation, the Mahdist state, and the Anglo-Egyptian condominium, to the independent republic of 1956 — marked by civil wars, the secession of South Sudan in 2011, and the fall of Omar al-Bashir in 2019.

The pyramids of Meroë, capital of the ancient Kingdom of Kush.

Ancient and medieval Sudan

Nubian kingdoms along the Nile traded and fought with pharaonic Egypt for millennia. The Kingdom of Kush, centred first at Napata and later at Meroë, conquered Egypt around 744 BC. After the fall of Meroë (c. 350 AD), the Christian kingdoms of Nobatia, Makuria and Alodia emerged; Makuria resisted Arab expansion and preserved Christianity for centuries. From the 14th to 16th centuries Islam spread, and the Funj Sultanate of Sennar (1504–1821) dominated the north while the Fur Sultanate ruled Darfur.

Turco-Egyptian and Mahdist Sudan

In 1820–21 Muhammad Ali of Egypt conquered Sudan ("the Turkiyya"). Khartoum was founded as the capital. Egyptian rule, associated with heavy taxation and the slave trade, provoked the revolt of Muhammad Ahmad, the self-proclaimed Mahdi, whose forces captured Khartoum in 1885, killing General Gordon. The Mahdist state survived under the Khalifa Abdallahi until its defeat by an Anglo-Egyptian army under Kitchener at Omdurman in 1898.

Condominium and independence

From 1899 Sudan was ruled as an Anglo-Egyptian condominium, in practice by Britain. The south was administered separately from the Muslim, Arabic-speaking north. Sudan became independent on 1 January 1956 under Prime Minister Ismail al-Azhari.

The republic

Independence was marred from the start by civil war between the north and the south (1955–1972 and 1983–2005). Military regimes alternated with brief democratic periods: Ibrahim Abboud (1958–64), Jaafar Nimeiri (1969–85), and Omar al-Bashir, who seized power in 1989 and ruled for thirty years, imposing Islamist rule and fighting the war in Darfur from 2003, for which he was indicted by the International Criminal Court. The 2005 Comprehensive Peace Agreement led to South Sudan's independence in July 2011. Mass protests forced al-Bashir from power in April 2019; a transitional government under Abdalla Hamdok was followed by a military coup in 2021 and, from April 2023, a devastating war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces.
