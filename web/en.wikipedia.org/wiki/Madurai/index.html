Madurai (Tamil: மதுரை, Maturai) is a major city in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu, situated on the banks of the Vaigai River. One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, it was the capital of the Pandya kingdom and a centre of Tamil learning for over two millennia. Madurai is famous for the vast Meenakshi Amman Temple complex at its heart, around which the old city is laid out in concentric streets in the shape of a lotus.

History

Madurai is mentioned by Greek ambassador Megasthenes in the 3rd century BC and in the works of Roman historians; it traded extensively with Rome. It was the seat of the Tamil Sangams, legendary academies of poets and scholars. After periods under the Kalabhras, Cholas, and the Delhi Sultanate's Madurai Sultanate, the city flourished under the Vijayanagara Empire and its Nayak governors in the 16th and 17th centuries, who built much of the present temple complex and the Thirumalai Nayakkar Palace (1636). The city passed to the Nawab of Arcot and then to the British, becoming part of independent India in 1947.

Meenakshi Temple

The Meenakshi Sundareswarar Temple, dedicated to the goddess Meenakshi (a form of Parvati) and her consort Sundareswarar (Shiva), dominates the city. Its fourteen gopurams (gateway towers) rise up to 52 metres and are covered with thousands of painted stucco figures. The temple draws tens of thousands of visitors daily and hosts the month-long Meenakshi Thirukalyanam (celestial wedding) festival each spring.

Economy and culture

Madurai is a centre for textiles, jasmine cultivation (the famed Madurai malli), rubber, information technology, and education, with institutions including Madurai Kamaraj University. Its street food culture and round-the-clock activity have earned it the nickname "Thoonga Nagaram" — the city that never sleeps.
