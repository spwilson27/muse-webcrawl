Coaching

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A coaching session: the coach supports the client's own goal-setting and problem-solving

Coaching is a form of development in which an experienced person, called a coach, supports a learner or client in achieving a specific personal or professional goal by providing training and guidance. Unlike mentoring or therapy, coaching focuses on present performance and future outcomes rather than on the past, and the coach typically facilitates the client's own solutions instead of giving direct advice.

Types of coaching

  • Sports coaching — the original sense: a coach directs training and strategy for athletes and teams.
  • Executive/business coaching — leadership and performance development for managers and professionals.
  • Life coaching — personal goals, wellbeing, career transitions, and relationships.
  • Health coaching — support for lifestyle and behaviour change.
  • Career coaching — job search, advancement, and vocational direction.

Methods

Common tools include powerful questioning, active listening, the GROW model (Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward), accountability structures, and 360-degree feedback. Professional bodies such as the International Coaching Federation (ICF) set competency standards and accredit training programs; coaching itself is largely unregulated in most countries.

History

The word "coach" comes from the horse-drawn carriage of Kocs, Hungary, later adopted at Oxford University in the 1830s for tutors who "carried" students through exams. Sports usage followed, and management/life coaching expanded from the 1970s–90s onwards, drawing on humanistic psychology and athletics performance methods popularised by Timothy Gallwey's The Inner Game of Tennis (1974).
