Northeast Caucasian languages

Distribution of Northeast Caucasian languages

The Northeast Caucasian languages, also called Nakh-Daghestanian or Caspian languages, are a language family spoken in the Russian republics of Dagestan, Chechnya and Ingushetia, and in northern Azerbaijan and parts of Georgia. The family comprises around 30 languages spoken by roughly four million people.

Branches

The family is usually divided into the Nakh branch — Chechen, Ingush and the moribund Bats language of Georgia — and the Daghestanian branches, which include Avar, Dargwa, Lak, Lezgian, Tabasaran, the Tsezic languages, and the small Khinalug language. Classification within Daghestanian remains debated among specialists.

Features

Northeast Caucasian languages are famous for their large consonant inventories, including ejective and pharyngeal consonants; Lezgian's relative Tabasaran was long cited as having one of the world's largest consonant systems. Most are ergative in alignment and have elaborate systems of noun classes (genders) marked by agreement prefixes. Verbal morphology is rich, while word order is typically subject–object–verb.

Status

Several languages of the family, such as Chechen and Avar, have millions or hundreds of thousands of speakers and literary standards, usually written in Cyrillic. Many of the smaller Daghestanian languages spoken in single mountain villages are endangered as speakers shift to Russian or to larger regional languages.
