Violence Against Women Act

The Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) is a United States federal law (Title IV of the Violent Crime Control and Law Enforcement Act, H.R. 3355) signed by President Bill Clinton on September 13, 1994. The Act provided $1.6 billion toward investigation and prosecution of violent crimes against women, imposed automatic and mandatory restitution on those convicted, and allowed civil redress when prosecutors chose not to prosecute.

Provisions

VAWA created the Office on Violence Against Women within the Department of Justice, funded rape crisis centres and domestic violence shelters, established the National Domestic Violence Hotline, and strengthened federal penalties for repeat sex offenders and interstate domestic violence. A civil rights remedy allowing victims to sue attackers in federal court was struck down by the Supreme Court in United States v. Morrison (2000).

Reauthorizations

The Act was reauthorized by Congress in 2000, 2005, and 2013 — the 2013 reauthorization extended protections to Native American women on tribal lands, LGBT victims, and immigrants. After lapsing in 2019 amid disputes over provisions concerning firearm possession by convicted abusers and tribal jurisdiction, VAWA was again reauthorized in March 2022 as part of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, with expanded tribal criminal jurisdiction over non-Native perpetrators of certain crimes.

Then-Senator Joe Biden, the bill's principal sponsor, regarded it as his signature legislative accomplishment on violence against women.
