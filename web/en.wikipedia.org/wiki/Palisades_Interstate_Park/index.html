Palisades Interstate Park is a protected area along the western shore of the Hudson River in northeastern New Jersey and southeastern New York, preserving the Hudson Palisades cliffs and the riverfront below them. It is administered by the Palisades Interstate Park Commission, an interstate agency created in 1900 by New Jersey and New York—the first such bi-state park commission in the United States.

History

In the late nineteenth century, quarrying for trap rock was rapidly destroying the Palisades cliffs facing Manhattan. Conservationists, supported by figures such as Theodore Roosevelt (then governor of New York) and by the New Jersey Federation of Women's Clubs, campaigned to save the cliffs. The two states established the commission in 1900, and philanthropist J. P. Morgan provided funds to halt quarrying and purchase the land. Later benefactors, notably John D. Rockefeller Jr., funded the acquisition of the clifftop land and the construction of the Palisades Interstate Parkway.

Features

The New Jersey section stretches about 12 miles (19 km) from Fort Lee to the state line at Alpine, with more than 30 miles of hiking trails, including the Long Path and Shore Trail, riverfront picnic areas, boat basins, and the historic Kearney House and Fort Lee Historic Park near the George Washington Bridge. The commission's New York holdings expanded to include Bear Mountain and Harriman State Parks and numerous other properties, making the system one of the largest regional park networks in the eastern United States.
