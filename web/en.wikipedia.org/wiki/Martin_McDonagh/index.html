Martin Faranan McDonagh (born 26 March 1970) is a British-Irish playwright and filmmaker. He is known for his absurdist dark humour which often challenges the modern theatre aesthetic. He has won numerous accolades including an Academy Award, six BAFTA Awards, four Golden Globe Awards, three Olivier Awards, and five Tony Awards.

Born in London to Irish parents, McDonagh spent summers in County Galway, the setting of his early plays. His first six plays — including The Beauty Queen of Leenane (1996), The Cripple of Inishmaan (1996), and The Lieutenant of Inishmore (2001) — were staged to acclaim in Ireland, London, and New York, making him one of the most performed living Irish playwrights. The Pillowman (2003) won the Olivier Award for Best New Play, and A Behanding in Spokane (2010) and Hangmen (2015) extended his stage success.

Film career

McDonagh won the Academy Award for Best Live Action Short Film for Six Shooter (2004). His feature debut, In Bruges (2008), a dark comedy about two hitmen hiding in Belgium, earned him an Oscar nomination and a BAFTA for its screenplay. Seven Psychopaths followed in 2012. Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri (2017) won the Golden Globe and BAFTA for Best Film and Academy Awards for Frances McDormand and Sam Rockwell. The Banshees of Inisherin (2022), set on a fictional Irish island and starring Colin Farrell and Brendan Gleeson, won the Volpi Cup and Best Screenplay at Venice, three Golden Globes, and received nine Academy Award nominations.
