Caffeic acid

IUPAC name3-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)prop-2-enoic acid
FormulaC9H8O4
Molar mass180.16 g/mol
AppearanceYellow crystalline solid
Melting point223–225 °C (decomp.)

Caffeic acid is an organic compound classified as a hydroxycinnamic acid. This yellow crystalline solid consists of both phenolic and acrylic functional groups. It is found in all plants because it is an intermediate in the biosynthesis of lignin, one of the principal components of woody plant biomass and its residues.

Occurrence

Caffeic acid occurs in many foods, especially coffee, where much of it is present as chlorogenic acid, its ester with quinic acid — roasting breaks chlorogenic acid down and releases caffeic acid. It is also abundant in herbs such as thyme and sage, in sunflower seeds, apples, and many other fruits and vegetables. Despite the similar name, it is unrelated chemically to caffeine.

Biosynthesis

In plants, caffeic acid is formed by hydroxylation of p-coumaric acid in the phenylpropanoid pathway, which begins with phenylalanine. It is a key precursor of ferulic acid, coniferyl alcohol, and other building blocks of lignin and of numerous plant phenolics.

Pharmacology and safety

Caffeic acid has antioxidant activity in vitro and is one of many dietary phenolics studied for anti-inflammatory and other biological effects, though evidence for health benefits in humans is limited. The International Agency for Research on Cancer classified caffeic acid as a Group 2B agent ("possibly carcinogenic to humans") on the basis of animal studies at high doses, a classification it shares with many common dietary substances.

Related compounds

  • Chlorogenic acid — its quinic ester, abundant in green coffee
  • Ferulic acid — its methylated derivative
  • Caffeine — a chemically unrelated alkaloid found in coffee
