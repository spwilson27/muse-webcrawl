Ileocecal valve

The ileocecal valve is a sphincter muscle valve in the digestive tract that separates the small intestine from the large intestine. It lies at the junction of the ileum, the final segment of the small intestine, and the cecum of the large intestine.

Function

The valve regulates the flow of chyme from the ileum into the cecum and limits reflux of colonic contents — which are rich in bacteria — back into the small intestine. It also helps control the rate of emptying so that nutrients can be absorbed.

Clinical significance

Disorders involving the region include Crohn's disease, which commonly affects the terminal ileum and valve, and intussusception in children, which often begins near the ileocecal junction.
