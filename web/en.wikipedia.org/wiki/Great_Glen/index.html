Overview

The Great Glen (Glen Albyn, "Glen of Scotland") is a glacial trough and geological fault zone running about 100 km across the Scottish Highlands from Inverness on the Moray Firth to Fort William on Loch Linnhe. The Great Glen Fault displaces the older rocks on either side by roughly 100 km and remains seismically active at a low level.

The lochs and canal

A chain of ribbon lochs — Ness, Oich and Lochy — fills the glen; Thomas Telford's Caledonian Canal (opened 1822) links them for shipping between the North Sea and the Atlantic. Loch Ness is the largest loch by volume in the British Isles and is famed for its monster legend. The Great Glen Way long-distance trail follows the fault line.
