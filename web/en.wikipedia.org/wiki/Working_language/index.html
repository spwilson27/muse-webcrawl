Working language

A working language is a language given a unique legal status in a supranational company, society, state or other body as its primary means of communication, at least for internal work and correspondence.

Examples

The United Nations Secretariat works in English and French, while the UN has six official languages. The European Commission works mainly in English, French and German. Many multinational firms adopt English as their corporate working language even where it is not the local language.

Distinctions

A working language differs from an official language, which has legal status for all public acts; an organisation may have more official languages than the smaller set in which day-to-day work is actually done.
