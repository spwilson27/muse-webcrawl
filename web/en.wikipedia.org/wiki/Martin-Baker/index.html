Martin-Baker

Martin-Baker Aircraft Co. Ltd. is a British manufacturer of ejection seats and safety-related equipment for aviation. The company was originally founded as an aircraft manufacturer in 1934 by Captain (later Sir) James Martin and Captain Valentine Baker; after Baker was killed in a flying accident in 1942, Martin turned the firm's expertise to the problem of pilot escape, and Martin-Baker became the world's leading ejection seat maker.

Ejection seats

The company tested its first ejection seat in 1946, when fitter Bernard Lynch made the first live ejection in Britain. Its seats use explosive cartridges and rocket motors to clear the aircraft, followed by automatic parachute deployment, and modern "zero-zero" seats can operate safely at zero altitude and zero airspeed. Martin-Baker seats equip most Western military aircraft, including the F-35 Lightning II, Eurofighter Typhoon, and F/A-18.

Lives saved

Martin-Baker maintains a running count of lives saved by its seats — more than 7,600 — and pilots who eject successfully are invited to join the Ejection Tie Club, receiving a distinctive tie and certificate. The company remains family-owned and is based at Higher Denham, Buckinghamshire.
