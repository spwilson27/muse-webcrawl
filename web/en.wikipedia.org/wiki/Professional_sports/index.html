Professional sports

Professional sport is a major global entertainment industry.

In professional sports, as opposed to amateur sports, the participants receive payment for their performance. Professionalism in sport has come to the fore through a combination of two developments: the growth of mass media and increased leisure time, which allowed larger audiences to pay to watch sport, and the resulting revenues from gate receipts, broadcasting rights and sponsorship.

History

Payment in sport is ancient — athletes in classical Greece could earn substantial prizes — but modern professional sport developed in the 19th century. In England, cricket and horse racing were professional early, and association football legalised professionalism in 1885 after controversy over working-class players being paid. In the United States, baseball's National League was founded in 1876, and professional leagues in American football, basketball and ice hockey followed in the early 20th century. Many sports maintained strict amateur rules long after: the Olympic Games excluded professionals for most of the 20th century before gradually admitting them.

Economics

Modern professional leagues are big businesses. Revenue comes principally from media rights, ticket sales, sponsorship and merchandising. Leagues organise competition in different ways: North American leagues are typically closed franchise systems with drafts, salary caps and revenue sharing, while European football leagues operate open systems of promotion and relegation with largely uncapped spending. Top athletes in major sports command salaries and endorsement income among the highest of any entertainers.

Governance and issues

Professional sports are governed by leagues and federations that set rules, organise fixtures and discipline participants. Recurring issues include the balance of competitive power between rich and poor teams, players' rights and collective bargaining, doping, match-fixing and gambling, and the public cost of stadium construction.
