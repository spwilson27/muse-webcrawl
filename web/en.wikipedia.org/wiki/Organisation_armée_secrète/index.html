Organisation armée secrète

The Organisation armée secrète (OAS, "Secret Army Organisation") was a far-right French paramilitary organisation that campaigned to prevent Algerian independence during the Algerian War (1954–1962). Founded in Madrid in January 1961 by officers including Raoul Salan and Jean-Jacques Susini, the OAS carried out bombings and assassinations in Algeria and metropolitan France, and attempted to assassinate President Charles de Gaulle.

Background and formation

After de Gaulle's 1959 declaration of Algerian self-determination, die-hard supporters of Algérie française — pied-noir settlers, ultra officers, and politicians — concluded that the government intended to abandon French Algeria. The failed "Week of the Barricades" in Algiers (January 1960) radicalised the movement. The OAS was formed in exile in Franco's Spain, merging settler activist networks with dissident military men, and adopted terror tactics copied in part from the FLN it opposed.

Campaign

Following the failed Generals' Putsch of April 1961, in which Salan and other OAS leaders seized Algiers for four days, the clandestine organisation waged a campaign of plastic explosive bombings (plasticages), assassinations of officials and Muslim civilians, and scorched-earth destruction in the final months before independence. It attempted to kill de Gaulle several times, most famously in the Petit-Clamart ambush of 22 August 1962, led by Jean Bastien-Thiry, who was executed in 1963 — the last person executed by firing squad in France. The campaign failed: the Évian Accords of March 1962 confirmed Algerian independence on 5 July 1962, accompanied by the mass exodus of the pieds-noirs.

Aftermath

Salan was captured in April 1962 and sentenced to life imprisonment; other leaders fled abroad. Amnesty laws of 1966 and 1968 freed and later rehabilitated the surviving members, and a 1982 law reinstated the pensions of the putschist generals. The OAS remains a byword for far-right terrorism in French political memory and inspired Frederick Forsyth's novel The Day of the Jackal.
