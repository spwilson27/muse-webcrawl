Chern character

Schematic of the Chern character mapping K-theory to cohomology (image placeholder)

The Chern character is a homomorphism from K-theory to ordinary cohomology, introduced by Shiing-Shen Chern. It assigns to a complex vector bundle a formal power series in cohomology classes built from its Chern classes.

Definition

For a complex vector bundle E with Chern roots x1, …, xn, the Chern character is defined by ch(E) = Σ exi. Expanding the exponentials gives ch(E) = rank(E) + c1(E) + (1/2)(c12 − 2c2) + (1/6)(c13 − 3c1c2 + 3c3) + …, where ci are the Chern classes of E.

Properties

The Chern character is additive on direct sums and multiplicative on tensor products: ch(E ⊕ F) = ch(E) + ch(F) and ch(E ⊗ F) = ch(E)·ch(F). It therefore descends to a ring homomorphism from the Grothendieck group K(X) to the (rationally completed) cohomology ring H*(X; Q). After tensoring with the rationals it becomes an isomorphism — this is one way of characterising the relationship between K-theory and ordinary cohomology.

Applications

The Chern character appears centrally in the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem and in the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, where it is combined with the Todd class. In physics it appears in the description of D-brane charges in string theory.
