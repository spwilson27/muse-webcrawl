Ceiriog Valley

The Ceiriog Valley (Welsh: Dyffryn Ceiriog) is a valley in Wrexham County Borough, north-east Wales, through which flows the River Ceiriog (Afon Ceiriog). Running from the Berwyn Mountains west of Llanarmon Dyffryn Ceiriog eastward to join the River Dee near Chirk, the valley is noted for its scenery and literary associations, and was called a "little Switzerland" by David Lloyd George.

Geography

The River Ceiriog rises in the Berwyns and flows about 29 km (18 miles) east through the villages of Llanarmon Dyffryn Ceiriog, Tregeiriog, Pandy, Glyn Ceiriog and Pontfadog, before passing under the Chirk Aqueduct near its confluence with the Dee. The valley is steep-sided and wooded in its lower reaches, opening into moorland at its head.

History and culture

The Battle of Crogen (1165), in which the forces of Henry II were ambushed and repulsed by the Welsh under Owain Gwynedd, is traditionally located in the valley near Castle Mill. The Ceiriog Memorial Institute in Glyn Ceiriog commemorates the valley's strong Welsh-language literary tradition: poets associated with the valley include Huw Morys (the "Nightingale of the Ceiriog"), John Ceiriog Hughes ("Ceiriog", 1832–1887), one of Wales's most popular lyric poets, and Robert ("Cynddelw") Ellis. Chirk Castle, a National Trust property, stands near the valley mouth.

Quarrying

Granite and slate quarries in the valley once employed many local men; the Glyn Valley Tramway (1873–1935) carried slate and granite from the quarries at Glyn Ceiriog down to the Shropshire Union Canal and the railway at Chirk.
