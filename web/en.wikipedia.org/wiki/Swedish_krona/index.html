Swedish krona

Swedish krona
Swedish: svensk krona
Code: SEK
Symbol: kr
Central bank: Sveriges Riksbank
Subunit: öre (1/100)

The Swedish krona (Swedish: krona, plural kronor; code SEK) is the official currency of Sweden. One krona is divided into 100 öre, although öre coins have not circulated since 2010 and prices are rounded to the nearest krona for cash payments. The krona is issued by Sweden's central bank, Sveriges Riksbank, the world's oldest central bank, founded in 1668.

History

The krona was introduced in 1873, replacing the riksdaler at par, when Sweden and Denmark formed the Scandinavian Monetary Union; Norway joined two years later. The union dissolved around the outbreak of the First World War, but all three countries retained "crown" currencies of their own.

Sweden maintained various fixed exchange-rate regimes through the twentieth century. After a severe banking and currency crisis, the krona was floated on 19 November 1992, and the Riksbank adopted a formal inflation target of 2 per cent in 1993. In a 2003 referendum, Swedish voters rejected adopting the euro, and Sweden has remained outside the eurozone while remaining an EU member state.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 1, 2, 5 and 10 kronor. A new series of banknotes was introduced in 2015–2016 in denominations of 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1,000 kronor, featuring Swedish cultural figures such as Astrid Lindgren, Evert Taube, Greta Garbo, Ingmar Bergman, Birgit Nilsson and Dag Hammarskjöld.

Cashless society

Sweden is among the world's most cashless societies: most retail payments are made by card or by the mobile payment system Swish, and many banks and shops no longer handle cash. The Riksbank has investigated issuing a digital complement to cash, the e-krona.
