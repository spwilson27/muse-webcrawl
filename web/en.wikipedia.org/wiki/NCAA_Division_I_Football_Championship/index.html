The NCAA Division I Football Championship is the annual playoff tournament that determines the national champion of the NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision (FCS), the second tier of Division I college football. First contested in 1978 when the level was known as Division I-AA, it is the only NCAA football championship decided by a bracket playoff of its size at the Division I level.

Format

Twenty-four teams qualify: conference champions receive automatic bids and the remainder are selected at large by a committee. The top eight seeds receive first-round byes and host games on campus sites; the semifinals follow on campus, and the championship game has been played at Toyota Stadium in Frisco, Texas, since the 2010 season.

Champions

North Dakota State dominates the record book, winning ten championships between 2011 and 2024, including five consecutive titles from 2011 to 2015. Georgia Southern won six titles (and has since moved to FBS), Youngstown State four, and Appalachian State three in a row (2005–2007) before also moving up. South Dakota State won back-to-back championships in 2022 and 2023.

History

The championship began as a four-team event in 1978, won by Florida A&M, and expanded through fields of 8, 12, 16 and 20 teams before reaching 24 in 2013. The subdivision was renamed from I-AA to FCS in 2006. In 2025 the final was announced to be moving to Nashville, Tennessee, from the 2025 season championship game onward.
