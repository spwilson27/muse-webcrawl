Mexican literature

Mexican literature is one of the most prolific and influential in the Spanish-speaking world. It encompasses works produced in the territory of modern Mexico from pre-Hispanic times to the present, written in Spanish and in indigenous languages such as Nahuatl and Yucatec Maya.

Pre-Hispanic and colonial literature

Pre-Hispanic literature includes Nahuatl poetry attributed to figures such as Nezahualcoyotl, the poet-king of Texcoco, preserved after the conquest by indigenous scribes. Colonial literature features the chronicles of Bernal Díaz del Castillo, the poetry and theatre of Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz — the great Baroque poet of New Spain — and the history of Francisco Javier Clavijero.

Nineteenth and twentieth centuries

The 19th century produced the first Mexican novel, José Joaquín Fernández de Lizardi's El Periquillo Sarniento (1816), and the poetry of Manuel José Othón. The Mexican Revolution inspired the "novel of the Revolution", including Mariano Azuela's Los de abajo (1915). In the 20th century, Juan Rulfo's Pedro Páramo (1955) became a landmark of magical realism, while Octavio Paz — Nobel laureate in Literature in 1990 — and Carlos Fuentes achieved international acclaim. Other major figures include Alfonso Reyes, Rosario Castellanos, Elena Poniatowska and Jorge Ibargüengoitia.

Contemporary literature

Recent generations include writers such as Valeria Luiselli, Fernanda Melchor and Yuri Herrera, noted for work addressing migration, violence and memory.
