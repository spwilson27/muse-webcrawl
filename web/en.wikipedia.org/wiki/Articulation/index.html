Articulation

Articulation may refer to several related concepts. In phonetics and speech science — the sense treated in this article — articulation is the movement of the tongue, lips, jaw and other speech organs (the articulators) to produce speech sounds. More generally, the word denotes any joint or jointed connection: in anatomy a joint is an articulation between bones; in music, articulation describes how notes are connected or separated (as in legato and staccato); and in ordinary usage, to articulate is to express an idea clearly in words.

Articulation in phonetics

Speech sounds are produced by modifying the flow of air from the lungs with the vocal tract. Consonants are classified by their place of articulation — the point where obstruction occurs, such as bilabial (both lips, as in /p/), alveolar (tongue against the alveolar ridge, /t/), velar (back of the tongue against the soft palate, /k/) and glottal (at the glottis, /h/) — and by their manner of articulation, including plosives, fricatives, nasals, approximants and laterals. Vowels are described by the height and frontness of the tongue and the shape of the lips. The International Phonetic Alphabet organises consonants in a chart by place and manner of articulation.

Articulators

The active articulators are the lower lip, the tongue (tip, blade, front, back and root), the lower jaw and the vocal folds. The passive articulators — the surfaces against which the active ones act — include the upper lip, the teeth, the alveolar ridge, the hard palate and the velum. Coarticulation is the influence of neighbouring sounds on one another in continuous speech.

Disorders

An articulation disorder is a difficulty producing particular speech sounds correctly, common in childhood speech-language pathology and distinct from phonological disorders, which involve patterns of sound errors. Assessment and therapy are provided by speech-language pathologists (speech and language therapists).

Other senses

  • Anatomy: a joint, the point of connection between two bones.
  • Music: the transition or continuity between notes, marked by slurs, accents, staccato dots and similar signs.
  • Vehicles and machinery: a jointed connection, as in an articulated lorry or articulated bus, which bends at a pivot.
  • Education: the formal coordination of curricula or credit transfer between institutions.
