Dunedin railway station

Dunedin railway station in Dunedin, New Zealand, is one of the city's most prominent architectural landmarks. Designed by George Troup, it opened in 1906 and is built in an eclectic revived Flemish Renaissance style from dark basalt and Oamaru stone.

Architecture

The station is noted for its elaborate ornamentation, tiled booking hall with Royal Doulton porcelain friezes, and a mosaic floor of almost 750,000 tiles. Troup earned the nickname "Gingerbread George" for the building's decorative style. It is registered as a Category I heritage place by Heritage New Zealand.

Services

Regular passenger services declined during the twentieth century; today the station is principally used by the Taieri Gorge Railway tourist trains operated by Dunedin Railways, and the building also houses the New Zealand Sports Hall of Fame and the Otago Art Society gallery.
