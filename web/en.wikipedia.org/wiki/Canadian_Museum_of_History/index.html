Canadian Museum of History

The Canadian Museum of History (French: Musée canadien de l'histoire), formerly the Canadian Museum of Civilization, is Canada's national museum of human history. It is located in Gatineau, Quebec, across the Ottawa River from Parliament Hill in Ottawa. The museum's primary purpose is to collect, study, preserve, and present material objects that illuminate the human history of Canada and the cultural diversity of its people.

The museum is the most popular and most visited museum in Canada, attracting roughly 1.2 million visitors each year. Its collections include more than four million artifacts, specimens, archival documents, works of art, and sound and visual recordings.

History

The museum traces its origins to the Geological Survey of Canada, which began collecting anthropological material in 1856. It became the anthropology division of the National Museum of Canada and, in 1968, the National Museum of Man. In 1986 it was renamed the Canadian Museum of Civilization. In 1989 it moved to its present building, a landmark of curvilinear limestone designed by architect Douglas Cardinal, whose forms evoke the Canadian Shield, glaciers, and the flowing waters of the Ottawa River. In 2013 the museum was renamed the Canadian Museum of History, with a revised mandate focused on Canadian history.

Exhibitions

The Grand Hall, the largest interior space, presents towering totem poles and house fronts of the First Peoples of the Pacific Coast, the largest indoor collection of totem poles in the world. The First Peoples Hall presents the history and contemporary life of Indigenous peoples in Canada. The Canadian History Hall, opened on 1 July 2017 for Canada 150, is the museum's largest and most comprehensive exhibition, telling the story of Canada and its peoples from the human settlement of North America to the present day. The museum complex also houses the Canadian Children's Museum and a large-format theatre.
