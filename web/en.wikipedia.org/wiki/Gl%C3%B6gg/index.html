Glögg

Glögg is a spiced, usually alcoholic, mulled wine or spirit drink associated with winter traditions in Nordic countries, particularly Sweden, Finland, and Estonia. It is traditionally served warm during Advent and at Christmas.

Preparation

Glögg is typically made by heating red wine (or, in stronger versions, spirits such as vodka or aquavit) with sugar and spices such as cinnamon, cardamom, ginger, cloves, and bitter orange. It is served in small cups with raisins and blanched almonds. Non-alcoholic versions made from fruit juices are also common.

History

Spiced heated wines have a long history in northern Europe, where they were consumed for warmth and were once believed to have health benefits. Swedish glögg parties (glöggmingel) are a feature of the pre-Christmas season, and bottled ready-made glögg is sold widely, including in Sweden through the state alcohol monopoly.
