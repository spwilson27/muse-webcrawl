Shaba I

DateMarch - May 1977
LocationShaba Province, Zaire
ResultZairian government victory with foreign support

Shaba I was a conflict in Zaire's Shaba Province (formerly Katanga) in 1977, when fighters of the Congolese National Liberation Front (FLNC) crossed from Angola into Shaba and advanced on the mining town of Kolwezi. The incursion was repelled after France and Morocco intervened in support of President Mobutu Sese Seko.

Background

The FLNC was composed largely of former Katangese gendarmes who had fled to Angola after the failed Katanga secession of the 1960s and later fought alongside the MPLA in the Angolan civil war. In March 1977 several thousand FLNC fighters entered Shaba, quickly occupying towns as Zairian army units fell back.

Intervention and outcome

France airlifted Moroccan troops to Shaba in April 1977, in an operation supported logistically by the United States and Belgium. The combined force retook the occupied towns by late May, and the FLNC withdrew into Angola. A second, larger invasion, Shaba II, followed in 1978.
