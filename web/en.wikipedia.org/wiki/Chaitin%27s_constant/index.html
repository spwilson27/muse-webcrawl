Chaitin's constant

In the computer science subfield of algorithmic information theory, a Chaitin constant (Chaitin omega number) is the halting probability of a universal prefix-free Turing machine. Introduced by Gregory Chaitin, it is a real number between 0 and 1 whose binary expansion encodes the solution to the halting problem.

Definition

Formally, given a prefix-free universal Turing machine U, the halting probability ΩU is the probability that U halts when its input is a bit string whose bits are generated one by one by independent fair coin tosses. It can be written as the sum over all halting programs p of 2−|p|.

Properties

Chaitin constants are normal numbers, are not computable, and are algorithmically random: knowing the first n bits of Ω determines the halting status of all programs up to length n. They are also Turing equivalent to the halting problem.

History

Chaitin introduced the concept in the 1970s as a concrete example of randomness and incompleteness in mathematics, connecting algorithmic information theory with Gödel's incompleteness theorems.
