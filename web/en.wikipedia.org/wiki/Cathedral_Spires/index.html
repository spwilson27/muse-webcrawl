Cathedral Spires

Cathedral Spires
LocationMount Wilson, Blue Mountains, New South Wales, Australia
Rock typeSandstone (Narrabeen Group)
SettingWollangambe wilderness area, Blue Mountains National Park region

The Cathedral Spires are a formation of tall, narrow sandstone pinnacles near Mount Wilson in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia. The pinnacles, which rise like the spires of a Gothic cathedral from the surrounding bush, are a product of differential erosion of the region's Narrabeen Group sandstones, in which harder bands resisted weathering while softer rock was stripped away, leaving isolated towers and fins.

Geology

The Blue Mountains plateau consists of nearly horizontal Triassic sedimentary rocks — quartz sandstones interbedded with shales — uplifted during the Cenozoic. Over millions of years, streams cut deep gorges into the plateau, and cliff lines retreated as softer layers were undercut. Narrow remnant pillars such as the Cathedral Spires, the more famous Three Sisters near Katoomba, and the pagoda formations of the Gardens of Stone are all expressions of this process of case-hardened sandstone weathering.

Visiting

The formations lie in rugged canyon country frequented by bushwalkers and canyoners exploring the Wollangambe River system. The area forms part of the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area, inscribed by UNESCO in 2000 for its exceptional eucalypt-dominated ecosystems and geodiversity.
