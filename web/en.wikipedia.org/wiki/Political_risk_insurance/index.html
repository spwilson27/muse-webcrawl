Political risk insurance

Political risk insurance is a type of insurance that protects investors, lenders and businesses against financial losses caused by political events in a host country. It is purchased by companies making foreign direct investments, by exporters, and by banks financing cross-border projects.

Covered perils

Typical coverage includes:

  • Expropriation — confiscation, nationalization or creeping expropriation of assets by a host government;
  • Political violence — war, revolution, insurrection, civil strife and terrorism;
  • Currency inconvertibility and transfer restriction — inability to convert local currency earnings or repatriate them;
  • Breach of contract and non-honouring of sovereign financial obligations;
  • Wrongful calling of guarantees such as bid and performance bonds.

Providers

Public providers include national export credit agencies and the United States International Development Finance Corporation (successor to OPIC), while multilateral cover is offered by the World Bank's Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA). A substantial private market, centred partly on Lloyd's of London and specialty insurers, offers shorter-tenor and more flexible policies.

Assessment

Underwriting relies on political risk analysis of a country's stability, rule of law, macroeconomic position and history of treatment of foreign investors. Premiums vary widely by country and sector, from well under one per cent to several per cent of the insured amount per year. See also political risk and expropriation.
