The Royal Navy (RN) is the naval warfare force of the United Kingdom, the senior service of the British Armed Forces. Founded in the 16th century, it was the world's dominant naval power from the late 17th century until the Second World War, playing a central role in the establishment and defence of the British Empire.

History

Henry VIII created a standing "Navy Royal" with its own dockyards and administration. The Navy fought the Spanish Armada (1588), the Dutch in the Anglo-Dutch Wars, and France through the 18th century, achieving global supremacy after victories such as Trafalgar (1805) under Horatio Nelson. In the 19th century it suppressed the slave trade and underwrote the Pax Britannica; the launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906 revolutionised battleship design. In two world wars it fought the Battle of Jutland, the Battle of the Atlantic, and campaigns in the Mediterranean and Pacific. Since the 1960s it has operated the United Kingdom's nuclear deterrent, today carried by Vanguard-class submarines (Continuous At-Sea Deterrent).

The modern fleet

The modern Royal Navy operates two Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers, nuclear-powered attack and ballistic-missile submarines, Type 45 destroyers and Type 23 (and forthcoming Type 26 and Type 31) frigates, amphibious ships, minehunters, patrol vessels, and the Royal Fleet Auxiliary. Specialist arms include the Submarine Service, the Fleet Air Arm, and the Royal Marines. Its principal bases are Portsmouth, Devonport (Plymouth), and Clyde (Faslane) in Scotland.
