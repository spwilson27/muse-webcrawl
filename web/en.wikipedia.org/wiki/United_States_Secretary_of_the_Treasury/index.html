United States Secretary of the Treasury

The United States Secretary of the Treasury is the head of the US Department of the Treasury and the chief financial officer of the federal government. A member of the President's Cabinet, the Secretary is the principal economic adviser to the President, responsible for formulating and recommending domestic and international financial, economic and tax policy, managing the public debt, and overseeing the production of currency and coinage. The Secretary's signature appears on US paper currency.

History and role

The office was created by Congress on 2 September 1789. Its first holder, Alexander Hamilton (1789–1795), established the nation's financial system: funding the national debt, creating the First Bank of the United States and the US Mint, and organising what became the Coast Guard. The Secretary is fifth in the presidential line of succession and is designated a statutory member of the National Security Council. In modern times the Secretary plays a central role in crisis management — from the Latin American debt crisis to the 2008 financial crisis, when Secretary Henry Paulson administered the Troubled Asset Relief Program — and in sanctions policy through the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control.

Duties

The Secretary oversees bureaus including the Internal Revenue Service, the Bureau of the Fiscal Service, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, the US Mint and the Bureau of Engraving and Printing. The Secretary chairs the Financial Stability Oversight Council (since the Dodd–Frank Act of 2010) and serves as US governor of the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank.

Notable secretaries have included Albert Gallatin, the longest-serving (1801–1814); Salmon P. Chase, who financed the Union in the Civil War and later became Chief Justice; Andrew Mellon; Henry Morgenthau Jr.; and Robert Rubin and Janet Yellen, the first woman to hold the post (2021–2025) after chairing the Federal Reserve. Secretaries are appointed by the President with Senate confirmation.
