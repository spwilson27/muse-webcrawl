A military, also known collectively as armed forces, is a heavily armed, highly organised force primarily intended for warfare. It is typically authorised and maintained by a sovereign state, with its members identifiable by a distinct military uniform. It may consist of one or more military branches, principally an army, navy and air force, and in many states also marines, a coast guard or a space force.

Organisation

Militaries are organised hierarchically by rank, from enlisted personnel through non-commissioned officers to commissioned officers, and grouped into units of increasing size (squads, platoons, companies, battalions, brigades, divisions, corps and armies). The primary mission of the military is the defence of the state and its interests against external armed threats; many states also employ their militaries for internal security, disaster relief, peacekeeping and ceremonial duties. Reserve forces and paramilitary organisations may supplement the regular armed forces.

Personnel and recruitment

Personnel may be volunteers serving under enlistment contracts, conscripts fulfilling compulsory military service, or a mixture of both. Since the late twentieth century many states, including the United States (from 1973) and most Western European countries, have moved to all-volunteer professional forces. Global military spending exceeds two trillion US dollars annually, with the largest spenders including the United States, China, Russia and India.

History

Organised warfare dates to prehistory, but standing professional armies emerged with the great agrarian empires — Assyria, Persia, Rome and Han China. The modern military developed from early modern European reforms (Maurice of Nassau, Gustavus Adolphus), the levée en masse of revolutionary France, and the staff systems of nineteenth-century Prussia. World Wars I and II drove mass mobilisation, mechanisation and air power; the Cold War added nuclear deterrence, and recent decades have emphasised joint operations, precision weapons, drones, cyber and space capabilities.

Military in society

Militaries are governed by military law and, in democracies, by the principle of civilian control of the military. The laws of armed conflict, codified in the Geneva Conventions, regulate conduct in war. Civil–military relations, military coups, veterans' affairs and the cultural place of the armed forces ("militarism" and its critics) are major topics in political science and sociology.
