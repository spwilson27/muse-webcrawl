Claire Lee Chennault (September 6, 1893 – July 27, 1958) was an American military aviator best known for his leadership of the "Flying Tigers" and the United States Army Air Forces in China during World War II.

Early life and career

Chennault was raised in northeast Texas and attended Louisiana State Normal School. He learned to fly in the U.S. Army Air Service after World War I and became a leading advocate of pursuit (fighter) aviation at a time when bomber doctrine dominated the Air Corps. His aggressive ideas and friction with superiors led to his retirement as a captain in 1937.

China and the Flying Tigers

Recruited by Madame Chiang Kai-shek to survey the Chinese Air Force, Chennault organized the 1st American Volunteer Group (AVG) — American pilots flying Curtiss P-40s against the Japanese. The AVG, nicknamed the Flying Tigers, entered combat in December 1941 and achieved an outsized combat record, credited with destroying nearly 300 Japanese aircraft while losing 12 of its own in air combat.

Fourteenth Air Force

Reinstated as a major general in 1942, Chennault commanded the China Air Task Force and then the Fourteenth Air Force, championing close air support and interdiction over the Hump supply route strategy. His disagreements with Joseph Stilwell over China strategy were legendary.

Postwar life

After the war he founded Civil Air Transport (CAT), later Air America, which flew humanitarian and covert missions across Asia. He died of lung cancer in 1958 and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
