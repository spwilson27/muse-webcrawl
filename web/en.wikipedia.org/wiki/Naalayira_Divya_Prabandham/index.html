Naalayira Divya Prabandham


Naalayira Divya Prabandham
Religion: Sri Vaishnavism (Hinduism)
Language: Tamil
Verses: 4,000
Authors: The twelve Alvars
Period: c. 6th–9th centuries CE

The Naalayira Divya Prabandham (Tamil: நாலாயிர திவ்விய பிரபந்தம், "Four Thousand Divine Hymns") is a collection of 4,000 Tamil verses (Naalayiram) composed by the twelve Alvar saints of South India. It is the canonical scripture of Sri Vaishnavism, revered as the "Tamil Veda" and recited in Vishnu temples alongside the Sanskrit Vedas.

Compilation

Tradition holds that the hymns were largely lost until the theologian Nathamuni (9th–10th century) recovered and compiled them, arranging them with music; the recovery is associated with his hearing verses of Nammalvar's Tiruvaymoli and travelling to retrieve the full corpus. The collection is traditionally divided into four parts: Mudhalayiram, Periya Tirumoli, Iyarpa, and Tiruvaymoli.

Contents

The hymns praise Vishnu (Narayana) and his incarnations, especially Krishna and Rama, and are addressed largely to the deities of the 108 Divya Desam pilgrimage temples. Major works within the collection include Nammalvar's Tiruvaymoli, Periyalvar's Tiruppallandu, Andal's Tiruppavai and Nachiyar Tirumoli, and the hymns of Tirumangai Alvar.

Practice

Verses from the Prabandham are sung daily in Sri Vaishnava temples, prominently during festivals such as Vaikuntha Ekadashi and the Adhyayana Utsavam, when the entire 4,000 verses are recited.
