Vladislav Surkov

Born: 21 September 1964, Solntsevo, Lipetsk Oblast, USSR
Roles: Deputy Chief of Staff of the Presidential Administration (1999–2011); Deputy Prime Minister (2011–2013); presidential aide (2013–2020)
Known for: "Sovereign democracy", managed politics in Putin-era Russia

Vladislav Yuryevich Surkov (born 1964) is a Russian politician and businessman who served as a senior Kremlin official under Vladimir Putin and Dmitry Medvedev. Long described as the Kremlin's chief ideologist and "grey cardinal", he is credited with designing the system of "managed democracy" and coining the concept of sovereign democracy, as well as nurturing pro-Kremlin youth movements and a tightly controlled party system.

Early life and business career

Surkov was born in Lipetsk Oblast; by his own account his father was Chechen, and he spent his early childhood in Chechnya before his parents separated. After military service and studies in directing and economics, he became a prominent advertising and public-relations figure in the early post-Soviet business world, working with Mikhail Khodorkovsky's Bank Menatep and later Alfa Bank, and at the ORT television channel.

Kremlin career

In 1999 Surkov joined the Presidential Administration, rising to deputy chief of staff responsible for domestic politics. He supervised the creation and management of political parties — including the "party of power", United Russia — the marginalisation of independent media and opposition, and the patriotic youth movement Nashi. His 2006 speeches articulated "sovereign democracy", arguing Russia should adapt democratic forms to its own traditions. Under Medvedev's presidency he promoted modernisation rhetoric while retaining control of the political process.

Mass protests after the December 2011 parliamentary elections weakened his position; he was moved to the government as deputy prime minister and resigned in May 2013. He returned that September as a presidential aide overseeing relations with the post-Soviet states, and was widely reported to be the Kremlin's point man for the conflict in eastern Ukraine, a role reflected in the leaked "SurkovLeaks" correspondence. He was dismissed by Putin in February 2020.

Writings and image

Surkov is associated with the novel Almost Zero (2009), published under the pseudonym Natan Dubovitsky and widely attributed to him, a satire of the cynical PR-politics world he helped build. Western profiles have compared his blending of avant-garde aesthetics and authoritarian politics to performance art. He was placed under US and EU sanctions in 2014 over Ukraine. In 2019 he published an essay proclaiming "Putinism" a durable, exportable ideology of the "deep state" of the people.
