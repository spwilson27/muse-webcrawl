Ratan Tata

Ratan Naval Tata (28 December 1937 – 9 October 2024) was an Indian industrialist and philanthropist who served as chairman of Tata Sons, the holding company of the Tata Group, from 1991 to 2012, and briefly as interim chairman from 2016 to 2017. Under his leadership, the Tata Group transformed from a largely India-focused conglomerate into a global business, with group revenues growing many times over.

Early life and education

Tata was born in Bombay (now Mumbai) into the prominent Tata family, a Parsi Zoroastrian family of industrialists. He studied architecture at Cornell University, graduating in 1962, and later completed an advanced management programme at Harvard Business School. He joined the Tata Group in 1961, working on the shop floor of Tata Steel.

Career

Tata succeeded J. R. D. Tata as chairman in 1991, just as India liberalised its economy. He consolidated the group's many companies under the Tata brand and led a series of landmark global acquisitions: Tetley Tea by Tata Tea (2000), the Anglo-Dutch steelmaker Corus by Tata Steel (2007), and Jaguar Land Rover by Tata Motors (2008). Domestically, he championed the Tata Indica (1998) and the Tata Nano (2008), the latter conceived as an affordable car for Indian families. After stepping down in 2012, he became chairman emeritus and a prominent startup investor and philanthropist through the Tata Trusts, which own a majority of Tata Sons' shares and fund health, education, and rural development.

Honours and death

Tata received the Padma Bhushan in 2000 and the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian honour, in 2008. He died in Mumbai on 9 October 2024, at the age of 86, and was given a state funeral in Maharashtra.
