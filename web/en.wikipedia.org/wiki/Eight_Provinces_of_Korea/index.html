Eight Provinces of Korea

The Eight Provinces of Korea (팔도, Paldo) were the eight provinces of Korea during the Joseon dynasty. The boundaries of the eight provinces remained largely unchanged from their establishment in 1413 until 1895, a span of nearly 500 years, and formed the basis of administrative divisions — and of regional dialects and identities — that persist in modified form on the Korean Peninsula today.

History

The eight-province system was completed in 1413 under King Taejong of Joseon. In 1895, as part of the Gabo Reforms, the provinces were replaced by 23 districts (bu), but the following year the country was reorganised into thirteen provinces, several of which were created by splitting the traditional eight. Following the division of Korea in 1945, both North and South Korea retained province systems descended from the eight provinces. The word Paldo is still used colloquially to mean "the whole country", as in Paldo gangssan ("the land of the eight provinces").
