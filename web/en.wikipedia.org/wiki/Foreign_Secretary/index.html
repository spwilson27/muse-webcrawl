The Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs, also known as the Foreign Secretary, is a secretary of state in the Government of the United Kingdom, with responsibility for the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO). The role is one of the four Great Offices of State, alongside the Prime Minister, Chancellor of the Exchequer and Home Secretary.

The Foreign Secretary is responsible for British foreign policy, relations with foreign states and the European Union, British nationals overseas, and (since the 2020 merger of the Foreign Office with the Department for International Development) international development aid and the overseas territories. The incumbent works from King Charles Street in Whitehall and uses Chevening House in Kent as an official country residence.

History

The office was created in 1782, when the Northern and Southern Departments were reorganised into the Home Office and Foreign Office; Charles James Fox became the first Foreign Secretary. Earlier, separate secretaries of state handled relations with northern (Protestant) and southern (Catholic) Europe. The office was merged with the Commonwealth Office in 1968 to form the Foreign and Commonwealth Office, and in 2020 the FCDO was created by absorbing the Department for International Development.

Notable holders include Lord Castlereagh, George Canning, Lord Palmerston, Lord Salisbury, Edward Grey (in office at the outbreak of the First World War), Anthony Eden, Ernest Bevin (architect of the Western alliance and NATO), William Hague and Boris Johnson, who later became prime minister. Margaret Beckett became the first woman to hold the post in 2006.

Responsibilities

  • Overall responsibility for the FCDO and its worldwide network of embassies and consulates
  • British foreign policy, including relations with individual countries and international organisations
  • Oversight of the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6) and GCHQ jointly with the Prime Minister
  • International development policy and humanitarian aid
  • British Overseas Territories (except their defence arrangements)
