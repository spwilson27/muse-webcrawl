The Canadian dollar (French: dollar canadien; ISO code CAD) is the currency of Canada. It is among the world's most heavily traded currencies, typically ranking in the top ten by foreign-exchange volume, and is held as a reserve currency by a number of central banks. It is often called the loonie, after the common loon pictured on the one-dollar coin introduced in 1987; the two-dollar coin (1996) is nicknamed the "toonie".

History

British North American colonies used a mixture of British, French, Spanish, and American monies before the Province of Canada adopted a decimal dollar in 1858, following the model of the United States. After Confederation (1867), the federal Uniform Currency Act of 1871 consolidated the dollar across the new Dominion. Canada left the gold standard definitively in the 1930s; the Bank of Canada, founded in 1935, became the sole issuer of banknotes. The dollar floated from 1950 to 1962, was pegged to the US dollar from 1962 to 1970, and has floated since — a policy framework credited with helping Canada weather the 1997 Asian crisis without the interest-rate shock suffered by some economies.

Exchange rate

Because Canada is a major commodity exporter, the dollar's value correlates strongly with oil and commodity prices. It fell to a record low of about US$0.62 in 2002, then climbed past parity with the US dollar in 2007 — closing above US$1.10 in November 2007 at its modern peak — during the commodity boom, before settling back below parity in the following decade.

Coins and banknotes

Coins are struck by the Royal Canadian Mint in Winnipeg and Ottawa in denominations of 5, 10, 25, and 50 cents and 1 and 2 dollars; the penny was withdrawn from circulation in 2013. Banknotes are denominations of $5, $10, $20, $50, and $100; since 2011 they have been printed on polymer substrate, beginning with the Frontier Series, and feature figures such as Wilfrid Laurier, John A. Macdonald, Queen Elizabeth II/Canadian themes, William Lyon Mackenzie King, and Robert Borden. The vertical $10 note of 2018, honouring civil-rights pioneer Viola Desmond, won international design recognition.
