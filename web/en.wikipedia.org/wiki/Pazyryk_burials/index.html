Pazyryk burials

The Pazyryk burials are a number of Scythian (Saka) Iron Age tombs found in the Pazyryk Valley and on the Ukok plateau in the Altai Mountains of Siberia, near the borders of Russia, Kazakhstan, China and Mongolia. The tombs, dating from the 5th to the 3rd centuries BCE, are kurgans: large earthen mounds containing wooden chambers covered over with logs.

Excavation and preservation

The first tomb was excavated in the 1920s by M. P. Gryaznov; further famous excavations were carried out by Sergei Rudenko from 1947 to 1949. Because water had seeped into the tombs and frozen, their contents were preserved in ice for roughly 2,500 years: felt hangings, a pile carpet (the oldest surviving in the world), silk garments from China, embroidered cloth, a Persian-style carpet, and the mummified, tattooed bodies of people and horses. Finds are held mainly in the Hermitage Museum in Saint Petersburg and the State History Museum in Moscow.

The Ice Maiden

In 1993 a Russian archaeological team led by Natalya Polosmak excavated the tomb of the so-called Siberian Ice Maiden on the Ukok plateau: a woman of the Pazyryk culture buried with elaborate tattoos of deer and griffins, a silk blouse, and six horses. In 2014 the Ukok burial chamber was transferred to the Gorno-Altaysk National Museum in the Altai Republic. Later discoveries in 2020 included a wooden structure resembling a children's crib at the necropolis Kak-Kul.
