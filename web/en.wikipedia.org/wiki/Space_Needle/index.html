The Space Needle is an observation tower in Seattle, Washington, United States, considered an icon of the city. It was built in the Seattle Center for the 1962 World's Fair, the Century 21 Exposition, whose theme was "The Age of Space". Standing 605 feet (184 m) tall, it was once the tallest structure west of the Mississippi River and is built to withstand winds of up to 200 mph (320 km/h) and earthquakes of up to 9.0 magnitude.

History

The design combined the ideas of businessman Edward E. Carlson, who sketched a giant balloon-tethered tower inspired by the Stuttgart TV tower in Germany, and architect John Graham, who conceived the flying-saucer-shaped restaurant and observation deck. Victor Steinbrueck's profile, evoking a female figure, gave the tower its slender wasp-waisted tripod form. Construction took just over a year, finishing in April 1962, days before the fair opened.

During the fair, nearly 20,000 people a day rode the elevators to the top. Astronauts, celebrities, and Elvis Presley (in the 1963 film It Happened at the World's Fair) made it famous.

Renovations

The tower underwent a major renovation in 2017–2018 known as the "spacelift", which replaced the observation deck's floor with the Loupe — the world's first rotating glass floor — and fitted floor-to-ceiling glass barriers outside. The revolving SkyCity restaurant was replaced by a wine bar and cafe concepts in later changes. The tower is privately owned by the Wright family and draws over a million visitors a year.

Description

The observation deck, at 520 feet (160 m), offers views of downtown Seattle, Mount Rainier, Puget Sound, and the Cascade and Olympic mountain ranges. Three elevators ascend at 10 mph (16 km/h), taking 41 seconds. The structure's foundation extends 30 feet (9.1 m) underground and weighs as much as the tower above it, giving the structure an unusually low centre of gravity.
