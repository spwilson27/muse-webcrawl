Face (professional wrestling)

In professional wrestling, a face (babyface) is a heroic, "good guy" or "fan favorite" wrestler, booked by the promotion with the aim of being cheered and supported by the audience. They are portrayed as heroes in an angle with a series of unlikeable opponents known as heels. Not everything a face wrestler does must be heroic: faces are expected only to be clapped for or encouraged by the fans.

History

The traditional babyface is expected to follow the rules, exhibit sportsmanship, and win matches cleanly. In the 1980s, characters such as Hulk Hogan exemplified the classic American babyface, urging fans to train, say their prayers, and eat their vitamins.

Modern usage

Contemporary wrestling has blurred the line between faces and heels, with antihero characters and "tweeners" who operate between the two extremes. Crowd reactions in the modern era sometimes dictate alignment changes regardless of booking plans, leading promotions to turn wrestlers whose reactions shift over time.

Characteristics

Faces typically use high-flying or crowd-pleasing offense, rally the audience during "hot tags" in tag team matches, and adhere to the rules even when a heel opponent cheats. A face who breaks character too often risks losing the sympathy that sustains the role.
