Pueblo Revolt

Pueblo Revolt of 1680
Also: Popé's Rebellion
Date: 10–21 August 1680 (expulsion); Spanish return 1692–96
Location: Santa Fe de Nuevo México
Leader: Popé (Po'pay) of Ohkay Owingeh

The Pueblo Revolt of 1680, also known as Popé's Rebellion, was an uprising of most of the indigenous Pueblo people against the Spanish colonisers in the province of Santa Fe de Nuevo México, in present-day New Mexico, United States. It drove the Spanish out of the region for twelve years.

Causes

Resentment built over decades of forced labour (encomienda and repartimiento), Franciscan suppression of Pueblo religion — including the 1675 flogging and hanging of religious leaders, among them Popé — and drought, famine and intensified Apache and Navajo raids Spanish defences could not stop.

The uprising

Popé coordinated a simultaneous rising using runners carrying knotted cords counting down the days. Beginning 10 August 1680, Pueblo fighters killed about 400 Spanish settlers and 21 of 33 Franciscan missionaries, then besieged Santa Fe. Governor Antonio de Otermín led roughly 1,000 survivors in retreat to El Paso del Norte. Churches were destroyed and traditional religion restored.

Aftermath

Internal divisions weakened the Pueblo confederation after Popé's death (c. 1688). Diego de Vargas reconquered New Mexico in 1692–1696, though a second revolt in 1696 was suppressed. Spanish rule afterwards was more accommodationist, and Pueblo land grants and religious tolerance were better respected; the revolt remains the only successful Indigenous expulsion of a European colonial power in North America for such a duration.
