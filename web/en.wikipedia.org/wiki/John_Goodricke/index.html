John Goodricke

John Goodricke (1764–1786) was an English amateur astronomer who, though deaf and dying at only 21, made foundational discoveries about variable stars, including the first explanation of the periodic dimming of Algol.

Discoveries

Observing from York with his friend Edward Pigott, Goodricke measured Algol's period of about 2 days 20 hours in 1782–83 and proposed that its variations were caused by a large body periodically passing in front of the star — the eclipsing-binary explanation. He also determined the period of Delta Cephei (1784) and discovered the variability of Beta Lyrae and Eta Aquilae.

Recognition

Goodricke presented his Algol results to the Royal Society in 1783, received its Copley Medal, and was elected a Fellow shortly before his death from pneumonia in 1786. The Goodricke–Pigott observatory legacy and the lunar crater Goodricke commemorate him.
