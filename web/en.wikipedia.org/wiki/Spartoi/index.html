Spartoi

Cadmus sowing the dragon's teeth

In Greek mythology, the Spartoi (Greek: Σπαρτοί, literally "sown men", from σπείρω, "to sow") were a company of fully armed warriors who sprang from the earth when the dragon's teeth of Ares' dragon were sown like seed. They are associated above all with the foundation myth of Thebes, and they have no connection with the city of Sparta or its citizens, the Spartiates, despite the resemblance of the names.

Cadmus and the Theban Spartoi

Cadmus, the Phoenician founder of Thebes, killed the dragon of Ares that guarded the spring of Dirce after it devoured his companions. On Athena's instructions he sowed half of the dragon's teeth in the ground. Armed men immediately sprang up — the Spartoi. Reasoning that they would fight for him or against him, Cadmus threw a stone among them; each warrior, believing himself attacked by another, fell upon his neighbours, and they fought until only five remained: Echion, Udaeus, Chthonius, Hyperenor, and Pelorus. These five survivors helped Cadmus build Thebes, and the great Theban noble families claimed descent from them.

Jason and the Colchian Spartoi

The other half of the dragon's teeth passed to King Aeëtes of Colchis. As one of the trials imposed on Jason in his quest for the Golden Fleece, Aeëtes required him to yoke fire-breathing bulls, plough a field, and sow the teeth, defeating the warriors who sprang from them. Warned by Medea, who loved him, Jason threw a stone into the midst of the earthborn warriors, who — as at Thebes — turned upon and destroyed one another, enabling him to complete the task. The story appears in Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica and later mythographers such as Apollodorus.

Interpretation

The Spartoi exemplify the Greek conception of certain peoples as autochthonous — born from their own soil — a claim Theban aristocrats used to root their ancestry in the land itself. "Sown men" who spring up armed also embody the perennial theme that violence sown in the earth (and, in Thebes' case, within kin) perpetually reproduces itself: the descendants of the Spartoi, including Pentheus and the house of Laius and Oedipus, inherit a city founded in bloodshed.
