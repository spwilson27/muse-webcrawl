Zentrum Paul Klee

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zentrum Paul Klee

Location: Bern, Switzerland

Opened: 20 June 2005

Architect: Renzo Piano

Collection: about 4,000 works by Paul Klee

Type: Art museum and cultural centre

The Zentrum Paul Klee (Paul Klee Centre) is a museum and cultural centre in Bern, Switzerland, dedicated to the artist Paul Klee (1879–1940), who spent much of his life in the city. It opened in June 2005 and holds the world's largest collection of a single artist's work from the classical modern period — roughly 4,000 paintings, watercolours, and drawings, about 40 percent of Klee's entire oeuvre.

The building

The building, designed by the Italian architect Renzo Piano, lies on the eastern edge of Bern at Schöngrün. Its form consists of three undulating "hills" of steel and glass that rise from the landscape like a wave, echoing the surrounding farmland and the Bernese Alps. Each hill has a distinct function: exhibitions, a concert hall and events spaces, and a research and children's museum (Kindermuseum Creaviva). The initiative came from the plastic surgeon and collector Maurice E. Müller and Klee's daughter-in-law Livia Klee-Meyer, who donated the collection to the city and canton of Bern.

Activities

In addition to rotating exhibitions drawn from the collection and loans, the Zentrum hosts concerts, readings, conferences, and art-education programmes, presenting itself as a multidisciplinary cultural centre rather than a conventional museum.
