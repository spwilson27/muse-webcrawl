Josiah Wedgwood

Josiah Wedgwood FRS (12 July 1730 - 3 January 1795) was an English potter, entrepreneur and abolitionist. Founder of the Wedgwood pottery company, he is credited with industrializing the manufacture of pottery and has been described as one of the pioneers of modern marketing.

Early life

Wedgwood was born in Burslem, Staffordshire, into a family of potters. Smallpox left him with a weakened knee, later amputated, which kept him from the potter's wheel and turned him toward design and experiment. After apprenticeships and partnerships, he founded his own business in 1759.

The Wedgwood company

Wedgwood perfected creamware - a refined, cream-colored earthenware he marketed as "Queen's Ware" after winning the patronage of Queen Charlotte - and black basalt ware. His most famous invention was jasperware (from about 1775), an unglazed stoneware, typically blue with white classical relief decoration, still the firm's signature product. At his Etruria works, opened in 1769 with his partner Thomas Bentley, he applied factory division of labor, rigorous quality control, and innovative marketing: showrooms, catalogues, celebrity endorsement and money-back guarantees. His copy of the Portland Vase (1790) was a triumph of the potter's art.

Science and reform

Wedgwood was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society for inventing a pyrometer to measure kiln temperatures, and he was a member of the Lunar Society alongside Erasmus Darwin (his friend and later co-grandfather of Charles Darwin). He campaigned for canals, including the Trent and Mersey Canal, and was a prominent abolitionist: his 1787 medallion of an enslaved man in chains, inscribed "Am I Not a Man and a Brother," became an icon of the anti-slavery movement. He died at Etruria in 1795, leaving one of Britain's great industrial fortunes.
