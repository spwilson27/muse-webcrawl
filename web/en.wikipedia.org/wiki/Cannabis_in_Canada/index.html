Cannabis in Canada is legal for both recreational and medical purposes. Canada became the second country in the world, after Uruguay, to formally legalize the cultivation, possession, acquisition and consumption of cannabis and its by-products, and the first G7 and G20 nation to do so. Legalization for recreational use took effect on 17 October 2018 under the Cannabis Act.

History

Cannabis was prohibited in 1923 under the Opium and Narcotic Drug Act, decades before it was widely used in Canada. The 1969–1972 Le Dain Commission recommended decriminalisation, but reform stalled for decades. Medical access was recognised following the 2000 R v. Parker decision, leading to federal medical marihuana programmes from 2001. The Liberal Party under Justin Trudeau campaigned on legalization in 2015 and introduced the Cannabis Act in 2017.

Regulation

The federal government licenses producers and sets criminal-law boundaries, while provinces and territories regulate distribution and retail, and set minimum ages (18, 19 or 21 depending on the province). Adults may publicly possess up to 30 grams of dried cannabis, buy from licensed retailers, and grow up to four plants per household in most provinces. Edibles, extracts and topicals became legal in October 2019 ("Legalization 2.0").

Effects and debate

Post-legalization studies track use rates (which rose modestly), the displacement of the illegal market, road safety, youth use and public-health outcomes. Canada's experience is frequently cited internationally in debates about drug policy reform.
