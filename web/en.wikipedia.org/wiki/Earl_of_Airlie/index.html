Earl of Airlie

Earl of Airlie is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, created on 2 April 1639 for James Ogilvy, 2nd Lord Ogilvy of Airlie, by King Charles I. The earls are members of the Ogilvy family, one of the oldest and most prominent noble families of Angus, whose chiefs had held the hereditary office of Sheriff of Angus. The earldom is held with the subsidiary titles Lord Ogilvy of Airlie (created 1491) and Lord Ogilvy of Alith and Lintrathen (1639).

History

The Ogilvys supported the Stuart cause: the 4th Earl was attainted for his role in the Jacobite rising of 1715, and the title was restored to his descendant only in 1826, an interval during which the de facto earls lived partly in exile. Later earls were prominent courtiers and soldiers; David Ogilvy, 12th Earl of Airlie, served as Lord Chamberlain to Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother. The family seat is Cortachy Castle in Angus, and Airlie Castle is also associated with the family.

Cultural note

The ballad "The Bonnie Hoose o' Airlie" recounts the burning of Airlie Castle in 1640 by the forces of the Earl of Argyll during the Covenanting conflict with the Ogilvys, who supported Charles I.
