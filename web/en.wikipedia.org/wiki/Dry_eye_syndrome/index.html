Dry eye syndrome

Dry eye syndrome, also known as keratoconjunctivitis sicca or dry eye disease, is a multifactorial disease of the tears and ocular surface that results in symptoms of discomfort, visual disturbance, and tear film instability, with potential damage to the ocular surface. It is accompanied by increased osmolarity of the tear film and inflammation of the ocular surface.

Signs and symptoms

Typical symptoms include dryness, grittiness or a foreign-body sensation, burning, stinging, redness, sensitivity to light, blurred or fluctuating vision, and — paradoxically — reflex watery eyes. Symptoms often worsen with prolonged reading, screen use, wind, air conditioning, and at the end of the day.

Causes

Dry eye arises from either decreased tear production (aqueous-deficient dry eye) or increased tear evaporation (evaporative dry eye), most often due to meibomian gland dysfunction. Contributing factors include ageing, female sex, autoimmune diseases (notably Sjögren's syndrome), medications such as antihistamines and some antidepressants, contact lens wear, refractive surgery, low-humidity environments, and vitamin A deficiency.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines symptom questionnaires with tests such as tear break-up time, Schirmer's test of tear production, ocular surface staining with fluorescein or lissamine green, tear osmolarity measurement, and assessment of the meibomian glands.

Treatment

Management is stepwise: education and environmental measures; artificial tear substitutes and lubricating ointments; warm compresses and lid hygiene for meibomian gland dysfunction; anti-inflammatory therapy with topical cyclosporine or lifitegrast; tear conservation by punctal plugs or cautery; autologous serum drops; and, in severe disease, therapeutic contact lenses or surgery. Omega-3 supplementation has been widely used, though large trials such as DREAM (2018) found no benefit over placebo.

Epidemiology

Dry eye is one of the most common reasons for eye-care visits; prevalence estimates range from about 5% to over 30% of adults depending on definitions, rising with age and higher in women.
