AAA (computer security)

AAA stands for Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting. It is a framework for intelligently controlling access to computer resources, enforcing policies, auditing usage, and providing the information necessary to bill for services. The AAA concept is often used in network access servers and in protocols such as RADIUS, Diameter, and TACACS+.

Authentication

Authentication refers to the process by which a system establishes or confirms the identity of a user or device. Methods include passwords, digital certificates, smart cards, and multi-factor authentication combining several factors such as something the user knows, has, or is.

Authorization

Authorization is the function of specifying access rights and privileges to resources related to information security. It determines what an authenticated identity is allowed to do, such as which networks, files, or services may be accessed.

Accounting

Accounting measures the resources a user consumes during access, such as session time, volume of data sent and received, and the services used. Accounting records support billing, trend analysis, and capacity planning.

AAA servers and protocols

AAA servers communicate with network access servers (NAS) and gateways. RADIUS is the most widely deployed protocol, while its successor Diameter adds improved reliability and extensibility, particularly in mobile networks.
