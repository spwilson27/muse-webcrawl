Think tank

A think tank, or policy institute, is an organization that performs research and advocacy concerning topics such as social policy, political strategy, economics, military, technology, and culture. Most think tanks are non-governmental and non-profit, though some are semi-autonomous government agencies or corporate-affiliated.

History

The term originated in the Second World War for secure rooms where military plans were discussed, and was later applied to research contractors such as the RAND Corporation (founded 1948). Earlier institutions include the Brookings Institution (1916) and Britain's Fabian Society (1884).

Types and influence

Think tanks range from academic and contract research organisations to advocacy bodies with clear ideological commitments—conservative (e.g. the Heritage Foundation), liberal (e.g. Center for American Progress), libertarian (e.g. the Cato Institute)—and publish reports, brief legislators, and supply experts to media and government.

Criticism

Critics raise questions about funding transparency, donor influence on research agendas, and the revolving door between think tanks and government. Annual rankings such as the Global Go To Think Tank Index attempt comparative assessment.
