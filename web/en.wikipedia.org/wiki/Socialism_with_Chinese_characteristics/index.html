Socialism with Chinese characteristics

"Socialism with Chinese characteristics" is the official ideology of the Chinese Communist Party.

Socialism with Chinese characteristics (Chinese: 中国特色社会主义) is the official ideology and theoretical framework of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and the People's Republic of China. The term was introduced by Deng Xiaoping in 1982, at the opening of the 12th National Congress of the CCP, to describe China's path of adapting Marxism to Chinese conditions — in practice, legitimizing the market-oriented reforms launched under the policy of "reform and opening up" begun in 1978 while maintaining one-party rule and public ownership as, in official terminology, the "mainstay" of the economy.

Content

Official doctrine holds that China is in the "primary stage of socialism," a condition of relative underdevelopment that will last for a century or more, during which the central task is developing the productive forces. This justified household farming, special economic zones, foreign investment, private enterprise, and China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001, alongside a continuing large state sector. The framework has been elaborated through successive theoretical contributions attributed to CCP leaders: Deng Xiaoping Theory, Jiang Zemin's "Three Represents," Hu Jintao's "Scientific Outlook on Development," and Xi Jinping Thought, which since 2017 has been written into the party constitution as the guiding ideology for a "new era."

Interpretations and debate

Defenders within China argue the concept is a creative application of Marxism that has delivered rapid growth and mass poverty reduction. Critics abroad and dissident Marxists within China have questioned whether China's system — featuring billionaires, stock markets, and extensive private property — can meaningfully be called socialism, variously describing it as state capitalism or market Leninism. Chinese official discourse rejects those labels, insisting the system remains socialist because of the CCP's leadership, public ownership of land, and state direction of development.
