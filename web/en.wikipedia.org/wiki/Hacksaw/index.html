Hacksaw

A hacksaw is a fine-toothed saw, originally and principally used for cutting metal, but also able to cut other materials such as plastic and wood. Most hacksaws are hand saws with a bow frame with a handle, which hold a thin, disposable blade under tension.

Design

The standard hand hacksaw frame holds blades 10 or 12 inches (250–300 mm) long. Blades are specified by teeth per inch (TPI), commonly 14, 18, 24 or 32; finer blades are used for thinner material. So-called "junior hacksaws" take 6-inch blades. Power hacksaws, including reciprocating and band types, are widely used in workshops.

Use

The blade is tensioned in the frame with the teeth pointing forward, so the saw cuts on the push stroke. Hacksaws are used by plumbers, electricians and metalworkers to cut pipe, conduit, bolts and sheet metal.
