Baia Mare

Baia Mare is a city in northwestern Romania, the capital of Maramureș County, located on the Săsar River in the Baia Mare valley near the Gutâi Mountains. Its name means "large mine," reflecting its centuries of history as a centre for mining gold and other precious metals. The city has a population of roughly 110,000–120,000.

History

The settlement developed as a mining town in the Kingdom of Hungary, first documented in the 14th century (as Rivulus Dominarum), and received privileges from Hungarian kings encouraging German-speaking miners to settle. Its old town preserves medieval features such as Stephen's Tower (Turnul Ștefan), built in the 15th century as the bell tower of a Gothic church. With Transylvania, the city passed between Hungary, the Ottoman sphere, and Austria over the centuries, and became part of Romania after the First World War, apart from a period of Hungarian administration between 1940 and 1944.

Cyanide spill of 2000

Baia Mare gained international attention on 30 January 2000, when a tailings dam at the Aurul gold processing plant burst, releasing about 100,000 cubic metres of cyanide-contaminated water into the Săsar, Lăpuș, Someș, Tisza, and ultimately the Danube. The spill killed vast quantities of fish in Hungary and Yugoslavia and was described as one of Europe's worst environmental disasters since Chernobyl.

Culture

In the late 19th century Baia Mare hosted an influential artists' colony and school of painting founded by Simon Hollósy, which helped shape modern Hungarian and Romanian painting. The city's museums, theatres, and the surrounding wooden-church country of Maramureș remain cultural draws.
