Urbi et Orbi

Urbi et Orbi
LatinUrbi et Orbi — "to the City [of Rome] and to the World"
TypePapal blessing
Given byThe Pope
OccasionsEaster, Christmas, election of a new pope; extraordinary occasions

Urbi et Orbi (Latin for "to the City [of Rome] and to the World") is a papal blessing given by the Pope on certain solemn occasions. Addressed not only to the people of Rome but to the whole world, it is the most solemn form of blessing the pope can bestow, and is accompanied by a plenary indulgence for those who receive it under the usual conditions — including, since the 20th century, those following by radio, television, or the internet.

Occasions

The blessing is given each year at Easter and at Christmas, delivered from the central loggia of St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City before a crowd in St. Peter's Square. It is also imparted by a newly elected pope immediately after his election, before he presents himself to the public. On rare extraordinary occasions it may be given at other times: most notably, on 27 March 2020 Pope Francis gave a special Urbi et Orbi blessing in an empty St. Peter's Square during the COVID-19 pandemic, an event broadcast worldwide.

Rite

Before the blessing, the pope customarily addresses greetings — at Easter and Christmas often delivered in dozens of languages — and a short message reflecting on the feast and on world events. Cardinals announce the indulgence, and the blessing is then imparted in Latin with the Trinitarian formula, making the sign of the cross. Historically the blessing was also given from the basilicas of St. John Lateran and Santa Maria Maggiore on other feasts, and by visiting popes in adapted form, but its fixed home since the modern era is St. Peter's.

History

The formula developed in the 13th century, drawing on the ancient conception of Rome as caput mundi (head of the world). It became attached to the great feasts and to papal coronations and elections as the ceremonial expression of the pope's dual role as Bishop of Rome and pastor of the universal Church.
