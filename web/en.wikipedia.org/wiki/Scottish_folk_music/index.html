Scottish folk music

Scottish folk music (also Scottish traditional music) is the traditional music of Scotland, encompassing Gaelic song, Scots ballads and dance tunes, pipe music, fiddle traditions and work songs. It has both ancient roots and a continuous living tradition, and has influenced music-making throughout the Scottish diaspora.

Forms

Important forms include the great pipe music ceòl mòr (piobaireachd) and lighter pipe marches, strathspeys and reels; fiddle music from regions such as Shetland, the North-East and Cape Breton in Canada; Gaelic òrain mhòra (great songs), waulking songs and psalm singing; and the narrative ballads of the Scottish Borders and North-East, collected by Francis James Child. Dance music centres on reels, jigs, strathspeys and hornpipes, played for ceilidh dancing. The bothy ballads of farm servants and the songs of Robert Burns occupy a central place in the repertoire.

Revival

A folk revival from the 1950s, led by figures such as Hamish Henderson, Ewan MacColl and the singers of the School of Scottish Studies, documented tradition-bearers like Jeannie Robertson and spurred a lasting traditional music scene. Modern performers — including Aly Bain, Battlefield Band, Capercaillie, Julie Fowlis and Karine Polwart — have combined traditional material with contemporary arrangements, while the Celtic Connections festival and the BBC Radio Scotland Young Traditional Musician award sustain new generations of players.
