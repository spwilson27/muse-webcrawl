Typeface anatomy

Typeface anatomy describes the graphic elements that make up letters in a typeface. A specialised vocabulary names the parts: a stem is the main vertical stroke; a crossbar joins two stems (as in H) or crosses one (as in t); a bowl is a curved stroke enclosing a counter; an aperture is the partially enclosed opening of letters such as c or e; ascenders rise above the x-height in letters like b and h, while descenders fall below the baseline in g, p and y.

Lines and measures

Letterforms are positioned relative to the baseline, the x-height (height of the lowercase x), the cap height, and the ascender and descender lines. Serifs, terminals, spurs, ears, tails and ligatures further distinguish type designs, and the consistent treatment of these features gives a family of fonts its character.
