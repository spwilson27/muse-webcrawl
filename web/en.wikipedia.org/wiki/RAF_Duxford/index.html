RAF Duxford

Image placeholder: the historic hangars of Duxford, now part of Imperial War Museum Duxford.

RAF Duxford was a Royal Air Force station near Duxford in Cambridgeshire, England. Built during the First World War and completed in 1918, it became a fighter station during the interwar years and played a prominent part in the Battle of Britain in 1940 as part of No. 12 Group, Fighter Command. It was later used by the United States Army Air Forces, and after the war by RAF jet fighters until its closure as an operational station in 1961.

History

Duxford was one of the earliest RAF stations and one of the best preserved examples of a First World War airfield. During the Battle of Britain it was home to No. 19 Squadron, the first to operate the Spitfire, and to the "Big Wing" associated with Douglas Bader's No. 242 Squadron and Trafford Leigh-Mallory. From 1943 to 1945 it hosted the USAAF's 78th Fighter Group, flying P-47 Thunderbolts and later P-51 Mustangs on escort missions over Europe.

Museum use

Since 1976 the site has been occupied by Imperial War Museum Duxford, a branch of the Imperial War Museum with extensive aviation collections, historic hangars and regular flying displays. The airfield remains active for historic aircraft operations and is one of the best-known aviation museum sites in Europe.
