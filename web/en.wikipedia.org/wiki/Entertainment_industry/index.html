Entertainment industry

The entertainment industry (also called show business or showbiz) is part of the tertiary sector of the economy and comprises the businesses that provide entertainment to audiences: film, television, recorded music, theatre, publishing, video games, live performance and spectator sport. It is a major global employer and exporter, dominated historically by the United States, whose Hollywood film industry and popular music have shaped taste worldwide.

Sectors

The film industry centres on production hubs including Hollywood, Mumbai (Bollywood) and Lagos (Nollywood). The music industry is dominated by three major record groups (Universal, Sony and Warner) and increasingly by streaming services. Television and, since the 2010s, subscription streaming platforms such as Netflix and Disney+ compete for audiences. The video game industry, now larger in revenue than film and recorded music combined, and live entertainment — concerts, festivals, sports and theatre — complete the sector.

History

Commercial entertainment grew with urbanisation and mass media in the nineteenth century: music halls, vaudeville and popular theatre gave way to cinema in the early twentieth century, then radio, television and home video. The digital revolution disrupted established business models — file sharing hit recorded music in the 2000s and streaming transformed film and television distribution in the 2010s — while creating new categories such as esports and influencer content.
