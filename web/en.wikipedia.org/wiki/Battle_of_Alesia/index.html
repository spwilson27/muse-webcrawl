Battle of Alesia

Battle of Alesia
Date: September–October 52 BC
Location: Alesia (near modern Alise-Sainte-Reine, France)
Combatants: Roman Republic vs Gallic confederation
Commanders: Julius Caesar vs Vercingetorix
Result: Decisive Roman victory; end of major Gallic resistance

The Battle of Alesia (52 BC) was the decisive engagement of the Gallic Wars: Julius Caesar's Roman army besieged the Gallic leader Vercingetorix in the hilltop oppidum of Alesia and defeated both the garrison and a huge Gallic relief army, effectively ending organised resistance to Roman rule in Gaul.

The siege

After a failed assault at Gergovia, Caesar pursued Vercingetorix to Alesia. Caesar built an 18 km circumvallation facing inward — walls, towers, ditches, and traps (caltrops, stakes, "lilies") — and then a second, longer contravallation facing outward to hold off the relief force. The garrison starved; Vercingetorix expelled non-combatants, whom Caesar refused to let through his lines. In the final battles the Romans, with Caesar conspicuous in his red cloak and his lieutenant Labienus and cavalry under Mark Antony, repelled attacks on both fronts.

Aftermath

Vercingetorix surrendered — later sources describe him riding out and laying his arms at Caesar's feet. He was held for six years and executed after Caesar's triumph in 46 BC. Our main source is Caesar's own Commentarii de Bello Gallico, so Gallic numbers (a relief army of a quarter-million) are likely inflated. Archaeology at Alise-Sainte-Reine since Napoleon III's excavations has confirmed the siege works.
