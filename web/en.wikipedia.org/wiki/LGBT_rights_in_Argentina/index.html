LGBT rights in Argentina

Lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer (LGBTQ) people in Argentina enjoy some of the most advanced rights in the world. Same-sex marriage was legalized nationwide in 2010, making Argentina the first country in Latin America and the tenth in the world to do so. Buenos Aires is frequently cited as one of the world's most gay-friendly cities.

Same-sex marriage

On 15 July 2010, the Argentine Senate approved the Equal Marriage Law, extending full marriage rights to same-sex couples, including adoption rights. Before national legislation, civil unions were available in some jurisdictions, including Buenos Aires from 2003.

Gender identity

The Gender Identity Law of 2012 allows adults to change their legal sex and name on official documents based solely on self-declaration, without requiring surgery, hormone therapy, or judicial approval. Argentina's model has been widely praised as among the most progressive in the world.

Anti-discrimination protections

Anti-discrimination protections exist in various provinces and cities, and in 2020 the government established a quota reserving one percent of public sector jobs for transgender people.
