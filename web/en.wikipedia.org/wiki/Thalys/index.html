Overview

Thalys was an international high-speed train service between Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam and Cologne (with seasonal services to the Alps and Provence), launched in 1996 and named for a word coined to be pronounceable across its countries' languages. Its distinctive deep-red PBKA and PBA trainsets, built by Alstom on TGV technology, ran at up to 300 km/h on high-speed lines.

History

Thalys was operated by a joint venture of the French (SNCF), Belgian (SNCB) and Dutch (NS) railways with Deutsche Bahn participation. In 2015 it became an independent company (THI Factory). Its trains were the target of the foiled August 2015 attack by Ayoub El Khazzani, subdued by passengers including three Americans. After the Eurostar–Thalys holding merger (Green Speed, completed 2022), the Thalys brand was retired in September–October 2023 in favour of Eurostar, though the red trains continue in service.
