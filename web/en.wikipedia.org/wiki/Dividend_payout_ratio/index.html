Dividend payout ratio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The dividend payout ratio is the fraction of net income a firm pays to its shareholders as dividends:

Payout ratio = Total dividends ÷ Net income

It can also be expressed per share, as dividends per share divided by earnings per share. The part of earnings not paid out is retained by the firm for investment or to pay off debt, and is measured by the retention ratio (or plowback ratio), which is simply one minus the payout ratio.

Interpretation

The ratio shows how much of a company's profits are returned to shareholders versus kept for growth. Mature companies in stable industries commonly pay out a substantial share of earnings, while fast-growing firms typically have low or zero payout ratios, preferring to reinvest. A very high payout ratio (above 100%) means the company is paying out more than it earns, which is generally unsustainable and may signal that the dividend is at risk of being cut; a ratio persistently near or below zero may indicate losses.

Use in valuation

Together with return on equity, the retention ratio determines a firm's sustainable growth rate (g = ROE × retention ratio), a key input to the Gordon growth model for valuing dividend-paying stocks. Some analysts prefer to calculate the payout ratio using free cash flow rather than accounting earnings, to judge how comfortably dividends are covered by cash.
