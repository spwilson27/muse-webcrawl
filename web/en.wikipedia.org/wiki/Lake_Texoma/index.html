Lake Texoma is a reservoir on the Red River at the border of Texas and Oklahoma, formed by Denison Dam. It is one of the largest reservoirs in the United States, with a surface area of about 89,000 acres and roughly 580 miles of shoreline, and serves flood control, water supply, hydroelectric power, and recreation for the Texoma region.

History

Denison Dam was built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers during the Second World War and completed in 1944; the lake's name combines "Texas" and "Oklahoma". The creation of the reservoir flooded farmland, roads, and several communities, and displaced residents on both sides of the river. President Dwight D. Eisenhower was born in nearby Denison, Texas, and the lake area includes Eisenhower State Park.

Recreation and ecology

Lake Texoma is one of the most popular recreation reservoirs in the country, drawing millions of visitors to dozens of parks, marinas, and two national wildlife refuges (Hagerman and Tishomingo) on its shores. It is famed for its striped bass fishery — the lake's stripers reproduce naturally, unusual among inland reservoirs — as well as catfish and crappie. A saltwater intrusion issue from natural salt flats on the upper Red River has long affected downstream water quality and spurred studies of chloride control.
