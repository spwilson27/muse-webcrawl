The Destroyer (fiction)

The Destroyer is a series of paperback novels about the adventures of Remo Williams, an American assassin trained in the fictional Korean martial art of Sinanju. Created by Warren Murphy and Richard Sapir, the series began with Created, the Destroyer in 1971 and ran for more than 150 volumes, becoming one of the most successful series in the men's adventure genre.

Premise

Remo Williams, a Newark policeman framed for murder and sentenced to death, is spared and recruited by a secret government organisation called CURE, which operates outside constitutional limits to fight crime and corruption. He is trained by Chiun, the elderly Korean Master of Sinanju, the "sun source" of all martial arts, from which — in the series' mythology — disciplines such as karate and kung fu are mere degraded fragments.

The novels blend violent action with broad satire of American politics, bureaucracy, popular culture and the spy-fiction genre itself. As the series progressed, increasingly fantastic opponents appeared, including androids, superhuman killers, and reincarnations of ancient beings.

Authors and adaptations

Murphy and Sapir wrote the early books together; later volumes were written by Murphy and a succession of collaborators and ghost writers, including Molly Cochran and Robert J. Randisi. The series was adapted into the 1985 film Remo Williams: The Adventure Begins, starring Fred Ward as Williams and Joel Grey as Chiun, and a television pilot, Remo Williams: The Prophecy (1988). A short-lived Marvel Comics series also appeared.
