Godrevy Lighthouse

LocationGodrevy Island, St Ives Bay, Cornwall, England
Built1858–1859
Designed byJames Walker
Height26 m (85 ft)
OperatorTrinity House

Godrevy Lighthouse is a lighthouse on Godrevy Island in St Ives Bay, Cornwall, England, off Godrevy Head. The white octagonal tower, 26 metres (85 ft) tall, was built in 1858–59 to mark the dangerous Stones reef, which had been the scene of many shipwrecks, including that of the SS Nile in 1854 with the loss of all on board.

Designed by the engineer James Walker for Trinity House, the lighthouse originally had keepers housed in cottages on the island; it was automated in 1939 and its fog bell was removed. In 2012 its light was replaced by a solar-powered LED light mounted on a steel platform nearby, and the tower itself ceased to be the active light, though it remains a daymark.

In literature

Godrevy Lighthouse is widely held to have inspired Virginia Woolf's novel To the Lighthouse (1927); Woolf holidayed as a child at Talland House in nearby St Ives, from which the island lighthouse is visible across the bay.
