Middle frontal gyrus

Middle frontal gyrus
Latin: gyrus frontalis medius
Part of: Frontal lobe
Artery: Anterior cerebral artery

The middle frontal gyrus is a convolution (gyrus) of the frontal lobe of the human brain. It lies between the superior frontal sulcus above and the inferior frontal sulcus below, and occupies roughly the middle third of the dorsolateral surface of the frontal lobe. It is one of the largest gyri of the frontal lobe and plays an important role in higher cognitive functions.

Anatomy

The middle frontal gyrus is separated from the superior frontal gyrus by the superior frontal sulcus and from the inferior frontal gyrus by the inferior frontal sulcus. Its posterior boundary is the precentral sulcus, beyond which lies the precentral gyrus and primary motor cortex. The gyrus roughly corresponds to parts of Brodmann areas 6, 8, 9 and 46.

Function

The middle frontal gyrus forms part of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, a region implicated in working memory, attention, planning and executive function. Functional imaging studies associate the right middle frontal gyrus with the reorientation of attention and inhibitory control, while the left middle frontal gyrus contributes to verbal working memory and semantic processing. Lesions can produce deficits in attention, planning and the organisation of goal-directed behaviour.
