Jin–Song Wars

The Jin–Song Wars were a series of conflicts in the 12th and 13th centuries between the Jurchen-led Jin dynasty and the Han Chinese Song dynasty in China. The wars began with the Jin conquest of northern China and ended only when both states were overwhelmed by the Mongol Empire.

Jingkang incident

In 1127 Jin forces captured the Song capital Kaifeng in the Jingkang incident, seizing the emperors Qinzong and Huizong. The Song court fled south and re-established itself at Hangzhou as the Southern Song.

Later campaigns

Decades of warfare and negotiation followed, including campaigns by the Song general Yue Fei and the Treaty of Shaoxing (1141), which fixed the border along the Huai River. The Jin dynasty fell to the Mongols in 1234, and the Southern Song followed in 1279.
