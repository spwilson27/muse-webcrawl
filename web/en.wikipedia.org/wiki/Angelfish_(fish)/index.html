Angelfish (fish)

Angelfish
Scientific classificationFamily Cichlidae; genus Pterophyllum
SpeciesP. scalare, P. altum, P. leopoldi
OriginAmazon Basin, South America

Angelfish are freshwater fish of the genus Pterophyllum in the cichlid family, native to the Amazon, Orinoco and Essequibo river basins of South America. They are named for their distinctive laterally compressed, disc-like bodies and long, trailing fins. The common angelfish, Pterophyllum scalare, has been bred in captivity for a century and is one of the most popular tropical aquarium fish.

Description

Wild angelfish are silver with dark vertical bars that they can lighten or darken with mood and surroundings. Their deep, thin bodies and elongated dorsal, anal and pelvic fins suit slow movement among submerged roots and vegetation. Captive breeding has produced numerous colour varieties — marble, koi, gold, black, albino and veil-tailed forms — many of which no longer show the wild striping.

Behaviour and care

Angelfish are generally peaceful but are opportunistic predators that will eat very small fish and fry. They form monogamous pairs, lay eggs on flat surfaces such as broad leaves or slate, and both parents guard and fan the eggs and young — unusual parental care that contributed to their popularity. In the aquarium they need tall tanks, warm water (roughly 24–28 °C), and are kept in species and community setups worldwide.

Confusion with marine angelfish

The name "angelfish" also applies to the unrelated marine angelfishes of the family Pomacanthidae, brightly coloured reef fish, and to the freshwater angelfish's own look-alikes in trade. The genus name Pterophyllum means "winged leaf", a reference to the sail-like fins.
