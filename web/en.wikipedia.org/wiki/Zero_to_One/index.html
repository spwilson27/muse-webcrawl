Zero to One

Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future is a 2014 book by entrepreneur and investor Peter Thiel, co-authored with Blake Masters. It is based on notes Masters took in Thiel's 2012 Stanford Law School class on startups, which became widely circulated online before the book's publication by Crown Business.

Ideas

The book's central distinction is between "horizontal" progress — copying things that work, going from 1 to n, associated with globalization — and "vertical" progress — doing new things, going from 0 to 1, associated with technology. Thiel argues that great companies are built on secrets, that competition is destructive of profits while monopolies earned through unique products are beneficial, and that founders should ask the "contrarian question": "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" Other chapters cover the power law in venture capital, the value of sales and distribution, and the role of definite optimism about the future.

Reception

Zero to One became a bestseller and a widely read text in the technology industry, praised for its distinctive arguments and criticised by some for overstating the virtues of monopoly and its author's contrarian persona. It has remained a standard reference in startup culture.
