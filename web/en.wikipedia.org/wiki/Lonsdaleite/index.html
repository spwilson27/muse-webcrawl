Lonsdaleite, also called hexagonal diamond in reference to its crystal structure, is an allotrope of carbon with a hexagonal lattice distinct from the cubic lattice of conventional diamond. It is named in honour of the British crystallographer Dame Kathleen Lonsdale.

Occurrence and properties

Lonsdaleite was first identified in 1967 in the Canyon Diablo meteorite at Barringer Crater, Arizona, where it formed when graphite-bearing meteorites struck the Earth and the heat and pressure of impact converted graphite into a diamond-like structure preserving the hexagonal layering. It has since been reported at other impact sites, including the Tunguska area and Ries Crater.

Natural lonsdaleite is usually impure and faulted, with measured hardness lower than diamond. However, theoretical calculations and experiments on pure material suggest defect-free lonsdaleite could be harder than diamond along certain directions. Its distinct existence as a pure phase was debated for decades; work in 2020 confirmed genuinely pure lonsdaleite crystals in ureilite meteorites.
