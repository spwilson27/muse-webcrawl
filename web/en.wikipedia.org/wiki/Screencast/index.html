Screencast

A screencast is a digital video recording of a computer screen output, often containing audio narration. The term screencast compares with the related term screenshot; whereas screenshot generates a single picture of a computer screen, a screencast is essentially a movie of the changes over time that a user sees on a computer screen, enhanced with audio narration.

History

The term was popularised in 2004 by columnist Jon Udell, who ran a reader contest to name the genre of narrated screen-capture movies then emerging from software such as Lotus ScreenCam (1990s) and Macromedia/TechSmith products like Camtasia. Udell championed screencasting as a way to show, rather than tell, how software works. The growth of broadband video hosting in the mid-2000s made screencasts a mainstream medium for education and marketing.

Uses

Screencasts are used for software tutorials and online courses (massive open online courses rely heavily on them), product demonstrations, bug reports and user-support replies, distance-teaching lessons (notably in the flipped classroom model), programming instruction and code reviews, and documentation of digital workflows for research or compliance. Noted educational screencasters include Salman Khan, whose narrated digital-blackboard lessons grew into Khan Academy.

Production

Screencasts are made with screen recording software, then usually edited to trim errors, add titles, captions, callouts and zooms, and synchronise narration — either recorded live during capture or dubbed afterwards. Best practice includes scripting, recording at a resolution appropriate for the hosting platform, using a good microphone, and adding captions for accessibility.
