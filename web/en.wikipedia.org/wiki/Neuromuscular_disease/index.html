Neuromuscular disease

Neuromuscular disease
SpecialtyNeurology, neuromuscular medicine
Components affectedMotor neurons, peripheral nerves, neuromuscular junctions, muscle fibres
ExamplesALS, myasthenia gravis, muscular dystrophies, peripheral neuropathies

Neuromuscular diseases are a broad group of conditions that affect the functioning of the muscles, either directly, as primary muscle disease, or indirectly, through diseases of the nerves or neuromuscular junctions that control them. The motor unit — a motor neuron in the spinal cord or brainstem, its axon, and all the muscle fibres it supplies — is the anatomical basis for classification. Problems with the control centres in the brain or spinal cord itself are usually considered separately; neuromuscular disease properly concerns the components between the anterior horn cell and the muscle fibre.

Classification

  • Motor neuron diseases — including amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and spinal muscular atrophy, in which motor neurons degenerate.
  • Peripheral neuropathies — disorders of peripheral nerves, such as Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease and Guillain–Barré syndrome.
  • Neuromuscular junction disorders — myasthenia gravis, Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome, botulism and congenital myasthenic syndromes.
  • Myopathies — primary muscle diseases, including the muscular dystrophies (such as Duchenne and myotonic dystrophy), metabolic and mitochondrial myopathies, inflammatory myopathies and channelopathies.

Signs and symptoms

Typical presentations include muscle weakness, wasting, cramps, fasciculations, fatigue, and in junction disorders fatigable weakness that fluctuates through the day. Involvement of bulbar and respiratory muscles can cause difficulty swallowing, speaking and breathing, and respiratory failure is a common cause of death in advanced disease.

Diagnosis

Investigation combines clinical examination with blood tests (notably creatine kinase), electromyography and nerve conduction studies, muscle or nerve biopsy, imaging, and genetic testing, which has become central as hundreds of causative genes have been identified.

Treatment

Many neuromuscular diseases have no cure, and care focuses on physiotherapy, orthoses, respiratory support and treatment of complications. Some are treatable: immunosuppression in myasthenia gravis and inflammatory myopathies, enzyme replacement in Pompe disease, and newer gene-based therapies such as nusinersen and onasemnogene abeparvovec for spinal muscular atrophy.
