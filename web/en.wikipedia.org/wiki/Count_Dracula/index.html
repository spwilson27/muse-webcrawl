Count Dracula is the title character of Bram Stoker's 1897 Gothic horror novel Dracula. He is an undead, centuries-old vampire and Transylvanian nobleman who lives in a decaying castle in the Carpathian Mountains. Dracula is generally considered the prototypical and archetypal vampire in subsequent works of fiction, and he remains one of the most famous characters in English literature, having appeared in hundreds of films, plays and works of popular culture.

Castle Dracula, perched above the Borgo Pass in Transylvania.

Character in the novel

In the novel, the solicitor Jonathan Harker travels to Castle Dracula to assist the Count with the purchase of an English estate, Carfax Abbey near Purfleet. Dracula is initially courteous and learned — he has taught himself English from books — but Harker soon realises he is a prisoner, and that his host is a supernatural creature who casts no reflection, crawls down the castle wall "in his lizard fashion", and sustains himself on blood. Dracula travels to England aboard the Demeter, preys upon Lucy Westenra, and is hunted by a group led by Abraham Van Helsing. He is ultimately destroyed in Transylvania by the hunters, who drive a kukri knife through his heart and behead him (in Stoker's text his body at once crumbles to dust).

Powers and weaknesses

Stoker's Dracula possesses great physical strength, the ability to shapeshift into a bat, a wolf, dust or mist, command over animals and the weather, telepathic influence over his victims, and the power to create other vampires. His limitations are equally elaborate: he must rest in earth from his homeland, cannot enter a dwelling uninvited, loses his powers at daybreak, is repelled by garlic, crucifixes and the consecrated host, and cannot cross running water except at the slack or flood of the tide.

Origins

Stoker encountered the name "Dracula" in William Wilkinson's An Account of the Principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia (1820), which noted that "Dracula" meant "devil" in Wallachian and was a surname of the voivodes. He associated the name with Vlad III of Wallachia (Vlad the Impaler), whose father Vlad II took the name "Dracul" on joining the Order of the Dragon. How far Stoker modelled his Count on Vlad remains a subject of scholarly debate; the novel's Dracula presents himself instead as a Szekely descendant of Attila who fought the Turks. Stoker's working title for the character was "Count Wampyr", and his research also drew on Emily Gerard's writings on Transylvanian folklore and on earlier vampire fiction such as Carmilla (1872) and The Vampyre (1819).

Portrayals

Dracula has been portrayed on screen by, among many others, Max Schreck (as Count Orlok in the unauthorised Nosferatu, 1922), Bela Lugosi (1931), Christopher Lee (the Hammer films, beginning 1958), Klaus Kinski (1979), Frank Langella (1979), Gary Oldman (1992) and Claes Bang (2020). Lugosi's evening-dress portrayal and Lee's feral aristocrat fixed the two dominant visual templates for the character.

Influence

The character helped codify the conventions of vampire fiction — fangs, hypnotic charm, the stake through the heart, and the vampire hunter — and is the most frequently portrayed literary character in film after Sherlock Holmes by some counts.
