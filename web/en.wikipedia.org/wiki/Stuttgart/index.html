Stuttgart (German: [ˈʃtʊtɡaʁt]) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Baden-Württemberg. It is located on the Neckar river in a fertile valley known as the Stuttgarter Kessel (Stuttgart Cauldron) about an hour's drive from the Swabian Jura and the Black Forest. Stuttgart is Germany's sixth-largest city and one of its most important industrial centres, known worldwide as the home of Mercedes-Benz and Porsche and often described as the "cradle of the automobile".

History

Stuttgart's origins lie in a stud farm (Stuotgarten, "mare garden") founded around 950 AD by Duke Liudolf of Swabia. It grew as the residence of the counts, later dukes and kings, of Württemberg, whose Old and New Palaces still dominate the Schlossplatz. The city industrialised in the nineteenth century; Gottlieb Daimler built his early automobiles in the suburb of Cannstatt, and Robert Bosch founded his electrical company here. Stuttgart was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt in a modern style. In 1952 it became capital of the newly merged state of Baden-Württemberg.

Economy

The Stuttgart region is one of Europe's strongest high-technology and manufacturing economies. Alongside the headquarters of Mercedes-Benz Group and Porsche, it hosts Bosch, Mahle, and a dense network of engineering suppliers ("Mittelstand"), plus IBM and Hewlett-Packard's German operations. The region has among the lowest unemployment rates in Germany and a high proportion of jobs in research and development.

Culture and landmarks

  • Schlossplatz and the Neues Schloss (New Palace)
  • Mercedes-Benz Museum and Porsche Museum
  • Staatsgalerie Stuttgart art museum
  • Wilhelma, a combined zoo and botanical garden in a Moorish-style palace complex
  • The Fernsehturm Stuttgart (1956), the world's first television tower built in reinforced concrete
  • VfB Stuttgart, the city's Bundesliga football club
  • The Cannstatter Volksfest, one of the world's largest beer festivals

Transport

Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof is a major rail hub; the controversial Stuttgart 21 project is rebuilding it as an underground through-station. The city has an S-Bahn and Stadtbahn network and an international airport south of the city near Leinfelden-Echterdingen.
