Sir William Richard Shaboe Doll CH OBE FRS (28 October 1912 – 24 July 2005) was a British physician and epidemiologist who, with Austin Bradford Hill, provided the first strong statistical evidence linking cigarette smoking to lung cancer and other diseases. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern epidemiology.

Early life and career

Doll was born in Hampton, Middlesex, and educated at Westminster School and King's College, Cambridge, where he read mathematics before switching to medicine at St Thomas's Hospital, qualifying in 1937. He served in the Royal Army Medical Corps during the Second World War. After the war he joined the Medical Research Council's Statistical Research Unit under Austin Bradford Hill.

Smoking research

In 1950 Doll and Hill published a case-control study of patients in London hospitals showing that smokers were far more likely than non-smokers to have carcinoma of the lung. To test the finding prospectively, they launched the British Doctors Study in 1951, sending questionnaires to some 40,000 British physicians and following them — ultimately for 50 years — through death records. The study progressively demonstrated that cigarette smoking caused lung cancer, heart disease and many other conditions, that risk rose with the amount smoked, and that stopping smoking at any age conferred substantial benefit; lifelong smokers lost on average about ten years of life expectancy. Doll himself gave up smoking during the early work.

Other work

Doll established the carcinogenicity of several occupational and environmental exposures, including asbestos, nickel and ionising radiation (following survivors of the Hiroshima bombing), and investigated the role of the contraceptive pill and of diet in disease. In 1969 he became Regius Professor of Medicine at the University of Oxford, where he founded Green College and the Clinical Trial Service Unit with Richard Peto. His 1981 monograph with Peto, The Causes of Cancer, systematically estimated the avoidable risks of cancer.

Honours and later life

Doll was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1966, knighted in 1971, and appointed a Companion of Honour in 1996. He remained scientifically active into his nineties and died in Oxford in 2005. After his death it emerged that he had undertaken consultancy work for chemical companies in the 1980s, a disclosure that prompted debate about conflicts of interest, though his central findings were unaffected.
