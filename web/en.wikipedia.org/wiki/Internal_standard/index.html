Internal standard

An internal standard in analytical chemistry is a substance added in a known amount to every sample, blank and calibration standard in an analysis. The ratio of the analyte signal to the internal standard signal is used for quantification, correcting for losses during sample preparation, variations in injection volume and instrumental drift.

Selection

A suitable internal standard behaves like the analyte but is distinguishable from it by the detector. In gas and liquid chromatography it should elute near the analyte without overlapping other peaks; in mass spectrometry, isotopically labelled analogues of the analyte — differing only in mass — are ideal internal standards, and their use is known as isotope dilution.

Applications

Internal standards are routine in chromatography, atomic spectroscopy and clinical assays, where they improve precision and accuracy compared with external calibration alone.
