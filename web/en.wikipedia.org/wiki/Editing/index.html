Editing

Editing is the process of selecting and preparing written, photographic, visual, audible or cinematic material for publication or presentation. It involves correcting, condensing, organising and modifying material to improve its accuracy, clarity, consistency and fitness for its audience and medium. A person who edits is called an editor.

Types of editing

In publishing, editing is conventionally divided into stages. Developmental or structural editing addresses content, organisation and argument. Copy editing (line editing) corrects grammar, spelling, punctuation and style and checks internal consistency. Proofreading is the final check of typeset proofs for errors before printing. Other forms include substantive editing, technical editing, fact-checking, film editing, photo editing and audio editing, each adapted to its medium.

Practices and tools

Editors apply style guides — such as The Chicago Manual of Style or Associated Press style — to keep usage consistent, and traditionally marked manuscripts with standardised proofreading symbols. Digital workflows have replaced much paper markup with tracked changes in word processors and specialised editing software, but the underlying editorial judgements remain the same.

Related fields

Editing overlaps with authorship in varying degrees: ghostwriting, abridgement and translation all involve editorial intervention. In film and broadcasting, the editor assembles raw footage into a finished work, a role considered central to the art of cinema. In scholarly publishing, peer review and journal editors gatekeep academic quality.
