Growth habit

Field: Botany

Examples: Tree, shrub, vine, herb

In botany, habit is the characteristic form in which a given species of plant grows. The term covers the plant's overall shape, size, and growth pattern, including whether its stems are woody or herbaceous and whether it grows upright, trailing, climbing, or spreading.

Categories

Common habit categories include trees, shrubs, subshrubs, vines and lianas, grasses, and herbs. Habits are also described by terms such as erect, prostrate, decumbent, scandent (climbing), caespitose (tufted), and rosette-forming. Habit is used in plant identification and in horticulture, where a plant's mature habit determines its placement in gardens and landscapes.

Plasticity

Habit is partly genetic and partly shaped by environment: the same species may grow as a compact shrub in exposed conditions and as a taller, looser plant in shelter.
