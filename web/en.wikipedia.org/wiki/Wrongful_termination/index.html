Wrongful termination

Wrongful termination, also called wrongful dismissal or wrongful discharge, is a situation in which an employee's contract of employment has been terminated by the employer in violation of the employee's legal rights — for example because the firing breached an employment contract, an anti-discrimination statute, or public policy. Wrongful termination is distinct from unfair dismissal, a broader statutory concept in some jurisdictions, and from constructive dismissal, in which an employee resigns because of the employer's conduct.

United States

Most employment in the United States is "at will", meaning either party may end it at any time for almost any reason. Wrongful termination claims nevertheless arise through several exceptions:

  • Discrimination: Federal laws such as Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Age Discrimination in Employment Act, and the Americans with Disabilities Act prohibit firing based on race, sex, religion, national origin, age (40 and over), disability, and other protected characteristics.
  • Retaliation: Firing an employee for engaging in protected activity — such as reporting safety violations, filing a wage claim, or whistleblowing — is unlawful under many statutes.
  • Implied contract: Statements in employee handbooks or oral promises may create an implied contract limiting the employer's right to fire.
  • Public policy: Many states recognize a tort claim where an employee is fired for refusing to commit an illegal act, exercising a legal right, or performing a public duty such as jury service.
  • Implied covenant of good faith: A minority of states limit terminations made in bad faith to deprive an employee of earned benefits or commissions.

Remedies

Successful claimants may recover back pay, front pay, lost benefits, compensatory and (in some cases) punitive damages, and reinstatement. Many disputes are resolved through settlement or arbitration, as employment contracts increasingly contain mandatory arbitration clauses.

Other jurisdictions

Many countries give employees stronger baseline protection: employers must generally show a fair reason for dismissal and follow a fair procedure, and employees with a qualifying period of service may claim unfair dismissal before an employment tribunal. Notice periods and severance requirements also vary widely.
