Quantum Fisher information

The quantum Fisher information (QFI) is a central quantity in quantum metrology. It generalizes the classical Fisher information to the quantum setting and quantifies the maximum precision with which an unknown parameter θ encoded in a quantum state ρ(θ) can be estimated. Formally it is defined through the symmetric logarithmic derivative L, satisfying dρ/dθ = (Lρ + ρL)/2, with FQ = Tr(ρL²).

Quantum Cramér–Rao bound

The QFI sets the quantum Cramér–Rao bound: the variance of any unbiased estimator of θ is at least 1/(νFQ) for ν repetitions. Unlike the classical Fisher information, the QFI is a property of the state alone, independent of the measurement; it equals the classical Fisher information maximized over all possible quantum measurements (POVMs).

Applications

QFI is used to identify states useful for interferometry — entangled states whose QFI exceeds the shot-noise limit witness multipartite entanglement — and it connects to quantum geometry through its relation to the fidelity susceptibility and the Bures metric.
