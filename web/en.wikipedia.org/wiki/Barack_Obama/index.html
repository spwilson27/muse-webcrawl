Barack Hussein Obama II (born 4 August 1961) is an American politician who served as the 44th president of the United States from 2009 to 2017 — the first African-American president. A member of the Democratic Party, he previously served as a U.S. senator from Illinois and in the Illinois Senate.

Early life and career

Born in Honolulu to a Kenyan father and a mother from Kansas, Obama was raised in Hawaii and Indonesia. He graduated from Columbia University (1983), worked as a community organiser in Chicago, and earned a law degree from Harvard, where he was the first Black president of the Harvard Law Review. His 2004 Democratic National Convention keynote speech launched him to national prominence, and he was elected to the U.S. Senate that year.

Presidency

Obama defeated John McCain in 2008 and Mitt Romney in 2012. Major achievements include the Affordable Care Act (2010, "Obamacare"), the economic stimulus and financial reforms responding to the Great Recession, the operation that killed Osama bin Laden (2011), the Paris climate agreement, the Iran nuclear deal (JCPOA, 2015), and normalisation of relations with Cuba. He received the 2009 Nobel Peace Prize. His administration ended the U.S. combat mission in Iraq, expanded LGBTQ rights (marriage equality was upheld by the Supreme Court in 2015), and issued executive actions on immigration.

Post-presidency

Obama remained in Washington, D.C., founded the Obama Foundation, and built the Obama Presidential Center in Chicago. His memoir A Promised Land (2020) followed earlier bestsellers Dreams from My Father and The Audacity of Hope.
