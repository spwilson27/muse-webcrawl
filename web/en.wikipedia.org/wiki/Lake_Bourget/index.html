Lake Bourget

Lake Bourget (French: Lac du Bourget) is a lake in the Savoie department of eastern France, at the southern edge of the Jura Mountains. It is the largest natural lake entirely within France, with a surface area of about 44.5 square kilometres and a maximum depth of 145 metres.

Geography

The lake lies at an elevation of about 231 metres and is drained northwards by the Canal de Savières into the Rhône. The spa town of Aix-les-Bains stands on its eastern shore, while the city of Chambéry lies a short distance to the south. The steep western shore is dominated by the Mont du Chat.

History and culture

Bronze Age pile dwellings around the lake are included in the UNESCO World Heritage list of prehistoric lake settlements. The lake is celebrated in French literature, most famously in Alphonse de Lamartine's poem "Le Lac" (1820), inspired by the lake and his love for Julie Charles.

Ecology

The lake is home to fish such as lavaret and Arctic char, and extensive reed beds on its southern shore form an important wetland habitat. It is popular for sailing, rowing, swimming, and other water sports.
