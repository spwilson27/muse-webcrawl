Canada Day (French: Fête du Canada) is the national day of Canada. A federal statutory holiday, it celebrates the anniversary of Canadian Confederation, which occurred on 1 July 1867 with the passing of the British North America Act, 1867, when the three separate colonies of the United Canadas, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick were united into a single dominion within the British Empire called Canada.

Originally called Dominion Day (French: Le Jour de la Confédération), the holiday was renamed in 1982, the same year that the Canadian constitution was patriated by the Canada Act, 1982. Canada Day celebrations take place throughout the country, as well as in various locations around the world attended by Canadians living abroad.

Commemoration

Canada Day is often referred to as "Canada's birthday", particularly in the popular press. However, the term was first used for the 100th anniversary of Confederation in 1967. The milestone was marked by extensive celebrations, including the opening of the Centennial flame on Parliament Hill and the Expo 67 world's fair in Montreal.

Celebrations

Most communities across the country host organized celebrations for Canada Day, typically outdoor public events, such as parades, carnivals, festivals, barbecues, air and maritime shows, fireworks, and free musical concerts, as well as citizenship ceremonies. There is no standard mode of celebration for Canada Day; the Canadian journalist Andrew Cohen called Canada Day "Canada's primary collective moment of self-realization," noting the day is celebrated "in a thousand different ways".

The largest celebration is held in the capital, Ottawa, where the main festivities take place on Parliament Hill and are broadcast nationwide. Celebrations also take place in other major cities.
