La Soufrière (Saint Vincent)

La Soufrière is an active stratovolcano on the Caribbean island of Saint Vincent, in the country of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Rising to 1,234 metres (4,049 ft), it is the island's highest peak and its only active volcano. The name is French for "the sulfur outlet," referring to sulfur deposits near its crater, and it shares the name with volcanoes on Guadeloupe and Saint Lucia.

Eruptive history

La Soufrière has a record of violent eruptions. An eruption in 1718 was followed by a devastating explosive eruption on 7 May 1902, which killed about 1,680 people — many of them Indigenous Kalinago — only a day before Mount Pelée destroyed Saint-Pierre on Martinique. An eruption in 1971–72 grew a lava dome in the crater lake without explosions. The 1979 eruption began on Good Friday, 13 April, with explosive blasts that forced the evacuation of some 20,000 people from the north of the island but caused no direct deaths, after which an effusive phase built a new lava dome.

2020–2021 eruption

After a quiet dome-building phase beginning in December 2020, La Soufrière erupted explosively on 9 April 2021. Ash columns rose over 10 km, pyroclastic flows descended the flanks, and ashfall blanketed the island and reached Barbados. Around 16,000–20,000 residents of the northern "Red Zone" were evacuated; there were no direct fatalities, but agriculture and water supplies were severely damaged. Explosive activity continued through April 2021 before the volcano returned to quiet dome growth.

Monitoring

The volcano is monitored by the Seismic Research Centre of the University of the West Indies, whose observations underpin the island's hazard-zone and evacuation system.
