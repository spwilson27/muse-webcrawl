Fianna Fáil

Fianna Fáil – The Republican Party (Irish: Fianna Fáil – An Páirtí Poblachtánach) is a conservative and Christian-democratic political party in Ireland. Founded in 1926 by Éamon de Valera after a split from Sinn Féin, it has historically been one of the two dominant parties in Irish politics, spending more time in government than any other party.

History

De Valera and his supporters left Sinn Féin in 1926 over the party's refusal to take seats in the Dáil while the oath of allegiance to the British Crown was required. Fianna Fáil entered the Dáil in 1927 and first took power in 1932. Under de Valera it drafted the 1937 Constitution of Ireland. The party led governments for most of the twentieth century, under leaders including Seán Lemass, Jack Lynch, Charles Haughey, Albert Reynolds and Bertie Ahern. After heavy losses in the 2011 election following the Irish financial crisis, the party rebuilt and returned to government in coalition arrangements, with Micheál Martin serving as Taoiseach.

Ideology and policies

Fianna Fáil describes itself as a republican party committed to a united Ireland achieved by peaceful means. It is generally positioned in the centre to centre-right of Irish politics, and is affiliated at European level with the Renew Europe group (formerly ALDE).
