Lanzhou

Lanzhou 兰州
Prefecture-level city, capital of Gansu, China
Population (2020 census): approx. 4.36 million
Elevation: about 1,600 m
Known for: Yellow River crossing, beef noodles

Lanzhou (Chinese: 兰州; pinyin: Lánzhōu) is the capital and largest city of Gansu Province in northwestern China. A prefecture-level city, it lies on the banks of the Yellow River, which flows through the narrow valley in which the city is built, and historically served as a major crossing point and a key stop on the northern Silk Road and the Hexi Corridor route westward.

History

The area was incorporated into the Chinese state under the Qin and Han dynasties; a county was established here as early as the first century BC, and the city grew as a fortified garrison and trading post guarding the passage between central China and the western regions. Under the Sui dynasty the name Lanzhou ("Lan prefecture") was established. For centuries the Yellow River could be crossed here only by ferry or floating bridge; the first permanent bridge, now the Zhongshan Bridge, was built with German engineering in 1907–1909.

In the twentieth century Lanzhou became a centre of China's oil-refining and petrochemical industries, supplied by the Yumen oilfields, and a major rail junction. Its position made it a terminus and staging point during the Second Sino-Japanese War and later a hub of heavy industry in the interior. Rapid growth and industry also gave it a longstanding reputation for air pollution, which has been substantially reduced since the 2010s.

Geography and climate

Lanzhou occupies a long, narrow valley flanked by loess hills and mountains, at an elevation of roughly 1,600 metres. It has a cold semi-arid to temperate continental climate with large temperature swings, low rainfall, and abundant sunshine. The Yellow River is the city's defining feature, lined with parks, temples such as White Pagoda Hill (Baita Shan), and the riverside promenade.

Culture

Lanzhou is famous throughout China for its hand-pulled beef noodles, Lanzhou lamian—a bowl of clear beef broth, hand-stretched noodles, sliced beef, radish, chilli oil and herbs, traditionally eaten for breakfast. The city is also noted for its watermelons, lilies, and the giant waterwheels once used for irrigation along the river.

Transport

Lanzhou is a principal rail hub of northwest China, where the Longhai, Baotou–Lanzhou, Lanzhou–Xinjiang and Lanzhou–Chongqing lines meet, served by Lanzhou West high-speed station. Lanzhou Zhongchuan International Airport serves the city, and a metro system opened in 2019.
