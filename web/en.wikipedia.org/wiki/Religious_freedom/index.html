Freedom of religion

Freedom of religion, or religious liberty, is the principle that supports the freedom of an individual or community, in public or private, to manifest religion or belief in teaching, practice, worship and observance. It generally includes the freedom to change religion or belief, and freedom not to profess any religion (atheism or agnosticism).

Legal recognition

Religious freedom is recognised in Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. Many constitutions protect it, including the First Amendment to the United States Constitution, Article 9 of the European Convention on Human Rights, and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. In practice, the degree of religious freedom varies widely between countries, and issues such as religious dress, conscientious objection, blasphemy laws and state religions remain contested. It is considered by many to be a fundamental human right, though its limits — where religious practice collides with other rights — are widely debated.
