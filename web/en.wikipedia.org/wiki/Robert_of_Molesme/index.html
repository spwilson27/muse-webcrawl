Robert of Molesme

Bornc. 1028, near Troyes, France
Died17 April 1111, Molesme, France
Venerated inRoman Catholic Church
Canonised1222, by Pope Honorius III
Feast26 January (with the Cistercian founders); 17 April

Saint Robert of Molesme (c. 1028 – 17 April 1111) was a French abbot and a founder of the Cistercian Order. Dissatisfied with the relaxed observance of Benedictine houses of his day, he spent his life attempting a stricter return to the Rule of Saint Benedict.

Life

Born to a noble family near Troyes, Robert entered the abbey of Montier-la-Celle at fifteen and rose to become prior and then abbot of Saint-Michel-de-Tonnerre. Called in 1075 to lead a group of hermits in the forest of Collan, he founded the abbey of Molesme, which prospered but, in his judgment, again drifted from strict observance. In 1098 Robert left with twenty-one companions for the remote marshland of Cîteaux in Burgundy, where they founded the "New Monastery" that became the mother house of the Cistercian Order.

Return to Molesme

The monks of Molesme petitioned Pope Urban II to order Robert back, and in 1100 he returned as their abbot, remaining there until his death in 1111. The community at Cîteaux, under his successors Alberic and Stephen Harding, flourished and — with the arrival of Bernard of Clairvaux in 1112 — reshaped Western monasticism. Robert was canonised in 1222 and is commemorated as a co-founder of the order alongside Alberic and Stephen.
