Tiananmen

Tiananmen (simplified Chinese: 天安门; traditional Chinese: 天安門; pinyin: Tiān'ānmén), literally the "Gate of Heavenly Peace", is a monumental gate in the city centre of Beijing, China, the southern entrance to the Imperial City, through which one enters the Forbidden City. Built in 1417 during the Ming dynasty as the Chengtianmen, it received its present name during the Qing dynasty.

History

The gate was the site of proclamations by emperors and, on 1 October 1949, of Mao Zedong's proclamation of the founding of the People's Republic of China. A giant portrait of Mao hangs on the gate, flanked by slogans reading "Long live the People's Republic of China" and "Long live the great solidarity of the world's peoples".

The square

South of the gate lies Tiananmen Square, one of the largest city squares in the world, containing the Monument to the People's Heroes, the Great Hall of the People, the National Museum of China, and the Mausoleum of Mao Zedong.
