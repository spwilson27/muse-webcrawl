The Congressional Medal of Honor Society (CMOHS) is a non-profit organisation of United States military recipients of the Medal of Honor, the nation's highest award for military valour. It is the only organisation chartered by the United States Congress whose membership consists exclusively of Medal of Honor recipients; the Society was incorporated under a federal charter enacted in 1958 (Public Law 85-642). Its headquarters are located aboard the museum ship USS Yorktown at Patriots Point Naval and Maritime Museum in Mount Pleasant, South Carolina.

History and purpose

The Society traces its roots to informal associations of recipients and to the Legion of Valor, and was formally chartered in 1958 to create a bond of brotherhood among living recipients, to protect and preserve the dignity of the medal, and to promote patriotism and the values the medal represents. The Society holds conventions of recipients, maintains the official record and database of all Medal of Honor recipients (more than 3,500 since the medal's creation during the Civil War), and works with Congress and the Department of Defense on matters affecting recipients — including protecting the medal against unauthorised manufacture, sale and fraudulent claims, goals advanced by the Stolen Valor Acts of 2005 and 2013.

Programs

The Society's Medal of Honor Character Development Program is a free curriculum for schools built around the values of courage, commitment, integrity, sacrifice, citizenship and patriotism, using the stories of recipients as teaching examples. The Society and its affiliated foundation support recipient outreach, scholarships, and the Citizen Honors Awards, which recognise civilians for acts of heroism or selfless service. Membership declines with time as elderly recipients die; fewer than 70 living recipients remain in the 2020s, the youngest being veterans of the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan.
