The Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) is a private research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1861 in response to the increasing industrialisation of the United States, MIT adopted a model combining teaching with laboratory-based research and has since become one of the world's leading institutions in science, engineering and technology.

History

MIT was chartered by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts in 1861 at the urging of geologist William Barton Rogers, and opened in Boston's Back Bay in 1865. It moved across the Charles River to its present Cambridge campus in 1916, centred on the neoclassical Great Dome. During the Second World War and the Cold War, MIT laboratories such as the Radiation Laboratory and Lincoln Laboratory made major contributions to radar, computing and defence research.

Academics and research

The Institute is organised into five schools — Architecture and Planning; Engineering; Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences; Management (Sloan); and Science — plus the Schwarzman College of Computing. MIT is consistently ranked among the top universities in the world. Its faculty and alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners and Fields medallists. Major research centres include the MIT Media Lab, the Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL) and the Broad Institute (with Harvard). MIT is also known for its entrepreneurial culture: companies founded by alumni include Intel, Bose, Dropbox and iRobot, and for its tradition of elaborate student pranks ("hacks").
