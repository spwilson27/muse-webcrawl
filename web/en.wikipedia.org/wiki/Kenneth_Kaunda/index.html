Overview

Kenneth David Kaunda (1924–2021) was a Zambian independence leader and the first President of Zambia, serving from independence in 1964 until 1991. A teacher and son of a Church of Scotland missionary from Nyasaland, he founded the United National Independence Party (UNIP) and led Northern Rhodesia to independence from the Central African Federation.

Presidency and legacy

Kaunda governed by his philosophy of "Zambian Humanism", nationalised the copper mines, and made Zambia a frontline state supporting liberation movements in Rhodesia, South Africa, Namibia, Angola and Mozambique — at heavy economic cost, hosting the ANC and ZANU/ZAPU exile movements. Copper price collapse and debt drove decline through the 1980s; after riots and pressure he accepted multiparty elections in 1991 and — rare among his generation of African presidents — peacefully conceded defeat to Frederick Chiluba. He later campaigned against HIV/AIDS, having lost a son to the disease. He is remembered as "KK" and Zambia's founding father.
