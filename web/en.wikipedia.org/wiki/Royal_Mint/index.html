Royal Mint

The Royal Mint
Founded: c. 886 AD
Headquarters: Llantrisant, Wales
Owner: HM Treasury
Products: Circulating coins, commemorative coins, bullion, medals

The Royal Mint is the United Kingdom's oldest company and the official maker of British coins. It is a government-owned company operating under an executive agency of HM Treasury, and it strikes all circulating coinage for the UK as well as coins and medals for countries around the world.

History

The Mint traces its history back more than 1,100 years, to a mint established in London around AD 886. For centuries it was based in the Tower of London, where Isaac Newton served as Warden and then Master of the Mint from 1696 until his death, famously pursuing counterfeiters and overseeing the Great Recoinage. In the early 19th century the Mint moved to purpose-built premises on Tower Hill.

By the 1960s the Tower Hill site was too small, and ahead of decimalisation the Mint relocated to a large new site at Llantrisant in South Wales, officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1968. It now operates on a site of over 35 acres with production capacity of billions of coins per year.

Operations

As well as UK circulation coins — including the twelve-sided one-pound coin introduced in 2017 — the Royal Mint produces commemorative issues, bullion products such as the Britannia coin, investors' products, and official medals, including the medals for the London 2012 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Its visitor attraction, the Royal Mint Experience, opened in 2016.
