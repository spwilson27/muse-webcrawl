The Governor of New South Wales is the representative of the Australian monarch in the state of New South Wales. The governor performs the same constitutional and ceremonial functions at the state level as the Governor-General does federally: appointing the premier and ministers, presiding over the Executive Council, granting royal assent to legislation, and serving as a non-partisan head of state for the state. It is the oldest continuously held vice-regal office in Australia, dating from the arrival of Captain Arthur Phillip in 1788.

History

Phillip governed the penal colony of New South Wales with near-absolute authority. Successive naval and military governors — including Lachlan Macquarie, under whom the colony was transformed — ruled until responsible government arrived in 1856, after which the governor's role became constitutional and ceremonial. Notable episodes include Governor William Bligh's overthrow in the Rum Rebellion of 1808, Australia's only armed seizure of a government. Until 2001 the governor lived at Government House, Sydney; after a period at Cranbrook, the office returned to Government House in 2011.

Role today

The governor acts on the advice of the state ministry except in exceptional "reserve power" situations, such as appointing a premier when an election result is unclear. Governors are appointed by the monarch on the premier's advice; since the mid-twentieth century most appointees have been Australian-born, and the post has been held by figures from the military, law and public life, including the first woman, Marie Bashir (2001–2014).
