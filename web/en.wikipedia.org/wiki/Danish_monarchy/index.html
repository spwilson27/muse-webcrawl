Monarchy of Denmark

The monarchy of Denmark is a constitutional institution and the highest office in the Kingdom of Denmark. The reigning monarch is King Frederik X, who succeeded his mother, Queen Margrethe II, on her abdication on 14 January 2024.

History

The Danish monarchy traces its origins to the Viking kings of the 10th century; Gorm the Old (reigned c. 936–958) is generally regarded as the first historically recognised king, and his son Harald Bluetooth raised the Jelling runestones proclaiming that he had "won all of Denmark and Norway and made the Danes Christian". Denmark was an elective monarchy in the medieval period, became hereditary in 1660 with the introduction of absolute monarchy under Frederick III, and turned constitutional with the June Constitution of 1849. Margrethe II's accession in 1972 followed a 1953 constitutional amendment allowing female succession; a 2009 amendment introduced absolute primogeniture.

Constitutional role

Under the Constitution of 1953, executive power is vested in the monarch but exercised through ministers; the monarch formally appoints the prime minister after parliamentary negotiations, presides over the Council of State, signs laws, accredits ambassadors and serves as head of the Evangelical Lutheran Church of Denmark. In practice the monarch's role is ceremonial and unifying, above party politics. The royal house, the House of Glücksburg, is a branch of the House of Oldenburg.

Residences and succession

The monarch's Copenhagen residence is Amalienborg; other palaces include Fredensborg, Marselisborg and Gråsten. Frederik X married Mary Donaldson of Australia in 2004; the heir apparent is Crown Prince Christian.
