Maynooth University (Irish: Ollscoil Mhá Nuad), formally the National University of Ireland, Maynooth (NUIM), is a constituent university of the National University of Ireland located in Maynooth, County Kildare, Ireland. It was Ireland's youngest university until Technological University Dublin was established in 2019, having been founded by the Universities Act, 1997, from the secular faculties of the now separate St Patrick's College, Maynooth, which was founded in 1795.

The university consists of two connected campuses: an older southern campus, with 19th-century buildings, shared with St Patrick's College, and, across a public road, a modern northern campus. Maynooth University is internationally recognised for research and teaching across the arts, humanities, social sciences, science and engineering. It offers a wide range of undergraduate and postgraduate degrees, and is particularly noted for subjects such as computer science, mathematics, anthropology, geography and sociology.

History

The institution traces its origins to St Patrick's College, Maynooth, founded in 1795 as a seminary for the education of Catholic priests. In 1966 the college opened its doors to lay students, and in 1997 the secular faculties were separated to form the National University of Ireland, Maynooth. In 2014, the university adopted the trading name Maynooth University.

Academics

The university is organised into three faculties: the Faculty of Arts, Celtic Studies and Philosophy; the Faculty of Science and Engineering; and the Faculty of Social Sciences. It is home to research institutes including the Hamilton Institute, the National Institute for Regional and Spatial Analysis, and the Institute of Immunology.

Student life

Maynooth is Ireland's only university town. The campus has an active students' union and over one hundred clubs and societies. Accommodation is provided on the North Campus, and the town's proximity to Dublin makes it a popular choice for students.
