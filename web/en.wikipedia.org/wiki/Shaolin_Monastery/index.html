The Shaolin Monastery (少林寺, Shàolín Sì) is a Chan Buddhist temple near Dengfeng in Henan Province, China. Founded in 495 CE at the foot of Mount Song, it is traditionally regarded as the birthplace of Chan Buddhism and is world-famous for its association with Chinese martial arts, particularly Shaolin kung fu.

History

The monastery was built by Emperor Xiaowen of the Northern Wei for the Indian monk Batuo. According to tradition, Bodhidharma, the legendary founder of Chan (Zen) Buddhism, arrived around the 5th–6th century and meditated facing a wall in a nearby cave for nine years. Shaolin monks famously aided the Tang dynasty prince Li Shimin in 621, and the temple received imperial patronage thereafter. The monastery was destroyed and rebuilt several times, most severely in 1928 when the warlord Shi Yousan burned many of its buildings and manuscripts.

Shaolin kung fu

Shaolin martial arts combine Chan philosophy with combat training; the saying "all martial arts under heaven originated from Shaolin" reflects its prestige. Styles emphasise forms (taolu), staff and other weapons, and qigong practices such as the "iron shirt". The temple's martial tradition was popularised globally by the 1982 film The Shaolin Temple starring Jet Li.

Pagoda Forest

West of the monastery stands the Pagoda Forest, a cemetery of more than 240 brick and stone stupas containing the ashes of eminent monks, dating from the Tang to the Qing dynasties. Together with other historic monuments of Dengfeng, the site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010.

Modern era

Today the monastery is a major tourist destination and cultural brand, with performance troupes touring worldwide and affiliated schools training thousands of students in Dengfeng.
