Lieutenant Colonel Herbert Jones VC OBE (14 May 1940 – 28 May 1982), known as "H" Jones, was a British Army officer and a posthumous recipient of the Victoria Cross, the highest award for gallantry in the face of the enemy that can be awarded to British and Commonwealth forces. He was killed leading an attack during the Battle of Goose Green in the Falklands War while commanding 2nd Battalion, The Parachute Regiment (2 PARA).

Early life and career

Jones was born in Putney, London, the son of an American artist father and a Welsh mother. He was educated at St Peter's School, Seaford, and Eton College, and commissioned from Sandhurst into the Devonshire and Dorset Regiment in 1960. He served in Northern Ireland, where he was appointed OBE for his service, and in 1979 took command of 2 PARA.

Battle of Goose Green

On 28 May 1982, 2 PARA attacked Argentine positions defending the settlements of Darwin and Goose Green. The attack stalled in daylight under heavy fire from well-sited trenches along Darwin Hill. Seeing his adjutant killed trying to silence one position, Jones led a small group up a gully toward an Argentine trench. Sub-machine gun in hand, he charged the position alone across open ground and was shot and mortally wounded within sight of the enemy trench; moments later the position was overrun, and the renewed momentum helped carry the battalion to victory — the first major land battle of the war. The Argentine garrison surrendered the following day.

Citation and legacy

His Victoria Cross citation praised his "devotion to duty and leadership ... beyond the call of duty," while acknowledging the controversy that a battalion commander had died doing a section commander's job — a debate still rehearsed in military studies of command. Jones is buried at San Carlos War Cemetery in the Falklands. His widow Sara Jones founded the H Jones Bursary Fund; his son Rupert Jones later became a senior British Army officer.
