Auriga is a constellation in the northern celestial hemisphere, one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and among the 88 modern constellations. Its name is Latin for "the charioteer," and it is traditionally depicted as a man holding the reins of a chariot, carrying a goat on his shoulder and two kids under his arm.

Notable features

The brightest star, Capella (Alpha Aurigae), is the sixth-brightest star in the night sky — actually a system of four stars in two binary pairs. Other notable stars include Menkalinan (Beta Aurigae), and the unusual eclipsing binaries Epsilon and Zeta Aurigae. Epsilon Aurigae has an extraordinarily long orbital period of about 27 years and dims when a companion object with a huge dust disk passes in front of it.

Because the Milky Way passes through Auriga, the constellation hosts several bright open clusters: M36, M37 and M38, all popular targets for amateur telescopes, plus the Flaming Star Nebula (IC 405) illuminated by the runaway star AE Aurigae.

Mythology

Auriga is commonly associated with Erichthonius of Athens, a legendary king credited with inventing the four-horse chariot, though it has also been linked with the goatherd who raised the goat Amalthea, whose star Capella represents her.
