Pinkerton, founded as the Pinkerton National Detective Agency, is a private security guard and detective agency established in the United States around 1850 by the Scottish immigrant Allan Pinkerton. Its agents performed services ranging from private investigation to security guarding and, controversially, strikebreaking and labour espionage, making "Pinkerton" a byword in American labour history.

Early history

Pinkerton had been Chicago's first detective. His agency won fame guarding railroads and hunting outlaw gangs, and in 1861 claimed to have foiled an assassination plot against president-elect Abraham Lincoln in Baltimore, smuggling him into Washington by night train. During the Civil War, Pinkerton (under the alias Major E. J. Allen) ran the Union's intelligence service for General McClellan, though his overestimates of Confederate strength were criticised. The agency's trademark — an open eye above the slogan "We Never Sleep" — gave rise to the slang term "private eye."

Labour conflicts

In the late nineteenth century the agency was the principal private force hired by industrialists against unions. Its role in the Great Railroad Strike of 1877, the Homestead Strike of 1892 — where a barge-load of agents fought a gun battle with strikers at Andrew Carnegie's steelworks, leaving several dead — and the pursuit of the Molly Maguires made it notorious. By the 1890s its manpower reportedly exceeded the standing US Army, prompting Ohio to outlaw it and Congress, after Homestead, to pass the Anti-Pinkerton Act of 1893, restricting the federal government's use of such agencies.

Later history

The agency persisted through the twentieth century as a guard and investigations firm, moving away from strikebreaking. It was acquired by Securitas AB in 1999 and today operates as a risk-management and corporate investigations subsidiary, retaining the Pinkerton name.
