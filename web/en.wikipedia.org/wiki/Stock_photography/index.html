Stock photography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stock photography is the supply of photographs that are often licensed for specific uses. The stock photo industry, which began to gain hold in the 1920s, has established models including traditional macrostock photography, midstock photography, and microstock photography. Conventional stock agencies charge from several hundred to several thousand US dollars per image, while microstock agencies sell images for as little as a few cents. Stock images are used by publishers, advertisers, website designers, and businesses to illustrate material without the cost of commissioning an original photo shoot.

History

Early picture libraries supplied newspapers and magazines with photographs of newsworthy subjects. Agencies such as Bettmann Archive and Hulton Archive built vast collections, while firms founded in the late 20th century — notably Getty Images and Corbis — consolidated the industry and moved distribution online. The rise of digital photography and the internet in the 2000s produced the microstock model, in which large numbers of contributors upload images to agencies such as Shutterstock and iStock, selling licenses at low prices in high volume.

Licensing

Stock images are typically sold under one of two models. Royalty-free licenses allow the buyer to use an image multiple times after a single payment, subject to restrictions. Rights-managed licenses price each use according to factors such as size, placement, duration, and territory. Photographs featuring identifiable people or property generally require model or property releases for commercial use; images without releases may still be licensed for editorial use.

Themes

Stock libraries are organized by searchable keywords and concepts. Popular subjects include business and technology, health and medicine, food, travel, nature, and abstract backgrounds, as well as seasonal and lifestyle imagery designed to illustrate broad concepts such as teamwork, success, or stress.
