Gendarmenmarkt

LocationMitte, Berlin, Germany
LandmarksKonzerthaus Berlin, French Cathedral, German Cathedral
Built17th–18th century

The Gendarmenmarkt is a square in Berlin, Germany, widely regarded as one of the most beautiful squares in the city. It is the site of the Konzerthaus Berlin concert hall and the French and German Cathedrals, whose matching domed towers frame the square.

History

The square was originally built in 1688 as the Linden-Markt, part of the Friedrichstadt district laid out for a growing population that included French Protestant (Huguenot) immigrants who had settled in Berlin after the Edict of Potsdam. It took its present name in 1799 from the Regiment Gens d'Armes, a Prussian cuirassier regiment whose stables stood on the site in the 18th century.

The French Cathedral (Französischer Dom) was built for the Huguenot community in 1701–1705, and the German Cathedral (Deutscher Dom) followed in 1708; both received their distinctive domed towers in the 1780s under Carl von Gontard. The concert hall, designed by Karl Friedrich Schinkel and opened in 1821 as the Schauspielhaus theatre, stands between them.

Later history

The square was heavily damaged in the Second World War and lay in East Berlin thereafter; it was renamed Platz der Akademie in 1950. Restoration of the three principal buildings was completed during the 1980s, and the square reverted to the name Gendarmenmarkt after German reunification. Today it hosts open-air concerts and one of Berlin's best-known Christmas markets, and a statue of the poet Friedrich Schiller stands before the concert hall.
