Jean Drapeau CC GOQ (18 February 1916 – 12 August 1999) was a Canadian politician. He served as mayor of Montreal from 1954 to 1957 and from 1960 to 1986, one of the longest tenures in the city's history. A polarizing figure, he transformed Montreal with grand projects including the Metro, Expo 67, and the 1976 Summer Olympics.

Career

Drapeau, a lawyer, first won the mayoralty in 1954 on an anti-corruption platform as head of the Civic Action League. Defeated in 1957, he founded the Civic Party and returned to office in 1960, remaining mayor for 26 years. Under his administration Montreal built its underground Metro (opened 1966), hosted the Universal and International Exhibition Expo 67 — widely considered the most successful world's fair of the century — and staged events such as Man and His World and the 1976 Olympic Games.

The Olympics left the city with a debt of over one billion dollars that took three decades to pay off, and Drapeau's famous remark that "the Olympics can no more lose money than a man can have a baby" became notorious. His administration was also marked by aggressive urban renewal and the demolition of older neighbourhoods, criticized by heritage advocates.

Legacy

Drapeau is remembered as the mayor who brought Montreal onto the world stage. Parc Jean-Drapeau, encompassing the Expo 67 islands of Île Sainte-Hélène and Île Notre-Dame, bears his name.
