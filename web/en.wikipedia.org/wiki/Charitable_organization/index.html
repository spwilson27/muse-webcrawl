Charitable organization

A charitable organization or charity is an organization whose primary objectives are philanthropy and social well-being, such as educational, religious, or other activities serving the public interest or common good. Charitable organizations generally do not distribute profits to owners or shareholders, and in most jurisdictions they receive favourable tax treatment and may accept tax-deductible donations.

Legal definition

The legal definition of a charitable organization (and of charity) varies between countries and in some instances between regions of the same country. Regulators typically require that a charity's purposes fall within recognized charitable categories — the relief of poverty, the advancement of education or religion, the promotion of health, and other purposes beneficial to the community — and that it operate exclusively for public benefit.

History

Organized charity has roots in religious almsgiving in many cultures. In England, the Charitable Uses Act 1601 (the "Statute of Elizabeth") provided an influential early legal framework, and its preamble long served as a reference for charitable purposes in common-law jurisdictions. The modern charitable sector expanded greatly in the 19th and 20th centuries with the growth of foundations, hospitals, universities, and international relief organizations.

Governance and finance

Charities are usually governed by a board of trustees or directors who owe fiduciary duties to the organization. Income comes from donations, grants, legacies, and sometimes trading activities. Many countries require registered charities to file annual accounts and reports, and regulators may investigate misconduct or mismanagement.
