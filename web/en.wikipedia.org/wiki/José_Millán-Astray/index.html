José Millán-Astray

José Millán-Astray y Terreros (5 July 1879 – 1 January 1954) was a Spanish soldier, founder and first commander of the Spanish Legion, and a close associate of Francisco Franco. Wounded repeatedly in Morocco, losing an arm and an eye, he cultivated the Legion's cult of death embodied in its motto "¡Viva la Muerte!" (Long live death!).

During the Spanish Civil War he headed Franco's propaganda office. He is remembered for his notorious confrontation with philosopher Miguel de Unamuno at the University of Salamanca in October 1936, where Unamuno denounced the Nationalist rising. Millán-Astray later fell from favour and died in Madrid in 1954.
