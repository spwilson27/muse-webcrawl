Associate company

Ownership relationships: parent, subsidiary, associate

An associate company (or associate) is a company over which another company has significant influence, but which it does not control as a subsidiary. Under International Accounting Standard 28 (IAS 28), significant influence is presumed when an investor holds 20% or more of the voting power of the investee, unless it can be clearly demonstrated otherwise; holdings below 20% are presumed not to confer significant influence.

Significant influence

Significant influence is the power to participate in an associate's financial and operating policy decisions without controlling them. Evidence includes representation on the board of directors, participation in policy-making, material transactions between the companies, interchange of managerial personnel, or provision of essential technical information.

Accounting treatment

Investments in associates are generally accounted for using the equity method: the investment is initially recorded at cost, and the carrying amount is then adjusted for the investor's share of the associate's profits or losses, which is recognised in the investor's income statement. Unlike a subsidiary, an associate's assets, liabilities and results are not consolidated line by line. The concept contrasts with a joint venture, where control is shared jointly by agreement, and with a subsidiary, where the parent has outright control, usually through majority voting rights.
