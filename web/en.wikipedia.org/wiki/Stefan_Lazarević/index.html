Stefan Lazarević (Serbian Cyrillic: Стефан Лазаревић; c. 1377 – 19 July 1427), also known as Stefan the Tall (Стефан Високи), was a Serbian ruler, Prince of Serbia (1389–1402) and Despot of Serbia (1402–1427). The son of Prince Lazar Hrebeljanović, who was killed at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, Stefan is regarded as one of the finest medieval knights and statesmen, and a major patron of arts and literature, under whom Serbia experienced a cultural flowering known as the Serbian late medieval renaissance.

Reign

Stefan was a minor when his father died fighting the Ottomans at Kosovo Polje; his mother, Princess Milica, ruled as regent and accepted Ottoman vassalage. As a vassal, Stefan led Serbian heavy cavalry in Ottoman service at the battles of Rovine (1395), Nicopolis (1396), and Ankara (1402) — where his charge famously nearly rescued the captured Sultan Bayezid I from Timur. After Ankara dissolved Ottoman suzerainty, Stefan accepted the title of despot from the Byzantine emperor Manuel II in Constantinople in 1402.

He then navigated between the Ottoman Empire and Hungary: in 1403–1404 he became a vassal of King Sigismund of Hungary, receiving Belgrade, which he rebuilt as Serbia's capital, and joining the chivalric Order of the Dragon. He fought civil wars against his brother Vuk and Ottoman-backed rivals, survived the Ottoman interregnum, and in his later years secured a lasting peace, kept Serbia prosperous through its silver mines (notably Novo Brdo), and designated his nephew Đurađ Branković as heir. He died suddenly in 1427 while hunting.

Cultural legacy

Stefan founded the fortified monastery of Manasija (Resava), center of the Resava School of transcription and translation, and the monastery of Kalenić. Himself a writer, he composed the Slovo ljubve ("A Homily on Love"), one of the finest works of medieval Serbian literature. He was canonized by the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1927, five hundred years after his death.
