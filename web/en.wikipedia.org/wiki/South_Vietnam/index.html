South Vietnam, officially the Republic of Vietnam (Vietnamese: Việt Nam Cộng hòa), was a state in Southeast Asia that existed from 1955 to 1975. It was established in the southern half of Vietnam after the 1954 Geneva partition and fought the communist North (North Vietnam) and the Viet Cong insurgency in the Vietnam War, backed by the United States, until its collapse in 1975.

History

The State of Vietnam under Emperor Bảo Đại was succeeded in 1955 by the Republic of Vietnam under Ngo Dinh Diem, who refused the unification elections foreseen at Geneva. Diem's authoritarian rule and persecution of Buddhists provoked crisis; he was overthrown and killed in a US-backed coup in November 1963. After two years of revolving juntas, Nguyen Van Thieu became president in 1967.

Vietnam War

The National Liberation Front (Viet Cong), supported by the North, waged guerrilla war in the countryside while US combat troops — peaking at over 540,000 in 1969 — fought major battles from Ia Drang to the Tet Offensive (1968). The Paris Peace Accords of January 1973 withdrew US forces. With American aid cut and the North's conventional offensive under way, South Vietnam collapsed in the spring of 1975; on 30 April, North Vietnamese tanks entered the Independence Palace in Saigon, and President Duong Van Minh surrendered unconditionally.

Aftermath

Saigon was renamed Ho Chi Minh City, hundreds of thousands of South Vietnamese fled as boat people or were sent to re-education camps, and the country was formally reunified as the Socialist Republic of Vietnam on 2 July 1976. A large diaspora, especially in the United States, preserves the Republic's flag and memory.
