Survey of India

The Survey of India is India's central engineering agency in charge of mapping and surveying. Founded in 1767 under the East India Company, it is one of the oldest scientific departments of the Government of India, and works under the Department of Science and Technology. Its headquarters is at Dehradun, Uttarakhand.

History

The Survey is famous for the Great Trigonometrical Survey, begun in 1802 under William Lambton and continued by George Everest, which measured the great arc of the meridian across India and determined the heights of Himalayan peaks. Peak XV was calculated to be the world's highest mountain and was later named Mount Everest after the Surveyor General. The survey also employed the "pundits", Indian explorers who secretly mapped Tibet and Central Asia.

Work

The Survey of India produces topographic maps of the country, maintains geodetic control networks and tide gauges, and supplies mapping for defence, planning and disaster management. The Surveyor General of India heads the organisation.
