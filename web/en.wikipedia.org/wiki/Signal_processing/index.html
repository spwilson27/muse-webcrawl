Signal processing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: An analogue waveform being sampled into digital values]

Signal processing is an electrical engineering subfield that focuses on analysing, modifying, and synthesising signals such as sound, images, and scientific measurements. Signals are representations of quantities varying over time, space, or other dimensions.

Analogue signal processing (filters, modulators) long predates computers; digital signal processing (DSP) took over from the 1960s–70s as the fast Fourier transform (1965) and cheap processors arrived, enabling filtering, compression, and analysis in software and dedicated chips.

Core ideas

A signal's frequency content (its spectrum) is analysed with the Fourier transform; systems are characterised by their impulse and frequency responses, and convolution relates input to output. Sampling theory (Nyquist–Shannon) states that a band-limited signal must be sampled at more than twice its highest frequency to be reconstructed exactly — the basis of digital audio (CD: 44.1 kHz).

Modern topics include adaptive filtering, wavelet analysis, compressed sensing, and statistical signal processing (detection and estimation theory).

Applications

Telecommunications (modems, mobile networks), audio and image/video compression and enhancement, radar and sonar, medical imaging (CT, MRI reconstruction), seismology, and control systems all depend on signal processing.
