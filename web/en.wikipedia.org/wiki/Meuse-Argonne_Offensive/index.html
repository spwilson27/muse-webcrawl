Meuse-Argonne Offensive

Placeholder image: American infantry advancing during the Meuse-Argonne Offensive
Meuse-Argonne Offensive
Part of the Hundred Days Offensive, World War I
Date: September 26 – November 11, 1918
Location: Argonne Forest and Meuse River, France
Result: Allied victory
American commander: Gen. John J. Pershing

The Meuse-Argonne Offensive (also known as the Battle of the Argonne Forest) was a major part of the final Allied offensive of World War I that stretched along the entire Western Front. It was fought from September 26, 1918, until the Armistice of November 11, 1918, a total of 47 days. The offensive was the largest in United States military history, involving 1.2 million American soldiers, and is the second deadliest battle in American history.

Background

Following the successful reduction of the Saint-Mihiel salient in mid-September 1918, General John J. Pershing, commander of the American Expeditionary Forces (AEF), redeployed his First Army to the sector between the Meuse River and the Argonne Forest. Marshal Ferdinand Foch's grand plan for the autumn called for converging attacks toward the vital rail centre of Sedan and the Briey iron basin, which supplied the German war effort. Opposite the Americans stood the German Fifth Army, defending the formidable Hindenburg Line positions anchored on the heights of Montfaucon and the dense, ravine-cut Argonne Forest.

The battle

The attack opened on September 26 after a three-hour artillery preparation by some 2,700 guns. Inexperienced American divisions initially made good progress on the open Meuse heights but bogged down in front of Montfaucon and inside the Argonne, where German machine guns in prepared positions and collapsing logistics on the few available roads caused heavy casualties. Pershing reorganised, bringing in veteran divisions, and a renewed general assault on October 4 began grinding through the Kriemhilde Stellung, the main German defensive line.

During this phase Sergeant Alvin C. York won fame near Chatel-Chéhéry on October 8, and the "Lost Battalion" of the 77th Division was surrounded and held out in the Argonne between October 2 and 8. On October 12 Pershing handed the First Army to Lieutenant General Hunter Liggett and took command of an American army group, with a new Second Army under Robert Lee Bullard. Liggett paused to reorganise before the final assault of November 1, which broke through the German line with a massive artillery barrage. American forces then advanced rapidly toward Sedan, cutting the key railway, while on their left the French Fourth Army under Henri Gouraud advanced in step.

Casualties and legacy

American casualties were about 26,277 killed and 95,786 wounded, making the Meuse-Argonne the deadliest battle in American military history at the time. German losses were estimated at around 126,000. The offensive demonstrated that the AEF had become a decisive mass army, though at great cost in lives attributable in part to inexperience and frontal tactics. The Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery at Romagne-sous-Montfaucon, the largest American military cemetery in Europe, holds the graves of more than 14,000 war dead.
