Hero's journey

Also called: Monomyth
Proposed by: Joseph Campbell (1949)
Key work: The Hero with a Thousand Faces

The hero's journey, or monomyth, is a narrative template identified by mythologist Joseph Campbell in his 1949 book The Hero with a Thousand Faces. It describes a common pattern found in myths worldwide: a hero departs from the ordinary world, undergoes trials and transformation, and returns home changed.

Structure

Campbell divided the journey into three acts: Departure (the call to adventure, refusal of the call, meeting the mentor, crossing the threshold), Initiation (tests, allies and enemies, the ordeal, the reward), and Return (the road back, resurrection, and return with the elixir). Later writers, notably Christopher Vogler, condensed the pattern into twelve stages widely used in screenwriting.

Influence

The monomyth strongly influenced modern storytelling, including George Lucas's Star Wars. It remains a standard framework in film, literature, and game design, though scholars have criticized it as overly reductive and culturally narrow.
