The wine region of Saint-Émilion in Bordeaux, France, maintains a classification of its wines that, unlike the Bordeaux Wine Official Classification of 1855, is revised approximately every ten years. The first classification was published in 1955, and revisions allow estates to be promoted or demoted according to the quality of their recent vintages, terroir, and production standards.

Tiers

Within the Saint-Émilion Grand Cru appellation, classified estates fall into three ranks:

  • Premier grand cru classé A — the highest tier. In the 2022 classification it comprised Château Figeac and Château Pavie.
  • Premier grand cru classé B — the second tier of premiers grands crus classés.
  • Grand cru classé — the third tier.

For much of the classification's history, Château Cheval Blanc and Château Ausone stood alone at the top. Both announced in 2021 that they would not apply for the 2022 classification, and Château Angélus also withdrew, reshaping the top tier.

2006 controversy

The 2006 classification was challenged in court by demoted estates, and in 2007–2009 French administrative courts suspended and annulled parts of it, temporarily reinstating the 1996 classification until the 2012 revision resolved the dispute. The episode led to reforms in the tasting and evaluation process for later classifications.

Evaluation

Classification candidates are judged on blind tastings of multiple vintages, inspection of vineyards and cellars, reputation and market price, and viticultural and winemaking practices. Unclassified Saint-Émilion Grand Cru wines exist in larger numbers below the classified tiers, as the Grand Cru designation itself is an appellation standard rather than the classification.
