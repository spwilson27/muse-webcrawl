Noumenon

In philosophy, a noumenon (plural noumena) is a thing as it is in itself, independent of human sense and perception, contrasted with a phenomenon, a thing as it appears to an observer.

Kant

Immanuel Kant developed the distinction in the Critique of Pure Reason (1781). For Kant, humans can know only phenomena structured by space, time and the categories of understanding; noumena, or things-in-themselves, lie beyond possible experience, though the concept serves as a necessary limit on knowledge claims.

Debate

Later philosophers including Schopenhauer, Hegel and contemporary realists have disputed whether the noumenon is knowable or even coherent.
