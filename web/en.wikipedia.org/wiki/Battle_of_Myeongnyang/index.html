Battle of Myeongnyang

The Battle of Myeongnyang was fought on 26 October 1597 in the Myeongnyang Strait off south-western Korea during the Imjin War. Admiral Yi Sun-sin, with only 13 Korean warships, defeated a Japanese fleet of over 100 warships (from a fleet of some 330 vessels), one of the most remarkable naval victories in history.

Background

After the disaster at Chilcheollyang destroyed most of the Korean navy under Won Gyun, the disgraced Yi Sun-sin was recalled. He gathered the survivors — 13 panokseon — and chose the narrow Myeongnyang Strait, famed for fierce, reversing tidal currents and whirlpools, where numbers would count for little.

Battle

Yi's flagship fought almost alone at first before his captains joined. The strait's current carried disabled Japanese ships into each other and prevented the Japanese from surrounding the Koreans; Korean cannon outranged Japanese weapons, whose strength lay in boarding. When the tide turned, the Japanese withdrew, having lost dozens of ships and commander Kurushima Michifusa.

Aftermath

The victory saved the Korean navy's existence, denied Japan control of the Yellow Sea supply route, and restored morale. Yi followed it with the final victory at Noryang in 1598, where he was killed.
