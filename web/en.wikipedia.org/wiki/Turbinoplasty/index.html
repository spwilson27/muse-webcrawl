Turbinoplasty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

SpecialtyOtorhinolaryngology
IndicationsNasal obstruction from turbinate hypertrophy

Turbinoplasty is a surgical procedure on the nasal turbinates (conchae) intended to reduce their size and improve nasal airflow in patients with chronic nasal obstruction caused by turbinate hypertrophy that has not responded to medical treatment such as intranasal corticosteroids. Unlike total turbinectomy, turbinoplasty aims to preserve the turbinate bone and mucosal function while reducing the bulky submucosal tissue.

Indications

The inferior turbinates most commonly undergo turbinoplasty. Hypertrophy may result from allergic or vasomotor rhinitis, chronic rhinosinusitis, or compensatory enlargement opposite a deviated nasal septum. The operation is frequently performed together with septoplasty.

Techniques

Techniques include submucosal resection of turbinate bone and soft tissue, outfracture of the turbinate, microdebrider-assisted submucosal resection, radiofrequency ablation, coblation, and laser or ultrasound reduction. Endoscopic submucosal turbinoplasty typically involves an incision in the turbinate head, elevation of a mucosal flap, removal of part of the turbinate bone and lateral mucosa, and repositioning of the flap.

Risks and outcomes

Risks include bleeding, crusting, adhesions, recurrence of hypertrophy, and, rarely with aggressive resection, empty nose syndrome, a paradoxical sensation of nasal obstruction. Studies generally report improved nasal breathing and quality of life in appropriately selected patients, with mucosal-sparing techniques preferred to preserve the humidifying and airflow-sensing functions of the turbinates.
