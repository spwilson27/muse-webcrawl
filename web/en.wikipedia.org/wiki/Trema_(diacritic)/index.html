Trema (diacritic)

The trema (also called diaeresis) is a diacritical mark consisting of two dots placed over a letter, most commonly a vowel. Its name comes from the Greek word τρῆμα (trêma), meaning a hole or perforation. Although the trema and the umlaut are written identically (¨), they have different origins and functions, and in modern typographic terminology the two are distinguished.

Diaeresis

As a diaeresis, the mark indicates that the letter beneath it is pronounced separately from an adjacent vowel — that the two vowels form separate syllables rather than a diphthong. Examples include French Noël and naïve, English naïve and coöperate (a use once common in The New Yorker), and Catalan diürn. In Spanish, the diaeresis over u (ü) shows that the u is pronounced in the combinations güe and güi, as in pingüino.

Umlaut

In German and several other languages, two dots mark the umlaut — a fronted or mutated vowel sound — in letters such as ä, ö and ü. In German these letters are treated as variants of a/o/u (or expanded to ae/oe/ue when the character is unavailable) rather than as separate letters of the alphabet.

Usage in other languages

Two-dot letters are also independent letters in languages such as Swedish and Finnish (ä, ö), it appears in Dutch for diaeresis (België), and in transliteration systems for various languages. In Unicode, precomposed characters exist for the common combinations, alongside the combining diaeresis U+0308.
