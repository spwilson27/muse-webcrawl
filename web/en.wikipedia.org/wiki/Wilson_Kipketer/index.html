Wilson Kosgei Kipketer (born 12 December 1972) is a Kenyan-born Danish former middle-distance runner who dominated the men's 800 metres in the 1990s. He won three consecutive World Championship titles (1995, 1997, 1999), set the 800 m world record three times in 1997 — his best of 1:41.11 stood for thirteen years — and won Olympic silver in 2000 and bronze in 2004.

Early life and move to Denmark

Kipketer was born in Kapchemoiywo in Kenya's Rift Valley and was talent-spotted at school by the 1968 Olympic champion Kipchoge Keino. In 1990 he travelled to Denmark as an exchange student to study electronic engineering in Copenhagen and settled there, eventually obtaining Danish citizenship in 1996. He famously ran barefoot in his youth on Kenyan soil before taking up spikes.

Career

Kipketer broke through in 1995, winning the World Championship in Gothenburg. Because he was not yet a Danish citizen, he was controversially barred from the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, where he would have been the overwhelming favourite; the gold medal went to Norway's Vebjørn Rodal.

In the summer of 1997 Kipketer equalled and then broke Sebastian Coe's long-standing world record, running 1:41.24 in Zurich and then 1:41.11 in Cologne — a time that stood as the world record until David Rudisha broke it in 2010. He also held the indoor 800 m world record of 1:42.67, set in 1997. In 1998 he fell ill with malaria after a visit to Kenya, which interrupted his career, but he returned to win a third world title in Seville in 1999.

At the Sydney 2000 Olympics Kipketer was beaten in a tactical final by Germany's Nils Schumann and took silver; he won bronze at Athens 2004 before retiring in 2005.

Legacy

Kipketer is regarded as one of the greatest 800 m runners in history. After retirement he has worked as a coach, an athletics official with World Athletics and the Monaco-based Peace and Sport organisation, and a member of the International Olympic Committee.
