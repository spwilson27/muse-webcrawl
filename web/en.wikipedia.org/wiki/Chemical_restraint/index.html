Chemical restraint

A chemical restraint is a form of medical restraint in which a drug is used to restrict the freedom or movement of a person, or to sedate them, usually in emergency psychiatric situations. Commonly used agents include benzodiazepines, such as lorazepam or midazolam, and antipsychotics, such as haloperidol.

Use

Chemical restraints are used in emergency departments and psychiatric facilities to manage acute agitation and aggression that poses a risk to the patient or others. Their use is regulated in many jurisdictions and is controversial, particularly in long-term care settings and in the management of people with dementia, where sedative medication given for staff convenience rather than patient benefit is discouraged or prohibited.

Risks

Adverse effects can include oversedation, respiratory depression, extrapyramidal symptoms, and paradoxical disinhibition. Guidelines generally call for the least restrictive intervention and for de-escalation techniques to be attempted first.
