World Chess Champion

A World Chess Champion is the winner of the World Chess Championship, the title generally regarded as the highest honour in the game of chess. The championship is decided in a match between the reigning champion and a challenger who emerges from a qualification cycle run by FIDE, the world chess federation.

History

The first generally recognised world championship match was played in 1886 between Wilhelm Steinitz and Johannes Zukertort, which Steinitz won. Steinitz's successors as undisputed champion were Emanuel Lasker, José Raúl Capablanca, Alexander Alekhine, Max Euwe, and then the Soviet champions beginning with Mikhail Botvinnik in 1948.

From 1948 the championship was administered by FIDE. Champions of the post-war era included Mikhail Tal, Tigran Petrosian, Boris Spassky, Bobby Fischer, Anatoly Karpov and Garry Kasparov. In 1993 Kasparov and challenger Nigel Short broke away from FIDE to play under the Professional Chess Association, splitting the title for thirteen years. The titles were reunified in 2006 when Vladimir Kramnik defeated Veselin Topalov. Subsequent champions have included Viswanathan Anand, Magnus Carlsen, Ding Liren and Gukesh Dommaraju.

Qualification

The challenger is determined through the Candidates Tournament, an eight-player event whose participants qualify via the FIDE World Cup, the FIDE Grand Swiss, the FIDE Circuit and rating. The championship match is typically played over 14 classical games, with rapid and blitz tiebreaks if the score is level.
