Primo Michele Levi (31 July 1919 – 11 April 1987) was an Italian chemist, partisan, writer, and Holocaust survivor. He is best known for his account of a year spent as a prisoner in the Auschwitz concentration camp, If This Is a Man (1947; published in the US as Survival in Auschwitz), widely regarded as one of the most important works of the twentieth century. His books combine scientific precision with profound moral reflection on human dignity, memory, and responsibility.

Life

Levi was born in Turin to a liberal Jewish family and trained as a chemist at the University of Turin, graduating in 1941 despite the racial laws restricting Jewish students. In 1943 he joined a partisan group in the Italian resistance; he was captured by Fascist militia in December 1943 and, having admitted to being Jewish, was deported to Auschwitz in February 1944. His chemistry qualifications helped secure work in the IG Farben Buna laboratory at Monowitz, and an illness in the camp infirmary spared him the death march when the camp was evacuated in January 1945. Liberated by the Red Army, he returned to Turin after a long detour recounted in The Truce (1963).

Writing

Levi worked as an industrial chemist (at the SIVA paint factory) until 1977 while writing. If This Is a Man initially sold modestly but gained a wide readership after its 1958 Einaudi reissue. The Periodic Table (1975), a collection of autobiographical stories each named after a chemical element, brought international fame; the Royal Institution later named it the best science book ever written. His final book, The Drowned and the Saved (1986), examined the "grey zone" of moral compromise in the camps, survivor guilt, and the duty of memory. Levi died on 11 April 1987 after falling from the stairwell of his Turin apartment building; the death is generally regarded as suicide, though some have argued it may have been accidental.
