Shareholders' equity
Also called: Stockholders' equity, owners' equity
Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Shareholders' equity (or stockholders' equity) is the residual interest in the assets of a company after deducting its liabilities. It represents the amount that would be returned to shareholders if all assets were liquidated and all debts repaid. On the balance sheet it follows directly from the accounting equation: Equity = Assets − Liabilities.

Components

Shareholders' equity typically comprises share capital (the par or stated value of issued shares), additional paid-in capital (amounts paid above par), retained earnings (accumulated profits not distributed as dividends), accumulated other comprehensive income, and treasury stock (repurchased shares, shown as a deduction).

Statement of changes in equity

Companies present a statement of changes in equity reconciling opening and closing balances, showing profit or loss for the period, other comprehensive income, dividends declared, share issues and buybacks, and other owner transactions.

Book value

Dividing total shareholders' equity by the number of outstanding shares gives book value per share. A negative equity balance can indicate insolvency on a balance-sheet basis, though market value may remain positive if investors expect future profits.

Related concepts

For sole proprietorships and partnerships the equivalent is owners' equity or partners' capital. Return on equity (ROE), net income divided by average shareholders' equity, is a widely used measure of profitability relative to owners' investment.
