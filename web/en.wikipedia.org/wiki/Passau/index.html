Passau

Placeholder image

Passau is a city in Lower Bavaria, Germany, on the border with Austria. It is often called the "City of Three Rivers" (Dreiflüssestadt) because the Danube is joined at Passau by the Inn from the south and the Ilz from the north. It has a population of around 50,000.

History

The site was first a Celtic settlement known as Boiodurum, later a Roman fort. The Treaty of Passau was signed here in 1552, an agreement that paved the way for religious peace in the Holy Roman Empire. The city was a prince-bishopric for many centuries, and much of its old town was rebuilt in an Italian Baroque style after a devastating fire in 1662.

Landmarks

St. Stephen's Cathedral (Stephansdom) dominates the old town and houses one of the largest church organs in the world, with some 17,000 pipes. The Veste Oberhaus fortress, founded in 1219 by the prince-bishops, overlooks the city from a hill between the Danube and the Ilz.

Passau is a starting point for river cruises on the Danube and a popular stop for cyclists following the Danube cycle path.
