Three Sisters (New South Wales)

PeaksMeehni (922 m), Wimlah (918 m), Gunnedoo (906 m)
Rock typeSandstone
ParkBlue Mountains National Park

The Three Sisters seen from Echo Point, Katoomba.

The Three Sisters are an unusual rock formation in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, on the north escarpment of the Jamison Valley near Katoomba. The three weathered sandstone peaks, named Meehni, Wimlah and Gunnedoo, stand just over 900 metres above sea level and are among the most famous landmarks in the region, viewed by millions of visitors from Echo Point. They are part of the Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area.

Formation

The Blue Mountains are a deeply dissected sandstone plateau. The Three Sisters were carved by erosion: wind, rain and running water attacked softer layers of the cliff line and wore away the rock around them, leaving three pinnacles of the more resistant sandstone. The process continues, and the Sisters will eventually collapse into the valley; one of the peaks has already lost height to erosion in the relatively recent past.

Aboriginal legend

A widely told Gundungurra Dreaming story explains the formation. Three beautiful sisters of the Katoomba tribe — Meehni, Wimlah and Gunnedoo — fell in love with three brothers of the neighbouring Nepean tribe, but tribal law forbade the marriages. The brothers resolved to take the sisters by force, provoking a battle. To protect the sisters, the witchdoctor Tyawan turned them into stone, intending to reverse the spell when the danger passed; but Tyawan was killed in the fighting, and since he alone knew how to lift the spell, the sisters remain as stone. The formation is a place of cultural significance to the Gundungurra people, and climbing the Sisters is discouraged out of respect.

Tourism

Echo Point lookout, a short walk from Katoomba, provides the classic view, and floodlighting at night shows the peaks above the dark valley. A walking track leads to the Giant Stairway, a steep staircase of more than 800 steps descending past the first Sister into the Jamison Valley, linking with the Federal Pass track at the valley floor.
