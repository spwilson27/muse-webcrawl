Te Aute College is a boys' boarding school at Pukehou, near Hastings in the Hawke's Bay region of New Zealand. Founded in 1854 with land gifted by local Māori chiefs, it became one of the most influential Māori schools in the country.

History

Under headmaster John Thornton in the late 19th century, Te Aute produced an extraordinary generation of Māori leaders — including Āpirana Ngata, Te Rangi Hīroa (Peter Buck) and Māui Pōmare — who formed the Young Māori Party and transformed Māori politics and scholarship. The school emphasised academic education alongside agriculture and Māori language and culture.

Today

Te Aute remains a state-integrated Anglican school with a strong emphasis on Māori identity, te reo Māori, and rugby, and continues to educate boys from across New Zealand.
