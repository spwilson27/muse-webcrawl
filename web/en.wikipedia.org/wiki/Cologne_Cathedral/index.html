Cologne Cathedral (German: Kölner Dom, officially Hohe Domkirche St. Peter und Maria) is a Catholic cathedral in Cologne, Germany. It is the seat of the Archbishop of Cologne and the largest Gothic church in Northern Europe. Construction began in 1248 but was halted in 1560 unfinished; work resumed in 1842 to the original medieval plan and was completed in 1880. Its twin spires made it the tallest building in the world from 1880 to 1884. The cathedral houses the Shrine of the Three Kings, believed to contain the relics of the Biblical Magi.

History

The site, near the Rhine, has held churches since the 4th century. In 1164 the relics of the Three Kings were brought from Milan, making Cologne a major pilgrimage centre and prompting Archbishop Konrad von Hochstaden to lay the foundation stone of a vast new Gothic cathedral in 1248, modelled on Amiens and Paris's Sainte-Chapelle. The choir was consecrated in 1322, but construction slowed and stopped in the 16th century, leaving the south tower capped by a great medieval crane that became a city landmark for centuries.

Completion and modern era

Romantic-era enthusiasm for the Middle Ages, plus the rediscovery of the original façade drawing, revived the project; Prussian funding enabled completion between 1842 and 1880, celebrated in the presence of Kaiser Wilhelm I. At completion the cathedral was the world's tallest structure. It survived Allied bombing in the Second World War — hit by some 70 bombs — though surrounding Cologne was devastated; its intact spires served as a navigational landmark. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1996, and it draws around six million visitors a year.

Treasures

  • Shrine of the Three Kings — a monumental gilt reliquary (c. 1190–1225), the largest in the Western world.
  • Gero Crucifix (c. 970) — among the oldest large crucifixes north of the Alps.
  • Milan Madonna and extensive medieval stained glass, including the modern Richter window (2007).

Architecture

The cruciform basilica has a five-aisled nave 144.5 m long, a projecting transept, and an ambulatory with seven radiating chapels. The twin spires rise to 157 m, and its choir has the largest height-to-width ratio of any medieval church.
