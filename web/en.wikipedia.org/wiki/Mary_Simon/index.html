Mary Jeannie May Simon (born 21 August 1947) is a Canadian civil servant, diplomat, and former broadcaster who has served as the 30th governor general of Canada since 26 July 2021. Simon is Inuk, making her the first Indigenous person to hold the office, and the first to serve as the Canadian viceregal representative.

Mary Simon, the first Indigenous governor general of Canada.

Early life and career

Simon was born in Kangiqsualujjuaq (then Fort George) in northern Quebec, to an Inuk mother and a father who managed a Hudson's Bay Company post. She began her career as a radio broadcaster with CBC Northern Service in the 1970s, then moved into leadership roles in Inuit organisations: she served on the board of the Northern Quebec Inuit Association, helped negotiate the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement (1975), and was an executive of the Inuit Circumpolar Council, contributing to its international work.

Diplomatic service

From 1994 to 2004 Simon was Canada's first Ambassador for Circumpolar Affairs, in which role she was a key figure in the creation of the Arctic Council (1996). She then served as Canada's ambassador to Denmark (1999–2002, concurrently). She was president of Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami, the national Inuit organisation, from 2006 to 2012, and played a central role in negotiating the implementation of the Nunavik Inuit Land Claims Agreement.

Governor General

Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced Simon's appointment on 6 July 2021, following the resignation of Julie Payette. She was installed on 26 July 2021, delivering her installation speech in Inuktitut, English and French. As governor general she is the representative of the Canadian monarch, Charles III, and commander-in-chief of the Canadian Armed Forces. Her tenure has included the first royal tour of Charles III as King of Canada and an emphasis on reconciliation with Indigenous peoples.
