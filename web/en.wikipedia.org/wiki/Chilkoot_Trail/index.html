Chilkoot Trail

The Chilkoot Trail is a 33-mile (53 km) trail through the Coast Mountains that leads from Dyea, Alaska, in the United States, to Bennett Lake, British Columbia, in Canada. It was a major access route from the coast to the Yukon interior during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897–1899 and is often called the "meanest 33 miles in history".

History

The route was long used by the Tlingit people as a trade route — the "grease trail" over which eulachon oil and other coastal goods were traded to interior peoples. During the Klondike Gold Rush, tens of thousands of prospectors ("stampeders") climbed the trail, most famously the steep final ascent to Chilkoot Pass known as the "Golden Stairs", some 1,500 steps cut into snow and ice. The North-West Mounted Police required each stampeder to bring a year's supply of food and gear — roughly a ton of goods — which most carried over the pass in repeated trips.

An avalanche on Palm Sunday, 3 April 1898, killed more than 60 people on the trail. Aerial tramways built in 1897–98 eased the climb, and the completion of the White Pass and Yukon Route railway in 1900 ended the trail's role as a main route.

Today

The trail is jointly managed by Parks Canada and the U.S. National Park Service as the Chilkoot Trail National Historic Site of Canada and part of Klondike Gold Rush National Historical Park. It is a popular multi-day hike, with permits required during the summer season.
