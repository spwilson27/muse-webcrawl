Gelawdewos (Geʽez: ገላውዲዎስ, c. 1521/1522 – 23 March 1559), also known by his throne name Asnaf Sagad I, was Emperor of Ethiopia from 1540 until his death. A member of the Solomonic dynasty and a son of Lebna Dengel, his reign was dominated by the aftermath of the Ethiopian–Adal war and by conflict with the Ottoman-backed forces of the Adal Sultanate.

Reign

Gelawdewos came to the throne during the invasion of Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi (Ahmad Gragn), which had devastated the Christian highlands. With the help of a small Portuguese expedition under Cristóvão da Gama, Ethiopian forces defeated and killed Ahmad at the Battle of Wayna Daga in February 1543, turning the tide of the war. Gelawdewos then campaigned to restore imperial authority over regions that had broken away during the invasion.

Relations with the Portuguese Jesuits

The emperor resisted Portuguese pressure to bring Ethiopia into communion with Rome. He is credited with the Confession of Claudius (1555), a statement of Ethiopian Orthodox doctrine addressed to the Jesuit missionaries, defending practices such as circumcision, observance of the Sabbath, and the Ethiopian canon. His resistance set the pattern for later Ethiopian suspicion of Catholic missions.

Death

Gelawdewos was killed in battle on 23 March 1559 fighting Nur ibn Mujahid, the emir of Harar, who invaded Ethiopia in revenge for Ahmad's death. His head was taken to Harar, and he was succeeded by his brother Menas.
