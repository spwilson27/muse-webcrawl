Markos Vamvakaris (Greek: Μάρκος Βαμβακάρης; 10 May 1905 – 8 February 1972) was a Greek musician, singer and songwriter, widely regarded as the founding figure and "patriarch" of rebetiko, the urban Greek folk music associated with the port city subculture of Piraeus. A virtuoso of the bouzouki, he led the celebrated Piraeus Quartet (Tetras) and composed songs that became standards of the genre.

Life

Vamvakaris was born in Ano Syros, on the island of Syros, into a Roman Catholic family of peasants and musicians. He left school young and in 1917 moved to Piraeus, where he worked in a succession of manual jobs — including in a slaughterhouse — while frequenting the tekédes (hashish dens) and tavernas where rebetiko musicians played. He taught himself bouzouki and by the late 1920s was performing professionally.

In 1934 he formed the famous Piraeus Quartet with Giorgos Batis, Anestis Delias and Stratos Pagioumtzis, whose performances at his own club in Piraeus did much to popularise rebetiko. His recording career began in 1932–33; he is often credited as the first rebetiko artist to record with bouzouki. The censorship imposed by the Metaxas regime from 1936 restricted the genre's hashish- and underworld-themed repertoire.

Later years and legacy

Vamvakaris's popularity declined after the Second World War as musical tastes changed, and he lived in poverty for a period. A revival of interest in rebetiko in the 1960s, championed by younger composers and scholars, restored his reputation and income; his songs were re-recorded by popular singers, and he was recognised as a central figure of Greek music. He died in Athens in 1972.

Notable songs

  • "Frangosyriani" (Φραγκοσυριανή, "The Girl from Syros") — among the best-known rebetiko songs
  • "Ta matoklada sou lampoun" ("Your Eyelashes Shine")
  • "Osoi ehoun polla lefta" ("Those Who Have Much Money")
  • "I foni tou argile" ("The Voice of the Hookah")
