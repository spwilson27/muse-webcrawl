Philo Farnsworth

Philo Taylor Farnsworth
Born: 19 August 1906, Beaver, Utah, US
Died: 11 March 1971 (aged 64), Salt Lake City, Utah, US
Occupation: Inventor
Known for: First fully electronic television system; image dissector; fusor

Philo Taylor Farnsworth (19 August 1906 – 11 March 1971) was an American inventor best known for creating the first fully functional, all-electronic image pickup device (the "image dissector") and the first complete electronic television system. He developed television as a teenager and later worked on nuclear fusion with his "fusor" device.

Early life and the idea of television

Farnsworth was born in a log cabin near Beaver, Utah, and raised in Idaho. A scientifically gifted schoolboy, he later said the idea for scanning images line by line — inspired by the back-and-forth pattern of ploughing a field — came to him at age 14 or 15; his high-school chemistry teacher, Justin Tolman, preserved a sketch Farnsworth drew in 1922 that later proved decisive in patent litigation.

Electronic television

Backed by San Francisco investors, Farnsworth made his first successful electronic television transmission on 7 September 1927, sending the image of a straight line with his image dissector camera tube. He demonstrated the system publicly in 1928. His priority was challenged by RCA under Vladimir Zworykin, but after a long patent interference the US Patent Office ruled in Farnsworth's favour in 1935, and RCA eventually took a licence — one of the rare occasions on which RCA paid royalties to an outside inventor.

Later work and death

Farnsworth's company struggled commercially, and after the Second World War he turned to research on radar, the electron microscope and especially nuclear fusion, developing the Farnsworth–Hirsch fusor, a small inertial-electrostatic confinement device now used as a neutron source. He died of pneumonia in Salt Lake City in 1971, comparatively obscure; his contributions were recognised posthumously, including induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame and a US postage stamp.
