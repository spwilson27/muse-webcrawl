Daytime Emmy Awards

The Daytime Emmy Awards are an American accolade bestowed by the National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences (NATAS) in recognition of excellence in American daytime television programming. They are one of the major categories of the Emmy Awards, alongside the Primetime, Sports, News and Documentary, and Children's and Family Emmys.

The Emmy statuette, awarded across daytime drama, talk, game and children's programming.

History

Daytime programming was first honoured at the Primetime Emmy ceremonies of the early 1970s, but as the genre grew the awards were spun off into their own ceremony. The first standalone Daytime Emmy Awards were held in 1974 in New York City, hosted by Barbara Walters and Peter Marshall. For many years the televised ceremony centred on soap operas, talk shows and game shows, with figures such as Oprah Winfrey, Bob Barker and Dick Clark receiving lifetime achievement honours.

Categories and changes

Major categories include Outstanding Drama Series, Outstanding Talk Series, Outstanding Game Show, and lead and supporting acting awards in drama series; performers such as Susan Lucci, who won on her 19th nomination in 1999, became emblematic of the ceremony. As network daytime schedules shrank and programming moved online, the awards expanded to digital drama categories. In 2022, children's programming was separated from the Daytime Emmys into the new Children's and Family Emmy Awards.
