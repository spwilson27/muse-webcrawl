London Marathon

London Marathon
Location: London, England
First held: 29 March 1981
Distance: 26.2 miles (42.195 km)
Primary sponsor: Tata Consultancy Services

The London Marathon (presently known as the TCS London Marathon) is an annual marathon held in London, England. It is the largest annual fundraising event in the world, with participants raising over £1 billion for good causes since the first event in 1981. The race is part of the World Marathon Majors and is traditionally held in April, though the 2020–2022 editions were affected by the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The event was founded by former Olympic champion Chris Brasher and athlete John Disley, inspired by the New York City Marathon. The first London Marathon took place on 29 March 1981 with 7,747 entrants, of whom 6,255 finished. Since then the field has grown to more than 40,000 finishers, with a public ballot attracting hundreds of thousands of applications.

Course

The mostly flat course starts in Blackheath and Greenwich, loops through south-east London, crosses the River Thames at Tower Bridge, passes through the Docklands and Canary Wharf, then turns west along the Embankment before finishing on The Mall in front of Buckingham Palace.

Records and notable winners

The course record for men is held by Kelvin Kiptum of Kenya, who ran 2:01:25 in 2023 — the second-fastest marathon time in history at that point. Paula Radcliffe's women's world record of 2:15:25, set in London in 2003, stood for 16 years. Eliud Kipchoge won the men's race a record four times. The event also includes elite wheelchair races and a mass-participation field, many running in costume for charity.

Charity

The London Marathon is the world's largest annual one-day fundraising event; in recent editions runners have raised more than £60 million in a single year for charitable causes.
