Deor

"Deor" (also "Deor's Lament") is an Old English poem found in the late-10th-century Exeter Book. It is a lament in which the speaker, Deor, a scop (poet) who has lost his position and patron, consoles himself by recalling the misfortunes of legendary figures from Germanic heroic tradition — each stanza ending with the refrain Þæs ofereode, þisses swa mæg ("That passed away, so may this").

Content

The poem's stanzas allude to Weland the smith, held captive and hamstrung by King Niðhad; to Beadohild, grieving her brothers' deaths and her own pregnancy; to the lovers Mæðhild and Geat; to Theodoric the Ostrogoth; and to Ermanaric, the tyrannical Gothic king. In the final stanza Deor applies the refrain to himself: he once served the lord of the Heodenings in prosperity, but has been displaced by the rival poet Heorrenda, and hopes his own trouble, like theirs, will pass.

Form and interpretation

"Deor" is unusual among Old English poems for its stanzaic structure and refrain. Scholars have read it as a poem of consolation (a consolatio in the Boethian tradition), a begging poem seeking a new patron, or a meditation on fortune's mutability. The allusions assume an audience well versed in Germanic legend, several episodes of which are otherwise lost.
