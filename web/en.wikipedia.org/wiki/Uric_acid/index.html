Uric acid

FormulaC5H4N4O3
Molar mass168.11 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystals

Uric acid is a heterocyclic compound of carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, and hydrogen with the formula C5H4N4O3. It forms ions and salts known as urates and acid urates, such as ammonium acid urate. Uric acid is a product of the metabolic breakdown of purine nucleotides, and is a normal component of urine. High levels of uric acid in the blood are called hyperuricemia and can lead to gout and are associated with other medical conditions, including diabetes and kidney stone formation.

Biology

In humans and other great apes, uric acid is the final product of purine catabolism, produced by the enzyme xanthine oxidase, because the gene for urate oxidase (uricase), which in most other mammals breaks uric acid down into soluble allantoin, was lost to mutation during primate evolution. Birds and reptiles excrete nitrogenous waste mainly as uric acid — a water-conserving adaptation, since the nearly insoluble acid can be excreted as a paste with little water loss.

Clinical significance

When uric acid accumulates and exceeds its solubility in blood, monosodium urate crystals can deposit in joints and tissues, provoking the intensely painful inflammatory arthritis known as gout. Management includes dietary changes and drugs that either reduce production (xanthine oxidase inhibitors such as allopurinol and febuxostat) or increase excretion. Uric acid also acts as an antioxidant in plasma, scavenging free radicals, a role proposed to partly compensate for the loss of endogenous vitamin C synthesis in primates.
