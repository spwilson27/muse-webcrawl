Processing fluency

Processing fluency is the subjective experience of ease with which people process information. Fluency is a metacognitive cue: people draw on how easily something comes to mind or is perceived when making judgments about it. High processing fluency typically evokes positive affect and is misattributed to qualities of the stimulus itself, such as its familiarity, truth, or beauty.


Text in an easy-to-read font is judged more favourably.

Types

Researchers distinguish several kinds of fluency. Perceptual fluency concerns the ease of identifying a stimulus (affected by clarity, contrast, and prior exposure); conceptual fluency concerns the ease of processing its meaning; and retrieval fluency concerns the ease with which information comes to mind. Repetition, clear presentation, and predictable structure all increase fluency.

Effects

Fluency influences many judgments. Repeated statements are judged more true (the illusory truth effect); easy-to-pronounce names and stock ticker symbols are evaluated more positively; rhyming aphorisms are judged more accurate ("rhyme-as-reason"); and statements in clear fonts are judged more credible. Conversely, deliberately induced disfluency — for example, presenting material in a hard-to-read font — can prompt more analytical thinking in some experimental settings, though the robustness of this effect has been debated.
