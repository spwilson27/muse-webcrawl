Benzoquinone (C₆H₄O₂) is a quinone with a single benzene ring. Two isomers are known: 1,4-benzoquinone, commonly called simply benzoquinone, p-benzoquinone or quinone, and 1,2-benzoquinone (o-benzoquinone), which is less stable and less commonly encountered.

1,4-Benzoquinone

1,4-Benzoquinone is a bright yellow solid with a characteristic irritating odour resembling chlorine and ozone. It is a cyclic conjugated diketone: the six-membered ring contains two carbonyl groups opposite one another and two carbon–carbon double bonds. It sublimes readily and is volatile with steam.

Production and preparation

Industrially, 1,4-benzoquinone is produced by the oxidation of hydroquinone or by routes from aniline or phenol. In the laboratory it is classically prepared by oxidising hydroquinone with reagents such as sodium chlorate and sulfuric acid, or by oxidising aniline with manganese dioxide or chromic acid.

Chemistry

Benzoquinone is an oxidising agent and a versatile dienophile in Diels–Alder reactions. It is reversibly reduced to hydroquinone; the quinone/hydroquinone couple is a classic reversible redox system, exploited historically in the quinhydrone electrode for measuring pH. With hydroquinone it forms the dark green 1:1 charge-transfer complex quinhydrone. Quinones undergo addition reactions with nucleophiles, and the parent compound readily forms derivatives such as chloranil (tetrachloro-1,4-benzoquinone), a stronger oxidant.

Applications and occurrence

1,4-Benzoquinone is used as an intermediate in the manufacture of hydroquinone (a photographic developer and polymerisation inhibitor), as an oxidant in organic synthesis, and as a tanning and dyeing agent. Quinone structures occur widely in nature — in coenzyme Q, vitamin K and many pigments — and bombardier beetles spray hot benzoquinones as a defensive secretion. The compound is toxic and irritating to skin, eyes and respiratory tract.

1,2-Benzoquinone

The ortho isomer is a red, unstable solid prepared by oxidising catechol, for example with silver oxide. It is a reactive heterodienophile and is mainly of theoretical and mechanistic interest; enzyme-generated o-quinones are important intermediates in melanin formation and the browning of cut fruit.
