Email address

An email address identifies an email box to which messages are delivered. The format of an email address is local-part@domain, where the local part may be up to 64 octets and the domain may have a maximum of 255 octets. The modern Internet email address format was standardized in RFC 5322 and related documents.

Syntax

The local part identifies a specific mailbox within a domain and is technically case-sensitive, although most providers treat it as case-insensitive. It may contain letters, digits, and certain special characters, or be a quoted string containing spaces and other characters. The domain part follows the rules of hostnames: a series of labels separated by dots, such as example.com. Domains may also be written as address literals in brackets.

History

The use of the @ sign to separate the user from the host was introduced by Ray Tomlinson in 1971 for networked email on the ARPANET. Earlier systems had used addresses internal to a single timesharing machine.

Validation

Validating email addresses is notoriously subtle: the full syntax permits comments, quoted strings, and internationalized characters, while many real-world systems impose stricter limits. Disposable addresses, plus-addressing (subaddressing), and catch-all domains complicate verification further.
