Oliver Loving (December 4, 1812 – September 25, 1867) was an American rancher and cattle drover. Together with Charles Goodnight, he developed the Goodnight–Loving Trail, one of the most heavily used cattle trails of the American West. His death at the hands of Comanche raiders and his dying wish to be buried in Texas inspired the plot of Larry McMurtry's Pulitzer Prize-winning novel Lonesome Dove.

Early life

Loving was born in Kentucky and moved to Texas in 1843, settling in the Peters Colony area and eventually acquiring land in Palo Pinto and Parker counties. By the 1850s he was running cattle and freighting goods, and in 1860 he drove a herd overland to Colorado — one of the first Texas cattle drives to the mining regions.

The Goodnight–Loving Trail

During the Civil War, Loving supplied beef to the Confederacy. After the war, the Texas cattle market was depressed while demand was high at western forts and reservations. In 1866 Loving partnered with the younger Charles Goodnight, and together they blazed a trail from Fort Belknap, Texas, southwest to the Pecos River, then north to Fort Sumner, New Mexico, where they sold cattle to the government. The route avoided hostile territory on the more direct northern trails and was soon extended toward Colorado and Wyoming, where it fed into the wider trail network.

Death

In the summer of 1867, on a drive up the trail, Loving rode ahead with scout Bill Wilson to arrange contracts at Fort Sumner. Near the Pecos River the two were attacked by Comanches; Loving was wounded and Wilson escaped to summon help. Loving hid along the river for days, subsisting on raw fish, before being rescued, but gangrene had set in. A leg was amputated at Fort Sumner, and he died there on September 25, 1867. As he had wished, Goodnight later had his body exhumed and carried back to Texas for burial in Weatherford's Greenwood Cemetery.

Legacy

Loving County, Texas, and the town of Loving, New Mexico, are named for him. The character Augustus "Gus" McCrae in Lonesome Dove was modelled on Loving, and Goodnight on Captain Woodrow Call; McCrae's death and the hauling of his body back to Texas closely mirror Loving's story.
