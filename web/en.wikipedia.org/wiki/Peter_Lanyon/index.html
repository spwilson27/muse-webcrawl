George Peter Lanyon (8 February 1918 – 31 August 1964) was a British painter of landscapes leaning heavily towards abstraction. Lanyon was one of the most important artists to emerge from the St Ives School of art, and his work — abstracted from the Cornish landscape he intimately knew — combined European modernism and American abstract expressionism with a distinctive sense of place.

Life

Born in St Ives, Cornwall, Lanyon took lessons from Borlase Smart and later studied briefly at the Euston Road School in London before serving in the Royal Air Force during the Second World War. After the war he became a central figure among the St Ives artists, associating with Ben Nicholson, Barbara Hepworth and Naum Gabo, though his work increasingly drew on direct bodily experience of the landscape: walking, climbing, and later gliding over the Cornish coast.

Work

Lanyon's mature paintings of the 1950s and early 1960s, such as Porthole and Thermal (1960), evoke weather, cliffs, mines and sea through sweeping gestural brushwork. He exhibited internationally, including a successful show in New York in 1957, and his reputation grew steadily. He died in 1964, aged 46, from injuries sustained in a gliding accident.

Legacy

Lanyon is now regarded as a major figure in post-war British art. A major retrospective was held at Tate St Ives in 2010–11, and his works are held by public collections including Tate and the Arts Council Collection.
