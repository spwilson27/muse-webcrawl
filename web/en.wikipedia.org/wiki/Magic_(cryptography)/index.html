Magic (cryptography)

Magic was an Allied cryptonym for decrypted Japanese diplomatic communications during the Second World War. The information was gathered through the interception and decryption of messages sent in the Purple cipher, the Japanese Foreign Office's most secure diplomatic system, by the United States Army's Signal Intelligence Service (SIS) and the United States Navy's codebreaking organization, OP-20-G.

Background

The Japanese "Purple" system (known to its operators as the Type 97 cipher machine, or 97-shiki ōbun injiki in Japanese) was an electromechanical stepping-switch system used by the Foreign Ministry for its most sensitive traffic with major embassies, including Berlin, Moscow, and Washington. American cryptanalysts under William Friedman, and in particular Frank Rowlett and Leo Rosen, succeeded in reconstructing the machine's logic without ever seeing an actual device; by September 1940 the Americans were reading Purple traffic currently.

Distribution and secrecy

Magic material was among the most tightly held secrets of the war. Distribution was restricted to a small circle of senior officials in Washington, including the President, the Secretaries of State, War, and the Navy, and top military staff. Decrypts were delivered by courier, read in the recipient's presence, and returned. Remarkably, the Japanese never realized their cipher had been broken, attributing leaks to espionage or carelessness.

Role in the war

Magic decrypts provided advance insight into Japanese diplomatic intentions before and during the war, including the breakdown of negotiations with the United States in late 1941, though they did not reveal the specific target of the attack on Pearl Harbor. Magic also supplied intelligence on German strategy through the reports of the Japanese ambassador in Berlin, Hiroshi Ōshima, whose detailed accounts of meetings with Adolf Hitler and of German defenses in France were read by Allied leaders and proved especially valuable in the lead-up to the Normandy landings.

Controversy

After the war, Magic became a focus of congressional investigations into the Pearl Harbor disaster, as critics questioned whether Washington officials who read the decrypts should have foreseen the attack. The public disclosure of the decrypts in the 1945–46 hearings revealed the success to the world for the first time.
