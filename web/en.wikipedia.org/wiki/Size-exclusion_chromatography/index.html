Size-exclusion chromatography

Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) is a chromatographic method in which molecules in solution are separated by their size, and in some cases molecular weight. It is usually applied to large molecules or macromolecular complexes such as proteins and industrial polymers. When an aqueous solution is used, the technique is known as gel-filtration chromatography; when an organic solvent is used, it is called gel permeation chromatography (GPC).

Unlike other forms of chromatography, separation does not depend on binding to the stationary phase. A porous gel allows smaller molecules to enter its pores and be delayed, while larger molecules are excluded and elute first. SEC is widely used for desalting, buffer exchange, and estimating the molecular weight of proteins.

Principle

The column is packed with beads containing pores of controlled size. Molecules larger than the pores travel only through the spaces between beads and emerge in the void volume; progressively smaller molecules penetrate further into the pore volume and elute later. A calibration curve of standards relates elution volume to molecular size.

Applications

Applications include purification of proteins and antibodies, analysis of polymer molecular-weight distributions, and quality control in biotechnology.
