Utrecht University


The University Hall, Dom Square

Utrecht University (Dutch: Universiteit Utrecht) is a public research university in Utrecht, the Netherlands, founded on 26 March 1636. It is one of the oldest and largest Dutch universities, with roughly 39,000 students and seven faculties: Humanities; Social and Behavioural Sciences; Law, Economics and Governance; Geosciences; Science; Medicine; and Veterinary Medicine — the only veterinary faculty in the Netherlands.

History

The university grew from Utrecht's Illustrious School, elevated to university status in 1636; the philosopher René Descartes lived in Utrecht during its early years, and the theologian Gisbertus Voetius was its first rector. It was briefly demoted under Napoleon but restored in 1815. In 1936 the university celebrated its tercentenary; the modern campus at Utrecht Science Park (De Uithof) now houses most faculties alongside the University Medical Center Utrecht.

Research and notable people

Utrecht consistently ranks among Europe's top research universities. Affiliated Nobel laureates include Wilhelm Röntgen, Jacobus van 't Hoff (the first Nobel laureate in Chemistry, 1901), Gerard 't Hooft and Martinus Veltman (Physics, 1999). It is a member of the League of European Research Universities and runs interdisciplinary strategic themes in dynamics of youth, institutions for open societies, life sciences and sustainability.
