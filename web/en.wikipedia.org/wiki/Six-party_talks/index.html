Six-party talks

The six-party talks were a series of multilateral negotiations held intermittently from 2003 to 2009, hosted by China in Beijing, aimed at resolving concerns about North Korea's nuclear weapons programme. The participants were North Korea, South Korea, China, the United States, Japan and Russia.

Background

The talks began after North Korea withdrew from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty in 2003 and expelled international inspectors, escalating the second North Korean nuclear crisis. Earlier bilateral and four-party formats had failed, and China convened the six parties in August 2003.

Key outcomes

In the Joint Statement of 19 September 2005, North Korea committed to abandoning all nuclear weapons and existing nuclear programmes in exchange for security assurances, economic cooperation and discussions on normalising relations. Implementation soon stalled over financial sanctions and verification disputes. In 2007, an "action for action" agreement led North Korea to shut down its Yongbyon nuclear complex in return for fuel oil and aid, and in 2008 it demolished a cooling tower at the site.

Collapse

Negotiations broke down over how to verify North Korea's declaration of its programmes. Following international condemnation of a North Korean rocket launch in April 2009, Pyongyang withdrew from the talks and expelled inspectors, and it conducted its second nuclear test the following month. The talks were never resumed, though the format has periodically been proposed as a model for renewed diplomacy.
