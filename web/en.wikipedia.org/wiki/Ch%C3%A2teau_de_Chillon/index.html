Château de Chillon

The Château de Chillon is an island castle situated on Lake Geneva near Villeneuve, close to Montreux, in the canton of Vaud, Switzerland. Built on a rocky islet at the eastern end of the lake, it controlled the strategic road and shipping route between Italy (via the Great St Bernard Pass) and northern Europe. It is Switzerland's most visited historic monument.

History

The site was occupied from the Bronze Age; the castle took shape under the Counts of Savoy from the 11th to 13th centuries, serving as a summer residence and toll station. The Bernese conquered it in 1536, freeing the prisoner François Bonivard, whose six-year imprisonment in the dungeon inspired Lord Byron's poem The Prisoner of Chillon (1816). From 1798 it belonged to the canton of Vaud.

Architecture

The castle comprises some 25 buildings clustered around courtyards, combining a fortress facing the mountain road with a princely residence overlooking the lake. Notable rooms include the great halls, the chapel with 14th-century paintings, and the underground vaults cut into the rock.
