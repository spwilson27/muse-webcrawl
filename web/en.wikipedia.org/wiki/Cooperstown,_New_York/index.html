Cooperstown, New York

Cooperstown is a village in and the county seat of Otsego County, New York, United States. Located at the southern end of Otsego Lake, it had a population of roughly 1,800 at the 2020 census. The village is best known as the home of the National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum.

History

Cooperstown was founded in 1786 by Judge William Cooper, father of the novelist James Fenimore Cooper, whose Leatherstocking Tales are set in the region. The younger Cooper is buried in the village at Christ Churchyard. In the nineteenth century the area was a center of hop growing.

Baseball

According to a once-accepted legend, Abner Doubleday invented baseball in Cooperstown in 1839 — a story historians now regard as a myth, but one that led to the Hall of Fame's dedication there in 1939. The annual induction ceremony each summer draws tens of thousands of visitors, and Doubleday Field hosts amateur and exhibition games.

Other attractions

The village and its surroundings include the Fenimore Art Museum, the Farmers' Museum, the Glimmerglass Festival (opera), and Otsego Lake. Much of the village is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Cooperstown Historic District.
