National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum

Location25 Main Street, Cooperstown, New York, United States
EstablishedJune 12, 1939
TypeSports hall of fame and museum
CollectionBaseball artefacts, photographs, and archives

The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum is a history museum and hall of fame in Cooperstown, New York, operated by private interests. It serves as the central point of the history of baseball in the United States and displays baseball-related artefacts and exhibits honouring those who have excelled in playing, managing, and serving the sport. The Hall's motto is "Preserving History, Honoring Excellence, Connecting Generations".

History

The Hall of Fame was established in 1939 by Stephen Carlton Clark, heir to the Singer sewing machine fortune and owner of the Clark estates in Cooperstown. Cooperstown was chosen partly because of the (now discredited) Mills Commission story that Abner Doubleday had invented baseball there in 1839. The first class, elected in 1936, comprised Ty Cobb, Babe Ruth, Honus Wagner, Christy Mathewson, and Walter Johnson; the museum was dedicated on June 12, 1939.

Elections

Players are elected primarily by the Baseball Writers' Association of America (BBWAA), which votes on retired players who have been inactive for at least five years and played at least ten major league seasons; a candidate needs 75 percent of votes cast. Separate era committees consider executives, umpires, managers, and players overlooked by the BBWAA, and the Hall also recognises Negro leagues figures. Induction ceremonies are held each summer in Cooperstown.

Museum

The museum's three floors display artefacts from throughout baseball history, a gallery of bronze plaques of all inductees, a theatre, and an extensive library and archive used by researchers. Nearby Doubleday Field hosts exhibition games.
