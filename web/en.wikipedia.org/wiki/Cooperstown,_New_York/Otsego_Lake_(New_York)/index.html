Otsego Lake is a 4,046-acre (16.37 km²) lake located in Otsego County in the U.S. state of New York. It is the source of the Susquehanna River and the largest lake in the county. The Village of Cooperstown is located at the lake's southern end. The lake is also known in literature as "Glimmerglass", the name given to it by James Fenimore Cooper in his Leatherstocking Tales.

Geography

The lake lies in a glacial valley and stretches roughly north–south for about eight miles, with a maximum depth of around 167 feet (51 m). Its outlet at Cooperstown gives rise to the Susquehanna River, which flows south through New York, Pennsylvania and Maryland to Chesapeake Bay. Glimmerglass State Park occupies part of the north-eastern shore, and Hyde Hall, a historic neoclassical mansion, overlooks the lake nearby.

History and literature

The lake's name derives from a Native American word meaning "place of the rock", referring to a large boulder near the outlet, known as Council Rock, a traditional meeting place. William Cooper founded Cooperstown at the lake's foot in the 1780s, and his son James Fenimore Cooper set much of The Pioneers and The Deerslayer on its shores under the name Glimmerglass.

Ecology and recreation

Otsego Lake supports a cold-water fishery including lake trout, walleye (reintroduced), bass and perch, and is used for boating, swimming and sailing. The SUNY Oneonta Biological Field Station studies and monitors the lake's water quality; the lake also serves as the drinking-water source for Cooperstown.
