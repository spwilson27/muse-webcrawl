Jigme Lingpa (Tibetan: འཇིགས་མེད་གླིང་པ་, Wylie: 'Jigs-med gling-pa, 1729/30–1798) was a Tibetan Buddhist tertön (treasure revealer) of the Nyingma school, considered one of the most important figures in the transmission of the Dzogchen teachings. He is best known for revealing the Longchen Nyingthig ("Heart Essence of the Vast Expanse"), a cycle of teachings that became the dominant Dzogchen lineage in the Nyingma tradition.

Life

Born in the Yarlung Valley, Jigme Lingpa had little formal education in youth and worked as a labourer before entering retreat at Palri Thegchok Ling monastery. During a three-year retreat beginning in his late twenties, he experienced a series of visions of the 14th-century master Longchenpa, in which he received the transmission of the Longchen Nyingthig as a mind treasure (gongter). He later founded the monastery of Tharpaling in Bhutan-border regions of Tibet and gathered many disciples.

Works

His collected works fill nine volumes. They include the Yönten Dzö (Treasury of Precious Qualities), a verse commentary on the stages of the Buddhist path that remains a standard textbook, and his autobiographies, notably the Dakki's Grand Secret-Talk. His students included the first Dodrupchen Rinpoche, Jigme Trinle Özer, and he was a teacher of the Drikung and Bhutanese hierarchs.

Legacy

The Longchen Nyingthig is today the most widely practised Dzogchen cycle, central to the teachings of later masters such as Patrul Rinpoche and Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche. Jigme Lingpa is regarded as an emanation of both King Trisong Detsen and Longchenpa.
