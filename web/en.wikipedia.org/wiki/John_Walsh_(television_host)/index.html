John Edward Walsh Jr. (born December 26, 1945) is an American television host, victims' advocate and former hotel developer. After the abduction and murder of his six-year-old son Adam in 1981, Walsh became a prominent campaigner for missing children and victims' rights, co-founding the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children and hosting the long-running television programme America's Most Wanted, which was credited with the capture of more than a thousand fugitives.

Murder of Adam Walsh

On July 27, 1981, Walsh's son Adam was abducted from a Sears department store in Hollywood, Florida; his severed head was found two weeks later, though his body was never recovered. The case galvanised American attention to missing children. Serial killer Ottis Toole confessed to the murder, recanted, and was named responsible by police in 2008, when the case was formally closed. The 1983 television film Adam and its sequel spread the Walshes' campaign nationwide.

Advocacy

The Walshes helped lobby for the Missing Children's Assistance Act of 1984 and co-founded the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC). The federal "Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act" of 2006, establishing a national sex-offender registry framework, is named in his son's memory. The "Code Adam" in-store missing-child alert programme also commemorates Adam Walsh.

Television career

America's Most Wanted premiered on Fox in 1988 with Walsh as host, profiling fugitives and inviting viewer tips; over its runs on Fox, Lifetime and later revivals it was credited with over 1,000 captures, including several FBI Ten Most Wanted fugitives. Walsh subsequently hosted The Hunt with John Walsh on CNN (2014–2017) and In Pursuit with John Walsh on Investigation Discovery (2019–2024, with his son Callahan Walsh). A revival of America's Most Wanted with Walsh returned to Fox in 2024.
