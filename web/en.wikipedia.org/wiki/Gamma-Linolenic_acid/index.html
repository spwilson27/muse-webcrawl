Gamma-Linolenic acid

γ-Linolenic acid (GLA)
Chemical formula: C18H30O2
Type: Omega-6 fatty acid (18:3 n-6)
Sources: Evening primrose oil, borage oil, blackcurrant seed oil
Precursor to: Dihomo-γ-linolenic acid (DGLA)

Gamma-linolenic acid or GLA (γ-linolenic acid) is an omega-6 fatty acid found primarily in seed oils. In the body, GLA is converted to dihomo-γ-linolenic acid (DGLA), a precursor of prostaglandin E1 and other eicosanoids with anti-inflammatory properties.


Structure of GLA

Biochemistry

Humans synthesise GLA from linoleic acid by the enzyme delta-6-desaturase; the conversion can be limited, prompting dietary supplementation. GLA-rich oils include evening primrose oil (8–10% GLA), borage oil (about 20–24%) and blackcurrant seed oil.

Medical use

GLA supplements are marketed for eczema, diabetic neuropathy and other conditions, though evidence of benefit is mixed; a Cochrane review found evening primrose and borage oils did not significantly improve eczema symptoms.
