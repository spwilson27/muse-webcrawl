The pope is the Bishop of Rome and the visible head of the worldwide Catholic Church, and sovereign of the Vatican City State. Catholics believe the pope succeeds Saint Peter, to whom Christ entrusted leadership of the Church ('Tu es Petrus'); the office (the papacy) is one of the oldest institutions in the world, with a traditional list of 266+ popes.

The pope is elected for life by the College of Cardinals in a conclave (resignations are rare — Benedict XVI in 2013 was the first in nearly 600 years). Papal authority, defined by Vatican I (1870) as including infallibility when speaking ex cathedra on faith and morals, was balanced by collegiality at Vatican II. Beyond 1.4 billion Catholics, the pope is a global moral and diplomatic figure: recent popes include John Paul II (1978–2005), Benedict XVI (2005–2013) and Francis (from 2013, the first from the Americas and the first Jesuit). Successor details after Francis's death in April 2025 are beyond reliable recall here and deliberately not asserted.
