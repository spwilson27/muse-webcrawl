The Irish Free State (Irish: Saorstát Éireann) was a state established in December 1922 under the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921. It comprised 26 of the island of Ireland's 32 counties; the six north-eastern counties, which formed Northern Ireland, exercised their right under the treaty to opt out of the new state within a month of its establishment. The Free State was a dominion of the British Empire, with the British monarch as head of state, represented in Dublin by a Governor-General.

The state was created amid the turmoil that followed the Irish War of Independence. Its first government, led by W. T. Cosgrave of Cumann na nGaedheal, had to fight a civil war (1922–1923) against anti-Treaty republicans led by Éamon de Valera, who rejected the dominion status and the oath of allegiance to the Crown required of parliamentarians. The Free State endured until 29 December 1937, when the new Constitution of Ireland, enacted under de Valera's Fianna Fáil government, replaced it with a state known simply as Ireland (Éire).

Background

The Easter Rising of 1916 and the subsequent War of Independence (1919–1921) made the old system of British rule in Ireland untenable. Following a truce in July 1921, negotiations in London produced the Anglo-Irish Treaty, signed on 6 December 1921. The treaty granted Ireland dominion status comparable to that of Canada, but required members of the new parliament (the Oireachtas) to swear an oath of allegiance to the Irish constitution and fidelity to the King. The Dáil approved the treaty narrowly, by 64 votes to 57, and the split over its terms precipitated the Civil War.

The Civil War

Fighting broke out in June 1922 when government forces attacked anti-Treaty forces occupying the Four Courts in Dublin. The war lasted until May 1923, when de Valera ordered a ceasefire and the dumping of arms. The Free State government, led by Cosgrave after the deaths of Arthur Griffith and Michael Collins in August 1922, won the war but at the cost of executions and internments that left lasting bitterness in Irish politics.

Government and politics

The Constitution of the Irish Free State (1922) established a bicameral parliament, the Oireachtas, consisting of Dáil Éireann and Seanad Éireann, and an Executive Council headed by its President. Cosgrave's Cumann na nGaedheal governed from 1922 to 1932, pursuing conservative fiscal policies and consolidating the institutions of the state, including an unarmed police force, the Garda Síochána.

De Valera founded Fianna Fáil in 1926, entered the Dáil in 1927, and won the general election of 1932. In office he systematically dismantled the Treaty settlement: abolishing the oath of allegiance, withholding land annuities from Britain (provoking the "Economic War"), removing the Governor-General's role, and finally introducing the 1937 Constitution.

Economy

The Free State's economy was overwhelmingly agricultural, with cattle exports to Britain dominating trade. The Economic War of 1932–1938, triggered by de Valera's withholding of land annuities, severely damaged the cattle trade and led to tariffs on both sides. In 1928 the state introduced its own coinage and, later, the Irish pound, pegged to sterling.

End of the Free State

On 29 December 1937 the Constitution of Ireland came into force, replacing the Free State constitution. The new document named the state "Éire, or, in the English language, Ireland", created the office of President of Ireland, and removed most references to the British Crown, though the Crown retained a residual role in external relations until the Republic of Ireland Act came into force in 1949.
