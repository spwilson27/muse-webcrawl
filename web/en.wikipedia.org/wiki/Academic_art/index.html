Academic art, or academicism, is a style of painting, sculpture and architecture produced under the influence of European academies of art. Specifically, academic art is the art and artists influenced by the standards of the French Académie des Beaux-Arts, which was practised under the movements of Neoclassicism and Romanticism, and the art that followed these two movements in the attempt to synthesise both of their styles.

Characteristics

Academic art emphasised rigorous training in drawing from the live model and classical statuary, mastery of anatomy and perspective, and a polished, highly finished surface that concealed brushwork. A strict hierarchy of genres ranked history painting — grand scenes from history, mythology and religion — highest, followed by portraiture, scenes of everyday life, landscape and still life.

The academic system

Academies controlled artistic education and exhibition, most famously through the Paris Salon, the official exhibition of the Académie des Beaux-Arts. Prizes such as the Prix de Rome, which funded study at the French Academy in Rome, structured artists' careers. The École des Beaux-Arts in Paris was the leading teaching institution.

Reaction and legacy

In the later 19th century, academic art was challenged by the Realists, the Impressionists and other avant-garde movements, and "academic" became a pejorative term for formulaic, conservative work. In the late 20th and 21st centuries, academic painters such as Bouguereau and Gérôme have been re-evaluated, and atelier-style training has revived in classical realism schools.
