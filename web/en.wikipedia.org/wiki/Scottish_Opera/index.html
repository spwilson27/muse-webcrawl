Scottish Opera is the national opera company of Scotland and one of the five national performing arts companies funded directly by the Scottish Government. Founded in 1962 by conductor Alexander Gibson, it is based in Glasgow, where the Theatre Royal has been its home theatre since 1975, and it tours to Edinburgh, Aberdeen, and Inverness as well as smaller venues across Scotland.

History

Gibson, the first Scottish-born principal conductor of the Scottish National Orchestra, founded the company to bring professional opera of international standard to Scotland. Its first season in 1962 included productions of Madama Butterfly and Otello. Under Gibson (music director until 1986) the company won a strong reputation, mounting full Wagner cycles, Scottish premieres, and new commissions. His successors as music director included John Mauceri and Sir Richard Armstrong; Francesco Corti served from 2007 to 2013, followed by Stuart Stratford.

Productions and commissions

The company has staged notable productions including a celebrated Ring cycle (completed 2003), and Opera Highlights tours taking small-scale productions to dozens of rural venues. It has commissioned many new works, notably by Scottish composers such as James MacMillan, whose first opera Inés de Castro premiered in 1996 and whose The Sacrifice was co-produced with Welsh National Opera. The company's Opera Unwrapped and education programmes introduce opera to new audiences, and its Emerging Artists scheme supports young singers and repetiteurs.

Administration

Productions are created at the Scottish Opera Production Studios at Edington Street in Glasgow, and the Orchestra of Scottish Opera — formed from former players of the BBC Scottish Symphony Orchestra in 1980 — accompanies performances. In 2005 the company faced a financial crisis that led to the loss of its full-time chorus, later restructured; it has since operated with a freelance chorus.
