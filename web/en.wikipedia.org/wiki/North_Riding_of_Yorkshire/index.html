North Riding of Yorkshire

The North Riding of Yorkshire was a subdivision of Yorkshire, England, alongside the East and West Ridings. The word "riding" derives from the Old Norse þriðjungr, meaning a third part. The North Riding stretched from the Pennines to the North Sea coast, bounded by the River Tees to the north and the River Ouse and Derwent to the south.

History

The ridings originated in the Viking period and functioned for centuries as administrative and judicial divisions. From 1889 the North Riding had its own county council, based at Northallerton. Under the Local Government Act 1972, effective 1974, the administrative county was abolished; most of its territory became the northern part of the new county of North Yorkshire, the north-east (including Middlesbrough and Redcar) went to Cleveland (later unitary authorities within the ceremonial county), and the Startforth area transferred to County Durham.

Geography

The riding contains two national parks: the North York Moors and part of the Yorkshire Dales. Principal towns include Middlesbrough, Scarborough, Whitby, Richmond, Northallerton and Harrogate (historically on the West Riding border). The flag of the North Riding, registered in 2013, features a white rose on a blue, green and yellow cross design representing the moors, dales and coast.
