"The Man from Snowy River" is a poem by Australian bush poet Banjo Paterson, first published in The Bulletin magazine on 26 April 1890, and later as the title work of his first book of verse, The Man from Snowy River and Other Verses (1895). It is one of Australia's most famous poems and a cornerstone of the bush ballad tradition.

Story

A valuable colt — the prize of a racing stud, worth a thousand pounds — has escaped and joined the wild brumbies in the Snowy Mountains. The district's finest horsemen gather to run the mob down. Among them is a slight, unknown young rider on a small, weedy horse, dismissed by the others until the old stockman Clancy vouches for him: he is "the man from Snowy River". When the brumbies plunge down a near-vertical slope that stops every other rider, the young man follows alone, rides the terrible descent at full gallop, turns the herd single-handed, and brings them home — his horse covered in blood from the ordeal, his courage proven.

Inspiration and legacy

The poem is often linked to real Snowy Mountains horsemen such as Jack Riley of Corryong, whom Paterson met, though Paterson never confirmed a single model. It inspired the successful 1982 film The Man from Snowy River and its sequel, a television series, and an arena spectacular; the image of Paterson and lines from the poem appeared on the Australian ten-dollar note. Recitations of the poem remain a fixture of Australian country life, and annual festivals in Corryong celebrate the legend.
