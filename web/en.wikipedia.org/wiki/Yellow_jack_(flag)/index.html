Yellow jack (flag)

TypeSignal flag / quarantine flag
Meaning (historical)Ship under quarantine (disease aboard)
Modern ICS equivalentFlag "Q" (Quebec) — plain yellow

The yellow jack is a plain yellow flag historically flown by ships to indicate that they were under quarantine because of infectious disease aboard — the name coming from both the flag's colour and its association with yellow fever, itself nicknamed "yellow jack".

Usage

From the age of sail, ports required arriving ships that had sickness aboard, or that came from ports where plague or yellow fever was present, to fly a yellow flag and remain at anchor in a quarantine station until cleared by health authorities. Over time the meaning inverted in international practice: under the International Code of Signals, the plain yellow flag is the letter flag "Q" (Quebec), and a ship flying it on arrival requests free pratique — declaring, in effect, that it is healthy and asking permission to enter port.

Other uses

"Yellow Jack" was also the name affectionately given to the yellow fever epidemics that repeatedly struck ports in the Americas. In the Royal Navy, a yellow flag flown at a naval hospital or hospital ship likewise signalled infectious disease.
