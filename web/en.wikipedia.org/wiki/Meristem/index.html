Meristem

A meristem is a type of tissue found in plants. It consists of undifferentiated cells (meristematic cells) capable of cell division. Cells in the meristem can develop into all the other tissues and organs that occur in plants. These cells continue to divide until they differentiate and lose the ability to divide. Meristems are the plant equivalent of stem cell niches in animals, and they allow a plant to grow throughout its life.

Types

Apical meristems

Apical meristems are found at the tips of shoots and roots and produce primary growth—increase in length. The shoot apical meristem generates leaves and flowers and maintains itself through a stem cell population regulated by the WUSCHEL–CLAVATA feedback loop. The root apical meristem is protected by the root cap and organised around a quiescent centre.

Lateral meristems

Lateral meristems—the vascular cambium and cork cambium—produce secondary growth, increasing the girth of stems and roots in woody plants. The vascular cambium produces secondary xylem (wood) inward and secondary phloem outward.

Intercalary meristems

Intercalary meristems occur at the bases of nodes and leaf blades, especially in grasses, allowing regrowth after grazing or mowing.

Primary meristems

Apical meristems give rise to three primary meristems: the protoderm (forming epidermis), the ground meristem (forming ground tissues), and the procambium (forming primary vascular tissues).
