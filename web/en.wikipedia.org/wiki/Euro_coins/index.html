Euro coins

Euro coins are the coins of the euro, the official currency of the eurozone. There are eight denominations: 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 50 cents, and €1 and €2. The coins entered circulation on 1 January 2002, though they were minted with dates from 1999 onwards. Every coin has a common reverse side showing its value, designed by the Belgian artist Luc Luycx, and a national obverse side chosen by the issuing country. All coins are legal tender throughout the eurozone regardless of which country issued them.

Design

The common sides depict maps of Europe. The original designs on the 10, 20 and 50 cent coins showed the then fifteen member states separately, while the €1 and €2 coins showed the European Union without internal borders; after EU enlargement the designs were updated from 2007 to show a map of the whole of Europe. The 1, 2 and 5 cent coins show Europe in relation to the globe, with latitude and longitude lines.

National sides reflect each country's identity: monarchs' portraits in the monarchies, famous artworks and monuments, national symbols and notable figures. Member states may change their national sides under certain conditions, and the €2 coin's national side is also used for commemorative issues, which each country may issue twice a year (in addition to occasional joint programmes, such as the 35th anniversary of the Erasmus programme in 2022).

The 20 cent coin has a distinctive "Spanish flower" shape with seven notches, intended to help blind people distinguish it. Several countries, including Finland and the Netherlands, round cash payments to the nearest five cents and no longer issue 1 and 2 cent coins for general circulation, though the coins remain legal tender.

Issuing countries

Euro coins are issued by the eurozone member states, and also by four microstates — Andorra, Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City — under monetary agreements with the EU. Coins from the microstates are scarce and command collector premiums.
