Anglesey

Anglesey (Welsh: Ynys Môn) is an island off the northwest coast of Wales, separated from the mainland by the Menai Strait. At 712 square kilometres it is the largest island in Wales and the fifth-largest in the British Isles, with a population of about 69,000.

Anglesey was a stronghold of the druids, attacked by Rome (in 60-61 CE under Suetonius Paulinus), and later the seat of the princes of Gwynedd at Aberffraw. The island is connected to the mainland by the Menai Suspension Bridge (1826, by Thomas Telford) and the Britannia Bridge. Holyhead is a major ferry port for Ireland. The village of Llanfairpwllgwyngyll is famous for its extended name, among the longest place names in Europe. Welsh is spoken by a majority of the island's residents.
