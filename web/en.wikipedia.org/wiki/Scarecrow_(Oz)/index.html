Scarecrow (Oz)

Scarecrow
First appearance: The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900)
Created by: L. Frank Baum
Portrayed by: Ray Bolger (1939 film)

The Scarecrow is a fictional character created by L. Frank Baum, first appearing in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900). He is a living scarecrow stuffed with straw who longs for brains, believing himself foolish, although throughout the story he repeatedly demonstrates cleverness and resourcefulness. He is the first companion Dorothy Gale meets on the Yellow Brick Road, having been freed by her from the pole on which he was hung in a Munchkin cornfield.

Role in the Oz books

At the end of the first book, the Wizard grants the Scarecrow a head filled with bran, needles, and pins — symbolic "brains" — and leaves him as ruler of the Emerald City. In The Marvelous Land of Oz (1904) he is deposed by General Jinjur's army and flees with his companions. Baum's later books give him elaborate backstories and adventures; in The Scarecrow of Oz (1915) he plays a leading role.

Portrayals

The character's best-known portrayal is by Ray Bolger in MGM's 1939 film The Wizard of Oz, whose loose-limbed dance in "If I Only Had a Brain" became iconic. Michael Jackson played the Scarecrow in the 1978 film The Wiz.

Interpretations

Some scholars read Baum's character as an allegory of the American farmer in the Populist era — perceived as ignorant but in fact shrewd and practical.
