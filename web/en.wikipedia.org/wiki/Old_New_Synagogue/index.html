Old New Synagogue

The Old New Synagogue, Prague, completed in 1270.

LocationJosefov, Prague, Czech Republic
Completed1270
StyleEarly Gothic
StatusActive synagogue; museum site

The Old New Synagogue (Czech: Staronová synagoga; German: Altneu-Synagoge), situated in Josefov, Prague, is Europe's oldest active synagogue and one of the oldest surviving synagogue buildings anywhere. Completed in 1270 in early Gothic style, it is one of the first Gothic buildings in Prague and the city's oldest surviving Jewish house of worship. It has remained in continuous use, with an interruption during the Nazi occupation of 1942–1945.

Name

The curious name is explained by a number of traditions. One holds that the building was called "new" when built to distinguish it from an even older synagogue, and retained the name as newer synagogues arose; another legend derives it from the Hebrew al tenai ("on condition") — angels, it was said, brought stones from the Temple in Jerusalem on condition they be returned when the Messiah comes.

Architecture

The synagogue is a rectangular hall whose two-nave interior is divided by two massive octagonal pillars supporting a rib-vaulted ceiling — unusually, the vaulting uses five ribs per bay rather than four, avoiding any suggestion of a cross. Twelve lancet windows light the hall. The eastern wall houses the Torah ark; a low wall separates the women's gallery, added in later centuries, whose occupants could hear services through small openings. The twin gables of the steep roof are a landmark of the former Jewish Town. The building survived the demolition of the ghetto in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when most of Josefov was rebuilt.

Golem legend

The synagogue is inseparably associated with Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal, d. 1609), who served here, and with the legend of the Golem of Prague — the clay creature said to have been animated to defend the ghetto, whose remains are popularly believed to rest in the synagogue's attic (genizah). The attic can still be reached only by an external ladder, and its exploration is restricted.
