János Bihari

János Bihari (21 October 1764 – 26 April 1827) was a Hungarian Romani violinist and composer, celebrated as the "king of Gypsy violinists" and a central figure in the development of the verbunkos style of Hungarian music.

Born21 October 1764, Nagyabony, Kingdom of Hungary
Died26 April 1827, Pest, Kingdom of Hungary
OccupationViolinist, composer
GenreVerbunkos, Hungarian folk music

Career

Bihari led a celebrated band in Pest and Vienna, performing at imperial and aristocratic occasions, including entertainments connected with the Congress of Vienna. His playing impressed Beethoven and other Viennese musicians, and themes associated with him influenced later composers.

Works and legacy

About 84 melodies attributed to Bihari survive through the publications of contemporaries, including slow hallgató pieces and fiery friss dances. The Rákóczi March in the form transmitted by Bihari's tradition was later orchestrated by Berlioz in La damnation de Faust. Franz Liszt devoted admiring pages to Bihari in his book on Romani music.
