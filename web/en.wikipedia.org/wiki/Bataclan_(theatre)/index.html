The Bataclan is a theatre and concert hall in the 11th arrondissement of Paris, France, located at 50 Boulevard Voltaire. Designed by architect Charles Duval and opened in 1865, it was originally built in a Chinoiserie style with a pagoda-like roof, of which only the façade's lower portion survives. Its name derives from Ba-Ta-Clan, an operetta by Jacques Offenbach.

History

The hall hosted vaudeville, café-concert revues — Maurice Chevalier had early successes there — and later cinema, before returning to live music as a rock and pop venue in the later 20th century. Many major artists have performed at the Bataclan, and it became one of Paris's best-known mid-sized concert venues.

2015 attack and reopening

On 13 November 2015, during a concert by the American rock band Eagles of Death Metal, three gunmen attacked the Bataclan as part of a coordinated series of terrorist attacks in Paris, killing 90 people at the venue. After extensive renovation, the hall reopened in November 2016 with a concert by Sting, and it continues to operate as a concert venue and a site of commemoration.
