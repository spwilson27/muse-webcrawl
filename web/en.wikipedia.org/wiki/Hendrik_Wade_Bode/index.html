Hendrik Wade Bode
Born: 24 December 1905, Madison, Wisconsin
Died: 21 June 1982, Cambridge, Massachusetts
Known for: Bode plot, control theory

Hendrik Wade Bode (December 24, 1905 – June 21, 1982) was an American engineer, researcher and inventor of Dutch descent. He made important contributions to control theory and telecommunications, and is known for the Bode plot, a graphical method for analysing the frequency response of feedback amplifiers and control systems that remains a standard tool of engineering practice.

Career

Bode studied mathematics at Ohio State University and joined Bell Telephone Laboratories in 1926 while completing his doctorate at Columbia University (PhD 1935). At Bell Labs he worked on electronic filters and equalisers and, from the late 1930s, on feedback amplifier design, publishing the classic monograph Network Analysis and Feedback Amplifier Design (1945). During the Second World War he applied feedback control to automatic anti-aircraft fire-control systems. After the war he became director of mathematical research and later vice-president of Bell Labs, and following retirement in 1967 he served as Gordon McKay Professor of Systems Engineering at Harvard University.

Contributions

His name is attached to the Bode plot (magnitude and phase diagrams plotted against logarithmic frequency), Bode's sensitivity integral (a fundamental limitation on feedback design, sometimes called the "waterbed effect"), and the Bode gain–phase relation linking the magnitude and phase of a minimum-phase system. He was a founding member of the U.S. National Academy of Engineering and received numerous honours, including the IEEE Edison Medal in 1975.
