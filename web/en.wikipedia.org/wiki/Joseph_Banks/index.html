Joseph Banks

Died19 June 1820, Isleworth, London, England

Sir Joseph Banks, 1st Baronet (24 February 1743 – 19 June 1820) was an English naturalist, botanist and patron of the natural sciences. He accompanied Captain James Cook on the first great voyage of HMS Endeavour (1768–1771), which visited Brazil, Tahiti, New Zealand and Australia, and he served as president of the Royal Society for a record 41 years.

Early life

Banks was born in London to a wealthy Lincolnshire landowning family and educated at Harrow, Eton and Christ Church, Oxford, where he pursued botany largely on his own initiative. In 1766 he voyaged to Newfoundland and Labrador to collect plants, publishing the first Linnaean descriptions of its flora and fauna.

The Endeavour voyage

In 1768 Banks spent some £10,000 of his own fortune to equip himself, the botanist Daniel Solander and artists including Sydney Parkinson to join Cook's expedition. The party collected thousands of specimens, including some 1,400 plant species new to European science from Australia's east coast; Cook named the landing site Botany Bay in honour of their work. Banks became a celebrity on his return and was presented to George III.

Later career

Elected president of the Royal Society in 1778, Banks advised the Crown on science, directed the Royal Botanic Gardens at Kew as its unofficial head — sending collectors worldwide and making it a centre of economic botany — and advocated for the colonisation of Australia, including the settlement at Botany Bay. He supported the breeding of merino sheep, the introduction of tea to India and the breadfruit voyages of William Bligh. More than 80 species bear the name banksii, and the genus Banksia was named for him.
