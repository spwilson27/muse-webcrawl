Phototropin

Phototropins contain two light-sensitive LOV domains and a serine/threonine kinase domain.

Phototropins are blue-light photoreceptor proteins found in plants that mediate phototropism, chloroplast movement, stomatal opening and other responses to blue light. They are members of a family of proteins sensitive to light, oxygen and voltage (the LOV domain family) and are characterised by the presence of two LOV domains, each binding a flavin mononucleotide (FMN) chromophore, together with a C-terminal serine/threonine kinase domain.

Function

Phototropins were first identified in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana, which has two phototropins, PHOT1 and PHOT2. When blue light is absorbed, the FMN chromophore forms a covalent adduct with a conserved cysteine residue, triggering a conformational change that activates the kinase domain and initiates downstream signalling.

PHOT1 and PHOT2 act redundantly in many responses but differ in light sensitivity: PHOT1 is more sensitive and mediates responses to low-intensity blue light, while PHOT2 functions at higher light intensities, including the chloroplast avoidance response that protects the photosynthetic apparatus from excessive light.

Responses mediated

  • Phototropism — bending of shoots toward light
  • Chloroplast accumulation and avoidance movements
  • Stomatal opening
  • Leaf expansion and flattening
  • Inhibition of hypocotyl elongation

Phototropins are distinct from other plant photoreceptors such as the phytochromes (red/far-red light) and cryptochromes (blue/UV-A light), although their signalling pathways interact extensively.
