General Conference on Weights and Measures

The General Conference on Weights and Measures (French: Conférence générale des poids et mesures, CGPM) is the supreme authority of the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM), the intergovernmental organisation established by the Metre Convention.

CGPM
Formation: 1889 (1st meeting)
Headquarters: Sèvres, France (BIPM)
Parent: Metre Convention

Role

The CGPM comprises delegates of the governments of Member States and observers from Associates. It meets in Paris, usually every four years, receives the report of the International Committee for Weights and Measures (CIPM), discusses and examines arrangements required to ensure the propagation and improvement of the International System of Units (SI), and endorses new fundamental metrological determinations.

Key decisions

The 1st CGPM (1889) sanctioned the international prototypes of the metre and kilogram. The 11th (1960) established the SI. The 26th CGPM (2018) approved the landmark redefinition of the SI, effective 20 May 2019, fixing exact values for the Planck constant, elementary charge, Boltzmann constant and Avogadro constant, so all units derive from defining constants. The 27th meeting was held in 2022. See also International System of Units.
