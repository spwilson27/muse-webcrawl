Selection (biology)

Selection, in biology, is the differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype. It is a key mechanism of evolution, the change in the heritable traits characteristic of a population over generations. Charles Darwin popularised the term "natural selection", contrasting it with artificial selection, which in his view is intentional, whereas natural selection is not.

Variation exists within all populations of organisms. This occurs partly because random mutations arise in the genome of an individual organism, and their offspring can inherit such mutations. Throughout the lives of the individuals, their genomes interact with their environments to cause variations in traits. The environment includes the molecular biology of the cell, other cells, other individuals, populations, species, as well as the abiotic environment.

Types of selection

Biologists distinguish several modes of selection. Natural selection arises from differential reproductive success imposed by the environment. Artificial selection (selective breeding) is imposed deliberately by humans, as in domesticated plants and animals. Sexual selection results from competition for mates and from mate choice. Selection may also be classified by its effect on trait distributions: directional selection favours one extreme, stabilising selection favours intermediate values, and disruptive selection favours both extremes.

Mechanism

Individuals with traits better adapted to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing those traits to the next generation. Over many generations this process can produce adaptations that make organisms well suited to particular ecological niches, and may eventually give rise to new species. Selection acts together with other evolutionary mechanisms such as mutation, genetic drift and gene flow.

History

The idea of selection was developed by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace in the mid-19th century and presented to the Linnean Society in 1858. Darwin elaborated the theory in On the Origin of Species (1859), drawing an analogy with the artificial selection practised by plant and animal breeders.
