Brillouin and Langevin functions

The Brillouin function and the Langevin function are special functions that arise in the statistical mechanics of magnetism. They describe the magnetisation of a paramagnetic material in an applied magnetic field, in the quantum-mechanical (Brillouin) and classical (Langevin) treatments respectively. Both are named after their originators, Léon Brillouin and Paul Langevin.

Brillouin function

The Brillouin function BJ(x), for total angular momentum quantum number J, is defined by:

BJ(x) = ((2J+1)/(2J)) coth(((2J+1)/(2J)) x) − (1/(2J)) coth(x/(2J))

The magnetisation of a paramagnet of N atoms per unit volume, each with magnetic moment gμBJ, is M = N g μB J BJ(x), where x = g μB J B / (kB T), B is the applied field, kB is Boltzmann's constant and T is temperature.

For large fields or low temperatures (x → ∞), BJ(x) approaches 1, corresponding to saturation of the magnetisation. For small x, it behaves linearly, BJ(x) ≈ (J+1)x/(3J), which yields Curie's law.

Langevin function

The Langevin function is the classical limit of the Brillouin function as J → ∞:

L(x) = coth(x) − 1/x

It appears in the classical theory of paramagnetism, in which atomic magnetic moments are treated as freely rotating classical vectors, and also in the theory of diamagnetic and dielectric materials and the statistical mechanics of polymers, where it describes the extension of a polymer chain under force.

Special cases

  • For J = 1/2, the Brillouin function reduces to B1/2(x) = tanh(x).
  • For J = 1, B1(x) = (3/2) coth(3x/2) − (1/2) coth(x/2).
