Battle of Flodden

The Battle of Flodden, also known as the Battle of Flodden Field, was fought on 9 September 1513 during the War of the League of Cambrai between the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Scotland, and resulted in an English victory. The battle was fought near Branxton in Northumberland, in northern England, between an invading Scots army under King James IV and an English army commanded by the Earl of Surrey. James IV was killed in the fighting, becoming the last monarch from the island of Great Britain to die in battle.

Background

When Henry VIII invaded France in 1513, James IV honoured the Auld Alliance by declaring war and invading northern England. The Scots army, the largest Scotland had ever fielded, crossed the Tweed in August and took Norham Castle.

The battle

Surrey outmanoeuvred the Scots, crossing the River Till at Twizel Bridge and cutting off their line of retreat. Forced to abandon their strong position on Flodden Hill, the Scottish schiltrons advanced downhill into wet ground, where their long pikes became unmanageable and were defeated by English billmen. James IV was killed close to Surrey's own position.

Aftermath

The Scottish dead included the king, an archbishop, two bishops, and a generation of nobles and clan chiefs — a catastrophe commemorated in the lament "The Flowers of the Forest". Scotland was left with an infant king, James V, and a regency under his mother, Margaret Tudor. In numbers of men engaged, Flodden was the largest battle ever fought between the two kingdoms.
