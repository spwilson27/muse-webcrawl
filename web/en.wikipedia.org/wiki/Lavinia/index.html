Lavinia

In Roman mythology, Lavinia was the daughter of King Latinus of Latium and his wife Amata, and the second wife of the Trojan hero Aeneas. Their marriage, central to Virgil's Aeneid, symbolised the alliance between the Trojans and the Latins and the foundation of the Roman people.

Lavinia had been promised to Turnus, king of the Rutuli, and Aeneas's claim to her hand sparked the war that fills the second half of the Aeneid, ending with Aeneas killing Turnus. Omens surrounding Lavinia — her hair seeming to catch fire without harm — foretold her fame and the coming war.

After Aeneas's death Lavinia bore his posthumous son Silvius. The town of Lavinium was said to be named for her. Ursula K. Le Guin's 2008 novel Lavinia retells the story from her perspective.
