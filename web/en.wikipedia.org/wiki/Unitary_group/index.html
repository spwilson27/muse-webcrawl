Unitary group

In mathematics, the unitary group of degree n, denoted U(n), is the group of n × n unitary complex matrices under matrix multiplication. A matrix U is unitary when its conjugate transpose is also its inverse:

U*U = UU* = I

Equivalently, unitary matrices preserve the standard Hermitian inner product on complex n-space, including lengths and angles.

Properties

U(n) is a compact real Lie group of real dimension n². The determinant of a unitary matrix is a complex number of absolute value one. The subgroup of unitary matrices with determinant exactly one is the special unitary group SU(n), of dimension n² − 1.

For n = 1, U(1) consists of complex numbers of modulus one and can be identified with the circle. It is the gauge group of electromagnetism in quantum field theory. U(n) can also be characterized as the group of linear transformations preserving a Hermitian form.

Applications

Unitary transformations are central to quantum mechanics because they preserve total probability: time evolution and symmetry operations on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces are represented by unitary operators. They also arise in signal processing, numerical linear algebra, representation theory and the study of Lie groups. Unlike the orthogonal group, which preserves a real inner product, the unitary group preserves a complex Hermitian inner product.
