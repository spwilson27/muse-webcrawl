Raków, Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship

Raków is a town in Kielce County, Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship, in south-central Poland. It is the seat of the gmina (administrative district) called Gmina Raków, and lies roughly 35 kilometres south-east of the regional capital, Kielce.

History

Raków was founded in 1569 by Jan Sienieński and quickly became famous as a centre of the Polish Brethren, the anti-Trinitarian Reformed church. The town hosted the Racovian Academy, a renowned school that drew students from across Europe, and the Racovian Catechism of 1605 was named after the town. The prosperity of this religious community ended during the Counter-Reformation: the academy was closed in 1638, and following the expulsion of the Polish Brethren from the Commonwealth in 1658 the town declined.

Raków lost its town charter in 1869 in the aftermath of the January Uprising and remained a village for over a century and a half. Its town rights were restored on 1 January 2025.
