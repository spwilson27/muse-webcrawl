Giovanni Battista Giraldi

Born: November 1504, Ferrara, Duchy of Ferrara

Died: December 30, 1573 (aged 69), Ferrara

Occupation: Novelist, poet, playwright

Notable works: Gli Hecatommithi (1565), Orbecche (1541)

Giovanni Battista Giraldi (November 1504 – December 30, 1573), often known by his nickname Cinthio (also spelled Cinzio), was an Italian novelist, poet, and dramatist from Ferrara. He is remembered for his collection of tales Gli Hecatommithi, which supplied the plots for Shakespeare's Othello and Measure for Measure, and for his Senecan tragedy Orbecche, a landmark in the revival of classical tragedy.

Life

Giraldi studied medicine and philosophy at the University of Ferrara, where he succeeded Celio Calcagnini in the chair of natural philosophy in 1525 and later became professor of literature. He served as secretary to Duke Ercole II d'Este and then to his successor Alfonso II. A bitter literary feud with a rival secretary, Giovan Battista Pigna, damaged his standing at court in the 1560s, and Giraldi spent his later years in reduced circumstances at Mondovì and Pavia before returning to Ferrara, where he died in 1573.

Works

His tragedy Orbecche, performed in 1541 and published in 1543, was the first regular tragedy in Italian modelled on Seneca, notorious for its on-stage horrors and ghosts. He defended its violence and the five-act structure in critical writings that influenced Renaissance dramatic theory, and argued the case for the romance epic in his Discorso intorno al comporre dei romanzi (1554), championing Ariosto against strict Aristotelians.

Gli Hecatommithi ("The Hundred Tales"), published in 1565, is a collection of 112 novellas framed by a company of nobles fleeing Rome after the Sack of 1527. Its tale of a Moorish captain murdered-for-love provided Shakespeare with the plot of Othello, while another story of a deputy who propositions a condemned man's sister furnished the plot of Measure for Measure.
