Frankenstein

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Creature in a 1910-style illustration: tall, yellow-eyed, stitched and eloquent, standing over the sleeping Victor Frankenstein]

Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus is an 1818 novel written by English author Mary Shelley. The novel is about a young scientist named Victor Frankenstein who creates a sapient creature from corpses and various body parts in an unorthodox scientific experiment. Shelley started writing the story when she was 18 and the first edition was published anonymously in London on 1 January 1818, when she was 20.

Conceived during the 'Year Without a Summer' at the Villa Diodati, the novel is told in Walton's letters framing Victor's confession and the Creature's own eloquent narrative — an abandoned child-figure who learns language by watching a family and turns to violence only when spurned by all. Often called the first science-fiction novel, Frankenstein gave culture a permanent myth: not the grunting monster of film, but the question of what creators owe their creations.
