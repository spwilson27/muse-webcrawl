Western Design

The Western Design was an English military expedition conceived by Oliver Cromwell and the Council of State of the Commonwealth of England in 1654, during the First Anglo-Spanish War (1654–1660). Its aim was to seize Spanish possessions in the Caribbean, establish an English foothold in the Americas and break Spain's monopoly of American trade.

The expedition

A fleet under Admiral William Penn, carrying troops commanded by General Robert Venables, sailed from Portsmouth in December 1654. Reinforced in Barbados with colonial volunteers, the expedition attacked Santo Domingo on Hispaniola in April 1655 but was repulsed with heavy losses, owing to poor planning, disease and Spanish resistance.

Capture of Jamaica

Seeking a consolation success, Penn and Venables turned to the lightly defended island of Jamaica, landing in May 1655 and taking Spanish Town. Spain never recovered the island; its possession was formally recognised by the Treaty of Madrid in 1670. Jamaica became England's principal sugar colony and a base for buccaneers at Port Royal. Penn and Venables, returning in disgrace, were briefly imprisoned in the Tower of London. The Western Design nonetheless marked the beginning of England's permanent territorial empire in the Caribbean.
