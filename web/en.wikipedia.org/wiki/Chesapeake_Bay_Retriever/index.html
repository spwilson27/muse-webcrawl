The Chesapeake Bay Retriever is a large, strong, powerfully built breed of dog developed in the Chesapeake Bay area of the United States during the 19th century by American duck hunters to retrieve waterfowl under the most adverse weather and water conditions, often having to break ice during the course of many strenuous multiple retrievals. Affectionately known as the "Chessie", it is the state dog of Maryland, designated in 1964.

History

The breed traces to 1807, when an English brig was wrecked off the Maryland coast and two Newfoundland puppies, a male named Sailor and a female named Canton, were rescued and bred with local retrieving dogs. Generations of market waterfowl hunters selected for stamina, a dense oily coat that sheds icy water, and a powerful build able to retrieve hundreds of ducks a day from the frigid Bay.

Characteristics

The Chesapeake's coat is distinctive: a harsh, wavy outer coat over a dense, fine, woolly undercoat, rich in natural oil that resists water penetration. Its eyes are characteristically yellowish or amber. Chessies are known for intelligence, courage, a strong work ethic, and a somewhat more independent and protective temperament than other retriever breeds. They excel in hunt tests, obedience and as working retrievers in cold climates.

Health

Like other large retrievers, the breed can be affected by hip dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy and degenerative myelopathy, for which genetic screening is available.
