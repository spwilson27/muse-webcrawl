Wassenaar Arrangement

The Wassenaar Arrangement on Export Controls for Conventional Arms and Dual-Use Goods and Technologies is a multilateral export control regime established in 1996 to promote transparency and responsibility in transfers of conventional arms and dual-use goods, so that such transfers do not contribute to destabilising accumulations. Its secretariat is in Vienna, Austria, and it has 42 participating states.

History and operation

Agreed in Wassenaar, the Netherlands, in December 1995 and formally established in July 1996, the Arrangement succeeded the Cold War-era CoCom regime but, unlike CoCom, is not directed against any specific state. Participating states maintain national export controls on items in the Arrangement's lists of dual-use goods and munitions, and exchange information on transfers and denials of sensitive items.

Control lists

The lists cover conventional arms in categories derived from the UN Register of Conventional Arms, together with dual-use technologies including electronics, computers, telecommunications, sensors, lasers, advanced materials and, since 2013, certain intrusion software and IP network surveillance technologies — additions that drew debate over their effects on security research. Decisions are taken by consensus and implemented nationally; the Arrangement imposes no legally binding obligations or veto rights over individual transfers.
