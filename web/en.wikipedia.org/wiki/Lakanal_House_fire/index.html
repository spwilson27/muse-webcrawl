The Lakanal House fire occurred on 3 July 2009 in Camberwell, London, when a fire spread rapidly through Lakanal House, a 14-storey tower block on the Sceaux Gardens Estate. Six people were killed, including three children, and more than twenty were injured. The fire became a landmark case in British building-safety history, foreshadowing issues that returned to national attention after the Grenfell Tower fire of 2017.

The building

Lakanal House was built in 1959–60 by the London County Council. It contained 98 flats arranged as maisonettes around a central corridor, with a single central staircase serving as the escape route, in accordance with the design philosophy of the time, which assumed that fires would be contained within the flat of origin ("compartmentation") and that residents elsewhere should "stay put".

The fire

The fire started in a flat on the ninth floor, caused by an electrical fault in a television. It spread upwards and outwards with unexpected speed: burning material fell and ignited flats below, and the fire travelled through the building's external wall panels and the suspended ceilings of the corridors, breaching the intended fire compartments. Some residents who remained in their flats in line with "stay put" advice became trapped; the six victims died in a flat on the eleventh floor where several families had gathered.

Inquest and consequences

The inquest (2013) concluded that the deaths were contributed to by the rapid external fire spread, the failure of fire-resisting structures between flats, and confusion about evacuation advice. The coroner wrote to the government recommending a review of building regulations guidance and the retrofitting of sprinklers in high-rise residential buildings. The London Borough of Southwark, the landlord, pleaded guilty in 2017 to four offences under fire-safety law and was fined £570,000.

Lakanal House was refurbished and remains occupied. Warnings arising from the fire — about combustible external panels, compartmentation failures and stay-put policies — were widely cited after the Grenfell Tower fire eight years later.
