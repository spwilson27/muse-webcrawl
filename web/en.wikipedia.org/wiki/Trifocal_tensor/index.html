Trifocal tensor

In computer vision, the trifocal tensor (also often called the trilinear tensor) is a 3×3×3 array of numbers that encodes all the projective geometric relationships among three views of a scene. It is the three-view analogue of the fundamental matrix, which encodes the geometry of two views (epipolar geometry).

Properties

Whereas the fundamental matrix relates corresponding points in two images, the trifocal tensor relates corresponding points and lines across three images. A point in one view corresponds to a line in each of the other two; the tensor provides point-point-point, point-line-line and line-line-line incidence constraints — the multilinear constraints — that any genuine triple of correspondences must satisfy. From the tensor, the three camera matrices can be recovered up to a common projective transformation, and the trifocal tensor itself can be computed from the three pairs of fundamental matrices or estimated directly from at least six point correspondences (by a linear algorithm).

Applications

The trifocal tensor is used in view transfer — predicting where a point seen in two images will appear in a third — which supports image-based rendering, video interpolation, structure-from-motion pipelines and visual odometry. It gives stronger constraints than pairwise geometry alone and can resolve ambiguities that arise from degenerate configurations in two-view reconstruction.
