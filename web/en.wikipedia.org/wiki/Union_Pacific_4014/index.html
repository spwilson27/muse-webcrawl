Union Pacific 4014

Union Pacific 4014 is a steam locomotive owned and operated by the Union Pacific Railroad (UP) as part of its heritage fleet. It is a four-cylinder simple articulated 4-8-8-4 "Big Boy" type built in 1941 by the American Locomotive Company (ALCO) of Schenectady, New York.

History

No. 4014 was one of twenty-five Big Boys built for Union Pacific to haul freight over the Wasatch Range between Ogden, Utah and Green River, Wyoming. It was retired in 1961 after travelling over a million miles, and was donated to the Railway and Locomotive Historical Society in Pomona, California.

Restoration and excursion service

Union Pacific reacquired the locomotive in 2013 and moved it to its steam shops in Cheyenne, Wyoming. After a multi-year restoration — including conversion from coal to oil firing — No. 4014 returned to steam in May 2019, debuting during celebrations of the 150th anniversary of the completion of the first transcontinental railroad. It is the world's largest operating steam locomotive and tours the Union Pacific system on excursions.
