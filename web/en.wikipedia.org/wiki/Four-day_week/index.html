Four-day week

The four-day week is an arrangement where a workplace or place of education has its employees or students work or attend school over a course of four days per week rather than the more customary five. This arrangement can be a part of flexible working hours, and is sometimes used to cut costs, improve work–life balance, or maintain productivity with fewer working days.

Models

Two main models exist. In the "100-80-100" model promoted by advocacy groups such as 4 Day Week Global, employees receive 100% of pay for 80% of the time in exchange for maintaining 100% of productivity. In compressed-hours models, employees work the same total hours over four longer days (for example, four 10-hour days).

Trials and evidence

Large-scale trials in Iceland (2015–2019) found productivity remained the same or improved and worker well-being increased. A 2022 United Kingdom pilot involving 61 companies reported stable or improved revenue and reduced staff attrition, with most participating companies continuing the policy. Microsoft Japan reported a 40% productivity increase in a 2019 trial. Critics note that results vary by sector and that compressed schedules can increase daily fatigue.
