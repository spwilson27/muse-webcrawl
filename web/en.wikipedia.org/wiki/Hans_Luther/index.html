Hans Luther

Hans Luther (10 March 1879 – 11 May 1962) was a German politician, lawyer and banker who served as Chancellor of Germany from January 1925 to May 1926. He later served as president of the Reichsbank (1930–1933) and as German ambassador to the United States (1933–1937).

Early career

Luther studied law and entered municipal administration, becoming mayor (Oberbürgermeister) of Essen in 1918. In the Weimar Republic he served as Minister of Food and Agriculture and then as Minister of Finance under Gustav Stresemann and Wilhelm Marx, playing a central role in ending the hyperinflation of 1923 through the introduction of the Rentenmark and fiscal stabilisation.

Chancellorship

Appointed Chancellor in January 1925, Luther headed a centre-right coalition. His government negotiated the Locarno Treaties of 1925, which normalised relations with France and Britain and prepared Germany's entry into the League of Nations. His second cabinet fell in May 1926 over a controversy concerning the presidential flag decree.

Later life

Luther became president of the Reichsbank in 1930 and resisted inflationary credit policies. Removed by the Nazi regime in 1933, he was sent as ambassador to Washington, serving until 1937. After the Second World War he advised on administrative reform in West Germany.
