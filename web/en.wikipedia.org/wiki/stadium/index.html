Stadium

A modern multi-purpose stadium.

A stadium (plural stadiums or stadia) is a place or venue for (mostly) outdoor sports, concerts, or other events, consisting of a field or stage either partly or completely surrounded by a tiered structure designed to allow spectators to stand or sit and view the event. The term derives from the Greek stadion, a unit of length (about 192 metres) and the footrace of that distance held in venues such as Olympia.

Many stadiums are named after sponsors, place names, or notable figures. The largest stadium in the world by capacity is the Narendra Modi Stadium in Ahmedabad, India (over 130,000, for cricket), followed by North Korea's Rungrado 1st of May Stadium. Famous examples include Wembley Stadium in London, the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro, Camp Nou in Barcelona, and the Colosseum of ancient Rome as a historic predecessor form.

Design

Stadium design must accommodate sightlines, crowd circulation and safety (a focus sharpened by disasters such as Hillsborough in 1989), pitch dimensions for the relevant sports codes, media and hospitality facilities, and increasingly retractable roofs and hybrid natural-synthetic turf. Domed and indoor stadiums are classed as arenas in North American usage. Modern venues emphasize multi-use programming — sports, concerts, and conferences — to remain financially viable.
