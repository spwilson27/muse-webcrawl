University of Gastronomic Sciences

The University of Gastronomic Sciences (Italian: Università degli Studi di Scienze Gastronomiche, UNISG) is a private university in Pollenzo, a frazione of Bra in Piedmont, Italy. Founded in 2004 by Carlo Petrini, the founder of the Slow Food movement, it was the first university in the world devoted entirely to the study of food and gastronomy.

Programmes

The university is housed in the Agenzia di Pollenzo, a nineteenth-century Savoy royal estate that also hosts the Wine Bank. It offers undergraduate and graduate degrees in gastronomic sciences, combining humanities, food science and technology, economics, and ecology. A hallmark of the curriculum is the study trip, in which students visit food producers and communities around the world to learn about local food cultures firsthand.

Background

UNISG emerged from Slow Food's philosophy of "good, clean and fair" food. Its students come from dozens of countries, and graduates work in food production, journalism, policy, and the restaurant world. The university is legally recognized by the Italian Ministry of Education.
