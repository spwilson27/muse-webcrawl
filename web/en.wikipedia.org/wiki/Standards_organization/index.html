Standards organization

A standards organization, standards body or standards developing organization (SDO) is an organisation whose primary function is developing, coordinating and publishing technical standards intended to address the needs of adopters. Standards may be voluntary consensus documents, produced through committees of interested parties, or regulatory standards imposed by governments.

International bodies

The three principal international standards organisations recognised by the WTO are the International Organization for Standardization (ISO, founded 1947, Geneva), the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC, 1906), and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU, 1865), a UN agency. ISO's standards — from the ISO 9000 quality family to paper sizes and country codes — are developed by national member bodies and adopted worldwide.

National and sectoral bodies

National bodies include BSI (United Kingdom, 1901 — the world's first national standards body), DIN (Germany), AFNOR (France), JISC (Japan) and ANSI (United States), which accredits developers such as ASME, ASTM International and IEEE-SA rather than writing most standards itself. Sector-specific organisations include the W3C and IETF for the internet, CEN and CENELEC in Europe, SAE International for automotive and aerospace, and 3GPP for mobile telecommunications.

Process and status

Consensus standards are drafted by technical committees, circulated for public comment, and revised periodically. They acquire legal force when referenced by legislation or contracts. Standards accepted by the whole industry through wide adoption are called de facto, while those issued by recognised bodies are de jure; open standards are publicly available for implementation, sometimes royalty-free.
