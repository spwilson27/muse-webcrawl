Round goby

The round goby (Neogobius melanostomus) is a bottom-dwelling fish of the family Gobiidae, native to the Black Sea and Caspian Sea basins of Eurasia. It has become a notorious invasive species in the Baltic Sea and in the Great Lakes of North America, where it was introduced in the ballast water of ships.

Description

Round gobies are soft-bodied fish, typically 10–25 cm long, mottled grey-brown with a characteristic black spot on the first dorsal fin. Like other gobies they have fused pelvic fins forming a suction disc that anchors them to rocks in currents. Males guarding nests turn almost black.

Behaviour and diet

The species is aggressive and territorial, feeding on molluscs (including invasive zebra and quagga mussels), crustaceans, insect larvae, small fish and the eggs of native fish. Females can spawn repeatedly through the summer, and males defend eggs under rocks, giving the goby a high reproductive rate.

Invasion

First recorded in the St. Clair River in 1990, the round goby spread throughout the Great Lakes within a decade and into the Mississippi basin via the Chicago canal system. It competes with and displaces native bottom fish such as sculpins and darters, eats the eggs of sport fish, and transfers botulism toxin from mussels to fish-eating birds. It has also spread through the rivers and canals of Europe into the Baltic and North Sea basins.
