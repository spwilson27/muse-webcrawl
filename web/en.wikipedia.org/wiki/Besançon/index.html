Besançon (French: [bəzɑ̃sɔ̃]) is a city in eastern France, close to the border with Switzerland. It is the prefecture of the Doubs department and was the regional capital of Franche-Comté until 2016, when it became part of Bourgogne-Franche-Comté. The historic city centre sits in a natural oxbow of the river Doubs, surrounded by hills, and is overlooked by the 17th-century Citadel designed by Vauban.

History

The site was known to Julius Caesar as Vesontio, capital of the Sequani, and became a Roman city of consequence on the route between Italy and the Rhine. A free imperial city in the Middle Ages, Besançon passed to Spain with the rest of Franche-Comté before being definitively annexed by France under the Treaty of Nijmegen in 1678. Louis XIV commissioned Vauban to build the massive Citadel that still dominates the city; its fortifications, together with the old town's defences, were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008.

Watchmaking

From the late 18th century, when Swiss watchmaker Laurent Mégevand established workshops in the city, Besançon became the capital of French watchmaking. At its peak the industry employed thousands, and the city retains a horological school, a time museum, and an astronomical clock in its cathedral famous for its 30,000 parts and multiple dials.

Culture

Besançon is the birthplace of Victor Hugo, Charles Nodier, and the Lumière brothers, Auguste and Louis. The University of Franche-Comté, founded in 1423 in Dole and later moved to Besançon, gives the city a large student population. The Citadel houses several museums and a zoo, and the city is known for its extensive green spaces, ranking among France's greenest cities.
