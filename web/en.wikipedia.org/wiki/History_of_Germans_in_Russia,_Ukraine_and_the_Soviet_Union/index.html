History of Germans in Russia, Ukraine and the Soviet Union

The history of Germans in Russia, Ukraine and the Soviet Union begins with German merchants and artisans in medieval Rus' and expands greatly after Catherine the Great's manifestos of 1762–63 invited foreign settlers, leading to large Volga German and Black Sea German colonies.

Imperial era

Settlers received land, tax exemptions and self-administration, founding hundreds of farming colonies along the Volga and in New Russia (present-day Ukraine). Baltic Germans formed an influential elite in the empire's western provinces. In the late 19th century many emigrated to the Americas as privileges were withdrawn and Russification advanced.

Soviet era

The Volga German ASSR existed from 1924 until 1941, when, after the German invasion, Stalin ordered the deportation of Soviet Germans to Siberia and Kazakhstan. After 1955 restrictions eased slowly; from the late 1980s large numbers emigrated to Germany as Aussiedler.
