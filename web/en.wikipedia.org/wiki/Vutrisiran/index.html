Vutrisiran

Vutrisiran, sold under the brand name Amvuttra, is a medication used to treat hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis (hATTR amyloidosis) with polyneuropathy, and later also transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy. It was developed by Alnylam Pharmaceuticals.

Mechanism of action

Vutrisiran is a small interfering RNA (siRNA) that uses RNA interference to silence the TTR gene in the liver, where most transthyretin protein is made. It is chemically stabilised and conjugated to a GalNAc (N-acetylgalactosamine) ligand that targets the asialoglycoprotein receptor on hepatocytes, allowing delivery by subcutaneous injection once every three months. By reducing production of both mutant and wild-type transthyretin, it slows the formation of the amyloid deposits that damage nerves and the heart.

Approval

The US Food and Drug Administration approved vutrisiran in June 2022 for adults with polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis, based on the HELIOS-A trial, which showed improvements in neuropathy impairment and quality of life compared with the earlier siRNA drug patisiran. In March 2025 its approval was extended to transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM), based on the HELIOS-B trial, which showed reduced mortality and cardiovascular events — making it the first RNAi therapeutic approved for that condition.

Adverse effects

Common adverse effects include pain in the extremities, arthralgia (joint pain), dyspnoea and injection-site reactions. Because transthyretin transports vitamin A, patients' vitamin A levels fall and supplementation is recommended.
