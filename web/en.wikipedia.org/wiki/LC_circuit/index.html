LC circuit

An LC circuit, also called a resonant circuit, tank circuit, or tuned circuit, is an electric circuit consisting of an inductor, represented by the letter L, and a capacitor, represented by the letter C, connected together. The circuit can act as an electrical resonator, an electrical analogue of a tuning fork, storing energy oscillating at the circuit's resonant frequency.

Operation

An LC circuit consists of an inductor and a capacitor connected in series or in parallel. The natural frequency at which the circuit oscillates — the resonant frequency — is determined by the values of inductance and capacitance: f = 1 / (2π√(LC)). At resonance, energy cycles back and forth between the magnetic field of the inductor and the electric field of the capacitor.

If a charged capacitor is connected across an inductor, charge begins to flow, building a magnetic field around the inductor and reducing the voltage across the capacitor. When the magnetic field fully collapses, it induces current in the opposite sense, recharging the capacitor with the opposite polarity. In an ideal lossless circuit this oscillation would continue indefinitely; in practice, resistance in the components and connecting wires dissipates energy as heat, damping the oscillation.

Applications

LC circuits are used either for generating signals at a particular frequency, or picking out a signal at a particular frequency from a more complex signal. They are key components in many electronic devices, particularly radio equipment, used in circuits such as oscillators, filters, tuners, and frequency mixers. The bandwidth and selectivity of an LC circuit are described by its Q factor.
