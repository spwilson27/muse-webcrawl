Non-local means

Non-local means is an algorithm in image processing for image denoising. Unlike "local" methods such as Gaussian smoothing or anisotropic diffusion, which compute the new value of a pixel from its immediate neighbourhood, non-local means takes a weighted average of pixels across the whole image — or a large search window — weighting each pixel by the similarity between the patch around it and the patch around the target pixel.

Principle

Natural images contain many repeated structures: textures, edges, and patterns recur in many places. The algorithm, introduced by Antoni Buades, Bartomeu Coll, and Jean-Michel Morel in 2005, exploits this redundancy. The restored value of a pixel is a weighted mean of all pixels in the image, with weights decaying exponentially with the squared Euclidean distance between the corresponding image patches and a filtering parameter h controlling the strength of smoothing.

Performance and variants

Non-local means preserves edges and fine texture far better than local filters, at the cost of high computational complexity, since every pair of patches in a search window must be compared. Many accelerations and variants have been proposed, including integral-image implementations, blockwise versions, and adaptations to video and volumetric data. The method helped inspire later patch-based and learning-based denoising algorithms such as BM3D.
