Azacitidine

Azacitidine, sold under the brand name Vidaza among others, is a medication used to treat myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and acute myeloid leukemia (AML). It is a chemical analogue of the nucleoside cytidine and acts as a hypomethylating agent.

Medical uses

Azacitidine is indicated for the treatment of patients with myelodysplastic syndromes, including refractory anemia, chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, and acute myeloid leukemia with 20–30% blasts. In 2020, an oral formulation (Onureg) was approved in the United States for continued treatment of adults with AML who achieved first complete remission after intensive induction chemotherapy.

Mechanism of action

Azacitidine is a pyrimidine nucleoside analogue of cytidine. At low doses it incorporates into DNA and RNA and irreversibly binds DNA methyltransferases, leading to hypomethylation of DNA and reactivation of silenced genes, including tumor suppressor genes. At higher doses it exerts direct cytotoxic effects on abnormal hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, fever, diarrhea, injection-site reactions, constipation, and myelosuppression (low blood counts), which can lead to infections and bleeding. Renal toxicity has also been reported.

History

Azacitidine was first synthesized in the 1960s and was initially investigated as an anticancer agent. It was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2004 for the treatment of myelodysplastic syndromes, making it the first drug approved for that indication.
