Magyarosaurus

Magyarosaurus is a genus of dwarf sauropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous period (Maastrichtian stage) of what is now Romania. It is one of the smallest-known adult sauropods, with mature individuals measuring only about 6 metres (20 ft) in length and weighing near one tonne. The type species, Magyarosaurus dacus, was described by Friedrich von Huene in 1932 from fossils found in the Hațeg Basin of Transylvania.

Magyarosaurus is a classic example of insular dwarfism: during the Late Cretaceous, the Hațeg region was an island in the Tethys Sea, and limited resources selected for smaller body sizes in its dinosaur fauna, which also included the dwarf hadrosaur Telmatosaurus and the theropod Balaur bondoc.

Discovery and naming

Remains were first found by Baron Franz Nopcsa, who in 1915 assigned them to Titanosaurus dacus. Von Huene later erected the new genus name Magyarosaurus, meaning "Hungarian lizard," referring to the region's history, combined with the species epithet dacus for the ancient Dacians.

Description

Histological studies of its bones show slowed growth rates and confirm that even the largest specimens were fully grown adults, supporting the dwarfism interpretation. Magyarosaurus was a titanosaur, a member of the great radiation of Cretaceous sauropods, and like other titanosaurs it was a quadrupedal herbivore with a long neck and tail.

Paleoecology

Magyarosaurus lived about 70 million years ago alongside other Hațeg island fauna, including rhabdodontids, nodosaurids such as Struthiosaurus, pterosaurs including the giant Hatzegopteryx, and various small mammals and lizards.
