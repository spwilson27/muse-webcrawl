MSN Messenger (later renamed Windows Live Messenger) was an instant messaging client developed by Microsoft. Launched in 1999 to compete with AOL Instant Messenger, it connected to the Microsoft Messenger service and became one of the most widely used messaging programs of the 2000s.

History

The first version offered simple text chat with a contact list. Later releases added voice and video calls, file transfers, games, emoticons, customisable display pictures, "nudges" that shook the contact's chat window, and "winks" — animated on-screen graphics. The service was renamed Windows Live Messenger in 2005 as part of Microsoft's Windows Live branding.

At its peak, the service had more than 300 million monthly users. In 2012, Microsoft announced it would retire the service in favour of Skype, which it had acquired in 2011. Windows Live Messenger was shut down in most countries in April 2013; the service ended in China, its last market, on 31 October 2014.

Features and culture

MSN Messenger was a fixture of early-2000s internet culture, particularly among teenagers, who used personal status messages, display names, and emoticons as forms of self-expression. The client integrated with Hotmail and other Microsoft services through a Microsoft account (then called a .NET Passport).
