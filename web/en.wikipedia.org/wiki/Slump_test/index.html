Slump test

Measuring the slump of fresh concrete after removing the cone

The concrete slump test measures the consistency and workability of fresh concrete before it sets. The test uses the slump cone (Abrams cone), a truncated cone mould 300 mm high, which is filled with concrete, compacted, and then lifted away; the decrease in height of the unsupported concrete — the "slump" — indicates its fluidity.

Procedure

The dampened cone is placed on a flat, rigid base plate and filled in three layers of equal volume, each rodded 25 times with a standard 16 mm tamping rod. The top is struck off level, the cone is raised vertically, and the slump is measured as the difference between the height of the mould and the highest point of the slumped concrete specimen. Standards include ASTM C143 in the United States and EN 12350-2 in Europe.

Interpretation

A true slump shows the concrete subsiding evenly; in a shear slump one half slides away, suggesting poor cohesion, and in a collapse slump the mix is too wet. Typical specified slumps range from near zero for stiff mixes used in road construction to 100 mm or more for heavily reinforced sections. Self-compacting concrete is too fluid for the test and is assessed with the slump flow test instead.

Limitations

The slump test is a simple field test but does not directly measure workability in the rheological sense; results depend on aggregate grading and the test is unsuitable for very dry or very wet mixes.
