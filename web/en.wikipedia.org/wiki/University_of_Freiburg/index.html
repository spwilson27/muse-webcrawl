University of Freiburg

The University of Freiburg (German: Albert-Ludwigs-Universität Freiburg) is a public research university located in Freiburg im Breisgau, Baden-Württemberg, Germany. Founded in 1457 by Archduke Albert VI of Austria, it is one of Germany's oldest universities and a leading research institution with around 25,000 students.

History

The university opened in 1460 with the four traditional faculties and grew into a centre of humanism; Erasmus of Rotterdam lived and taught in Freiburg from 1529. It passed between Habsburg and French control, was re-founded by Grand Duke Ludwig I of Baden in 1820 — hence its double name — and expanded into a comprehensive modern university. Freiburg was one of the eleven German universities selected in the Excellence Initiative.

Notable people

Philosopher Martin Heidegger served as rector in 1933–34. Other figures associated with Freiburg include Edmund Husserl, founder of phenomenology; economist Friedrich Hayek; sociologist Max Weber, who held a chair there in the 1890s; and Hannah Arendt, who studied in Freiburg. Numerous Nobel laureates have taught or studied at the university.
