AC Cobra

AC Cobra
Manufacturer: AC Cars / Shelby American
Production: 1962–1967
Class: Sports car / roadster
Engine: Ford V8, 260–427 cu in

The AC Cobra, sold in the United States as the Shelby Cobra, is a sports car manufactured by British company AC Cars with a Ford V8 engine. It was produced intermittently in both the United Kingdom and later the United States from 1962 to 1967.

History

In 1961 American racing driver and designer Carroll Shelby approached AC Cars with a proposal to fit a Ford V8 engine into the chassis of the AC Ace. The resulting Cobra combined a lightweight British roadster body with large-displacement American power, and quickly became a dominant force in sports-car racing.

Models

The Mark I and II cars used 260 and 289 cu in engines. The Mark III of 1965 used the 427 cu in (7.0 L) big-block, with flared arches and a stronger chassis; in competition form the Cobra won the 1965 FIA World Championship for GT Manufacturers, and the related Daytona Coupe won its class at Le Mans.

Legacy

Original Cobras are among the most valuable and most copied classic cars; numerous replica manufacturers have built continuations and tributes. See also Carroll Shelby.
