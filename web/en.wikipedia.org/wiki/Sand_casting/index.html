Sand casting

Sand casting, also known as sand moulded casting, is a metal casting process characterized by using sand known as casting sand as the mould material. The term also applies to an object produced by the process. More than 60% of all metal castings are produced by sand casting, because the moulds are inexpensive, the process tolerates very high melting temperatures, and parts from grams to hundreds of tonnes can be made.

Process

A pattern — a replica of the desired part, slightly oversized to allow for shrinkage — is packed in a mixture of sand and a bonding agent (clay and water for "green sand", or chemical resins) inside a two-part flask: the upper cope and lower drag. Removing the pattern leaves a cavity; cores of bonded sand form internal passages. A gating system of channels (sprue and runners) guides molten metal into the cavity, while risers feed extra metal as the casting solidifies and shrinks. After cooling, the mould is broken away — each sand mould is used only once — and the casting is cleaned and finished.

Materials and applications

Nearly any alloy can be sand cast, including steels, cast iron, aluminium, bronze, and magnesium. Typical products include engine blocks and cylinder heads, pump housings, large gears, machine-tool bases, pipes, and sculpture. Sand casting offers lower tooling cost than die casting or investment casting but poorer surface finish and dimensional accuracy, and it is suited to low and medium production volumes and very large parts.

Variants

  • Green sand casting — moist clay-bonded sand; the most common method.
  • Air-set / no-bake casting — chemically bonded sand that hardens at room temperature.
  • Shell moulding — thin resin-coated sand shells baked around a heated pattern, giving better finish.
