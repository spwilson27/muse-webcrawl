Ratz (Hebrew: ר״ץ), officially the Movement for Civil Rights and Peace, was a left-wing political party in Israel that existed from 1973 to 1997. Its name is the Hebrew acronym of its full title, Reshima Tziburit / HaTenu'a LeZkhuyot HaEzrah VeLaShalom. Founded by Shulamit Aloni after she left the Alignment, Ratz championed civil liberties, the separation of religion and state, women's rights and a negotiated two-state solution to the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

History

Aloni, a lawyer and journalist who had clashed with the Labour establishment over religious coercion and consumer rights, formed Ratz ahead of the 1973 Knesset elections and won three seats. She served briefly as a minister without portfolio in Yitzhak Rabin's first government before resigning over the appointment of a National Religious Party minister. In opposition, Ratz pressed for a written constitution, civil marriage, public transport on Shabbat and an end to religious exemptions from military service.

Peak and merger

The party reached its high point in the 1992 elections, winning six seats and entering Rabin's coalition government, with Aloni serving as Minister of Education. In March 1992 Ratz had already joined with Shinui and Mapam to form the Meretz alliance; in 1997 the three parties formally merged into a single party, Meretz, ending Ratz's independent existence. Its legacy persists in Israeli debates over civil rights, religious pluralism and peace activism, and through the Israel Women's Network and other civic organisations Aloni founded.
