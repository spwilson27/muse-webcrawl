The Randall–Sundrum model (also called the RS model, or warped geometry model) is a theoretical framework in particle physics proposed in 1999 by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum. It describes the universe as a five-dimensional anti-de Sitter space in which the elementary particles of the Standard Model are confined to a four-dimensional "brane," and it offers a geometric solution to the hierarchy problem — the enormous gap between the electroweak scale and the Planck scale.

The two models

In the original RS1 model, the fifth dimension is finite and bounded by two branes: the Planck brane, where gravity is strong, and the TeV brane, where the Standard Model resides. Because the metric of the extra dimension is exponentially "warped," energy scales on the TeV brane are suppressed by a warp factor of about 10−16 relative to the Planck brane, generating the weak scale from the Planck scale with only a modest size for the extra dimension. The RS2 model instead takes the fifth dimension to be infinite, with a single brane, and shows that four-dimensional gravity can still be recovered because gravity is localized near the brane.

Phenomenology

The model predicts Kaluza–Klein excitations of the graviton that could appear as resonances at the Large Hadron Collider, and it is closely connected to the AdS/CFT correspondence, under which the warped geometry is dual to a strongly coupled four-dimensional conformal field theory. No experimental evidence for the model's distinctive signatures has been found, and collider searches have placed lower bounds on the masses of the predicted graviton modes.
