Stoner film

A stoner film, also known as a stoner movie, is a subgenre of comedy film based around the recreational use of cannabis. Generally, cannabis use is one of the main themes and inspires much of the plot. They are often representative of cannabis culture.

Description

Many stoner movies have certain elements and themes in common. The template usually involves a protagonist or protagonists (often two friends in a variation of the buddy film) who have cannabis, are attempting to find more cannabis, or are experiencing the effects of cannabis. Missions in stoner movies are often ridiculous, with the characters frequently failing to remember what they set out to do or getting lost in tangential adventures.

Notable examples

The series of films from 1978 to 1985 starring Cheech and Chong are archetypal "stoner movies". The historic film Reefer Madness (1936) has also become popular as a stoner film in satire, because its message is an exaggerated warning against cannabis. Later classics include Dazed and Confused (1993), Friday (1995), The Big Lebowski (1998), Half Baked (1998), Harold & Kumar Go to White Castle (2004), and Pineapple Express (2008).

Stoner cross-genre

The stoner comedy has been combined with other genres: the stoner horror film (for example, Idle Hands and the Evil Bong series), the stoner action film, and the stoner road movie. Television series such as That '70s Show and Workaholics popularized stoner comedy for network and cable audiences.
