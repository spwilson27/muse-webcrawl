Alien hand syndrome
SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsA limb moves involuntarily, seemingly with a purpose of its own; loss of sense of ownership
CausesDamage to the corpus callosum, medial frontal lobe, or parietal lobe; stroke; neurodegeneration (e.g. corticobasal degeneration)
OnsetAfter brain injury or surgery

Alien hand syndrome (also called anarchic hand) is a rare neurological disorder in which a person's hand — occasionally a leg — performs complex, apparently purposeful movements that are not under the person's voluntary control. Patients often personify the limb, reporting that it "has a mind of its own", and may have to restrain it with the unaffected hand.

Signs and behaviour

The affected hand may unbutton clothing, grasp objects, interfere with tasks performed by the other hand, or in dramatic reported cases act aggressively toward the patient. The person retains sensation in the limb and usually knows it is their own body part anatomically, yet disowns its actions. Intermanual conflict — one hand undoing what the other has done — is characteristic of the callosal form; frontal variants feature compulsive grasping and groping, while a posterior (parietal) variant presents with levitation and withdrawal of the limb.

Causes

The syndrome classically follows surgical section of the corpus callosum (callosotomy) performed for epilepsy, and also occurs with strokes, tumours, aneurysms affecting the medial frontal lobe and supplementary motor area, and neurodegenerative diseases such as corticobasal degeneration and Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease.

Management

There is no cure; management is behavioural. Techniques include distracting the hand by giving it an object to hold, mirror-box and visual-feedback therapy, spatial recognition training, and, experimentally, injections of botulinum toxin or application of a restraining mitt.

In popular culture

The condition inspired the disobedient hand of Dr Strangelove in Stanley Kubrick's 1964 film Dr. Strangelove, although the term "alien hand" was only coined later, by the 1970s.
