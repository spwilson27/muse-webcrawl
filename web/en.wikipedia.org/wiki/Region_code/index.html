Region code

Region codes are a digital rights management technique introduced for DVDs, and later adapted for Blu-ray Discs and some video games, that allows film distributors to control aspects of a release, including content, release date, and price, according to region.

DVD regions

DVDs use region codes to restrict playback to players sold in a particular geographic area. There are eight commonly recognised codes:

CodeRegion
1United States, Canada, Bermuda, US territories
2Europe (except Russia, Ukraine, Belarus), Middle East, Egypt, Japan, South Africa, Eswatini, Lesotho, Greenland, French overseas departments
3Southeast Asia, South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macau
4South America, Central America, Caribbean, Mexico, New Zealand, Australia, Papua New Guinea, much of Oceania
5Africa, Russia and the rest of Asia (except China, Mongolia and the Southeast)
6China
7Reserved for future use (found in use on protected screener copies of films in Asia)
8International venues such as aircraft, cruise ships, spacecraft

Blu-ray regions

Blu-ray Discs use a simplified system of three regions: A (the Americas, most of East and Southeast Asia), B (Europe, Africa, Australia, New Zealand) and C (the rest of Asia, Russia, China).

Criticism

Region coding has been criticised for restricting legitimate purchasers and facilitating price discrimination. Many players can be modified, or were sold region-free, and the practice has declined with the rise of streaming.
