TypeSwitched-mode power supply
TopologyNon-isolated DC–DC step-down converter

A buck converter (also called a step-down converter) is a DC-to-DC power converter that steps down voltage from its input (supply) to its output (load) while stepping up current. It is a class of switched-mode power supply containing at least two semiconductors (a diode and a transistor, or two transistors in synchronous designs) and an inductor.

Operation

A controller switches the transistor on and off at high frequency. When the switch is on, current flows from the input through the inductor to the load and the output capacitor; when it is off, the inductor current freewheels through the diode (or a second transistor). The output voltage in continuous conduction mode is approximately the duty cycle multiplied by the input voltage, Vout ≈ D × Vin. A low-pass filter formed by the inductor and output capacitor smooths the pulsed current into a steady DC output.

Applications

Buck converters are among the most widely used circuits in electronics: they regulate power for computer processors (voltage regulator modules), USB-powered devices, battery chargers, LED drivers and point-of-load supplies, prized for efficiencies often exceeding 90 per cent, far better than linear regulators when the voltage difference is large.
