Pallas's cat
Scientific nameOtocolobus manul
FamilyFelidae
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)
RangeCentral Asian steppes and highlands

The Pallas's cat (Otocolobus manul), also known as the manul, is a small wild cat with a broad but fragmented distribution in the grasslands and montane steppes of Central Asia, from Iran and the Caucasus east to Mongolia and northwestern China. It is named after the German naturalist Peter Simon Pallas, who described it in 1776.

Description

About the size of a domestic cat, the manul has a stocky build, short legs, and exceptionally dense, long fur that gives it a stout, fluffy appearance and insulates it against cold winters. Its pupils contract into small circles rather than vertical slits, an unusual trait among small cats. The coat is ochre with dark vertical bars on the torso and forelegs, and the ears are set low and wide apart.

Behaviour and ecology

Pallas's cats are solitary and primarily crepuscular hunters of pikas, small rodents, gerbils, voles, and ground birds. They shelter in rock crevices and marmot burrows. Unusually for felids, their breeding season is extremely short — females are in estrus for only one or two days.

Conservation

Threats include habitat degradation, prey depletion from pika-poisoning campaigns, and hunting for fur historically. The species is listed as Least Concern but populations are declining in parts of the range and it is protected across much of it.
