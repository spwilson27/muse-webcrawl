Paul Ricard

Born9 July 1909, Marseille, France
Died7 November 1997, Signes, France
OccupationIndustrialist, founder of Ricard
Known forCreating Ricard pastis; Circuit Paul Ricard

Paul Louis Marius Ricard (9 July 1909 – 7 November 1997) was a French industrialist who created the anise-flavoured aperitif Ricard, the first commercially successful pastis, and built his family firm into one half of the drinks group Pernod Ricard, one of the largest spirits companies in the world.

The creation of pastis

Born in Marseille, where his father traded in wine, the young Ricard went into the family business instead of university. Absinthe had been banned in France in 1915, leaving a gap for aniseed aperitifs; home-distilled substitutes circulated during Prohibition-era restrictions. In 1932, after experimenting in a small laboratory, Ricard launched his own drink — a 45% ABV anise and liquorice aperitif diluted with water, which turns it milky — and campaigned with the slogan "Ricard, le vrai pastis de Marseille". When pastis production was authorised (after wartime prohibition under Vichy), Ricard's brand, backed by innovative marketing — distinctive yellow-and-blue livery, branded water jugs, sponsored cycling and sport — became and remains France's best-selling pastis.

Expansion and merger

Ricard diversified into other spirits and wines, becoming a fierce rival of the Pernod family firm, which made a competing anise drink. In 1975 the two rivals merged to form Pernod Ricard, with Paul Ricard retiring from executive roles soon after and his son Patrick later leading the group to global expansion. Paul Ricard was also a notable patron of the Mediterranean environment: he bought the island of Bendor near Bandol, developed it as an artists' retreat, and in 1950 founded, with others, an oceanographic institute there; he served as mayor of Signes in the Var for many years.

Circuit Paul Ricard

A motorsport enthusiast, Ricard financed the construction of the Circuit Paul Ricard at Le Castellet in Provence, opened in 1970, long noted for its long Mistral straight, safety run-off areas and later its distinctive blue-and-red striped abrasive zones as a high-tech test track (HTTT). The circuit hosted the French Formula One Grand Prix many times, returning to the calendar in 2018 after a long absence. He died at Signes in 1997, aged 88.
