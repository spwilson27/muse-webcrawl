Miguel Díaz-Canel

Miguel Díaz-Canel
Born: 20 April 1960, Placetas, Cuba
Office: President of Cuba (since 2019; previously President of the Council of State, 2018–2019)
Party: Communist Party of Cuba

Miguel Mario Díaz-Canel Bermúdez (born 20 April 1960) is a Cuban politician who has served as the president of Cuba since 2019 and as first secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba since 2021. He is the first person born after the Cuban Revolution, and the first person not named Castro, to lead the country since 1959.

Early life and career

Díaz-Canel was born in Placetas, Villa Clara Province, and graduated as an electronics engineer from the Central University of Las Villas in 1982. He rose through the Young Communist League and served as provincial party secretary in Villa Clara and Holguín, gaining a reputation as an accessible, bicycle-riding official. In 2009 he became Minister of Higher Education, and in 2013 first vice-president of the Council of State, making him Raúl Castro's deputy and designated successor.

Presidency

On 19 April 2018 Díaz-Canel succeeded Raúl Castro as head of state, and under the new 2019 constitution he took the restored title of President of the Republic. In April 2021 he also succeeded Castro as first secretary of the Communist Party. His tenure has been marked by a severe economic crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic, the nationwide anti-government protests of July 2021, widespread emigration, and periodic blackouts and shortages. His government oversaw monetary unification in 2021, whose inflationary effects compounded economic hardship, while maintaining the one-party system and alliance with the armed forces' economic conglomerate.
