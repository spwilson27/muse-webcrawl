The House of Representatives (Dutch: Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal, literally "Second Chamber of the States General") is the lower house of the bicameral parliament of the Netherlands, the States General, the other being the Senate (Eerste Kamer). It has 150 seats, filled through elections using a method of proportional representation, with a term of up to four years.

Functions

The House of Representatives is the primary legislative chamber: it has the right to propose and amend legislation, whereas the Senate can only approve or reject bills. Members (MPs) may question ministers, hold debates, submit motions, and conduct parliamentary inquiries. Together with the ministers, the House forms the core of political accountability in the Dutch parliamentary system; the government requires the confidence, or at least tolerance, of a majority in the chamber.

Elections

All Dutch citizens aged 18 and over may vote. Elections use a single nationwide constituency with open party lists and one of the lowest effective thresholds in the world, resulting in a highly fragmented multi-party system in which coalition governments are the norm. The 150 seats are allocated by the D'Hondt method after parties pass a threshold equal to one full seat's share of the vote.

History

The Tweede Kamer was established in 1815 when the States General became bicameral. Direct elections were introduced in 1848 under the constitution of Johan Rudolph Thorbecke, and universal suffrage followed in the early 20th century. The chamber sits at the Binnenhof in The Hague, though it temporarily relocated during the complex's extensive renovation.
