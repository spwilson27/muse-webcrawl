St. Peter's Basilica

The Papal Basilica of Saint Peter in the Vatican (Italian: Basilica Papale di San Pietro in Vaticano), or simply St. Peter's Basilica, is a church of the Italian High Renaissance located in Vatican City, the papal enclave in Rome. Designed principally by Donato Bramante, Michelangelo, Carlo Maderno, and Gian Lorenzo Bernini, it is the most renowned work of Renaissance architecture and, by interior measure, the largest church in the world. Catholic tradition holds that the basilica is the burial site of Saint Peter, chief of the apostles, whose tomb lies beneath the high altar.

History

The present basilica replaced Old St. Peter's, built by Emperor Constantine in the 4th century over the site venerated as Peter's grave. By the 15th century the old church was dilapidated, and Pope Julius II commissioned Bramante in 1506 to design an entirely new church on a Greek-cross plan crowned by a vast dome. Successive architects—including Raphael and Antonio da Sangallo—modified the design; Michelangelo, taking charge in 1546 at age 71, reasserted a centralised plan and designed the great dome, completed after his death by Giacomo della Porta in 1590. Carlo Maderno lengthened the nave and added the façade, and the church was consecrated in 1626. Bernini later created the vast elliptical St. Peter's Square with its colonnades and the bronze baldachin over the papal altar.

Architecture and art

The dome, the largest of its kind, dominates the Roman skyline; its silhouette inspired countless later domes, including those of St Paul's Cathedral and the United States Capitol. Inside, the basilica houses Michelangelo's Pietà (1498–99), carved when he was 24, Bernini's Baldachin and Cathedra Petri, and numerous papal tombs. The nave's floor bears markers comparing the lengths of other great churches. Inscribed around the dome's base are Christ's words to Peter: "You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church."

Significance

St. Peter's is one of the four major basilicas of Rome and the most prominent church of Roman Catholicism, though it is neither the Pope's cathedral (that is St John Lateran) nor formally a cathedral at all. It is the site of papal ceremonies drawing tens of thousands, and its square hosts the Pope's weekly audiences and Easter and Christmas blessings. The basilica and Vatican City together form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
