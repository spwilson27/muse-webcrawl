The Bishop of Truro is the ordinary of the Church of England's Diocese of Truro in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers the county of Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly, and the bishop's seat is at Truro Cathedral, notable as one of the few Anglican cathedrals built from scratch in England since the Reformation.

History

For centuries Cornwall belonged to the Diocese of Exeter. The Diocese of Truro was created in 1876 to serve the growing county, and Edward White Benson became its first bishop in 1877; he designed the famous Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols in 1880 before becoming Archbishop of Canterbury. He laid the foundation stones of the new cathedral, designed by J. L. Pearson, in 1880. The Bishop of Truro is assisted by the suffragan Bishop of St Germans. In 2018 Philip Mounstephen became bishop; he later moved to Winchester, and the see was subsequently filled by Hugh Nelson, formerly Bishop of St Germans.
