Bishop of Oxford

The Bishop of Oxford is the diocesan bishop of the Church of England Diocese of Oxford, covering Oxfordshire, Berkshire and Buckinghamshire. Considered from the vantage point of the Bishop of Truro, Oxford illustrates the older pattern of English sees: founded by Henry VIII in 1542, seated in a former college chapel that doubles as a cathedral, and ranked among the five bishops who take seats in the House of Lords ahead of ordinary seniority.

Role and history

The see was created at the Reformation, initially seated at Osney Abbey and then at Christ Church, whose chapel is the cathedral. The diocese grew in the 19th century by absorbing the archdeaconries of Berkshire and Buckinghamshire. Its bishops have included figures such as Samuel Wilberforce, and the bishop is assisted by area bishops of Dorchester, Buckingham and Reading.

Comparison with Truro

Where the Bishop of Truro leads a compact, single-county Cornish diocese founded in the Victorian church revival, the Bishop of Oxford leads one of the Church of England's largest dioceses by area and pays, and its holder is often drawn from academic life connected to the University of Oxford. Both bishops belong to the Province of Canterbury and share in the General Synod and House of Bishops.
