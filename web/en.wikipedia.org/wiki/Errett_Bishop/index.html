Errett Bishop

Errett Albert Bishop (July 14, 1928 – April 14, 1983) was an American mathematician known for founding constructive analysis.

Errett Albert Bishop (July 14, 1928 – April 14, 1983) was an American mathematician who did fundamental work in functional analysis and measure theory before becoming the leading figure of modern constructive mathematics. His book Foundations of Constructive Analysis (1967) demonstrated that large parts of classical analysis could be rebuilt on constructive foundations without loss of content.

Early life and classical work

Bishop was born in Newton, Kansas, the son of a mathematics professor; his sister Mary Bishop Aull also became a mathematician. He took his doctorate at the University of Chicago in 1954 under Paul Halmos. In classical mathematics he proved results including the Bishop–Phelps theorem on support functionals of convex sets, the Bishop–de Leeuw theorem in function algebras, and generalisations of the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, and he wrote with R. R. Phelps the influential monograph on function algebras. He joined the University of California, San Diego in 1965.

Constructive turn

In the mid-1960s Bishop became convinced that classical mathematics, by relying on non-constructive principles such as the law of excluded middle, had drifted from numerical meaning. In Foundations of Constructive Analysis he systematically developed real analysis, measure theory and functional analysis in a framework where every existence claim provides an algorithm. Unlike Brouwer's intuitionism, Bishop's constructivism added no controversial axioms, so his theorems remain valid classically, intuitionistically and recursively — a "common core" for schools of foundations. His critique anticipated later developments in computer-formalised mathematics, and his school was carried on by students and collaborators including Douglas Bridges and Hajime Ishihara.

Later years

Bishop's polemical lectures on the foundations crisis, collected posthumously, remain widely read. He died of cancer in 1983 at the age of 54.
