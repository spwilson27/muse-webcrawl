New York State Canal System

The New York State Canal System (formerly called the New York State Barge Canal) is a successor to the Erie Canal and other canals within New York State. The 525-mile (845 km) system is composed of the Erie Canal, the Oswego Canal, the Cayuga–Seneca Canal, and the Champlain Canal, and is operated by the New York State Canal Corporation.

History

In 1903 New York voters approved a referendum to rebuild the state's canals as a barge canal system, deepening and widening the channels and replacing aqueducts with movable dams and locks on canalised rivers. The new system opened in 1918. Commercial traffic, once dominated by grain and bulk cargo, declined through the 20th century as railways and highways took freight, and the St. Lawrence Seaway diverted shipping from 1959.

The system today

The canals are now used mainly for recreation — pleasure boats, tour boats and paddlers — along with some commercial traffic and uses such as water supply and flood control. The system's locks can accommodate vessels up to about 300 feet long and 43.5 feet wide. In 2017 the Canal Corporation became a subsidiary of the New York Power Authority. The canal corridor is promoted as a heritage and tourism region, with the Erie Canalway National Heritage Corridor following its route.
