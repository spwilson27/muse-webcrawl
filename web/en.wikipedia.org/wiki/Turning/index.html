Turning

Turning is a machining process in which a cutting tool moves linearly while the workpiece rotates about its own axis, removing material to produce cylindrical or conical surfaces. It is performed on a lathe and is one of the oldest and most fundamental manufacturing processes, used for both metalworking and woodturning.

Operations

Common turning operations include facing (machining the end of the workpiece), straight and taper turning, grooving and parting off, threading, boring (enlarging an internal diameter), knurling, and form turning. The principal parameters are cutting speed, feed rate, and depth of cut, chosen according to workpiece material, tool material (high-speed steel, carbide, ceramic, or diamond), and desired surface finish.

Equipment

Turning is done on engine lathes, turret lathes, and CNC turning centres, which may combine turning with milling in mill-turn machines. Woodturning lathes turn bowls, spindles, and furniture parts between centres or on a faceplate. Precision turning achieves tolerances of a few micrometres and fine finishes for shafts, bearings, and engine components.
