Samuel M. Raimi (born 23 October 1959) is an American filmmaker best known for creating the Evil Dead horror franchise and directing the original Spider-Man trilogy (2002–2007).

Career

Raimi rose to prominence with the low-budget horror hit The Evil Dead (1981), shot with friends including actor Bruce Campbell, and its sequels Evil Dead II (1987) and Army of Darkness (1992). His inventive, kinetic camera style — sometimes called "Sam-O-Cam" — became a signature. In the 1990s he moved between genres with Darkman (1990), the western The Quick and the Dead (1995) and the thriller A Simple Plan (1998).

His Spider-Man films with Tobey Maguire were major box-office successes that helped define the modern superhero genre. He later directed Drag Me to Hell (2009), Oz the Great and Powerful (2013) and Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022), and has produced numerous horror films through Ghost House Pictures.
