The Sumitomo Group (Japanese: 住友グループ, Sumitomo Gurūpu) is a Japanese corporate group and one of the largest keiretsu, descended from the Sumitomo zaibatsu. Its origins lie in a Kyoto shop founded around 1615 by Masatomo Sumitomo selling medicines and books, and in the copper-refining business of his brother-in-law Riemon Soga, whose nanban-buki refining technique became the foundation of Sumitomo copper production. From 1691 the family operated the Besshi copper mine on Shikoku, one of the largest copper mines in the world for nearly three centuries.

History

During the Meiji era the house diversified into banking (Sumitomo Bank, 1895), steel, machinery, chemicals, insurance, and warehousing. The zaibatsu holding company was dissolved by the Allied occupation authorities after the Second World War, but the member companies regrouped after the occupation around cross-shareholdings and the Hakasuikai ("White Water Society") presidents' council, forming the modern keiretsu. The group takes its name from its founder, and its members use the igeta "well-frame" trademark derived from the Sumitomo family crest.

Business principles

The group traces its ethos to Masatomo Sumitomo's Monjuin Shiigaki ("Founder's Precepts", c. 1650), which emphasises integrity, sound management, and not pursuing immoral or speculative profit — principles summarised as placing prime importance on integrity and sound management, and not pursuing easy gains.
