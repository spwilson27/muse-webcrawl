Madhavrao I (14 February 1745 – 18 November 1772) was the fourth Peshwa of the Maratha Confederacy, succeeding his father Balaji Baji Rao (Nanasaheb) in 1761. He came to power at the age of sixteen in the aftermath of the catastrophic Maratha defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat, and in a reign of eleven years restored Maratha power and prestige, defeating the Nizam of Hyderabad and Hyder Ali of Mysore, curbing the ambitions of his uncle Raghunath Rao, and re-establishing Maratha authority in northern India.

Early reign and regency

Madhavrao was appointed Peshwa on 20 July 1761. Because of his youth, his uncle Raghunath Rao acted as regent, but the two soon fell into conflict. Madhavrao relied on able administrators and soldiers including Nana Fadnavis, Mahadji Shinde, and Malhar Rao Holkar. In 1763 the Marathas under his leadership defeated the Nizam at the Battle of Rakshasbhuvan, a victory that enhanced his standing; tensions with Raghunath Rao nevertheless escalated into open war, and in 1768 the uncle was defeated and placed under confinement at Shaniwar Wada in Pune.

Campaigns

Madhavrao repeatedly campaigned against Hyder Ali of Mysore, defeating him in a series of engagements between 1764 and 1772 despite setbacks and illness. He reformed the administration, curbed the power of the hereditary deshmukhs, and improved revenue collection and military organisation. In the north, Maratha armies under his direction recovered lost ground: in 1771 they reoccupied Delhi, and in January 1772 they restored the Mughal emperor Shah Alam II to the capital under Maratha protection, undoing much of the Panipat settlement of 1761.

Death and legacy

Madhavrao's health, weakened by intestinal tuberculosis (or a related chronic illness), deteriorated in 1772, and he died at Theur, near Pune, on 18 November 1772, aged only 27. He was succeeded by his younger brother Narayan Rao. Historians, including James Grant Duff, have rated Madhavrao among the ablest of the Peshwas, crediting his short reign with the revival of the Maratha state after Panipat.
