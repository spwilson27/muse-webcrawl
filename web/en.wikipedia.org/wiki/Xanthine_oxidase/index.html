Xanthine oxidase

Xanthine oxidase (XO, sometimes XAO) is a form of xanthine oxidoreductase, an enzyme that generates reactive oxygen species and catalyses the oxidation of hypoxanthine to xanthine and of xanthine to uric acid, the final steps of purine degradation in humans. It is a large metalloflavoprotein found especially in the liver and intestine, and in considerable quantities in milk.

Structure and reaction

The enzyme is a homodimer of about 300 kDa. Each subunit contains a molybdopterin centre where substrate oxidation occurs, two iron–sulfur clusters, and a flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD) domain where oxygen is reduced. The oxidase form uses molecular oxygen as the electron acceptor, producing superoxide and hydrogen peroxide; it arises from a dehydrogenase form (xanthine dehydrogenase) by reversible oxidation of sulfhydryl groups or irreversible proteolysis, a conversion associated with tissue injury and ischemia–reperfusion.

Clinical significance

Because xanthine oxidase produces uric acid, its inhibition is the principal treatment for gout and hyperuricemia. Allopurinol — and its metabolite oxipurinol — and the non-purine inhibitor febuxostat block the enzyme and lower urate production. Defects in the enzyme (xanthinuria) lead to excessive xanthine excretion and xanthine kidney stones. The reactive oxygen species generated by the enzyme have also been implicated in cardiovascular and inflammatory conditions.
