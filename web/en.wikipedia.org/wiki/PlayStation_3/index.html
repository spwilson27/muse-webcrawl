PlayStation 3

The original PlayStation 3 (2006)

The PlayStation 3 (PS3) is a home video game console developed by Sony Computer Entertainment, released in Japan and North America in November 2006 and in Europe in March 2007. It is the successor to the PlayStation 2 and competed with Microsoft's Xbox 360 and Nintendo's Wii in the seventh generation of consoles.

Hardware

The PS3 was built around the Cell Broadband Engine, a multi-core processor co-developed by Sony, Toshiba and IBM, paired with the RSX graphics processor from Nvidia. It was the first console to use Blu-ray Disc as its standard game medium and offered HDMI output, built-in Wi-Fi (on most models), and the PlayStation Network online service, launched alongside the console.

Reception and sales

The console's launch was difficult: a high price ($499–$599 in the US), manufacturing costs, and a slow start in exclusive games meant it initially trailed its rivals. Slimmer, cheaper revisions in 2009 and 2012, together with acclaimed games such as Uncharted 2, The Last of Us, Metal Gear Solid 4 and Gran Turismo 5, revived its fortunes. By the end of production in 2017, the PS3 had sold over 87 million units worldwide. It was succeeded by the PlayStation 4 in 2013.
