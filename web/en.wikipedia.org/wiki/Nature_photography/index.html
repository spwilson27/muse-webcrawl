Nature photography

Nature photography is a wide range of photography devoted to natural elements such as landscapes, wildlife, plants, and close-ups of natural scenes and textures. Nature photography tends to emphasise the aesthetic value of the picture over other photographic genres such as photojournalism and documentary photography, although it often serves conservation causes as well.

Categories

The field is commonly divided into landscape photography, wildlife photography, macro and close-up photography of small subjects, and underwater photography. Competitions such as Wildlife Photographer of the Year and Nature Photographer of the Year showcase work in these categories, generally requiring that images not be excessively manipulated and that captive animals be disclosed.

History

Early landscape photographers such as Carleton Watkins and William Henry Jackson documented the American West in the 1860s and 1870s; Jackson's photographs of Yellowstone helped persuade the United States Congress to establish the world's first national park in 1872. In the twentieth century, Ansel Adams elevated landscape photography to fine art and used it in Sierra Club conservation campaigns. Colour nature photography reached mass audiences through magazines such as National Geographic.

Technique and ethics

Nature photographers commonly use tripods, long telephoto lenses for wildlife, neutral-density and polarising filters for landscapes, and techniques such as focus stacking for macro work. Ethical codes promoted by organisations such as the North American Nature Photography Association stress putting the welfare of subjects and habitats first: no baiting or harassment of animals, no trampling of fragile vegetation, and honest captioning.
