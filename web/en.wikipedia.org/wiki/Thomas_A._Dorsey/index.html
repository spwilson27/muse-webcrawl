Thomas A. Dorsey

Born: July 1, 1899, Villa Rica, Georgia, U.S.
Died: January 23, 1993 (aged 93), Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Genres: Gospel, blues
Occupations: Composer, pianist, singer
Known as: "The father of black gospel music"

Dorsey at the piano

Thomas Andrew Dorsey (July 1, 1899 – January 23, 1993) was an American musician, composer, and Christian evangelist influential in the development of early blues and 20th-century gospel music. He is widely regarded as the father of black gospel music.

Early career

Born in Villa Rica, Georgia, the son of a Baptist preacher, Dorsey moved to Atlanta and then to Chicago in 1916. As "Georgia Tom", he became a successful blues pianist and composer, recording with guitarist Tampa Red — their 1928 hit "It's Tight Like That" was a bestseller — and accompanying singers such as Ma Rainey.

Gospel music

Following personal tragedy — the deaths of his wife Nettie and their infant son in 1932 — Dorsey devoted himself entirely to sacred music. He wrote his most famous song, "Take My Hand, Precious Lord", in the aftermath of that loss; it became a standard of the civil rights era and a favourite of Martin Luther King Jr. In 1932 he founded the Pilgrim Baptist Church choir in Chicago and co-founded the National Convention of Gospel Choirs and Choruses, tirelessly promoting gospel music across the United States. His songs, including "Peace in the Valley", reshaped African-American worship by blending blues feeling with sacred texts.

Legacy

Dorsey wrote more than 400 blues and jazz songs and some 800 gospel songs. He was inducted into the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame and posthumously recognized with a Grammy Hall of Fame award for "Take My Hand, Precious Lord".
