The Wayside

The Wayside is a historic house in Concord, Massachusetts, United States, notable as the only home owned by author Nathaniel Hawthorne and for its associations with the Alcott family and children's author Margaret Sidney. It is now part of Minute Man National Historical Park.

History

The house began as a farmhouse along the Lexington Road (the "Battle Road" of April 19, 1775). In 1845 the educator Bronson Alcott bought it and named it "Hillside"; his family — including young Louisa May Alcott, who later drew on these years in Little Women — lived there until 1848, and the property sheltered fugitive slaves on the Underground Railroad. Hawthorne purchased the house in 1852, renamed it The Wayside, and lived there on and off until his death in 1864, adding a study tower he called his "sky parlour." There he wrote his last, unfinished works. In 1883 the house was bought by Boston publisher Daniel Lothrop and his wife Harriett, who wrote the "Five Little Peppers" books as Margaret Sidney; they preserved the house as a literary shrine. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1963 and transferred to the National Park Service in 1965.
