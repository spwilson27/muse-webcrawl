Suruga Trough

The Suruga Trough (Japanese: 駿河トラフ, Suruga Torafu) is a submarine trough running along the floor of Suruga Bay off the Pacific coast of Honshu, Japan. It marks the plate boundary where the Philippine Sea Plate subducts beneath the Eurasian (Amur) Plate, and it is the northern continuation of the Nankai Trough system.

Geology

The trough extends roughly 60 kilometres through Suruga Bay, reaching depths where the bay floor drops steeply — Suruga Bay is Japan's deepest bay, exceeding 2,000 metres. West of the Izu Peninsula the boundary continues as the Suruga Trough; eastward and southward it connects with the Sagami Trough. The subduction here converges at several centimetres per year, loading the plate interface with strain that is released in great earthquakes.

Tokai earthquakes

The Suruga Trough is the expected source of the long-anticipated "Tokai earthquake", a magnitude-8-class event beneath the Tokai region. Historical great earthquakes along the Nankai and Suruga troughs include the 1854 Ansei-Tokai earthquake and the 1944 Tonankai earthquake; the Tokai segment, however, did not rupture in the 1940s sequence, and Japanese authorities have monitored it intensively since the late 1970s under the Large-Scale Earthquake Countermeasures Act. The region's dense instrumentation makes it one of the most closely watched seismic zones in the world.

Ecology

The deep waters above the trough support a notable deep-sea fauna, studied by Japanese research submersibles and ROVs; Suruga Bay's steep profile brings deep-water species unusually close to shore.
