Interior (topology)

In mathematics, specifically in topology, the interior of a subset S of a topological space X is the union of all subsets of S that are open in X. A point that is in the interior of S is an interior point of S. The interior of S, denoted int(S), is the largest open set contained in S.

Definitions

A point x is an interior point of S if there exists a neighbourhood of x that is entirely contained in S. Equivalently, int(S) = X minus the closure of the complement of S, making interior and closure dual operations. The set of all boundary points of S equals its closure minus its interior.

Examples

  • In the real line, the interior of the closed interval [a, b] is the open interval (a, b).
  • The interior of the rational numbers within the reals is empty, because every open interval contains irrational numbers.
  • In the discrete topology every set equals its interior; in the indiscrete topology only the empty set and the whole space have nonempty interior possibilities.

Properties

The interior operator is monotone, idempotent (int(int(S)) = int(S)), and preserves finite intersections. It satisfies the four Kuratowski interior axioms, dual to the closure axioms. A set is open if and only if it equals its own interior.
