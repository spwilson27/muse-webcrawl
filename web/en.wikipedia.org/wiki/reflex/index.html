Reflex

The knee-jerk reflex arc: sensory input triggers a motor response via the spinal cord.

In biology, a reflex, or reflex action, is an involuntary, unplanned sequence or action and nearly instantaneous response to a stimulus. Reflexes are mediated by neural pathways called reflex arcs, which can act on an impulse before it reaches the brain, allowing faster protective responses. Scientific use of the term begins with the work of René Descartes and was developed by physiologists including Marshall Hall and Ivan Pavlov.

Types

Somatic reflexes include the patellar (knee-jerk) stretch reflex, the withdrawal (flexor) reflex from painful stimuli, and the crossed extensor reflex; autonomic reflexes govern pupil constriction (pupillary light reflex), salivation, swallowing, and the baroreceptor regulation of blood pressure. Newborns display primitive reflexes such as rooting, sucking, grasping, and the Moro reflex, which normally disappear as the nervous system matures.

Clinical significance

Testing deep tendon reflexes with a reflex hammer is a standard part of neurological examination. Exaggerated (hyperreflexia) or diminished (hyporeflexia) responses help localize lesions to the upper or lower motor neurons. Reflexes may also be conditioned — Pavlov's experiments showed that neutral stimuli can come to elicit reflex-like responses through association.
