Janka hardness test

The Janka hardness test (from the Austrian-born emigrant Gabriel Janka, 1864–1932) measures the resistance of a sample of wood to denting and wear. It measures the force required to embed an 11.28-millimetre-diameter (7⁄16 in) steel ball into wood to half the ball's diameter.

Method

The test, standardised as ASTM D143, leaves a circular indentation of 100 mm². Results are expressed in units of force: pounds-force (lbf) in the United States, kilograms-force in some countries, or newtons elsewhere. The test is usually performed on tangential, radial and end surfaces, and the commonly quoted figure is for the side grain.

Use

Janka ratings are the standard reference for comparing the suitability of woods for flooring and furniture. Ratings range from about 70 lbf for balsa to over 5,000 lbf for the hardest commercial timbers such as Australian buloke; common flooring species include red oak (1,290 lbf), hard maple (1,450 lbf) and Brazilian cherry (around 2,350 lbf or higher). The figures guide expectations of dent resistance, though they do not measure wear of the finish.
