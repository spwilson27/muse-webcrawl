Action Française

Action Française (AF) was a French far-right monarchist and nationalist political movement, founded in 1899 in the turmoil of the Dreyfus affair. Its leading figure was the writer and political theorist Charles Maurras, whose doctrine of "integral nationalism" placed the nation above all and advocated restoring the monarchy under the House of Orléans.

History

The movement's newspaper, L'Action française, edited by Léon Daudet, became one of the most influential — and vitriolic — journals of the French Third Republic, and its street militia, the Camelots du Roi, engaged in political violence. Condemned by Pope Pius XI in 1926, the movement nonetheless retained influence through the 1930s. Maurras welcomed the defeat of 1940 as a "divine surprise" and supported the Vichy regime; after the Liberation he was sentenced to life imprisonment and the movement was banned, though a successor newspaper and a small royalist organisation persisted under the name.
