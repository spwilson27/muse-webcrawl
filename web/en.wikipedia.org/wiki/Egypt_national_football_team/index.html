The Egypt national football team (Arabic: منتخب مصر لكرة القدم), nicknamed "the Pharaohs", represents Egypt in men's international football and is governed by the Egyptian Football Association (EFA), the governing body of football in Egypt. The team's historical stadium was the Cairo International Stadium, although matches are sometimes played at Borg El Arab Stadium in Alexandria.

Egypt is the most successful national team in Africa, having won the Africa Cup of Nations a record seven times: in 1957, 1959, 1986, 1998, 2006, 2008, and 2010. The victories of 2006, 2008, and 2010 formed an unprecedented three consecutive titles. Egypt was the first African nation to participate in the FIFA World Cup, appearing at the 1934 tournament in Italy, and has qualified three times in total (1934, 1990, and 2018).

History

The Egyptian Football Association was founded in 1921, and Egypt played its first international match in 1920 at the Antwerp Olympic Games, losing to Italy. Egypt won the inaugural Africa Cup of Nations in Sudan in 1957 and retained the title on home soil in 1959, competing as part of the United Arab Republic.

After a long gap, Egypt won its third continental title in 1986, defeating Cameroon in the final in Cairo. The golden generation of the 2000s, coached by Hassan Shehata and featuring players such as Mohamed Aboutrika, Ahmed Hassan, Essam El-Hadary, and Amr Zaki, dominated African football, winning three straight Africa Cup of Nations in 2006, 2008, and 2010.

Egypt returned to the World Cup in 2018 after a 28-year absence, inspired by Mohamed Salah, though the team was eliminated in the group stage. Egypt reached the final of the 2021 Africa Cup of Nations (held in 2022 in Cameroon), losing to Senegal on penalties.
