Diffuse interstellar bands

Image: Spectrum with diffuse interstellar bands

Diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs) are absorption features seen in the spectra of astronomical objects in the Milky Way and other galaxies. They are caused by the absorption of light by the interstellar medium along the line of sight to a bright background source. More than 500 bands have been catalogued, mostly in the visible and near-infrared.

History

The bands were first noted by Mary Lea Heger in 1919, whose observations were published in 1922; further bands were catalogued by Paul Merrill in the 1930s. Unlike ordinary interstellar absorption lines from atoms and small molecules, the DIBs are broad — several ångströms wide — implying carriers that are large molecules or solids.

Carriers

The identity of the DIB carriers was one of the longest-standing unsolved problems in spectroscopy. Leading candidates include polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, fullerenes, and other large carbon-bearing molecules. In 2015, laboratory spectra confirmed that the ionised fullerene C60+ is responsible for two near-infrared DIBs at 9577 and 9632 ångströms — the first definitive identification of a DIB carrier.
