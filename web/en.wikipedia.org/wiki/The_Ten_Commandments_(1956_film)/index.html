The Ten Commandments (1956 film)

The Ten Commandments is a 1956 American epic religious drama film produced, directed, and narrated by Cecil B. DeMille, shot in VistaVision (color by Technicolor), and released by Paramount Pictures. Based on the Bible's Book of Exodus, it dramatizes the life of Moses: his adoption into the Egyptian royal family, his discovery of his Hebrew heritage, his exile and marriage in Midian, his calling at the Burning Bush, and his leadership of the Exodus of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt, culminating in the parting of the Red Sea and the receiving of the Ten Commandments on Mount Sinai.

Plot

Rescued from Pharaoh's decree of death for Hebrew male infants, the baby Moses is raised as a prince of Egypt alongside Rameses, heir to the throne, and is courted by the princess Nefretiri. On learning of his true birth, Moses is exiled to the desert and becomes a shepherd in Midian, marrying Sephora. Called by God from the Burning Bush, he returns to Egypt to demand the Israelites' release; after the Ten Plagues and the first Passover, Rameses lets the people go, then pursues them to the Red Sea, which Moses parts by God's power. At Mount Sinai, while receiving the Law, Moses discovers the people's worship of the Golden Calf. Decades later, at the edge of the Promised Land, the aged Moses blesses Joshua and departs to Mount Nebo.

Production and reception

The film was DeMille's last and most successful work, a remake of his own 1923 silent film of the same name. Filmed partly on location in Egypt and the Sinai with sets of enormous scale, it employed one of the largest casts ever assembled, and its parting of the Red Sea—created by combining film of water dumped from huge tanks played in reverse—remains a landmark special effect. It won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects and was nominated for Best Picture. Adjusted for inflation, it is among the highest-grossing films of all time, and it has been broadcast annually on American television around Easter and Passover since 1973.
