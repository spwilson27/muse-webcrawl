Ici Radio-Canada Télé (stylised as ICI Radio-Canada TéLé) is a Canadian French-language free-to-air television network owned by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (known in French as the Société Radio-Canada). It is the French-language counterpart of CBC Television.

History

The network began broadcasting on 6 September 1952 from Montreal station CBFT, initially with bilingual programming before becoming fully French-language. It became the dominant television service for francophone Canada, especially Quebec, producing landmark programming such as the téléroman genre and the long-running hockey broadcasts of La Soirée du hockey.

Programming

The network airs news (Le Téléjournal), drama series, comedy, talk shows and cultural programming, largely produced in-house in Montreal. It is available across Canada via owned-and-operated stations, affiliates and cable.

Branding

In 2013 the service was rebranded "Ici Radio-Canada Télé" as part of a controversial rebranding that emphasised the word "Ici" ("here") across Radio-Canada's French-language services.
