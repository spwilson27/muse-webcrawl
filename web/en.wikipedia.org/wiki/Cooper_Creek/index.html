Cooper Creek (formerly Cooper's Creek) is one of the most famous and longest rivers in Australia. It forms where the Thomson River and the Barcoo River meet near Windorah in south-western Queensland, and flows about 1,300 kilometres south-west through the arid Channel Country into South Australia, reaching Lake Eyre only in exceptional flood years.

A waterhole on Cooper Creek in the Channel Country.

Course and hydrology

Rather than a single channel, Cooper Creek in its lower reaches is a vast network of braided channels, waterholes, and floodplains that can spread tens of kilometres wide in flood. Flow is entirely dependent on monsoonal rain far to the north; the creek may not flow for years, then carry enormous volumes. The basin is one of the largest in the world still free of major dams and irrigation diversions, and floods trigger spectacular breeding events for waterbirds such as pelicans.

History

The creek was named by explorer Charles Sturt in 1845 for Charles Cooper, the first Chief Justice of South Australia. It is best known for the Burke and Wills expedition of 1860–61: Robert O'Hara Burke and William John Wills died at a camp beside the creek near present-day Innamincka, and the sole survivor, John King, was kept alive by the local Yandruwandha Aboriginal people. The "Dig Tree" at the depot camp remains a historic site.

Present day

The Cooper Basin beneath the region is also a significant source of oil and natural gas, centred on Moomba. Towns on or near the creek include Windorah, Innamincka, and Birdsville (on the nearby Diamantina River).
