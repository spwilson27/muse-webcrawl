Dunfermline

Dunfermline (Scots: Dunfaurlin, Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Phàrlain) is a city and former royal burgh in Fife, Scotland, situated on high ground 5 kilometres northwest of the Firth of Forth. Historically one of the capitals of Scotland, it is the birthplace of King Charles I and of the industrialist-philanthropist Andrew Carnegie, and was granted city status in 2022 as part of the Platinum Jubilee civic honours.

History

Dunfermline became a royal centre under Malcolm III and Saint Margaret in the 11th century. Dunfermline Abbey, founded as a priory by Margaret and refounded by David I in 1128, became the burial place of Scottish monarchs, including Robert the Bruce, whose remains (apart from his heart) rest there. The adjacent Dunfermline Palace was a favoured royal residence; Charles I, the last Scottish-born monarch, was born there in 1600.

Landmarks and culture

Pittencrieff Park ("the Glen"), gifted to the town by Carnegie in 1903, adjoins the abbey ruins. Carnegie's birthplace cottage and the Dunfermline Carnegie Library (1883, the first of his libraries) stand nearby. The town's football club, Dunfermline Athletic, plays at East End Park and famously won the Scottish Cup in 1961 and 1968 under Jock Stein and George Farm. Amazon operates a major fulfilment centre in the town, a significant local employer.
