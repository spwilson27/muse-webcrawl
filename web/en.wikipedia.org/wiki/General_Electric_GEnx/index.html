General Electric GEnx

[Image placeholder: GEnx engine]

The General Electric GEnx ("General Electric Next-generation") is a high-bypass turbofan jet engine produced by GE Aerospace (formerly GE Aviation) for the Boeing 747-8 and 787 Dreamliner. Derived from the GE90, it incorporates technology from the CF6 and GE90 programmes and competes with the Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 on the 787.

Design

The GEnx uses a composite fan case and 18 composite fan blades with a bypass ratio of about 9.6:1 in the 787 variant, along with a counter-rotating low-pressure spool architecture, a lean-burn TAPS (twin-annular pre-swirl) combustor for lower emissions, and extensive use of bleedless systems on the 787, where the engine drives large electrical generators instead of supplying pneumatic bleed air. Thrust ratings range from roughly 53,000 to 75,000 pounds depending on variant (GEnx-1B for the 787, GEnx-2B for the 747-8).

Service history

The engine first ran in 2006 and entered service with the 747-8 Freighter in 2011 and the 787 in 2012. Early service revealed icing-related thrust-loss events at high altitude, leading to airworthiness directives and operational restrictions while GE introduced software and hardware fixes. A performance improvement package (PIP) increased durability and fuel efficiency. The GEnx became the best-selling engine on the 787, winning a majority of orders against the Trent 1000.
