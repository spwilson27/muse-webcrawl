Mansfield Dam is a concrete gravity dam on the Colorado River of Texas in Travis County, about 13 miles northwest of Austin. Built by the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) between 1937 and 1941, it impounds Lake Travis and was the largest of the Highland Lakes dams, created for flood control, water storage and hydroelectric power.

History

Originally called Marshall Ford Dam, the structure was renamed in 1941 for Congressman Joseph J. Mansfield, a champion of the project. Construction began in the Depression era with federal funding and employed thousands. Ranchland in the river valley was inundated as Lake Travis filled, covering the old community of Marshall Ford.

Features

The dam stands about 278 feet high and 7,089 feet long, including spillway sections with 24 floodgates. Its powerhouse contains three generators with a combined capacity of about 108 megawatts. Farm Road 620 crosses the Colorado just downstream, and Jones Park, at the dam's base, is a popular recreation site.
