Westonbirt, The National Arboretum is an arboretum in Gloucestershire, England, about three miles southwest of Tetbury. Founded in 1829 by Robert Stayner Holford and developed by his son George, it is one of the finest collections of temperate trees and shrubs in the world, with some 15,000 specimens from around the globe.

Collections

The arboretum divides into the Old Arboretum, laid out in the picturesque style with grand avenues, and Silk Wood, an ancient semi-natural woodland. Highlights include national collections of maples and other genera, and the site is especially famous for its autumn colour. A treetop walkway, opened in 2016, carries visitors through the canopy of the Old Arboretum.

Management

Westonbirt has been managed by the Forestry Commission (now Forestry England) since 1956 and receives several hundred thousand visitors a year.
