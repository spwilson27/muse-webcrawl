Mingachevir (Azerbaijani: Mingəçevir) is the fourth-largest city in Azerbaijan, with a population of around 106,000. It is located on the banks of the Kura River, about 323 kilometres west of Baku. The city grew up around the construction of the Mingachevir Hydroelectric Power Station and its dam, which created the Mingachevir Reservoir — the largest reservoir in Azerbaijan and the Caucasus — and it is often called the "city of lights" for its role in generating the country's electricity.

The Mingachevir dam and reservoir on the Kura River.

History

The Mingachevir area is archaeologically rich: excavations from the 1940s onward uncovered settlement layers and burial grounds spanning the Bronze Age to the late Middle Ages, including finds associated with Caucasian Albania. The modern city was founded in 1948 in connection with the dam project, which had been begun in 1945 and was completed in 1953–1954. German prisoners of war were among the labourers used in its construction. Textile, cable and machinery plants followed, making Mingachevir an industrial centre of the Azerbaijan SSR.

Energy

The Mingachevir Hydro Power Plant (about 420 MW) and the later Azerbaijan Thermal Power Plant (2,400 MW, the largest power station in the country) make the city the centre of Azerbaijan's electricity industry. The reservoir also supplies irrigation water through the Upper Karabakh and Upper Shirvan canals.

Sport

Mingachevir is a centre for rowing and canoeing in Azerbaijan; its "Kur" Olympic training base hosted the canoe sprint events of the 2015 European Games in Baku. The city's football club is Energetik FC, reflecting its energy-industry identity.
