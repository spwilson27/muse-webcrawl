Tsukihime (Japanese: 月姫, "Moon Princess") is a Japanese adult visual novel created by the dōjin circle Type-Moon, written by Kinoko Nasu with art by Takashi Takeuchi. Released in parts at Comiket in 2000, it became a landmark of dōjin game culture and launched Type-Moon's commercial success, preceding their later hit Fate/stay night. A ground-up remake, Tsukihime: A Piece of Blue Glass Moon, was released for PlayStation 4 and Nintendo Switch in 2021, with an English localisation in 2024.

Plot

Shiki Tohno, a high-school student who survived a near-fatal accident as a child, can see "Lines of Death" on all things—the Mystic Eyes of Death Perception—letting him kill anything by tracing those lines. Returning to his family's mansion after years away, he impulsively kills a strange woman in the street, who reappears the next day unharmed: she is Arcueid Brunestud, a vampire princess hunting a reincarnating enemy, and she conscripts the terrified Shiki as her bodyguard. The story branches into routes focusing on Arcueid and the church executor Ciel (the "Near Side" of the moon) and on the maids Hisui and Kohaku and Shiki's sister Akiha (the "Far Side"), exploring the Tohno family's dark secrets.

Reception and adaptations

Produced on a small budget, Tsukihime sold remarkably for a dōjin title and is credited with transforming the visual novel medium's ambitions. It was adapted into a manga (2003–2010) by Sasaki Shōnen and a 12-episode anime television series, Lunar Legend Tsukihime (2003), the latter poorly received by fans of the original. The fighting-game spin-off Melty Blood (2002) became a long-running franchise in its own right. The 2021 remake rewrote and expanded the Near Side routes with modern production values and new characters, with a sequel covering the Far Side routes announced.
