Menominee Indian Reservation

Menominee Indian Reservation
Location: Wisconsin, United States
Area: approx. 235,000 acres
Tribal headquarters: Keshena

The Menominee Indian Reservation is an Indian reservation located in northeastern Wisconsin held in trust by the United States for the Menominee Tribe of Wisconsin. The reservation is largely coextensive with Menominee County, Wisconsin, and is one of only two counties in the eastern United States composed almost entirely of reservation land (the other being portions of New York). Its largest community is Keshena, where the tribal headquarters is located.

History

The reservation was established by treaty in 1854, when the Menominee ceded much of their remaining land in Wisconsin but retained a tract along the Wolf River. During the federal termination era, the Menominee Termination Act of 1954 ended federal recognition and converted the reservation into Menominee County in 1961. Termination proved economically disastrous, and after sustained activism—including the organization DRUMS (Determination of Rights and Unity for Menominee Shareholders)—Congress passed the Menominee Restoration Act in 1973, restoring federal recognition and trust status. The Menominee were the first terminated tribe to be restored.

Forestry

The reservation is renowned for its sustainably managed forest, one of the most famous examples of sustainable forestry in North America. Roughly 220,000 acres are forested, and the tribe has practiced sustained-yield selective harvesting since the nineteenth century; over more than a century of logging, the standing timber volume has increased while billions of board feet have been harvested. The Menominee forest is often described as healthier and more diverse than when management began.

Communities

Communities on the reservation include Keshena, Neopit, Zoar, and Legend Lake. The reservation's population is approximately 3,000–3,500, most of whom are enrolled Menominee tribal members.
