Thoughtworks (stylised as ThoughtWorks until 2021) is a global technology consultancy that provides software design, development, and consulting services. Founded in Chicago in 1993 by Roy Singham, the company grew into one of the best-known firms associated with agile software development.

History and influence

Thoughtworks consultants were prominent among the signatories and popularisers of the Agile Manifesto (2001); Martin Fowler, the company's chief scientist, has written influential works on refactoring, continuous integration, and microservices. The company developed or popularised practices and tools including continuous delivery, the Thoughtworks Technology Radar (a periodic assessment of tools and techniques), and products such as the build tool CruiseControl and the test-automation framework Selenium, which originated at the company. It also published the manifesto for software craftsmanship and other industry-shaping material.

Ownership

Thoughtworks was sold to the private equity firm Apax Partners in 2017 and listed on the NASDAQ in 2021. In 2024–2025 the company agreed to be taken private again by Apax Partners. The company has offices in many countries across the Americas, Europe, Asia, and Australia, and is known for an emphasis on social and economic justice within its business model.
