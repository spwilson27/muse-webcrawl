Inhour

The inhour (inverse hour) is an obsolete unit of reactivity of a nuclear reactor. It was defined as the reactivity that would give a reactor an asymptotic period of one hour — that is, the neutron population would increase by a factor of e in one hour.

Definition

Because the period depends on the reactor's neutron generation time, the inhour is not an absolute measure of reactivity: the same reactivity in dollars or pcm corresponds to different numbers of inhours in different reactor designs. Reactors with shorter neutron lifetimes change power more rapidly for a given reactivity insertion.

Usage

The unit arose in early reactor operations and appears in older literature and operating logs. Modern practice expresses reactivity in dimensionless units such as Δk/k, pcm (per cent mille), or dollars and cents based on the delayed neutron fraction.
