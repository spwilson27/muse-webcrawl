Manawydan fab Llŷr is a figure of Welsh mythology, the son of Llŷr and brother of Bran the Blessed and Branwen. He is the central character of the Third Branch of the Mabinogion, Manawydan fab Llŷr, and appears in the Second Branch as one of the seven survivors of Bran's disastrous expedition to Ireland. He is often compared with the Irish sea god Manannán mac Lir, with whom he shares a name and maritime associations.

In the Third Branch

Returning from Ireland, Manawydan declines to press a claim to the throne and travels with his friend Pryderi to Dyfed, where he marries Pryderi's widowed mother, Rhiannon. A magical mist descends on Dyfed, emptying the land of all its people and livestock except Manawydan, Rhiannon, Pryderi and Pryderi's wife Cigfa. The four support themselves by crafts in English towns, but are repeatedly driven out by jealous local tradesmen.

Back in Dyfed, Pryderi and Rhiannon are trapped by an enchanted golden bowl in a mysterious fortress and vanish. Manawydan, farming with Cigfa, catches a mouse stealing his wheat and resolves to hang it. Interceding strangers offer increasing ransom; the mouse proves to be the transformed wife of Llwyd ap Cil Coed, who had cast the enchantment on Dyfed in revenge for an insult to his friend Gwawl. Manawydan negotiates the lifting of the spell and the release of Pryderi and Rhiannon, restoring Dyfed.

Character

Manawydan is portrayed as patient, resourceful and self-controlled, in contrast to the impulsive heroes of the other branches; his tale is notable for its grounding in work, craft and negotiation rather than battle.
