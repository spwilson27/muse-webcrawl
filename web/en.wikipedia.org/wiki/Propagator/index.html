Propagator

In quantum field theory, a propagator is a function that species the probability amplitude for a particle to travel from one place to another in a given period of time, or to travel with a certain energy and momentum. In Feynman diagrams, which serve to calculate the rate of collisions in quantum field theory, virtual particles contribute their propagator to the rate of the scattering event described by the respective diagram.

Non-relativistic propagators

In non-relativistic quantum mechanics, the propagator gives the probability amplitude for a particle to travel from one spatial point at one time to another spatial point at a later time; mathematically it is the Green's function for the Schrödinger equation, and the wave function at a later time can be obtained by convolving the propagator with the initial wave function.

Relativistic propagators

In relativistic field theory, the scalar (Feynman) propagator is the Green's function of the Klein–Gordon equation, expressed in momentum space as i/(p2 − m2 + iε). Analogous expressions exist for fermions (the Dirac propagator) and for gauge bosons, each tied to the field's equations of motion and to the choice of gauge or boundary conditions. Propagators also appear outside physics: in horticulture, a propagator is a covered tray or mini-greenhouse used to germinate seeds and root cuttings. See also Feynman diagram.
