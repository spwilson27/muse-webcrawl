Glasgow Outcome Scale

The Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) is a global rating of functional outcome after brain injury, introduced by Bryan Jennett and Michael Bond in 1975. It classifies patients into five ordered categories based on their ability to function independently.

Categories

The five categories are: (1) death; (2) vegetative state; (3) severe disability — conscious but dependent; (4) moderate disability — disabled but independent; and (5) good recovery — resumption of normal life, possibly with minor deficits.

Extended scale

The Glasgow Outcome Scale–Extended (GOSE) subdivides the upper three categories into eight levels in total, using a structured interview, and is widely used as an endpoint in trials of traumatic brain injury treatment. It is distinct from the Glasgow Coma Scale, which measures acute consciousness rather than outcome.
