The European perch (Perca fluviatilis), also called the common perch, redfin perch or simply perch, is a predatory freshwater fish native to Europe and northern Asia. It is recognisable by its greenish flanks with dark vertical bars, spiny first dorsal fin and reddish-orange lower fins.

Description

European perch are deep-bodied perciform fish, commonly 25–30 cm long and around 1 kg, though exceptional specimens exceed 50 cm. The first dorsal fin is spiny and bears a black spot at its rear; the pelvic, anal and caudal fins are tinged red or orange.

Distribution and ecology

The species inhabits lakes, ponds, slow rivers and brackish estuaries across Europe and Siberia. It has been widely introduced elsewhere, including Australia — where it is known as the redfin and regarded as a pest — New Zealand and South Africa. Young perch form shoals and feed on invertebrates and small fish; larger individuals are increasingly piscivorous, and cannibalism is common. Spawning takes place in spring, the female laying long ribbons of eggs over vegetation and submerged branches.

Relationship with humans

Perch are a valued angling species and a popular food fish, particularly in central and northern Europe, where fillets of small perch are a traditional dish. The species is the national fish of Finland.
