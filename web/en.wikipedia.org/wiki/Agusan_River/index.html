The Agusan River is a river in the Caraga region of northeastern Mindanao in the Philippines. It is the third-longest river in the country, at roughly 390 kilometres (240 mi), and drains one of the largest river basins in the Philippines, covering much of the provinces of Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, and Davao de Oro.

Course

The river's headwaters rise in the mountains of Davao Oriental and Davao de Oro (formerly Compostela Valley) in southeastern Mindanao. It flows northward through the broad, fertile Agusan Valley between the Diwata Mountains to the east and the central cordillera to the west, passing the municipalities of Compostela, Trento, Bunawan, Esperanza, and others, before emptying into Butuan Bay at the city of Butuan.

Agusan Marsh

In its middle course the river spreads into the Agusan Marsh, a vast wetland of about 15,000 hectares (with a much larger floodplain) of lakes, swamp forest, and floating settlements. The marsh acts as a natural flood-retention basin and is one of the most ecologically significant wetlands in the Philippines, home to species such as the Philippine crocodile. It was declared a wildlife sanctuary and listed as a Ramsar site.

Use and floods

The river has historically been a transport artery for the logging and agricultural produce of the valley, and its basin supports rice, banana, and palm-oil cultivation. The low-lying valley is prone to severe flooding during the northeast monsoon and tropical cyclones, periodically inundating towns along the river.
