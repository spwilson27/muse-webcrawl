Melrose, Scottish Borders

Melrose (Scottish Gaelic: Maol Ros, "bare moor") is a small town and civil parish in the Scottish Borders, Scotland, historically in Roxburghshire. It lies on the River Tweed, in the shadow of the Eildon Hills, and is best known for the ruins of Melrose Abbey and as the birthplace of rugby sevens.

History

The original monastery of Old Melrose was founded around 660 AD by St Aidan and is associated with St Cuthbert. The Cistercian abbey of Melrose was founded in 1136 by David I of Scotland; its ruins, largely dating from later rebuilding after English attacks, include some of the finest Gothic architecture in Scotland. Robert the Bruce's heart is traditionally said to be buried at the abbey.

Rugby

Melrose is famous as the home of rugby sevens: the game was devised in 1883 by local butcher Ned Haig and his employer David Sanderson as a fundraising event for Melrose RFC. The annual Melrose Sevens tournament is held each spring and the town's Greenyards ground is a pilgrimage site for rugby fans.
