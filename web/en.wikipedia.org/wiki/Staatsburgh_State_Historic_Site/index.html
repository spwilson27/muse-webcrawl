Staatsburgh State Historic Site

Staatsburgh State Historic Site, also known as Mills Mansion, is a historic estate and mansion located in Staatsburg, in the town of Hyde Park, Dutchess County, New York, overlooking the Hudson River. The Beaux-Arts mansion was the country home of Ruth Livingston Mills and Ogden Mills, prominent figures of Gilded Age society. It is preserved by the New York State Office of Parks, Recreation and Historic Preservation and is open to the public for tours and events.

History

The estate's history reaches back to the colonial era: the land formed part of a patent granted in the late seventeenth century, and the hamlet of Staatsburg takes its name from Dr. Samuel Staats, an early settler. In the nineteenth century the property passed to Morgan Lewis, a governor of New York, whose great-granddaughter Ruth Livingston married the financier Ogden Mills.

In 1895 the Millses commissioned the architectural firm McKim, Mead & White, with Stanford White overseeing the design, to greatly enlarge the existing house into a 65-room Beaux-Arts mansion clad in white, with lavish interiors in French styles of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The estate, of roughly 1,600 acres at its height, served as the family's autumn residence and a hub of Gilded Age entertaining.

Donation and preservation

In 1937, Gladys Mills Phipps donated the mansion and some 192 acres to the State of New York in memory of her parents. The house retains much of its original furniture, artwork, and decorative objects, and is interpreted today as an example of the great country houses of the Hudson Valley. The grounds, with views west across the Hudson River to the Catskill Mountains, are open year-round; the mansion offers guided tours, themed exhibitions, and popular seasonal programmes such as its Gilded Age Christmas displays.
