Foreign Protestants

The Foreign Protestants were a group of non-British Protestant settlers recruited by the British government in the mid-18th century to settle in Nova Scotia, Canada. Between 1749 and 1753, approximately 2,500 settlers — mostly from the Palatinate and other German-speaking regions, the principality of Montbéliard, and Switzerland — emigrated to the colony, seeking religious freedom, land, and relief from war and hardship in Europe.

Settlement

The immigrants initially landed at Halifax, founded in 1749, where many spent several difficult years waiting for promised farmland, suffering disease and poor conditions. In 1753, the majority were relocated to found the town of Lunenburg on the South Shore, laid out on a grid plan typical of British colonial towns. The settlers brought with them distinctive building traditions, fishing and farming practices, and Lutheran and Reformed congregations. Their German language survived in the community for generations.

Legacy

The Foreign Protestants became some of the founding families of Lunenburg County, and their descendants spread throughout Nova Scotia. Old Town Lunenburg is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised for its well-preserved British colonial grid plan and wooden architecture. The settlers' surnames — many anglicised over time — remain common in the region.
