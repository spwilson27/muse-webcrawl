Afghan Civil War (1992–1996)
Date28 April 1992 – 27 September 1996
LocationAfghanistan, centred on Kabul
ResultTaliban victory; capture of Kabul and proclamation of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan; conflict continued as the 1996–2001 civil war
BelligerentsIslamic State of Afghanistan (Rabbani/Massoud) and allies; Hezb-e Islami (Hekmatyar); Junbish (Dostum); Hezb-e Wahdat; Taliban (from 1994)

The Afghan Civil War of 1992–1996 was the war fought between rival mujahideen factions for control of Afghanistan after the collapse of the communist Republic of Afghanistan in April 1992. The power-sharing arrangements of the Peshawar Accord quickly broke down, and the capital Kabul was devastated by artillery and rocket battles between the forces of the Islamic State of Afghanistan government (President Burhanuddin Rabbani and his defence minister Ahmad Shah Massoud), Gulbuddin Hekmatyar's Hezb-e Islami, Abdul Rashid Dostum's Junbish militia and the Hazara Hezb-e Wahdat, among others.

Course of the war

Fighting in Kabul began almost immediately in 1992 and continued in shifting alliances — Dostum's forces allied with and later against both Massoud and Hekmatyar; the 1993 Afshar operation and other episodes saw mass atrocities against civilians, documented by human-rights groups. Tens of thousands of Kabul residents were killed and much of the city destroyed. In late 1994 the Taliban movement emerged from Kandahar, presenting itself as a force for order, and advanced rapidly with Pakistani support, taking Herat in 1995 and, after failed offensives, capturing Kabul on 27 September 1996. The deposed government forces withdrew to the northeast to form the Northern Alliance, and the war continued in a new phase until 2001.

Foreign involvement and consequences

Pakistan, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Russia, India and Uzbekistan all backed favoured factions with money, arms and sanctuary. The war destroyed what remained of Afghan state institutions, fragmented the country among warlords, drove millions into exile, and created the conditions in which the Taliban took power and al-Qaeda found sanctuary — setting the stage for the post-2001 conflict.
