The Automatic Identification System (AIS) is an automatic tracking system that uses transponders on ships and is used by vessel traffic services (VTS). AIS transceivers broadcast information — including the vessel's identity (MMSI and name), position, course and speed from GPS — at regular intervals on two dedicated VHF channels, so that nearby ships and shore stations can track traffic and avoid collisions.

Operation

Class A transponders report dynamic data every 2 to 10 seconds while underway depending on speed, and static and voyage-related data (ship type, dimensions, destination, draught) every six minutes. Class B devices for smaller craft transmit less frequently at lower power. Time slots are organised by self-organising time-division multiple access (SOTDMA). Satellite AIS receivers extend coverage to the open ocean.

Applications

AIS data feeds maritime domain awareness, search and rescue, fisheries monitoring and commercial ship-tracking websites. Because AIS transmissions are unauthenticated, vessels can falsify or discontinue transmissions ("going dark"), an issue in sanctions evasion and illegal fishing; spoofed AIS positions have also been documented.
