OriginDalmatian coast, Croatia

The Marasca cherry (Croatian: višnja maraska) is a type of sour Morello cherry of the cultivar Prunus cerasus var. marasca, grown mainly on the Dalmatian coast of Croatia, particularly around Zadar. Small, dark, and almost black when ripe, it has a dry flesh and a distinctive bitter-almond flavour contributed by its high content of aromatic compounds.

Uses

Marasca cherries are the traditional base for maraschino liqueur, produced since the 16th century by Dalmatian merchants and famously by the Luxardo firm, founded in Zara (Zadar) in 1821. Distilled cherry wine sweetened and aged gives the clear liqueur used in classic cocktails such as the Aviation and the Last Word. Traditionally made cocktail garnish cherries were preserved in maraschino liqueur; the mass-produced bright-red "maraschino cherry" of today is a different, sweetened product, usually based on Royal Ann cherries.

Cultivation

The variety thrives in the dry, rocky soils of the Zadar hinterland and Dalmatian islands. Harvested by hand in early summer, the fruit is also used for juices, jams, and cherry brandy (višnjevac).
