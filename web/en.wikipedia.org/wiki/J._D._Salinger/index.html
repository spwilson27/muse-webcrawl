Jerome David Salinger (1919–2010).

Jerome David Salinger (January 1, 1919 – January 27, 2010) was an American author best known for his 1951 novel The Catcher in the Rye. Raised in Manhattan, Salinger began writing short stories while in secondary school and published several in the early 1940s before serving in the Second World War, where he landed at Utah Beach on D-Day and fought in the Battle of the Bulge.

The Catcher in the Rye

Salinger's only novel follows the disaffected teenager Holden Caulfield over a few days in New York City after his expulsion from prep school. Written in Holden's distinctive vernacular voice, it became an enduring emblem of adolescent alienation and remains widely read and frequently challenged; it has also been notoriously associated with several violent acts, including the murder of John Lennon, whose killer carried a copy.

Later work and seclusion

Salinger followed the novel with the story collections Nine Stories (1953), Franny and Zooey (1961), and Raise High the Roof Beam, Carpenters and Seymour: An Introduction (1963), many centred on the fictional Glass family. His last published work, the novella "Hapworth 16, 1924," appeared in The New Yorker in 1965. Thereafter he withdrew to Cornish, New Hampshire, living as a recluse, publishing nothing further, and vigorously defending his privacy in court while reports suggested he continued to write daily. He died of natural causes at his home in 2010, aged 91.
