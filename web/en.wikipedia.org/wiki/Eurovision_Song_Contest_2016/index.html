The Eurovision Song Contest 2016 was the 61st edition of the Eurovision Song Contest. It took place in Stockholm, Sweden, following the country's victory at the 2015 contest with the song "Heroes" by Måns Zelmerlöw. Organised by the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) and host broadcaster Sveriges Television (SVT), the contest was held at the Globe Arena and consisted of two semi-finals on 10 and 12 May, and a final on 14 May 2016. The shows were presented by Petra Mede and Måns Zelmerlöw.

Forty-two countries participated. The contest was won by Ukraine with the song "1944", performed and written by Jamala. Australia finished second, achieving its best result to date, and Russia placed third.

Format changes

The 2016 contest introduced the most significant change to the voting system since 1975. Each country's jury vote and televote were awarded separately as two distinct sets of points, rather than being combined into a single score. This doubled the points available and was designed to reduce the effect of neighbourly voting and increase suspense during the announcement of results. Under the new system, the winner was not known until the final televotes were revealed.

Winning song

"1944" was inspired by the deportation of the Crimean Tatars under Joseph Stalin, drawing on the experiences of Jamala's great-grandmother. Its victory was widely discussed because of the song's historical and political themes. Ukraine returned to the contest in 2016 after withdrawing in 2015.
