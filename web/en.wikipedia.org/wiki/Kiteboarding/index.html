Kiteboarding, also known as kitesurfing, is a water sport that combines aspects of surfing, windsurfing, paragliding, snowboarding and wakeboarding. A kiteboarder harnesses the power of the wind with a large controllable power kite to be propelled across the water on a kiteboard, a board similar to a surfboard or wakeboard but without bindings in most disciplines. Hydrofoiling and course racing variants use foilboards and specialised race boards.

The sport grew rapidly from its commercialisation in the late 1990s, and its racing class, Formula Kite, made its Olympic debut at the Paris 2024 Summer Olympics.

History

In the 19th century, Samuel Cody developed kites capable of man-carrying and used them to cross the English Channel in a collapsible canvas boat. In 1977 Gijsbertus Adrianus Panhuise obtained the first patent for a water sport using a parachute-like sail connected by a harness. Through the 1980s and 1990s, inventors including the Legaignoux brothers in France and Bill and Cory Roeseler in the United States developed practical inflatable kites and two-line re-launchable systems. Dominique and Bruno Legaignoux patented the inflatable leading-edge kite design in 1984, which became the basis of most modern kites. Commercial kiteboarding took off around 1998–2000, and the sport's first world championships were held in Hawaii.

Equipment

Kites

Modern power kites are typically leading-edge inflatables (LEIs) or foil kites, sized between about 5 and 18 square metres depending on wind strength and rider weight. Kites are flown on four or five lines connected to a control bar, which the rider uses to steer the kite and adjust its angle of attack ("sheeting in and out") to control power.

Boards

The most common board is the twin-tip, which can be ridden in either direction. Directional surf-style boards are used for wave riding, and hydrofoil boards — mounted on a mast and wing — allow riding in lighter winds and are used in Olympic racing.

Harness and safety

Riders wear a waist or seat harness that connects the control bar's chicken loop to the body, transferring the kite's pull away from the arms. Safety systems include a quick-release that detaches the kite from the rider and a kite leash that flags the kite out on one line.

Styles

Freeride is recreational cruising; freestyle emphasises jumps and unhooked tricks; wave riding uses surf-style boards on ocean waves; course racing and slalom are regatta disciplines; and big-air competitions reward height and hang time. Snowkiting and landboarding apply the same kites to snow and land.

Safety

Kiteboarding can be hazardous when practised without training: gusts, offshore winds and obstacles near launch areas are the main risks. Lessons from certified instructors, appropriate safety equipment and adherence to local right-of-way rules are strongly recommended.
