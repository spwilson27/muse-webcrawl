Tamada

A tamada (Georgian: თამადა) is a toastmaster at a Georgian feast (supra). The tamada presides over the table, sets the order and themes of toasts, and is responsible for maintaining the rhythm of drinking, eating, conversation and song throughout the banquet. The role is considered an honour and demands eloquence, wit, knowledge of tradition and the ability to gauge the mood of the guests.

Duties

The tamada proposes the toasts in a traditional order — commonly beginning with a toast to God or to peace, then to children, to parents (living and departed), to women, to friendship, to the homeland, and to the guests — elaborating each theme with stories, poems and blessings. No one drinks out of turn; guests may ask the tamada's permission to offer their own toast, and a toast may be "passed on" (alaverdi) to another guest, who must elaborate it before drinking. The tamada may appoint a deputy, the merikipe, who pours wine and enforces the tamada's decisions.

Cultural significance

Statues of a tamada — a seated figure raising a drinking horn — are a well-known symbol of Tbilisi, based on a 7th-century BC bronze figurine discovered in western Georgia. In modern Russian and Ukrainian usage, the word tamada has broadened to mean a professional master of ceremonies at weddings and celebrations.
