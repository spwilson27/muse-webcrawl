M61 Vulcan

M61 Vulcan
Type: Rotary cannon
Origin: United States
Manufacturer: General Dynamics
Calibre: 20×102 mm
Rate of fire: up to 6,000 rounds/min
In service: 1959–present

The M61 Vulcan is a hydraulically, electrically, or pneumatically driven, six-barrel, air-cooled, electrically fired Gatling-style rotary cannon which fires 20 mm rounds at an extremely high rate — typically 6,000 rounds per minute. Developed by General Electric in the late 1940s–1950s as Project Vulcan, it has been the standard internal gun of US fighter aircraft for over half a century.

Design

The six barrels rotate as a cluster; each barrel fires once per revolution, spreading heat and wear and allowing the very high rate of fire. Ammunition is fed linklessly from a drum in most aircraft installations. The cannon uses electric priming and requires external power to spin up, reaching full rate in about half a second.

Variants and applications

The M61A1 is fitted in the F-14, F-15, F-16, and F/A-18; the lighter M61A2 was developed for the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and F-22 Raptor. The gun also arms the Phalanx CIWS naval close-in weapon system (as the Mk 15) and the M163 VADS self-propelled anti-aircraft gun (M168). A three-barrel 25 mm derivative, the GAU-22/A, arms the F-35.

History

After World War II the US Air Force sought a gun able to hit fast jet aircraft in brief firing windows. Scaling up the 19th-century Gatling principle with electric drive, General Electric delivered the T171, standardized as the M61 in 1959; it first saw combat in Vietnam on the F-105 and later the F-4 Phantom II.
