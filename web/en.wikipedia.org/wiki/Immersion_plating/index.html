Immersion plating

Immersion plating is a chemical (electroless) plating process in which a metal object is coated by immersing it in a solution containing ions of a more noble metal. The coating forms by a displacement (galvanic replacement) reaction: atoms of the less noble substrate dissolve while ions of the coating metal are reduced and deposited on the surface.

Characteristics

Because the reaction depends on the substrate itself, deposition stops once the surface is fully covered, so immersion coatings are typically very thin — often well under a micrometre. Unlike electroplating, no external current is required.

Applications

Common examples include immersion gold in the ENIG (electroless nickel immersion gold) surface finish for printed circuit boards, immersion tin and immersion silver finishes, and the tinning of copper cookware.
