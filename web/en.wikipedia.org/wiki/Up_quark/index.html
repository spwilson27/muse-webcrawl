The up quark or u quark (symbol: u) is the lightest of all quarks, a type of elementary particle, and a significant constituent of matter. It, along with the down quark, forms the neutrons (one up quark, two down quarks) and protons (two up quarks, one down quark) of atomic nuclei. It is part of the first generation of matter, has an electric charge of +2/3 e and a bare mass of 2.2 MeV/c². Like all quarks, the up quark is an elementary fermion with spin 1/2, and experiences all four fundamental interactions: gravitation, electromagnetism, weak interactions, and strong interactions.

History

The existence of the up quark was first postulated in 1964, when Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig developed the quark model. Evidence for quarks came from deep inelastic scattering experiments at the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center in 1968, which showed point-like constituents inside the proton. The up antiquark is denoted ū.

Properties

Up quarks have flavour quantum numbers including isospin of +1/2. They combine with down quarks through the strong force, mediated by gluons, to make up the nucleons that form almost all ordinary visible matter. Because the up quark is lighter than the down quark, the proton is lighter than the neutron and is stable, which is why hydrogen nuclei do not decay. The mass of the up quark, quoted in the MS-bar scheme at 2 GeV, is approximately 2.2 MeV/c², though it had been notoriously difficult to determine precisely because quarks are confined and never observed in isolation.
