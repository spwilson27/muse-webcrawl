Tuffi (born 1947 in India; died 1989 in Paris) was a female Asian elephant who became famous in Germany after she fell from the Wuppertal Schwebebahn suspension monorail into the River Wupper in 1950, surviving with minor injuries. She remains a beloved mascot of the city of Wuppertal.

The incident

On 21 July 1950 the circus director Franz Althoff arranged a publicity ride in which the four-year-old Tuffi travelled in a Schwebebahn carriage over the Wupper, accompanied by journalists and officials. Disturbed by the rattling car and the flashing cameras, the young elephant trumpeted, broke through the side of the carriage, and plunged about twelve metres into the river below. She suffered only minor injuries, as did no one else — though several passengers were hurt in the ensuing panic. Althoff was fined for the stunt. The famous photograph of Tuffi in the river is actually a photomontage produced afterwards, which became one of Germany's best-known postcard images.

Later life and remembrance

Tuffi continued performing and was eventually sold to the Cirque Alexis Gruss in Paris, where she died in 1989. In Wuppertal she is commemorated by a mural of her likeness on a building near the spot, by the naming of a local milk brand ("Tuffi" dairy products), and by countless souvenirs; the spot where she landed is marked beneath the line between Adlerbrücke and Alter Markt stations.
