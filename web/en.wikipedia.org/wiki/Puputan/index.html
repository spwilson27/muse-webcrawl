Puputan

[Image: The puputan of 1906: Balinese royalty advancing on Dutch guns at Denpasar (contemporary illustration)]

Overview

A puputan is a mass ritual suicide in Balinese culture, historically performed by the royalty and court of a Balinese kingdom when facing defeat and dishonour at the hands of an enemy — the court, dressed in white ceremonial clothing, would march out to meet death rather than surrender. The term derives from Balinese puput, "finished" or "ended".

History

The most famous puputans occurred during the Dutch conquest of Bali: at Denpasar in 1906, when some 1,000 Balinese of the Badung court, including the raja, died before Dutch rifles (many by their own or attendants' krises), and at Klungkung in 1908, the last Balinese kingdom. Reports of the ritual killing shocked Dutch opinion and pushed the colonial government toward its "Ethical Policy" and preservationist stance toward Balinese culture. Puputan is distinct from mesatya (widow self-immolation), also practised in old Bali.
