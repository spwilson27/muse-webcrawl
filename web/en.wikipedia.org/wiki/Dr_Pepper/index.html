Dr Pepper is a carbonated soft drink created in the 1880s by pharmacist Charles Alderton in Waco, Texas, and first served around 1885. It is marketed as having a unique flavour, said by the company to be a blend of 23 flavours, and is neither a cola nor a root beer; the recipe remains a trade secret. Dr Pepper predates Coca-Cola by about a year and is one of the oldest major soft drinks in the United States.

History

Alderton, working at Morrison's Old Corner Drug Store in Waco, experimented with fruit syrup combinations and offered the result to store owner Wade Morrison, who found it popular with customers. The drink was introduced nationally at the 1904 Louisiana Purchase Exposition in St. Louis. The origin of the name is disputed; one common story holds that Morrison named it after Dr. Charles Pepper, a Virginia physician whose daughter Morrison had courted — a tale the company itself treats cautiously.

Marketing

Memorable advertising campaigns include the "10-2-4" slogan urging a drink at those hours, "Be a Pepper" from the 1980s, and "23 flavours". The period after "Dr" was dropped from the logo in the 1950s for legibility. The Dr Pepper Museum occupies the 1906 bottling plant in Waco. In much of the United States the brand is owned by Keurig Dr Pepper, while in many other countries it is licensed to Coca-Cola or PepsiCo bottlers.

Variants

Variants include Diet Dr Pepper (1962), Dr Pepper Cherry, Dr Pepper Strawberries & Cream and numerous limited editions. Dublin Dr Pepper, made in Dublin, Texas with cane sugar until 2012, became a cult favourite among enthusiasts.
