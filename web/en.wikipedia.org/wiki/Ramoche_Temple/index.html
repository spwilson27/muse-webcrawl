Ramoche Temple

Ramoche Temple (Tibetan: ར་མོ་ཆེ་དགོན་པ་) is a Buddhist temple in Lhasa, Tibet, considered one of the city's most important temples after the Jokhang Temple, of which it is sometimes called the sister temple.

History

The temple was built in the mid-7th century, in the time of Emperor Songtsen Gampo, originally to house a statue of the Buddha brought to Tibet by the Chinese princess Wencheng. After the statue — the Jowo Mikyo Dorje — was later moved to the Jokhang, Ramoche received the Jokhang's Jowo Sakyamuni statue; the two statues were subsequently returned to their present arrangement, with the revered Jowo in the Jokhang and the Mikyo Dorje in Ramoche.

The temple was repeatedly damaged by fire and invasion, including during the Mongol incursions and the Cultural Revolution, and underwent substantial restoration in the 1980s. It covers about 4,000 square metres, combines Tibetan and Han Chinese architectural features, and forms part of the pilgrimage circuit of old Lhasa.
