Pariser Platz (German: [paˈʁiːzɐ plats], literally "Paris Square") is a square in the centre of Berlin, Germany, situated by the Brandenburg Gate at the end of the Unter den Linden boulevard. The square is named after the French capital Paris to commemorate the victory of the Sixth Coalition over France and the occupation of Paris in 1814.

History

The square was laid out between 1732 and 1734 as part of the baroque expansion of Berlin under Frederick William I and was initially called Quarré or Viereck. It was renamed Pariser Platz in 1814. In the 19th and early 20th centuries it was one of Berlin's finest addresses, home to aristocratic palaces, the French embassy (Palais Beauvais), and from 1860 the rebuilt Academy of Arts. Max Liebermann had his home and studio at Pariser Platz 7.

The square was heavily damaged in the Second World War. In the divided city it lay in the death strip along the Berlin Wall, in East Berlin just behind the Brandenburg Gate, and its ruins were cleared, leaving an empty space for four decades.

Redevelopment

After German reunification in 1990, Pariser Platz was rebuilt under strict design guidelines requiring stone façades and heights matching the historic fabric. New buildings include the Academy of Arts (on the site of its predecessor), the French Embassy, the DZ Bank building by Frank Gehry (with its glass-domed conference room), the American Embassy (opened 2008), and the reconstructed Hotel Adlon (reopened 1997). The square is now a pedestrian-oriented space and a principal ceremonial gateway of the reunified capital.
