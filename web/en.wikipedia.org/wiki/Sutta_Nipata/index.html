Sutta Nipata

The Sutta Nipāta ("Section of Discourses") is a Buddhist scripture, a collection of 72 suttas in verse and mixed prose and verse, forming part of the Khuddaka Nikāya of the Sutta Piṭaka in the Pāli Canon of Theravada Buddhism. It is considered by many scholars to contain some of the oldest material in the canon, preserving an early stratum of Buddhist teaching focused on the wandering ascetic life.

Contents

The work is divided into five chapters (vagga):

  • Uraga Vagga — beginning with the Uraga Sutta ("The Snake"), on shedding defilements as a snake sheds its skin.
  • Cūḷa Vagga — including the Ratana Sutta (on the "three jewels"), the Mahāmaṅgala Sutta (the "great blessings", widely chanted as a protective text), and the discourse to the brahmin Dhāniya.
  • Mahā Vagga — containing the famous Pabbajjā and Padhāna suttas about the Buddha's renunciation and struggle with Māra.
  • Aṭṭhaka Vagga ("Octads") — sixteen poems praised for their austere teaching on non-clinging and the abandonment of views; commented on in the canon itself (the Niddesa) and echoed in Chinese Āgama parallels.
  • Pārāyana Vagga ("The Way to the Beyond") — sixteen questions posed to the Buddha by the students of the brahmin Bāvarī, plus a prologue and epilogue.

Other well-known pieces include the Mettā Sutta (Karaṇīyamettā Sutta) on loving-kindness, the Kasibhāradvāja Sutta, and the Khaggavisāṇa Sutta, the "Rhinoceros" discourse praising solitary wandering.

Dating and significance

The Aṭṭhaka and Pārāyana vaggas are frequently dated by scholars to the earliest period of Buddhist literature, partly because of their archaic language and because they attracted an ancient commentary. The collection emphasises renunciation, non-attachment, meditation, and freedom from dogmatic views rather than monastic rules or systematic doctrine, giving it lasting influence; the Mettā Sutta and Mahāmaṅgala Sutta remain among the most recited texts in Theravada countries.
