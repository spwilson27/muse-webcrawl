Israʼiliyyat

Field: Islamic studies, tafsir

Meaning: Narratives of Israelite origin

Sources: Jewish and Christian traditions

Israʼiliyyat (Arabic: إسرائيليات, "Israelite matters") is a genre of narrative material in Islamic tradition originating from Jewish and Christian sources, used especially in Quranic exegesis (tafsir) and the stories of the prophets (qisas al-anbiya). These narratives fill in details about biblical figures mentioned briefly in the Quran, drawing on traditions transmitted by converts to Islam and other informants familiar with the Bible and midrash.

Attitudes toward the genre

Classical scholars took varied positions: some, like al-Tabari, transmitted Israʼiliyyat extensively with chains of narration; others, notably Ibn Kathir and Ibn Taymiyya, urged caution, dividing the material into reports consistent with the Quran, reports contradicting it (to be rejected), and reports neither confirmed nor denied (about which one suspends judgment).

Content

Typical Israʼiliyyat include elaborated accounts of creation, the patriarchs, Moses, and other prophets, sometimes attributed to transmitters such as Ka'b al-Ahbar and Wahb ibn Munabbih. Modern scholarship studies the genre as evidence of cultural exchange in the early Islamic world.
