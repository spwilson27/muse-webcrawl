Firefighting in France

Firefighting in France is carried out by the sapeurs-pompiers, organized principally at the level of the departments through the departmental fire and rescue services (SDIS). France has approximately 250,000 firefighters, of whom the great majority — around 80 percent — are volunteers; the remainder are professionals employed by local government, plus military firefighters in Paris and Marseille. The French fire services respond to millions of interventions each year, the majority of which are emergency medical and rescue calls rather than fires.

Organization

Each of France's departments has a Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS), a public body responsible for fire stations, personnel and equipment within its territory. Overall policy and coordination are overseen by the Direction générale de la sécurité civile et de la gestion des crises (DGSCGC) within the Ministry of the Interior.

Military fire brigades

  • The Paris Fire Brigade (Brigade de sapeurs-pompiers de Paris, BSPP), founded by Napoleon in 1811 after the fire at the Austrian embassy ball, is a unit of the French Army and serves Paris and the surrounding petite couronne departments.
  • The Marseille Naval Fire Battalion (Bataillon de marins-pompiers de Marseille), founded in 1939, is a unit of the French Navy.

History

Organized fire protection in Paris dates to royal ordinances of the early 18th century and the introduction of hand pumps. The Paris brigade's military status dates from an imperial decree of 18 September 1811. In the 20th century, the French services increasingly took on emergency medical response, and the volunteer model was embedded in the departmental structure by reforms in the 1990s.
