SPICE (Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis) is a general-purpose, open-source analogue electronic circuit simulator. It is a program used in integrated circuit and board-level design to check the integrity of circuit designs and to predict circuit behaviour before manufacture.

History

SPICE was developed at the Electronics Research Laboratory of the University of California, Berkeley, under the direction of Donald Pederson, building on the earlier CANCER program. SPICE1 was released in 1973; the widely used SPICE2, written in FORTRAN under Laurence Nagel, followed in 1975; SPICE3 (1985) was rewritten in C by Thomas Quarles. Because Berkeley distributed the source code freely, SPICE became the industry standard for circuit simulation.

Use and derivatives

The user describes a circuit as a text-based netlist of components (resistors, capacitors, transistors and so on) and specifies analyses such as DC operating point, AC frequency response and transient simulation. Commercial descendants and compatible programs include PSpice, HSPICE, LTspice and the open-source ngspice. The SPICE netlist format remains a lingua franca of electronic design automation.
