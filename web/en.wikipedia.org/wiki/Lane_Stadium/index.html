Lane Stadium is a college football stadium in Blacksburg, Virginia, on the campus of Virginia Tech. It is the home field of the Virginia Tech Hokies football team. The playing surface is named Worsham Field. Since opening in 1965 it has been expanded repeatedly and now seats 65,632, making it one of the larger stadiums in the Atlantic Coast Conference, and it is consistently ranked among the most intimidating home environments in American college football.

History

The stadium is named for Edward Hudson Lane Sr., a Virginia Tech alumnus and founder of the Lane Company (maker of cedar chests), who led the fundraising for its construction. It replaced Miles Stadium; the first game was played in 1965 while construction was still incomplete, and the stadium was finished in 1968. Major expansions added the south end zone seating (1999 and 2002), a new press box and luxury suites on the west side (2005), and the enclosed west stands, bringing capacity to its present figure. At about 2,057 feet (627 m) elevation it is one of the highest FBS stadiums east of the Mississippi.

Atmosphere and traditions

Lane Stadium is famed for the Hokies' entrance: the team runs onto the field to Metallica's "Enter Sandman" while the crowd jumps in unison — an effect so vigorous it has registered on local seismographs — and for the playing of "Stick It In" by the marching band and the lunch pail tradition honouring the blue-collar "Lunch Pail Defense" of longtime coordinator Bud Foster. After the April 2007 campus shooting, the stadium hosted the university's convocation and a memorial game atmosphere that drew national attention; Virginia Tech's first home game of the 2007 season at Lane was one of the most emotional in the sport's history.
