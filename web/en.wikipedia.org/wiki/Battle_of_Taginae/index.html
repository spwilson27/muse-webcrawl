Battle of Taginae

The Battle of Taginae (also Battle of Busta Gallorum) was fought in July 552 in the Apennines of Italy during Justinian's Gothic War. The Byzantine general Narses decisively defeated the Ostrogoths; their king Totila was mortally wounded, and Byzantine reconquest of Italy became likely.

Background

Totila had recovered most of Italy from the Byzantines over a decade. In 552 the eunuch general Narses arrived with a large, well-funded army by way of the Adriatic coast and marched to meet Totila on the Via Flaminia near Taginae.

Battle

Narses deployed dismounted Lombard and Herul infantry in the centre with archers on both wings angled forward — a crescent. Gothic cavalry charges, launched before their infantry arrived and after Totila's famous delayed start (including, in Procopius's account, a display of horsemanship), were shot down from front and flanks. A final Byzantine advance routed the Goths; some 6,000 were said to have fallen. Totila died of wounds fleeing the field.

Aftermath

Narses took Rome and destroyed the last Gothic army under Teias at Mons Lactarius later that year, completing the reconquest — though Italy was left devastated and soon faced Lombard invasion.
