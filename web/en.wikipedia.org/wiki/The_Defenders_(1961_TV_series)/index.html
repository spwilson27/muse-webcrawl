The Defenders (1961 TV series)

The Defenders is an American courtroom drama television series that aired on CBS from September 16, 1961, to September 9, 1965. Created by Reginald Rose, it starred E. G. Marshall as Lawrence Preston and Robert Reed as his son Kenneth, a father-and-son team of defence attorneys in New York City. The series ran for 132 episodes.

Content

Unlike most legal dramas of its era, The Defenders centred on trials of contentious social issues, often framing cases so that the Prestons argued the unpopular side: abortion, capital punishment, blacklisting, civil rights, euthanasia, immigration and censorship all featured. Rose had originated the concept in a 1957 Studio One episode. Many episodes were written by Rose and other notable television dramatists, and guest stars included future stars such as Dustin Hoffman, Robert Duvall and Jon Voight.

Reception and legacy

The series won numerous Emmy Awards, including for outstanding drama series, and is regarded as one of the most socially engaged American programmes of the early 1960s. A 1962 episode addressing abortion, "The Benefactor," famously lost its regular sponsors and had to be carried by a replacement advertiser. A short-lived 1997–98 Showtime revival reunited Marshall with a new cast.
