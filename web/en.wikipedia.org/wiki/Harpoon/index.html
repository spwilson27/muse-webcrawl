Harpoon

A harpoon is a long spear-like implement used in fishing, whaling, sealing, and other marine hunting to catch large fish or marine mammals such as whales. It accomplishes this by impaling the target and securing it with barb-like claws, allowing the hunter to use a rope or chain attached to the harpoon to haul the catch in.

History

Harpoons are among the oldest hunting tools: barbed bone harpoons from the Stone Age have been found in Africa dating back tens of thousands of years, and they were central to Arctic Inuit hunting technology, where the toggling harpoon head — which turns sideways under the skin of the prey after impact — was developed. Hand-thrown harpoons were used by Basque, Dutch, British, and American whalers for centuries.

Modern harpoons

In the 19th century the explosive harpoon, fired from a cannon mounted on a steam-powered catcher boat, revolutionised industrial whaling; Svend Foyn of Norway is credited with its development in the 1860s–70s. The harpoon carries a grenade that detonates inside the whale. Harpoon cannons remain in use in the small-scale whaling that continues today, while harpoons are also used in sport spearfishing in the form of spearguns, and a space probe lander (Philae) was equipped with harpoons intended to anchor it to a comet.

Types

  • Hand harpoons — thrown or thrust by hand, including the toggle-head type.
  • Explosive harpoons — cannon-fired with a grenade tip, used in whaling.
  • Spearguns — elastic- or pneumatic-powered, used in spearfishing.
