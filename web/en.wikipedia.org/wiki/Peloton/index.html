Peloton

DefinitionThe main pack of riders in a bicycle road race
OriginFrench: "little ball/platoon"
Key benefitReduced aerodynamic drag (drafting)

The peloton: riders save energy by sheltering from the wind behind others.

In bicycle road racing, the peloton (from French, originally meaning "little ball" and by extension a small military unit) is the main group or pack of riders. Riding in a large group saves energy because cyclists benefit from the slipstream (draft) of those ahead, reducing aerodynamic drag by a substantial margin—studies suggest energy savings of 30% or more in the middle of the bunch.

Tactics

The peloton's behaviour shapes race strategy. Riders cooperate at the front to chase breakaways, while teams protect their leaders from wind and position them for climbs, sprints or crosswind "echelons". Because no single team wants to do all the work, breakaways sometimes survive; conversely, a motivated peloton, especially with sprinters' teams, almost always reels in escapees on flat stages. Crashes within the dense pack are a recurring hazard, and race rules such as the "3-kilometre rule" protect riders' times when incidents occur near the finish.

Other uses

The term is also applied metaphorically to the main field in other sports and, by extension, to the fitness company Peloton Interactive, maker of connected exercise bikes, which takes its name from the cycling pack.
