Skeleton (sport)

Skeleton is a winter sliding sport in which a person rides a small sled, known as a skeleton bobsled or sled, down a frozen track while lying face down and head-first. The sport is governed by the International Bobsleigh and Skeleton Federation (IBSF) and is contested at the Winter Olympic Games.

History

Skeleton originated in St. Moritz, Switzerland, in the late 19th century, where riders raced head-first down the Cresta Run on toboggan-like sleds. The name is said to derive from the sled's stripped-down, skeletal appearance. It appeared at the Winter Olympics in 1928 and 1948, both times at St. Moritz, before being dropped from the programme. After decades as a niche sport, skeleton was reintroduced permanently at the 2002 Salt Lake City Games, with events for men and women.

Competition

Athletes begin with a running start, pushing the sled over roughly 40 metres before diving onto it. Steering is achieved through subtle shifts of body weight and pressure from the knees and shoulders; there are no brakes or mechanical steering. Speeds exceed 130 km/h on Olympic tracks of about 1,200–1,500 metres, and riders experience forces of up to 5 g in corners. Races typically consist of multiple timed heats, with the lowest aggregate time winning.

Equipment

The sled is a low steel-and-fibreglass pod fitted with two polished steel runners. Strict weight limits apply to the combined athlete and sled. Competitors wear skinsuits and helmets with chin guards, and spiked shoes for the start.
