WWE (an initialism of its former legal name, World Wrestling Entertainment) is an American professional wrestling promotion and media company, the largest wrestling promotion in the world. Founded by Jess McMahon and Toots Mondt as the Capitol Wrestling Corporation in 1953, it was built into a national and then global entertainment business under Vincent J. McMahon and especially his son Vince McMahon.

History

The promotion withdrew from the National Wrestling Alliance in 1963 to become the World Wide Wrestling Federation, shortened to the World Wrestling Federation (WWF) in 1979. In the 1980s, Vince McMahon's national expansion and the "Rock 'n' Wrestling" era — headlined by Hulk Hogan and events such as the first WrestleMania in 1985 — transformed the business. The late-1990s "Attitude Era", featuring Stone Cold Steve Austin and The Rock, won the Monday Night Wars against rival WCW, which WWE bought in 2001.

In 2002, after a legal dispute with the World Wildlife Fund, the company became World Wrestling Entertainment, later shortening its brand to WWE. In September 2023, WWE merged with the UFC under Endeavor's control to form TKO Group Holdings, with Vince McMahon departing the company in January 2024; creative direction passed to Paul "Triple H" Levesque.

Programming and business

WWE's flagship television programmes are Raw, SmackDown and the developmental brand NXT, supplemented by monthly premium live events — WrestleMania, Royal Rumble, SummerSlam, Survivor Series and Money in the Bank — distributed via broadcast, cable and streaming deals, including a landmark agreement moving Raw to Netflix from 2025. Revenue comes chiefly from media rights, live events, licensing and consumer products.
