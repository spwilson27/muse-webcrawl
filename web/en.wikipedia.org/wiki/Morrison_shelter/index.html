Morrison shelter

The Morrison shelter, officially termed the Table (Indoor) Shelter, was a type of indoor air-raid shelter used in Britain during the Second World War. Designed by John Baker and named after Herbert Morrison, the Minister of Home Security, it was introduced in 1941 as an alternative to the outdoor Anderson shelter.

Design

The Morrison shelter consisted of a heavy steel plate top, about 1.8 m by 1.2 m (6 ft by 4 ft), supported on steel angle legs, with wire-mesh side panels. It doubled as a household table during the day and could accommodate two adults and a child sleeping beneath it. It was designed to withstand the debris of a collapsing house, though not a direct bomb hit.

Distribution and use

Shelters were supplied free to households earning below a set income; by mid-1944 around half a million had been distributed, and about 1.1 million by the end of the war. Post-war surveys found the shelter had performed well in protecting occupants of bombed houses.
