Herring

FamilyClupeidae
Key speciesAtlantic herring (Clupea harengus), Pacific herring (C. pallasii)
HabitatNorth Atlantic and North Pacific

Herring are forage fish in the family Clupeidae, most commonly species of the genus Clupea, found in shallow temperate waters of the North Atlantic and North Pacific.

Biology

Herring are small, silvery, schooling fish feeding on plankton, and prey for seabirds, marine mammals and larger fish. Atlantic herring can live 25 years and form vast shoals.

Fisheries and food

Among the most commercially important fish in history, herring underpinned Hanseatic and Dutch economies and are eaten fresh, smoked (kippers), pickled, fermented (surströmming) and as rollmops. Stocks collapsed in parts of the North Sea in the 1970s, prompting fishing bans and quota management.

In culture

The phrase 'red herring' for a distraction traces uncertainly to strong-smelling cured herring.
