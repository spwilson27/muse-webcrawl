Proxima Centauri b is an exoplanet orbiting in the habitable zone of the red dwarf star Proxima Centauri, the closest star to the Sun, at a distance of about 4.25 light-years (1.3 parsecs) from Earth in the constellation Centaurus. Announced in August 2016 by astronomers led by Guillem Anglada-Escudé using the European Southern Observatory's HARPS spectrograph, it is the nearest known exoplanet to the Solar System.

Characteristics

The planet orbits its star at about 0.05 AU with a period of roughly 11.2 Earth days. Radial velocity measurements give a minimum mass of about 1.07 Earth masses; its true mass and radius are unknown because it does not transit its star. Its equilibrium temperature is estimated near 234 K (−39 °C), and with an Earth-like atmosphere its surface could potentially support liquid water.

Habitability

Proxima Centauri b lies within the habitable zone, but its prospects for habitability are uncertain. Proxima Centauri is a flare star, so the planet is exposed to intense stellar flares and X-ray radiation, and it is probably tidally locked, with a permanent day and night side. Whether it retains an atmosphere or surface water remains unknown; it is a prime target for future telescopes and for the Breakthrough Starshot concept of gram-scale interstellar probes.
