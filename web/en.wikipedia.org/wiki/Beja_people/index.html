Beja people

Total population: approx. 1.9–2.2 million

Regions: Eastern Sudan, Eritrea, Egypt

Languages: Beja (Bidhaawyeet), Arabic, Tigre

Religion: Islam (predominantly Sunni)

The Beja people are an ethnic group native to the Eastern Desert and Red Sea Hills of north-eastern Africa, inhabiting parts of eastern Sudan, Eritrea, and south-eastern Egypt. They speak Beja (Bidhaawyeet), a Cushitic language of the Afroasiatic family, and are traditionally nomadic pastoralists herding camels, sheep, and goats.

History

The Beja are often identified with the ancient Blemmyes, a people attested in Greek and Roman sources who lived between the Nile and the Red Sea. In antiquity they were known as fierce desert warriors who raided Roman Egypt and controlled trade routes to the Red Sea ports. During the medieval period the Beja kingdoms maintained relations — sometimes hostile, sometimes commercial — with Muslim Egypt and Christian Nubia. Islam spread gradually among the Beja from the medieval period onward, and by the modern era most Beja were Muslim.

In the 19th century, Beja warriors of the Hadendoa clan fought in the Mahdist War against Anglo-Egyptian forces and were immortalised in Rudyard Kipling's poem "Fuzzy-Wuzzy". In the modern era, many Beja have felt politically marginalised within Sudan, and the Beja Congress was formed in 1957 to advocate for the interests of eastern Sudan.

Culture and society

Beja society is organised into clans and lineages. Traditional Beja men are known for their large afro hairstyles (tiffa), shaped with butter, and for carrying swords and shields. Coffee ceremonies, sword dances, and poetry are important features of Beja culture. While many Beja remain nomadic herders, others have settled in towns such as Port Sudan, Kassala, and Tokar, working in ports, agriculture, and trade.

Subgroups

Major Beja groups include the Hadendoa, Bisharin, Amarar, Beni-Amer, Ababda, and Halanga. Some of these groups have adopted Arabic or Tigre as their primary language while retaining Beja identity.
