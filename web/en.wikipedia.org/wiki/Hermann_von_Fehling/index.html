Hermann von Fehling

Hermann Christian von Fehling (9 June 1812 – 1 July 1885) was a German chemist best known for Fehling's solution, a reagent used to distinguish between water-soluble carbohydrate and ketone functional groups and to test for reducing sugars.

Life and career

Fehling was born in Lübeck and studied chemistry at Heidelberg, where he later worked in the laboratory of Leopold Gmelin; he also spent time in Paris with Jean-Baptiste Dumas. In 1839 he became professor of chemistry at the Polytechnikum in Stuttgart, a post he held for the rest of his career.

Work

In 1849 Fehling described the copper-tartrate reagent that bears his name: an alkaline solution of copper(II) sulfate complexed with potassium sodium tartrate, which is reduced to brick-red copper(I) oxide by aldehydes and reducing sugars but not by most ketones. Fehling's solution became a standard tool of clinical chemistry for detecting glucose in urine in the management of diabetes. He also investigated benzoic acid, succinic acid and the chemistry of bread-making, and served as a long-time editor of the Handwörterbuch der reinen und angewandten Chemie.

Death

Fehling died in Stuttgart in 1885. His test was later refined by Benedict's and Barfoed's reagents but Fehling's name remains attached to the classical qualitative analysis taught in laboratories today.
