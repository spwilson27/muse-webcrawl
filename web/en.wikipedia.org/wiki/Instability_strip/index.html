Instability strip

The instability strip is a narrow, nearly vertical region on the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram occupied by pulsating variable stars (including RR Lyrae variables, classical Cepheid variables, and Delta Scuti variables).

The instability strip intersects the main sequence in the region of A- and F-type stars (1–2 solar masses) and extends to the region of the brightest supergiants.

Pulsation mechanism

Stars in the strip pulsate because of the kappa mechanism: partially ionized helium in a layer of the star alternately absorbs and releases energy, driving radial oscillations. When the helium layer is hot enough that helium is doubly ionized, the strip ends — this explains the red and blue edges of the strip.
