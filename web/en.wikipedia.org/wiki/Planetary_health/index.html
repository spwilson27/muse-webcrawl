Planetary health

Human health depends on the health of natural systems

Planetary health is a field and a movement focused on understanding and addressing the impacts of human disruptions to Earth's natural systems on human health and all life on Earth. It holds that the health of human civilisation and the state of the natural systems on which it depends are inextricably linked.

Origins

The concept was prominently articulated by the Rockefeller Foundation–Lancet Commission on Planetary Health, whose 2015 report, "Safeguarding human health in the Anthropocene epoch", argued that humanity's progress in health over the past century has come at the cost of degrading the planet's life-support systems: a stable climate, biodiversity, fertile land, freshwater, and functioning ecosystems.

Scope

Planetary health examines threats including climate change, biodiversity loss, land degradation, water scarcity, pollution, and ocean acidification, and their consequences for nutrition, infectious disease, non-communicable disease, displacement, and mental health. It is closely related to the planetary boundaries framework, to One Health, and to ecohealth, but emphasises human health outcomes and the transformation of human systems – food, energy, cities, and economies – to operate within safe environmental limits.

Institutions

The Planetary Health Alliance, founded in 2016 and based at Harvard University, coordinates a consortium of universities, NGOs, and research institutes. The journal Lancet Planetary Health launched in 2017. Planetary health education has since been incorporated into medical and public-health curricula in a number of countries.
