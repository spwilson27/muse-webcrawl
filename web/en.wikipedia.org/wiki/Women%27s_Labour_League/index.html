Women's Labour League
Founded: 1906
Dissolved: 1918 (merged into the Labour Party)
Key figures: Margaret MacDonald, Mary Macarthur

The Women's Labour League (WLL) was a pressure group founded in London in 1906 to promote the political representation of working-class women within the British Labour movement. It was affiliated with the Labour Party and campaigned for women's suffrage, improved maternity and child welfare, school meals, and better housing.

History

The League originated at a conference convened by Margaret MacDonald, wife of Labour leader Ramsay MacDonald, together with trade unionist Mary Macarthur and others. It established branches across Britain and published pamphlets and a journal, The Labour Woman. The League combined support for adult suffrage with practical welfare work, and many of its members later became Labour's first women councillors and MPs.

Dissolution

After the Labour Party's 1918 constitution created individual membership with dedicated women's sections, the League wound itself up and its members transferred into the new structure. Its journal continued as the party women's paper.
