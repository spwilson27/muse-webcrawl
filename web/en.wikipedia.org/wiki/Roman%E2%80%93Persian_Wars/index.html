CombatantsRoman Republic/Empire and Byzantine Empire vs Parthian and Sasanian empires

The Roman–Persian Wars were a series of conflicts between the Greco-Roman world and successive Iranian empires — first Parthia, then the Sasanian Empire — spanning nearly seven centuries, from 54 BC to AD 628. They were among the longest-running rivalries of the ancient world.

Course

Hostilities began with Crassus's disastrous invasion of Parthia, crushed at the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC. Over subsequent centuries Roman and Persian armies fought repeatedly over Armenia and northern Mesopotamia. The final and most destructive phase, the Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628, saw Persian armies reach Constantinople before the emperor Heraclius counter-attacked deep into Persia, restoring the status quo.

Consequences

The exhausting final war left both empires weakened just as the early Muslim conquests began; Persia fell entirely, and Byzantium lost its eastern provinces.
