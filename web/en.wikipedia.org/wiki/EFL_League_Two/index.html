EFL League Two is the fourth level of the English football league system and the third and lowest division of the English Football League (EFL). It was known as the Football League Fourth Division from its creation in 1958 until 1992, then as the Third Division until it was renamed League Two in 2004.

Structure

The division is contested by 24 clubs, each playing the others twice, home and away, for a total of 46 matches per club per season. At the end of the season the top three clubs are promoted automatically to League One, and the clubs finishing fourth to seventh contest play-offs for the fourth promotion place. The bottom two clubs are relegated to the National League, ending their spell in the EFL; they are replaced by the National League champions and the winners of the National League play-offs.

History

The Fourth Division was created in 1958 by merging the regional Third Divisions North and South into national Third and Fourth Divisions. In 1992, following the creation of the Premier League, it became the Third Division, and in 2004 the Football League rebranded its divisions as the Championship, League One and League Two. Since the 2016 rebranding of the Football League as the English Football League, the division has been officially the EFL League Two.
