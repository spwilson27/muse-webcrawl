Flensburg Government

SeatFlensburg (Mürwik), Germany
Existed2–23 May 1945
Head of stateKarl Dönitz (Reichspräsident)

The Flensburg Government (German: Flensburger Regierung) was the short-lived government of Nazi Germany during the final days of the Second World War in Europe. After Adolf Hitler's suicide on 30 April 1945, his testament named Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz as Reich President. Dönitz established his administration at the naval academy in Mürwik, a district of Flensburg near the Danish border, on 3 May 1945.

Activities

The government, with Lutz Graf Schwerin von Krosigk as leading minister, attempted to continue administering German territory not yet occupied and, above all, to surrender German forces piecemeal to the Western Allies while continuing resistance against the Soviet Union — a strategy the Allies refused. Representatives of the government signed the unconditional surrender of all German armed forces at Reims on 7 May and ratified it in Berlin on 8 May 1945, ending the war in Europe.

Arrest and dissolution

The Western Allies tolerated the Flensburg administration briefly, chiefly to transmit surrender orders to German forces, but never recognised it as a legitimate government. On 23 May 1945, British troops arrested Dönitz and his ministers aboard the liner Patria in Flensburg harbour, formally dissolving the government. Its leading members were subsequently tried at Nuremberg.
