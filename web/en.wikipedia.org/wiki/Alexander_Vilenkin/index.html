Alexander Vilenkin

Alexander Vilenkin (born 13 May 1949) is a Ukrainian-American theoretical physicist and cosmologist, long based at Tufts University, where he directed the Institute of Cosmology. He is known for his work on cosmic inflation, cosmic strings, and the multiverse.

Career and work

Born in Kharkiv, Ukraine, Vilenkin studied physics at Kharkiv University but, after being denied admission to graduate study in the Soviet Union, emigrated to the United States in 1976. He completed his doctorate and joined Tufts University.

Vilenkin proposed the idea of eternal inflation, in which inflationary expansion never ends everywhere, continually producing "pocket universes". Together with Arvind Borde and Alan Guth, he proved the Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem (2003), which states that spacetimes that have on average been expanding must be past-incomplete — often taken to imply that the universe (or multiverse) had a beginning. He has also worked on the quantum creation of the universe "from nothing" via tunnelling, cosmic strings, and the measure problem in eternal inflation. His popular book Many Worlds in One (2006) presents the multiverse idea to a general audience.
