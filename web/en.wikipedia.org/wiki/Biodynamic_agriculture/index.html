Biodynamic agriculture

Biodynamic agriculture is a form of alternative agriculture based on pseudo-scientific and esoteric concepts initially developed in 1924 by Rudolf Steiner (1861–1925). It was the first of the organic farming movements.

Biodynamic agriculture
Founder: Rudolf Steiner
Origin: 1924 Agriculture Course, Koberwitz
Certification: Demeter International

Practices

Biodynamic farms treat soil, plants and livestock as an interconnected, largely self-sustaining system, avoiding synthetic fertilisers and pesticides. Distinctive practices include nine "preparations" made from manure, herbs and minerals (such as horn manure 500 and horn silica 501), composting, and planting calendars considering lunar and cosmic rhythms.

Certification and wine

Demeter International certifies biodynamic products. The approach is especially visible in wine, where many notable vineyards have adopted it.

Scientific assessment

Research finds biodynamic farms often have good soil quality similar to organic farms, but no consistent evidence for effects of the specific preparations or cosmic calendars beyond organic practices; those elements are regarded as pseudoscientific.
