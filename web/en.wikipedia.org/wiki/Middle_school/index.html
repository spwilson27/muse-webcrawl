Middle school

A middle school, also known as an intermediate school or (historically in the United States) junior high school, is an educational stage between primary (elementary) school and secondary (high) school. In the United States, middle schools most commonly serve grades 6 through 8, for students roughly 11 to 14 years old, although configurations such as 5–8 and 7–9 also exist. Similar intermediate stages exist in many other countries, for example middle schools in China and South Korea, and in India and Japan.

The middle school model

The American middle school movement, which gained ground from the 1960s–70s onward, sought to replace the junior high (a miniature high school for grades 7–9) with a structure designed around early adolescents' developmental needs. Its hallmarks include interdisciplinary teams of teachers who share the same group of students, advisory periods, exploratory courses (such as languages, technology, and the arts) alongside the core subjects, block scheduling, and an emphasis on guidance and gradual independence in preparation for high school.

International variations

In England and Wales, "middle schools" deemed primary or secondary once served ages 8–12 or 9–13 under three-tier systems, most of which have been abolished in favour of two-tier primary/secondary organization. In Japan, junior high schools (chūgakkō) cover grades 7–9 and form the final stage of compulsory education. In China, junior middle school likewise covers grades 7–9 before senior middle school. Systems differ widely in whether the stage is a separate institution at all.

Research and debate

Educators and researchers debate whether moving students to a separate school in early adolescence helps or harms achievement; some studies find test-score dips associated with transitions, and some districts have returned to K–8 schools, while others defend the middle school concept when its distinctive practices (teaming, advisory, exploration) are genuinely implemented rather than merely renaming a junior high.
