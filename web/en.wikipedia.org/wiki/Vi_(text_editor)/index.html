Vi (text editor)

Original authorBill Joy
Initial release1976
Written inC
Operating systemUnix, Unix-like
TypeText editor

vi (pronounced "vee-eye") is a screen-oriented text editor originally created for the Unix operating system. The portable subset of the behaviour of vi and programs based on it, and the ex editor language supported within these programs, is described by the Single Unix Specification and POSIX.

vi was written by Bill Joy in 1976 at the University of California, Berkeley, as the visual mode for the ex line editor, which Joy had written with Chuck Haley. It was optimised for the slow 300-baud modem connections and the ADM-3A terminal of the time, which explains design decisions such as the use of the Esc key and the hjkl keys for cursor movement.

Interface

vi is a modal editor: it operates in different modes, principally normal mode, in which keystrokes are interpreted as commands, and insert mode, in which typed text is entered into the document. Commands are composable: an operator such as d (delete) combines with a motion such as w (word) to form dw, deleting a word. This grammar of editing commands is one of vi's most influential features.

Influence

Because vi was shipped with virtually every Unix system, it became a de facto standard editor. Its design inspired numerous clones and successors, most notably Vim ("Vi IMproved"), released by Bram Moolenaar in 1991, which added multiple undo levels, syntax highlighting, and extensive customisation. The rivalry between vi and Emacs users is a well-known part of hacker culture, sometimes jokingly called the "editor war".
