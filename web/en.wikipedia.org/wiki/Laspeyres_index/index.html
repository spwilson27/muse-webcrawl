Laspeyres index

The Laspeyres index is a price index formula used in price statistics, named after German economist Étienne Laspeyres (1834–1913), who devised it in 1864. It measures the change in the cost of purchasing a fixed basket of goods and services, where the quantities are those of the base period.

Formula

Laspeyres price index = (sum over goods of current-period price × base-period quantity) ÷ (sum over goods of base-period price × base-period quantity) × 100.

Comparison with Paasche

The rival Paasche index weights quantities from the current period. Because consumers substitute away from goods whose prices rise, a Laspeyres index tends to overstate inflation (substitution bias), while a Paasche index tends to understate it. The Fisher index, the geometric mean of the two, is often called "ideal". Many national consumer price indices are modified Laspeyres (Lowe) indices, using quantities from a period before the price reference period and updating the basket periodically.

Quantity index

An analogous Laspeyres quantity index uses base-period prices as weights to measure changes in volumes.
