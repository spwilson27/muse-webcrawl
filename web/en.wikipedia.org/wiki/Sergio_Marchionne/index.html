Sergio Marchionne

Sergio Marchionne

Born: 17 June 1952, Chieti, Italy
Died: 25 July 2018 (aged 66), Zurich, Switzerland
Occupation: Business executive
Known for: CEO of Fiat and Fiat Chrysler Automobiles; chairman of Ferrari and SGS

Sergio Marchionne (17 June 1952 – 25 July 2018) was an Italian-Canadian businessman widely credited with rescuing Fiat from near-bankruptcy and engineering its merger with Chrysler to create Fiat Chrysler Automobiles (FCA). Known for his black sweaters, blunt speech, and punishing work schedule, he was one of the most prominent automotive executives of his era, simultaneously serving at various times as CEO of Fiat and Chrysler, chairman of Ferrari and of the Swiss testing company SGS, and chairman of CNH Industrial.

Early life and career

Marchionne was born in Chieti, Abruzzo, Italy, and emigrated with his family to Toronto, Canada, at age 13. He earned degrees in philosophy (University of Toronto), commerce (University of Windsor), and law (Osgoode Hall), qualified as a barrister and chartered accountant, and worked in tax, accounting, and corporate development — including at the packaging firm Lawson Mardon and the aluminum company Alusuisse-Lonza — before becoming CEO of the Swiss testing and certification group SGS in 2002, where he rapidly restored profitability.

Fiat turnaround

Appointed to Fiat's board in 2003 and chief executive in June 2004, Marchionne inherited a company losing billions of euros. He restructured management, cut costs, renegotiated a costly put option with General Motors — extracting a $2 billion settlement in 2005 that recapitalized Fiat — and revitalized the product line with successes such as the new Fiat 500 (2007). Fiat Group returned to profit within about two years of his arrival.

The Chrysler merger

In 2009, as Chrysler emerged from bankruptcy with U.S. and Canadian government support, Fiat took an initial 20% stake and Marchionne became Chrysler's CEO. He merged the companies' operations, repaid government loans ahead of schedule, and in 2014 completed the full combination as Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, listing the group in New York and Milan. He spun off Ferrari in 2015–16, refocused FCA on profitable brands — Jeep, Ram, Alfa Romeo, and Maserati — while dropping low-margin car lines in North America, and by mid-2018 had eliminated the group's industrial net debt, as he had pledged. Unsuccessfully, he publicly advocated industry consolidation, including a proposed merger with General Motors in 2015.

Death and legacy

In July 2018, following complications after shoulder surgery in Zurich — it later emerged he had been receiving treatment for a serious illness, reported as invasive cancer — Marchionne was replaced as CEO by Mike Manley on 21 July and died four days later, aged 66. Tributes from industry and political leaders credited him with saving both Fiat and Chrysler and tens of thousands of jobs, while labor critics recalled hard bargaining and his relocation of Fiat's legal headquarters out of Italy. He is remembered as one of the auto industry's most consequential turnaround artists; the merger of FCA with PSA Group to form Stellantis, agreed in 2019, followed the consolidation logic he had championed.
