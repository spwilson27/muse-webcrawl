Essential nutrient

An essential nutrient is a nutrient required for normal physiological function that cannot be synthesized by the body, or not in adequate amounts, and therefore must be obtained from the diet. Essential nutrients for humans include certain amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins and minerals.

Categories

Essential amino acids: nine of the twenty standard amino acids (histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine). Essential fatty acids: linoleic and alpha-linolenic acids. Vitamins: thirteen recognized, from vitamin A to K, most of which must come from food. Minerals: macrominerals such as calcium, potassium and magnesium, plus trace elements including iron, zinc and iodine.

Deficiency

Lack of an essential nutrient causes specific deficiency diseases—scurvy (vitamin C), beriberi (thiamine), pellagra (niacin), goitre (iodine), anaemia (iron, B12)—which historically drove the discovery of vitamins in the early 20th century. Reference intakes (DRIs/RDAs) quantify requirements.
