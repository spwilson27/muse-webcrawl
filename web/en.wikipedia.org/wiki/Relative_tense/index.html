Relative tense

In linguistics, a relative tense is a grammatical tense that locates an event in time relative not to the moment of speaking but to some other reference point, such as another event or a time established in the discourse. This contrasts with absolute tense, which is anchored to the time of utterance.

Examples

English forms often analysed as relative tenses include the pluperfect ("I had eaten"), which places an event before a past reference time, and the future perfect ("I will have eaten"), which places an event before a future reference time. The framework of speech time, reference time and event time was formalised by Hans Reichenbach in 1947.

Distribution

Many languages, such as some in West Africa and Amazonia, have extensive relative tense systems in subordinate clauses, where the tense of the subordinate verb indicates simultaneity, anteriority or posteriority relative to the main clause event.
