Laser resurfacing

Laser resurfacing, also known as laser skin resurfacing or a laser peel, is a cosmetic procedure that uses concentrated beams of light to remove damaged outer layers of skin and stimulate the growth of new collagen fibres, improving the appearance of wrinkles, scars, and blemishes.

Types

Ablative lasers, such as carbon dioxide (CO₂) and erbium lasers, vaporise the outer skin layers and produce the most dramatic results but require longer recovery. Non-ablative lasers heat the underlying tissue without removing the surface, giving milder improvement with less downtime. Fractional lasers treat only a fraction of the skin in a grid pattern, speeding healing.

Risks

Risks include redness and swelling, infection, scarring, and changes in pigmentation, particularly hyperpigmentation in darker skin types.
