National Liberation Front (Greece)

The National Liberation Front (Greek: Εθνικό Απελευθερωτικό Μέτωπο, Ethnikó Apeleftherotikó Métopo), commonly known by its initials EAM, was the largest resistance organization in Axis-occupied Greece during the Second World War. Founded on 27 September 1941, shortly after the Axis occupation of Greece began, it was established by the Communist Party of Greece (KKE) together with smaller socialist and agrarian parties.

Organization

EAM built a mass movement with affiliated organizations for workers, women, and youth (EPON). Its military arm was the Greek People's Liberation Army (ELAS), which grew into the largest resistance army in Greece, liberating large areas of the mountainous interior — "Free Greece" — where EAM established schools, local government, and courts through its Political Committee of National Liberation (PEEA), a provisional government formed in 1944.

Liberation and aftermath

EAM's dominance alarmed the Greek government-in-exile and the British, who supported rival resistance groups. Rivalry with the republican EDES turned violent during the occupation. After liberation in October 1944, confrontation between EAM and the British-backed government erupted in the Dekemvriana clashes in Athens in December 1944, followed by the Varkiza Agreement of February 1945, under which ELAS was disarmed. The conflict was a prelude to the Greek Civil War (1946–1949).

Legacy

EAM mobilized hundreds of thousands, perhaps millions, of Greeks in resistance, administration, and social welfare, and is remembered as both a national liberation movement and the vehicle that brought the Greek left close to power, shaping Greek politics for decades.
