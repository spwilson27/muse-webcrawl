Seokguram

Seokguram (Korean: 석굴암) is a hermitage and part of the Bulguksa temple complex in Gyeongju, South Korea. The grotto, built in the 8th century on the slopes of Mount Toham, is an artificial stone cave temple housing a monumental statue of the Buddha, and is regarded as a masterpiece of Buddhist art in the Far East. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995, together with Bulguksa.

Construction

Construction began in 751 under the Silla chief minister Kim Daeseong, during the reign of King Gyeongdeok, and was completed in 774, after Kim's death. Unlike Indian and Chinese cave temples carved into living rock, Seokguram was built from hundreds of precisely cut granite blocks assembled into a rectangular antechamber and a circular main rotunda, then covered with earth to resemble a natural cave.

The Buddha statue

The main rotunda contains a seated Buddha about 3.5 metres high on a lotus pedestal, depicting the bhumisparsha (earth-touching) mudra. It is surrounded by reliefs and niches holding bodhisattvas, disciples, and guardian figures, including an Eleven-faced Avalokitesvara carved on the rear wall. The statue is considered one of the finest examples of Silla-period sculpture.

Conservation

Forgotten for centuries, the grotto was rediscovered in the early 20th century and controversially renovated under Japanese colonial rule, with concrete repairs that disturbed its original humidity control. The Buddha is now protected behind glass, and visitors view the rotunda from the antechamber.
