Starbucks

Type: Public

Industry: Coffee shops

Founded: March 30, 1971; Pike Place Market, Seattle, Washington, U.S.

Founders: Jerry Baldwin, Zev Siegl, Gordon Bowker

Headquarters: Seattle, Washington, U.S.

Products: Coffee beverages, teas, baked goods

Starbucks Corporation is an American multinational chain of coffeehouses and roastery reserves headquartered in Seattle, Washington. It was founded in 1971 by Jerry Baldwin, Zev Siegl, and Gordon Bowker at Seattle's Pike Place Market. Initially selling coffee beans and equipment, the company began serving brewed coffee after Howard Schultz joined in 1982 and, following his purchase of the company in 1987, expanded rapidly into a global chain of coffee shops.

History

The first Starbucks store sold roasted coffee beans, tea, and spices rather than drinks. Schultz, inspired by the espresso bars of Milan, opened his own Il Giornale chain and acquired Starbucks in 1987, merging the two. The company went public in 1992 and expanded aggressively through the 1990s and 2000s, becoming the world's largest coffeehouse chain, with tens of thousands of stores in more than 80 countries.

Products

Starbucks sells espresso-based drinks such as lattes and cappuccinos, brewed coffee, Frappuccino blended beverages, teas, refreshers, and food items including pastries and sandwiches. Many beverages are highly customisable, and the company's seasonal offerings, such as the Pumpkin Spice Latte, have become notable cultural phenomena.

Criticism and controversies

The company has faced criticism over tax practices, labour relations, including unionisation campaigns at its stores, and the environmental impact of its disposable cups. It has also been involved in disputes regarding fair trade sourcing.
