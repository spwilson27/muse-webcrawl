Equity carve-out

An equity carve-out (also called a partial IPO) is a corporate restructuring strategy in which a parent company sells a minority stake in a subsidiary to the public through an initial public offering, while retaining majority control of the subsidiary. Unlike a spin-off, in which shares are distributed to existing shareholders, a carve-out raises cash for the parent.

Rationale

Companies pursue equity carve-outs to unlock the value of a subsidiary whose worth may be obscured within a conglomerate, to raise capital without relinquishing control, to create a separate currency for acquisitions and employee incentives in the subsidiary, and to improve market transparency. A carve-out is sometimes a first step toward a later full spin-off or sale of the remaining stake.

Process

Typically the parent sells roughly 20 percent or less of the subsidiary's shares, so that the transaction can qualify for tax-free treatment under United States tax law if followed by a distribution of the remaining shares. The carved-out unit becomes a separately listed public company with its own financial reporting, while the parent consolidates its results.
