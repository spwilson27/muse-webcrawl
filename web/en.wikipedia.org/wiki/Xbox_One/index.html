Xbox One

The Xbox One is a home video game console developed by Microsoft, announced in May 2013 and released in November 2013 as the successor to the Xbox 360 and the third console of the Xbox series. It competed in the eighth generation with Sony's PlayStation 4 and Nintendo's Wii U and Switch. Microsoft emphasised the console as an "all-in-one entertainment system", reflected in its name.

Launch controversy and revisions

The console's original policies — an Internet connection requirement, restrictions on used games, and a bundled Kinect sensor that had to remain connected — drew heavy criticism before launch, and Microsoft reversed most of them within weeks of the announcement. The Kinect was later unbundled. Revised models included the Xbox One S (2016), smaller with 4K video playback and HDR, and the Xbox One X (2017), marketed as the most powerful console of its generation with native 4K rendering in supported games. The disc-less Xbox One S All-Digital Edition followed in 2019.

Software and legacy

The Xbox One introduced large-scale backward compatibility with Xbox 360 (and later original Xbox) games, the Xbox Game Pass subscription service (2017), and the xCloud game streaming service. Sales were estimated at around 58 million units — substantially fewer than the PS4 — and production of all models ended in 2020 as Microsoft transitioned to the Xbox Series X/S.
