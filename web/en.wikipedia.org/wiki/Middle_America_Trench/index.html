Middle America Trench

The Middle America Trench is an oceanic trench in the eastern Pacific Ocean off the southwestern coast of Middle America, stretching from central Mexico to Costa Rica. It marks the convergent plate boundary where the Cocos Plate — and, in the north, the Rivera Plate — subducts beneath the North American and Caribbean plates.

Geology

The trench runs roughly 2,750 km and reaches depths of about 6,700 m. Subduction along it feeds the volcanoes of Mexico's Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Central American volcanic arc, and generates frequent large earthquakes, including the 1985 Mexico City earthquake offshore Michoacán and the 2017 Puebla earthquake further inland.
