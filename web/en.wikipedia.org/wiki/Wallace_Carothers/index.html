Wallace Carothers

Wallace Hume Carothers (April 27, 1896 – April 29, 1937) was an American chemist who led the DuPont research programme that produced neoprene rubber and nylon, the first fully synthetic fibre. His systematic studies of polymerisation laid the theoretical foundations of modern polymer science.

Career

Carothers earned his doctorate at the University of Illinois in 1924 and taught at Harvard before DuPont recruited him in 1928 to direct fundamental research at its Experimental Station in Wilmington, Delaware. His group clarified the chemistry of condensation polymerisation and produced the first commercially successful synthetic rubber, neoprene (announced 1930). In 1934 his team drew strong fibres from a polyamide melt, and on February 28, 1935, they synthesised polyamide 6-6 — nylon — from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid. DuPont announced nylon in 1938, and nylon stockings went on sale in 1940, transforming the textile industry.

Personal life and legacy

Carothers struggled with depression throughout his life. He married Helen Sweetman in 1936, but his mental health deteriorated and he died by suicide in Philadelphia in April 1937, before nylon reached the market. Despite his early death, he held dozens of patents and is regarded as one of the most important industrial chemists of the twentieth century; DuPont later named a research facility in his honour.
