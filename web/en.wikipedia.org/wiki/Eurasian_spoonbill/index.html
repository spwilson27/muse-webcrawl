Eurasian spoonbill

Eurasian spoonbill
Scientific name: Platalea leucorodia
Family: Threskiornithidae (ibises and spoonbills)
Length: 70–95 cm
Wingspan: 115–135 cm
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The Eurasian spoonbill (Platalea leucorodia), or common spoonbill, is a wading bird of the ibis and spoonbill family Threskiornithidae. It is a Palearctic species, breeding from the United Kingdom and Spain across Europe and Asia to Japan, and in North Africa. Most northern populations are migratory, wintering further south in Africa and Asia.

Description

The Eurasian spoonbill is unmistakable: an almost entirely white bird with long dark legs and a long black bill flattened and widened into a spoon shape at the tip. Breeding adults develop a crest and a yellow patch on the breast. Three subspecies are recognised, differing mainly in size and bill colour pattern.

Behaviour

Spoonbills feed in shallow wetlands, sweeping the partly opened bill from side to side through the water to catch small fish, crustaceans, molluscs and aquatic insects by touch. They breed colonially, often with herons and egrets, building stick nests in trees, reedbeds or on islands. Populations declined sharply in the 20th century owing to wetland drainage and persecution but have partially recovered; the species recolonised Britain as a breeding bird in the 21st century after an absence of centuries.
