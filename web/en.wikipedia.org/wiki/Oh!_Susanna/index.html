"Oh! Susanna" is a song by American composer Stephen Foster, first published in 1848. Written for the minstrel stage, it became one of the most popular American songs of the 19th century and an unofficial anthem of the California Gold Rush, whose emigrants adapted its lyrics for the journey west ("I came from Salem City with my washbowl on my knee").

Composition and publication

Foster, then working as a bookkeeper in Cincinnati, wrote the song in 1847, naming it for his deceased sister Susanna. It was first performed in Pittsburgh in September 1847 and published by W. C. Peters of Cincinnati in February 1848. It quickly became Foster's first great success, sung by minstrel troupes across the country. By some counts more than twenty editions appeared within a few years — most unlicensed, earning Foster little, though the roughly $100 he received and the fame it brought launched his career as America's first professional songwriter.

Lyrics and melody

The best-known verse runs: "I came from Alabama with my banjo on my knee / I'm going to Louisiana, my true love for to see." The melody, in a bright major-key polka rhythm, is easy to sing and has become a standard children's song, though the original dialect lyrics belong to the blackface minstrel tradition and modern performances typically use cleaned-up versions. The tune has been recorded by countless artists, from Burl Ives to Neil Young and James Taylor, and appears in films, cartoons and school songbooks worldwide.

Legacy

"Oh! Susanna" is regarded as a landmark of American popular music: it marked the emergence of a distinctively American vernacular song and of the commercial popular-song industry. It is not to be confused with the racehorse Oh So Sharp.
