Clyde is a small town in Central Otago, New Zealand. It lies on the Clutha River / Mata-Au, some 5 kilometres (3.1 mi) north-west of Alexandra and about 75 kilometres (47 mi) from Queenstown. The town stands at the western end of the Cromwell Gorge, beneath the Clyde Dam, one of New Zealand's largest hydroelectric power stations.

History

Clyde grew from the gold-mining settlement originally known as Dunstan, founded during the Otago gold rush of the 1860s after gold was discovered in the Clutha River by Horatio Hartley and Christopher Reilly in 1862. The settlement was renamed Clyde, after Lord Clyde (Colin Campbell), a British field marshal of the Crimean War era. For a time it was one of the most populous towns in New Zealand, serving the Dunstan goldfields.

Many of the town's historic buildings survive, built from local schist stone, including the old Dunstan Hotel, the former courthouse, and stone cottages along Sunderland Street. Clyde became the administrative centre of the Dunstan goldfields and an important staging point on the road between Dunedin and the Wakatipu district.

Clyde Dam

Construction of the Clyde Dam began in 1979 and the dam was completed in 1993, creating Lake Dunstan upstream. The dam is New Zealand's third-largest hydroelectric dam, with a generating capacity of 464 megawatts. The project was controversial: filling the Cromwell Gorge flooded orchards, historic sites, and part of the old town of Cromwell, and required stabilisation work on the gorge's schist slopes on a massive scale.

Present day

Today Clyde is a quiet heritage town known for its preserved gold-rush streetscape, cafes, and stone fruit orchards in the surrounding district. It is the start (or finish) point of the Otago Central Rail Trail, a 150-kilometre cycle trail following the former railway line to Middlemarch, and lies close to the Clutha Gold Trail along the river. The town also serves the surrounding vineyards of the Alexandra basin, part of the Central Otago wine region noted for pinot noir.
