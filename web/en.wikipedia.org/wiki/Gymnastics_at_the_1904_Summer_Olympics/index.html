Gymnastics at the 1904 Summer Olympics

VenueFrancis Gymnasium, Washington University in St. Louis
Dates1–2 July and 28 October 1904
Competitors119
Nations3

Gymnastics at the 1904 Summer Olympics was contested at the Games of the III Olympiad in St. Louis, Missouri, held in conjunction with the Louisiana Purchase Exposition (the St. Louis World's Fair). A total of 11 events were held, including apparatus events, combined exercises, a team competition, and unusual combined events mixing gymnastics with athletics disciplines such as the long jump, shot put, and 100-yard dash. All competitors were men, and the United States dominated, winning most of the medals.

Competition

Two separate gymnastic programmes were held: an international "Turner" competition in July and the Olympic championships in October. The team all-around was won by the Philadelphia Turngemeinde. Anton Heida of the United States was the most successful gymnast, winning five gold medals (pommel horse, vault, horizontal bar, combined exercises, and team) plus a silver. George Eyser won six medals despite having a wooden leg, including golds on the parallel bars, vault, and rope climbing. Other champions included Herman Glass (rings) and Edward Hennig (club swinging and triathlon).

Events

  • Individual all-around
  • Combined (three events and four events)
  • Triathlon (gymnastics and athletics)
  • Team all-around
  • Parallel bars, horizontal bar, pommel horse, rings, vault
  • Club swinging and rope climbing

Most participants were American members of German-American Turner societies, with small contingents from Germany and Switzerland. The 1904 gymnastics programme remains notable for its mixture of gymnastic and track-and-field elements, a format not repeated at later Games.
