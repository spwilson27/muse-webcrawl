The Grapes of Wrath is a 1940 American drama film directed by John Ford, based on John Steinbeck's 1939 Pulitzer Prize–winning novel of the same name. It stars Henry Fonda as Tom Joad and Jane Darwell as Ma Joad.

Plot

During the Dust Bowl of the Great Depression, the Joad family is driven from its Oklahoma farm by drought, bank foreclosures and mechanisation. Loading everything onto a battered truck, they join the migration of "Okies" west along Route 66 to California in search of work, encountering exploitation, squalid camps and strikebreaking — as well as a clean, decent government-run camp. The film notably softens the novel's ending: the family is scattered and diminished but endures, closing with Ma Joad's famous affirmation, "We're the people that live."

Reception

The film won Academy Awards for Best Director (Ford) and Best Supporting Actress (Darwell) and was nominated for Best Picture. It is regularly ranked among the greatest American films, and in 1989 it was among the first films selected for preservation in the National Film Registry. Gregg Toland's deep, stark black-and-white cinematography is widely admired.
