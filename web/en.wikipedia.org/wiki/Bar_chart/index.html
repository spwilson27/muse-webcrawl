Bar chart

TypeStatistical chart
First describedWilliam Playfair, 1786
RepresentsCategorical data by bar length or height
VariantsGrouped, stacked, horizontal

A bar chart or bar graph is a chart or graph that presents categorical data with rectangular bars whose heights or lengths are proportional to the values they represent. The bars can be plotted vertically or horizontally; a vertical bar chart is sometimes called a column chart.

History

The bar chart is generally credited to the Scottish engineer and economist William Playfair, who published charts of Scotland's imports and exports in his Commercial and Political Atlas (1786), the same work that introduced several foundational statistical graphics. Earlier medieval diagrams, such as Nicole Oresme's 14th-century figures, anticipated aspects of the form.

Usage

Bar charts are used to compare values across discrete categories—for example, sales by region or populations by country—where one axis shows the categories and the other a measured value starting at zero. Departures from a zero baseline are considered misleading, since bar length is the visual encoding. Common variants include grouped charts, placing several series side by side; stacked charts, dividing each bar into components; and diverging charts for positive and negative values. A bar chart for continuous, binned data is a histogram, whose bars touch to indicate continuity.

Design considerations

Statisticians and designers such as Edward Tufte recommend minimal "chartjunk", direct labelling, consistent ordering of categories, and avoiding three-dimensional effects that distort perceived lengths.
