MPLA

The People's Movement for the Liberation of Angola (Portuguese: Movimento Popular de Libertação de Angola, MPLA) is a left-wing political party that has ruled Angola since the country's independence from Portugal in 1975. The MPLA fought against the Portuguese Army in the Angolan War of Independence (1961–1974) and defeated rival movements in the Angolan Civil War (1975–2002), after which it consolidated power as the governing party.

History

The MPLA was founded in 1956 through the merger of the Angolan Communist Party (PCA) and the Party of the United Struggle for Africans in Angola (PLUAA). Its first leader was Mário Pinto de Andrade, succeeded in 1962 by Agostinho Neto, who became the first president of independent Angola in 1975. During the liberation struggle, the MPLA received support from the Soviet Union, Cuba, and other socialist states, while its rivals — the FNLA and UNITA — drew support from the United States, South Africa, and China at various points.

On 11 November 1975, Neto proclaimed the independence of the People's Republic of Angola. The ensuing civil war against UNITA and the FNLA lasted until 2002, ending after the death of UNITA leader Jonas Savimbi. Under President José Eduardo dos Santos, who succeeded Neto in 1979 and governed until 2017, the MPLA transitioned from a one-party Marxist-Leninist state to a formally multiparty system while retaining control of government. João Lourenço became president in 2017.

Ideology and elections

Originally a Marxist-Leninist movement, the MPLA moved toward social democracy and a market economy after the end of the Cold War. It won the 2008, 2012, 2017, and 2022 legislative elections, though its majority declined over time, and it remains a member of the Socialist International.
