Oxaliplatin


Clinical data: Trade name Eloxatin. Routes: intravenous. Elimination half-life: ~391 hours (terminal).

Oxaliplatin, sold under the brand name Eloxatin, is a platinum-based antineoplastic (chemotherapy) medication used in the treatment of colorectal cancer. It is typically administered together with fluorouracil and leucovorin in a combination known as FOLFOX, and with capecitabine as CAPOX (XELOX).

Medical uses

Oxaliplatin is indicated for adjuvant treatment of stage III colon cancer after complete resection of the primary tumor, and for the treatment of advanced colorectal cancer. It is also used off-label in pancreatic, gastric, and other gastrointestinal malignancies, often in the FOLFIRINOX regimen for pancreatic cancer.

Mechanism of action

Like other platinum coordination complexes such as cisplatin and carboplatin, oxaliplatin forms intrastrand and interstrand cross-links in DNA, blocking replication and transcription and triggering apoptosis. Its bulky diaminocyclohexane (DACH) ligand produces adducts that are not recognized by the mismatch repair system, which is thought to explain its activity in colorectal tumors that are relatively resistant to cisplatin.

Adverse effects

The dose-limiting toxicity of oxaliplatin is peripheral neuropathy. An acute, cold-triggered dysesthesia occurs in most patients during or shortly after infusion, and a chronic cumulative sensory neuropathy can persist. Other common effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, myelosuppression, and fatigue. Unlike cisplatin it is only mildly nephrotoxic and ototoxic.

History

Oxaliplatin was discovered in 1976 by Yoshinori Kidani at Nagoya City University and was developed by Debiopharm; it gained approval in Europe in 1996 and in the United States in 2002. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
