The Cat in the Hat is a 1957 children's book written and illustrated by the American author Theodor Geisel under his pen name Dr. Seuss. The story centers on a tall anthropomorphic cat wearing a red-and-white-striped top hat and a red bow tie, who appears at the home of Sally and her brother on a rainy day when their mother is away, and turns the household upside down with his chaotic games before cleaning everything up just before she returns.

Geisel wrote the book in response to a debate in the United States about literacy in early childhood and the perceived dullness of traditional primers such as those featuring Dick and Jane. He was challenged to write a book using only words a first-grader should know; the finished book uses a vocabulary of just 236 distinct words. Published by Random House, it was an immediate critical and commercial success and helped launch the Beginner Books imprint.

Plot

On a cold, wet day, a boy and his sister Sally sit bored at home, watching the rain, while their mother is out. A tall cat in a striped hat walks in uninvited, ignoring the protests of the children's pet fish, and offers to entertain them with tricks. He balances a series of household objects until he falls and makes a mess, then introduces Thing One and Thing Two, two small blue-haired creatures released from a large red crate, who fly kites indoors and knock down everything in sight. The boy finally catches the Things in a net. The Cat, dejected, leaves — but returns with a machine that cleans up the entire house moments before the mother comes home. The story ends with the narrator asking the reader: what would you tell your mother?

Background

The book's origins lie in the 1954 article by John Hersey in Life magazine criticizing the "pallid" primers used in American schools, and Rudolf Flesch's 1955 bestseller Why Johnny Can't Read. William Spaulding of Houghton Mifflin challenged Geisel to "write me a story that first-graders can't put down." Geisel worked from a list of some 350 elementary words, reducing it to 236 in the finished text. According to Geisel, the title came first: he decided to find a rhyme for "cat" and "hat" was the first word on the list that rhymed.

Reception and legacy

The Cat in the Hat sold over 450,000 copies within three years of publication and has since sold more than 16 million copies worldwide. It spawned a sequel, The Cat in the Hat Comes Back (1958), and the Cat became the mascot of Beginner Books and an icon of children's literacy. The story was adapted as an animated television special in 1971 and as a live-action feature film in 2003 starring Mike Myers, which was poorly received by critics. The Cat in the Hat also appears as host of the PBS Kids series The Cat in the Hat Knows a Lot About That! The book's role in reshaping early reading instruction is often compared with that of other category-defining works in their fields.
