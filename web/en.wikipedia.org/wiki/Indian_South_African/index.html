Indian South Africans in Durban

Indian South Africans are South Africans of Indian origin, the descendants of indentured labourers brought to the British colony of Natal from 1860 onward and of "passenger Indians" — merchants and traders who arrived freely from the 1870s. They form the largest Indian diaspora community in Africa and are concentrated in Durban, which has one of the largest Indian populations of any city outside India.

History

The first indentured Indians arrived aboard the SS Truro in November 1860 to work on Natal's sugarcane plantations. Between 1860 and 1911 roughly 150,000 indentured labourers came, mostly from Tamil- and Telugu-speaking regions and the Hindi belt. Discriminatory laws restricted Indian trade, movement, and land ownership; resistance to these laws was led in the 1890s and 1900s by the young lawyer Mohandas Gandhi, who developed his philosophy of satyagraha in South Africa. Under apartheid, Indians were classified as a separate population group, confined to designated areas under the Group Areas Act, and granted only limited, segregated political representation until universal suffrage in 1994.

Culture

The community is religiously diverse — largely Hindu and Muslim, with a Christian minority — and retains festivals such as Diwali and Eid, Indian cuisines (including Durban's famous bunny chow), and languages including Tamil, Hindi, Gujarati, Telugu, and Urdu, though English is now the dominant home language.
