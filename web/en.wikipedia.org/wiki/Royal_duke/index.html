Royal duke

A royal duke is a member of a royal family who holds the noble title of duke and the style of Royal Highness. In the United Kingdom, royal dukedoms are traditionally conferred on senior male-line members of the royal family, frequently upon their marriage.

British royal dukedoms

Several dukedoms are traditionally associated with the British monarchy, including the dukedoms of Cornwall and Rothesay (held by the heir apparent), and the dukedoms of York, Edinburgh, Sussex, Cambridge and Gloucester. The title Duke of Edinburgh was held for most of his life by Prince Philip, consort of Elizabeth II.

The sovereign is also Duke of Lancaster and Duke of Normandy (in respect of the Channel Islands), titles used regardless of the sovereign's gender.

Other monarchies

In other European monarchies, princes of the blood may likewise hold ducal titles, although the term "royal duke" is most distinctively associated with the British system of peerage.
