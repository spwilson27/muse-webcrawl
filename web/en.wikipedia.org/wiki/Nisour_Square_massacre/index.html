Nisour Square massacre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Nisour Square, Baghdad

The Nisour Square massacre occurred on 16 September 2007, when employees of Blackwater Security Consulting (now Academi), a private military company contracted by the United States government to provide security in Iraq, shot at Iraqi civilians, killing 17 and injuring 20 in Nisour Square, Baghdad, while escorting a U.S. Embassy convoy. The killings outraged Iraqis and strained relations between Iraq and the United States.

The shooting

The Blackwater convoy, tasked with escorting officials of the U.S. Agency for International Development, halted traffic at the square. Guards opened fire on vehicles and pedestrians, including a white Kia sedan carrying a young medical student and his mother; both were killed. Iraqi police and army officers at the scene reported that the convoy was not under fire. Blackwater maintained its guards had responded to an attack, a claim contradicted by FBI investigators, who found that at least 14 of the 17 killings were unjustified.

Legal proceedings

In 2008 the U.S. Department of Justice charged five guards with manslaughter and weapons offences; the case was dismissed in 2009 over the misuse of statements the guards had been compelled to give, then reinstated on appeal. In October 2014, four guards were convicted: Nicholas Slatten of first-degree murder, and Paul Slough, Evan Liberty, and Dustin Heard of voluntary manslaughter and firearms charges. A federal appeals court ordered resentencing, Slatten was retried and convicted again in 2018, and in December 2020 all four men were pardoned by President Donald Trump — a decision condemned by Iraqi officials and United Nations human rights experts.

Aftermath

The incident intensified Iraqi demands to end legal immunity for foreign contractors. The 2008 U.S.–Iraq Status of Forces Agreement subsequently placed private contractors under Iraqi jurisdiction, and Iraq revoked Blackwater's operating licence. The massacre remains a symbol of the accountability problems surrounding privatised military services in war zones.
