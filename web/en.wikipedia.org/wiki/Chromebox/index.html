A Chromebox mini PC

A Chromebox is a small-form-factor personal computer that runs Google's ChromeOS operating system. The devices are designed primarily for use while connected to the Internet, with most applications and data residing in the cloud rather than on the machine itself. Chromeboxes are the desktop counterpart to the Chromebook line of laptop computers.

History

The first Chromebox was released by Samsung in May 2012, as part of the second generation of ChromeOS devices alongside an updated Chromebook. Marketed as an inexpensive and compact desktop for schools, businesses, kiosks, and call centers, the early Chromebox was praised for its fast boot times, low price, and simple management through Google's web-based admin console.

Subsequent models were produced by Hewlett-Packard, Dell, Asus, Acer, and Lenovo. Higher-end configurations, including models with Intel Core i5 and i7 processors, were aimed at video-conferencing deployments such as Google Meet hardware kits, where a Chromebox serves as the computing unit driving a conference-room display, camera, and speakerphone.

Hardware

Chromeboxes are typically sold without a display, keyboard, or mouse, which the user supplies separately. Most models are small enough to be mounted behind a monitor using a VESA mount. Ports commonly include USB, HDMI or DisplayPort video outputs, Ethernet, and an SD card reader. Storage is generally a small solid-state drive, supplemented by cloud storage through Google Drive.

Software and updates

Like other ChromeOS devices, Chromeboxes receive automatic software updates from Google for a fixed support period, after which the device no longer receives new versions of the operating system. Many newer models support running Android applications from the Google Play Store and Linux applications through a virtual machine environment.
