Johnny Marr (born John Martin Maher, 31 October 1963) is an English musician, songwriter and singer. He achieved fame as the guitarist and co-songwriter of the Smiths, who were active from 1982 to 1987. His shimmering, layered guitar style — drawing on jangle pop, rockabilly and African highlife — helped define the sound of British indie music and has made him one of the most influential guitarists of his generation.

The Smiths

Marr formed the Smiths in Manchester in 1982 with singer Morrissey. Marr composed the music and Morrissey the lyrics for the band's four studio albums — The Smiths (1984), Meat Is Murder (1985), The Queen Is Dead (1986) and Strangeways, Here We Come (1987) — producing songs such as "This Charming Man", "How Soon Is Now?" and "There Is a Light That Never Goes Out". Exhausted by the band's workload and disagreements, Marr left in 1987, ending the Smiths.

Later career

After the Smiths, Marr played with The Pretenders and The The, and formed Electronic with New Order's Bernard Sumner, releasing the hit single "Getting Away with It" (1989). In the 2000s he led Johnny Marr and the Healers, then joined the American band Modest Mouse (contributing to the number-one album We Were Dead Before the Ship Even Sank, 2007) and the Cribs. A well-regarded session guitarist, he has also worked with Talking Heads, Pet Shop Boys, Bryan Ferry and Hans Zimmer, composing with Zimmer for film scores.

Solo work

Marr's solo albums — The Messenger (2013), Playland (2014), Call the Comet (2018) and the double album Fever Dreams Pts 1–4 (2022) — were critical and commercial successes in the UK, the last reaching number 4 on the albums chart. His autobiography, Set the Boy Free, was published in 2016.

Style and influence

Playing mainly Fender Jaguars and Rickenbackers, Marr favours intricate arpeggios, chiming harmonics and densely overdubbed parts over conventional solos. He is routinely ranked among the greatest guitarists of all time by music publications, and guitarists from Radiohead's Ed O'Brien to countless indie bands cite him as a formative influence.
