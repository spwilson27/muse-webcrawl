Miguel de Unamuno

Miguel de Unamuno
Born: 29 September 1864, Bilbao, Spain
Died: 31 December 1936, Salamanca, Spain
Occupation: Novelist, poet, essayist, philosopher
Movement: Generation of '98

Miguel de Unamuno y Jugo (29 September 1864 – 31 December 1936) was a Spanish essayist, novelist, poet, playwright, philosopher and academic associated with the Generation of '98. He served as rector of the University of Salamanca and is best known for his philosophical work The Tragic Sense of Life (1913) and the novel Abel Sánchez (1917).

Life

Born in Bilbao to Basque parents, Unamuno studied philosophy and letters in Madrid before winning the chair of Greek at Salamanca in 1891; he became rector in 1901. His public criticism of the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera led to his exile to Fuerteventura and then France in 1924; he returned in triumph in 1930. Initially sympathetic to the military uprising of 1936, he recoiled from its brutality, famously confronting General Millán Astray at the University of Salamanca on 12 October 1936 with words to the effect of "You will win, but you will not convince." He died under house arrest two months later.

Thought

Unamuno's philosophy centres on the "man of flesh and bone" and his hunger for personal immortality, which reason denies but faith demands — the "tragic sense of life." He coined the term intrahistoria for the quiet, undocumented life of ordinary people beneath the surface of events, and called his own fictional method the nivola, a self-conscious novel form exemplified by Niebla (Mist, 1914), in which a character rebels against his author.
