The Pontifical Swiss Guard (German: Päpstliche Schweizergarde) is an armed force and honour guard unit maintained by the Holy See that protects the Pope and the Apostolic Palace within Vatican City. Established in 1506 under Pope Julius II, it is among the oldest military units in continuous operation, and is often regarded as the smallest army in the world.

History

Swiss mercenaries were famed across Renaissance Europe for their discipline and effectiveness. Pope Julius II recruited a permanent guard of 150 Swiss soldiers, who arrived in Rome on 22 January 1506. The Guard's most famous action was the defence of Pope Clement VII during the Sack of Rome on 6 May 1527, when 147 of the 189 guardsmen died fighting on the steps of St Peter's and covering the Pope's escape to Castel Sant'Angelo. New recruits still swear their oath each year on 6 May in commemoration.

Duties and recruitment

Guardsmen protect the Pope, guard the entrances to Vatican City, and perform ceremonial duties. Recruits must be Swiss citizens, Catholic, male, between 19 and 30 years old, at least 174 cm tall, and have completed basic training with the Swiss Armed Forces. Although famous for their Renaissance-style blue, red, and yellow uniforms and halberds, the Guard is also a modern security force trained in firearms, close protection, and counter-terrorism.
