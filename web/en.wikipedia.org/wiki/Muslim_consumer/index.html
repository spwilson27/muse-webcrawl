Muslim consumer

The term Muslim consumer refers to consumers whose purchasing behaviour is influenced by Islamic principles and values, including requirements that products and services be halal (permissible) and tayyib (wholesome). With a global Muslim population of roughly two billion, Muslim consumers form one of the world's largest consumer segments.

Market significance

Industry reports, such as the State of the Global Islamic Economy Report, estimate Muslim consumer spending across food, fashion, travel, media, pharmaceuticals and cosmetics at well over two trillion US dollars annually. Key markets include Indonesia, Malaysia, the Gulf states, Turkey and large Muslim minorities in Europe and North America.

Characteristics

Muslim consumer behaviour is shaped by halal certification requirements for food and other products, avoidance of interest-based finance (leading to demand for Islamic banking and finance), modest fashion preferences, and halal tourism services. Marketers increasingly segment Muslim consumers by degree of religiosity, generation and region, and brands have developed dedicated halal product lines and Ramadan marketing campaigns.
