Jimmy Perry

James Perry OBE (20 September 1923 – 23 October 2016) was an English scriptwriter and actor. He co-created, with David Croft, some of Britain's most popular television sitcoms, including Dad's Army (1968–1977), It Ain't Half Hot Mum (1974–1981) and Hi-de-Hi! (1980–1988).

Perry in later life.

Early life

Perry was born in Barnes, London. During the Second World War, while still a teenager, he served in the Home Guard — the direct inspiration for the young Private Pike in Dad's Army — and later in the Royal Artillery, performing in army concert parties in India and Burma, experiences he drew on for It Ain't Half Hot Mum. After the war he trained at RADA and worked as an actor and entertainer at holiday camps, the model for Hi-de-Hi!.

Partnership with David Croft

Perry conceived Dad's Army and took the scripts to producer David Croft; their partnership lasted for decades and produced a string of hit series built on ensemble casts, catchphrases and affectionate period settings. Perry also wrote the Dad's Army theme tune, "Who Do You Think You Are Kidding, Mr Hitler?", sung by Bud Flanagan, which won an Ivor Novello Award.

Legacy

Perry was appointed OBE in 1978. Dad's Army remains one of the most repeated programmes on British television, and Perry's work is credited with shaping the classic British sitcom. He died in 2016, aged 93.
