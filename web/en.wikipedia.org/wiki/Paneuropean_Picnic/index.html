Paneuropean Picnic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Paneuropean Picnic (German: Paneuropäisches Picknick; Hungarian: Páneurópai piknik) was a peace demonstration held on 19 August 1989 at the Austro-Hungarian border near Sopron, Hungary, at which a border gate was symbolically opened — and several hundred East German holidaymakers seized the moment to flee to Austria, in the largest mass escape from East Germany since the Berlin Wall was built. The event is widely regarded as a decisive step toward the fall of the Iron Curtain and German reunification.

Background

The picnic was organized by members of the Hungarian opposition parties, including the Hungarian Democratic Forum, under the patronage of Otto von Habsburg and Hungarian minister Imre Pozsgay, ostensibly to test the reaction of Mikhail Gorbachev's Soviet Union to a brief opening of the border. Hungary had begun dismantling its border fence in May 1989, and thousands of East Germans had travelled to Hungary hoping to cross westward.

The event

At the planned ceremony, Hungarian border guards were to allow a small delegation through an old gate on the road between St. Margarethen and Sopronkőhida. Leaflets advertising the picnic had reached East German tourists, and when the gate opened, hundreds pushed through. In successive waves, roughly 600–900 East Germans crossed into Austria; the Hungarian guards, under orders not to use force, did not intervene. A famous press photograph shows the crowd streaming through the broken gate.

Consequences

Moscow did not react, emboldening further escapes; on 11 September Hungary formally opened its border to East Germans, tens of thousands fled, and the pressure contributed to the collapse of the East German regime and the opening of the Berlin Wall on 9 November 1989. A memorial at the picnic site commemorates the event, and anniversaries have drawn German and Hungarian leaders, including Angela Merkel and Viktor Orbán in 2009.
