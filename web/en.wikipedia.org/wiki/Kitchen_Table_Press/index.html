Kitchen Table: Women of Color Press

Kitchen Table: Women of Color Press
Founded1980 (Boston); incorporated New York, 1981
FoundersBarbara Smith, Audre Lorde, Cherríe Moraga and others
TypeFeminist publishing house
FocusWriting by women of colour
StatusDefunct (mid-1990s)

Kitchen Table: Women of Color Press was an American feminist publishing house devoted to publishing writing by women of colour, founded in 1980 and active until the mid-1990s. It grew out of discussions among Black feminist activists including Barbara Smith, Audre Lorde and Cherríe Moraga, and is commonly described as the first United States publisher run entirely by women of colour for the work of women of colour. The name evokes the kitchen table as the traditional centre of women's work and conversation in the home.

Publications

The press's founding publication was the pamphlet of the Combahee River Collective statement's successors: it issued This Bridge Called My Back: Writings by Radical Women of Color (1981, co-published with Persephone Press; second edition 1983), edited by Moraga and Gloria E. Anzaldúa, a landmark anthology of women-of-colour feminism. Other notable titles include the "Freedom Organizing Pamphlet" series, the anthology Home Girls: A Black Feminist Anthology (1983), edited by Barbara Smith, and Cuentos: Stories by Latinas (1983).

Significance

Kitchen Table works were taken up in women's studies programmes across the United States and are credited with helping establish women-of-colour feminism as a field. Though small and chronically underfunded, the press demonstrated a model of activist publishing controlled by the communities whose writing it circulated.
