Walldorf

Country: Germany
State: Baden-Württemberg
District: Rhein-Neckar-Kreis
Population: about 16,000

Walldorf is a town in the Rhein-Neckar-Kreis district of Baden-Württemberg in southwestern Germany, near Heidelberg and Mannheim. It is best known internationally as the headquarters of SAP SE, the multinational enterprise software company, which is the town's dominant employer and one of the largest technology firms in Europe.

History

Walldorf was first mentioned in a document of 770 AD in the records of Lorsch Abbey. For centuries it was a modest agricultural community in the Upper Rhine Plain, noted for asparagus and tobacco cultivation. In the nineteenth century, Johann Jacob Astor, who went on to become one of the richest men in the United States through the fur trade and New York real estate, was born in Walldorf in 1763; the town maintains an Astorhaus museum in his memory and retains sister-city links with Astoria, Oregon, and Astoria, New York.

SAP

SAP was founded in nearby Weinheim in 1972 by five former IBM engineers and moved its headquarters to Walldorf in 1977. Its sprawling campus transformed the town, giving it a population and tax base far beyond its size and making Walldorf one of the wealthiest municipalities in Germany relative to its population. Hasso Plattner, a co-founder of SAP, was born in Berlin but is closely associated with the town, and Dietmar Hopp, another co-founder and benefactor of the local football club TSG Hoffenheim in nearby Sinsheim, grew up in the region.

Transport and twin towns

The town is served by the Wiesloch-Walldorf station on the Rhine Valley Railway and lies close to the A5 and A6 motorways. Walldorf is twinned with Astoria, Oregon, and other towns associated with the Astor name.
