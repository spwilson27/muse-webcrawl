ISKCON

The International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), known colloquially as the Hare Krishna movement, is a Gaudiya Vaishnava Hindu religious organization. It was founded in 1966 in New York City by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada. Its core beliefs are based on Hindu scriptures, particularly the Bhagavad Gita and the Bhagavata Purana. ISKCON is part of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, which has had adherents in India since the late 15th century.

Beliefs and practices

Devotees practice bhakti yoga, centred on devotion to Krishna as the supreme deity. The most famous and publicly known practice is the chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra — "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare" — both privately on prayer beads (japa) and publicly in congregational chanting (kirtan). Members follow four regulative principles: no meat-eating, no illicit sex, no gambling, and no intoxication.

History

Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada arrived in the United States in 1965 and incorporated ISKCON in July 1966. The movement spread rapidly during the counterculture of the late 1960s, attracting young followers in North America and Europe. Prabhupada translated and published many Vaishnava texts through the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust. He died in 1977, after which the society has been governed by its Governing Body Commission.

Organization and activities

ISKCON operates hundreds of temples, rural communities, schools and restaurants worldwide. Its Food for Life programme distributes vegetarian food relief. Major temples include those in Mayapur (West Bengal), Vrindavan, and the Temple of the Vedic Planetarium. The movement is also known for its Ratha Yatra chariot festivals held in cities around the world.
