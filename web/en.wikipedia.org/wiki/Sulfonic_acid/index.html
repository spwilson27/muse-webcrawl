Sulfonic acid

A sulfonic acid (or sulphonic acid) is a member of the class of organosulfur compounds with the general formula R–S(=O)2–OH, where R is an alkyl or aryl group. The –SO3H group is strongly acidic — sulfonic acids are among the strongest organic acids — because the resulting sulfonate anion is stabilised by resonance delocalisation over three oxygen atoms. Their salts and esters are called sulfonates.

Preparation

Aromatic sulfonic acids are made by sulfonation of arenes with concentrated or fuming sulfuric acid, a reversible electrophilic aromatic substitution. Aliphatic sulfonic acids are prepared by sulfoxidation or sulfochlorination of alkanes, or by oxidation of thiols. The amino acid taurine is a naturally occurring aminosulfonic acid.

Applications

Sulfonic acids and their salts are workhorse industrial chemicals. Alkylbenzene sulfonates are the principal surfactants in laundry detergents. Strong sulfonic acids such as methanesulfonic acid and p-toluenesulfonic acid are used as acid catalysts in organic synthesis. Sulfonated polystyrene resins (such as Nafion and ion-exchange resins) serve as solid acid catalysts, water softeners and proton-exchange membranes in fuel cells. The sulfonamide ("sulfa") drugs, though amides rather than acids, share the sulfur(VI) centre. Dyes and pigments are often solubilised by sulfonic acid groups, and lignosulfonates from the paper industry are used as dispersants and binders.
