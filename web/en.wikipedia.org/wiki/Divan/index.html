Divan

A divan (from Persian dīvān) is a piece of couch-like seating furniture, consisting of a mattress resting directly on a low platform or the floor, often placed lengthwise against a wall with cushions along the back. The word and the furniture form derive from the diwan, a council chamber or office in the Middle East, where such cushioned platforms lined the walls.

History

In the Ottoman Empire and across the Middle East and North Africa, a divan was a long raised platform, covered with a mattress and cushions, running along the walls of a room, used for sitting and reclining. European travellers encountered the style through contact with the Levant, and the divan entered Western furniture design in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, becoming especially fashionable in France during the nineteenth century as a low sofa without a full back or arms.

Uses and related meanings

Divans also became associated with the psychiatrist's or psychoanalyst's consulting room, where the patient reclines on a couch or divan. The same Persian word dīvān gave rise to other senses: a collection of poems by one author (a divan of poetry, as in Goethe's West–Eastern Divan), a government council or court (the Imperial Council of the Ottoman Empire), and historically a customs house or register office.

Bed divans

In British usage, a "divan bed" denotes a bed whose base is a solid, often storage-containing upholstered box (sometimes with drawers) on which the mattress rests, as distinct from a slatted frame.
