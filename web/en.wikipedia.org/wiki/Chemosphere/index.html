Chemosphere

The chemosphere is the region of a planet's atmosphere in which chemical reactions driven by solar ultraviolet radiation — photochemical reactions — predominate. On Earth the term usually refers to the upper stratosphere and the mesosphere, roughly between 30 and 110 km above the surface, where ozone formation and destruction, and the photodissociation of oxygen and other gases, are the dominant processes.

Processes

In the chemosphere, energetic ultraviolet photons break apart molecules of oxygen, nitrogen and water vapour, generating reactive species such as atomic oxygen, hydroxyl radicals and ozone. The Chapman cycle describes the photochemical production and loss of ozone in this region, and reactions involving chlorine and nitrogen compounds here are responsible for ozone depletion. Because photochemistry switches off at night, the composition of the chemosphere shows a strong day–night variation.
