Democratic confederalism

Democratic confederalism (Kurdish: Konfederalîzma Demokratîk), also known as Kurdish communalism or Apoism, is a political concept theorized by Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) leader Abdullah Öcalan about a system of democratic self-organization with the features of a confederation based on the principles of autonomy, direct democracy, political ecology, feminism ("Jineology"), multiculturalism, self-defense, self-governance and elements of a cooperative economy.

Influenced by the social ecology and libertarian municipalism of the American theorist Murray Bookchin, Öcalan reformulated the political goals of the Kurdish national movement, abandoning the old aspiration for an independent Kurdish nation-state in favour of a stateless model of radical democracy.

Principles

Democratic confederalism rejects the nation-state as a structure of power and instead proposes a network of self-governing communes, neighbourhood assemblies and councils, linked in confederal structures from the local to the regional level. Its stated pillars include the liberation of women, ecology, and the coexistence of diverse ethnic and religious communities.

Implementation

The concept forms the ideological basis of the multi-ethnic political system administered in the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (Rojava), where communes, co-presidencies shared between a man and a woman, and women's autonomous institutions have been established since 2012, against the background of the Syrian civil war.
