John Cox Stevens

John Cox Stevens (1785–1857) was an American yachtsman and sportsman, founder and first commodore of the New York Yacht Club, and a member of the syndicate that built the schooner America, winner of the race that became the America's Cup.

Yachting

Stevens, scion of the Stevens family of Hoboken engineers and inventors, helped found the New York Yacht Club in 1844 aboard his schooner Gimcrack. In 1851 the syndicate's yacht America defeated the British fleet around the Isle of Wight, winning the £100 Cup that was renamed the America's Cup and entrusted to the club under a deed of gift.

Other pursuits

A member of the family that pioneered American steam navigation and railroads, Stevens was also an early promoter of cricket in the United States and president of sporting clubs in the New York area.
