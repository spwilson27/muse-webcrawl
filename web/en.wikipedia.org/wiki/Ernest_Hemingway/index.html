Ernest Hemingway

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Ernest Hemingway in Key West in the 1930s: bearded, sunburnt, holding a marlin beside his boat Pilar]

Ernest Miller Hemingway (21 July 1899 – 2 July 1961) was an American novelist, short-story writer and journalist. His economical and understated style — the 'iceberg theory', in which most of the meaning lies beneath the surface — had a strong influence on 20th-century fiction, while his adventurous lifestyle and public image influenced later generations.

An ambulance driver wounded in the First World War, Hemingway joined the Paris expatriate circle of Gertrude Stein's 'Lost Generation'. His novels include The Sun Also Rises (1926), A Farewell to Arms (1929), For Whom the Bell Tolls (1940) and The Old Man and the Sea (1952), which contributed to his Nobel Prize in Literature in 1954. He reported on the Spanish Civil War and the Second World War, lived for years in Cuba and Key West, and died by suicide in Idaho in 1961.
