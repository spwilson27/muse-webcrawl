Mortarboard

The square academic cap, graduate cap, cap, mortarboard (because of its similarity in appearance to the mortarboard used by bricklayers to hold mortar) or Oxford cap, is an item of academic dress consisting of a horizontal square board fixed upon a skull-cap, with a tassel attached to the centre. Together with a gown, it forms part of the formal dress worn by students and academics at many universities, especially at graduation ceremonies.

History and use

The mortarboard is believed to have developed from the biretta, a similar square cap worn by Roman Catholic clergy. It became a standard part of academic regalia in Britain and the United States during the 19th and 20th centuries. By tradition at graduation, the tassel may be moved from one side of the cap to the other to mark the conferral of the degree, and graduates in the United States often throw their caps into the air at the end of the ceremony.

Variations

British undergraduates historically wore the cap with the tassel, while masters of arts and doctors may wear variants with different fittings. Some universities use a soft cap or "pudding hat" variant. Doctoral graduates at some institutions wear the Tudor bonnet instead.
