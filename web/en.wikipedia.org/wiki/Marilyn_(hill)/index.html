A Marilyn is a mountain or hill in the United Kingdom, Ireland or surrounding islands with a topographic prominence of at least 150 metres (492 feet), regardless of absolute height. The classification was devised by Alan Dawson and published in his 1992 book The Relative Hills of Britain. The name is a playful reference to the Munros of Scotland and the film star Marilyn Monroe: a Marilyn is defined by its "relative" prominence, punning on a phrase associated with Monroe's figure.

Definition

Prominence measures how much a summit stands out from the surrounding terrain: it is the height of the peak relative to the lowest contour line encircling it and no higher summit. A hill qualifies as a Marilyn if its prominence is 150 m or more on all sides, even if the summit itself is modest — some Marilyns are under 200 m high, while every British and Irish summit of any classification that meets the 150 m prominence test is included.

Distribution

The list contains around 2,000 hills: the great majority in Scotland, with several hundred in England and Wales and a substantial number in Ireland, plus hills on offshore islands. The highest Marilyn is Ben Nevis (1,345 m), Scotland's and Britain's highest mountain. Famous Marilyns include Snowdon (Yr Wyddfa), Scafell Pike, Ben Macdui, and low but distinctive hills such as Stac Pollaidh and The Storr's Old Man on Skye.

Bagging

Climbing all the Marilyns in a region is a popular challenge among hillwalkers ("Marilyn bagging"). Because the list depends on accurate surveying, occasional re-surveys promote or demote hills near the 150 m threshold. See also Munro, Topographic prominence, and Lists of mountains.
