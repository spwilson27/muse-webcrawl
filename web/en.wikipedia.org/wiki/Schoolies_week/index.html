Schoolies week

LocationAustralia (chiefly the Gold Coast, Queensland)
DateLate November – early December
ParticipantsYear 12 school leavers ("schoolies")
Established1970s (Gold Coast tradition)

Schoolies week (or simply Schoolies) is an Australian tradition of week-long holidays for Year 12 school leavers, held at the end of the school year, generally in late November and early December. The most popular destination is Surfers Paradise on the Gold Coast, Queensland, which draws tens of thousands of graduates; other destinations include Byron Bay, the Sunshine Coast, Rottnest Island, Victor Harbor, Lorne, and overseas locations such as Bali and Fiji.

History

The tradition traces to the 1970s, when graduating students began gathering at the Gold Coast for end-of-school celebrations. It grew rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s into a major organised event with concerts, beach parties and official entertainment programmes, supported by state and local governments.

Celebrations and organisation

On the Gold Coast, official Schoolies events are concentrated in a fenced, alcohol-free entertainment hub in Surfers Paradise with live music and activities. Volunteers known as "Red Frogs" — named after the red frog lollies they hand out — provide support, hydration stations and pancake breakfasts to partying school leavers, alongside police, medical services and community groups.

Criticism and safety

Schoolies has long been associated with binge drinking, drug use, violence and accidents, including balcony falls, and attracts extensive media coverage each year. Older gatecrashers known as "toolies" are a recurring concern for police. State governments and schools run pre-event education campaigns, and destinations have progressively formalised safety arrangements.
