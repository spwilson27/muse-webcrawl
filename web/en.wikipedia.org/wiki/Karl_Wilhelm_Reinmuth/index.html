Karl Wilhelm Reinmuth

Karl Wilhelm Reinmuth
Born: 4 April 1892, Heidelberg
Died: 6 May 1979, Heidelberg
Known for: Discovery of ~395 asteroids

Karl Wilhelm Reinmuth (4 April 1892 – 6 May 1979) was a German astronomer and a prolific discoverer of minor planets, credited with the discovery of nearly 400 asteroids while working at the Heidelberg-Königstuhl State Observatory.

Career

Reinmuth began work at Heidelberg Observatory in 1912 and remained there, apart from wartime service, until his retirement in 1957. Using photographic plates taken with the Bruce telescope, he systematically surveyed the sky for moving objects.

Discoveries

His discoveries include the near-Earth asteroid 1862 Apollo, the prototype of the Apollo group, found in 1932, and 69230 Hermes, recovered decades after its discovery. The main-belt asteroid 1111 Reinmuthia is named in his honour.
