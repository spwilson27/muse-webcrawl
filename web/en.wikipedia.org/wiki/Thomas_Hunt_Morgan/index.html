Thomas Hunt Morgan

Thomas Hunt Morgan (1866–1945) was an American biologist whose experiments with the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster established that genes lie on chromosomes. He received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1933.

The fly room

At Columbia University, Morgan's cramped ‘fly room’ bred millions of fruit flies. In 1910 a white-eyed male mutant appeared; its inheritance pattern showed the gene was on the X chromosome — the first gene mapped to a specific chromosome.

His students — Alfred Sturtevant (who made the first genetic map as an undergraduate), Calvin Bridges and Hermann Muller — founded classical genetics. Morgan set out the synthesis in The Mechanism of Mendelian Heredity (1915).

Later work

Morgan moved to Caltech in 1928 and built its biology division. Initially sceptical of both Mendelism and Darwinian selection, he became their great experimental unifier; his work underpins Population genetics.
