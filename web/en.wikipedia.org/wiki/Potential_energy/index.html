Potential energy

Potential energy is the energy held by an object because of its position relative to other objects, stresses within itself, its electric charge, or other factors. In the International System of Units the unit of potential energy is the joule (J), the same as for any form of energy.

Forms

Gravitational potential energy is associated with the position of a mass in a gravitational field; near Earth's surface it is given by U = mgh, where m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration, and h is height above a chosen reference. Elastic potential energy is stored in deformed materials such as a stretched spring, described by Hooke's law as ½kx². Electric potential energy arises between charged particles, and chemical potential energy is stored in molecular bonds and released in reactions such as combustion.

Concept

Potential energy can be defined only for conservative forces, for which the work done is independent of the path taken. The force is the negative gradient of the potential energy function: F = −∇U. Because only differences in potential energy are measurable, the zero of potential is arbitrary. The term "potential energy" was introduced by the Scottish engineer and physicist William Rankine in the 19th century, building on the older concept of vis viva and Leibniz's ideas of stored "dead force."

Applications

Potential energy underlies hydroelectric power (water stored at height), pumped-storage schemes, archery and catapults (elastic energy), and roller coasters, which trade height for speed as potential energy converts to kinetic energy.
