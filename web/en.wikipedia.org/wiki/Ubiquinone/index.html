Ubiquinone

Ubiquinone, also known as coenzyme Q or CoQ, is a quinone compound found in the membranes of most aerobic organisms, from bacteria to mammals. The name derives from its ubiquitous presence in biological systems. In humans the predominant form is coenzyme Q10, whose side chain contains ten isoprenoid units.

Function

Ubiquinone is a mobile electron carrier in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, shuttling electrons from complexes I and II to complex III while contributing to the proton gradient used to synthesize ATP. Its reduced form, ubiquinol, also acts as a lipid-soluble antioxidant.

Discovery and supplements

Coenzyme Q was discovered in beef heart mitochondria by Frederick Crane and colleagues in 1957, and its structure was worked out soon after; Peter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory explained its central role in energy transduction and contributed to his Nobel Prize. CoQ10 is widely sold as a dietary supplement, though evidence for most claimed benefits is limited.
