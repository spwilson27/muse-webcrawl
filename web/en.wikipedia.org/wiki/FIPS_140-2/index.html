FIPS 140-2

FIPS Publication 140-2, Security Requirements for Cryptographic Modules, is a US government Federal Information Processing Standard issued by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in 2001. It specifies security requirements for cryptographic modules — hardware, software or firmware that implements approved security functions — used to protect sensitive but unclassified information in US federal systems.

Security levels

The standard defines four increasing levels. Level 1 requires production-grade equipment and externally tested algorithms. Level 2 adds tamper-evident coatings or seals and role-based authentication. Level 3 adds tamper resistance and response (such as zeroization of keys on tampering), identity-based authentication, and separation of critical security parameters. Level 4 adds a complete protective envelope and environmental failure protection. Requirements span eleven areas, from module interfaces and key management to physical security and mitigation of side-channel attacks.

Validation and succession

Modules are tested by accredited laboratories under the Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP), run jointly by NIST and the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security; validated modules appear on a public list and procurement rules often mandate them. In 2019 NIST published the successor FIPS 140-3, aligned with the international standard ISO/IEC 19790, and FIPS 140-2 validations were progressively moved to historical status.
