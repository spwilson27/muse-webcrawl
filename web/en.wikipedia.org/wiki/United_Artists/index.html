United Artists (UA) is an American film production and distribution company founded in 1919 by Charlie Chaplin, Mary Pickford, Douglas Fairbanks and D. W. Griffith so that artists could control their own work — prompting studio executive Richard Rowland's quip that "the lunatics have taken over the asylum".

History

UA distributed its founders' films (Chaplin's The Gold Rush, City Lights) and independent producers' work, but rarely owned a studio lot. Near collapse by 1951, it was revived by Arthur Krim and Robert Benjamin, who financed independent producers and backed the James Bond franchise (from 1962), the Beatles' A Hard Day's Night, West Side Story, One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest and Rocky. Sold to Transamerica in 1967, UA was bought by MGM in 1981 after the losses of Heaven's Gate (1980). It continues as a label, later under Amazon MGM Studios. It also had a records arm — compare Studio One.
