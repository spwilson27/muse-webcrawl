Rockwell Automation, Inc. is an American provider of industrial automation and digital transformation technologies. Headquartered in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, it employs roughly 29,000 people and serves customers in more than 100 countries. Its best-known brands include Allen-Bradley and FactoryTalk software.

History

The company traces its origins to the Allen-Bradley Company, founded in 1903 by Lynde Bradley and Dr. Stanton Allen in Milwaukee. Allen-Bradley became famous for its motor starters and later for programmable logic controllers (PLCs). Rockwell International acquired Allen-Bradley in 1985 for $1.65 billion. After Rockwell International split its businesses in the late 1990s, the automation unit became Rockwell Automation in 2002.

Products and services

Rockwell Automation's portfolio covers control systems, motor control devices, sensors, human-machine interfaces, industrial networks, and software for manufacturing execution and analytics under its "Connected Enterprise" strategy. Its Logix control platform and Studio 5000 design environment are widely used in discrete and process manufacturing. Competitors include Siemens, Schneider Electric, Emerson Electric, and ABB.
