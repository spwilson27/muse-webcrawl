John William Heisman (23 October 1869 – 3 October 1936) was an American football player and coach of football, basketball and baseball, and a sportswriter and actor. One of the game's great early innovators, he is credited with popularising the forward pass, developing the centre snap, the hidden-ball play and the "Heisman shift," and he is the namesake of the Heisman Trophy, awarded annually to the most outstanding player in college football.

Early life and playing career

Heisman was born in Cleveland, Ohio, and raised in Titusville, Pennsylvania. He played football at Brown University (1887–89) and at the University of Pennsylvania while earning a law degree (1890–92), playing guard, tackle, centre and end despite weighing only about 158 pounds. After graduating he turned to coaching rather than law.

Coaching career

Heisman coached Oberlin (where his 1892 team beat Ohio State twice and nearly beat Michigan), Akron, Auburn, Clemson — where he also helped start the baseball and track programs — and from 1904 to 1919 Georgia Tech, his longest and most successful tenure. At Tech his Golden Tornado teams won four consecutive conference titles and the 1917 national championship, with a 33-game unbeaten streak. His 1916 Georgia Tech team inflicted the most lopsided victory in college football history, 222–0 over Cumberland. Later stops included Penn, Washington & Jefferson and Rice. His career college football record was 186–70–18.

Innovations and rules advocacy

Having witnessed a desperation long pass in an 1895 game, Heisman campaigned for a decade to legalise the forward pass, finally succeeding in 1906. He introduced the centre's direct snap to the quarterback (replacing the rolled ball), the handoff and the scoreboard, advocated dividing the game into quarters, and devised the audible "hike" snap signal. As a rules committee member and editor of the official rule book he shaped the modern sport, and the stiff-arm pose later immortalised in the trophy is echoed by his name.

The trophy

After coaching, Heisman became athletic director of the Downtown Athletic Club in New York. The club created an award for the best college player east of the Mississippi in 1935 — renamed nationally the following year — and after Heisman's death in October 1936 the trophy was renamed in his honour. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame as a coach in 1954.
