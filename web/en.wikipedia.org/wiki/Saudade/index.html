Saudade

Saudade (Portuguese: [sɐwˈðaðɨ]) is a Portuguese and Galician word describing a deep emotional state of melancholic longing for something or someone absent — a person, a place, a past time — often carrying the bittersweet knowledge that the object of longing may never return. It is frequently cited as an example of an "untranslatable" word and is regarded as a defining sentiment of Portuguese and Brazilian culture.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Latin solitas (solitude) through Old Portuguese soidade and suidade. It appears in medieval Galician-Portuguese lyric poetry (the cantigas de amigo) and gained depth during Portugal's Age of Discoveries, when sailors and emigrants remained far from home for years. Saudade was elaborated by the Portuguese cultural movement known as Saudosismo, led by the poet Teixeira de Pascoaes in the early 20th century, which treated it as the spiritual essence of the Portuguese people.

In music and culture

Saudade is the emotional core of fado, Portugal's emblematic song genre, famously performed by Amália Rodrigues, and of Brazilian bossa nova — the classic song "Chega de Saudade" (1958, "No More Blues") by Tom Jobim and Vinícius de Moraes is often called the first bossa nova song. In Cape Verde, the related concept sodade was immortalised by Cesária Évora. The Galician form morriña expresses a similar homesickness. Writers describe saudade not as simple sadness but as a pleasurable ache: the presence of absence, honouring what was loved by feeling its loss.
