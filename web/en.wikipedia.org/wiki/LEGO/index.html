Lego is a line of plastic construction toys manufactured by the Lego Group, a privately held company based in Billund, Denmark. Its flagship product is the interlocking brick: colourful studded blocks that can be assembled and reassembled to build vehicles, buildings, creatures and almost anything else. The name combines the Danish words leg godt, "play well".

History

The company was founded in 1932 by carpenter Ole Kirk Christiansen, making wooden toys; it began producing plastic bricks in 1947 and patented the modern stud-and-tube coupling design in 1958, which made bricks durably clutch together — and remain compatible with bricks made today. The minifigure arrived in 1978, and themed ranges from Town and Castle to licensed Star Wars sets made Lego the world's largest toy company by the 2010s.

The group nearly collapsed in 2003 under debt and unfocused expansion; a turnaround under Jørgen Vig Knudstorp refocused on the core brick, licensed themes and hits such as The Lego Movie (2014).

The system

Lego elements are made to tolerances of a few micrometres, and the "system of play" means every set interoperates. Lines include City, Technic, Duplo for toddlers, Friends, Ninjago, Architecture and the adult-oriented Icons range. Legoland theme parks and a large adult fan community (AFOLs) extend the brand well beyond children.
