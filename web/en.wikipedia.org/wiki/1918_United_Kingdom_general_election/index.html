1918 United Kingdom general election

The 1918 United Kingdom general election was held on Saturday 14 December 1918, five weeks after the Armistice that ended the First World War. It is often called the "coupon election" because candidates of the governing coalition were endorsed by a letter — nicknamed "the coupon" — signed by Prime Minister David Lloyd George and Conservative leader Bonar Law. The coalition won a landslide, while in Ireland Sinn Féin swept the nationalist vote, transforming British and Irish politics.

Background

The election was the first held under the Representation of the People Act 1918, which enfranchised all men over 21 and women over 30 who met a property qualification, nearly tripling the electorate. It was the first general election since December 1910, Parliament having been prolonged during the war. Lloyd George's coalition of Conservatives and Coalition Liberals campaigned on his wartime leadership and promises of a land "fit for heroes".

Results

The coalition won 473 seats and a huge parliamentary majority. Asquith's independent Liberals were reduced to a rump, and Asquith himself lost his seat; the Liberal Party never again formed a majority government. Labour, though outpaced in seats, substantially increased its vote share and emerged as the principal opposition in Great Britain.

Ireland

In Ireland the election was revolutionary. Sinn Féin won 73 of the 105 Irish seats on a platform of abstention and independence, annihilating the Irish Parliamentary Party. Its elected members refused to take their seats at Westminster and instead constituted the First Dáil in Dublin in January 1919, opening the Irish War of Independence. Countess Markievicz, elected for Dublin St Patrick's, became the first woman elected to the House of Commons, though as a Sinn Féin abstentionist she did not take her seat; the first woman to sit was Nancy Astor, elected in 1919.
