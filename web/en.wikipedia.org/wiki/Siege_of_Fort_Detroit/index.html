Siege of Fort Detroit

Date9 May – October 1763
LocationFort Detroit, Michigan
ResultBritish victory

The Siege of Fort Detroit was an ultimately unsuccessful attempt by North American Indians to capture Fort Detroit from the British during Pontiac's War. Beginning on 9 May 1763, the Ottawa leader Pontiac led some 300 warriors in a failed surprise entry and then settled into a siege.

Course of the siege

The garrison under Major Henry Gladwin, forewarned of the plot, held out behind the palisade, resupplied by armed schooners on the Detroit River despite attacks such as the Battle of Bloody Run (31 July 1763). As autumn came and French support failed to materialise, Pontiac's coalition dissolved and the siege was lifted at the end of October.

Significance

The siege was the centrepiece of Pontiac's War, the broad Indigenous uprising against British occupation of the Great Lakes and Ohio Country after the French and Indian War.
