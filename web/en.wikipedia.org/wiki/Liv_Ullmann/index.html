Liv Ullmann

Born16 December 1938, Tokyo, Japan
NationalityNorwegian
OccupationsActress, director, writer

Liv Johanne Ullmann (born 16 December 1938) is a Norwegian actress, director and writer. Recognised as one of the greatest European actresses, she is best known for her collaborations with Swedish filmmaker Ingmar Bergman.

Career

Born in Tokyo to Norwegian parents, Ullmann began acting in Norway in the mid-1950s. Her international breakthrough came with Bergman's Persona (1966), followed by Shame (1968), Cries and Whispers (1972), Scenes from a Marriage (1973), Face to Face (1976, Academy Award nomination), and Autumn Sonata (1978). She was Oscar-nominated also for The Emigrants (1971). As a director she made Sofie (1992), Faithless (2000, from Bergman's script), and Miss Julie (2014).

Honours

Ullmann co-founded the Women's Refugee Commission, served as a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador, and received an Honorary Academy Award in 2022 for her body of work. She had a daughter, writer Linn Ullmann, with Bergman.
