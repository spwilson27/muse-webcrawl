New Age

New Age is a range of spiritual or religious practices and beliefs which rapidly grew in Western society during the early 1970s. Its highly eclectic and unsystematic structure makes a precise definition difficult. Although many scholars consider it a religious movement, its adherents typically see it as spiritual or as unifying Mind-Body-Spirit, and rarely use the term New Age themselves. Many scholars of the subject call it the New Age movement, although others contest this term and suggest it is better seen as a milieu or zeitgeist.

Beliefs and practices

As a form of Western esotericism, the New Age movement drew heavily upon a number of older esoteric traditions, in particular those that emerged from the occultist current that developed during the eighteenth century. Such prominent influences include the work of Emanuel Swedenborg and Franz Mesmer, as well as the Spiritualist, New Thought and Theosophical movements. A number of mid-twentieth-century influences, such as the UFO religions of the 1950s, the counterculture of the 1960s and the Human Potential Movement, also exerted an influence.

Common New Age themes include a holistic worldview, belief in a coming transformation of human consciousness (sometimes linked to the astrological "Age of Aquarius"), reincarnation, karma, healing practices such as crystal healing and Reiki, and the idea that individuals create their own reality.

Reception

The movement has been criticised by mainstream Christian organisations and by sceptics, who regard many of its claims as pseudoscientific, while scholars of religion debate whether it constitutes a coherent movement at all. Commercially, New Age ideas spawned a large industry of books, music and retreats, and concepts such as mindfulness and yoga entered mainstream culture partly through it.
