Word-sense disambiguation

In computational linguistics, word-sense disambiguation (WSD) is the problem of determining which sense of a polysemous word is used in a given context — for example, whether bass refers to a fish or a musical instrument.

Approaches

Early systems used hand-written knowledge and dictionary definitions, notably Lesk's algorithm, which counts overlap between a context and glosses. Supervised methods train classifiers on sense-tagged corpora such as SemCor, using WordNet as the sense inventory. Modern neural models use contextual embeddings from large language models.

Evaluation

WSD is evaluated at Senseval and SemEval workshops against human-annotated test sets; inter-annotator agreement sets a practical ceiling, since fine-grained sense distinctions are difficult even for people.

Applications

Disambiguation supports machine translation, information retrieval, and question answering, where choosing the wrong sense changes meaning.
