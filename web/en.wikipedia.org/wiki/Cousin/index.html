Cousin

A cousin is a relative who shares one or more common ancestors with another person, but is not a direct ancestor or descendant. In its most common use, the term refers to a first cousin: the child of one's aunt or uncle, with whom one shares a set of grandparents. Systems of kinship terminology classify cousins by degree (how many generations separate the shared ancestor) and removal (difference in generational level).

Degrees and removals

First cousins are the children of siblings. Second cousins are the grandchildren of siblings — that is, the children of first cousins — and share great-grandparents. The relationship "once removed" indicates a one-generation gap: one's first cousin once removed is either a parent's first cousin or a first cousin's child. Third cousins share great-great-grandparents, and so on.

Other types

Half cousins share only one grandparent (through half-siblings). Double cousins arise when two siblings marry two siblings from another family, so their children are cousins on both sides and share all four grandparents. Kinship terms vary greatly between languages and cultures; many languages do not distinguish cousins by sex, while others have separate words for maternal and paternal cousins and for cross-cousins and parallel cousins.

Consanguinity

First cousins share on average about one-eighth (12.5%) of their genes. In many cultures, marriage between first cousins is traditional and legal, while in others it is restricted; see cousin marriage.
