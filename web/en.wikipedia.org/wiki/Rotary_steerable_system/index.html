Rotary steerable system

A rotary steerable system (RSS) is a form of drilling technology used in directional drilling. It employs specialised downhole equipment to replace the conventional mud motor, allowing the wellbore to be steered while the entire drill string continues to rotate from the surface.

Advantages

Because the drillstring never stops rotating, RSS drilling avoids the "sliding" mode of mud-motor drilling, in which the stationary string drags on the borehole wall. Continuous rotation gives better hole cleaning, smoother wellbores with less tortuosity, higher rates of penetration and improved weight transfer to the bit — all of which make RSS the preferred technology for extended-reach, deepwater and other high-cost wells, where its higher day rate is outweighed by time savings and well quality.

Steering mechanisms

  • Push-the-bit systems use extendable pads that press against the borehole wall to push the bit in the opposite direction.
  • Point-the-bit systems tilt the bit axis relative to the tool body by flexing an internal shaft, pointing the bit in the desired direction.
  • Hybrid systems combine both principles.

Control

The tool is directed by commands sent from the surface (typically encoded in mud-pulse or flow variations) and by pre-programmed targets, using its internal magnetometers, accelerometers and gyroscopes, together with measurement-while-drilling data, to hold inclination and azimuth automatically. Commercial systems were introduced in the 1990s by the major oilfield service companies and are now standard in complex wells.
