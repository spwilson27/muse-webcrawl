Put–call parity

Put–call parity is a principle in financial mathematics that defines a relationship between the price of a European call option and a European put option, both with the same underlying asset, strike price and expiration date. The principle states that a portfolio consisting of a long call option and a short put option is equivalent to a forward contract on the underlying combined with a cash position.

Statement

In its common form, C − P = S − K/(1+r)T, where C and P are the prices of the call and put, S is the current price of the underlying, K is the strike price, r is the risk-free interest rate and T is the time to expiry. The relationship holds because the two portfolios have identical payoffs at expiration and must therefore have the same value today; otherwise an arbitrage opportunity would exist.

History

Versions of the parity condition appeared in the early twentieth century in the writings of options traders and were formalised by Hans Stoll in 1969. It underlies the derivation of the Black–Scholes model and is widely used by traders to detect mispricing between options markets.
