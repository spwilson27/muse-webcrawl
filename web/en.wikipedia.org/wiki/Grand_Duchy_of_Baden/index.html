Grand Duchy of Baden

The Grand Duchy of Baden (German: Großherzogtum Baden) was a state in south-west Germany that existed from 1806 to 1918. Formed from the Margraviate of Baden during the Napoleonic era, it was a member of the Confederation of the Rhine, the German Confederation, and from 1871 of the German Empire.

History

Under Charles Frederick, the margraviate was vastly enlarged by the mediatisation of smaller territories and raised to a grand duchy in 1806. Baden acquired a reputation as a model liberal state: its 1818 constitution was among the most progressive in Germany, and its universities at Heidelberg and Freiburg flourished. Baden sided with Austria in the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 but joined the new German Empire in 1871.

End of the monarchy

Grand Duke Frederick II abdicated on 22 November 1918 during the German Revolution, ending the rule of the House of Zähringen; Baden became a republic within Weimar Germany and today forms part of the state of Baden-Württemberg.
