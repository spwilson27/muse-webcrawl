Charles F. Sabel (born 1947) is an American political scientist and legal scholar. He is the Maurice T. Moore Professor of Law at Columbia Law School, and was previously a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. With Michael Piore he co-authored The Second Industrial Divide (1984), an influential account of the crisis of mass production and the rise of flexible specialisation.

Charles Sabel, scholar of governance and political economy

Work

Sabel's early work, including Work and Politics (1982), examined labour movements and industrial politics in advanced economies. The Second Industrial Divide, written with Michael J. Piore, argued that the mass-production model pioneered by Ford was entering a period of crisis, and that craft-based, flexible production — as seen in the industrial districts of the "Third Italy" — pointed to an alternative path of economic organisation.

Experimentalist governance

In later work, often with collaborators such as Jonathan Zeitlin and Michael Dorf, Sabel developed the theory of "democratic experimentalism" and "experimentalist governance": systems of public policy in which goals and standards are provisionally set, local units experiment with different means of achieving them, and results are pooled and compared so that standards are continuously revised. The framework has been widely applied to the governance of the European Union, global regulation, labour standards and environmental policy. His books include A Constitution of Democratic Experimentalism (with Michael Dorf, 1998) and Fixing the Climate (with David Victor, 2022), which applies experimentalist ideas to decarbonisation.
