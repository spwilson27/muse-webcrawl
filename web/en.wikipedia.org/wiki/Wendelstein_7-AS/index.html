Wendelstein 7-AS (W7-AS, from Advanced Stellarator) was an experimental stellarator for nuclear fusion research operated by the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics (IPP) at Garching, Germany, from 1988 to 2002. It was the first of a new generation of "advanced" stellarators whose magnetic fields were shaped by three-dimensional computer optimisation rather than by classical symmetric coil arrangements, and it was the direct forerunner of the much larger Wendelstein 7-X device at Greifswald.

Design

Unlike classical stellarators, which use continuous helical windings, W7-AS used 45 non-planar modular superconducting-precursor coils (normal-conducting copper coils) arranged in five periods to produce a twisted plasma with a major radius of 2.0 m and an average minor radius of about 0.18 m. The configuration could vary the rotational transform and magnetic shear flexibly, allowing researchers to test optimisation principles such as reduced neoclassical transport and improved equilibrium at high plasma pressure.

Results

W7-AS demonstrated that modular-coil advanced stellarators were practical, verified significant elements of the optimisation concept, and discovered the "high-density H-mode" (HDH) regime, in which energy confinement improved with density while impurity accumulation was avoided. It was shut down in 2002 as IPP concentrated its stellarator programme on Wendelstein 7-X, which began operation in 2015.
