Internet phenomenon

An Internet phenomenon is a social phenomenon that spreads via the Internet, especially through social media and content-sharing platforms. The term overlaps with "viral" content and with Internet memes—units of culture (images, videos, phrases, challenges, or formats) that are copied, remixed, and circulated by users.

Viral images and videos can reach global audiences within hours.

History

Early internet phenomena spread by email and forums in the 1990s—chain letters, the Dancing Baby animation (1996), and "All your base are belong to us" (2000). Broadband and platforms such as YouTube (2005), Facebook, Reddit, and Twitter accelerated diffusion in the 2000s, producing phenomena such as the Star Wars Kid, Rickrolling, Lolcats and the "Harlem Shake". In the 2010s–2020s, TikTok challenges, livestream culture, and image macros became dominant forms; challenges range from the charitable Ice Bucket Challenge (2014), which raised over $100 million for ALS research, to dangerous stunts criticized by health authorities.

Characteristics

  • Virality — rapid exponential spread through sharing and algorithmic recommendation.
  • Remixability — templates invite imitation and variation, a process Richard Dawkins' original concept of the meme analogized to gene replication.
  • Short lifecycle — most phenomena peak and fade within days or weeks, though some become durable parts of internet culture.
  • Commercial uptake — brands and political campaigns deliberately engineer or appropriate phenomena, with mixed success.

Impact

Internet phenomena influence slang, marketing, fundraising, and political communication, but also enable misinformation cascades, harassment campaigns, and risky challenges. Researchers study them within memetics, media studies, and computational social science.
