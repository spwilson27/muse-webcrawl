The United States Army Central, formerly the Third United States Army, is a military formation of the United States Army and the land component of United States Central Command. It was famously commanded by General George S. Patton during the Second World War.

History

The Third Army was first activated in France in November 1918, at the end of the First World War, and served as part of the occupation force in Germany before being inactivated in 1919. It was reactivated in 1932.

Second World War

After training in the United States and deployment to England, the Third Army became operational in France on 1 August 1944 under Patton. It spearheaded the breakout from Normandy, raced across France, relieved the besieged garrison at Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, crossed the Rhine in March 1945, and advanced into Germany, Austria, and Czechoslovakia before the war ended.

Later service

The Third Army was headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia (Fort McPherson) for much of the Cold War era and was inactivated in 1974. Reactivated in 1982 as the Army component of Central Command, it served as the land headquarters for Operation Desert Storm (1991) — the ground offensive that liberated Kuwait — and later directed land operations in Iraq and Afghanistan from forward headquarters at Camp Arifjan, Kuwait. Its headquarters moved to Shaw Air Force Base, South Carolina, in 2011.
