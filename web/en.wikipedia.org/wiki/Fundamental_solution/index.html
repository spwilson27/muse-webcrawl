Fundamental solution

In mathematics, a fundamental solution for a linear partial differential operator L is the formulation in the language of distribution theory of the older idea of a Green's function (although, unlike Green's functions, fundamental solutions do not address boundary conditions). In terms of the Dirac delta function δ(x), a fundamental solution F is a solution of the inhomogeneous equation LF = δ(x).

This concept was introduced by Lars Hörmander. Once a fundamental solution is known, solutions of the original inhomogeneous equation can be found by convolution. Fundamental solutions are widely used in the study of the Laplace equation, the heat equation and the wave equation. For example, the fundamental solution of the Laplacian in three dimensions is proportional to 1/r, and that of the one-dimensional heat equation involves the Gaussian kernel.
