Tau Boötis is a binary star system in the constellation Boötes, about 51 light-years from the Sun. It consists of a yellow-white F-type main-sequence star and a smaller red dwarf companion.

Planetary system

In 1996 a planet, Tau Boötis b, was announced around the primary star — one of the earliest exoplanets discovered by the radial velocity method. It is a "hot Jupiter" with at least four times the mass of Jupiter, orbiting in just 3.3 days at a distance of about 0.05 AU. The planet is tidally locked to its star, and unusually the star's rotation appears synchronised with the planet's orbit. In 2012 the planet's thermal emission and carbon monoxide signature were detected, allowing its orbital inclination and true mass to be estimated.
