The Jubilee line is a London Underground line that runs from Stanmore in the north-west to Stratford in the east, serving 27 stations over 36.2 kilometres (22.5 miles). Opened in 1979 and named to mark the Silver Jubilee of Elizabeth II, it is the most recent line fully opened on the Underground network; its 1999 extension through the Docklands and the South Bank created some of London's most celebrated modern station architecture.

History

The original Jubilee line, opened on 1 May 1979, took over the Bakerloo line's Stanmore branch between Baker Street and Stanmore and added a short new tunnel to Charing Cross, via Bond Street and Green Park. It had been planned since the 1930s as the "Fleet line" (after the River Fleet) and was renamed for the Queen's Silver Jubilee of 1977.

The Jubilee line extension

In the 1990s the line was extended from Green Park eastward through Westminster, Waterloo, London Bridge, Canary Wharf and North Greenwich to Stratford, serving the redeveloped Docklands and the Millennium Dome (now The O2). Opened in stages during 1999, the extension's stations — designed under Roland Paoletti with architects including Foster + Partners (Canary Wharf) and Michael Hopkins (Westminster) — are famous for their cavernous concrete interiors and were the first on the Underground to feature platform-edge doors. The Charing Cross branch closed in 1999, though its platforms survive for filming and occasional use.

Services and trains

The Jubilee line is worked by 1996 Stock trains, with automatic train operation in the central and eastern sections. It is one of the busiest Underground lines, carrying well over 200 million passengers a year before the pandemic, and provides key interchanges at Bond Street (Elizabeth line), London Bridge and Stratford. Night Tube services run on the line on Friday and Saturday nights.
