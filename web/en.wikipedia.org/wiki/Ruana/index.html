Ruana

Ruana
Type: Woollen poncho-like garment
Origin: Andean regions of Colombia and Venezuela
Material: Sheep's wool

A ruana is a poncho-style outer garment native to the cold Andean highlands of Colombia and Venezuela. It is a thick, sleeveless woollen blanket-like cloak, usually square or rectangular, with a slit or hole for the head, worn draped over the shoulders and torso.

Description and use

Traditional ruanas are handwoven from sheep's wool, most famously in the department of Boyacá, Colombia — the town of Nobsa is renowned for its ruana production. They are typically made in natural shades of brown, grey, black and white, sometimes with stripes. The garment provides essential warmth in high-altitude climates and is worn by farmers and rural workers as well as being a symbol of regional identity; the Colombian ruana is celebrated each year at festivals such as the International Ruana Festival in Nobsa.

Unlike the poncho, which usually has a central head opening, some ruanas are entirely open at the front and wrapped around the body. The garment has pre-Columbian roots among the Muisca people, combined with Spanish colonial weaving techniques.

Related garments

The ruana belongs to a family of Andean and Latin American wraps that includes the poncho, the Venezuelan cobija and the Chilean chamanto. Ruana-like knitted wraps also became fashionable in women's clothing in the twentieth century.
