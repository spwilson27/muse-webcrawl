The Juilliard School is a private performing arts conservatory in New York City, widely regarded as one of the world's leading schools for music, dance and drama. Located at Lincoln Center, it enrolls roughly 800 undergraduate and graduate students and accepts only a small fraction of applicants.

History

Juilliard traces its origins to the Institute of Musical Art, founded in 1905 by Frank Damrosch, and to the Juilliard Graduate School, endowed in 1920 by the textile merchant Augustus Juilliard's bequest. The two merged in 1926 as the Juilliard School of Music. The school added a dance division in 1951 and, in 1968, a drama division under John Houseman and Michel Saint-Denis, whose first class — the famed "Group 1" — included Kevin Kline and Patti LuPone. In 1969 the school moved to Lincoln Center.

Programs and people

Music students study in orchestral, chamber, jazz, historical-performance and vocal programs; faculty have included Itzhak Perlman and Emanuel Ax. Alumni range from classical figures such as Van Cliburn, Renée Fleming and Yo-Yo Ma to actors including Robin Williams, Jessica Chastain and Adam Driver, and dancers and choreographers of the Martha Graham and Paul Taylor companies. The Juilliard String Quartet, founded in 1946, is resident at the school.

Juilliard's free or low-cost concerts number in the hundreds each year, and its pre-college division trains school-age musicians on Saturdays. A sister campus, the Tianjin Juilliard School, opened in China in 2020.
