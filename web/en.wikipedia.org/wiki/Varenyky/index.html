Varenyky

Varenyky (Ukrainian: вареники) are filled dumplings made by wrapping unleavened dough around a savoury or sweet filling and cooking in boiling water. They are a staple of Ukrainian cuisine and are closely related to Polish pierogi, Russian vareniki and Slovak pirohy.

Preparation

Dough of flour, water, and sometimes egg or sour cream is rolled thin, cut into circles, filled, folded into half-moons and sealed. They are boiled until they float, then often tossed with butter or fried onions (shkvarky) and served with sour cream (smetana).

Fillings

Common savoury fillings include mashed potato with cheese, sauerkraut, mushrooms, minced meat, and cottage cheese. Sweet versions are filled with cherries, blueberries, strawberries or sweetened curd, and served with sugar and sour cream. Lazy varenyky are made by mixing curd directly into the dough.

Cultural significance

Varenyky are a national dish of Ukraine, served at holidays and family gatherings, and feature in folk songs and literature, notably in Gogol's tales. Ukrainian diaspora communities popularised them in North America, where church and community suppers often centre on them.
