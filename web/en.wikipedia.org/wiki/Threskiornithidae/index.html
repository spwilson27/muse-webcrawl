FamilyThreskiornithidae
SubfamiliesThreskiornithinae (ibises), Plataleinae (spoonbills)

The family Threskiornithidae includes 36 species of large wading birds divided into two subfamilies: the ibises and the spoonbills. Members of the family have long, broad wings and typically fly with neck and legs extended. They are found on all continents except Antarctica, mostly in wetlands, where they feed on small aquatic animals.

Characteristics

Ibises have long, down-curved bills used to probe mud and shallow water for crustaceans, molluscs, frogs, and insects. Spoonbills have flattened, spoon-shaped bills that they sweep from side to side through the water. Most species nest in trees or reed beds, often in colonies with other wading birds such as herons.

Taxonomy

The family was formerly placed in the order Ciconiiformes but is now grouped with pelicans and herons in the Pelecaniformes. Well-known ibises include the sacred ibis, scarlet ibis, glossy ibis, northern bald ibis, and the Australian white ibis. The six spoonbill species include the Eurasian spoonbill and the roseate spoonbill.

Conservation

Several species are threatened, including the crested ibis of East Asia and the northern bald ibis, both subjects of intensive conservation and reintroduction programmes. The giant ibis of Cambodia is critically endangered.
