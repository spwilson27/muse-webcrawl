The Battle of Kostiuchnówka was fought from 4 to 6 July 1916 between the Polish Legions fighting alongside Austria-Hungary and forces of the Russian Empire during the Brusilov Offensive of the First World War. It is considered the largest and most bitter battle fought by the Polish Legions.

Background

During the Brusilov Offensive, the Legions' roughly 5,500 men held positions around Kostiuchnówka in Volhynia, facing a Russian force many times larger.

The battle

Russian attacks began on 4 July with massive artillery preparation. Polish positions such as the Reduta Piłsudskiego ("Piłsudski's Redoubt") held for two days; the Legions eventually withdrew in good order, having delayed the Russian advance. Polish losses were around 2,000 killed, wounded and missing.

Legacy

The battle became a founding legend of Polish arms; Józef Piłsudski called it the Legions' greatest trial. Its performance helped win German and Austro-Hungarian recognition of the Polish cause, reflected in the Act of 5 November 1916.
