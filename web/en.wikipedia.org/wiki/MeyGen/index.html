MeyGen

MeyGen is a tidal stream energy project in the Inner Sound of the Pentland Firth, off the north coast of Scotland between Caithness and the island of Stroma. It is among the largest tidal stream arrays in the world and the first to supply power to the grid from a multi-turbine installation.

Development

The site exploits some of the fastest tidal currents in the world, with flows in the Pentland Firth reaching several metres per second. The project was developed by MeyGen Limited, later part of SAE Renewables (formerly Atlantis Resources and SIMEC Atlantis Energy). The seabed lease from the Crown Estate covers a large area of the Inner Sound, and the site has consented capacity far beyond the initial phases.

Phases

The first phase, Phase 1A, installed four 1.5 MW turbines — three built by Andritz Hydro Hammerfest and one Atlantis AR1500 — on gravity-based foundations in 2016–2017, giving a total capacity of 6 MW. The array began exporting power to the grid in 2016 and passed successive generation milestones, demonstrating reliable operation of tidal stream turbines over multiple years. Subsequent phases are planned to scale the project up substantially, with ambitions for several hundred megawatts at the site.

Significance

MeyGen is frequently cited as the world's flagship tidal stream project: it was the first tidal array financed on a project-finance basis and the first to generate power from multiple turbines at a single site. Its operational record has informed the design of later tidal projects in the United Kingdom, France, Canada and elsewhere.
