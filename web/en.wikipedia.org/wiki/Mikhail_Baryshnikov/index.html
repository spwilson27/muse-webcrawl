Mikhail Baryshnikov

Mikhail Baryshnikov
Born: 27 January 1948, Riga, Latvian SSR
Occupation: Dancer, choreographer, actor
Known for: Kirov Ballet; American Ballet Theatre; White Oak Dance Project

Mikhail Nikolayevich Baryshnikov (born 27 January 1948) is a Latvian-American dancer, choreographer and actor, widely regarded as one of the greatest male ballet dancers of the 20th century. After a celebrated career with the Kirov Ballet in Leningrad, he defected to the West while on tour in Canada in 1974, becoming a principal dancer and later artistic director of American Ballet Theatre.

Early life and Kirov career

Baryshnikov was born in Riga to Russian parents and trained at the Vaganova Academy in Leningrad under Alexander Pushkin. Joining the Kirov in 1967, he was quickly recognised for his extraordinary elevation, multiple pirouettes and expressive acting, winning the gold medal at the 1969 Moscow International Ballet Competition. Roles were created for him, including Albrecht in Giselle and the title role in Vestris.

Career in the West

Frustrated by the Kirov's conservative repertoire, Baryshnikov defected in Toronto on 29 June 1974. He joined American Ballet Theatre, partnered Gelsey Kirkland, and worked with modern choreographers including Twyla Tharp, who created Push Comes to Shove for him. He served as ABT's artistic director from 1980 to 1989, co-founded the White Oak Dance Project with Mark Morris in 1990, and in 2005 founded the Baryshnikov Arts Center in New York.

As an actor he was nominated for an Academy Award for The Turning Point (1977) and appeared in the film White Nights (1985) and the television series Sex and the City.
