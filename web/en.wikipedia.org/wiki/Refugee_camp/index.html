Refugee camp

A refugee camp is a temporary settlement built to receive refugees and people in refugee-like situations. Refugee camps usually accommodate displaced people who have fled their home country because of war or persecution, and are normally administered by a government, the United Nations (particularly the UNHCR), or international and non-governmental organisations.

Facilities and life

Camps are intended as temporary shelter but often become long-term: services include basic housing (tents, later semi-permanent shelters), food distribution, water and sanitation, health clinics, and primary schooling. Average stay in a protracted camp situation can extend to many years or even decades, and some camps — such as Dadaab and Kakuma in Kenya, or the Palestinian camps administered by UNRWA since 1948 — have effectively become permanent settlements housing generations of residents.

Governance and issues

Residents are commonly restricted in movement and employment, creating dependence on aid. Camps face recurring challenges including overcrowding, disease outbreaks, insecurity, gender-based violence, and environmental strain on host regions. Alternatives to camps — such as local integration or urban settlement with assistance — are increasingly promoted by UNHCR, though camps remain the default response to large sudden displacement.

Notable camps

  • Dadaab (Kenya) — long one of the world's largest refugee camp complexes, hosting mainly Somali refugees
  • Kutupalong (Bangladesh) — the largest camp by population after the 2017 Rohingya exodus from Myanmar
  • Zaatari (Jordan) — established 2012 for Syrian refugees
  • Bidi Bidi (Uganda) — established for South Sudanese refugees
