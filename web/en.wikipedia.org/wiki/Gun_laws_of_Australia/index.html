Gun laws of Australia

Gun laws of Australia are the laws that regulate firearms in Australia, administered primarily by state and territory governments but structured nationally by the National Firearms Agreement reached in 1996 after the Port Arthur massacre.

History before 1996

Before federation, the Australian colonies each regulated firearms, and after 1901 the states retained this power. Laws tightened at various points during the 20th century, but major national reform followed a series of mass shootings in the 1980s and 1990s, culminating at Port Arthur, Tasmania, on 28 April 1996.

The National Firearms Agreement

The NFA, negotiated under Prime Minister John Howard, banned semi-automatic rifles and many shotguns, imposed a 28-day waiting period, required licensing and registration, safe storage, and a genuine reason for ownership, and funded a buyback and amnesty in which over 650,000 guns were surrendered and destroyed. The Agreement was updated in 2017.

Current regulation

Licences are issued by state police; categories range from A (air and rimfire rifles) through B (centrefire rifles), C and D (semi-automatics, largely restricted to certain occupational users), to H (handguns, mainly for target shooting club members). There are roughly 3.5 million registered firearms in civilian hands. Australia records comparatively low rates of firearm homicide.
