Black Country derby

TeamsWest Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers
RegionBlack Country, West Midlands, England
First meeting1886 (FA Cup)

The Black Country derby is a football rivalry between West Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers, the two leading clubs of the Black Country in the West Midlands of England. It is regarded as one of the oldest and fiercest derbies in English football.

History

The clubs first met competitively in the FA Cup in 1886, a match Albion won. Both were founder members of the Football League in 1888. The rivalry reached its peak in the 1950s, when Wolves were champions of England three times and Albion, with their celebrated 1953-54 side, finished runners-up and won the FA Cup.

Character

The two grounds, The Hawthorns and Molineux, are about eleven miles apart. Meetings have often been marked by a tense atmosphere and occasional crowd trouble, notably in the play-off semi-finals of 2007 and an FA Cup tie in 2024. Because the clubs have frequently been in different divisions, seasons without a derby are common, which heightens anticipation when they do meet.
