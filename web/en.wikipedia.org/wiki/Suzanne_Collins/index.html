Suzanne Collins

Suzanne Collins (born August 10, 1962) is an American author and television writer best known as the author of the best-selling The Hunger Games trilogy. Before that she wrote the five-book middle-grade series The Underland Chronicles. Collins began her career in children's television, writing for shows including Clarissa Explains It All and The Mystery Files of Shelby Woo. The Hunger Games (2008), Catching Fire (2009), and Mockingjay (2010) have sold tens of millions of copies worldwide and were adapted into a major film franchise; prequels The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes (2020) and Sunrise on the Reaping (2025) followed.

Early life and career

Collins was born in Hartford, Connecticut, the daughter of a U.S. Air Force officer who served in Vietnam; the family moved frequently, and her father's accounts of war shaped her later writing. She studied theatre and telecommunications at Indiana University and earned a master's degree in dramatic writing from New York University. From 1991 she wrote for children's television on Nickelodeon and other networks.

The Hunger Games

Inspired by channel-surfing between reality television and news coverage of the Iraq War, and by the Greek myth of Theseus and the Minotaur, Collins created the dystopian nation of Panem, where the Capitol forces children from its districts to fight to the death in a televised spectacle. The trilogy follows Katniss Everdeen and became one of the best-selling book series of all time; the Lionsgate film adaptations (2012–2015), starring Jennifer Lawrence, grossed nearly $3 billion worldwide.
