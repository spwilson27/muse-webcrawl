Light poetry

Light poetry
Also known asLight verse, comic verse
CharacteristicsHumour, wit, wordplay, regular metre and rhyme, ostensibly frivolous subjects
Notable writersEdward Lear, Lewis Carroll, Ogden Nash, Dorothy Parker, Wendy Cope

Light poetry or light verse is poetry that attempts to be humorous. Light poems are usually brief, and can be on a frivolous subject, or pithily state an everyday observation in a humorous way. They can also have a serious underlying subtext, and many range from bawdy verse to complex and genuinely sophisticated wordplay.

Although the term is comparatively recent, the practice is ancient: Aristophanes, Catullus, Martial and the authors of the Greek Anthology all wrote comic and satirical verse. In English, the tradition runs through John Skelton, Restoration rakes, the satirists of the Augustan age, and the great Victorian nonsense writers.

Forms

Light verse favours tight formal control: the limerick, clerihew, double dactyl, triolet, ballade and vers de société all belong to its repertoire. Nonsense verse — the work of Edward Lear and Lewis Carroll — is often classed within it, as are parody, epigram and the humorous use of strict forms such as the villanelle.

History

The modern genre was defined in the late 19th and early 20th centuries in magazines such as Punch, and by writers including W. S. Gilbert, whose Bab Ballads preceded his Savoy operas. In the United States, Ogden Nash became famous for his deliberately unruly rhymes and lines of wildly varying length, while Dorothy Parker perfected the acid short lyric. W. H. Auden, who edited an influential anthology, The Oxford Book of Light Verse (1938), argued that light verse was not a minor form but one rooted in folk song and social life.

Practitioners

Notable modern practitioners include John Betjeman, Kingsley Amis, Wendy Cope — whose parodies and crisp lyrics have reached a wide audience — Roger McGough and Billy Collins. Critics sometimes dismiss light verse as trivial beside "serious" poetry, a judgement its defenders answer by pointing to the technical difficulty of doing it well.
