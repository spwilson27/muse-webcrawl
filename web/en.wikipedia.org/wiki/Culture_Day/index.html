Culture Day (文化の日, Bunka no Hi) is a national holiday in Japan celebrated annually on 3 November. It promotes culture, the arts and academic achievement, and is marked by art exhibitions, parades, school culture festivals and award ceremonies across the country.

History

3 November was originally celebrated as Tenchōsetsu, the birthday of Emperor Meiji (born 3 November 1852). After the Second World War the date gained a second significance: the Constitution of Japan was promulgated on 3 November 1946. When the new constitution took effect in 1947, the day was redesignated. Under the Public Holiday Law of 1948 it became Culture Day, dedicated to "loving freedom and peace and promoting culture" — values associated with the postwar constitution.

Observance

On Culture Day the Japanese government announces the recipients of the Order of Culture (Bunka-kunshō), one of the nation's highest honours, awarded by the Emperor at the Imperial Palace to individuals who have made outstanding contributions to art, literature, science and technology. The Persons of Cultural Merit are also announced around this time.

Many museums, galleries and aquariums offer free admission, and schools and universities hold bunkasai (culture festivals) featuring student exhibitions, performances and food stalls. The day is statistically among the clearest of the year in the Tokyo region, a fact often remarked upon. In Kamakura and elsewhere, traditional processions and martial-arts demonstrations are held, and the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force marks the day with ceremonial dress aboard its vessels.
