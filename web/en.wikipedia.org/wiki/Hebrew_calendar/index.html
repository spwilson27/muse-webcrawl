The Hebrew calendar (Hebrew: הַלּוּחַ הָעִבְרִי), also called the Jewish calendar, is a lunisolar calendar used predominantly for Jewish religious observances and by the State of Israel.

Structure

Months are based on the Moon, 29 or 30 days, while years are aligned with the solar cycle through a 19-year Metonic cycle in which 7 years have an extra month, Adar I. Months begin at Nisan in spring: Nisan, Iyar, Sivan, Tammuz, Av, Elul, Tishrei, Cheshvan, Kislev, Tevet, Shevat, Adar.

History

Originally determined by observation of the new moon and declaration by the Sanhedrin, the calendar was fixed in arithmetic form traditionally attributed to Hillel II in the 4th century CE. Years are counted from Anno Mundi.

Holidays

The calendar determines Passover, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, and other festivals. The day begins at sunset.

Weeks

The seven-day week culminates in Shabbat. See also Hebrew language.
