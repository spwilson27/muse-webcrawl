Hangdang

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hangdang (Korean: 항당) was a government office or rank-related institution of the Silla kingdom of Korea. The term appears in historical records concerning the administration and ceremonial hierarchy of Silla, one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, and is discussed in studies of the kingdom's officialdom and its bone-rank system.

Background

Silla's government was organised around the golpum (bone-rank) system, under which aristocratic status determined eligibility for office. Offices such as the Hangdang formed part of the court's administrative and ritual apparatus, and officials' duties and titles are recorded in sources such as the Samguk Sagi, the twelfth-century history of the Three Kingdoms compiled by Kim Bu-sik.

Sources

Modern knowledge of Silla offices derives chiefly from the Samguk Sagi and the Samguk Yusa, together with epigraphic evidence from steles and inscriptions. Details of the Hangdang's precise functions are sparse, and scholarly reconstructions of the office differ.
