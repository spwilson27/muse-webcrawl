Ivan Meštrović

Ivan Meštrović (15 August 1883 – 16 January 1962) was a Croatian sculptor, architect, and writer, widely regarded as the greatest Croatian sculptor and one of the most prominent sculptors of the 20th century. Auguste Rodin praised him as a phenomenon among sculptors.

Born15 August 1883, Vrpolje, Austria-Hungary
Died16 January 1962, South Bend, Indiana, US
Known forSculpture, architecture
WorksVictor monument (Belgrade), Gregory of Nin (Split), Meštrović Pavilion (Zagreb)

Career

Born in Slavonia and raised in Dalmatia, Meštrović studied in Split and Vienna, exhibiting with the Vienna Secession. His monumental works in a classical, expressive style include the Pobednik (Victor) in Belgrade, the statue of Gregory of Nin in Split—whose toe is rubbed for luck—and the Mausoleum of the Račić family in Cavtat.

Later life

Imprisoned briefly by the Ustaše in 1941, he emigrated after the war, becoming a professor at Syracuse University and then the University of Notre Dame in the United States, where he died in 1962.
