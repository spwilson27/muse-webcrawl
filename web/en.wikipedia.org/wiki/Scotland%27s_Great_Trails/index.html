Scotland's Great Trails

Scotland's Great Trails are a network of long-distance walking and cycling routes in Scotland, managed by NatureScot in partnership with local authorities and land managers. There are 29 trails in the network, totaling roughly 3,000 km (1,900 mi), each waymarked with distinctive thistle-in-a-hexagon signage.

History

The first formally designated route was the West Highland Way, opened in 1980. The network grew to include the Great Glen Way, Southern Upland Way (the longest, at about 340 km), Speyside Way, Fife Coastal Path, Rob Roy Way, John Muir Way, and the coast-to-coast routes popularized for mountain biking and trail running.

Use

The trails are estimated to attract well over a million visits a year and contribute significantly to rural tourism. Access rights under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 underpin responsible access along the routes.
