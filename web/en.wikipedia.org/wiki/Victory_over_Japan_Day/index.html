Victory over Japan Day

Victory over Japan Day (V-J Day) is the day on which Imperial Japan surrendered in World War II, in effect bringing the hostilities to an end. The term has been applied to both of the days of 14 and 15 August 1945, when Japan announced its acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration, and to 2 September 1945, when the Japanese instrument of surrender was formally signed aboard the USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay.

Announcement of surrender

Following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August) and the Soviet declaration of war and invasion of Manchuria, Emperor Hirohito intervened to break a deadlock in the Japanese government. On 14 August Japan accepted the Allied terms, and on 15 August (Japan time) the Emperor's recorded Gyokuon-hōsō broadcast announced the surrender to the Japanese public — the first time most had heard his voice. An attempted military coup the night before failed. News of the acceptance reached the United States on 14 August, prompting celebrations there and the famous Alfred Eisenstaedt photograph V-J Day in Times Square.

Formal surrender

The formal ceremony took place on 2 September 1945 aboard the battleship USS Missouri. Foreign Minister Mamoru Shigemitsu signed for the Japanese government and General Yoshijirō Umezu for the armed forces; General Douglas MacArthur presided and signed for the Allied powers, followed by representatives of the United States, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, Australia, Canada, France, the Netherlands and New Zealand.

Commemoration

The United Kingdom marks V-J Day on 15 August; the United States officially commemorates 2 September (Rhode Island long observed a state holiday in August). China marks Victory over Japan Day on 3 September, the day after the signing. In 2020 the UK and other countries held major 75th-anniversary commemorations.
