Brothers in Arms (album)

Brothers in Arms is the fifth studio album by the British rock band Dire Straits, released on 17 May 1985. One of the best-selling albums of all time, with sales of more than 30 million copies worldwide, it was among the first albums recorded entirely digitally and became a landmark release of the compact disc era — by some measures the first CD to sell a million copies.

Background

Frontman Mark Knopfler wrote the songs in the wake of the Falklands War; the title track, inspired by a remark of his father's, is an anti-war lament. The album was recorded at AIR Studios on Montserrat with drummer Omar Hakim and keyboardist Guy Fletcher joining the band.

"Money for Nothing", co-written with Sting (who sings the "I want my MTV" falsetto), won a Grammy Award and its computer-animated video became an MTV staple; the album won the Grammy for Best Engineered Album, Non-Classical, and the Brit Award for Best British Album.

Legacy

The album topped charts in more than twenty countries and spent 14 weeks at number one in the UK. It consistently ranks among the greatest albums of the 1980s and remains a benchmark for hi-fi demonstration recordings.
