Uveitis

Uveitis
Specialty: Ophthalmology
Symptoms: Red eye, pain, blurred vision, light sensitivity
Causes: Infectious, autoimmune, idiopathic
Treatment: Corticosteroids, immunosuppressants

Uveitis is inflammation of the uvea, the pigmented middle layer of the eye, which consists of the iris, the ciliary body and the choroid. It is one of the leading causes of preventable blindness worldwide and can affect people of any age, though it most often presents in working-age adults.

Classification

Uveitis is classified anatomically by the part of the uveal tract primarily involved:

  • Anterior uveitis (iritis, iridocyclitis) — the most common form, affecting the iris and anterior chamber.
  • Intermediate uveitis — centred on the vitreous and pars plana, often with "snowbanking" seen on examination.
  • Posterior uveitis — involving the retina and choroid at the back of the eye.
  • Panuveitis — inflammation throughout the entire uveal tract.

Causes

Roughly half of cases are idiopathic. Known causes include infections (herpes simplex, toxoplasmosis, tuberculosis, syphilis), systemic autoimmune diseases (ankylosing spondylitis, Behçet's disease, sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease), trauma, and rarely malignancy masquerading as inflammation.

Symptoms

Anterior uveitis typically causes eye pain, redness, photophobia and blurred vision. Posterior disease may instead present with floaters and painless visual loss. Complications of untreated or recurrent uveitis include cataract, glaucoma, macular oedema, synechiae and permanent vision loss.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on slit-lamp examination showing cells and flare in the anterior chamber, dilated fundus examination, and targeted laboratory testing to identify an underlying cause. Treatment aims to suppress inflammation: corticosteroid eye drops for anterior disease, periocular or systemic steroids for more severe disease, and steroid-sparing immunosuppressive agents or biologics (such as adalimumab) for chronic or recurrent cases. Infectious uveitis additionally requires antimicrobial therapy.
