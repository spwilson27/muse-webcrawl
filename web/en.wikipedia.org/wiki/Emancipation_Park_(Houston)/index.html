Emancipation Park is a public park in the Third Ward of Houston, Texas. Founded in 1872 by a group of formerly enslaved people, it is the oldest public park in Houston and one of the oldest in the state. The park became a centre of Juneteenth celebrations, commemorating the announcement of emancipation in Texas on 19 June 1865.

History

In 1872, members of the Antioch Missionary Baptist Church and Trinity Methodist Episcopal Church, led by the Reverend Jack Yates and Richard Brock, pooled $800 to purchase ten acres of land so that freed people would have a place to celebrate Juneteenth freely. It was the only municipal park open to African Americans during the era of segregation.

Facilities and renovation

The park's recreation centre was designed by John Chase, the first licensed African American architect in Texas. A major $33 million renovation, led by the Emancipation Park Conservancy with design by Perkins+Will, was completed in 2017, adding the Emancipation Center building, a new cultural centre, plaza, performance spaces, gardens and a swimming pool while preserving historic elements.

Significance

The park is a designated Houston landmark and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It remains the focal point of Houston's annual Juneteenth festivities and a symbol of African American heritage in Texas.
