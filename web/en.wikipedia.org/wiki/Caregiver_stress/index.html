Caregiver stress

Caregiver stress, also called caregiver syndrome or caregiver burnout, is a state of physical, emotional and mental exhaustion that may accompany providing care for another person, typically a family member with a chronic illness, disability or the frailties of old age. It describes the strain experienced by caregivers who feel overwhelmed by the demands of the role, and it is associated with measurable effects on the caregiver's own health.

Symptoms and effects

Signs include fatigue, disturbed sleep, irritability, anxiety, depression, social withdrawal, and neglect of the caregiver's own medical needs. Studies of dementia caregivers in particular have found elevated levels of stress hormones, weakened immune function, and higher rates of cardiovascular disease compared with non-caregiving controls. Severe chronic stress in spousal caregivers has been associated with increased mortality risk.

Risk factors

Caregiver stress is greater when the care recipient has behavioural problems (as in dementia), when care hours are long, when the caregiver co-resides with the recipient, and when social support and respite are lacking. Female caregivers and those with financial strain report higher burden on average.

Management

Interventions include respite care, caregiver support groups, psychoeducational programmes, counselling, and practical help with care tasks. Screening instruments such as the Zarit Burden Interview are used to quantify caregiver burden in clinical and research settings.
