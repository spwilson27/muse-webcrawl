The Nintendo DS is a foldable handheld game console produced by Nintendo, released in 2004. The initials "DS" stand for both "Dual Screen" and "Developers' System". The console introduced two LCD screens, the lower of which is a touchscreen, along with a built-in microphone and support for wireless connectivity. It is backward compatible with Game Boy Advance cartridges.

History

Announced in January 2004 as a "third pillar" alongside the Game Boy Advance and GameCube, the DS launched first in North America on 21 November 2004, followed by Japan on 2 December. Its design emphasised novel input methods over raw processing power, part of Nintendo's "Blue Ocean" strategy of expanding the gaming audience. Titles such as Nintendogs, Brain Age, New Super Mario Bros., Mario Kart DS, and the Pokémon Diamond and Pearl games helped drive adoption among non-traditional players.

Hardware revisions

The Nintendo DS Lite (2006) offered a slimmer body and brighter screens. The Nintendo DSi (2008) added two cameras, larger screens, an SD card slot, and the downloadable DSiWare service, while dropping the Game Boy Advance slot; the DSi XL (2009) enlarged the screens further.

Sales and legacy

With 154.02 million units sold worldwide, the Nintendo DS is the best-selling handheld game console of all time and the second-best-selling video game console overall, behind only the PlayStation 2. More than 948 million software units were sold. The DS family was succeeded by the Nintendo 3DS in 2011, and production ended in 2014.
