Mellifont Abbey

Mellifont Abbey (Irish: An Mhainistir Mhór, "the big abbey") was a Cistercian abbey located near Drogheda in County Louth, Ireland. Founded in 1142 by St Malachy, Archbishop of Armagh, it was the first Cistercian monastery in Ireland and the mother house of the order in the country, from which many daughter houses were founded.

History

Malachy, who had visited Clairvaux and befriended St Bernard, brought Cistercian monks — trained in France — to found Mellifont on land granted by Donnchad Ua Cerbaill, king of Airgialla. The abbey was consecrated in 1157 at a great synod attended by Irish kings and bishops. Mellifont was dissolved in 1539 during the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII. In the 17th century the ruins were associated with William of Orange, who used the site during the Battle of the Boyne campaign. Dervorgilla, wife of Tigernán Ua Ruairc, whose abduction/elopement helped precipitate the Norman invasion of Ireland, is traditionally said to be buried here.

Remains

The surviving ruins include the octagonal lavabo (washing place), parts of the chapter house, and sections of the Romanesque cloister arcade. The site is managed by the Office of Public Works and is a popular visitor attraction near the Boyne valley.
