Ubuntu is a Linux distribution based on Debian and composed mostly of free and open-source software. Developed by Canonical Ltd. and a community of contributors, Ubuntu is released in multiple editions — Desktop, Server, and Core for IoT devices — and is one of the most popular Linux distributions for desktop computers, servers, and cloud deployments.

The Ubuntu desktop with its "Circle of Friends" logo.

History

Ubuntu was first released on 20 October 2004 by South African entrepreneur Mark Shuttleworth's company Canonical. Its name comes from the Nguni philosophy of ubuntu, often translated as "I am because we are" or "humanity towards others". Early releases were mailed free of charge on CD through the ShipIt programme. Ubuntu quickly became the most widely used desktop Linux distribution.

New versions are released every six months, in April and October; every fourth release (every two years, in April of even-numbered years) is a Long Term Support (LTS) release, supported for five years or more. Version numbers take the form YY.MM, and each release has an alliterative animal codename, such as "Jammy Jellyfish" (22.04 LTS).

Features

The default desktop uses the GNOME environment customised with a dock and the Yaru theme. Software is managed through APT and the Snap package system. Ubuntu includes the LibreOffice suite, Firefox, and Thunderbird by default, and offers extensive language support and accessibility features. Official flavours with other desktop environments include Kubuntu (KDE), Xubuntu (Xfce), and Lubuntu (LXQt).

Reception and use

Ubuntu is widely used on cloud platforms and servers, and forms the basis of many derivative distributions. It has been praised for its ease of installation and use, and criticised at times for decisions such as the promotion of Snap packages and, historically, the Amazon search integration in the Unity desktop (discontinued).
