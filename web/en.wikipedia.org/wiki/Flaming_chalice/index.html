Flaming chalice

The flaming chalice is the principal symbol of Unitarian Universalism and of the Unitarian tradition more broadly. It consists of a cup or goblet bearing a flame, and is lit at the beginning of most Unitarian Universalist worship services, often accompanied by opening words; children in many congregations learn a simple chalice-lighting verse. The design most widely used today shows the chalice within two overlapping rings, adopted as the logo of the Unitarian Universalist Association.

Origins

The symbol was created during the Second World War by the Austrian artist Hans Deutsch for the Unitarian Service Committee, the relief organisation founded in 1940 to aid people fleeing Nazi persecution. Deutsch, himself a refugee, drew the chalice — modelled on the oil lamps of ancient Greek and Roman altars — as a discreet emblem for the committee's documents and safe-conduct papers across Europe. The flaming chalice has also been linked to Jan Hus, the Czech reformer burned at the stake in 1415, whose followers, the Hussites, used the chalice as their emblem; Czech Unitarians drew on the same heritage.

Interpretations

Unitarian Universalism is a non-creedal faith, and the flaming chalice carries no official doctrine; congregations and individuals assign it many meanings. Common interpretations include the flame of truth and knowledge, the light of reason, the warmth of community, the fire of commitment, and a beacon of sanctuary recalling the symbol's wartime use guiding refugees to safety. The chalice itself is read as the vessel of community that holds these things.

Practice

Lighting the chalice opens most Sunday services in Unitarian Universalist congregations in North America and Unitarian services elsewhere; the flame is extinguished at the service's end. The ritual spread from the 1980s onward as a unifying practice for a denomination whose congregations vary widely in theology and format. Chalices range from plain ceramic bowls made by members to commissioned metalwork, and small travelling chalices are used in homes and small-group ministries.
