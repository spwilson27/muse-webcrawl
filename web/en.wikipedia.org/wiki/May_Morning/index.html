May Morning

May Morning is an annual celebration held at Magdalen College, Oxford, on 1 May. At 6 a.m. the college choir sings hymns from the top of Magdalen Tower to greet the coming of spring, before crowds gathered on Magdalen Bridge and along the High Street. The ceremony, a fixture of Oxford life for centuries, draws thousands of spectators, many of whom have celebrated through the previous night.

History

The tradition is often traced to the dedication of Magdalen Tower in 1509, when a choir sang at its completion. Its best-known element, the singing of the Latin hymn Hymnus Eucharisticus, composed in the 17th century by Benjamin Rogers, a Magdalen fellow, opens the ceremony, followed by madrigals and other songs. Morris dancing follows in the streets below, along with other folk performances.

Magdalen Bridge jumping

For many years revellers leapt from Magdalen Bridge into the River Cherwell after the singing, despite the water being only a few feet deep. The practice caused numerous injuries, including serious ones in 2005, after which the bridge was closed on May Morning by the authorities, largely ending the custom.
