Caldo verde (pronounced [ˈkaldu ˈveɾðɨ], Portuguese for "green broth") is a traditional Portuguese soup made from finely shredded green cabbage or collard greens, potatoes, onion, garlic and olive oil, usually served with slices of chouriço sausage. It is widely regarded as a national dish of Portugal.

Origin and preparation

The soup originated in the Minho province of northern Portugal. Potatoes are simmered with onion and garlic and puréed to form a creamy base; the greens — traditionally couve tronchuda (Portuguese cabbage) or, outside Portugal, collards or kale — are cut into extremely fine ribbons (chiffonade) and blanched briefly in the hot broth so they stay vivid green. A few slices of chouriço or linguiça are added, and the soup is finished with a drizzle of olive oil. It is classically accompanied by broa, a dense cornbread.

Cultural role

Caldo verde is served at celebrations and festivals throughout Portugal, notably during the Festa de Santo António in Lisbon and São João in Porto, and is a staple of Portuguese restaurants and homes abroad, including in Brazil and Portuguese diaspora communities. In 2011 it was chosen as one of the "Seven Wonders of Portuguese Gastronomy."
