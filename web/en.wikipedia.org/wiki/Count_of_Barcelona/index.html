Count of Barcelona

The comital title of Barcelona, held from the 9th century.

Count of Barcelona (Catalan: Comte de Barcelona; Spanish: Conde de Barcelona) was the title of the rulers of the County of Barcelona, which, by the 11th and 12th centuries, came to dominate the other Catalan counties and effectively to lead the Principality of Catalonia. The title was later inherited by the kings of Aragon and then by the monarchs of Spain.

History

The county originated in the Carolingian Spanish March: Charlemagne and his successors appointed counts to govern Barcelona after its capture from the Moors in 801. Early counts were Frankish appointees, but under Wilfred the Hairy (Guifré el Pilós), who united several Catalan counties in the late 9th century, the title became hereditary in his dynasty, the House of Barcelona.

Successive counts, including Ramon Berenguer I and Ramon Berenguer III, expanded the county's power and influence across Catalonia and into southern France. In 1137, Ramon Berenguer IV married Petronilla of Aragon, uniting the county dynastically with the Kingdom of Aragon to form the Crown of Aragon, whose rulers continued to style themselves Counts of Barcelona. Thereafter the title passed to the Trastámara and Habsburg monarchs and eventually to the Spanish crown.

Modern usage

Juan Carlos I of Spain granted the title to his father, Juan de Borbón, who used "Count of Barcelona" as his title in exile; after Juan's death in 1993, the title reverted to the Spanish crown. It remains among the historical titles of the King of Spain.
