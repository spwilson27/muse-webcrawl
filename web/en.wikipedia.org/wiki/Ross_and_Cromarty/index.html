Ross and Cromarty

Ross and Cromarty (Scottish Gaelic: Ros agus Cromba) is a historic county and a lieutenancy area in the Highlands of Scotland. It was formed in 1889 by the merger of the counties of Ross-shire and Cromartyshire — the latter an eccentric patchwork of enclaves scattered inside Ross-shire — and it survived as a local government county until 1975 and as a district of Highland Region until 1996. It now forms part of the Highland council area.

Geography

The area stretches across northern Scotland from the North Sea coast near Dingwall and the Cromarty Firth to the Atlantic coast at Wester Ross, including Applecross, Gairloch and Ullapool, and also includes the Isle of Lewis in the Outer Hebrides. Its landscapes range from the fertile farmland of the Black Isle and Easter Ross to the rugged Torridon mountains. The county town was Dingwall.

History

Ross was an ancient earldom, held by the Clan Ross and later the Mackenzies, while Cromartyshire's scattered parcels arose from lands acquired by the Cromarty Mackenzies in the 17th century. The region was affected by the Jacobite risings and the Highland Clearances, and its economy was historically based on crofting, fishing and whisky distilling. The Cromarty Firth later became a centre for offshore oil rig construction and repair.
