The Battle of Tolvajärvi was fought on 12 December 1939 during the Winter War between Finland and the Soviet Union. Colonel Paavo Talvela's group, with the attack led by Lieutenant Colonel Aaro Pajari, counter-attacked and defeated the Soviet 139th Rifle Division around the frozen lakes of Tolvajärvi in Ladoga Karelia. It was Finland's first major offensive victory of the war and a significant morale boost.

Background

After invading on 30 November 1939, Soviet forces advanced north of Lake Ladoga toward the Finnish rear, capturing Tolvajärvi and threatening the flank of the Finnish IV Corps. Talvela was sent to take command of the sector with orders to retake the ground.

The battle

Pajari's plan sent Finnish troops on skis across the frozen lakes Hirvasjärvi and Tolvajärvi to strike the Soviet positions from unexpected directions, while other forces attacked frontally along the roads. After fierce fighting on 12 December the Finns captured the Tolvajärvi village and tourist hotel area and drove the 139th Division back; fighting and retreats across the ice inflicted very heavy Soviet losses.

Aftermath

The Finns pressed on toward Ägläjärvi and the Aittojoki river line by Christmas. Talvela and Pajari became celebrated commanders, and Tolvajärvi entered Finnish memory as proof that the Red Army could be beaten in the forests by mobile, ski-borne troops.
