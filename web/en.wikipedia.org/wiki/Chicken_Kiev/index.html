Chicken Kiev (Russian: котлета по-киевски, kotleta po-kiyevski; Ukrainian: котлета по-київськи, kotleta po-kyivski, literally "cutlet Kyiv-style") is a dish made of a chicken fillet pounded and rolled around cold garlic butter, then coated in eggs and breadcrumbs, and either fried or baked. In general, it is a type of filled chicken cutlet known in Russian and Ukrainian cuisines.

History

The origin of the dish is disputed. Russian food historian William Pokhlyobkin claimed it was invented in 1912 at the Merchants' Club in St. Petersburg as "Novo-Mikhailovsky cutlets", later renamed in a Kyiv restaurant. Others attribute it to French chefs working in Russia in the 18th or 19th century, pointing to related French dishes such as the côtelette de volaille. The dish became a standard of Soviet restaurants and a popular ready meal in Britain, where Marks & Spencer introduced the "Chicken Kiev" in 1979 as its first chilled ready meal.

Preparation

A boneless, skinless chicken breast is butterflied, pounded flat, and wrapped around a chilled piece of garlic-and-herb butter. The parcel is breaded and cooked so that the butter melts inside; when the diner cuts into it, the butter spurts out — a feature that has given the dish a reputation for requiring care (and, in Soviet restaurants, printed warnings to diners).
