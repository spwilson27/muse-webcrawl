Erotomania

Erotomania
Also called: de Clérambault's syndrome
Specialty: Psychiatry
Type: Delusional disorder (erotomanic type)
Key feature: Fixed false belief that another person is in love with the patient

Erotomania, also known as de Clérambault's syndrome, is a type of delusional disorder in which the affected person believes, without foundation, that another person is in love with them. The object of the belief is typically of higher social status — a celebrity, superior, or stranger — and the patient interprets neutral or even rejecting gestures, glances and messages as secret declarations of love.

Clinical picture

The delusion is usually systematised and persistent: the patient is convinced the other person initiated the attachment, that obstacles (a spouse, social conventions, intermediaries) prevent open declaration, and that signs confirming the love are everywhere. Behaviour can include letter-writing, gift-giving, persistent attempts at contact, and surveillance; in some cases this escalates to stalking behaviour, and erotomania is a recognised diagnosis in a minority of stalking cases. Outside the specific delusion, functioning may otherwise be relatively preserved, although the condition can also occur within schizophrenia, bipolar mania, or other psychotic illnesses rather than as an isolated delusional disorder.

History

The French psychiatrist Gaëtan Gatian de Clérambault described the syndrome in 1921 ("les psychoses passionnelles"), distinguishing the primary form in a young woman convinced of the love of an older man of higher rank. The term itself is older: earlier writers used "erotomania" for excessive, morbid love generally, and the obsession of unrequited love figures in literature long before its psychiatric definition.

Treatment

Treatment generally involves antipsychotic medication, with psychotherapy playing a supporting role; prognosis is better when erotomania is secondary to another treatable disorder. Management also addresses risk: assessment of stalking behaviour and safety of the person targeted is a standard part of care.
