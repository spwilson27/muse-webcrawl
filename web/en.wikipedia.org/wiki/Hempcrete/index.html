Hempcrete

Hempcrete
CompositionHemp hurd (shiv), lime binder, water
DensityRoughly 300–500 kg/m³
UsesWall infill insulation, floors, roof insulation

Hempcrete, also known as hemp-lime, is a bio-composite material made from the inner woody core of the hemp plant (hemp hurd or shiv), mixed with a lime-based binder and water. It is used as a material for construction and insulation. It is marketed under names like Hempcrete, Canobiote, Canosmose, Isochanvre, and (in France) béton de chanvre.

Properties

Hempcrete is lightweight — about one-eighth the density of ordinary concrete — and is not load-bearing; it is normally cast around a structural timber or steel frame as infill. It provides insulation, regulates humidity by absorbing and releasing moisture vapour ("breathability"), resists mould and pests, and is fire-resistant. Because the hemp plant absorbs carbon dioxide as it grows and the lime slowly re-carbonates, hempcrete is often described as carbon-negative or carbon-storing.

History and use

Hemp-lime construction was pioneered in France in the 1980s and 1990s, initially for renovating timber-framed buildings, and spread to the United Kingdom, Ireland, and North America as industrial hemp cultivation was legalised. The material is typically cast in situ into formwork, spray-applied, or used as pre-cast blocks and panels.

Limitations

Hempcrete has low compressive strength compared with concrete, dries slowly, and in some jurisdictions faces building-code and supply-chain barriers linked to the regulation of industrial hemp.
