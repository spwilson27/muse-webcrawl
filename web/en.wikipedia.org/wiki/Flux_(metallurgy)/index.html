Flux (metallurgy)

Fluxes clean metal surfaces, prevent oxidation and promote the flow of molten metal in joining and smelting.

In metallurgy, a flux (from Latin fluxus, "flow") is a chemical agent that cleans, purifies or promotes flowing. Fluxes serve several related purposes: they dissolve or prevent metal oxides that interfere with joining, shield molten metal from the air, lower the melting point of refractory impurities so they separate as slag, and improve wetting so that filler metal spreads over the surfaces to be joined.

Soldering and brazing

In soldering, flux removes the thin oxide film on copper, brass or tin surfaces and prevents re-oxidation during heating, allowing solder to wet and bond. Traditional fluxes include rosin (colophony) for electronics, often delivered as the core of solder wire, and more aggressive inorganic fluxes based on zinc chloride ("killed spirits") for plumbing and sheet metal. Electronics uses "no-clean" and water-soluble organic-acid fluxes whose residues are designed to be harmless or easily removed. In brazing, borax and boric-acid/fluoride mixtures serve as fluxes at higher temperatures.

Welding

Shielded metal arc welding uses electrodes coated with flux that decomposes to form a shielding gas and a slag blanket over the weld; submerged arc and flux-cored arc welding similarly bury the arc under granular flux or a flux core. The flux also can add alloying elements and deoxidisers to the weld metal.

Smelting and refining

In smelting, fluxes such as limestone are added to furnaces to combine with gangue minerals and ash, forming a low-melting slag that floats on the molten metal and can be tapped off — essential in the blast furnace production of iron. In aluminium production, cryolite serves as the flux-solvent for alumina in the Hall–Héroult process. Foundry and refining fluxes also scavenge inclusions and dissolved gases.

Health and handling

Flux fumes, particularly from rosin and fluoride-bearing fluxes, can irritate the respiratory tract, and occupational asthma is a recognised hazard of electronics soldering; extraction and ventilation are standard controls.
