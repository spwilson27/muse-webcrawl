Adamantios Korais

Adamantios Korais (Greek: Αδαμάντιος Κοραής; 27 April 1748 – 6 April 1833) was a Greek scholar of the Enlightenment and a central figure of the modern Greek national revival. From Paris, where he spent most of his adult life, he prepared the intellectual ground for Greek independence through editions of classical texts, essays on education, and his advocacy of Katharevousa, a "purified" form of the Greek language.

Life

Korais was born in Smyrna to a Chiot silk merchant's family, studied in Amsterdam while working in the family trade, and took a medical degree at Montpellier before settling in Paris in 1788. There he witnessed the Revolution, whose principles shaped his political writings, and devoted himself to scholarship, living modestly on publishing and the patronage of wealthy Greek merchants, notably the Zosimas brothers.

Work

His great project, the "Greek Library" (Elliniki Vivliothiki), issued critical editions of Hippocrates, Aristotle, Plutarch, Strabo and others, each prefaced by long essays (the Prolegomena) that doubled as a programme for national education and reform. He argued for a language midway between ancient Greek and the spoken demotic — corrected, as he put it, without being severed from the people — a compromise that dominated official Greek until 1976 and fuelled the long "language question". Korais corresponded with Thomas Jefferson, whose advice he sought on a constitution for Greece; though he died in 1833, two years after independence was internationally recognised, he had never returned to Greece.
