Steven Patrick Morrissey (born 22 May 1959), known mononymously as Morrissey, is an English singer and songwriter. He came to prominence as the frontman and lyricist of the rock band the Smiths, who were active from 1982 to 1987. He has since pursued a successful solo career, placing ten albums in the UK top ten.

Morrissey is widely regarded as one of the most influential lyricists in British popular music.

Early life

Morrissey was born in Davyhulme, Lancashire, to Irish Catholic working-class parents who had emigrated from Dublin. A bookish and withdrawn teenager, he became a passionate music fan, writing letters to the music press and briefly fronting the punk band the Nosebleeds.

The Smiths

In 1982 Morrissey formed the Smiths with guitarist Johnny Marr. His literate, witty, and often melancholic lyrics — addressing alienation, desire, and English working-class life — combined with Marr's jangle-pop guitar to make the band one of the most acclaimed British acts of the 1980s, with albums including Meat Is Murder (1985) and The Queen Is Dead (1986). The band split in 1987 amid tensions between Morrissey and Marr.

Solo career

Morrissey's debut solo album, Viva Hate (1988), reached number one in the UK. After a commercial dip, he returned to prominence with Your Arsenal (1992) and Vauxhall and I (1994), which included the hit "The More You Ignore Me, the Closer I Get". Following a quieter period in Los Angeles, he returned with You Are the Quarry (2004). His memoir, Autobiography, was published in 2013 as a Penguin Classic, and his novel List of the Lost followed in 2015.

Views and controversies

A committed vegetarian from adolescence and a prominent animal-rights advocate, Morrissey has also attracted sustained controversy for outspoken political statements, including remarks on immigration and support for fringe political figures, which have strained his public standing in later years.

Legacy

Morrissey is frequently cited as a defining figure of indie music; his lyrical style and stage persona — gladioli, hearing aid, and quiff — made him an enduring cult icon, with a particularly devoted following in the United States and among Mexican-American fans in Los Angeles.
