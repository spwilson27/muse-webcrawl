Syro-Malabar Church

The Syro-Malabar Church is an Eastern Catholic church based in Kerala, India, in full communion with the Holy See in Rome. It is the largest of the Saint Thomas Christian (Nasrani) denominations, with about 4.5 million believers, and is a major archiepiscopal church within the Catholic communion. The church follows the East Syriac liturgical tradition, celebrating the Holy Qurbana, historically attributed to Saint Thomas the Apostle, who tradition holds evangelized the Malabar Coast in AD 52.

The head of the church is the Major Archbishop of Ernakulam–Angamaly, whose see is at Kakkanad, Kochi. The church has dioceses (eparchies) throughout India and, increasingly, among the Indian diaspora in the United States, Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom, and the Gulf states.

History

The Saint Thomas Christians maintained communion with the Church of the East in Persia for over a thousand years, receiving bishops from the Patriarch of Babylon. Portuguese colonization in the 16th century brought Latin ecclesiastical jurisdiction, and tensions over Latinization culminated in the Coonan Cross Oath of 1653, after which the community split. The portion that later entered into full communion with Rome while retaining its East Syriac heritage became the Syro-Malabar Church, formally constituted as a distinct particular church by 1923, when Pope Pius XI established its hierarchy.

Liturgy and identity

The church celebrates the Holy Qurbana according to the Anaphora of Addai and Mari, one of the oldest eucharistic prayers in Christianity. In the later 20th century the liturgy was revised amid an ongoing internal debate between advocates of restoring the traditional East Syriac (Chaldean) form and those favoring a more Indianized celebration; a uniform mode of celebration, combining a partly facing-the-people and partly facing-the-altar form, was mandated in 2021, though its implementation met resistance in some dioceses.

The church is noted for its dense network of schools, colleges, hospitals, and religious congregations in Kerala, and for its many missionaries serving throughout India.
