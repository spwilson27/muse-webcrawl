Validity (statistics)

Validity is the extent to which a concept, conclusion, or measurement is well-founded and corresponds accurately to the real world. In statistics and psychometrics, a valid measurement measures what it claims to measure; validity is distinct from reliability, since a measure can be consistently wrong.

Types

Several kinds of validity are distinguished in research design and testing:

  • Construct validity — whether a measure adequately represents the underlying theoretical construct.
  • Content validity — whether a test covers the full range of the domain it samples.
  • Criterion validity — how well a measure predicts or correlates with an outcome (concurrent and predictive validity).
  • Internal validity — whether a study's design supports causal conclusions, free of confounding.
  • External validity — whether results generalise to other populations, settings, and times.
  • Face validity — whether a measure superficially appears to measure what it intends.

Modern measurement theory increasingly treats validity as a unified concept concerning the evidence supporting the interpretation and use of measurements, as set out in the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing.
