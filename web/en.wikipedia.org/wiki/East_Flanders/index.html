East Flanders

East Flanders (Dutch: Oost-Vlaanderen) is a province of Belgium in the Flemish Region. It borders (clockwise from the North) the Dutch province of Zeeland and the Belgian provinces of Antwerp, Flemish Brabant, Hainaut and West Flanders. It has an area of about 3,007 km², divided into six administrative districts containing 60 municipalities, and a population of over 1.5 million. The capital is Ghent, one of Belgium's largest and most historic cities.

History

The province takes its name from the historic County of Flanders, one of the great principalities of the medieval Low Countries. The province of East Flanders was created during the period of French rule as the department of Escaut (Scheldt) and took its modern form after Belgian independence in 1830. Its territory corresponds roughly to the eastern part of the old county, east of the former imperial boundary on the Scheldt.

Geography and economy

The land is mostly low-lying polder and gently rolling country drained by the Scheldt and Leie rivers. Ghent, at their confluence, hosts a major inland port connected to the North Sea by the Ghent–Terneuzen Canal. The economy combines port logistics, steel and automotive industry around Ghent, chemicals, textiles around Aalst and Sint-Niklaas, and intensive agriculture and floriculture. Other significant towns include Aalst, Sint-Niklaas, Dendermonde, Oudenaarde, and Lokeren.
