The Liberal Party of Australia is a centre-right political party in Australia and one of the two major parties in Australian politics, along with the Australian Labor Party. Founded in 1944 by Robert Menzies, it has governed Australia for most of the post-war period, almost always in a permanent Coalition with the National Party. The party combines economically liberal policies — free enterprise, low taxation, deregulation — with socially conservative positions among many of its members, and describes itself as a "broad church" of liberal and conservative traditions.

History

The Liberal Party was formed at a conference in Canberra in October 1944, uniting a dozen non-Labor parties and organisations after the collapse of the United Australia Party. Under Menzies, it won government at the 1949 election and held office for 23 years, with Menzies himself serving until 1966 — the longest-serving prime ministership in Australian history. After losing office in 1972 to Gough Whitlam's Labor government, the Liberals under Malcolm Fraser returned to power amid the 1975 constitutional crisis and governed until 1983. John Howard led the party to victory in 1996 and governed for eleven years. Following a period of leadership instability (Abbott, Turnbull, Morrison), the Coalition lost the 2022 election to Anthony Albanese's Labor government, and suffered a heavy defeat at the 2025 federal election, after which Sussan Ley became the party's first female federal leader.

Structure and philosophy

The party is a federation of state and territory divisions with substantial autonomy; in Queensland, the merged Liberal National Party (LNP) and in the Northern Territory the Country Liberal Party are affiliated organisations. Menzies framed the party's founding philosophy around the "forgotten people" — the middle class — and opposition to socialism, while later leaders emphasised economic reform: the Howard government introduced the goods and services tax (GST) in 2000 and extensive industrial-relations changes. Internally the party has recurrently debated climate and energy policy, which contributed to several leadership changes in the 2010s and electoral losses to community independents ("teals") in formerly safe urban seats in 2022 and 2025.

Leaders

Liberal prime ministers have included Robert Menzies, Harold Holt, John Gorton, William McMahon, Malcolm Fraser, John Howard, Tony Abbott, Malcolm Turnbull, and Scott Morrison. The party's deputy leader traditionally becomes Deputy Prime Minister when in Coalition government, though that office goes to the Nationals leader.
