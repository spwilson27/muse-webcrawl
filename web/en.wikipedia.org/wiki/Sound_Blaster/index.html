Sound Blaster
Manufacturer: Creative Technology
Introduced: 1989
Type: Sound card family

Sound Blaster is a family of sound cards designed by Singaporean company Creative Technology (known in the US as Creative Labs). Released in 1989, the original Sound Blaster became the dominant de facto standard for PC audio through the 1990s.

History

The original Sound Blaster combined an 11-voice Yamaha FM synthesis chip (OPL2) with a digital audio channel capable of recording and playing back samples, plus a game port with MIDI support. Successive models — Sound Blaster Pro, Sound Blaster 16 (with CD-quality 16-bit audio), AWE32/AWE64 (wavetable synthesis), Live!, Audigy, and the X-Fi series — tracked the evolution of PC multimedia and gaming audio, including positional audio technologies such as EAX.

Decline and legacy

As motherboard-integrated audio improved in the 2000s, the discrete sound card market shrank. The Sound Blaster name continues on external DACs, gaming headsets, and USB audio devices. "Sound Blaster compatible" was for years a key specification for DOS games, shaping the design of PC audio software.
