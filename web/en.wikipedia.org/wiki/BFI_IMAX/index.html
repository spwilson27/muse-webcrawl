The BFI IMAX is an IMAX cinema in the South Bank district of London, just north of Waterloo station. Operated by the British Film Institute (BFI) and managed by Odeon Cinemas since 2012, it houses Britain's largest cinema screen — at over 26 metres wide and 20 metres high, roughly the height of five double-decker buses — with a seating capacity of just under 500.

Building and history

The cinema, designed by architect Bryan Avery, opened on 1 May 1999. It occupies a traffic island in the centre of the Waterloo roundabout; the auditorium sits inside a distinctive cylindrical glass drum whose exterior has famously carried giant advertising wraps and film artwork, making the building itself a London landmark. The building stands on the site of the former Cardboard City, an encampment of homeless people in the Bullring underpasses cleared in the late 1990s. The former entrance walkway and underpass still bear commemorative traces of that history.

Projection and programming

The BFI IMAX shows IMAX documentaries, blockbuster releases — often screening 70 mm IMAX film prints for directors such as Christopher Nolan, whose premieres of films including Dunkirk and Oppenheimer drew long queues — and special seasons, all-night screenings and festival events, including as part of the BFI London Film Festival. Following a refurbishment and reopening in 2022, the cinema combined dual-laser IMAX digital projection with retention of its 15/70 film projector, one of very few venues worldwide able to show true 70 mm IMAX prints.
