Digital forensics

Image: Forensic disk imaging

Digital forensics (sometimes known as digital forensic science) is a branch of forensic science encompassing the recovery, investigation, examination, and analysis of material found in digital devices, often in relation to mobile devices and computer crime. The term was originally used as a synonym of computer forensics but has expanded to cover the investigation of all devices capable of storing digital data.

Process

Investigations typically follow four phases: seizure (identifying and securing devices and data), acquisition (creating exact forensic images, usually with write blockers and cryptographic hashing to verify integrity), analysis (recovering deleted files, examining logs, timelines, and artifacts), and reporting (documenting findings for use in legal proceedings). Maintaining an unbroken chain of custody is essential for evidence to be admissible.

Branches

Sub-disciplines include computer forensics, mobile device forensics, network forensics, forensic data analysis, and database forensics. Related techniques such as malware analysis and memory forensics address volatile data and malicious software. Digital forensics is used both by law enforcement and in corporate investigations and incident response.

History

The field emerged in the 1980s as personal computers spread, with early practitioners drawn from law enforcement and systems administration. Dedicated tools such as EnCase and the open-source Sleuth Kit, professional certifications, and academic programmes developed from the 1990s onward.
