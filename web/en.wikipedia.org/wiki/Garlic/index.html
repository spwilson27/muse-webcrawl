Garlic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Garlic bulbs and cloves with a braid of garlic]

Garlic (Allium sativum) is a species of bulbous flowering plant in the onion genus, native to Central Asia, used worldwide as a seasoning and traditional medicine. Its pungent flavour, raw-sharp and sweet when cooked, underpins Mediterranean, Asian, Latin American and many other cuisines — it is inseparable from the soffritto, the wok and the salsa alike.

Egyptian pyramid builders, Greek athletes and Roman soldiers all consumed garlic; it was credited with repelling everything from plague to vampires. Its chemistry rests on allicin, released when cells are crushed and rapidly transformed into the sulphur compounds responsible for its aroma — and for "garlic breath". Medicinal claims (cardiovascular, antimicrobial) are partly supported for blood pressure, modestly.

1. Cultivation

Cultivation

Garlic is propagated from cloves, planted in autumn in temperate climates; hardneck and softneck types differ in stalk and storage. China produces the great majority of the world's crop.
