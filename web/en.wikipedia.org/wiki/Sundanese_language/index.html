Sundanese language

Sundanese (Basa Sunda) is a Malayo-Polynesian language spoken by the Sundanese people of western Java, Indonesia. With roughly 32 million native speakers, it is the second most widely spoken regional language in Indonesia after Javanese.

Native toIndonesia (West Java, Banten, Jakarta)
Native speakersAbout 32 million
Language familyAustronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Sundanese
Writing systemLatin; historically Sundanese script (Aksara Sunda)

Speech levels

Sundanese is noted for its elaborate speech-level system, distinguishing polite (lemes) from familiar (loma) vocabulary according to the status of the addressee and the person spoken about — a feature shared with Javanese. Word choice across verbs, pronouns and common nouns changes with the level.

Dialects and script

Dialects include those of Banten, Bogor, Priangan (considered standard) and Cirebon. The language was traditionally written in the Sundanese script, of Indic origin, which has seen a modern revival alongside the dominant Latin alphabet; Pegon (Arabic-based) script was also used for religious texts.
