Meghalaya (from Sanskrit megha, "cloud", and ālaya, "abode" — "the abode of clouds") is a state in northeast India, bordered by Assam to the north and east and by Bangladesh to the south and west. Its capital is Shillong. Meghalaya was formed on 21 January 1972 by carving out two districts from Assam: the United Khasi Hills and Jaintia Hills, and the Garo Hills.

Geography

The state is a hilly plateau region about 300 km long east–west and 100 km wide, with elevations between 150 and 1,961 m (the highest point is Shillong Peak). Meghalaya is among the wettest places on Earth: the village of Mawsynram and the town of Cherrapunji (Sohra) in the Khasi Hills vie for the world record for highest average annual rainfall, receiving around 11–12 metres of rain per year, mainly during the southwest monsoon.

People and culture

The majority of the population belongs to Scheduled Tribes, principally the Khasi, Garo and Jaintia peoples. Khasi and Garo societies are famously matrilineal: descent and inheritance pass through the female line, and the youngest daughter traditionally inherits the family property. Most inhabitants are Christians, with significant Hindu and indigenous (Niam Khasi/Niamtre and Songsarek) minorities.

The Khasi Hills are known for their living root bridges, formed by guiding the aerial roots of rubber fig trees across streams over generations; the double-decker bridge at Nongriat is a celebrated example. Sacred forest groves such as Mawphlang preserve patches of ancient forest protected by religious tradition.

Economy

Agriculture employs most of the workforce, with rice, maize, potatoes, turmeric, ginger and the prized Lakadong turmeric among the main products; shifting (jhum) cultivation is still practised in places. The state has deposits of coal, limestone, uranium and sillimanite, though coal mining — particularly unregulated "rat-hole" mining — has been controversial and was banned by the National Green Tribunal in 2014.
