Disk encryption

Disk encryption is the use of encryption to protect data stored on a computer's storage media so that it cannot be read without the correct key, even if the device is lost, stolen, or accessed by an unauthorised party. Encryption may be performed by software or by dedicated hardware in the drive itself.

Full-disk encryption

Full-disk encryption (FDE) encrypts every bit of data on a volume, including the operating system. Well-known implementations include BitLocker (Microsoft Windows), FileVault (macOS), and LUKS/dm-crypt (Linux). Typically the user supplies a password or PIN at boot, which unwraps a master key stored under hardware protection such as a Trusted Platform Module (TPM).

Considerations

Disk encryption protects data at rest but not data on a running, unlocked system. Common modes such as XTS are designed for sector-addressable storage. Risks include weak passphrases, cold-boot attacks against memory, and key-management failures that can render data irrecoverable. File-level and filesystem-level encryption are related approaches that encrypt selected data instead of whole volumes.
