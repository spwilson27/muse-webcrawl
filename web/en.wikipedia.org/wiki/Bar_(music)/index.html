Bar (music)

In musical notation, a bar (or measure in American English) is a segment of music bounded by vertical lines, known as bar lines, usually indicating one cycle of the meter defined by the time signature. A typical melody might be notated in a succession of bars, each containing the number of beats given by the time signature — for example, four quarter-note beats in 4/4 time.

Types of bar line

A single light bar line separates measures. A light-heavy (final) bar line marks the end of a piece or section. Double bar lines separate sections of a work, and repeat signs — a light-heavy bar line with two dots — instruct the performer to repeat a passage. Dashed bar lines may subdivide long measures for readability without changing the meter.

History

Bar lines came into regular use in the late 16th and 17th centuries, initially to help align parts in ensemble scores rather than to mark metrical units. By the Baroque era they were standard, though some 20th-century and contemporary composers omit them to free the rhythmic flow, and mensural or chant notation never uses them.

Hypermeasure

Beyond the individual bar, bars often group into regular hypermeasures — larger metrical units of two, four or eight bars that shape phrasing, much as beats group into bars. Much Western popular and classical music is built in four-bar phrases.
