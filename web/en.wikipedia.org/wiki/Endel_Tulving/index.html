Endel Tulving

Born: 26 May 1927, Petseri, Estonia (now Pechory, Russia)
Died: 11 September 2023 (aged 96), Mississauga, Ontario, Canada
Known for: Episodic memory; encoding specificity principle
Fields: Cognitive psychology, neuroscience

Endel Tulving (26 May 1927 – 11 September 2023) was an Estonian-born Canadian experimental psychologist and cognitive neuroscientist whose research on human memory made him one of the founders of modern memory studies.

Life and career

Tulving was born in Petseri, Estonia. His family fled the Soviet occupation in 1944, and he eventually settled in Canada, studying at the University of Toronto, where he earned his bachelor's and master's degrees. He completed his PhD at Harvard University in 1957 under George A. Miller and returned to Toronto, spending most of his career there, and later worked with the Rotman Research Institute at Baycrest.

Contributions

Tulving is best known for distinguishing episodic memory — memory for personally experienced events — from semantic memory — general knowledge of facts. His 1972 chapter "Episodic and Semantic Memory" reshaped the field. He also formulated the encoding specificity principle: memory is best when the conditions at retrieval match those at encoding. His studies with patient K.C., who had lost episodic memory while retaining semantic knowledge, provided influential neuropsychological evidence for the distinction. He further proposed that episodic memory supports "mental time travel", which he called chronesthesia.

Honours

Tulving was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and the Royal Society of Canada, was made an Officer of the Order of Canada, and received the Gairdner Foundation International Award (2005). A 2002 review ranked him among the most cited psychologists of the 20th century.
