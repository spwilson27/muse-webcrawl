The Élysée Palace (French: Palais de l'Élysée) is the official residence of the President of the French Republic. Located in the 8th arrondissement of Paris on the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré near the Champs-Élysées, it contains the presidential office and residence and hosts the weekly meetings of the Council of Ministers. By metonymy, "l'Élysée" commonly refers to the French presidency itself.

History

The hôtel particulier was built between 1718 and 1722 for the Count of Évreux, Louis Henri de La Tour d'Auvergne, to designs by Armand-Claude Mollet. It later passed to Madame de Pompadour, to Joachim Murat — who received the abdication of Napoleon in 1815 — and to the Duchess of Berry. In 1848 the National Assembly designated it the residence of the President of the Republic, and under the Third Republic it became the permanent presidential seat.

Description

The palace comprises the main corps de logis with state apartments — including the Salon des Ambassadeurs, where the President receives foreign envoys, and the Salon Doré, the President's office — the eastern and western wings, and a garden of some two hectares stretching toward the Champs-Élysées. Beneath the palace lies the Jupiter command post, a fortified bunker from which the President can direct France's nuclear forces.

The Élysée employs several hundred staff. Its silverware, furniture, and wine cellar are celebrated; when President Emmanuel Macron's administration auctioned part of the cellar surplus and gold-plated presidential china, the palace attracted renewed public attention. State dinners for visiting heads of state are held in the Salle des Fêtes.
