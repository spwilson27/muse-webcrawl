The Dominate was the later phase of Roman imperial government

Dominate

The Dominate is the name sometimes given to the later phase of government in the ancient Roman Empire, following the earlier period known as the Principate. The Dominate is usually taken to begin with the accession of Diocletian in 284 AD, when the emperor became openly an absolute ruler (dominus, "lord" or "master") rather than, as under Augustus and his successors, a princeps — nominally the first citizen among equals.

Characteristics

Under the Dominate the emperor adopted elaborate court ceremony influenced by eastern monarchies: the diadem, prostration (proskynesis/adoration of the purple), and a sacral style of rule. Diocletian divided the empire and established the Tetrarchy, greatly expanded the bureaucracy and army, separated civil from military authority in the provinces, and attempted economic controls such as the Edict on Maximum Prices (301).

Use of the term

The Principate/Dominate contrast, popularised by 19th-century historians, is now often qualified by scholars, who note continuities in imperial power from Augustus onward. Nevertheless the term remains a convenient label for the late Roman governmental system of the 3rd to 6th centuries, which the Byzantine Empire inherited.
