Administrative division

Administrative divisions (abbreviated as ADM) are geographical areas into which a particular independent sovereign state is divided. Such a unit usually has an administrative authority with the power to make administrative or policy decisions for its area. Administrative divisions are conceptually separate from dependent territories, with the former being an integral part of the state and the other being only under some lesser form of control. However, in practice the terminology is used loosely, and many entities called administrative divisions are in fact dependencies.

Terminology

The names of administrative divisions vary enormously between languages and countries, and translation is often inexact. Common terms include:

  • Province — used by many countries including Canada, China (省级), and Spain;
  • State — used in federal systems such as the United States, Germany (Länder), India, and Australia;
  • Region — used in France, Italy, and Chile;
  • Department — used in France and many Latin American countries;
  • Prefecture — used in Japan, Greece, and historically China;
  • County — used as a second-level division in the United States and England;
  • District, municipality, commune, and parish — typically lower-level divisions.

Levels and hierarchy

Most countries organize divisions into a hierarchy of two to four levels: for example, a country may be divided into states or provinces, which are divided into counties or districts, which are divided into municipalities or townships. Each level typically has its own governmental functions — larger units handle regional planning, policing, and infrastructure, while the smallest units handle local services such as waste collection and civil registration. Statistical standards such as the European Union's NUTS classification harmonize these varying hierarchies for comparison.

Comparison

The largest first-level administrative division in the world by area is Russia's Sakha Republic, at about 3.1 million square kilometres, while the most populous is India's Uttar Pradesh, with over 200 million inhabitants — more than most countries. At the other extreme, some states such as Vatican City and Monaco have no subdivisions at all, and small unitary states may have only municipalities below the national government.
