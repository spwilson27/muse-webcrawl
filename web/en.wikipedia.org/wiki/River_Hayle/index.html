River Hayle

The River Hayle (Cornish: Heyl, meaning "estuary") is a small river in west Cornwall, England. It rises south-east of Crowan and flows about 12 miles (19 km) north-west through the St Erth valley to enter St Ives Bay at Hayle, where it forms a tidal estuary.

Course

The river passes the villages of Relubbas and St Erth before reaching Hayle. The Hayle estuary, a ria (drowned river valley), is split by road and rail crossings into tidal pools including Copperhouse Pool and Carnsew Pool. During the Industrial Revolution the estuary was a busy industrial port serving the Cornish mining industry: Harveys of Hayle built some of the world's largest beam engines there, and the rival firm of Cornish Copper operated from Copperhouse at the eastern end.

Wildlife

The Hayle Estuary is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest and an RSPB reserve, valued for the wading birds and wildfowl that feed on its mudflats in winter. The surrounding area forms part of the Cornwall Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.
