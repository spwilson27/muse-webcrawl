Black bloc

TypeProtest tactic
Origins1980s West Germany (autonomist movement)
CharacteristicsBlack clothing, masks, anonymity, affinity groups

A black bloc is a tactic used by protesters who wear black clothing, ski masks, scarves, and other face coverings to conceal their identities and appear as a unified mass. It is associated mainly with anarchist and autonomist movements.

History

The tactic emerged in West Germany in the 1980s, where the Schwarzer Block formed at demonstrations in defence of squats and against nuclear power. It gained international attention at the 1999 Seattle WTO protests, where black bloc participants damaged property of multinational corporations, and at subsequent anti-globalization and G8/G20 summit protests.

Tactics and debate

Participants maintain that masking protects them from surveillance and retaliation and that the bloc provides mutual support and anonymity within demonstrations. Black blocs have been both defended as a legitimate form of protest and criticized — by police, media, and other protesters — for property destruction and for allegedly escalating confrontations.
