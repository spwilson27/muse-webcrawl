Hexosaminidase

Hexosaminidase (EC 3.2.1.52) is a family of glycoside hydrolase enzymes involved in the hydrolysis of terminal N-acetyl-D-hexosamine residues in N-acetyl-β-D-hexosaminides. In humans the most important hexosaminidases are the lysosomal enzymes hexosaminidase A and hexosaminidase B, which degrade gangliosides and other glycolipids and glycosaminoglycans.

Structure

Hexosaminidase A is a heterodimer of an α subunit (encoded by the HEXA gene on chromosome 15) and a β subunit (encoded by HEXB on chromosome 5). Hexosaminidase B is a homodimer of two β subunits, and the minor form hexosaminidase S is a homodimer of α subunits. Degradation of GM2 ganglioside by hexosaminidase A additionally requires the small GM2 activator protein (GM2A).

Function

Within the lysosome, hexosaminidase A cleaves a terminal N-acetylgalactosamine residue from GM2 ganglioside, converting it to GM3 ganglioside — a step essential for the normal turnover of neuronal membrane lipids.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of the α subunit causes Tay–Sachs disease, an autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder in which GM2 ganglioside accumulates in neurons, causing progressive neurodegeneration. Deficiency of the β subunit causes Sandhoff disease, which combines the features of Tay–Sachs with visceral involvement because both hexosaminidase A and B are lost. Enzyme assay of hexosaminidase activity in blood is used for carrier screening, notably in Ashkenazi Jewish populations where Tay–Sachs carrier frequency is elevated.
