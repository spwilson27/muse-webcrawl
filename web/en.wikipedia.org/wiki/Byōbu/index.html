Byōbu

Byōbu (屏風, "wind wall") are Japanese folding screens made from several joined panels, bearing decorative painting and calligraphy, used to separate interiors and enclose private spaces. Byōbu were introduced to Japan from China in the eighth century and developed into one of the most important formats of Japanese painting.

Construction

A byōbu consists of wooden lattice frames covered with silk or paper, joined by paper hinges so the screen folds in a zigzag. Standard screens have six panels (rokkyoku); pairs are common. Many are covered with gold leaf (kin-byōbu), which reflects light in dim interiors.

Art

Byōbu provided large surfaces for the celebrated works of the Rinpa and Kano schools: Tawaraya Sōtatsu's Wind God and Thunder God screens, and Ogata Kōrin's Red and White Plum Blossoms, are among the most famous. Screens were displayed at festivals, behind guests of honour, and at tea ceremonies, where a smaller two-panel type, the furosaki byōbu, is used.
