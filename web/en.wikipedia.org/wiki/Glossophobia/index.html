Glossophobia

Glossophobia or speech anxiety is the fear of public speaking. The word comes from the Greek γλῶσσα (glōssa, "tongue") and φόβος (phobos, "fear"). It is a common form of social anxiety; surveys frequently find that a large share of adults report some fear of speaking in public, sometimes ranking it above other common fears.

Symptoms and causes

Symptoms include rapid heartbeat, trembling voice and hands, dry mouth, blushing, nausea and a feeling of panic. The fear is thought to arise from a combination of genetic predisposition, learned experience and the social threat of being judged. Performance anxiety in actors and musicians is closely related.

Treatment

Effective approaches include cognitive behavioural therapy, systematic desensitisation and graduated practice, voice and breathing training, and joining speaking organisations such as Toastmasters International. In some cases beta blockers are used to control physical symptoms.
