The 1989 Polish legislative election, held on 4 and 18 June 1989, was the first partially free election in Poland since the Second World War and a decisive moment in the collapse of communism in Eastern Europe. Under the Round Table Agreement between the ruling Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR) and the opposition Solidarity movement, only 161 of the 460 seats in the Sejm were freely contested, while the restored Senate was elected entirely freely.

Campaign

Solidarity, led by Lech Wałęsa, campaigned through the Solidarity Citizens' Committee, fielding candidates photographed alongside Wałęsa in a famous series of posters. The communists and their allies expected their guaranteed majority and unopposed "national list" of senior figures to be ratified quietly.

Results

The result was a rout. Solidarity won all 161 contested Sejm seats and 99 of the 100 Senate seats. Most humiliatingly for the regime, 33 of the 35 unopposed candidates on the national list failed to win the required 50 per cent of votes and were eliminated, requiring hastily arranged run-offs in which the government side limped into the reserved seats. Turnout was about 62 per cent in the first round.

Consequences

Although the agreement guaranteed the communists the presidency and a Sejm majority, the election destroyed the regime's legitimacy. Wałęsa's slogan "yours the president, ours the premier" captured the emerging deal: in August 1989 Tadeusz Mazowiecki formed the first government led by a non-communist in the Soviet bloc, accelerating the revolutions of 1989 across Eastern Europe.
