Hadith of the pond of Khumm

The hadith of the pond of Khumm (Arabic: حديث الغدير) refers to a sermon delivered by the Islamic prophet Muhammad on 18 Dhu al-Hijjah in the year 10 AH (corresponding to March 632 AD), shortly after his Farewell Pilgrimage, at a pond (ghadir) in the area of Khumm, near al-Juhfa in present-day Saudi Arabia.

The event

According to the accounts, Muhammad halted the caravan of pilgrims returning from Mecca and, before a large gathering, took Ali ibn Abi Talib by the hand and declared, in the most widely transmitted wording, "Whoever takes me as his mawla, Ali is also his mawla" (Arabic: من كنت مولاه فعلي مولاه). He is also reported to have prayed for those who befriended Ali and against those who opposed him.

Interpretations

The meaning of the Arabic term mawla in this statement is a longstanding point of difference between Shia and Sunni Muslims. Shia tradition regards the declaration as a clear designation (nass) of Ali as Muhammad's successor in religious and political authority, and commemorates the event annually as Eid al-Ghadir. Sunni tradition generally interprets mawla in the sense of friend, ally or supporter, understanding the statement as an affirmation of Ali's standing and a response to complaints about him, rather than a formal appointment.

The event is recorded in both Shia and Sunni sources, including collections of hadith, although the interpretations and the emphasis placed upon it differ considerably.
