Botanical garden

A botanical garden or botanic garden is a garden dedicated to the collection, cultivation, preservation and display of a wide range of plants labelled with their botanical names. It may contain specialist collections such as cacti and other succulent plants, herb gardens, and plants from particular parts of the world, and may maintain greenhouses and shadehouses with tropical and alpine collections. Modern botanical gardens are usually run by universities or other scientific research organisations and are associated with herbaria and research programmes in plant taxonomy and systematics.

Functions

The functions of a botanical garden include documentation of plant diversity, scientific research, conservation of rare and endangered species, seed banking, education, and public display. Visitor services commonly include tours, educational workshops and exhibitions. Many serve as centres for ex situ conservation, cultivating threatened plants outside their natural habitats and supplying material for reintroduction.

History

The origins of modern botanical gardens lie in the physic gardens of Renaissance Italy, founded to grow medicinal herbs for university teaching: the Orto botanico di Pisa (1544) and the Orto botanico di Padova (1545), the latter still in its original location and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Gardens flourished with European exploration, as institutions such as the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (founded 1759) became hubs for the collection and economic exploitation of plants from around the world. Today there are over 1,700 botanical gardens worldwide, coordinated in part by Botanic Gardens Conservation International.
