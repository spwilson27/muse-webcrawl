Beijing South railway station is a large railway station in Fengtai District, Beijing, China. Opened on 1 August 2008 in time for the Beijing Olympics, it is the city's principal terminal for high-speed trains and one of the largest railway stations in Asia. It stands on the site of the former Yongdingmen station.

Design

The station was designed by the British firm TFP Farrells with the China Railway design institutes. Its most prominent feature is a vast elliptical roof — roughly 500 metres long — whose central skylight recalls the roof forms of traditional Chinese temples. Beneath the concourse, 24 platforms serve 13 tracks on the departure level, with arrivals, taxi ranks, and two subway stations (Lines 4 and 14 of the Beijing Subway) stacked on underground levels. At its opening it was the largest high-speed rail station in the world.

Services

Beijing South is the northern terminus of the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway, opened in 2011, which covers the 1,318 km (819 mi) to Shanghai in as little as about four and a half hours; of the Beijing–Tianjin intercity railway, the first 350 km/h service in China; and of high-speed services toward Harbin, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong via connections. The station handles hundreds of trains and large daily passenger volumes, peaking during the Spring Festival travel rush.
