American Association of Variable Star Observers

The American Association of Variable Star Observers (AAVSO) is a non-profit organization founded in 1911 to coordinate variable star observations made mainly by amateur astronomers. It is the world's largest association of variable star observers, and its International Database holds tens of millions of brightness estimates contributed over more than a century.

History

The AAVSO grew out of the variable star section work encouraged by Harvard College Observatory director Edward Charles Pickering, and was organised by William Tyler Olcott. Long headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, near Harvard, the association developed standardised charts, comparison-star sequences, and reporting methods that allowed amateurs to produce scientifically useful data. Leon Campbell served as its Recorder for many years, and Margaret Mayall directed it from 1949 to 1973.

Activities

Members observe variable stars visually and with CCD and DSLR photometry, monitoring eruptive, pulsating, and eclipsing variables. AAVSO data are used by professional astronomers to schedule satellite observations, follow supernovae and cataclysmic variables, and study long-term stellar behaviour. The association publishes the Journal of the AAVSO, issues observing alerts through its alert and special notices, and runs educational programs such as Citizen Sky.
