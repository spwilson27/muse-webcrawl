Car rental, also called car hire, is a service in which customers rent automobiles for short periods, generally ranging from a few hours to a few weeks, in return for a fee. Rental companies operate fleets of vehicles from offices in cities, airports and resorts, and renters typically must hold a driving licence, meet a minimum age (often 21–25), and provide a credit or debit card.

History

The industry began in the United States in the 1910s; Joe Saunders of Omaha, Nebraska, is often credited with renting out a Model T Ford in 1916. Walter L. Jacobs founded Rent-a-Car in Chicago in 1918, and John Hertz acquired it in 1923. After the Second World War, rentals became closely tied to air travel, with counters established at major airports; National Car Rental was founded in 1947 and Enterprise Rent-A-Car in 1957, the latter building its business on neighbourhood locations and insurance-replacement rentals.

Business model

Rates are usually calculated by time and mileage, with optional extras such as collision damage waiver, supplemental liability insurance, satellite navigation and child seats. Vehicles are classed by size (economy, compact, intermediate, full-size, premium, SUV and so on) and are typically sold out of the fleet into the used-car market after a period in service. Dynamic pricing adjusts rates to demand, and one-way rentals, monthly leases and peer-to-peer carsharing platforms such as Turo extend the basic model.
