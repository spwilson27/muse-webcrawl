Box office

Box office
Meaning 1Place where event tickets are sold
Meaning 2Ticket-sale revenue of a film or show
MeasuresGross receipts; admissions

A box office is a place where tickets are sold to the public for admission to an event. By extension, the term is used in the film and theatre industries for the commercial performance of a production — the total ticket revenue ("box office gross") a film or play earns, and phrases such as "box office hit" or "box office bomb".

Etymology

The term derives from the practice in Elizabethan theatres of collecting admission money in a box, and from the private boxes in theatres whose occupants paid separately. In the 18th and 19th centuries the box office became the office at a theatre where box seats and other tickets were sold.

Film box office

For films, box office reporting distinguishes the domestic market (the United States and Canada, in industry usage) from international territories; their sum is the worldwide gross. Gross receipts are shared between exhibitors and distributors under rental agreements that typically favour the distributor in opening weeks. Key measures include the opening weekend, per-theatre average, and drop-off in subsequent weeks ("legs"). Because grosses are not adjusted for ticket-price inflation, comparisons across eras also use estimated admissions.

The highest-grossing films worldwide include Avatar (2009), Avengers: Endgame (2019) and Titanic (1997), each earning well over two billion US dollars. Weekend box office charts, published by trade papers such as Variety since the 1920s, remain a staple of entertainment journalism.
