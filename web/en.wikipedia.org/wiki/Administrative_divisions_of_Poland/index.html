Administrative divisions of Poland

The administrative divisions of Poland since 1999 have been based on three levels of subdivision. The territory of Poland is divided into voivodeships (provinces); these are further divided into powiats (counties), and these in turn are divided into gminas (communes or municipalities). Major cities normally have the status of both gmina and powiat.

Voivodeships

Poland has 16 voivodeships, including Masovian (containing Warsaw), Lesser Poland (Kraków), Silesian, Greater Poland (Poznań), Lower Silesian (Wrocław), and Pomeranian (Gdańsk). Each voivodeship has a dual administration: a voivode appointed by the central government and an elected regional assembly (sejmik) with its own marshal and executive board.

Lower levels

There are 314 land powiats and 66 city powiats (cities with powiat rights). Below them are roughly 2,477 gminas, classified as urban, rural, or urban-rural, which provide most local public services. The current three-tier system took effect on 1 January 1999, replacing the 49 smaller voivodeships of the 1975–1998 system.
