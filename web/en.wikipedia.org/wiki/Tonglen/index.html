Tonglen

TypeMeditation practice
TraditionTibetan Buddhism
Meaning"Giving and taking" (sending and receiving)

Tonglen (Tibetan: གཏོང་ལེན་, "giving and taking" or "sending and receiving") is a meditation practice in Tibetan Buddhism for cultivating compassion. The practitioner visualises taking in the suffering of others on the in-breath and sending out happiness, relief and well-being to them on the out-breath.

Practice

Tonglen is one of the central practices of lojong (mind training), the slogans-based training associated with the eleventh-century teacher Chekawa Yeshe Dorje's Seven Points of Mind Training, itself rooted in the teachings of Atiśa. In a typical session, the meditator first rests in open awareness, then visualises a person or group who is suffering, breathes in their pain in the form of dark, heavy smoke, and breathes out cool, bright light symbolising whatever would relieve them. The practice is then extended to all beings. It deliberately reverses the habitual pattern of avoiding pain and grasping at pleasure, and is used to develop bodhicitta, the resolve to awaken for the benefit of others.

Modern use

Tonglen has been widely taught in the West by teachers such as Pema Chödrön and has been adapted for use in hospice care and self-compassion training.
