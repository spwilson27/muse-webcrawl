Leak detection

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leak detection is the process of identifying and locating leaks in pressurized systems, pipelines, vessels, and containers that carry liquids or gases. Leak detection is used in industries including water supply, oil and gas, chemical processing, refrigeration, and vacuum technology, both to prevent loss of product and to protect safety and the environment.

Methods

Methods of leak detection include:

  • Acoustic leak detection – listening devices, correlators, and geophones detect the sound of fluid escaping under pressure; widely used on buried water mains.
  • Pressure and flow monitoring – computational pipeline monitoring compares measured pressure, flow, and volume balance against a model to infer leaks.
  • Tracer gas methods – systems are pressurized with helium or hydrogen and a sensitive detector (such as a helium mass spectrometer leak detector) sniffs for escaping gas; the standard method in vacuum and refrigeration work.
  • Bubble testing – a soap solution applied to joints foams at the site of a gas leak.
  • Infrared thermography and ground-penetrating radar – used to find moisture or voids caused by leaks.
  • Smart water meters and district metering – minimum night-flow analysis identifies leakage in distribution networks.

Applications

Water utilities lose a substantial fraction of treated water to leakage; non-revenue water commonly exceeds 20–30% in many systems, making leak detection economically important. Pipeline regulations in many jurisdictions require operators of hazardous liquid and natural gas pipelines to run leak detection programs, and undetected pipeline leaks have contributed to major environmental disasters.
