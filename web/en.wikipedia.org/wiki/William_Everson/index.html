William Everson

William Everson (September 10, 1912 – June 3, 1994), also known as Brother Antoninus, was an American poet, typesetter, and literary critic associated with the San Francisco Renaissance.

Born: September 10, 1912, Sacramento, California
Died: June 3, 1994, Santa Cruz, California
Occupation: Poet, printer, critic
Movement: San Francisco Renaissance

Life

Born in Sacramento and raised in Fresno, Everson attended Fresno State College. A conscientious objector during World War II, he worked in Civilian Public Service camps in Oregon, where he began printing poetry. In 1949 he joined the Dominican Order as Brother Antoninus, a period that produced his most intense religious poetry; he left the order in 1969.

Poetry and printing

Everson was a master printer at the University of California, Santa Cruz, where he ran the Lime Kiln Press. His work explored nature, eros, and Catholic mysticism. Collections include These Are the Ravens (1935), The Residual Years (1968), and Man-Fate: The Swan Song of Brother Antoninus (1974).

Legacy

He influenced West Coast poetry alongside Kenneth Rexroth and Robinson Jeffers. See also Beat Generation and San Francisco Renaissance.
