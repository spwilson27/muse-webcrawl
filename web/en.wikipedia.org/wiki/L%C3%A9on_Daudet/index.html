Léon Daudet (16 November 1867 – 2 July 1942) was a French journalist, writer and politician, a leading figure of the monarchist movement Action Française alongside Charles Maurras. The son of the novelist Alphonse Daudet, he became one of the most vitriolic polemicists of the French far right.

Early life

Daudet grew up in a literary household frequented by writers such as Émile Zola and Edmond de Goncourt. He studied medicine briefly before turning to journalism and fiction, publishing novels and memoirs including Fantômes et vivants.

Action Française

An anti-Dreyfusard during the Dreyfus Affair, Daudet co-founded the newspaper L'Action française in 1908 and became its editor. He sat in the Chamber of Deputies from 1919 to 1924. His campaigns mixed monarchism, nationalism and attacks on the Republic, Jews, Freemasons and communists.

Later years

The death of his son Philippe in 1923, which Daudet insisted was a police murder, consumed his later career. Convicted of libel, he fled to Belgium in 1927 before being pardoned. He supported the Vichy regime of Marshal Pétain and died at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence in 1942.
