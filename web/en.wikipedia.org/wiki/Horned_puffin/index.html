Horned puffin

Horned puffin
Scientific nameFratercula corniculata
FamilyAlcidae
ConservationLeast Concern (IUCN)

The horned puffin (Fratercula corniculata) is an auk of the North Pacific, closely resembling the Atlantic puffin. It is named for the fleshy black "horn" that projects above the eye of breeding adults. Adults have a white face with a dark horn, a largely white belly, black upperparts, and a deep, laterally compressed bill that becomes bright yellow with a reddish tip in the breeding season.

Distribution and habitat

Horned puffins breed in colonies on rocky islands and sea cliffs along the coasts of Alaska, British Columbia, and north-eastern Siberia, including the Aleutian Islands and the Gulf of Alaska. Outside the breeding season they range widely over the open North Pacific.

Behaviour

The species nests in burrows or rock crevices, laying a single egg. It forages by pursuit-diving, "flying" underwater with its wings to catch small fish such as sand lance and capelin, which it can carry crosswise in its bill in numbers at a time. Like other puffins, it is a strong but direct flier with rapid wingbeats.
