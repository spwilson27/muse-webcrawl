Verlan


Verlan is strongly associated with the youth culture of the French banlieues

Verlan (French pronunciation: [vɛʁlɑ̃]) is a form of French slang involving the inversion of syllables in a word. The name itself is an example: verlan is the inverted form of l'envers ("the reverse"). Verlan emerged in working-class and immigrant communities in the suburbs (banlieues) of Paris in the mid-twentieth century and became a hallmark of French youth language, spreading into mainstream speech, advertising, and popular music.

Formation

Verlan typically swaps the syllables of a disyllabic word, often with vowel changes or truncation:

l'enversverlan"the reverse"
bizarrezarbibizarre, weird
femmemeufwoman
flickeufpoliceman
arabebeur → rebeuArab (Maghrebi French)
fêteteufparty
lourdrelouheavy; annoying
énervévénèreangry

Some words have been verlanised twice: beur (from arabe) became so mainstream that it was itself inverted to rebeu. Monosyllabic words are usually given an artificial second syllable before inversion.

History and social role

Syllable-inversion argots existed in French for centuries (such as largonji and louchébem), but verlan in its modern form is associated with post-war suburban youth, particularly the children of North and West African immigrants. It functioned both as a playful code opaque to authority and as a badge of identity. From the 1980s, French hip-hop and films such as La Haine (1995) carried verlan nationwide; a number of words (e.g., meuf, relou) have entered general informal French.

Equivalent forms

Comparable inversion slangs include English back slang and Pig Latin, Spanish vesre, Serbian šatra, and the Indonesian prokem's syllable swaps.
