The Better Business Bureau (BBB), founded in 1912, is a private, nonprofit organisation whose stated mission is to focus on advancing marketplace trust. It is not affiliated with any governmental agency. Businesses that affiliate with the BBB and adhere to its standards do so through industry self-regulation.

History

The BBB grew out of the "vigilance committees" formed by advertising clubs in the early 20th century to combat fraudulent advertising. The first local bureaus were organised in 1912 under a national body that eventually became the Council of Better Business Bureaus, headquartered in Arlington, Virginia. In 2020 the organisation was restructured as the International Association of Better Business Bureaus.

Operations

Local BBBs collect and provide free business reviews, process and attempt to mediate consumer complaints, and offer dispute-resolution services. The BBB assigns letter-grade ratings (A+ through F) to businesses based on factors including complaint history and how complaints were resolved. Businesses may apply for BBB accreditation, which requires meeting standards of honesty in advertising and responsiveness to complaints, and permits them to display the BBB torch seal.

Criticism

The organisation has been criticised for its pay-for-accreditation model; a 2010 investigative report alleged that some local branches gave higher ratings to businesses that paid accreditation fees, prompting reforms including the removal of a Los Angeles-area chapter. Consumer advocates note that BBB ratings reflect complaint handling rather than product quality.
