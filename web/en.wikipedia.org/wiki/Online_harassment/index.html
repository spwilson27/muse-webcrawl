Online harassment

Related conceptsCyberbullying, cyberstalking, doxing, swatting
Common venuesSocial media, forums, messaging, gaming
Key driversAnonymity, online disinhibition effect

Online harassment is the use of digital communication to bully, threaten, stalk, or otherwise target a person or group. It ranges from insulting messages and coordinated abuse campaigns ("brigading") to doxing (publishing private information), swatting (false emergency reports), non-consensual image sharing, and threats of violence. When directed at minors it overlaps with cyberbullying; persistent pursuit of an individual overlaps with cyberstalking.

Prevalence

Surveys by the Pew Research Center have repeatedly found that large minorities of internet users — around four in ten American adults in the 2021 survey — have personally experienced some form of online harassment, with younger adults, women, and public figures reporting higher rates of severe forms such as sexual harassment, stalking, and sustained campaigns. Journalists, politicians, activists, and women in gaming and technology have been prominent targets of organised harassment campaigns.

Forms

  • Flaming and trolling — inflammatory or provocative abuse.
  • Doxing — releasing addresses, phone numbers, or other private data to invite offline harm.
  • Revenge porn / image-based abuse — distributing intimate images without consent.
  • Swatting — hoax calls sending armed police to a victim's home.
  • Dogpiling — mass coordinated abuse overwhelming a target.
  • Impersonation and hacked accounts.

Causes

Researchers attribute online harassment in part to the online disinhibition effect: anonymity, invisibility, and asynchronicity reduce empathy and restraint. Platform architectures that reward engagement can amplify abusive content, while jurisdictional gaps make enforcement difficult.

Responses and law

Responses include platform moderation (blocking, reporting, content removal), anti-harassment legislation — many jurisdictions have enacted laws against cyberstalking, doxing, threats, and non-consensual imagery — and support organisations that help targets secure their accounts and document abuse. Debates continue over balancing enforcement against free-expression rights, and over the responsibility of platforms versus individual perpetrators.
