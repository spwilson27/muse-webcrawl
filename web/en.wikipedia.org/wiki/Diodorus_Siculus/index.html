Diodorus Siculus was a Greek historian from Agyrium in Sicily who lived in the first century BC. He is best known for the Bibliotheca historica (Historical Library), a monumental universal history in forty books, of which fifteen survive complete and others in fragments.

The Bibliotheca historica

Diodorus wrote that he spent thirty years on the work, travelling widely in Europe and Asia to visit the places he described. The history ran from mythical times down to the beginning of Julius Caesar's Gallic Wars (60/59 BC). Books 1–5 treat the geography and legendary history of Egypt, Asia, Ethiopia, Greece and the islands; books 11–20, which survive intact, cover Greek and Roman history from the Persian Wars to the successors of Alexander the Great.

Value as a source

Although Diodorus was a compiler rather than an original researcher, and his work is uneven, the Bibliotheca preserves large amounts of material from earlier historians whose works are otherwise lost, including Ephorus, Hieronymus of Cardia and Polybius. For some periods — such as the history of Sicily, the Diadochi, and Agathocles — he is a principal surviving source.
