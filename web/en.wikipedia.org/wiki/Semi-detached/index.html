Semi-detached

Semi-detached housing consists of pairs of houses built side by side as units sharing a party wall. Usually each house's layout is a mirror image of its twin. Semi-detached homes are a dominant suburban form in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Canada and Australia, where they are colloquially known as "semis" or, in duplex-style arrangements, by local terms.

History

The form emerged in 19th-century Britain as a compromise between the terraced house and the detached villa, offering working- and middle-class families a garden and greater privacy at lower cost than a fully detached house. The garden city movement and interwar suburban expansion made the semi the archetypal British suburban home; large estates of semis were built by speculative developers and local authorities in the 1920s and 1930s.

Characteristics

A semi-detached pair shares one common wall, so each dwelling has three exposed sides, windows on three elevations, and usually front and rear gardens and side access. Cost, land use and density fall between terraced and detached housing.
