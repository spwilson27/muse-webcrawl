Capital good
TypeProduced durable good
Used forProducing other goods and services
ExamplesMachinery, factories, tools, vehicles, computers
Factory machinery — a classic capital good

A capital good is a durable good that is used in the production of other goods or services. Capital goods are one of the three types of producer goods, the others being intermediate goods and raw materials. They are also known as intermediate goods in some classifications, or as "capital" in the economic sense of physical capital.

Businesses use capital goods — factory equipment, machine tools, buildings, computers, vehicles — to make products and provide services. Unlike consumer goods, capital goods are not purchased for final consumption but to generate future output, and they typically depreciate over their working lives. Investment in capital goods is a key driver of productivity growth and is measured in national accounts as gross fixed capital formation.

Distinctions

The same item can be a capital good or a consumer good depending on its use: a car bought by a taxi company is a capital good, while the same car bought by a family is a consumer good. Capital goods differ from financial capital, which refers to funds used to purchase them.
