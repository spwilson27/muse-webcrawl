Tribal Historic Preservation Office

A Tribal Historic Preservation Office (THPO) is an office established by a federally recognized Native American tribe in the United States to carry out historic preservation functions on tribal lands. THPOs were authorized by the 1992 amendments to the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA).

Functions

Where a tribe has an approved THPO, its Tribal Historic Preservation Officer assumes some or all of the duties of the State Historic Preservation Officer on tribal land, under an agreement with the National Park Service. These duties include maintaining inventories of cultural resources, nominating properties to the National Register of Historic Places, reviewing federal undertakings under Section 106 of the NHPA, and protecting archaeological sites, burials, sacred places, and traditional cultural properties.

Growth

The first THPO agreements were signed in the mid-1990s; since then well over a hundred tribes have established offices, supported in part by Historic Preservation Fund grants administered by the National Park Service. THPOs also play a role in consultations under laws such as the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act.
