Amenhotep I (Ancient Egyptian: ı͗mn-ḥtp, "Amun is satisfied"), also called Amenophis I, was the second pharaoh of the 18th Dynasty of Egypt, ruling from roughly 1526 to 1506 BC. He was the son of Ahmose I and Ahmose-Nefertari, and inherited a kingdom freshly reunified after the expulsion of the Hyksos.

Reign

Amenhotep I consolidated Egypt's borders with campaigns in Nubia and possibly Libya, and his reign is generally seen as a period of peace and internal development. He is credited with administrative and religious reforms, and may have founded the workers' village at Deir el-Medina, whose inhabitants built the royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings. His Horus name, "Bull who conquers the lands", reflects his military reputation in Nubia, where he campaigned against the Kushites and established administration as far south as the Third Cataract region.

Death and deification

Amenhotep I died around 1506 BC and was succeeded by Thutmose I, apparently not a direct descendant. Unusually among pharaohs, Amenhotep I was deified after death and worshipped as a patron god, together with his mother Ahmose-Nefertari, especially by the workmen of Deir el-Medina. His mummy, found in the Deir el-Bahari cache (DB320), remained wrapped and was examined by CT scanning in 2021, which revealed he died around age 35.
