Conwy Suspension Bridge

The Conwy Suspension Bridge is a Grade I listed bridge spanning the River Conwy at Conwy, north Wales. Designed by Thomas Telford and completed in 1826, it formed part of the A5 London-to-Holyhead road improvement scheme and replaced the ferry that had previously crossed the estuary below Conwy Castle.

Design and construction

The bridge has a main span of 99.7 m (327 ft). Its cast-iron towers were deliberately styled as miniature castle turrets, complete with crenellations, so that the bridge would harmonize with the adjacent medieval castle. The deck was originally suspended from chains of wrought-iron eyebars; additional steel cables were added in 1903 to strengthen the structure. Telford simultaneously built the similar suspension bridge across the Menai Strait, and the two projects shared engineering techniques.

Later history

The bridge carried the A55 North Wales Coast road until a new road bridge opened alongside it in 1958, after which it was restricted to pedestrians. It is now in the care of the National Trust, and a restored toll-keeper's house stands at its eastern end. Together with Stephenson's Conwy Railway Bridge of 1849, the three crossings at Conwy illustrate three generations of British bridge engineering.
