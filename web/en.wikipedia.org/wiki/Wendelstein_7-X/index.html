The Wendelstein 7-X (W7-X) reactor is an experimental stellarator built in Greifswald, Germany, by the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics (IPP). Completed in October 2015, it is the world's largest stellarator and is designed to investigate the suitability of this type of device for a future fusion power plant.

Design

Unlike a tokamak, a stellarator confines plasma in a twisted magnetic field generated entirely by external coils, requiring no plasma current. W7-X uses 50 non-planar and 20 planar superconducting coils to produce a highly optimised three-dimensional magnetic field, computed to minimise particle losses. The machine aims to demonstrate plasma discharges lasting up to 30 minutes — a key advantage of the stellarator concept for continuous operation.

Operation

First helium plasma was produced in December 2015, followed by hydrogen plasma in February 2016. Subsequent campaigns set stellarator records for plasma temperature, density and confinement; in 2023 the device sustained a discharge of eight minutes with an energy turnover of 1.3 gigajoules. Upgrades including a water-cooled divertor have progressively extended its performance toward long-pulse, high-power operation.
