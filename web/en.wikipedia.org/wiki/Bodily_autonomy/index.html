Bodily autonomy

[Image placeholder: Bodily autonomy]

Bodily autonomy is the concept that individuals have the right to make decisions about their own bodies and physical integrity without coercion or external control. It is closely related to the notions of bodily integrity and personal liberty, and is invoked in debates over medicine, reproduction, sexuality and end-of-life care.

Applications

In medical ethics, bodily autonomy underlies the doctrine of informed consent: competent patients may refuse treatment even when refusal risks death. The principle is also central to arguments concerning abortion rights, contraception, organ donation, vaccination mandates and the rights of intersex and transgender people.

Legal recognition

Bodily autonomy is reflected in various international human rights instruments, including provisions of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, and in constitutional protections against unreasonable search and seizure and involuntary servitude in the United States. Its precise scope remains contested between proponents of broad individual liberty and those who argue for balancing it against public health and the rights of others.
