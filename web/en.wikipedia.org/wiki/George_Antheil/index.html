George Antheil (8 July 1900 – 12 February 1959) was an American avant-garde composer, pianist, author, and inventor whose modernist works and self-promotion earned him the nickname "the bad boy of music".

Career

Antheil toured Europe as a pianist in the 1920s and settled in Paris, where he associated with figures such as James Joyce, Ezra Pound, and Erik Satie. His most famous work, the Ballet Mécanique (1924), was scored for player pianos, percussion, electric bells, and aeroplane propellers; its 1926 Paris premiere caused a sensation. He later moved to Hollywood, where he composed scores for films including The Plainsman and In a Lonely Place.

Invention

With actress Hedy Lamarr, Antheil co-invented a frequency-hopping spread-spectrum system for guiding torpedoes, patented in 1942. The technology later became a foundation of wireless communication and was recognised by their induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
