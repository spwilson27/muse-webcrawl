Cinematography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A cinematographer behind a large film camera on a dolly track, silhouetted against set lights]
A cinematographer behind a large film camera on a dolly track, silhouetted against set lights

Cinematography is the art of motion-picture photography — the craft of the cinematographer (director of photography), who with the director determines a film's visual style through camera placement and movement, lens choice, lighting, framing and the photographic medium itself. The word derives from the Greek kinema (movement) and graphein (to write): writing in movement.

Craft

The cinematographer leads the camera, lighting and grip departments, translating the director's intentions into images: selecting aspect ratio and lenses (anamorphic versus spherical), designing lighting (from classical three-point to available-light naturalism), choosing film stocks or digital sensors and colour pipelines, and operating or supervising camera movement — dollies, Steadicam, cranes, drones and handheld. Pre-visualisation, camera tests and on-set colour grading (the 'look') are increasingly digital-era duties.

History and masters

Early cameramen became artists as film matured: Billy Bitzer with Griffith, Karl Freund in German expressionism, Gregg Toland's deep-focus work on Citizen Kane (1941). Post-war colour and widescreen, the French New Wave's lightweight location shooting (Raoul Coutard), and masters such as Vittorio Storaro, Gordon Willis ('the Prince of Darkness', The Godfather), Roger Deakins and Emmanuel Lubezki (long-take virtuosity in Birdman and The Revenant) mark the craft's eras. The American Society of Cinematographers (founded 1919) is its leading professional body.

Digital transition

Since the 2000s digital capture has overtaken 35mm film for most production — Deakins's work on Skyfall (2012) was a watershed — while LED volumes and virtual production re-stage 'location' lighting in the studio. Debates persist over the look of film grain versus digital cleanliness and over virtual cinematography in fully animated/VFX films.
