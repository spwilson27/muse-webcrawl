The CAF Champions League, known for sponsorship purposes as the TotalEnergies CAF Champions League, is an annual club football competition organised by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and contested by the top-division clubs of African football associations. It is the most prestigious club competition in African football, the continental equivalent of the UEFA Champions League.

History

The competition began in 1964 as the African Cup of Champions Clubs, with Cameroon's Oryx Douala the first winners. For its first three decades it was a straight knockout tournament for league champions only. In 1997 it was relaunched as the CAF Champions League with a group stage and substantially increased prize money, and entry was later expanded to include runners-up from the strongest leagues. The final was traditionally played over two legs, with a single-match final used in some recent editions before a return to the two-legged format.

Format

Clubs enter through preliminary and first qualifying rounds played on a home-and-away basis. Sixteen surviving teams are drawn into four groups of four, playing a round-robin; the top two in each group advance to the quarter-finals, followed by semi-finals and the final. The champion qualifies for the FIFA Intercontinental Cup (formerly the Club World Cup) and plays the CAF Confederation Cup winner in the CAF Super Cup.

Records

Egypt's Al Ahly is by far the most successful club, with a record twelve titles, followed by Zamalek and TP Mazembe with five each. Egyptian clubs have dominated the competition, with North African teams from Morocco, Tunisia, and Algeria also winning multiple titles.
