Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation

The Foundation's headquarters and gardens in Lisbon.

The Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation (Portuguese: Fundação Calouste Gulbenkian) is a Portuguese institution founded in 1956 in accordance with the will of the Armenian oil magnate Calouste Gulbenkian, who had lived in Lisbon from 1942 until his death in 1955. One of the wealthiest charitable foundations in Europe, it promotes the arts, philanthropy, science and education.

Headquarters and facilities

The Foundation's headquarters in Lisbon, inaugurated in 1969, comprise an administrative complex, auditoriums, and the Calouste Gulbenkian Museum, which houses the founder's private art collection — spanning Egyptian antiquities, Islamic art, Chinese porcelain and European painting, including works by Rembrandt, Monet and Renoir. The surrounding gardens are a notable green space in the city. A second museum, the Centro de Arte Moderna (CAM), opened in 1983 and is devoted to modern and contemporary art.

Activities

The Foundation maintains the Gulbenkian Orchestra and Choir, supports libraries across Portugal, funds research (including through the Instituto Gulbenkian de Ciência, founded in 1961), and runs programmes in social cohesion, sustainability, and support for Armenian communities worldwide. It has delegations in Paris and London, the latter long known for its support of Portuguese studies and community projects in the United Kingdom.
