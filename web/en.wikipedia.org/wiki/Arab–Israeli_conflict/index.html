Arab–Israeli conflict

The Arab–Israeli conflict is the phenomenon involving political tension, military conflicts, and other disputes between various Arab countries and Israel, which escalated during the 20th century, but had mostly faded by the early 21st century.

Arab–Israeli conflict
Date1948 – present (main phase 1948–1982)
LocationMiddle East
PartiesIsrael; Egypt, Syria, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon and others at various times

Background

The roots lie in Zionism and Arab nationalism in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, competing claims to Palestine under Ottoman and then British Mandate rule, the 1917 Balfour Declaration, and the 1947 UN Partition Plan.

Major wars

Wars include the 1948 Arab–Israeli War following Israel's declaration of independence, the Suez Crisis (1956), the Six-Day War (1967) in which Israel captured the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Sinai and Golan Heights, the War of Attrition, the Yom Kippur War (1973), and the 1982 Lebanon War.

Peace process

Egypt signed a peace treaty with Israel in 1979 after the Camp David Accords, and Jordan in 1994. The Oslo Accords (1993–95) addressed the Israeli–Palestinian conflict. The Abraham Accords (2020) normalised relations with the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco and Sudan.
