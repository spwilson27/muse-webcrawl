Country: Russia

Volgograd (Russian: Волгоград) is a city in southwestern Russia, situated on the western bank of the Volga River. It is the administrative centre of Volgograd Oblast. The city was founded in 1589 as the fortress of Tsaritsyn, renamed Stalingrad in 1925, and given its present name in 1961. Its population is about one million.

History

Tsaritsyn guarded the Volga trade route at the portage to the Don River. During the Russian Civil War it was fiercely contested; Joseph Stalin participated in its defence, and the city was renamed Stalingrad in his honour in 1925.

Battle of Stalingrad

The city is best known for the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–1943), one of the largest and bloodiest battles in history and a turning point of the Second World War. The German Sixth Army was encircled and destroyed; the city itself was reduced to ruins. The battle is commemorated by the vast memorial complex on Mamayev Kurgan, crowned by the 85-metre statue The Motherland Calls, one of the tallest statues in the world.

Renaming

As part of de-Stalinization, the city was renamed Volgograd ("Volga city") in 1961. Proposals to restore the name Stalingrad periodically surface; since 2013 the name "Stalingrad" is used ceremonially on certain patriotic holidays.

Geography and economy

Volgograd stretches some 60 km along the Volga and is one of the longest cities in Russia. It is an industrial centre with metallurgy, machine building and chemicals, and a river port. The Volga–Don Canal, opened in 1952, begins near the city. Volgograd was a host city of the 2018 FIFA World Cup.
