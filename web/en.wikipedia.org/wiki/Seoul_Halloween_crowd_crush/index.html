Seoul Halloween crowd crush

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

On 29 October 2022, during Halloween festivities in the Itaewon neighbourhood of Seoul, South Korea, a crowd crush occurred in a narrow, sloped alleyway near the Hamilton Hotel. The disaster killed 159 people and injured 196 others, making it one of the deadliest crowd crushes in modern history. Most victims were young adults in their twenties and thirties, and 27 of the dead were foreign nationals.

Background

Itaewon is a popular nightlife district. The 2022 celebrations were the first Halloween since the lifting of most COVID-19 restrictions, and an estimated 100,000 people gathered in the area. The event had no single organiser, and police presence was limited, with crowd-control resources largely directed elsewhere in the city.

The crush

From the evening onwards the alleys became dangerously congested. Emergency calls reporting crushing began hours before the fatal event. At about 22:15 local time, people in the narrow alley — about 3.2 metres wide — began to fall on the slope, and others piled on top in a progressive crowd collapse. Many victims died of compressive asphyxia while standing. Rescuers were hampered by the dense crowds, and bystanders and emergency workers performed CPR on dozens of people in the streets.

Aftermath

President Yoon Suk Yeol declared a period of national mourning until 5 November. Investigations criticised the inadequate police planning and response; several officials, including the Yongsan police chief, were prosecuted, and in 2024 the Yongsan district chief and a senior police officer received prison sentences for negligent handling of the disaster. The tragedy renewed scrutiny of crowd safety at unorganised mass events worldwide.
