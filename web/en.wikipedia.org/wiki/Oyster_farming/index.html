Oyster farming

Oyster farming is the aquaculture practice of breeding and harvesting oysters for food, pearls, and shells. It has been practised since Roman times and today is a major coastal industry in France, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, and the United States.

Methods

Farmers collect oyster spat (larvae) on cultch such as old shells, then grow oysters on the seabed, in trays, in suspended bags and cages, or on longlines and rafts. Suspended culture keeps oysters off muddy bottoms and away from some predators.

Species and environment

Commonly farmed species include the Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas), the Eastern oyster, the Sydney rock oyster, and the European flat oyster. Oysters filter water as they feed, so farms can improve local water quality, though disease, pollution, and harmful algal blooms are persistent risks.
