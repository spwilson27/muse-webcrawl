Forth Road Bridge

The Forth Road Bridge is a suspension bridge in east central Scotland. The bridge opened in 1964 and at the time was the longest suspension bridge span outside the United States and the fourth-longest span in the world. It spans the Firth of Forth, connecting Edinburgh, at South Queensferry, to Fife, at North Queensferry. The bridge replaced a centuries-old ferry service (the Queen's Ferry, named after Saint Margaret of Scotland) and runs parallel to the famous Forth Bridge, the 1890 railway cantilever bridge about 900 metres to the east.

Since the opening of the Queensferry Crossing in 2017, the Forth Road Bridge has been dedicated to public transport, cyclists and pedestrians, forming part of a managed three-bridge crossing of the Forth.

History

Proposals for a road bridge across the Forth dated to the nineteenth century, but authorisation came only in 1947 and construction began in 1958 under a consortium of contractors, with Sir William Arrol & Co. prominent in the steelwork. Queen Elizabeth II opened the bridge on 4 September 1964. It carried the A9 (later A90/M90 corridor) between Edinburgh and the north-east of Scotland. Seven workers died during construction.

Corrosion and replacement

Traffic greatly exceeded design expectations, and inspections from 2003 onward found significant corrosion and loss of strength in the main suspension cables, despite acoustic monitoring and dehumidification systems installed to slow deterioration. The Scottish Government decided to build a replacement cable-stayed bridge, the Queensferry Crossing, which opened on 30 August 2017. The Forth Road Bridge then closed for refurbishment and reopened in 2018 as a dedicated public transport corridor, also carrying the footways and cycle paths across the estuary. In December 2015, a defect in a truss end link forced an emergency three-week closure of the bridge before the new crossing opened, causing major national disruption and accelerating scrutiny of its condition.

Listing

In 2017 the bridge was designated a Category A listed structure by Historic Environment Scotland, recognising it as a landmark of post-war engineering alongside its Victorian neighbour, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
