IRCAM

IRCAM, beside the Centre Pompidou in Paris.

IRCAM (French: Institut de recherche et coordination acoustique/musique, English: Institute for Research and Coordination in Acoustics/Music) is a French institute dedicated to the research of music and sound, especially in the fields of avant-garde and electro-acoustical art music. It is situated next to, and organisationally linked with, the Centre Pompidou in Paris. The institute was founded by composer and conductor Pierre Boulez and opened in 1977.

History

In 1970, French president Georges Pompidou commissioned Boulez to create an institute for research into contemporary music as part of the planned arts complex on the Plateau Beaubourg. Boulez directed IRCAM from its opening until 1992. Most of the institute is built underground, beneath the Stravinsky Fountain, to provide acoustic isolation; it includes the Espace de projection, a concert hall whose acoustic properties can be varied by movable panels.

Research and software

IRCAM has been a major centre for computer music research. Notable technologies developed there include:

  • Max, the graphical programming environment created by Miller Puckette in the 1980s, later developed commercially as Max/MSP;
  • OpenMusic, a visual programming language for composition;
  • Modalys and other physical modelling synthesis tools;
  • Research in sound analysis and synthesis, spatial audio, music information retrieval, and real-time interaction between performers and computers.

Works premiered or developed at IRCAM include Boulez's Répons and pieces by Tristan Murail, Kaija Saariaho, and other composers associated with spectral music and live electronics.

Activities

The institute hosts concerts, the ManiFeste festival and academy, doctoral research in association with French universities, and a technology transfer programme that licenses software to industry. Its research teams combine musicians, scientists, and engineers in a single organisation unusual in the arts world.
