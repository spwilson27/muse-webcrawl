Alpine skiing at the 1968 Winter Olympics

Alpine skiing at the 1968 Winter Olympics consisted of six events, held from 9 to 17 February at Chamrousse, France, during the Games of Grenoble. The events were the downhill, giant slalom, and slalom for both men and women. The Games are best remembered for the dominance of French star Jean-Claude Killy, who won all three men's events.

Men's events

In the men's downhill, Jean-Claude Killy of France edged compatriot Guy Périllat. Killy also won the giant slalom. The slalom produced drama: Austria's Karl Schranz was controversially disqualified after a re-run following a claim that a course worker had crossed his path, and victory passed to Killy, completing a clean sweep of the three gold medals and emulating Toni Sailer's 1956 feat.

Women's events

Canada's Nancy Greene won the giant slalom and took silver in the slalom behind France's Marielle Goitschel, who had won the giant slalom silver. The downhill gold went to Austria's Olga Pall. Greene's performances made her Canada's top female athlete of the era.

World championships

From 1948 to 1980 the Olympic alpine events doubled as the FIS Alpine World Ski Championships; combined titles (not Olympic events) were also awarded on the basis of results across the three races.
