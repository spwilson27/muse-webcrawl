Mercedes-Benz W25

The W25, first of the Silver Arrows
CategoryGrand Prix (750 kg formula)
ConstructorMercedes-Benz
Years raced1934–1936
EngineSupercharged straight-8, 3.4–4.7 L

The Mercedes-Benz W25 was a Grand Prix racing car designed by Mercedes-Benz for the 1934 season, in response to the new international 750 kg formula. The W25 was the first of the cars that became known as the "Silver Arrows," and it re-established Mercedes-Benz at the top of Grand Prix racing alongside its great rival Auto Union.

Design

Designed under Hans Nibel and Max Wagner, the W25 combined a lightweight chassis with a supercharged straight-eight engine. The initial 3.36-litre M25 engine produced about 354 horsepower; later versions grew to 4.7 litres and over 490 horsepower. The car featured independent suspension and hydraulic brakes, advanced features for its time, and a streamlined closed-wheel body was used at fast circuits such as Avus.

The Silver Arrow legend

According to team manager Alfred Neubauer's famous account, at scrutineering for the 1934 Eifelrennen the painted white car weighed slightly over the 750 kg limit, so the paint was stripped overnight, revealing the gleaming aluminium bodywork — the origin of the "Silver Arrow" name. Historians have questioned details of the story, but the silver livery became a permanent Mercedes trademark.

Racing record

Driven by Rudolf Caracciola, Luigi Fagioli, and Manfred von Brauchitsch, the W25 won major races including the 1935 European Championship for Caracciola, with victories at the French, Belgian, Swiss, and Spanish Grands Prix. The car was succeeded by the W125 in 1937 after rule changes.
