Berth (moorings)

A berth is a designated location in a port or harbour where a vessel can be moored, usually for the purposes of loading and unloading cargo or passengers. It may be provided by a wharf, a quay, a pier or a jetty, and the term is also applied to the space allotted to a vessel at anchor or in a marina.

Types

Berths are typically identified by number and by their intended use, such as container berths, bulk cargo berths, tanker berths and passenger berths. The characteristics of a berth — its length, the depth of water alongside, and the equipment available, such as cranes — determine the size and type of vessel it can accommodate.

Operations

Port authorities allocate berths to visiting ships, and a vessel's time at berth is a key measure of port efficiency. Fees known as berthage may be charged for the use of a berth. In marinas, a berth rented for a pleasure craft is often called a slip in North American usage.
