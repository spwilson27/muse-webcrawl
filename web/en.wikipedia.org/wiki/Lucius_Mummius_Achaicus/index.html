Lucius Mummius Achaicus

Lucius Mummius (2nd century BC), later surnamed Achaicus, was a Roman statesman and general who completed the conquest of Greece with the destruction of Corinth in 146 BC, the same year Carthage was destroyed.

Career

A novus homo (new man) from a plebeian family, Mummius served as praetor in Hispania Ulterior in 154 BC, where he campaigned successfully against the Lusitanians and earned a triumph. Elected consul for 146 BC, he was sent to Greece to take command in the Achaean War after the praetor Quintus Caecilius Metellus had defeated the Achaean League's forces.

Sack of Corinth

Mummius defeated the Achaean army under Diaeus at the Isthmus and entered Corinth unopposed. Acting on the Senate's orders, he razed the city: the men were killed, the women and children enslaved, and vast quantities of Greek art and treasure were shipped to Rome, where they filled his triumph and were dedicated in temples and public places. Greece was reorganised under Roman supervision, and Mummius received the honorific surname Achaicus.

Ancient authors mocked Mummius as uncultured, claiming he warned the contractors transporting Corinthian masterpieces that if they lost any they would have to replace them with new ones — yet he was also remembered as honest, leaving his family no richer than he found it. He later served as censor in 142 BC.
