Balmoral Castle


Balmoral Castle, Aberdeenshire.

Balmoral Castle is a large estate house in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, and a residence of the British royal family. It is near the village of Crathie, 14 kilometres west of Ballater and 80 kilometres west of Aberdeen. The estate was purchased for Queen Victoria in 1852 by her husband Prince Albert, and the present house was completed in 1856. Balmoral remains the private property of the monarch and is not part of the Crown Estate.

History

The Balmoral estate had earlier belonged to the Farquharson family, and a castle stood on the site from the fifteenth century. Victoria and Albert first visited the area in 1842 and leased Balmoral in 1848; finding the climate and scenery agreeable — Victoria called it "my dear paradise in the Highlands" — Albert bought the estate outright in 1852 for some 32,000 guineas.

The old house proving too small, the present castle was designed by the Aberdeen architect William Smith under Albert's close direction, in a Scots Baronial style executed in local granite. The foundation stone was laid in 1853 and the building was completed in 1856; the old castle was then demolished. The estate grew to roughly 50,000 acres (20,000 hectares), with some 150 buildings, working farms, grouse moors, and forestry.

Royal residence

Successive monarchs have used Balmoral as a summer residence. Queen Elizabeth II traditionally spent late summer and early autumn there. In September 1997, following the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, the royal family's initial seclusion at Balmoral and the absence of a flag at half-mast over Buckingham Palace became a focus of public controversy, depicted in the film The Queen (2006).

Queen Elizabeth II died at Balmoral on 8 September 2022, the first monarch to die there; her coffin lay in the castle ballroom before the journey to Edinburgh and London. King Charles III continues the tradition of summer residence at Balmoral.

The estate today

The gardens, ballroom, and grounds are open to the public seasonally when the royal family is not in residence. The estate includes the Birkhall house, home of Charles and Camilla before his accession, and Craigowan Lodge. Wildlife management, including deer stalking and conservation of capercaillie and Caledonian pinewood habitat, forms a major part of estate operations.
