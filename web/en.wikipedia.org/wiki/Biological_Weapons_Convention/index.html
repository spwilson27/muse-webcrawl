Biological Weapons Convention

The Biological Weapons Convention (BWC), or Biological and Toxin Weapons Convention (BTWC), is a disarmament treaty that effectively bans biological and toxin weapons by prohibiting their development, production, acquisition, transfer, stockpiling and use.

Biological Weapons Convention
Signed: 10 April 1972, London, Moscow, Washington
Effective: 26 March 1975
Parties: 189 states parties (approx.)
Depositaries: UK, Russia, US

Provisions

Signatories undertake never in any circumstances to develop, produce, stockpile or otherwise acquire or retain microbial or other biological agents or toxins of types and quantities unjustifiable for peaceful purposes, nor weapons or delivery means designed to use them.

History and implementation

The BWC built on the 1925 Geneva Protocol banning use. Unlike the Chemical Weapons Convention, it has no formal verification organisation; states hold review conferences roughly every five years and operate an Implementation Support Unit in Geneva. Attempts in the 1990s to negotiate a verification protocol failed.

Issues

Alleged historical violations, notably the Soviet Biopreparat programme and the 1979 Sverdlovsk anthrax leak, have fuelled debate about compliance and dual-use research.
