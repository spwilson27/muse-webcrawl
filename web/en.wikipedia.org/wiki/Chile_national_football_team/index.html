The Chile national football team (Spanish: Selección de fútbol de Chile) represents Chile in men's international football competitions and is controlled by the Football Federation of Chile, established in 1895. The team is nicknamed La Roja ("The Red One") for its red home shirt.

Chile has appeared in nine FIFA World Cup tournaments, most notably finishing third as hosts in 1962. After decades without a major trophy, Chile won its first Copa América title on home soil in 2015 and defended it at the Copa América Centenario in the United States in 2016, defeating Argentina on penalties in both finals.

History

Chile played its first international in 1910 against Argentina and was a founding participant in the South American Championship, forerunner of the Copa América. The team reached the semi-finals of the 1930 inaugural World Cup pools era competitions and enjoyed strong results in the 1950s and 1960s, culminating in third place at the 1962 World Cup at home, where Leonel Sánchez was among the tournament's top scorers.

Following a long decline that included suspension from the 1994 World Cup qualifiers after the Maracanazo incident of 1989, Chile revived under Marcelo Bielsa from 2007, qualifying for the 2010 World Cup with an attacking style. The "golden generation" — including Alexis Sánchez, Arturo Vidal, Claudio Bravo and Gary Medel — delivered the back-to-back Copa América titles under Jorge Sampaoli and Juan Antonio Pizzi, and a runners-up finish at the 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup.

Rivalries and stadium

Chile's principal rivals are Peru, in the Clásico del Pacífico, and Argentina. Home matches are usually played at the Estadio Nacional in Santiago, which hosted the 1962 World Cup final.
