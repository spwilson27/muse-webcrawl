Arnhem is a city and municipality in the eastern Netherlands, situated on both banks of the rivers Nederrijn and Sint-Jansbeek. It is the capital of the province of Gelderland and lies about 16 kilometres north of the German border. Arnhem had its origins in a settlement on higher ground away from the river and received city rights in 1233 from Count Otto II of Guelders.

History

The oldest archaeological findings near Arnhem date to the Stone Age. The city grew as a trading place and was repeatedly damaged by fires and sieges in the Middle Ages; it joined the Hanseatic League and was fortified with walls and towers, parts of which — such as the Sabelspoort gate — survive. In the 19th century Arnhem became a fashionable residential town for wealthy sugar and tobacco planters returning from the Dutch East Indies, earning it a lush, park-like character.

Battle of Arnhem

In September 1944 Arnhem was the objective of Operation Market Garden, the Allied attempt to seize bridges over the Rhine. British paratroops of the 1st Airborne Division held one end of the road bridge under Lieutenant-Colonel John Frost, but the operation failed and the city was devastated; the population was evacuated by the German occupiers. The bridge was later renamed the John Frost Bridge in his honour, and the story was popularised by the film A Bridge Too Far (1977).

Culture and landmarks

Arnhem is home to the Netherlands Open Air Museum (Openluchtmuseum), Burgers' Zoo, the Airborne Museum at Hartenstein in nearby Oosterbeek, and the Sonsbeek Park. The city is a centre for fashion and design, hosting the ArtEZ University of the Arts, and is noted for its extensive trolleybus network, one of the few remaining in Western Europe.
