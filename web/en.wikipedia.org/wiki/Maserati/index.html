Maserati

Maserati S.p.A. (Italian: [mazeˈraːti]) is an Italian luxury vehicle manufacturer founded in Bologna on 1 December 1914 by the Maserati brothers: Alfieri, Bindo, Carlo, Ettore, and Ernesto. The company's emblem, a trident, is based on the Fountain of Neptune in Bologna's Piazza Maggiore. Since 2021, Maserati has been part of the Stellantis group.

History

The Maserati brothers began in the automobile business in the early 20th century, initially building racing cars for Diatto before founding their own workshop. Their first car, the Tipo 26 of 1926, won its class at the Targa Florio. Maserati built a strong racing reputation through the 1930s–1950s: Wilbur Shaw won the Indianapolis 500 in a Maserati 8CTF in 1939 and 1940, and Juan Manuel Fangio won the 1957 Formula One World Championship in a Maserati 250F.

In 1937 the surviving brothers sold the company to the Orsi family, who moved it to Modena. Citroën acquired Maserati in 1968, a period that produced models such as the Bora and Khamsin, before Alejandro de Tomaso took control in 1975 with the backing of the Italian state. Fiat acquired Maserati in 1993; under Fiat and later Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, the marque was repositioned as a luxury grand-touring brand alongside, and later under, Ferrari's orbit.

Modern range

Modern Maserati models have included the Quattroporte luxury saloon, the Ghibli, the GranTurismo and GranCabrio grand tourers, the Levante SUV, and the MC20 mid-engined sports car, powered by the in-house "Nettuno" twin-turbo V6. The smaller Grecale SUV joined the range in 2022. Maserati has announced an electrification programme under the "Folgore" name, with battery-electric versions of its models, and returned to top-level racing in Formula E.
