Kiyoshi Shiga

Kiyoshi Shiga (February 7, 1871 - January 25, 1957) was a Japanese physician and bacteriologist. In 1897, during a severe dysentery epidemic in Japan, he isolated the bacterium causing bacillary dysentery, named Shigella dysenteriae after him, and the genus Shigella bears his name. He also identified Shiga toxin.

Shiga worked with Kitasato Shibasaburō at the Institute for Infectious Diseases in Tokyo, studied in Germany with Paul Ehrlich, developed a dysentery serum and vaccine attempts, and later served as president of Keijō Imperial University in Korea and dean in Tokyo. His work founded modern understanding of shigellosis.
