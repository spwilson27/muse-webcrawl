Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan

The Jōmon Prehistoric Sites in Northern Japan are a group of 17 archaeological sites in Hokkaido and northern Honshu (Aomori, Iwate, and Akita prefectures) that were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2021. They preserve evidence of the sedentary hunter-fisher-gatherer culture of the Jōmon period, which flourished for more than 10,000 years from about 13,000 BCE.

Component sites

The serial property includes the Sannai-Maruyama site, one of the largest Jōmon settlements known, the Ōdai Yamamoto site, where some of Japan's oldest pottery has been found, the stone circles of Ōyu and Isedōtai, and the Kakinoshima and Kitakogane shell middens in Hokkaido.

Significance

The sites document a rare example of a fully developed sedentary society based on hunting, fishing, and gathering rather than agriculture, together with its spiritual culture, expressed in clay figurines (dogū), lacquerware, and monumental stone circles. UNESCO recognised them as outstanding testimony to the emergence and development of a pre-agricultural sedentary society.
