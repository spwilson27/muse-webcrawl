Diabetic nephropathy

Diabetic nephropathy
Other names: Diabetic kidney disease
Specialty: Nephrology, endocrinology
Causes: Long-standing diabetes mellitus

Diabetic nephropathy, also known as diabetic kidney disease, is the chronic loss of kidney function occurring in those with diabetes mellitus. It is one of the most important long-term complications of diabetes and a leading cause of kidney failure worldwide.

Signs and symptoms

Early diabetic nephropathy usually causes no symptoms. As the disease progresses, protein is lost in the urine (albuminuria, then overt proteinuria), filtration capacity declines, and patients may develop swelling, fatigue and eventually the symptoms of kidney failure. It is commonly accompanied by high blood pressure and other diabetic complications such as retinopathy.

Pathophysiology

Chronically elevated blood glucose damages the glomerular filtration barrier in the kidneys. Characteristic changes include thickening of the glomerular basement membrane, expansion of the mesangium, and nodular glomerulosclerosis (Kimmelstiel–Wilson nodules). Hyperfiltration in the early stages contributes to progressive injury.

Diagnosis and management

Screening relies on urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Management centres on tight glycaemic and blood pressure control; ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers slow progression, and SGLT2 inhibitors have shown significant kidney-protective benefits. Advanced disease may require dialysis or kidney transplantation.
