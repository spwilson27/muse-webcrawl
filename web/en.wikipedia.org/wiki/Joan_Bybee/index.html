Joan Lea Bybee (born February 11, 1945) is an American linguist known for her work in phonology, morphology, and grammaticalization, and as a leading proponent of usage-based approaches to language. She is a professor emerita at the University of New Mexico.

Career

Bybee received her B.A. from the University of Texas at Austin and her Ph.D. in linguistics from the University of California, Los Angeles, in 1973. She taught at the State University of New York at Buffalo before moving to the University of New Mexico in 1983, where she spent the rest of her career. She served as president of the Linguistic Society of America in 2004.

Research

Bybee's research holds that the structure of language emerges from language use: the frequency with which words and constructions are used shapes their mental representation and their patterns of change. In Morphology: A Study of the Relation between Meaning and Form (1985) she examined cross-linguistic patterns in inflectional systems, and in a series of studies with co-authors she contributed to the theory of grammaticalization—the process by which lexical items evolve into grammatical markers—summarized in The Evolution of Grammar (1994, with William Pagliuca and Revere Perkins). Her books Phonology and Language Use (2001) and Frequency of Use and the Organization of Language (2007) develop exemplar-based models in which phonetic reduction and chunking effects follow from token frequency.
