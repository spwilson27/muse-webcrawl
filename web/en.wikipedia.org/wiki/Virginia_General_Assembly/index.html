The Virginia General Assembly is the legislative body of the Commonwealth of Virginia, the first and oldest continuous law-making body in the Western Hemisphere, and the first elected legislative assembly in the New World, established on July 30, 1619.

The General Assembly is a bicameral body consisting of a lower house, the Virginia House of Delegates, with 100 members, and an upper house, the Senate of Virginia, with 40 members. Senators serve four-year terms, and Delegates serve two-year terms. Combined, the General Assembly consists of 140 elected representatives from an equal number of constituent districts across the commonwealth.

History

The General Assembly was established by the Virginia Company of London. Initially a unicameral body of company appointees and elected burgesses meeting at Jamestown, it became bicameral in 1642 with the creation of separate houses for the governor's council and the House of Burgesses. The assembly continued in Williamsburg after the capital moved in 1699 and in Richmond from 1780. Thomas Jefferson regarded the founding of the assembly in 1619—alongside the arrival of the first enslaved Africans in Virginia the same year—as a defining moment in American history; the assembly has met continuously ever since, surviving the transition from colony to commonwealth in 1776.

Sessions and procedure

The General Assembly meets annually, beginning on the second Wednesday in January, at the Virginia State Capitol in Richmond, designed by Thomas Jefferson. Regular sessions last 60 calendar days in even-numbered years and 30 days (customarily extended to 46) in odd-numbered years, when a biennial budget is adopted in even years and amended in odd years. Members are part-time legislators. The governor may call special sessions, and bills passed by the assembly are subject to gubernatorial veto, which the assembly may override by a two-thirds vote of both houses. The assembly also elects judges and certain state officials, including the Auditor of Public Accounts.

Capitol

The Virginia State Capitol, completed in 1788 and expanded with wings in 1906 and an underground extension in 2007, houses the chambers: the Senate meets in the north wing and the House of Delegates in the south wing. The historic House chamber contains Jean-Antoine Houdon's statue of George Washington.
