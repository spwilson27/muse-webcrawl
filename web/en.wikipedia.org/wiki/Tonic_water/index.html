Tonic water

Tonic water (often called Indian tonic water) is a carbonated soft drink flavoured with quinine, which gives it a distinctive bitter taste. It is most famously mixed with gin to make a gin and tonic.

History

Quinine, extracted from the bark of the South American cinchona tree, was used to prevent and treat malaria. In 19th-century British India, officers mixed their daily bitter quinine dose with soda water and sugar — and with gin — producing the gin and tonic. Commercial tonic water was patented in 1858 and marketed by firms such as Schweppes, whose "Indian Quinine Tonic" supplied the colonial market. Modern regulations cap quinine levels (for example 83 mg/L in the United States), far below antimalarial doses.

Properties

Because quinine fluoresces, tonic water glows bright blue under ultraviolet light. Most brands are sweetened with sugar or high-fructose corn syrup; "slimline" versions use artificial sweeteners. Tonic water is also used in cocktails such as the espresso tonic and vodka tonic, and bitter lemon is a close relative.
