Southwestern Ontario

Southwestern Ontario is a secondary region of Southern Ontario in the Canadian province of Ontario. It occupies most of the Ontario Peninsula bounded by Lake Huron and Georgian Bay to the north, the St. Clair River, Lake St. Clair and the Detroit River to the west, Lake Erie to the south, and the Grand River valley and Waterloo Region to the east. Roughly 2.6 million people live in the region.

Geography and communities

The largest city is London, Ontario, followed by Windsor, Kitchener, Cambridge, Sarnia, Chatham-Kent, Stratford, Guelph-area communities and Woodstock. The region includes the counties of Essex, Kent, Lambton, Middlesex, Oxford, Perth, Huron, Bruce, Grey, Elgin, Norfolk and Haldimand, along with parts of the Waterloo and Wellington areas depending on definition.

Economy

Southwestern Ontario is one of Canada's richest agricultural regions, growing corn, soybeans, tobacco (historically), ginseng and greenhouse vegetables around Leamington. Manufacturing is centred on automotive production and parts in Windsor (across from Detroit), Cambridge, Woodstock and Ingersoll, alongside food processing, energy and chemical industries in Sarnia's "Chemical Valley". Tourism features the beaches of Lake Huron and Lake Erie, the Stratford Festival, and Point Pelee National Park, the southernmost point of mainland Canada.

History

The area was heavily contested in the War of 1812, including the Battle of the Thames in 1813, and was a principal destination of the Underground Railroad, with Black settlements at Buxton, Chatham and Windsor. Colonel Thomas Talbot's settlement scheme shaped much of the Lake Erie shore's development in the early nineteenth century.
