Tashkent Declaration

The Tashkent Declaration was a peace agreement signed on 10 January 1966 between India and Pakistan in Tashkent, Uzbek SSR, USSR, formally ending the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965. It was brokered by the Soviet Union under Premier Alexei Kosygin.

Terms

Under the declaration, both countries agreed to withdraw their armed forces to positions held before 5 August 1965 (to be completed by 25 February 1966), to restore normal diplomatic and economic relations, to exchange prisoners of war, to renounce the use of force and to settle disputes by peaceful means, and not to interfere in each other's internal affairs. Notably, it contained no settlement of the underlying Kashmir dispute, and India returned strategically captured territory, including the Haji Pir Pass.

Aftermath

Indian Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri died in Tashkent in the early hours of 11 January 1966, the day after signing, officially of a heart attack — an event that gave rise to persistent speculation. In India the declaration drew criticism for surrendering military gains without resolving Kashmir; in Pakistan it damaged President Ayub Khan's standing, and Foreign Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto broke with him soon after. The agreement nevertheless restored the status quo ante and normalised relations until the war of 1971.
