The Red House is the seat of the Parliament of Trinidad and Tobago, located on Woodford Square in Port of Spain. The building, painted a distinctive red, is one of the capital's most prominent landmarks and a symbol of the country's government.

History

The original Red House was constructed between 1844 and 1848 as a government office complex, housing the courts and the colonial administration. Its red colour dates from 1897, when it was painted for Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee. In 1903, during a debate on a new water ordinance, rioters set the building ablaze; sixteen people were killed in the ensuing disorder and the structure was gutted. It was rebuilt in 1906–1907 in an ornate French Renaissance style with Beaux-Arts influences, featuring a cupola and a central rotunda.

The Red House hosted the Legislative Council and, after independence in 1962, the Parliament of Trinidad and Tobago. On 27 July 1990 members of the Jamaat al Muslimeen stormed the building during an attempted coup, holding Prime Minister A. N. R. Robinson and parliamentarians hostage for six days; bullet marks from the siege remain visible, and an eternal flame memorial on the grounds commemorates those killed.

Archaeology

During restoration work in 2013, human remains believed to be of pre-Columbian Amerindian origin were unearthed beneath the building, indicating the site had been an indigenous burial ground centuries before European settlement.

Restoration and present use

Parliament relocated to the waterfront Tower D of the International Waterfront Centre in 2012 while the ageing Red House underwent extensive restoration. Parliament returned to the restored Red House in 2020, resuming sittings there after nearly a decade away.
