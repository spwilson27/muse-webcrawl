The Three Mothers

[Hero image placeholder: promotional still from Dario Argento's Suspiria.]

The Three Mothers (Italian: Le Tre Madri) is a trilogy of supernatural horror films by Italian director Dario Argento, consisting of Suspiria (1977), Inferno (1980) and Mother of Tears (2007). The trilogy centres on three ancient and evil witches: Mater Suspiriorum (the Mother of Sighs), Mater Tenebrarum (the Mother of Darkness) and Mater Lachrymarum (the Mother of Tears).

Concept

The concept derives from a passage in Thomas De Quincey's Suspiria de Profundis (1845), a sequel to Confessions of an English Opium-Eater, which describes three sister goddesses of sorrow: Our Lady of Tears, Our Lady of Sighs and Our Lady of Darkness. In Argento's films, each witch rules from a house built for her in a different city — Freiburg, New York and Rome respectively — designed by the architect and alchemist E. Varelli.

Films

Suspiria follows an American ballet student who discovers her German dance academy is run by a coven. Inferno follows a young poet investigating the history of a New York apartment building. The final film, Mother of Tears, concludes the saga in Rome nearly three decades later.
