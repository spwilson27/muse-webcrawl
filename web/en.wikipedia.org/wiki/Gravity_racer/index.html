Gravity racer

A gravity racer or soapbox is a motor-less vehicle which is raced on a downhill course either against the clock or against another competitor. The vehicles are propelled by gravity alone and must be fitted with an effective brake. Although most are built for the purpose of recreation, gravity racing is also a competitive sport, with classes for children and adults.

History

The sport originated in the United States in the 1930s, when children built cars from wooden soap crates and roller-skate wheels. The Soap Box Derby, founded in Dayton, Ohio in 1934 and later centred in Akron, became the best-known competition for young builders. In the United Kingdom the vehicles are traditionally called "bogies" or "trolleys", and in Australia "billy carts".

Design

Gravity racers are typically built around a simple chassis with four wheels, steering controlled by the driver's hands or feet, and a hand- or foot-operated brake. Competitive rules usually limit weight, wheel size, and materials. Performance depends on minimising rolling resistance and aerodynamic drag and on maximising the total mass within the rules, since all competitors accelerate under the same gravity.

Notable events include the Red Bull Soapbox Race, a light-hearted international series featuring elaborate themed vehicles. See also go-kart for the powered counterpart.
