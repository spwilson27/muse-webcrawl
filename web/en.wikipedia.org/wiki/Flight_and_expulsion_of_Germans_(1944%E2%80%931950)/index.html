Flight and expulsion of Germans (1944–1950)

Flight and expulsion of Germans
Date: 1944–1950
People affected: 12–14.6 million ethnic Germans
Regions: East Prussia, Silesia, Pomerania, Sudetenland and elsewhere
Deaths: estimates range from c. 500,000 to 2.5 million

The flight and expulsion of Germans during and after the Second World War (1944–1950) was the forced migration of ethnic Germans (Volksdeutsche) and German citizens (Reichsdeutsche) from Central and Eastern Europe. Between 12 and 14.6 million Germans fled or were expelled from territories including the former eastern territories of Germany ceded to Poland and the Soviet Union, and from Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Yugoslavia and Romania.

Course of events

The first phase involved flight before the advancing Red Army in 1944–45, including the disastrous evacuations from East Prussia. After the Potsdam Agreement sanctioned "orderly and humane" transfers, organised expulsions continued until about 1950. Expellees were resettled in occupied Germany, where their integration became a major political issue in the Federal Republic. Death toll estimates vary widely and remain controversial.
