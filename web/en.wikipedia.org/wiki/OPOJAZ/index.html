OPOJAZ

OPOJAZ (Russian: ОПОЯЗ, an acronym for Общество изучения поэтического языка, "Society for the Study of Poetic Language") was a group of literary scholars founded in Petrograd (now Saint Petersburg) in 1916. Together with the Moscow Linguistic Circle, it formed the institutional core of Russian Formalism, one of the most influential movements in twentieth-century literary theory.

Members and ideas

Leading members included Viktor Shklovsky, Boris Eichenbaum, Yuri Tynianov, Osip Brik, and (in close association) Roman Jakobson. The Formalists insisted that literary study should focus on literariness — the devices and techniques that make a text a work of art — rather than on biography, psychology, or social background. Shklovsky's 1917 essay "Art as Technique" introduced the celebrated concept of defamiliarization (ostranenie): art makes the familiar strange so that it can be perceived freshly. Other signature topics included the distinction between story (fabula) and plot (syuzhet), the "dominant" element of a work, and the evolution of literary forms.

Decline and legacy

Through the 1920s the Formalists came under mounting ideological attack in the Soviet Union for neglecting the social content of literature; Trotsky criticised them in Literature and Revolution (1924), and by about 1930 the movement had dissolved under political pressure. Its ideas survived abroad, carried by Jakobson into the Prague Linguistic Circle and later into American academia, where Formalism shaped structuralism and much of modern narratology.
