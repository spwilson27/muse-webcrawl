Sinawi (시나위) is a genre of traditional Korean music in which an ensemble improvises together, traditionally to accompany shamanic rituals (gut) and folk dance in the southwestern Jeolla region. It is regarded as one of the most sophisticated forms of Korean folk music and a fountainhead of genres such as sanjo: each musician weaves independent melodic lines around shared rhythmic cycles, producing a dense, heterophonic texture that moves from slow, sorrowful passages to fast, ecstatic climaxes.

A sinawi ensemble, whose members improvise within shared rhythmic cycles called jangdan.

Performance

A sinawi ensemble typically combines melodic instruments — the daegeum (large bamboo flute), piri (oboe), haegeum (fiddle), and the zithers geomungo and gayageum — with percussion led by the janggu hourglass drum. Performance proceeds through a sequence of rhythmic cycles (jangdan) of increasing speed and intensity, beginning with slow cycles such as jinyangjo and progressing through jungmori and jajinmori to a frenzied conclusion, mirroring the emotional arc of the shamanic rite, in which the music mediates between spirits and the human community.

Origins and development

Sinawi grew out of the ritual music of hereditary shaman musicians in Jeolla Province; its name is thought by some scholars to derive from sinawe, an old word associated with the music of the ancient Silla kingdom, though the etymology is debated. In the twentieth century sinawi was adapted for the concert stage, detached from ritual performance, and recognised within South Korea's system for preserving Important Intangible Cultural Properties. Its improvisatory interplay strongly influenced the solo sanjo repertoire, which distils the ensemble's melodic vocabulary for a single instrument with drum accompaniment.
