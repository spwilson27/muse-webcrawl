Operation Desert Storm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Operation Desert Storm
Dates17 January – 28 February 1991 (42 days)
ContextCombat phase of the Gulf War, following Iraq's invasion of Kuwait (2 August 1990)
CoalitionUS-led alliance of 35+ nations under UN Security Council authorisation; commander Gen. Norman Schwarzkopf
OutcomeKuwait liberated; Iraqi army expelled and largely destroyed; ceasefire with Iraq still ruled by Saddam Hussein

Operation Desert Storm was the combat phase of the Gulf War conducted by a United States-led coalition of 35 nations against Iraq between 17 January and 28 February 1991, to liberate Kuwait, invaded and annexed by Saddam Hussein's Iraq in August 1990. After a five-month build-up in Saudi Arabia (Operation Desert Shield) and a UN deadline of 15 January, the coalition opened with one of history's largest air campaigns — stealth aircraft, cruise missiles and precision-guided bombs against Baghdad's command system and Iraq's air defences — the first war televised live from the enemy capital, which made CNN's reporters and the Pentagon's bomb-camera briefings global phenomena.

The 100-hour ground war

After 38 days of bombing (and Iraq's Scud missile attacks on Israel and Saudi Arabia, answered by Patriot batteries), the ground offensive of 24 February executed Schwarzkopf's "left hook": coalition armour swept through the empty desert west of Kuwait while amphibious feints pinned Iraqi divisions on the coast, encircling the occupying army, whose remnants fled up the Basra road — the "Highway of Death" — as Kuwait City was liberated. President George H.W. Bush halted operations after 100 hours of ground combat, with Kuwait freed, coalition deaths remarkably low (under 400, some by friendly fire) against Iraqi military deaths estimated in the tens of thousands. Critics then and since questioned stopping short of Baghdad; the settlement left Saddam in power to crush the Shia and Kurdish uprisings the coalition had encouraged, imposed sanctions and no-fly zones through the 1990s, and set the stage — via "unfinished business" politics and the long containment — for the 2003 Iraq War. As a demonstration of post-Cold War American power, precision warfare and coalition diplomacy under UN colours, Desert Storm remained the template Washington hoped to repeat.
