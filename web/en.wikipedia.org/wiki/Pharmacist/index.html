Pharmacist

A pharmacist, also known as a chemist in Commonwealth English or a druggist in North American English, is a healthcare professional who is primarily engaged in the control, preparation, and dispensing of medicines, and who provides pharmaceutical care to patients. Pharmacists have various roles and responsibilities: dispensing prescription drugs and advising patients and other healthcare professionals on their proper use; providing immunizations; conducting health and wellness screenings; and overseeing medication therapy management.

Roles and practice settings

Community pharmacists work in retail pharmacies, dispensing medications and counseling patients on dosage, side effects, and drug interactions. Hospital and clinical pharmacists work as members of interdisciplinary care teams, advising on drug selection and dosing, monitoring therapy, and preparing specialized products such as intravenous admixtures and chemotherapy. Pharmacists also work in industry, regulatory agencies, academia, and research.

Education and licensing

In the United States, pharmacists earn a Doctor of Pharmacy (Pharm.D.) degree and must pass the NAPLEX licensure examination. In the United Kingdom, pharmacists complete an MPharm degree followed by a foundation training year and registration with the General Pharmaceutical Council. Requirements vary by jurisdiction but generally combine university study in pharmacology, pharmaceutics, medicinal chemistry, and pharmacy practice with supervised practical training.

Scope of practice

The scope of pharmacy practice has expanded steadily, encompassing prescribing authority in some jurisdictions, point-of-care testing, chronic disease management, and public health services such as vaccination campaigns. Pharmacists are among the most accessible healthcare professionals and frequently serve as a first point of contact for minor ailments.
