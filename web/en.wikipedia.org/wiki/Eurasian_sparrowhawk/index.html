The Eurasian sparrowhawk (Accipiter nisus), also known simply as the sparrowhawk, is a small bird of prey in the family Accipitridae. Adult males have bluish-grey upperparts and orange-barred underparts; females and juveniles are brown above with brown bars below. It is a widespread predator of woodland birds across Europe, Asia and northern Africa.

Behaviour

Sparrowhawks are specialist hunters of small birds, relying on surprise and short, fast pursuits through cover. Females, up to 25% larger than males, take larger prey such as pigeons and thrushes.

Population history

Numbers crashed in the 1950s–60s because of organochlorine pesticides such as DDT, which caused eggshell thinning. Following bans on these chemicals, populations recovered strongly, and the species is now common, including in urban gardens.
