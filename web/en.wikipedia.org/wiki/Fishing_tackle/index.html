Fishing tackle

DefinitionEquipment used by anglers when fishing
Main componentsRod, reel, line, hooks, lures or bait
Terminal tackleItems attached to the end of the line

Fishing tackle is the equipment used by anglers (recreational fishers) for the sport of fishing. Nearly any equipment or gear used for fishing can be called tackle, but the term most often refers to the rod, reel, line, hooks, lures, floats, weights and related apparatus used to present a bait or lure to a fish and to land it once hooked.

Rod, reel and line

The fishing rod is a long tapered pole, historically of bamboo or cane and now usually of fibreglass or carbon fibre, used to cast the line and to play a hooked fish. The reel is a spool mounted on the rod that stores, releases and retrieves the line; common designs include the spinning reel, baitcasting reel and fly reel. Fishing line is most often monofilament nylon, though braided lines and fluorocarbon are also widely used.

Terminal tackle

The gear tied to the end of the line is called terminal tackle. It includes the hook, which catches in the fish's mouth; sinkers or weights, which carry the bait down; floats or bobbers, which suspend the bait at a chosen depth and signal bites; and swivels and snaps, which reduce line twist and make changing lures easier. Hooks are finished with either natural bait — worms, insects, pieces of fish — or artificial lures such as spinners, spoons, plugs, soft plastics and flies, designed to imitate prey.

Accessories

Anglers also carry nets for landing fish, pliers and disgorgers for removing hooks, tackle boxes for organising small items, and waders for fishing in the water. Modern accessories include electronic fish finders using sonar, bite alarms, and polarised sunglasses for spotting fish.

History

Fishing gear is ancient: hooks of bone, shell and thorn survive from prehistory, and the rod-and-line method was described by writers such as Izaak Walton in The Compleat Angler (1653). The multiplying reel appeared in the eighteenth century, and the twentieth century brought synthetic lines, lightweight synthetic rods and mass-produced lures, turning tackle into a major sporting-goods industry.
