Colin Andrew Firth CBE (born 10 September 1960) is an English actor and activist. He is the recipient of numerous accolades including an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award and a Golden Globe. His portrayal of Mr Darcy in the 1995 BBC television adaptation of Pride and Prejudice made him a household name in Britain, and he won the Academy Award for Best Actor for playing King George VI in The King's Speech (2010).

Early life

Firth was born in Grayshott, Hampshire, to academic parents; his mother was a comparative religion lecturer and his father a history lecturer. He spent part of his childhood in Nigeria, where his parents taught, and later attended schools in Winchester and Southampton. After studying at the Drama Centre London, he made his stage and film debuts in the early 1980s.

Career

Firth gained attention for roles in Another Country (1984) and the television drama Tumbledown (1988). The success of Pride and Prejudice led to film roles in The English Patient (1996), Shakespeare in Love (1998) and Bridget Jones's Diary (2001), in which he played Mark Darcy, a modern echo of his Austen character. He appeared in Love Actually (2003), Girl with a Pearl Earring (2003) and Mamma Mia! (2008). His performance in A Single Man (2009) earned him a BAFTA Award, and The King's Speech brought him the Academy Award. He later starred in the Kingsman spy films and the drama Supernova (2020).

Activism

Firth has campaigned on behalf of indigenous peoples' rights with Survival International, supported asylum seekers and refugee causes, and co-founded the production company Raindog Films. He was appointed Commander of the Order of the British Empire (CBE) in 2011.
