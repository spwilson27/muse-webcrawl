Sandworm is a Russian advanced persistent threat (APT) hacking group attributed by Western governments and security researchers to Unit 74455 of the GRU, Russia's military intelligence agency. Named by the firm iSIGHT Partners in 2014 after references to Frank Herbert's Dune found in its code ("sandworm" and Arrakis allusions), the group is considered responsible for some of the most destructive cyberattacks ever carried out by a state actor.

Operations

  • Ukraine blackouts (2015 and 2016): Sandworm's intrusions into Ukrainian electricity distribution companies caused power outages affecting hundreds of thousands of customers; the 2016 attack used the Industroyer (CrashOverride) malware designed to manipulate grid control systems.
  • NotPetya (2017): A supply-chain compromise of Ukrainian accounting software released a wiper disguised as ransomware that spread globally, causing an estimated $10 billion in damage to companies including Maersk, Merck, and FedEx subsidiary TNT Express.
  • Olympic Destroyer (2018): An attack disrupting IT systems at the Pyeongchang Winter Olympics opening ceremony, initially seeded with false flags pointing to other states.
  • Exim and espionage campaigns: exploitation of a vulnerability in the Exim mail server (CVE-2019-10149) and long-running espionage against NATO, EU, and Eastern European targets.

Indictments

In October 2020 the United States Department of Justice unsealed an indictment charging six alleged GRU officers of Unit 74455 with the group's major attacks, including NotPetya and the interference operations around the 2017 French elections. The group remained active during Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, deploying further wiper malware against Ukrainian infrastructure.
