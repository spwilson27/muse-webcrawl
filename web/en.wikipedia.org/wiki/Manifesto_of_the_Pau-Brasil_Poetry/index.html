Manifesto of the Pau-Brasil Poetry

The Manifesto of the Pau-Brasil Poetry (Manifesto da Poesia Pau-Brasil) is a literary manifesto written by the Brazilian poet Oswald de Andrade and first published in the Rio de Janeiro newspaper Correio da Manhã on 18 March 1924. It is a founding document of Brazilian modernism, proposing a national poetry that would be an exportable Brazilian product — like brazilwood (pau-brasil), the colony's first export commodity.

Content

The manifesto advocates a poetry of primitivism and modernity combined: "Poetry exists in facts." It calls for verse rooted in everyday Brazilian speech, humour, and technological progress, rejecting the erudite, imported, bookish poetry of the Parnassian tradition. It proposes the synthesis of the "forest and the school," of archaic Brazil and the modern world of aeroplanes and cinema, and demands the "millionaire contribution of all the errors" of popular language — "As we speak. As we are."

The Pau-Brasil movement found its fullest poetic expression in Andrade's collection Pau-Brasil (1925), illustrated by Tarsila do Amaral, and later developed into the more radical Anthropophagist movement, proclaimed in the Manifesto Antropófago (1928).

Context

The manifesto appeared two years after the São Paulo Modern Art Week of 1922, in which Andrade had participated alongside Mário de Andrade, Tarsila do Amaral, Anita Malfatti, and others who sought to define a distinctly Brazilian modern culture.
