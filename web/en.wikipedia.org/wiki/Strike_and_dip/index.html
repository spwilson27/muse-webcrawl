Strike and dip

Strike and dip of an inclined planar surface

In geology, strike and dip is a measurement convention used to describe the orientation, or attitude, of a planar geologic feature such as a sedimentary bed, fault, joint or foliation. The measurement allows the three-dimensional geometry of rock structures to be recorded on maps and reconstructed in cross-sections.

Definitions

The strike is the direction of the line formed by the intersection of the feature with a horizontal plane—equivalently, the compass bearing of a horizontal line drawn on the surface. The dip is the angle between the feature and the horizontal plane, measured perpendicular to the strike in the direction the surface slopes downward; it ranges from 0° (horizontal) to 90° (vertical). The dip direction is always 90° from the strike. Because a strike line has two opposite bearings, conventions such as the "right-hand rule" (strike chosen so the plane dips to the right) remove ambiguity.

Measurement

Geologists measure strike and dip in the field with a compass-clinometer such as a Brunton compass, aligning the compass edge with the surface to obtain strike and using the clinometer for dip. On geologic maps, a T-shaped strike-and-dip symbol marks the measurement: the long bar is parallel to strike and the short tick points in the dip direction, often labelled with the dip angle.

Related terms

For linear features such as fold axes or slickensides, orientation is given by trend and plunge. Apparent dip is the dip seen in a vertical section not perpendicular to strike; it is always less than the true dip. Strike and dip measurements underpin structural-geology analyses such as stereonet plotting.
