History of Equatorial Guinea

The history of Equatorial Guinea is marked by Portuguese and then Spanish colonisation, independence in 1968, and two long authoritarian regimes.

Colonial period

Portugal ceded the island of Fernando Pó (Bioko) and mainland claims to Spain in 1778. Spanish Guinea developed cocoa plantations on Bioko worked largely by migrant labour.

Independence

Equatorial Guinea became independent on 12 October 1968 under President Francisco Macías Nguema, whose brutal rule killed or exiled a large share of the population. He was overthrown in 1979 by his nephew Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo. Offshore oil discovered in the 1990s transformed the economy without ending one-party dominance.
