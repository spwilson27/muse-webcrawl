Vedic astrology

Vedic astrology, traditionally known as Jyotiṣa (from Sanskrit jyotiṣa, "the science of light"), is the traditional Hindu system of astrology, one of the six Vedangas, or auxiliary disciplines connected with the study of the Vedas. It is still widely practised in India and across the Hindu diaspora for purposes such as choosing auspicious times (muhūrta), matchmaking for marriage, and interpreting birth charts (kuṇḍalī).

Principles

Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, anchored to the fixed stars, rather than the tropical zodiac used in most Western astrology; the difference between the two, the ayanāṁśa, is currently about 24 degrees. The chart is built around the ascendant (lagna) and the twelve signs (rāśis), twelve houses (bhāvas), and nine "planets" or grahas: the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn, plus the lunar nodes Rāhu and Ketu. Uniquely, the system divides the zodiac into 27 lunar mansions (nakṣatras), which underpin its distinctive system of predictive time periods.

Dashas and techniques

The best-known predictive method is the Vimśottarī daśā, a 120-year cycle of planetary periods apportioned according to the Moon's nakshatra at birth, used to time events in a person's life. Other elements include divisional charts (vargas), yogas (special planetary combinations), aspects (dṛṣṭi), and remedial measures such as gemstones, mantras and charity traditionally prescribed to mitigate unfavourable indications.

History and literature

Seeds of the system appear in the Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa, a treatise on the calendar. The classical synthesis developed in the early centuries CE — influenced in part by Hellenistic astrology transmitted from the West — in works such as the Yavana Jātaka, and reached mature form in Varāhamihira's Bṛhat Jātaka (6th century CE) and later texts like the Phaladīpikā and Jātaka Pārijāta. Jyotiṣa remains culturally influential; its claims are regarded by scientists as pseudoscientific, though it is studied historically as a significant branch of pre-modern astronomy and mathematics in India.
