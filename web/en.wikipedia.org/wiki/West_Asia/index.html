West Asia (or Western Asia) is the westernmost region of Asia, as defined by most academics, United Nations bodies and other institutions. It comprises Anatolia, the Arabian Peninsula, the Levant, Mesopotamia, the Iranian plateau and often the South Caucasus. The region is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the west, Central Asia to the north and east, and the Arabian Sea to the south.

Geography and definition

The term overlaps substantially with Middle East, though "West Asia" excludes Egypt (usually) and avoids the Eurocentric framing of "Middle East". The UN geoscheme for Western Asia includes Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bahrain, Cyprus, Georgia, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Palestine, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates and Yemen; Iran is classed by the UN under Southern Asia, though many other definitions include it in West Asia.

History

West Asia is home to some of the world's earliest civilisations: Mesopotamia and the Fertile Crescent saw the first cities, writing and agriculture, and the region was the birthplace of Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Its position between Europe, Africa and Asia made it a crossroads of trade along the Silk Road and, in the modern era, a major centre of petroleum production.
