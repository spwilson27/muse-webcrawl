Sour (cocktail)

A sour is a family of mixed drinks containing a base spirit, lemon or lime juice, and a sweetener such as sugar syrup, following the classic structure of spirit, citrus, and sugar. Sours are among the oldest cocktail families, described in Jerry Thomas's 1862 bartender's guide, and likely derive from punches carried aboard ships, where citrus juice helped prevent scurvy.

Structure

The standard sour ratio is roughly two parts spirit, one part citrus, and one part sweetener, shaken with ice and served straight up or on the rocks. Adding soda water lengthens the drink into a fizz, while a float of red wine makes a New York sour, and shaking with egg white produces a silky "Boston sour" style texture.

Common varieties

Well-known sours include the whiskey sour, the pisco sour (the national drink of both Peru and Chile), the amaretto sour, the gin sour, and derivatives such as the daiquiri (rum, lime, sugar) and the margarita (tequila, lime, orange liqueur), which many classifications treat as sours.
