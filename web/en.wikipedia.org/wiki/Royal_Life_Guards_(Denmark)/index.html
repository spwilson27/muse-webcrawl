Royal Life Guards (Denmark)

The Royal Life Guards (Danish: Den Kongelige Livgarde) is an infantry regiment of the Royal Danish Army, founded on 30 June 1658 by King Frederick III. The regiment has a dual role: it is an operational infantry unit that has served in Denmark's wars from the Dano-Swedish wars to the conflicts in Iraq and Afghanistan, and it furnishes the ceremonial guard for the Danish monarchy, mounting daily guard at Amalienborg Palace in Copenhagen and at other royal residences.

History

Created as the king's household foot guards during the war with Sweden, the Life Guards defended Copenhagen in the assault of 1659 and subsequently fought in the Scanian War, the Great Northern War, the Napoleonic-era conflicts with Britain — including the Copenhagen bombardments of 1801 and 1807 — and the Schleswig wars of 1848–50 and 1864. In the Second World War, guardsmen fought German forces during the invasion of 9 April 1940, and members resisted during the occupation; in August 1943 the regiment was disarmed and interned by the Germans. In the post-war period the Life Guards developed as a mechanised infantry regiment while retaining its guard role, and deployed battalions and companies to Cyprus, the Balkans, Iraq and Afghanistan, where it suffered casualties in Helmand province.

Ceremonial role

The regiment's Guard Company (Vagtkompagniet) performs the daily changing of the guard: at noon the guard marches from Rosenborg Castle through central Copenhagen to Amalienborg, accompanied by the Royal Life Guards Music Corps when the monarch is in residence. Guards wear a distinctive uniform of blue tunic, white crossbelt and tall black bearskin cap, carrying sabre and rifle. The regiment also provides guards of honour for state visits and the guard at Fredensborg Palace and Marselisborg Palace when the royal family is in residence.
