Consultant (medicine)

In the United Kingdom, Ireland and much of the Commonwealth, a consultant is a senior physician or surgeon who has completed specialist training and holds ultimate clinical responsibility for patients under their care in a National Health Service hospital.

Role

Consultants lead clinical teams, supervise junior doctors, run outpatient clinics and operating lists, and are the named responsible clinician for admitted patients. In surgery they are often addressed as "Mr", "Mrs", "Ms" or "Miss" rather than "Dr", a tradition dating to the barber-surgeons.

Training pathway

After medical school and the two-year Foundation Programme, doctors enter specialty training (typically 5–8 years). Appointment to a consultant post requires entry on the GMC Specialist Register via a Certificate of Completion of Training (CCT).

Equivalents elsewhere

The closest equivalents are the attending physician in the United States and the médecin chef de service in France. Unlike US attendings, UK consultants are usually salaried NHS employees, though many also practise privately.
