Rabi' al-awwal (Arabic: ربيع الأول) is the third month of the Islamic lunar calendar, following Muharram and Safar. Its name means "the first [month] of spring", a relic of the pre-Islamic Arabian calendar's seasonal associations.

Significance

The month is best known for Mawlid, the commemoration of the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, traditionally dated to 12 Rabi' al-awwal and celebrated as a public holiday in many Muslim-majority countries with processions, recitations and charity. Muhammad is also believed by many traditions to have died in the same month in 11 AH (632 CE), and his migration (Hijrah) to Medina reached Quba and then Medina during Rabi' al-awwal.

Observance

Views on Mawlid celebrations vary: they are central to Sufi practice and widely observed by Sunni and Shia communities, while some movements regard them as an unwarranted innovation. In Shia tradition, 17 Rabi' al-awwal is marked as the Prophet's birthday and the birth of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq.
