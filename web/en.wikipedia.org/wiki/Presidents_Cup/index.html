The Presidents Cup is a biennial men's team golf competition between a team representing the United States and an International team drawn from the rest of the world excluding Europe — whose players compete instead in the Ryder Cup. Created by the PGA Tour and first played in 1994 in Virginia, it is held in the years between Ryder Cups and alternates between venues in the United States and in International-team countries such as Australia, South Africa, Canada and South Korea.

Format and history

Twelve-player teams contest 30 matches over four days — foursomes and fourball sessions followed by twelve singles — with a point per match and the trophy requiring 15½ points to win outright. Unlike the Ryder Cup, the Presidents Cup has been one-sided for most of its history: the United States has lost only once, at Royal Melbourne in 1998, and the 2003 match in South Africa ended in a famous tie, shared after darkness halted a playoff between Tiger Woods and Ernie Els. Jack Nicklaus and Gary Player were the captains for that edition. The event donates its proceeds to charity rather than paying prize money.
