The Republic of Mahabad (Kurdish: کۆماری مەهاباد, Komara Mahabadê) was a short-lived Kurdish self-governing unrecognized state in present-day northwestern Iran, proclaimed in January 1946 under the leadership of Qazi Muhammad and backed by the Soviet Union, which then occupied northern Iran. Centred on the city of Mahabad, it was the second modern Kurdish state after the Republic of Ararat.

History

Following the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran in 1941, the area around Mahabad lay in the Soviet occupation zone. The Democratic Party of Iranian Kurdistan (KDPI) was founded in 1945, and on 22 January 1946 Qazi Muhammad proclaimed the republic in Mahabad's main square. The government introduced Kurdish-language education and publishing, formed a small army under Mustafa Barzani's peshmerga, and issued its own stamps and currency plans.

Under international pressure the Soviet Union withdrew from Iran in 1946. In December 1946 Iranian government forces re-entered Mahabad without major resistance. Qazi Muhammad and other leaders were arrested, tried, and hanged in Mahabad's main square on 31 March 1947. Although it lasted only about a year, the republic remains a powerful symbol of Kurdish national aspirations.
