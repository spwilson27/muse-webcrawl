Natchitoches, Louisiana

Natchitoches (pronounced /ˈnækətəʃ/ NAK-ə-təsh) is a city in and the parish seat of Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana, United States. Established in 1714 by French explorer Louis Juchereau de St. Denis, it is the oldest permanent settlement in the Louisiana Purchase territory. The population was about 18,000 at the 2020 census. The city lies on the banks of Cane River Lake, a former channel of the Red River.

History

St. Denis founded Fort St. Jean Baptiste in 1714 to trade with the local Caddo peoples — the town takes its name from the Natchitoches tribe — and to block Spanish expansion from Texas. The settlement flourished as a frontier trading post linking French Louisiana with Spanish Mexico. It passed to Spain in 1762, returned to France, and was sold to the United States with the Louisiana Purchase in 1803. In the 19th century it was a major cotton port on the Red River; when the river shifted course in the 1830s, the town was left on the resulting oxbow lake, Cane River Lake, and commerce passed to newer river towns.

Culture and landmarks

The Natchitoches Historic District, along brick-paved Front Street, preserves Creole and French colonial architecture. Nearby are the Cane River Creole National Historical Park plantations, including Oakland and Magnolia, and Melrose Plantation, associated with the Creole artist Clementine Hunter. The city is the setting of the film Steel Magnolias (1989), adapted from Robert Harling's play. Natchitoches is famous for its meat pies — savoury fried turnovers — celebrated at an annual Meat Pie Festival, and for its Christmas Festival of Lights, held since 1927, which draws large crowds to the riverbank each December. Northwestern State University of Louisiana, founded in 1884, is the city's largest institution.
