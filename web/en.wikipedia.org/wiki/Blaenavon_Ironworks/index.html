Blaenavon Ironworks

Location: Blaenavon, Torfaen, Wales
Built: 1789
Status: Museum; part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site

Blaenavon Ironworks is a former industrial site in Blaenavon, south Wales, now a museum. Opened in 1789, it was one of the most important ironworks of the early Industrial Revolution, exploiting local coal, iron ore and limestone.

History

The works were developed by Thomas Hill and partners. Three blast furnaces were built initially, with more added in the 19th century; pig iron output supplied forges and export markets. After ironmaking ceased, the site's Gilchrist-Thomas process for steelmaking was demonstrated here in 1878.

World Heritage status

In 2000 the Blaenavon Industrial Landscape, including the ironworks and the nearby Big Pit coal mine, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The surviving furnaces, cast house and workers' housing at Stack Square are preserved by Cadw.
