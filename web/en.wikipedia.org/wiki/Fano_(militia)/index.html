Fano (Amharic: ፋኖ) is an Amhara ethno-nationalist militia and armed movement in Ethiopia. The name, historically used for patriotic guerrilla fighters — including those who resisted the Italian occupation in the 1930s — came to be applied to loosely organised Amhara youth groups that mobilised during the political crises of the 2010s and fought alongside the Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF) and Eritrean forces during the Tigray War (2020–2022).

Background

Fano groups gained prominence amid protests and violence in the Amhara Region and disputes over territories such as Welkait, which Amhara activists claim was annexed by Tigray in the 1990s. During the Tigray War, Fano fighters operated with government forces, but relations with Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's administration deteriorated after the 2022 Pretoria peace agreement with the Tigray People's Liberation Front.

Conflict from 2023

In 2023 the federal government moved to integrate regional special forces into the national army and police. Resistance in Amhara escalated into open conflict from mid-2023: Fano factions seized or contested towns including Gondar and Lalibela, fought federal troops using guerrilla tactics, and prompted a state of emergency in the region. The movement remains decentralised, without a single command, and different factions have at times announced efforts to form a unified front. The conflict has caused heavy civilian casualties and displacement and has been accompanied by reports of abuses by multiple parties.
