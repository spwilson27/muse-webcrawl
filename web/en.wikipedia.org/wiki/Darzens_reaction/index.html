Darzens reaction

The Darzens reaction (also known as the Darzens condensation or glycidic ester condensation) is a chemical reaction between a ketone or aldehyde and an α-halo ester in the presence of a base to form an α,β-epoxy ester, called a glycidic ester. The reaction was discovered by the French chemist Georges Darzens in 1904.

The Darzens reaction converts a carbonyl compound and an α-halo ester into an α,β-epoxy ester.

Reaction mechanism

The process begins when a strong base, commonly an alkoxide, deprotonates the α-carbon of the halo ester to give a resonance-stabilized enolate. The enolate carbon attacks the carbonyl carbon of the aldehyde or ketone in a nucleophilic addition, forming an alkoxide. In the final step, the alkoxide oxygen displaces the halide intramolecularly in an SN2 fashion, closing the three-membered epoxide ring.

Applications

Hydrolysis and decarboxylation of the glycidic ester product affords an aldehyde or ketone extended by one carbon relative to the starting carbonyl compound, making the Darzens reaction a useful chain-extension method in synthesis. The reaction proceeds under milder conditions than many aldol-type condensations and tolerates a range of substituents on both partners. Variants using α-halo ketones, amides and sulfones are also known.
