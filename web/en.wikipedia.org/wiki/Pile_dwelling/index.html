Pile dwelling

Pile dwellings (also known as stilt houses or lake dwellings) are houses raised on piles over the surface of a lake, river, marsh, or other body of water. Prehistoric pile dwellings are known from the Neolithic and Bronze Age around the Alps and in other regions of Europe, and similar forms of construction persist in parts of Southeast Asia, Oceania, and South America.

Prehistoric pile dwellings around the Alps

Remains of pile-dwelling settlements have been found in lakes and wetlands in Switzerland, Germany, Austria, France, Italy, and Slovenia. In 2011 a group of 111 sites was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as "Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps." Preserved in waterlogged sediments, the wooden piles, tools, textiles, and food remains offer an exceptionally detailed picture of early farming communities between roughly 5000 and 500 BC.

Reasons for construction

Early interpretations imagined villages built over open water for defence. Modern research suggests many settlements stood on marshy shores, with piles serving to stabilise foundations on soft ground, protect against flooding, and keep dwellings dry. Comparable stilt settlements today, such as those of the Bajau or on Lake Maracaibo, show the continuing practicality of the form.
