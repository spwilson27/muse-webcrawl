Toyota Mirai

The Toyota Mirai is a mid-size hydrogen fuel-cell electric sedan produced by Toyota. Mirai (未来) is Japanese for "future." Launched in 2014, it was one of the first mass-produced dedicated fuel-cell vehicles sold commercially, alongside the Hyundai Nexo.

First generation (2014–2020)

The first-generation Mirai (JPD10) used Toyota's TFCS fuel-cell system, combining a fuel-cell stack of about 114 kW with a hybrid battery buffer and carbon-fibre hydrogen tanks storing fuel at 700 bar. Its range was roughly 500 km (EPA-rated around 312 miles), and its only tailpipe emission was water vapour. Sales were limited mainly to California, Japan, and parts of Europe where hydrogen refuelling existed.

Second generation (2020– )

The second-generation Mirai (JPD20), introduced for 2021, moved to the rear-wheel-drive GA-L platform shared with the Lexus LS, with sleeker styling, a larger 141 kW stack, three hydrogen tanks, and an EPA range up to about 402 miles for the XLE grade. Despite critical praise, sales remained constrained by sparse hydrogen infrastructure and high fuel prices, especially in California.
