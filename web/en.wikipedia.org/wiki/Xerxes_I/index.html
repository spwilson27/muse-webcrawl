Xerxes I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Relief of Xerxes I from the Gate of All Nations at Persepolis]

Xerxes I (c. 518–465 BC), known as Xerxes the Great, was the fourth king of the Achaemenid Empire, reigning from 486 to 465 BC. Son and successor of Darius I, he crushed revolts in Egypt and Babylon, then led the second and greatest Persian invasion of Greece (480–479 BC) — bridging the Hellespont, cutting a canal through Mount Athos, burning Athens — only to see his fleet destroyed at Salamis and his army at Plataea. The rest of his reign built at Persepolis and Susa; he was assassinated in a court intrigue in 465 BC. In Greek tradition Xerxes is the archetypal hubristic despot; Persian sources present a legitimate king completing his father's work.

The invasion of Greece

Xerxes prepared for four years: a canal through the Athos peninsula (to avoid the cape where Mardonius' fleet had wrecked), double pontoon bridges over the Hellespont — lashed, in Herodotus' famous story, after he had the sea whipped for destroying the first bridge — and supply depots along the route. His host (modern estimates 100,000–300,000 fighting men, against Herodotus' millions) forced Thermopylae, took and burned evacuated Athens, and then, watching from a throne above Salamis, lost the fleet that sustained it. Xerxes withdrew to Asia, leaving Mardonius to be destroyed at Plataea in 479 BC.

Later reign and death

After 479 BC Xerxes campaigned no more in person; the empire's frontier in the Aegean receded under Athenian pressure. He completed Darius' building programme — the Gate of All Nations at Persepolis bears his name — and administered an empire still at its greatest extent. In 465 BC he was murdered in his bedchamber by Artabanus, commander of the guard (the details in Ctesias and later sources are tangled); his son Artaxerxes I succeeded after a brief succession struggle. Ahasuerus of the Book of Esther is traditionally identified with Xerxes.
