Andrés Bonifacio

Andrés Bonifacio y de Castro (November 30, 1863 – May 10, 1897) was a Filipino revolutionary leader, often called "the Father of the Philippine Revolution." He was a founder and later the Supremo (supreme leader) of the Kataas-taasang, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan, the secret society known as the Katipunan, which launched the Philippine Revolution against the Spanish Empire in 1896.

Early life

Bonifacio was born in Tondo, Manila, the eldest of six children of Santiago Bonifacio and Catalina de Castro. Orphaned at fourteen, he supported his siblings by working as a clerk, warehouse keeper and salesman, educating himself by reading books on the French Revolution, the lives of American presidents, and novels including José Rizal's Noli Me Tangere. In 1892 he joined Rizal's Liga Filipina, and when Rizal was arrested and exiled, Bonifacio and others founded the Katipunan on July 7, 1892, to pursue independence by armed revolt.

Katipunan and the outbreak of revolution

As Supremo of the Katipunan, Bonifacio organized its council, publications (including the newspaper Kalayaan) and recruitment across Manila and neighboring provinces. When Spanish authorities discovered the society in August 1896, Bonifacio called his followers to arms at the Cry of Pugad Lawin (or Balintawak), tearing their cedulas (residence certificates) in defiance. The revolution's first major engagement, the Battle of San Juan del Monte (Pinaglabanan) on August 30, 1896, ended in defeat, but the revolt spread through the provinces.

Conflict with Aguinaldo and death

Revolutionary conventions at Tejeros in March 1897 elected Emilio Aguinaldo president of a new revolutionary government and demoted Bonifacio, who rejected the proceedings. Arrested on Aguinaldo's orders, Bonifacio was tried by a military court for sedition and treason and, with his brother Procopio, was executed on May 10, 1897 in Maragondon, Cavite. His wife, Gregoria de Jesús, had earlier searched for him in the mountains. The circumstances of his death remain a subject of debate among Filipino historians.

Legacy

Bonifacio is a national hero of the Philippines; his birthday, November 30, is a national holiday (Bonifacio Day). The Bonifacio Monument in Caloocan, designed by Guillermo Tolentino, is among the country's most famous landmarks, and his portrait has appeared on Philippine currency.
