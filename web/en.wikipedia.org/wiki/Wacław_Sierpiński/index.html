Wacław Sierpiński

Wacław Franciszek Sierpiński
Born: 14 March 1882, Warsaw
Died: 21 October 1969, Warsaw
Fields: mathematics — set theory, number theory, topology

Wacław Franciszek Sierpiński (1882–1969) was a Polish mathematician known for contributions to set theory, number theory, the theory of functions and topology. He authored over 700 papers and 50 books.

Work

Sierpiński is associated with the fractal objects named after him: the Sierpiński triangle (1915), the Sierpiński carpet, and the Sierpiński curve, as well as Sierpiński numbers in number theory. He worked extensively on the axiom of choice and the continuum hypothesis, and on transfinite numbers. With Stefan Banach and others he helped found the Polish School of Mathematics and the journal Fundamenta Mathematicae (1920).

Life

Educated at the University of Warsaw, he was briefly interned by the Russians during the First World War and worked in Moscow with Nikolai Luzin. After the war he returned to Warsaw. During the Second World War he taught in the underground university. The lunar crater Sierpiński is named after him.
