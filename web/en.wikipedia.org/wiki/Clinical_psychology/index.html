Clinical psychology

Clinical psychology
Focus: Assessment and treatment of mental disorders
Key activities: Psychological testing, psychotherapy, research
Founder figure: Lightner Witmer (first clinic, 1896)
Training models: Boulder (scientist–practitioner), Vail (PsyD)

Clinical psychology is an integration of human science, theory and clinical knowledge for the purpose of understanding, preventing and relieving psychologically-based distress or dysfunction and promoting subjective well-being and personal development. Its central practices are psychological assessment and psychotherapy, along with research, teaching and consultation.

History

The field is conventionally dated to 1896, when Lightner Witmer opened the first psychological clinic at the University of Pennsylvania and coined the term "clinical psychology". It expanded greatly after the Second World War, driven by the need to assess and treat returning veterans, and by the 1949 Boulder Conference, which established the scientist–practitioner model of doctoral training.

Assessment

Clinical psychologists administer and interpret psychological tests — of intelligence, personality, neuropsychological functioning and symptoms — and conduct clinical interviews and behavioural observations to formulate diagnoses and treatment plans.

Treatment approaches

Psychotherapy in clinical psychology draws on several schools: psychodynamic, humanistic, behavioural and cognitive approaches. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and its variants are among the most extensively researched treatments. Many practitioners adopt an integrative or eclectic stance. In most jurisdictions clinical psychologists do not prescribe medication (a role of psychiatrists), though a few US states grant limited prescriptive authority after additional training.

Professional practice

Clinical psychologists work in hospitals, private practice, universities, schools, prisons and community mental health services, typically requiring a doctorate (PhD or PsyD) and licensure. It is one of the largest specialty areas within psychology.
