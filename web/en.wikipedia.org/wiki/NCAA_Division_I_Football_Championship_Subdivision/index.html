The NCAA Division I Football Championship Subdivision (FCS) is the second-highest level of college football in the United States, after the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS). Comprising more than 120 schools, the FCS is governed by the NCAA, which administers its season-ending single-elimination playoff — the only NCAA-run championship in Division I football.

The FCS champion is crowned through a 24-team NCAA playoff each December and January.

Overview

FCS programmes operate with smaller budgets and stadiums than FBS counterparts and may award up to 63 scholarship equivalencies, which can be divided among more players. Members include the Ivy League (whose teams long abstained from the postseason before agreeing to participate from 2025), the historically Black Southwestern Athletic Conference and Mid-Eastern Athletic Conference, whose champions meet instead in the Celebration Bowl, and conferences such as the Missouri Valley Football Conference, Big Sky, and CAA Football.

Playoffs

The FCS championship concludes with a 24-team bracket: conference champions receive automatic bids and the remainder are selected at large, with the top eight seeds receiving first-round byes. The championship game has been held in Frisco, Texas, since the 2010 season. North Dakota State is the subdivision's dominant programme, winning ten national titles between 2011 and 2024; other multiple champions include Georgia Southern, Appalachian State (which later moved to the FBS), Montana, Youngstown State, and South Dakota State.

History

Division I football split in 1978 into I-A and I-AA; the first I-AA championship was played that year. In 2006 the NCAA renamed the levels to align terminology with the bowl/playoff distinction: I-A became the FBS and I-AA became the FCS. The playoff field grew from 4 teams in 1978 to 16 by 1986, 20 in 2010, and 24 in 2013. FCS teams occasionally defeat FBS opponents — most famously Appalachian State's 2007 upset of Michigan — and the subdivisions' "guarantee games" are an important revenue source for FCS athletic departments.
