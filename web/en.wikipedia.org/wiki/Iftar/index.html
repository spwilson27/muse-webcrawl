Iftar

Iftar (إفطار)
When: sunset (maghrib)
During: Ramadan and other fasts
Often begins with: dates and water

Iftar (Arabic: إفطار, ʾifṭār) is the fast-breaking evening meal of Muslims during Ramadan, taken at sunset after the maghrib call to prayer. It is the counterpart of suhur, the pre-dawn meal.

Practice

Following the example attributed to the Prophet Muhammad, many break the fast with dates and water or milk, then pray before the main meal. Iftar is often communal: families gather, mosques and charities host free meals, and tables spread into streets in cities from Cairo to Jakarta. Cannon fire traditionally marked sunset in some countries; in the UAE and elsewhere the Ramadan cannon continues ceremonially.

Foods

Regional dishes include harira soup in Morocco, samosas and pakoras in South Asia, qatayef sweets in the Levant, and kolak in Indonesia. In 2023, 'Iftar and its socio-cultural traditions' was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity as a multinational nomination.

Significance

Beyond eating, iftar embodies generosity and community: feeding a fasting person is considered especially meritorious, and interfaith and civic iftars are common in many countries.
