Normandy

CountryFrance
CapitalRouen
Known forD-Day beaches, Mont-Saint-Michel, Camembert
[Image: The cliffs of Étretat, Normandy]
The cliffs of Étretat, Normandy

Normandy is a region of northern France facing the English Channel, named for the Norse ("Northmen") settlers whose leader Rollo received the land in 911. From here William the Conqueror launched the Norman Conquest of England in 1066.

Character

Normandy's landscapes run from the chalk cliffs of Étretat and the Cotentin peninsula to the bocage hedgerows of the interior and the Seine valley past Rouen, its historic capital. Mont-Saint-Michel rises from its tidal bay on the Breton border. The region is famed for Camembert, cider and Calvados, and for the D-Day landing beaches of 6 June 1944 — Utah, Omaha, Gold, Juno and Sword — with their cemeteries and museums. The administrative region formed in 2016 reunites the former Upper and Lower Normandy.
