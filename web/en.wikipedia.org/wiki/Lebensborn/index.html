Lebensborn e.V. (literally "Fount of Life") was a state-supported, SS-run association in Nazi Germany founded by Heinrich Himmler in 1935. Its declared purpose was to raise the birth rate of children classified as "racially pure" and "healthy" according to Nazi racial doctrine, by providing maternity homes where unmarried mothers could give birth in secrecy and by arranging adoptions into SS families. In occupied Europe the programme expanded into the kidnapping and Germanisation of foreign children deemed racially suitable.

Maternity homes

The first Lebensborn home opened at Steinhöring in Bavaria in 1936; homes followed across Germany and, during the war, in occupied Norway, Belgium, France, and elsewhere. Mothers and fathers were screened for supposed Aryan ancestry and health. Around 8,000 children were born in the German homes and perhaps 12,000 in Norway, where children of German soldiers and Norwegian women were registered and supported. Contrary to popular myth, the homes were not "stud farms": most mothers arrived already pregnant, and Himmler's hopes that SS men would father children there were only partially realised.

Kidnapping of children

In occupied Poland and other eastern territories, the programme took a criminal turn: children judged racially valuable were seized from their families or orphanages, transported to Germany, given German names, and placed with German families; those who failed selection risked death in camps. Historians estimate that tens of thousands of Polish children were taken, of whom only a minority were recovered after the war. This aspect of Lebensborn featured in the RuSHA Trial at Nuremberg, though its leaders were acquitted of kidnapping charges on evidential grounds.

Aftermath

Lebensborn children often faced stigma after the war, particularly in Norway, where many suffered discrimination and abuse and only received official acknowledgement and compensation decades later. Anni-Frid Lyngstad of ABBA, born in Norway to a Norwegian mother and a German soldier father, is among the best-known Lebensborn children.
