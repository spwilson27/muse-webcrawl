Shooting (association football)

Shooting in association football is the act of kicking the ball toward the opponent's goal in an attempt to score. A shot that would enter the goal if not blocked is recorded as a shot on target; shots are a fundamental statistic in match analysis and player evaluation.

Techniques

Common striking techniques include the instep (laces) drive for power, the inside-of-the-foot placed shot for accuracy, the curled or "finesse" shot with spin, the volley and half-volley struck before the ball bounces or as it drops, the header from crosses, and the chipped shot over an advancing goalkeeper. Penalty kicks and free kicks near goal are specialised forms of shooting from dead-ball situations.

Tactics and analysis

Teams design attacking patterns to create high-quality shooting chances close to goal, since shot location strongly predicts scoring probability — the basis of the expected goals (xG) metric. Defences counter by pressing the shooter, blocking lanes, and forcing attempts from distance and wide angles. A player's shooting is often evaluated by goals, shot volume, shot accuracy and conversion rate.
