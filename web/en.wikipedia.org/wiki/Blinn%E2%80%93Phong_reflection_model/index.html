Blinn–Phong reflection model

The Blinn–Phong reflection model, also called the modified Phong reflection model, is a modification of the Phong reflection model developed by Jim Blinn in 1977. It computes the specular highlight of a surface using the halfway vector between the light direction and the view direction, rather than the reflected light vector used in Phong's original formulation.

Formulation

For a light direction L, a view direction V and a surface normal N, the halfway vector is H = normalize(L + V). The specular term is then proportional to (N·H) raised to a shininess power. Because N·H changes more slowly than the Phong reflection term and the halfway vector can sometimes be precomputed (for distant lights and viewers), Blinn–Phong is cheaper to evaluate and avoids the highlight clipping artefacts that could appear in the original model.

Usage

Blinn–Phong became the default shading model of the fixed-function pipelines of OpenGL and Direct3D, and it remains common in real-time graphics because of its simplicity and speed, even as physically based rendering (PBR) models have taken over in modern engines. Like Phong shading, it is an empirical model, not a physically accurate simulation of light reflection.
