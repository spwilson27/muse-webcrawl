The MAS Modèle 36 is a French bolt-action rifle adopted in 1936 and produced by the Manufacture d'Armes de Saint-Étienne. Chambered for the rimless 7.5×54mm French cartridge, it served as the standard infantry rifle of the French Army through the Second World War and into the post-war colonial conflicts, remaining in reserve and second-line use for decades afterwards.

Design

The MAS-36 was designed as a simple, robust and economical weapon for mass production. Its bolt has only a small number of parts, with two rear locking lugs and a characteristically bent-forward bolt handle positioned close to the trigger for rapid manipulation. The rifle has no manual safety catch, a feature it shares with many French designs of the period; the soldier was expected to carry the rifle with an empty chamber until ready to fire.

The five-round magazine is loaded with stripper clips or individual cartridges. The aperture rear sight is adjustable for range, and the weapon was issued with a distinctive cruciform spike bayonet that reversed into a tube beneath the barrel when not in use.

Variants

Variants included the MAS-36 CR39, a folding-stock version for paratroopers; the MAS-36 LG48, fitted to fire rifle grenades; and the MAS 36/51, an updated post-war model with a grenade-launching sight and muzzle device. The action later formed the basis of the FR F1 precision rifle used by French snipers.

Service

Production began too late for the rifle to equip the whole French Army before the Battle of France in 1940, and many units still carried older Lebel and Berthier rifles. The MAS-36 went on to serve in the First Indochina War and the Algerian War before being replaced in front-line service by the semi-automatic MAS-49. Large numbers were exported or passed to former French colonies, where the type remained in use well into the late twentieth century.
