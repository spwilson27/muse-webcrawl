The Egypt Cup is the main domestic knockout football cup competition in Egypt and the oldest football tournament in Africa and the Arab world, first held in the 1921–22 season. It is organised by the Egyptian Football Association and is open to clubs from all divisions of Egyptian football. The competition was known for sponsorship reasons in various years, and its winner traditionally meets the Egyptian Premier League champion in the Egyptian Super Cup.

History

The first edition, in 1921–22, was won by Zamalek, who defeated Al Ittihad Alexandria in the final. Cairo giants Al Ahly and Zamalek have dominated the competition ever since; Al Ahly is the most successful club in its history with 39 titles, followed by Zamalek with 28. Two early finals, in 1942–43 and 1957–58, ended with the trophy shared between Al Ahly and Zamalek after drawn matches. The cup has occasionally been cancelled or suspended — during the Second World War era for some years, after the 1973 October War, and during political unrest — but has been held almost continuously since 1921.

Format

The modern competition is a straight knockout tournament, with Premier League clubs entering in the round of 32. Ties are decided in a single match, with extra time and penalties if level. The cup winner qualifies for the CAF Confederation Cup (unless it has already qualified for the CAF Champions League through the league, in which case the place passes on) and for the Egyptian Super Cup.

Other winners

Beyond the two Cairo giants, the trophy has been won by Tersana, Al Masry, Al Ittihad Alexandria, Ismaily, Al Mokawloon Al Arab (Arab Contractors), ENPPI, Haras El Hodoud, Wadi Degla-era challengers, and Pyramids FC, whose 2024 triumph was their first major honour.
