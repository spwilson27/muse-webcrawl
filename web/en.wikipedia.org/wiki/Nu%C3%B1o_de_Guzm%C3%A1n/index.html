Nuño de Guzmán

Nuño Beltrán de Guzmán (c. 1490 – 1558) was a Spanish conquistador and colonial administrator notorious for his brutality during the conquest of western Mexico. As president of the first Audiencia of New Spain and founder of the province of Nueva Galicia, he waged savage campaigns against the indigenous peoples of the present-day states of Jalisco, Nayarit, Sinaloa, and Michoacán.

Career

Guzmán arrived in New Spain in 1525 as governor of Pánuco, where he sold hundreds of Huastec people into slavery in the Caribbean. Appointed head of the first Audiencia in 1528—intended partly as a check on Hernán Cortés—he governed tyrannically and feuded with churchmen such as Juan de Zumárraga.

In 1529 he launched a great entrada westward with thousands of indigenous allies, enslaving and torturing populations, including the Purépecha king Tangaxuan II, whom he executed. His forces founded early, repeatedly relocated settlements of Guadalajara and conquered the region organized as Nueva Galicia. Complaints about his cruelty led to his arrest in 1536; he was sent back to Spain in chains, tried, and died in obscurity, remembered in Mexican history as one of the cruelest of the conquistadors.
