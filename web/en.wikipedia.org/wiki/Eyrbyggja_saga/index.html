Eyrbyggja saga

Eyrbyggja saga (Old Norse pronunciation: [ˈœyrˌbyɡːjɑ ˈsɑɣɑ]) is one of the sagas of Icelanders. Written by an anonymous Icelander in the 13th century, it tells the history of the families who settled around the Snæfellsnes peninsula in western Iceland, particularly the descendants of Þórólfr Mostrarskegg and the chieftain Snorri Þorgrímsson (Snorri the Priest).

The name means the "Saga of the inhabitants of Eyri", a farm in Álftafjörður. Unlike sagas focused on a single hero, Eyrbyggja is a broad chronicle covering events from the settlement of Iceland (c. 870) into the early 11th century, with famous episodes including the blood feud between Snorri and Arnkel, the haunting at Fróðá (one of the most elaborate ghost stories in Old Norse literature), Viking expeditions, and the conversion of Iceland to Christianity.

Manuscripts and scholarship

The saga survives in several medieval manuscripts and fragments, though no complete vellum copy exists; the text is reconstructed from paper copies and fragments such as those in Vatnshyrna. Scholars debate its relationship with Laxdæla saga and its use of earlier written sources, including possibly lost sagas and Ari Þorgilsson's Íslendingabók.
