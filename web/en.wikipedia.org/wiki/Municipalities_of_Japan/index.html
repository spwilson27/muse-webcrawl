Municipalities of Japan

Japan is divided into 47 prefectures, which are in turn subdivided into municipalities. As of the 2020s there are roughly 1,700 municipalities in Japan, a number greatly reduced from over 10,000 in the 19th century by successive waves of government-promoted mergers, most recently the "Great Heisei Mergers" of the 2000s.

Types

  • Cities (shi) — generally with populations of at least 50,000. Large cities may be designated as designated cities, core cities, or special cities, with progressively greater delegated powers; designated cities are divided into wards (ku) that are not themselves municipalities.
  • Towns (machi or chō).
  • Villages (mura or son), the smallest municipalities.
  • Special wards (tokubetsu-ku) of Tokyo, which function much like cities within the Tokyo Metropolis.

Government

Each municipality is headed by a directly elected mayor and a unicameral assembly, under the Local Autonomy Law. Municipalities provide resident registration, primary and junior high schools, water and waste services, local roads, and welfare services, funded by local taxes and transfers from the national government.
