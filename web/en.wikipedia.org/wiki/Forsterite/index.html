Forsterite

CategoryNesosilicate
FormulaMg2SiO4
Crystal systemOrthorhombic
Hardness7 (Mohs)
ColorColorless, green, yellow, white

Forsterite (Mg2SiO4; commonly abbreviated as Fo) is the magnesium-rich end-member of the olivine solid solution series. It is isomorphous with the iron-rich end-member, fayalite. Forsterite crystallizes in the orthorhombic system (space group Pbnm) with cell parameters a 4.75 Å, b 10.20 Å and c 5.98 Å.

Occurrence

Forsterite-rich olivine is the most abundant mineral in the upper mantle above a depth of about 400 km; the pyroxenes are also important minerals in this upper part of the mantle. Although forsterite is also a common mineral in igneous rocks such as basalt, pure forsterite is rare, occurring chiefly in metamorphosed impure dolomitic limestones. Forsterite also occurs in a variety of extraterrestrial materials, including stony meteorites and cometary dust.

Gemstones

Transparent forsterite of gem quality is known as peridot, one of the traditional birthstones for August. The finest peridot is a pure grass-green color, and the presence of iron is what gives the gem its characteristic green hue.
