Emilio Mola

Emilio Mola Vidal (9 July 1887 – 3 June 1937) was a Spanish military commander who was one of the three leaders of the Nationalist coup of July 1936 that started the Spanish Civil War. He is credited with coining the phrase "fifth column", and was known among the conspirators as el Director ("the Director") for his central role in planning the uprising.

Early career

Mola was born in Placetas, Cuba, then still a Spanish colony, where his father was stationed as an officer. He entered the Toledo Infantry Academy in 1904 and served extensively in Morocco, rising through the ranks during the Rif War. In 1930 he became Director-General of Security under the government of Dámaso Berenguer, and after the proclamation of the Second Republic in 1931 he was imprisoned and prosecuted before being amnestied.

The 1936 coup

After the victory of the Popular Front in the elections of February 1936, Mola was appointed military governor of Pamplona in Navarre, a posting intended to remove him from the centre of power that instead placed him in a strongly conservative and Carlist region ideal for conspiring. From there he co-ordinated the military conspiracy: his secret instructions to garrisons laid out the plan for the rising of 17–18 July 1936.

When the coup failed to take the whole country at once, Mola commanded the Nationalist Army of the North. In the autumn of 1936 he advanced on Madrid with four columns; in a famous radio interview he said that a "fifth column" of supporters inside the capital would rise to help him, giving rise to the expression now used worldwide for clandestine sympathisers within a besieged group.

Death

Mola died on 3 June 1937 when the aircraft carrying him crashed in bad weather near Alcocero, in the province of Burgos. After the deaths of Mola and José Sanjurjo, Francisco Franco remained the undisputed leader of the Nationalist side.
