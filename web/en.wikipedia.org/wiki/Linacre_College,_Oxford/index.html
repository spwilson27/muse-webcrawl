Linacre College is a constituent college of the University of Oxford in England, admitting graduate students only. Founded in 1962, it was the university's first graduate college and the first Oxford college to admit men and women on equal terms.

History

The college is named for Thomas Linacre (c. 1460–1524), a royal physician, humanist scholar and founder of the Royal College of Physicians, who was a fellow of All Souls. Linacre began in premises on St Aldate's and moved to its present site at the junction of St Cross Road and Longwall Street, in a Victorian former convent (Cherwell Edge). It received its royal charter and full collegiate status in 1986.

College life

Linacre is known for its international membership, strengths in environmental and interdisciplinary study — it hosts the Environmental Change Institute's close ties — and an egalitarian ethos: fellows and students share a common room and dining arrangements without a high table. The college was the first in Oxford to achieve carbon-neutral status and fair-trade status.
