The Primetime Emmy Award is an American award bestowed by the Academy of Television Arts & Sciences (ATAS) in recognition of excellence in American primetime television programming. It is considered one of the four major annual American entertainment awards, together with the Academy Award (film), the Grammy Award (music), and the Tony Award (theatre); winning all four is known as achieving EGOT status.

Overview

The first Emmy ceremony was held on 25 January 1949 at the Hollywood Athletic Club, honoring shows produced in the Los Angeles area; the awards soon became national. The statuette — a winged woman representing art, holding aloft an atom representing science — was designed by television engineer Louis McManus. "Emmy" derives from "Immy", a nickname for the image orthicon camera tube. The Primetime Emmys are one of several Emmy award events; others include the Daytime, Sports, News and Documentary, and International Emmys, administered by sister organizations.

Categories and ceremony

Major categories include Outstanding Drama Series, Comedy Series, and Limited or Anthology Series, plus acting, directing, and writing awards for each genre. Most craft and guest categories are presented at the Creative Arts Emmy ceremonies held in the preceding week, while the televised Primetime ceremony rotates among the major broadcast networks. Eligibility generally covers programs aired between June 1 and May 31 in primetime (6 p.m.–2 a.m.) nationally. Record-setting winners include Game of Thrones (59 Emmys, the most for a drama), Frasier (37, a comedy record long held before being surpassed by newer series), and performers such as Julia Louis-Dreyfus and Cloris Leachman.

History notes

The awards adapted to cable television in the 1990s–2000s — HBO's The Sopranos was the first cable series to win Outstanding Drama (2004) — and to streaming in the 2010s, with Netflix's The Crown sweeping the drama categories in 2021. The 75th ceremony was delayed to January 2024 by the 2023 writers' and actors' strikes. The awards remain the television industry's most prominent peer-voted honour.
