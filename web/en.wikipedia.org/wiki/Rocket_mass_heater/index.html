Rocket mass heater

A rocket mass heater (also rocket stove mass heater) is a type of efficient wood-burning heating appliance, originating from the rocket stove, that stores most of its heat in a thermal mass — typically a cob or masonry bench — and releases it slowly into the room over many hours. Its exhaust gases are typically close to room temperature and consist mainly of steam and carbon dioxide, because nearly all the fuel is burned and nearly all the heat extracted.

Design

Wood is fed into a small, insulated L- or J-shaped combustion chamber. A tall, insulated internal chimney (the "heat riser") creates a strong draught and very high combustion temperatures that burn off smoke and creosote. Hot gases then pass beneath a surrounding barrel — which radiates immediate heat — before travelling through a long horizontal flue embedded in the mass bench, surrendering their remaining heat before exiting.

History

The design was developed in the 1970s and 1980s at the Aprovecho Research Center in Oregon, and popularised for home heating by Ianto Evans and Leslie Jackson, whose book Rocket Mass Heaters (2004) spread the design through the permaculture and natural building movements.
