Lakenvelder cattle

The Lakenvelder is a breed of domestic cattle originating in the Netherlands and Germany. It is best known for its striking "belted" colour pattern: a broad band of white hair encircling the body between the shoulders and hips, contrasting with a solid black — or, less commonly, dusky red — head and hindquarters. The name derives from a Dutch word for a sheet or blanket (laken), as if a white cloth had been wrapped around the animal's middle.

Lakenvelders are medium-sized, hardy, dual-purpose cattle. Cows typically weigh around 500–600 kg. The breed is noted for good fertility, longevity, easy calving and efficient forage conversion, and it historically served small farms as a combined dairy and beef animal.

History

Belted cattle have been depicted in Dutch art since the 17th century. The modern breed was consolidated in the Netherlands and the neighbouring German region of Westphalia in the 19th and 20th centuries; a Dutch herd book was established in the 1980s. Numbers fell sharply with the dominance of the Holstein-Friesian, and the Lakenvelder is now a rare breed, conserved by specialist breeders in the Netherlands and Germany and exported in small numbers to other countries, including the United Kingdom, North America and Australia.

Similar breeds

The belted pattern also characterises the Dutch Belted of the United States (derived from the same stock), the Belted Galloway of Scotland, and the Welsh Belted and Sheeted Somerset, though these are genetically and historically distinct breeds.
