The Churchill River is a major river in Labrador, in the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador. Flowing 856 kilometres from the Smallwood Reservoir in western Labrador eastward to Lake Melville and the Atlantic Ocean, it drains a basin of nearly 80,000 square kilometres. The river was historically known as the Hamilton River; it was renamed in 1965 in honour of Sir Winston Churchill.

Course

The river's headwaters lie on the Labrador Plateau. Much of its upper flow is impounded in the Smallwood Reservoir, one of the largest artificial lakes in the world by surface area, created for the Churchill Falls Generating Station. Below the falls — where the river drops some 300 metres over a short distance through a deep gorge — the Churchill flows northeast past the town of Happy Valley-Goose Bay before entering Lake Melville, a tidal fjord connected to Hamilton Inlet and the Labrador Sea.

Hydroelectric development

The Churchill Falls project, completed in the early 1970s, was one of the largest underground powerhouses in the world, with an installed capacity of about 5,400 megawatts; most of its output is sold to Hydro-Québec under a long-term contract whose terms have remained a source of grievance in Newfoundland and Labrador. A second project, the Muskrat Falls generating station on the lower Churchill, was developed in the 2010s as part of the Lower Churchill Project, amid controversy over costs, mercury methylation concerns raised by Indigenous communities, and flooding.

Ecology and communities

The river supports Atlantic salmon and other fisheries, and its valley is used by the Innu Nation and other Indigenous peoples whose traditional territories span the basin. The towns of Churchill Falls and Happy Valley-Goose Bay are the principal settlements.
