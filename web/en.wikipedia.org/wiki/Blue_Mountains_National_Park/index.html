Blue Mountains National Park is a national park in New South Wales, Australia, about 80 kilometres west of Sydney. It forms part of the Greater Blue Mountains Area, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000 for its outstanding representation of eucalypt evolution and diversity. The park is renowned for its deeply incised sandstone plateaus, gorges, waterfalls and the blue haze — caused by volatile oils released by eucalyptus trees — that gives the mountains their name.

Geography and features

The park protects much of the catchment of the Grose River and the valleys of the Jamison, Megalong and Kanimbla. Famous lookouts and formations include Echo Point and the Three Sisters near Katoomba, Wentworth Falls, Govetts Leap and the Grand Canyon walking track near Blackheath. Towns of the Blue Mountains — Katoomba, Leura, Wentworth Falls and others — sit along the ridge-top highway and railway, surrounded by park land.

Flora, fauna and heritage

The park contains a high diversity of eucalypt species, the rare Wollemi pine in nearby Wollemi National Park having been discovered in 1994, and habitats for species such as the spotted-tailed quoll and the Blue Mountains tree frog. Aboriginal heritage is extensive: the region is the country of the Gundungurra and Darug peoples, and contains rock art, engraving and occupation sites. The park suffered severe damage in the 2019–20 Australian bushfire season but has since shown strong regeneration.
