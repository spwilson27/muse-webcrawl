Akutagawa Prize

Native name: 芥川龍之介賞
Country: Japan
First awarded: 1935
Presented by: Society for the Promotion of Japanese Literature
Frequency: Twice yearly

The Akutagawa Prize (芥川龍之介賞, Akutagawa Ryūnosuke Shō) is a Japanese literary award presented biannually, in January and July, to the best serious work of pure literature (junbungaku) by a new or rising author published in a newspaper or magazine. It is generally considered Japan's most prestigious literary prize for new writers.

History

The prize was established in 1935 by Kikuchi Kan, editor of Bungeishunjū magazine, in memory of his friend, the writer Ryūnosuke Akutagawa (1892–1927). It is sponsored by the Society for the Promotion of Japanese Literature. Its counterpart for popular/entertainment fiction is the Naoki Prize, founded at the same time.

Selection and prize

A committee of established writers selects the winner, whose announcement attracts wide media attention and typically produces a bestseller. The recipient receives a pocket watch and one million yen. Notable winners include Kenzaburō Ōe, Yōko Ogawa, Ryu Murakami, and Risa Wataya, who in 2004 at age 19 became one of its youngest recipients. See Japanese literature.

Controversies

The prize has periodically sparked debate about literary versus commercial values, and in some rounds no award is given when the committee finds no work worthy.
