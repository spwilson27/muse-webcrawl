Tomáš Masaryk

Tomáš Masaryk most commonly refers to Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk (1850–1937), the philosopher, sociologist and statesman who became the first President of Czechoslovakia in 1918.

Overview

Born in Hodonín in the Austrian Empire, Masaryk rose from humble origins to become a professor of philosophy in Prague and a deputy in the Austrian parliament. His defence of scholarly integrity in affairs such as the Hilsner trial made him a controversial but respected public figure.

Role in founding Czechoslovakia

During the First World War he led the Czechoslovak independence movement in exile, negotiated with the Allied powers, and helped organise the Czechoslovak Legions in Russia, France and Italy. The independent republic he helped create endured as a democracy until the Munich Agreement of 1938.
