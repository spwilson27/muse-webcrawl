Bring Back Our Girls

FoundedApril 2014
OriginAbuja, Nigeria
FocusRelease of the abducted Chibok schoolgirls; education and security
Key figuresObiageli Ezekwesili, Aisha Yesufu and other co-conveners

Bring Back Our Girls (BBOG) is a Nigerian advocacy group and global social media campaign that emerged in April 2014 after the Islamist militant group Boko Haram abducted 276 schoolgirls from the Government Girls Secondary School in Chibok, Borno State, north-eastern Nigeria, on the night of 14–15 April 2014.

Origins

The phrase began as a call at a rally in Abuja addressed by former World Bank vice-president Obiageli Ezekwesili, who urged the crowd to demand that the government "bring back our girls." The hashtag #BringBackOurGirls spread rapidly on Twitter and other social media, drawing global attention; prominent figures including US First Lady Michelle Obama were photographed holding signs with the slogan. Marches and daily sit-outs began at the Unity Fountain in Abuja, led largely by women wearing red.

Activities and demands

The movement demanded that the Nigerian government act decisively to rescue the abducted girls, provide support to their families, secure schools in the north-east, and address the wider insurgency. Members maintained regular protests for years and criticised successive governments for the slow pace of rescue efforts.

Releases

Some girls escaped shortly after the abduction. In October 2016, 21 girls were released following negotiations brokered with the help of the International Committee of the Red Cross and the Swiss government; in May 2017 a further 82 were freed in exchange for detained Boko Haram suspects. Other girls were later found or rescued by the military, or escaped, while many remained missing for years; some are believed to have died in captivity or been married to fighters.

Legacy

Bring Back Our Girls became one of the most prominent examples of hashtag activism and helped focus international attention on the Boko Haram insurgency and on the safety of girls' education worldwide. Its activists have continued to mark anniversaries of the abduction and to advocate for other abducted children.
