Sheldon Allan Silverstein (September 25, 1930 – May 10, 1999) was an American writer, poet, cartoonist, singer-songwriter and playwright. He is best known for his children's books of poetry and drawings, including The Giving Tree (1964), Where the Sidewalk Ends (1974) and A Light in the Attic (1981), characterised by playful language, simple line drawings and a mixture of whimsy and melancholy.

Early life and cartoons

Born in Chicago, Silverstein began drawing as a child and studied art before being drafted into the US Army in 1953, serving in Japan and Korea, where he drew cartoons for the military newspaper Pacific Stars and Stripes. After returning he became a cartoonist for Playboy, contributing cartoons and travel features for decades.

Children's books

Encouraged by editor Ursula Nordstrom at Harper & Row, Silverstein published Lafcadio: The Lion Who Shot Back (1963) and then The Giving Tree, a parable of a boy and a self-sacrificing tree that became a perennial bestseller and a subject of debate over its meaning. His poetry collections — Where the Sidewalk Ends, A Light in the Attic and Falling Up (1996) — introduced generations of children to poems such as "Sick", "Sarah Cynthia Sylvia Stout" and "The Unicorn". Posthumous collections include Runny Babbit (2005) and Every Thing on It (2011).

Music and theatre

Silverstein wrote many songs, most famously "A Boy Named Sue", a hit for Johnny Cash in 1969, and material for Dr. Hook & the Medicine Show including "The Cover of Rolling Stone" and "Sylvia's Mother". He also wrote the song "The Unicorn", popularised by The Irish Rovers, and co-wrote the screenplay for Things Change (1988) with David Mamet.

Death and legacy

Silverstein died of a heart attack in Key West, Florida, in 1999. His books have been translated into dozens of languages and have sold tens of millions of copies; he was inducted into the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2002.
