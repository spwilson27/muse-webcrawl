Al Anbar Governorate

The Euphrates near Ramadi, capital of Anbar

Al Anbar Governorate (Arabic: محافظة الأنبار) is the largest governorate of Iraq by area, occupying the western third of the country. Its capital is Ramadi. Anbar borders Syria, Jordan and Saudi Arabia, and its population is overwhelmingly Sunni Arab, historically organised around powerful tribes such as the Dulaim. The Euphrates river runs the length of the province, and most inhabitants live in a string of cities along its banks, including Fallujah, Hit, Haditha and Al-Qa'im.

Geography

Much of Anbar is desert plateau forming the northeastern edge of the Syrian Desert, broken by the Euphrates valley and by depressions and oases. Lake Tharthar and Lake Habbaniyah lie in the east, and the Haditha Dam on the Euphrates generates hydroelectric power. The Baghdad–Amman–Damascus highways cross the province, making it Iraq's main overland gateway to the west.

Recent history

After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, Anbar became the epicentre of the Sunni insurgency; Fallujah and Ramadi saw some of the war's heaviest fighting. In 2006–2007, tribal leaders formed the Anbar Awakening, allying with US forces against Al-Qaeda in Iraq and sharply reducing violence. A decade later the Islamic State seized much of the province in 2014, and Iraqi forces, with coalition support, recaptured its cities by 2017 amid extensive destruction, particularly in Ramadi and Fallujah.
