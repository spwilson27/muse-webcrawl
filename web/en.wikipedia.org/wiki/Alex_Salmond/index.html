Alex Salmond

Alexander Elliot Anderson Salmond (31 December 1954 – 12 October 2024) was a Scottish politician who served as First Minister of Scotland from 2007 to 2014 and leader of the Scottish National Party (SNP) on two occasions, from 1990 to 2000 and from 2004 to 2014. He was the central figure in the modern Scottish independence movement and led the Yes Scotland campaign in the 2014 independence referendum.

Career

Educated at the University of St Andrews, Salmond worked as an economist for the Royal Bank of Scotland before being elected MP for Banff and Buchan in 1987. He became SNP leader in 1990 and was a leading campaigner in the 1997 devolution referendum. Elected to the new Scottish Parliament in 1999, he led the SNP group at Westminster from 2004 after a period away from frontline Scottish politics.

In the 2007 Scottish Parliament election the SNP won one more seat than Labour, and Salmond became First Minister of a minority government. The SNP won an outright majority in 2011 — an outcome the electoral system had been designed to prevent — and Salmond negotiated the Edinburgh Agreement with the UK government, setting a referendum for 18 September 2014. When 55% voted No, Salmond announced his resignation and was succeeded by Nicola Sturgeon.

He returned to Westminster as MP for Gordon in 2015, losing the seat in 2017. In 2020 he was acquitted of all charges in a high-profile trial, and in 2021 he became leader of the new Alba Party. He died suddenly in North Macedonia in October 2024 while attending a conference.
