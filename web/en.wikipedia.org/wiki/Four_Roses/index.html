Four Roses

Four Roses is a brand of Kentucky straight bourbon whiskey produced by the Four Roses Distillery in Lawrenceburg, Kentucky. The distillery was founded by Paul Jones Jr. in Louisville in 1888, and the brand survived Prohibition as one of a handful of companies licensed to sell whiskey for medicinal purposes.

History

According to company lore, the name commemorates Jones's marriage proposal, accepted by a Southern belle who wore a corsage of four red roses to a ball. In 1943 the distillery was acquired by Seagram, which shifted the brand's focus in the United States to blended whiskey while selling the straight bourbon abroad — where it became a best-seller in Europe and Japan. In 2002 the brand passed to the Kirin Brewery Company of Japan, and straight bourbon returned to the American market under master distillers Jim Rutledge and later Brent Elliott.

Production

Four Roses is unusual in using two mash bills and five proprietary yeast strains, producing ten distinct bourbon recipes that are blended or bottled as single-barrel releases. Its expressions include the Yellow Label, Small Batch, Single Barrel and Small Batch Select.
