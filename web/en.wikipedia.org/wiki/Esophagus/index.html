Esophagus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The esophagus (oesophagus in British English) is the muscular tube, about 25 cm long in adults, that carries swallowed food and liquid from the pharynx to the Stomach. It passes through the neck, the chest behind the Trachea and heart, and through the diaphragm to join the stomach.

Function

Waves of coordinated muscle contraction (peristalsis), together with gravity, move a swallowed bolus down in seconds. Sphincters at the top and bottom keep the tube closed between swallows; the lower sphincter's failure allows stomach acid to reflux, causing heartburn and gastro-oesophageal reflux disease.

Clinical significance

Disorders include reflux disease and its complication Barrett's oesophagus, swallowing disorders (dysphagia), inflammation, and oesophageal cancer, which is strongly linked to smoking, alcohol and chronic reflux. The esophagus is examined by endoscopy and is a site where swallowed objects or food can lodge — a common emergency in children and older adults.
