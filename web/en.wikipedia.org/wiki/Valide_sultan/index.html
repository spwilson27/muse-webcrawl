Valide sultan

Valide sultan (Ottoman Turkish: والده سلطان, "mother sultan") was the title held by the mother of a reigning Ottoman sultan. The valide sultan was the most powerful woman in the imperial harem and often one of the most influential figures in the empire, acting as head of the harem, manager of a vast household and charitable endowments, and sometimes as regent for a young sultan.

History

The first woman to hold the title officially was Hafsa Sultan, mother of Suleiman the Magnificent, in the 16th century. Before that, mothers of sultans were styled with titles such as hatun. The title valide sultan formalised her precedence above even the sultan's favourite consorts (haseki sultan).

Power and role

The valide sultan controlled the harem hierarchy, corresponded with foreign rulers — Kösem Sultan and Safiye Sultan exchanged letters with European queens — and used her enormous stipend to fund mosques, hospitals, soup kitchens and fountains through waqf endowments. Complexes such as the Yeni Valide Mosque in Istanbul remain monuments to their patronage.

Sultanate of Women

During the period known as the Sultanate of Women (roughly 1533–1656), a succession of powerful valide sultans — Hürrem Sultan as haseki, then Nurbanu, Safiye, Kösem and Turhan Sultan — exercised extraordinary political influence, culminating in Turhan's appointment of Köprülü Mehmed Pasha as grand vizier, which ended the era.

Notable valide sultans

  • Hafsa Sultan, mother of Suleiman the Magnificent
  • Nurbanu Sultan, mother of Murad III
  • Safiye Sultan, mother of Mehmed III
  • Kösem Sultan, mother of Murad IV and Ibrahim
  • Turhan Sultan, mother of Mehmed IV
