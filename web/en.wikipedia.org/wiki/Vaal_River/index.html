Vaal River

Vaal River
Length: approx. 1,120 km
Source: Sterkfontein Beacon, Mpumalanga
Mouth: confluence with the Orange River near Douglas

The Vaal River (Afrikaans/Dutch: Vaalrivier) is the largest tributary of the Orange River in South Africa. It rises near Sterkfontein Beacon in Mpumalanga province, close to Breyten, and flows westward for some 1,120 kilometres before joining the Orange near Douglas in the Northern Cape.

Course

From its source on the Highveld the Vaal flows southwest, forming much of the boundary between Mpumalanga and the Free State, then between Gauteng and North West province, and finally between the Free State and the Northern Cape. It passes the towns of Standerton, Vereeniging, Vanderbijlpark, Parys, Orkney, Bloemhof, Christiana, Warrenton and Kimberley's vicinity before meeting the Orange River.

Importance

The Vaal is the economic lifeline of Gauteng, South Africa's industrial heartland. The Vaal Dam, completed in 1938, and the Vaal Barrage supply water to Johannesburg, Pretoria and the surrounding mining and industrial region, while the river also supports irrigation agriculture along its lower course. The discovery of gold in the Witwatersrand and diamonds near the river's mouth shaped the region's history.

History

The river's name comes from the Dutch vaal, meaning "pale" or "drab", after its muddy-grey waters, and was rendered in Sesotho as Lekwa. The Vaal formed the southern boundary of the South African Republic (Transvaal), and the discovery of the world's largest diamond, near its banks, and gold rushes on its tributaries drove 19th-century settlement.

Pollution

Decades of mining, industrial discharge and failing municipal sewage works — notably in the Emfuleni municipality — have severely polluted the middle Vaal, prompting government intervention and cleanup programmes.
