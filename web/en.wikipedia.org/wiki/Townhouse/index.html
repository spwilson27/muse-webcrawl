Townhouse

A townhouse (or townhome) is a type of terraced or row housing: a multi-storey dwelling that shares one or both side walls with neighbouring houses but has its own entrance at street level and its own plot of land. Townhouses are usually arranged in rows or clusters within urban or suburban developments.

History and usage of the term

Historically, a town house was the city residence of a noble or wealthy family that also owned a country house; the terraced houses of Georgian London and Edinburgh are classic examples. In modern North American usage, "townhouse" describes a housing tenure and form: unlike a condominium apartment, the townhouse owner typically owns the land beneath the unit as well as its exterior, though a homeowners association may maintain common areas. In Britain the equivalent common term is "terraced house", with "town house" retained for prestigious properties.

Characteristics

Townhouses offer more space and privacy than apartments and lower maintenance than detached houses, and are often built near city centres or transit. Typical features include two or three storeys, a small front or rear garden, and parking integrated into the ground floor. Stacked townhouses, common in Canadian cities, place separate units above one another within a townhouse-form building.
