Grootfontein

Grootfontein (Afrikaans for "large fountain") is a city in the Otjozondjupa Region of northeastern Namibia. It is the entry point of the so-called "Golden Triangle" of agriculture, formed by Grootfontein, Otavi, and Tsumeb, a region notable for maize farming. The town had a population of roughly 24,000 in 2023.

History

The settlement developed around a large spring used by the local Haiǁom people. In 1885, Dorsland (Thirstland) Trekker Boers from South Africa established the short-lived Republic of Upingtonia at Grootfontein; the venture collapsed by 1887. Under German colonial rule, a military station was founded in 1896, and the town grew as an agricultural and mining-service center on the railway between Swakopmund and Tsumeb.

During the South African military presence in the 1970s and 1980s, Grootfontein hosted a large military base and airfield, one of the biggest in the territory. After Namibian independence in 1990, the base passed to the Namibian Defence Force.

Geography and sights

Grootfontein lies at an elevation of about 1,460 m in a landscape of bushveld and limestone hills. About 60 km to the west lies the Hoba meteorite, the largest known single meteorite on Earth, composed mostly of iron and weighing an estimated 60 tonnes. The meteorite, declared a national monument, fell less than 80,000 years ago and is a local tourist attraction. The town has a museum in the old German fort (Altes Fort) from 1896.

Economy

The town serves surrounding commercial farms producing maize, groundnuts, and cattle, and provides services for the mining towns of the Otavi Mountain Land. It lies on the B8 road to the Caprivi Strip and on the TransNamib railway.
