Leica II

The Leica II (Model D) is a 35 mm rangefinder camera introduced by Ernst Leitz of Wetzlar, Germany, in 1932. It was the first Leica with a built-in rangefinder coupled to the lens focusing mount, and the first to offer factory interchangeable lenses as part of a complete system. Together with its predecessor, the lens-interchangeable Leica I Model C of 1930, the Leica II defined the standard screw lens mount — the 39 mm "Leica thread mount" — used by dozens of manufacturers for decades.

Features

The Leica II combined the compact body of the earlier models with a coupled rangefinder whose windows sit at either end of the top housing, alongside a separate viewfinder window. Focusing is read directly from the lens's distance scale, and the cloth focal-plane shutter offers speeds from 1/20 to 1/500 second plus bulb. Standard lenses included the collapsible Elmar 50 mm f/3.5, and the system grew to include wide-angle, telephoto and high-speed Summar lenses.

Development

The model was produced until 1948, evolving through wartime and post-war variants (including flash-synchronised models leading to the Leica IIf and IIIf series). Its successors, the III series, added slow shutter speeds on a separate dial; the line culminated in the Leica IIIg of 1957, the last screw-mount model before the bayonet-mount M series took over as the flagship.

Legacy

The Leica II's combination of rangefinder focusing, interchangeable lenses and pocketable size made it the definitive reportage camera of the 1930s and inspired numerous copies and derivatives, notably the Soviet FED and Zorki cameras and Canon's early rangefinders in Japan.
