USS Essex (CV-9)

USS Essex (CV/CVA/CVS-9) was an aircraft carrier and the lead ship of the 24-ship Essex class, the largest class of capital warships of the 20th century. Commissioned on 31 December 1942, she was the fourth US Navy ship to bear the name, and became one of the most decorated carriers of World War II.

Class: Essex-class aircraft carrier
Launched: 31 July 1942
Commissioned: 31 December 1942
Decommissioned: 30 June 1969
Fate: Scrapped 1975
Displacement: ~27,100 tons (standard)

World War II

After shakedown, Essex joined the fast carrier task forces in the Pacific in mid-1943. She participated in the raid on Marcus Island, the Tarawa operation, strikes on Kwajalein, the great carrier raid on Truk (Operation Hailstone, February 1944), the Marianas campaign and the Battle of the Philippine Sea, the Battle of Leyte Gulf (where her aircraft helped sink the Japanese battleship Musashi), and the final strikes on the Japanese home islands, including attacks on Tokyo and support for the Okinawa invasion. She survived typhoons and kamikaze threats without major damage and earned 13 battle stars and a Presidential Unit Citation.

Postwar service

Reactivated for the Korean War, Essex operated off Korea in 1951–53, redesignated an attack carrier (CVA-9). Modernized with an angled deck and enclosed bow under the SCB-125 program, she later served as an antisubmarine carrier (CVS-9) in the Atlantic. In October 1962 she participated in the naval quarantine during the Cuban Missile Crisis, and in 1968 she was the prime recovery ship for the Apollo 7 mission, recovering astronauts Wally Schirra, Donn Eisele and Walter Cunningham.

Decommissioning

Essex was decommissioned on 30 June 1969 and stricken in 1973. She was sold for scrapping in 1975. Her name lives on in later amphibious assault ships, and the class she led remains a benchmark of carrier design.
