Saint Helier (Jèrriais: Saint Hélyi) is one of the twelve parishes of Jersey and is the island's capital and largest town. Located on the south coast of the island, it contains roughly a third of Jersey's population and is the historic centre of the island's government, commerce and port.

History

The parish is named after Saint Helier (Helerius), a 6th-century hermit and martyr who lived on a rock in the bay where Elizabeth Castle now stands, and who is the patron saint of Jersey. The town grew around its harbour; major land reclamation in the 19th and 20th centuries greatly enlarged it. During the German occupation of the Channel Islands in the Second World War (1940–1945), Saint Helier was the centre of the occupation administration. Liberation Day, 9 May, is celebrated annually in Liberation Square.

Landmarks

Elizabeth Castle, built on a tidal islet in St Aubin's Bay beginning in the late 16th century, guards the harbour approaches. Other landmarks include the Royal Square, site of the Battle of Jersey in 1781, the States Chamber, the Parish Church, the Central Market, and the Jersey Museum. The statue of George II in the Royal Square and the Liberation monument are familiar symbols of the town.

Government

The parish is administered by a Connétable (constable) and the Parish Assembly, and sends Deputies and Senators to the States Assembly, Jersey's legislature, which meets in the States Chamber in Saint Helier.

Transport

The town's port handles ferries to England, France and the other Channel Islands, as well as freight. Liberation Station is the island's main bus terminus.
