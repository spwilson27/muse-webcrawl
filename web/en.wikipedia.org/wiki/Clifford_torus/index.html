Clifford torus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stereographic projection of a Clifford torus, a surface in four-dimensional space

In geometric topology, the Clifford torus is the simplest and most symmetric flat embedding of the torus S1 × S1 in four-dimensional space. It is named after the English mathematician William Kingdon Clifford.

Definition

Consider the unit 3-sphere S3 in R4 (or equivalently the complex plane C2 with |z1|2 + |z2|2 = 1). The Clifford torus is the set of points where |z1| = |z2| = 1/√2. As each coordinate traces a circle, the product is a torus embedded in S3.

C = { (z1, z2) ∈ C2 : |z1| = |z2| = 1/√2 }

Properties

  • Flatness: unlike the familiar torus of revolution in R3, the Clifford torus has zero Gaussian curvature; its intrinsic geometry is that of a flat square with opposite sides identified.
  • Minimality: it is a minimal surface in S3, and it divides the 3-sphere into two congruent solid tori.
  • Symmetry: its isometry group is unusually large, acting transitively on the torus.

Visualisation

Because it lives in four dimensions, the Clifford torus is usually shown via stereographic projection into R3, where it appears as a torus of revolution. Animations rotating in R4 reveal its full symmetry.
