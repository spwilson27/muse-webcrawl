Matrix logarithm
Inverse operation of the matrix exponential

In mathematics, a matrix logarithm is another matrix such that when exponentiated, it gives the original matrix. It is thus a generalization of the scalar logarithm and in some sense an inverse function of the matrix exponential. Not all matrices have a logarithm and those matrices that do have a logarithm may have more than one logarithm. The study of logarithms of matrices leads to Lie theory since when a matrix has a logarithm then it is in a Lie group and the logarithm is the corresponding element of the Lie algebra vector space.

Definition

Given a matrix B, another matrix A is said to be a matrix logarithm of B if eA = B, where eA is the matrix exponential of A, defined by the power series expansion that generalizes the exponential function.

Properties

For invertible matrices the logarithm interacts with products only under commuting conditions. If A and B commute, then log(AB) = log A + log B for suitable branches. The principal logarithm of an invertible matrix with no eigenvalues on the negative real axis is the unique logarithm whose eigenvalues lie in the strip with imaginary parts strictly between −π and π.

Computation

Methods include diagonalization when the matrix is diagonalizable, use of the Jordan canonical form, and inverse scaling and squaring methods combined with Padé approximation, which are standard in numerical software.

Applications

Matrix logarithms appear in the study of linear differential equations, statistics (for example in modelling covariance matrices), and in the parametrization of rotation and transformation groups in physics and computer graphics.
