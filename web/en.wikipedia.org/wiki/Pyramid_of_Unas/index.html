The Pyramid of Unas is a smooth-sided pyramid built in the 24th century BC for the Egyptian pharaoh Unas, the last king of the Fifth Dynasty. It is located in the pyramid field at Saqqara, near the Step Pyramid of Djoser. Though now largely ruined, it is of exceptional importance because its burial chambers contain the earliest known example of the Pyramid Texts.

Construction

The pyramid was originally about 43 metres high with a base of about 57.75 metres. Its core was built of roughly cut local limestone blocks in six steps, originally encased in fine white Tura limestone. The casing was later robbed, leaving the stepped core exposed and giving the monument the appearance of a rubble mound.

Substructure and the Pyramid Texts

The substructure follows the standard plan of late Old Kingdom royal pyramids: a descending corridor leads to a vestibule, a horizontal passage guarded by three granite portcullises, an antechamber, and a burial chamber containing a black greywacke sarcophagus. The walls of the antechamber, burial chamber and parts of the corridor are inscribed with columns of hieroglyphs filled with blue pigment — the Pyramid Texts, the oldest known corpus of ancient Egyptian religious literature. The spells were intended to protect the king's remains and ensure his resurrection and ascension to the heavens.

Mortuary complex

The complex includes a mortuary temple against the pyramid's east face, a long causeway decorated with reliefs — including a famous scene of starving desert nomads, sometimes taken as evidence of famine — and a valley temple near the edge of the floodplain. Two large boat pits were found beside the causeway.
