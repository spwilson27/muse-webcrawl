Signal mirror

A signal mirror, also called a signalling mirror, survival mirror or (in its military directional form) a heliograph mirror, is a simple optical signalling device that reflects sunlight toward a target — typically a rescue aircraft, ship or distant party — to attract attention or transmit simple coded messages. Aimed flashes from a small hand mirror can be seen at distances of tens of kilometres in clear air, making the signal mirror one of the most effective items in a survival kit.

Design

Modern survival mirrors are usually made of shatter-resistant acrylic or metal, roughly the size of a playing card, with a small hole in the centre. The back or coating around the hole forms a retroreflective aiming aid: the user holds the mirror close to the eye, sights the target through the hole, and adjusts the mirror until the bright spot of reflected light cast on the hand or face is aligned with a grid or "fireball" seen in the sighting hole, which indicates the beam is on target. Some models use a crosshair grid; military heliographs are larger tripod-mounted mirrors with precise aiming mechanisms.

Use

In daylight, the user flashes the beam across the target repeatedly; a sweep of three flashes, or the SOS pattern, is conventional for distress. Reflected sunlight remains effective even on hazy days and can reach aircraft at altitude — rescues have been credited to mirror flashes spotted from well over 15 km (10 miles) away, and under ideal conditions flashes have been reported visible at distances approaching 100 km. The device requires no power and is standard issue in aircraft and life-raft survival kits.

History

Military heliography — signalling with mirror flashes in Morse-like code — was developed in the later 19th century and used extensively in colonial campaigns and both world wars; the British Army's heliograph could communicate across desert and mountain distances far beyond the reach of flags or lamps. The compact survival mirror with a sighting hole was standardised during the Second World War for aircrew, and its descendants remain in military and civilian emergency kits today.
