Pour le Mérite


Labelled image placeholder: the Blue Max, the Pour le Mérite cross

The Pour le Mérite (French for "For Merit") is an order of merit established in 1740 by King Frederick II of Prussia. It was Prussia's — and later the German Empire's — highest order of merit for military officers, and a separate class for science and the arts, founded in 1842, continues to exist today in the Federal Republic of Germany.

Military class

The military order was awarded strictly for extraordinary personal achievement in war and carried great prestige. During the First World War the decoration, a blue-enamelled Maltese cross worn at the neck, became famous as the "Blue Max" (Blauer Max), associated above all with fighter aces such as Max Immelmann and Manfred von Richthofen. The last military awards were made in 1918, and the order lapsed with the fall of the monarchy.

Civil class

In 1842 Frederick William IV added a class for science and the arts (Pour le Mérite für Wissenschaften und Künste), on the advice of Alexander von Humboldt. Membership is limited to a small number of German and foreign scholars and artists elected by the order's chapter. Presidents of the Federal Republic serve as its protectors, and the order remains one of Germany's most distinguished honours for intellectual and artistic achievement.

Insignia

The badge is a Maltese cross in gold with blue enamel, bearing the crowned initial F of Frederick II and the words Pour le Mérite on its arms. The civil class uses a distinct ring-shaped golden medallion inscribed with the order's name.
