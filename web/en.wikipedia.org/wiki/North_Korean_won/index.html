North Korean won

[Image: North Korean won notes]

The North Korean won, officially the Korean People's won (Korean: 조선민주주의인민공화국 원; symbol: ₩; code: KPW), is the official currency of North Korea. It is issued by the Central Bank of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea and is subdivided into 100 chon. The won is not freely convertible, and its use by foreigners has historically been restricted, with visitors instead using euros or Chinese yuan.

History

The won became North Korea's currency in 1947, replacing the Korean yen. It was revalued in 1959 and again in 1979, and underwent a controversial redenomination in November–December 2009, in which old notes were exchanged at 100:1 with strict limits; the measure disrupted markets and savings and was followed by inflation and a reported official apology. Separate series of notes were long issued for visitors from socialist and capitalist countries.

Coins and banknotes

Current banknotes circulate in denominations of ₩5, ₩10, ₩50, ₩100, ₩200, ₩500, ₩1,000, ₩2,000, and ₩5,000, featuring imagery such as the Mangyongdae birthplace of Kim Il Sung, the Arch of Triumph, and portraits associated with the leadership. Coins are rarely used in practice. Because of the command economy, official exchange rates differ greatly from black-market rates, and much everyday commerce is conducted in foreign currency or through the semi-legal jangmadang markets.
