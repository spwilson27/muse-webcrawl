Saka era

Saka era (Śaka era)
Epoch: 78 CE
Used in: India (national calendar from 1957), Nepal, Southeast Asia
Conversion: Saka year + 78 = CE (approx.)

The Saka era (also Shaka era or Śaka Samvat) is a historical calendar era used in India and neighbouring regions, with its year zero in 78 CE. It was long associated with the Western Satraps and the Indo-Scythian ("Saka") peoples, though various origin traditions connect it with the emperor Kanishka or with King Shalivahana.

Use

The era became widespread in inscriptions across India and Southeast Asia, including Cambodia and Java, where it appears in early Sanskrit records. In 1957 the reformed Indian national calendar adopted the Saka era, with Chaitra as its first month; Saka dates appear alongside Gregorian dates in official Indian publications and the Gazette of India.

Conversion

To convert a Saka date to the Gregorian calendar, add 78 for most of the year (79 for dates after the start of the Gregorian year and before Chaitra).
