The Three Coffins

The Three Coffins (published in the United Kingdom as The Hollow Man) is a 1935 locked-room mystery novel by American author John Dickson Carr, featuring his series detective Dr Gideon Fell. It is frequently cited as one of the greatest locked-room mysteries ever written.

Plot

Professor Charles Grimaud is visited by a mysterious masked stranger who speaks of a man who has risen from the grave and threatens his life. Shortly afterwards Grimaud is found shot dead in his locked study, with no footprints in the snow outside and no weapon in the room; the same night, the illusionist Pierre Fley is shot in a snow-covered street in full view of witnesses, yet no gunman is seen. Dr Fell must untangle the legends of three brothers from Transylvania, said to have been buried alive, that shadow both crimes. The novel is famous for Chapter 17, the "locked-room lecture," in which Fell — openly acknowledging that he is a character in a detective story — delivers a celebrated taxonomy of the methods by which locked-room murders can be committed.

Reception

In a 1981 poll of mystery writers and critics conducted by Edward D. Hoch, The Three Coffins was voted the best locked-room mystery of all time. It remains Carr's most famous novel and a cornerstone of Golden Age detective fiction, noted for its gothic atmosphere, ingenuity, and fair-play construction.
