Digital Markets Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Digital Markets Act (DMA) is an EU regulation (Regulation (EU) 2022/1925) that aims to make digital markets fairer and more contestable. Rather than relying on lengthy competition cases after harm has occurred, the DMA imposes a list of obligations and prohibitions ex ante on very large online platforms designated as gatekeepers — companies that control "core platform services" such as search engines, app stores, operating systems, social networks, and messaging services. It entered into force on 1 November 2022 and its main obligations became applicable on 2 May 2023.

Gatekeeper designation

A company is presumed to be a gatekeeper if it has annual EU turnover of at least €7.5 billion (or market capitalisation of at least €75 billion), provides a core platform service in at least three member states, and has more than 45 million monthly active end users and 10,000 yearly active business users in the EU. In September 2023 the Commission designated six gatekeepers — Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, ByteDance, Meta, and Microsoft — covering 22 core platform services; Booking.com was added in 2024 for its online intermediation service. Designated gatekeepers had six months to comply, with full obligations applying from 7 March 2024.

Obligations

Key "do's and don'ts" include:

  • Allowing users to uninstall pre-installed apps and to install third-party apps and app stores
  • Not favouring the gatekeeper's own products in rankings ("self-preferencing")
  • Not combining personal data across services without consent
  • Allowing business users to promote offers and conclude contracts outside the platform
  • Making number-independent interpersonal communications services (messaging) interoperable with third parties on request
  • Not requiring developers to use the gatekeeper's payment or identification services

Enforcement and impact

The European Commission enforces the DMA directly, with powers to fine, impose behavioural or structural remedies, and, for systematic non-compliance, potentially require divestiture. The Act has prompted visible product changes in the EU, including alternative app marketplaces and browser choice screens on iOS, and choice screens for search and browsers on Android. The Commission opened its first non-compliance proceedings in March 2024 against Alphabet, Apple, and Meta, and issued preliminary findings later that year. Alongside the Digital Services Act, the DMA forms the centrepiece of the EU's digital rulebook, and has been compared with the United Kingdom's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act.
