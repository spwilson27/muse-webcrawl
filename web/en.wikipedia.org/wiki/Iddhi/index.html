Iddhi

Iddhi (Pali; Sanskrit: ṛddhi) is a Buddhist term for supernormal powers or psychic abilities said to be acquired through advanced meditation and spiritual attainment. Iddhis are listed among the fruits of the contemplative life in the early Buddhist texts, such as the Samaññaphala Sutta, and form part of the six higher knowledges (abhiññā).

The iddhi powers

The iddhi-vidha, the variety of psychic powers, is described canonically as including: being one, one becomes many; being many, one becomes one; appearing and vanishing; passing through walls and mountains without obstruction; diving in and out of the earth; walking on water; flying cross-legged through the air; touching the sun and moon; and travelling bodily as far as the Brahma world. Other iddhis include the divine ear (hearing sounds at a distance), knowledge of others' minds, recollection of past lives and the divine eye.

Attitude in the tradition

While the texts accept that iddhis arise naturally from mastery of the four jhānas and the four bases of spiritual power (iddhipāda: desire, energy, mind and investigation), the Buddha discouraged displaying them for show. The Kevatta Sutta recounts his refusal to perform miracles for laypeople, ranking the "miracle of instruction" above psychic displays, and the monastic rules prohibit monks from exhibiting supernormal powers to householders.
