The Alpheios (Greek: Αλφειός, Latin: Alpheus) is the main stream of the Peloponnese, Greece, and the longest river in the peninsula at about 110 kilometres. Its source is near the village of Dorizas in Arcadia, and it flows westward past the ancient sanctuary of Olympia before emptying into the Ionian Sea south of Pyrgos in Elis.

Course

The river rises in the mountains of southeastern Arcadia, flows underground for stretches through karst sinks (katavothres) near the Arcadian plain — a feature noted by ancient writers — and re-emerges before running northwest past Megalopolis. Joined by the Ladon and Erymanthos, it enters Elis, passes the archaeological site of Olympia together with its tributary the Kladeos, and reaches the sea in a broad, marshy delta.

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Alpheus was a river god who fell in love with the nymph Arethusa. Fleeing him, Arethusa was transformed by Artemis into a spring on the island of Ortygia at Syracuse in Sicily; Alpheus was said to flow beneath the sea to mingle his waters with hers — a legend mentioned by Pindar, Virgil and Ovid. The river is also connected with the fifth labour of Heracles, who was said to have diverted the Alpheios and Peneios to cleanse the Augean stables in a single day.

Modern use

The river's waters are used for irrigation in Elis and Arcadia, and a dam near Flokas diverts water for agriculture. The Alpheios valley around Olympia is a protected archaeological and natural landscape.
