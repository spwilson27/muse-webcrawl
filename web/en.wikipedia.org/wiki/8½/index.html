8½

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Guido Anselmi in his white suit and black hat amid the crowd at the spa, megaphone in hand, directing a film that does not exist]

8½ is a 1963 Italian comedy-drama film directed by Federico Fellini, starring Marcello Mastroianni as Guido Anselmi, a famous film director suffering creative block while preparing a science-fiction epic. As Guido retreats to a spa, the film dissolves among his memories, fantasies, mistresses, wife, producer and childhood — the title counting Fellini's filmography to date (eight features and a half, by his accounting of shared credits).

It won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and is a fixture of all-time top tens — the definitive film about filmmaking, and about the male artist's evasions, before the term 'meta-cinema' existed.

Form

The opening traffic-jam dream — Guido floating from his car, tugged down by a rope on his ankle — announced cinema's freest passage between reality and dream. Nino Rota's circus-inflected score, Gianni Di Venanzo's black-and-white, the harem fantasy, the press-conference suicide fantasy and the final round-dance of every character around the circus ring: each sequence became a template. Fellini claimed the film cured his own block by making it his subject.

Influence

Woody Allen's Stardust Memories, Bob Fosse's All That Jazz, the musical Nine (1982; film 2009) and a century of directors' films-about-directing descend from it. It is also the summit of the Fellini-Mastroianni partnership and of Italian cinema's golden age.
