Alfred George Knudson Jr. (9 August 1922 – 10 July 2016) was an American physician and geneticist best known for formulating the "two-hit hypothesis" of cancer causation, one of the foundational ideas of cancer genetics.

Two-hit hypothesis

In 1971, studying retinoblastoma, a rare childhood eye cancer, Knudson statistically compared hereditary and non-hereditary cases and proposed that the tumour develops only after two mutational events ("hits") disable both copies of a protective gene. Children with the inherited form are born with one hit already present, so tumours develop earlier and in both eyes. The hypothesis predicted the existence of tumour suppressor genes, confirmed when the RB1 gene was identified in the 1980s. The two-hit model became a paradigm for understanding how loss of tumour suppressor function drives cancer.

Career and honours

Knudson trained at Columbia and Caltech, worked on childhood cancers in Houston, and spent much of his career at the Fox Chase Cancer Center in Philadelphia. He received the Albert Lasker Award for Clinical Medical Research in 1998 and the Kyoto Prize in 2004.
