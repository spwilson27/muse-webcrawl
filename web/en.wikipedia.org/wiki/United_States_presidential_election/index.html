United States presidential election

The election of the president and vice president of the United States is an indirect election held every four years on Election Day — the Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Voters in each state choose members of the Electoral College, who then elect the president.

Presidential election
Frequency: Every 4 years
Electoral College: 538 electors
Needed to win: 270 electoral votes
First election: 1788–89

Process

Parties nominate candidates through primaries and caucuses and national conventions. In the general election, most states award all their electoral votes to the statewide popular-vote winner (winner-take-all); Maine and Nebraska use a congressional-district method. Electors meet in December; Congress counts the votes in January; the winner is inaugurated on January 20.

Eligibility

The president must be a natural-born citizen, at least 35 years old, and a U.S. resident for 14 years. The Twenty-second Amendment limits a person to two elected terms.

History

Five elections (1824, 1876, 1888, 2000, 2016) produced a president who lost the national popular vote. Contingent elections in the House of Representatives decided 1800 and 1824.
