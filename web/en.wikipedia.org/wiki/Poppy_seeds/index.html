Poppy seeds

Poppy seeds are the kidney-shaped oilseeds obtained from the opium poppy (Papaver somniferum). Used for thousands of years as a food and a source of oil, the tiny seeds — about a millimetre long — are harvested from dried seed pods and are a familiar ingredient in baking and cooking across Europe, the Middle East, and South Asia.

Culinary use

Poppy seeds are sprinkled whole on breads and bagels, ground into fillings for Central and Eastern European pastries such as makowiec and beigli, and used in Indian cooking — often ground with spices or as a thickener in curries, where white poppy seeds (posto in Bengali cuisine) are prized. Pressed poppy seed oil is a mild culinary and artist's oil.

Properties and legality

The seeds themselves contain only trace amounts of opium alkaloids, but surface contamination from pod latex can yield measurable morphine and codeine, occasionally enough to trigger workplace drug tests — a documented phenomenon. Cultivation of the opium poppy for seed is legal in many countries under licence; major producers include Turkey, the Czech Republic, and Australia.
