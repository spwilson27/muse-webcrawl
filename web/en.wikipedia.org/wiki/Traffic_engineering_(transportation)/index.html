Traffic engineering (transportation)

Traffic engineering is a branch of civil engineering that uses engineering techniques to achieve the safe and efficient movement of people and goods on roadways. It focuses mainly on research for safe and efficient traffic flow, such as road geometry, sidewalks and crosswalks, cycling infrastructure, traffic signs, road surface markings and traffic lights.

Scope

Traffic engineers study traffic characteristics — volumes, speeds, and densities — and relationships such as the fundamental diagram linking flow, speed and occupancy. Their work includes signal timing, intersection design, parking studies, travel demand forecasting, and the analysis of congestion. Typical tools are traffic simulation models and the capacity procedures of the Highway Capacity Manual.

History

The discipline emerged in the 1920s and 1930s with mass motorisation; early milestones include the first electric traffic signals, one-way streets, and the Miller Act standards in the United States. The Institute of Transportation Engineers, founded in 1930, is its main professional body.

Traffic systems

Modern practice includes intelligent transportation systems: adaptive signal control, ramp metering, variable message signs and connected-vehicle data. Contemporary traffic engineering increasingly balances vehicle throughput against safety for pedestrians and cyclists, public transport priority and environmental effects such as emissions and noise.
