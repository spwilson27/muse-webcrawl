The Sabor (Croatian: Hrvatski sabor, the Croatian Parliament) is the unicameral legislative body of the Republic of Croatia. Under the Constitution it is the representative body of the citizens and is vested with legislative power. It consists of 151 members elected for a four-year term on the basis of direct, universal and equal suffrage by secret ballot.

History

The Sabor traces its origins to the assemblies of Croatian nobility in the Middle Ages. The first recorded session dates to 1273 at Slavonski Brod, and the diet at Cetin in 1527 elected Ferdinand of Habsburg as king of Croatia after the Battle of Mohács. Through centuries of Habsburg and Austro-Hungarian rule, the Sabor asserted Croatian state rights, notably in the Croatian–Hungarian Settlement of 1868, which granted autonomy in internal affairs.

The Sabor was restored as a multi-party parliament in 1990, and on 25 June 1991 it declared Croatia's independence from Yugoslavia. The chamber elects the government, adopts the budget and constitution, and ratifies international treaties.

Composition

Members are elected from ten territorial constituencies, with additional seats for the Croatian diaspora and for national minorities, including reserved seats for Serb, Hungarian, Italian, Czech and Slovak, and other minorities. Sessions are held at the Parliament Palace on St Mark's Square in Zagreb, next to the seat of government.
