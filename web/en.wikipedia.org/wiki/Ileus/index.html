Ileus

Ileus is a disruption of the normal propulsive ability of the intestine. It can be caused by a mechanical obstruction (obstructive or mechanical ileus) or by the failure of normal intestinal peristalsis (paralytic ileus or pseudo-obstruction). Symptoms include abdominal pain and distension, vomiting, constipation and inability to pass gas.

Causes

Mechanical obstruction may result from adhesions after surgery, hernias, tumours, gallstones or volvulus. Paralytic ileus commonly follows abdominal surgery and can also be triggered by peritonitis, certain medications (especially opioids), metabolic disturbances and spinal injury.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines clinical examination with imaging: abdominal X-rays may show dilated bowel loops and air-fluid levels, while CT scanning helps identify the site and cause of obstruction. Auscultation may reveal high-pitched bowel sounds in obstruction or silence in paralytic ileus.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause. Bowel rest, nasogastric decompression and intravenous fluids are standard supportive measures. Mechanical obstructions often require surgery, particularly if strangulation or ischaemia is suspected, whereas postoperative paralytic ileus usually resolves spontaneously within a few days.
