Organ scholar

An organ scholar is a young musician, typically a university student, who is appointed to play the organ and assist with the music of a college chapel, cathedral, or parish church in return for a scholarship, accommodation, or fees. The system is especially well developed in the collegiate chapels of the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge and in English cathedrals.

Role

Organ scholars accompany the chapel choir at daily services — particularly choral Evensong — play voluntaries before and after services, and increasingly share in conducting and training the choir under the supervision of the director of music or master of the choristers. The post is a training ground: many distinguished British organists and choral directors began as organ scholars.

Selection

At Oxford and Cambridge, organ scholarships are awarded through auditions held before university entry, involving organ performance, keyboard skills such as sight-reading and transposition, and, at some colleges, choir training. Scholars usually read for a degree in any subject while holding the award, though many study music. Cathedrals also appoint organ scholars, often recent graduates, for a year or two of full-time experience.
