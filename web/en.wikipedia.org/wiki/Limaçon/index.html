Limaçon

In geometry, a limaçon or limacon, also called a limaçon of Pascal after Étienne Pascal, is a roulette formed by the path of a point fixed to a circle when the circle rolls around the outside of another circle of equal radius. Its name comes from the Latin limax, meaning "snail", referring to the shape of some of its forms.

Equation

In polar coordinates the limaçon has the equation

r = b + a cos θ

where the ratio of b to a determines the shape. When b = a the curve is a cardioid. When b ≥ 2a the curve is convex. When a < b < 2a it is dimpled, and when b < a it has an inner loop. In Cartesian coordinates the equation is a quartic: (x² + y² − ax)² = b²(x² + y²).

History

The curve was first investigated by Albrecht Dürer, who gave a method for drawing it in his Underweysung der Messung (1525). Étienne Pascal, father of Blaise Pascal, studied it, and Gilles de Roberval named it. The limaçon is a special case of the epitrochoid and a bicircular rational quartic curve; the cardioid and the trisectrix of Maclaurin arise as special members of the family — indeed the limaçon with an inner loop can be used as an angle trisector, hence its alternative name, the trisectrix.
