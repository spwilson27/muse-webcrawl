Pet

A pet, or companion animal, is an animal kept primarily for a person's company or entertainment rather than as a working animal, livestock, or laboratory animal. The most popular pets worldwide include dogs, cats, fish, birds, and small mammals such as rabbits and hamsters.

History

Pet-keeping has ancient roots: dogs were domesticated at least 15,000 years ago, and cats several thousand years later. Keeping animals purely for companionship became widespread among broader social classes in the 19th century, alongside the development of commercial pet food and veterinary care.

Effects and responsibilities

Studies associate pet ownership with companionship, exercise, and some health benefits, though findings are mixed. Ownership entails legal and ethical responsibilities for feeding, shelter, and medical care, and is regulated in many places by licensing, welfare laws, and restrictions on exotic species. Abandonment and overpopulation in shelters remain significant concerns.
