Kansas City

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kansas City skyline from the Liberty Memorial at dusk
The Kansas City skyline from the Liberty Memorial at dusk

Kansas City most commonly refers to the metropolitan area centred on two adjacent cities of the same name at the confluence of the Kansas and Missouri rivers: Kansas City, Missouri (the larger, population about 510,000) and Kansas City, Kansas. The metro area (about 2.2 million) is famous for jazz, barbecue, fountains (more than Rome, it claims) and stockyard-and-rail heritage.

2. Today

History

Founded in the 1830s as a Missouri River port and outfitting point for the Santa Fe, Oregon and California trails ('Westport' landing), Kansas City became a railroad hub and, in the twentieth century, a byword for wide-open machine politics (the Pendergast era) and a cradle of jazz (Count Basie, Charlie Parker) and Negro-league baseball (the Monarchs; the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum stands in the 18th and Vine district). Walt Disney opened his first studio (Laugh-O-Gram) here.

Today

The metro economy runs on logistics, health care, engineering (a national architecture/engineering cluster) and sports — the Chiefs (Super Bowl champions in 2020, 2023 and 2024) and Royals, with Arrowhead Stadium set to host 2026 FIFA World Cup matches. Kansas City barbecue — burnt ends, sweet sauce, 100-plus barbecue restaurants — is its edible flag.
