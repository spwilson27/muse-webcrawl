FastTrack is a peer-to-peer (P2P) protocol that was used by the Kazaa, Grokster, iMesh and Morpheus file sharing programs. FastTrack was the most popular file sharing network in 2003, used mainly to exchange MP3 music files. At its peak the network had millions of simultaneous users, surpassing the early Napster system it succeeded.

History

The protocol was developed by the Estonian programmers Jaan Tallinn, Ahti Heinla and Priit Kasesalu of Bluemoon Interactive — the same team that later created Skype — and was licensed through Sharman Networks and related companies associated with Niklas Zennström and Janus Friis. The first client, KaZaA, was released in 2001, shortly before Napster's shutdown, and the network grew explosively thereafter.

Technology

Unlike Napster, FastTrack used a decentralised "second generation" P2P architecture. Ordinary nodes connected to high-bandwidth, high-uptime supernodes, which maintained indexes of the files shared by their attached nodes and relayed search queries between supernodes. This removed the single central index server whose existence had doomed Napster, and made the network scalable and hard to shut down. The protocol was proprietary and encrypted; files were verified with UUHash, a fast but weak hashing scheme that made the network notably vulnerable to corrupted and mislabelled files and to pollution attacks by content owners.

Decline

FastTrack clients were widely criticised for bundling adware and spyware. Litigation culminated in the U.S. Supreme Court's MGM Studios v. Grokster decision (2005) and a 2006 settlement in which Sharman Networks paid over $100 million to the recording industry and agreed to convert KaZaA into a licensed service. Users migrated to other networks such as eDonkey2000 and BitTorrent, and FastTrack fell into disuse.
