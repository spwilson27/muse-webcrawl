A Tale of Two Cities

A Tale of Two Cities is an 1859 historical novel by Charles Dickens, set in London and Paris before and during the French Revolution. The novel tells the story of the French Doctor Manette, his 18-year-long imprisonment in the Bastille in Paris, and his release to live in London with his daughter Lucie, whom he had never met. The story is set against conditions that led up to the French Revolution and the Reign of Terror. First published in weekly instalments in Dickens's magazine All the Year Round, it is among the best-selling novels of all time, with an estimated 200 million copies sold.

Plot

Jarvis Lorry of Tellson's Bank travels to Paris to retrieve Dr Alexandre Manette, long imprisoned in the Bastille and now "recalled to life". In London, Manette's daughter Lucie marries Charles Darnay, a French émigré who has renounced his aristocratic family, the Evrémondes. During the Revolution, Darnay returns to Paris to aid a former servant and is arrested and condemned as an aristocrat. Sydney Carton, a dissipated English lawyer in love with Lucie, exploits his resemblance to Darnay and his access through the spy Barsad to take Darnay's place in prison, going to the guillotine in his stead with the famous closing words: "It is a far, far better thing that I do, than I have ever done; it is a far, far better rest that I go to than I have ever known."

Themes and style

Opening with the celebrated sentence "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times", the novel contrasts resurrection and sacrifice with mob violence and vengeance, embodied in Madame Defarge and her knitting of the names of the condemned. Dickens drew on Thomas Carlyle's history of the French Revolution for background.
