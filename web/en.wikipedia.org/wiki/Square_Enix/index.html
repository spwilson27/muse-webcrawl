Square Enix Holdings Co., Ltd. is a Japanese multinational holding company, a video game publisher and entertainment conglomerate. It was formed on 1 April 2003 by the merger of Square (founded 1986), developer of the Final Fantasy series, and Enix (founded 1975), publisher of the Dragon Quest series — Japan's two most successful role-playing game houses. The group's best-known franchises also include Kingdom Hearts (with Disney), NieR, Chrono, Mana, SaGa, and Octopath Traveler, as well as the manga imprint Gangan Comics and the arcade company Taito.

History

The merger was partly prompted by Square's heavy losses on the film Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within (2001). After combining, the company expanded into Western development by acquiring Eidos Interactive in 2009, gaining Tomb Raider, Deus Ex, and Hitman-era studios in Montreal and elsewhere. In 2022 Square Enix sold most of its Western studios and IPs — Crystal Dynamics, Eidos-Montréal, and franchises including Tomb Raider — to Sweden's Embracer Group for $300 million, refocusing on Japanese development. Online games are a major revenue pillar, led by the subscription MMO Final Fantasy XIV, whose 2013 relaunch (A Realm Reborn) turned an initially disastrous release into one of the genre's biggest successes.

Business

Beyond games, Square Enix publishes manga through Monthly Shōnen Gangan and related magazines — titles such as Fullmetal Alchemist and Soul Eater originated there — operates amusement facilities and merchandising through Taito, and produces anime, films, and music. Major recent releases include Final Fantasy XVI (2023), Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (2024), and Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake (2024). In 2024 the company announced a restructuring toward multiplatform releases following underperforming console-exclusive titles.
