Ulfilas (also Wulfila, c. 311–383) was a Gothic bishop, missionary, and Bible translator who created the Gothic alphabet and translated the Bible from Greek into the Gothic language. His name in Gothic, Wulfila, means "little wolf". He is credited with converting many Goths to Arian Christianity and is sometimes called the "apostle of the Goths".

The Codex Argenteus, a 6th-century manuscript preserving much of Ulfilas's Gothic translation of the Gospels.

Life

Ulfilas was probably born to a Cappadocian Greek family taken captive by Gothic raiders and raised among the Goths north of the Danube. Fluent in Gothic, Greek, and Latin, he was sent as part of an embassy to Constantinople, where he was consecrated bishop by Eusebius of Nicomedia around 341. Returning to the Goths, he preached for several years before persecution by a Gothic chieftain drove him and his converts across the Danube into Roman territory, where they settled near Nicopolis in Moesia.

The Gothic Bible

To make Scripture accessible, Ulfilas devised an alphabet for Gothic based chiefly on Greek letters, with some Latin and runic elements, and translated the Bible. According to tradition, he omitted the Books of Kings, fearing their accounts of warfare would inflame the Goths' martial instincts. His translation is the principal surviving text in Gothic and the earliest substantial record of any Germanic language. The most famous manuscript, the Codex Argenteus, written in silver and gold ink on purple parchment, is preserved at Uppsala University Library.

Theology and legacy

Ulfilas held Homoian Arian views, confessing the Son to be "like" the Father rather than of the same substance; through his influence, Arian Christianity became the faith of the Goths and, in turn, of other Germanic peoples including the Vandals, Lombards, and Visigoths, shaping their relations with the Nicene Roman world for centuries.
