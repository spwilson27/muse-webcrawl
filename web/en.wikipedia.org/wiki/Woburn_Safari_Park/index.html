Woburn Safari Park is a safari park in Woburn, Bedfordshire, England. Opened in May 1970 by the 13th Duke of Bedford, it was created within the deer park of Woburn Abbey following the success of the drive-through reserves at Longleat. Visitors drive their own vehicles through large enclosures holding free-roaming African and Asian animals, and can then explore a foot safari with smaller animals and attractions.

History

The park was established as part of the Bedford Estates' efforts to make Woburn Abbey self-financing. Its herds descend in part from the famous Woburn deer park, where the 11th Duke of Bedford helped save Père David's deer from extinction and reintroduced the species to China. The safari park opened with lions, tigers, elephants, and giraffes, and expanded over the following decades with new reserves and conservation programmes.

Animals and attractions

The road safari passes through reserves for northern plains animals, African savannah species such as white rhino, giraffe, zebra, oryx and ostrich, a carnivore section with lions, tigers, and bears, and an African forest section. The leisure area includes a foot safari with elephants, sea lions, penguins, lemurs and wallabies, a Go Ape high-ropes course, a miniature railway, and soft-play and boating facilities. The park participates in European breeding programmes (EEPs) for endangered species including Rothschild's giraffe and Asian elephants.
