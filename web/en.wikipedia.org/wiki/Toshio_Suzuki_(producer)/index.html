Toshio Suzuki (born August 19, 1948) is a Japanese film producer of anime and a long-time colleague of Hayao Miyazaki, as well as a co-founder and the president of Studio Ghibli. Suzuki is renowned as one of Japan's most successful producers after the enormous box office success (in Japan) of many Ghibli films. According to Miyazaki, "If it were not for Mr. Suzuki, there would not have been Studio Ghibli."

Career

Suzuki began his career at Tokuma Shoten, the publishing company that later became the parent of Studio Ghibli. He joined the editorial staff of the manga magazine Comic Box and later worked on the anime magazine Animage, where he served as editor-in-chief. At Animage he first met Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata, and he was involved in the production of Miyazaki's film Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984).

In 1985, Suzuki helped found Studio Ghibli together with Miyazaki, Takahata and Yasuyoshi Tokuma. He served as a producer on nearly all of the studio's feature films, including Castle in the Sky (1986), My Neighbor Totoro (1988), Grave of the Fireflies (1988), Princess Mononoke (1997) and Spirited Away (2001), the latter of which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Role at Studio Ghibli

Suzuki became president of Studio Ghibli in 2005, succeeding Isao Takahata. He stepped down as the studio's general manager in 2023 but remained a central figure in its operations and public representation. He is widely credited with Ghibli's marketing strategies, including the famous decision to send a replica katana with a "no cuts" message to Harvey Weinstein during the North American release negotiations for Princess Mononoke.
