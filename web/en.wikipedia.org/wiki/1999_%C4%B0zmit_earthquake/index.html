1999 İzmit earthquake

Date17 August 1999, 03:01 local time
Magnitude7.6 Mw
EpicentreNear Gölcük, Kocaeli Province, Turkey
Casualties17,127–18,373 dead; ~43,953 injured

The 1999 İzmit earthquake (also known as the Gölcük earthquake or the Marmara earthquake) struck northwestern Turkey at 03:01 local time on 17 August 1999. The magnitude 7.6 shock on the North Anatolian Fault ruptured a segment about 130 km long, devastating İzmit, Gölcük, Yalova, Sakarya, and parts of Istanbul.

The earthquake

The rupture propagated along the western North Anatolian Fault at shallow depth. Ground subsidence at Gölcük dropped parts of the shoreline into the Gulf of İzmit, and a small tsunami occurred in the Sea of Marmara. The quake was followed three months later by the magnitude 7.2 Düzce earthquake to the east.

Damage and casualties

Official figures list at least 17,127 deaths and some 44,000 injuries; unofficial estimates run considerably higher. About 120,000 buildings were damaged beyond repair and roughly 600,000 people were left homeless. Investigation revealed widespread violation of building codes — poor concrete and missing reinforcement — which became a national scandal.

Response

The Turkish state's initial response was criticised as slow, while international search-and-rescue teams, notably from Greece, drew wide praise and produced a celebrated thaw in Greek–Turkish relations known as "earthquake diplomacy". A fire at the Tüpraş refinery in İzmit burned for days.

Consequences

The disaster led to stricter building regulation and inspection regimes in Turkey, the creation of a compulsory earthquake insurance scheme (DASK), and heightened public awareness that a similar rupture near Istanbul remains a major risk.
