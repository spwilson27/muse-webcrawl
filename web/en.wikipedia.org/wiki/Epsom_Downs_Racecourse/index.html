Epsom Downs Racecourse is a Grade 1 horse racing venue on the Epsom Downs near Epsom, Surrey, England. Set on public chalk downland high on the North Downs, it hosts the Epsom Derby — Britain's richest horse race and one of the five British Classics — together with the Epsom Oaks and the Coronation Cup, all run at the two-day Derby Festival in early June.

The field rounds Tattenham Corner in front of the grandstands at Epsom.

The course

The Derby course is unlike almost any other in world racing. Roughly horseshoe-shaped and a mile and a half in length, it begins with a climb for the first half-mile, runs downhill — sharply, around the sweeping left-hand bend of Tattenham Corner — and finishes with a demanding uphill straight of about half a mile, complicated by a camber that pulls horses toward the far rail. The combination of gradient, bend, and camber makes Epsom a severe test of a young thoroughbred's balance, agility, and stamina, and many outstanding horses have failed to act on the track. The home straight also provides a five-furlong sprint course that is among the fastest in the world, running almost entirely downhill.

History

Racing on the Downs dates to at least the seventeenth century — a race is recorded in 1661 — and the meeting was a fashionable fixture of the spa town's season. The Oaks was established in 1779 and the Derby in 1780, famously decided (by legend, on the toss of a coin) between the Earl of Derby and Sir Charles Bunbury. The first grandstand was built in 1829–30. The Downs have repeatedly drawn vast crowds — historically free to watch from much of the hill — and major moments of British history: the 1913 Derby, at which suffragette Emily Davison fatally collided with the King's horse, and wartime transfers of the races to Newmarket. The modern Queen Elizabeth II Stand opened in 1992, and the Duchess's Stand, named after the Duchess of Cornwall, opened in 2009. Queen Elizabeth II, a frequent attendee, celebrated her Platinum Jubilee year in 2022 with Derby Day events at the course.

The Derby Festival

The fixture's three Group 1 races are run over the full Derby course of one mile, four furlongs and six yards: the Oaks for three-year-old fillies on Friday (Ladies' Day) and the Derby and Coronation Cup on Saturday. The Derby remains the most prestigious race in Britain for three-year-old colts and the middle leg of the English Triple Crown, between the 2,000 Guineas and the St Leger.
