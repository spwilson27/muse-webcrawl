World record

A world record is usually the best global and most important performance that is ever recorded and officially verified in a specific skill, sport, or other kind of activity. The book Guinness World Records, first published in 1955, collates and publishes notable records of all types, from first and best to worst human achievements, to extremes in the natural world and beyond.

Terminology

In the United States the form "world's record" was formerly popular. Within sport, world records are ratified by each sport's international governing body — for example, World Athletics ratifies world records in track and field athletics, and FINA (World Aquatics) does so in swimming. Marks usually must be set under controlled conditions, with approved equipment and, in athletics, within limits on following wind for sprint and jump events.

Other uses

The phrase is also used for achievements outside sport, such as the largest, tallest, fastest or longest structures and vehicles, the heaviest animals, extreme weather measurements, and huge feats of collecting or endurance. Records are frequently broken as technology, training and nutrition improve; some records, such as long-standing marks in athletics from the 1980s, have endured for decades.

Guinness World Records

Guinness World Records began as a reference book produced by the Guinness brewery to settle pub arguments, and grew into an organisation that adjudicates tens of thousands of record categories. Its annual book is among the best-selling copyrighted books of all time.
