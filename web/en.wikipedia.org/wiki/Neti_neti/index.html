Neti neti

Neti neti (Sanskrit: नेति नेति) is a Sanskrit expression meaning "not this, not this", derived from na iti — "not so". It is a method of analytical meditation and negating description used in Advaita Vedanta to arrive at an understanding of the Self (Atman) and of Brahman by systematically rejecting every candidate identification.

Origin

The phrase appears in the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, in the teaching of the sage Yajnavalkya, who describes the Self as that of which one can only say "not this, not this": it is ungraspable, indestructible, unattached, and unbound, and is not injured or destroyed.

Method

In practice, the meditator successively negates identification with the body, the senses, the mind, the intellect, and all objects of perception — "I am not this" — since whatever can be observed cannot be the observing Self. What remains when all superimpositions (adhyasa) are removed is pure consciousness itself, which is never an object and therefore can only be pointed to by negation.

Relation to apophatic theology

The approach parallels the via negativa (negative theology) in other traditions, in which ultimate reality is characterised by what it is not, because positive predicates would limit it. Adi Shankara's commentaries emphasise that neti neti does not deny the existence of Brahman but denies that any finite description applies to it.

Interpretation

Commentators note that the phrase is a directive (pratishedha) rather than a mere description: it instructs the seeker to withdraw identification from the not-Self. Swami Vivekananda and modern teachers have used neti neti as the analytical counterpart to the positive affirmation of the great sayings (mahavakya) such as "tat tvam asi" — "thou art that".
