The Lebanese Forces (Arabic: القوات اللبنانية, al-Quwwāt al-Lubnāniyya) is a Lebanese Christian-based political party and a former militia during the Lebanese Civil War. Founded in 1976 as the umbrella military organisation of the Lebanese Front, it united the armed wings of the Kataeb Party and other Christian movements under Bachir Gemayel, who became a dominant figure in the war before his assassination in 1982, shortly after being elected president.

After the civil war ended with the Taif Agreement, the militia was disbanded in 1990–91 under the amnesty law, and the Lebanese Forces continued as a political party. Its leader Samir Geagea was imprisoned from 1994 to 2005, the only major militia leader tried after the war; he was released following the Cedar Revolution. The party subsequently participated in the March 14 Alliance and became the largest Christian party in parliament after the 2022 general election.

History

The Lebanese Forces were formed in 1976 by merging the Kataeb Regulatory Forces with the militias of the National Liberal Party (the Tigers), the Guardians of the Cedars, al-Tanzim and others, in the framework of the Lebanese Front's coordination with the Lebanese Army. Commanded by Bachir Gemayel from 1977, the LF fought the Syrian army and Palestinian and Lebanese leftist forces, notably at the battle of Zahle (1981). Gemayel's assassination in September 1982, days before he was to take office as president, precipitated a leadership struggle resolved when Samir Geagea took control in 1986.

Political positions

The party advocates Lebanese sovereignty, the disarmament of non-state armed groups including Hezbollah, and a strong central state. It sits broadly within the opposition to Hezbollah-aligned governments and has contested elections on anti-corruption and reform platforms.
