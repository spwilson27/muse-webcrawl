John Louis Emil Dreyer (13 February 1852 – 14 September 1926) was a Danish–Irish astronomer best known for compiling the New General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars (NGC, 1888), which — with its two Index Catalogue supplements (1895 and 1908) — listed nearly 7,850 deep-sky objects whose "NGC" designations are still used by astronomers today.

Life

Born Johan Ludvig Emil Dreyer in Copenhagen, he developed an early passion for astronomy and in 1874 went to Ireland as assistant at Birr Castle, where the Earl of Rosse's great reflectors, including the 72-inch "Leviathan of Parsonstown", were in use. In 1878 he moved to Dunsink Observatory, Dublin, under Robert Stawell Ball, and in 1882 he became director of the Armagh Observatory, a post he held until 1916. After retiring he settled in Oxford, where he died in 1926.

The New General Catalogue

At Armagh, Dreyer revised and extended John Herschel's General Catalogue of Nebulae (1864), incorporating new observations and correcting errors. Published by the Royal Astronomical Society in 1888, the NGC listed 7,840 objects; the Index Catalogues (IC I, 1895; IC II, 1908) added 5,386 more. Nearly all bright galaxies, nebulae and star clusters are still routinely referred to by their NGC or IC numbers.

History of astronomy

Dreyer was also a leading historian of astronomy. He edited the collected works of Tycho Brahe (15 volumes, 1913–1929) and wrote History of the Planetary Systems from Thales to Kepler (1906), for decades a standard account of early cosmology, as well as a biography of Tycho Brahe (1890). He received the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1916.
