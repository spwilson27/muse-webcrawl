Marne (French pronunciation: [maʁn]) is a department in the Grand Est region of north-eastern France, named after the river Marne which flows through it. Its prefecture is Châlons-en-Champagne, while its largest city is Reims, the historic coronation city of the kings of France and the centre of the Champagne wine trade.

History

Marne was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution, from part of the former province of Champagne. The department was the scene of major fighting in the First World War, including the First Battle of the Marne (1914), which halted the German advance on Paris, and the Second Battle of the Marne (1918). The region's chalk plains and trench lines — around Verdun, the Chemin des Dames and the Argonne — were among the most devastated in France.

Geography and economy

The department is largely an agricultural plain of chalk soils. To the west, the hills around Reims and Épernay form the heart of the Champagne vineyard: the Champagne wine region, with its houses, cellars and villages, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015. Cereals, oilseeds and sugar beet are grown in the eastern plains.

Main towns

Principal communes include Reims, Châlons-en-Champagne, Épernay, Vitry-le-François, Sainte-Menehould and Sézanne. Reims Cathedral, where French kings were crowned, is one of France's great Gothic churches.
