Legal person (legal entity)

In law, a legal person — also called a legal entity, juridical person, or juristic person — is any person or organization that can do the things a human person is usually able to do in law, such as enter into contracts, incur or acquire debts, and sue or be sued. Legal persons are of two kinds: natural persons (individual human beings) and non-natural or artificial persons, which are entities created by law.

Non-natural persons

Non-natural legal persons include corporations, companies, partnerships in some jurisdictions, limited liability companies, incorporated associations, foundations, trade unions, municipalities, and states themselves. Their central feature is separate legal personality: the entity exists independently of the individuals who own, manage, or belong to it, so its rights and liabilities are its own. The principle was famously confirmed for companies in the English case Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897].

A related advantage is limited liability: in entities that confer it, the members' financial responsibility for the entity's debts is limited, typically to the amount they have invested or agreed to contribute.

Creation and dissolution

Artificial persons usually come into existence by a legal act such as registration (incorporation), the grant of a charter, or a statute, and can be ended by dissolution or winding up. Because they can act only through human agents — directors, officers, employees — the law attributes those agents' acts and, in some circumstances, their mental states to the entity, for example in corporate criminal liability.

Extensions of personhood

Legal personhood has occasionally been extended beyond humans and organizations. In India, Hindu deities have been treated as juristic persons capable of owning temple property. In New Zealand, the Whanganui River was granted legal personhood by the Te Awa Tupua Act 2017, with guardians appointed to act in its interests; similar rights-of-nature provisions exist in Ecuador's constitution and in Colombian court rulings on the Atrato River. Campaigns seeking personhood for great apes and other animals have generally failed in court, as in the New York litigation over the chimpanzees Tommy and Kiko and the elephant Happy.
