Intervertebral disc

Intervertebral disc
SystemMusculoskeletal / skeletal
LocationBetween adjacent vertebrae of the spine
ComponentsNucleus pulposus; annulus fibrosus; cartilaginous endplates

An intervertebral disc (or intervertebral fibrocartilage) lies between adjacent vertebrae in the vertebral column. Each disc forms a fibrocartilaginous joint (a symphysis), to allow slight movement of the vertebrae, to act as a ligament to hold the vertebrae together, and to function as a shock absorber for the spine. There are 23 discs in the human spine: 6 in the neck (cervical), 12 in the mid-back (thoracic), and 5 in the lower back (lumbar).

Structure of an intervertebral disc: the gel-like nucleus pulposus is enclosed by the fibrous annulus.

Structure

Each disc consists of an outer annulus fibrosus, concentric lamellae of fibrocartilage rich in type I collagen whose fibres run obliquely in alternating directions to resist torsion, and an inner nucleus pulposus, a gelatinous core of type II collagen, proteoglycans (mainly aggrecan) and water (70–90% in youth) that distributes pressure evenly. Thin cartilaginous endplates anchor the disc to the vertebral bodies. Discs are largely avascular and aneural in the inner regions, receiving nutrients by diffusion from the endplates.

Function

Discs separate the vertebrae, bear compressive loads, and permit limited flexion, extension, lateral bending and rotation. Together they account for roughly a quarter of the length of the spinal column; daily fluid loss and reabsorption account for small diurnal height changes.

Clinical significance

With ageing and degeneration, the nucleus dehydrates and annular tears may allow herniation ("slipped disc"), in which nucleus material protrudes and can compress spinal nerves, causing radiculopathy such as sciatica. Degenerative disc disease is a major contributor to chronic low back and neck pain. Treatments range from physiotherapy and analgesia to epidural injections and surgery (discectomy, laminectomy, spinal fusion, or artificial disc replacement). Discitis is an uncommon infection of the disc space.
