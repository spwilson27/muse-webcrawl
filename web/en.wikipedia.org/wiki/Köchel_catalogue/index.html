Köchel catalogue

The Köchel catalogue (German: Köchel-Verzeichnis) is an inclusive, chronological catalogue of compositions by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, created by Ludwig Ritter von Köchel and first published in 1862. Works are identified by "K." (or "KV") numbers, as in "Symphony No. 41 in C major, K. 551".

History

Köchel, an Austrian musicologist, botanist and geologist, spent decades collecting and dating Mozart's output from manuscripts, letters and early editions. His catalogue listed 626 works in roughly chronological order. Subsequent editions by Paul von Waldersee (1905), Alfred Einstein (1937, the third edition, widely cited as K³), and Franz Giegling, Alexander Weinmann and Gerd Sievers (1964, sixth edition, K⁶) revised dates and attributions, generating alternative numbers for some works.

Numbering

Because revisions renumbered pieces, scholars often cite both the original and the latest numbers, e.g. the Piano Sonata No. 16 in C major, K. 545 / KV 300d in older editions. The catalogue also includes appendices (Anhang, abbreviated Anh.) for lost, fragmentary, doubtful and misattributed works.

Use today

Köchel numbers remain the standard reference system for Mozart's music in recordings, concert programmes and scholarship, despite ongoing research refining the chronology of the works.
