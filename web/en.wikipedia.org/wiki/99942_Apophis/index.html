99942 Apophis

99942 Apophis
Near-Earth asteroid (Aten group)
Discovered 2004
Estimated diameter: about 340–370 metres.

99942 Apophis is a near-Earth asteroid that caused a brief period of public concern after its discovery in 2004, when early orbit calculations suggested a small probability of impact with Earth in 2029. Later observations eliminated that risk. Apophis will nevertheless make an exceptionally close approach on 13 April 2029, passing within the distance of geosynchronous satellites and becoming visible to the naked eye from parts of Europe, Africa and Asia.

Discovery and impact predictions

Astronomers Roy Tucker, David Tholen and Fabrizio Bernardi discovered the object at Kitt Peak National Observatory in June 2004. Because the initial observation arc was short, uncertainty in its orbit briefly produced a Torino scale rating of 4, the highest ever assigned. Radar and precovery observations rapidly refined the orbit and ruled out a 2029 collision, and by 2021 astronomers had also ruled out impacts for at least a century. The asteroid is named for Apophis, the Egyptian deity associated with chaos and darkness.

Physical characteristics

Radar images indicate an elongated, possibly bilobed body, sometimes compared to a peanut, roughly 370 metres long. It is classified as an Sq-type asteroid, with a surface composition broadly similar to ordinary chondrite meteorites. Apophis rotates slowly and in a tumbling state. Earth’s gravity during the 2029 encounter is expected to alter its orbit, moving it from the Aten group to the Apollo group, and may change its spin and surface through tidal stresses.

Exploration

NASA redirected the OSIRIS-REx spacecraft after its return from Bennu; renamed OSIRIS-APEX, it is planned to rendezvous with Apophis shortly after the 2029 flyby and study the effects of the encounter. Other agencies and companies have proposed flyby or rendezvous missions timed to the approach, making Apophis a natural laboratory for planetary defence and asteroid science.
