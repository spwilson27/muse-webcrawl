Dashboard (business)

A dashboard is a type of graphical user interface which often provides at-a-glance views of key performance indicators (KPIs) relevant to a particular objective or business process. In other usage, "dashboard" is another name for a "progress report" or "report", and it is considered a form of data visualization. The term is borrowed from the automobile dashboard, where drivers monitor the major functions of a vehicle at a glance through the instrument cluster.

Types

  • Strategic dashboards support executives with high-level measures of organisational health, typically linked to balanced-scorecard objectives and updated daily or weekly.
  • Analytical dashboards include more context, comparisons, and history, and let users drill down into the data to investigate causes.
  • Operational dashboards display short time-frame, frequently refreshed data — call-centre queues, production status, website traffic — for front-line monitoring.

Features and design

Business dashboards consolidate data from multiple source systems through business-intelligence tools, presenting charts, gauges, maps, and exception alerts on a single screen. Good design practice emphasises showing only decision-relevant metrics, visualising each measure against a target or benchmark, and resisting clutter; critics of dashboard software note that poorly chosen KPIs can mislead, encouraging managers to optimise the displayed numbers rather than underlying performance.

History

The concept descends from the executive information systems of the 1980s and the balanced scorecard framework of Kaplan and Norton in the 1990s, and it spread widely in the 2000s with web-based business intelligence software from vendors such as Tableau, Microsoft (Power BI), and Qlik.
