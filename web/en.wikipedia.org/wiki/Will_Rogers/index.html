Will Rogers

Will Rogers (1879–1935), humorist and entertainer.

William Penn Adair Rogers (4 November 1879 – 15 August 1935) was an American stage and film actor, vaudeville performer, cowboy, humorist, newspaper columnist and social commentator from Oklahoma. Known as "Oklahoma's Favorite Son", he became one of the most popular and beloved public figures in the United States in the 1920s and 1930s.

Career

Born in the Cherokee Nation, Indian Territory, Rogers began as a trick roper in Wild West shows and vaudeville, joining the Ziegfeld Follies in 1915, where his rope tricks were increasingly accompanied by wry commentary on politics and everyday life. He went on to star in silent and talking films for Fox, write a syndicated daily newspaper column read by millions, broadcast on radio, and lecture across the country.

Humour

Rogers's folksy wit was aimed at politicians of all parties: "I am not a member of any organized political party. I am a Democrat." His most famous aphorism, "I never met a man I didn't like", epitomised his genial public persona.

Death and legacy

An enthusiastic aviator, Rogers died in 1935 when the plane piloted by Wiley Post crashed near Point Barrow, Alaska. His death prompted a national outpouring of grief; airports, schools, highways and the Will Rogers Memorial in Claremore, Oklahoma, bear his name.
