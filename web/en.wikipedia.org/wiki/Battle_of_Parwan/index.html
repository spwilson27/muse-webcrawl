The Battle of Parwan was fought in the spring of 1221 near Parwan, north of Ghazni in present-day Afghanistan, between the Khwarezmian prince Jalal al-Din Mangburni and a Mongol army under Shigi Qutuqu, the adopted son of Genghis Khan. It was the only significant Mongol defeat of the Khwarezmian campaign — a victory that sparked revolts across Khwarezm and drew Genghis Khan himself south to destroy Jalal al-Din's army at the Battle of Indus.

Background

While Genghis Khan campaigned in Khwarezm proper, Jalal al-Din — whose father, Shah Muhammad II, had died in flight — rallied an army in Afghanistan, drawing Turks, Afghans and Ghurids to his banner. He won an initial clash with a Mongol force near Kandahar, then took up a position in a narrow valley at Parwan, where the broken ground negated the Mongols' mounted archery.

The battle

Jalal al-Din dismounted much of his army to fight on foot as archers, with his cavalry held in divisions to counter-attack. Over two days of bitter fighting the Khwarezmians gradually drove the Mongols back; a feigned retreat by one Khwarezmian wing — or, in other accounts, a decisive charge on the second day after Jalal al-Din remounted his men — broke the Mongol army, which fled with heavy loss. It was the first time a Mongol army had been beaten in a pitched battle in the western campaign.

Aftermath

The victory electrified the conquered cities: Herat and other towns rose against their Mongol garrisons, which were massacred — and in turn annihilated when the Mongols retook them. But the triumph was squandered: Jalal al-Din's commanders quarrelled over the division of spoils (in one account, over a captured horse), and the Afghan and Ghurid contingents deserted. With barely half his army, Jalal al-Din could not face Genghis Khan, who marched in person to settle the matter, caught him on the Indus in November 1221, and destroyed his army.
