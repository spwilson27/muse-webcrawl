Kremlin Wall Necropolis

The Kremlin Wall Necropolis is the former national cemetery of the Soviet Union, located in Red Square in Moscow, alongside the Moscow Kremlin Wall. Burials there began in November 1917, when 240 pro-Bolshevik victims of the October Revolution were buried in mass graves in Red Square. The necropolis is centred on Lenin's Mausoleum, and the wall itself contains the cremated remains of many prominent Soviet figures in its niches.

History

The tradition began on 10 November 1917, when the victims of the fighting in Moscow during the October Revolution were buried in two mass graves near the Kremlin wall. After Lenin's death in 1924, his embalmed body was placed in a mausoleum in the centre of the necropolis, and burial near his tomb became the highest posthumous honour in the Soviet Union.

Individual graves behind the mausoleum hold Soviet leaders including Joseph Stalin (whose body was displayed beside Lenin's from 1953 until its removal in 1961), Leonid Brezhnev, Yuri Andropov, Konstantin Chernenko, and other senior figures such as Mikhail Kalinin, Andrei Zhdanov, and the military commander Georgy Zhukov's ashes in the wall. The wall's niches contain the urns of cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin, writer Maxim Gorky, rocket designer Sergei Korolev, and foreign communists including the American journalist John Reed and the German politician Clara Zetkin.

Later years

The last burial in the necropolis was that of Konstantin Chernenko in March 1985. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union, burial there ceased, though the site remains a place of commemoration, guarded ceremonially and visited by veterans and communist sympathisers on anniversaries. Debate has periodically arisen in Russia over whether the necropolis and Lenin's body should be removed from Red Square, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
