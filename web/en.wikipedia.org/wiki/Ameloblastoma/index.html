Ameloblastoma

Ameloblastoma
Type: Benign odontogenic tumour
Usual location: posterior mandible
Treatment: surgical excision

Ameloblastoma is a rare, benign or cancerous tumour of odontogenic epithelium (ameloblasts, or the cells that form tooth enamel) much more commonly appearing in the lower jaw than the upper jaw. It arises from remnants of the dental lamina and related tissues. Although classified as benign, ameloblastoma is locally aggressive, grows slowly, and can cause extensive destruction of the jaw if untreated.

Signs and presentation

Patients commonly present with a painless, slowly expanding swelling of the jaw, most often in the posterior mandible near the molar region and ramus. Radiographs typically show a multilocular ("soap-bubble" or "honeycomb") radiolucency. Types include conventional (multicystic), unicystic, and peripheral (extraosseous) variants.

Treatment

Treatment is primarily surgical. Because conservative removal has a high recurrence rate, wide resection with clear margins is often recommended for conventional ameloblastoma, sometimes requiring jaw reconstruction. Rarely, the tumour metastasises or undergoes malignant transformation (ameloblastic carcinoma). Many tumours harbour BRAF V600E or SMO mutations, which has opened possibilities for targeted therapy in selected cases.
