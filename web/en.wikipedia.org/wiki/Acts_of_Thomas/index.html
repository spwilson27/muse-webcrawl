Acts of Thomas

The Acts of Thomas is an early Christian apocryphal work, written in Syriac probably in the early third century AD in the region of Edessa. It is one of the most complete of the apocryphal acts of the apostles, recounting the mission of Thomas the Apostle ("Judas Thomas," presented as the twin of Jesus) to India.

[Image: Saint Thomas, apostle to India]

Contents

In the narrative, the apostles cast lots for the nations of the world; Thomas draws India but reluctantly refuses until Jesus sells him as a slave-carpenter to a merchant of King Gundaphorus. In India Thomas builds the king a heavenly palace by spending the construction funds on the poor, converts royalty and nobility, performs miracles and exorcisms, and — in a second mission — serves King Misdaeus, whose wife and family he converts, leading to his martyrdom by spears. The work contains the famous Hymn of the Pearl, a Gnostic allegory of the soul's descent and return, and the Hymn of the Bride. Encratite in outlook, it preaches abstinence from marriage as central to salvation.

Legacy

The Acts underpins the tradition of the Saint Thomas Christians of Kerala, who trace their origins to Thomas's mission in India. The text survives complete in Syriac and Greek versions.
