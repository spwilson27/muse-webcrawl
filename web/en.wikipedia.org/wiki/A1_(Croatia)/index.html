A1 (Croatia)

A1 motorway
Country: Croatia
Route: Zagreb – Split – Dubrovnik direction
Length: about 480 km including completed southern sections.

The A1 is Croatia’s principal north–south motorway, running from Zagreb through Karlovac, the Lika region and Dalmatia toward Split and onward toward Ploče and the Dubrovnik area. It transformed travel between the capital and the Adriatic coast, replacing long journeys over mountain roads with a high-speed tolled route and becoming central to Croatian tourism and freight movement.

Route

From Zagreb the motorway passes Karlovac and follows the corridor through Bosiljevo, where it connects with the A6 toward Rijeka. It continues through tunnels and viaducts across Lika, passes Zadar and Šibenik, reaches Split and Dugopolje, then continues south past Vrgorac toward Ploče. The Pelješac Bridge, opened in 2022, connected the southernmost part of Croatia around Dubrovnik to the rest of the national road network without crossing Bosnia and Herzegovina at Neum, and further motorway construction southward has been planned and advanced in stages.

Engineering

The A1 is known for major structures built through difficult karst and mountain terrain. The Sveti Rok Tunnel carries the road beneath the Velebit range, while long viaducts and the Krka Bridge near Skradin cross deep river valleys. Seasonal winds, especially the bora, can force restrictions on exposed sections.

Importance

Before the motorway, summer traffic to Dalmatia depended on slower coastal and inland roads. The A1 shortened Zagreb–Split journeys to a few hours and, together with other Croatian motorways, formed part of the state-building and European-integration programme after independence. Tolls and heavy holiday congestion remain recurring public topics.
