Brunn–Minkowski theorem

FieldMathematical analysis; convex geometry
Named afterHermann Brunn; Hermann Minkowski
Statement typeInequality for volumes of Minkowski sums

In mathematics, the Brunn–Minkowski theorem (or Brunn–Minkowski inequality) is an inequality relating the volumes (Lebesgue measures) of compact subsets of Euclidean space. It is a fundamental result in the theory of convex bodies and in functional analysis.

Statement

Let A and B be non-empty compact subsets of n-dimensional Euclidean space, and let A + B denote their Minkowski sum, the set of all sums a + b with a in A and b in B. Then the n-th root of the volume of A + B is at least the sum of the n-th roots of the volumes of A and B. Equivalently, volume raised to the power 1/n is a concave function under Minkowski addition.

Significance

The theorem, with early versions due to Hermann Brunn (1887) and Hermann Minkowski (1896), implies the classical isoperimetric inequality and underlies results such as the Prékopa–Leindler inequality. Equality holds for homothetic convex bodies. The inequality is a cornerstone of the Brunn–Minkowski theory of mixed volumes.
