Augusta is a small city in St. Charles County, Missouri, United States, situated on the north bank of the Missouri River in the heart of Missouri's wine country. Founded in 1836 by Leonard Harold, a follower of Daniel Boone, the town was originally named Mount Pleasant. It is renowned for its wineries and is regarded as the first designated wine district in the United States.

History

German immigrants settled the area in the 1830s, attracted by reports comparing the Missouri River valley to the Rhineland. They planted vineyards on the south-facing bluffs, and Augusta became an important wine-producing centre. In 1980 the Augusta AVA (American Viticultural Area) was established — the first AVA designated in the United States, preceding even Napa Valley's designation.

Wine country

Today Augusta and the surrounding hills are home to several wineries, including Mount Pleasant Estates (founded in 1859), Augusta Winery, and Montelle Winery, perched on the bluffs above the town. The area forms part of the Missouri Rhineland, a stretch of the river valley settled heavily by Germans.

Recreation

The Katy Trail, a rail trail running across Missouri along the former Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad corridor, passes through Augusta, making the town a popular stop for cyclists. The town hosts an annual Harvest Festival and "Swingin' in the Vines" events, and its historic district preserves many 19th-century brick buildings.

Geography

Augusta lies about 35 miles west of St. Louis, on State Route 94. The surrounding terrain consists of steep river bluffs and rolling hills drained by tributaries of the Missouri River.
