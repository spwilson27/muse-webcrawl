The American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command was a short-lived joint Allied supreme command established in January 1942 to coordinate the defence of South-East Asia and the South West Pacific against Japanese advances in the opening months of the Pacific War. It was the first truly multinational Allied command of the Second World War.

Formation

Following the Arcadia Conference in Washington, the ABDA Command was activated on 15 January 1942 under British General Sir Archibald Wavell, with headquarters initially at Lembang near Bandung on Java. Its area stretched from Burma and Malaya through the Dutch East Indies to northern Australia. American General George Brett served as deputy commander; subordinate commands covered land forces (under Dutch General Hein ter Poorten), air forces (Air Chief Marshal Sir Richard Peirse, later Brett), and naval forces (US Admiral Thomas C. Hart, succeeded by Dutch Admiral Conrad Helfrich).

Operations and dissolution

ABDA forces faced overwhelming Japanese air and naval superiority. Singapore fell on 15 February 1942, and Japanese landings on Java followed. On 25 February 1942, with the East Indies indefensible, the command was dissolved and Wavell returned to India. Days later, the ABDA naval striking force was destroyed at the Battle of the Java Sea (27 February 1942), where Admiral Karel Doorman was killed. Java surrendered in early March.

Legacy

Despite its failure, ABDA provided lessons in coalition warfare that informed later unified commands, notably MacArthur's South West Pacific Area and Mountbatten's South East Asia Command.
