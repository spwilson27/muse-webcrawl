Leon Cooper

DiedOctober 23, 2024 (aged 94), Providence, Rhode Island, U.S.

AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1972)

Leon N. Cooper (February 28, 1930 – October 23, 2024) was an American theoretical physicist and neuroscientist. He shared the 1972 Nobel Prize in Physics with John Bardeen and John Robert Schrieffer for the BCS theory of Superconductivity, founded on his demonstration that electrons in a metal can bind into Cooper pairs.

Career

Cooper received his PhD from Columbia University in 1954 and, after postdoctoral work, joined Bardeen at the University of Illinois. In 1956 he published the calculation showing the Fermi sea is unstable to pair formation — the "Cooper problem" — which became the foundation of the BCS theory completed the following year. From 1958 he was at Brown University, where he later turned to neuroscience, co-developing the Bienenstock–Cooper–Munro theory of synaptic plasticity and founding research institutes in brain science.
