Vir Chakra

The Vir Chakra (pronounced Veer Chakra) is an Indian wartime military bravery award presented for acts of gallantry on the battlefield, on land, at sea, or in the air. It is third in precedence among India's wartime gallantry awards, after the Param Vir Chakra and the Maha Vir Chakra, and is the equivalent of the British Military Cross and the American Silver Star in relative standing.

History

The award was instituted by the President of India on 26 January 1950, with effect from 15 August 1947, replacing British honours for Indian personnel after independence. It may be awarded posthumously and can be conferred on members of the Army, Navy, and Air Force, reserve and territorial forces, and in some cases civilians serving with the armed forces.

Design

The medal is a circular silver medal 1 3/8 inches in diameter bearing, on the obverse, a five-pointed star with the national emblem at its centre, and on the reverse the words "Vir Chakra" in Hindi and English around a lotus motif. It is worn on the left breast suspended from a ribbon that is half deep orange and half dark blue.

Recipients

More than a thousand awards have been made for operations in the wars of 1947–48, 1962, 1965, 1971, the Kargil conflict of 1999, and subsequent counter-insurgency and peacekeeping operations. Recipients use the post-nominal letters VrC.
