Isotropic etching

Isotropic etching is a method used in microfabrication to remove material from a substrate in all directions at equal rates. Unlike anisotropic etching, which etches preferentially in particular crystallographic or vertical directions, an isotropic etchant attacks the material uniformly, undercutting the mask by roughly the same amount as the depth of the etch.

Mechanism

In wet chemical etching, isotropic profiles are typical: the etchant reaches exposed surfaces from all sides and removes material laterally as well as vertically. The resulting trench profile is approximately a quarter-circle under each mask edge. Common isotropic etchants include mixtures of hydrofluoric, nitric and acetic acids (HNA) for silicon, and hydrofluoric acid for silicon dioxide.

Applications

Isotropic etching is used to release microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) structures by undercutting sacrificial layers, to shape glass and metal parts, and for defect delineation in semiconductor wafers. Its lack of directionality is a drawback where narrow, deep, vertical-walled features are needed, which instead require anisotropic dry processes such as deep reactive-ion etching.
