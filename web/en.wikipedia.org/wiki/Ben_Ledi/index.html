Ben Ledi (Scottish Gaelic: Beinn Leitir) is a mountain in the Trossachs area of Scotland, near the village of Callander in the council area of Stirling. At 879 metres (2,884 ft), it is the highest peak in the immediate area south of the Highlands boundary and is classified as a Corbett and a Marilyn.

The name is sometimes interpreted as "hill of the slope", though a popular folk etymology connects it with the Beltane fires once said to have been lit on its summit. In earlier times the hill was the focus of May Day ceremonies.

Ascent

The most popular route begins from a car park on the A84 road near Kilmahog, following a constructed path up the south-eastern ridge through forestry onto open hillside, past a small lochan, to the summit cairn and trig point. The summit offers extensive views over the Firth of Forth to the east, the Arrochar Alps to the west, and on clear days as far as the Isle of Arran.

In literature

Ben Ledi stands at the heart of the landscape celebrated by Sir Walter Scott in his poem The Lady of the Lake (1810), set around nearby Loch Katrine, which helped make the Trossachs a celebrated destination for early tourism.
