Willemstad (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈʋɪləmstɑt]) is the capital and largest city of Curaçao, an island in the southern Caribbean Sea that forms a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. The city was founded in 1634, shortly after the Dutch West India Company captured the island from Spain, and was named after Prince William II of Orange.

The city centre, with its unique architecture blending Dutch colonial styles with Caribbean influences, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997 under the name "Historic Area of Willemstad, Inner City and Harbour, Curaçao".

History

The Dutch founded a trading post at the Schottegat, a large natural harbour, in 1634. Over the following decades the settlement grew on both sides of the entrance to the harbour, around Sint Anna Bay. The two oldest districts, Punda on the eastern side and Otrobanda ("the other side") on the western side, were connected by ferry until the Queen Emma Bridge, a floating pontoon bridge nicknamed the "Swinging Old Lady", opened in 1888. The much higher Queen Juliana Bridge, opened in 1974, now carries road traffic across the bay.

Willemstad developed into an important centre for trade, including the transatlantic slave trade, and later for oil refining after the Royal Dutch Shell refinery was built in 1918 to process Venezuelan crude. The refinery was one of the largest in the world and dominated the island's economy for much of the twentieth century.

Districts

Punda, the oldest quarter, is known for the brightly coloured gabled buildings of the Handelskade along the waterfront. An often-repeated legend holds that the buildings were painted in pastel colours on the orders of Governor Albert Kikkert, who claimed the glare of the white buildings gave him headaches. Otrobanda contains the Kura Hulanda museum complex documenting the history of slavery on the island. Other districts include Pietermaai, Scharloo and Santa Maria.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Fort Amsterdam, which houses government offices; the Mikvé Israel-Emanuel Synagogue (1732), the oldest synagogue in continuous use in the Americas, whose floor is covered in sand; and the Curaçao Museum. The floating market, where Venezuelan boats sell produce and fish, is a popular attraction in Punda.

Transport

Willemstad is served by Curaçao International Airport (Hato), about 12 kilometres northwest of the city. The port remains an important cruise ship destination.
