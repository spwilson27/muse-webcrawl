The Artaxiad dynasty (also Artashesian dynasty) ruled the ancient Kingdom of Armenia from 189 BC until AD 12. Founded by Artaxias I, a former Seleucid general, the dynasty raised Armenia to the status of a major power, reaching its zenith under Tigranes the Great.


Coin of Tigranes the Great, the dynasty's greatest king

Origins

After the Seleucid defeat at Magnesia (190 BC), the strategoi Artaxias and Zariadres declared themselves kings in Greater Armenia and Sophene respectively. Artaxias founded the capital Artaxata on the Araxes river, reportedly on the advice of Hannibal, and organised the kingdom's administration and landholding.

Tigranes the Great

Under Tigranes II (r. 95–55 BC), Armenia briefly became an empire stretching from the Caspian to the Mediterranean: he annexed Syria and parts of Mesopotamia and Cilicia, founded Tigranocerta, and assumed the title "King of Kings." Roman victories under Lucullus and Pompey ended Armenian expansion, and Armenia became a buffer between Rome and Parthia.

Decline

Later Artaxiads ruled under Roman or Parthian suzerainty. Artaxias II, Artavasdes II (captured by Mark Antony), Tigranes III and IV and Queen Erato successively held the throne; with Erato's deposition in AD 12 the line ended, and Armenia passed to foreign-appointed kings until the Arsacids took over.
