Ventriloquism

Ventriloquism is an act of stagecraft in which a performer (a ventriloquist) makes their voice appear to come from another source, usually a puppet or "dummy" operated by the performer. The name derives from Latin venter (belly) and loqui (to speak) — "speaking from the belly" — reflecting the ancient belief that the voice originated in the stomach.

Technique

Ventriloquists speak with minimal lip movement, substituting sounds that can be produced without the lips for bilabial sounds (b, p, m). The illusion is strengthened by visual misdirection: the audience watches the dummy's moving mouth and associates the voice with it — an effect related to the ventriloquism effect studied in perception psychology, in which vision captures the apparent location of a sound.

History

The practice has ancient roots in religious divination, where priests appeared to make statues or spirits speak. It became a music-hall and vaudeville entertainment in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Famous performers include Edgar Bergen with Charlie McCarthy, Shari Lewis with Lamb Chop, and Jeff Dunham. Ventriloquism remains popular in variety shows, children's entertainment and comedy.
