John Clifton Bogle (May 8, 1929 – January 16, 2019) was an American investor and financial executive, best known as the founder of The Vanguard Group and the creator of the first index mutual fund available to individual investors. He is credited with popularising index investing and dramatically lowering the cost of investing for ordinary savers.

Early life and career

Bogle's family lost its fortune in the Great Depression. He won a scholarship to Blair Academy and then Princeton, where his senior thesis on mutual funds brought him to the attention of Wellington Management. He rose to lead Wellington, but a disastrous merger led to his firing in 1974.

Vanguard and the index fund

In 1974 Bogle founded Vanguard, structured uniquely as a mutual company owned by its own funds — and therefore by its investors — so that it operates at cost. In 1976 he launched the First Index Investment Trust (now the Vanguard 500 Index Fund), tracking the S&P 500. Derided at first as "Bogle's folly," index funds eventually overtook actively managed funds in assets as evidence accumulated that most active managers fail to beat the market after fees. Bogle served as Vanguard's chairman and CEO until 1996 and remained a public advocate for low costs and long-term investing until his death.

Books and influence

Bogle wrote ten books, including Common Sense on Mutual Funds (1999) and The Little Book of Common Sense Investing (2007). His devoted followers call themselves "Bogleheads." Warren Buffett praised him as having "done more for American investors than any other individual." He was named one of the world's "four investment giants of the twentieth century" by Fortune in 1999 and appeared on the Time 100 list in 2004.
