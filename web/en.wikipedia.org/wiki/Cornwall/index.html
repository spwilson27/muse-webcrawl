Cornwall (Cornish: Kernow) is a ceremonial county in South West England, forming a peninsula bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the north and west, Devon to the east, and the English Channel to the south. It is recognised as one of the Celtic nations, with its own language, Cornish.

History

Inhabited since prehistory, Cornwall was a major source of tin mined and traded across Europe from the Bronze Age. It retained a distinct Brythonic culture and language into the early modern period. In the 18th and 19th centuries Cornish mining — tin and copper — reached industrial scale, and the Cornish diaspora spread mining expertise worldwide.

Geography

The county has a long, rugged coastline, moorlands including Bodmin Moor, and a mild maritime climate popular with tourists. The unitary authority is Cornwall Council, based in Truro, its only city.

Culture

Symbols include the flag of Saint Piran (a white cross on black), the Cornish pasty, and the revived Cornish language. In 2014 Cornish people were granted national minority status under the Council of Europe framework.
