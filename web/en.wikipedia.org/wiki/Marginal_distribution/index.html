Marginal distribution

In probability theory and statistics, the marginal distribution of a subset of a collection of random variables is the probability distribution of the variables in that subset, considered without reference to the values of the others.

Definition

For two discrete random variables X and Y with joint probability mass function P(X = x, Y = y), the marginal distribution of X is obtained by summing the joint probabilities over all values of Y:

P(X = x) = Σy P(X = x, Y = y)

For continuous variables, the marginal density of X is obtained by integrating the joint density over Y. The term "marginal" comes from the practice of entering these sums in the margins of a contingency table of joint frequencies.

Relation to conditional distributions

The marginal distribution contrasts with the conditional distribution, which gives the probabilities of X given a known value of Y. If X and Y are independent, the marginal distribution of X equals its conditional distribution for every value of Y, and the joint distribution factorizes into the product of the marginals.
