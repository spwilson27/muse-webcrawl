The Watchers (Aramaic: עִיר, ʿiyr, "watcher, awake one"; Greek: ἐγρήγοροι, egrēgoroi) are angels mentioned in the biblical Book of Daniel and developed at length in Second Temple Jewish literature, above all the Book of Enoch. In the developed myth they are angels sent to earth who transgress by taking human wives and teaching forbidden arts, becoming the archetype of the fallen angel.

In the texts

In Daniel 4, King Nebuchadnezzar sees a "watcher, a holy one" descend from heaven in a dream. The Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 1–36) expands Genesis 6's brief account of the "sons of God": two hundred Watchers under Shemihazah descend on Mount Hermon, marry human women and father the giant Nephilim; Azazel and others teach metalwork, weapons, cosmetics and sorcery, corrupting humanity and precipitating the Flood. The archangels bind the Watchers beneath the earth until the final judgment. The Book of Jubilees and the Damascus Document preserve related traditions, and the myth influenced early Christian and later occult interpretations of fallen angels.

Legacy

The Watchers feature prominently in the Ethiopian church's canon (which retains 1 Enoch), in scholarly reconstructions of apocalyptic Judaism, and in modern popular culture, literature and games. Some scholars compare them with the Mesopotamian apkallu sages.
