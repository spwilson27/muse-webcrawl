Soil texture

Soil texture is a classification used in soil science to describe the relative proportions of different grain sizes of mineral particles in a soil. The three size classes are sand (0.05–2.0 mm), silt (0.002–0.05 mm), and clay (less than 0.002 mm), per the USDA system. Texture strongly influences water retention, drainage, aeration, and fertility.

Texture classes

Combinations of the three fractions define texture classes — sand, loamy sand, sandy loam, loam, silt loam, silt, sandy clay loam, clay loam, silty clay loam, sandy clay, silty clay, and clay — conventionally plotted on a triangular diagram. Loam, a balanced mixture, is considered ideal for most agriculture because it retains moisture and nutrients while draining well.

Determination

Texture is measured in the laboratory by sieving and sedimentation (hydrometer or pipette methods) to determine particle-size distribution, or estimated in the field by the "feel method", in which a moistened sample is rubbed between the fingers and rolled into ribbons; clay content is judged by stickiness and ribbon length.

Effects

Sandy soils drain quickly and warm early but hold few nutrients; clay soils hold water and nutrients but drain poorly and can be difficult to work; silty soils are fertile but prone to compaction and erosion.
