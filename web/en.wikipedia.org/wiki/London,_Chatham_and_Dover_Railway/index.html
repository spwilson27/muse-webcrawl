London, Chatham and Dover Railway

The LCDR coat of arms combined the arms of London, Rochester, Chatham and Dover

The London, Chatham and Dover Railway (LCDR) was a railway company in south-eastern England created on 1 August 1859, when the East Kent Railway changed its name. Its lines ran through London and northern and eastern Kent to the ports of Dover and Folkestone, and it was fiercely competitive with the neighbouring South Eastern Railway (SER).

History

The LCDR built lines to serve the growing London suburbs and the Kent coast, reaching Dover in 1861 and opening the Chatham Main Line through north Kent. Chronic financial weakness followed over-ambitious expansion: in 1866 the collapse of the bank Overend, Gurney and Company, to which the LCDR had heavy exposure, pushed the company into a decade under a court-appointed receiver, during which construction stalled and shareholders received nothing.

Fierce rivalry with the SER in the 1860s and 1870s produced duplicated routes and ruinous fare competition. Relations improved gradually, and from 1 January 1899 the two companies formed a single management committee, the South Eastern and Chatham Railway Companies Joint Management Committee, while remaining legally separate. Both companies passed into the Southern Railway at the 1923 grouping.

Legacy

The LCDR's principal routes — the Chatham Main Line and the line through Bromley and Herne Hill — remain important commuter arteries into London Victoria today. Its motto, Invicta, the symbol of Kent, appeared on its locomotives and crests.
