Second shift


Coined by: Arlie Russell Hochschild
Key work: The Second Shift (1989)

The second shift is a term coined and popularised by sociologist and author Arlie Russell Hochschild in her 1989 book The Second Shift: Working Parents and the Revolution at Home. It describes the unpaid labour performed at home — housework and the care of children — in addition to paid work performed in the formal sector. In the book, Hochschild and co-author Anne Machung studied couples in the San Francisco Bay Area and found that women still did most of the household work even when both partners worked full-time jobs.

Findings

Hochschild calculated that women worked roughly fifteen hours more per week than men, adding up to an extra month of twenty-four-hour days each year, which she called the "leisure gap." She described the "stalled revolution": women had entered the workforce in large numbers, but workplaces and men's contributions at home had not adapted in kind. The book also introduced related concepts such as the "economy of gratitude" between spouses.

Legacy

The second shift became a widely used concept in sociology, feminist economics, and public debate about work–life balance. Subsequent time-use research in many countries has continued to find a gender gap in unpaid domestic and care work, though its size varies and has narrowed in some places. The term is distinguished from "double day" and relates to the later concept of the mental load.
