The Beautiful South

The Beautiful South were an English pop rock group formed in Hull in 1988 by Paul Heaton and Dave Rotheray, former members of the Housemartins. The band were noted for pairing bright, melodic, easy-listening arrangements with Heaton's sardonic and often darkly comic lyrics about drinking, debt, infidelity and working-class life. Their ironic name was chosen because most members came from the north of England.

History

The group's debut single, "Song for Whoever", reached number two in the UK in 1989, and the album Welcome to the Beautiful South established their signature sound, with female co-vocalists (first Briana Corrigan and later Jacqui Abbott) answering Heaton's lead. In 1990 they reached number one with the duet "A Little Time", which won the Brit Award for Best British Video. The compilation Carry On up the Charts (1994), collecting the singles to date, became one of the fastest-selling albums in British chart history and is said to be owned by one in seven British households. Subsequent albums included Blue Is the Colour (1996), containing the hit "Don't Marry Her", Quench (1998) and Painting It Red (2000).

Break-up and aftermath

The band announced their split on 31 January 2007, explaining the decision with characteristic economy as due to "musical similarities". Heaton pursued a solo career and later a successful partnership with Jacqui Abbott, releasing several top-five albums; Dave Rotheray formed the Homespun project and later the Good Citizens. Over their career the Beautiful South sold over 15 million records worldwide.
