Epicycloid

Type: Roulette curve
Generated by: A point on a circle rolling around the outside of another circle
Special cases: Cardioid, nephroid

An epicycloid is a plane curve produced by tracing the path of a chosen point on the circumference of a circle — called an epicycle — which rolls without slipping around the outside of a fixed circle. It belongs to the family of curves known as roulettes, and is closely related to the hypocycloid, which is generated by a circle rolling inside the fixed circle.

Equations

If the fixed circle has radius R and the rolling circle has radius r, the epicycloid can be described parametrically. With k = R/r, the curve has k cusps when k is an integer. When the two circles have equal radii (k = 1) the curve is a cardioid; when R = 2r it is a nephroid.

History

Curves of this type were studied by ancient Greek astronomers in connection with the epicyclic model of planetary motion, and were later treated by Albrecht Dürer, who described epicycloids in his work on geometry in 1525. Ole Rømer and other seventeenth-century mathematicians investigated the curve's use in gear tooth profiles, since epicycloidal teeth reduce friction and wear.

Properties

The arc length and area enclosed by an epicycloid can be computed in closed form. For a curve with parameter k, the area is (k + 1)(k + 2)πr² and the arc length is 8(k + 1)r when k is an integer. Evolutes of an epicycloid are similar epicycloids, a property that made them attractive in horology and gear design.
