Recent African origin of modern humans

The recent African origin of modern humans, also called the Out of Africa theory or recent single-origin hypothesis, is the dominant scientific model of the geographic origin and early migration of anatomically modern humans (Homo sapiens). It proposes that modern humans evolved in Africa — with fossils at Jebel Irhoud in Morocco dated to about 300,000 years ago among the earliest evidence — and that one or more waves of migration out of Africa, principally between roughly 70,000 and 50,000 years ago, populated the rest of the world, largely replacing archaic human populations such as Neanderthals and Denisovans.

Evidence

The model is supported by converging lines of evidence. Genetic studies show that African populations harbour the greatest genetic diversity, with diversity declining with distance from Africa — the signature of a serial founder effect. Mitochondrial DNA and Y-chromosome lineages coalesce to African ancestors ("Mitochondrial Eve" and "Y-chromosomal Adam") living within the last few hundred thousand years. The fossil record, including Omo remains in Ethiopia (~230,000 years old) and Herto (~160,000 years old), documents early modern morphology in Africa long before comparable fossils appear elsewhere.

Interbreeding with archaic humans

Ancient DNA research since 2010 has refined the model: non-African genomes carry roughly 1–4% Neanderthal ancestry, and some Oceanian populations carry additional Denisovan ancestry, showing that departing populations interbred to a limited extent with archaic groups they encountered. This "leaky replacement" picture is often termed assimilation or "Out of Africa with hybridisation."

Dispersal routes

Early excursions into the Levant (e.g., Misliya Cave, ~180,000 years ago; Skhul and Qafzeh, ~90,000–120,000 years ago) appear not to have led to lasting settlement. The major successful dispersal, associated with genetic lineage L3, crossed to Arabia and spread along southern Asia, reaching Australia by at least 50,000–65,000 years ago and Europe by about 45,000 years ago, with the Americas settled via Beringia within the last ~20,000 years.

Competing models

The theory displaced the multiregional origin hypothesis, which proposed parallel evolution of modern humans from archaic populations across the Old World. While a strict multiregional model is no longer supported, elements of it survive in the recognition of archaic admixture.
