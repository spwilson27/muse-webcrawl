Lauraceae, or the laurel family, is a family of flowering plants in the order Laurales, comprising about 50 genera and several thousand species of mostly evergreen trees and shrubs, distributed mainly in warm and tropical regions, especially Southeast Asia and South America. Many species are aromatic, with simple, entire leaves containing oil cells.

Economic importance

The family includes several important crops and flavourings: avocado (Persea americana), cinnamon and camphor (Cinnamomum species), bay leaves from Laurus nobilis, and sassafras. Many Lauraceae are also valuable timber trees in tropical forests, and laurel forests (laurisilva) survive as relict ecosystems in Macaronesia and elsewhere.

Genera

Major genera include Laurus, Cinnamomum, Persea, Ocotea, Litsea and Lindera. The parasitic genus Cassytha, a vine resembling dodder, is an unusual herbaceous member of the family.
