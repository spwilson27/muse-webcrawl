Epichloë

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Epichloë is a genus of ascomycete fungi in the family Clavicipitaceae. Species of Epichloë are systemic, often seed-transmitted endophytes of cool-season grasses, living between the cells of their hosts. Many are known only in their asexual state, formerly classified in the genus Neotyphodium, which was merged into Epichloë in 2014.

Symbiosis and effects

The relationship between Epichloë endophytes and their grass hosts ranges from mutualistic to pathogenic. In the mutualistic state the fungus grows intercellularly without symptoms and passes vertically through seed, often benefiting the plant with drought tolerance and defenses: the fungi produce alkaloids — including ergot alkaloids, lolitrems, peramine, and lolines — that deter insect and mammalian herbivores. These alkaloids also cause livestock disorders such as fescue toxicosis and ryegrass staggers in animals grazing infected pasture.

Sexual species can instead produce external fruiting structures (stromata) that wrap around and "choke" the developing inflorescence of the grass, preventing seed set; these species spread horizontally by ascospores, with mating mediated by Botanophila flies that carry spermatia between stromata.

Agricultural significance

Because endophyte-infected turf and forage grasses can be hardier, plant breeders have selected "novel endophyte" strains that protect the grass from insects while producing few of the alkaloids harmful to livestock.
