Foundation Programme

The Foundation Programme is a two-year structured programme of workplace-based learning for junior doctors in the United Kingdom that forms a bridge between medical school and specialty or general practice training. It was introduced in 2005 as part of the Modernising Medical Careers reforms, replacing the previous pre-registration house officer (PRHO) and senior house officer (SHO) grades for new graduates.

Structure

The programme consists of two years, Foundation Year 1 (F1) and Foundation Year 2 (F2). Doctors in F1 work under provisional registration with the General Medical Council and, on satisfactory completion, become eligible for full registration. F2 doctors hold full registration and take on increasing responsibility.

Each year is typically divided into three four-month rotations (placements) across a range of specialties, which may include general medicine, general surgery, general practice, psychiatry, emergency medicine, and other hospital or community settings. Trainees follow a national curriculum and must demonstrate achievement of a set of professional capabilities, recorded in an electronic portfolio and reviewed at an Annual Review of Competence Progression (ARCP).

Recruitment

Final-year medical students apply through a national process coordinated by the UK Foundation Programme Office. Allocation is based on a combination of the applicant's educational performance and their score on the Situational Judgement Test (SJT). From 2024, allocation moved to a preference-informed system based on computer-generated ranking. Applicants rank foundation schools, and posts are allocated accordingly.

Specialised programmes

Variants of the programme include the Specialised Foundation Programme (formerly the Academic Foundation Programme), which provides additional time for research, medical education, or leadership and management. Standalone F2 posts are also available for doctors who completed F1 outside the UK system or who wish to take a break before specialty training.
