Paschal Triduum

The Paschal Triduum (Latin: Triduum Paschale), also called the Easter Triduum or the Three Days, is the three-day period at the heart of the Christian liturgical year that celebrates the Passion, death, burial and Resurrection of Jesus Christ. It begins with the evening Mass of the Lord's Supper on Maundy Thursday, reaches its high point in the Easter Vigil, and concludes with Evening Prayer on Easter Sunday.

The three days

Maundy Thursday commemorates the Last Supper and the institution of the Eucharist, with the ritual washing of feet and the stripping of the altars. Good Friday is a day of fasting and mourning marked by the reading of the Passion, veneration of the Cross and a communion service without a Mass. Holy Saturday is a quiet day of waiting, ending after nightfall in the Easter Vigil: the blessing of new fire, the lighting of the Paschal candle, the singing of the Exsultet, a series of readings, baptisms and the first Mass of Easter.

Significance

The Triduum is treated liturgically not as three separate feasts but as one continuous celebration of the paschal mystery. Its present form in the Roman Rite owes much to the reforms of Pope Pius XII in 1955 and the later revision of Holy Week under Paul VI.
