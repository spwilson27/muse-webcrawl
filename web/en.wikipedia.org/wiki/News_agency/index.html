News agency

[Image: A 20th-century teletype room receiving wire copy]

A news agency (or wire service) is an organisation of journalists that gathers news and sells it to subscribing media — newspapers, broadcasters and websites — which republish it, often without a byline. The great agencies arose with the telegraph in the 19th century: Havas (1835), the Associated Press (1846), Reuters (1851) and their successors still supply much of the world's basic news file.

Economics and reach

Agencies pool the cost of global newsgathering across thousands of clients. The largest — AP, Reuters, AFP — maintain bureaus worldwide and file in many languages and media (text, photos, video, data). State agencies such as Xinhua and TASS combine this role with representing their governments' voice.

Influence

Because most outlets rewrite agency copy for routine stories, agency decisions about what to cover and how to phrase it quietly shape the global news agenda — the basis of the saying that the wires 'report the first draft of history'.
