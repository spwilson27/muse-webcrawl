Early life

James V of Scotland (10 April 1512 – 14 December 1542) was the fourth Stewart king of that name, the only surviving legitimate son of James IV and Margaret Tudor, sister of Henry VIII. Crowned at Stirling at seventeen months after his father's death at Flodden, he endured a turbulent minority of regencies and noble faction before seizing personal power in 1528 by escaping the custody of Archibald Douglas, 6th Earl of Angus.

Government and finance

James proved one of the most financially astute Scottish kings: he obtained from the papacy a levy on clerical incomes, augmented crown lands and feudal dues, and founded the College of Justice in 1532, institutionalising Scotland's central civil court. His justice ayres into the Borders, Highlands, and Isles, punctuated by exemplary hangings, extended royal government further than any predecessor's. He also patronised the poet and herald Sir David Lyndsay, whose Satire of the Three Estates was performed at his court.

Court and building

Two French marriages — to Madeleine of Valois (d. 1537) and to Mary of Guise — made his court a centre of Renaissance culture and funded a great building campaign: the palaces of Stirling, Falkland, Linlithgow, and Holyrood were remodelled in French Renaissance style, Stirling's Palace block (c. 1538–42) being the masterpiece of the reign.

Relations with England

James refused Henry VIII's pressure to break with Rome and seize church wealth on the English model, confirmed the Franco-Scottish Auld Alliance, and in 1541 snubbed a planned meeting with Henry at York. War followed; after a successful border raid (the Battle of Haddon Rig), the Scots were crushed at Solway Moss on 24 November 1542.

Legacy

James died weeks later at Falkland, aged thirty, leaving the throne to his six-day-old daughter, Mary, Queen of Scots — inaugurating the regency struggles and Rough Wooing that reshaped Scotland. History remembers a king both splendid and severe: a builder and patron whose exactions enriched the crown while embittering the nobility who deserted his cause in 1542.
