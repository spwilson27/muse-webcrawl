Cation

A cation is a positively charged ion — an atom or molecule that has lost one or more electrons, giving it a net positive charge. The term, proposed by Faraday and Whewell, comes from the Greek kata ('down'), because cations migrate toward the cathode in electrolysis.

Formation and examples

Metals typically form cations: sodium forms Na+, calcium Ca2+, aluminium Al3+. Polyatomic cations include ammonium (NH4+) and hydronium (H3O+). The charge is written as a superscript following the number of charges.

Properties

Cations are generally smaller than their parent atoms. Their interactions with anions form ionic compounds; cation exchange is important in soil chemistry, water softening, and biology, where Na+, K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ are essential electrolytes.
