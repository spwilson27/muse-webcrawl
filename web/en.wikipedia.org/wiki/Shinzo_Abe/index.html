Shinzo Abe (安倍 晋三, Abe Shinzō, September 21, 1954 – July 8, 2022) was a Japanese politician who served as Prime Minister of Japan and President of the Liberal Democratic Party from 2006 to 2007 and again from 2012 to 2020. He is the longest-serving prime minister in Japanese history, in office for a total of eight years and nine months. Abe was assassinated in July 2022 while delivering a campaign speech in Nara, the first former Japanese prime minister to be assassinated since the 1930s.

Early life and family

Abe was born into one of Japan's most prominent political families: his maternal grandfather, Nobusuke Kishi, was prime minister (1957–1960), his great-uncle Eisaku Satō also held the office, and his father, Shintarō Abe, served as foreign minister. After studying political science at Seikei University and briefly at the University of Southern California, Abe worked at Kobe Steel and then as an aide to his father before being elected to the House of Representatives in 1993 from Yamaguchi Prefecture.

First premiership (2006–2007)

As chief cabinet secretary under Junichiro Koizumi, Abe gained prominence for a hard line on North Korea over the abduction of Japanese citizens. Elected LDP president in September 2006, at 52 he became Japan's first prime minister born after the Second World War. His government revised the Fundamental Law of Education and upgraded the Defense Agency to a full ministry, but was eroded by scandals and the party's defeat in the 2007 upper house election; Abe resigned in September 2007, citing his chronic ulcerative colitis.

Second premiership (2012–2020)

Returning to the LDP leadership in 2012, Abe led the party back to power that December. His economic programme, Abenomics, combined aggressive monetary easing by the Bank of Japan, flexible fiscal policy and structural reform — the "three arrows" — aimed at ending deflation. Abroad, Abe strengthened the US–Japan alliance, passed controversial 2015 security legislation permitting collective self-defence, promoted the "Free and Open Indo-Pacific" concept, and improved ties with India, Australia and Southeast Asia; relations with China and South Korea remained strained by history issues. He secured Tokyo's successful bid for the 2020 Summer Olympics. Abe resigned in August 2020 when his ulcerative colitis relapsed, and was succeeded by Yoshihide Suga.

Assassination

On July 8, 2022, while campaigning for LDP candidates in Nara, Abe was shot twice with a homemade firearm by Tetsuya Yamagami, who later described a grievance against the Unification Church, whose ties to Abe and the LDP he resented. Abe was pronounced dead that afternoon. His state funeral on September 27, 2022, was attended by dignitaries from around the world and prompted public debate in Japan over its cost and over the church's political connections.
