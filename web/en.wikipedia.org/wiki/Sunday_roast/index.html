Sunday roast

Sunday roast
Alternative namesSunday lunch, Sunday dinner, roast dinner
Place of originUnited Kingdom
Main ingredientsRoasted meat, roast potatoes, vegetables, gravy, Yorkshire pudding

A Sunday roast is a traditional meal of British origin that is typically served on Sunday, consisting of roasted meat, roasted potatoes and accompaniments such as Yorkshire pudding, stuffing, gravy and various vegetables. The meal is a mainstay of British food culture and is widely eaten in the United Kingdom, Ireland and Commonwealth countries including Australia, New Zealand, Canada and South Africa, where it is often served in pubs as a "carvery" lunch.

Typical components

The centrepiece may be roast beef, pork (with crackling and apple sauce), lamb (with mint sauce) or chicken (with bread sauce), or a vegetarian or vegan alternative such as a nut roast. Roast potatoes are traditionally cooked in the meat fat or goose fat; accompanying vegetables commonly include carrots, parsnips, Brussels sprouts, peas, cauliflower cheese and braised red cabbage. Beef is classically served with Yorkshire pudding, horseradish cream and English mustard; Yorkshire pudding may accompany other meats as well in modern practice. The meal is finished with gravy made from the meat juices, and is sometimes followed by a pudding such as apple crumble or sticky toffee pudding.

Origins

The tradition is often traced to medieval England, when village serfs rewarded after Sunday service were given spit-roasted oxen by their masters, and to the practice of placing a joint of meat in the baker's oven on the way to church, to be collected on the way home. The Yeomen Warders of the Tower of London earned the nickname "Beefeaters" from their generous ration of roast beef. The Sunday roast became a defining ritual of British domestic life in the 19th and 20th centuries, and although surveys suggest home cooking of the meal has declined, it remains a fixture of pub and restaurant menus.
