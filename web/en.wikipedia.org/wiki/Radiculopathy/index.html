Radiculopathy

Radiculopathy is a condition caused by compression, inflammation or injury of a spinal nerve root, producing pain, numbness, tingling or weakness that radiates along the course of the affected nerve. It is sometimes described by patients as a "pinched nerve". The symptoms typically follow the dermatome or myotome supplied by the involved root.

Causes

The most common cause is mechanical compression of the nerve root as it exits the spinal canal, usually by a herniated intervertebral disc or by bony spurs (osteophytes) associated with degenerative spondylosis and foraminal stenosis. Other causes include trauma, tumours, infection and diabetes. Cervical radiculopathy affects the neck and arm; lumbar radiculopathy affects the lower back and leg, and when it involves the sciatic nerve distribution it is commonly called sciatica.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on the history and neurological examination, including testing of sensation, muscle strength and reflexes. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) can demonstrate root compression, while nerve conduction studies and electromyography help confirm the level and severity of involvement and exclude peripheral nerve disorders.

Treatment

Most cases resolve with conservative management: activity modification, physiotherapy, analgesics and anti-inflammatory medication. Epidural steroid injections may help in selected patients. Surgery, such as discectomy or foraminotomy, is considered when there is progressive neurological deficit, cauda equina syndrome, or persistent disabling pain despite conservative care.
