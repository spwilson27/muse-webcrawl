Kawase Hasui (川瀬 巴水, 18 May 1883 – 7 November 1957) was a Japanese artist and one of the most prominent print designers of the shin-hanga ("new prints") movement. Working chiefly with the publisher Watanabe Shōzaburō, he created more than 600 woodblock prints, celebrated for their serene, atmospheric landscapes of Japan in snow, rain, moonlight, and dusk.

Life and career

Born Kawase Bunjiirō in Tokyo, Hasui studied Western-style painting and worked as an illustrator before turning to woodblock print design. From 1918 he published through Watanabe, travelling throughout Japan to sketch famous places (meisho) — temples, villages, snowy mountain scenes such as "Snow at Zōjōji Temple" and "Twenty Views of Tokyo" — rendered with a painterly sensibility unusual in traditional ukiyo-e.

Work and legacy

The 1923 Great Kantō earthquake destroyed Watanabe's workshop and Hasui's early blocks, so pre-1923 prints are especially rare. In 1956 the Japanese government designated his print "Snow in Zōjōji Temple" an Intangible Cultural Property and honoured Hasui as a Living National Treasure. His work was popular with Western collectors — Steve Jobs famously collected his prints — and remains widely reproduced.
