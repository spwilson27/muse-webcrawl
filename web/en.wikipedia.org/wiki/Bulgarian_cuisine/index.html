Bulgarian cuisine

Bulgarian cuisine (Bulgarian: българска кухня, bǎlgarska kuhnja) is a representative of the cuisine of Southeast Europe. It essentially shares characteristics with other Balkan cuisines, while also showing influences from Greek, Turkish, Middle Eastern, and to a lesser extent Italian, Mediterranean and Hungarian cuisines. Bulgarian food is known for the abundance of vegetables and dairy products, particularly yogurt and sirene, a white brined cheese similar to feta.

Characteristics

Bulgarian cuisine is marked by a wide variety of salads that accompany every meal, as well as by hearty soups and stews. Dishes are frequently seasoned with herbs and spices such as savory (chubritsa), spearmint, and paprika. Pork is the most common meat, followed by chicken and lamb. The long-standing tradition of dairy farming means yogurt features prominently; Bulgarian yogurt, made with Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus, is renowned worldwide.

Traditional dishes

Well-known Bulgarian dishes include banitsa, a baked filo pastry filled with whisked eggs, yogurt and sirene; shopska salad, a chopped salad of tomatoes, cucumbers, onions and peppers topped with grated sirene; tarator, a cold yogurt and cucumber soup served in summer; moussaka, a potato and minced-meat casserole; sarmi, vine or cabbage leaves stuffed with rice and meat; and kebapche, grilled minced meat with spices. Lyutenitsa, a spicy relish of peppers and tomatoes, is a household staple.

Breads and pastries

Bread is central to Bulgarian meals and to ritual traditions; village breads are often decorated for holidays. Pogacha is a round leavened bread served at celebrations. Pastries such as mekitsa (fried dough served with jam or cheese) and baklava, adopted from Ottoman cuisine, are popular sweets.

Beverages

Bulgaria has an ancient winemaking tradition dating to Thracian times. Rakia, a fruit brandy usually made from grapes or plums, is the national spirit. Boza, a mildly fermented malt drink, and ayran, a yogurt-based drink, are also common.
