Jumble sale

A jumble sale, also called a rummage sale in the United States, is an event at which donated used goods are sold cheaply to raise money for an organization, typically a charity, church, school, or community group.

Description

Members of the community donate unwanted items — clothing, books, toys, bric-à-brac, and household goods — which volunteers sort and display, traditionally in heaped piles (the "jumble") on tables in a hall. Prices are low, and unsold goods are often bagged and sold by weight to textile recyclers or passed to charity shops.

History and culture

Jumble sales were a staple of British village and parish life in the twentieth century, known for brisk queues at opening time and keen bargain-hunting. The rise of permanent charity shops and online resale has reduced their frequency, but they remain common fundraising events for schools, Scout and Guide groups, and places of worship.

Related forms

Similar events include car boot sales, flea markets, garage sales, white elephant sales, and bring-and-buy sales, the latter traditionally stocked with homemade goods and produce.
