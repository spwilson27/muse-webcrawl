Leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland

The title of Leading sire in Great Britain & Ireland is awarded to the Thoroughbred stallion whose progeny have won the most prize money in flat racing in Great Britain and Ireland during a given season. The championship has been recorded since the 18th century and is a key measure of a stallion's commercial success.

History

Early dominant sires included Herod, Highflyer, and Sir Peter Teazle around the turn of the 19th century. In the 20th century, stallions such as Nearco, Hyperion, and Northern Dancer (through his sons) shaped the breed. Sadler's Wells won the title a record 14 times, dominating from the early 1990s to 2004. His son Galileo then won the championship 12 times between 2008 and 2020, and Galileo's son Frankel has continued the family's dominance in the 2020s.

Significance

The leading sire title strongly influences stud fees and yearling prices, as breeders seek stallions whose offspring succeed at the highest level in Britain and Ireland's valuable flat races.
