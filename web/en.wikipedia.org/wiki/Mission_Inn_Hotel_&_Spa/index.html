Mission Inn Hotel & Spa

LocationRiverside, California, United States
Opened1876 (as Glenwood Cottages)
Architectural styleMission Revival / eclectic
DesignationNational Historic Landmark

The Mission Inn Hotel & Spa is a historic hotel in downtown Riverside, California. Originally a modest adobe boarding house opened by Christopher Miller in 1876, it was transformed by his son Frank Miller into a sprawling, fantastical complex in an eclectic blend of Mission Revival, Spanish Gothic, Moorish and other styles, built in stages from 1903 into the 1930s.

Description

Occupying an entire city block, the inn contains chapels, cloisters, a rotunda wing, gardens, catacombs-like arcades and collections of bells, crosses and religious art gathered by Miller from around the world. Its St. Francis Chapel features a gold-leaf altar and Tiffany stained-glass windows. The inn has hosted presidents, celebrities and a famous 1940 wedding — that of future President Richard Nixon and Pat Nixon, who married in the Presidential Lounge.

Later history

The inn declined after Miller's death in 1935 and passed through several owners before closing in 1985. It reopened in 1992 after an extensive restoration, and today operates as a hotel and spa. Its annual Festival of Lights, begun in 1993, draws visitors from across Southern California.
