Methanogen

Methanogens
DomainArchaea
PhylumPrimarily Euryarchaeota
MetabolismAnaerobic; produce methane

Methanogens are anaerobic archaea that produce methane as a metabolic byproduct in anoxic conditions. They are a phylogenetically diverse group, unified by their shared energy metabolism, known as methanogenesis. Methanogens are common in wetlands, where they are responsible for marsh gas, and in the digestive tracts of animals such as ruminants and humans, where they are responsible for the methane content of belching and flatulence.

Metabolism

Methanogenesis involves the reduction of substrates such as carbon dioxide, acetate, and methylated compounds, using coenzymes unique to these organisms, including coenzyme M, methanofuran, and methanopterin. Most methanogens are hydrogenotrophic, reducing CO2 with hydrogen gas. Acetoclastic methanogens split acetate into methane and carbon dioxide.

Ecology

Methanogens live in anoxic environments including swamps, lake sediments, hydrothermal vents, sewage digesters, and the guts of ruminants and termites. They are an important part of the global carbon cycle and a significant natural source of atmospheric methane, a potent greenhouse gas.

Examples

Well-studied methanogens include Methanobacterium, Methanocaldococcus jannaschii, Methanosarcina barkeri, and Methanopyrus kandleri, the latter notable for growth at very high temperatures.
