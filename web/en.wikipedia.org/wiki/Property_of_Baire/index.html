Property of Baire

In the mathematical field of descriptive set theory, a subset of a Polish space has the property of Baire (named after René Baire) if it differs from an open set by a meagre set; that is, the symmetric difference of the set and some open set is of the first category. Sets with the property of Baire are also called Baire sets or are said to be Baire measurable.

Definition

A set A has the property of Baire if there exists an open set U such that A Δ U is meagre, i.e. a countable union of nowhere dense sets. Equivalently, A has the property of Baire in the restricted sense if for every nonempty perfect subset E of the ambient space, the intersection A ∩ E has the property of Baire relative to E.

Properties

The collection of sets with the property of Baire forms a σ-algebra, and it contains all open sets, all closed sets, and hence all Borel sets. Every analytic set (a continuous image of a Borel set) has the property of Baire; this is a classical theorem of Lusin and Sierpiński. Under the axiom of choice there exist sets of real numbers without the property of Baire — for example, any Bernstein set and any Vitali set lack it.

Relation to measurability

The property of Baire is the topological analogue of Lebesgue measurability: open sets play the role of measurable approximations, and meagre sets play the role of null sets. This analogy is deep but imperfect, since there exist sets that are meagre yet of full Lebesgue measure, and comeagre sets of measure zero. The analogy underlies the theory of category analogues of measure-theoretic theorems, such as the Baire category theorem itself and the Pettis lemma in topological groups.

Applications

In the theory of Polish groups and descriptive dynamics, the property of Baire is a standard regularity hypothesis: for example, any homomorphism between Polish groups that is Baire measurable is automatically continuous (the Pettis theorem). In set theory, forcing axioms and determinacy hypotheses extend the property to wider classes of sets — under projective determinacy, every projective set has the property of Baire.
