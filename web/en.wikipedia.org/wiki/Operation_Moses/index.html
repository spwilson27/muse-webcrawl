Operation Moses (Hebrew: מבצע משה, Mivtza Moshe) was the covert evacuation of Ethiopian Jews (the Beta Israel community) from Sudan during a famine in 1984–85. The operation, named after the biblical Moses, was carried out by the Israeli Mossad in cooperation with the Sudanese security services and with the assistance of the United States.

Background

Thousands of Beta Israel had fled drought and persecution in Ethiopia on foot, reaching refugee camps in neighbouring Sudan, where many died of disease and malnutrition. Israel, which recognised the community's right to immigrate under the Law of Return, arranged a secret airlift from Khartoum with the tacit consent of Sudanese president Jaafar Nimeiri, on condition of total secrecy.

The airlift

Between 21 November 1984 and 5 January 1985, some 30 flights by Trans European Airways — a charter company owned by George Gutelman — carried approximately 8,000 Ethiopian Jews from Khartoum via Brussels to Israel. In January 1985 the operation was leaked to the press: after the story was confirmed publicly by Israeli officials, Arab states pressured Sudan and the airlift was halted abruptly, leaving several thousand Beta Israel stranded in Sudan.

Aftermath

In March 1985, in Operation Sheba, US Air Force C-130s flew roughly 500 more Ethiopian Jews from Sudan to Israel. Most of those left behind eventually reached Israel in later operations, culminating in Operation Solomon in 1991, which airlifted over 14,000 people in 36 hours. Operation Moses remains a landmark in the ingathering of the Beta Israel, though it was marked by family separations and a difficult absorption process in Israel.
