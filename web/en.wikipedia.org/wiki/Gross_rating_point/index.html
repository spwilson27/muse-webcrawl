Gross rating point

FieldAdvertising, media planning
AbbreviationGRP
FormulaReach (%) × average frequency
Related measuresTarget rating point (TRP), impressions, cost per point

In advertising, a gross rating point (GRP) measures the total size of the audience reached by a media schedule. One rating point represents one percent of the potential audience; GRP is the sum of ratings for all insertions in a campaign, or equivalently the percentage of the target population reached multiplied by the average number of times they were exposed.

Calculation

GRP = Reach × Frequency. For example, if 60% of the target audience sees an advertisement an average of three times, the campaign delivers 180 GRPs. Because the measure counts duplicate exposures, GRPs can exceed 100. When calculated only for a specific demographic target, the figure is called a target rating point (TRP).

Use

Media planners use GRPs to compare the weight of campaigns across markets and media, and to set budgets using cost per point. GRPs are standard in television buying and are also adapted for radio, outdoor, and digital advertising, where the related measure of gross impressions is common.

Criticism

GRP measures exposure, not attention, engagement, or sales effect, and critics argue it is an increasingly crude metric in fragmented digital media environments.
