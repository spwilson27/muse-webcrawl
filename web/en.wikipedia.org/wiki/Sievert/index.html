Sievert

Unit of: Equivalent dose
Symbol: Sv
Named after: Rolf Maximilian Sievert
SI base units: m²·s⁻² (= J/kg)

The sievert (symbol: Sv) is the SI unit of equivalent dose and effective dose of ionising radiation. It measures the health effect of radiation on the human body, weighting the absorbed dose by the biological effectiveness of the radiation type and, for effective dose, by the sensitivity of the tissues irradiated.

Definition

One sievert equals one joule of energy absorbed per kilogram of matter (the same dimensions as the gray), multiplied by the radiation weighting factor wR — 1 for photons and electrons, 20 for alpha particles. Because the sievert is large for most exposures, doses are usually given in millisieverts (mSv) or microsieverts (µSv): average natural background exposure is about 2–3 mSv per year.

Health context

Occupational limits are commonly set at 20 mSv per year averaged over five years. A single acute dose of about 1 Sv causes radiation sickness, and doses of 4–5 Sv are frequently fatal without treatment. The unit is named after the Swedish medical physicist Rolf Maximilian Sievert, a pioneer of radiation protection.
