Doris Day

Doris Day (born Doris Mary Anne Kappelhoff; April 3, 1922 – May 13, 2019) was an American actress and singer. She began her career as a big band singer in 1939, achieving commercial success in 1945 with recordings including "Sentimental Journey" and "My Dreams Are Getting Better All the Time". She went on to star in 39 films and recorded more than 650 songs.

Film career

Day's screen debut was in Romance on the High Seas (1948). She starred in musicals, dramas, and comedies including Calamity Jane (1953), Alfred Hitchcock's The Man Who Knew Too Much (1956) — in which she introduced her signature song "Que Sera, Sera (Whatever Will Be, Will Be)" — and a string of romantic comedies with Rock Hudson, beginning with Pillow Talk (1959), which earned her an Academy Award nomination. She was one of the biggest box-office stars of the late 1950s and 1960s.

Television and later life

From 1968 to 1973 she starred in the sitcom The Doris Day Show. After retiring from acting, she devoted herself to animal welfare, founding the Doris Day Animal Foundation and co-founding the Doris Day Animal League. She lived in Carmel Valley, California, and received honours including the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2004 and a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. She died in 2019 at the age of 97.
