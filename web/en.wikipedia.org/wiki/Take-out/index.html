Take-out

Take-out
Also calledTakeaway (UK, Australia, NZ); carry-out; to-go; takeout
TypePrepared food sold for off-premises consumption

Take-out (North American English), also called takeaway (British, Australian, and New Zealand English), carry-out, or food to go, is prepared food purchased at a restaurant or fast food outlet to be eaten elsewhere. The concept is ancient — street vendors sold ready-to-eat food in classical antiquity, and taverns and cookshops in medieval and early modern cities served customers who did not cook at home — but it became a defining feature of modern food culture with the rise of fast food chains in the 20th century and, later, app-based delivery.

History

Excavations at Pompeii reveal thermopolia, counters selling hot food and drink to passersby, indicating take-out commerce nearly two thousand years ago. In 19th-century Britain, fish and chip shops provided wrapped take-away meals to working-class customers, while in the United States oyster houses, delicatessens, and Chinese restaurants popularized food taken home — American Chinese take-out, packaged in the iconic folded paper "oyster pail," became a cultural fixture in the 20th century. The postwar spread of automobiles encouraged drive-in and drive-through service, and pizza delivery chains industrialized hot food brought to the door.

Modern industry

Today take-out spans casual independent shops, global fast-food chains, ghost kitchens (delivery-only restaurants), and third-party platforms such as DoorDash, Uber Eats, Deliveroo, and Meituan, which aggregate menus and couriers. The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated take-out and delivery as dining rooms closed, and many restaurants permanently restructured around off-premises sales. A persistent concern is packaging waste: disposable containers, cutlery, and bags have prompted regulations ranging from plastic bans to reusable-container schemes in many jurisdictions.

Terminology

"Take-out" and "takeout" predominate in the United States and Canada; "takeaway" predominates in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, where a "takeaway" also denotes the shop itself. In Scotland "carry-out" is common, and in parts of the US the phrase refers also to packaged alcohol sold for consumption off the premises.
