Lower Sorbian (endonym: dolnoserbšćina) is a West Slavic minority language spoken in eastern Germany, in the historical region of Lower Lusatia around the city of Cottbus (Lower Sorbian: Chóśebuz) in Brandenburg. Together with Upper Sorbian, spoken further south in Saxony around Bautzen, it forms the Sorbian subgroup of West Slavic languages, most closely related to Polish, Kashubian and the extinct Polabian.

Features

Lower Sorbian retains the dual number (alongside singular and plural) and has preserved the simple past tenses (aorist and imperfect) that most other Slavic languages have lost — though in modern Lower Sorbian these tenses are receding under German influence. Its phonology includes the sounds ś and ź and a characteristic change of g to h (as in Upper Sorbian and Czech). The language is written in a Latin alphabet with diacritics.

Status

Lower Sorbian is severely endangered. Centuries of Germanization, industrialisation of the Lusatian lignite-mining region and the stigma attached to the language reduced its speaker base; today most native speakers are elderly, and the number of fluent speakers is usually estimated at only several thousand. Revitalisation efforts include the Witaj immersion kindergarten programme, Lower Sorbian schooling, the newspaper Nowy Casnik, and the work of the Domowina umbrella organisation and the Sorbian Institute. Unlike the standard language based on the Cottbus dialect, many remaining native speakers speak transitional dialects around Vetschau and Lübbenau.
