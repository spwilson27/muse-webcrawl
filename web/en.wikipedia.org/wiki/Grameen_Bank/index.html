Grameen Bank

Grameen Bank (Bengali: গ্রামীণ ব্যাংক) is a microfinance organisation and community development bank founded in Bangladesh. It makes small loans (known as microcredit) to the impoverished without requiring collateral, and is credited with popularising microcredit worldwide. The bank and its founder, Muhammad Yunus, jointly received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2006.

History

Muhammad Yunus began the project in the village of Jobra in 1976, lending small sums from his own pocket to poor villagers. The experiment became an independent bank by government ordinance in 1983. Its model rests on solidarity lending: borrowers form small groups, meet weekly, and take collective responsibility for repayment, which historically produced very high repayment rates.

Operations

The bank lends overwhelmingly to women, who make up the great majority of its millions of borrowers, and is owned largely by the borrowers themselves. It also accepts deposits and has inspired "Grameen-style" replications in dozens of countries. Yunus was removed as managing director by Bangladesh Bank in 2011, a decision he contested, and the government later increased its role in the bank's governance.
