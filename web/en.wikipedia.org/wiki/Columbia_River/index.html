Columbia River

The Columbia River is the largest river in the Pacific Northwest region of North America by discharge and the fourth-largest in the United States by volume. It flows about 2,000 kilometres from Columbia Lake in British Columbia, Canada, south through Washington, then west along the Washington-Oregon border to the Pacific Ocean near Astoria.

The Columbia and its tributaries — above all the Snake River — drain about 670,000 square kilometres. Its canyon through the Cascade Range, the Columbia River Gorge, is a national scenic area of waterfalls and windsurfing. Fourteen hydroelectric dams on the main stem, including Grand Coulee and Bonneville, made it the largest hydroelectric system in North America, but they devastated the legendary salmon runs that Lewis and Clark marvelled at in 1805.
