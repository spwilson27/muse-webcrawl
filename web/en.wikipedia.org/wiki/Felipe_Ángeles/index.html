Felipe Ángeles

Felipe Ángeles Ramírez (13 June 1868 – 26 November 1919) was a Mexican military officer, artillery specialist and hero of the Mexican Revolution. Regarded as one of the most professional and humane commanders of the conflict, he served Francisco Madero and later became Pancho Villa's most trusted strategist before being captured and executed by the government of Venustiano Carranza.

Career

Born in Zacualtipán, Hidalgo, Ángeles graduated from the Heroic Military Academy at Chapultepec and studied artillery in France, becoming the Mexican army's leading expert in the subject. As director of the Military College under President Madero he professionalised officer training. After Victoriano Huerta's coup and Madero's murder in 1913, Ángeles joined the Constitutionalist forces and then Villa's División del Norte, where his artillery direction was decisive in victories such as the Battle of Zacatecas in 1914.

Exile, return and death

After Villa's defeats in 1915 Ángeles went into exile in the United States. He returned to Mexico in 1918 seeking national reconciliation, rejoined Villa briefly, and was captured by Carrancista troops in 1919. Tried by a court-martial in Chihuahua amid wide public sympathy, he was executed by firing squad on 26 November 1919. Mexico City's second international airport, opened in 2022, was named Felipe Ángeles International Airport in his honour.
