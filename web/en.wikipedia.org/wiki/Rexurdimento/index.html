Rexurdimento

The Rexurdimento (Galician for "resurgence" or "revival") was a cultural and literary movement in nineteenth-century Galicia, Spain, which revived the Galician language as a vehicle for literature after centuries — the so-called Séculos Escuros (Dark Centuries) — in which it had largely ceased to be written. It parallels the Catalan Renaixença.

Beginnings

The movement is conventionally dated from the publication in 1863 of Cantares gallegos ("Galician Songs") by Rosalía de Castro, the first major book of poetry in Galician of the modern era. Earlier nineteenth-century writers, and the political regionalism of the provincialist movement of the 1840s, had prepared the ground. The Xogos Florais (Floral Games) of A Coruña in 1861 also encouraged poetry in Galician.

Principal figures

Alongside Rosalía de Castro, the central poets were Manuel Curros Enríquez, whose Aires da miña terra (1880) brought a socially critical voice, and Eduardo Pondal, author of Queixumes dos pinos (1886) and of the poem that became the Galician anthem, Os Pinos. Writers such as Valentín Lamas Carvajal promoted prose and the press in Galician.

Legacy

The Rexurdimento restored Galician literary prestige and fed the later development of Galician nationalism and the Irmandades da Fala of the early twentieth century. 17 May, the anniversary of the publication of Cantares gallegos, is celebrated as Galician Literature Day (Día das Letras Galegas).
