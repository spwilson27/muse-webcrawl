Laulau

Laulau
Place of originHawaii
Main ingredientsPork (or chicken, beef, salted butterfish), taro (luau) leaves, ti leaves
ServingSteamed; traditionally cooked in an imu

Laulau is a traditional Native Hawaiian dish consisting of meat — classically pork, sometimes combined with salted butterfish — wrapped in taro leaves (kalo, called luau leaves), bundled in ti leaves, and steamed until very tender. The name refers to the leaf bundle itself. Laulau is a staple of the Hawaiian plate lunch and of the traditional luau feast.

Preparation

Cubes of fatty pork (often shoulder, sometimes with chicken, beef, or salted fish) are seasoned with Hawaiian sea salt, wrapped first in several young taro leaves, then enclosed in ti leaves (kī), which are inedible and serve purely as a wrapper. The bundles are tied and steamed for several hours — traditionally in an imu, an underground oven — until the taro leaves soften into a spinach-like consistency and the meat is fork-tender.

Variations and serving

Modern versions substitute spinach or collard greens where taro leaves are unavailable, and use aluminium foil or kitchen twine in place of ti-leaf ties. Laulau is commonly served with rice, lomi-lomi salmon, poi, and haupia as part of a plate lunch or luau plate.
