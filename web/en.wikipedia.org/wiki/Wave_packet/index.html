Wave packet

In physics, a wave packet is a short burst or envelope of wave action that travels as a unit. It is formed by superimposing many plane waves of different wavelengths, whose interference confines the oscillation to a limited region of space.

Quantum mechanics

Wave packets are central to the quantum description of particles: a particle's wavefunction is commonly modelled as a wave packet whose spatial extent represents the uncertainty in its position. The packet's group velocity corresponds to the particle's classical velocity, while its spread in momentum is inversely related to its spatial width, an expression of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

Dispersion

In a dispersive medium, different wavelength components travel at different speeds, so a wave packet broadens over time and its shape changes. In a non-dispersive medium, the packet propagates without changing shape.

Applications

Wave packets describe pulses in optics and telecommunications, seismic waves, and the propagation of signals in waveguides and plasmas.
