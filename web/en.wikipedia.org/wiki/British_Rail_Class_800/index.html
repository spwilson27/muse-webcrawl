British Rail Class 800

The British Rail Class 800, branded by Great Western Railway (GWR) as the Intercity Express Train (IET) and by London North Eastern Railway (LNER) as the Azuma, is a type of bi-mode multiple unit built by Hitachi Rail under the British Government's Intercity Express Programme. The trains entered service in October 2017.

A Class 800/1 Azuma, operated by LNER on the East Coast Main Line.

Design

Class 800 units are electro-diesel bi-mode trains, able to run from 25 kV AC overhead wires or from underfloor MTU diesel generator units, allowing through running beyond electrified routes. They are formed of five or nine cars, seat up to about 650 passengers in nine-car form, and have a maximum service speed of 125 mph (201 km/h). Eighty units were built for GWR (Class 800/0 and 800/3) and LNER (800/1 and 800/2), most assembled at Hitachi's Newton Aycliffe factory in County Durham.

Service

GWR introduced the trains on services from London Paddington to Bristol, South Wales and the Cotswolds, replacing High Speed Trains; LNER's Azumas took over East Coast Main Line services from 2019. In May 2021 the fleet — together with related Classes 801, 802 and 803 — was temporarily withdrawn after fatigue cracks were found in bogie and yaw-damper components, causing widespread disruption before a repair programme returned them to service.
