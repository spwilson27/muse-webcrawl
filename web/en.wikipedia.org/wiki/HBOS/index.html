HBOS plc was a banking and insurance company in the United Kingdom, formed in 2001 by the merger of Halifax plc and the Bank of Scotland. Its name derived from Halifax Bank of Scotland. The group became one of Britain's largest mortgage lenders and deposit takers, operating brands including Halifax, Bank of Scotland, Birmingham Midshires and Intelligent Finance.

History

The Bank of Scotland, founded in 1695, was one of the world's oldest banks; the Halifax had grown from a building society founded in 1853 into the UK's largest mortgage lender before demutualising in 1997. Their merger created a group intended to challenge the "big four" English clearing banks. HBOS expanded aggressively in retail and corporate lending during the early 2000s, becoming Britain's biggest mortgage lender with roughly a fifth of the market.

Financial crisis and takeover

During the 2008 financial crisis, HBOS was heavily exposed to property lending and dependent on wholesale funding markets that froze after the collapse of Lehman Brothers. In September 2008 a government-brokered takeover by Lloyds TSB was announced, requiring a controversial change to competition law. The acquisition completed in January 2009, creating Lloyds Banking Group, and the same rescue saw the UK government take a large stake in the combined bank. Subsequent inquiries, including a 2015 regulatory report, criticised HBOS's board and supervisors for the bank's risk-taking and weak controls.
