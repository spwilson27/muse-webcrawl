Famine in Yemen

Since 2016, Yemen has faced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises, including widespread famine-like conditions driven by the Yemeni civil war that began in 2014. The conflict between the Houthi movement and the internationally recognised government, backed by a Saudi-led coalition, devastated the economy of the Arab world's poorest country and left millions facing acute food insecurity.

Causes

Yemen imported the great majority of its food before the war, making it highly vulnerable to disruption. Bombing of ports, roads, markets and farms, a blockade and restrictions on the key port of Hodeidah, the collapse of the currency, unpaid public salaries, and the destruction of water and health infrastructure combined to push food prices beyond the reach of much of the population. The war also displaced millions internally, and a massive cholera outbreak — the largest recorded — compounded malnutrition, particularly among children.

Scale

United Nations agencies repeatedly warned that Yemen was on the brink of, or experiencing pockets of, famine. At various points more than half the population — over 16 million people — faced acute food insecurity, and hundreds of thousands of children suffered severe acute malnutrition. The UN estimated that hundreds of thousands of people died from indirect causes of the war such as hunger and disease by the early 2020s, and described Yemen as the world's worst humanitarian crisis.

International response

The World Food Programme mounted one of its largest operations in Yemen, though funding shortfalls repeatedly forced ration cuts. Humanitarian access was hampered by bureaucracy, checkpoints and insecurity imposed by all parties. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 temporarily reduced fighting and improved some access, but the underlying drivers of hunger — economic collapse, import dependence and damaged infrastructure — persisted.
