Dynamometer car

A dynamometer car is a railroad maintenance-of-way car used to measure various locomotive performance characteristics: drawbar pull, tractive effort, speed, power output, braking and riding qualities. Placed directly behind the locomotive under test, it records data on the locomotive's actual performance while hauling a train.

TypeRailway test vehicle
MeasuresDrawbar pull, speed, power, distance
PositionCoupled directly behind the locomotive

Equipment

The car's central instrument is a dynamometer connected to the coupler, which measures the force exerted by the locomotive; historically this was recorded by a stylus on a moving paper chart alongside speed and distance traces, while observers noted boiler pressure, throttle position and other data from the locomotive. Modern cars use electronic sensors and computer logging.

Famous examples

Dynamometer cars were essential to the official locomotive trials of the steam era. In Britain, the London and North Eastern Railway's dynamometer car recorded the 126 mph world steam record run of Mallard in 1938, and the preserved car is now in the collection of the National Railway Museum. In North America, railroads and locomotive builders such as the Pennsylvania Railroad and Union Pacific maintained dynamometer cars for acceptance and research testing.

Modern use

While dedicated dynamometer cars are less common today, instrumented test cars and portable dynamometer equipment continue to be used for locomotive acceptance testing, fuel-consumption studies and high-speed train development.
