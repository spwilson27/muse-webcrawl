Stylus (computing)

In computing, a stylus (or stylus pen) is a small pen-shaped instrument whose position on a computer monitor or touchscreen can be detected and used as an input device. Styluses are used with graphics tablets, tablet computers, smartphones, and personal digital assistants (PDAs), enabling precise pointing, drawing, and handwriting input.

Types

Passive styluses conduct the user's touch to a capacitive screen and require no power or electronics. Active styluses contain electronic components that communicate with a digitiser layer in the display, supporting features such as pressure sensitivity, tilt detection, palm rejection, and hover tracking. Notable examples include the Apple Pencil, Microsoft's Surface Pen, and Samsung's S Pen, which uses electromagnetic resonance technology developed by Wacom.

History

Stylus input dates to early systems such as the RAND Tablet of the 1960s and was central to 1990s PDAs such as the Apple Newton and PalmPilot, which relied on resistive touchscreens. The rise of capacitive multi-touch after 2007 reduced reliance on styluses for phones, but they remain important for digital art, note-taking, and design work.
