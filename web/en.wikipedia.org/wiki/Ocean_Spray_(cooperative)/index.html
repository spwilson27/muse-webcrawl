Ocean Spray Cranberries, Inc. is an American agricultural cooperative of cranberry growers, headquartered in Massachusetts. Founded in 1930, it is one of the best-known cranberry producers in North America and markets cranberry juices, dried cranberries (sold as Craisins), sauces, and fresh fruit under the Ocean Spray brand.

History

The cooperative was formed by three independent cranberry companies led by growers including Marcus Urann, who had pioneered cranberry sauce in a can. Ocean Spray expanded demand for cranberries beyond the holiday season by popularising cranberry juice drinks; its cranberry juice cocktail and later blends such as Cran-Apple helped create the modern juice aisle. In the 1990s it introduced sweetened dried cranberries, marketed as Craisins.

Cooperative structure

Ocean Spray is owned by several hundred cranberry-growing families in the United States (chiefly Massachusetts, Wisconsin, New Jersey, Oregon and Washington) and Canada, together with a smaller number of grapefruit growers in Florida. Growers deliver their harvest to the cooperative, which processes and markets it and returns the proceeds.

Products and marketing

The brand's long-running advertising campaigns have shown two growers standing waist-deep in a flooded cranberry bog during harvest. Wet harvesting — flooding the bogs so the buoyant berries float — is a signature image of the industry.
