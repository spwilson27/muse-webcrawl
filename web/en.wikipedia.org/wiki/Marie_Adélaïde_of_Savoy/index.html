Marie Adélaïde of Savoy (6 December 1685 – 12 February 1712) was the wife of Louis, Dauphin of France, Duke of Burgundy, and the mother of Louis XV. The eldest daughter of Victor Amadeus II, Duke of Savoy, and Anne Marie d'Orléans, she came to the French court as a child bride and became the favourite of the ageing Louis XIV, bringing vivacity to Versailles. She died of measles aged twenty-six, days before her husband, in an epidemic that devastated the French royal family.

Marriage

Her marriage was arranged under the Treaty of Turin (1696), which detached Savoy from the League of Augsburg during the Nine Years' War. Marie Adélaïde arrived in France at the age of eleven and was married to the Dauphin's eldest son, Louis, Duke of Burgundy, on 7 December 1697. Educated at Saint-Cyr under the eye of Madame de Maintenon, she charmed Louis XIV, who treated her almost as his own daughter.

Dauphine

The couple had three sons: a first son who died in infancy in 1705, Louis, Duke of Brittany (1707–1712), and Louis, Duke of Anjou, the future Louis XV (1710–1774). On the death of the Grand Dauphin in April 1711, her husband became Dauphin and she Dauphine of France, raising hopes that the couple would reform the court.

Death

In February 1712 an epidemic of measles struck Versailles. Marie Adélaïde fell ill and died on 12 February 1712. Her grief-stricken husband, who had nursed her, contracted the disease and died six days later, on 18 February. Their elder surviving son, the little Duke of Brittany, died the following month, leaving the two-year-old Duke of Anjou — saved, legend says, by his governess who kept the doctors from bleeding him — as heir to the throne. The couple were buried together at Saint-Denis.
