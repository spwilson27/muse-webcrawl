Trenitalia is the primary train operator in Italy. A subsidiary of Ferrovie dello Stato Italiane (FS), itself wholly owned by the Italian state, Trenitalia was created in 2000 following the deregulation and corporatisation of Italian railways required by European Union directives, which separated train operations from infrastructure management (handled by Rete Ferroviaria Italiana, RFI).

Services

Trenitalia runs virtually all regional services under contract to Italy's regions, most domestic long-distance trains, and international services to neighbouring countries. Its premium high-speed brand is Le Frecce ("The Arrows"): the Frecciarossa (Red Arrow), Frecciargento (Silver Arrow), and Frecciabianca (White Arrow) trains. The flagship Frecciarossa 1000, built by Hitachi Rail Italy and Bombardier (later Alstom), operates at up to 300 km/h in service on the Turin–Milan–Bologna–Florence–Rome–Naples–Salerno high-speed corridor, competing with the private operator Italo.

Operations abroad

Through Trenitalia's international subsidiaries, the group operates abroad: in the United Kingdom it ran the Avanti West Coast franchise (in a joint venture with FirstGroup, as Trenitalia UK/First Trenitalia) from 2019; in France, Trenitalia France launched Paris–Lyon–Milan services in 2021, the first foreign entrant on French high-speed lines; it also holds stakes in operations in Germany (Netinera) and Greece (Hellenic Train, formerly TrainOSE).

Fleet and tickets

The fleet ranges from the ETR 500 and Frecciarossa 1000 high-speed sets to Jazz, Rock, and Pop regional trains introduced in a fleet-renewal programme. Long-distance fares use yield management with classes such as Standard, Premium, Business, and Executive on Frecciarossa services, while regional fares are set by the regions. The Intercity and Intercity Notte (night) services operate under a public service contract with the state.
