Naval tactics

Naval tactics are the methods used by naval forces to engage and defeat an enemy fleet in battle. They concern the deployment and manoeuvre of warships before and during combat, and are distinguished from naval strategy, which governs the overall conduct of a war at sea.

Age of oar and sail

Ancient galley warfare centred on ramming and boarding, exemplified by battles such as Salamis and Actium. In the age of sail, fleets fought in line-ahead formation to maximise broadside firepower; celebrated manoeuvres included "crossing the T" and Nelson's breaking of the line at Trafalgar in 1805.

Steam, steel and air power

Steam propulsion, rifled guns, torpedoes and armour transformed tactics in the nineteenth century. In the twentieth, the aircraft carrier displaced the battleship as the capital ship, and radar, submarines and guided missiles progressively extended the range and complexity of naval engagements.
