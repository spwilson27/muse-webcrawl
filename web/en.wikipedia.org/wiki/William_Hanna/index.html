William Hanna

Born: 14 July 1910, Melrose, New Mexico
Died: 22 March 2001, Los Angeles, California
Occupation: Animator, director, producer
Known for: Co-founder of Hanna-Barbera; Tom and Jerry

William Denby Hanna (July 14, 1910 – March 22, 2001) was an American animator and cartoonist, co-founder with Joseph Barbera of the Hanna-Barbera animation studio. With Barbera he created Tom and Jerry and produced a string of landmark television cartoons including The Flintstones, Yogi Bear, The Jetsons, and Scooby-Doo.

Early career

Hanna joined Harman and Ising's studio in 1930 and moved to MGM's cartoon studio in 1937, where he was paired with Barbera. Their first collaboration, Puss Gets the Boot (1940), introduced the cat-and-mouse duo that became Tom and Jerry; the series won seven Academy Awards.

Hanna-Barbera

When MGM closed its cartoon studio in 1957, Hanna and Barbera founded their own company to produce animation for television. Using limited animation techniques suited to TV budgets, they launched The Huckleberry Hound Show (1958) and then The Flintstones (1960), the first animated prime-time sitcom. Hanna-Barbera came to dominate American television animation for three decades.

Legacy

Hanna's partnership with Barbera lasted more than sixty years. He died in Los Angeles in 2001, aged 90.
