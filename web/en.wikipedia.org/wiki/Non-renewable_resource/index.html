Non-renewable resource

A non-renewable resource is a natural resource that cannot be readily replaced by natural means on a timescale useful to humans. Most fossil fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas), metal ores, minerals and nuclear fuels are non-renewable because their formation took geological ages.

Categories

Fossil fuels formed from ancient organic matter over hundreds of millions of years. Metal ores and industrial minerals exist in fixed crustal stocks, though metals can be recycled indefinitely in principle. Groundwater in deep aquifers and fertile soil can also behave as effectively non-renewable.

Economics and sustainability

Depletion raises questions formalised in the Hotelling rule and Hubbert peak theory (e.g. "peak oil"). Economists debate how far technological change and substitution can offset scarcity. Environmental concerns — especially climate change from fossil fuel combustion — drive transitions to renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power.
