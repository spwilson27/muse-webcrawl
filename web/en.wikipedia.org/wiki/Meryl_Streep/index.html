Mary Louise "Meryl" Streep (born June 22, 1949) is an American actress. Often described as the greatest actress of her generation, she is known for her versatility, meticulous character work and mastery of accents. Streep holds the record for the most Academy Award nominations of any actor, with 21, and has won three times. She has also received a record 33 Golden Globe nominations, winning eight.

Early life and education

Streep was born in Summit, New Jersey, and raised in Bernardsville. She began singing training as a child and acted in school productions. She studied drama at Vassar College, where one of her teachers was Jean Arthur, and earned a Master of Fine Arts from the Yale School of Drama in 1975. At Yale she performed in numerous stage productions alongside classmates including Sigourney Weaver.

Career

Breakthrough (1976–1982)

After stage work in New York, including productions at the Public Theater under Joseph Papp, Streep made her film debut in Julia (1977). She won an Emmy for the miniseries Holocaust (1978) and received her first Oscar nomination for The Deer Hunter (1978). She won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for Kramer vs. Kramer (1979) and Best Actress for her portrayal of a Polish survivor in Sophie's Choice (1982), a performance frequently ranked among the finest in cinema history.

Established star (1983–1999)

Streep headlined a succession of acclaimed films, including Silkwood (1983), Out of Africa (1985), A Cry in the Dark (1988), Postcards from the Edge (1990), the comedy Death Becomes Her (1992), The Bridges of Madison County (1995), Marvin's Room (1996) and One True Thing (1998), earning Oscar nominations for nearly all of them.

Later career (2000–present)

In the 2000s Streep achieved renewed popular and critical success with Adaptation (2002), The Hours (2002), the miniseries Angels in America (2003, Emmy Award), The Devil Wears Prada (2006), Doubt (2008), Mamma Mia! (2008) — among the highest-grossing films of her career — and Julie & Julia (2009). She won her third Oscar for playing Margaret Thatcher in The Iron Lady (2011). Subsequent notable work includes August: Osage County (2013), Into the Woods (2014), Florence Foster Jenkins (2016), The Post (2017), the series Big Little Lies (2019) and Only Murders in the Building (2023).

Honours and legacy

Streep has received the AFI Life Achievement Award (2004), a Kennedy Center Honor (2011), the Presidential Medal of Freedom from Barack Obama (2014), and the Cecil B. DeMille Award (2017), at whose ceremony her acceptance speech criticising then-President-elect Donald Trump drew worldwide attention. Critics have consistently praised her chameleonic ability to inhabit characters across genres, nationalities and historical periods.

Personal life

Streep was in a relationship with actor John Cazale from 1976 until his death from lung cancer in 1978. Later that year she married sculptor Don Gummer; they have four children, and in 2023 it was confirmed the couple had separated years earlier. Streep is known for philanthropy supporting arts education, women's history and environmental causes, and for narrating documentaries and audiobooks.
