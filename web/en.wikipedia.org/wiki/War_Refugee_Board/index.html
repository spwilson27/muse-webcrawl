War Refugee Board

The War Refugee Board (WRB) was an agency of the United States government established by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 22 January 1944 to rescue victims of Nazi persecution during the Holocaust. Despite being created late in the war and given limited direct funding, the Board is credited with helping to save as many as 200,000 Jews and other refugees.

Origins

The Board grew out of mounting evidence of the Nazi extermination programme and frustration — voiced in a pointed Treasury Department memorandum, the "Report to the Secretary on the Acquiescence of This Government in the Murder of the Jews" — that the State Department had obstructed rescue and relief efforts. Roosevelt's Executive Order 9417 created the Board under the Secretaries of State, Treasury, and War, with John W. Pehle, a Treasury official, as executive director. Pehle told the President the new agency was intended to fully implement American policy of saving "the victims of enemy oppression who are in imminent danger of death".

Activities

The WRB worked through neutral diplomats, the Red Cross, resistance networks, and private relief organisations such as the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee, whose funds it helped license past wartime restrictions. It pressured neutral countries to accept refugees, opened the Fort Ontario Emergency Refugee Shelter in Oswego, New York — the only refugee camp established in the United States during the war — and sponsored operations from Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, Spain, and Portugal. Its representatives included Ira Hirschmann in Turkey and, most famously, it supported the work of Raoul Wallenberg in Budapest, whose protective passports and safe houses saved thousands of Hungarian Jews in 1944. The Board also distributed the Auschwitz Protocols and pressed, unsuccessfully, for the bombing of the Auschwitz killing installations.

Assessment

Historians generally credit the WRB with substantial achievements given its late start and small staff of roughly thirty, while noting that earlier action might have saved far more. The Board was dissolved in September 1945.
