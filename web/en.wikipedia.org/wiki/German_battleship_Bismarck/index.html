German battleship Bismarck

Bismarck was the first of two Bismarck-class battleships built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine. Named after Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, the ship was laid down at the Blohm & Voss shipyard in Hamburg in July 1936 and launched in February 1939. Work was completed in August 1940, when she was commissioned into the German fleet. Together with her sister Tirpitz, she was one of the largest battleships ever built by Germany, and among the largest ever constructed by any European power.

Operation Rheinübung

In May 1941 Bismarck, under Captain Ernst Lindemann and Fleet Chief Admiral Günther Lütjens, sortied into the North Atlantic with the heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen to attack Allied merchant shipping — Operation Rheinübung. In the Battle of the Denmark Strait on 24 May, Bismarck's shells struck the British battlecruiser HMS Hood, which blew up and sank with all but three of her crew; the battleship Prince of Wales was also damaged. Bismarck herself took hits that caused a serious fuel leak, forcing Lütjens to head for Brest in occupied France.

Loss

A massive Royal Navy pursuit followed. On 26 May a torpedo from a Fairey Swordfish biplane launched by HMS Ark Royal jammed Bismarck's rudders, leaving her unable to manoeuvre. The next morning, 27 May 1941, she was engaged by the battleships King George V and Rodney and heavy cruisers; battered and burning, she sank that morning — whether finally by British gunfire and torpedoes or by scuttling charges remains debated. Only 114–115 of her crew of over 2,200 survived.

Wreck

The wreck was located in June 1989 by oceanographer Robert Ballard, lying upright at a depth of about 4,800 metres (15,700 ft), some 650 km west of Brest. Expeditions have since documented the site extensively; it is treated as a war grave.
