Sri Lankan Tamils

Sri Lankan Tamils, also known as Ceylon Tamils or Eelam Tamils, are Tamils native to the South Asian island state of Sri Lanka. They constitute the largest ethnic minority in the country, about 11% of the population (roughly 2.3 million people by the 2012 census), and are concentrated mainly in the Northern and Eastern provinces.

History

Tamil-speaking communities have lived on the island since at least the early centuries BCE, with a medieval Tamil kingdom centred on Jaffna. Sri Lankan Tamils are distinguished from the Indian Tamils of the central highlands, descendants of plantation labourers brought by the British in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Culture and religion

Most Sri Lankan Tamils are Hindus, predominantly Shaivites; significant minorities are Christians (mostly Roman Catholics). Their culture has its own dialect of Tamil, its own literary tradition, and distinctive cuisine and temple festivals.

Civil war and diaspora

Post-independence discrimination and violence, culminating in the Sri Lankan Civil War (1983–2009), drove large-scale emigration; a global diaspora of perhaps a million people now lives chiefly in Canada, the United Kingdom, Germany, India, Switzerland, Australia and France.
