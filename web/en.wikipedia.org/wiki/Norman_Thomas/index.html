Norman Thomas

Norman Mattoon Thomas (November 20, 1884 – December 19, 1968) was an American Presbyterian minister who became the principal leader of the Socialist Party of America. He was its presidential candidate six times, from 1928 to 1948, and for decades was the most prominent democratic socialist voice in the United States.

Norman Thomas
Born: 20 November 1884, Marion, Ohio
Died: 19 December 1968, Huntington, New York
Party: Socialist Party of America
Known for: Six presidential campaigns

Life and career

Trained as a minister at Princeton and Union Theological Seminary, Thomas moved from parish work to pacifism (he opposed American entry into the First World War), civil liberties (he helped found the ACLU), and socialist politics. As presidential nominee his best result came in 1932, with about 885,000 votes amid the Great Depression.

Views and legacy

Thomas championed civil rights, opposed both fascism and Stalinist communism, and advocated a gradual, democratic road to socialism. Many of his once-radical proposals — Social Security, unemployment insurance, a minimum wage — were later enacted by the New Deal, prompting his quip that Americans would never adopt socialism under that name but had adopted much of his platform under the name of liberalism.
