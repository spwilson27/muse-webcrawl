The Diocese of Chelmsford is a Church of England diocese in the Province of Canterbury, covering the county of Essex and the five London boroughs of Barking and Dagenham, Havering, Newham, Redbridge and Waltham Forest. Its cathedral is Chelmsford Cathedral and its bishop is the Bishop of Chelmsford. With a population of several million, it is one of the largest dioceses in the Church of England.

History

The diocese was created in 1914, largely from territory of the ancient Diocese of London, to serve the rapidly growing population of Essex and suburban east London. The parish church of St Mary the Virgin in Chelmsford, rebuilt in the 15th and 16th centuries, became the cathedral.

Structure

Because of its size the diocese is divided into three episcopal areas, each under a suffragan (area) bishop: the Bishop of Barking, the Bishop of Bradwell and the Bishop of Colchester. These correspond broadly to the archdeaconries of the diocese. The area bishops work with the diocesan bishop in overseeing more than 500 parishes and a large number of church schools.
