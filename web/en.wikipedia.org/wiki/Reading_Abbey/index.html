Reading Abbey

Reading Abbey is a large, ruined abbey in the centre of the town of Reading, Berkshire, England. It was founded by King Henry I in 1121, dedicated to St Mary and St John the Evangelist, and grew into one of the richest and most important monasteries in medieval England before its dissolution in 1539.

History

Henry I founded the abbey as his intended burial place and was interred before the high altar in 1136. The abbey held a famous relic, the hand of St James, and hosted parliaments, councils, royal weddings, and the first performance of the earliest known English song with music, "Sumer is icumen in". Its abbots sat in the House of Lords. At the Dissolution, the last abbot, Hugh Faringdon, refused to surrender and was executed for treason in 1539; the abbey was looted and decayed into a quarry for building stone.

The ruins today

The surviving ruins — including the chapter house, refectory, and fragments of the great church — stand in the Forbury Gardens area. After an extensive conservation programme ("Reading Abbey Revealed"), the ruins reopened to the public in 2018 and form the core of the Reading Abbey Quarter, alongside the intact Abbey Gateway and St Laurence's Church.
