The Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) is an independent voluntary consumer organisation headquartered in St Albans, England, which promotes real ale, real cider and perry, and traditional British pubs and clubs. It is now one of the largest single-issue consumer groups in the United Kingdom.

History

CAMRA was founded in 1971 by Michael Hardman, Graham Lees, Jim Makin and Bill Mellor, journalists and drinkers dismayed at the domination of British brewing by a handful of national companies producing pasteurised, filtered and artificially carbonated "keg" beer. Originally called the Campaign for the Revitalisation of Ale, it quickly adopted its present name.

Activities

The campaign publishes the Good Beer Guide, issued annually since 1974, and organises hundreds of beer festivals, most famously the Great British Beer Festival, held since 1977. CAMRA defines real ale as beer brewed from traditional ingredients, matured by secondary fermentation in the container from which it is dispensed, and served without the use of extraneous carbon dioxide. It campaigns on pub closures, beer duty, planning protections for pubs, and — after a 2018 revitalisation review — broadened its remit on disputed issues such as keg craft beer.

Influence

CAMRA is widely credited with sparking the revival of traditional British brewing: the number of UK breweries grew from under 100 in the 1970s to over 2,000 at its modern peak, and its model inspired consumer beer movements abroad, including aspects of the American craft beer scene.
