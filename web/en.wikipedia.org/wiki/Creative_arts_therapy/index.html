Creative arts therapy

Creative arts therapy is an umbrella term for healthcare professions that use the creative process of art-making to improve and enhance the physical, mental and emotional well-being of individuals. The creative arts therapies include art therapy, music therapy, dance/movement therapy, drama therapy, poetry therapy and psychodrama.

Approach

Unlike purely verbal psychotherapy, creative arts therapies engage clients in making or experiencing art — drawing, painting, improvising music, moving or enacting scenes — as the primary means of communication and exploration. Therapists are trained both in their art form and in psychotherapy, and the process and products of the creative work become material for reflection and treatment. Proponents hold that artistic activity can reach emotions that are difficult to verbalise, promote insight and self-expression, and support rehabilitation.

Settings and evidence

Creative arts therapists work in hospitals, schools, mental-health services, hospices, prisons, rehabilitation centres and private practice with children, adults and older people, including those with trauma, dementia, developmental disorders, addiction and chronic illness. In many jurisdictions the professions are licensed or registered, with graduate-level training required. Evidence reviews suggest benefits for anxiety, mood and quality of life in several populations, though the quantity and quality of research varies by modality and condition, and the therapies are generally used as complements to, rather than replacements for, standard medical and psychological care.
