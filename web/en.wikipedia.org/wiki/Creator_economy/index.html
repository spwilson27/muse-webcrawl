Creator economy

Independent creators monetise content directly through platform revenue, sponsorships and fan payments.

The creator economy is a software-driven economy in which content creators, curators and community builders — sometimes collectively called creators — monetise their skills and creativity through digital platforms. It encompasses independent videographers, writers, podcasters, streamers, influencers and other online personalities, together with the software tools and services built to serve them.

History

The term gained currency around 2020–2021 as venture capital investment in creator-focused startups grew. Its antecedents include blogging in the early 2000s, the YouTube Partner Program (launched 2007), which shared advertising revenue with uploaders, and the rise of influencer marketing on Instagram, Twitch, TikTok and other platforms. Analysts commonly estimate the number of people creating content online in the tens of millions, though only a small fraction earn a full-time living.

Monetisation

Creators earn income through advertising revenue shares, brand sponsorships, subscriptions and memberships, tips and donations, merchandise, courses, and platform creator funds. Services such as Patreon, Substack, OnlyFans and Ko-fi allow fans to pay creators directly, while platforms retain percentages of transactions.

Criticism

Commentators note the extreme inequality of earnings — a "power law" in which a small minority capture most revenue — dependence on opaque platform algorithms, burnout, and the transfer of risk from media companies to individuals. Others argue the creator economy democratises media production and lets niche communities support specialised content that traditional gatekeepers would ignore.
