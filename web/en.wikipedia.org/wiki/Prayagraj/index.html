Overview

Prayagraj, known until 2018 as Allahabad, is a city in Uttar Pradesh, northern India, at the confluence (sangam) of the Ganges and Yamuna rivers — with the Saraswati, a river of Hindu tradition believed to join unseen. Prayag, 'place of sacrifice', is among the holiest cities of Hinduism, and every twelve years the city hosts the Kumbh Mela, the largest religious gathering on Earth, when tens of millions of pilgrims bathe at the sangam.

Akbar built the great Allahabad Fort at the confluence in 1583 and gave the city its later name, Ilahabad. The city was an early centre of the Indian independence movement — Anand Bhavan, the Nehru family home, is preserved here — and Allahabad University (1887) is one of India's oldest.

Modern city

Prayagraj is a divisional headquarters and rail junction; its official rename in 2018 restored the older name ahead of the 2019 Ardh Kumbh. The Allahabad High Court remains one of India's principal courts under its historic name.
