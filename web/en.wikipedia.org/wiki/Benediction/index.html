Benediction

[Image placeholder: A priest giving a benediction with raised hand at the close of a church service]

A benediction (Latin bene, "well" + dicere, "to speak" — literally, "a good word") is a short invocation for divine help, blessing and guidance, usually at the end of a church service. More broadly, the word can refer to any expression of blessing, praise or good wishes, and in Roman Catholic practice also to particular objects, persons and devotions blessed by the Church.

In Christian worship

The practice derives from the priestly blessing of the Hebrew Bible: "The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face to shine upon you..." (Numbers 6:24–26), prescribed to Aaron and his sons. In Jewish worship this blessing, the Birkat Kohanim, is still pronounced by priests. Christian liturgies conclude with blessings drawn from the New Testament, notably the apostolic benediction of 2 Corinthians 13:14 ("The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all"), which became a standard ending in Reformed and many other Protestant services. In Anglican and Lutheran worship the benediction follows the sermon or the communion and precedes the dismissal.

Catholic benedictions

The Roman Catholic Church distinguishes the blessing of persons from blessings of objects — churches, altars, rosaries, medals such as the Miraculous Medal, and sacramentals generally — many of which are reserved to bishops or priests and have prescribed formulas in the Rituale Romanum. A distinct devotion is Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, in which the congregation is blessed with the consecrated host displayed in a monstrance.

Related usage

In a secular sense, a benediction is any solemn expression of approval or good wishes. The counterpart, a curse or condemnation, is a malediction. The verb "to bless" likewise appears in phrases such as "blessing a ship" at its launching.
