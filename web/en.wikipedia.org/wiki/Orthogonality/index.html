Orthogonality

In elementary geometry, two lines are orthogonal when they meet at a right angle.

In mathematics, orthogonality is the generalization of the geometric notion of perpendicularity. Two vectors in an inner product space are orthogonal if their inner product is zero; a set of vectors is orthonormal if its members are mutually orthogonal and each has unit length.

Etymology and geometry

The word comes from the Ancient Greek orthogṓnios — "upright angle" — via Latin orthogonius. In Euclidean geometry, two lines or planes are orthogonal when they form a right (90°) angle. In higher dimensions and abstract spaces, the algebraic condition of a vanishing inner product replaces the geometric picture.

Applications

Orthogonal concepts pervade mathematics and its applications: orthogonal polynomials and functions in analysis, orthogonal matrices (whose rows and columns form orthonormal bases) in linear algebra, orthogonal projections, and orthogonal designs in statistics. In computing and engineering the term is used more loosely for independent or non-interfering features — for example, the orthogonality of instructions in a processor architecture or of signals in orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing.
