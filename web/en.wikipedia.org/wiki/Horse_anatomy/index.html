Horse anatomy

External points of the horse, used in describing conformation.

Horse anatomy encompasses the gross and microscopic anatomy of horses and other equids. Because horses have been domesticated and selectively bred for riding, driving, and draught work for millennia, their anatomy is described with a specialised vocabulary covering external points — such as the withers, poll, cannon bone, and fetlock — as well as the skeletal, muscular, digestive, and respiratory systems.

Skeletal system

The horse has approximately 205 bones. Its limbs are adapted for speed: it stands on a single toe (the third digit) protected by the hoof, the elongated cannon bone providing length of stride. The vertebral column and a specialised stay apparatus of tendons and ligaments allow the horse to rest standing with minimal muscular effort.

Digestive system

Horses are hindgut fermenters. A small stomach gives way to a small intestine and then a greatly enlarged caecum and colon, where microbes ferment fibrous forage. This anatomy suits near-continuous grazing but makes the horse prone to colic, and it cannot vomit, so digestive disturbances can be life-threatening.

Hoof

The hoof is a cornified capsule enclosing the pedal bone and sensitive laminae. Its wall, sole, and frog absorb concussion and aid circulation in the limb. Regular trimming or shoeing is required in domestic horses because hoof growth outpaces wear on soft ground.
