Jazz piano

Jazz piano is the use of the piano in jazz, encompassing a century of evolving styles from ragtime and stride to bebop, modal and contemporary jazz.

History

Jelly Roll Morton helped translate ragtime into jazz in early New Orleans. In 1920s Harlem, stride pianists such as James P. Johnson, Willie "the Lion" Smith and Fats Waller developed a virtuosic style with a strong left-hand "stride" between bass notes and chords. Earl Hines's horn-like right-hand lines influenced a generation, and Art Tatum's dazzling technique set a standard for solo piano.

Later styles

Bud Powell brought bebop language to the piano; Thelonious Monk developed a percussive, idiosyncratic style; Bill Evans reshaped harmony with rootless voicings and impressionistic touch in trio playing; Oscar Peterson combined swing and virtuosity; McCoy Tyner and Herbie Hancock defined post-bop and modal piano; Keith Jarrett and Chick Corea carried the instrument into contemporary jazz.

Technique

Jazz pianists comp — accompanying soloists with rhythmic chord voicings — as well as solo, using shell voicings, rootless chords, block chords and quartal harmony.
