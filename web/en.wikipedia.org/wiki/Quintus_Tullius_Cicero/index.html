Quintus Tullius Cicero

Quintus Tullius Cicero

Born: 102 BC
Died: 43 BC
Known for: Soldier, governor of Asia; brother of Cicero

Quintus Tullius Cicero (102 BC – 43 BC) was a Roman statesman and military commander, the younger brother of the famous orator Marcus Tullius Cicero. He served as praetor, as governor of the province of Asia, and as a legate under Julius Caesar in Gaul and under his brother in Cilicia.

Career

Quintus held the quaestorship and praetorship (62 BC) and governed Asia for three years. In Gaul, as Caesar's legate, he famously defended his winter camp against a siege by the Nervii in 54 BC until relieved by Caesar, an episode described in the Commentarii de Bello Gallico. In the civil war he sided with Pompey, was pardoned by Caesar, and later opposed Antony.

Writings and death

He is credited with the Commentariolum Petitionis ("Handbook on Electioneering"), a letter of campaign advice addressed to his brother, though its authenticity is debated, and he wrote poetry and tragedies that have not survived. Proscribed by the Second Triumvirate, he was captured and killed in 43 BC, shortly before his brother's murder.
