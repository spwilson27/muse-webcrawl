Bath is a city in Somerset, south-west England, known for its Roman-built baths and Georgian architecture. Situated in the valley of the River Avon, 97 miles (156 km) west of London, it was founded by the Romans as Aquae Sulis around natural hot springs — the only ones in Britain — and became a fashionable spa town in the Georgian era.

History

The Romans built a temple and bathing complex at the springs from about AD 60–70, dedicated to the goddess Sulis Minerva. After a long decline, Bath was transformed in the 18th century under figures such as Beau Nash and architects John Wood the Elder and Younger, who built the Circus and the Royal Crescent in honey-coloured Bath stone, making the city a byword for elegant Palladian town planning.

Landmarks and culture

Principal sights include the Roman Baths museum, Bath Abbey, Pulteney Bridge, the Circus and the Royal Crescent. Jane Austen lived in Bath from 1801 to 1806 and set parts of Northanger Abbey and Persuasion there. The city was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987 and again in 2021 as part of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.
