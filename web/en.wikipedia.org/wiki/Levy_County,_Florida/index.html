Levy County is a county located on the Gulf coast of the U.S. state of Florida. As of the 2020 census, the population was 42,915. Its county seat is Bronson. The county lies on Florida's "Nature Coast" and includes the coastal community of Cedar Key, a historic fishing and clamming village on a group of offshore islands.

History

Levy County was created in 1845, the year Florida became a state, and named for David Levy Yulee, a territorial delegate to Congress and one of Florida's first United States senators. The county's Gulf ports shipped cedar, turpentine, and seafood in the nineteenth century; Cedar Key was the western terminus of the Florida Railroad, the state's first cross-peninsula railroad, completed in 1861. The Rosewood massacre of 1923, in which the predominantly Black town of Rosewood was destroyed, took place in Levy County.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of 1,413 square miles, of which 1,118 square miles are land and 295 square miles are water. The Suwannee River forms part of the county's northern border, and the Withlacoochee River part of its southern border. Much of the coastline is protected within the Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge and the Lower Suwannee National Wildlife Refuge.

Communities

  • Cities: Cedar Key, Chiefland, Williston
  • Towns: Bronson, Inglis, Otter Creek, Yankeetown
  • Unincorporated communities: Gulf Hammock, Morriston, Rosewood

Economy

Agriculture, forestry, and aquaculture—particularly hard clam farming around Cedar Key—are important to the county economy, along with tourism at Cedar Key and the county's springs and wildlife refuges.
