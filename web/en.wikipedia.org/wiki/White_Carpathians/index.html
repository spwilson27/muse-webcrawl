White Carpathians

The White Carpathians (Czech and Slovak: Bílé Karpaty / Biele Karpaty) are a mountain range on the border between the Czech Republic and Slovakia, forming the westernmost part of the Carpathian Mountains. The highest peak is Veľká Javorina (970 m).

Nature

The range is renowned for its species-rich orchid meadows, among the most diverse in Central Europe, maintained by centuries of traditional mowing and grazing, and for extensive beech and fir forests. Both the Czech and Slovak sides are protected landscape areas, and the region is a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Characteristic settlements include the dispersed Kopanice hamlets and villages known for folk architecture, embroidery and fruit distilling traditions.

Geology

Geologically the White Carpathians belong to the flysch belt of the Outer Western Carpathians, built of alternating sandstones and shales, with klippen limestone outcrops such as Vršatec giving the range its "white" name.
