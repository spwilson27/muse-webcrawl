The Tren Maya (sometimes also Mayan Train or Maya Train) is a 1,554-kilometre (966 mi) intercity railway in Mexico that traverses the Yucatán Peninsula in a circuit beginning in Palenque, Chiapas, and travelling northeast towards Cancún, Quintana Roo, via two routes that encircle the peninsula. The project aims to connect tourist destinations in the Caribbean with lesser-known inland sites, including historic Maya sites, in order to stimulate economic development in southeastern Mexico.

History

The railway was a flagship infrastructure project of President Andrés Manuel López Obrador, announced in 2018 and approved in a public consultation. Construction began in June 2020, with much of the work carried out by the Mexican Army's engineering corps alongside private contractors. Rolling stock (the Xiinbal, Janal and P'atal train types) was built by Alstom in Ciudad Sahagún, Hidalgo.

The first sections opened on 15 December 2023 between Campeche and Cancún, with further sections inaugurated through 2024, completing the full circuit. The line is operated by a company owned by the Secretariat of National Defense (SEDENA).

Criticism

The project has been strongly criticised by environmentalists, archaeologists and some local and Indigenous communities. Concerns include deforestation of jungle along the route, damage to cenotes and underground river systems (particularly on Section 5 near Playa del Carmen, where engineers erected a viaduct over sensitive cave systems), threats to wildlife such as jaguars, and inadequate consultation of Maya communities. Archaeological rescue work along the route, led by the INAH, nonetheless recovered large quantities of artifacts and structures. Costs rose substantially beyond initial estimates.

Route

The circuit comprises seven sections linking Palenque, Tenosique, Escárcega, Campeche, Mérida, Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Chetumal and Xpujil, passing near archaeological zones including Palenque, Uxmal, Chichén Itzá, Tulum and Calakmul.
