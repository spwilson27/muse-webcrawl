Goya Awards

The Goya Awards (Spanish: Premios Goya) are Spain's principal annual film awards, presented by the Academy of Cinematographic Arts and Sciences of Spain to honour the best in Spanish cinema. First presented in 1987, they are named after the painter Francisco Goya.

History

The Academy was founded in 1986, and the first ceremony was held in Madrid in March 1987. The award itself is a bronze bust of Goya based on a design associated with the sculptor Mariano Benlliure. The ceremony has since been hosted in cities including Barcelona, Seville, Málaga, and Valencia.

Categories

Awards are given in categories including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actor and Actress, Best New Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Ibero-American Film. An honorary Goya recognises career achievement.

Notable winners

Multiple Goya winners include directors Pedro Almodóvar and Alejandro Amenábar, and actors Javier Bardem and Penélope Cruz. The film with the most Goya Awards is The Sea Inside (2004), which won 14.
