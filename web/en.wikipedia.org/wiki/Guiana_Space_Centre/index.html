Guiana Space Centre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guiana Space Centre
LocationKourou, French Guiana
OperatorCNES / Arianespace / ESA
Established1964; first launch 1968
UsersAriane, Vega, (formerly) Soyuz
Europe's spaceport at Kourou.

The Guiana Space Centre (Centre spatial guyanais), near Kourou in French Guiana, is Europe's spaceport — the launch base for Ariane and Vega rockets and, between 2011 and 2022, for Russian Soyuz rockets flown from a pad built to Baikonur's pattern in the jungle. Sitting at 5° north latitude, it gives launchers nearly the full benefit of Earth's rotation for geostationary missions.

France chose the site in 1964 for its equatorial ocean-front geography and political stability; the first launch (a Véronique sounding rocket) followed in 1968. The town of Kourou transformed from a small settlement — infamous in history for the nearby Devil's Island penal colony — into a company town of the European space age.

Operations

From Kourou flew Europe's flagship missions: the James Webb Space Telescope on Ariane 5, JUICE, Galileo navigation satellites by the batch, and the ATV freighters to the International Space Station. Soyuz operations ended in 2022 when Russia withdrew its personnel after the invasion of Ukraine.
