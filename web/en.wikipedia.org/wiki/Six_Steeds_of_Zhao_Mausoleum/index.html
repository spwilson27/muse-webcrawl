Six Steeds of Zhao Mausoleum

The Six Steeds of Zhao Mausoleum (昭陵六駿, Zhāolíng Liùjùn) are six celebrated stone relief sculptures of horses from the Zhao Mausoleum (Zhaoling), the tomb of Emperor Taizong of Tang (r. 626–649) in Liquan County, Shaanxi, China. The reliefs depict the emperor's favourite battle chargers, commemorating the horses that carried him in the campaigns that unified China and founded the Tang dynasty.

Description

Each relief, carved in shallow bas-relief on a limestone panel roughly two metres across, portrays a single horse in profile, shown galloping, trotting, or standing. According to tradition, the designs were drawn by the court painter Yan Liben and carved by the sculptor Yan Lide. Each horse has an individual name and is accompanied by an inscription in which Taizong praised its deeds; several of the horses are shown wounded by arrows, emphasising their service in battle.

The six horses are named Saluzi ("Autumn Dew"), Quanmaogua, Shifachi, Baimawu, Telebiao, and Qingzhui.

History

The reliefs originally stood in the north portico of the Zhaoling mausoleum complex. Four of the original panels remain in China and are now displayed at the Xi'an Beilin Museum (Forest of Stone Steles Museum). Two panels — Saluzi and Quanmaogua — were removed in the early 20th century and sold abroad; they are now in the collection of the Penn Museum at the University of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia, a circumstance that has periodically prompted discussion about repatriation.

Legacy

The Six Steeds are regarded as masterpieces of Tang-dynasty stone carving, notable for combining naturalistic observation of equine anatomy and movement with the monumental restraint of funerary art. Copies and reproductions are displayed at the Zhaoling site.
