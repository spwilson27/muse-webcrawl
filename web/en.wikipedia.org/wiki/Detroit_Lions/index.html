The Detroit Lions are a professional American football team based in Detroit, Michigan. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) North division. The team plays its home games at Ford Field in downtown Detroit and has hosted a Thanksgiving Day game annually since 1934.

History

The franchise began in 1930 as the Portsmouth Spartans of Portsmouth, Ohio, and moved to Detroit in 1934, taking the Lions name. Led by quarterback Bobby Layne, the Lions won three NFL championships in the 1950s (1952, 1953, 1957). The following decades brought long stretches of futility, highlighted by stars such as Barry Sanders and Calvin Johnson, and the league's first 0–16 season in 2008.

Recent years

Under head coach Dan Campbell and general manager Brad Holmes, the Lions rebuilt into contenders, winning the NFC North in 2023 — their first division title in three decades — and reaching the NFC Championship Game, then posting a franchise-best 15–2 record in 2024.
