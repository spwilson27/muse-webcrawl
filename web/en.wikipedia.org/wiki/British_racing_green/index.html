British racing green

British racing green (), or BRG, is the traditional international motor racing colour of the United Kingdom. The shade most associated with the name is a deep, rich green (RGB 0, 66, 37). It has no single fixed standard, and historic examples range from light olive to near-black green.

A 1920s Bentley in British racing green.

Origins

International racing colours were allocated in the Gordon Bennett Cup races of the early 1900s: blue for France, red for Italy, white (later silver) for Germany and yellow for Belgium. Britain, hosting the 1903 Cup in Ireland after victory in 1902, could not use red, white or blue — all taken by competitors or reserved — and adopted green, in part as a tribute to Ireland, the host. Napier, the winning make in 1902, already painted its cars green, and the colour stuck.

Use

British racing green was carried by famous teams including Bentley at Le Mans in the 1920s, Vanwall — whose 1958 Constructors' Championship was the first won by a British team — Cooper, Lotus, BRM and Jaguar. When Jaguar returned to Formula One in 2000 it raced in green, and Aston Martin's Formula One team adopted a modernised racing green from 2021. The colour remains a popular choice for British sports cars generally.
