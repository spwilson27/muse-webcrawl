Mickey Mouse is an American cartoon character co-created in 1928 by Walt Disney and Ub Iwerks. The longtime icon and mascot of the Walt Disney Company, Mickey is an anthropomorphic mouse who typically wears red shorts, large yellow shoes and white gloves. He first appeared in the short film Steamboat Willie (1928), one of the first sound cartoons, and became one of the world's most recognisable fictional characters.

Creation

Mickey was created as a replacement for Oswald the Lucky Rabbit, whose rights Disney had lost to Universal Pictures. Developed in secret, the character starred in the silent shorts Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho before Steamboat Willie, released on 18 November 1928 at the Colony Theater in New York, made him a star. Disney himself provided Mickey's falsetto voice from 1928 to 1947.

History

During the 1930s Mickey starred in more than 130 films, including The Band Concert (1935), his first colour cartoon, and the "Sorcerer's Apprentice" segment of Fantasia (1940). He received ten Academy Award nominations for Best Animated Short, winning for Lend a Paw (1941). As the more mischievous Donald Duck and Goofy rose in popularity, Mickey's film appearances declined, but he grew into a global corporate symbol, central to Disney theme parks, merchandising and the Mickey Mouse Club television series (1955–1959). In 1978 he became the first cartoon character to receive a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. A new series of theatrical-style shorts beginning in 2013, and the online series The Wonderful World of Mickey Mouse, revived the character's popularity with younger audiences.

Copyright status

The version of Mickey Mouse appearing in Steamboat Willie entered the public domain in the United States on 1 January 2024, following decades of extensions to copyright law — the Copyright Term Extension Act of 1998 was popularly nicknamed the "Mickey Mouse Protection Act". Later versions of the character remain protected, and Disney retains trademark rights in Mickey as a brand identifier.
