Solastalgia

Solastalgia is a neologism coined by Australian philosopher Glenn Albrecht in 2003 to describe the distress caused by environmental change affecting a person's home environment while they remain in it. It combines the Latin solacium (comfort) and desolare (to devastate or abandon) with the Greek root -algia (pain), echoing "nostalgia." Whereas nostalgia is homesickness for a place one has left, solastalgia is the homesickness one feels while still at home, because the home environment is being transformed — by drought, mining, deforestation, war, or climate change.

Development of the concept

Albrecht developed the term from research with communities in the Upper Hunter Valley of New South Wales, Australia, affected by open-cut coal mining and prolonged drought. Residents reported grief, loss of solace, and a sense of powerlessness as familiar landscapes were degraded around them. Albrecht published the concept in his 2005 paper "Solastalgia: A New Concept in Human Health and Identity" and elaborated it in his 2019 book Earth Emotions, which also proposed related terms such as terrafurie and the hoped-for era of the "Symbiocene."

Use and influence

Solastalgia has been taken up in environmental psychology, public health, the arts, and climate literature as part of the vocabulary of ecological grief and climate anxiety. Studies have documented experiences consistent with solastalgia among Inuit communities facing sea-ice loss, farmers during the Australian Millennium Drought, and survivors of wildfires and disasters. Researchers have developed psychometric scales to measure it, while some caution that the concept overlaps with place attachment, eco-anxiety, and general environmental distress. The word has appeared in art installations, music, and popular writing about climate change, notably in discussions of "climate grief."
