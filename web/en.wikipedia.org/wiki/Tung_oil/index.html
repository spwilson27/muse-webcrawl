Tung oil or China wood oil is a drying oil obtained by pressing the seed from the nut of the tung tree (Vernicia fordii, formerly Aleurites fordii). The oil and its use are believed to have originated in ancient China and appear in the writings of Confucius around 400 BC. Raw tung oil tends to dry to a fine, wrinkled finish; commercial products are usually heat-treated or blended to produce a smooth, hard, water-resistant coating.

Composition

The predominant fatty acid in tung oil is α-eleostearic acid, a conjugated triene that makes up about 80 percent of the oil. This high proportion of conjugated double bonds explains tung oil's unusually fast drying and its great resistance to water and alkalis compared with linseed oil and other drying oils.

Uses

Tung oil has traditionally been used in China to waterproof wood, paper umbrellas, and the hulls of wooden boats, mixed with lime as a caulking compound. Today it is valued as a food-safe wood finish for furniture, flooring, cutting boards, and musical instruments, where its polymerised film resists water, alcohol, and food acids. It is also used in paints, varnishes, linoleum, oilcloth, and printing inks.

Cultivation

The tung tree is native to southern China, Myanmar, and northern Vietnam. Commercial plantations were established in the twentieth century in Argentina, Paraguay, and the southern United States, where the Gulf Coast tung industry flourished before hurricanes and synthetic substitutes reduced production.
