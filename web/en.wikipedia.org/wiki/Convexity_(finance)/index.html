Convexity (finance)

In mathematical finance, convexity refers to non-linearities in a financial model. In other words, if the price of an underlying variable changes, the price of a derivative changes in a non-linear fashion. A portfolio of options has convexity because the value of an option is a convex function of the underlying asset price; the second derivative of the price with respect to the underlying is captured by the Greek gamma.

Bond convexity

For bonds, convexity is a measure of the curvature of the relationship between bond prices and interest rates, supplementing duration, which measures the first-order (linear) sensitivity. The percentage change in a bond's price can be approximated by the duration effect plus a convexity adjustment proportional to the square of the change in yield. Because the price–yield curve is convex, a bond's price rises more when yields fall than it drops when yields rise by the same amount — a desirable property for investors, all else equal.

Negative convexity

Callable bonds and mortgage-backed securities can exhibit negative convexity: as rates fall, prepayment or call becomes more likely, capping price appreciation and bending the price–yield curve the other way.
