Yangon (formerly Rangoon) is the largest city in Myanmar and was its capital until the military government abruptly moved the seat of government to the purpose-built city of Naypyidaw in 2005–06. Yangon remains the country's commercial and cultural heart, located where the Yangon and Bago rivers meet, about 30 km from the Gulf of Martaban in the Indian Ocean system.

History and sights

The city grew around the Shwedagon Pagoda — a 99-metre gilded stupa said to enshrine relics of four Buddhas and venerated for over a millennium — and the fishing village of Dagon. King Alaungpaya founded Yangon ("end of strife") in 1755; the British annexed it in the Second Anglo-Burmese War (1852), rebuilt it as Rangoon with a grid plan and grand colonial buildings, and made it one of Asia's great ports, drawing large Indian and Chinese communities. Independence came in 1948; decades of military rule and isolation followed, then a partial opening in the 2010s that brought investment and restored many colonial facades, and the February 2021 military coup, which triggered nationwide protests centred heavily on Yangon and a devastating civil conflict that continues to shape life in the city and country.
