Raions of Ukraine

Raions (Ukrainian: райони) are the second-level administrative subdivisions of Ukraine, below the oblasts (provinces) and above the hromadas (municipalities). A raion, a term inherited from the Soviet administrative system (Russian: район, "district"), typically encompasses several cities, towns and villages around an administrative centre.

The 2020 reform

Before 2020 Ukraine had 490 raions, many of them small and duplicating the functions of decentralised local government. As part of the decentralisation reform, the Verkhovna Rada adopted a resolution on 17 July 2020 that abolished the old districts and created 136 new, much larger raions. At the same time, amalgamated territorial communities (hromadas) took over most local services, leaving raion councils and state administrations with a reduced coordinating role.

Administration

Each raion has an elected raion council and a raion state administration whose head is appointed by the President of Ukraine on the advice of the Cabinet of Ministers. The reform also applied to the Autonomous Republic of Crimea, where raions were reorganised on paper, though the peninsula remains outside Kyiv's effective control. Raions in territories occupied during the Russo-Ukrainian war likewise exist formally within Ukraine's administrative framework.

Statistics

The 136 raions vary greatly in population, from under 100,000 to more than a million in the largest metropolitan districts such as Kharkiv and Dnipro raions.
