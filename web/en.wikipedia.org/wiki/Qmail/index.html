qmail is a mail transfer agent (MTA) that runs on Unix. It was written as a more secure replacement for Sendmail by Daniel J. Bernstein, a mathematician, starting around 1995 and first released in 1997. qmail was designed around a philosophy of minimal, modular components, each running with the least privilege necessary and communicating only through well-defined channels.

Architecture

Instead of one large program, qmail is a pipeline of small utilities: qmail-smtpd accepts mail over SMTP, qmail-queue stores it in the queue directory, qmail-send schedules delivery, qmail-lspawn and qmail-rspawn handle local and remote delivery, and qmail-clean tidies the queue. qmail introduced the Maildir mailbox format, in which each message is stored in its own file, avoiding the locking problems of the traditional mbox format. It has no monolithic configuration file; each tunable is a single file in the control directory.

Security

Bernstein offered a US$500 reward for anyone who could find a security hole in qmail, and for years none was claimed—the first publicly accepted vulnerability (a potential integer overflow) appeared only in 2005. This record, together with its modular design, made qmail a benchmark for secure software. In 2007, Bernstein placed qmail in the public domain, encouraging the community patches and forks that had long been maintained informally.

Legacy

qmail ran a significant share of the world's mail servers in the late 1990s and early 2000s, and its Maildir format is still widely used. Its influence extends to the design of later MTAs and to the broader practice of privilege-separated, least-privilege system design.
