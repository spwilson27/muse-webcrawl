Historical Villages of Portugal

The Historical Villages of Portugal (Portuguese: Aldeias Históricas de Portugal) are a group of twelve villages in the Beira Interior region of central Portugal, recognised for their medieval heritage, castle fortifications, and role in the historical defence of the Portuguese frontier with Spain. The programme was established by the Portuguese government in the 1990s to restore and promote the villages for cultural tourism.

The villages

The twelve villages are Almeida, Belmonte, Castelo Mendo, Castelo Novo, Castelo Rodrigo, Idanha-a-Velha, Linhares da Beira, Marialva, Monsanto, Piódão, Sortelha, and Trancoso. Many are built of granite or schist and crowned by castles dating from the Christian reconquest and the wars of independence with Castile. Monsanto, with its houses built among giant boulders, has become one of the best known.

Heritage

The network promotes walking routes, restoration of vernacular architecture, and local crafts and gastronomy, and the villages are linked by a Grande Rota (long-distance trail). Belmonte is noted for its Jewish heritage, and Idanha-a-Velha preserves Roman and Visigothic remains.
