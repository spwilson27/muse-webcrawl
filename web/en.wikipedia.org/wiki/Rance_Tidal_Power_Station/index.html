Rance Tidal Power Station

The Rance Tidal Power Station is a tidal power station located on the estuary of the Rance River in Brittany, France. Opened in 1966, it was the world's first tidal power station and remained the world's largest for 45 years, until the Sihwa Lake Tidal Power Station in South Korea surpassed it in 2011.

Description

The installation uses a barrage approximately 750 metres long across the Rance estuary, which has one of the largest tidal ranges in the world, averaging about 8 metres and reaching up to 13.5 metres at spring tides. The powerhouse contains 24 reversible bulb turbines of 10 megawatts each, for a total installed capacity of 240 MW. The turbines generate electricity from tidal flows in both directions and can also pump water to increase production during favourable periods. Annual output is around 500–600 GWh.

History

Plans to exploit the Rance tides date to the early 20th century. Construction began in 1961, requiring two cofferdams to dry out the estuary bed. The plant was inaugurated by President Charles de Gaulle on 26 November 1966, at a cost of about 620 million francs. It is operated by Électricité de France (EDF).

Other uses and impacts

The barrage carries a road linking Dinard and Saint-Malo, with a lock allowing boats to pass between the estuary and the sea. The plant is also a tourist attraction, drawing tens of thousands of visitors annually. Environmental effects include changes to sedimentation and the estuary's ecosystem, which has largely adapted over the decades since construction.
