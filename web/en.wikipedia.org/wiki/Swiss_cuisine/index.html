Swiss cuisine

Swiss cuisine reflects the country's regional diversity, blending influences from German, French and Italian cooking with Alpine farming traditions. It is internationally known for its cheeses and chocolate, and for dishes such as fondue, raclette and rösti.

Cheese and dairy

Switzerland produces hundreds of cheese varieties, including Emmental, Gruyère, Appenzeller and Tête de Moine. Fondue, melted cheese with wine into which bread is dipped, and raclette, cheese melted and scraped over potatoes, are national winter institutions.

Regional dishes

German-speaking Switzerland favours hearty fare such as rösti (crisp fried grated potato) and sausages; the French-speaking west is known for lake fish and malakoff cheese fritters; Italian-speaking Ticino serves polenta and risotto; and Graubünden offers barley soup and capuns.

Chocolate

Swiss chocolatiers pioneered milk chocolate and the conching process in the 19th century, and Switzerland remains a leading chocolate producer and the world's highest per-capita consumer.
