Angus (Scottish Gaelic: Aonghas) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland, and a historic county and registration county. It borders Aberdeenshire, Perth and Kinross, Dundee City and the North Sea. The administrative centre is Forfar, and the largest town is Arbroath.

Geography

Angus can be divided into three geographic areas: the fertile lowland of Strathmore in the centre; the Angus Glens — Glen Esk, Glen Prosen, Glen Clova, Glen Lethnot and Glen Isla — cutting into the Grampian foothills in the north-west; and the coastal plain along the North Sea, home to Arbroath, Montrose and Carnoustie.

History

The area was historically the province of the Pictish kingdom, rich in carved stones, and later a county known as Forfarshire until 1928. In 1320 the Declaration of Arbroath, asserting Scottish independence, was drawn up at Arbroath Abbey. Glamis Castle, in Strathmore, is the childhood home of Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother.

Economy

Agriculture is important, particularly soft fruit and the Aberdeen Angus cattle breed, which originated here. Arbroath is known for the Arbroath smokie, a smoked haddock delicacy.
