Ural River

The Ural River (Russian: Урал, Ural; Kazakh: Жайық, Zhayıq), known before 1775 as the Yaik River, is a river flowing through Russia and Kazakhstan in the continental border between Asia and Europe. It originates in the southern Ural Mountains and discharges into the Caspian Sea.

Course

At 2,428 kilometres (1,509 mi), it is the third-longest river in Europe after the Volga and the Danube. The river rises near Mount Kruglaya in the Ural Mountains, flows south through Magnitogorsk, then west past Orenburg, and continues into Kazakhstan past Oral (Uralsk) before forming a large swampy delta at Atyrau where it empties into the Caspian Sea.

Geography and boundary

The Ural River is traditionally regarded as part of the boundary between the continents of Europe and Asia: the portion west of the river lies in Europe and the portion east in Asia. This convention dates to the 18th-century work of Philip Johan von Strahlenberg and Vasily Tatishchev, accepted by the Imperial Russian Geographical Society.

Ecology

The river's delta and nearby wetlands are important habitats for birds and fish, including beluga sturgeon historically prized for caviar. Seasonal flooding from snowmelt shapes the floodplain forests of poplar and willow along its lower course.
