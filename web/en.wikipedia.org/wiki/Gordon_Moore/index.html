Gordon Earle Moore (3 January 1929 – 24 March 2023) was an American businessman, engineer, and the co-founder and emeritus chairman of Intel Corporation. He proposed Moore's law, the observation that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles approximately every two years.

Career

After a doctorate in chemistry from Caltech, Moore joined William Shockley's laboratory in 1956 but left with the "traitorous eight" to found Fairchild Semiconductor in 1957. In 1965, while at Fairchild, he published the article in Electronics magazine that set out the prediction later called Moore's law. In 1968 he and Robert Noyce founded Intel, where Moore served as executive vice president, president (1975), and chief executive officer (1979–1987), remaining chairman until 1997 and chairman emeritus thereafter.

Philanthropy and honours

With his wife Betty he established the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation in 2000, endowed with a gift of Intel stock, which supports environmental conservation, science, and the San Francisco Bay Area. Moore funded the Thirty Meter Telescope project and major university programmes. He received the National Medal of Technology (1990) and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2002), the highest civilian honour in the United States, and the IEEE Medal of Honor in 2008.
