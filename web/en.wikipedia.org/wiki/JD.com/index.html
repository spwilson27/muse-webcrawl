JD.com, Inc. (Chinese: 京东; pinyin: Jīngdōng), also known as Jingdong and formerly called 360buy, is a Chinese e-commerce company headquartered in Beijing. It is one of the two massive B2C online retailers in China by transaction volume and revenue, alongside Alibaba's Tmall.

History

The company was founded by Liu Qiangdong (Richard Liu) in 1998 as JD Multimedia, a chain of shops selling magneto-optical products in Beijing's Zhongguancun district. After the SARS outbreak of 2003 hurt offline sales, Liu moved the business online in 2004, and the retail platform grew rapidly on a promise of authentic goods — a contrast with counterfeit problems on rival marketplaces — backed by a self-operated logistics network.

JD.com listed on the Nasdaq in May 2014, in what was then the largest listing by a Chinese company in the United States that year; it completed a secondary listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in June 2020. Tencent and Walmart have held significant stakes, and the group has spun off or listed subsidiaries including JD Logistics, JD Health and JD Industrials.

Business

Unlike marketplace-only rivals, JD operates its own nationwide fulfilment infrastructure: hundreds of warehouses, a large last-mile delivery workforce, and extensive automation, including drone delivery pilots and automated warehouses. Its logistics arm, JD Logistics, was spun out and listed in Hong Kong in 2021. The group also runs JD Health, JD Cloud, and the discount platform Jingxi, and competes with Alibaba, Pinduoduo and Douyin in China's e-commerce market.
