Butylated hydroxytoluene

AbbreviationBHT
FormulaC15H24O
E numberE321
FunctionAntioxidant, preservative

BHT is a synthetic phenolic antioxidant.

Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), also known as dibutylhydroxytoluene, is a lipophilic organic compound, chemically a derivative of phenol, that is useful for its antioxidant properties. It is widely used to prevent oxidation in foods, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, and industrial products such as fuels and rubbers.

Production and mechanism

BHT is produced industrially by the reaction of p-cresol with isobutylene, catalysed by sulfuric acid or acidic zeolites. Like other hindered phenols, it acts as a radical scavenger: it donates a hydrogen atom to peroxy radicals formed during autoxidation of fats and oils, breaking the chain reaction that leads to rancidity.

Uses and safety

As food additive E321, BHT is permitted in many jurisdictions in low concentrations in fats, oils, chewing gum, and cereals; its close relative BHA (butylated hydroxyanisole) is often used alongside it. Regulatory reviews by agencies such as the FDA and EFSA have generally concluded it is safe at permitted levels, though high-dose animal studies have produced conflicting results about possible carcinogenic and endocrine effects, and some manufacturers have voluntarily removed it from products in response to consumer concern. BHT also has minor uses as an antioxidant in jet fuels, transformer oils, and embalming fluid.
