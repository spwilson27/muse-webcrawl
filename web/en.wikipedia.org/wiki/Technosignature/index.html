Technosignature

A technosignature (or technomarker) is any measurable property or effect that provides scientific evidence of past or present technology, and thus of intelligent life. Technosignatures are the technological counterpart of biosignatures, which indicate the presence of life whether intelligent or not. The search for technosignatures forms the basis of the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI).

Types

  • Radio and optical signals — deliberate beacons or leaked transmissions; the target of classic SETI programmes and projects such as Breakthrough Listen.
  • Megastructures — hypothetical constructions such as Dyson spheres, which might be detected through infrared waste heat or anomalous stellar dimming (as investigated for Tabby's Star).
  • Atmospheric technosignatures — industrial pollutants such as chlorofluorocarbons or nitrogen dioxide detectable in exoplanet atmospheres.
  • Artificial illumination — city lights on the night side of an exoplanet.
  • Waste heat — mid-infrared excesses surveyed by missions such as WISE in searches for galaxy-spanning civilisations.

History

The term gained prominence in the 2000s and 2010s, notably through the work of astronomer Jill Tarter, who proposed "technosignature" as a broader replacement for narrower terms. A 2018 NASA workshop report helped formalise the field and spur funding for technosignature research beyond traditional radio SETI.
