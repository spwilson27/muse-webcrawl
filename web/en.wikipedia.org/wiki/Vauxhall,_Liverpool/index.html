Vauxhall is an inner-city district of Liverpool, Merseyside, England, immediately north of the city centre. It lies within the Liverpool Riverside parliamentary constituency and forms an electoral ward of Liverpool City Council. The area is bounded approximately by the River Mersey and the northern docks to the west, Scotland Road to the east, Kirkdale to the north, and Everton and the city centre to the south and east.

History

The name derives from Vauxhall Gardens, a pleasure garden established in the early 19th century on the banks of the Mersey. During the Industrial Revolution, Vauxhall became one of Liverpool's most densely populated working-class districts, serving the docks and industries along the Leeds and Liverpool Canal. Rows of terraced housing and tenements housed a large Irish immigrant community, and the area around Scotland Road became known as Liverpool's "Little Ireland".

Slum clearances in the mid-20th century replaced many Victorian terraces with new housing, and the population fell substantially. Notable former institutions include the Tate & Lyle sugar refinery at Love Lane, once a major local employer.

Landmarks

St Anthony's Roman Catholic Church on Scotland Road, built in the 19th century, is a well-known landmark serving the historic Irish Catholic community. The area also contains the Stanley Dock complex to its west, part of Liverpool's historic docklands, and the Eldonians community village, an award-winning community-led housing development built on the former Tate & Lyle site in the 1980s.
