Spherical triangle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A spherical triangle is a figure formed on the surface of a sphere by three great-circle arcs intersecting pairwise in three vertices. It is the spherical analogue of the planar triangle, and its properties are the subject of spherical trigonometry.

Properties

The sides of a spherical triangle are measured as angles (or arc lengths), and the sum of its interior angles is always greater than 180° and less than 540°. The amount by which the angle sum exceeds 180°, called the spherical excess, determines the area of the triangle through Girard's theorem: area = R² × E, where R is the radius of the sphere and E the excess in radians. Unlike planar triangles, two spherical triangles with the same angles are congruent, and a spherical triangle can have two or even three right angles — for example, the triangle formed by the North Pole and two points on the equator a quarter-circumference apart.

Applications

Spherical triangles arise in navigation and astronomy, where the "navigational triangle" formed by the celestial pole, the observer's zenith and a celestial body is solved to find position and bearing.
