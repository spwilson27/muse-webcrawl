Charles Demuth

Charles Henry Buckius Demuth (November 8, 1883 – October 23, 1935) was an American painter who specialized in watercolors and turned to oils late in his career, developing a style of painting known as Precisionism. His most famous work, I Saw the Figure 5 in Gold (1928), was inspired by William Carlos Williams's poem "The Great Figure".

Life

Demuth was born in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, where his family ran a tobacco business. He studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts under Thomas Anshutz and William Merritt Chase, and travelled to Paris, where he encountered the work of the European avant-garde. He became part of the circle around Alfred Stieglitz and Marsden Hartley. A hip injury in childhood left him with a limp, and he suffered from diabetes, receiving some of the earliest insulin treatments in the United States.

Work

Demuth's early watercolors depict flowers, architectural subjects, and scenes of vaudeville and café life with delicate economy. In the 1920s he produced a series of "poster portraits" — symbolic portraits of friends such as William Carlos Williams, Georgia O'Keeffe, and Hartley rendered through objects and typography rather than likenesses. I Saw the Figure 5 in Gold (1928), now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, is the best known of these.

His Precisionist paintings of grain elevators, factories, and the Lancaster landscape present industrial America with clean, geometric clarity.

Legacy

The Demuth Museum in Lancaster preserves his home and studio. His work influenced later American modernists and Pop artists, and The Figure 5 in Gold helped inspire Robert Indiana's "LOVE" imagery.
