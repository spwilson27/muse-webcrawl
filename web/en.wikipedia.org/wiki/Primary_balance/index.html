Primary balance

In government finance, the primary balance is the fiscal balance excluding interest payments on outstanding public debt: it equals government revenues minus non-interest spending. A primary surplus occurs when revenues exceed non-interest expenditure, and a primary deficit when they fall short.

Role in debt dynamics

The primary balance is central to analysis of debt sustainability. The change in the debt-to-GDP ratio depends on the difference between the real interest rate on debt and the growth rate of GDP, offset by the primary balance: if the interest rate exceeds the growth rate, a government must run primary surpluses merely to keep the debt ratio stable. Specifically, the debt ratio stabilizes when the primary surplus equals the debt ratio multiplied by (r − g)/(1 + g).

Use in fiscal policy

The primary balance is a standard indicator in IMF and European Union fiscal surveillance, including the EU's fiscal rules, because it isolates the current discretionary stance of fiscal policy from the legacy cost of past borrowing. Cyclically adjusted primary balances are used to assess policy effort independently of economic fluctuations and interest costs.
