Fava bean

Scientific nameVicia faba
FamilyFabaceae (legumes)
OriginMediterranean / Near East
Other namesBroad bean, faba bean, horse bean

The fava bean (Vicia faba), also called the broad bean or faba bean, is a species of flowering plant in the pea and bean family, Fabaceae, grown for its large, flat edible seeds. It is one of the oldest cultivated legumes, domesticated in the Near East thousands of years ago.

Cultivation

The plant is a hardy annual with erect stems, pinnate leaves, and white flowers marked with black. Its long pods contain two to eight large seeds. Unusually among crop legumes, it tolerates cold and is sown in autumn or early spring in temperate climates; like other legumes it fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root-nodule bacteria, improving soil fertility.

Culinary use

Fava beans are eaten fresh, dried, or frozen and feature across Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cuisines: Egyptian ful medames, Greek and Italian spring dishes, and falafel made with beans rather than chickpeas. Young pods and shoots are also eaten. In China the beans are roasted as snacks and used in Sichuan doubanjiang.

Health note

The beans contain vicine and convicine, which can trigger haemolytic anaemia (favism) in people with the inherited enzyme deficiency G6PD deficiency — a condition long associated with fava-growing regions around the Mediterranean.
