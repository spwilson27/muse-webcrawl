Förster resonance energy transfer

Named afterTheodor Förster
TypeDipole–dipole coupling mechanism

Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET), also called fluorescence resonance energy transfer, is a mechanism describing the non-radiative transfer of excitation energy from a donor chromophore to an acceptor chromophore through dipole–dipole coupling. It is named after the German physicist Theodor Förster, who described it quantitatively in the 1940s.

Mechanism

When a donor fluorophore in its excited state lies close to an acceptor molecule whose absorption spectrum overlaps the donor's emission spectrum, energy can pass directly between them without emission of a photon. FRET efficiency falls with the sixth power of the donor–acceptor distance, making it sensitive over separations of roughly 1–10 nanometres and earning it the nickname "spectroscopic ruler".

Applications

FRET is widely used in biochemistry and cell biology to study protein–protein interactions, conformational changes, enzyme activity and the proximity of labelled molecules in living cells, often with pairs of fluorescent proteins such as CFP and YFP.
