Rachel Treweek (born 4 February 1963) is a British Anglican bishop. Since July 2015 she has been Bishop of Gloucester, the first woman to serve as a diocesan bishop in the Church of England, and the first woman to sit as a Lord Spiritual in the House of Lords.

Early life and ministry

Treweek was born in Broxbourne, Hertfordshire, and trained as a speech and language therapist, working in the National Health Service before training for ordination at Wycliffe Hall, Oxford. Ordained deacon in 1994 and priest in 1995, she served in the Diocese of London, including as vicar of St James-the-Less, Bethnal Green, and from 2011 as Archdeacon of Hackney. She was also a chaplain to Queen Elizabeth II.

Bishop of Gloucester

Her appointment as Bishop of Gloucester was announced in March 2015, and she was consecrated at Canterbury Cathedral on 22 July 2015 by Archbishop Justin Welby. Under the Lords Spiritual (Women) Act 2015, newly in force, she took precedence over longer-serving male bishops and was introduced to the House of Lords on 26 October 2015 — the first woman ever to sit on the bishops' benches.

Treweek also serves as Bishop to His Majesty's Prisons, the Church of England's lead bishop for prisons. She caused wide discussion in 2016 by suggesting that God should not invariably be referred to as "he," arguing for inclusive language in worship. She has spoken in the Lords on criminal justice, children in care and women's issues. In 2025 she announced her intention to retire as Bishop of Gloucester.
