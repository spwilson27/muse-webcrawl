Secrets & Lies (film)

Secrets & Lies is a 1996 British drama film written and directed by Mike Leigh. It tells the story of Hortense, a well-educated black optometrist who, after the death of her adoptive mother, searches for her birth mother — and discovers that she is Cynthia, a lonely white working-class factory worker. The film stars Brenda Blethyn, Marianne Jean-Baptiste, Timothy Spall, Phyllis Logan and Claire Rushbrook. It won the Palme d'Or at the 1996 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for five Academy Awards.

Plot

Hortense Cumberbatch, a successful black London optometrist, decides after her adoptive mother's death to trace her biological mother. The adoption agency's records lead her to Cynthia Purley, a white factory worker living in a rundown house with her sharp-tongued daughter Roxanne, a street sweeper. Cynthia initially denies the possibility, then breaks down and accepts the truth — she had given up the baby as a teenager. The two women begin a tentative friendship, while Cynthia's brother Maurice, a photographer, and his wife Monica struggle with secrets of their own. The film culminates at Roxanne's twenty-first birthday barbecue, where the accumulated secrets of the whole family erupt and are, imperfectly, reconciled.

Production

As with Leigh's other films, Secrets & Lies was developed through months of improvisation and rehearsal in which the actors built their characters, with the final script crystallised from that work. Remarkably, Brenda Blethyn did not know until the scene was filmed that the actress playing her daughter was black; her shocked reaction in their café meeting is genuine to the method.

Reception

The film was a critical triumph. It won the Palme d'Or and the Best Actress prize for Blethyn at Cannes, and received Academy Award nominations for Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actress (Blethyn), Best Supporting Actress (Jean-Baptiste) and Best Original Screenplay. It is frequently ranked among the greatest British films of the twentieth century.
