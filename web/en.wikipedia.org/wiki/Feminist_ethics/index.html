Feminist ethics

Feminist ethics is an approach to ethics that builds on the belief that traditional ethical theorizing has undervalued and underappreciated women's moral experience and seeks to reimagine ethics through a feminist lens. It emphasizes relationships, care, context, and the moral significance of emotion, challenging the emphasis in traditions such as Kantian ethics and utilitarianism on abstract universal rules and impartiality.

Ethics of care

A central strand is the ethics of care, developed from Carol Gilligan's In a Different Voice (1982), which argued that women often approach moral problems in terms of responsibilities within relationships rather than abstract rights. Philosophers such as Nel Noddings and Virginia Held elaborated care as a moral value and practice foundational to human life.

Themes

Feminist ethicists critique the public/private distinction, examine how power and oppression shape moral life, and argue that supposedly neutral moral theory can encode male experience as the norm. Feminist bioethics applies these insights to medicine and reproduction, while intersectional approaches stress that gender interacts with race, class, and other structures.
