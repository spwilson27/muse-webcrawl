The pound sterling (symbol: £; ISO code: GBP) is the currency of the United Kingdom and its associated territories. It is the world's oldest currency still in use, with origins in the eighth century, and is one of the most heavily traded currencies in foreign exchange markets. "Sterling" distinguishes the currency from other currencies called the pound, and in finance it is often referred to as the British pound or simply sterling.

History

The pound was originally literally a pound weight of silver: silver pennies were introduced under King Offa of Mercia in the late eighth century, modelled on the Frankish denier, and 240 pennies made up a pound of sterling silver. The Norman Conquest standardised the system of pounds, shillings and pence (£sd) — 12 pennies to the shilling, 20 shillings to the pound — which survived until Decimal Day, 15 February 1971, when the pound was divided into 100 new pence. The gold sovereign, guinea, florin and crown are among the many historic denominations. Sterling was on the gold standard for much of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and was the leading international reserve currency before being overtaken by the US dollar after the Second World War and the 1944 Bretton Woods conference.

Issuance

Monetary policy is set by the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee. In England and Wales the Bank of England has the monopoly of note issue; in Scotland and Northern Ireland several commercial banks issue their own sterling banknotes, backed by Bank of England notes. The Crown Dependencies and some British Overseas Territories issue local variants pegged at par, including the Jersey, Guernsey, Manx, Gibraltar, Falkland Islands and Saint Helena pounds.

International role

Sterling is the fourth most traded currency in the foreign exchange market (after the US dollar, euro and Japanese yen) and a significant reserve currency. The pound floats freely; notable episodes include the 1967 devaluation, Black Wednesday in 1992 (when the UK left the European Exchange Rate Mechanism), and sharp falls following the 2016 Brexit referendum.
