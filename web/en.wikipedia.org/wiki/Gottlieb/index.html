Gottlieb

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gottlieb (formally D. Gottlieb & Co.) was an American arcade game manufacturer founded by David Gottlieb in 1927 in Chicago, Illinois. The company became one of the most important names in pinball, pioneering many features of the modern game.

History

Gottlieb first made its name with the grip-testing machine and the countertop pin game Baffle Ball (1931), a huge seller in the early Depression years. Its 1947 machine Humpty Dumpty introduced the flipper — six of them — transforming pinball from a game of chance into a game of skill. Through the electromechanical era Gottlieb's "wedgehead" single-player machines were industry benchmarks, and titles such as Kings & Queens (1965) and El Dorado (1975) became classics.

In the solid-state era Gottlieb produced licensed successes such as The Amazing Spider-Man (1980) and later well-known tables including Haunted House (1982, the first with three playfield levels) and Stargate (1995). The company also made arcade video games, most famously Q*bert (1982), under the Mylstar name. After a series of ownership changes beginning with Columbia Pictures in 1976, the pinball operation closed in 1996; the Gottlieb name and rights passed to Premier Technology and later holding companies.
