Karatsuba algorithm

The Karatsuba algorithm is a fast multiplication algorithm discovered by Anatoly Karatsuba in 1960 and published in 1962. It was the first multiplication algorithm asymptotically faster than the grade-school algorithm, reducing the multiplication of two n-digit numbers to at most nlog23 ≈ n1.58 single-digit multiplications, instead of the n2 required by the naive method.

Idea

Writing two numbers as x = x1Bm + x0 and y = y1Bm + y0, the product xy needs the three quantities z2 = x1y1, z0 = x0y0, and the cross term. Karatsuba observed that the cross term can be recovered from a single additional multiplication, z1 = (x1 + x0)(y1 + y0) − z2 − z0, so three multiplications suffice rather than four. Applying the trick recursively yields the faster running time.

History and impact

Karatsuba's result refuted Andrey Kolmogorov's conjecture that multiplication required Ω(n2) operations. It opened the way to later algorithms such as Toom–Cook and the Schönhage–Strassen algorithm. Karatsuba multiplication is still used in practice for numbers of moderate size, where its low overhead beats more asymptotically advanced methods.
