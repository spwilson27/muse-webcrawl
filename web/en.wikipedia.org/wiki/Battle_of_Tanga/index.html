The Battle of Tanga, sometimes called the "Battle of the Bees", was fought from 3 to 5 November 1914 at the port of Tanga in German East Africa during the First World War. An amphibious landing by the British Indian Expeditionary Force B was repulsed by the much smaller German colonial Schutztruppe under Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck, in one of Britain's most embarrassing defeats of the war.

Background

Britain planned to invade German East Africa with two forces: one advancing from British East Africa toward Longido, and Indian Expeditionary Force B making a seaborne descent on Tanga, the colony's main port and railhead. A British cruiser's unauthorized demand for surrender gave the Germans warning and time to reinforce the town by rail.

The battle

Indian troops landed on 3 November south of the harbour and advanced on 4 November against well-positioned German askari. The attack stalled in plantations and bush; attacks by disturbed bees — whose swarms stung both sides but were blamed in British reports — added to the confusion, and fresh German counter-attacks drove the invaders back to their boats. The British re-embarked on 5 November, abandoning rifles, machine guns, telephones and large quantities of supplies and ammunition.

Aftermath

The captured materiel equipped several German companies and cemented Lettow-Vorbeck's strategy of guerrilla resistance, which tied down large Allied forces in East Africa for the rest of the war.
