Overview

Henry Gwyn Jeffreys Moseley (1887–1915) was an English physicist who, in 1913–14, measured the X-ray spectra of the elements and discovered Moseley's law: the frequency of characteristic X-rays varies regularly with a whole number — the atomic number Z — proving that an element's identity is fixed by the charge on its nucleus, not its atomic weight.

Impact and death

Moseley's work put the periodic table on a physical foundation, resolved anomalies (cobalt/nickel, tellurium/iodine ordering), showed exactly which elements remained undiscovered (including Z = 61, 72, 75), and helped Rutherford's nuclear model gain acceptance. Volunteering in 1914, he was shot by a sniper at Gallipoli on 10 August 1915, aged 27 — a loss widely mourned in science (Isaac Asimov called it perhaps the costliest single death of the war for humanity), and one reason Britain afterward kept scientists out of combat roles.
