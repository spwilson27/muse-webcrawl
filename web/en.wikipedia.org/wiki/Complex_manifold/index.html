Complex manifold

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Riemann sphere, the simplest compact complex manifold of dimension one

In differential geometry and complex geometry, a complex manifold is a manifold with an atlas of charts to the open unit disk (or polydisk) in complex space ℂn, such that the transition maps are holomorphic. The term is often used to contrast with real manifolds, whose charts map to open subsets of real space ℝn. A complex manifold of complex dimension n has real dimension 2n.

Definition

Formally, a complex manifold of complex dimension n is a topological space equipped with charts φi : Ui → Vi ⊂ ℂn covering the space, such that whenever two charts overlap, the transition map φj ∘ φi−1 is a holomorphic function between open subsets of ℂn. Equivalently, a complex manifold can be described as a real smooth manifold of dimension 2n with an integrable almost complex structure — an endomorphism J of the tangent bundle with J² = −1 whose Nijenhuis tensor vanishes (the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem).

Examples

  • Complex dimension one — complex manifolds of dimension one are Riemann surfaces; examples include the Riemann sphere ℂP1, complex tori, and the upper half-plane.
  • Complex projective space ℂPn, the space of complex lines through the origin in ℂn+1.
  • Complex tori ℂn/Λ, quotients by a lattice; in dimension ≥ 2 these need not be algebraic.
  • K3 surfaces and Calabi–Yau manifolds, important in algebraic geometry and string theory.
  • Hopf manifolds, which are compact complex manifolds that are not Kähler.

Structure

Because holomorphic functions are far more rigid than smooth functions, complex manifolds have much stronger structure than real manifolds. Important classes include Kähler manifolds, which carry a compatible Kähler metric and include all smooth complex projective varieties, and Stein manifolds, which behave like affine varieties in the analytic category. Sheaf theory and Dolbeault cohomology are principal tools for studying them. The Kodaira embedding theorem characterizes which compact complex manifolds can be embedded into projective space.
