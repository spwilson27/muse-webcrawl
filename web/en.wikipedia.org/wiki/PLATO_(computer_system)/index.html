PLATO (computer system)

PLATO (Programmed Logic for Automatic Teaching Operations) was the first generalised computer-assisted instruction system, developed at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign beginning in 1960.

Development

Conceived by Donald Bitzer, PLATO grew from a single terminal in 1960 to the large-scale PLATO IV system of the 1970s, which supported about a thousand simultaneous users on distinctive orange plasma display terminals with touchscreens. Lessons were written in the TUTOR authoring language.

Innovations

Although built for education, PLATO's users created many features now associated with online life: message boards (PLATO Notes, 1973), email, chat rooms (Talkomatic), discussion forums, instant messaging, remote screen sharing, and early multiplayer online games such as Empire and the first graphical MUDs. Lotus Notes was directly inspired by PLATO Notes.

Commercial history

Control Data Corporation commercialised PLATO from the late 1970s, and versions of the system continued in use for decades for training and education before the last systems were retired in the 2000s.
