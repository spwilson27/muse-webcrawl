Finnish People's Delegation

Finnish People's Delegation Finnish: Suomen kansanvaltuuskunta
Formation: 28 January 1918
Dissolved: 25 April 1918
Type: Revolutionary government
Chairman: Kullervo Manner
Capital: Helsinki (Viipuri in exile)
Context: Finnish Civil War

The Finnish People's Delegation (Finnish: Suomen kansanvaltuuskunta) was the government formed by the Social Democratic Party of Finland and the Red Guards at the start of the Finnish Civil War in January 1918. It governed the areas of southern Finland held by the Reds and proclaimed Finland a socialist republic, while the Senate of Finland, relocated to Vaasa, governed the White-held areas.

Formation and programme

The Delegation was established on 28 January 1918, the day after the Red Guards seized Helsinki. Led by Kullervo Manner, it included figures such as Yrjö Sirola, Oskari Tokoi, and Edvard Valpas-Hänninen. It issued a revolutionary programme promising democratic reforms, workers' rights, and land reform for tenant farmers, and negotiated a treaty of friendship with Soviet Russia, which recognised Finnish independence.

Downfall

After White forces under General Mannerheim, aided by German troops, won decisive victories in the spring of 1918, the Delegation fled Helsinki to Viipuri (Vyborg) and then into exile in Soviet Russia, where it was dissolved. Its surviving members later helped found the Communist Party of Finland in Moscow in August 1918.
