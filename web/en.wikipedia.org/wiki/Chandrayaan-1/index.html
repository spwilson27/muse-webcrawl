Chandrayaan-1

Chandrayaan-1 (from Sanskrit Chandra, "Moon" and yāna, "craft") was the first Indian lunar probe under the Chandrayaan programme. Launched by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) in October 2008, the spacecraft operated until August 2009 and made India the fourth country to place a spacecraft in lunar orbit and to impact the Moon's surface with a probe.

Chandrayaan-1 orbited the Moon at an altitude of about 100 km.

Mission

The orbiter carried eleven scientific instruments built by India, NASA, ESA and the Bulgarian Academy of Sciences, designed to map the Moon's chemical, mineralogical and geological features in three dimensions. A major goal was to search for water ice and hydroxyl on the lunar surface.

Moon Impact Probe

On 14 November 2008 the Moon Impact Probe separated from the orbiter and struck the lunar surface near Shackleton crater, making India the fourth entity to place a flag-bearing object on the Moon. Analysis of its mass spectrometer data provided evidence of water molecules in the lunar soil.

Findings

The Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3), a NASA instrument aboard, confirmed the presence of water molecules in the lunar polar regions — a landmark result announced in September 2009. Chandrayaan-1 also mapped lava tubes and ancient rilles, and detected magnesium-rich spinel deposits. Contact was lost on 29 August 2009, after the spacecraft had completed about 95% of its planned mission objectives.

Legacy

Chandrayaan-1 paved the way for Chandrayaan-2 (2019) and the successful soft landing of Chandrayaan-3 in 2023.
