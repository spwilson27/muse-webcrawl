Hunor Kelemen (born 18 October 1967) is a Romanian politician, writer, and veterinarian of Hungarian ethnicity. He has served as president of the Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (UDMR) since 2011, representing the Hungarian minority in Romanian politics. Kelemen has twice served as Minister of Culture and National Patrimony and was a Deputy Prime Minister of Romania from 2020 to 2023.

Early life and education

Kelemen was born in Cârța, Harghita County, in the Székely Land region of Transylvania. He studied veterinary medicine at the University of Agricultural Sciences and Veterinary Medicine in Cluj-Napoca, graduating in 1993, and later studied philosophy at Babeș-Bolyai University. Before entering national politics he worked as a veterinarian and as a journalist and editor for Hungarian-language publications in Romania. He has published volumes of poetry and prose in Hungarian.

Political career

Kelemen was elected to the Romanian Chamber of Deputies in 1996 and has been re-elected repeatedly. He served as Minister of Culture in the government of Emil Boc (2009–2012) and again briefly in the government of Victor Ponta (2014). The UDMR's withdrawal from the Ponta coalition in late 2014 was precipitated in part by disputes over cultural and minority issues.

In February 2011 Kelemen was elected president of the UDMR, succeeding Béla Markó. Under his leadership the party has generally pursued a strategy of participating in governing coalitions in exchange for advances on Hungarian minority interests, including language rights and regional development in Székely Land. In 2014 Kelemen ran as the UDMR's candidate in the Romanian presidential election, winning around 3.5 percent of the vote.

Following the 2020 parliamentary elections, the UDMR joined a coalition with the National Liberal Party and later the Social Democratic Party, and Kelemen became Deputy Prime Minister, a post he held until the coalition reconfiguration of June 2023.

Views

Kelemen advocates territorial and cultural autonomy for the Székely Hungarian community within Romania, bilingual signage and education, and restitution of properties confiscated under communism. He has been a prominent voice in debates over Romania's administrative regionalization and over the use of Hungarian symbols and language in public institutions.
