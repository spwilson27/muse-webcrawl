Sheep dog trial

A sheep dog trial — in the United States also called a herding trial or dog trial — is a competitive dog sport in which herding dogs, guided by their handlers, move sheep around a field, fences, gates and enclosures in a defined pattern. Time and accuracy score the contest, and the handler controls the dog by voice and whistle commands while remaining at a fixed post for most of the course. The sport is strongly associated with the Border Collie.

A Border Collie driving sheep through a gate during a trial.

History

Sheep dog trials originated in the hill-farming districts of Britain and Ireland in the 19th century. The first recorded trials took place in Bala, Wales, in 1873, where ten dogs competed before a crowd reported at several hundred. The International Sheep Dog Society (ISDS) was founded in 1906, and national and international championships followed. The sport spread to New Zealand, Australia, North America, and South Africa, where the working sheep dog is an essential farm tool.

The course

A standard course tests the elements of a shepherd's daily work:

  • Gather — the outrun, in which the dog runs out in a wide arc behind the sheep; the lift, the first contact that sets the sheep moving calmly; and the fetch, bringing the sheep in a straight line to the handler.
  • Drive — the dog drives the sheep away from the handler through one or two sets of gates.
  • Shed — separating designated sheep from the flock within a marked ring.
  • Pen — guiding the sheep into a small enclosure, the handler holding the gate rope.
  • Single (at championship level) — singling out one marked sheep.

Points are deducted for faults such as crossing the course, wide turns, splitting sheep, biting (gripping), or going off line. Most trials impose a time limit of roughly 10–15 minutes.

Commands

Handlers use short spoken commands — "come-bye" (clockwise around the flock), "away" (counter-clockwise), "lie down," "steady/take time," and "that'll do" (recall) — supplemented by individually learned whistle signals that carry over long distances.

In popular culture

Trials were televised for many years in the BBC series One Man and His Dog (1976–2013), which made the sport familiar to a wide audience in Britain.
