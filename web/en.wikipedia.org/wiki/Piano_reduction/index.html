Piano reduction

A piano reduction is an arrangement of music originally written for a larger ensemble, such as an orchestra or opera company, compressed so that it can be played on a single piano. Piano reductions preserve the essential harmonic, melodic, and rhythmic content of the full score within the capabilities of two hands.

Uses

Reductions are used for rehearsal — a vocal score with piano reduction allows singers and ballet dancers to rehearse without an orchestra — for study by conductors and students, for concerto soloists practising with an accompanist playing the orchestral part, and historically for domestic performance of symphonies and operas before sound recording.

Technique

Making an effective reduction requires selecting which orchestral lines are essential, redistributing others, and adapting figuration to the piano. Famous reductions include Liszt's virtuoso piano transcriptions of the Beethoven symphonies, which approach independent concert works.
