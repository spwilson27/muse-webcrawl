Carry On (film series)

The Carry On films are a British comedy film series of 31 productions released between 1958 and 1978, with a revival film Carry On Columbus in 1992. Produced by Peter Rogers and directed by Gerald Thomas for Rank and Anglo-Amalgamated, they are known for low-budget production, seaside-postcard bawdy humour, double entendres and a regular repertory company.

History

The series began with Carry On Sergeant (1958), originally a straight drama script adapted into comedy. Its success led to Carry On Nurse (1959), one of the most successful British films of its year. Films followed in institutions and professions — hospitals, schools, police, cab firms — and later in historical parodies including Carry On Cleo (1964), Carry On Up the Khyber (1968) and Carry On Henry (1971).

Regular cast

Core performers included Sid James, Kenneth Williams, Charles Hawtrey, Joan Sims, Kenneth Connor, Barbara Windsor, Hattie Jacques, Bernard Bresslaw, Jim Dale and Terry Scott. Their stock personas — lecherous everyman, pompous official, camp fussbudget — recurred across settings.

Legacy

Despite critical disdain, the films were consistently profitable and remain staples of British television. See also British comedy and Pinewood Studios.
