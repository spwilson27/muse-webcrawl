Frederick William, Elector of Brandenburg

BornFebruary 16, 1620, Berlin, Brandenburg
DiedApril 29, 1688 (aged 68), Potsdam, Brandenburg
ReignDecember 1, 1640 – April 29, 1688
HouseHohenzollern

Frederick William (German: Friedrich Wilhelm; 16 February 1620 – 29 April 1688) was Elector of Brandenburg and Duke of Prussia — ruler of Brandenburg-Prussia — from 1640 until his death. Known as the "Great Elector" (der Große Kurfürst), he is remembered for rebuilding his devastated territories after the Thirty Years' War, creating a standing army, and laying the foundations of the Prussian state.

Reign

Frederick William inherited scattered, war-ravaged lands at the end of the Thirty Years' War, whose population Brandenburg had lost grievously. Through the Peace of Westphalia (1648) he gained Eastern Pomerania and several territories, and by the Treaty of Wehlau (1657), confirmed at Oliva (1660), he secured full sovereignty over the Duchy of Prussia, ending Polish suzerainty.

Domestic reforms

The Great Elector curtailed the power of the provincial estates, built a standing army financed by taxes he controlled, and created an efficient bureaucracy. He promoted economic recovery and immigration, most famously through the Edict of Potsdam (1685), which welcomed Huguenots expelled from France; some 20,000 French Protestants settled in Brandenburg, greatly benefiting its economy and culture. He also founded colonies on the Gold Coast of Africa and built a small navy.

Wars

In the Northern Wars he maneuvered between Sweden and Poland to Prussia's advantage. His victory over the Swedes at the Battle of Fehrbellin (1675) — though a modest engagement — won him the title "Great Elector" and great prestige; his armies subsequently overran Swedish Pomerania before diplomacy restored most of it under French pressure.

Legacy

Frederick William transformed a conglomeration of territories into a unified power, and his political testament advised his successors on maintaining the army and alliances. He is regarded as the true founder of Brandenburg-Prussia, the state his great-grandson Frederick the Great would raise to a European great power.
