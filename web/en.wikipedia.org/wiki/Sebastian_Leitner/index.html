Sebastian Leitner (1919 in Salzburg – 1989) was an Austrian journalist and populariser of science, best known as the inventor of the Leitner system, a method of efficient learning using flashcards sorted by spaced repetition.

Life and work

Leitner studied law in Vienna, then turned to journalism after the Second World War, working as a foreign correspondent and later as a science editor and commentator on Austrian radio and television. He wrote widely read books about learning, memory and intelligence, aimed at a general audience, including So lernt man lernen ("How to Learn to Learn", 1972).

The Leitner system

In his 1972 book, Leitner described a card box divided into compartments holding flashcards. All cards begin in the first compartment and are reviewed frequently. A card answered correctly is promoted to the next compartment, which is reviewed less often; a card answered incorrectly returns to the first compartment. In this way the learner concentrates effort on the least-known material, and well-learned material is repeated at expanding intervals. The method is a practical implementation of spaced repetition and underlies many modern flashcard software programs such as Anki and SuperMemo-style scheduling systems.
