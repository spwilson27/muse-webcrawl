Acetazolamide

Trade namesDiamox, others
Drug classCarbonic anhydrase inhibitor
FormulaC4H6N4O3S2
RoutesBy mouth, intravenous

Acetazolamide, sold under the trade name Diamox among others, is a medication used to treat glaucoma, epilepsy, acute mountain sickness, periodic paralysis, idiopathic intracranial hypertension, heart failure and alkalisation of the urine. It is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor and appears on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Acetazolamide blocks the enzyme carbonic anhydrase, reducing the reabsorption of bicarbonate in the proximal tubule of the kidney. This produces a mild diuretic effect, lowers intraocular pressure by decreasing aqueous humour production, and induces a metabolic acidosis that stimulates ventilation — the basis of its use in altitude sickness prophylaxis.

Side effects

Common side effects include paraesthesia (tingling) of the extremities, fatigue, loss of appetite, altered taste, and increased urination. It is a sulfonamide derivative and is generally avoided in patients with significant sulfa allergy.

History

The drug was synthesised in the early 1950s and introduced into medicine in 1952, becoming the prototype of the carbonic anhydrase inhibitor class.
