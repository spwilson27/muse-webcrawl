Civil Code of Ethiopia

The Civil Code of Ethiopia is a comprehensive codification of private law enacted in 1960 during the reign of Emperor Haile Selassie. It was drafted principally by the French comparative-law scholar René David, commissioned by the imperial government as part of a programme to modernise Ethiopia's legal system with a set of codes covering civil, commercial, criminal, and procedural law.

Contents

The code contains over 3,300 articles arranged in books dealing with persons, family and succession, property and obligations (contracts and extra-contractual liability). It drew on a wide range of comparative sources, including French, Swiss, and other civil-law systems, and was intended to displace the customary laws applied by local courts, though in practice customary law continued to govern much of daily life, particularly in family and land matters.

Legacy

The Civil Code remains a foundational text of Ethiopian private law, though parts — notably family law and land law — have been superseded by later legislation such as the Revised Family Code of 2000 and the federal constitution of 1995. It is regarded as one of the most ambitious products of 20th-century comparative law drafting.
