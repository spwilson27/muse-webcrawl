The Quebec sovereignty movement seeks the independence of the province of Quebec from Canada, so that Quebec becomes a sovereign state. Rooted in French-Canadian nationalism and energised by the Quiet Revolution of the 1960s, the movement has twice put secession to a referendum and remains a central force in Quebec politics.

History

René Lévesque founded the Parti Québécois in 1968, advocating "sovereignty-association" — political independence combined with an economic partnership with Canada. Elected in 1976, the PQ held the 1980 referendum, which was defeated 59.6% to 40.4%. After the failure of the Meech Lake and Charlottetown constitutional accords, a second referendum in 1995 came within a whisker of success: the No side won by 50.58% to 49.42%, on a turnout of over 93%.

Aftermath

The federal government responded with the Supreme Court's 1998 Secession Reference and the Clarity Act (2000), requiring a clear question and a clear majority for negotiations. Support for sovereignty has since fluctuated, generally below the levels of the 1990s, while sovereigntist parties continue to compete at provincial and federal level.
