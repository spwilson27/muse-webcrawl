Royalty (payment)

A royalty payment is a payment made by one party to another that owns a particular asset, for the right to ongoing use of that asset. Royalties are usually agreed upon as a percentage of gross or net revenues derived from the use of an asset or a fixed price per unit sold of an item of such, but there are also other modes and metrics of compensation. A royalty interest is the right to collect a stream of future royalty payments.

A license agreement defines the terms under which a resource or property are licensed by one party to another, either without restriction or subject to a restriction on term, business or geographic territory, type of product, etc. License agreements can be regulated, particularly where a government is the resource owner, or they can be private contracts that follow a general structure. However, certain types of franchise agreements have comparable provisions.

Non-renewable resources

In the United States, mineral rights can be sold or conveyed separately from property rights, such as by a "surface owner" or "surface tenant" owning the land surface and a "mineral owner" who owns rights to the sub-surface minerals. A landholder with mineral rights may choose to sever the mineral rights from the surface rights, a process sometimes called "severance". A severed mineral right can be leased to a company, which in return pays the mineral owner a royalty on any oil, gas or other minerals produced.

Trademark

A trademark is a form of intellectual property, and is normally licensed rather than transferred. The licensor grants the licensee the right to use the mark in commerce, often in exchange for a royalty calculated as a percentage of sales or a fixed fee.

Franchise

Franchise royalties are paid by a franchisee to a franchisor on an ongoing basis, typically a percentage of gross sales, for the right to operate under the franchisor's brand and business system.

Copyright

Copyright royalties are paid to composers, publishers and other copyright owners for the use of their works. In music, performing rights royalties are collected by performing rights organisations such as ASCAP and BMI. Mechanical royalties are paid for the reproduction of musical compositions on recordings.

Book publishing

Book royalties are paid by publishers to authors, typically as a percentage of the list price or of net receipts, and often increase with sales volume (escalating royalties). Advances against royalties are commonly paid before publication.

Patent royalty

Patent holders may license their patents in exchange for royalties, commonly expressed as a percentage of the sales price of products practising the patent, or a per-unit fee.
