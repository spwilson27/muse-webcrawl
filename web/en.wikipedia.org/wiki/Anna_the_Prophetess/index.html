Anna the Prophetess

SourceGospel of Luke 2:36–38
ParentPhanuel, of the tribe of Asher
Feast day3 February (Catholic); various dates in Orthodox tradition

Anna the Prophetess is a figure mentioned in the Gospel of Luke in the New Testament. She appears during the Presentation of Jesus at the Temple in Jerusalem.

Biblical account

Luke describes Anna as a prophetess, the daughter of Phanuel of the tribe of Asher. She was of great age — according to the text, eighty-four years old — and had lived as a widow since shortly after her marriage. She never left the Temple, worshipping night and day with fasting and prayer. Coming up at the moment Simeon blessed the infant Jesus, she gave thanks to God and spoke about the child "to all who were looking for the redemption of Jerusalem".

Veneration

Anna is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches, and she is a frequent subject of art depicting the Presentation, including paintings by Rembrandt.
