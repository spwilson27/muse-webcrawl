Copper is a chemical element (symbol Cu, atomic number 29), a soft, reddish metal with very high thermal and electrical conductivity. Among the first metals used by humans — worked for over 10,000 years — it named an age (in the sequence Stone–Copper–Bronze) and alloys with tin form bronze.

Modern role

Copper wires the world: motors, grids, electronics and renewable-energy systems devour it, so the energy transition intensifies mining in Chile, Peru and Congo. It is antimicrobial, corrosion-resistant (green patina — the Statue of Liberty) and infinitely recyclable.
