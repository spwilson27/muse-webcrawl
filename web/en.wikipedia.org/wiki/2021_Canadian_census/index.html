The 2021 Canadian census, formally the 2021 Census of Population, was a nationwide enumeration conducted by Statistics Canada with census day on 11 May 2021. It counted 36,991,981 people, an increase of 5.2 per cent over the 2016 census — the fastest population growth rate among G7 countries, driven overwhelmingly by immigration. The census was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, which pushed most collection online and into telephone and paper channels, and its results underpin the redistribution of seats in the House of Commons and numerous federal transfer programmes.

Conduct

Households received invitation letters with secure access codes beginning in early May 2021; about 85 per cent completed the questionnaire online. A short-form questionnaire went to all households, while a long-form sample survey — the successor to the National Household Survey — went to one in four. Because of the pandemic, in-person follow-up was minimised and Statistics Canada conducted a special enumeration of collective dwellings and shelters. New content included questions on commuting under pandemic conditions, Canadian Armed Forces veteran status, and expanded gender questions: the 2021 census was the first to distinguish sex at birth from gender and to publish counts of transgender and non-binary people.

Key findings

Ontario remained the most populous province (14.2 million), while Alberta, British Columbia and Prince Edward Island grew fastest. Almost one in four Canadians (23 per cent) was an immigrant or the child of immigrants' record share, and more than 1.3 million people reported Indigenous identity. Population growth was heavily urban: the largest metropolitan areas — Toronto (6.2 million), Montreal and Vancouver — accounted for a growing share, while downtown cores grew faster than suburbs for the first time in decades, a pattern partially reversed as remote work spread. Canada's population was confirmed to be ageing, with the number of centenarians rising sharply, though immigration kept the working-age share comparatively high.

Use of results

Census counts determine each province's entitlement to federal seats under the representation formula; the subsequent redistribution added seats for Alberta, British Columbia and Ontario ahead of the next general election. The data also drive equalisation payments, electoral boundary commissions and municipal planning.
