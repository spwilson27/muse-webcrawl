Homological mirror symmetry

Homological mirror symmetry is a conjecture by Maxim Kontsevich (1994) proposing an equivalence between the derived category of coherent sheaves on a Calabi–Yau manifold and the Fukaya category of its mirror dual, a symplectic manifold whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds.

Background

The conjecture categorifies physical mirror symmetry in string theory, which exchanges complex and symplectic geometry between mirror pairs. The Strominger–Yau–Zaslow picture relates the two sides through special Lagrangian torus fibrations.

Results

The conjecture has been proved in cases including elliptic curves, abelian varieties, and certain quartic and toric examples, notably through work of Polishchuk–Zaslow and Seidel, and it has driven major developments in symplectic geometry and category theory.

References: Kontsevich (1994); Auroux, survey articles on mirror symmetry.
