Arba'in pilgrimage

The Arba'in pilgrimage is one of the largest annual religious gatherings in the world, in which millions of Shia Muslims travel, mostly on foot, to the city of Karbala in Iraq. It takes place on Arba'in, the fortieth day after Ashura, commemorating the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali at the Battle of Karbala in 680 CE.

Location: Karbala, Iraq
Occurs: 20 Safar (Islamic calendar)
Commemorates: Husayn ibn Ali
Attendance: Millions of pilgrims

The walk

Many pilgrims walk the roughly 80 kilometres from Najaf to Karbala over several days. Along the route, volunteers staff mawakib (service stations) providing free food, water, rest, and medical care to the pilgrims.

Significance

Under Saddam Hussein's regime the public observance was restricted; since 2003 the pilgrimage has grown enormously, drawing participants from Iraq, Iran, and many other countries, and has become a major expression of Shia religious identity.
