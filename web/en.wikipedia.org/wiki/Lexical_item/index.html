Lexical item

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A lexical item (or lexical unit, lexical entry) is a single unit of the lexicon of a language — roughly, a unit of meaning that is stored and treated as a whole. Lexical items are commonly equated with words, but the category is broader: it also covers multiword units such as idioms, phrasal verbs, collocations and fixed expressions whose meaning cannot be predicted from their parts, for example kick the bucket or by the way.

Characteristics

A lexical item typically has at least one sense and a corresponding form, and belongs to a syntactic category such as noun, verb or adjective. It may consist of one or more word forms: the item represented by the lemma go includes the forms go, goes, went and gone. Lexical items are distinguished from purely grammatical items such as inflectional affixes, although function words (prepositions, conjunctions, auxiliaries) are often counted as lexical items as well.

In lexicography and theory

In lexicography, lexical items correspond roughly to dictionary headwords. In lexical semantics and theories of lexicalisation, the notion helps analyse how complex meanings become packaged as single stored units, including compounds, derived words and conventionalised phrases. The total stock of lexical items in a language constitutes its lexicon.
