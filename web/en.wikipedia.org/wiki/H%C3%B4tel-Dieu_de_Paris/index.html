Hôtel-Dieu de Paris

The Hôtel-Dieu de Paris is the oldest hospital in Paris, France, traditionally said to have been founded in 651 AD by Saint Landry, bishop of Paris. It stands on the Île de la Cité, on the parvis of Notre-Dame Cathedral, and is administered by the Assistance Publique – Hôpitaux de Paris (AP-HP). Despite repeated fires and rebuildings over the centuries, it has operated continuously as a hospital and remains an important centre for emergency medicine, ophthalmology and the treatment of diabetes.

History

The hospital's foundation is attributed to Saint Landry, who around 651 established a house of shelter for the poor and sick near the cathedral. For centuries it was run by religious orders, notably the Augustinian canonesses, and was notorious for overcrowding: in the Middle Ages and early modern period, several patients often shared a single bed, and mortality rates were extremely high.

The building burned down several times, most destructively in 1772, prompting major reforms in hospital design. The present neo-classical building, constructed between 1865 and 1878 under Baron Haussmann's renovation of Paris to designs by Arthur-Stanislas Diet, occupies the southern side of the parvis of Notre-Dame.

Services

Today the Hôtel-Dieu provides emergency care, a major ophthalmology department, diabetology services and medical imaging. It also houses a centre for the forensic examination of victims of violence and serves as a teaching hospital affiliated with the University of Paris.
