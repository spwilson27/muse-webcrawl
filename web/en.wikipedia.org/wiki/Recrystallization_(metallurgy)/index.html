Recrystallization (metallurgy)

In metallurgy, recrystallization is a process by which deformed grains are replaced by a new set of strain-free grains that nucleate and grow until the original grains have been entirely consumed. It usually accompanies annealing of cold-worked metal and reduces strength and hardness while increasing ductility.

Mechanism

Driving force for recrystallization is the stored energy of dislocations introduced by plastic deformation. New grains nucleate at high-energy sites such as grain boundaries and deformation bands, then grow by boundary migration. The recrystallization temperature depends on purity, prior strain, and time; it is often around one-third to one-half of the absolute melting temperature.

Dynamic recrystallization

When recrystallization occurs during deformation itself — for example during hot rolling — it is called dynamic recrystallization and continuously refines the grain structure.
