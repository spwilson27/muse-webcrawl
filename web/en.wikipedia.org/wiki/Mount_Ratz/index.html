Mount Ratz is a mountain located in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, in the Boundary Ranges of the Coast Mountains, near the headwaters of the Stikine River. At 3,090 metres (10,138 ft) it is the highest peak in the Boundary Ranges and notable for its high topographic prominence and remote, ice-clad character.

Geography and climbing

The mountain rises above the Stikine Icecap region, an area of heavy snowfall and extensive glaciation, and lies within the traditional territory associated with the Tahltan people. Because of its remoteness and severe weather, Mount Ratz sees few ascents; the first recorded ascent was in 1970 by a party approaching overland from the Stikine basin. It is named in honour of William F. Ratz, a surveyor with the Canadian Geological Survey who worked in the region in the early 20th century.
