Roadie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Roadies setting up a stage before a concert

A roadie is a technician who travels with a band or artist on tour and handles some or all of the production and technical work for live performances. Roadies are also known collectively as the road crew. Their duties can include setting up and maintaining instruments and amplifiers, sound and lighting equipment, staging, pyrotechnics and backline gear, as well as driving, security and general logistics.

Roles

On large tours the road crew is organised hierarchically under a production manager and tour manager, with specialised roles such as:

  • Guitar and drum technicians — maintain, tune and set up instruments, and hand them to performers during shows.
  • Front of house and monitor engineers — operate the mixing consoles for the audience and on-stage sound.
  • Lighting technicians and designers — rig and operate lighting and video systems.
  • Riggers and carpenters — build stages, trusses and sets.
  • Backline technicians — handle amplifiers, keyboards and other shared equipment.

On small tours a single roadie may perform most of these tasks and also drive the tour van and sell merchandise.

History and culture

The roadie emerged as a distinct occupation with the growth of rock concert touring in the 1960s and 1970s, when increasingly elaborate stage productions required dedicated technical staff. The lifestyle — long hours, constant travel and close camaraderie with musicians — became a staple of rock culture, depicted in films such as Almost Famous and honoured in songs like Jackson Browne's "The Load-Out" and Motörhead's "(We Are) The Road Crew". Many roadies later became musicians, producers or industry executives; Lemmy Kilmister of Motörhead famously worked as a roadie for Jimi Hendrix.
