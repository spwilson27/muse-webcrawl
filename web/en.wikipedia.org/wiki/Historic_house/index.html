Historic house
Definition: A house considered of special architectural or historic interest

A historic house is a dwelling considered to be of special architectural, cultural or historical significance — for example because of its age, design, association with notable people or events, or rarity. Many historic houses are legally protected through listing or heritage registers, and many are open to the public as historic house museums.

Preservation

Protection regimes include listed-building status in the United Kingdom, the National Register of Historic Places in the United States, and national heritage lists elsewhere. Preservation typically controls alteration of significant fabric while allowing adaptive reuse. Organisations such as the National Trust and Historic England care for large collections of historic houses.

Challenges

Owners face high maintenance costs, conservation constraints and the economics of running large houses, issues that famously forced the sale or demolition of many British country houses in the 20th century.
