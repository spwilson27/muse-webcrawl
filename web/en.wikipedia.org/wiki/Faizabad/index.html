Faizabad

Faizabad, capital of Badakhshan Province, Afghanistan

Faizabad (also spelled Fayzabad) is the capital and largest city of Badakhshan Province in north-eastern Afghanistan. Situated on the Kokcha River at an elevation of about 1,200 metres, in a valley between mountains of the Hindu Kush, it is the commercial and administrative centre of the province. Its population has been estimated at around 120,000–150,000, predominantly Tajik with Uzbek, Hazara and Pashtun minorities.

History

The city grew around a fort and market town on routes linking Central Asia with the Wakhan Corridor and China; the region was known in earlier centuries for lapis lazuli from the nearby mines of Sar-e Sang, worked since antiquity. Faizabad served as the seat of local mirs of Badakhshan and was absorbed into the Afghan state in the 19th century. During the Soviet–Afghan War and the civil wars of the 1990s the city changed hands and hosted aid agencies; it remained largely outside Taliban control before 2001 as part of the Northern Alliance's territory. In August 2021 the Taliban captured the city during their nationwide offensive.

Geography and economy

Faizabad has a continental climate with hot summers and cold winters. The local economy rests on agriculture (wheat, rice, fruit), livestock, trade and government and NGO employment. Faizabad Airport connects the city with Kabul, though road access through the Salang route and the mountains is long and weather-dependent. The city is a staging point for travel to the Wakhan Corridor and the Afghan Pamir.
