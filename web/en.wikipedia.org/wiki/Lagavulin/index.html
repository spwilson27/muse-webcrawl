Lagavulin distillery is a Scotch whisky distillery at the village of Lagavulin on the south coast of the island of Islay, Scotland, between the neighbouring distilleries of Laphroaig and Ardbeg. Its name derives from the Gaelic Lag a' Mhuilinn, "the hollow of the mill". Distilling on the site is recorded from 1742, when illicit stills operated there; the legal distillery was founded by John Johnston in 1816.

Character

Lagavulin is renowned for an intensely peaty, smoky and maritime style, produced from heavily peated malt, a long fermentation and slow distillation in pear-shaped stills, then matured in oak casks. The core expression, the 16 Year Old, was included in Diageo's original "Classic Malts of Scotland" selection in 1988 and became one of the world's best-known single malts, popularised beyond whisky circles in part by its prominence in popular culture. Other bottlings include an 8 Year Old (released for the distillery's 200th anniversary in 2016), a Distillers Edition finished in Pedro Ximénez sherry casks, and limited cask-strength editions.

History

The distillery passed through the Mackie family firm (which also owned Laphroaig) and later the Distillers Company; it survived periods of closure during the world wars and the 1980s whisky downturn. In 1989 it was managed alongside the revived Port Ellen maltings. The visitor centre at Dunyvaig Castle's bay welcomes large numbers of Islay tourists each year.
