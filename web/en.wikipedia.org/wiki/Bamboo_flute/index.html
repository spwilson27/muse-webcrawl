Bamboo flute

Bamboo flutes are a family of wind instruments made from the hollow stems of bamboo, found in musical traditions across Asia, Africa, South America and Oceania. Bamboo's natural tube — light, strong and segmented by nodes — made it one of humanity's earliest flute materials; transverse, end-blown, duct and nose flutes have all been fashioned from it.

Bamboo flutes from several traditions

Notable types

  • Bansuri — the Indian transverse flute associated with the god Krishna, central to Hindustani classical music;
  • Shakuhachi — the Japanese end-blown flute of Zen Buddhist monks, made from the root end of madake bamboo;
  • Dizi and xiao — Chinese transverse and end-blown flutes, the dizi distinguished by its buzzing membrane hole (mokong);
  • Shinobue — a Japanese festival flute;
  • Quena and quenacho — notched flutes of the Andes, traditionally of bamboo or wood;
  • Tabor pipe, suling (Indonesia) and various African and Pacific flutes.

Construction

A bamboo flute is made by selecting a culm of suitable diameter and wall thickness, cutting between or through nodes, and boring or burning finger holes. The embouchure hole or notch is cut near the closed node, which acts as a natural stopper. Tuning is adjusted by hole placement and size; many traditions season and lacquer the bamboo to prevent cracking.
