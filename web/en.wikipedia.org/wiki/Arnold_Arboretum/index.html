Arnold Arboretum

LocationJamaica Plain, Boston, Massachusetts
Area281 acres (114 ha)
Established1872
Operated byHarvard University

The Arnold Arboretum of Harvard University is a botanical research institution and public park in the Jamaica Plain and Roslindale neighbourhoods of Boston, Massachusetts. Established in 1872, it is the oldest public arboretum in North America.

History

The arboretum was founded with funds from the estate of New Bedford whaling merchant James Arnold. Its landscape was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted in collaboration with the first director, Charles Sprague Sargent, and it forms part of Boston's Emerald Necklace park system. The land is owned by the City of Boston and leased to Harvard for a nominal sum under a 1,000-year lease.

Collections

The living collections include roughly 15,000 woody plants representing several thousand taxa, with notable collections of lilacs, maples, conifers and plants collected in East Asia by explorers such as Ernest Henry Wilson. The arboretum's herbarium and library support research in plant systematics and ecology, and its Lilac Sunday festival each May draws large crowds.
