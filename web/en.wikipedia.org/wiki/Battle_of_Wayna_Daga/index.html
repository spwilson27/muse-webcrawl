Battle of Wayna Daga

The Battle of Wayna Daga was fought on 21 February 1543 near Lake Tana in present-day Ethiopia, between the Christian Ethiopian Empire under Emperor Gelawdewos, assisted by a small Portuguese expeditionary force, and the forces of the Adal Sultanate under Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi. Imam Ahmad was killed in the battle, and his army collapsed, ending Adal's fourteen-year conquest of Ethiopia.

Background

Since 1529 Imam Ahmad's armies, equipped in part with Ottoman firearms, had overrun most of the Ethiopian highlands. Emperor Dawit II died in flight, and his successor Gelawdewos continued resistance. A Portuguese expedition of 400 musketeers under Cristóvão da Gama landed in 1541; Gama was captured and executed in 1542 after the Battle of Wofla, but about half his men survived to join Gelawdewos.

The battle

The two armies met at Wayna Daga, south of Lake Tana. In the fighting, Imam Ahmad was shot — Ethiopian tradition credits a Portuguese musketeer — and killed. Leaderless, the Adal army broke and was pursued and destroyed; the Imam's widow, Bati del Wambara, escaped with the remnants to Harar.

Aftermath

Wayna Daga ended the Ethiopian–Adal War. Adal power collapsed, though Harar and the eastern lowlands remained Muslim; both states were so weakened that the following decades saw the great Oromo migrations reshape the region. The Portuguese survivors settled in Ethiopia, and their presence opened a century of contact — and eventually conflict — over Jesuit missions at the Ethiopian court.
