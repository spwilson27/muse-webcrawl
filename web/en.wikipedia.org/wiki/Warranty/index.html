Warranty

A warranty is a promise or assurance, typically given in writing by a manufacturer or seller, that a product or service will meet certain standards of quality and performance, and that defects arising within a specified period will be repaired, replaced, or refunded without charge. Warranties are a standard feature of consumer transactions involving vehicles, appliances, electronics, and buildings.

Types

An express warranty is an explicit statement of fact or promise about the goods, made by description, sample, or affirmation. Implied warranties arise by operation of law: in the United States, the implied warranty of merchantability guarantees that goods are fit for their ordinary purposes, and the implied warranty of fitness applies where the buyer relies on the seller's judgment for a particular purpose. In the United States, the Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act (1975) regulates written consumer warranties and distinguishes "full" from "limited" warranties. An extended warranty or service contract is an optional, separately purchased extension of coverage beyond the standard term.

Breach and remedies

If a product fails to conform to its warranty, the buyer may be entitled to repair, replacement, or damages, depending on the terms and applicable consumer-protection law. Sellers may disclaim implied warranties only within limits set by statute, and many jurisdictions prohibit disclaimer where a written warranty is given.
