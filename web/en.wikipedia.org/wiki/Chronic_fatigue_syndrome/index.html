Chronic fatigue syndrome

Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS), also called myalgic encephalomyelitis (ME) or ME/CFS, is a debilitating chronic illness characterized by profound fatigue that does not improve with rest, post-exertional malaise (a worsening of symptoms after physical or mental effort), unrefreshing sleep, and cognitive difficulties often described as "brain fog." Orthostatic intolerance is also common.

Diagnosis

There is no diagnostic laboratory test for ME/CFS; diagnosis is clinical, based on case definitions such as the 2015 Institute of Medicine (National Academy of Medicine) criteria, which require substantial impairment lasting more than six months with post-exertional malaise and unrefreshing sleep, plus cognitive impairment or orthostatic intolerance. The cause is unknown; onset often follows an infection, and research implicates immune, metabolic, and autonomic dysfunction.

Management

No cure or approved drug treatment exists. Management focuses on symptom relief and energy management ("pacing") to avoid post-exertional crashes. The illness affects an estimated 0.2–0.4% of the population, is more common in women, and can leave a substantial minority of patients housebound or bedbound. Long COVID has drawn renewed research attention to infection-associated chronic illnesses including ME/CFS.
