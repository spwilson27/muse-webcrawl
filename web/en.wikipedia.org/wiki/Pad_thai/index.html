Pad thai

TypeRice noodle dish
CourseMain dish

Pad thai, or phad thai (Thai: ผัดไทย, RTGS: phat thai), is a stir-fried rice noodle dish commonly served as a street food in Thailand as part of the country's cuisine. It is typically made with rice noodles, shrimp, peanuts, scrambled egg and bean sprouts. The ingredients are fried in a wok with a sauce made from tamarind pulp, fish sauce, dried shrimp, garlic or shallots, red chili pepper and palm sugar.

History

Stir-fried noodles (the general category) were likely introduced to Thailand by Chinese settlers, and rice noodles are thought to have been brought by Chinese traders. The modern dish, however, was popularised — and arguably invented — during the period of Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram (Phibun) in the late 1930s and 1940s. As part of his campaign to modernise Thailand and build national identity, Phibun promoted the dish as a national food, encouraged noodle consumption to reduce rice exports' pressure and lower rice prices, and distributed recipes to street vendors. The name "pad thai" ("Thai-style stir fry") dates from this era.

Ingredients and preparation

Rice noodles are soaked until pliable, then stir-fried in a hot wok with egg, firm tofu, and a seasoned sauce. Protein may include shrimp, chicken or pork. The dish is finished with crushed roasted peanuts, bean sprouts, garlic chives and a wedge of lime, and is often accompanied by banana leaf and fresh vegetables. The flavour balance — sour from tamarind, salty from fish sauce, sweet from palm sugar, spicy from chili — is considered characteristic of Thai cooking.

Variations

Variants include pad thai wrapped in a thin omelette (pad thai hor khai), versions with pork or crab, and vegetarian versions using soy sauce in place of fish sauce. Outside Thailand, simplified versions often substitute ketchup for tamarind, giving a redder, sweeter sauce that is not traditional.

Cultural significance

Pad thai is listed among the most popular Thai dishes worldwide and is frequently cited in surveys of favourite foods. In Thailand it remains an inexpensive street food, while abroad it is one of the most recognisable dishes of Thai restaurants.
