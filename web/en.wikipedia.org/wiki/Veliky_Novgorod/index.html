Veliky Novgorod

Veliky Novgorod (Russian: Великий Новгород, "Great New Town"), known simply as Novgorod until 1999, is the administrative centre of Novgorod Oblast in northwestern Russia, situated on the Volkhov River just below its outflow from Lake Ilmen. One of the oldest and most important historic cities in Russia, it was the capital of the Novgorod Republic, a powerful medieval merchant state, and is famed for its kremlin and the Cathedral of St Sophia.

History

Novgorod is traditionally linked with the invitation of the Varangian prince Rurik in 862, making it one of the cradles of Rus' statehood. Under Yaroslav the Wise it flourished as a trading city connected to the Hanseatic League, exporting furs, wax and honey across northern Europe. From the 12th century the city governed itself through the veche assembly, inviting and expelling princes — among them Alexander Nevsky, who defended it against Swedish and German crusaders. Novgorod escaped destruction during the Mongol invasion and became a haven of Rus' culture, producing birch-bark letters that document everyday medieval literacy.

In 1478 Ivan III of Moscow annexed Novgorod, ending the republic; Ivan IV's oprichnina sacked the city in 1570. The city declined relative to St Petersburg but remained a provincial centre. It suffered heavy damage in the Second World War, was restored, and in 1992 its historic monuments were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In 1999 the city formally regained the name Veliky Novgorod.

Sights

The Novgorod Kremlin (Detinets) contains St Sophia Cathedral (1045–1050), among Russia's oldest churches, and the Millennium of Russia monument (1862). Across the river, Yaroslav's Court preserves a cluster of medieval churches built by merchant guilds.
