Non-well-founded set theory

Non-well-founded set theories are variants of axiomatic set theory that replace the axiom of regularity (foundation) with an axiom permitting sets that are members of themselves or that belong to infinite descending membership chains, such as a set x with x = {x}. Such sets are called hypersets.

Anti-foundation axioms

The most studied is Peter Aczel's Anti-Foundation Axiom (AFA), presented in his 1988 monograph Non-Well-Founded Sets. Under AFA, every system of set equations has a unique solution, and two hypersets are identical when they are bisimilar. Earlier proposals include the work of Forti and Honsell, and Finsler's set theory; Quine's New Foundations is sometimes also discussed in this context.

Applications

Hypersets have been applied in computer science to model circular and coinductive phenomena — processes, streams and self-referential data structures — and in philosophical treatments of semantic paradoxes and common knowledge.
