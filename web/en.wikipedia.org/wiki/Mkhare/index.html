Mkhare (Georgian: მხარე, mxare, literally "side" or "region") is a type of first-level administrative subdivision of Georgia, usually translated as "region" or "province". Georgia is divided into nine mkharebi, alongside two autonomous republics (Abkhazia and Adjara) and the capital city of Tbilisi.

Administration

The mkharebi were established by presidential decree in the mid-1990s as an intermediate tier of administration between the central government and the municipalities (former districts, or raioni). Each mkhare is headed by a state commissioner, commonly called the governor, who is appointed by the central government. Unlike the autonomous republics, the mkharebi have no elected legislatures of their own.

List of mkharebi

The nine regions are: Guria, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, and Shida Kartli.
