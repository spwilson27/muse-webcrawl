Northwest Caucasian languages

The Northwest Caucasian languages, also called Abkhaz–Adyghean or Pontic, are a language family spoken in the northwestern Caucasus, mainly in Russia (Adygea, Kabardino-Balkaria, Karachay-Cherkessia) and Abkhazia, and in diaspora communities in Turkey and the Middle East.

Languages

The family includes Abkhaz, Abaza, Kabardian (East Circassian), Adyghe (West Circassian), and the extinct Ubykh. They are noted for very large consonant inventories and small vowel systems, polysynthetic verbs, and ergative alignment.

Status

Ubykh died out in 1992 with its last native speaker Tevfik Esenç. The other languages survive but several varieties are endangered.
