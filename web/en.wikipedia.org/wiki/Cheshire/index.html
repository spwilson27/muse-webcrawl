Cheshire
Region: North West England
County town: Chester
Known for: Cheshire cheese, salt mining

Cheshire is a ceremonial county in North West England. Its county town is the cathedral city of Chester, and other major settlements include Warrington, Crewe, Macclesfield, Ellesmere Port, Nantwich and Northwich. The county borders Merseyside, Greater Manchester, Derbyshire, Staffordshire, Shropshire, and the Welsh county of Wrexham.

Economy and notable features

Cheshire is historically known for its salt mines around Northwich and Winsford, its crumbly white Cheshire cheese, and a largely rural landscape of dairy farming. It also hosts the Jodrell Bank Observatory with the Lovell Telescope.

Culture

The Cheshire Cat of Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland draws on the county's name and an older expression, "grinning like a Cheshire cat".
