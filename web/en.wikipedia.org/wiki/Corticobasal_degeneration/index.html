Corticobasal degeneration

Corticobasal degeneration (CBD) is a rare, progressive neurodegenerative disease characterized pathologically by the accumulation of abnormal tau protein in neurons and glia, particularly in the cerebral cortex and basal ganglia. It is one of the tauopathies and a cause of atypical parkinsonism. The clinical syndrome most often associated with it is called corticobasal syndrome (CBS), although CBS can also result from other pathologies, including Alzheimer's disease.

Signs and symptoms

Onset is typically in the sixth or seventh decade of life. The classic presentation is strikingly asymmetric: one limb becomes stiff, clumsy, and subject to dystonia and jerky myoclonus, with limb apraxia (inability to perform learned movements). The striking alien limb phenomenon, in which a hand seems to act with a will of its own, is characteristic. Other features include cortical sensory loss, asymmetric parkinsonism poorly responsive to levodopa, gait and balance problems, speech and language disturbance (aphasia), swallowing difficulty, and cognitive decline, which may be modest early on.

Pathology

Microscopically, CBD shows ballooned, achromatic neurons, tau-positive astrocytic plaques (a relatively specific feature), threads, and oligodendroglial coiled bodies; the tau is predominantly the 4-repeat isoform. Affected regions include the perirolandic cortex and substantia nigra. The disorder belongs to the same group as progressive supranuclear palsy and some forms of frontotemporal dementia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis during life is clinical, supported by MRI showing asymmetric frontoparietal atrophy; definitive diagnosis requires neuropathological examination, and imaging with tau-PET tracers is investigational. There is no disease-modifying therapy. Levodopa and other antiparkinsonian drugs give limited benefit; botulinum toxin injections can ease dystonia, and physical, occupational, and speech therapy are important. Survival averages roughly six to eight years from onset, death often resulting from complications such as pneumonia.
