Gary is a city in Lake County, Indiana, United States. Located along the southern shore of Lake Michigan about 25 miles (40 km) southeast of downtown Chicago, Gary was founded in 1906 by the United States Steel Corporation as the home of its Gary Steel Works, and was named for Elbert Henry Gary, the chairman of U.S. Steel.

History

Gary was built almost from scratch on sandy lakeshore land purchased by U.S. Steel. The Gary Works became one of the largest steel mills in the world, and the city grew rapidly, drawing immigrant workers from Europe and, during the Great Migration, African American workers from the South. By 1960 the population exceeded 178,000.

In 1967 Gary elected Richard G. Hatcher, one of the first African American mayors of a major American city. From the 1960s onward the city's fortunes declined as the American steel industry contracted: tens of thousands of steel jobs disappeared, the population fell by more than half, and large parts of the city fell into disrepair. Gary has frequently been cited as an example of the effects of deindustrialization on American manufacturing towns.

Geography and transport

Gary lies within the Chicago metropolitan area and is served by Interstate 90 (the Indiana Toll Road), I-80/94, and U.S. routes 6, 12, and 20. The Gary/Chicago International Airport serves the city, and the South Shore Line commuter railroad connects Gary to Chicago. Indiana Dunes National Park lies just east of the city.

Notable people

Gary is the hometown of the Jackson family of musicians, including Michael Jackson and Janet Jackson; the family's childhood home stands at 2300 Jackson Street. Other natives include actor Karl Malden, astronaut Frank Borman, and football player and actor Alex Karras.

Economy

Steel remains a significant employer through the Gary Works, though at a fraction of its peak workforce. Riverboat casinos on Lake Michigan, including the Majestic Star properties, have been part of the city's economy since the 1990s.
