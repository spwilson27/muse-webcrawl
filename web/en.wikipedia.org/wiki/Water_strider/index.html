The Gerridae are a family of insects in the order Hemiptera, commonly known as water striders, water skeeters, water bugs, pond skaters or jesus bugs. They are anatomically adapted to walk on the surface of water, distributing their weight over long, hydrophobic legs so that surface tension supports them without breaking the surface film.

Description

Water striders have slender bodies, typically 2–12 mm long (some species much larger), and characteristically long mid and hind legs used for propulsion and steering across the water surface, while the short front legs are used to grab prey. Their bodies and legs are covered with tiny water-repellent hairs (microsetae) that trap air and prevent them from becoming wet. They move by rowing strokes of the middle legs, reaching speeds many times their body length per second, and can also jump high off the surface to escape predators.

Ecology

Gerrids live on the surfaces of ponds, slow streams, marshes and even the open ocean — the five species of Halobates are among the very few insects to inhabit the open sea. They are predators and scavengers, sensing ripples to locate insects that have fallen onto the surface, which they pierce with their rostrum and suck dry. Males and females communicate through surface ripples, and mating behaviour — including male coercion and female resistance — has been extensively studied. Their own predators include birds, fish and frogs striking from below or above.
