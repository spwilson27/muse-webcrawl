Mohammad Karim Khan Zand (c. 1705 – 1 March 1779) was the founder of the Zand dynasty of Iran, which he ruled from 1751 until his death. He never assumed the title of shah, instead styling himself Vakil-e Ra'aya ("Deputy of the People"), and he made Shiraz his capital, presiding over a period of relative peace and prosperity in 18th-century Iran.

Rise to power

Karim Khan was a member of the Zand tribe of the Lak branch of the Lurs of western Iran. He rose as a military commander under Nader Shah. After Nader's assassination in 1747 threw Iran into civil war, Karim Khan gradually fought his way to supremacy over most of the country, defeating rivals including the Qajar chief Mohammad Hasan Khan and the Afghan commander Azad Khan. By the early 1760s he controlled nearly all of Iran except Khorasan.

Rule

From Shiraz, Karim Khan rebuilt Iran's economy after decades of warfare: he encouraged trade through the Persian Gulf port of Bushehr, welcomed foreign merchants including the British East India Company, reduced taxes, and sponsored building works in Shiraz such as the Vakil Mosque, the Vakil Bazaar and the Arg (citadel) of Karim Khan. He maintained nominal allegiance to a Safavid puppet, Ismail III, before ruling in his own right as Vakil.

His court was noted for relative tolerance and for the splendour described by foreign visitors, though succession was left unsettled: on his death in 1779 his sons and relatives fought a brutal civil war, which ended only in 1794 when Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar overthrew the last Zand ruler, Lotf Ali Khan.
