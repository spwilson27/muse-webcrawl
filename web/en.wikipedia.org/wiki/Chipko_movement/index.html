Chipko movement

The Chipko movement was a nonviolent forest conservation movement in India in the 1970s, in which villagers — prominently women — protected trees by embracing them to prevent loggers from felling them. "Chipko" means "to hug" or "to cling" in Hindi.

Origins

The movement began in 1973 in the Himalayan region of present-day Uttarakhand, inspired by earlier Bishnoi traditions of tree protection. A famous confrontation occurred in 1974 at Reni village, where women led by Gaura Devi guarded the forest while the men were away.

Leaders and impact

Figures such as Chandi Prasad Bhatt and Sunderlal Bahuguna spread the movement across the Garhwal Himalayas. It contributed to a 1980 moratorium on commercial felling above 1,000 metres in the region and influenced forest policy and environmental activism worldwide.
