History of Eritrea

The history of Eritrea covers one of the oldest inhabited regions of the world, home to early hominids and to the ancient kingdom of Aksum, and shaped by Italian colonisation, British administration, federation and annexation by Ethiopia, a thirty-year war of independence, and statehood since 1993.

Ancient history

Eritrea's Red Sea coast and highlands formed part of the Land of Punt known to ancient Egypt and hosted the kingdom of D'mt and then the powerful Aksumite Empire, whose port of Adulis traded with Rome, Arabia, and India. Aksum adopted Christianity in the 4th century AD; Islam arrived on the coast from the 7th century.

Colonial period

Italy assembled its colony of Eritrea between 1882 and 1890, making Asmara its capital and building infrastructure including the Eritrean Railway. Italian rule ended in 1941 when British forces defeated Italy in East Africa. A UN resolution federated Eritrea with Ethiopia in 1952 under the Ethiopian crown, but Emperor Haile Selassie progressively dismantled Eritrean autonomy and annexed the territory outright in 1962.

War of independence

Armed resistance began in 1961 and developed into a thirty-year war led first by the Eritrean Liberation Front and later by the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) under Isaias Afwerki. The EPLF liberated Asmara in May 1991 as Ethiopia's Derg regime collapsed. A UN-supervised referendum in April 1993 voted overwhelmingly for independence, formally declared on 24 May 1993.

Independent Eritrea

Eritrea fought a border war with Ethiopia in 1998–2000 that cost tens of thousands of lives and froze relations until a peace declaration in 2018. The country has been ruled since independence by President Isaias Afwerki and the PFDJ; indefinite national service and restrictions on political freedoms have drawn sustained international criticism.
