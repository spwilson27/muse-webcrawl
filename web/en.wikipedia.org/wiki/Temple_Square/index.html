Temple Square

Temple Square
Location: Salt Lake City, Utah, U.S.
Area: 10 acres (4.0 ha)
Owned by: The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints
Key buildings: Salt Lake Temple, Tabernacle, Assembly Hall, Conference Center (adjacent)

Temple Square is a 10-acre (4.0 ha) complex in downtown Salt Lake City, Utah, owned by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The square and its surrounding church-owned blocks form the world headquarters of the church and are among Utah's most visited attractions, drawing millions of visitors a year.

History

Shortly after Mormon pioneers arrived in the Salt Lake Valley in 1847, church president Brigham Young selected the site for the Salt Lake Temple. Construction of the granite temple began in 1853 and took 40 years; it was dedicated in 1893. The site was originally called the Temple Block; "Temple Square" came into common use in the 20th century.

Sites and buildings

  • Salt Lake Temple: the largest and best-known temple of the church, closed in 2019 for a major multi-year renovation and seismic upgrade.
  • Salt Lake Tabernacle: completed in 1867, famous for its acoustics and its pipe organ; home of the Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square.
  • Salt Lake Assembly Hall: a Gothic Revival chapel built in 1877–1882.
  • Sea Gull Monument: commemorating the 1848 "miracle of the gulls".
  • Visitors' centres, gardens, and reflecting pools, including seasonal flower plantings and the widely attended Christmas light displays.

The broader headquarters campus surrounding the square includes the Conference Center (opened in 2000, seating about 21,000), the Church History Library, the Church History Museum, the Joseph Smith Memorial Building, and the Relief Society Building.
