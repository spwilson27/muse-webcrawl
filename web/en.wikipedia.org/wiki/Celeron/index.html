Celeron

Celeron
Manufacturer: Intel
Introduced: April 1998
Market: budget desktop and mobile processors

Celeron is a brand name given by Intel to a range of low-cost microprocessors intended for budget personal computers. Introduced in April 1998, the first Celeron processors were based on the Pentium II design with reduced or initially absent L2 cache, making them cheaper to produce.

History

The original cacheless Celeron 266 and 300 MHz models performed poorly, but the subsequent Celeron 300A, with 128 KB of on-die L2 cache, became famous among enthusiasts for reliable overclocking to 450 MHz. Since then, Celeron models have been derived from successive Intel architectures — Pentium III, Pentium 4, Core, and later Atom and Core-derived designs — typically with fewer cores, smaller caches, and lower clock speeds than their Pentium and Core counterparts.

Positioning

In 2022 Intel announced it would retire the Celeron and Pentium brands for laptops in favour of the generic "Intel Processor" name, though the names continued in some segments.
