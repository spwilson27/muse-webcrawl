Script doctor

A script doctor is a screenwriter, often uncredited, hired by a studio or production company to rewrite or polish an existing screenplay. Script doctors are brought in to fix specific problems — structure, pacing, dialogue, characterisation, jokes or a weak third act — frequently under tight deadlines shortly before or even during production.

Work

Unlike the original writer, who develops a story over months, a script doctor is a specialist troubleshooter, paid premium weekly rates for rapid, targeted rewrites. Because Writers Guild rules govern screen credit, much of this work goes uncredited, and a small number of highly paid writers have built reputations as the industry's go-to fixers. Carrie Fisher, for example, was a celebrated script doctor on films of the 1990s, polishing dialogue on numerous productions.

Criticism

The practice is sometimes criticised for homogenising scripts and for the credit disputes it creates, but producers regard it as insurance on expensive productions where a flawed screenplay threatens a much larger investment.
