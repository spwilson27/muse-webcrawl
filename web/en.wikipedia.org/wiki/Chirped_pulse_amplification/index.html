Chirped pulse amplification

Chirped pulse amplification (CPA) is a technique for amplifying ultrashort laser pulses to very high peak powers without damaging the amplifying medium. It was developed by Donna Strickland and Gérard Mourou in the 1980s, work for which they shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Method

A short seed pulse is first stretched in time — "chirped" — so its peak power drops. The stretched pulse is then amplified safely, and finally recompressed to near its original duration, yielding peak powers in the terawatt to petawatt range.

Applications

CPA underlies modern high-intensity lasers used in laser eye surgery, precision machining, plasma physics and inertial-confinement fusion research.
