fastText

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

fastText is a library for learning word embeddings and text classification, created by Facebook's AI Research (FAIR) lab and released as open-source software in 2016. It is an extension of the word2vec model: instead of learning a single vector per word, fastText represents each word as a bag of character n-grams together with the whole word, and the word's embedding is the sum of its n-gram vectors. This subword modeling lets fastText construct vectors for out-of-vocabulary words and captures morphology, which is particularly useful for morphologically rich languages.

Features

The library supports both unsupervised training of word vectors (in skip-gram and continuous bag-of-words modes) and supervised text classification with a linear classifier trained efficiently on large corpora. Pre-trained vectors have been published for 157 languages, trained on Common Crawl and Wikipedia data. Practical techniques in fastText include hierarchical softmax, negative sampling, product quantization for compressing models, and autotuning of hyperparameters.

Reception

fastText has been widely used in industry and research for tasks such as language identification, sentiment analysis, and tag prediction, where its speed and modest memory footprint made it a practical alternative to deeper models.
