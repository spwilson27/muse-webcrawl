Yamabushi

Yamabushi (literally, "one who lies in the mountains") are Japanese mountain ascetic hermits. They are practitioners of Shugendō, a syncretic religion blending Shinto, Buddhism, Taoism, and shamanism, centred on rigorous discipline in sacred mountains.

Yamabushi
Religion: Shugendō
Region: Japan
Known for: Mountain asceticism
Sacred sites: Dewa Sanzan, Mount Ōmine

History

The tradition traces to En no Gyōja in the 7th–8th centuries. Yamabushi flourished in the Heian and medieval periods, serving as guides, healers, and exorcists, and were sometimes depicted as possessing supernatural powers — later feeding ninja and tengu folklore.

Attire and practice

Yamabushi wear distinctive checked jackets, a small black cap (tokin), white leggings, and carry a conch shell trumpet (horagai), staff, and prayer beads. Practices include waterfall austerities (takigyō), fire rituals, fasting, and pilgrimage over peaks such as the Dewa Sanzan in Yamagata.

Modern times

Shugendō was suppressed in the Meiji period when Shinto and Buddhism were separated, but revived after World War II. Today yamabushi training is open at temples such as Haguro.
