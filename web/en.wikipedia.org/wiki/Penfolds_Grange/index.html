Penfolds Grange

Penfolds Grange, Australia's most celebrated wine.

Penfolds Grange (until the 1989 vintage labelled Grange Hermitage) is an Australian wine produced by Penfolds and widely regarded as Australia's flagship red wine and one of the great wines of the world. Made predominantly from Shiraz with a small percentage of Cabernet Sauvignon, it is a multi-district blend, drawing fruit from vineyards across South Australia, and is matured in new American oak hogsheads.

History

Grange was created by winemaker Max Schubert, who, after a 1949 study trip to Bordeaux, resolved to make an Australian wine capable of ageing for decades. The first experimental vintage was 1951. Early releases were poorly received, and Penfolds ordered Schubert to stop production; he continued in secret, and acclaim for the matured early vintages led to official reinstatement with the 1960 vintage. Grange has since been made every year and has become a benchmark for Australian Shiraz, with celebrated vintages such as 1955, 1971, 1990, and 2008 receiving exceptional critical scores; the wine is listed as an Exceptional category wine in Langton's Classification of Australian Wine.

Style

Grange is a full-bodied, richly concentrated wine noted for ripe dark fruit, chocolate, spice, and savoury notes, firm tannins, and the sweet vanilla-coconut influence of new American oak. It typically requires a decade or more of cellaring and can age for several decades. Unusually for a prestige wine, it is deliberately blended across regions—including the Barossa Valley, Magill Estate near Adelaide, McLaren Vale, and other sites—rather than sourced from a single vineyard.
