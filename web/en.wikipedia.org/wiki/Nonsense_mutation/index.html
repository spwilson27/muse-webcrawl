Nonsense mutation

A nonsense mutation is a point mutation in a sequence of DNA that results in a premature stop codon, or a nonsense codon, in the transcribed mRNA, and in most cases a truncated protein that is often non-functional. It is one of the three main types of point mutation, alongside missense mutations (which substitute one amino acid for another) and silent mutations (which do not change the amino acid).

Mechanism and consequences

For example, a change of the codon CAG (glutamine) to TAG creates an amber stop codon, halting translation early. Cells often destroy such mRNA through nonsense-mediated decay, reducing the amount of abnormal protein. Nonsense mutations cause a significant fraction of inherited genetic diseases: examples include some forms of cystic fibrosis (e.g. in the CFTR gene), Duchenne muscular dystrophy, beta-thalassemia and many cases of neurofibromatosis type 1.

Therapeutic approaches

Because the genetic code's stop codons can occasionally be misread, drugs called read-through agents (such as ataluren, and aminoglycoside antibiotics like gentamicin) can promote ribosomes to insert an amino acid at a premature stop codon and produce full-length protein, a strategy investigated for nonsense-mediated genetic diseases.
