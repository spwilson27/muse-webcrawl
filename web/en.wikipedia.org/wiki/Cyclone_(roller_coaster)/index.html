Cyclone (roller coaster)

The Cyclone at Coney Island, opened in 1927.

The Cyclone is a wooden roller coaster located at Luna Park in Coney Island, Brooklyn, New York City. It opened on June 26, 1927, and is one of the most famous and influential roller coasters ever built, renowned for its steep drops, tight turns, and intense ride in a compact footprint. It was designated a New York City landmark in 1988 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1991.

Layout

The Cyclone stands about 85 feet (26 m) tall, with a first drop of roughly 85 feet at an angle of nearly 59 degrees, reaching speeds around 60 miles per hour (97 km/h). The track, about 2,640 feet (805 m) long, crosses over itself repeatedly in a dense twister layout with multiple drops and sharply banked turns, all in a wood structure with steel rails. The ride lasts roughly two minutes.

History

Built by Vernon Keenan and Harry C. Baker for the Rosenthal brothers at a cost of about $175,000, the Cyclone replaced the earlier Giant Racer on the site. It became an icon of Coney Island, surviving the decline of the area's amusement parks and a threatened demolition in the 1970s before being restored and reopened in 1975. It continues to operate each season under the management of Luna Park.

Legacy

The Cyclone's name and layout have been imitated by "Cyclone" clones and inspired coasters worldwide. Enthusiasts rank it among the greatest wooden coasters; it has appeared in countless films, songs, and works of art as a symbol of Coney Island and of classic American amusement rides.
