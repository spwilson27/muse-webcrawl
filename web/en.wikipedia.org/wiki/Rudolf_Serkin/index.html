Rudolf Serkin (28 March 1903 – 8 May 1991) was an Austrian-American classical pianist, widely regarded as one of the greatest Beethoven interpreters of the twentieth century and a revered figure in American musical life.

Life and career

Born to Russian Jewish parents in Bohemia, Serkin was a child prodigy who studied in Vienna with Richard Robert and composition with Arnold Schoenberg. He debuted with the Vienna Philharmonic at twelve. In the 1920s he formed a famous duo with violinist Adolf Busch, whose daughter Irene he later married. Fleeing the Nazis, he settled in the United States in 1939, joining the faculty of the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia.

Legacy

Serkin was a central figure in chamber music: he co-founded the Marlboro Music School and Festival in Vermont in 1951 and performed for decades with the Guarneri Quartet and cellist Pablo Casals at Prades. His many recordings for Columbia, especially of Beethoven, Mozart, Schubert, Brahms and Mendelssohn, are noted for their intellectual rigour and humility. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1964 and a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. His son Peter Serkin was also a noted pianist.
