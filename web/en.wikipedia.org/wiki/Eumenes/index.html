Eumenes

Eumenes of Cardia (c. 362 – 316 BC) was a Greek general and satrap, and one of the Diadochi who fought over the empire of Alexander the Great after his death. Uniquely among the major successors, he was a Greek rather than a Macedonian, and had served Alexander as his royal secretary rather than as a battlefield commander — handicaps he overcame by conspicuous loyalty to the Argead royal house and by outstanding generalship.

Career under Alexander and Perdiccas

Eumenes accompanied Alexander's expedition as chief secretary, keeping the royal journal. At the settlement of Babylon (323 BC) he received the unassigned satrapies of Cappadocia and Paphlagonia, which he conquered with the help of Leonnatus and Antigonus. He became the ablest supporter of the regent Perdiccas, defeating the rebel Craterus and Neoptolemus in Asia Minor (321 BC), though Perdiccas's murder in Egypt left him condemned by the other Macedonian leaders.

Wars against Antigonus

Appointed royal general in Asia by the regent Polyperchon, Eumenes took command of the Silver Shields, Alexander's veteran hypaspists, and campaigned against Antigonus Monophthalmus across Persia. After the hard-fought battles of Paraetacene (317 BC) and Gabiene (316 BC) — both tactically indecisive — his own troops, fearing for the families and baggage captured by Antigonus, handed Eumenes over to the enemy. Antigonus had him executed.

Legacy

Ancient writers admired Eumenes as the most skilful and honourable of the Successors; Plutarch's Life of Eumenes pairs him with Sertorius. His career illustrates how far talent could carry an outsider in the Macedonian military aristocracy — and where its limits lay.
