Kaempferol

IUPAC name3,5,7-trihydroxy-2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)-4H-1-benzopyran-4-one
FormulaC15H10O6
Molar mass286.24 g/mol
AppearanceYellow crystalline solid

Kaempferol (3,4′,5,7-tetrahydroxyflavone) is a natural flavonol, a type of flavonoid, found in a variety of plants and plant-derived foods. It is named after Engelbert Kaempfer, a German naturalist who described many East Asian plants.

Natural occurrence

Kaempferol is common in the human diet and occurs in tea, broccoli, cabbage, kale, beans, endive, leek, tomato, strawberries, grapes, Brussels sprouts, apples and other plant sources. Plants known to contain it include Aloe vera, Ginkgo biloba, Moringa oleifera and the spice saffron crocus (Crocus sativus), whose petals are a rich source.

Biosynthesis

Plants synthesise kaempferol from the amino acid phenylalanine through the general phenylpropanoid pathway. The precursor 4-coumaroyl-CoA combines with three molecules of malonyl-CoA to form the chalcone naringenin chalcone, which is isomerised to the flavanone naringenin. From naringenin, the enzyme flavanone 3-hydroxylase produces dihydrokaempferol, and the enzyme flavonol synthase then introduces a double bond to yield kaempferol.

Research

Kaempferol acts as an antioxidant in vitro and has been studied in preliminary research for possible effects on inflammation and cell signalling. It is a common aglycone found combined with sugar moieties as glycosides such as astragalin (kaempferol 3-O-glucoside).
