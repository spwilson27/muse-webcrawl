Saatchi & Saatchi is a British multinational advertising and communications agency founded in London in 1970 by brothers Maurice and Charles Saatchi. It became the largest advertising agency in the world during the 1980s through aggressive acquisitions, and since 2000 has been owned by the French group Publicis.

History

Charles Saatchi, a copywriter, and his brother Maurice founded the agency in 1970. Early notable work included the "pregnant man" poster for the Health Education Council (1970) and the Silk Cut cigarette campaign. For the 1979 general election, Saatchi & Saatchi created the Conservatives' "Labour Isn't Working" poster, one of the most famous political advertisements in British history, and worked on Margaret Thatcher's subsequent campaigns.

Through the 1980s the group acquired dozens of agencies and consultancy firms, including the American network Ted Bates Worldwide in 1986, briefly becoming the world's largest advertising group. Overreach and recession led to a shareholder revolt in 1994–95 that ousted the brothers, who went on to found M&C Saatchi. Publicis acquired the agency in 2000.

Notable campaigns

Beyond politics, famous Saatchi work includes the British Airways "Face" commercial (1989), Club 18-30's cheeky posters, and campaigns for Toyota, Procter & Gamble brands and T-Mobile, including the 2009 Liverpool Street station "Dance" flash mob.
