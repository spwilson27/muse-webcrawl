Anacardium, the cashews, is a genus of flowering plants in the family Anacardiaceae, native to tropical regions of the Americas. The best-known species is Anacardium occidentale, the cashew tree, which is widely cultivated for its nuts and its fleshy fruit, the cashew apple.

Description

Species of Anacardium are evergreen trees or shrubs with simple, alternate leaves and small flowers borne in panicles. The true fruit is a kidney-shaped drupe that grows at the end of a swollen receptacle (the hypocarp), which ripens into the soft, juicy accessory fruit.

Species and uses

The genus contains roughly twenty recognised species, including the cashew (A. occidentale), the wild cashew or espavé (A. excelsum) and A. humile, a small Cerrado shrub whose fruits are eaten in Brazil. Cashew nuts are a major export crop in Vietnam, India, Nigeria and Brazil, while the cashew apple is eaten fresh or made into juice and preserves where the tree is grown.
