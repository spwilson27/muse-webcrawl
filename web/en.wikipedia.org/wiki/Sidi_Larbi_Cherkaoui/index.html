Sidi Larbi Cherkaoui

Sidi Larbi Cherkaoui (born 10 June 1976) is a Belgian dancer and choreographer of Moroccan descent. One of the most prolific and acclaimed contemporary choreographers in the world, he has created more than 50 full-length works drawing on many dance traditions and exploring themes of spirituality, identity, and cultural difference. He is the artistic director of Ballet du Grand Théâtre de Genève (from 2022) and founder of the Antwerp-based company Eastman.

Career

Cherkaoui began dancing as a teenager, appearing on Belgian television shows, and trained at Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker's school P.A.R.T.S. in Brussels. He first gained attention as a dancer with the Flemish company Les Ballets C de la B under Alain Platel, performing in Iets op Bach (1998). His first choreographic piece, the duet Anonymous Society (1999), won several prizes.

Breakthrough works included Rien de rien (2000), Foi (2003, with Les Ballets C de la B), and Tempus Fugit (2004). His collaborations with the sculptor Antony Gormley and Shaolin monks produced Sutra (2008) with Sadler's Wells, one of his most widely toured works; with Gormley he also made Babel(words) (2010, with Damien Jalet) and Noetic (2014) for the Göteborg Opera Dance Company. Puz/zle (2012) premiered at the Festival d'Avignon. Other collaborations include work with Akram Khan (zero degrees, 2005), flamenco dancer María Pagés, and the monks of the Shaolin Temple.

Cherkaoui founded his own company, Eastman, in Antwerp in 2010. From 2015 to 2022 he was artistic director of Ballet Vlaanderen (Royal Ballet of Flanders). In 2022 he became artistic director of the Ballet du Grand Théâtre de Genève. He has also choreographed for opera, film, and pop — including Beyoncé and Jay-Z's "Apeshit" video scenes filmed at the Louvre, and work for Madonna's tours.

Style and themes

His movement language blends contemporary dance, hip-hop, capoeira, martial arts, and classical forms, often with live music from many cultures — Corsican polyphony, Japanese drumming, flamenco, qawwali, and Gregorian chant. Recurring themes include faith and doubt, the encounter between cultures, and the search for identity between his Flemish Catholic upbringing and Moroccan Muslim heritage.
