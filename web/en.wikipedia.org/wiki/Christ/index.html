Christ

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christ, from the Greek word Christos (Χριστός, "anointed one"), is a title for Jesus in Christianity, where he is regarded as the Messiah prophesied in the Hebrew Bible. The word is a translation of the Hebrew Mashiach (מָשִׁיחַ, "anointed"). Christians call Jesus "Jesus Christ" or "Christ Jesus", treating Christ as a title and, in common usage, as part of his name.

Etymology and usage

In the Septuagint, the Greek translation of the Hebrew scriptures, christos was used to translate mashiach, referring to an anointed king or priest. In the New Testament, the title is applied to Jesus from the earliest writings; the apostle Paul uses "Christ" so frequently that it functioned almost as a second name. The disciples were first called "Christians" at Antioch (Acts 11:26).

Christology

Christology is the branch of theology concerned with the nature of Christ — the relationship between his divine and human natures. The Council of Chalcedon (451) defined Christ as one person in two natures, fully God and fully man, a definition accepted by most historic churches. Titles used of Christ include Son of God, Son of Man, Lamb of God, Logos (the Word), and King of Kings.

In other religions

In Islam, Jesus (Isa) is regarded as al-Masih, the Messiah, and a prophet, though not divine. In Judaism, Jesus is not accepted as the Messiah.
