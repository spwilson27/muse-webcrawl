Generation Z

Generation Z
BornMid-to-late 1990s to early 2010s (often 1997–2012)
Preceded byMillennials (Generation Y)
Followed byGeneration Alpha
Other namesZoomers, iGeneration, digital natives

Generation Z, often shortened to Gen Z and colloquially called Zoomers, is the demographic cohort succeeding millennials and preceding Generation Alpha. Researchers and popular media typically use the mid-to-late 1990s as starting birth years — Pew Research Center uses 1997 — and the early 2010s as ending years, commonly 2012.

Generation Z is the first cohort to grow up with the mobile internet from childhood.

Characteristics

Generation Z is the first social generation to have grown up with access to the internet and portable digital technology from a young age, and its members are often described as digital natives. Studies describe the cohort, on average, as more risk-averse in some behaviours than millennials — with lower rates of teenage drinking and pregnancy in many Western countries — while reporting higher rates of anxiety and depression, a trend researchers link variously to social media, economic insecurity, and greater willingness to report mental-health problems.

Education, work, and politics

Gen Z members entered the workforce through the COVID-19 pandemic, which shaped expectations about remote work. Surveys in many countries find the cohort relatively concerned about climate change and cost of living, and more favourable toward diversity and social liberalism, though voting patterns vary widely by country and subgroup. Economically, the cohort came of age amid housing affordability pressures in much of the developed world.

Criticism of the concept

Demographers and sociologists caution that generational labels are imprecise: birth-year cutoffs are arbitrary, and differences within the cohort — by class, country, and education — are often larger than average differences between generations.
