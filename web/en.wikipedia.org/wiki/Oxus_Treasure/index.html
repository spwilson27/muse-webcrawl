Overview

The Oxus Treasure is the most important surviving collection of gold and silver from the Achaemenid Persian Empire, dating roughly to the 5th–4th centuries BC. Discovered in 1877–80 on the north bank of the Oxus (Amu Darya) — near Takht-i Kuwad in modern Tajikistan — it was carried by merchants who were robbed on the road to India and rescued, in the famous account, by Captain F. C. Burton of the British Indian service, who bought the pieces back.

Significance

Acquired largely by the British Museum (through the Augustus Wollaston Franks bequest), the treasure's masterpieces include a gold model four-horse chariot, griffin-headed armlets, scabbards, votive plaques and vessels blending Persian, Median, Greek and steppe styles — evidence of the empire's artistic reach. Tajikistan has sought its return; in 2007 it presented the British Museum with replicas for display in Dushanbe. Whether the hoard was temple treasure or a merchant's stock remains debated.
