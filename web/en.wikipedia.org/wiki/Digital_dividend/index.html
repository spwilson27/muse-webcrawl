Digital dividend

The digital dividend is the radio spectrum made available by the transition of terrestrial television broadcasting from analog to digital systems. Because digital television can carry many more channels in the same bandwidth — and requires less protection against interference — switching off analog transmissions frees substantial portions of the valuable UHF band, particularly around 700 and 800 MHz.

Allocation

Governments and regulators have generally divided the freed spectrum between additional digital broadcasting and new electronic communications services, notably mobile broadband, whose operators value the low frequencies for their wide-area coverage and building penetration. Many countries auctioned the spectrum, raising significant public revenue; the United States' 2008 auction of the 700 MHz band raised over $19 billion.

Debates

Policy debates concern how much spectrum to reserve for broadcasting, public-safety communications, and unlicensed or community uses, and how to manage cross-border coordination. A "second digital dividend" followed in many regions as television moved out of the 700 MHz band as well.
