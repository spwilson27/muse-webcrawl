Merit badge (Scouting America)

A merit badge is an award presented by Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America) to a Scout who completes the requirements for a specified area of study or skill. Merit badges encourage Scouts to explore subjects such as science, sports, trades, business, and citizenship, and to meet adult experts in those fields. The program offers more than 130 badges, more than at any point since its early decades.

History

The merit badge program began in 1911 with 57 badges, including subjects such as pioneering, electricity, and agriculture. The original badges were stamped or cast designs; the familiar embroidered cloth badges sewn onto a sash evolved over the following decades. The design of each badge is a small round patch with a pictogram of its subject.

Earning a badge

A Scout works with a registered merit badge counselor, an adult volunteer with expertise in the subject, and completes published requirements that may include written work, demonstrations, projects, and visits. Requirements are certified on an application ("blue card"), and the badge is presented at a troop meeting or court of honor. Badges are worn on a sash or in a display.

Role in advancement

Merit badges are central to advancement above the rank of First Class. The Eagle Scout rank—the program's highest—requires a minimum of 21 merit badges, including a set of required ("Eagle-required") badges such as First Aid, Citizenship in the Community, Cooking, Personal Fitness, and Environmental Science (or Sustainability), which are distinguished by a silver border rather than green.
