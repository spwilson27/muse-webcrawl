The 2001 New England Patriots season was the franchise's 32nd season in the National Football League and the second under head coach Bill Belichick. The Patriots finished 11–5, won the AFC East, and defeated the heavily favored St. Louis Rams in Super Bowl XXXVI to win the first championship in team history — launching a dynasty that would dominate the NFL for two decades.

Season narrative

After an 0–2 start, starting quarterback Drew Bledsoe was seriously injured in Week 2 by a hit from Jets linebacker Mo Lewis. Second-year backup Tom Brady, a sixth-round draft pick, took over and led the team to an 11–3 finish as starter. The season coincided with the September 11 attacks, which postponed Week 2's remaining games; Patriots guard Joe Andruzzi, whose brothers were New York firefighters, became a symbol of the team's season.

Playoffs

In the divisional round, the Patriots beat the Oakland Raiders 16–13 in overtime in a snowstorm — a game remembered for the controversial "tuck rule" ruling that preserved New England's comeback drive. They then upset the Pittsburgh Steelers 24–17 in the AFC Championship, with Bledsoe relieving an injured Brady. In Super Bowl XXXVI, as 14-point underdogs to the Rams' "Greatest Show on Turf," the Patriots won 20–17 on Adam Vinatieri's 48-yard field goal as time expired, after Brady drove the team into range in the final minute without timeouts.

Significance

The championship began the Brady–Belichick era; the Patriots would appear in nine Super Bowls and win six over the following eighteen seasons. It was also the final season at Foxboro Stadium.
