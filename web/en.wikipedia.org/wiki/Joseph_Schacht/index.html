Joseph Schacht

Joseph Franz Schacht (March 15, 1902 – August 4, 1969) was a German-British orientalist and historian of Islamic law whose work reshaped Western scholarship on the origins of Islamic jurisprudence and the hadith literature.

Career

Born in Ratibor, Silesia, Schacht studied theology and oriental languages at Breslau and Leipzig and became professor at Freiburg and then Königsberg. In the 1930s he moved to Egypt, teaching at Cairo University, and during the Second World War worked in Britain, taking British citizenship. He later held chairs at the University of Oxford and Columbia University.

Scholarship

In The Origins of Muhammadan Jurisprudence (1950), Schacht argued that Islamic law developed substantially during the first two centuries of Islam rather than deriving directly from the Quran and the practice of Muhammad, and that many hadith — traditions attributed to the Prophet — were put into circulation later by legal scholars and projected backwards through chains of transmission (isnads). His method of dating traditions by analysing their isnads profoundly influenced, and provoked continuing debate within, the field of Islamic studies. His concise An Introduction to Islamic Law (1964) remains a standard reference, and he edited the second edition of the Encyclopaedia of Islam.
