Vila Real (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈvilɐ ʁiˈal]) is a municipality and city in northern Portugal, capital of the district of Vila Real, in the historical province of Trás-os-Montes. The city population in 2001 was around 25,000, and the municipality had 49,574 inhabitants in the 2011 census.

Vila Real is situated on a plateau at the confluence of the Corgo and Cabril rivers, surrounded by the Alvão and Marão mountain ranges. It is an important regional centre for commerce, administration and higher education, and is home to the University of Trás-os-Montes and Alto Douro (UTAD).

History

The region of Vila Real was inhabited in prehistoric times, and Roman remains have been found in the area. The modern town was founded in 1272 by King Afonso III, and received a charter (foral) from King Denis in 1289. Vila Real flourished in the 17th and 18th centuries as the residence of nobles who built mansions in the town, and it became known for its wine production as part of the nearby Douro wine region, the oldest demarcated wine region in the world.

Main sights

Landmarks include the Gothic church of São Domingos (the cathedral), the church of São Pedro, the Casa de Mateus palace just outside the town, and the birthplace of the explorer Diogo Cão. The nearby Alvão Natural Park protects mountain landscapes and traditional villages. The city is also famous for its historic street circuit, which has hosted motor racing since 1931 and in recent years rounds of the World Touring Car Championship.

Climate

Vila Real has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters, moderated by its elevation of about 420 metres above sea level.
