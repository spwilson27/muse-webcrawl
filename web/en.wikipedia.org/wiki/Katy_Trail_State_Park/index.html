Katy Trail State Park is a state park in Missouri built on the corridor of the former Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad (the "Katy"). At roughly 240 miles (386 km) between Clinton and Machens, it is one of the longest rail trails in the United States, used by cyclists, hikers, and equestrians.

Route

Much of the trail follows the north bank of the Missouri River, passing through river towns including Rocheport, Boonville, Sedalia, Hermann, and Augusta, before reaching the St. Louis area at St. Charles and Machens. The section along the Missouri River bluffs near Rocheport, with its tunnel and limestone cliffs, is considered the most scenic. A spur connects to Columbia via the MKT Trail.

History

After the Katy railroad abandoned the corridor in 1986, the Missouri Department of Natural Resources acquired it under the federal railbanking programme, with the conversion legally contested by adjoining landowners; the US Supreme Court declined to hear the final challenge. A donation from Edward D. "Ted" Jones and his wife Pat helped fund construction, and the trail opened in stages through the 1990s, with the final segment to Machens completed in 2011.

Use

The trail's crushed-limestone surface supports long-distance touring; annual supported rides such as the Big BAM and the Katy Trail Ride draw thousands of participants. Flooding of the Missouri River periodically damages sections, notably in 2019.
