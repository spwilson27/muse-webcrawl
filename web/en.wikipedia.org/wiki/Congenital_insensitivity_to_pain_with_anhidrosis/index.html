Congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis

Congenital insensitivity to pain with anhidrosis (CIPA), also known as hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathy type IV (HSAN IV), is a rare autosomal recessive disorder of the nervous system characterized by the inability to feel pain and temperature, and by anhidrosis — the absence of sweating. It is caused by mutations in the NTRK1 gene, which encodes the TrkA receptor for nerve growth factor; without this signalling, the small sensory and sympathetic neurons that convey pain and control sweating fail to develop or survive.

Signs and symptoms

Signs usually appear in infancy, often as unexplained fevers: because affected children cannot sweat, they cannot regulate body temperature and may develop dangerous hyperthermia, a leading cause of early death in the condition. Children with CIPA do not react to painful stimuli and so accumulate injuries — burns, fractures, joint damage (including Charcot joints), corneal injuries, and self-inflicted wounds such as biting the tongue, lips, and fingers, especially during teething, which can cause severe oral damage. Intellectual disability of varying degree is common, as are repeated infections of bones and skin from unnoticed wounds.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is suggested clinically by painless injuries and absent sweating, supported by tests showing absent response to intradermal histamine, reduced numbers of unmyelinated nerve fibres on nerve biopsy, and confirmed by genetic testing for NTRK1 mutations. There is no cure; management is preventive and supportive: protective clothing and supervision to avoid injury, regular inspection of skin, eyes, teeth, and joints, prompt treatment of wounds and infections, and careful control of environmental temperature and fever.

Epidemiology

CIPA is extremely rare worldwide. Relatively higher frequencies have been reported in some populations with founder effects, including certain Japanese and Israeli-Bedouin communities. It is one of a family of hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathies (HSAN I–V); the related condition congenital insensitivity to pain without anhidrosis (HSAN V and SCN9A-related disorders) spares sweating.
