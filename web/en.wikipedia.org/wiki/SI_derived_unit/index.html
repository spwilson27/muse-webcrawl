SI derived unit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

SI derived units are units of measurement derived from the seven SI base units specified by the International System of Units (SI). They can be expressed as products of powers of the base units. Some derived units have special names and symbols, such as the hertz (Hz, frequency), newton (N, force), pascal (Pa, pressure), joule (J, energy), watt (W, power), coulomb (C, electric charge), volt (V, electric potential), farad (F, capacitance), ohm (Ω, resistance), siemens (S, conductance), weber (Wb, magnetic flux), tesla (T, magnetic flux density), henry (H, inductance), degree Celsius (°C, temperature), lumen (lm, luminous flux), lux (lx, illuminance), becquerel (Bq, radioactivity), gray (Gy, absorbed dose), sievert (Sv, equivalent dose) and katal (kat, catalytic activity).

Derived units without special names

Many quantities use derived units without special names, for example the square metre (m2) for area, the cubic metre (m3) for volume, the metre per second (m/s) for speed and velocity, and the kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m3) for density.

Supplementary and non-SI units

The radian (rad) and steradian (sr), used for plane and solid angles, are dimensionless derived units that were formerly classed as "supplementary units". Several non-SI units are accepted for use with the SI, including the litre, tonne, minute, hour, day and degree of arc.
