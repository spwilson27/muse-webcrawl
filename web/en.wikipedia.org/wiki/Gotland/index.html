Gotland is the largest island in Sweden and the largest island in the Baltic Sea, lying about 90 km east of the Swedish mainland. Together with nearby islands such as Fårö, it forms Gotland County and Gotland Municipality. The walled medieval town of Visby, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is its main settlement and port.

History

Gotland's wealthy merchant-farmers (the Gutes) grew rich on Baltic trade in the Viking Age and Middle Ages; more Viking silver hoards have been found on the island than anywhere else in Scandinavia. Visby became a leading member of the Hanseatic League until the Danish king Valdemar IV seized the island in 1361 after the Battle of Visby. Gotland passed permanently to Sweden under the Treaty of Brömsebro in 1645. The island preserves an exceptional stock of medieval churches, picture stones, and Visby's 3.4-km ring wall.

Geography and economy

Gotland is a flat limestone plateau with dramatic sea stacks called rauks along its coasts. Its economy rests on tourism (including the annual Medieval Week and the political Almedalen Week in Visby), agriculture, limestone and cement production, and food processing. The island's strategic position in the central Baltic gives it an important military role; Sweden remilitarised Gotland in the 2010s. The traditional language is Gutnish, now generally treated as a dialect of Swedish.
