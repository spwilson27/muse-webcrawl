Attitude and heading reference system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An attitude and heading reference system (AHRS) is a sensor system that provides an aircraft's — or other vehicle's — orientation in three-dimensional space: its attitude (pitch and roll), heading (yaw), and rates of turn. AHRS units are the solid-state successors to traditional gyroscopic flight instruments such as the attitude indicator and directional gyro.

Operation

A typical AHRS combines three-axis accelerometers, rate gyroscopes, and magnetometers (a flux valve or magnetoresistive sensor for magnetic heading). Modern units use MEMS sensors. Sensor data are fused — commonly with Kalman filtering — so that the accelerometers and magnetometers correct the long-term drift of the gyroscopes while the gyros provide smooth short-term response, and so that acceleration in turns and maneuvers does not corrupt the attitude solution.

Applications

AHRS is standard equipment in modern general-aviation glass cockpits, airliners' standby and primary flight displays, unmanned aerial vehicles, and helicopters, and it supplies the orientation data used by autopilots, flight directors, and synthetic vision systems. An AHRS differs from a full inertial navigation system (INS) in that it outputs attitude and heading rather than a self-contained position solution, though the two are often integrated with GNSS receivers in modern avionics suites.
