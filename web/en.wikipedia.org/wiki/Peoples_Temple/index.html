Peoples Temple


Founded: 1955, Indianapolis, Indiana
Founder: Jim Jones
Dissolved: 1978

The Peoples Temple of the Disciples of Christ, commonly shortened to Peoples Temple, was an American new religious movement founded in Indianapolis in 1955 by Jim Jones. The movement combined Christian and socialist ideals, practised racial integration unusual for its era, and grew influential in California politics during the 1970s. It ended in catastrophe on 18 November 1978, when more than 900 of its members died at Jonestown, Guyana, in a mass murder-suicide directed by Jones.

History

Jones established the church in Indianapolis, preaching racial equality and social justice and running community services. In 1965 he moved the congregation to Redwood Valley, California, later expanding to San Francisco and Los Angeles, where the Temple ran soup kitchens, drug rehabilitation, and care homes, and helped elect sympathetic politicians. Behind the public image, former members and journalists reported beatings, humiliation, financial exploitation, and Jones's growing paranoia and addiction.

Jonestown

In 1977, under media scrutiny, Jones relocated about a thousand followers to the Peoples Temple Agricultural Project—"Jonestown"—in the Guyanese jungle. On 18 November 1978, after a visit by Congressman Leo Ryan, Temple gunmen murdered Ryan and four others at a nearby airstrip; Jones then ordered his followers to drink cyanide-laced punch in what he called "revolutionary suicide". 918 people died at Jonestown, at the airstrip, and in Georgetown, including over 300 children—the largest deliberate loss of American civilian life until the September 11 attacks.

Legacy

The events gave rise to the idiom "drinking the Kool-Aid" and remain a landmark case in the study of cults, charismatic authority, and group coercion.
