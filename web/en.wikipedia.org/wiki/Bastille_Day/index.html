Bastille Day is the English name for the national day of France, celebrated on 14 July each year (French: Fête nationale du 14 juillet or simply le 14 juillet). It commemorates the storming of the Bastille on 14 July 1789, a turning point of the French Revolution, as well as the Fête de la Fédération held on the same date in 1790 to celebrate national unity.

Celebrations

The centrepiece is the military parade on the Champs-Élysées in Paris, the oldest and largest regular military parade in Europe, reviewed by the President of the Republic and attended by foreign dignitaries. Across France, towns hold fireworks, public balls — including the traditional firemen's balls (bals des pompiers) — concerts and communal meals.

History

The date was established as the national holiday by law in 1880. The 1989 bicentenary was marked by major celebrations and a G7 summit in Paris; the 2017 parade featured American troops in honour of the centenary of US entry into the First World War.
