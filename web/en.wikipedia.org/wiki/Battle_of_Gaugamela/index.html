The Battle of Gaugamela (gaw-gə-MEE-lə; Ancient Greek: Γαυγάμηλα), also called the Battle of Arbela, took place on 1 October 331 BC between the forces of Alexander the Great of Macedon and Darius III of Persia. It was the decisive battle of Alexander's conquest of the Achaemenid Empire: although outnumbered, Alexander's superior tactics shattered the Persian army, Darius fled the field, and within months Babylon, Susa and Persepolis had fallen.

Background

After his victories at the Granicus (334 BC) and Issus (333 BC), Alexander conquered the Levant and Egypt. Darius, having lost his family and baggage at Issus, assembled a new and much larger army from across his empire, including Bactrian cavalry, Indian troops with elephants, and scythed chariots, and chose a broad, level plain at Gaugamela — which he had levelled and cleared of obstacles — to maximise the effect of his superior numbers and chariots.

Prelude

Alexander's army of about 47,000 approached the plain in late September 331 BC. A lunar eclipse on 20 September was read as an omen favouring the Macedonians. Darius kept his men under arms all night, fearing a night attack, while Alexander let his troops sleep and eat; the exhausted Persian army stood in ranks until dawn.

The battle

Alexander deployed with a slanted, echeloned formation and refused flanks to guard against encirclement. Advancing obliquely to the right, he drew the Persian left-wing cavalry under Bessus away from the centre and neutralised the scythed chariots with skirmishers. When a gap opened in the Persian centre, Alexander wheeled his Companion cavalry and elite infantry into it in a great wedge and charged directly at Darius. As at Issus, Darius turned and fled; his centre collapsed. On the Macedonian left, Parmenion was hard pressed by Mazaeus, but the news of Darius' flight decided the day, and Alexander's pursuit continued until nightfall.

Aftermath

Darius escaped eastward but was murdered by his own satrap Bessus in 330 BC. Alexander entered Babylon, Susa and the ceremonial capital Persepolis, and was proclaimed King of Asia. Gaugamela is regarded as one of the great decisive battles of history and a masterpiece of tactical command.
