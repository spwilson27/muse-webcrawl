Academy Award for Best Actress

The Academy Award for Best Actress is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences for the best performance by an actress in a leading role, first awarded at the 1st Academy Awards in 1929 to Janet Gaynor.


Presented by: AMPAS
First awarded: 1929 (Janet Gaynor)
Most wins: Katharine Hepburn (4)

Records

Katharine Hepburn holds the record with four wins from twelve nominations. Meryl Streep has the most nominations in the category (17, plus supporting nominations). The youngest winner is Marlee Matlin (21, Children of a Lesser God); the oldest is Jessica Tandy (80, Driving Miss Daisy). Recent winners include Emma Stone (Poor Things, 2023 films) and Mikey Madison (Anora, 2024 films).

Selection

Nominees are chosen by Academy actors voting by secret ballot, with the winner selected by the full membership. Performances are eligible in the year of release in Los Angeles County.
