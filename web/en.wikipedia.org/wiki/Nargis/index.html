Nargis

Nargis Dutt (born Fatima Rashid; 1 June 1929 – 3 May 1981), known mononymously as Nargis, was an Indian actress and politician who is widely regarded as one of the greatest actresses in the history of Indian cinema. She starred in numerous commercially successful and critically acclaimed films of the 1940s and 1950s, most famously Mehboob Khan's epic Mother India (1957), which was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Early life and career

Nargis was born in Calcutta to Jaddanbai, a singer, actress and early filmmaker, and Abdul Rashid. She appeared on screen as a child artist, credited as Baby Nargis, before making her adult debut in Talashe Haq (1935) and achieving prominence with Mehboob Khan's Taqdeer (1943). Through the late 1940s she became one of the leading stars of Hindi cinema, appearing opposite Dilip Kumar in Andaz (1949) and forming an iconic screen pairing with Raj Kapoor in films such as Barsaat (1949), Awaara (1951), Shree 420 (1955) and Chori Chori (1956).

Mother India and later work

In Mother India Nargis played Radha, a poor village woman struggling to raise her sons against debt and natural disaster — a performance that became a defining image of Indian womanhood and won her the Filmfare Award for Best Actress. On the film's set a fire broke out and her co-star Sunil Dutt rescued her; they married in 1958, after which she largely retired from acting. She returned briefly in the 1967 film Raat Aur Din, winning the inaugural National Film Award for Best Actress.

Public life and death

With her husband she founded the Ajanta Arts Cultural Troupe, which performed for Indian soldiers, and she was active in charitable work for spastic children. In 1980 she was nominated to the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of India's parliament, but her tenure was cut short by illness. She died of pancreatic cancer on 3 May 1981, aged 51, days before the premiere of her son Sanjay Dutt's debut film. She was awarded the Padma Shri in 1958, and an award for outstanding actress of Indian cinema is named in her honour.
