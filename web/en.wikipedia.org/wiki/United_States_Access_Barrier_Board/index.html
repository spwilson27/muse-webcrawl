United States Access Board

Agency overview
Formed: 1973
Jurisdiction: United States federal government
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.

The United States Access Board (known as the Access Board) is an independent agency of the United States government devoted to accessibility for people with disabilities. The Board develops and maintains design criteria for the built environment, transit vehicles, telecommunications equipment, medical diagnostic equipment, and information technology under several laws, most notably the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and the Architectural Barriers Act (ABA).

History

The Board was created by Section 502 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, originally as the Architectural and Transportation Barriers Compliance Board. Its first task was to ensure compliance with the Architectural Barriers Act of 1968, which required facilities built, altered, or leased with federal funds to be accessible.

The Board's role expanded with the passage of the ADA in 1990, under which it issued the ADA Accessibility Guidelines (ADAAG). It later gained responsibilities under the Telecommunications Act of 1996 and Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act, covering electronic and information technology procured by the federal government.

Structure

The Board consists of 25 members: 13 appointed by the President from the general public, a majority of whom must be individuals with disabilities, and 12 representatives of federal departments and agencies, including the Departments of Transportation, Education, Health and Human Services, and the Department of Veterans Affairs.

Guidelines and standards

Standards issued by the Board include criteria for buildings and sites, streets and sidewalks, passenger vessels, public rights-of-way, and medical diagnostic equipment. Under Section 508, the Board maintains the standards incorporated into federal procurement rules for accessible information and communication technology.
