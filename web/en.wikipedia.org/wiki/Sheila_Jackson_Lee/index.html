Sheila Jackson Lee

Sheila Jackson Lee (née Jackson; February 12, 1950 – July 19, 2024) was an American lawyer and politician who served as the U.S. representative for Texas's 18th congressional district from 1995 until her death in 2024. A member of the Democratic Party, she represented a district anchored in downtown Houston.

Early life and career

Born in Queens, New York, Jackson Lee graduated from Yale University in 1972 and earned her law degree from the University of Virginia School of Law in 1975. After moving to Houston she worked as a lawyer, served as a municipal judge, and sat on the Houston City Council from 1990 to 1994. In 1994 she defeated incumbent Craig Washington in the Democratic primary and won the general election to the House of Representatives.

Congressional career

Jackson Lee sat on the Judiciary and Homeland Security committees and was a prominent voice on civil rights, criminal justice reform and immigration. She was the lead sponsor of the bill that made Juneteenth a federal holiday, enacted in 2021, and for years championed reparations study legislation (H.R. 40). Known for frequent House floor speeches, she was a fixture of the Congressional Black Caucus. In 2023 she ran unsuccessfully for mayor of Houston, losing the runoff to John Whitmire, and then filed for re-election to the House. She died in July 2024, shortly after announcing that she was being treated for pancreatic cancer.
