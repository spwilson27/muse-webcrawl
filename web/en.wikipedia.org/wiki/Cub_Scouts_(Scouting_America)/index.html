Cub Scouts (Scouting America)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cub Scouts is the Scouting America program for children in kindergarten through fifth grade.

Cub Scouts is a program of Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America) serving children in kindergarten through fifth grade (roughly ages 5 to 10). Founded in 1930, it is a family-centered program of activities, outdoor experiences, and character development designed to prepare children for the older Scouts BSA program. Cub Scouts belong to a pack, sponsored by a school, church, or civic organization, and meet in smaller age-based groups called dens. Since 2018, girls as well as boys have been able to join Cub Scouts.

Structure and ranks

Cub Scouts progress through ranks matched to school grades:

  • Lion — kindergarten
  • Tiger — first grade
  • Wolf — second grade
  • Bear — third grade
  • Webelos — fourth grade
  • Arrow of Light — fifth grade, the highest rank in Cub Scouting

To earn each rank, Scouts complete a set of required and elective "adventures" — activities covering citizenship, outdoors skills, fitness, family life, and science — recognized with belt loops and pins. All Cub Scouts first earn the Bobcat badge by learning the Scout Oath, Scout Law, motto ("Do Your Best"), and handshake. The Arrow of Light rank is the only Cub Scout award that can be worn on the Scouts BSA uniform.

Program and activities

Packs hold monthly meetings, outings, and service projects. Signature Cub Scout activities include the Pinewood Derby, in which Scouts race small wooden cars they build with adults; the Raingutter Regatta; campouts and day camps; and community service such as the Scouting for Food drive. Advancement and activities are intended to be done with parents or guardians, in keeping with the program's focus on the family.

History

Cub Scouting was inspired by the Wolf Cubs program Robert Baden-Powell created in Britain in 1916, drawing on themes from Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book. The Boy Scouts of America formally launched Cubbing in 1930 under James E. West, with Ernest Thompson Seton involved in its early design. The program was renamed Cub Scouting in 1949. In the 2010s and 2020s, the organization modernized the program: girls were admitted beginning in 2018, the parent organization rebranded as Scouting America in 2025, and the Cub Scout program received a simplified update of its adventures in 2024.
