Max Mathews

Max Vernon Mathews (November 13, 1926 – April 21, 2011) was an American engineer and pioneer of computer music. At Bell Telephone Laboratories he created the MUSIC series of programs, beginning with MUSIC I in 1957 — the first widely used system for generating sound digitally with a computer.

Career and work

Mathews studied electrical engineering at Caltech and MIT. At Bell Labs, his MUSIC-N programs introduced the concept of unit generators — software modules (oscillators, filters, envelopes) connected to build instruments — an architecture that underlies nearly all later music synthesis software, from Csound to modern digital audio workstations. In 1961 his system produced the famous rendition of "Daisy Bell," sung by an IBM 7094, which inspired the HAL 9000 scene in 2001: A Space Odyssey. With Joan Miller he developed the influential GROOVE system (1970) for real-time performance. Later at Stanford's CCRMA and at IRCAM in Paris, he created the Radio Baton and Conductor Program for expressive gestural control of electronic performance.

Legacy

Mathews is often called the "father of computer music." He was a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the National Academy of Engineering, and received numerous honors, including the IEEE's and the SEAMUS Award for lifetime achievement in electroacoustic music.
