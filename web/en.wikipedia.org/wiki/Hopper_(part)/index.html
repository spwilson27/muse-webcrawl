Hopper (part)


A conical hopper feeding bulk material

A hopper is a large, pyramidal or cone-shaped container used in industrial processes to hold particulate or flowable material, such as grain, coal, or ore, and to discharge it from the bottom. Hoppers are designed so that gravity feeds the contents downward through an outlet, often onto a conveyor belt, into a vehicle, or into another processing vessel.

Design

Hoppers are typically made of steel and have sloping sides that narrow toward a discharge opening. The angle of the walls must be steep enough for the stored material to flow reliably rather than bridge or cling—a problem analysed in bulk-solids handling using measures such as the angle of repose. Discharge may be controlled by gates, rotary valves, vibrating feeders, or screw conveyors. Specialised designs include mass-flow hoppers, which discharge all contents evenly, and funnel-flow hoppers, in which a central channel empties first.

Applications

Hoppers are used in agriculture (grain hoppers and hopper-bottom bins), mining, rail transport (hopper cars), shipping (hopper barges), food processing, pharmaceuticals, and manufacturing, where small feed hoppers supply machines such as plastic injection moulders. Railroad hopper cars, both covered and open, are among the most recognisable applications, carrying bulk commodities and emptying through bottom doors.
