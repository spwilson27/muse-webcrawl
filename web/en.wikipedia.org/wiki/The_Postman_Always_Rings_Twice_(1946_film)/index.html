The Postman Always Rings Twice is a 1946 American film noir directed by Tay Garnett and starring Lana Turner and John Garfield. It is based on James M. Cain's 1934 novel of the same name, and is regarded as a classic of the genre.

Plot

Drifter Frank Chambers takes a job at a roadside diner run by the older Nick Papadakis and falls for Nick's beautiful young wife, Cora. The lovers plot to murder Nick. Their first attempt fails, but a second succeeds, staged as a car accident. A suspicious district attorney nearly secures a conviction, but a legal manoeuvre by their defence lawyer saves them. Though legally free, Frank and Cora are bound together by guilt and mutual distrust. When Cora dies in a genuine car crash, Frank is convicted of her murder — the "postman" of fate, in the film's framing, ringing a second time.

Production and reception

Cain's novel had long been considered unfilmable under the Hollywood Production Code because of its adultery and violence; MGM finally brought it to the screen with alterations. Turner, dressed in white throughout much of the film in a striking visual motif, considered Cora one of her favourite roles. The film was a commercial success and remains one of the defining adaptations of Cain's work, alongside Double Indemnity. A later remake appeared in 1981, directed by Bob Rafelson and starring Jack Nicholson and Jessica Lange.
