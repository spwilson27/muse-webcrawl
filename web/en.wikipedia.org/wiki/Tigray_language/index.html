The Tigray language, commonly known as Tigrinya (ትግርኛ, təgrəñña), is an Ethiopian Semitic language spoken in the Tigray Region of northern Ethiopia and in Eritrea, where it is one of the most widely spoken languages. In Eritrea the language is usually called Tigrinya, and "Tigray" (ትግራይ) is also the name of the Ethiopian region where the closely related Tigrayan population lives. The language is sometimes distinguished in scholarship from the variety spoken by the Tigre people, which is a separate language (see Tigre language).

Geographic distribution

Tigrinya is spoken by the Tigrayan people of Ethiopia's Tigray Region and by the Tigrinya people of Eritrea, particularly in the central highlands around Asmara, as well as by large diaspora communities in Europe, North America, and Israel. In Ethiopia it is the official working language of the Tigray Region; in Eritrea it functions alongside Arabic and English as a working language of government and education.

Phonology and grammar

Tigrinya preserves a set of ejective consonants typical of Ethiopian Semitic languages, and has seven vowels. Like other Semitic languages, it builds words from consonantal roots with templatic vowel patterns. Verbs agree with their subjects in person, number, and gender; word order is generally subject–object–verb. The language retains pharyngeal consonants lost in most related Ethiopian languages except Tigre.

Writing system

Tigrinya is written in the Ge'ez script (ፊደል, fidel), an abugida in which each symbol represents a consonant–vowel syllable. It is written from left to right, and word separation in modern usage is by spaces. Traditional punctuation includes the Ge'ez word separator (፡) and sentence mark (።).

History

Tigrinya descends from the same North Ethiopian Semitic branch as the ancient language Ge'ez, which remains the liturgical language of the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox churches. Inscriptions in early forms related to Tigrinya date to the Aksumite period, but written Tigrinya literature developed mainly from the 19th century onward, with printed newspapers and books appearing under Italian colonial rule in Eritrea and later in Ethiopia.
