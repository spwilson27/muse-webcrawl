Culture of Kazakhstan

Eagle hunting and the yurt remain potent symbols of Kazakh nomadic heritage.

The culture of Kazakhstan grows out of the nomadic Turkic and Mongol traditions of the Eurasian steppe, layered with Persian, Russian and Soviet influences. Kazakhstan is home to more than 130 ethnic groups, and both Kazakh and Russian are widely spoken.

Nomadic heritage

For centuries Kazakh life centered on seasonal migration with herds of sheep, horses, cattle and camels, and on the felt-covered yurt (ui), whose wooden crown, the shanyrak, appears on the national emblem. Hospitality to guests, respect for elders, epic storytelling by akyns (bards) and hunting with golden eagles remain cherished traditions. Traditional sports include kökpar (a contest over a goat carcass), horse racing (baiga) and Kazakh wrestling.

Language and literature

Kazakh, a Kipchak Turkic language, moved from Arabic to Latin to Cyrillic scripts and is now transitioning back to Latin. The poet-philosopher Abai Qunanbaiuly is the towering figure of Kazakh literature; Mukhtar Auezov's novel The Path of Abai is a national classic. Music features the two-stringed dombra and the kobyz, and the works of composer Kurmangazy are national treasures.

Cuisine

Traditional food reflects the nomadic pantry: beshbarmak (boiled meat with flat noodles and onion broth) is the signature dish, alongside qazy (horse sausage), baursaks (fried dough), kumis (fermented mare's milk) and shubat (fermented camel's milk). Tea is served throughout the day.
