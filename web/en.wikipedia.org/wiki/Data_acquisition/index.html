Data acquisition

Data acquisition is the process of sampling signals that measure real-world physical conditions and converting the resulting samples into digital numeric values that can be manipulated by a computer. Data acquisition systems, abbreviated as DAQ or DAS, typically convert analog waveforms into digital values for processing.

Components

A data acquisition system generally consists of three main components:

  • Sensors (transducers), which convert physical parameters such as temperature, pressure, strain, or voltage into electrical signals.
  • Signal conditioning circuitry, which amplifies, filters, isolates, or otherwise prepares sensor signals for digitization.
  • Analog-to-digital converters (ADCs), which convert the conditioned analog signals into digital values, along with a computer and software for control, logging, and analysis.

History

Data acquisition began in the 1960s with dedicated computer systems used in industrial and laboratory settings. The introduction of the personal computer in the 1980s allowed DAQ hardware to be packaged as plug-in boards and external modules, greatly expanding the field. Modern systems may sample from a few channels to thousands of channels at rates from a few samples per second to many megasamples per second.

Applications

Data acquisition is used in scientific research, industrial automation, process control, test and measurement, environmental monitoring, and structural health monitoring. Software environments such as LabVIEW are commonly used to configure and program DAQ systems.
