Antichrist is a 2009 experimental psychological horror film written and directed by Danish filmmaker Lars von Trier. It stars Willem Dafoe and Charlotte Gainsbourg as a couple who retreat to a cabin in the woods after the death of their child.

Plot and themes

As the husband, a therapist, attempts to treat his wife's overwhelming grief, her condition worsens into psychosis and violence. The film explores grief, depression, misogyny, religion and nature's cruelty; von Trier conceived it while hospitalised with depression.

Reception

Premiering at Cannes, where Gainsbourg won Best Actress, Antichrist was notorious for its explicit sex and graphic violence, provoking walkouts and fierce debate, while many critics praised its cinematography and performances.
