Sir Cameron Anthony Mackintosh (born 17 October 1946) is a British theatrical producer, noted for producing many of the most successful musicals of the modern era, including Cats, Les Misérables, The Phantom of the Opera, Miss Saigon and Hamilton (in London). He transformed the business of musical theatre by developing long-running, internationally licensed productions of unprecedented scale.

Career

Mackintosh began in theatre as a stagehand and assistant stage manager before producing touring shows in the late 1960s. His breakthrough came with Andrew Lloyd Webber's Cats in 1981. He then produced the English-language version of Les Misérables (1985) with the Royal Shakespeare Company, Lloyd Webber's The Phantom of the Opera (1986), and Miss Saigon (1989), all of which ran for decades and toured worldwide. Other productions include Mary Poppins (with Disney), Oliver!, My Fair Lady and the London production of Hamilton.

Theatres

Through Delfont Mackintosh Theatres, Mackintosh owns or controls a major group of London West End theatres, including the Prince Edward, Prince of Wales, Novello, Noël Coward, Wyndham's, Gielgud, Apollo, Victoria Palace and Sondheim theatres. He has invested heavily in restoring these venues.

Honours

Mackintosh was knighted in 1996 for services to musical theatre. In 2014 he became the first British producer to be inducted into Broadway's Theater Hall of Fame, and his Cameron Mackintosh Foundation supports theatre education and training.
