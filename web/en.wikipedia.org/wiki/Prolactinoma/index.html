Prolactinoma

A prolactinoma is a benign tumour (adenoma) of the pituitary gland that produces excess amounts of the hormone prolactin. It is the most common type of functioning pituitary tumour. The resulting hyperprolactinaemia suppresses the reproductive hormones, causing symptoms such as irregular or absent menstruation and inappropriate milk production (galactorrhoea) in women, and erectile dysfunction and reduced libido in men; larger tumours (macroadenomas) can also press on nearby structures, notably the optic chiasm, causing visual field defects.

Classification

Prolactinomas are classified by size: microprolactinomas are under 10 mm in diameter and are far more common in women, while macroprolactinomas are 10 mm or larger and occur more equally between the sexes; "giant" prolactinomas exceed 40 mm. Serum prolactin roughly parallels tumour size, and very high levels (e.g. above ~250 µg/L) are strongly suggestive of a prolactinoma rather than other causes of hyperprolactinaemia such as medications or stalk compression (the "stalk effect").

Treatment

Unlike most pituitary tumours, prolactinomas are usually treated medically first. Dopamine agonists — cabergoline preferred, or bromocriptine — normalise prolactin, restore fertility and shrink the tumour in the large majority of patients, including most macroadenomas. Transsphenoidal surgery is reserved for drug intolerance or resistance, apoplexy, cerebrospinal fluid leak, or patient preference (and offers a chance of cure in small, well-circumscribed microadenomas). Radiotherapy is a last resort for aggressive or resistant cases. Some small, asymptomatic microprolactinomas may simply be monitored.
