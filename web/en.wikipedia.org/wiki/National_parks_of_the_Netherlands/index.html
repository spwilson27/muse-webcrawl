National parks of the Netherlands

Number: 21 national parks
First: Veluwezoom (1930, privately established)
Largest: Oosterschelde
Coverage: Around 3–4% of the Netherlands

The Netherlands has 21 national parks, protected landscapes designated by the national government and managed in cooperation with landowners such as Staatsbosbeheer, Natuurmonumenten, provincial landscape trusts and private owners. Because the Dutch landscape is almost entirely cultural, the parks protect heathlands, dunes, peat bogs, tidal flats, forests and polder wetlands rather than wilderness.

List

The parks include De Hoge Veluwe (heath and drift sands, with the Kröller-Müller Museum nearby), Veluwezoom, De Meinweg, De Maasduinen, De Groote Peel, Drents-Friese Wold, Dwingelderveld, Drentsche Aa, Duinen van Texel, Zuid-Kennemerland, Schiermonnikoog, Lauwersmeer, De Alde Feanen, Weerribben-Wieden (the largest freshwater wetland of northwest Europe), De Biesbosch (freshwater tidal delta), Oosterschelde (the largest park, an estuary famous for seals and porpoises), De Loonse en Drunense Duinen, De Zoom–Kalmthoutse Heide (cross-border with Belgium), Utrechtse Heuvelrug, Sallandse Heuvelrug and Nieuw Land (created 2018 around the Oostvaardersplassen).

Policy

From 2013, responsibility for national parks shifted largely to the provinces, and a new national programme has aimed to raise park quality and connect them into a national nature network (the former Ecologische Hoofdstructuur).
