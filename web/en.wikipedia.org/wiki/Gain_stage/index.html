Gain stage

A gain stage is a point within an electronic or audio signal chain at which the signal level is amplified by a discrete stage of circuitry, such as a transistor, vacuum tube, operational amplifier, or a digital gain process. In amplifier design, a single amplifier may consist of several cascaded gain stages, each contributing part of the total voltage or power gain.

In audio engineering, the term is most often used in the context of gain staging: the practice of setting the output level of each device or process in a signal chain so that every stage operates within its optimal range — high enough above its noise floor to give a good signal-to-noise ratio, but below the point at which it begins to clip or distort. Proper gain staging through a recording or mixing chain preserves headroom and minimises accumulated noise.

In guitar amplifiers

In guitar amplification, cascading multiple preamplifier gain stages is the classic method of producing high-gain distortion: the output of one stage overdrives the next, generating harmonics and sustain. Many modern high-gain amplifiers use four or more preamp gain stages for this purpose.

In recording and mixing

Gain staging applies at microphone preamplifiers, channel strips, plug-ins, mix buses and mastering processors. In digital systems, where internal processing is often floating-point, gain staging matters most at the analogue-to-digital and digital-to-analogue boundaries and at fixed-point plug-ins that model analogue saturation.
