Cha chaan teng

A typical cha chaan teng interior

A cha chaan teng (Chinese: 茶餐廳; Cantonese Yale: chàh chāan tēng; literally "tea restaurant") is a type of inexpensive restaurant commonly found in Hong Kong, Macau, and parts of Guangdong. They are known for eclectic and affordable menus, which include dishes from Hong Kong cuisine and Hong Kong-style Western cuisine. They originated from bing sutt (冰室), a type of inexpensive restaurant that served only drinks and ice cream.

Cha chaan tengs are also commonly found in overseas Chinatowns and in Chinese diaspora communities in countries such as Canada, the United States, and Australia, where they serve as informal gathering places for Hong Kong immigrants.

History

Cha chaan tengs emerged in Hong Kong after the Second World War, as local cooks adapted Western dishes to local tastes and budgets. At the time, Western restaurants were expensive and considered exclusive; cha chaan tengs offered affordable versions of Western food, such as pork chops, baked rice dishes, and French toast, prepared with Cantonese ingredients and techniques. The result is a distinctive fusion cuisine, sometimes called "soy sauce Western cuisine" (豉油西餐).

By the 1950s and 1960s, cha chaan tengs had become a fixture of working-class dining in Hong Kong, serving factory workers, office staff, and families. Their fast service, long opening hours, and low prices made them an enduring part of the city's food culture.

Menu

The menu of a cha chaan teng typically spans several categories:

  • Drinks: Hong Kong-style milk tea, coffee, yin yang (a mixture of coffee and milk tea), lemon tea, and Horlicks or Ovaltine drinks.
  • Breakfast sets: macaroni in soup with ham, instant noodles, eggs, and toast.
  • Baked dishes: baked pork chop rice, baked spaghetti Bolognese, and Swiss-sauce chicken wings.
  • Fried rice and noodles: Yangzhou fried rice, beef chow fun, and Singapore-style noodles.
  • Snacks: Hong Kong-style French toast served with butter and condensed milk, pineapple buns, and egg tarts.

Many cha chaan tengs offer "tea sets" (下午茶餐) in the afternoon, with discounted combinations of a drink and a snack.

Culture

Cha chaan tengs are noted for their brisk, no-frills service and cramped seating, where customers often share tables with strangers. Ordering is fast-paced, and waiters traditionally record orders in abbreviated shorthand. The establishments are widely regarded as a symbol of Hong Kong identity and everyday life, frequently appearing in Hong Kong films and television dramas.
