The Senhouse Roman Museum is an archaeological museum at Maryport in Cumbria, England, situated beside the remains of the Roman fort of Alauna, at the western end of the Hadrian's Wall frontier system. It houses the Netherhall collection, one of the oldest archaeological collections in Britain, assembled by the Senhouse family from the 1570s onwards.

Collection

The museum is renowned for its extraordinary series of Roman altars — the largest group of Roman altars from a single site in Britain. Most were dedicated to Jupiter Optimus Maximus by the commanders of the First Cohort of Spaniards (Cohors I Hispanorum), the auxiliary regiment garrisoned at Alauna, and were renewed at annual ceremonies. In 1870 a remarkable cache of seventeen altars was found buried in pits near the fort, a discovery long interpreted as ritual burial of the old altars each year; a major excavation in 2011 re-examined the pits and the temples associated with them.

The collection also includes inscribed stones, sculpture, and finds from the civilian settlement (vicus) outside the fort, interpreting the lives of soldiers and civilians on Rome's north-western frontier.
