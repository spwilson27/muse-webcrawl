Player character

A player character (also known as a playable character or PC) is a character in a game who is controlled by one of the players, in contrast to a non-player character (NPC) controlled by a gamemaster or by the game's software. The term originated in tabletop role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons and was carried into video games, where the player character is the avatar through which the player acts on the game world.

The player character is defined by a set of statistics and abilities recorded on a character sheet or in a game's character screen.

In role-playing games

In tabletop role-playing games, each player typically creates and portrays one player character, defined by attributes, skills and background, often summarised on a character sheet. The character is generated by a character creation process that may involve choosing a species or race, a class or profession, and assigning ability scores. Through play the character gains experience and advances, acquiring new abilities. The gamemaster controls all other characters and adjudicates the effects of the player characters' actions.

In video games

Video games offer several models of player character:

  • Fixed protagonists — a single predefined character with an established personality, such as Lara Croft or Geralt of Rivia.
  • Customisable characters — created by the player in a character editor, common in role-playing games and MMORPGs.
  • Silent protagonists — characters who do not speak, intended as a blank canvas for the player.
  • Multiple playable characters — games in which the player switches between or assembles a party of characters.
  • Avatar/unit control — in strategy and simulation games the "player character" may be diffuse, consisting of many units.
