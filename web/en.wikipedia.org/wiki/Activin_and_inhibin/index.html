Activin and inhibin

Activin and inhibin are two closely related protein complexes belonging to the transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) superfamily that have almost directly opposing effects on reproductive endocrinology: inhibin suppresses, and activin stimulates, the synthesis and secretion of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) by the pituitary gland.

Structure

Both are dimers of disulfide-linked subunits. Inhibins combine an α subunit with one of two β subunits (inhibin A: αβA; inhibin B: αβB), while activins consist of two β subunits (activin A: βAβA; activin B: βBβB; activin AB: βAβB).

Function

Inhibin is produced by the gonads — Sertoli cells in males and granulosa cells in females — and provides negative feedback on FSH. Activins are produced in many tissues and have wide-ranging roles in cell proliferation, differentiation, wound healing, inflammation and embryonic patterning, including the induction of mesoderm in early development.
