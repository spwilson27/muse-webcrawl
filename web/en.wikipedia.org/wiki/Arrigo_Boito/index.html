Arrigo Boito (24 February 1842 – 10 June 1918) was an Italian composer, poet and librettist. He is best remembered for his opera Mefistofele, based on Goethe's Faust, and for the libretti he supplied to Giuseppe Verdi for Otello (1887) and Falstaff (1893), works regarded as among the finest in the operatic repertoire.


Boito in later life

Early life and the Scapigliatura

Born in Padua, Boito studied at the Milan Conservatory. As a young man he was a leading figure of the Scapigliatura, the bohemian movement that sought to renew Italian art, and his early polemics against the conventions of Italian opera brought him into conflict with the establishment — including, initially, Verdi.

Mefistofele

Boito's own opera Mefistofele was premiered at La Scala in 1868 and failed disastrously in an overlong first version. Extensively revised and shortened, it triumphed at Bologna in 1875 and has remained in the repertoire, notable for its Prologue in Heaven and the title role's set pieces. His second opera, Nerone, was left unfinished at his death and completed by others for a 1924 premiere.

Collaboration with Verdi

Reconciled with Verdi through the publisher Giulio Ricordi, Boito revised the libretto of Simon Boccanegra (1881) and then created the texts of Otello and Falstaff, distilling Shakespeare with exceptional economy and poetic force. He also wrote libretti for Ponchielli (La Gioconda), Catalani and Faccio.
