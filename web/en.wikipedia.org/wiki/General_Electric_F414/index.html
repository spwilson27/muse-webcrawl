The General Electric F414 is an American afterburning turbofan jet engine in the 22,000 lbf (98 kN) thrust class, produced by GE Aerospace. Developed from the successful F404 engine of the F/A-18 Hornet, the F414 powers the larger F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and the EA-18G Growler electronic-warfare aircraft.

Development

The F414 grew out of GE's F404-GE-402 and studies for the cancelled A-12 Avenger II's F412 engine. Retaining the F404's basic architecture, it has a larger fan, delivering about 35% more thrust while fitting a similar footprint, with full-authority digital engine control (FADEC) and improved materials for durability and lower maintenance.

Variants and applications

  • F414-GE-400 — the standard Super Hornet engine.
  • F414G — single-engine variant for the Saab JAS 39 Gripen E/F, built with Swedish participation.
  • F414-GE-INS6 — selected for India's HAL Tejas Mk2 and AMCA programmes, with planned licensed production in India.
  • Enhanced Performance Engine (EPE) — proposed growth version offering up to about 20% more thrust.

More than 1,600 F414 engines had been delivered by the mid-2020s, accumulating several million flight hours with the U.S. Navy and export operators including Australia, Kuwait and Brazil (Gripen).
