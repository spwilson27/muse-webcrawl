Jamsetji Tata

Born: 3 March 1839, Navsari, British India
Died: 19 May 1904, Bad Nauheim, Germany
Known for: Founder of the Tata Group

Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata (3 March 1839 – 19 May 1904) was an Indian industrialist and philanthropist who founded the Tata Group, India's largest conglomerate, and laid the foundations of much of modern Indian industry. He is widely regarded as the "Father of Indian Industry".

Career

Born into a Parsi Zoroastrian family in Navsari, Gujarat, Tata joined his father's trading firm in Bombay and, after graduating from Elphinstone College, travelled extensively for the business. In 1868 he founded a trading company of his own. He built successful cotton mills — including the Empress Mills at Nagpur (1877) — introducing modern technology and comparatively progressive labour practices such as pensions.

Vision and projects

Tata conceived four great projects for India's development: a steel plant, a world-class institution of science education, a hydroelectric power scheme, and a grand hotel. Of these, only the Taj Mahal Hotel in Bombay (opened 1903) was completed in his lifetime. The others were realised by his successors: the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore (1909), Tata Steel at Sakchi — later named Jamshedpur in his honour (1907) — and Tata Hydroelectric power in the Western Ghats (1910s).

Philanthropy and legacy

Tata endowed scholarships that enabled Indians to study abroad, and the trusts created by his family remain among India's leading philanthropic institutions. The city of Jamshedpur bears his name, and his belief that industry should serve national development continues to shape the Tata Group's identity.
