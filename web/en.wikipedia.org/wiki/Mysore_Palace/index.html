Mysore Palace

Mysore Palace (Amba Vilas)

Location: Mysuru, Karnataka, India

Architect: Henry Irwin

Built: 1897–1912

Style: Indo-Saracenic

Mysore Palace illuminated, as seen during Dasara.

Mysore Palace, officially Amba Vilas Palace, is a historical palace and royal residence in Mysuru, Karnataka, India. It was the seat of the Wadiyar dynasty, the maharajas of the Kingdom of Mysore, and is one of India's most famous tourist attractions, drawing several million visitors a year — second only to the Taj Mahal. The present building, in Indo-Saracenic style with Hindu, Mughal, Rajput and Gothic elements, was designed by the British architect Henry Irwin.

History

A palace has stood on the site within the old fort since the 14th century and was repeatedly rebuilt. The mostly wooden predecessor burned down in 1896 during a royal wedding. The regent queen Kempananjammanni Devi commissioned a new palace, built between 1897 and 1912 at a cost of about 4.1 million rupees. The three-storey stone building is topped by a five-storey, 145-foot tower with a gilded dome.

Description

Highlights include the Kalyana Mantapa (marriage pavilion) with its stained glass and glazed tile floors, the ornate Durbar Hall, the Ambavilasa hall with carved rosewood doors inlaid with ivory, and the golden throne displayed during Dasara. Paintings by Raja Ravi Varma and others decorate the walls. The palace is illuminated with around 97,000 bulbs on Sundays, public holidays and throughout the Dasara festival, and the grounds contain twelve Hindu temples. The royal family retains a private residential wing. See also Mysore.
