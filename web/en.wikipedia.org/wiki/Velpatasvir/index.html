Velpatasvir

ClassNS5A inhibitor
UseHepatitis C treatment
CombinationWith sofosbuvir (Epclusa)
LicenceApproved 2016 (US)

Velpatasvir is a direct-acting antiviral medication used to treat hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection. It inhibits the viral NS5A protein, which is essential for viral replication and assembly.

Medical use

Velpatasvir is marketed in a fixed-dose combination tablet with the nucleotide polymerase inhibitor sofosbuvir under the brand name Epclusa. The combination was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2016 and was the first regimen able to treat all six major genotypes of hepatitis C with a single once-daily tablet. A typical course lasts 12 weeks, and cure rates (sustained virologic response) in clinical trials generally exceeded 95%. A three-drug combination adding voxilaprevir (Vosevi) is used for some patients who have previously failed NS5A-containing therapy.

Mechanism and interactions

By binding NS5A, velpatasvir blocks both viral RNA replication and the assembly of new virions. The drug is a substrate of drug transporters and its absorption is reduced by acid-suppressing medicines; co-administration with strong inducers of CYP enzymes and transporters, such as rifampin and St John's wort, is not recommended because it lowers velpatasvir levels.
