In mathematics, a Young tableau (plural: tableaux) is a combinatorial object useful in representation theory and algebraic geometry. It provides a convenient way to describe the group representations of the symmetric and general linear groups and to study their properties. A Young tableau is a filling of a Young diagram — the left-justified rows of boxes whose lengths form a partition — with numbers or other entries subject to ordering conditions.

Definitions

A tableau is semistandard if its entries increase weakly along rows and strictly down columns; it is standard if the entries are exactly the numbers 1, …, n, each used once, increasing across rows and down columns. For example, a standard Young tableau of shape (5,4,1) is:

1 3 4 6 10
2 5 7 8
9

Counting: the hook-length formula

The number of standard Young tableaux of a given shape is given by the Frame–Robinson–Thrall hook-length formula, which divides n! by the product of the hook lengths of all boxes in the diagram. Counts of semistandard tableaux are given by the hook-content formula, and, for a fixed bound on entries, equal the dimensions of Schur polynomials and of corresponding representations of the general linear group.

Applications

Young tableaux index the irreducible representations of the symmetric group (via Young symmetrizers) and basis elements of tensor representations of GL(n). The Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence uses pairs of tableaux of the same shape to encode permutations and matrices, connecting tableau combinatorics to the longest increasing subsequence problem. Skew tableaux, jeu de taquin, and the Littlewood–Richardson rule govern how representations decompose under restriction and tensor products.
