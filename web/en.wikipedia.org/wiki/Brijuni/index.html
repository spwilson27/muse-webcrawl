Brijuni
Country: Croatia
County: Istria
Area: about 7 km² (main islands)

Brijuni (Italian: Brioni) is a group of fourteen small islands in the Croatian part of the northern Adriatic Sea, off the coast of Istria near Pula. The two largest islands are Veliki Brijun and Mali Brijun. The archipelago is known for Brijuni National Park, established in 1983, and for its history as the summer residence of Yugoslav president Josip Broz Tito.

History

The islands were settled in Roman and Byzantine times and later belonged to Venice and Austria. In the late 19th century the industrialist Paul Kupelwieser developed them into an elite resort. After 1945 Tito used Veliki Brijun as his summer residence, hosting many world leaders there; the 1956 Brioni Meeting with Nehru and Nasser helped found the Non-Aligned Movement.

National park

Brijuni National Park features Mediterranean flora, a safari park with exotic animals gifted to Tito, Roman villa ruins at Verige Bay, and dinosaur footprints preserved in limestone.
