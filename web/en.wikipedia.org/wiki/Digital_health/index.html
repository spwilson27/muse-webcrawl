Digital health is the use of digital technologies to improve health and healthcare delivery. It is a broad umbrella covering electronic health records (EHRs), telemedicine and telehealth, mobile health (mHealth) apps, wearable sensors, remote patient monitoring, health information exchanges, and the application of artificial intelligence and big-data analytics to clinical and public-health problems.

Scope

The World Health Organization defines digital health as "the field of knowledge and practice associated with the development and use of digital technologies to improve health", encompassing eHealth and newer areas such as the Internet of Things, advanced computing and robotics. Uses include chronic-disease management, mental-health support apps, digital therapeutics (software delivering evidence-based interventions, sometimes regulated as medical devices), epidemic surveillance, and consumer wellness tracking.

Benefits and challenges

Advocates cite wider access to care (especially in rural and low-resource settings), earlier detection, patient empowerment, and efficiency gains; the COVID-19 pandemic greatly accelerated telehealth adoption. Criticisms and challenges include data privacy and security risks, interoperability failures between systems, the digital divide that can exclude elderly, poor or low-literacy patients, uncertain clinical evidence for many consumer health apps, and regulatory questions about software as a medical device.
