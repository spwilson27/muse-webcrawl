Sweden and the euro

[Image placeholder: Swedish krona and euro]

Sweden does not use the euro as its currency, retaining the Swedish krona, despite being a member of the European Union since 1995. Under its EU accession treaty Sweden is obliged to adopt the euro once it meets the convergence criteria, but the Swedish government has maintained that joining the Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM II), itself a prerequisite, is voluntary — and Sweden has deliberately avoided joining it.

Referendum of 2003

On 14 September 2003, Sweden held a referendum on adopting the euro. The "no" side won with about 56 per cent of the vote against roughly 42 per cent in favour, on a turnout of about 83 per cent. The result was respected by all major parties, and the question was shelved for the foreseeable future. The campaign was overshadowed by the assassination of Foreign Minister Anna Lindh, a prominent pro-euro campaigner, days before the vote.

Convergence status

Sweden generally meets most of the economic convergence criteria — on inflation, public finances, and long-term interest rates — but fails the exchange-rate criterion because the krona does not participate in ERM II. Successive convergence reports by the European Commission and the European Central Bank have noted this as the formal obstacle.

Public opinion

Support for euro adoption in opinion polls has usually been well below opposition, rising only briefly during periods of krona weakness. Most major political parties have agreed not to pursue the issue without a new referendum.

Coins

Because Sweden is expected eventually to adopt the euro, tentative discussion of Swedish euro coin designs has occurred, but no official designs have been selected.
