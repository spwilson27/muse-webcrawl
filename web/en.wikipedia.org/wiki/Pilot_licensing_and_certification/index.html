Pilot licensing and certification

Pilot licensing and certification covers the regulatory systems used worldwide to qualify pilots. Most systems follow the standards of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), while individual authorities — such as the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) in the United States and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) in Europe — administer examinations, flight tests, and medical certification.

International framework

ICAO Annex 1, Personnel Licensing, sets out minimum standards for licences including the private pilot licence (PPL), commercial pilot licence (CPL), multi-crew pilot licence (MPL), and airline transport pilot licence (ATPL). Ratings for instrument flight, instructor duties, and specific aircraft types are added to a base licence.

United States system

The FAA issues pilot certificates rather than licences; they do not expire, though currency requirements and medical certificates limit their use. Grades run from student and sport pilot through private, commercial, and airline transport pilot, with certificates also issued for flight instructors and remote (drone) pilots under Part 107.

European system

EASA licences are issued by member states under Part-FCL. They include the light aircraft pilot licence (LAPL), PPL, CPL, and ATPL, and are maintained through revalidation of class and type ratings and periodic medical examinations.

Medical certification

All systems require pilots to hold medical certificates graded to their privileges — most stringent for airline transport pilots — covering vision, hearing, cardiovascular health, and mental fitness.
