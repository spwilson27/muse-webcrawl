Biblical hermeneutics

Biblical hermeneutics is the study of the principles of interpretation concerning the books of the Bible. It is part of the broader field of hermeneutics, which involves the study of principles of interpretation for all forms of communication, both verbal and nonverbal. Biblical hermeneutics asks how the meaning of an ancient sacred text is to be discerned, and how that meaning applies to later readers.

Approaches

Literal and grammatical-historical interpretation

The grammatical-historical method seeks the meaning intended by the original author by attending to grammar, genre, and historical context. It dominated evangelical interpretation and underlies much expository preaching.

Allegorical interpretation

From the early church, interpreters such as Origen found spiritual senses beneath the literal text. The medieval quadriga distinguished four senses: literal, allegorical, moral (tropological), and anagogical.

Historical-critical interpretation

Building on biblical criticism, the historical-critical method uses source, form, and redaction analysis to reconstruct what the text meant in its original setting.

Canonical and theological interpretation

Canonical interpretation reads individual passages in light of the whole Bible as a unified canon, while theological interpretation of Scripture emphasizes the church's rule of faith as the proper context for reading.

The hermeneutical circle

Interpreters note a "hermeneutical circle": readers understand the parts of a text through the whole, and the whole through its parts, while their own pre-understanding shapes what they find. Philosophers such as Friedrich Schleiermacher, Wilhelm Dilthey, and Hans-Georg Gadamer developed this insight within general hermeneutics.
