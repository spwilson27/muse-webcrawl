DC Films was an American film production label of Warner Bros. Pictures dedicated to films based on characters from DC Comics. Created in 2016 in the wake of Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice, it oversaw the DC Extended Universe (DCEU) and standalone DC features until it was replaced by DC Studios in 2022.

History

DC Films was established in May 2016, with Geoff Johns and Jon Berg appointed to lead the division and bring greater creative cohesion to DC's film slate, which had begun with Man of Steel (2013). Following the troubled production and reception of Justice League (2017), Berg departed and Walter Hamada became president of DC Films in January 2018, steering the franchise towards a mix of connected and standalone films.

Under Hamada the label released successes including Aquaman (2018), Shazam! (2019), the standalone Joker (2019) and The Batman (2022), alongside DCEU entries such as Wonder Woman 1984, The Suicide Squad and Black Adam. Hamada also pursued direct-to-streaming films for HBO Max, including a planned Batgirl film that was controversially cancelled in 2022.

Dissolution

After the Warner Bros. Discovery merger in 2022, Hamada departed, and in October 2022 James Gunn and Peter Safran were appointed heads of the newly created DC Studios, which absorbed DC Films' role across film, television and animation, and began a rebooted DC Universe. DC Films ceased to exist as a separate label.
