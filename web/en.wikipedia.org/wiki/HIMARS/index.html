HIMARS

The M142 HIMARS (High Mobility Artillery Rocket System) is a light multiple launch rocket system developed in the late 1990s for the United States Army and manufactured by Lockheed Martin Missiles and Fire Control. Mounted on a standard 6×6 truck chassis, it carries a single pod of six GPS-guided rockets or one tactical ballistic missile, and can be transported by C-130 aircraft.

Design and armament

HIMARS uses the same launch pods and ammunition family as the tracked M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System, including GMLRS rockets with a range of about 70–84 km, extended-range variants exceeding 150 km, and the MGM-140 ATACMS missile with a range of up to about 300 km; the newer Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) is also being integrated. The wheeled chassis gives the system greater road speed and lower operating cost than the M270 at the price of half the ready firepower, and its "shoot-and-scoot" mobility is central to its survivability.

Service

HIMARS entered U.S. service in 2005 and was used in Afghanistan and against Islamic State in Iraq and Syria. It gained international prominence after the United States supplied systems to Ukraine from June 2022, where GMLRS strikes on command posts and logistics hubs were widely reported as a significant factor in the Russo-Ukrainian War. Export operators include several NATO and partner nations, and orders expanded sharply after 2022.
