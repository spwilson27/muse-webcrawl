King Clone

King Clone is the oldest known creosote bush (Larrea tridentata) clone in the Mojave Desert of California. Located on protected state land near Lucerne Valley, the ring of shrubs is estimated to be 11,700 years old, making it one of the oldest living organisms on Earth.

Description

The clone takes the form of a ring roughly 14 metres (46 feet) across. As a creosote bush grows, older branches in the centre die while new stems are produced outward; over millennia the ring expands and the genetic individual persists as genetically identical stems. The age estimate was produced by Frank Vasek of the University of California, Riverside, by measuring the rate of growth of the ring and extrapolating its radius, an estimate supported by radiocarbon dating of dead wood from the centre.

Significance

King Clone was identified and studied in the 1970s. Because the site is vulnerable to disturbance, the land around it was protected, and the exact location is kept relatively discreet. Clonal plants such as King Clone, the Pando aspen grove in Utah, and Old Tjikko in Sweden challenge ordinary notions of individual lifespan, persisting through vegetative reproduction for thousands of years.
