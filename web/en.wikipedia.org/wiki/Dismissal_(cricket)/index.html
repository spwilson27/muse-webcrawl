Dismissal (cricket)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In cricket, a dismissal occurs when a batter's innings is brought to an end by the opposing team. Other terms used are the batter being out, the batting side losing a wicket, and the fielding side taking a wicket. The ball becomes dead (no runs can be scored and no further wickets can fall) and the dismissed batter must leave the field of play for the rest of their team's innings, to be replaced by a teammate who has not yet batted.

A team innings is ended when ten of the eleven players have been dismissed, known as being "all out". A batter who retires hurt is counted as "not out" and may return later in the innings.

Methods of dismissal

Under the Laws of Cricket there are ten ways a batter can be dismissed, of which five are common and five are rare:

  • Caught – a fielder catches the ball after it is struck by the bat (or glove holding the bat) before it bounces.
  • Bowled – the bowler delivers a ball that hits the stumps and puts down the wicket.
  • Leg before wicket (LBW) – the ball would have hit the stumps but is intercepted by the batter's body.
  • Run out – a fielder puts down the wicket while the batters are running between the wickets.
  • Stumped – the wicket-keeper puts down the wicket while the striker is out of their crease after stepping forward to play a delivery.
  • Hit wicket – the striker dislodges their own wicket with bat or body while playing a shot or setting off for a run.
  • Hit the ball twice – the striker wilfully strikes the ball a second time, other than to guard their wicket.
  • Obstructing the field – the batter wilfully obstructs or distracts the fielding side.
  • Timed out – an incoming batter takes too long to be ready to face or for their partner to be ready.
  • Retired out – a batter retires without the umpire's permission for a reason other than injury and does not resume.

Scoring notation

On a scorecard, dismissals are recorded in forms such as "c Smith b Jones" (caught Smith, bowled Jones), "b Jones" for bowled, "lbw b Jones" for leg before wicket, and "run out (Smith)" for a run out. Caught is by far the most common method at the professional level, followed by bowled and LBW.
