An intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) is a class of black hole with mass significantly more than stellar-mass black holes (a few tens of solar masses) but less than supermassive black holes (millions to billions of solar masses) — roughly 102 to 105 solar masses. They are hypothesised to form by runaway mergers of stars and black holes in dense clusters, or as remnants of the first stars.

Observational evidence

Candidates include ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs) such as HLX-1 in the galaxy ESO 243-49, whose luminosity suggests a black hole of ~104 solar masses, and dense central objects in globular clusters such as Omega Centauri and 47 Tucanae, whose stellar dynamics hint at central masses of thousands of solar masses, though the evidence remains debated. In 2020 the gravitational-wave event GW190521 produced a remnant of about 142 solar masses — the first clearly detected black hole in the intermediate-mass range formed by a merger.

Formation

Proposed channels include the direct collapse of massive Population III stars, repeated mergers of stellar black holes retained in globular clusters, and the collapse of dense star clusters. IMBHs are candidate "seeds" from which supermassive black holes grew by accretion and mergers in the early universe.
