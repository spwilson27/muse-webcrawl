First Battle of El Alamein

The First Battle of El Alamein (1–27 July 1942) was a battle of the Western Desert campaign of the Second World War, fought in Egypt between Axis forces (Panzer Army Africa, commanded by Erwin Rommel) and Allied forces (the Eighth Army, commanded by Claude Auchinleck). The British prevented a second advance by Rommel into Egypt; the battle halted the Axis advance towards Alexandria, although at its end the front stabilised in positions that would be attacked again in August and decisively in October–November 1942.

Background

After the fall of Tobruk in June 1942, Rommel pursued the Eighth Army deep into Egypt. The El Alamein position — a roughly 40-mile front between the Mediterranean and the impassable Qattara Depression — could not be turned from the south, making it the last defensible line before Alexandria and the Nile Delta.

The battle

Rommel attacked on 1 July but failed to break through; Auchinleck then counter-attacked repeatedly through July, striking especially at the Italian formations. Battles around Ruweisat Ridge and Tel el Eisa, in which New Zealand, Australian, Indian and South African troops played leading parts, wore down the Panzer Army, whose supply lines were stretched to breaking point. By late July both sides were exhausted and the front congealed.

Aftermath

Stopping Rommel at El Alamein was a strategic turning point in the desert war: the Axis never again advanced in Egypt. Auchinleck was nevertheless replaced in August by Harold Alexander, with Bernard Montgomery taking command of the Eighth Army; Montgomery repelled Rommel at Alam el Halfa and won the Second Battle of El Alamein in October–November 1942.
