Narciso Martínez (October 29, 1911 – June 30, 1992) was a Mexican-American accordionist regarded as the father of Texas-Mexican conjunto music. Nicknamed 'El Huracán del Valle' ('The Hurricane of the Valley'), Martínez began recording in the 1930s and established the classic conjunto sound built around the diatonic button accordion paired with the bajo sexto, a twelve-string bass guitar.

Early life and career

Martínez was born in Reynosa, Tamaulipas, Mexico, and raised in La Paloma, Texas, in the Rio Grande Valley. He took up the accordion as a young man, learning from German and Czech polka traditions that circulated in South Texas, and began performing at dances and local events.

In 1936 he made his first recordings for the Bluebird label, including the polka 'La Chicharronera', which became a hit and launched his recording career.

Style and influence

Martínez's innovation was to strip the accordion ensemble down to a lean, danceable format: he played the melodic treble side of the accordion while Santiago Almeida's bajo sexto supplied rhythm and bass, omitting the accordion's bass buttons entirely. This template shaped conjunto for generations of musicians, including Tony De La Rosa and Flaco Jiménez.

Working day jobs for much of his life — including as a truck driver and at the Gladys Porter Zoo in Brownsville — Martínez continued recording for labels such as Ideal and was rediscovered by folk audiences in the 1970s and 1980s.

Recognition

Martínez was inducted into the Conjunto Music Hall of Fame in 1982 and received a National Heritage Fellowship from the National Endowment for the Arts in 1983, among other honours recognising his foundational role in Mexican-American music.
