Loch Fyne

Loch Fyne is a sea loch on the west coast of Argyll and Bute, Scotland. Extending about 65 kilometres (40 miles) inland from the Firth of Clyde, it is the longest of Scotland's sea lochs. The name derives from Gaelic, traditionally linked to Loch Fìne, "loch of the vine/wine" or "loch of the heroes".

Geography

The loch's mouth lies between the Cowal peninsula and the Kintyre peninsula, and it reaches north-eastward to its head near Cairndow. Towns and villages on its shores include Inveraray, the planned town built by the Dukes of Argyll in the eighteenth century, as well as Furnace, Lochgilphead, Ardrishaig, and Tarbert (where the loch comes within a short distance of the Firth of Clyde side at West Loch Tarbert, near the northern end of the Crinan Canal).

Economy and wildlife

Loch Fyne is noted for its seafood, especially its oysters and herring: the "Loch Fyne herring" was historically a prized fishery, and the name survives in a well-known chain of seafood restaurants founded from an oyster farm on the loch's shore. The waters support salmon farming, scallop diving, and populations of seals, otters, porpoises, and seabirds, and the surrounding hills are home to red deer.
