London Museum of Water & Steam

The standpipe tower at Kew Bridge, the museum's landmark

The London Museum of Water & Steam is an independent museum founded in 1975 as the Kew Bridge Steam Museum. It is located at the site of the old Kew Bridge Pumping Station in Brentford, London, on the north bank of the River Thames near Kew Bridge.

The site

The Kew Bridge waterworks were opened in 1838 by the Grand Junction Waterworks Company, which pumped Thames water to supply west London. The site preserves a collection of magnificent Cornish beam engines and rotative engines that once pumped drinking water to millions of Londoners. The most striking structure is the Italianate standpipe tower of 1867, a 65-metre campanile-style tower that is a local landmark and Grade I listed.

Collection

The museum houses what is believed to be the largest collection of working Cornish beam engines in the world, including the Dancers End engine and the Grand Junction 90-inch engine of 1846 and the 100-inch engine of 1869 — the largest working engine of its type. On steam days the engines are operated for visitors. The museum also displays smaller steam engines, diesel engines, a waterworks railway, and interactive exhibits telling the story of London's water supply from Roman times to the present day.

History of the museum

The pumping station's steam engines were retired by the late 1960s and early 1970s; volunteers formed a preservation trust and opened the steam museum in 1975. In 2014, following a major redevelopment, it relaunched under its present name with renewed galleries supported by heritage lottery funding.
