The State Emblem of India is the national emblem of the Republic of India and is used by the Government of India and its agencies. It is an adaptation of the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath, which was erected by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE.

Description

The emblem shows four Asiatic lions standing back to back (three visible in the two-dimensional depiction), symbolising power, courage, confidence and faith. The lions stand on a circular abacus carved with the figures of an elephant, a galloping horse, a bull and a lion, separated by chariot wheels representing the Dharmachakra. Below the capital is inscribed the motto Satyameva Jayate ("Truth Alone Triumphs") in Devanagari script, taken from the Mundaka Upanishad.

History and use

The emblem was adopted on 26 January 1950, the day India became a republic. The original Lion Capital is preserved in the Sarnath Museum. The use of the emblem is regulated by the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005. It appears on Indian currency, passports, official seals and government documents.
