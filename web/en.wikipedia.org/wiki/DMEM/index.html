DMEM

Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) is a standard cell culture medium widely used to support the growth of many different mammalian cell types in research laboratories. It is a modification of Basal Medium Eagle (BME), containing a higher concentration of amino acids and vitamins, and was developed by Renato Dulbecco and Marguerite Vogt.

Composition

The original formulation contains 1,000 mg/L of glucose and was first reported for culturing mouse embryonic cells. Later formulations with 4,500 mg/L glucose ("high glucose") were developed for other applications. DMEM contains inorganic salts, amino acids at roughly four times the concentration of BME, vitamins, glucose, and the pH indicator phenol red; it is buffered with sodium bicarbonate and requires a carbon-dioxide-enriched atmosphere (typically 5–10% CO2) to maintain physiological pH. For cell growth it is commonly supplemented with fetal bovine serum, antibiotics and L-glutamine (or stable substitutes).

Use

DMEM is routinely used for primary cells and established cell lines including fibroblasts, HeLa cells, HEK 293 cells and many others, in fields such as molecular biology, virology and cancer research. Variants include DMEM/F-12, a 1:1 mixture with Ham's F-12 nutrient mixture used for more demanding cell types and serum-free formulations.
