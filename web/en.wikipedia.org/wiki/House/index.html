House

A house is a single-unit residential building. It may range in complexity from a rudimentary hut to a complex structure of wood, masonry, concrete or other materials, outfitted with plumbing, electrical, heating, ventilation and air conditioning systems. Houses use a range of different roofing systems to keep precipitation such as rain from getting into the dwelling space. Houses may have doors or locks to secure the dwelling space and protect its inhabitants and contents from burglars or other trespassers.

History

The earliest dwellings were shelters of stone, wood, hides and other natural materials; with the rise of agriculture, permanent settlements formed and houses developed as fixed structures of mud-brick, timber and stone. In ancient Rome, the domus served the wealthy while the poor lived in insulae (apartment blocks). Medieval European houses were commonly timber-framed, and the Industrial Revolution brought mass-produced brick housing, suburbs and eventually prefabricated construction.

Types

Houses are commonly classified by their form and attachment to neighbouring buildings: the detached house stands alone; semi-detached houses share one wall with a neighbour; terraced (row) houses share both side walls; and townhouses are multi-floor urban dwellings. Other forms include cottages, bungalows, villas, mansions, farmhouses and mobile homes. Many houses are described by architectural style, such as Georgian, Victorian, Tudor Revival, ranch-style or modernist.

Construction

Most houses are built on foundations with a structural frame of wood, steel or masonry, insulated walls, and a weatherproof roof. Interior spaces typically include a living room, kitchen, bedrooms and bathrooms. Building codes regulate safety, energy efficiency and sanitation, and modern construction increasingly incorporates sustainable materials and renewable energy systems.

Legal and social aspects

Houses may be owner-occupied or rented, and in many economies a house is the largest asset a household owns. The concept of "home" extends beyond the physical structure to the sense of belonging and security associated with it.
