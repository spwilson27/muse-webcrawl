Naval aviation

Naval aviation is the application of military air power by navies, both from warships that embark aircraft and from land bases in support of naval operations. It encompasses carrier-based fighters and bombers, maritime patrol aircraft, anti-submarine warfare aircraft, helicopters, and unmanned aerial vehicles operated by naval services.

History

Naval aviation began in the years just before the First World War: in 1910 Eugene Ely made the first takeoff from a warship, and in 1911 the first landing aboard a ship. Seaplane carriers and early aircraft carriers served in the First World War, and Britain's HMS Argus (1918) was the first carrier with a full-length flight deck. Between the wars, navies — above all those of the United States, Japan, and Britain — built purpose-designed carriers and doctrine. The Second World War proved carrier aviation decisive in the Pacific at battles such as Midway, while land-based naval aircraft fought the Battle of the Atlantic against U-boats.

Roles

Modern naval aviation provides fleet air defence, strike warfare, anti-surface and anti-submarine warfare, airborne early warning, electronic warfare, mine countermeasures, search and rescue, and logistics. Operating from moving, pitching decks — aided by catapults, arresting gear, angled flight decks, and optical landing systems — is regarded as among the most demanding forms of military flying.

Major operators

The United States Navy operates the world's largest naval air arm, centred on supercarriers. Other significant naval aviation forces include those of the United Kingdom (Fleet Air Arm), France, China, India, Japan, and Russia; many smaller navies operate shore-based patrol aircraft and shipborne helicopters.
