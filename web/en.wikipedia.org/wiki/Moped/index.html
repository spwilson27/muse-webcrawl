A moped is a type of small motorcycle with an engine displacement of typically no more than 50 cc and a maximum design speed of about 45–50 km/h (28–31 mph). The name is a portmanteau of "motor" and "pedal," reflecting the bicycle-style pedals fitted to early models, which were used for starting and for assisting the engine. In many jurisdictions mopeds form a separate legal class from motorcycles, with lower licensing, registration, and insurance requirements — often open to younger riders — in exchange for restricted speed and exclusion from motorways.

History

The moped descends from early motorized bicycles of the early twentieth century and from lightweight machines such as the German NSU Quickly, launched in 1953, which sold in the millions. Post-war Europe adopted mopeds as cheap personal transport; French Mobylettes, Italian and Austrian models from makers such as Puch, and later Japanese machines from Honda, Yamaha, and Suzuki spread the type worldwide. In the 1970s, Japanese "sports mopeds" briefly flourished in Britain under learner laws restricting 16-year-olds to pedal-equipped 50 cc machines.

Classic mopeds used small air-cooled two-stroke engines, often with automatic or simple two-speed transmissions. Four-stroke and, increasingly, electric drivetrains have replaced two-strokes in new models as emissions rules tightened. The step-through "underbone" motorcycles ubiquitous in Southeast Asia (such as the Honda Super Cub, the best-selling motor vehicle in history) are sometimes loosely called mopeds, though they lack pedals and typically exceed moped engine limits.

Legal status

Definitions vary widely. The European Union's AM licence category covers two-wheelers up to 50 cc and 45 km/h, rideable from age 16 (14 in some member states). In the United States, definitions are set state by state, generally capping engine size at 50 cc and top speed near 30 mph, with some states requiring only a standard driver's licence. The United Kingdom allows 16-year-olds to ride mopeds restricted to 28 mph after compulsory basic training. Because of their low speed, mopeds are generally prohibited from motorways and freeways.

Distinction from scooters

Mopeds are traditionally distinguished from motor scooters by their larger wheels, bicycle-like frame with a horizontal top tube or low step-through, and (originally) pedals; scooters have a fully step-through frame, small wheels, bodywork enclosing the engine, and a floorboard for the rider's feet. Modern usage blurs the line, and small scooters are colloquially called mopeds in many places, though legally they may be classed as motorcycles if they exceed moped limits.
