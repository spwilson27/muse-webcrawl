Xin'an River

LocationAnhui and Zhejiang, China
LengthAbout 373 km (including headwaters)
MouthQiantang River

The Xin'an River (Chinese: 新安江) is a river in east-central China, flowing through Anhui and Zhejiang provinces. It is the upper course of the Qiantang River system and is noted for its clear water and mountainous scenery.

Course

The river rises in the hills near Xiuning County in southern Anhui, in the region of historic Huizhou. It flows generally eastward through She County and into Zhejiang, where its course is interrupted by the Xin'an River Dam, completed in 1959, which created Qiandao Lake (Thousand Island Lake), a major reservoir and tourist attraction. Below the dam the river continues to Jiande, where it joins the Lan River to form the Fuchun River, part of the Qiantang system.

History and culture

Historically the Xin'an was a vital trade route linking the tea- and ink-producing Huizhou region with Hangzhou. The river and its valley are celebrated in Chinese landscape painting and poetry, and the surrounding villages preserve much traditional Huizhou architecture.
