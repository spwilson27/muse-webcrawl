Surety

In finance and law, a surety is a person or organisation that takes responsibility for another's performance of an obligation, for example the payment of a debt or the fulfilment of a contract. The surety guarantees that if the principal debtor defaults, the surety will answer for the obligation. The arrangement is known as suretyship, and the written instrument is a surety bond.

The three parties

  • The principal: the person whose obligation is guaranteed
  • The obligee: the person to whom the obligation is owed
  • The surety: the person who guarantees the obligation

Uses

Surety bonds are common in construction, where contractors post performance bonds and payment bonds guaranteeing completion of a project and payment of subcontractors. In criminal procedure, a surety may guarantee that an accused person released from remand will attend court; commercial bail bondsmen operate on this model in the United States. Sureties also feature in customs, licensing, and court proceedings such as appeal bonds.

Rights of the surety

A surety that pays the obligee is generally entitled to be indemnified by the principal, and by subrogation may step into the obligee's shoes to enforce the obligee's rights against the principal. Co-sureties guaranteeing the same obligation may claim contribution from one another. Historically sureties were often private individuals — friends or family of the debtor — but modern suretyship is dominated by insurance companies.
