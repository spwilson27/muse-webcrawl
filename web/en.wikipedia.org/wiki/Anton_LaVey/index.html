Anton Szandor LaVey (born Howard Stanton Levey; 1930–1997) was an American author, musician and occultist who founded the Church of Satan in San Francisco in 1966 and codified LaVeyan Satanism in The Satanic Bible (1969). His philosophy treats "Satan" as a symbol of individualism, self-indulgence and earthly values rather than a supernatural being.

Life

LaVey's colourful self-reported biography — circus and carnival work, crime-scene photography, playing organ in burlesque houses — has been partly disputed by journalists, but it shaped his public persona. From his Victorian "Black House" in San Francisco he held Friday-night lectures that evolved into the Church of Satan, declared on Walpurgisnacht, 30 April 1966. He performed publicised Satanic baptisms, weddings and a funeral, attracting celebrities such as Jayne Mansfield and Sammy Davis Jr.

Works and legacy

His books include The Satanic Witch (1970) and The Satanic Rituals (1972). LaVeyan Satanism's Nine Satanic Statements emphasise indulgence, vital existence and personal responsibility. After his death in 1997 the Church of Satan passed to Peter H. Gilmore and Peggy Nadramia.
