de Havilland Mosquito

The de Havilland DH.98 Mosquito is a British twin-engined, multirole combat aircraft, designed during the Second World War. Unusual in that its airframe was constructed mostly of wood, it was nicknamed the "Wooden Wonder", or "Mossie". It served as a fast bomber, fighter-bomber, night fighter, maritime strike aircraft, and photo-reconnaissance platform.

Design and development

De Havilland proposed an unarmed bomber so fast it would need no defensive armament; initially sceptical, the Air Ministry ordered it in 1940. Its balsa-and-plywood sandwich construction conserved strategic metals and allowed furniture factories to build components. Powered by two Rolls-Royce Merlin engines, it was among the fastest operational aircraft of the war.

Service

More than 7,700 were built in Britain, Canada, and Australia. Famous operations included the Amiens prison raid and precision attacks on Gestapo headquarters. The Mosquito remained in RAF service into the 1950s.
