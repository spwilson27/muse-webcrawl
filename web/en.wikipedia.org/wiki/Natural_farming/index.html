Natural farming

Natural farming (自然農法, shizen nōhō) is an ecological farming approach established by the Japanese farmer and philosopher Masanobu Fukuoka (1913–2008). It minimises human intervention and works with the natural biodiversity of each farm's ecosystem, dispensing with tillage, chemical fertilisers, prepared compost and weeding by cultivation.

Principles

Fukuoka summarised his method in "do-nothing farming": no ploughing, no fertiliser, no pesticides and no weeding. Ground is kept covered with living plants such as white clover, straw mulch is returned to the fields, and grain is broadcast in clay seed balls. His 1975 book The One-Straw Revolution brought the approach to an international audience.

Related approaches

Natural farming is related to, but distinct from, organic farming, permaculture and the methods of Mokichi Okada. In India, the practice has been promoted as "zero budget natural farming", notably by Subhash Palekar, emphasising local inputs such as cow-dung preparations.
