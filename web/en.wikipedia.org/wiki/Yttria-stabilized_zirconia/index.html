Yttria-stabilized zirconia

Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ)
Chemical basisZrO2 doped with Y2O3
Common grades3Y-TZP (structural); 8YSZ (electrolyte)
Crystal structureTetragonal or cubic (stabilised)
Key propertiesHigh strength, toughness, oxygen-ion conductivity, low thermal conductivity

Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) is a ceramic material consisting of zirconium dioxide (zirconia, ZrO2) whose crystal structure is stabilised at room temperature by the addition of yttrium oxide (yttria, Y2O3). Pure zirconia undergoes disruptive phase changes on heating and cooling; yttria stabilises the high-temperature tetragonal or cubic phases and, by substituting Y3+ for Zr4+, creates oxygen vacancies that make the material a fast conductor of oxygen ions at elevated temperatures.

Grades and properties

Zirconia containing about 3 mol% yttria (3Y-TZP, tetragonal zirconia polycrystal) is predominantly tetragonal and is exceptionally strong and fracture-resistant for a ceramic, owing to transformation toughening: stressed tetragonal grains transform to the monoclinic phase and absorb crack energy. Material with about 8 mol% yttria (8YSZ) is fully stabilised in the cubic fluorite structure and has the highest oxygen-ion conductivity, at the cost of lower mechanical strength.

Applications

  • Solid oxide fuel cells and oxygen sensors: 8YSZ is the standard electrolyte in SOFCs and in the lambda sensors used in automobile exhaust systems, conducting O2− ions at temperatures of roughly 600–1000 °C.
  • Thermal barrier coatings: YSZ's very low thermal conductivity makes it the standard coating material protecting turbine blades in jet engines and gas turbines.
  • Dentistry and medicine: 3Y-TZP is widely used for dental crowns, bridges, and implants, and for ceramic femoral heads in hip replacements, because of its strength and tooth-like appearance.
  • Other uses: knife blades, grinding media, refractory components, and simulated gemstones (cubic zirconia is related stabilised zirconia crystal).
