The Zlín Region (Czech: Zlínský kraj) is an administrative unit (kraj) of the Czech Republic, located in the south-eastern part of the historical region of Moravia. Its capital and largest city is Zlín. The region borders Slovakia to the east, and within the Czech Republic it neighbours the South Moravian, Olomouc and Moravian-Silesian regions.

Geography

The terrain is largely hilly and mountainous. The White Carpathians (Bílé Karpaty) run along the Slovak border, the Hostýn-Vsetín Mountains and the Vizovice Highlands occupy the interior, and the region's highest peak is Čertův mlýn (1,206 m) in the Moravian-Silesian Beskids. The main river is the Morava, which drains the western lowlands. A large part of the White Carpathians is a protected landscape area and a UNESCO biosphere reserve, noted for its species-rich orchid meadows.

Administrative division

The region is divided into four districts: Kroměříž, Uherské Hradiště, Vsetín and Zlín. Major towns include Kroměříž — whose castle and gardens are a UNESCO World Heritage Site — Uherské Hradiště, Vsetín, Valašské Meziříčí, Otrokovice, Rožnov pod Radhoštěm (known for its open-air Wallachian museum) and the spa town of Luhačovice.

Economy and culture

The regional economy was shaped by the Baťa shoe company, founded in Zlín in 1894, which transformed the capital into a famous functionalist company town. Engineering, plastics and rubber processing (including tire production in Otrokovice), and food processing remain important. The region preserves strong Moravian folk traditions, particularly in Moravian Slovakia (Slovácko) in the south — wine-growing, folk costume festivals such as the Ride of the Kings (Jízda králů) in Vlčnov, inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list — and in Moravian Wallachia in the north-east, noted for its timbered architecture and pastoral heritage.
