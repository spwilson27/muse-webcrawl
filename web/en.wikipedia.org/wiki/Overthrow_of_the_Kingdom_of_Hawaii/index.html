Overthrow of the Kingdom of Hawaii

ʻIolani Palace, seat of the Hawaiian monarchy

Date: 17 January 1893

Location: Honolulu, Kingdom of Hawaii

Result: Monarchy overthrown; Provisional Government proclaimed

The overthrow of the Kingdom of Hawaii began on 14 January 1893 with a coup d'état against Queen Liliʻuokalani in Honolulu. A group of American and European businessmen and residents, organised as the Committee of Safety and largely of non-native Hawaiian ancestry, deposed the queen and proclaimed a Provisional Government under Sanford B. Dole, with the support of the United States Minister to Hawaii, John L. Stevens, who landed troops from USS Boston.

The immediate trigger was Liliʻuokalani's attempt to promulgate a new constitution restoring powers of the monarchy and voting rights of Native Hawaiians that had been curtailed by the "Bayonet Constitution" of 1887. Fearing bloodshed, the queen yielded her authority under protest, appealing to the United States government to restore her.

Aftermath

President Grover Cleveland's investigator, James Henderson Blount, concluded that the overthrow was illegal and that US officials had played an improper role, but the Provisional Government refused to step down. In 1894 the Republic of Hawaii was proclaimed. Following a failed counter-rebellion in 1895, Liliʻuokalani formally abdicated. Hawaii was annexed by the United States through the Newlands Resolution in 1898 and became a US territory in 1900.

In 1993, on the centenary of the overthrow, the US Congress passed the Apology Resolution, signed by President Bill Clinton, acknowledging the illegality of the overthrow and the United States' role in it. The event remains central to the Hawaiian sovereignty movement.
