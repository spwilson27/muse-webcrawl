Gromov–Witten invariant

In mathematics, Gromov–Witten (GW) invariants are rational numbers that, in certain situations, count curves with prescribed conditions in a given symplectic manifold. They are named after Mikhail Gromov, whose 1985 work on pseudoholomorphic curves founded the subject, and Edward Witten, who proposed their application to topological string theory. GW invariants may be assembled into a generating function, the Gromov–Witten potential, which is the central object of quantum cohomology.

Definition

Given a closed symplectic manifold X, a genus g, a number n of marked points, and a homology class A, the Gromov–Witten invariants are defined via an integral over the moduli space of stable maps from nodal Riemann surfaces into X — a space compactified following Deligne and Mumford's theory of stable curves. Because the moduli space has boundary strata and obstructions, the invariant is generally defined using a virtual fundamental class, which produces rational rather than integer values.

Relation to enumerative geometry

In favorable cases, for example for Calabi–Yau threefolds, Gromov–Witten invariants encode counts of rational curves that classical enumerative geometers sought. A famous prediction from mirror symmetry (Candelas, de la Ossa, Green, and Parkes, 1991) gave the number of rational curves of each degree on the quintic threefold, numbers that were subsequently verified mathematically and dramatically demonstrated the power of the theory.

Computation

Important computational tools include the WDVV equations (associativity relations), localization with respect to torus actions, and the correspondence with other curve-counting theories such as Donaldson–Thomas invariants and Pandharipande–Thomas invariants (the MNOP correspondence). The Gromov–Witten theory of a point leads to intersection theory on the moduli space of curves, governed by Kontsevich's proof of Witten's conjecture.
