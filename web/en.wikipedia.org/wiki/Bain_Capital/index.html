Bain Capital, LP is an American private investment firm based in Boston, Massachusetts, United States. It specialises in private equity, venture capital, credit, public equity, impact investing, life sciences, crypto, tech opportunities, partnership opportunities, special situations, and real estate. Bain Capital invests across a range of industry sectors and geographic regions and manages approximately $185 billion of investor capital.

History

The firm was founded in 1984 by partners from the consulting firm Bain & Company, including Bill Bain and Mitt Romney, who became its first chief executive. Initially focused on venture capital and leveraged buyouts of young companies, Bain Capital built a reputation with early investments in companies such as Staples (founded 1986 with Bain backing) and later in large buyouts including Toys "R" Us, Burger King, Domino's Pizza, Dunkin' Brands, Hospital Corporation of America (HCA) and Clear Channel Communications.

Romney left the firm in the 1990s to enter politics; his tenure at Bain became a central issue in his 2012 presidential campaign, with critics highlighting layoffs at some portfolio companies and supporters pointing to jobs created at others. The firm subsequently diversified into credit (through Bain Capital Credit, formerly Sankaty Advisors), public equity, real estate, life sciences and double-impact investing, and expanded internationally with offices in Europe and Asia.

Business areas

Bain Capital's principal activities include private equity (its flagship buyout funds), venture and growth capital (Bain Capital Ventures), credit and distressed debt, special situations, real estate, life sciences, and technology opportunities funds. Its affiliated public-equity arm dates to the founding of Brookside Capital. The firm is one of the largest alternative investment managers in the world.
