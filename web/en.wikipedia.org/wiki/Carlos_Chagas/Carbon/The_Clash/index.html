The Clash were an English rock band formed in London in 1976. Emerging from the first wave of British punk rock, they are widely regarded as one of the most influential bands of their era, celebrated for their politically charged lyrics, musical adventurousness, and the slogan, coined by the music press, "The Only Band That Matters."

History

The band's classic line-up consisted of Joe Strummer (lead vocals, rhythm guitar), Mick Jones (lead guitar, vocals), Paul Simonon (bass), and Nicky "Topper" Headon (drums). After supporting the Sex Pistols and signing to CBS Records, they released their self-titled debut album in 1977, followed by Give 'Em Enough Rope (1978).

London Calling and after

Their third album, London Calling (1979), a sprawling double album drawing on rockabilly, reggae, ska, jazz, and rhythm and blues, is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of all time. The triple album Sandinista! (1980) pushed their eclecticism further, while Combat Rock (1982) brought their biggest American hits, "Rock the Casbah" and "Should I Stay or Should I Go." Internal tensions led to Headon's departure in 1982 and Jones's dismissal in 1983; a final album, Cut the Crap (1985), was poorly received, and the band broke up in 1986.

Legacy

The Clash fused punk energy with reggae rhythms and left-wing politics, protesting unemployment, racism, and British and American foreign policy. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003, a few months after Strummer's death in December 2002.
