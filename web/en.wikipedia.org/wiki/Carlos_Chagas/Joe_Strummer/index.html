John Graham Mellor (21 August 1952 – 22 December 2002), known professionally as Joe Strummer, was a British musician best known as the co-founder, lyricist, rhythm guitarist and lead vocalist of the punk rock band The Clash, formed in 1976.

Early life

Strummer was born in Ankara, Turkey, where his father worked for the British Foreign Office. He spent his childhood travelling between postings in Cairo, Mexico City and Bonn before being sent to boarding school in England. After school he played in pub rock bands including The 101ers, adopting the stage name "Strummer" from his strumming guitar style.

The Clash

In 1976 Strummer left The 101ers to form The Clash with Mick Jones, Paul Simonon and others. The band became one of the defining acts of British punk, expanding its sound on albums such as London Calling (1979) and Sandinista! (1980) to embrace reggae, dub, funk and rockabilly. Strummer's politically charged lyrics addressed unemployment, racial tension and anti-imperialism. The Clash disbanded in 1986.

Later career and death

Strummer acted in films, scored soundtracks, briefly fronted The Pogues, and in 1999 formed The Mescaleros, releasing three albums blending world music with rock. He died suddenly of an undiagnosed congenital heart defect in December 2002, aged 50. The Clash were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003.
