Punk rock

Punk rock (also known simply as punk) is a music genre that emerged in the mid-1970s. Rooted in 1960s garage rock, punk bands rejected the perceived excesses of mainstream rock, favouring short, fast songs, stripped-down instrumentation and anti-establishment lyrics delivered with a do-it-yourself ethic.

Origins

Precursors included The Stooges, MC5, the New York Dolls and the Velvet Underground. In New York, the scene centred on clubs such as CBGB produced the Ramones, Patti Smith, Television and Talking Heads. In London, the Sex Pistols, The Clash and The Damned ignited a cultural explosion in 1976–77, accompanied by distinctive fashion associated with Malcolm McLaren and Vivienne Westwood.

Characteristics

Punk songs typically use simple chord progressions, distorted guitars and shouted or declamatory vocals, often lasting under three minutes. Lyrical themes include alienation, boredom, unemployment and critiques of consumerism and authority. Independent labels, fanzines and self-booked tours embodied the movement's DIY ethos.

Later developments

The 1980s saw the faster, harder hardcore punk scene in the United States (Black Flag, Minor Threat, Bad Brains) and the politically explicit anarcho-punk movement in Britain. In the 1990s punk broke into the mainstream through pop punk and the success of bands such as Green Day, The Offspring and Blink-182, while punk's aesthetic and ethics continued to influence alternative rock, indie music and youth culture worldwide.
