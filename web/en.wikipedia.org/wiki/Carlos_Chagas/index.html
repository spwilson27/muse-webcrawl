Carlos Ribeiro Justiniano Chagas (9 July 1879 – 8 November 1934) was a Brazilian sanitary physician, parasitologist and bacteriologist who in 1909 discovered the infectious disease that bears his name, Chagas disease (American trypanosomiasis) — a unique achievement in which a single researcher identified the causative organism, its insect vector, and the clinical disease.

Discovery

Working under Oswaldo Cruz on malaria control during railway construction in Minas Gerais, Chagas examined the blood-sucking triatomine bugs ("barbeiros") that infested rural dwellings and found a new flagellate protozoan in their gut, which he named Trypanosoma cruzi after his mentor. He then identified the parasite in the blood of a febrile child, Berenice, and described the disease's acute and chronic phases, including its characteristic damage to the heart and digestive tract. The chronic form now affects an estimated 6–7 million people, mostly in Latin America.

Later career

Chagas directed the Oswaldo Cruz Institute in Rio de Janeiro and Brazil's national public health department, leading campaigns against epidemics in the Amazon and the Brazilian interior. He was twice nominated for the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
