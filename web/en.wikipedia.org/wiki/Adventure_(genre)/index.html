Adventure (genre)

The adventure genre is a genre of fiction in which an adventure — an exciting undertaking involving risk and physical danger — forms the main storyline. Adventure fiction often overlaps with other genres, including action, swashbuckler, and war fiction, and appears across literature, film, television, and video games.

Characteristics

Adventure stories typically feature a protagonist who journeys to exotic or dangerous places, confronts antagonists and obstacles, and pursues a goal such as treasure, rescue, or discovery. The plot proceeds through a rapid succession of episodes, with travel and action emphasized over character development or thematic complexity.

History

The genre descends from ancient epics and medieval romances, flourished in the nineteenth-century novel with authors such as Alexandre Dumas, Robert Louis Stevenson, Jules Verne, and H. Rider Haggard, and expanded into pulp magazines and cinema serials in the twentieth century. Classic examples include The Count of Monte Cristo, Treasure Island, King Solomon's Mines, and the Indiana Jones film series.

Subgenres

Subgenres and neighboring forms include the lost-world story, the Robinsonade, nautical adventure, spy adventure, and survival fiction. Adventure video games form a separate medium-specific genre centered on exploration and puzzle-solving.
