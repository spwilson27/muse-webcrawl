Khwaja Ghulam Farid (1845–1901)

Died24 July 1901, Chachran
BuriedMithankot, Punjab, Pakistan

Khwaja Ghulam Farid (Punjabi/Saraiki: خواجہ غلام فرید; 1845–1901) was a 19th-century Punjabi and Saraiki Sufi poet and saint of the Chishti order, revered across the southern Punjab of present-day Pakistan. He is considered the greatest poet of the Saraiki language, and his kafis — short mystical lyrics — remain central to the musical and devotional culture of the region.

Life

Ghulam Farid was born in Chachran in the princely state of Bahawalpur, into a family of scholars and Sufis who traced descent from Umar, the second caliph. Orphaned young, he was raised by his elder brother, Fakhruddin, who became his spiritual guide. He memorised the Quran by the age of eight and mastered Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Punjabi, Sindhi, Braj and Saraiki. The Nawab of Bahawalpur, Sadiq Muhammad Khan IV, became his devoted disciple. Farid spent long periods in retreat in the Rohi (Cholistan) desert, whose landscape of dunes, wells and desert flora fills his poetry with imagery of thirst, wandering and longing. He performed the Hajj pilgrimage in the 1870s.

Poetry

His Diwan-e-Farid collects hundreds of kafis in Saraiki, along with verse in other languages. The poems proclaim the Sufi doctrine of the unity of being (wahdat al-wujud), seeing God in every aspect of creation, and they voice an inclusive humanism across religious boundaries — Farid wrote with reverence of Hindu yogis as well as Muslim saints. His verses also contain veiled criticism of religious formalism, feudal arrogance and the British colonial order. Set to music, the kafis are sung at his shrine and by folk and classical performers throughout Pakistan; singers such as Pathanay Khan made recordings of his verses famous nationwide.

Legacy

Farid died at Chachran in 1901 and was buried at Mithankot, where his white-domed shrine draws large crowds, especially during his annual urs festival. His poetry is a cornerstone of Saraiki identity; universities, literary societies and the Khwaja Farid awards honour his name, and his verses are taught in schools in the Saraiki-speaking region.
