Cambyses II (Old Persian: 𐎣𐎲𐎢𐎪𐎡𐎹, Kabūjiya) was the second King of Kings of the Achaemenid Empire, reigning from 530 to 522 BC. The eldest son of Cyrus the Great, he is best known for conquering Egypt in 525 BC, thereby bringing the largest and richest region yet under Persian rule, and for the mystery surrounding his death and the succession crisis that elevated Darius I.

Early life

Cambyses was the son of Cyrus and Cassandane, a Persian noblewoman. Before his father's death he governed Babylon and was associated with the kingship. On Cyrus's death in 530 BC he succeeded without apparent opposition.

Conquest of Egypt

In 525 BC Cambyses invaded Egypt, defeating Pharaoh Psamtik III at the Battle of Pelusium — according to later Greek tradition the Persians exploited the Egyptians' reverence for cats, an anecdote modern historians doubt. Memphis fell after a siege, and Cambyses assumed the pharaonic throne as founder of Egypt's Twenty-seventh Dynasty. From Egypt he launched expeditions south into Nubia with mixed success and west toward Libya and Carthage, which failed; Herodotus also records the famous story of a Persian army of 50,000 lost in a sandstorm en route to the oracle of Amun at Siwa — the "Lost Army of Cambyses," whose fate remains debated.

Reputation and death

Herodotus portrayed Cambyses as a mad and sacrilegious tyrant in Egypt, but Egyptian sources suggest a more measured ruler who adopted local customs, though later Persian-period scandals coloured the Greek account. In 522 BC, while Cambyses was still in Egypt or returning, a revolt broke out in Persia led by a man claiming to be his brother Bardiya (Smerdis). Cambyses died in Syria — by accident, suicide or assassination, the sources differ — and Darius, a distant relative, seized the throne after killing the claimant, whom Darius's inscriptions call the impostor Gaumata.
