Richard Martin Lloyd Walters (born January 14, 1965), better known as Slick Rick, is an English-born American rapper and record producer. Nicknamed "Rick the Ruler," he rose to prominence with Doug E. Fresh & the Get Fresh Crew in the mid-1980s, and their songs "The Show" and "La Di Da Di" are foundational hip hop classics, among the most sampled in the genre's history.

Early life

Walters was born in Mitcham, South London, to Jamaican parents and moved with his family to the Bronx, New York, in 1976. Blinded in his right eye in infancy by broken glass, he adopted the eyepatch that became part of his image, along with Kangol hats and heavy gold chains.

Career

His debut album The Great Adventures of Slick Rick (1988, Def Jam) is a landmark of narrative rap, containing "Children's Story," "Mona Lisa," and "Teenage Love," and is frequently ranked among the greatest hip hop albums ever made. Legal troubles — including a prison term in the 1990s following a shooting incident — stalled his career, though he released further albums including The Ruler's Back (1991), Behind Bars (1994), and The Art of Storytelling (1999). After long-running immigration proceedings, he became a U.S. citizen in 2016, and in 2025 released Victory, his first album in 26 years.

Style and influence

Slick Rick is regarded as hip hop's foremost storyteller, famous for his smooth, British-tinged flow, humor, and character voices. He has profoundly influenced artists including The Notorious B.I.G., Eminem, and Snoop Dogg.
