Weizmann Institute of Science

The Weizmann Institute of Science (Hebrew: מכון ויצמן למדע) is a public research university and multidisciplinary research institute in Rehovot, Israel. It is devoted entirely to graduate and postgraduate studies in the natural and exact sciences and is consistently ranked among the world's leading research institutions.

History

The institute was founded in 1934 by Chaim Weizmann — chemist and later the first President of Israel — as the Daniel Sieff Research Institute. It was expanded and renamed in Weizmann's honour in 1949. Its scientists have made major contributions including the development of early electronic computers (the WEIZAC, built in 1954–55, was one of the first computers in the world), research leading to the drug Copaxone for multiple sclerosis, and work on photosynthesis, immunology and particle physics.

Organisation

The institute comprises five faculties — Biology, Biochemistry, Chemistry, Physics, and Mathematics and Computer Science — with around 250 research groups, some 1,200 scientists and about 1,200 graduate students and postdoctoral fellows. Its Feinberg Graduate School awards MSc and PhD degrees. Research on campus also produced Ada Yonath, who won the 2009 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for studies of the ribosome.
