Otago Central Railway

LocationOtago, South Island, New Zealand
TerminiWingari (near Dunedin) – Clyde (originally Cromwell)
Length~185 km (original line)
Opened1879–1921 in stages

The Otago Central Railway was a railway line in the Otago region of New Zealand's South Island, built to open up the Central Otago goldfields and farming districts. It branched from the Main South Line at Wingatui, near Dunedin, and pushed inland through the Taieri Gorge and the Maniototo plain to Alexandra and Clyde; it was completed to Cromwell in 1921.

History

Construction began in 1879. The spectacular section through the Taieri Gorge, with its tunnels and high viaducts, slowed progress, and the line reached Middlemarch in 1891, Ranfurly in 1898 and Alexandra in 1906. The final extension to Cromwell served the Cromwell Gorge until the gorge was flooded by Lake Dunstan behind the Clyde Dam in the 1990s, when the terminus was cut back to Clyde.

Closure and legacy

Freight traffic — wool, livestock, fruit and schist stone — declined with road competition, and regular services beyond Taieri Gorge ended in 1990. The Dunedin-to-Middlemarch section survived as the Taieri Gorge Railway (later Dunedin Railways), a popular tourist line. The abandoned formation beyond Middlemarch was converted into the Otago Central Rail Trail, opened in 2000, a 150 km walking and cycling trail that became the model for New Zealand's national cycle trail network.
