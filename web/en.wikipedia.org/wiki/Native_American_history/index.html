History of Native Americans in the United States

Native American history
ScopeIndigenous peoples within the present-day United States
PeriodsPre-Columbian; colonisation; removal; allotment; termination; self-determination

The history of Native Americans in the United States spans more than twelve thousand years, from the first settlement of the Americas through the diverse civilisations of the pre-Columbian era, the upheavals of European colonisation, and the survival and resurgence of Indigenous nations to the present day.

Pre-Columbian era

Indigenous peoples developed hundreds of distinct cultures and languages across North America. Societies ranged from the mound-building cities of the Mississippi valley, such as Cahokia, to the cliff dwellings and irrigation networks of the Southwest, the confederacies of the Haudenosaunee in the Northeast, and the complex chiefdoms of the Pacific Northwest.

Colonisation and expansion

From the 16th century, European contact brought epidemic disease, warfare and dispossession. Under the United States, policies included treaty-making and its violation, the Indian Removal Act of 1830 and the Trail of Tears, the Indian Wars of the plains, and the allotment of communal lands under the Dawes Act of 1887, which stripped tribes of some ninety million acres.

Twentieth century to today

The Indian Reorganization Act of 1934 reversed allotment and encouraged tribal self-government, but was followed in the 1950s by termination and relocation policies. The self-determination era, from the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act of 1975 onward, saw tribes rebuild governments, win land and water settlements, and expand economic enterprises, including gaming.
