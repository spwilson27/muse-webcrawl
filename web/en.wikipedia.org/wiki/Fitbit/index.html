Fitbit is an American consumer electronics and fitness company, now a subsidiary of Google, best known for its wearable activity trackers and smartwatches that measure steps, heart rate, sleep and other health metrics. Its products include the Charge, Versa, Sense and Inspire lines, as well as the Aria smart scale.

History

The company was founded in 2007 by James Park and Eric Friedman. Its first device, the original Fitbit Tracker (2009), was a clip-on pedometer using a three-dimensional accelerometer. Fitbit went public on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2015 in one of the largest consumer-electronics IPOs of its time. It later expanded into smartwatches, acquiring assets of the smartwatch pioneer Pebble in 2016.

Acquisition by Google

Google announced its acquisition of Fitbit in November 2019 for approximately $2.1 billion; the deal closed in January 2021 after regulatory reviews in the United States and Europe, with commitments concerning health-data use and Android interoperability. Fitbit technology was subsequently integrated into the Pixel Watch line, and the brand continues as "Fitbit by Google".
