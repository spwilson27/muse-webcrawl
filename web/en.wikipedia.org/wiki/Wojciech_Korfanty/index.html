Wojciech Korfanty

Wojciech Korfanty (born Adalbert Korfanty; 20 April 1873 – 17 August 1939) was a Polish activist, journalist and politician, and a member of the German parliaments, the Reichstag and the Prussian Landtag. He was a leading figure in the movement for the incorporation of Upper Silesia into the reborn Polish state after the First World War, and led the Third Silesian Uprising in 1921.

Career

Born into a Polish-speaking mining family in Upper Silesia, Korfanty studied in Berlin and Breslau and became a journalist and organiser for the Polish national movement. In 1903 he was elected to the Reichstag, and in October 1918 he delivered a celebrated speech demanding the union of all Polish lands, including Upper Silesia, with a resurrected Poland.

Silesian plebiscite and uprisings

Appointed Polish plebiscite commissioner for Upper Silesia, Korfanty campaigned in the 1921 plebiscite. When the results seemed likely to award most of the industrial district to Germany, he led the Third Silesian Uprising (May–July 1921). The League of Nations ultimately partitioned Upper Silesia, giving Poland the eastern part including Katowice and most of the mines and industry.

Later life

Korfanty served briefly as deputy prime minister in 1923 and sat in the Polish Sejm, later opposing Józef Piłsudski's Sanation regime. He emigrated to Czechoslovakia in 1935; returning to Poland in April 1939, he was arrested and died in Warsaw that August, shortly before the German invasion.
