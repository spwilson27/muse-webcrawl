Bilkent University (Turkish: Bilkent Üniversitesi) is a private, non-profit research university located in Ankara, Turkey. Founded in 1984 by the academic and philanthropist İhsan Doğramacı, it was the first private university in Turkey. Its name is an acronym of bilim kenti, Turkish for "city of science."

Bilkent University's main campus in Ankara.

History

Doğramacı, a prominent physician and educator who had also founded Hacettepe University, established Bilkent through his foundations with the aim of creating a university of international standard where instruction would be in English. The first students were admitted in 1986. The university grew rapidly, developing a large campus west of central Ankara together with affiliated schools, a symphony orchestra, and one of Turkey's largest academic libraries.

Academics

Bilkent comprises faculties of engineering, science, economics and administrative sciences, humanities and letters, business administration, law, education, art and architecture, and music and performing arts, along with graduate schools and vocational programs. It is consistently ranked among the top universities in Turkey and appears in international rankings such as the Times Higher Education and QS lists. Its National Nanotechnology Research Center (UNAM) is a leading Turkish research institute.

Campus and culture

The campus hosts the Bilkent Symphony Orchestra, concert halls, museums, and extensive sports facilities. The university's library has historically been noted for its large collection and long opening hours. Many Bilkent graduates pursue postgraduate study abroad, and the university maintains exchange agreements with institutions worldwide.
