Requiescat in pace

Requiescat in pace is a Latin blessing that appears in the Catholic Requiem Mass and other funerary and commemoration services: "Et lux perpetua luceat eis. Dona eis requiem sempiternam..." The blessing is formed from the noun requies (rest, repose) and the preposition in, plus pace, the ablative of pax, peace: "may [he/she] rest in peace". The fuller phrase runs Dona eis requiem, et lux perpetua luceat eis: "grant them rest, and let perpetual light shine upon them".

Usage

The initials RIP, from either the Latin Requiescat in pace or its English rendering "rest in peace", have long been used as an epitaph engraved on Christian tombstones. The phrase also echoes Isaiah 57:2 and the Jewish expression "peace be upon him or her". It is now used widely in English-speaking cultures — and in forms such as the Italian riposi in pace and Spanish descanse en paz — among both Christians and non-Christians to show sympathy on the death of a loved one, including in obituaries, condolences, and public mourning.
