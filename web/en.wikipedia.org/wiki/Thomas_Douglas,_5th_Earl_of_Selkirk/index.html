Thomas Douglas, 5th Earl of Selkirk

Born: 20 June 1771, St Mary's Isle, Kirkcudbrightshire, Scotland
Died: 8 April 1820 (aged 48), Pau, France
Known for: Founding the Red River Colony in Manitoba
Occupation: Philanthropist, colonizer

The Earl of Selkirk

Thomas Douglas, 5th Earl of Selkirk (20 June 1771 – 8 April 1820) was a Scottish nobleman and philanthropist who promoted emigration from the Scottish Highlands and sponsored settlements in British North America, most famously the Red River Colony in present-day Manitoba, Canada.

Highland emigration

The seventh son of the 4th Earl, Thomas Douglas unexpectedly inherited the earldom in 1799. Concerned by the displacement of Highland crofters during the Clearances, he published Observations on the Present State of the Highlands of Scotland (1805) and organized emigrant settlements at Belfast, Prince Edward Island (1803), and at Baldoon in Upper Canada (1804).

Red River Colony

In 1811, having acquired a controlling interest in the Hudson's Bay Company, Selkirk obtained a grant of some 116,000 square miles — the District of Assiniboia — in the Red River Valley. His settlers, recruited largely from Sutherland, arrived from 1812 under Miles Macdonell and established the Red River Colony (Selkirk Settlement).

The colony provoked violent conflict with the rival North West Company and its Métis allies, culminating in the Battle of Seven Oaks (1816), in which the colony's governor Robert Semple and 20 men were killed. Selkirk visited the settlement in 1817, treated with local First Nations, and named the parish of Kildonan. Litigation and ill health consumed his remaining years; he died at Pau, France, in 1820.

Legacy

The city of Selkirk, Manitoba, and numerous schools and streets in Winnipeg commemorate him; the colony he founded grew into the province of Manitoba, established in 1870.
