Hero and Leander

Hero and Leander is a Greek myth of two lovers divided by the Hellespont: Hero, a priestess of Aphrodite at Sestos, and Leander, a youth of Abydos on the opposite shore.

The myth

Each night Hero lit a lamp atop her tower and Leander swam the strait guided by it. One stormy winter night the wind extinguished the lamp; Leander drowned, and when his body washed ashore Hero threw herself from the tower to join him in death.

In literature

The fullest ancient telling is the epyllion by Musaeus (5th–6th century CE). Ovid treated it in the Heroides. In English, Christopher Marlowe began a celebrated poem completed by George Chapman (1598), alluded to by Shakespeare, Byron — who swam the Hellespont in imitation in 1810 — and many others.

Legacy

The story became an archetype of love overcoming, and succumbing to, separation by water, inspiring paintings, operas and Handel's cantata Ero e Leandro.
