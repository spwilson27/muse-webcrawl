Mannose

Mannose is a sugar monomer of the aldohexose series of carbohydrates. It is a C-2 epimer of glucose. Its molecular formula is C6H12O6. Mannose is important in human metabolism, especially in the glycosylation of certain proteins; several congenital disorders of glycosylation are associated with mutations in enzymes involved in mannose metabolism.

Structure and properties

D-Mannose differs from D-glucose only in the stereochemistry at carbon 2. It has a sweet taste and is soluble in water. In solution it exists predominantly in its pyranose ring forms (α and β anomers). The name derives from "manna", the sweet secretion of the flowering ash, historically used as a source.

Biochemistry

Free mannose is absorbed from the diet and phosphorylated by hexokinase to mannose-6-phosphate, which is either isomerised to fructose-6-phosphate for glycolysis or converted to GDP-mannose, the donor for building N-linked glycans. Mannose residues are a core component of N-glycan structures on glycoproteins and of glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchors.

Sources and uses

Mannose occurs in fruits such as cranberries, peaches and oranges, and in polysaccharides called mannans in plant cell walls and yeast. D-mannose supplements are marketed for urinary tract health, on the basis that mannose can block adhesion of certain E. coli to the bladder wall, though clinical evidence remains limited.
