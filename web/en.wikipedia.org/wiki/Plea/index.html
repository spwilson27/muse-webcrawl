Plea

In legal terms, a plea is simply an answer to a claim made by someone in a criminal case under common law using the adversarial system. Colloquially, a plea has come to mean the assertion by a criminal defendant, at arraignment or otherwise, in response to a criminal charge — principally "guilty" or "not guilty".

[Image: A defendant entering a plea at arraignment]

Types of plea

  • Guilty — the defendant admits the offence charged; the court proceeds to sentence.
  • Not guilty — the defendant denies the offence and the case proceeds to trial.
  • Nolo contendere (United States) — "no contest": the defendant accepts conviction without admitting guilt, and the plea generally cannot be used as an admission in later civil proceedings.
  • Alford plea (United States) — the defendant pleads guilty while maintaining innocence, acknowledging that the prosecution has sufficient evidence to convict.

Plea bargaining

In the United States and some other jurisdictions, most criminal cases are resolved by plea bargaining, in which the defendant agrees to plead guilty, often to a lesser charge or in exchange for a more lenient sentence. In England and Wales, defendants receive credit in sentencing for an early guilty plea, though formal charge bargaining is more limited.
