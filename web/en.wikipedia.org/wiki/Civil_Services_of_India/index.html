Civil Services of India

The Civil Services of India comprise the permanent executive branch of the Republic of India. They carry out the administration of the Government of India and of the states, implementing policy and delivering public services. The services are broadly classified into All India Services, Central Civil Services and State Civil Services.

Structure

The three All India Services are the Indian Administrative Service (IAS), the Indian Police Service (IPS) and the Indian Forest Service (IFoS); their officers serve both the central government and state governments. Central Civil Services include the Indian Foreign Service, the Indian Revenue Service and many Group A and Group B services organised by ministry and function.

Recruitment

Recruitment to most services is through the Civil Services Examination conducted annually by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), one of the most competitive examinations in the world, with hundreds of thousands of applicants for roughly a thousand posts. The tradition descends from the Indian Civil Service of British India, often described as the "steel frame" of the administration.

Role and criticism

Civil servants staff ministries, district administration and regulatory bodies, and are formally politically neutral. The services have been praised for holding together a vast and diverse country, and criticised for red tape, corruption and resistance to reform; successive commissions have recommended changes in training, specialisation and accountability.
