Augusto César Sandino
Born: May 18, 1895, Niquinohomo, Nicaragua
Died: February 21, 1934 (aged 38), Managua, Nicaragua
Occupation: Revolutionary leader
Known for: Guerrilla resistance to the US occupation of Nicaragua

Augusto César Sandino (May 18, 1895 – February 21, 1934) was a Nicaraguan revolutionary and leader of a rebellion against the United States military occupation of Nicaragua between 1927 and 1933. Known as "the General of Free Men", he led the Army in Defence of the National Sovereignty of Nicaragua from the mountains of the Segovias.

Rebellion

After working in Honduras, Guatemala and Mexico, Sandino returned to Nicaragua in 1926 and joined the Liberal side in the civil war. When Liberal leaders accepted a US-brokered settlement in 1927, Sandino refused to disarm and waged a guerrilla campaign against the US Marines and the Nicaraguan National Guard. The Marines withdrew in 1933, and Sandino negotiated peace with the government of Juan Bautista Sacasa.

Death and legacy

In February 1934 Sandino was arrested and executed by National Guard troops under Anastasio Somoza García after a dinner at the presidential palace. He later became a national hero in Nicaragua, and the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN), founded in 1961, took its name from him.
