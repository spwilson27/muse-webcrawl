German occupation of Serbia

Territory: Territory of the Military Commander in Serbia
Period: April 1941 – October 1944
Capital: Belgrade
Collaborationist government: Government of National Salvation under Milan Nedić (from August 1941)

The German occupation of Serbia refers to the period during World War II when the central part of the Kingdom of Yugoslavia was occupied by Nazi Germany following the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941. The area was administered as the Territory of the Military Commander in Serbia, the only part of partitioned Yugoslavia placed under direct German military government.

Administration and collaboration

Germany initially ruled through a commissary administration under Milan Aćimović and, from 29 August 1941, through the puppet Government of National Salvation headed by General Milan Nedić. Real power rested with the German military commander and the SS and police apparatus. Serbia's pre-war territory was reduced: the Banat was administered separately by its ethnic German minority, while border regions were annexed by Bulgaria, Hungary, Italy, and the Independent State of Croatia.

Repression and the Holocaust

The occupation authorities applied a brutal reprisal policy — 100 hostages shot for every German soldier killed, 50 for every one wounded. Mass executions at Kraljevo and Kragujevac in October 1941 killed several thousand civilians. Serbia's Jewish population was almost entirely murdered by mid-1942, many at the Sajmište concentration camp in Belgrade; Serbia was declared Judenfrei ("free of Jews") in August 1942.

Resistance and liberation

Resistance came from the royalist Chetniks of Draža Mihailović and from the communist-led Partisans under Tito, whose July 1941 uprising briefly liberated territory around Užice. After fierce fighting and shifting alliances, Belgrade was liberated on 20 October 1944 by the Partisans and the Red Army.
