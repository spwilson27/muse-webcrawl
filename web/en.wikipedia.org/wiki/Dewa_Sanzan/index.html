The Dewa Sanzan (出羽三山, "Three Mountains of Dewa") are three sacred mountains in Yamagata Prefecture, in Japan's former Dewa Province: Mount Haguro, Mount Gassan, and Mount Yudono. Together they form one of the oldest centres of the Shugendō tradition of mountain asceticism and remain an important pilgrimage destination.

Significance

Tradition holds that the mountains were opened as a religious site in 593 by Prince Hachiko, son of Emperor Sushun. The three peaks are understood to represent the present life (Haguro), past life and death (Gassan, the highest at 1,984 m), and future life and rebirth (Yudono), so that a pilgrimage through all three constitutes a symbolic journey of rebirth. The mountains are sacred in both Shinto and Shugendō, and are tended by yamabushi mountain ascetics.

Sites

Mount Haguro is accessible year-round and features a celebrated five-story pagoda, a National Treasure, and a shrine complex reached by 2,446 stone steps through ancient cedar forest. Mount Gassan, with its summit shrine, is open only in summer due to heavy snow. Mount Yudono is considered the most sacred and secret of the three; photography is prohibited at its shrine, whose object of worship is a large reddish rock with a hot spring.

Pilgrimage

The Dewa Sanzan drew large numbers of pilgrims in the Edo period and continue to attract visitors, including those undertaking yamabushi training retreats involving fasting, waterfall meditation, and mountain trekking.
