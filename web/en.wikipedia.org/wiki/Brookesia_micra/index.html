Brookesia micra

Kingdom:Animalia
Class:Reptilia
Order:Squamata
Family:Chamaeleonidae
Genus:Brookesia
Species:B. micra

Brookesia micra, also known as the Nosy Hara leaf chameleon, is a species of chameleon from the island of Nosy Hara in Antsiranana, Madagascar. At the time of its description in 2012 it was the smallest known chameleon and among the smallest reptiles in the world.

A juvenile B. micra on a match head, showing its minute size.

Description

Adult males measure only about 16 mm (0.6 in) from snout to vent, with a total length under 30 mm including the tail; females are slightly larger. The species was discovered by a team led by Frank Glaw of the Bavarian State Collection of Zoology during an expedition to the Nosy Hara archipelago, and was formally described in the journal PLOS ONE in 2012. Its specific name derives from the Greek mikros, "small".

Behaviour and habitat

Like other Brookesia leaf chameleons, B. micra forages by day in the leaf litter of dry forest, feeding on tiny invertebrates, and climbs onto low branches at night to sleep — a habit that made it easier for researchers to locate specimens by torchlight. Its extreme miniaturisation is thought to be a case of island dwarfism. The species is threatened by habitat loss from logging, and its tiny range makes it especially vulnerable.
