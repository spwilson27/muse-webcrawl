Great Divide Mountain Bike Route

The Great Divide Mountain Bike Route (GDMBR) is a long-distance off-road bicycle touring route created and mapped by Adventure Cycling Association. It runs from Banff, Alberta, Canada, to Antelope Wells, New Mexico, United States, roughly following the Continental Divide through the Rocky Mountains. At roughly 3,100 miles (5,000 km), most of it on unpaved roads and trails, it is regarded as the longest off-pavement bicycle route in the world.

Route

The route passes through Alberta and British Columbia, then through Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado, and New Mexico. It crosses the Continental Divide some thirty times and includes long, remote segments — notably Wyoming's Great Divide Basin — where water and resupply are scarce. Riders are almost entirely self-supported, carrying camping gear and food between small towns.

Tour Divide

The route is the course of the Tour Divide, an annual self-supported, underground mountain bike race held each June starting in Banff. Elite racers complete the route in roughly two weeks; most touring cyclists take six to ten weeks. The race inspired the growth of the modern bikepacking movement.
