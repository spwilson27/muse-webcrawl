Deutsche Post (GDR)

ServicesMail, parcels, telecommunications, postal banking

The Deutsche Post (DP) was the state postal and telecommunications service of the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) from 1949 until German reunification in 1990. It operated under the Ministry for Post and Telecommunications and combined mail and parcel delivery, the telephone network, broadcasting transmission and postal savings services in a single state organisation.

History

Postal services in the Soviet occupation zone were reorganised after 1945 under a central directorate. With the founding of the GDR in 1949, the Deutsche Post became the republic's postal administration. It was a major employer and one of the country's most visible state institutions, recognisable by its yellow vehicles, post offices and the post horn emblem.

Stamps

The Deutsche Post issued its own postage stamps from 1949 to 1990, producing a large and varied output that included many issues aimed at collectors abroad, an important source of hard currency for the GDR. Its philatelic service in East Berlin sold mint stamps internationally throughout the division of Germany.

Reunification

Following the currency and economic union of 1990 and formal reunification on 3 October 1990, the Deutsche Post was absorbed into the (West German) Deutsche Bundespost; GDR stamps ceased to be valid for postage at the end of 1990.
