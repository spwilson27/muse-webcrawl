LocationRütli meadow, Lake Lucerne

The Rütlischwur ("Oath of the Rütli") is a legendary oath sworn by representatives of the three Swiss forest cantons — Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden — on the Rütli, a meadow above Lake Lucerne. In Swiss national tradition it marks the founding act of the Old Swiss Confederacy.

The legend

According to later chronicles such as the White Book of Sarnen and Aegidius Tschudi's account, the three conspirators — traditionally named Werner Stauffacher, Walter Fürst and Arnold von Melchtal — met with thirty companions and swore mutual aid against the bailiffs of the Habsburgs. The oath is famously depicted in Friedrich Schiller's 1804 play Wilhelm Tell, whose version of the oath ("We will be a single people of brothers...") became canonical.

Historical assessment

While the Federal Charter of 1291 is a genuine document of alliance, the Rütli oath itself is regarded by historians as legend, first recorded in sources of the 15th and 16th centuries. The Rütli meadow nonetheless remains a national symbol, the site of an annual report by the Swiss army's commander and of celebrations on Swiss National Day, 1 August.
