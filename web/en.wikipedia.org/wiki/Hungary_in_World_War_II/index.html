Hungary in World War II

During World War II, the Kingdom of Hungary under Regent Miklós Horthy was a member of the Axis powers. Seeking to revise the Treaty of Trianon, Hungary aligned with Nazi Germany and regained territories through the Vienna Awards of 1938 and 1940. Hungarian forces participated in the invasions of Yugoslavia (1941) and the Soviet Union (1941).

Path to war

Hungary joined the Tripartite Pact in November 1940. In April 1941 it joined the invasion of Yugoslavia, and in June 1941 declared war on the Soviet Union, committing the Second Army to the Eastern Front, where it was largely destroyed at the Don bend in January 1943.

German occupation and the Holocaust

As Horthy sought an exit from the war, Germany occupied Hungary in March 1944 (Operation Margarethe). Under the Sztójay government, more than 400,000 Hungarian Jews were deported, mostly to Auschwitz, in spring–summer 1944. Horthy's attempted armistice in October 1944 led to his removal and the installation of Ferenc Szálasi's Arrow Cross regime.

End of the war

The Siege of Budapest (December 1944 – February 1945) devastated the capital. Soviet forces completed the occupation of Hungary by April 1945. The 1947 Paris Peace Treaty restored Hungary's Trianon borders. Roughly 300,000 Hungarian soldiers and 600,000 civilians, including most deported Jews, perished.
