Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend (18 April 1674 – 21 June 1738) was an English Whig statesman who, with his brother-in-law Robert Walpole, directed British policy for much of the 1720s. He is nicknamed "Turnip Townshend" for his role in popularising crop rotation with turnips.

Political career

Townshend negotiated the Treaty of The Hague (1709) as ambassador to the Dutch Republic. After the Hanoverian succession he served as Secretary of State, but a split with the Stanhope–Sunderland ministry saw him dismissed in 1716. Restored in 1721, he worked closely with Walpole, handling foreign policy through the treaties of Hanover, Paris, and Seville. Rivalry with Walpole led to his resignation in 1730.

Agriculture

At Raynham Hall in Norfolk, Townshend promoted the four-course Norfolk rotation — wheat, turnips, barley, and clover — which eliminated the need for fallow land and provided winter fodder, a key innovation of the British Agricultural Revolution. The nickname "Turnip Townshend" was coined by satirists but his improvements were real and widely adopted.
