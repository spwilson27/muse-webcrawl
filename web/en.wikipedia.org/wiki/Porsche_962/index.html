Porsche 962

The Porsche 962 was a sports-prototype racing car designed and built by Porsche. Created to replace the Porsche 956, it was introduced at the end of 1984 and went on to become the most successful sports racing car of the 1980s, dominating the World Sportscar Championship and North America's IMSA GT Championship.

Design

The 962 was created primarily to comply with IMSA's rules, which required the driver's feet to be behind the front axle line; the wheelbase was therefore lengthened relative to the 956. It used an aluminium monocoque and a twin-turbocharged flat-six engine, in IMSA form initially air-cooled and limited to a single turbocharger, and in Group C form water-cooled and producing up to around 700 horsepower.

Racing record

The car won the 24 Hours of Le Mans overall in 1986 and 1987 with the factory Rothmans Porsche team, and privately entered 962s continued winning for years. Dauer produced a road-going version, the Dauer 962 Le Mans, a variant of which controversially won Le Mans again in 1994. In total, 91 chassis were built.
