Scottish names

Scottish names are personal names used in Scotland, derived from Gaelic, Scots, Norse, Norman French, and Anglo-Saxon sources. Traditional Gaelic naming customs included patronymics formed with mac ("son of") and nic ("daughter of"), many of which became fixed surnames.

Given names

Common male names of Gaelic origin include Alasdair, Angus (Aonghas), Calum, Hamish (a vocative form of Seumas), Lachlan and Ewan. Female names include Ailsa, Catriona, Eilidh, Fiona, Isla, Mairi and Morag. Many names were anglicised from Gaelic originals, and some, such as Ian, were in turn re-borrowed into general English usage.

Surnames

Scottish surnames often reflect clan affiliation (MacDonald, Campbell, Stewart), occupation (Smith, Taylor, Wright), or place of origin. The prefixes Mac- and Mc- both derive from Gaelic mac, and names beginning with them are among the most recognisable markers of Scottish ancestry worldwide.
