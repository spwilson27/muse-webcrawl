Kimberlé Crenshaw

Kimberlé Williams Crenshaw (born May 5, 1959) is an American civil rights advocate and a leading scholar of critical race theory. She is a professor at the UCLA School of Law and at Columbia Law School, where she specialises in race and gender issues. Crenshaw is best known for coining the term intersectionality in 1989, for her work on the structural and political dimensions of violence against women of colour, and as co-founder of the African American Policy Forum, including its #SayHerName campaign.

Education and career

Crenshaw was born in Canton, Ohio. She earned her bachelor's degree from Cornell University in 1981, her J.D. from Harvard Law School in 1984, and an LL.M. from the University of Wisconsin in 1985, where she was a William H. Hastie Fellow and a law clerk to Wisconsin Supreme Court Justice Shirley Abrahamson. She joined the UCLA School of Law faculty in 1986 and was a founding organiser of the Critical Race Theory Workshop of 1989, at which the movement's name was adopted. In 1991 she assisted the legal team representing Anita Hill during Clarence Thomas's Supreme Court confirmation hearings.

Intersectionality

In her 1989 article "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex" (University of Chicago Legal Forum), Crenshaw argued that anti-discrimination law, feminist theory, and anti-racist politics each proceeded on a "single-axis framework" that erased Black women, whose experiences of discrimination could not be captured by adding race and gender claims treated separately; her analysis drew on cases such as DeGraffenreid v. General Motors, in which a court refused to let Black women sue as a combined protected class. The metaphor of an intersection — traffic flowing from several directions, with injury attributable to any or all of them — gave the concept its name. Her 1991 article "Mapping the Margins" (Stanford Law Review) extended the theory to structural, political, and representational dimensions of violence against women of colour. Intersectionality has since become a central framework in sociology, law, and public policy, and a frequent object of political controversy.

Advocacy

Crenshaw co-founded the African American Policy Forum (AAPF) think tank and serves as its executive director. The AAPF's #SayHerName campaign (launched 2014–15) publicises the killings of Black women by police. She hosts the podcast Intersectionality Matters! and has been a prominent public commentator in debates over critical race theory in schools, which she has defended while noting that the legislative campaigns against it often target a caricature of the field.
