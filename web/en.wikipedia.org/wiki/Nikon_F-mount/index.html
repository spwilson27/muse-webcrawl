Nikon F-mount

The Nikon F-mount is a bayonet lens mount introduced by Nikon in 1959 with the Nikon F camera. With a 44 mm throat diameter and 46.5 mm flange focal distance, it remained in continuous use for over six decades — one of the longest-lived mounts in photography — across Nikon's film and digital SLRs.

Evolution

The basic three-lug bayonet stayed unchanged while the systems around it evolved: pre-AI lenses (1959–77), AI and AI-S automatic-indexing lenses, autofocus AF and AF-D lenses with mechanical drive, AF-S lenses with in-lens motors, and electronically controlled AF-P and E-type apertures. Later bodies variously dropped mechanical couplings, so compatibility between any given lens–body pair depends on its generation.

Legacy

Because the mount persisted, vast numbers of Nikkor lenses — and third-party lenses from Sigma, Tamron, Tokina and others — remain usable on modern bodies. Nikon ended F-mount DSLR production as it moved to the mirrorless Z-mount (2018), with F lenses usable via the FTZ adapter.
