Granita (in Italian also granita siciliana) is a semi-frozen dessert made from sugar, water, and various flavorings, originating in Sicily. It is related to sorbet and Italian ice, but has a coarser, crystalline texture because it is frozen without churning and is scraped with a fork as it sets.

History

Granita descends from the sherbet drinks brought to Sicily during Arab rule, when snow was collected from Mount Etna, stored in neviere ice houses, and flavored with fruit and flowers. Sicilian towns developed distinct styles: in Catania the texture is coarse and often chocolate-based; in Palermo and Trapani, lemon, almond, and mulberry versions predominate.

Serving

In Sicily, granita is a breakfast food in summer, served with a soft brioche col tuppo for dipping, often topped with whipped cream. Classic flavors include lemon, coffee, almond, pistachio, mulberry (gelsi), and jasmine. Espresso granita with cream is a Roman specialty. Unlike a snow cone, which tops crushed ice with syrup, granita's flavor is frozen throughout the mixture.
