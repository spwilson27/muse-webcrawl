Maxwell relations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Maxwell's relations are a set of equations in thermodynamics which are derivable from the symmetry of second derivatives and from the definitions of the thermodynamic potentials. These relations are named for physicist James Clerk Maxwell. They express the equality of certain second mixed partial derivatives of the thermodynamic potentials with respect to their natural variables.

The four Maxwell relations

From the internal energy U(S, V), enthalpy H(S, P), Helmholtz free energy A(T, V) and Gibbs free energy G(T, P) one obtains respectively:

(∂T/∂V)S = −(∂P/∂S)V;   (∂T/∂P)S = (∂V/∂S)P;
(∂S/∂V)T = (∂P/∂T)V;   (∂S/∂P)T = −(∂V/∂T)P

Derivation

The relations follow from Clairaut's theorem on the equality of mixed partials applied to the exact differentials of the four thermodynamic potentials, for example dU = T dS − P dV and dG = −S dT + V dP. Because these potentials are state functions, their second cross-derivatives must be equal, which generates the relations directly.

Use

Maxwell relations allow quantities that are hard to measure — such as the change of entropy with volume or pressure — to be replaced by easily measured quantities such as thermal expansion and compressibility. They are fundamental to deriving the thermodynamic equations of state used in chemistry and engineering.
