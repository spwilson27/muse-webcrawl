Golden Spike National Historical Park

Golden Spike National Historical Park is a United States National Historical Park located at Promontory Summit, north of the Great Salt Lake in east-central Box Elder County, Utah. It commemorates the completion of the first transcontinental railroad, where the Central Pacific Railroad and the Union Pacific Railroad met on May 10, 1869.

The Golden Spike ceremony

At the ceremony, Central Pacific president Leland Stanford drove the final, ceremonial "Golden Spike" — an 18-karat gold spike — into a pre-drilled hole in a laurelwood tie, joining the rails of the two companies. The event was telegraphed instantly across the country and celebrated as one of the great engineering achievements of the nineteenth century. The original golden spike is now held by the Cantor Arts Center at Stanford University.

The park

Authorised as a National Historic Site in 1965 and redesignated a National Historical Park in the John D. Dingell Jr. Conservation, Management, and Recreation Act of 2019, the park operates working replicas of the two locomotives present in 1869 — the Central Pacific's Jupiter and Union Pacific's No. 119 — which are re-enacted nose-to-nose each summer. The park also preserves grades, cuts and fills of both railroads stretching miles in either direction, and hosts an annual re-enactment on May 10.
