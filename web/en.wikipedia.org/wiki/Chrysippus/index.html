Chrysippus

Chrysippus of Soli
Born: c. 279 BCE, Soli, Cilicia
Died: c. 206 BCE, Athens
School: Stoicism
Role: Third head (scholarch) of the Stoa

Chrysippus of Soli (c. 279 – c. 206 BCE) was a Greek Stoic philosopher, the third head of the Stoic school in Athens after Zeno of Citium and Cleanthes. He systematised Stoicism so thoroughly that it was said, 'Had there been no Chrysippus, there would have been no Stoa.'

Works and thought

Reputedly the author of over 700 works — none surviving complete — Chrysippus developed Stoic logic (a propositional logic rivalling Aristotle's syllogistic), physics, and ethics. He defended determinism and fate while arguing for moral responsibility (compatibilism), using examples such as the cylinder whose rolling depends on both a push and its own shape. He wrote extensively on the passions, which he treated as false judgements.

Death

Ancient sources give colourful accounts of his death, one claiming he died of laughter after seeing a donkey eat figs and joking it should be given wine to wash them down — a story likely apocryphal.
