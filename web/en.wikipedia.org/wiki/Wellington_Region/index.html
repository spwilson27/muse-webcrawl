Wellington Region

The Wellington Region (Māori: Te Whanganui-a-Tara) is a local government region of New Zealand that occupies the southern tip of the North Island. It is administered by the Wellington Regional Council, branded as Greater Wellington. The region covers an area of about 8,049 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 550,000 in the mid-2020s, making it the third most populous region in the country.

Geography

The region is centred on Wellington Harbour (Port Nicholson) and extends north along both coasts of the lower North Island. It includes the cities of Wellington, Porirua, Lower Hutt and Upper Hutt, which together form the Wellington metropolitan area, as well as the Kapiti Coast district and the three Wairarapa districts of Masterton, Carterton and South Wairarapa. The landscape is dominated by fault-bounded hill ranges — the Remutaka, Tararua and Aorangi ranges — separated by the Hutt Valley and the Wairarapa plain, which contains Lake Wairarapa, one of New Zealand's largest lakes.

History

Māori settlement of the area dates back many centuries, with iwi including Ngāti Ira, Rangitāne, Muaūpoko, Ngāti Toa and Te Āti Awa associated with the region. European settlement began in 1840 under the New Zealand Company. Wellington became the capital of New Zealand in 1865. The regional council in its modern form dates from local government reforms in 1989.

Economy and transport

As the seat of central government, the region's economy is dominated by public administration, professional services, finance, education and a growing film and digital sector centred on companies such as Weta Workshop. The region is served by Wellington International Airport, the Port of Wellington, the Interislander and Bluebridge Cook Strait ferries, and a suburban rail network of five lines converging on Wellington railway station.
