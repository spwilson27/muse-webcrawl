PDP-8

PDP-8
Manufacturer: Digital Equipment Corporation
Type: 12-bit minicomputer
Introduced: 1965
Units sold: over 50,000

The PDP-8 is a family of 12-bit minicomputers produced by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC). Introduced in 1965, the original PDP-8 is generally regarded as the first commercially successful minicomputer.

Design

The PDP-8 used a very small instruction set built around a single accumulator, with a basic model offering 4,096 12-bit words of core memory. Its low price — around US$18,500 for the original model — put computing within reach of laboratories, schools and small companies for the first time.

Models

Successive models included the PDP-8/I and PDP-8/E, among the first computers built with integrated circuits, and the CMOS-based Intersil 6120 chip, which put the PDP-8 architecture into a single microprocessor used in DEC's DECmate word processors.

Significance

More than 50,000 systems were sold, making the PDP-8 the best-selling computer of its era. It established the minicomputer industry and the OEM business model, and its simple design remains a popular subject for emulation and study.
