Shawwal (Arabic: شَوَّال, Shawwāl) is the tenth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, following Ramadan and preceding Dhu al-Qa'dah. The name is traditionally derived from an Arabic word connected with the raising or carrying of a tail, said to refer to the season when camels were pregnant or raised their tails; the month was named before Islam and retained in the Islamic calendar.

Eid al-Fitr

The first day of Shawwal is Eid al-Fitr, the festival marking the end of the month-long dawn-to-sunset fasting of Ramadan. Fasting is prohibited on this day. Muslims mark it with a special congregational prayer, festive meals, gifts (especially to children), and the obligatory charity Zakat al-Fitr, given before the Eid prayer so that the poor can also celebrate. The festival is a public holiday across most Muslim-majority countries.

Six days of Shawwal

A well-known hadith states that whoever fasts Ramadan and then follows it with six days of Shawwal is rewarded as if they had fasted the whole year, since each good deed is counted tenfold (Ramadan counting as ten months and the six days as two more). Many Muslims therefore fast six additional days in Shawwal, consecutively or spread through the month; scholars differ on details, and the practice is voluntary, unlike the Ramadan fast.

Other observances

The month also sees the beginning of the Hajj season — Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'dah, and Dhu al-Hijjah are the "months of Hajj" named in the Qur'an — though the pilgrimage rites themselves occur in Dhu al-Hijjah. Weddings are traditionally considered blessed in Shawwal, recalling the marriage of Muhammad to Aisha, which took place in this month.
