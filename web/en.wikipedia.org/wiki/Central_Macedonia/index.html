Central Macedonia

Central Macedonia (Greek: Κεντρική Μακεδονία, Kentriki Makedonia) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece, consisting of the central part of the geographical and historical region of Macedonia. With a population of about 1.8 million, it is the country's second-most populous region. Its capital and largest city is Thessaloniki, Greece's second city and a major port on the Thermaic Gulf.

Geography

The region stretches from Mount Olympus, the highest mountain in Greece, in the south to the borders with North Macedonia and Bulgaria in the north, and includes the Chalkidiki peninsula, famous for its resorts and for Mount Athos, the self-governing monastic community. Major rivers include the Axios (Vardar), Aliakmon, Strymon, and Loudias, whose plains form some of Greece's most productive farmland.

Economy and history

Central Macedonia is Greece's principal agricultural export region, producing peaches, rice, tobacco, cotton, and wine, and it is a centre of food processing and logistics. The region contains the archaeological sites of Vergina (ancient Aigai), Pella, and Dion, capitals and sanctuaries of the ancient Macedonian kingdom. The administrative region was created in the reforms of 1987 and adjusted under the Kallikratis programme.
