Mount Waddington is the highest peak entirely within the Canadian province of British Columbia, rising 4,019 metres (13,186 ft) in the remote Waddington Range of the Coast Mountains. Surrounded by vast icefields and some of the most rugged terrain in North America, it is renowned among mountaineers for its severe weather and difficult access.

Exploration and first ascent

The peak was sighted by surveyors in the 19th century and named for entrepreneur Alfred Waddington. In the 1920s and 1930s its true height and location were disputed — it was known as "Mystery Mountain" — until aerial surveys fixed its position. After several attempts, Fritz Wiessner and William House made the first ascent in 1936, a landmark climb in North American mountaineering.

Geography

The mountain overlooks the Franklin and Tiedemann glaciers and the head of Knight and Bute Inlets. Approaches typically require helicopter support or long ski traverses, and the peak sees few ascents compared with other major Canadian summits.
