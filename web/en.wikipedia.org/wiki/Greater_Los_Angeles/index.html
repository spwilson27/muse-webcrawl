Greater Los Angeles

Country: United States

State: California

Principal city: Los Angeles

Population (CSA, 2020): about 18.6 million

Greater Los Angeles is the most populous metropolitan area in the U.S. state of California, encompassing five counties in Southern California extending from Ventura County in the west to San Bernardino County and Riverside County in the east, with Los Angeles County in the center and Orange County to the southeast. The combined statistical area (CSA) is the second-largest in the United States, after that of New York City.

Definitions

The term "Greater Los Angeles" most often refers to the Los Angeles–Long Beach, CA Combined Statistical Area, defined by the U.S. Census Bureau. It comprises the Los Angeles metropolitan area (Los Angeles and Orange counties) and the Inland Empire (San Bernardino and Riverside counties), together with Ventura County.

Geography

The region spans a broad coastal plain bounded by mountains, including the San Gabriel Mountains and San Bernardino Mountains, and extends inland across desert valleys. Its urbanized area is famous for extensive suburban sprawl linked by a large freeway network.

Demographics

With a population of roughly 18.6 million at the 2020 census, Greater Los Angeles is one of the most ethnically diverse metropolitan regions in the world, with large Hispanic and Latino, Asian American, and other communities. Its economy, if counted as a separate entity, would rank among the largest of any metropolitan region globally.

Transportation

The region is served by Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) and several other commercial airports, the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach (together among the busiest container port complexes in the world), an extensive freeway system, and Metrolink commuter rail.
