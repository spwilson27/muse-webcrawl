Ha'il (Arabic: حائل, Ḥāʾil) is a city in north-western Saudi Arabia and the capital of Ha'il Province. Situated in the Shammar Mountains at the foot of Mount Aja, it lies on historical caravan and pilgrimage routes crossing the Nafud desert.

History

From the 1830s until 1921 Ha'il was the capital of the Emirate of Jabal Shammar, ruled by the Rashid dynasty, long rivals of the Saudis of Riyadh. Ibn Saud captured the city in 1921, completing the unification of Najd. The city was also a notable stopping point described by travellers such as Charles Doughty and Lady Anne Blunt, and is associated with the legendary generous figure Hatim al-Tai.

Modern city

Today Ha'il is a regional agricultural and commercial centre known for the annual Ha'il Rally (Baja) desert motor race and for nearby rock-art sites of Jabal Umm Sinman at Jubbah, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list.
