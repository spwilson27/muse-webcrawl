The Marias massacre (also known as the Baker massacre) was the killing of a camp of peaceful Piegan Blackfeet by United States Army troops on 23 January 1870, along the Marias River in Montana Territory.

Background

The Army sought a band under Mountain Chief blamed for raids and the killing of a rancher. Major Eugene M. Baker's command instead attacked the camp of Heavy Runner, a chief who held a written safe-conduct from the Indian Bureau and whose people were suffering from smallpox.

The attack

At dawn Baker's soldiers fired into the sleeping camp. Estimates of the dead range from 173 to 217, overwhelmingly women, children and old men; the camp was then burned. Mountain Chief's band was not found.

Aftermath

Public outrage in the East contributed to President Grant's Peace Policy toward Native Americans. No one was punished, and Baker's career continued. The massacre is remembered by the Blackfeet Nation with annual commemorations.
