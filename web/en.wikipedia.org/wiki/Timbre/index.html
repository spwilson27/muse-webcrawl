Timbre

In music, timbre (pronounced /ˈtæmbər/), also known as tone colour or tone quality, is the perceived sound quality of a musical note, sound or tone. Timbre is what allows a listener to distinguish two sounds that have the same pitch and loudness — for example, a clarinet and an oboe playing the same note. The American National Standards Institute defines timbre as the attribute by which a listener can judge two sounds with the same loudness and pitch to be dissimilar.

Physical basis

Timbre arises principally from the harmonic spectrum of a sound — the relative strengths of the fundamental and its overtones — together with its temporal envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release) and transient features such as the breath noise of a flute or the hammer strike of a piano. Helmholtz's 19th-century work on sensation of tone established that steady-state spectrum is the dominant cue.

Perception

Perceptual studies, including multidimensional scaling of listener judgements, show that timbre perception is multidimensional and cannot be captured by a single scale. Descriptive words used by musicians — bright, dark, warm, reedy, brassy — map loosely onto spectral centroid and other measurable features.
