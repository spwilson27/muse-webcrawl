Conservative Party (UK)

The Conservative and Unionist Party, generally known as the Conservative Party or the Tories, is a centre-right to right-wing political party in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1834 from the Tory Party and the Peelite faction, it is one of the two dominant parties in British politics alongside Labour, and has governed the UK for long periods totalling more than any other party.

History

Robert Peel's Tamworth Manifesto (1834) founded modern Conservatism. The party split over repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846, dominated late-Victorian politics under Disraeli and Salisbury, and governed much of the 20th century under Churchill, Macmillan, and Margaret Thatcher (1979–1990), whose Thatcherism reshaped the British economy. After defeat in 1997, the party returned to power in 2010 under David Cameron in coalition, won majorities in 2015 and 2019 under Cameron and Boris Johnson — overseeing Brexit — then suffered a heavy defeat at the 2024 general election and moved into opposition.

Ideology and factions

Conservatism in the party spans One Nation conservatism, free-market Thatcherites, and socially conservative traditionalists, with recurring divisions over Europe, taxation, and immigration.

Organisation

The leader is elected by MPs and members. The party is strongest historically in England, particularly the South East and rural constituencies.
