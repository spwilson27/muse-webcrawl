Wonderbag

A Wonderbag is a non-electric, portable heat-retention cooker (a modern form of the haybox) developed in South Africa. Food is brought to the boil in a pot on a stove, and the pot is then placed inside the insulated bag, where cooking continues slowly for several hours using only the retained heat.

History

The Wonderbag was developed by South African entrepreneur Sarah Collins, who launched the product in 2008 during a period of severe rolling blackouts (load-shedding) in South Africa. Collins recalled the traditional haybox cookers used on farms and designed a compact version filled with recycled polystyrene insulation and covered in fabric, including traditional shweshwe prints. The venture operated on a buy-one-give-one model, with purchased bags subsidising bags distributed to low-income households.

Use

To cook with a Wonderbag, a dish such as a stew, curry, rice or soup is first heated to boiling point and simmered briefly — typically five to fifteen minutes — on an ordinary stove. The lidded pot is then set on the bag's base cushion, the insulating top cushion is placed over it, and the drawstring is pulled tight. Inside, temperatures stay high enough to continue cooking for up to about twelve hours, tenderising meat and cooking grains without further fuel.

Benefits

Because the bulk of cooking happens off the fire, the device substantially reduces consumption of firewood, paraffin, gas or electricity — savings of roughly a third to a half of cooking fuel are commonly claimed. In communities dependent on gathered firewood, this lessens deforestation and the time (usually women's and girls' time) spent collecting fuel, and reduces indoor smoke exposure and burn risks. The Wonderbag has attracted carbon-credit financing and has been distributed through partnerships in South Africa and other African countries; by the mid-2010s hundreds of thousands of units had been sold or given away.
