The Sevan–Hrazdan Cascade is a complex of seven hydroelectric power stations on the Hrazdan River in Armenia. The cascade exploits the drop of roughly 1,000 metres between Lake Sevan and the Ararat plain, using the river's flow both for electricity generation and, in combination with canals, for irrigation of farmland around Yerevan.

History

The scheme was developed under the Soviet Union, with construction beginning in the 1930s and the stations completed by the early 1960s. The stations, from upstream to downstream, include Sevan, Hrazdan, Argel, Arzni, Kanaker, Yerevan-1, and Yerevan-3. The heavy use of Lake Sevan's water in the mid-20th century contributed to a major fall in the lake's level, which later Armenian policy sought to reverse by restricting outflows and diverting other rivers into the lake.

Operation

The cascade has an installed capacity of about 560 MW and historically supplied a significant share of Armenia's electricity. It was privatised in the 2000s and later acquired by RusHydro's Armenian subsidiary; its stations have undergone refurbishment programmes to extend their working lives.
