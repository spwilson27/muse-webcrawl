The Bakhtiari people (Persian: مردم بختیاری) are an Iranian tribal group inhabiting the Zagros Mountains of southwestern Iran, principally in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province and adjoining parts of Khuzestan, Isfahan and Lorestan. They speak the Bakhtiari dialect of Luri, a Southwestern Iranian language closely related to Persian. Traditionally nomadic pastoralists, the Bakhtiari are famous for one of the world's most arduous seasonal migrations, and they played a decisive role in modern Iranian history, including the Persian Constitutional Revolution.

Organisation and migration

Bakhtiari society is organised in a hierarchy of tribes, clans and families under khans. The confederation is traditionally divided into two great branches, the Haft Lang ("seven legs") and the Chahar Lang ("four legs"). Nomadic families undertake the kooch, migrating twice a year between winter quarters (garmsir) in the lowlands of Khuzestan and summer pastures (sardsir) high in the Zagros — crossing passes above 3,000 metres, sometimes through snow, with all their livestock and possessions. The journey was documented in Merian C. Cooper's pioneering 1925 documentary film Grass: A Nation's Battle for Life. Since the mid-20th century many Bakhtiari have settled in towns and cities such as Shahrekord, Izeh and Masjed Soleyman, though seasonal migration continues among some families.

History

The Bakhtiari rose to prominence under Safavid and Qajar rule, providing cavalry to the shahs. In 1909 Bakhtiari tribal forces under Sardar As'ad marched on Tehran and helped restore the constitution during the Persian Constitutional Revolution — an event that made the khans kingmakers in national politics. The discovery of oil at Masjed Soleyman in 1908, in Bakhtiari territory, brought the Anglo-Persian Oil Company into the region and transformed tribal fortunes; Bakhtiari khans received a share of revenues and guarded the oilfields and pipelines. Under Reza Shah in the 1930s, the tribes were forcibly settled and their khans' power broken, with devastating losses of livestock. Notable Bakhtiari include Soraya Esfandiary-Bakhtiary, the second wife of Mohammad Reza Shah, and the poet Pejman Bakhtiari.

Culture

Bakhtiari culture is expressed in distinctive dress — men wear wide black felt trousers (dabit), striped woollen cloaks (choqa) and round felt caps — in woven carpets and gelims, epic oral poetry, and wedding and funeral ceremonies accompanied by dohol and sorna music. Women wear brightly layered coin-trimmed headdresses and embroidered velvet jackets, and are renowned as weavers.
