Garryaceae is a small family of flowering plants in the order Garryales. In the APG IV system it comprises two genera: Garrya, the silk-tassel bushes of western North America and Central America, and Aucuba, the spotted laurels of eastern Asia, which was formerly placed in its own family, Aucubaceae.

Description

Members of the family are evergreen shrubs or small trees with simple, opposite, leathery leaves. The plants are dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate individuals. Flowers are small and wind-pollinated, borne in catkin-like inflorescences in Garrya and in panicles in Aucuba. The fruit is a berry containing one or two seeds.

Distribution

Garrya species occur from the western United States through Mexico to Guatemala and in the Caribbean, while Aucuba is native to the Himalayas, China, Korea and Japan. Both genera are widely grown as ornamental garden shrubs.

Classification

The family is placed in the order Garryales together with the Eucommiaceae. Earlier systems placed Garrya in the Cornaceae or in a monotypic family of its own; molecular phylogenetic studies support the two-genus circumscription adopted by APG.
