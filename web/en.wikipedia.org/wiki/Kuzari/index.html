Kuzari

The Kuzari, full title The Book of Refutation and Proof on Behalf of the Despised Religion, is a work of Jewish philosophy written in Judeo-Arabic by the Spanish rabbi and poet Judah Halevi around 1140, shortly before his departure for the Land of Israel. It is structured as a dialogue between the pagan king of the Khazars and a rabbi.

Frame story

The work draws on the story of the conversion of the Khazar king — traditionally named Bulan — who, seeking the true religion, interviews a philosopher, a Christian scholar, a Muslim scholar and finally a Jewish rabbi. Dissatisfied with the abstract God of the philosophers, the king is drawn to the rabbi's appeal to historical experience: the revelation at Sinai witnessed by an entire people.

Philosophy

Halevi argues that religious truth is grounded not in metaphysical speculation but in the historical experience of Israel, transmitted through an unbroken tradition. The dialogue defends Judaism as revealed religion, explores the superiority claimed for prophecy and the Land of Israel, and critiques rationalist philosophy. Judah ibn Tibbon's Hebrew translation made the work widely accessible, and it became one of the most influential texts of medieval Jewish thought.

Influence

The Kuzari has been studied continuously for nearly nine centuries, influencing Jewish apologetics, Kabbalah, and modern thinkers; interpretations range from rationalist readings by scholars such as Leo Strauss to fideistic readings emphasizing the primacy of revelation.
