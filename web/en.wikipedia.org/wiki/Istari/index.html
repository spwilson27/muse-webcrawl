Istari

The Istari, the order of Wizards sent to Middle-earth in the Third Age.

The Istari (singular Istar) are a fictional order of Wizards in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium of Middle-earth. In the Quenya language their name means "the Wise Ones." They were Maiar — angelic spirits of the same order as Sauron — sent by the Valar from Valinor around the year 1000 of the Third Age to aid the free peoples against the rising shadow of Sauron.

The five Wizards

Five Istari are named: Saruman the White, chief of the order; Gandalf the Grey (later the White); Radagast the Brown, concerned chiefly with animals and plants; and the two Blue Wizards, Alatar and Pallando, who travelled into the far east and south of Middle-earth and did not return in the tales told in the West.

Mission and constraints

The Istari were forbidden to match Sauron in direct displays of power or to dominate Elves and Men by force or fear. They were embodied in the forms of old men, subject to hunger, weariness, and pain, and were to work by counsel and encouragement. Of the five, only Gandalf remained faithful to the mission to its end; Saruman fell into pride and sought the One Ring for himself, while Radagast became absorbed in the natural world and largely withdrew from the struggle.

In Tolkien's writings

The Istari appear in The Lord of the Rings and are discussed at length in Unfinished Tales, in the essay "The Istari," which describes their choosing by the Valar and their arrival by ship at the Grey Havens, where Círdan entrusted Gandalf with the ring Narya.
