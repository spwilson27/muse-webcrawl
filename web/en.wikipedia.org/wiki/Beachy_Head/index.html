Beachy Head is a chalk headland in East Sussex, England, immediately east of the Seven Sisters cliffs. It is the highest chalk sea cliff in Britain, rising to 162 metres (531 ft) above sea level. The peak allows views from Dungeness in the east to the Isle of Wight in the west. The name is a corruption of the French Beauchef ("beautiful headland"), recorded from the 13th century.

Geography and geology

The cliffs are part of the South Downs chalk ridge meeting the English Channel. The chalk is continually eroded by the sea, and rockfalls are frequent; the cliff face slowly retreats while remaining nearly vertical. Below the headland stands the Beachy Head Lighthouse, built in 1902 at the foot of the cliff (replacing the earlier Belle Tout lighthouse of 1834, which stands on the downs above), its red-and-white bands painted to be visible by day.

History

The headland gave its name to the Battle of Beachy Head (1690), a French naval victory over an Anglo-Dutch fleet during the Nine Years' War. The area has long been notorious as a suicide location — among the most frequented in the world — and is patrolled by volunteer chaplaincy teams; the subject is handled sensitively by local authorities, and the cliffs are also prized by walkers on the South Downs Way, birdwatchers and paragliders. The headland and surrounding downland lie within the South Downs National Park and are largely owned and conserved by Eastbourne Borough Council.

In culture

Beachy Head appears in films, television and literature, from the 1979 film Quadrophenia to numerous period dramas; its stark white cliffs are an emblem of the Sussex coast.
