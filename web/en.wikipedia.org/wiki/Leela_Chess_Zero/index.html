Leela Chess Zero (abbreviated LcZero, Lc0) is a free and open-source chess engine and distributed computing project, based on DeepMind's AlphaZero ideas and adapted from the Leela Zero Go engine. Like AlphaZero, Leela Chess Zero learns chess entirely through self-play reinforcement learning, using a deep neural network combined with Monte Carlo tree search, rather than a hand-crafted evaluation function.

History

The project was announced in early 2018, shortly after DeepMind published details of AlphaZero's victories over Stockfish. Programmer Gary Linscott, a Stockfish developer, and Gian-Carlo Pascutto, author of the Leela Zero Go engine, helped launch the effort to reproduce AlphaZero-style learning on commodity hardware. Training was crowdsourced: thousands of volunteers contributed GPU time through a distributed client that played self-play games and uploaded them to a central server, which periodically produced stronger networks.

Strength and competition

Leela Chess Zero rose quickly to the top tier of computer chess. It won the Top Chess Engine Championship (TCEC) in season 15 (2019), defeating Stockfish in the superfinal, and won further TCEC titles and cup events in subsequent seasons. Its play is noted for a positional, human-like style, long-term piece sacrifices and strong opening preparation derived from its network evaluations. The engine runs best on GPUs; a version called "LeelaFish" was also adapted for hybrid use alongside Stockfish.

Technology

LcZero uses the UCI protocol and is compatible with most chess graphical interfaces. Its search combines the neural network's policy and value outputs with a variant of the PUCT algorithm. Networks distributed by the project have ranged from small sizes runnable on CPUs to large transformer-influenced architectures requiring modern graphics cards.
