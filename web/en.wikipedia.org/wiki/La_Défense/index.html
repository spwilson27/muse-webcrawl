La Défense

La Défense (French pronunciation: [la de.fɑ̃s]) is a major business district in France, located 3 kilometres (1.9 mi) west of the city limits of Paris. It is part of the Paris metropolitan area and straddles the communes of Puteaux, Courbevoie and Nanterre in the Hauts-de-Seine department. La Défense is Europe's largest purpose-built business district, with some 3.5 million square metres of office space, around 180,000 daily workers and the headquarters or major offices of many of France's and the world's largest companies.

History

The district takes its name from a monument to the soldiers who defended Paris during the Franco-Prussian War, erected in 1883. Development as an office district began in 1958 with the creation of a public development authority (EPAD), explicitly to concentrate modern towers outside the historic centre, along the extension of Paris's historic axis westward from the Louvre and the Champs-Élysées through the Arc de Triomphe. The first landmark was the CNIT exhibition hall (1958); office towers followed from the mid-1960s. Successive generations of towers grew taller, and in 1989 the Grande Arche — a vast hollow cube designed by Johann Otto von Spreckelsen — was completed as the axis's culminating monument for the bicentennial of the French Revolution.

Architecture and layout

La Défense is built on a raised pedestrian deck (the dalle) above its roads and car parks, so that the esplanade is largely traffic-free. It contains several of Europe's tallest buildings, including Tour First, Tour Majunga and the twin towers of Hermitage Plaza (project). Public art is integral to the plan, with works by Joan Miró, Alexander Calder and others. The central esplanade is served by one of France's busiest transport interchanges, connecting Métro line 1, RER A, Transilien trains, trams and buses.

Economy

The district hosts the headquarters of major French groups in banking, insurance, energy and pharmaceuticals, along with the Paris La Défense Arena, one of Europe's largest indoor venues (opened 2017). Management of the district today rests with the local public establishment Paris La Défense, created in 2018.
