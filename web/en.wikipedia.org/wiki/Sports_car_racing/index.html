Sports car racing


Endurance racing is the flagship form of sports car competition.

Sports car racing is a form of motor racing involving highly modified, purpose-built racing cars and production-based grand tourers, competing on road courses and street circuits. It is distinguished by endurance racing — contests lasting from several hours to a full day, with teams of two or more drivers sharing each car — and by multi-class fields in which prototypes and GT cars race simultaneously.

Major series and events

The crown event is the 24 Hours of Le Mans, first held in 1923 and now the centrepiece of the FIA World Endurance Championship. In North America, the IMSA SportsCar Championship includes the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring. Historic classics include the Mille Miglia and Targa Florio road races.

Classes

Top-level entries are prototypes — currently the Hypercar class at Le Mans and the GTP class in IMSA — followed by LMP2 cars and production-based GT3 machinery. Balance-of-performance rules are used to equalise competing designs.

History

Sports car racing grew out of early twentieth-century road racing in Europe. The World Sportscar Championship (1953–1992) and the spectacular Group C era of the 1980s produced iconic machinery from manufacturers such as Porsche, Jaguar, Audi, and Toyota.
