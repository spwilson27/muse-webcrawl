Mikoshi

Mikoshi
Japanese神輿
ReligionShinto
PurposePortable shrine transporting a kami during festivals
Typical weightUp to about one tonne for large examples

A mikoshi (神輿) is a sacred portable Shinto shrine in which a kami (deity) is believed to ride while being carried through a neighbourhood during a festival (matsuri). Shaped like a miniature, richly decorated building — often lacquered black and gold, with a roof crowned by a phoenix or other ornament — it is borne on poles on the shoulders of dozens of carriers dressed in festival coats (happi), who chant rhythmically ("wasshoi") and deliberately rock and jolt the shrine, a motion thought to entertain the kami and spread its blessings.

Function and ritual

Before a festival, priests transfer the kami from the main shrine (honden) into the mikoshi in a ceremony called senza-sai. The procession tours the parish (ujiko) so the deity can bless the community, its homes and businesses, before the spirit is returned. Famous mikoshi processions include Tokyo's Sanja Matsuri at Asakusa, where around one hundred mikoshi parade, some weighing about a tonne and carried by rotating teams.

Construction

Mikoshi are built by specialist craftsmen from wood, lacquered and adorned with gold leaf, metal fittings, silk cords and bells (in some traditions). Their design echoes shrine architecture or a palanquin (norimono) used by nobility; related forms include the smaller children's mikoshi and the floats (dashi) of other festivals.
