Death of Ayrton Senna

The death of Brazilian racing driver Ayrton Senna occurred on 1 May 1994 during the San Marino Grand Prix at the Imola Circuit in Italy.

Senna, driving for Williams, crashed at the high-speed Tamburello corner on lap 7 while leading the race. A piece of suspension penetrated his helmet, causing fatal head injuries. His death, a day after Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying, shocked the sporting world.

Aftermath

Brazil declared three days of national mourning, and an estimated three million people attended Senna's funeral procession in São Paulo. The tragedies prompted major safety reforms in Formula One, including redesigned circuits, improved crash structures and the eventual introduction of the halo device. Italian prosecutors pursued a lengthy manslaughter case centred on the car's modified steering column.
