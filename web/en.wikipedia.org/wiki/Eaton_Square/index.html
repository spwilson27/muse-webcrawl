Eaton Square is a large rectangular garden square in Belgravia, London, and one of the grandest residential squares in the city. Laid out from 1827 on land belonging to the Grosvenor family, it was built principally by the master builder Thomas Cubitt, with facades designed by Cubitt and by Seth Smith. The square takes its name from Eaton Hall in Cheshire, the Grosvenors' country seat.

Architecture and layout

Eaton Square is unusually elongated — some 365 metres (about 1,200 feet) long — and is divided by King's Road into two unequal parts. It consists of six groups of three-storey stucco-fronted terraces in the Italianate style, most of which are Grade II* listed buildings. The central gardens are private to residents. Many of the houses, originally single family residences, have since been converted into apartments, company headquarters and diplomatic premises.

Notable residents

The square has long been associated with political and aristocratic figures. Neville Chamberlain lived at No. 37 before becoming Prime Minister, and the actress Vivien Leigh lived at No. 54 from 1956 until her death. The square and its surroundings have frequently appeared among the most expensive residential addresses in Britain. St Peter's Church, a classical-style Anglican church, stands at the eastern end of the square.
