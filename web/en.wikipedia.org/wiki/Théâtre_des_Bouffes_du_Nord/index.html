Théâtre des Bouffes du Nord
Address: 37 bis boulevard de la Chapelle, 10th arrondissement, Paris
Capacity: approx. 530
Opened: 1876
Known for: Peter Brook's seasons, 1974–2008

The Théâtre des Bouffes du Nord is a theatre at 37 bis boulevard de la Chapelle in the 10th arrondissement of Paris, near the Gare du Nord. Built in 1876, it enjoyed early success before decades of decline; it was rescued from dereliction in 1974 by director Peter Brook and producer Micheline Rozan, who made it the home of their International Centre for Theatre Research and one of the most celebrated experimental theatre spaces in the world.

The theatre's deliberately unrestored auditorium, with its scarred, faded walls.

History

The theatre opened in 1876 and passed through many hands and uses — café-concert, revue, and popular drama — before closing and falling into ruin after the Second World War. When Brook and Rozan took it over in 1974 they restored it only as much as safety required, preserving its fire-scarred, peeling interior. Brook regarded the raw, burned shell as an ideal "rough space": intimate, flexible, and charged with atmosphere.

Peter Brook era

From 1974 to 2008 the Bouffes du Nord hosted Brook's landmark productions, including Timon of Athens, The Conference of the Birds (1979), the nine-hour stage adaptation of the Mahabharata (1985), The Tempest, Don Giovanni, The Suit, and minimalist versions of Hamlet and Bizet's Carmen (La Tragédie de Carmen). Brook's international company, drawn from many countries and theatrical traditions, performed in a stripped, actor-centred style that influenced theatre worldwide.

Recent years

Brook stepped down in 2008, passing artistic direction to Olivier Mantei and Olivier Poubelle, who have maintained a programme of theatre, opera, and concerts. The building, listed as a historic monument, remains famous for its unique acoustics and haunting dilapidated beauty.
