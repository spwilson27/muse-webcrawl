Katyń is a 2007 Polish historical drama film directed by Andrzej Wajda, about the Katyn massacre of 1940, in which the Soviet NKVD murdered some 22,000 Polish officers and intellectuals. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. The subject was deeply personal to Wajda: his father, Jakub Wajda, was among the officers murdered at Kharkiv, while his mother survived.

Plot

The film follows several interwoven stories from the viewpoint of the victims' families. Anna, the wife of cavalry officer Andrzej, waits in vain for her husband's return and refuses to believe he is dead; other strands follow a general's wife in Kraków, the sister of a murdered pilot who defies both Nazi and communist propaganda over her brother's gravestone, and a young man torn over whether to serve the new communist order. The stories converge on the truth of the massacre, which the final, unflinching sequence depicts directly, showing the systematic execution of the prisoners.

Production and reception

The film premiered on 17 September 2007, the anniversary of the Soviet invasion of Poland in 1939, and was a major cultural event in Poland, where it was seen by millions. International critics praised its restrained, elegiac tone and its focus on the "Katyn lie" — the decades in which the USSR blamed the massacre on Nazi Germany. It was released in Russia only in limited screenings, amid continuing sensitivities over the subject.
