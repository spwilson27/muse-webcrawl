Colorado River (Texas)

The Colorado River is an approximately 862-mile (1,387 km) river in the U.S. state of Texas. It is the 18th longest river in the United States and the longest river with both its source and its mouth within Texas. It should not be confused with the larger Colorado River of the southwestern United States and Mexico.

Course

The river rises on the Llano Estacado near Lamesa in Dawson County and flows generally southeast across the Edwards Plateau and central Texas, through the Hill Country and the city of Austin, then across the coastal plain to empty into Matagorda Bay on the Gulf of Mexico near Matagorda. Major tributaries include the Concho, San Saba, Llano, Pedernales and Guadalupe rivers.

Dams and lakes

A chain of reservoirs known as the Texas Highland Lakes — including Lake Buchanan, Inks Lake, Lake LBJ, Lake Marble Falls, Lake Travis and Lady Bird Lake in Austin — was built in the 1930s and 1940s by the Lower Colorado River Authority for flood control, hydroelectric power and water supply after devastating floods, notably those of 1900 and 1935. The river is intensively managed and is a principal water source for Austin and downstream users, including rice irrigation on the coastal plain.

History

Spanish explorers were the first Europeans to document the river; its name (Spanish for "reddish") refers to the muddy, red-tinted colour of its upper waters, and was at times applied confusingly to the Brazos River. The river has supported settlement since Stephen F. Austin's colony was established along its lower reaches in the 1820s. The river remains central to central Texas's water supply amid recurring drought and rapid population growth.
