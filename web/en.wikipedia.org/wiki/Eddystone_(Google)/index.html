Eddystone was an open Bluetooth Low Energy beacon profile developed by Google and released in July 2015. Named after the Eddystone Lighthouse in England, it defined several frame types that small radio transmitters ("beacons") could broadcast to nearby smartphones for location-based services, and it served as a successor to Google's involvement in the "Physical Web" concept.

Frame types

Eddystone defined four frame types: Eddystone-UID, broadcasting a unique 16-byte beacon identifier; Eddystone-URL, broadcasting a compressed URL that nearby devices could open without an app; Eddystone-TLM, telemetry reporting beacon health such as battery voltage and temperature; and Eddystone-EID, an encrypted ephemeral identifier intended to address privacy and spoofing concerns, resolvable only by authorised clients.

History and decline

Eddystone competed with Apple's iBeacon protocol but was cross-platform and openly specified. Google integrated support into Android and Chrome, and used it for Nearby Notifications, which surfaced beacon content on phones. Citing spam and low usefulness, Google discontinued Nearby Notifications in December 2018, and later deprecated related proximity APIs, after which Eddystone saw sharply reduced deployment, though the specification remains publicly available.
