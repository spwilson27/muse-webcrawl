Free Officers Movement (Egypt)

The Free Officers (Arabic: الضباط الأحرار, al-Ḍubbāṭ al-Aḥrār) were a clandestine group of nationalist officers in the Egyptian Army who organised the Egyptian revolution of 1952, overthrowing King Farouk and the Muhammad Ali dynasty and transforming Egypt into a republic.

Founded: 1949
Leaders: Gamal Abdel Nasser, Muhammad Naguib, Anwar Sadat, Abdel Hakim Amer
Key event: Coup of 22–23 July 1952
Outcome: Abolition of the monarchy; Republic of Egypt (1953)

Formation

The movement coalesced among junior officers radicalised by corruption at home and humiliated by Egypt's defeat in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, in which faulty weapons and poor leadership were widely blamed on the palace and the old political order. From 1949 Gamal Abdel Nasser organised a secret cell structure distributing leaflets demanding reform of the army and an end to British occupation. The officers' defiance grew open when, in January 1952, their candidate won the Army Officers' Club elections against the King's wishes.

The 1952 coup

On the night of 22–23 July 1952, fearing arrest, the Free Officers seized army headquarters, radio stations and key points in Cairo almost without bloodshed. General Muhammad Naguib, a respected senior officer, served as the movement's public figurehead. King Farouk abdicated on 26 July and sailed into exile, leaving his infant son nominally on the throne; the monarchy was formally abolished and a republic proclaimed on 18 June 1953.

In power

Governing through the Revolutionary Command Council, the Free Officers dissolved the old political parties, enacted sweeping land reform in September 1952, and negotiated the evacuation of British troops from the Suez Canal Zone (completed 1956). A power struggle between Naguib and Nasser ended in 1954 with Nasser supreme; he became president in 1956 and led Egypt until his death in 1970. Fellow Free Officer Anwar Sadat succeeded him, so the movement's alumni ruled Egypt for three decades and its legacy — military dominance of the state — long outlasted them.
