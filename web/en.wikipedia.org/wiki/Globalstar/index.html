Globalstar, Inc. is an American satellite communications company that operates a low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite constellation for satellite phone and low-speed data communications. Its network provides voice and data services in remote areas beyond the reach of terrestrial cellular networks, and its SPOT family of devices offers satellite messaging and GPS tracking for outdoor and emergency use.

History

The Globalstar project was launched in 1991 as a joint venture involving Loral Corporation and Qualcomm. Between 1998 and 2000 the company launched 52 satellites, including spares, into low Earth orbit. Commercial service began in 2000, shortly before the collapse of the first generation of satellite telephone ventures; Globalstar filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in 2002 and emerged in 2004 under the control of Thermo Capital Partners.

Second-generation constellation

Beginning in 2010, Globalstar launched 24 second-generation satellites built by Thales Alenia Space, restoring and improving service quality. The second-generation system supports the company's SPOT satellite messengers, commercial Internet of Things (IoT) services, and duplex voice and data products.

Services

Unlike the geostationary-based systems of some competitors, Globalstar uses a "bent pipe" architecture in which satellites relay signals to gateway earth stations. The company gained wider public attention in 2022, when Apple partnered with Globalstar to provide the Emergency SOS via satellite feature on the iPhone 14 and later models, with Globalstar allocating the majority of its network capacity to the service.
