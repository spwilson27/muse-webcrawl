Handwriting recognition

Handwriting recognition (HWR), also known as handwritten text recognition (HTR), is the ability of a computer to receive and interpret intelligible handwritten input from sources such as paper documents, photographs, touch-screens and other devices. The image of the written text may be sensed "off line" from a piece of paper by optical scanning (optical character recognition), or the movements of a pen tip may be sensed "on line", for example by a pen-based computer screen surface.

Approaches

Offline recognition processes a static image and is used to digitise archives and postal addresses. Online recognition uses the sequence and timing of strokes, providing extra information that usually improves accuracy. Modern systems employ machine learning — historically hidden Markov models and today deep neural networks such as recurrent networks and transformers — and language models to disambiguate characters.

History

Commercial systems appeared in the 1980s in postal sorting, and consumer devices such as the Apple Newton (1993) popularised pen input despite mixed reviews. Handwriting recognition is now built into tablets, smartphones, and note-taking applications.
