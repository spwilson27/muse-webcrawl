Akzidenz-Grotesk

Akzidenz-Grotesk is a grotesque sans-serif typeface originally released by the H. Berthold AG type foundry of Berlin in 1896 under the name Accidenz-Grotesk. One of the first widely used sans-serif faces designed for everyday ("jobbing", German Akzidenz) printing rather than display, it accumulated weights and widths from numerous anonymous foundry designers over the following decades and became a foundational face of Swiss typography and the International Typographic Style.

Influence

Akzidenz-Grotesk's neutral, unadorned letterforms profoundly influenced mid-twentieth-century design: it was a principal model for Max Miedinger's Helvetica (1957) and Adrian Frutiger's Univers, and it informed the signage and corporate identity programmes of the post-war era. In North America it was sold by Amsterdam Continental as "Standard" or "Basic Commercial". Long overshadowed by Helvetica, the face has been revived digitally by Berthold and is again widely used by designers seeking a slightly warmer, quirkier alternative — its 'g' is double-storey, its 't' has a flat top, and its capitals are somewhat narrower than Helvetica's.
