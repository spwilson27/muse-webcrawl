Peter Joseph William Debye (24 March 1884 – 2 November 1966) was a Dutch-American physicist and physical chemist, and Nobel laureate in Chemistry. Born Petrus Josephus Wilhelmus Debije in Maastricht, he studied at Aachen and Munich under Arnold Sommerfeld, and held professorships at Utrecht, Zürich, Leipzig, and Berlin, where he directed the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics.

Scientific work

Debye's contributions spanned many areas of physics and chemistry. He developed the Debye model of the heat capacity of solids (1912), extending Einstein's theory using quantized elastic waves (phonons). With Erich Hückel he formulated the Debye–Hückel theory of electrolyte solutions (1923), introducing the concept of Debye screening; the Debye length remains a fundamental quantity in plasma and solution physics. The unit of electric dipole moment, the debye (D), is named after him for his work on dipole moments and molecular structure.

He also contributed the Debye–Scherrer powder method of X-ray diffraction, the Debye–Waller factor, and theories of dielectric relaxation (the Debye relaxation model). He received the 1936 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for his contributions to our knowledge of molecular structure through his investigations on dipole moments and on the diffraction of X-rays and electrons in gases."

Later career

In 1940 Debye emigrated to the United States, becoming a professor at Cornell University, where he remained for the rest of his career, working on light scattering by polymers and other topics. His wartime departure from Berlin and conduct during the Nazi period have been the subject of historical debate. He died in Ithaca, New York, in 1966.
