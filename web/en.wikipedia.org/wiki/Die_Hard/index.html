Die Hard

Image: Die Hard theatrical poster

Die Hard is a 1988 American action film directed by John McTiernan and written by Jeb Stuart and Steven E. de Souza, based on the 1979 novel Nothing Lasts Forever by Roderick Thorp. It stars Bruce Willis as New York City police detective John McClane, who is caught up in a terrorist takeover of a Los Angeles skyscraper while visiting his estranged wife during a Christmas Eve party.

Plot

McClane arrives at the Nakatomi Plaza Christmas party hoping to reconcile with his wife Holly. A group of German thieves led by Hans Gruber (Alan Rickman) seizes the building and takes the partygoers hostage, intending to steal $640 million in negotiable bearer bonds from the vault. McClane slips away undetected and wages a one-man guerrilla campaign against the thieves, aided from outside by LAPD Sergeant Al Powell, while the FBI's bungled interventions escalate the danger. In the climax, McClane confronts Gruber, who falls to his death from the building's upper floors.

Cast

Bruce Willis as John McClane; Alan Rickman as Hans Gruber; Alexander Godunov as Karl; Bonnie Bedelia as Holly Gennaro McClane; Reginald VelJohnson as Sergeant Al Powell; Paul Gleason as Deputy Police Chief Dwayne T. Robinson.

Reception and legacy

Released in July 1988, the film grossed over $140 million worldwide and received four Academy Award nominations. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest action films ever made, redefined the genre around a vulnerable, wisecracking everyman hero, and launched the Die Hard franchise, which includes four sequels. Its premise spawned a wave of imitators described as "Die Hard on a..." (bus, ship, plane, and so on). The film's Christmas setting has also made it a perennial subject of the debate over whether it counts as a Christmas film.
