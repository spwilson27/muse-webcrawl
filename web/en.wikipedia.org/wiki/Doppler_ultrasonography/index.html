Doppler ultrasonography

PurposeNon-invasive assessment of blood flow through vessels and the heart
Based onMedical ultrasound and the Doppler effect
Common formsSpectral Doppler, colour Doppler, power Doppler, duplex ultrasound

Doppler ultrasonography is a medical ultrasound technique that uses the Doppler effect to measure the movement of materials within the body, most commonly the flow of blood through arteries and veins. By analysing the frequency shift of echoes reflected from moving red blood cells, the method allows clinicians to visualise flow direction and velocity, detect blockages and valve problems, and assess organs and unborn babies without ionising radiation or invasive procedures.

Colour Doppler imaging maps blood flow direction and velocity onto a greyscale ultrasound image.

Techniques

Spectral Doppler displays flow velocity over time as a waveform, either in continuous-wave or pulsed-wave form. Colour Doppler superimposes a colour map of flow direction — conventionally red for flow toward and blue for flow away from the transducer — on the anatomical image. Power Doppler maps the amplitude of the Doppler signal and is more sensitive to slow flow. Duplex ultrasound combines conventional B-mode imaging with Doppler measurement. In echocardiography, Doppler techniques measure blood flow through the heart chambers and valves and estimate pressures.

Clinical uses

  • Diagnosing deep vein thrombosis and venous insufficiency
  • Evaluating carotid artery stenosis and stroke risk
  • Detecting peripheral arterial disease and aneurysms
  • Assessing congenital heart defects and valvular disease (via echocardiography)
  • Monitoring fetal circulation and umbilical artery flow in pregnancy
  • Evaluating blood flow in transplanted organs and tumours
