House of Beaufort

The House of Beaufort is an English noble family founded by the legitimised children of John of Gaunt, Duke of Lancaster, and his mistress (later wife) Katherine Swynford. The name comes from Gaunt's castle of Beaufort in Champagne, France.

Origins

Gaunt's four Beaufort children — John, Henry, Thomas and Joan — were legitimised by Richard II in 1397, though Henry IV later barred them from the succession. John Beaufort became Earl of Somerset; his descendants included Margaret Beaufort, mother of Henry VII, through whom the bloodline reached the throne.

Wars of the Roses

The Beauforts were leading Lancastrian magnates: Edmund Beaufort, 2nd Duke of Somerset, was killed at St Albans in 1455, and his sons died in the Lancastrian defeats of the 1460s and 1471. The senior male line ended in 1471.

Later descendants

The present Dukes of Beaufort descend from Charles Somerset, an illegitimate son of Henry Beaufort, 3rd Duke of Somerset; the family seat is Badminton House in Gloucestershire.
