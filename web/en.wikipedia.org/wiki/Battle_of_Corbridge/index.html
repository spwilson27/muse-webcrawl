The Battle of Corbridge was fought in 918 at Corbridge in Northumberland between the Norse-Gael king Ragnall ua Ímair and an alliance of Constantine II, king of Alba (Scotland), and Ealdred I of Bamburgh. It formed part of the struggle for control of Northumbria after the Norse reconquest of York.

Background

Ragnall, a grandson of Ivar the Boneless, had been expelled from Dublin and campaigned in western Scotland before turning on Northumbria. Ealdred, driven from his lands, sought help from Constantine, and the allies marched south to confront Ragnall near the River Tyne.

Battle

According to the Annals of Ulster and later chronicle accounts, Ragnall divided his army into four divisions, one of them hidden in reserve. The Scots broke three of the divisions but were then struck by the concealed fourth under Ragnall himself. Constantine's army withdrew in good order after nightfall; neither side could claim a clear triumph, though the Scots suffered the loss of many leading men.

Aftermath

Whatever its tactical result, the battle did not halt Ragnall: he went on to take York and establish himself as king of Northumbria, while Ealdred retained only the northern part of his former territory around Bamburgh.
