Jadeite

CategoryPyroxene mineral
FormulaNaAlSi2O6
ColourGreen, white, lilac, blue-green, black
Hardness6.5–7 (Mohs)
LustreVitreous to greasy

Jadeite is a pyroxene mineral with composition NaAlSi2O6. It is one of two minerals recognised as the gemstone jade, the other being nephrite. Jadeite is the rarer and more valuable of the two, and fine emerald-green translucent material, known as Imperial jade, is among the most prized gemstones in the world.

Properties

Jadeite is a hard, extremely tough mineral with a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7. Its toughness results from its interlocking granular crystal structure. Colours range from white through various shades of green — the green deriving from chromium and iron impurities — to lavender, yellow, orange, blue-green and black.

Occurrence

Major sources of jadeite include the Hpakan region of Kachin State in Myanmar (Burma), the Motagua valley of Guatemala, Japan, Russia and California. Jadeite forms in high-pressure, low-temperature metamorphic rocks associated with subduction zones.

Cultural significance

Jadeite was highly valued in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica, where Maya, Olmec and Aztec cultures carved it into masks, ornaments and ritual objects; in China, jadeite (feicui) imported from Burma from the 18th century onwards became the most coveted form of jade.
