The Panther tank, officially the Panzerkampfwagen V Panther (ordnance inventory designation Sd.Kfz. 171), was a German medium tank deployed during World War II from mid-1943 to the end of the war in 1945. It is widely regarded as one of the best tank designs of the war, combining sloped armour, a powerful long-barrelled 7.5 cm gun and good mobility.

Development

The Panther was developed in direct response to the Soviet T-34, encountered during the invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, and to counter the Soviet KV-1. MAN's design, selected over Daimler-Benz's, borrowed the T-34's sloped armour while retaining German standards of gun performance, optics and crew layout. Production began in January 1943.

Combat history

The Panther debuted at the Battle of Kursk in July 1943, where teething troubles — engine fires and breakdowns — marred its performance, though its gun and frontal armour proved formidable. Improved Ausf. A and Ausf. G models followed, and Panthers fought on the Eastern Front, in Italy, and in Normandy and northwest Europe, where they posed a severe threat to Allied armour. Weaknesses included unreliable final drives, side armour vulnerable to anti-tank rifles and guns, and Germany's worsening shortages of fuel and trained crews. About 6,000 were built, alongside the Jagdpanther tank destroyer and recovery variants based on the chassis.

Legacy

After the war, the French army operated captured Panthers into the early 1950s, and the Panther's gun influenced the French AMX-50 programme. Captured examples are displayed in museums worldwide; its balance of firepower, protection and mobility is often cited as a forerunner of the modern main battle tank concept.
