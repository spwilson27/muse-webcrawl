Alpha decay

Alpha decay is a type of radioactive decay in which an atomic nucleus emits an alpha particle — two protons and two neutrons bound together, identical to a helium-4 nucleus — and thereby transforms into a nucleus with mass number reduced by four and atomic number reduced by two.

Alpha decay occurs mostly in heavy nuclei such as uranium, radium and polonium. Alpha particles are slow, heavy and highly ionising but weakly penetrating: they are stopped by a sheet of paper or the outer layer of skin, though alpha emitters inhaled or ingested (such as radon or polonium-210) are hazardous inside the body. Quantum tunnelling — explained by George Gamow in 1928 — accounts for how alpha particles escape the nucleus despite its potential barrier.

Examples

Uranium-238 alpha-decays to thorium-234 with a half-life of about 4.5 billion years; americium-241 in household smoke detectors alpha-decays to neptunium-237. Alpha decay powers the heat sources of some deep-space probes via plutonium-238.
