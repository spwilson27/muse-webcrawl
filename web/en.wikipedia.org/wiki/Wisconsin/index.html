Wisconsin

Wisconsin is a state in the Upper Midwestern United States. It borders Minnesota to the west, Iowa to the southwest, Illinois to the south, Lake Michigan to the east, Michigan to the northeast, and Lake Superior to the north. Its capital is Madison, and its largest city is Milwaukee. Wisconsin is the 25th-largest state by area and the 20th-most populous, with about 5.9 million residents.

Geography

The state is known for its more than 15,000 lakes, extensive forests in the north, and the scenic driftless area in the southwest, which escaped glaciation. Major rivers include the Mississippi on the western border and the Wisconsin River. The Door Peninsula, jutting into Lake Michigan, is a popular resort region.

Economy

Wisconsin is a leading agricultural state, nicknamed "America's Dairyland" for its cheese and milk production; it produces about a quarter of the nation's cheese. Manufacturing — including paper products, machinery, and food processing — and health care, education, and tourism are also major sectors. Milwaukee is home to companies such as Harley-Davidson and Miller Brewing.

History

Indigenous peoples have lived in the region for thousands of years; French explorers arrived in the 1630s, and the area passed to Britain and then the United States. Wisconsin became a territory in 1836 and the 30th state on May 29, 1848. Large numbers of German, Scandinavian, and other European immigrants shaped its culture in the nineteenth century, and in the early twentieth century the state pioneered Progressive Era reforms under Governor Robert M. La Follette.
