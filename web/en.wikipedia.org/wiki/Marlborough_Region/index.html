The Marlborough Region, commonly known simply as Marlborough, is one of the regions of New Zealand, located at the north-eastern end of the South Island. It is a unitary authority — a district administered by the Marlborough District Council, which performs both regional and territorial functions. Its main town is Blenheim, and its principal port is Picton, the South Island terminal of the Cook Strait ferries.

Geography

Marlborough is known for two contrasting landscapes: the drowned river valleys of the Marlborough Sounds in the north, a maze of sea-filled valleys and islands around Queen Charlotte Sound and Pelorus Sound, and the dry, sunny Wairau Plain in the south, sheltered by mountain ranges. The region includes D'Urville Island and the port of Havelock, known for greenshell mussels. The Awatere Valley lies to the south-east.

Wine

Marlborough is New Zealand's largest wine-producing region, accounting for roughly three-quarters of national production. Commercial viticulture began in the 1970s, and the region's intensely flavoured Sauvignon blanc, first promoted internationally in the 1980s, became world famous and established New Zealand's reputation in global wine markets. Pinot noir and other varieties are also grown. The combination of long sunshine hours, cool nights and stony, free-draining soils is credited for the distinctive style.

Economy and other features

Besides wine, the region's economy includes pastoral farming, forestry, aquaculture (notably salmon and mussel farming in the Sounds), aviation at Woodbourne (home to RNZAF Base Woodbourne and the Omaka Aviation Heritage Centre), and tourism. Marlborough enjoys some of New Zealand's highest sunshine hours, recorded at Blenheim.
