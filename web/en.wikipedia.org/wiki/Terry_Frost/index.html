Sir Terry Frost RA (13 October 1915 – 1 September 2003) was an English artist. One of the leading British abstract painters of his generation, he was associated with the St Ives School in Cornwall and known for his bold use of colour and forms inspired by the Cornish landscape and harbour scenes.

Early life and career

Frost was born in Leamington Spa and left school at 14. During the Second World War he served with the commandos and was taken prisoner in Crete in 1941; while a prisoner of war he began to paint. After the war he studied at Camberwell School of Art under Victor Pasmore and others, and later became an assistant to sculptor Barbara Hepworth in St Ives.

Work

Frost's paintings developed from observations of boats, harbours, and the movement of water and light in St Ives and later Newlyn, abstracted into semicircles, arcs, and vivid colour fields — notably the Walk Along the Quay series. He taught at Bath Academy of Art, Corsham, and was Professor of Painting at the University of Reading. He was elected a Royal Academician in 1992 and knighted in 1998.
