Andrei I Yuryevich, commonly known as Andrei Bogolyubsky ("Andrew the God-Loving," c. 1111 – 29 June 1174), was Grand Prince of Vladimir from 1157 until his death. A son of Yuri Dolgorukiy, he transferred the political centre of Rus' from Kiev to the north-east, building up Vladimir as the new capital and laying the foundations of the principality of Vladimir-Suzdal, the seedbed of the later Russian state centred on Moscow.

Reign

In 1155 Andrei removed the revered icon of the Theotokos from Vyshhorod to Vladimir, defying his father. On becoming prince in 1157 he refused to reside in older Suzdal and instead built his residence at Bogolyubovo (from which his nickname derives) and adorned Vladimir with white-stone churches, including the Dormition Cathedral (1158–1160) and the Golden Gate; the exquisite Church of the Intercession on the Nerl (1165) is also credited to his reign. In 1169 his coalition sacked Kiev and installed a puppet prince, decisively shifting power north-east — yet Andrei himself never settled in Kiev. He campaigned against Volga Bulgaria and Novgorod, promoted autocracy against boyar and veche traditions, and sought, unsuccessfully, a separate metropolitan see for Vladimir.

Death and legacy

Andrei was murdered in 1174 by a conspiracy of boyars at Bogolyubovo. A succession war followed, but Vladimir's supremacy endured, and Andrei was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church (feast day 4 July). His building programme in white stone is now a UNESCO World Heritage ensemble.
