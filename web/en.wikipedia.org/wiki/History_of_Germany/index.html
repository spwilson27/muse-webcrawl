The history of Germany begins with the Germanic tribes of antiquity and the medieval Holy Roman Empire, passes through the fragmentation of the early modern period, unification under Otto von Bismarck in 1871, the catastrophes of the two World Wars and the Nazi dictatorship, forty years of division during the Cold War, and reunification in 1990.

Germany's borders changed repeatedly between the medieval empire and the reunified republic of 1990.

Early history

Germanic tribes, described by Tacitus in Germania, resisted Roman expansion; the victory of Arminius at the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest in 9 AD halted Roman conquest east of the Rhine. During the Migration Period the Franks built a kingdom that, under Charlemagne, became the Carolingian Empire. The Treaty of Verdun (843) split it, the eastern part forming the basis of the later German kingdom.

Holy Roman Empire

Otto I was crowned emperor in 962, founding the Holy Roman Empire. The empire was decentralised: princes, bishops and free cities held much power. It was the scene of the Investiture Controversy, the Hanseatic League's trading power, the Reformation launched by Martin Luther in 1517, and the devastation of the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648), ended by the Peace of Westphalia. The empire was dissolved in 1806 under pressure from Napoleon.

Confederation and unification

The Congress of Vienna (1815) created the German Confederation of 39 states dominated by Austria and Prussia. The revolutions of 1848 failed to unify Germany under a liberal constitution. Prussia's minister-president Bismarck achieved unification through wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866) and France (1870–71); the German Empire was proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871.

Empire, Weimar and Nazi Germany

The empire industrialised rapidly but was defeated in the First World War, leading to the abdication of Wilhelm II and the Weimar Republic (1919). Hyperinflation (1923) and the Great Depression undermined democracy. Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party seized power in 1933, establishing a totalitarian regime that persecuted and murdered six million Jews in the Holocaust and launched the Second World War in 1939, ending in total defeat in 1945.

Division and reunification

Germany was divided into the capitalist Federal Republic (West) and the communist German Democratic Republic (East). West Germany experienced an "economic miracle" and joined NATO and the European Community; East Germany joined the Warsaw Pact. The Berlin Wall, built in 1961, fell on 9 November 1989, and Germany reunified on 3 October 1990. The reunified Federal Republic became a central member of the European Union and adopted the euro in 1999.
