The Flint–Worcester tornado outbreak sequence was a severe weather event in June 1953 that produced devastating tornadoes in Michigan and Massachusetts, together killing about 247 people. It remains one of the deadliest tornado sequences in United States history.

Flint tornado

On the evening of 8 June 1953, an F5 tornado struck the northern suburbs of Flint, Michigan, killing 116 people and injuring hundreds. It was the deadliest tornado in Michigan history and the last U.S. tornado to claim more than 100 lives until the 2011 Joplin tornado. The same day, tornadoes in southeastern Michigan and Ohio killed dozens more.

Worcester tornado

On 9 June the storm system moved into New England, where an F4 tornado tore through Worcester County, Massachusetts, killing 94 people and destroying thousands of homes in Worcester and surrounding towns. It remains the deadliest tornado in New England history and caused damage as far as the Boston suburbs with falling debris.

Legacy

The disasters prompted national debate about tornado warnings and disaster preparedness, and helped spur expansion of U.S. weather radar networks in the following years.
