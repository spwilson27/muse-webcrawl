State (polity)

A state is a political entity that exercises sovereign authority over a defined territory and population. Max Weber famously defined the state as the organisation claiming a monopoly on the legitimate use of physical force within a territory.

Criteria of statehood

Under the Montevideo Convention (1933), a state should possess a permanent population, a defined territory, a government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states. Whether statehood depends on legal recognition (constitutive theory) or merely on meeting these criteria (declarative theory) remains debated in international law.

Types

States may be unitary or federal, republics or monarchies, and nation-states where political and national identity largely coincide. Modern states provide public goods, law, and security, financed through taxation.
