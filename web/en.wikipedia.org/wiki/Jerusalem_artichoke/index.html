Jerusalem artichoke

The Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus), also called the sunchoke, is a species of sunflower native to eastern North America, grown for its edible tubers.

Description

The plant grows 1.5–3 metres tall with small yellow flowers. Its knobbly tubers resemble ginger root and have a crisp texture and sweet, nutty flavour when cooked. Unlike potatoes, they store carbohydrate mainly as inulin rather than starch.

History and name

Cultivated by Native Americans before European contact, the tuber was brought to Europe in the early 1600s. Despite the name, it has no connection to Jerusalem or to globe artichokes; "Jerusalem" is thought to be a corruption of the Italian girasole (sunflower). See also sunflower.
