Ningirsu

DomainWar, agriculture, storms; patron god of Girsu
Cult centreGirsu (state of Lagash)
ParentsEnlil and Ninhursag
SymbolsEagle (Imdugud/Anzu), lion, mace

Ningirsu (Sumerian: "Lord of Girsu") was a Mesopotamian god of war, agriculture, and healing, and the patron deity of the city of Girsu in the state of Lagash in ancient Sumer. He was regarded as a son of Enlil and was closely associated, and eventually largely identified, with the god Ninurta.

Cult and mythology

Ningirsu's principal temple at Girsu was the Eninnu, "the House of Fifty", repeatedly rebuilt by the rulers of Lagash. The ensi Gudea (c. 2144–2124 BCE) left famous inscriptions and statues recording his rebuilding of the Eninnu after receiving instructions from the god in a dream. Earlier, Eannatum of Lagash credited Ningirsu with victory over the neighbouring city of Umma, commemorated on the Stele of the Vultures, in which the god is shown holding the defeated enemies in a great net.

Character

As an agricultural deity Ningirsu governed the spring floods and the fertility of the fields; as a warrior he defended Lagash against its enemies. His sacred animal and emblem was the lion-headed eagle Imdugud, and his wife was the goddess Bau (Baba). In later periods worship of Ningirsu declined with the fortunes of Lagash, while his attributes merged into those of Ninurta in the wider Babylonian tradition.
