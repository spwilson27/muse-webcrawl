Calcite, the principal carbonate mineral

Carbonate minerals are minerals containing the carbonate ion, CO32−, combined with metal cations. More than 200 carbonate minerals are known. The most common are calcite and aragonite (both CaCO3), which make up the sedimentary rocks limestone and chalk, and dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2).

Groups

In the Strunz classification, carbonates form class V. The calcite group (calcite, magnesite, siderite, rhodochrosite, smithsonite) and dolomite group share related crystal structures; the aragonite group includes aragonite, witherite, and strontianite. Hydrated and hydroxyl carbonates such as malachite and azurite are prized as pigments and ornamental stones, and several carbonates are important ores of iron, zinc, lead, and copper.

Formation and significance

Carbonates precipitate from water in marine and lacustrine environments, often through the activity of organisms — shells, corals, and microbialites — and form karst landscapes where dissolved by acidic groundwater. Carbonate rocks store a vast share of Earth's carbon and are central to the long-term carbon cycle; their dissolution and precipitation buffer ocean chemistry.
