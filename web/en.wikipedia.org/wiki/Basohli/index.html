Basohli

Basohli (also spelled Basholi) is a town in the Kathua district of the union territory of Jammu and Kashmir, India. It lies on the banks of the Ravi River, near the border with Punjab and Himachal Pradesh, at an elevation of roughly 570 metres above sea level. The town is celebrated across India as the birthplace of the Basohli school of Pahari miniature painting, one of the earliest and most distinctive styles of Indian hill painting.

History

Basohli was the seat of a small Rajput hill state founded in the late 17th century. Its rulers, of the Pal dynasty, were notable patrons of the arts. Under Raja Kirpal Pal (reigned c. 1678–1693), the town became a flourishing centre of painting, producing vividly coloured miniatures characterised by bold lines, stylised faces with large lotus-shaped eyes, and a rich palette of red, yellow and blue. The celebrated illustrated series of the Rasamanjari (c. 1690s), attributed to the painter Devidasa working for Kirpal Pal's court, is regarded as a landmark of the Basohli style.

Basohli paintings drew heavily on Vaishnavite themes — the Bhagavata Purana, the Ramayana and the Gita Govinda — as well as portraits of local rulers and ragamala series. The style later influenced the development of the Kangra school of painting in the 18th century.

Geography and administration

Basohli is a municipal committee town within Kathua district. The Atal Setu bridge across the Ravi near Basohli, opened in 2015, is a notable cable-stayed bridge linking Jammu and Kashmir with Punjab. The town serves as a gateway to the hill regions of Bani and Bhaderwah.

Basohli painting

The Basohli school is considered the earliest expression of Pahari painting. Its hallmarks include flat picture planes, intense primary colours, beetle-wing cases used to simulate emerald jewellery, and distinctive facial types. Major collections of Basohli works are held by museums in India and abroad.
