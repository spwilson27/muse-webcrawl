The Land Transport Authority (Abbreviation: LTA) is a statutory board under the Ministry of Transport of the Government of Singapore. It is responsible for planning, operating, and maintaining Singapore's land transport infrastructure and systems, including the Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) rail network, roads, bus services planning, cycling infrastructure, and the regulation of vehicles and road use.

History

The LTA was established on 1 September 1995 through the merger of several agencies and departments, including the Registry of Vehicles, the Mass Rapid Transit Corporation, the Roads and Transportation Division of the Public Works Department, and the Land Transportation Division of the former Ministry of Communications. The merger unified land transport planning and regulation in a single body.

Responsibilities

LTA plans and builds MRT and LRT lines and owns the rail infrastructure under Singapore's New Rail Financing Framework, under which operating assets are leased to operators such as SMRT and SBS Transit. It manages road construction and maintenance, traffic management and the Electronic Road Pricing (ERP) congestion charging system, vehicle registration and ownership policies including the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) quota system, and the regulation of taxis and private-hire cars. It also administers cycling paths and active-mobility rules, and carries out public transport service planning, contracting bus services under the Bus Contracting Model introduced in 2016.

Initiatives

Major undertakings have included successive expansions of the MRT network (Downtown, Thomson–East Coast, Jurong Region and Cross Island lines), the ERP 2.0 satellite-based road pricing system, and the Land Transport Master Plan 2040, which targets a city where 9 in 10 peak-period journeys to the nearest neighbourhood centre take under 45 minutes, with expanded rail and active mobility networks.
