Judd Apatow (born 6 December 1967) is an American director, producer, screenwriter and comedian. Through his company Apatow Productions, he became one of the most influential figures in American screen comedy in the 2000s, known for blends of raunchy humour and emotional sincerity, and for launching or advancing the careers of performers such as Seth Rogen, Jonah Hill, Jason Segel and Lena Dunham.

Early career

Apatow grew up on Long Island, New York, and began performing stand-up as a teenager. He wrote for television series including The Ben Stiller Show (winning an Emmy) and The Larry Sanders Show. He created the acclaimed but short-lived series Freaks and Geeks (1999–2000) and Undeclared (2001–2002), both now regarded as cult classics.

Film breakthrough

Apatow's feature directorial debut, The 40-Year-Old Virgin (2005), was a major hit, followed by Knocked Up (2007), Funny People (2009), This Is 40 (2012), Trainwreck (2015) and The King of Staten Island (2020). As a producer he was behind films including Anchorman, Superbad, Forgetting Sarah Marshall, Step Brothers, Bridesmaids and The Big Sick, and the HBO series Girls, which he executive produced with creator Lena Dunham.

He has also directed documentaries, including The Zen Diaries of Garry Shandling (2018), about his mentor. He is married to actress Leslie Mann, who appears in many of his films; their daughters Maude and Iris Apatow are also actors.
