Daifuku (Japanese: 大福, literally "great luck"), or daifukumochi (大福餅), is a Japanese confection consisting of a small round mochi (glutinous rice cake) stuffed with a sweet filling, most commonly anko, a sweetened red bean paste made from azuki beans. Daifuku come in many varieties; the name is said to be a punning wish for good fortune, replacing the older name harabuto-mochi ("belly-thick rice cake").

Varieties

Common types include plain white, pale green yomogi (mugwort) and pink-tinted daifuku filled with anko, or with shiroan (white bean paste). Variants filled with whole fruits are popular: ichigo daifuku contains a whole strawberry and became fashionable in the 1980s, while others enclose melon, mandarin segments, chestnuts or coffee-flavoured cream. Mame daifuku contains whole soybeans or azuki beans kneaded into the mochi skin, and modern versions may be filled with ice cream, a preparation also marketed internationally as "mochi ice cream". Because fresh daifuku hardens quickly, it is best eaten on the day it is made or kept frozen.
