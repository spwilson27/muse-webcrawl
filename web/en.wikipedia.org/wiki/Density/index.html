Density

Density
Symbolρ (rho), sometimes D
SI unitkilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³)
Definitionmass divided by volume

Density (volumetric mass density or specific mass) is the ratio of a substance's mass to its volume. Its symbol is most often ρ, the Greek letter rho. Mathematically, density is defined as:

ρ = m / V

The SI unit is the kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³). The density of water is about 1,000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³) at 4 °C. Among common substances, osmium and iridium are the densest elements (~22,600 kg/m³), while aerogels and gases are among the least dense; the average density of the Earth is about 5,515 kg/m³.

History

A famous anecdote tells how Archimedes, asked to determine whether a crown was pure gold, realised while bathing that the volume of water displaced equals the volume of the immersed body, allowing density — and thus composition — to be checked; he is said to have run into the street shouting "Eureka!"

Measurement and uses

Density is measured with hydrometers, pycnometers and hydrostatic weighing, and varies with temperature and pressure, especially for gases. It governs buoyancy, underpins calculations in fluid mechanics, and is used to characterise materials and assess purity. Related concepts include relative density (specific gravity), number density and population density.
