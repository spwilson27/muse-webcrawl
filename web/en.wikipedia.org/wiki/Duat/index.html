Duat

The Duat (Ancient Egyptian: dwꜣt, also transliterated Tuat or Tuaut) is the realm of the dead in ancient Egyptian mythology. It was the underworld world through which the sun god Ra traveled each night on his solar barque, battling the serpent Apophis, and where the souls of the dead underwent judgment in the Hall of Maat before Osiris.

Description

The Duat was envisioned as a landscape of rivers, lakes of fire, islands and caverns, divided into twelve hours or regions corresponding to the hours of the night. Funerary texts such as the Amduat ("That Which Is in the Afterworld") and the Book of Gates describe its geography in detail, knowledge of which helped the deceased navigate safely.

Judgment of the dead

In the Duat, the heart of the deceased was weighed against the feather of Maat, goddess of truth. Those whose hearts were heavy with wrongdoing were devoured by the monster Ammit; those who passed joined the blessed dead and, in many beliefs, accompanied Ra on his nightly journey, being reborn with him at dawn.

Inhabitants

Osiris ruled the Duat as king of the dead, assisted by deities such as Anubis, Thoth and the four sons of Horus. The region was also populated by demons and gatekeepers whose names the deceased had to know to pass.
