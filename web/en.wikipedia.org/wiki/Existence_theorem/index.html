Existence theorem

In mathematics, an existence theorem is a theorem which asserts the existence of some object. It might be a statement which begins with the phrase "there exist(s)", or it might be a universal statement whose last quantifier is existential. Existence theorems may be proved constructively, by exhibiting the object, or non-constructively, by indirect argument — for example using the law of excluded middle, the intermediate value theorem, compactness arguments, or fixed-point theorems such as the Brouwer fixed-point theorem.

A classic example is the proof that there exist irrational numbers a and b such that ab is rational, which proceeds by cases without deciding which witness works. Constructive mathematics rejects purely non-constructive existence proofs, requiring an explicit construction or algorithm.

Related notions include uniqueness theorems and existence-and-uniqueness results, such as those for solutions of differential equations given by the Picard–Lindelöf theorem.
