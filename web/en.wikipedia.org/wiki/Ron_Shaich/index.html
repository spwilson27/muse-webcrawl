Ron Shaich

Ron M. Shaich (born 1953) is an American businessman best known as the founder and former chairman and chief executive officer of Panera Bread. He built the bakery-café chain from a small group of stores into one of the largest restaurant companies in the United States and is credited as a pioneer of the fast-casual segment of the restaurant industry.

Career

Shaich graduated from Clark University and earned an MBA from Harvard Business School. After working in retail, he co-founded the Au Bon Pain bakery café chain in 1981. In 1993 Au Bon Pain acquired the Saint Louis Bread Company, a group of bakery cafés in Missouri. Shaich concluded that the bakery-café concept had greater potential than Au Bon Pain itself; in 1999 the company sold Au Bon Pain and renamed itself Panera Bread after the Saint Louis chain.

As CEO, Shaich expanded Panera from a few dozen locations to more than 2,000 cafés across North America, emphasising fresh-baked bread, clean ingredients and digital ordering. He stepped down as CEO at the end of 2017 and as chairman in 2018, shortly after Panera was acquired by JAB Holding Company for approximately $7.5 billion. He later became an activist investor and public commentator on the restaurant industry and on food policy, including issues of food insecurity — Panera operated a number of pay-what-you-can "Panera Cares" community cafés.
