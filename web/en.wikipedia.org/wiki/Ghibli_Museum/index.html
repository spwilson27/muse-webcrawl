The Ghibli Museum (三鷹の森ジブリ美術館, Mitaka no Mori Jiburi Bijutsukan, "Mitaka Forest Ghibli Museum") is a museum showcasing the work of the Japanese animation studio Studio Ghibli. Located in Inokashira Park in Mitaka, a western suburb of Tokyo, Japan, it opened on 1 October 2001. The museum was designed by Studio Ghibli director Hayao Miyazaki himself, using storyboards similar to those he creates for his films.

Exhibits and features

The museum is deliberately designed like a place to be explored and "gotten lost" in, with winding staircases, small doorways, and bridges. Photography is prohibited inside, to preserve the sense of discovery. Permanent exhibits include a recreation of the animators' studio, displays explaining the mechanics of animation (including a three-dimensional zoetrope-like display of characters from My Neighbor Totoro), and rooms themed after the films.

  • Saturn Theatre — a small cinema screening short films made exclusively for the museum by Studio Ghibli, such as Mei and the Kittenbus, The Day I Harvested a Star, and Mr. Dough and the Egg Princess; visitors may see one short per visit.
  • Catbus room — a play area for children up to 12 featuring a large plush Catbus from My Neighbor Totoro.
  • Rooftop garden — with a life-size statue of the Robot Soldier from Castle in the Sky and the Laputa stone.
  • Straw Hat Café and the Mamma Aiuto! gift shop (named after the sky pirates in Porco Rosso).
  • Rotating special exhibitions on animation and illustration, sometimes featuring other studios and artists.

Tickets

Admission is by advance reservation only, with tickets released for a given month on the 10th of the preceding month; entry is capped to keep the museum uncrowded. Tickets are sold through Lawson convenience stores in Japan and through designated travel agencies overseas. A dedicated Ghibli-themed bus connects the museum with Mitaka Station.
