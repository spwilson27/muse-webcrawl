Stele of the Vultures

The Stele of the Vultures is a monument from ancient Sumer, dating to the Early Dynastic III period (around 2600–2350 BC), now in the Louvre Museum in Paris. It commemorates the victory of Eannatum, king of Lagash, over the neighbouring city-state of Umma, and is one of the earliest known historical documents and war monuments.

Description

The carved limestone stele, of which only fragments survive, was originally about 1.8 metres tall. One side shows the god Ningirsu holding defeated enemies in a net; the other shows Eannatum leading his soldiers, marching in phalanx formation over the corpses of the enemy, while vultures carry off the heads and limbs of the slain — the image from which the stele takes its name.

Text

The cuneiform inscription recounts the border conflict between Lagash and Umma over the fertile Gu'edena plain and the oaths imposed on Umma's ruler. It is a key source for early Mesopotamian warfare, kingship and diplomacy.
