William Henry (chemist)

William Henry
Born: 12 December 1774, Manchester, England
Died: 2 September 1836 (aged 61)
Known for: Henry's law

William Henry (12 December 1774 – 2 September 1836) was an English chemist. He was the son of Thomas Henry, an apothecary and founder of the Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society. Henry studied medicine at Edinburgh, taking his MD in 1807, but ill health interrupted his practice and he devoted himself mainly to chemical research, especially on gases.

Henry's law

In 1803 Henry published experiments on the quantity of gases absorbed by water at different temperatures and pressures. The result, now known as Henry's law, states that at constant temperature, the amount of a gas dissolved in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas above the liquid. The law is fundamental to the chemistry of gas solubility, with applications ranging from carbonated beverages to decompression theory for divers.

Life and work

Henry was a close friend of John Dalton and supported the development of Dalton's atomic theory, helping to communicate and defend it. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1809 and awarded the Copley Medal that year. His textbook Elements of Experimental Chemistry was a standard work that went through many editions. Beset by chronic pain from a childhood injury and by depression, Henry took his own life in 1836.
