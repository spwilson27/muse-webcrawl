The Basilica of San Petronio is a minor basilica and church of the Archdiocese of Bologna in northern Italy. Dedicated to Saint Petronius, the city's patron saint, it dominates Piazza Maggiore. Construction began in 1390 to designs by Antonio di Vincenzo, and the church, built by the city commune rather than the diocese, was intended to rival St Peter's in Rome in scale — an ambition checked by papal intervention.

Architecture

The basilica is one of the largest churches in the world by volume, measuring about 132 metres long and 60 metres wide, with a nave vault 45 metres high. Its vast brick façade was never completed: only the lower portion received its intended marble revetment, with sculpted portals by Jacopo della Quercia. The interior is divided into a nave and side aisles with twenty-two chapels richly decorated with frescoes and altarpieces.

Features

Inside, Giovanni Domenico Cassini constructed a celebrated meridian line on the pavement in 1655, used to verify the calendar and astronomical observations. The basilica also houses one of the oldest organs in the world still in use, built by Lorenzo da Prato in 1471–75, and the frescoes of the Bolognini Chapel depicting Heaven and Hell.
