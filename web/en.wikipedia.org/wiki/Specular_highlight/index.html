Specular highlight

A specular highlight is the bright spot of light that appears on shiny objects when illuminated. In computer graphics, specular highlights are the mirror-like component of reflection, distinguished from the scattered diffuse component, and they are essential to conveying the glossiness and shape of a rendered surface.

Reflection models

The Phong reflection model computes specular reflection as proportional to (R·V)^α, where R is the reflected light direction, V the viewer direction, and α a shininess exponent. The Blinn–Phong model instead uses the half-vector between light and viewer and is generally cheaper and less prone to artifacts. Modern physically based rendering uses microfacet distributions such as GGX to compute specular highlights from surface roughness.

Properties

The size and intensity of a highlight depend on surface roughness: smooth surfaces produce small, sharp highlights, while rough surfaces spread the highlight over a broader area. On metals the highlight takes the color of the material; on dielectrics such as plastic it takes the color of the light source. Highlights also move consistently with viewpoint, an important cue for perceiving shape, material, and light position.

Applications

Accurate specular highlights matter in product visualization, film rendering, and real-time games. Techniques such as environment mapping and image-based lighting approximate the complex highlight patterns produced by area lights and surrounding geometry.
