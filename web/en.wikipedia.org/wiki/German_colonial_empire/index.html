German colonial empire

The German colonial empire (German: Deutsches Kolonialreich) comprised the overseas colonies of the German Empire from 1884 until their loss during and after the First World War. At its height it was the third-largest colonial empire of its time, covering roughly 2.6 million km² in Africa and the Pacific, though it was acquired late, administered thinly, and — with a few exceptions — never economically profitable to the Reich.

Origins

Chancellor Otto von Bismarck initially opposed colonial acquisitions, but in 1884 — under pressure from merchants and colonial lobbies, and for diplomatic advantage against Britain and France — he granted imperial charters to the acquisitions of Adolf Lüderitz in South West Africa, Carl Peters in East Africa, and firms in West Africa and New Guinea. The Berlin Conference of 1884–85 set the rules for the European partition of Africa, in which Germany became a major participant.

The colonies

In Africa Germany held German East Africa (the largest colony), German South West Africa (present-day Namibia), Kamerun, and Togoland. In the Pacific it held German New Guinea with the Bismarck Archipelago and northern Solomon Islands, the Caroline, Mariana, Palau and Marshall Islands (purchased from Spain in 1899), German Samoa, and the leased Kiautschou Bay territory around Qingdao (Tsingtao) in China, seized in 1897. Colonial rule was exercised by chartered companies at first, then by imperial governors with small Schutztruppe garrisons of European officers and African or local soldiers.

Violence and the Herero and Nama genocide

German rule was marked by forced labour, land expropriation, and brutal suppression of resistance, including the Maji Maji Rebellion in East Africa (1905–07). In South West Africa, the uprising of the Herero and Nama peoples in 1904–08 was met by General Lothar von Trotha's extermination order; tens of thousands — the majority of the Herero population — died in the desert or in concentration camps. The events are recognized as the first genocide of the 20th century; Germany formally acknowledged it as genocide in 2021 and agreed to a reconciliation programme with Namibia.

End of the empire

During the First World War the colonies were occupied by Allied forces — Togoland and Qingdao in 1914, South West Africa in 1915, Kamerun in 1916, and East Africa only at the armistice in November 1918. The Treaty of Versailles (1919) stripped Germany of all colonies, which were redistributed as League of Nations mandates among Britain, France, Belgium, South Africa, Japan, Australia, and New Zealand.
