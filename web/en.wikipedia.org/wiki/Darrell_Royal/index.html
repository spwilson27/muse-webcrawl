Darrell Royal

Darrell K Royal (July 6, 1924 – November 7, 2012) was an American college football player and coach. He served as head coach at Mississippi State University (1954–1955), the University of Washington (1956), and the University of Texas at Austin (1957–1976), where he won three national championships (1963, 1969, 1970) and became the winningest coach in Texas Longhorns history.

Playing career

Royal played quarterback and defensive back at the University of Oklahoma under Bud Wilkinson, earning All-America honours as a defensive back in 1949; he still holds school records for interceptions. He briefly played professional football in Canada before entering coaching.

Coaching career

At Texas, Royal built a dynasty, winning eleven Southwest Conference titles and never having a losing season in 20 years. His 1968 team, with offensive coordinator Emory Bellard, pioneered the wishbone formation, which powered the 1969 "Game of the Century" victory over Arkansas and a 30-game winning streak. Royal retired in 1976 with a career record of 184–60–5 and also served as Texas's athletic director until 1980.

Legacy

Royal was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1983. In 1996 the University of Texas renamed its stadium Darrell K Royal–Texas Memorial Stadium in his honour.
