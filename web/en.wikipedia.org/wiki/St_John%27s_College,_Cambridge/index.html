St John's College is a constituent college of the University of Cambridge. Founded in 1511 by Lady Margaret Beaufort, mother of Henry VII, it is one of the largest colleges in Cambridge, with a large undergraduate and graduate community.

Buildings

The college stands on the site of the thirteenth-century Hospital of St John. Its courts span five centuries of architecture: First Court retains Tudor brickwork, the chapel is a Victorian Gothic building modelled on Sainte-Chapelle, and the famous covered Bridge of Sighs (1831) links the old college to New Court across the River Cam.

Notable alumni

Alumni include the poet William Wordsworth, abolitionist William Wilberforce, prime minister Lord Palmerston, and physicist Paul Dirac. The college choir is internationally renowned.
