Tawhid

Tawhid (التوحيد)
Meaning: Oneness of God
Religion: Islam
Related: Shahada, shirk (opposite)

Tawhid is the central theological concept in Islam: the indivisible oneness and uniqueness of God (Allah). It asserts strict monotheism — that God is one, has no partners, equals, or offspring — and it forms the first part of the shahada, the Islamic declaration of faith: there is no god but God.

Meaning

The word derives from the Arabic verb wahhada, to unify or declare one. Classical theology analyses tawhid in categories such as the oneness of God's lordship (rububiyya), oneness in worship (uluhiyya), and oneness of names and attributes (asma wa-sifat). Its opposite is shirk, associating partners with God, considered the gravest sin in Islam.

In the Quran and theology

Chapter 112 of the Quran, Al-Ikhlas, is a concise statement: God is One, the Eternal Refuge, who neither begets nor is born, and there is none like Him. Debates over tawhid — especially over divine attributes and the createdness of the Quran — shaped early Islamic theology, and the concept is emphasised in Sufi metaphysics as the unity of existence and in reform movements calling for worship directed to God alone.
