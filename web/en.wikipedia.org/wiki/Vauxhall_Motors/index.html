Vauxhall Motors Limited is a British car manufacturer based in Chalton, Bedfordshire, England. The company, along with its sister brand Opel, has been part of the multinational automotive group Stellantis since January 2021. Vauxhall-branded vehicles are manufactured at plants in Ellesmere Port and Luton, the latter also producing light commercial vehicles.

History

The company was founded by Alexander Wilson in 1857 in the Vauxhall district of London as a manufacturer of pumps and marine engines. Car production began in 1903 with a 5 hp single-cylinder model. In 1905 the company moved to Luton, Bedfordshire. Vauxhall built a reputation for sporting cars, including the Prince Henry of 1911, often regarded as one of the first true sports cars, and the 30-98.

In 1925 Vauxhall was acquired by the American manufacturer General Motors (GM). Under GM ownership the company moved into mass-market family cars, producing models such as the Cadet, Wyvern, Velox, Victor, Viva, Cavalier and Astra. From the late 1970s most Vauxhall cars were rebadged versions of Opel designs. In 2017 GM sold Vauxhall and Opel to the French Groupe PSA, which merged with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles in 2021 to form Stellantis.

Models

Long-running nameplates include the Corsa supermini (introduced 1993, replacing the Nova), the Astra family car (since 1979) and the Insignia. The Vivaro van is built at Luton. In the 2020s the range has shifted toward electrified and fully electric models, in line with Stellantis policy.

Motorsport

Vauxhall enjoyed considerable success in the British Touring Car Championship, particularly in the 1990s and 2000s with the Cavalier, Vectra and Astra, winning multiple drivers' and manufacturers' titles.
