Protestant Church in Germany

The Protestant Church in Germany (German: Evangelische Kirche in Deutschland, abbreviated EKD) is a federation of twenty Lutheran, Reformed (Calvinist), and United (Prussian Union) Protestant regional churches (Landeskirchen) in Germany, which collectively encompass the vast majority of the country's Protestants. With roughly 18–19 million members in recent years — around a fifth of the German population — it is one of the largest Protestant church bodies in the world, though membership has been declining for decades.

History

The EKD was founded in 1945 at Treysa, in the aftermath of the Second World War, uniting the regional churches that had been subjected to Nazi pressure through the state-aligned "German Christians" movement and opposed by the Confessing Church. During the division of Germany, the East German regional churches withdrew to form their own federation in 1969, rejoining the EKD after reunification in 1991. The organisation's seat is in Hanover.

Structure

The EKD is a federation rather than a single church: its member Landeskirchen remain autonomous in doctrine and administration. The governing bodies of the EKD are the Synod (its parliament), the Council (executive), and the Church Conference of regional church leaders. The Council is headed by a chairperson (Ratsvorsitzender), elected for a six-year term. The EKD coordinates common tasks such as the church tax framework, military and prison chaplaincy, development work through Brot für die Welt, and the church's media and publishing activities.

Membership and role

Alongside the Roman Catholic Church, the EKD enjoys the status of a public-law corporation in Germany, and its members pay a church tax (Kirchensteuer) collected by the state. The church runs extensive diaconal (social welfare) services through Diakonie Deutschland, one of the country's largest employers. Declining membership — driven by secularisation and formal resignations — has led to restructuring, parish mergers and debates about the church's future structure and finances.
