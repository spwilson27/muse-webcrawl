Simple machine

Simple machine
TypesLever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, screw
Concept fromAncient Greek and Renaissance mechanics
[Image: A labelled diagram of the six classical simple machines: lever, pulley, wheel, incline, wedge and screw]

A simple machine is a mechanical device that changes the direction or magnitude of a force, trading distance for force (or vice versa) without adding energy. The classical list, settled by Renaissance writers from ancient sources, comprises six: the lever, the wheel and axle, the pulley, the inclined plane, the wedge and the screw.

Mechanical advantage

Each machine is characterised by its mechanical advantage — the ratio of output to input force — limited in practice by friction. Archimedes is said to have boasted that with a lever long enough he could move the Earth. Simple machines are the building blocks of compound machines and of the engines and tools built from them, from wind turbines' gears to cranes.
