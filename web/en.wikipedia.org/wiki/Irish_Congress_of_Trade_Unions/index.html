Irish Congress of Trade Unions

The Irish Congress of Trade Unions (ICTU; Irish: Comhdháil na gCeardchumann), often known simply as Congress, is the national trade union centre in Ireland, representing unions in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. It is affiliated to the European Trade Union Confederation and the International Trade Union Confederation.

History

Congress was founded in 1959 by the merger of the rival Irish Trade Union Congress (founded 1894) and the Congress of Irish Unions (founded 1945 after a split over links with British-based unions and the politics of partition). The reunification created a single centre for the whole island of Ireland, a structure that survived the Northern Ireland conflict. Membership peaked at over 700,000 in the early 1980s and stands in the region of 600,000–800,000 members across dozens of affiliated unions, depending on the year and measure used.

Role

From 1987 to 2009 Congress was a central participant in Ireland's "social partnership" process, negotiating a series of national agreements with government and employers covering pay, taxation, and social policy. Since the financial crisis ended partnership, Congress has focused on collective bargaining support, campaigns on workers' rights, pensions, and public services, and representing affiliated unions before government and the Oireachtas. Its president serves a two-year term and its general secretary directs the Dublin headquarters.

Structure

Policy is set by a Biennial Delegate Conference. Congress also maintains a Northern Ireland committee, reflecting its all-island character, and the organisation is unique among European national centres in spanning two jurisdictions.
