Mach number

Mach number
SymbolM, Ma
DefinitionRatio of object speed to speed of sound in the surrounding medium
Named afterErnst Mach

In fluid dynamics, the Mach number (M) is a dimensionless quantity representing the ratio of flow velocity past a boundary to the local speed of sound. It is named after the Austrian physicist Ernst Mach. Where M = u/c, u is the local flow velocity and c is the speed of sound in the medium.

A vapour cone forms around an aircraft near Mach 1

Classification of regimes

  • Subsonic: M < 0.8 (roughly)
  • Transonic: M ≈ 0.8–1.2
  • Supersonic: M ≈ 1.2–5
  • Hypersonic: M > 5

At Mach 1 an object travels at the speed of sound — about 343 m/s (1,235 km/h) in dry air at 20 °C at sea level, but considerably less at high altitude where the air is colder. The speed of sound depends only on the temperature of the medium, not its pressure or density, for an ideal gas.

Use in aviation

High-speed aircraft carry a Machmeter, since at altitude the limiting factor for flight is often the Mach number rather than indicated airspeed. The sound barrier was first officially broken in level flight by Chuck Yeager in the Bell X-1 on 14 October 1947. The fastest crewed aircraft, the SR-71 Blackbird, cruised above Mach 3.
