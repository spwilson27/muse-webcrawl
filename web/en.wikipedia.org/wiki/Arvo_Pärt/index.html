Arvo Pärt

Born: 11 September 1935, Paide, Estonia

Occupation: Composer

Style: Minimalism, tintinnabuli, sacred music

Arvo Pärt (born 11 September 1935) is an Estonian composer of classical and sacred music. Since the late 1970s he has worked in a self-invented compositional technique called tintinnabuli, characterised by diatonic melodies moving stepwise against bell-like triadic figures. He is one of the most performed living composers in the world.

Biography

Pärt was born in Paide, in central Estonia, and grew up in Tallinn. He studied at the Tallinn Music Middle School and the Tallinn Conservatoire, graduating in 1963. His early works were serial and neoclassical; pieces such as Nekrolog (1960) and the Symphony No. 1 (1963) attracted official displeasure in the Soviet Union. After a period of creative silence in the early 1970s, during which he studied early music and Gregorian chant, he emerged with the tintinnabuli style in works such as Für Alina (1976), Tabula Rasa (1977), Spiegel im Spiegel (1978) and Fratres (1977).

In 1980 Pärt emigrated with his family to Vienna, later settling in Berlin. Following Estonian independence he returned to live in Estonia. In 2010 he and his wife Nora founded the Arvo Pärt Centre in Laulasmaa, which houses the composer's personal archive.

Tintinnabuli

The word derives from the Latin tintinnabulum (a little bell). In this technique two voices are strictly paired: a melodic voice moves by step around a central pitch, while a tintinnabuli voice sounds only notes of the tonic triad. Pärt has compared the style to bells and to the ringing of a single chord. His religious works, mostly setting Latin texts, include the Passio (1982), Te Deum (1984), Magnificat (1989), Berliner Messe (1990) and Da pacem Domine (2004).
