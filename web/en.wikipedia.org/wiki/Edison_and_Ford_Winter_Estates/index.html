Edison and Ford Winter Estates

Location2350 McGregor Boulevard, Fort Myers, Florida, U.S.
Area21 acres (8.5 ha)
Built1886 (Edison), 1911 (Ford estate)
DesignationNational Register of Historic Places; Florida Historic Landmark

The Edison and Ford Winter Estates are a historical site and museum in Fort Myers, Florida, preserving the adjacent winter homes of inventor Thomas Edison ("Seminole Lodge") and automobile magnate Henry Ford ("The Mangoes"). The 21-acre property on the Caloosahatchee River includes the houses, botanical gardens, Edison's laboratory, and a museum of inventions.

History

Edison first visited Fort Myers in 1885 and purchased land along the river, completing his winter home, Seminole Lodge, in 1886; he returned most winters for the rest of his life. Here in 1928 he built the Edison Botanical Research Laboratory, part of the project with Ford and Harvey Firestone to find a domestic source of rubber, testing thousands of plants including goldenrod. Ford, Edison's friend and admirer, bought the neighbouring estate in 1916. Presidents, industrialists, and celebrities were frequent guests of both men.

The site today

Edison's widow, Mina, deeded Seminole Lodge to the City of Fort Myers in 1947; the Ford estate was acquired in 1988, and the two are managed together as a single attraction. The gardens contain more than a thousand varieties of plants, including one of the largest banyan trees in the continental United States, planted around 1927 as a four-foot sapling. The museum displays hundreds of Edison's inventions and artefacts, and the site hosts an annual Festival of Light celebrating Edison's birthday in February.
