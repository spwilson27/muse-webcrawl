Comarques of Catalonia

The comarques of Catalonia (Catalan: comarques de Catalunya; singular comarca) are a level of local government and territorial division in Catalonia, Spain, intermediate between the provinces and the municipalities. Catalonia is divided into over forty comarques, each governed by a comarcal council (consell comarcal). The comarca corresponds roughly to the English concept of a county or district, though the term descends from the Catalan word for a traditional market area.

Map of Catalonia's comarcal divisions.

History

The comarques were first formally established by the Generalitat of Catalonia in 1936, during the Second Spanish Republic, building on a territorial division study by geographer Pau Vila. They were abolished under the Franco regime and restored after the return of democracy: the modern system was legislated in 1987, creating 41 comarques, with the Val d'Aran — an Occitan-speaking valley with its own special status — treated distinctly. The comarca of Moianès was added in 2015 after a local referendum, bringing the total to 42.

Functions

Comarcal councils coordinate services that are impractical for small municipalities to run alone, including waste management, water supply, social services, school transport and meals, and local road maintenance. They also serve statistical, planning, and cultural purposes. Well-known comarques include Barcelonès (containing Barcelona), Gironès, Segrià, Osona, Bages, Maresme, Vallès Occidental, and Alt Empordà.
