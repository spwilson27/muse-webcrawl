Quartz crisis

The quartz crisis (also called the quartz revolution) was an upheaval in the watch industry caused by the advent of quartz watches in the 1970s and early 1980s, which largely replaced mechanical watches around the world. It severely affected the Swiss watch industry in particular, which remained focused on traditional mechanical watches while the new technology emerged from Japan.

History

The first quartz wristwatch, Seiko's Astron 35SQ, was released in December 1969. Quartz movements, regulated by a vibrating quartz crystal powered by a battery, were far more accurate and far cheaper to mass-produce than mechanical movements. Japanese makers such as Seiko, Citizen, and Casio rapidly captured the world market.

Impact on Switzerland

Between 1970 and the mid-1980s, Swiss watch employment fell from around 90,000 to under 30,000, and the number of Swiss watch companies dropped from about 1,600 to 600. The Swiss industry was rescued by consolidation into what became the Swatch Group and by the launch of the Swatch watch in 1983, which combined Swiss manufacture with low-cost quartz technology and fashion marketing. Mechanical watches were subsequently repositioned as luxury goods, and the Swiss industry recovered in value terms.
