VH1

VH1 (stylized as VH-1 until 1994; originally an initialism for Video Hits One) is an American basic cable television network owned by Paramount Global through its MTV Entertainment Group division. It was created by Warner-Amex Satellite Entertainment, at the time a division of Warner Communications and the original owner of MTV, and launched on January 1, 1985.

History

VH1 was launched to appeal to an older demographic than MTV, playing adult contemporary and soft-rock music videos with on-camera video jockeys. In 1987 Viacom acquired VH1 along with MTV Networks. In the mid-1990s the channel found a stronger identity with series such as Pop-Up Video and the documentary strand Behind the Music.

Programming

From the 2000s VH1 shifted toward celebrity and nostalgia programming — the I Love the '80s franchise, The Surreal Life and other "celebreality" shows — and later toward reality series including Love & Hip Hop and RuPaul's Drag Race (which moved to MTV in 2023 after airing on VH1 from 2017). Under its "Save the Music" foundation the channel also supported music education in American public schools.
