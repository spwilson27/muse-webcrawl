Herman Hollerith (29 February 1860 – 17 November 1929) was an American statistician, inventor, and businessman who developed an electromechanical tabulating machine for punched cards to assist in summarising information, which was used triumphantly in the 1890 United States census.

Census work

Hollerith joined the US Census Bureau in 1879 and, inspired by railway conductors' punched tickets, devised a system in which data were recorded as holes in paper cards and counted electrically. The 1890 census, facing a rapidly growing population, adopted his machines and finished tabulation years faster than the manual processing of the 1880 census.

Company and legacy

In 1896 he founded the Tabulating Machine Company, which in 1911 merged with other firms to form the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company — renamed IBM in 1924. Hollerith cards (later "IBM cards") became the standard medium of data processing for much of the 20th century, and the 80-column card format persisted for decades.
