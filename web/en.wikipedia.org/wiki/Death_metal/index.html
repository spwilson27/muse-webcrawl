Death metal

Stylistic originsThrash metal, early black metal, hardcore punk
Cultural originsMid-1980s, United States (Florida)
Typical featuresDown-tuned distorted guitars, blast beats, growled vocals

Death metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music that emerged in the mid-1980s. It typically employs heavily distorted, down-tuned guitars, fast palm-muted riffs, deep growling vocals, aggressive drumming with frequent blast beats and double-kick patterns, and lyrics often dealing with death, violence and horror themes.

History

The genre developed from thrash metal through bands such as Possessed, whose 1985 album Seven Churches included the song "Death Metal", and Death, led by Chuck Schuldiner. A major scene coalesced in Tampa, Florida, around Morrisound Recording, producing bands including Morbid Angel, Obituary, Deicide and Cannibal Corpse; other centres included Stockholm, Sweden, home of Entombed and Dismember.

Subgenres

Death metal diversified into numerous subgenres, including brutal death metal, melodic death metal (led by Gothenburg bands such as In Flames and Dark Tranquillity), technical death metal, death-doom and deathcore.

Characteristics

Songs often use complex, chromatic riffing and abrupt tempo and time-signature changes. Vocals range from deep guttural growls to higher shrieks, and the genre's graphic lyrical and artistic imagery has periodically attracted controversy.
