Songdo

Songdo, officially the Songdo International Business District, is a planned "smart city" built from scratch on about 600 hectares (roughly 1,500 acres) of reclaimed land along the waterfront of Incheon, South Korea, some 30 km southwest of Seoul. Part of the Incheon Free Economic Zone, it was developed from the early 2000s by Gale International and POSCO E&C from a master plan by Kohn Pedersen Fox. Songdo is frequently cited as a prototype of the ubiquitous, sensor-rich smart city — and as a case study in the challenges of filling a top-down planned city with residents and businesses.

Design and features

Modelled in part on cities such as New York (Central Park), Venice (canals), and Paris (boulevards), Songdo devotes a large share of its area — about 40% of the IBD — to green space, including the 41-hectare Central Park with a seawater canal. Signature buildings include the Northeast Asia Trade Tower, for a time South Korea's tallest building; the curving G-Tower, which houses UN offices including the Green Climate Fund secretariat; the Tri-Bowl cultural centre; and the Songdo Convensia convention centre. The Incheon Bridge (2009), among the world's longest cable-stayed bridges, links Songdo to Incheon International Airport.

Built in partnership with Cisco, the city embedded sensors and networked services throughout: pneumatic waste collection (no garbage trucks), telepresence screens in homes, traffic and energy monitoring, and integrated building systems. It was designed for LEED-certified green building at district scale.

Development and criticism

Construction began in the early 2000s; the district was substantially complete by the mid-2010s, though population and office occupancy grew more slowly than projected, and critics have called Songdo sterile or "a city in search of people". Its population has nevertheless climbed steadily, it hosts campuses (including SUNY Korea and other universities in the Incheon Global Campus), international schools such as Chadwick International, UN agencies, and a growing biotech cluster around firms like Samsung Biologics and Celltrion. Commentators treat Songdo as both an ambitious experiment in sustainable, technology-driven urbanism and a cautionary tale about master-planned smart cities.
