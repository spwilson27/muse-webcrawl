Financial analysis

Financial analysis
FieldFinance, accounting
InputsFinancial statements, market data
OutputsValuation, credit assessment, forecasts
Key toolsRatio analysis, DCF, trend analysis

Financial analysis (also referred to as financial statement analysis or accounting analysis) is the process of evaluating businesses, projects, budgets and other finance-related transactions to determine their performance and suitability. It is performed by professionals who prepare reports using ratios and other techniques derived from a company's financial statements and other data.

Purpose

Financial analysis is used by both internal and external stakeholders. Managers use it to make operating and financing decisions; investors use it to assess whether a security is undervalued or overvalued; lenders and credit rating agencies use it to judge creditworthiness; and regulators use it to monitor markets. Typical goals include evaluating profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency and growth prospects.

Methods

  • Horizontal analysis compares financial data over a series of reporting periods to identify trends.
  • Vertical analysis expresses each line item as a proportion of a base figure, such as total revenue or total assets.
  • Ratio analysis computes relationships such as the current ratio, debt-to-equity ratio, return on equity and profit margins, and compares them with industry benchmarks.
  • Cash flow analysis examines operating, investing and financing cash flows to assess a firm's ability to generate cash.
  • Valuation techniques, most prominently discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis and comparable-company multiples, estimate the intrinsic value of an asset or company.

Analysts

Financial analysts work for investment banks, asset managers, insurance companies, corporations and rating agencies. Buy-side analysts evaluate investments for their own firms' portfolios, while sell-side analysts publish research for clients of brokerages. Professional designations such as the Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) charter are widely recognised in the field.

Limitations

Financial statements are prepared under accounting standards that involve estimates and judgements, are historical in nature, and may not capture intangibles or off-balance-sheet risks. Analysts therefore adjust reported figures and combine quantitative analysis with qualitative assessment of management, competition and economic conditions.
