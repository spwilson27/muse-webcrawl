Tone row

A tone row or note row (German: Reihe or Tonreihe) is a non-repetitive ordering of a set of pitch classes, typically the twelve notes of the equal-tempered chromatic scale. It is the basic unit of the twelve-tone technique and of serialism.

Transformations

A row may be transformed by transposition, inversion (mirroring its intervals), retrograde (reversal), and retrograde inversion. These operations yield up to 48 related forms that a composer can deploy while preserving the row's intervallic identity.

History

Arnold Schoenberg developed the concept around 1921 as a means of composing without a tonic while avoiding tonal implications. Josef Matthias Hauer independently developed a related system of "tropes." Anton Webern's rows often embed symmetries that he exploited structurally.

Serialism

After the Second World War, composers such as Pierre Boulez and Milton Babbitt generalized the row principle to rhythm, dynamics, and timbre, producing total serialism.
