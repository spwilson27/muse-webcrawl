History of Turkey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Istanbul, the former Constantinople, straddles Europe and Asia.

The history of Turkey encompasses Anatolia and eastern Thrace from prehistory — including Hattians, Hittites, Greeks, Romans and Byzantines — through the arrival of the Seljuk Turks after 1071, the rise and fall of the Ottoman Empire (c. 1299–1922), and the Republic of Turkey founded by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk in 1923.

Ancient Anatolia and Byzantium

Anatolia hosted some of the earliest Neolithic settlements (Çatalhöyük) and the Hittite Empire of the Bronze Age. Greek colonies lined its coasts; the peninsula became part of the Roman and then Byzantine empires, with Constantinople founded in 330 CE as the imperial capital. Arab and later Seljuk Turkish incursions reshaped it; the Seljuk victory at Manzikert in 1071 opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement, and the Sultanate of Rum and beyliks followed.

The Ottoman Empire

From a small frontier beylik, the Ottomans conquered the Balkans, took Constantinople in 1453 under Mehmed II, and under Selim I and Suleiman the Magnificent built an empire spanning three continents, controlling the eastern Mediterranean, the Red Sea and the holy cities of Islam. Defeats from Lepanto (1571) and Vienna (1683) onward, nationalist revolts, and reform efforts (the Tanzimat) could not halt decline. The empire entered the First World War with the Central Powers, lost its Arab provinces, and faced Allied occupation and the Armenian genocide and Greek–Turkish population exchange in its final years.

The Republic

Mustafa Kemal's national movement won the Turkish War of Independence (1919–1923); the Republic was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, followed by sweeping secularising reforms — abolition of the caliphate, a Swiss-based civil code, Latin alphabet (1928) and women's suffrage (1934). Turkey stayed neutral for most of the Second World War, joined NATO in 1952, and alternated civilian rule with military interventions (1960, 1971, 1980, 1997). The Kurdish conflict with the PKK began in 1984. Under Recep Tayyip Erdoğan (prime minister 2003–2014, president since 2014) the country shifted toward a presidential system after the failed coup of 2016 and the 2017 referendum.
