Fomepizole

Trade nameAntizol
Other names4-Methylpyrazole
FormulaC4H6N2
RoutesIntravenous

Fomepizole, also known as 4-methylpyrazole, is a medication used to treat methanol and ethylene glycol poisoning. It may be used alone or in combination with hemodialysis. It is given by injection into a vein.

Common side effects include headache, nausea, dizziness, and drowsiness. It is unclear if use during pregnancy is safe for the baby. Fomepizole works by blocking the enzyme that converts methanol and ethylene glycol to their toxic breakdown products.

Medical uses

Fomepizole is a competitive inhibitor of the enzyme alcohol dehydrogenase, found in the liver. This enzyme plays a key role in the metabolism of ethylene glycol and methanol:

  • Ethylene glycol is first metabolised to glycolaldehyde, and then to glycolic acid, glyoxylic acid, and finally oxalic acid, which can cause renal failure and metabolic acidosis.
  • Methanol is first metabolised to formaldehyde and then to formic acid, which is responsible for the visual disturbances (including blindness) and acidosis seen in methanol poisoning.

By inhibiting alcohol dehydrogenase, fomepizole slows the production of these toxic metabolites, buying time for the parent compounds to be eliminated, typically with the aid of hemodialysis. Fomepizole was approved for medical use in the United States in 1997 and in France in 1999. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
