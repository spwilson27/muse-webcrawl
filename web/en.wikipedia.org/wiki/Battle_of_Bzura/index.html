Battle of the Bzura

The Battle of the Bzura was the largest battle of the Invasion of Poland of 1939 and the most important Polish counter-offensive of the campaign. Fought from 9 to 19 September 1939 in the region west of Warsaw, along the Bzura River around Kutno, Łowicz and Sochaczew, it pitted the Polish Poznań and Pomorze Armies under General Tadeusz Kutrzeba against the German 8th and 10th Armies. The Polish attack initially achieved surprise and drove back the German 8th Army, but the Germans concentrated overwhelming forces, including most of their armoured and air strength in Poland, and destroyed the Polish armies in the Kutno pocket. More than 150,000 Polish soldiers were taken prisoner.

Background

By the second week of the invasion, the Poznań Army, which had not been heavily engaged in western Poland, was retreating east toward Warsaw, while the battered Pomorze Army fell back from the Polish Corridor. Kutrzeba proposed striking southward into the exposed flank and rear of the German 8th Army advancing on Warsaw — an idea approved by the Polish commander-in-chief, Marshal Edward Rydz-Śmigły. The attack was planned without knowing that German forces were already swinging to encircle the Poles from the north.

Battle

The offensive opened on the night of 9/10 September. Polish divisions crossed the Bzura and struck the German 30th Infantry Division, recapturing Łowicz and Łęczyca and advancing toward Stryków, threatening the rear of the German forces closing on Warsaw and briefly forcing the Germans to divert major forces, including panzer divisions and the Luftwaffe's main strength, to the Bzura sector. From 14 September the Germans counterattacked with superior numbers and complete air supremacy; Polish attempts to break out eastward across the Bzura toward Warsaw were cut down, among them the famous charge of the Wielkopolska and Podolska cavalry brigades through the Kampinos Forest. Only remnants — perhaps 30,000–40,000 men — reached Warsaw and Modlin. Kutrzeba himself broke through to the capital; Bortnowski was captured. By 19 September the pocket was eliminated.

Significance

Although doomed, the Bzura battle delayed the German assault on Warsaw by roughly a week, forced the Wehrmacht to fight the largest set-piece battle of the campaign, and consumed supplies and formations earmarked for the advance east. In Polish memory it stands as the campaign's great example of offensive spirit in a hopeless strategic situation.
