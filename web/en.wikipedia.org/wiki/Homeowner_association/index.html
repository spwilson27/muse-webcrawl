Homeowner association

A homeowner association (HOA) is an organization in a subdivision, planned community, or condominium building that makes and enforces rules for the properties and residents, and maintains common areas. Those who purchase property within an HOA's jurisdiction automatically become members and are required to pay dues, known as HOA fees or assessments. HOAs are usually formed by the real-estate developer during construction of a community and later transferred to resident control; they are governed by elected boards under covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CC&Rs) recorded against the properties.

Scope and powers

HOAs number in the hundreds of thousands in the United States, encompassing tens of millions of housing units — a large and growing share of American homes. They typically maintain landscaping, roads, lighting, and amenities such as pools and clubhouses; enforce aesthetic and behavioral rules (exterior paint colors, parking, pets, rentals); and levy regular and special assessments. Their enforcement powers, derived from the recorded covenants and state law, may include fines, liens, and in some states foreclosure for unpaid assessments. Condominium associations and cooperative boards perform analogous functions for attached housing.

Criticism and regulation

Supporters argue HOAs preserve property values and provide services municipalities do not. Critics cite arbitrary or intrusive rule enforcement, lack of due process, financial mismanagement, and foreclosure over small debts. Many states have enacted statutes regulating HOA governance, disclosure, meetings, records access, and foreclosure powers, and case law has analogized HOAs to "mini-governments" or businesses in different contexts.
