Anne, Queen of Great Britain

Anne (6 February 1665 – 1 August 1714) was Queen of England, Scotland and Ireland from 1702, and Queen of Great Britain and Ireland following the political union of England and Scotland on 1 May 1707, until her death in 1714. She was the last monarch of the House of Stuart.

Early life and accession

Anne was the second daughter of the future James II and his first wife Anne Hyde. Raised as an Anglican despite her father's Catholicism, she married Prince George of Denmark in 1683. During the Glorious Revolution of 1688 she sided against her father and supported the accession of her sister Mary and brother-in-law William III. The death of her only surviving child, the Duke of Gloucester, in 1700 prompted the Act of Settlement 1701, which settled the succession on the Protestant House of Hanover. Anne succeeded William in 1702.

Reign

Anne's reign was dominated by the War of the Spanish Succession, in which England, allied with the Dutch and Austria, won notable victories under the Duke of Marlborough, whose wife Sarah Churchill was Anne's intimate friend and influential favourite until their famous falling-out. Domestically, the Acts of Union 1707 united England and Scotland into Great Britain. Politics was fiercely divided between Whigs and Tories; Anne presided over the development of the two-party system and cabinet government, and was the last British monarch to veto a bill passed by Parliament (1708).

Family and death

Despite seventeen pregnancies, none of Anne's children survived to adulthood — a personal tragedy that shaped the succession question throughout her reign. Plagued by ill health, including gout, she died at Kensington Palace on 1 August 1714 and was succeeded, under the Act of Settlement, by her second cousin George I of Hanover.
