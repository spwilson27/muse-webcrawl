Third International Theory

Author: Muammar Gaddafi

Set out in: The Green Book (1975–1979)

Applied in: Libya's Jamahiriya system

The Third International Theory was the political philosophy of Libyan leader Muammar Gaddafi, presented as an alternative to both capitalism and communism. It was set out in The Green Book and formed the ideological basis of Libya's Jamahiriya ('state of the masses') from 1977.

Principles

The theory rejected parliamentary representation as a fraud on democracy, proposing instead direct rule through people's congresses and committees. Economically it favoured partnership over wage labour and condemned rent and profit; socially it drew on Islam, Arab nationalism and Bedouin traditions.

In practice

Critics argued real power remained with Gaddafi and revolutionary committees, making the system authoritarian despite its participatory language. See History of Libya under Muammar Gaddafi.
