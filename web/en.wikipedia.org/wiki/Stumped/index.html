Stumped

A wicket-keeper stump­ing a batsman who has left his crease

Stumped is a method of dismissing a batsman in cricket, governed by Law 39 of the Laws of Cricket. A batsman is out stumped if, in receiving a delivery, the batsman steps outside the popping crease and the wicket-keeper puts the wicket down (breaks the stumps with the ball) while the batsman is out of the crease and not attempting a run.

Conditions

The dismissal can only be effected by the wicket-keeper, and only off a lawful delivery — a batsman cannot be stumped off a no-ball, although they can be run out. If the batsman is out of the crease while attempting a run, a dismissal by the keeper counts as a run out rather than a stumping. Stumpings are most common against spin bowling, when batsmen advance down the pitch to attack and are beaten by turn or flight. The dismissal is credited to the bowler.

Records

Indian wicket-keeper Mahendra Singh Dhoni holds the record for the most stumpings in international cricket, and is renowned for the speed of his glovework. In Test cricket, Australia's Bert Oldfield and India's Syed Kirmani are among the leading keepers by stumpings. Wide balls that pass the batsman can still produce a stumping, and keepers standing up to medium-pace bowling occasionally achieve stumpings off quick deliveries as well.
