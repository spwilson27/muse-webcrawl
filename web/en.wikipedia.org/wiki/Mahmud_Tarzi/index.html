Mahmud Tarzi

Mahmud Beg Tarzi (1865–1933) was an Afghan journalist, politician, and diplomat, widely regarded as the father of Afghan journalism and a leading figure of the Young Afghan modernist movement. He served as Afghanistan's foreign minister under King Amanullah Khan.

Early life and exile

Tarzi was born in Ghazni into the influential Mohammadzai family. His father, Ghulam Muhammad Tarzi, a poet and regional leader, was exiled by Amir Abdur Rahman Khan in 1881, and the family settled in Damascus, Syria. There Tarzi was exposed to Ottoman reformist and Young Turk ideas, studied languages and literature, and traveled in Europe.

Return and journalism

Tarzi returned to Afghanistan in 1903 under Amir Habibullah Khan. In 1911 he founded the newspaper Seraj al-Akhbar (Lamp of the News), which became a highly influential voice for modernization, nationalism, education, and constitutional reform, and helped inspire a generation of Afghan reformers.

Political career

After Afghanistan gained independence in 1919, Tarzi became foreign minister and led early diplomatic missions establishing relations with foreign states. He was a close adviser to King Amanullah, whose reform program he strongly influenced; his daughter Soraya became queen. Following Amanullah's fall, Tarzi lived in exile in Turkey, where he died in Istanbul in 1933.
