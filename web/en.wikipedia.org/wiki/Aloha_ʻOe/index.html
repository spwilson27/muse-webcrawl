"Aloha ʻOe" ("Farewell to Thee") is a Hawaiian song written by Liliʻuokalani, the last monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii. It is her best-known composition and one of the most recognisable Hawaiian songs in the world, traditionally sung as a farewell at gatherings, ceremonies and departures from the islands.


Liliʻuokalani, the song's composer

History

Liliʻuokalani, then a princess, composed the song around 1878. According to tradition, she was inspired on a horseback ride to the Boyd ranch at Maunawili on the windward side of Oʻahu, where she witnessed a tender farewell embrace between a young Hawaiian girl and a colonel of the Royal Household guards. The melody of the chorus bears a resemblance to the earlier song "The Lone Rock by the Sea", and elements of the tune recall Charles Crozat Converse's "When This Cruel War Is Over".

The song was first performed publicly by the Royal Hawaiian Band in San Francisco, and it was published in Boston in 1884 by the Pacific Music Company. Originally a love song, it gradually took on the character of a lament and farewell, an association deepened by its performance as the deposed queen farewelled visitors during her imprisonment after the overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy in 1893–1895.

Lyrics

The chorus, the best-known part of the song, reads:

Aloha ʻoe, aloha ʻoe
E ke onaona noho i ka lipo
One fond embrace, a hoʻi aʻe au
Until we meet again.

The verses mix Hawaiian and English, describing rain sweeping through the forest, the rosy glow of sunset on the ʻāhihi lehua blossoms, and the singer's love returning like a bird to its nest.

Cultural impact

"Aloha ʻOe" has been recorded by numerous artists, including Elvis Presley (in the 1961 film Blue Hawaii), Johnny Cash, and Israel Kamakawiwoʻole. An instrumental version features in the Disney film Lilo & Stitch. The song is sung at the closing ceremonies of schools and civic events throughout Hawaii, and its melody has become a near-universal musical symbol of the islands, played by airport bands and at the departures of ships and aircraft.
