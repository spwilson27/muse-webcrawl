The Province of Salamanca (Spanish: Provincia de Salamanca) is a province of western Spain, in the southwestern part of the autonomous community of Castile and León. It borders Portugal to the west and the Spanish provinces of Zamora, Valladolid, Ávila, Cáceres.

Geography

The province occupies the northern Iberian plateau (Meseta), drained by the Duero (Douro) and its tributaries the Tormes, Águeda and Yeltes. In the south the land rises into the Sierra de Francia and Sierra de Béjar. The dehesa — open oak parkland used for grazing Iberian pigs and fighting bulls — is characteristic of the western plains.

History and culture

The capital, Salamanca, is famous for its university, founded in 1218 and among the oldest in Europe, and for its sandstone old city, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other notable towns include Ciudad Rodrigo, Béjar, Peñaranda de Bracamonte and La Alberca. The province is known for cured ham (jamón ibérico) from Guijuelo and for its bull-breeding estates.

Economy

Agriculture and livestock dominate the rural economy, alongside services and education in the capital. Like much of inland Spain, the province faces population decline and ageing.
