Leisure

Leisure is free time spent away from business, work, job hunting, domestic chores, and education, as well as necessary activities such as eating and sleeping. The distinction between leisure and unavoidable activities is not always rigid: people sometimes do work-oriented tasks for pleasure as well as for long-term utility.

History

The word derives from the Latin licere, "to be permitted". In classical Greece, scholē — leisure devoted to learning — was considered the basis of education, and the English word "school" descends from it. Mass leisure is a product of industrialisation: as working hours fell in the 19th and 20th centuries, first in Britain and then across the industrialised world, a commercial leisure industry of music halls, seaside resorts, organised sport and later cinema, radio and television grew up to serve workers' free time.

Types of leisure

Sociologists distinguish active leisure, such as sport, hobbies and volunteering, from passive leisure, such as watching television. The Canadian sociologist Robert Stebbins proposed the concept of "serious leisure" — the systematic pursuit of an amateur, hobbyist or volunteer activity that is substantial and fulfilling — in contrast to "casual leisure". Play, recreation and tourism are closely related forms.

Sociology of leisure

Leisure studies examines how free time is distributed by class, gender, age and employment. Thorstein Veblen's The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899) introduced the idea of conspicuous leisure as a display of status. Feminist scholarship has emphasised that women's leisure is often constrained by unpaid domestic labour, while recent research considers the effects of digital media and smartphones on the boundary between work and free time.
