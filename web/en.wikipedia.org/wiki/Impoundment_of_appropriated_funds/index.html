Impoundment of appropriated funds

Impoundment is an act by a President of the United States of not spending money that has been appropriated by Congress. Historically, presidents from Thomas Jefferson onward withheld appropriated funds, but the practice became a major constitutional controversy under President Richard Nixon, who used impoundment extensively to block programs he opposed.

Impoundment Control Act of 1974

In response to Nixon's impoundments, Congress enacted the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act of 1974. The Act distinguishes between rescissions, in which the President proposes that funds be permanently cancelled and Congress must approve within 45 days, and deferrals, temporary delays in spending that Congress may overturn. If Congress does not approve a rescission, the funds must be released and spent.

Legal challenges

In Train v. City of New York (1975), the Supreme Court held that the administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency could not allot less than the full amounts authorized by statute. The Government Accountability Office monitors impoundments and has issued opinions finding violations of the Act when agencies withheld funds for policy reasons rather than permissible programmatic delays.
