Inmos

Inmos International plc was a British semiconductor company founded in 1978 by Iann Barron, Tony Petritz and Paul Petritz, with backing from the UK government's National Enterprise Board. Based in Bristol, Inmos was created to give Britain a presence in advanced microelectronics, manufacturing memory chips (SRAM and DRAM) at plants in Colorado Springs, USA, and South Wales.

The transputer

Inmos is best remembered for the transputer, launched in 1985: a pioneering microprocessor designed for parallel computing, with a built-in memory interface and high-speed serial communication links allowing many transputers to be connected into arrays. The transputer was programmed in occam, a language based on Tony Hoare's CSP (communicating sequential processes) model. Transputers were used in embedded systems, robotics, the Meiko Computing Surface supercomputers, and even the graphics hardware of some arcade and workstation products.

Later history

Inmos was sold to Thorn EMI in 1984 and then to SGS-Thomson (now STMicroelectronics) in 1989. The transputer line was discontinued in the mid-1990s as general-purpose processors and standard interconnects overtook it, but its ideas — processors with integrated communications, and process-oriented concurrency — influenced later multicore and many-core designs. Alumni of Inmos went on to found numerous Bristol-area technology companies.
