A co-driver is the navigator of a rally car in the sport of rallying, who sits in the front passenger seat. The co-driver's job is to navigate, give the driver detailed instructions about the road ahead (called pace notes), and manage timing, strategy, and communications with officials.

Role

During a special stage the co-driver reads pace notes aloud, describing corners by severity, crests, hazards, and distances, allowing the driver to drive at speed over roads they cannot fully see. On road sections between stages, the co-driver handles the road book, time cards, and route instructions, ensuring the crew checks in at controls exactly on schedule to avoid penalties.

Pace notes

Pace notes are created during reconnaissance ("recce") runs before the event, when the crew drives the stage at road speed and the driver dictates shorthand descriptions, which the co-driver writes and later reads back at rally speed. Numerical systems (1–6 or 1–10) grade corner tightness.

In other motorsport

In off-road events such as the Dakar Rally, navigators plot routes through open terrain using road books and GPS. Historically, endurance racing also used riding mechanics who performed a co-driver-like role.
