Battle of the Imjin River

The Battle of the Imjin River, also known as the Battle of Solma-ri or the Battle of Gloster Hill, was fought from 22 to 25 April 1951 during the Korean War. Troops of the British 29th Infantry Brigade, supported by Belgian, Filipino and American units, held positions along the Imjin River against the Chinese People's Volunteer Army's spring offensive, delaying the advance on Seoul. The stand of the 1st Battalion, Gloucestershire Regiment, on Hill 235 — "Gloster Hill" — became one of the most famous actions of the war.

Background

The Chinese Fifth Phase Offensive opened on 22 April 1951 with the aim of destroying UN forces north of Seoul. The 29th Brigade, spread thinly over a wide front, guarded the historic invasion route across the Imjin.

The battle

Chinese forces crossed the river at night and overwhelmed outlying positions. The Gloucesters, on the left of the brigade line, were surrounded on Hill 235 and resisted repeated assaults for two days, supported by artillery — including their own regimental guns firing over open sights — and by Centurion tanks of the 8th Hussars on the valley floor. Relief attempts failed, and on 25 April the brigade withdrew under orders. Fewer than 70 of the Gloucesters' roughly 750 men escaped; the rest were killed or captured, including their commanding officer, Lieutenant-Colonel James Carne, who was awarded the Victoria Cross, as was Lieutenant Philip Curtis, killed in the action.

Legacy

Although a local defeat, the brigade's stand blunted the offensive's thrust toward Seoul and bought time for UN reserves to form a new line. The battle is commemorated annually in Gloucester and at the Gloster Valley memorial in South Korea; the Gloucesters' defence is often described as among the finest battalion actions in British military history.
