Kingdom of Strathclyde

The Kingdom of Strathclyde (Latin: Cumbria; Scottish Gaelic: Srath Chluaidh; Welsh: Ystrad Clud) was a kingdom of the Britons in south-western Scotland and north-western England, historically known as the kingdom of the Clyde Britons, centred on Dumbarton Rock (Alt Clud, "Rock of the Clyde"). It emerged after the Roman withdrawal from Britain and flourished from the 5th century until its lands were absorbed into the Kingdom of Scotland and the Kingdom of England in the 11th and 12th centuries.

History

The kingdom grew out of the territory of the Damnonii, a British tribe recorded in Roman sources. Its kings ruled from Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug commanding the Firth of Clyde, one of the most formidable strongholds in early medieval Britain. In 870 the Viking kings of Dublin, Amlaíb Conung and Ívarr, besieged and captured Alt Clud after a four-month blockade, carrying off many captives; thereafter the political centre of the kingdom seems to have shifted upriver to Govan and Partick on the Clyde.

During the 10th century the kingdom expanded southward into what is now Cumbria, whence the Latin name Cumbria derives. Strathclyde kings such as Dyfnwal (died 975, on pilgrimage to Rome) operated in close, if subordinate, alliance with the kings of Alba. The last clearly recorded king, Owain Foel (Owen the Bald), fought alongside Malcolm II of Scotland at the Battle of Carham in 1018.

Decline and absorption

After Owain Foel, the kingdom faded from the record. By the reign of David I of Scotland (1124–1153), the former Strathclyde territories were firmly part of the Scottish kingdom, though the region retained a distinct identity, and the Cumbric language probably survived into the 12th century. The diocese of Glasgow, refounded under David, perpetuated something of the kingdom's ecclesiastical geography.

Language

The people of Strathclyde spoke Cumbric, a Brittonic Celtic language closely related to Welsh. Place names across the Clyde valley, Lanarkshire, Ayrshire and Cumbria preserve Cumbric elements such as glas (grey-green), pen (head, end) and lanerc (clearing, as in Lanark).
