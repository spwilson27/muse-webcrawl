Amal Movement

The Amal Movement is a Lebanese political party and former militia associated mainly with Lebanon's Shia Muslim community. Its name, Amal, means “hope” in Arabic and was originally an acronym for Afwaj al-Muqawama al-Lubnaniyya, the Lebanese Resistance Regiments.

Origins

Amal grew out of the Movement of the Deprived founded by Musa al-Sadr in the 1970s to demand political and economic inclusion for Shia communities in southern Lebanon, the Beqaa Valley and Beirut's southern suburbs. During the Lebanese Civil War its armed wing became one of the country's major militias.

Politics

After the Taif Agreement, Amal entered electoral politics. Long led by Nabih Berri, speaker of parliament for decades, it has usually allied with Hezbollah while retaining its own patronage networks and parliamentary bloc. Amal's influence rests on a mixture of communal representation, state positions and local organisation.
