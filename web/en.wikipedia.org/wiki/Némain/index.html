Némain

In Irish mythology, Némain (Modern Irish: Neamháin) is a goddess of the frenzy and havoc of war. Her name is usually interpreted as "frenzy", "panic", or "venom". She is one of the trio of war goddesses often grouped together as the Morrígna, alongside Badb and Macha — though in other texts the third member is the Morrígan herself, or Anand.

Némain personifies the panic and confusion of battle.

Role in the tales

Némain's characteristic power is to sow supernatural confusion among armies. In the Táin Bó Cúailnge (Cattle Raid of Cooley), she shrieks over the hosts of Connacht so terribly that warriors die of fright and the army falls into disorder — an episode that illustrates her role as a personification of battle-panic rather than a combatant in her own right. Medieval glossaries and genealogical texts name her as the wife of Neit, a god of war, and count her among the Tuatha Dé Danann. She is also sometimes identified with, or treated as a byname of, Badb, with whom she shares the association with terrifying cries over the battlefield.

Interpretation

Scholars have compared Némain with the Continental Celtic goddess Nemetona, whose name derives from nemeto-, "sacred grove", although the etymology of Némain herself is debated. Like the other Irish war goddesses, she is generally understood not as a goddess who fights but as an embodiment of the terror, madness, and doom of war itself.
