Second Summer of Love

The Second Summer of Love is a name given to the summers of 1988 and 1989 in the United Kingdom, marked by the explosive rise of acid house music, warehouse parties and illegal outdoor raves, and the widespread recreational use of the drug MDMA ("ecstasy"). The name echoes the original Summer of Love of 1967 in San Francisco.

Background

British DJs and clubbers returning from holidays in Ibiza in 1987 — among them Paul Oakenfold, Danny Rampling and Nicky Holloway — brought back the Balearic blend of house music and ecstasy culture. Rampling's London club Shoom, opened in late 1987, is often credited as a starting point. By the summer of 1988 acid house parties had spread across the country, symbolised by the yellow smiley face borrowed from acid house flyers.

Events and reaction

In 1989 large unlicensed raves — including events organised by promoters such as Sunrise, Energy and Biology — drew tens of thousands to fields and aircraft hangars, frequently around London's M25 orbital motorway. Tabloid coverage oscillated between moral panic and fascination. The government responded with increased penalties in the Entertainments (Increased Penalties) Act 1990 and, later, the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, famous for targeting music "characterised by the emission of a succession of repetitive beats."

Legacy

The Second Summer of Love reshaped British popular music and nightlife, launching superstar-DJ culture, the superclub era of brands such as Ministry of Sound and Cream, and feeding directly into Madchester and Britpop-era youth culture.
