Baccalauréat

The baccalauréat (French pronunciation: [bakaloʁea]), often known in France colloquially as the bac, is a French national academic qualification that students can obtain at the completion of their secondary education, roughly at age 18. Created under Napoleon I by decree of 17 March 1808, it is the main school-leaving diploma and generally required for university entrance.

Structure

Students follow one of several streams. Following reforms phased in from 2019–2021, the general baccalauréat combines continuous assessment with final examinations in chosen specialities, French (taken a year early), philosophy and a "grand oral". There are also technological and vocational baccalauréats. Results are expressed as an average over 20, with honours (mentions) from assez bien to très bien.

Equivalent qualifications

Similar qualifications include the German Abitur and the British A-levels. A separate International Option (OIB) and the Franco-German Abibac exist for bilingual students. Each year around 700,000–800,000 candidates sit the baccalauréat, and pass rates in the general stream are typically around 90%.
