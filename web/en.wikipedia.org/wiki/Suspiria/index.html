Suspiria

Suspiria is a 1977 Italian supernatural horror film directed by Dario Argento, who co-wrote the screenplay with Daria Nicolodi, partially based on Thomas De Quincey's 1845 essay Suspiria de Profundis. The film stars Jessica Harper as an American ballet student who transfers to a prestigious European dance academy and discovers it is a front for a sinister conspiracy involving a coven of witches.

Plot

Suzy Bannion arrives in Freiburg during a storm to attend a famed ballet academy. As students begin to die in bizarre and gruesome circumstances, Suzy investigates and uncovers that the school's staff serve the ancient witch Mater Suspiriorum, the Mother of Sighs.

Production

Argento shot the film in vivid Technicolor, imbibing the print to heighten its saturated reds, blues and greens. The score by progressive rock band Goblin is regarded as one of the most celebrated in horror cinema.

Legacy

Suspiria is considered a landmark of Italian horror and of the giallo genre, widely placed on lists of the greatest horror films ever made. A remake directed by Luca Guadagnino was released in 2018.
