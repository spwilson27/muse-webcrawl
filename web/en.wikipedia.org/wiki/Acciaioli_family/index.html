Acciaioli family

The Acciaioli family (also spelled Acciaiuoli or Acciajuoli) was a prominent Florentine banking and noble family that rose to prominence in the 13th and 14th centuries. Originating in Brescia, the family settled in Florence, where they became wealthy through banking and international trade, and later ruled the Duchy of Athens in Greece.

History

The family founded one of the great banking companies of medieval Europe, the Compagnia dei Bardi's rival in scale, with branches across Italy, France, England and the Levant. Their banking house collapsed in the 1340s during the financial crisis associated with the defaults of Edward III of England, but members of the family retained influence through landholdings and political office.

Dukes of Athens

Nerio I Acciaioli seized Athens in 1388, and his descendants ruled the Duchy of Athens until the Ottoman conquest in 1458-1460. The family also held Corinth, Thebes and other territories in Frankish Greece, becoming one of the last Latin dynasties in the region.

Florentine branch

In Florence, the Acciaioli remained important patrons of the arts and letters. Donato Acciaioli (1429-1478) was a noted humanist and translator of Plutarch and Aristotle. The family palace stood near Santo Spirito, and their chapel in the church of Santi Apostoli contains notable Renaissance artwork.
