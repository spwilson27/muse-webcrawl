Heterosexuality

Heterosexuality is romantic attraction, sexual attraction or sexual behavior between people of the opposite sex or gender. As a sexual orientation, it is an enduring pattern of emotional, romantic, and/or sexual attractions to persons of the opposite sex; it also refers to a person's sense of identity based on those attractions, related behaviors, and membership in a community of others who share those attractions. Someone who is heterosexual is commonly referred to as straight.

Terminology

The word heterosexual is a loan translation combining the Greek prefix hetero- ("other, different") with the Latin stem sex-. The term was coined in the late 19th century in the writings of Karl Maria Kertbenny, who also coined homosexual; the earliest uses of both words appeared in an 1868 letter and an 1869 pamphlet arguing against Prussian anti-sodomy laws.

Demographics

Heterosexuality is the most common sexual orientation worldwide. Surveys in Western countries typically find that the large majority of adults — commonly around 90% or more in many studies — identify as heterosexual, though reported figures vary with methodology and cultural context.

Biology and origins

No single factor has been shown to determine sexual orientation. Research suggests a combination of genetic, hormonal and environmental influences; large genome-wide studies have found many small genetic associations with same-sex behaviour, accounting for only part of its variation. Sexual orientation is generally understood by major medical and psychological organisations not to be a choice, and efforts to change it are not supported by evidence.

Society and law

Opposite-sex marriage and relationships are legally recognised in virtually all jurisdictions. The concept of heteronormativity describes the assumption that heterosexuality is the default or normative orientation; scholars of gender and sexuality have examined how this assumption shapes institutions such as marriage, family law and education.
