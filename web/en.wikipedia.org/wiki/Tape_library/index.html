Tape library

TypeData storage device
ComponentsTape drives, cartridge slots, robotic picker
Common mediaLTO cartridges
UseBackup, archiving, cold storage

A tape library, also called a tape silo or tape jukebox, is a data storage system that contains multiple magnetic tape drives, a store of tape cartridges arranged in slots, and a robot that moves cartridges between the slots and the drives. Libraries range from small desktop autoloaders holding a handful of cartridges to enterprise systems holding tens of thousands.

Operation

Backup and archive software addresses cartridges by barcode labels read by the library's scanner. When data is requested, the robot retrieves the correct cartridge, loads it into a free drive, and the drive reads or writes sequentially. Because tapes are removable and cheap per terabyte, libraries are widely used for long-term archival and air-gapped protection against ransomware.

History

Large automated cartridge systems appeared in the 1980s, notably IBM's 3840-series and StorageTek's silo libraries. Modern libraries use LTO media and scale by adding frames, supporting capacities of hundreds of petabytes in hyperscale archives.
