Ajmer is a city in the north-western Indian state of Rajasthan, surrounded by the Aravalli Mountains. It is the administrative headquarters of Ajmer district and lies about 135 kilometres (84 mi) south-west of the state capital, Jaipur, and some 390 kilometres from New Delhi. Ajmer is one of the major and oldest cities of Rajasthan and an important centre of pilgrimage for both Hindus and Muslims.

History

Tradition attributes the founding of Ajmer to the Chauhan ruler Ajayaraja I in the 7th century CE; the city — originally Ajayameru, "invincible hill" — was a capital of the Chauhan dynasty. The Chauhans built Taragarh Fort on the hill above the city, often claimed to be one of the oldest hill forts in India. In 1193 the city fell to Muhammad of Ghor after the Second Battle of Tarain, and it later passed through the hands of the Delhi Sultanate, Mewar and the Marathas.

Under the Mughals, Ajmer became a favoured residence: Akbar made annual pilgrimages to the shrine of the Sufi saint Muinuddin Chishti and built a palace in the city; Jahangir received the English ambassador Sir Thomas Roe here in 1616. Ajmer was ceded to the British in 1818 and became the centre of the small province of Ajmer-Merwara, administered directly from Delhi. After independence it was briefly a separate state before merging into Rajasthan in 1956.

Religion and culture

Ajmer is best known for the Ajmer Sharif Dargah, the tomb of Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti, founder of the Chishti order in India, which draws hundreds of thousands of devotees during the annual Urs festival. The Jain temples of Nareli and the Soniji ki Nasiyan, and the nearby Hindu pilgrimage town of Pushkar on its sacred lake, add to the region's religious importance. The city is also home to Mayo College, founded in 1875 and one of India's oldest public schools.

Geography and economy

Ajmer sits at the foot of Taragarh hill beside the Ana Sagar, an artificial lake built in the 12th century, with the smaller Foy Sagar nearby. The city is a significant railway junction with a large railway workshop, and its economy rests on government services, railways, trade and pilgrimage tourism.
