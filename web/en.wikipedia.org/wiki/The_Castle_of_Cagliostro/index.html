The Castle of Cagliostro


Lupin III: The Castle of Cagliostro (1979)

The Castle of Cagliostro (Japanese: ルパン三世 カリオストロの城, Rupan Sansei: Kariosutoro no Shiro), also known as Lupin III: The Castle of Cagliostro, is a 1979 Japanese animated adventure film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki. It is the second feature film in the Lupin III franchise, based on the manga by Monkey Punch, and was Miyazaki's feature directorial debut.

Plot

The master thief Arsène Lupin III and his partner Jigen rob a casino in Monte Carlo, only to discover the money is counterfeit — the legendary "Goat Bills" traced to the tiny duchy of Cagliostro. There Lupin rescues Lady Clarisse, the duchy's princess, from a forced marriage to the villainous Count Cagliostro, who seeks the lost treasure of the Cagliostro family. Joined by the swordsman Goemon, the rival thief Fujiko, and his nemesis Inspector Zenigata, Lupin storms the Count's castle in a race to its celebrated clock tower climax.

Reception and legacy

The film underperformed on its first release in Japan but has since been re-evaluated as a classic of anime and one of the greatest adventure films ever made, earning the nickname "the greatest adventure film never known in America" from The New York Times coverage of its later rediscovery. Its clock-tower sequence was homaged in Disney's The Great Mouse Detective (1986), and its gentle, romantic version of Lupin profoundly influenced later entries in the franchise and Miyazaki's own subsequent career at Studio Ghibli.
