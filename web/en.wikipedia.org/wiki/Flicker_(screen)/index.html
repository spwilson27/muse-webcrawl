Flicker (screen)

A flicker is a visible change in brightness between cycles displayed on a video display. It applies especially to the refresh interval on a cathode-ray tube (CRT) television or computer monitor, but can also occur in other display technologies. Flicker is most noticeable when the refresh rate is low, and can cause eye strain and headaches with prolonged viewing.

Brightness variation during display refresh

Causes

On a CRT, the image is drawn by an electron beam scanning the phosphor coating of the screen. Because phosphor brightness decays quickly after excitation, each part of the image fades before it is redrawn. If the refresh rate is too low, the human visual system perceives the resulting brightness oscillation as flicker. Flicker is more apparent in peripheral vision and at higher brightness.

Critical flicker fusion frequency

The threshold at which flicker becomes imperceptible is called the flicker fusion threshold. Depending on brightness and viewing conditions it lies roughly between 60 and 90 Hz for most people, though it can be higher in peripheral vision. CRT computer monitors were therefore commonly run at refresh rates of 75 Hz or above.

Other display types

Liquid-crystal displays hold their pixels at a steady state between frames and generally do not flicker in the same way, though some use pulse-width modulation to dim the backlight, which can produce low-frequency flicker. In video recordings, mains-powered lighting can also introduce flicker through interaction with the camera's frame rate.
