Ma'luf

Ma'luf (Arabic: مألوف, meaning "familiar" or "customary") is a genre of art music in the Maghreb region of North Africa, derived from the Andalusian musical tradition of Muslim Spain. It is performed in Algeria, Libya, and Tunisia, each with its own regional variant.

Ma'luf
Origin: al-Andalus / Maghreb
Countries: Algeria, Libya, Tunisia
Structure: Nuba suite
Instruments: Oud, rabab, nay, qanun, percussion

Structure

Ma'luf is organised into suites called nuba, each built on a particular melodic mode (tab') and comprising a sequence of instrumental and vocal movements in contrasting rhythms. A full nuba can take an hour or more to perform.

History

The tradition traces its roots to the court music of al-Andalus, carried to North Africa by Muslim and Jewish refugees after the fall of Granada in 1492. In the 20th century, institutions such as Tunisia's Rashidiyya Institute worked to notate and preserve the repertoire.
