Summative assessment

Summative assessment, or summative evaluation, is the assessment of participants in an educational program at its conclusion, designed to evaluate student learning against a standard or benchmark. It contrasts with formative assessment, which takes place during instruction and is intended to guide teaching and learning.

Examples

Common examples of summative assessments include final examinations, end-of-unit or end-of-chapter tests, culminating projects and portfolios, standardized tests, and final performances or presentations. Results are typically recorded as scores or grades and are often used for decisions about promotion, certification, or admission.

Purpose

Summative assessments serve accountability and reporting purposes: they provide evidence of what students have learned, inform stakeholders such as parents and administrators, and can be used to judge the effectiveness of a curriculum or program. Because they occur at the end of instruction, they offer limited opportunity for the feedback to improve that particular cohort's learning.

Criticism

Critics argue that heavy reliance on high-stakes summative testing can narrow the curriculum, encourage teaching to the test, and disadvantage some groups of students. Advocates respond that common standards and comparable measures are necessary for fairness and accountability in large education systems.
