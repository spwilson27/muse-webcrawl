Biblical epic

GenreEpic film
SubjectStories from the Bible
Peak era1950s Hollywood

A biblical epic is a film genre that dramatises stories from the Bible on a large scale, with lavish sets, large casts and themes of faith, morality and divine intervention. The genre flourished in Hollywood in the 1950s.

History

Silent-era directors such as Cecil B. DeMille made early biblical spectacles, including The Ten Commandments (1923). The genre peaked after the Second World War with films such as Samson and Delilah (1949), Quo Vadis (1951), The Robe (1953), DeMille's remake of The Ten Commandments (1956) and Ben-Hur (1959), which won eleven Academy Awards.

Characteristics

Biblical epics combine religious reverence with spectacle: mass crowd scenes, miracles rendered with special effects, and morally charged clashes between faith and tyranny. Later examples include The Passion of the Christ (2004), Noah (2014) and Exodus: Gods and Kings (2014).
