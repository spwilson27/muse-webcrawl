Jeremiah (c. 650 – c. 570 BC), also called the "weeping prophet", was one of the major prophets of the Hebrew Bible. His ministry spanned the last decades of the Kingdom of Judah, from the reign of Josiah through the destruction of Jerusalem by Nebuchadnezzar II in 586 BC.

Ministry

Called to prophecy as a youth, Jeremiah warned that Judah's unfaithfulness would bring Babylonian conquest, counselled submission to Babylon as God's judgment, and was persecuted, imprisoned, and accused of treason for his message. After Jerusalem fell, he remained with the remnant in Judah; when the governor Gedaliah was assassinated, the survivors fled to Egypt, taking Jeremiah with them against his will.

Writings

The Book of Jeremiah — the longest book in the Bible — was dictated to his scribe Baruch and contains oracles, biographical narrative, and the famous promise of a "new covenant" (Jeremiah 31:31). The Book of Lamentations is traditionally attributed to him.
