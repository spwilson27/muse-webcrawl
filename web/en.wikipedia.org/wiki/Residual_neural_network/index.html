Residual neural network

A residual neural network (ResNet) is a deep learning architecture introduced by Kaiming He, Xiangyu Zhang, Shaoqing Ren, and Jian Sun of Microsoft Research in their 2015 paper "Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition". Its defining feature is the skip connection (shortcut or residual connection), which lets the input of a block bypass its layers and be added to its output.

Residual learning

Instead of learning a desired mapping H(x) directly, layers fit a residual F(x) = H(x) − x, so the output is y = F(x) + x. Residual blocks ease optimization by allowing gradients to flow through the identity path, mitigating the vanishing-gradient and degradation problems that prevented training very deep plain networks. ResNets of 50, 101, and 152 layers won the 2015 ImageNet competition (ILSVRC) with a top-5 error of 3.57%.

Variants and influence

Variants include pre-activation ResNets, ResNeXt (grouped convolutions), Wide ResNets, and DenseNets (concatenative skips). Skip connections are now ubiquitous, appearing in transformers and many other architectures.
