Trojan (celestial body)

In astronomy, a trojan is a small celestial body that shares the orbit of a larger one, remaining in a stable configuration at one of the two Lagrange points 60° ahead of (L4) or behind (L5) the larger body, where the combined gravitational forces provide equilibrium.

Jupiter trojans

The term originally referred to the Jupiter trojans, asteroids clustered around Jupiter's L4 and L5 points. More than a million Jupiter trojans larger than one kilometre are thought to exist — comparable in number to the main asteroid belt. By convention they are named after heroes of the Trojan War, with the L4 group called the "Greek camp" and the L5 group the "Trojan camp". The first, 588 Achilles, was discovered by Max Wolf in 1906. NASA's Lucy spacecraft, launched in 2021, is the first mission to visit Jupiter trojans.

Other trojans

Trojans have also been found for Neptune, Mars, Earth (2010 TK7 and 2020 XL5), Uranus, and Venus (a quasi-satellite configuration), and several moons of Saturn host trojan moons — for example Telesto and Calypso accompany Tethys.
