Pleasure

Pleasure in a shared experience

Pleasure or happiness in the broad sense is a mental state that people experience as positive, enjoyable, or worth seeking. It includes more specific states such as joy, delight, entertainment, and ecstasy, and is contrasted with displeasure, pain, and suffering.

Philosophy

The role of pleasure in the good life has been debated since antiquity. Epicurus taught that pleasure – understood as the absence of pain and tranquillity of mind (ataraxia) – is the goal of life, while Aristippus and the Cyrenaics championed immediate bodily pleasures. Plato and Aristotle treated pleasure as the completion of activity rather than an end in itself. Hedonism holds that pleasure is the only intrinsic good; Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill built utilitarianism on the maximisation of pleasure and minimisation of pain, Mill distinguishing "higher" and "lower" pleasures.

Psychology and neuroscience

In modern psychology pleasure is one pole of core affect – the valence dimension of emotional experience. Neuroscientific research distinguishes "wanting" (incentive motivation, strongly dopaminergic) from "liking" (the hedonic experience itself, associated with small "hedonic hotspots" in the nucleus accumbens and ventral pallidum involving opioid and endocannabinoid signalling). The mesolimbic reward system underlies both, and its dysregulation is implicated in addiction and anhedonia, the inability to feel pleasure.

Varieties

Pleasures commonly distinguished include sensory pleasures (taste, touch), aesthetic pleasure in beauty and art, the pleasure of play and humour, intellectual pleasures such as solving a problem, and social pleasures in company and affection. Philosophers also note pleasures of anticipation and memory alongside present enjoyment.
