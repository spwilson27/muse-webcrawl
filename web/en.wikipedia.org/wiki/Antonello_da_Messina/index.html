Antonello da Messina (c. 1430 – February 1479), properly Antonello di Giovanni di Antonio, was an Italian painter from Messina, Sicily, active during the Early Renaissance. Renowned for his portraits and for his command of oil painting, he was instrumental in transmitting Netherlandish oil technique and detailed realism into Italian art, and his visit to Venice in 1475–1476 influenced Venetian painters including Giovanni Bellini.

Life and training

Little is documented of Antonello's training. He probably trained in Naples, then an international court city where works by Jan van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden, and Petrus Christus could be seen; a later (and probably legendary) story had him study in Flanders. He worked in Sicily through the 1460s and early 1470s, producing altarpieces such as the San Gregorio Polyptych (1473).

Venice and late work

In Venice in 1475–1476 he painted the San Cassiano Altarpiece, of which fragments survive, and received major commissions. His portraits — combining Flemish attention to surface and light with Italian monumentality — set the pattern for Venetian portraiture. Masterworks include St Jerome in His Study (c. 1475, National Gallery, London), the Virgin Annunciate (Palermo), the Salvator Mundi, and the Condottiero portrait in the Louvre. He returned to Messina, where he died in 1479.
