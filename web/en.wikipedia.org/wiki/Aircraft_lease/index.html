Aircraft lease

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Airliners operated under leasing arrangements (image placeholder)

An aircraft lease is a contractual arrangement in which an airline or other operator obtains the use of an aircraft from an owner (lessor) — typically a leasing company or another airline — without purchasing it. A large share of the world's commercial fleet, roughly half of it, is leased rather than owned outright by the operating airline. Leasing preserves airline capital, provides fleet flexibility, and gives access to modern aircraft without balance-sheet debt.

Types of lease

  • Operating lease (dry lease) — the lessor supplies only the aircraft; the airline provides crew, maintenance, and insurance. Terms commonly run from a few years to about a decade, after which the aircraft is returned or the lease renewed.
  • Finance (capital) lease — a long-term arrangement resembling a purchase, in which the lessee bears most ownership risks and may acquire the aircraft at term end.
  • Wet lease (ACMI) — the lessor provides aircraft, crew, maintenance, and insurance; used for short-term capacity, seasonal peaks, or start-up operations. A damp lease supplies the aircraft and cockpit crew but not cabin crew.

The leasing industry

Specialist lessors such as AerCap, BOC Aviation, and Air Lease Corporation purchase aircraft directly from manufacturers — often through sale-and-leaseback transactions in which an airline sells newly delivered aircraft to a lessor and leases them back — and place them with operators worldwide. Ireland, particularly Dublin and Shannon, is the industry's principal hub, owing to its tax treaties, legal framework, and aviation expertise; the Cape Town Convention of 2001 harmonised international rules on creditors' rights in aircraft.

Economics

Monthly lease rates for popular narrowbody aircraft are typically in the hundreds of thousands of US dollars, depending on aircraft age, type, and market conditions. Airlines must return aircraft in specified maintenance condition and often pay maintenance reserves during the term.
