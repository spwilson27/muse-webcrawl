Kelvin problem

The Kelvin problem asks how space can be partitioned into cells of equal volume with the least surface area per cell, i.e., what is the most efficient foam structure. Lord Kelvin proposed a solution in 1887 based on the truncated octahedron, whose slightly curved faces form what became known as the Kelvin structure.

Kelvin structure

Kelvin's candidate fills space with copies of a truncated octahedron — a fourteen-faced polyhedron with six square and eight hexagonal faces — with the hexagonal faces slightly curved to satisfy Plateau's laws for soap films. For more than a century it was believed optimal.

Weaire–Phelan structure

In 1993, Denis Weaire and Robert Phelan found a better structure using two different cell shapes — an irregular dodecahedron and a tetrakaidecahedron — with about 0.3% less surface area. The Weaire–Phelan structure was used in the design of the Beijing National Aquatics Center (the "Water Cube") for the 2008 Olympics. Whether it is optimal remains unproven.
