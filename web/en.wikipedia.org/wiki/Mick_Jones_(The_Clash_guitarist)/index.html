Mick Jones (The Clash guitarist)

Michael Geoffrey Jones (born 26 June 1955) is a British musician, singer and songwriter best known as the co-founder, lead guitarist and co-lead vocalist of the punk rock band the Clash, one of the most influential groups in rock history. After the Clash he founded Big Audio Dynamite, which fused punk with dance, hip-hop and reggae, and later collaborated with Damon Albarn in Gorillaz and The Good, the Bad & the Queen. Jones was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of the Clash in 2003.

The Clash

Jones grew up largely with his grandmother in south London and formed the proto-punk band London SS before joining Joe Strummer, Paul Simonon and (eventually) Topper Headon to form the Clash in 1976. Jones was the band's principal composer, writing the music to most of Strummer's lyrics and singing lead on several signature songs, including "Train in Vain", "Should I Stay or Should I Go" and "Lost in the Supermarket". His melodic, eclectic guitar work drove the band's expansion from punk into reggae, funk, rockabilly and hip-hop on albums such as London Calling (1979) and Sandinista! (1980). Growing tensions led Strummer and Simonon to fire him in 1983.

Big Audio Dynamite and later work

Jones formed Big Audio Dynamite (B.A.D.) in 1984 with film-maker Don Letts; the band scored hits with "E=MC²", "Medicine Show" and the 1991 US number one modern-rock track "Rush". He later worked as a producer (notably for The Libertines' debut Up the Bracket), reunited with Tony James in Carbon/Silicon, played guitar on Gorillaz's Plastic Beach tour, and curated the Rock and Roll Public Library, an archive of punk memorabilia drawn from his own collection. In 2011 he briefly reunited B.A.D.'s original line-up.
