The Whisky a Go Go is a historic nightclub on the Sunset Strip in West Hollywood, California, United States. It opened on 16 January 1964 and became one of the most famous rock and roll venues in the world, a launching pad for artists including The Doors, Van Halen, Guns N' Roses, Mötley Crüe, and Led Zeppelin. It was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006.

History

The venue was founded by former Chicago policeman Elmer Valentine and partners, in a former bank building at the corner of Sunset Boulevard and Clark Street. It took its name from the French discothèque style — "à gogo" meaning "in abundance" — and popularised go-go dancing, with female dancers performing in glass cages suspended above the floor, an innovation said to have arisen because the DJ booth left no room for a dance floor.

Johnny Rivers recorded his hit live album at the club in 1964. The Doors served as the house band in 1966 until they were fired after performing the Oedipal section of "The End". In the 1980s the club became a centre of the glam metal scene. It remains an active all-ages live music venue.
