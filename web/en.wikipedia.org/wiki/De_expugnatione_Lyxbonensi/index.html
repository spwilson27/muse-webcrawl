De expugnatione Lyxbonensi

De expugnatione Lyxbonensi
English: On the Conquest of Lisbon
Author: attributed to "Osbernus" (Raol?)
Language: Latin
Date: c. 1147–1148

De expugnatione Lyxbonensi ("On the Conquest of Lisbon") is a Latin eyewitness account of the Siege of Lisbon in 1147, when a fleet of northern European crusaders on their way to the Holy Land for the Second Crusade helped King Afonso I of Portugal capture Lisbon from its Muslim rulers. It is one of the most vivid narrative sources for the crusading movement and for twelfth-century warfare.

The text

The work takes the form of a letter written by an Anglo-Norman priest who accompanied the expedition — traditionally identified as "Osbernus", though the Cambridge manuscript (Corpus Christi College, MS 470) is associated with a priest named Raol. It describes the fleet's gathering at Dartmouth, the voyage, storm losses, the negotiations with Afonso at Porto, the debates among the crusaders about whether to stop at Lisbon, the construction of siege engines, the surrender of the city on 24 October 1147, and its aftermath, including disputes over plunder. Famous within the text is the sermon attributed to Pedro Pitões, bishop of Porto, urging the crusaders to attack Lisbon.

Significance

Because so few crusade narratives were written by ordinary participants rather than court historians, the letter is prized by historians for its detail on siege tactics, crusader motivation, and relations between the northerners and the Portuguese. It was edited and translated by Charles Wendell David in 1936, an edition long standard in English-language scholarship.
