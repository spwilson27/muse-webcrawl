The Anglo-Australian Telescope (AAT) is a 3.9-metre equatorially mounted telescope operated by the Australian Astronomical Observatory and situated at the Siding Spring Observatory site, New South Wales, Australia, at an altitude of a little over 1,100 metres. Funded jointly by the United Kingdom and Australia, it was commissioned in 1974 and for many years was among the five largest optical telescopes in the world.

History and instruments

Construction began in the late 1960s; the mirror was cast by Owens-Illinois and figured by Grubb Parsons. The AAT's innovative computer control system was one of the first on a major telescope. Its instruments have included the Two Degree Field (2dF) multi-object spectrograph, whose 400 optical fibres enabled the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey of over 220,000 galaxies, and the AAOmega spectrograph, the HERMES high-resolution spectrograph used for the GALAH galactic archaeology survey, and the IRIS2 infrared imager.

Since 2010 the telescope has been funded and operated solely by Australia following the United Kingdom's withdrawal from the partnership. The AAT remains a productive research instrument, particularly for large spectroscopic surveys of stars and galaxies.
