Jack Cole (choreographer)

Jack Cole (born John Ewing Richter; April 27, 1911 – February 17, 1974) was an American dancer, choreographer, and theatre director widely regarded as the father of theatrical jazz dance. His fusion of ballet, modern dance, ethnic forms (especially East Indian), and acrobatics defined the vocabulary of dance in Broadway and Hollywood musicals.

Career

Cole began with the Denishawn company, studying under Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn, then performed with the Humphrey-Weidman modern dance company before moving into nightclub and Broadway work. At Columbia Pictures in the 1940s he created signature numbers for stars including Rita Hayworth (Gilda), Betty Grable, and Jane Russell, and famously coached Marilyn Monroe for Gentlemen Prefer Blondes (1953) — including "Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend" — and River of No Return.

Style and legacy

Cole's demanding classes became a crucible for dancers such as Matt Mattox, Buzz Miller, and Carol Haney, and his style — isolated movements, knee slides, angular arms, and explosive ensemble unison — strongly influenced Bob Fosse and Jerome Robbins. His exercises survive in jazz dance training worldwide.
