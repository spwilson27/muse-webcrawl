Conference of Presidents of Major American Jewish Organizations

The Conference of Presidents of Major American Jewish Organizations (often simply the Conference of Presidents) is an umbrella organization of American Jewish groups, founded in 1955. It comprises more than fifty national Jewish organizations across the political and religious spectrum and serves as a forum for coordinating their positions on foreign policy, especially concerning Israel and the Middle East.

History

The Conference was formed in the mid-1950s, with encouragement from Israeli and American officials, to give the U.S. government a single address for consultation with the organized Jewish community on issues affecting Israel. Nahum Goldmann was central to its founding, and Philip Klutznick served as a prominent early chairman. During the 1970s and 1980s the conference became an influential voice in Washington on Soviet Jewry, emigration, and Middle East policy.

Structure and role

The organization is led by a chair (the Conference's elected lay leader) and a chief executive (for many years executive vice chairman Malcolm Hoenlein, who served from 1986 to 2019). It convenes meetings of its member organizations' presidents, issues consensus statements, meets with U.S. presidents and foreign leaders, and organizes missions to Israel and other countries. Critics have sometimes questioned how fully its consensus positions reflect the diversity of American Jewish opinion.
