Great Acceleration

The Great Acceleration is the dramatic, continuous surge in human activity and its impacts on Earth's geology and ecosystems beginning around the mid-20th century (c. 1950). It is a central candidate marker for the start of the proposed Anthropocene epoch.

Startc. 1950
Key indicatorsCO₂, population, GDP, nitrogen, water use
Proposed bySteffen et al., IGBP (2004/2015)

Indicators

Will Steffen and colleagues charted 24 'hockey-stick' graphs: socio-economic trends (population, real GDP, foreign investment, energy, fertilisers, dams, transport, telecommunications) and Earth-system trends (carbon dioxide, methane, ocean acidification, tropical forest loss, biosphere degradation) all inflect sharply after 1950.

Significance

Causes include post-war economic growth, fossil fuels, the Green Revolution and globalisation. The Great Acceleration underpins planetary-boundaries research and debates over dating the Anthropocene. See also Green Revolution.
