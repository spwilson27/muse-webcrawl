Descriptive statistics

Descriptive statistics are statistics that quantitatively describe or summarise features of a collection of information, usually a data set. Descriptive statistics are distinguished from inferential statistics: a descriptive statistic describes what the data show, whereas inferential statistics aim to draw conclusions that extend beyond the data at hand — for example, generalising from a sample to a population, or choosing between competing models.

Use in statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics provide simple summaries about a sample and the measures computed from it. Together with simple graphical displays such as histograms, bar charts, box plots and scatter plots, they form the foundation of virtually every quantitative analysis of data, and are usually reported in the preliminary stages of an investigation before any formal inference is attempted.

Measures of central tendency

The most familiar summaries describe the centre of a distribution. The mean is the arithmetic average of the values; the median is the middle value when the data are ordered; and the mode is the most frequently occurring value. Which measure is most informative depends on the shape of the distribution and the presence of outliers.

Measures of dispersion

Measures of spread describe how variable the data are. These include the range (maximum minus minimum), the interquartile range, the variance and its square root, the standard deviation. Standardised scores and measures of association, such as the correlation coefficient, describe relationships within and between variables. Multivariate descriptive statistics extend these ideas to data recorded on several variables at once.
