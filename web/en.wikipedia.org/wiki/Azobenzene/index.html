Azobenzene

Azobenzene
FormulaC12H10N2
AppearanceOrange-red crystals
Melting point69 °C (trans isomer)

Azobenzene is a chemical compound composed of two phenyl rings linked by an N=N double bond. It is the parent compound of the large class of azo dyes and is famous for its photochromism: the trans isomer converts to the cis isomer on irradiation with ultraviolet light, and reverts thermally or with visible light.

Photoisomerisation

The light-driven trans–cis switching changes the molecule's geometry and dipole moment, making azobenzene a prototypical molecular photoswitch used in photopharmacology, data storage research and light-responsive materials.

History

Azobenzene was first described in 1834 by Eilhard Mitscherlich, who obtained it by distilling nitrobenzene with alcoholic potash.
