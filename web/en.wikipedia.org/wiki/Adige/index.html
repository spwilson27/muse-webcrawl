Adige

Adige (Etsch)
Length: 410 km (255 mi)
Source: Reschen Pass, South Tyrol
Mouth: Adriatic Sea, near Chioggia
Basin size: about 12,100 km²
Cities: Merano, Bolzano, Trento, Verona, Legnago

The Adige (Italian: Adige [ˈaːdidʒe]; German: Etsch) is the second-longest river in Italy, after the Po, rising in the Alps in the province of South Tyrol, near the Italian border with Austria and Switzerland, and flowing 410 kilometres (255 mi) through most of northeastern Italy to the Adriatic Sea.

Course

The river's source is near the Reschen Pass (1,504 metres) close to the borders with Austria and Switzerland, above the artificial lake Reschensee, whose submerged church tower is a well-known landmark. The Adige flows through the Vinschgau (Val Venosta) valley past Merano, then through Bolzano, where it is joined by the Eisack (Isarco), and on to Trento. South of Trento it enters the Vallagarina and flows past Rovereto, then through a narrow gorge into the Po Valley at Verona. From Verona it turns southeast across the plain, passing Legnago and Rovigo's province, and reaches the Adriatic Sea just south of the mouth of the Brenta and near Chioggia.

History

The Adige valley has been a principal north–south route across the Alps since antiquity, part of the corridor later followed by the Via Claudia Augusta and by the modern Brenner railway and motorway. The river is famous in German literature and was long paired with the Rhine in the first stanza of the Deutschlandlied ("von der Etsch bis an den Belt"). Because of its Alpine regime and history of devastating floods in the plain, the lower course was extensively regulated with levees and diversions in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Economy

The upper valley is noted for intensive apple orchards; the river and its tributaries support numerous hydroelectric plants, and its waters irrigate farmland across the Venetian plain.
