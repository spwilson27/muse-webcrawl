Curry powder is a spice mixture based on the concept of the South Asian dishes collectively known as curry. Most commercially produced curry powder relies on a base of turmeric, which gives it its characteristic yellow color, together with coriander, cumin, fenugreek, and chili peppers in varying proportions.

History

Curry powder is a Western invention, created for British colonists and merchants who wanted to reproduce the flavor of Indian dishes at home without blending individual spices. It was advertised in Britain as early as the late 18th century, and premixed powders were among the earliest branded food products sold there. The mixture does not correspond to any single traditional Indian blend; Indian cooks typically combine spices per dish, using blends such as garam masala or freshly ground mixtures.

Varieties

Standard supermarket curry powders are usually mild or "Madras" style (hotter, more chili). Caribbean curry powders, shaped by Indo-Caribbean communities in Trinidad, Guyana, and Jamaica, often feature allspice and different chili profiles. Japanese curry, introduced via the British Royal Navy, uses a sweetened, thickened powder or roux block.

Usage

Curry powder flavors dishes across many cuisines, from British coronation chicken and currywurst sauces in Germany to Singapore noodles and South African Cape Malay curries.
