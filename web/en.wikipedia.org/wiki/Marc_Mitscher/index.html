Marc Andrew Mitscher (26 January 1887 – 3 February 1947) was a pioneer in naval aviation in the United States Navy and a senior commander in the Pacific theatre of World War II. A 1910 graduate of the U.S. Naval Academy, he was one of the Navy's earliest aviators (Naval Aviator No. 33) and in 1919 took part in the first transatlantic flight attempt, piloting the flying boat NC-1.

World War II

Mitscher commanded the carrier USS Hornet at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, whose air groups — most famously Torpedo Squadron 8 — suffered devastating losses. He later commanded air forces in the Solomons before taking command of the Fast Carrier Task Force, designated Task Force 58 (or 38 under Admiral Halsey), in early 1944.

Under Mitscher, the fast carriers spearheaded the Central Pacific drive: the raids on Truk, the Battle of the Philippine Sea — where his decision to launch a late-afternoon strike and then turn on the fleet's lights to guide returning pilots home became legendary — the Battle of Leyte Gulf, and the strikes supporting Iwo Jima and Okinawa. Promoted to admiral in 1946, he became Commander in Chief, U.S. Atlantic Fleet, and died of a heart attack in February 1947 while in that command. Two destroyers have been named USS Mitscher in his honour.
