Trajan's Dacian Wars

Date101–102 and 105–106 CE
LocationDacia (modern Romania)
ResultRoman victory; Dacia annexed as a province
CombatantsRoman Empire vs. Dacian kingdom

Trajan's Dacian Wars (101–102 and 105–106) were two military campaigns fought between the Roman Empire and Dacia during the reign of Emperor Trajan. The conflicts ended in Roman victory and the annexation of Dacia as the empire's newest province, bringing Rome control of the region's gold and silver mines.

Background

Dacia, ruled by King Decebalus, had clashed with Rome under Domitian, who had been obliged to accept an unfavourable peace that included Roman subsidies. Trajan, seeking to end the payments, strengthen the Danube frontier, and secure imperial prestige, prepared a large-scale invasion.

The wars

In the first war (101–102), Roman forces crossed the Danube — aided by a great bridge built by Apollodorus of Damascus — and defeated the Dacians, imposing a settlement. After Decebalus rebuilt his power and attacked Roman garrisons, Trajan launched the second war (105–106), besieging and capturing the Dacian capital Sarmizegetusa. Decebalus fled and took his own life; his head was sent to Rome. The wars are depicted in the spiral relief of Trajan's Column in the Forum of Trajan.

Aftermath

Dacia became a Roman province, was colonised by settlers from across the empire, and remained under Roman control until the withdrawal ordered by Aurelian in 271 CE. The conquest provided enormous booty that funded Trajan's building programme in Rome.
