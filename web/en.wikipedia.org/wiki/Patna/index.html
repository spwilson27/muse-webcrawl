Patna

Patna (Hindi: पटना) is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Bihar. Situated on the southern bank of the Ganges near its confluence with the Gandak, Son and Punpun rivers, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places in the world, with a history spanning more than 2,500 years.

History

Ancient Patna was known as Pataliputra, capital successively of the Magadha kingdom and of the Maurya and Gupta empires. Under Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE it was among the largest cities on earth. The Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang described its palaces and learning. The city declined after the Gupta period, was revived under Sher Shah Suri in the 16th century, and became a major trading centre under the Mughals and the British, who made it capital of Bihar and Orissa province in 1912.

Geography and demographics

Patna lies in the middle Ganges plain at about 53 metres elevation and has a humid subtropical climate with very hot summers and monsoon rains. The metropolitan population exceeds two million. Hindi is the principal language, with Magahi, Maithili, Bhojpuri and Urdu also widely spoken.

Landmarks and institutions

Landmarks include the Golghar granary (1786), Patna Museum, the Mahavir Mandir, Takht Sri Patna Sahib — birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh — and the Bihar Museum. Patna University, founded 1917, is among India's oldest. The city is a major rail, road and river transport hub for eastern India.
