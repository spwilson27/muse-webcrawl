Josip Jelačić Bužimski (1801–1859) was a Croatian nobleman, general and statesman who served as Ban (viceroy) of Croatia from 1848. He is remembered as a national hero in Croatia for abolishing serfdom and for his role in the revolutions of 1848.

Early life and career

Born in Petrovaradin into a military family of the Croatian Military Frontier, Jelačić was educated at the Theresian Military Academy in Vienna and pursued a career in the Imperial Austrian Army, rising to the rank of colonel.

Ban of Croatia

Appointed Ban in March 1848, Jelačić convened the Croatian Sabor and issued a proclamation abolishing serfdom in Croatia. When the Hungarian revolutionary government refused Croatian demands for autonomy, he led an army across the Drava River against Hungary in support of the Habsburg crown.

Revolutions of 1848

Jelačić's forces fought at Pákozd and later joined the imperial army that suppressed the Vienna uprising and the Hungarian revolution. His campaign contributed to the restoration of imperial authority under Emperor Franz Joseph I.

Legacy

The central square of Zagreb, Ban Jelačić Square, is named after him, and his equestrian statue stands there. His portrait appears on Croatian currency, and he remains one of the most celebrated figures in Croatian history.
