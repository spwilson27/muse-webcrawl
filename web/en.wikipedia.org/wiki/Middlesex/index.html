Middlesex

Middlesex is a historic county of England, now mostly within Greater London. It took its name from the Middle Saxons, the people of the province between the East and West Saxons, and for centuries it contained the City of London and Westminster, the core of the English (later British) government, while remaining administratively separate. In 1889 the southeastern part, including the metropolis, was transferred to the new County of London; the remainder had its own county council until 1965, when Middlesex was almost entirely absorbed into Greater London, with small parts going to Hertfordshire and Surrey.

Geography

Middlesex was one of the smaller English counties, bounded by the Thames to the south, the Lea to the east, and the Colne to the west, with Hertfordshire to the north. Largely low-lying London Clay country, it was transformed from market gardens and villages into dense suburbs by the Victorian and interwar expansion of London. Places now in London that were historically Middlesex include Hampstead, Islington, Hackney, Tottenham, Enfield, Harrow, Hounslow, Uxbridge, Ealing, and Twickenham.

Survival of the name

Although Middlesex ceased to be an administrative county in 1965, the name persists in institutions: Middlesex University, Middlesex County Cricket Club (based at Lord's), the postal county used in addresses until 1996, and numerous regimental, sporting, and civic bodies. Campaigners, including a Middlesex Day (16 May), keep the identity alive, and the historic county remains a recognized cultural and geographical entity distinct from the modern boroughs that replaced it.
