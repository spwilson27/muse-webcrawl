Health metrics

Health metrics are quantitative measures used to assess the health status of individuals and populations, the performance of health systems, and the burden of disease. Standard metrics underpin epidemiology, health policy, priority setting and the evaluation of interventions.

Population health measures

  • Mortality measures: crude and age-standardised death rates, infant and child mortality rates, maternal mortality ratio, cause-specific mortality.
  • Life expectancy: expected years of life at birth or at a given age, and health-adjusted life expectancy (HALE), which accounts for years lived in less than full health.
  • Morbidity measures: incidence and prevalence of diseases and risk factors.
  • Summary measures: disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), combining years of life lost (YLL) and years lived with disability (YLD); quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), used in health economics.

Health system metrics

System performance is tracked through indicators of access (immunisation coverage, skilled birth attendance), quality (hospital mortality, readmissions, patient safety events), equity (distribution of outcomes by sex, income, region) and efficiency (health expenditure per capita, amenable mortality). Organisations such as the World Health Organization, the World Bank and the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) publish standard indicator sets; the Global Burden of Disease study is the largest systematic effort to quantify health loss worldwide.

Challenges

Metrics depend on vital registration quality, which remains incomplete in many low-income countries, requiring statistical modelling. Composite indicators involve value judgements about disability weights and discounting, and indicator-driven targets can distort behaviour ("gaming") when used for accountability.
