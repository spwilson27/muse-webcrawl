Gamble House


The Gamble House (1908–09) in Pasadena, California, is a landmark of the American Arts and Crafts movement, designed by Greene and Greene.

The Gamble House, also known as the David B. Gamble House, is an iconic American Arts and Crafts house in Pasadena, California, designed by the architectural firm Greene and Greene. Built in 1908–1909 as a home for David B. Gamble of the Procter & Gamble company and his wife Mary, it is regarded as a masterpiece of American residential architecture.

Design

The three-story house is built of redwood with wide overhanging eaves, exposed rafter tails, and open sleeping porches taking advantage of the California climate. The Greene brothers designed not only the building but its furniture, lighting, textiles, and gardens, achieving a total unity of design influenced by Japanese wooden architecture and English Arts and Crafts ideals. Interiors feature rich woods — teak, mahogany, and oak — with intricate joinery, art glass, and built-in furnishings; the entrance doors carry a celebrated art-glass panel depicting a California oak.

Preservation

The house remained in the Gamble family until 1966, when it was donated to the City of Pasadena in a joint agreement with the University of Southern California School of Architecture, which administers it. It is a National Historic Landmark and is open to the public for tours. It appeared as Doc Brown's house in the film Back to the Future (its interiors inspired the workshop scenes).
