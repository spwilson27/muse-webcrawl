John Bunyan (baptised 30 November 1628 – 31 August 1688) was an English writer and Puritan preacher. He is best remembered as the author of The Pilgrim's Progress (1678), an allegory of the Christian life that became one of the most published books of all time, never out of print and translated into more languages than any book except the Bible.

Early life

Bunyan was born at Elstow, near Bedford, the son of a tinker (a mender of pots and pans). He had little schooling and followed his father's trade. During the English Civil War he served in the Parliamentarian garrison at Newport Pagnell (1644–47), an experience reflected in his later writing. Returning to his trade, he married, and — as he recounts in his spiritual autobiography Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners (1666) — passed through years of religious terror and despair before finding assurance of salvation and joining the Bedford Separatist congregation of John Gifford around 1653.

Preaching and imprisonment

Bunyan soon became a popular Nonconformist preacher. After the Restoration he was arrested in November 1660 for preaching without a licence and refusing to conform to the Church of England. He spent most of the next twelve years in Bedford county gaol, supporting his family by making shoelaces, and there began writing religious works. Released in 1672 under the Declaration of Indulgence, he became pastor of the Bedford meeting, but was imprisoned again for about six months in 1677, during which he completed The Pilgrim's Progress.

The Pilgrim's Progress

Published in February 1678, The Pilgrim's Progress from This World to That Which Is to Come follows the everyman hero Christian as he flees the City of Destruction and journeys to the Celestial City, passing the Slough of Despond, Vanity Fair, and the Delectable Mountains, and meeting characters such as Evangelist, Faithful, Hopeful and Mr. Worldly Wiseman. Its plain, vivid prose — rooted in the English of the King James Bible and of common speech — made it a household book in England and America for three centuries. The Second Part followed in 1684.

Later life and other works

Bunyan published some sixty works, including The Life and Death of Mr. Badman (1680) and The Holy War (1682). In his last years he enjoyed great popularity as a preacher in London as well as Bedford. He died of a fever in August 1688 after riding through heavy rain to reconcile a father and son, and was buried in Bunhill Fields, London.

Legacy

Bunyan's influence on English literature, hymnody ("He Who Would Valiant Be") and the development of the novel is immense; his characters and place-names entered the English language. The John Bunyan Museum in Bedford preserves his memory, and a statue stands on Bedford's St Peter's Green.
