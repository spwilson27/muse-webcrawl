Lost in Translation (film)

Lost in Translation is a 2003 romantic comedy-drama film written and directed by Sofia Coppola. Bill Murray stars as Bob Harris, an aging American film star who travels to Tokyo to appear in whisky advertisements, and Scarlett Johansson plays Charlotte, a young college graduate accompanying her photographer husband. The two form an unlikely bond amid their shared insomnia, alienation and culture shock in a Tokyo hotel.

Plot

Bob Harris, in a mid-life slump, struggles with the absurdities of filming Japanese commercials while his marriage stagnates back home. Charlotte, neglected by her work-absorbed husband, questions her own future. The pair meet at the hotel bar and spend several nights exploring Tokyo's nightlife with Charlotte's Japanese friends, visiting karaoke bars, arcades and clubs. Their relationship deepens into a chaste intimacy; at their parting at the airport, Bob whispers something inaudible to Charlotte — a moment that became one of the film's most discussed scenes — before they separate.

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing about $119 million against a $4 million budget. Coppola won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and the film was nominated for Best Picture, Best Director and Best Actor (Murray, who won a BAFTA and a Golden Globe). It is frequently ranked among the best films of the 2000s. See also Sofia Coppola.
