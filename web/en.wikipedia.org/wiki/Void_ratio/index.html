Void ratio


Phase diagram of a soil sample showing voids and solids

The void ratio of a mixture or composite is the ratio of the volume of voids to the volume of solids. It is a key parameter in materials science and geotechnical engineering, where it describes how densely a soil or granular material is packed.

Definition

The void ratio is defined as:

e = VV / VS

where VV is the volume of voids (gas- or liquid-filled) and VS is the volume of solid particles. It is related to porosity n by e = n / (1 − n), and conversely n = e / (1 + e). Unlike porosity, void ratio can exceed 1, as in soft clays.

Engineering significance

Void ratio controls a soil's compressibility, permeability and shear strength. Loose sands have high void ratios that decrease on densification; sensitive clays may naturally sit at void ratios above their liquid-limit values. Consolidation testing tracks the change of void ratio with effective stress, plotted as an e–log p curve.
