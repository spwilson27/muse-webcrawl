Meiji era

Period of Japanese history

23 October 1868 – 30 July 1912

Preceded by: Keiō era / Edo period

Followed by: Taishō era

Emperor: Meiji

The Meiji era (明治時代, Meiji jidai) was an era of Japanese history that extended from October 23, 1868, to July 30, 1912. It was the first era of the reign of Emperor Meiji, following the Meiji Restoration, during which Japan rapidly modernised, industrialised, and emerged as a great power.

Meiji Restoration and reforms

The Restoration of 1868 ended the Tokugawa shogunate and restored practical imperial rule. The new government abolished the feudal domains (han) in 1871, created prefectures, ended the hereditary privileges of the samurai class, introduced conscription (1873), a land-tax reform, a modern banking system, railways, telegraph lines, and compulsory education. The Iwakura Mission (1871–73) studied Western institutions, and thousands of foreign advisers (oyatoi gaikokujin) were employed.

Constitution and politics

The Meiji Constitution, promulgated in 1889 and effective from 1890, established a constitutional monarchy with the emperor as sovereign, an elected lower house (Diet), and cabinets led by elder statesmen (genrō). Political parties developed despite limited suffrage, extended gradually over the era.

Wars and expansion

Japan defeated Qing China in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), gaining Taiwan, and defeated Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) — the first modern victory of an Asian power over a European great power. Korea was made a protectorate in 1905 and annexed in 1910.

Culture

The era's slogan bunmei-kaika ("civilisation and enlightenment") brought Western dress, architecture, literature, and science into daily life, while the government also promoted State Shinto and imperial ideology. The era ended with Emperor Meiji's death on 30 July 1912.
