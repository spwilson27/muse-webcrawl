The Air Force Reserve Command (AFRC) is a major command of the United States Air Force and the organizational headquarters of the Air Force Reserve, the federal reserve component of the Air Force. Headquartered at Robins Air Force Base, Georgia, AFRC commands all Air Force Reserve units except those mobilized or gained by other major commands, comprising roughly 70,000 Selected Reserve airmen, hundreds of aircraft, and units at dozens of locations across the United States.

History

The Air Force Reserve traces its origins to the post-Second World War reorganization of American air power and the Air Force Reserve Act of 1948. For much of its history the reserve was administered by the Continental Air Command and later the Air Force Reserve (AFRES), a separate operating agency. On 17 February 1997, AFRES was elevated to a major command and redesignated Air Force Reserve Command, giving the reserve a four-star-level organizational standing within the active-duty structure and a direct voice in Air Force planning.

Mission and organization

AFRC's mission is to provide combat-ready forces for the full range of Air Force operations, from strategic airlift and aerial refueling to fighter, rescue, weather reconnaissance, and space operations. The command is organized into numbered air forces: Fourth Air Force (airlift and tanker forces), Tenth Air Force (fighter, rescue, and special missions), and Twenty-Second Air Force (airlift). Many reserve units operate in "associate" arrangements, sharing aircraft with active-duty units, while others own their equipment outright. The command also operates the famous "Hurricane Hunters" of the 53rd Weather Reconnaissance Squadron.

Operations

Air Force reservists have been mobilized for every major American contingency since Korea, including Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm, the Balkans campaigns, and the post-2001 wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, in which tens of thousands of AFRC members were activated. In peacetime, reservists support airlift channels, aeromedical evacuation, aerial firefighting with MAFFS-equipped C-130s, and disaster relief at home.
