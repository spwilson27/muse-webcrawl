Japanese counter word

In Japanese, counter words or counters (助数詞, josūshi) are measure words used with numerals to count things, actions and events. A noun cannot ordinarily be counted directly; instead the numeral combines with a counter chosen according to the kind of object being counted.

Usage

The typical order is noun + particle + numeral + counter, as in inu ga san-biki ("three dogs"), where 匹 (hiki, here voiced biki) counts small animals. Common counters include 本 (hon) for long cylindrical objects, 枚 (mai) for flat objects, 冊 (satsu) for books, 台 (dai) for machines and vehicles, 人 (nin) for people, and 個 (ko) as a general default. There are hundreds of counters, though many are rare or specialised.

Phonology

Counters undergo sound changes (rendaku voicing and gemination) depending on the preceding numeral: e.g. 本 is read hon, but ippon (1), sanbon (3), nanbon (how many). Certain numeral-plus-counter combinations also use irregular readings, such as hitori (one person) and futari (two people).

Origin

Most counters derive from Chinese measure words borrowed with Sino-Japanese vocabulary, while a few, such as つ (tsu) for general objects in the native counting system, are native Japanese.
