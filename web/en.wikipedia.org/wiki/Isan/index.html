Isan

Isan (Thai: อีสาน), also written as Isaan, Issan or Esan, is the north-eastern region of Thailand. It comprises 20 provinces on the Khorat Plateau, bordered by the Mekong River to the north and east, by Cambodia to the south-east, and by the Phetchabun Mountains to the west, separating it from central Thailand.

Geography and population

Isan covers about one-third of Thailand's area and holds roughly one-third of its population, some 22 million people. Major cities include Khon Kaen, Udon Thani, Nakhon Ratchasima (Korat) and Ubon Ratchathani. The Mun and Chi rivers drain the plateau into the Mekong.

Culture and language

The region's culture is strongly influenced by neighbouring Laos; the Isan language is closely related to Lao, though written in the Thai script. Isan is known for mor lam music, silk weaving, sticky rice cuisine — including larb, som tam and grilled chicken — and festivals such as the Rocket Festival (Bun Bang Fai). Historically poorer than central Thailand, Isan has supplied much of Bangkok's migrant labour force.

History

The area was successively influenced by the Khmer Empire, Lan Xang and Siam; it was fully integrated into the Thai state in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
