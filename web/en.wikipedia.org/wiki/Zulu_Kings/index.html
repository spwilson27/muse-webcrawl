Zulu Kings

The Zulu Kings are the monarchs of the Zulu people of southern Africa, ruling the Zulu Kingdom (Zulu: inkosi is the title of the king). The monarchy traces its lineage to the chiefs of the Zulu clan of the Nguni peoples and rose to prominence in the early nineteenth century.

History

The Zulu clan was a minor Nguni chiefdom until Shaka kaSenzangakhona (Shaka Zulu) consolidated power around 1816, forging a large kingdom through military innovation and conquest during the period known as the Mfecane. Shaka was succeeded by his half-brothers Dingane and Mpande. Mpande's son Cetshwayo fought the British in the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879, winning the Battle of Isandhlwana before the kingdom was defeated and partitioned.

Later kings included Dinuzulu, Solomon kaDinuzulu, Cyprian Bhekuzulu, and Goodwill Zwelithini kaBhekuzulu, who reigned from 1968 until his death in 2021. He was succeeded by Misuzulu kaZwelithini, whose coronation took place in 2022.

Role today

The Zulu king is a traditional leader recognised under South African law, with largely ceremonial and cultural authority, presiding over events such as the annual Reed Dance (Umkhosi womHlanga) and the first fruits ceremony (Umkhosi wokwEshwama). The royal house is centred in KwaZulu-Natal, with the principal palace at Nongoma.

List of kings

  • Mpande kaSenzangakhona (1840–1872)
  • Cetshwayo kaMpande (1872–1884)
  • Dinuzulu kaCetshwayo (1884–1913)
  • Solomon kaDinuzulu (1913–1933)
  • Cyprian Bhekuzulu kaSolomon (1948–1968)
  • Goodwill Zwelithini kaBhekuzulu (1968–2021)
  • Misuzulu kaZwelithini (2021–present)
