Center of pressure

The center of pressure is the point where the total sum of a pressure field acts on a body, causing a force to act through that point. The total force vector acting at the center of pressure is the value of the integrated vectorial pressure field. The resultant force and center of pressure location produce equivalent force and moment on the body as the original pressure field. Pressure fields occur in both static and dynamic fluid mechanics. Specification of the center of pressure, the reference point from which the center of pressure is referenced, and the associated force vector allows the moment generated about any point to be computed by translation from the reference point that yields the desired center.

In aerodynamics

For an aircraft wing or airfoil, the center of pressure is the point at which the resultant aerodynamic force (lift and drag combined) can be considered to act, so that it produces no pitching moment. Its position varies with angle of attack: on a typical cambered airfoil it moves forward as angle of attack increases, and moves markedly as the flow regime passes from subsonic to supersonic, shifting from roughly the quarter-chord point to near the half-chord point. Because the center of pressure moves, aerodynamicists often prefer to express pitching moments about the aerodynamic center, where the moment coefficient is approximately constant, and treat lift and drag as acting there.

Stability

The relationship between the center of pressure and the center of gravity determines the static stability of aircraft, rockets, and missiles. For a rocket or missile to be passively stable, its center of pressure must lie behind (below) its center of gravity, so that aerodynamic forces restore the vehicle to alignment with the airflow — the principle used in model rocketry design. In naval architecture, the analogous concept for hulls under sail or water pressure affects the balance of vessels.

In biomechanics

In biomechanics and podiatry, "center of pressure" denotes the point of application of the ground reaction force vector under the foot, whose path during walking (the center-of-pressure trajectory) is measured with force platforms to study balance and gait.
