The Battle of Magdala was fought on 13 April 1868 at the mountain fortress of Magdala in Ethiopia, the climax of the British Expedition to Abyssinia of 1867–68. A British-Indian force under Sir Robert Napier stormed the stronghold of Emperor Tewodros II to free the European envoys and missionaries whom he had imprisoned.

Background

Tewodros II, whose unanswered letter to Queen Victoria and deteriorating domestic position left him increasingly isolated, had seized the British consul Charles Duncan Cameron and other Europeans in 1864 and held them at Magdala. After a relief mission under Hormuzd Rassam was also detained, Britain mounted a major expedition from Bombay. Napier landed at Zula on the Red Sea coast in late 1867 and marched some 400 miles inland through difficult terrain, building roads, a railway from the coast, and supply depots, with a force of about 13,000 troops and many thousands of camp followers and animals.

The battle

The expedition reached the Magdala plateau in April 1868. On 10 April, at the Battle of Arogee, Tewodros's army attacked the British advanced guard and was repulsed with heavy losses by breech-loading rifles and artillery. On 13 April Napier's engineers blasted the gates of the fortress and infantry stormed the plateau; resistance collapsed within minutes. Tewodros, refusing capture, shot himself with a pistol that had originally been a gift from Queen Victoria. The prisoners were released unharmed. British casualties in the storming were remarkably light, while Ethiopian losses at Arogee and Magdala numbered in the thousands.

Aftermath

Napier destroyed Magdala's fortifications and artillery, distributed or removed its treasures — including manuscripts, crowns, and the processional cross whose return Ethiopia has long sought — and withdrew to the coast, leaving Ethiopia to its regional rulers. Napier was raised to the peerage as Baron Napier of Magdala. The looting of Magdala's cultural property has made the battle a prominent case in modern debates over the restitution of museum objects.
