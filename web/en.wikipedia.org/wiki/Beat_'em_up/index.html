A beat 'em up (also known as a brawler) is a genre of video game in which the player character must fight through waves of enemies using hand-to-hand combat, often with simple combinations of punches, kicks, throws, and sometimes weapons. Beat 'em ups are typically presented from a side-scrolling perspective, with the player advancing through levels while defeating groups of opponents, frequently in cooperative multiplayer.

History

The genre's foundations were laid by Irem's Kung-Fu Master (1984), which introduced side-scrolling martial-arts combat against successive enemies. Technōs Japan's Renegade (1986) added a belt-scrolling arena in which the player could move in all directions, and the studio's Double Dragon (1987) popularized two-player cooperative play, weapons, and a combo-based moveset, becoming the template for the genre's golden age.

During the late 1980s and early 1990s, beat 'em ups dominated arcades. Capcom's Final Fight (1989) and licensed titles such as Konami's Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (1989) and The Simpsons (1991) supported up to four simultaneous players. Sega's Streets of Rage series (1991–1994) became a flagship of the genre on home consoles. As three-dimensional graphics became standard in the mid-1990s, the genre declined in arcades, though titles such as Dynasty Warriors carried its crowd-combat formula into 3D.

Gameplay

Players typically traverse linear stages divided into sections; progress is blocked until all enemies in a section are defeated. Common mechanics include life bars, continues, special attacks that consume health, breakable objects containing food or weapons, and end-of-level boss fights. Many games allow two or more players to cooperate, a feature central to the genre's arcade appeal.

Revival

Independent developers revived the genre in the 2010s and 2020s with titles such as Castle Crashers, Scott Pilgrim vs. the World: The Game, Streets of Rage 4 (2020), and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Shredder's Revenge (2022), combining retro presentation with modern design.
