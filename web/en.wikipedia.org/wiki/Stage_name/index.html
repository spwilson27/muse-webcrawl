Stage name

A stage name is a pseudonym used by actors, comedians, musicians, and other performing artists in place of their legal name. Stage names may be adopted for many reasons, including to avoid confusion with another performer, to project a particular image, to conceal one's birth name or origins, to fit the conventions of a genre, or simply because the performer prefers a more distinctive or memorable name.

Reasons for use

Performers adopt stage names for a variety of reasons. A name may be considered too difficult to spell or pronounce, too plain, or potentially confusing with an existing celebrity. Screen Actors Guild rules historically required members to have unique professional names, prompting many actors to register a variant name. Others choose names that better fit their persona or genre: heavy metal musicians, rappers, and professional wrestlers frequently adopt dramatic or menacing names.

Some performers change their names to avoid discrimination. In the early and mid-20th century, many Jewish, Asian, and other ethnic-minority performers in Hollywood anglicized their names; examples include Issur Danielovitch, who became Kirk Douglas, and Margarita Cansino, who became Rita Hayworth. Conversely, some performers adopt names reflecting their heritage as a statement of identity.

Examples

Famous stage names include:

  • Marilyn Monroe, born Norma Jeane Mortenson
  • John Wayne, born Marion Robert Morrison
  • Mark Twain, born Samuel Clemens (a pen name that also served as a stage name for lectures)
  • David Bowie, born David Robert Jones, who changed his surname to avoid confusion with Davy Jones of the Monkees
  • Elton John, born Reginald Kenneth Dwight
  • Lady Gaga, born Stefani Joanne Angelina Germanotta
  • Stefán Karl Stefánsson performed under his own name, while many K-pop idols use single stage names

Legal aspects

A stage name generally does not replace a person's legal name for contracts, taxation, or identification, though some performers legally change their names to their stage names. Professional names can acquire trademark-like protection in some jurisdictions. In unionized industries, credits are recorded under the registered professional name.

In other cultures

In Japanese entertainment, geisha and kabuki actors traditionally take professional names (geimei); kabuki names are often inherited through acting families. Chinese opera performers historically trained under a master's surname. In professional wrestling, ring names are central to character identity and are frequently owned by promotions.
