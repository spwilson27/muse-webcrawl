Racial equality

Racial equality is the concept that all people of all races should be treated equally under the law and have equal opportunities, regardless of racial or ethnic background.

History

Movements for racial equality include abolitionism, the U.S. civil rights movement led in part by Martin Luther King Jr., the anti-apartheid movement in South Africa, and campaigns against colonialism. Landmark law includes the International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (1965).

Concepts

Racial equality is distinguished from colour-blindness and is related to social equality, affirmative action, and transitional justice after systemic discrimination.

Contemporary issues

Disparities in income, housing, education, health and criminal justice remain central concerns in many countries.
