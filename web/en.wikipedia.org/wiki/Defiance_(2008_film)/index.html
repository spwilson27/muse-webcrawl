Defiance is a 2008 American war film directed by Edward Zwick and starring Daniel Craig, Liev Schreiber, and Jamie Bell. It dramatises the true story of the Bielski brothers, Polish Jews who, after escaping the Nazi invasion of their homeland in 1941, hid in the Naliboki forest of what is now Belarus and built a community that rescued more than 1,200 Jews by the end of the Second World War.

Plot

After their parents are murdered, Tuvia Bielski (Craig) leads his brothers Zus (Schreiber) and Asael (Bell) into the forest. What begins as a hiding place becomes a partisan camp — a forest shtetl with a kitchen, hospital, school, and workshops — that takes in any Jew who reaches it, even as tensions between Tuvia's rescue-first philosophy and Zus's desire to fight alongside Soviet partisans threaten to split the group. The film culminates in the camp's perilous flight across a marsh ahead of a German assault.

Production and reception

The film was shot in Lithuania and based on historian Nechama Tec's 1993 book. It received mixed reviews: critics praised the performances and the rescue of a little-known story, while some historians questioned compressions and embellishments. James Newton Howard's score, featuring violin solos by Joshua Bell, was nominated for the Golden Globe for Best Original Score.

Historical note

The Bielski otriad is regarded as the largest armed rescue of Jews by Jews during the Holocaust; descendants of those saved number in the tens of thousands.
