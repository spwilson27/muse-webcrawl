Big Brothers Big Sisters of America is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation whose mission is to "create and support one-to-one mentoring relationships that ignite the power and promise of youth." Adult volunteers ("Bigs") are matched with children ("Littles"), generally aged 5 to young adulthood, to build supportive mentoring relationships.

History

The organisation traces its origins to 1904, when New York court clerk Ernest Kent Coulter organised 39 volunteers to mentor boys appearing in court, founding Big Brothers. In parallel, Catholic "Ladies of Charity" befriended girls in New York children's court, a root of the Big Sisters movement. The two movements merged in 1977 to form Big Brothers Big Sisters of America. The federation now comprises more than 230 local agencies across the United States.

Programmes

The core model is community-based mentoring, in which matched pairs meet regularly for activities in the community, and site-based mentoring, usually in schools, where volunteers meet children at a set location. Special initiatives include Bigs in Blue (police officers as mentors), Military Mentoring, and workplace/mentoring programmes for high-school students. Matches are screened, trained and professionally supported.

Evidence of impact

A widely cited 1995 randomised study by Public/Private Ventures found that after 18 months, mentored youth were significantly less likely than a control group to have started using drugs or alcohol, to have hit someone, or to have skipped school, and showed improved academic performance and family relationships. The organisation's model is frequently cited in mentoring research and policy.
