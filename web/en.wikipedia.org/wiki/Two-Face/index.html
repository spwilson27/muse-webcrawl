Two-Face is a supervillain appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics, commonly as an adversary of the superhero Batman. The character was created by Bob Kane and Bill Finger and first appeared in Detective Comics #66 (August 1942).

Two-Face is the alter ego of Harvey Dent, a former Gotham City district attorney whose face was hideously scarred on one side (in most versions by acid thrown by mob boss Sal Maroni during a trial). The trauma fractured his psyche, and he became obsessed with duality and chance, making decisions — criminal and otherwise — by flipping a two-headed coin, one side of which he scarred to match his face.

Fictional character biography

Harvey Dent was a crusading district attorney allied with Batman and Commissioner James Gordon in an informal triumvirate against organised crime in Gotham. After his disfigurement he embarked on a crime spree themed around the number two, repeatedly crossing paths with Batman while struggling with a split personality torn between his lawful former self and his violent new persona.

In other media

The character has appeared in numerous Batman adaptations. He was played by Billy Dee Williams (as Harvey Dent) in Batman (1989), by Tommy Lee Jones in Batman Forever (1995), and by Aaron Eckhart in The Dark Knight (2008), the latter portrayal following Dent's arc from district attorney to vengeful killer within a single film. Two-Face is a recurring villain in Batman: The Animated Series and many video games.
