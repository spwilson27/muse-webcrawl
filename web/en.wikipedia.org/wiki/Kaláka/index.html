Kaláka

Kaláka is a Hungarian folk music group formed in Budapest in 1969. The band is best known for setting Hungarian poetry to music, performing the works of poets such as Sándor Petőfi, János Arany, Endre Ady, Attila József and Sándor Weöres, as well as for its music for children.

History

Kaláka was founded by brothers Dániel and Miklós Gryllus together with István Faragó and Péter Dabasi; Miklós Radványi joined soon after. The group's name comes from the Hungarian word kaláka, meaning communal work performed together, traditionally for harvesting or house-building. Over more than five decades the ensemble has released dozens of albums and given thousands of concerts in Hungary and abroad.

Style and repertoire

The band's arrangements blend Central European folk traditions with instruments including guitar, mandolin, cello, double bass, clarinet, panpipes and folk flutes. Their settings of children's verse by Sándor Weöres and others made them household names in Hungary, and their annual Kaláka Festival, held in Eger and later Miskolc since 1980, became one of Hungary's oldest folk music festivals.
