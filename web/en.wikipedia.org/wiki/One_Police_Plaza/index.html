One Police Plaza

One Police Plaza (often abbreviated as 1PP) is the headquarters of the New York City Police Department (NYPD). The building is located on Park Row in the Civic Center neighborhood of Manhattan, New York City, near New York City Hall and the Brooklyn Bridge.

History

The NYPD previously operated from headquarters at 240 Centre Street, a Beaux-Arts building in Nolita that served the department from 1909. By the 1960s the old headquarters was considered inadequate, and a new complex was planned as part of the Civic Center urban renewal area. Construction of the new building began in the late 1960s, and the department moved into One Police Plaza in 1973.

The 14-story Brutalist-style building was designed so that its plaza could serve ceremonial functions. The plaza contains the department's memorial wall honoring officers killed in the line of duty.

Security

After the September 11 attacks, security around One Police Plaza was tightened considerably. Park Row, which passes in front of the building, was closed to most traffic, a measure that drew complaints from residents of nearby Chinatown, who argued the closures restricted access to their neighborhood. Portions of the street were later partially reopened following litigation and negotiation.

Facilities

The building houses the offices of the Police Commissioner and senior department leadership, the Real Time Crime Center, and various administrative units. A press room, historically known as "the Shack," hosts reporters covering the department.
