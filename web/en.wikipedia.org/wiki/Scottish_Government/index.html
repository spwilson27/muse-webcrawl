The Scottish Government (Scottish Gaelic: Riaghaltas na h-Alba) is the devolved government of Scotland, responsible for most domestic policy areas including health, education, justice, transport, taxation within devolved limits, and the environment. It was established in 1999 under the Scotland Act 1998, following the 1997 devolution referendum, initially under the name "Scottish Executive" (rebranded by ministers in 2007 and recognised legally in the Scotland Act 2012).

Structure

The government is led by the First Minister, nominated by the Scottish Parliament and appointed by the monarch, who heads the Scottish Cabinet of cabinet secretaries. Ministers are supported by a permanent civil service headed by the Permanent Secretary, headquartered at St Andrew's House on Calton Hill, Edinburgh. Scotland's senior law officers, the Lord Advocate and the Solicitor General, are members of the government; the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service runs public prosecutions.

Powers

Under the devolution settlement, all matters not reserved to Westminster are devolved; reserved matters include the constitution, foreign affairs, defence, immigration, and most pensions and macroeconomic policy. The Scotland Acts 2012 and 2016 added substantial fiscal powers, including control of rates and bands of income tax on non-savings income, land and buildings transaction tax, and some welfare competencies.

History

Labor-led coalition governments under Donald Dewar, Henry McLeish and Jack McConnell ran the Executive from 1999 to 2007. The Scottish National Party formed a minority government in 2007 under Alex Salmond, won a majority in 2011, and held the 2014 independence referendum, in which voters chose to remain in the United Kingdom by 55% to 45%. Nicola Sturgeon served as First Minister from 2014 to 2023 — the longest tenure — followed by Humza Yousaf (2023–2024) and John Swinney (from May 2024).
