Cesare Lombroso

Cesare Lombroso (born Ezechia Marco Lombroso; 6 November 1835 – 19 October 1909) was an Italian criminologist, phrenologist, physician and founder of the Italian school of criminology.

Cesare Lombroso
Born: 6 November 1835, Verona
Died: 19 October 1909, Turin
Known for: Criminal Man (1876), born criminal theory
Field: Criminology, anthropology

Born criminal theory

In L'uomo delinquente (1876), Lombroso argued criminals were evolutionary throwbacks ("atavisms") identifiable by physical stigmata such as skull shape and facial features. He rejected the classical school's emphasis on free will, championing instead empirical, anthropological study of the criminal.

Criticism and legacy

His biological determinism is now largely discredited as pseudoscientific and influenced by racial prejudices of his era, though he is still credited with shifting criminology toward empirical observation and considering social and economic factors in later editions of his work.
