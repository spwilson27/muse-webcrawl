Thiruvalluvar

Native name: திருவள்ளுவர்
Occupation: Poet, philosopher
Language: Tamil
Notable work: Tirukkural
Period: Disputed; commonly placed between the 3rd century BCE and 5th century CE

Statue of Thiruvalluvar at Kanyakumari

Thiruvalluvar (Tamil: திருவள்ளுவர்), commonly known as Valluvar, was a celebrated Tamil poet and philosopher. He is best known as the author of the Tirukkural, a classic Tamil text consisting of 1,330 short couplets (kurals) on ethics, politics, economics, and love, divided among the three books of virtue (aram), wealth (porul), and love (inbam).

Life

Almost nothing is known with certainty about Valluvar's life. Traditional accounts place him in Mylapore, in present-day Chennai, and variously identify him as a weaver or a scholar at the court of a Pandya king. His religious identity has been claimed by Hindus and Jains alike, and the Tirukkural is notable for its broadly non-sectarian ethical outlook; estimates of his date range over many centuries.

Legacy

  • The Thiruvalluvar statue at Kanyakumari, Tamil Nadu — a 41-metre (133 ft) stone statue, its height symbolizing the 133 chapters of the Tirukkural — was unveiled in 2000.
  • Valluvar Kottam in Chennai is a major monument and auditorium in his honour.
  • The Tamil calendar's Thiruvalluvar year, used officially in Tamil Nadu, counts from a traditional birth year of 31 BCE.
  • Thiruvalluvar Day is observed in Tamil Nadu and Puducherry in mid-January.

The Tirukkural has been translated into dozens of languages and is among the most widely translated non-religious texts in the world.
