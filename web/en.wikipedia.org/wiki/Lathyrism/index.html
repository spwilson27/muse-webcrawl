Lathyrism

Lathyrism
SpecialtyNeurology
SymptomsSpastic paralysis of the legs, muscle stiffness, weakness
CausesProlonged heavy consumption of Lathyrus sativus (grass pea) containing the toxin ODAP

Lathyrism is a neurological disease caused by eating large amounts of legumes of the genus Lathyrus over a prolonged period, principally the grass pea Lathyrus sativus. Its main form, neurolathyrism, is caused by the excitatory amino acid toxin ODAP (β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid), which overstimulates glutamate receptors and damages upper motor neurons, producing irreversible spastic paralysis of the legs. A second form, osteolathyrism, caused by a different toxin (β-aminopropionitrile) that interferes with collagen cross-linking, affects the skeleton and connective tissues and is seen mainly in animals. Lathyrism is one of the oldest known neurotoxic diseases and still occurs in parts of South Asia and the Horn of Africa during droughts and famines, when the hardy, drought-resistant grass pea becomes a dominant food.

Signs and symptoms

Neurolathyrism typically appears suddenly after months of a diet heavily based on grass pea. Early symptoms include heaviness and stiffness of the legs, cramps, and difficulty walking; the disease progresses to a spastic paraparesis in which the legs become rigid and crossing (scissoring) may occur. Severe cases leave the victim unable to walk without sticks or crutches. The arms and upper body are usually spared, sensation is intact, and the damage is irreversible, though it is not progressive once consumption of the toxin stops. Young adult men are disproportionately affected, for reasons not fully understood.

History

The condition was described in ancient texts, including the works of Hippocrates, and it devastated communities dependent on the pea during famines — among them populations in Spain during and after the Civil War (where it was known as the "ailment of the almorta"), India, Bangladesh, and Ethiopia. Francisco Goya is sometimes said to have depicted its effects. In the twentieth century the Indian research of scientists at the National Institute of Nutrition identified and characterised the toxin ODAP.

Prevention

Lathyrism is preventable by limiting the proportion of grass pea in the diet, by processing methods that reduce the toxin (soaking and boiling in excess water, parboiling, and fermentation), and by developing low-toxin varieties of the crop. Because L. sativus is exceptionally hardy and nutritious apart from its toxin, plant breeders continue to work on making it safe rather than abandoning it; sales of the pea have at times been banned in parts of India, though the crop remains widely grown and eaten.
