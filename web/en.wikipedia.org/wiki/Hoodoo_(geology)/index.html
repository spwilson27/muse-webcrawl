Hoodoo (geology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hoodoos in the Bryce Canyon amphitheater, Utah

A hoodoo (also called a tent rock, fairy chimney, or earth pyramid) is a tall, thin spire of rock formed by erosion that protrudes from the bottom of an arid basin or badlands. Hoodoos typically consist of relatively soft rock topped by harder, less easily eroded stone that protects the column from the elements. They are found principally in desert areas and generally range in height from that of an average human to heights exceeding a 10-story building.

The most famous hoodoo formations are found in Bryce Canyon National Park in southern Utah, where thousands of orange, red, and white spires fill natural amphitheaters along the eastern edge of the Paunsaugunt Plateau.

Formation

Hoodoos form where sedimentary rock layers of differing hardness are eroded unevenly. At Bryce Canyon, the Claron Formation's limestone, siltstone, and dolomite layers were uplifted and then sculpted by frost wedging — water seeping into cracks, freezing, and expanding — together with rain and gravity. A resistant caprock of dolomite protects the softer rock beneath, allowing a spire to persist while surrounding material is stripped away. When the caprock eventually falls, the unprotected column erodes rapidly.

Distribution

Besides Bryce Canyon, hoodoos occur in the Colorado Plateau region, the badlands of Alberta (notably near Drumheller), Cappadocia in Turkey (where they are called fairy chimneys and were carved into dwellings and churches), Taiwan's Yehliu Geopark, and Serbia's Đavolja Varoš. The formations at Bryce occur at elevations of 2,400 to 2,700 metres (8,000 to 9,000 feet), where more than 200 freeze-thaw cycles per year accelerate erosion.

In culture

Paiute mythology holds that the hoodoos of Bryce Canyon are the Legend People, turned to stone by the trickster Coyote for their bad deeds. Early Mormon settlers called the formations' hollows "prairie dog towns" and applied the word hoodoo, with its connotation of something eerie or unsettling, to the strange landscape.
