The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) is a United Nations agency tasked with helping countries eliminate poverty and achieve sustainable economic growth and human development. Headquartered in New York City, it is the largest UN development aid agency, with offices in 177 countries and territories.

History

UNDP was established on 22 November 1965 by UN General Assembly Resolution 2029 (XX), which merged the Expanded Programme of Technical Assistance (EPTA, founded 1949) and the Special Fund (founded 1958). It began operations on 1 January 1966.

Functions

UNDP works with national governments on development challenges in areas including democratic governance, poverty reduction, crisis prevention and recovery, environment and energy, and HIV/AIDS. It coordinates much of its country-level work through the UN Resident Coordinator system. Since 1990 it has published the annual Human Development Report and its centerpiece measure, the Human Development Index (HDI), developed under the leadership of economist Mahbub ul Haq with Amartya Sen.

Sustainable Development Goals

UNDP is a leading implementing agency for the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted in 2015 as the successor framework to the Millennium Development Goals. Its strategic plans align country programmes with the 17 goals and the 2030 Agenda.

Funding

UNDP is funded entirely by voluntary contributions from UN member states and other donors, amounting to roughly US$5 billion annually in recent years. Major contributors have historically included the United States, Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the European Union.

Administrators

The UNDP Administrator is the third-ranking official in the United Nations system. Past administrators include Paul G. Hoffman (the first), Bradford Morse, William Draper III, James Gustave Speth, Mark Malloch Brown, Kemal Derviş, Helen Clark (2009–2017), and Achim Steiner (2017–2025).
