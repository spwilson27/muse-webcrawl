Episcopal polity

Key officesBishop, priest, deacon

Episcopal polity is a form of church governance in which the chief local authorities are called bishops (from Greek episkopos, "overseer"). The bishop ordains clergy, governs a diocese, and is held in these traditions to stand in apostolic succession from the apostles.

Structure

Typically the threefold ministry of bishops, priests (presbyters) and deacons orders the church. Bishops may be grouped in provinces under archbishops or metropolitans, and in the Roman Catholic Church are in communion with the Pope. Synods and councils often share authority with bishops in varying degrees.

History and theology

Bishops as monarchical heads of local churches are described by Ignatius of Antioch in the early 2nd century. Supporters argue episcopacy preserves unity and continuity; Reformers such as the Presbyterians held that bishop and presbyter are one office in Scripture, while some episcopal churches retained bishops chiefly as a historic and pastoral ordering.
