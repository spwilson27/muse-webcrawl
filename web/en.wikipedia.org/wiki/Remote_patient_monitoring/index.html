Remote patient monitoring

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Home monitoring of vital signs

Remote patient monitoring (RPM) is a technology to enable monitoring of patients outside of conventional clinical settings, such as in the home or in a remote area, which may increase access to care and decrease healthcare delivery costs. RPM involves the constant remote care of patients by their physicians, often to track physical symptoms, chronic conditions, or post-operative rehabilitation.

Incorporating RPM in chronic-disease management can significantly improve an individual's quality of life, allowing patients to maintain independence, preventing complications, and minimizing personal costs. RPM facilitates these goals by delivering care through telecommunications.

Technology

RPM systems typically consist of sensors on peripheral devices — such as blood pressure cuffs, pulse oximeters, glucometers, scales, and wearable ECG patches — a local hub or mobile phone that gathers the data, and centralized software that transmits the information to clinicians for review. Alerts can be generated automatically when readings fall outside preset thresholds.

Applications

The most common applications of RPM are in managing hypertension, heart failure, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and post-surgical recovery. Hospital-at-home programs expanded substantially during the COVID-19 pandemic. In the United States, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services began reimbursing RPM under specific CPT codes in 2019, accelerating adoption.

Limitations

Concerns include data accuracy and patient adherence, the burden of alerts on clinical staff ("alarm fatigue"), reimbursement complexity, interoperability with electronic health records, and the digital divide affecting elderly and low-income patients.
