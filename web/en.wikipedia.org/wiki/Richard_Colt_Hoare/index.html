Richard Colt Hoare

Sir Richard Colt Hoare, 2nd Baronet (9 December 1758 – 19 May 1838) was an English antiquarian, archaeologist, artist and traveller of the 18th and 19th centuries, and a leading figure in the early scientific study of British prehistory. He inherited the Stourhead estate in Wiltshire and is especially remembered for The Ancient History of Wiltshire (1810–1821), produced with the excavator William Cunnington.

Life

Hoare was the son of Sir Richard Hoare, 1st Baronet, of the banking family of C. Hoare & Co. He inherited Stourhead from his grandfather Henry Hoare II, the creator of its famous landscape garden. After the death of his first wife he travelled extensively in Italy, returning with a large collection of drawings and later publishing accounts of his tours.

Archaeology

With William Cunnington, Hoare excavated hundreds of Bronze Age barrows on Salisbury Plain, including Bush Barrow, whose grave goods — including gold ornaments and the famous Bush Barrow lozenge — remain key finds of the Wessex culture. His motto, "We speak from facts, not theory," signalled a new empirical approach to field archaeology. He was the first to classify barrows as bowl, bell, pond and disc types and to recognise many as Bronze Age.

Legacy

Hoare's collections passed to the Wiltshire Museum in Devizes. Stourhead was given to the National Trust in 1946.
