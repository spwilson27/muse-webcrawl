Forts of Metz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The forts of Metz are the rings of fortifications built around the city of Metz in Lorraine, France, principally during the period of German annexation after 1871. Metz, a strategic fortress city near the border, was defended by an inner ring of detached forts built under the direction of General Hans Alexis von Biehler and a later, stronger outer ring constructed from the 1890s to 1916.

History

After the Franco-Prussian War, the German Empire annexed Alsace-Lorraine and fortified Metz as the principal stronghold on its western frontier. The first ring of "Biehler forts" (1870s) was followed by a second ring incorporating Festen — groups of works with armoured batteries, infantry positions, and underground barracks linked by tunnels, notably at Fort Driant (Feste Kronprinz) and Fort Jeanne d'Arc (Feste Kaiserin).

World Wars

The Metz forts saw little action in the First World War, as the front stabilised west of the city. In 1944, during the Battle of Metz, German forces used the forts to resist the U.S. Third Army for nearly three months; the fighting around Fort Driant was particularly costly. Many forts survive today, some accessible to visitors.
