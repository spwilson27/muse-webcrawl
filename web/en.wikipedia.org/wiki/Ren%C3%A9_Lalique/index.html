René Jules Lalique (6 April 1860 – 1 May 1945) was a French jeweller, medallist, and glass designer known for his creations of glass art, perfume bottles, vases, jewellery, chandeliers, clocks, and automobile hood ornaments. He was a defining figure of the Art Nouveau and Art Deco movements.

Career

Lalique trained as a goldsmith and worked for houses such as Cartier and Boucheron before opening his own workshop in Paris in 1885. His jewellery, inspired by nature and using enamel, horn, and semi-precious stones rather than large diamonds, brought him fame at the 1900 Paris Exposition Universelle.

Glass

From about 1907 he collaborated with perfumer François Coty on moulded-glass perfume bottles, then established glassworks at Combs-la-Ville and Wingen-sur-Moder. His firm produced car mascots, architectural glass panels for ocean liners and trains, and decorative objects in the opalescent glass for which the Lalique name remains famous.
