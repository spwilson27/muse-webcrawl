Rail fastening system

A rail fastening system is a means of fixing rails to railway sleepers (North American: ties) or other supports. The fastenings must hold the rails to the correct gauge, resist vertical, lateral and longitudinal forces, allow for thermal expansion, and provide a degree of elasticity and electrical insulation. The term usually refers to systems used with concrete, steel, or composite sleepers, as distinct from the traditional spiked fastenings used on timber sleepers.

Types

Traditional cut spikes driven into wooden sleepers were cheap but loosen over time; screw spikes and coach screws offer greater holding power. Modern elastic fastenings, such as the Pandrol clip (introduced in the 1950s), the Vossloh tension clamp, and various spring and shoulder systems, use spring steel elements that maintain a constant "toe load" clamping the rail foot. Baseplates, rail pads of rubber or polymer, insulators, and shoulders are combined into complete fastening assemblies, especially on concrete-sleeper track.

Functions

Fastenings restrain the rail against overturning and lateral displacement, transfer loads into the sleeper, damp vibration and noise, and maintain track geometry under repeated axle loads. On electrified lines they also insulate the rails from the sleepers for track-circuit signalling.
