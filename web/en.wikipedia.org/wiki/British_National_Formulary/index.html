British National Formulary

PublisherJointly by the British Medical Association and the Royal Pharmaceutical Society
First published1949 (predecessor); modern BNF from 1981
FrequencyTwice yearly in print; continuously online and via app
AudienceUK prescribers, pharmacists and other health professionals

The British National Formulary (BNF) is a United Kingdom pharmaceutical reference book containing a wide range of information and advice on prescribing and pharmacology, along with specific facts about almost all medicines available on the National Health Service (NHS). It is the standard medicines reference used by doctors, pharmacists, nurses and other prescribers throughout the UK.

Contents

The BNF gives details of drug indications, doses, contraindications, cautions, interactions, side effects and costs, organised by body system and therapeutic use, together with guidance notes on prescribing for particular groups such as children, the elderly, and patients with renal or hepatic impairment. A companion volume, the BNF for Children (BNFC), first published in 2005, covers paediatric prescribing. Content is drawn from manufacturers' literature, the MHRA, clinical literature and professional expertise, and is overseen by the Joint Formulary Committee.

History and access

The formulary traces its roots to wartime and early NHS formularies, with the first edition under the British National Formulary name appearing in 1949. The modern format dates from 1981. Print editions are issued every six months and distributed to NHS prescribers; in digital form the BNF is continuously updated through a website and a widely used smartphone app, which are free to NHS staff and, since 2017, the BNF website has been openly available to UK users.
