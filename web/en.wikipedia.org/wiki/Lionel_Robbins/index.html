Lionel Charles Robbins, Baron Robbins (22 November 1898 – 15 May 1984) was a British economist and a prominent member of the economics faculty at the London School of Economics (LSE). He is known for his definition of economics and for his role in moving Anglo-Saxon economics toward a more formal and scarcity-based orientation.

Definition of economics

In his Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science (1932), Robbins defined economics as "the science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses." This scarcity definition became one of the most widely cited definitions of the subject.

Career

Robbins was appointed professor at the LSE in 1929 and headed its economics department through much of the 1930s, recruiting figures such as Friedrich Hayek. He later served in government during the Second World War, chaired the Committee on Higher Education that produced the 1963 Robbins Report — the basis for a major expansion of British universities — and was the first chancellor of the University of Stirling.
