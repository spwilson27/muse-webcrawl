Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team

The "Silver Arrows" dominated the hybrid era, winning eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021.

The Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team is a German Formula One racing team and constructor based in Brackley, England, competing under a German licence. Owned by the Mercedes-Benz Group, Ineos and team principal Toto Wolff, the team is one of the most successful in the sport's history, winning eight consecutive World Constructors' Championships between 2014 and 2021.

History

Mercedes-Benz competed in Grand Prix racing before the war and in Formula One in 1954–1955, when Juan Manuel Fangio won two World Championships in the famous "Silver Arrows" before the company withdrew from motor racing after the 1955 Le Mans disaster. It returned as an engine supplier in 1994 and, in November 2009, purchased the championship-winning Brawn GP team, rebranding it as Mercedes GP for 2010. Michael Schumacher came out of retirement to drive alongside Nico Rosberg.

Hybrid-era dominance

With the introduction of turbo-hybrid power units in 2014, Mercedes began an unprecedented run: Lewis Hamilton (who joined in 2013) won six Drivers' Championships with the team, and Rosberg won the 2016 title before retiring. Mercedes power units, built at Brixworth by Mercedes-AMG High Performance Powertrains, set the class standard. The streak of constructors' titles ended in 2022 as ground-effect regulations returned and Red Bull Racing took over as the sport's benchmark; George Russell had by then replaced Valtteri Bottas alongside Hamilton, and Hamilton departed for Ferrari at the end of 2024, with Andrea Kimi Antonelli stepping up in 2025.

Organisation

The team operates from the Mercedes-Benz Grand Prix campus in Brackley, with engines developed in Brixworth. Technical leadership has included Ross Brawn, Paddy Lowe, James Allison and Mike Elliott.
