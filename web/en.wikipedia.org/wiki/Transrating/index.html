Transrating

Transrating is the process of converting a digital media stream — typically compressed audio or video — from one bitrate to another, usually lower, bitrate without changing its encoding format or resolution. Transrating is commonly used in cable television headends, satellite distribution and streaming delivery to adapt a high-bitrate feed to the bandwidth constraints of a distribution channel or subscriber tier.

How it works

Unlike transcoding, which fully decodes a stream and re-encodes it in a (possibly different) format, transrating operates primarily in the compressed domain. A transrater parses the incoming bitstream, decodes portions of the data — for video, typically the variable-length codes and quantized transform coefficients — and requantizes them more coarsely to reduce the data rate, then regenerates a compliant bitstream. Because motion vectors and other coding decisions are reused, transrating is much less computationally expensive than full transcoding, but the resulting quality is generally lower than an optimal re-encode at the same target rate.

Applications

  • Reducing the bitrate of broadcast feeds to fit satellite transponder or cable multiplex capacity (statistical multiplexing).
  • Delivering lower-bitrate versions of channels to hotels, ships and other closed distribution networks.
  • Rate shaping in some adaptive delivery and network DVR systems.

Related processes

Changing the resolution of a video stream is called transsizing, and converting between formats is transcoding; a system that performs any combination of the three is often simply called a transcoder in industry usage.
