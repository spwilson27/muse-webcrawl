Premchand

Dhanpat Rai Srivastava (31 July 1880 – 8 October 1936), better known by his pen name Munshi Premchand, was an Indian writer famous for his modern Hindustani literature. He is regarded as one of the foremost Hindi writers of the early twentieth century, and is often called Upanyas Samrat ("Emperor of Novels"). His works include fourteen novels, more than three hundred short stories, and numerous essays and translations.

Life

Premchand was born in Lamhi village near Varanasi and worked as a schoolteacher and later as a deputy sub-inspector of schools. He began writing in Urdu under the name Nawab Rai, and switched to Hindi after his first short-story collection was banned and burned by the British authorities in 1909. In 1921 he resigned his government post in response to Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement.

Works

His best-known novels include Godaan (1936, "The Gift of a Cow"), a classic of peasant life; Gaban (1931); Nirmala; and Sevasadan. His short stories, such as "Kafan" ("The Shroud"), "Idgah" and "Namak ka Daroga", are noted for their realism and treatment of poverty, caste, corruption and the condition of women. He also founded the literary journal Hans and presided over the first conference of the Progressive Writers' Association in 1936.
