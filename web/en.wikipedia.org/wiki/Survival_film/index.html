Survival film

The survival film is a film genre in which one or more characters make an effort at physical survival, usually in a hostile natural environment or after a disaster. It often overlaps with adventure, drama and thriller genres, and many survival films are based on true events.

Characteristics

Survival films typically isolate their protagonists — through shipwreck, plane crash, being lost in the wilderness, or catastrophe — and follow their attempts to find food, shelter and rescue, or to reach safety. The genre emphasises endurance, resourcefulness and the psychological effects of isolation.

Examples

Notable survival films include Cast Away (2000), 127 Hours (2010), Gravity (2013), The Revenant (2015), The Martian (2015), Alive (1993), based on the 1972 Andes flight disaster, and Society of the Snow (2023), which retold the same story. Earlier classics include Lord of the Flies (1963) and Deliverance (1972).

Related genres

The genre is related to the Robinsonade, named after Robinson Crusoe, and overlaps with disaster films, though survival films focus on individual endurance rather than large-scale spectacle.
