Evangelista Torricelli

Evangelista Torricelli (15 October 1608 – 25 October 1647) was an Italian physicist and mathematician, best known for his invention of the barometer, but also known for his advanced work in optics and work on the method of indivisibles.

Born15 October 1608, Rome
Died25 October 1647, Florence
Known forBarometer, Torricelli's law, Gabriel's horn
MentorGalileo Galilei

Work

A pupil of Benedetto Castelli and briefly secretary to Galileo in his final months, Torricelli succeeded him as mathematician to the Grand Duke of Tuscany. In 1643–44, with Vincenzo Viviani, he filled a tube with mercury and inverted it into a dish, creating the first mercury barometer and a sustained vacuum, and concluding that we live at the bottom of a "sea of air". Torricelli's law describes the speed of fluid flowing from an opening; his "Gabriel's horn" is a solid of finite volume but infinite surface area. The torr unit is named for him.
