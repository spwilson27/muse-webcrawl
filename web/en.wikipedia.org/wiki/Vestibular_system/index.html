Vestibular system
LocationInner ear
ComponentsSemicircular canals, utricle, saccule
FunctionBalance, spatial orientation, gaze stabilization
NerveVestibular nerve (CN VIII)

The vestibular system, in vertebrates, is a sensory system that creates the sense of balance and spatial orientation for the purpose of coordinating movement with balance. Together with the cochlea in the inner ear, it constitutes the labyrinth, most of which sits in the vestibule of the bony labyrinth.

Structure

The vestibular apparatus contains two types of organs. The three semicircular canals, arranged roughly at right angles to one another, detect rotational acceleration of the head: movement of the endolymph fluid bends the cupula in the ampulla of each canal, deflecting hair cells. The otolith organs — the utricle and saccule — detect linear acceleration and gravity, using a gelatinous layer weighted with calcium carbonate crystals (otoconia).

Function

Signals travel via the vestibular division of the vestibulocochlear nerve to the vestibular nuclei in the brainstem and the cerebellum. Outputs drive the vestibulo-ocular reflex, which stabilizes gaze during head movement, and vestibulospinal reflexes that maintain posture. The system works with vision and proprioception to give a coherent sense of motion and orientation.

Disorders

Dysfunction produces vertigo, dizziness and imbalance. Common disorders include benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV), Ménière's disease, vestibular neuritis and labyrinthitis.
