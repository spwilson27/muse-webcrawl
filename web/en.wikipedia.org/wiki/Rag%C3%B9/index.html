Ragù

Ragù (from French ragoût) is an Italian meat-based sauce, typically cooked slowly, that is commonly served with pasta. The term encompasses several regional varieties, most famously ragù alla bolognese from Bologna and ragù napoletano from Naples.

Varieties

Ragù alla bolognese is made with finely chopped or ground beef (often with pork or veal), soffritto of onion, carrot and celery, white wine, milk and a small amount of tomato; a recipe was officially registered by the Accademia Italiana della Cucina in 1982. Ragù napoletano instead cooks large pieces of meat — such as beef rolls (braciole) and pork — in a rich tomato sauce for many hours, the meat being served as a separate course.

History

Meat sauces for pasta developed in Italy from the late 18th century, influenced by French ragoût; Imola and Bologna are both associated with early recipes. Ragù is traditionally paired with flat pasta such as tagliatelle rather than spaghetti in its home region.

Usage

Beyond pasta, ragù is used in lasagne alla bolognese and other baked dishes, and the word has been adopted internationally for slow-cooked meat sauces.
