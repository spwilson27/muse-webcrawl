easyJet plc is a British multinational low-cost airline group headquartered at London Luton Airport. It operates flights on more than a thousand routes in over 30 countries through its affiliate airlines easyJet UK, easyJet Europe, and easyJet Switzerland. It is one of Europe's largest airlines by passengers carried and is listed on the London Stock Exchange as a constituent of the FTSE 250 Index.

History

easyJet was founded in 1995 by the Greek Cypriot businessman Stelios Haji-Ioannou, modelled on the US carrier Southwest Airlines. It began with two wet-leased Boeing 737s flying from Luton to Glasgow and Edinburgh, famously charging fares advertised as "as cheap as a pair of jeans". The airline pioneered internet ticket sales, sold seats only directly without travel agents, and used bright orange branding across its fleet of Airbus A320-family aircraft. Following Brexit it restructured with an Austrian-registered subsidiary, easyJet Europe, to preserve EU flying rights.

Business model

As a low-cost carrier, easyJet uses a single aircraft family, high utilisation, unallocated seating on early flights (later moving to assigned seating), and ancillary charges for bags, food, and seat selection. Unlike Ryanair, it favours primary airports and attracts business passengers.
