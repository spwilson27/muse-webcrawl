Air operator's certificate

Air operator's certificate
Issued byNational civil aviation authority
PurposeAuthorization for commercial air transport

An air operator's certificate (AOC) is the approval granted by a national aviation authority to an aircraft operator to allow it to use aircraft for commercial purposes — that is, to carry passengers or cargo for hire. An airline generally cannot operate scheduled commercial services without holding a valid AOC issued by the state in which it has its principal place of business.

Requirements

To obtain an AOC, an applicant must demonstrate to the regulator — for example, the Federal Aviation Administration in the United States or a national authority applying EASA rules in Europe — that it has the personnel, aircraft, facilities, and documented procedures to operate safely. Key elements include an accountable manager and named postholders for flight operations, maintenance, and training; an operations manual; an approved maintenance programme; and a safety management system. The certificate is accompanied by operations specifications defining the types of aircraft, routes, and special authorizations (such as low-visibility or long-range ETOPS operations) permitted.

Oversight

Regulators conduct continuing surveillance, audits, and inspections, and may suspend or revoke an AOC for safety deficiencies. Leasing arrangements, flags of convenience, and the use of foreign-registered aircraft have prompted international debate about effective oversight, and ICAO standards require the state of the operator to exercise genuine control.
