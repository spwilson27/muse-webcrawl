The Klingon language (Klingon: tlhIngan Hol, pronounced [ˈt͡ɬɪ.ŋɑn xol]) is a constructed language spoken by the fictional Klingons in the Star Trek universe. It was developed by linguist Marc Okrand for Star Trek III: The Search for Spock (1984), expanding on a few lines of dialogue devised by James Doohan for the first Star Trek film (1979).

Features

Klingon was deliberately designed to sound alien: it has an object–verb–subject word order, rare among natural languages; a consonant inventory including the voiceless lateral affricate /t͡ɬ/ (written tlh) and the uvular plosive /q/; and a heavily agglutinative verb morphology in which prefixes encode subject and object and suffixes are arranged in ordered classes. Okrand drew partly on North American indigenous languages such as Mutsun. Klingon is written in the Latin alphabet with unusual case conventions (q vs Q, tlh, gh, ng), and in the fictional pIqaD script.

Speakers and culture

Okrand codified the language in The Klingon Dictionary (1985, expanded 1992) and later works. The Klingon Language Institute, founded in 1992, promotes the language through conferences (the annual qep'a'), its journal HolQeD, and translation projects — works of Shakespeare including Hamlet and Much Ado About Nothing, the Epic of Gilgamesh, and the Tao Te Ching have been translated into Klingon. A small community of enthusiasts speaks it fluently; it has been offered as a course language on Duolingo since 2018, and in 2004 it briefly had its own edition of Wikipedia.
