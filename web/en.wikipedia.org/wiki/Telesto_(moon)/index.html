Telesto is a moon of Saturn. It was discovered in 1980 from ground-based observations and later confirmed by the Voyager probes; it received the provisional designation S/1980 S 13. It was named in 1983 after Telesto, a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys in Greek mythology.

Orbit

Telesto is a trojan moon of the larger moon Tethys: it orbits at Tethys's leading Lagrange point (L4), 60 degrees ahead of Tethys in the same orbit. Another small moon, Calypso, occupies the trailing L5 point. A similar arrangement exists for Helene and Polydeuces around Dione.

Surface

The Cassini spacecraft flew by Telesto in October 2005, returning images showing a surprisingly smooth, icy surface with relatively few impact craters, suggesting that loose regolith has flowed to soften older features. Telesto measures about 25 km in mean diameter.
