Shigeru Mizuki (Japanese: 水木 しげる, born Shigeru Mura, March 8, 1922 – November 30, 2015) was a Japanese manga artist and historian, best known for the popular yōkai series GeGeGe no Kitarō. A leading figure in both children's horror-comedy manga and in serious autobiographical and historical "gekiga" works about the Second World War, Mizuki is regarded as a master of the yōkai genre, having devoted much of his career to documenting Japanese folklore and ghost traditions.

Early life and war service

Raised in Sakaiminato, Tottori Prefecture, Mizuki was fascinated from childhood by ghost stories told by a local woman he called "Nonnonba". Drafted into the Imperial Japanese Army in 1942, he was sent to New Britain in Papua New Guinea, where he contracted malaria, witnessed the deaths of most of his comrades, and lost his left arm in an Allied bombing raid. His wartime experiences — and his sympathy with the local Tolai people while a prisoner — shaped his lifelong pacifism and his unsparing depiction of the absurdity and cruelty of the war.

Career

After the war Mizuki worked in the rental-book (kashihon) manga market, debuting in 1957 with Rocketman. His version of Kitarō, developed from an earlier rental series, became a national phenomenon when GeGeGe no Kitarō began serialisation in Weekly Shōnen Magazine in 1965: the adventures of Kitarō, last survivor of the Ghost Tribe, his father Medama-oyaji (a walking eyeball), and friends such as Nezumi Otoko, fighting and befriending yōkai. Anime adaptations in every decade since 1968 made the characters household names.

Mizuki's non-fiction manga includes the semi-autobiographical war account Onward Towards Our Noble Deaths (1973), the epic Showa: A History of Japan (1988–89) and the memoir Nonnonba (1977), which won the Best Album prize at the Angoulême International Comics Festival in 2007 — the first Japanese work so honoured. He also compiled yōkai encyclopaedias and travelled the world researching spirits and folklore. In 2010, his wife's memoir was adapted as the NHK morning drama GeGeGe no Nyōbō.

Legacy

Mizuki's hometown of Sakaiminato honours him with Mizuki Shigeru Road, lined with bronze statues of his characters, and the Mizuki Shigeru Memorial Museum. He received the Medal of Honor with Purple Ribbon (1991), the Order of the Rising Sun (2003) and the Person of Cultural Merit award (2010). He died in Tokyo on November 30, 2015, at the age of 93.
