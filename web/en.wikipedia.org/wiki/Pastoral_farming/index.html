Pastoral farming

Pastoral farming is a form of agriculture concerned with the production of livestock rather than growing crops. It involves raising grazing animals such as cattle, sheep, goats, and horses on pasture land for meat, milk, wool, and other animal products.

Characteristics

Pastoral farming is suited to land that is too dry, steep, cold, or infertile for reliable crop production, including the rangelands of Australia and New Zealand, the Eurasian steppe, the African savanna, and upland Britain. Systems range from extensive nomadic and transhumant pastoralism to intensive rotational grazing on improved pastures.

Issues

The sector faces questions of land degradation and overgrazing, methane emissions from ruminants, water use, and animal welfare, alongside its role in food production and rural livelihoods.
