Pudong

Pudong, officially the Pudong New Area, is a district of Shanghai, China, occupying some 1,210 km² on the eastern bank of the Huangpu River, opposite the historic Puxi districts. Its name means "east of the Huangpu". Once farmland and warehouses, it was transformed after 1990 into China's flagship financial and commercial zone, and had a population of over 5.6 million at the 2020 census.

Development

On 18 April 1990 the Chinese government announced the development of Pudong as a special policy zone, with tax incentives modelled on but more extensive than the special economic zones. The Lujiazui Finance and Trade Zone filled with landmark towers: the Oriental Pearl Tower (1994), the Jin Mao Tower (1999), the Shanghai World Financial Center (2008) and the 632-metre Shanghai Tower (2015), China's tallest building. Deng Xiaoping's 1992 southern tour re-energised the project after a slow start.

Economy and infrastructure

Pudong hosts the Shanghai Stock Exchange, the Shanghai Free-Trade Zone (established 2013, the first in mainland China), Waigaoqiao port facilities forming part of the world's busiest container port, and Shanghai Pudong International Airport (opened 1999), briefly linked to the city by the first commercial maglev line. Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park anchors its semiconductor and biotech industries, and the Lingang area hosts Tesla's Gigafactory Shanghai.

Administration

Pudong New Area was formally established in 1993; in 2009 it absorbed Nanhui District, doubling its area and adding the coastline and Lingang. Its GDP passed one trillion yuan in 2018, rivalling a mid-sized province.
