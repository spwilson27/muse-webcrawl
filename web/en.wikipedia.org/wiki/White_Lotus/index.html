White Lotus

The White Lotus (Chinese: 白蓮教; pinyin: Báiliánjiào) was a religious and political movement that appealed to many Han Chinese who found solace in the worship of Wusheng Laomu ("Birthless Old Mother"), a goddess credited with gathering her children at the end of the world to return them to their original home, the Paradise of the True Void. Emerging during China's Yuan dynasty, White Lotus beliefs and societies inspired repeated rebellions against ruling regimes over the following centuries.

The white lotus, emblem of purity and of the movement that took its name.

Origins

The name derives from earlier Pure Land Buddhist White Lotus societies — lay congregations devoted to Amitābha Buddha. Under the Mongol Yuan dynasty these devotional groups merged with Maitreyan and Manichaean-influenced millenarian beliefs. The Red Turban Rebellion (1351–68), whose leaders drew on White Lotus expectations of the coming Buddha Maitreya, helped Zhu Yuanzhang found the Ming dynasty — after which the new emperor, wary of sectarian power, banned the movement, driving it underground.

Rebellions

White Lotus networks repeatedly surfaced in revolt: the great White Lotus Rebellion (1794–1804) against the Qing dynasty in Sichuan, Hubei and Shaanxi drained the imperial treasury and exposed Qing decline, and the Eight Trigrams uprising of 1813 briefly penetrated the Forbidden City. "White Lotus" became partly a blanket label officials applied to heterodox sects, making its history hard to trace precisely. Its millenarian traditions fed into later Chinese secret societies and new religions such as Yiguandao.
