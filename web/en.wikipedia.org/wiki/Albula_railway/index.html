Albula railway

A train crossing the Landwasser Viaduct

The Albula Railway (German: Albulabahn) is a single-track metre-gauge railway line in the Swiss canton of Graubünden, operated by the Rhaetian Railway (RhB). Running from Thusis over the Albula Pass region to St. Moritz, it was opened in 1903–1904 and is celebrated for its spectacular mountain engineering — spiral tunnels, helical loops and soaring viaducts — built without rack assistance. In 2008 the Albula line, together with the Bernina Railway, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Rhaetian Railway in the Albula/Bernina Landscapes".

Route and engineering

The line climbs from Thusis (697 m) through 55 tunnels and over 144 bridges and viaducts to Preda, at the entrance of the 5.9 km Albula Tunnel beneath the Albula Pass, before descending through the Bever valley to St. Moritz (1,774 m). Its most famous structure is the Landwasser Viaduct, a curved limestone viaduct 136 m long and 65 m high whose track emerges directly from a tunnel in a near-vertical cliff. Between Bergün and Preda, the line gains height through a celebrated series of spiral tunnels and viaducts.

Services

The Albula line carries regional trains and the famous Glacier Express, which links St. Moritz with Zermatt. A new Albula Tunnel, built parallel to the original bore, entered service in 2024, replacing the 1903 tunnel for rail traffic while the old tunnel is being retained for safety and heritage purposes.
