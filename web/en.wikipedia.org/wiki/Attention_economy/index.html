Attention economy

Attention economy
FieldEconomics, information science, media studies
Key figuresHerbert A. Simon; Michael Goldhaber; Georg Franck
Core ideaHuman attention is a scarce resource in an information-rich environment

The attention economy refers to the incentives of organisations, particularly advertising-driven companies, to maximize the time and attention their users give to their products, and the implications of such incentives. It treats human attention as a scarce commodity: as content and information become abundant, attention becomes the limiting factor in consuming them.

Origins

The idea was articulated by psychologist and economist Herbert A. Simon, who observed that "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention". The term was popularised in the 1990s by writers such as Michael Goldhaber and Georg Franck, who described attention becoming a kind of currency in networked media.

Applications and criticism

The concept is widely used to analyse advertising, social media and online platforms, whose business models convert user attention into advertising revenue. This has motivated design techniques — notifications, infinite scroll, recommender systems — that critics argue exploit psychological vulnerabilities, fragment public discourse and harm mental health, prompting debates about regulation and "time well spent" design ethics.
