The Battle of Misrata was a battle of the First Libyan Civil War fought in and around the coastal city of Misrata in early 2011. It was one of the longest and bloodiest engagements of the war, in which rebel forces held the city through a gruelling siege by troops loyal to Muammar Gaddafi.

Background

Misrata, Libya's third-largest city and its principal commercial port, rose against Gaddafi's government in February 2011. Although the rebels took control of most of the city, loyalist forces — including the Khamis Brigade — retained positions on its outskirts and besieged it from late February, cutting land routes and subjecting the city to artillery, tank and sniper fire.

The siege

Through March and April 2011 the fighting concentrated along Tripoli Street, the city's main thoroughfare. Rebels, supplied precariously by sea from Benghazi, gradually pushed the loyalists back in intense street fighting. NATO air strikes, authorised under UN Security Council Resolution 1973, attacked Gaddafi's armour and artillery around the city. The port — the besieged population's lifeline — was repeatedly shelled and mined, but humanitarian and supply ships continued to run the blockade.

Outcome

By mid-May 2011 the rebels had captured Misrata airport and pushed loyalist forces out of artillery range of the city centre, effectively lifting the siege. The city's resistance became a symbol of the uprising, and Misratan brigades later played a leading role in the advance on Tripoli and in the final campaign at Sirte. Casualty estimates vary, but the battle is believed to have killed well over a thousand people, including many civilians.
