Sámi people


Regions: Sápmi (northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia's Kola Peninsula)
Languages: Sámi languages, Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish, Russian
Traditional livelihood: Reindeer herding, fishing, hunting

The Sámi people (also spelled Sami or Saami) are an Indigenous people of Sápmi, a region spanning the northern parts of Norway, Sweden and Finland and the Kola Peninsula in Russia. They are known for a distinctive culture centred historically on reindeer herding, fishing and handicrafts such as duodji.

History

The Sámi have inhabited Fennoscandia since prehistoric times. For centuries they were subject to taxation and assimilation policies by the surrounding nation states, including boarding schools that suppressed Sámi languages. In the late twentieth century Sámi parliaments were established in Norway, Sweden and Finland to represent Sámi interests.

Culture

Sámi culture includes the traditional clothing known as gákti, the vocal music tradition of joik, and colourful handicrafts. The Sámi flag, adopted in 1986, features a circle representing the sun and moon. Reindeer herding remains an important, legally protected livelihood in parts of the region.

Languages

The Sámi languages form a group of related Uralic languages. Northern Sámi is the most widely spoken; several others, such as Pite Sámi and Ume Sámi, are severely endangered.
