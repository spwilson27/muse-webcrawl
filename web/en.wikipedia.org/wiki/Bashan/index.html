Bashan (Hebrew: ha-Bashan, "the Bashan") is a biblical region in the Levant, east of the Jordan River, roughly corresponding to the modern Hauran plain and Golan Heights in southern Syria and northern Israel. Its rich volcanic soil made it famous in antiquity for grain, cattle and oak forests — the "bulls of Bashan" and "oaks of Bashan" are recurring biblical images.

In the Bible

According to the Hebrew Bible, Bashan was ruled by King Og of the Rephaim, whom Moses defeated at Edrei; the territory was then allotted to the half-tribe of Manasseh. Bashan is praised for its fertility in Deuteronomy and the Psalms ("a mountain of God is the mountain of Bashan"), and its fate is a subject of prophecy in Ezekiel, Amos and Zechariah.

Later history

In the Hellenistic and Roman periods the region was divided into districts including Gaulanitis, Batanaea, Trachonitis and Auranitis, and came under Herodian and later Roman rule. The basalt plateau of Bashan forms much of today's contested Golan Heights.
