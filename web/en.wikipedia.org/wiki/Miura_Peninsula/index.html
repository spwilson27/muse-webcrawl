Miura Peninsula

Location: Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan
Bounded by: Tokyo Bay (east), Sagami Bay (west)

The Miura Peninsula (三浦半島, Miura-hantō) is a peninsula in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, located south of Tokyo. It separates Tokyo Bay to the east from Sagami Bay to the west and forms the eastern boundary of the entrance to Tokyo Bay at the Uraga Channel.

Geography

The peninsula is hilly, with its highest point at Mount Ōgusu. Its western coast faces Sagami Bay and its eastern coast faces Tokyo Bay. The southern tip is at Jōgashima, connected by bridge to the city of Misaki in Miura.

Cities

Cities on the peninsula include Yokosuka, home to a major naval base used by both the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force and the United States Navy; Kamakura, the former shogunal capital; Zushi; Hayama; and Miura. The area is a popular weekend destination from Tokyo, known for beaches, fishing ports, and tuna from Misaki.

History

The peninsula takes its name from the Miura clan, a powerful warrior family of the Heian and Kamakura periods. In 1549, the missionary Francis Xavier landed near the peninsula, and in 1600 the English navigator William Adams came ashore in the area, later receiving a fief at Hemi in Yokosuka.
