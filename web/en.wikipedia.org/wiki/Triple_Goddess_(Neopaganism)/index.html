Triple Goddess (Neopaganism)

The Triple Goddess is a deity or archetype central to Wicca and other forms of Neopaganism. She is understood as a triunity of three distinct aspects or figures — the Maiden, the Mother, and the Crone — corresponding to the phases of the Moon and the stages of a woman's life.

Aspects

The Maiden corresponds to the waxing Moon, youth, beginnings, and enchantment. The Mother corresponds to the full Moon, fertility, maturity, and fulfilment. The Crone corresponds to the waning Moon, old age, wisdom, rest, and endings. Together they form a cycle of birth, life, death, and rebirth.

Origins

The modern concept draws on the work of poet and mythographer Robert Graves, whose book The White Goddess (1948) popularised the idea of a triadic goddess in European prehistory, itself influenced by earlier scholarship of Jane Ellen Harrison and the Cambridge Ritualists. Ancient triple-goddess figures such as Hecate and the Morrígan provided further inspiration, though historians debate how literally the ancient evidence supports the modern construct. In Wicca, the Triple Goddess is the feminine counterpart of the Horned God.
