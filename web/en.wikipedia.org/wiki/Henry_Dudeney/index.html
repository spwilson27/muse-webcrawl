Henry Ernest Dudeney (10 April 1857 – 24 April 1930) was an English author and mathematician who specialised in logic puzzles and mathematical games. He has been described as one of the country's greatest creators of puzzles, comparable to his American contemporary Sam Loyd.

Work

Dudeney contributed puzzles to The Strand Magazine and other periodicals for decades. His books include The Canterbury Puzzles (1907), Amusements in Mathematics (1917), and the posthumous Puzzles and Curious Problems and Modern Puzzles. Among his famous creations are the Haberdasher's dissection (dividing an equilateral triangle into four pieces that form a square) and investigations of Dudeney numbers.

Life

Largely self-taught, Dudeney worked as a clerk in the Civil Service while pursuing chess problems and puzzles; he was a founding member of a literary circle including Arthur Conan Doyle. In later life he lived in Lewes, Sussex.
