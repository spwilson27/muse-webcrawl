Itihasa (Sanskrit: इतिहास, itihāsa, literally "so indeed it was") is a category of Sanskrit literature in Hindu tradition comprising accounts of past events — a term often translated as "history", "legend" or "traditional history". Together with the Puranas, the itihasas belong to smriti ("that which is remembered"), as opposed to the revealed shruti of the Vedas. In the narrowest and most common usage, "the itihasas" refers to the two great Sanskrit epics: the Ramayana attributed to Valmiki, and the Mahabharata attributed to Vyasa.

Scope and definitions

The word is traditionally derived from iti ha āsa, "verily, thus it was". Ancient Indian classifications vary: some lists of knowledge include itihasa and purana together as a "fifth Veda". In the Mahabharata itself, the text calls itself both itihasa and kavya (poetry). Some definitions restrict itihasa to the epics; others include the Puranas and even works such as the Harivamsa, the supplement to the Mahabharata. The two epics recount, respectively, the life of Rama, prince of Ayodhya, and the dynastic struggle between the Pandavas and Kauravas culminating in the Kurukshetra War.

Character and function

Unlike the Vedas, the itihasas were traditionally accessible to all social classes, including women and Shudras, and they became the principal vehicle for transmitting dharma, ethics, political wisdom and theology — the Bhagavad Gita, embedded in the Mahabharata, is the most famous example. Within the tradition, the epics are treated as records of events that actually occurred in earlier yugas, and they have shaped Indian narrative art, theatre, dance and regional literatures for two millennia; versions exist in virtually every Indian language, such as Kamban's Tamil Ramavataram and Tulsidas's Hindi Ramcharitmanas.

Historicity and scholarship

Modern scholarship treats the epics as layered compositions that grew over centuries — the Mahabharata roughly between the 4th century BCE and the 4th century CE — and reads them cautiously as evidence for early Indian society, geography and values rather than as chronicles of datable events. Critical editions of both epics were produced in the 20th century at Pune's Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute. Debates over the historical status of the itihasas remain culturally and politically significant in India.
