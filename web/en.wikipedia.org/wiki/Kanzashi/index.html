Kanzashi

Kanzashi (簪) are hair ornaments used in traditional Japanese hairstyles. They first appeared during the Jōmon period, when decorative hairpins were believed to have protective or spiritual significance, and developed into elaborate works of craftsmanship during the Edo period.

History

During the Edo period (1603–1868), as women's hairstyles became larger and more complex, kanzashi grew increasingly ornate. Artisans crafted them from lacquered wood, gold, silver, tortoiseshell, silk, and other materials. Ornamentation could indicate the wearer's social status, age, marital status, and the season; geisha and maiko (apprentice geisha) in particular wear highly codified sets of kanzashi that change with the month.

Types

Common forms include the long hairpin (kanzashi proper), the ball-topped tama pin, the two-pronged hirauchi, fluttering metal bira-bira, and hana kanzashi — seasonal floral ornaments made by the tsumami technique, in which small squares of silk are folded into petals. Combs (kushi) and hairpins (kōgai) are closely related accessories.

Modern use

Today kanzashi are worn mainly with kimono on formal occasions, by brides, and by maiko and geisha. Antique kanzashi are widely collected, and the tsumami craft has been revived by hobbyists worldwide.
