Margaret Hamilton (software engineer)

Margaret Heafield Hamilton (born August 17, 1936) is an American computer scientist, systems engineer, and business owner. She was director of the Software Engineering Division of the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory, which developed on-board flight software for NASA’s Apollo program. She is credited with coining the term “software engineering”.

Apollo program

Hamilton’s team at MIT produced the software for the Apollo Guidance Computer. During the Apollo 11 landing on 20 July 1969, the computer began issuing 1201 and 1202 program alarms caused by an overloaded rendezvous radar input; because of the priority-driven design Hamilton had insisted upon, the system shed low-priority tasks and the landing proceeded successfully. A famous 1969 photograph shows her standing beside a stack of printed Apollo flight software listings nearly her own height.

Later work

In 1986 she founded Hamilton Technologies, Inc., based on her “Development Before the Fact” paradigm and Universal Systems Language. In 2003 NASA awarded her the NASA Exceptional Space Act Award, and in 2016 President Barack Obama presented her with the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
