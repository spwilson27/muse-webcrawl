Wolfman Jack

Robert Weston Smith (January 21, 1938 – July 1, 1995), known as Wolfman Jack, was an American disc jockey famous for his gravelly voice and howling on-air persona. He became nationally famous in the 1960s broadcasting rhythm and blues and rock and roll from high-powered Mexican "border blaster" stations, and later reached a new generation as a regular on television's The Midnight Special and through his appearance in the 1973 film American Graffiti.

Wolfman Jack in the studio

Early career

Smith was born in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up idolizing disc jockeys such as Alan Freed. He broke into radio in Virginia under names including "Daddy Jules" and "Roger Gordon", and by the early 1960s was working in Shreveport, Louisiana, where he developed the Wolfman character — a howling, wisecracking night creature — in part inspired by bluesman Howlin' Wolf and by the horror-film hosts of the era.

Border radio fame

In 1963 Smith began broadcasting over XERF-AM in Mexico, whose 250,000-watt signal reached most of North America at night, far exceeding the power limits of U.S. stations. Moving to XERB near Tijuana, he preached a mixture of rock and soul records, commercials, and sermons that made him a cult figure among American teenagers. His mysterious identity — few listeners knew what he looked like — added to his legend until George Lucas cast him as himself in American Graffiti.

Later years

Smith went on to syndicated radio shows, television hosting, and in 1971 became the announcer and frequent host of NBC's The Midnight Special. He continued touring and broadcasting until his death from a heart attack at his home in Belvidere, North Carolina, in 1995, shortly after returning from a promotional tour for his autobiography.
