Cinema of Japan

The cinema of Japan (日本映画, Nihon eiga) has a history spanning more than a century and is one of the oldest and largest film industries in the world. Japanese cinema produced a golden age in the 1950s, when directors such as Akira Kurosawa, Yasujirō Ozu, and Kenji Mizoguchi gained international acclaim, and it remains globally influential through genres including jidaigeki (period dramas), kaiju (monster films), horror, and anime.

Japanese films have been produced since the late 1890s.

Early history

The first motion pictures were shown in Japan in 1896–97, and the first Japanese film, a short documentary of street scenes, appeared in 1897. Early films were screened with a benshi, a live narrator who explained the action and voiced the characters — a tradition that persisted into the sound era. Studios such as Nikkatsu (founded 1912) and Shochiku (1920) built a thriving silent industry.

Golden age

The 1950s are regarded as the golden age of Japanese cinema. Kurosawa's Rashomon (1950) won the Golden Lion at Venice and introduced Japanese film to Western audiences; his Seven Samurai (1954) is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made. Ozu's Tokyo Story (1953) and Mizoguchi's Ugetsu (1953) are likewise canonical. In 1954 Ishirō Honda's Godzilla launched the kaiju genre.

New wave and decline

The 1960s brought the Japanese New Wave, with directors such as Nagisa Ōshima and Masahiro Shinoda challenging studio conventions. Television competition drove admissions down from a 1958 peak of over a billion, and the major studios retrenched; genre production continued through yakuza films, pink films, and the work of independent directors.

Modern era

From the 1990s, directors including Takeshi Kitano, Hirokazu Kore-eda, and Takashi Miike won festival recognition, while J-horror titles such as Ring (1998) inspired Hollywood remakes. Anime features from Studio Ghibli — notably Hayao Miyazaki's Spirited Away (2001), which won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature — became a dominant export. In 2021 Ryusuke Hamaguchi's Drive My Car won the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film.
