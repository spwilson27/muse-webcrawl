The Devil Wears Prada is a 2006 American comedy-drama film directed by David Frankel and produced by Wendy Finerman. The screenplay, by Aline Brosh McKenna, is based on Lauren Weisberger's 2003 novel of the same name. It stars Anne Hathaway as Andrea "Andy" Sachs, an aspiring journalist who takes a job as junior personal assistant to Miranda Priestly (Meryl Streep), the formidable editor-in-chief of the fictional fashion magazine Runway, widely understood to be modeled on Vogue's Anna Wintour, for whom Weisberger once worked.

Plot

Andy, indifferent to fashion, is gradually transformed by the job — coached by art director Nigel (Stanley Tucci) — and earns Miranda's trust with impossible feats, including obtaining the unpublished Harry Potter manuscript for Miranda's twins. Her success costs her relationship with her boyfriend Nate and her integrity, culminating in Paris, where Andy, warned that she is becoming like Miranda, throws her phone into a fountain and quits. She lands a newspaper job with her old clothes and values restored; Miranda, glimpsed from afar, allows herself a rare smile.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed over $326 million worldwide. Streep's understated, Oscar-nominated performance — soft-spoken rather than shouting, and humanized beyond the novel's portrait — redefined the character and is considered among her most iconic. Patricia Field's costume design, the most expensive in film history at the time, received acclaim. The film has remained a durable pop-cultural touchstone on ambition and workplace culture, and a stage musical adaptation with songs by Elton John premiered in Chicago in 2022 before transferring to London's West End in 2024.
