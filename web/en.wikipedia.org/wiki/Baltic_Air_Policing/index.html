Baltic Air Policing

Allied fighters on Baltic Air Policing duty
TypeNATO air policing mission
Began30 March 2004
BeneficiariesEstonia, Latvia, Lithuania
BasesŠiauliai (Lithuania), Ämari (Estonia)

Baltic Air Policing is a NATO air defence quick reaction alert mission safeguarding the airspace of the three Baltic states — Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania — which lack their own fighter aircraft. Since 30 March 2004, when the Baltic states joined NATO, Allied air forces have deployed fighters on rotation, normally for four-month tours, to intercept aircraft flying without flight plans or transponders near Allied airspace, most frequently Russian military aircraft transiting between mainland Russia and the Kaliningrad exclave.

Organization

Detachments are based principally at Šiauliai Air Base in Lithuania, with a second base at Ämari Air Base in Estonia used since 2014, when the mission was reinforced following Russia's annexation of Crimea. Command and control is provided through the Combined Air Operations Centre at Uedem, Germany, and Baltic control and reporting centres. Belgium flew the first rotation in 2004; most NATO members with fast-jet forces, including the United States, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Spain, Italy, and Poland, have participated.

Activity

Intercepts rose sharply after 2014 as Russian military flights in the Baltic region increased; Allied jets typically scramble to identify and shadow aircraft that are not communicating with air traffic control. The mission was further expanded after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, with additional deployments and supporting tanker and surveillance aircraft. NATO presents Baltic Air Policing as a peacetime collective-defence arrangement, identical in principle to air policing conducted over other Allied nations, and as a model later extended in concept to other regions.
