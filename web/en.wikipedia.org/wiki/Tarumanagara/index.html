Tarumanagara or Taruma Kingdom was an early Sundanese Indianised kingdom in western Java, whose territory corresponds roughly to modern-day Jakarta, Bogor, Bekasi and the Banten region. It flourished from about 358 to 669 CE and is among the earliest known kingdoms in Indonesian history, second only to Kutai in Kalimantan.

Sources

Knowledge of Tarumanagara rests chiefly on seven Sanskrit inscriptions in Pallava script attributed to its most famous king, Purnawarman, dated to the fifth century, and on Chinese dynastic records that mention a kingdom called To-lo-mo whose envoys visited China in the sixth and seventh centuries. The inscriptions include the Ciaruteun, Kebon Kopi, Jambu, Muara Cianten, Pasir Awi, Tugu and Lebak (Cidanghiang) stones.

Reign of Purnawarman

King Purnawarman, described as a devout worshipper of Vishnu, is the only ruler known in detail. The Ciaruteun inscription compares his footprints, carved in stone, to those of the god Vishnu. The Tugu inscription — the longest — records the excavation of the Candrabhaga river canal and the Gomati canal, a waterworks project said to have been completed in 21 days, celebrated with gifts of a thousand cattle to Brahmins.

Decline

Chinese sources cease mentioning the kingdom after the later seventh century. Tarumanagara appears to have weakened and fallen under the influence of the rising maritime power of Srivijaya, which reportedly attacked the region around 686. In Sundanese tradition the kingdom's inheritance passed to the successor state of Sunda, while the name survives in the Citarum River and the modern city of Tarumajaya.
