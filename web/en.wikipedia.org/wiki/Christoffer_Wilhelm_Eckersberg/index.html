Christoffer Wilhelm Eckersberg (January 2, 1783 – July 22, 1853) was a Danish painter and professor, widely regarded as the father of Danish painting. Through his teaching at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts he shaped the generation of the Danish Golden Age, instilling an exacting observation of nature, clear light and rigorous perspective.

Life and career

Born in southern Jutland, Eckersberg trained at the Academy in Copenhagen, winning its gold medal. Study in Paris under Jacques-Louis David (1811–1813) and three years in Rome (1813–1816) formed his neoclassical manner: in Rome he painted luminous, minutely observed views of the city's monuments alongside Thorvaldsen's circle. Returning to Denmark, he became professor at the Academy in 1818 and its director in 1827–1829.

Work

Eckersberg painted portraits of the Copenhagen bourgeoisie, altarpieces, and above all marine paintings of extraordinary precision, informed by his study of ships and perspective geometry — he wrote a treatise on linear perspective. Late works include studies of models at the Academy, notable for their unfurnished, daylight realism. His pupils included Christen Købke, Martinus Rørbye and Wilhelm Marstrand.

Legacy

Eckersberg died during the Copenhagen cholera epidemic of 1853. His insistence on painting directly from nature, and his serene, crystalline compositions, defined Danish art for decades; his Roman views and harbour scenes hang in the Statens Museum for Kunst in Copenhagen.
