Kogal

Kogal (コギャル, kogyaru) is a Japanese fashion and social subculture of young women, especially high school students, that emerged in the 1990s. The term is generally understood as a contraction relating to high school girls (kōkōsei) and the English word "gal".

Style

The classic kogal look features shortened school-uniform skirts, loose socks, dyed light-brown or blonde hair, tanned skin, heavy make-up, platform shoes, and accessories such as mobile phones decorated with straps and charms. Centres of the culture included Tokyo's Shibuya district and its 109 department store.

Cultural impact

Kogal were the subject of intense media attention and moral panic in 1990s Japan, and were associated with phenomena such as enjo kōsai debates. The style is regarded as an early form of the wider gyaru subculture.
