Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act

Enacted12 November 1999
Signed byPresident Bill Clinton
Also known asFinancial Services Modernization Act of 1999

The Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act (GLBA), also known as the Financial Services Modernization Act of 1999, is a United States federal law that repealed parts of the Glass–Steagall Act of 1933, removing barriers that prevented banks, securities firms, and insurance companies from affiliating.

Provisions

The Act permitted the creation of financial holding companies able to offer commercial banking, investment banking, and insurance under one roof, subject to Federal Reserve supervision. It also contains privacy provisions requiring financial institutions to disclose their information-sharing practices and safeguard customer data (the Privacy Rule and Safeguards Rule).

Debate

Supporters argued the law modernised an outdated financial system. Critics later blamed deregulation for contributing to the 2008 financial crisis, a claim its defenders dispute, noting that the institutions at the centre of the crisis were not the diversified holding companies the Act enabled.
