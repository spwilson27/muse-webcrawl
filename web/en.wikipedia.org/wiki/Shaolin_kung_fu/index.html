Shaolin kung fu

Shaolin kung fu (Chinese: 少林功夫; pinyin: Shàolín gōngfu), also called Shaolin wushu, is one of the oldest, largest and best-known styles of Chinese martial arts. It developed at the Shaolin Temple, a Chan Buddhist monastery on Mount Song in Henan Province, founded in the 5th century AD.

History

Tradition credits the Indian monk Bodhidharma with introducing exercises that evolved into Shaolin boxing, though historians treat this as legend. The temple's martial reputation is documented from the Tang dynasty, when Shaolin monks were rewarded for helping the future emperor Taizong in battle. During the Ming dynasty the monks fielded a celebrated fighting force against pirates and rebels, and manuals of the period record an extensive curriculum of barehand and weapons forms.

The temple was repeatedly damaged by war, most severely in 1928 when the warlord Shi Yousan set it ablaze. In the late 20th century the monastery was rebuilt, and the 1982 film Shaolin Temple, starring Jet Li, sparked a worldwide revival of interest; hundreds of martial arts schools now operate around Dengfeng.

Content and philosophy

Shaolin kung fu encompasses hundreds of forms (taolu), covering barehand sets imitating animals such as the tiger, crane, monkey and mantis, and weapons including the staff, spear, broadsword and straight sword. Training emphasises the unity of Chan Buddhism and martial practice — summarised in the saying "Chan and fist are one" — combining external strength with internal qigong cultivation.
