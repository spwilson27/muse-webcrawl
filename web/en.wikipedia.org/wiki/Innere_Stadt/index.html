The Innere Stadt (German pronunciation: [ˈɪnəʀə ʃtat]; "Inner City") is the 1st municipal district of Vienna, Austria, and the historic core of the city. Until the city walls were demolished in the 1850s, it was effectively coextensive with the city of Vienna itself. The district is encircled by the Ringstraße boulevard, which follows the course of the former fortifications, and by the Danube Canal to the northeast.

Geography and landmarks

The Innere Stadt contains many of Vienna's most famous landmarks, including St. Stephen's Cathedral (Stephansdom), the Hofburg Palace, the Vienna State Opera, the Albertina museum, and the Graben and Kärntner Straße shopping streets. The historic centre of Vienna, including the Innere Stadt, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

History

The district occupies the site of the Roman military camp of Vindobona. The medieval city grew within walls that withstood the Ottoman sieges of 1529 and 1683. After Emperor Franz Joseph I ordered the walls demolished in 1857, the Ringstraße era transformed the surrounding glacis into a grand boulevard lined with monumental public buildings. Because so much of Vienna's population and employment later shifted outward, the Innere Stadt today has a relatively small resident population but a very large daytime workforce.

Demographics

The district has roughly 16,000 residents, the fewest of Vienna's 23 districts, while hosting tens of thousands of jobs in government, tourism, retail, and finance. It is also the seat of Austria's federal government offices and many embassies and religious institutions.
