Laz language

Laz (Lazuri)
Native to: Turkey, Georgia
Family: Kartvelian (South Caucasian)
Status: Endangered

Laz (Laz: Lazuri) is a Kartvelian language spoken by the Laz people on the southeastern shore of the Black Sea, in northeastern Turkey and southwestern Georgia. It is closely related to Mingrelian, and together they form the Zan branch of the Kartvelian family, whose other members are Georgian and Svan.

The Laz-speaking region along the Black Sea coast.

Speakers and status

Estimates of speakers vary widely, from tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand; most Laz are bilingual in Turkish or Georgian, and the language is classified by UNESCO as endangered. Laz has no official status in Turkey, though elective courses have been offered since 2013.

Writing

Laz is written both in a Latin-based alphabet (developed for use in Turkey, notably by Fahri Lazoğlu) and in the Georgian Mkhedruli script in Georgia. Its rich consonant system includes ejective and uvular sounds typical of Caucasian languages.
