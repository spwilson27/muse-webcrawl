RAND Corporation

The RAND Corporation is an American nonprofit global policy think tank, research institute and public sector consulting firm. RAND engages in research and analysis in fields including the social sciences, technology and policing. Founded in 1948 from a United States Air Force project within the Douglas Aircraft Company, RAND — its name a contraction of "research and development" — pioneered systems analysis, game theory and scenario planning during the Cold War, and today operates federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) for the U.S. armed services, the Department of Homeland Security and health agencies, while also conducting independent policy research worldwide. Its headquarters are in Santa Monica, California.

History

Project RAND began in 1946 as a contract between the Army Air Forces and the Douglas Aircraft Company, advised by a circle that included General Henry "Hap" Arnold and scientist Edward Bowles. To safeguard its independence, RAND was incorporated as a nonprofit on 14 May 1948 with a loan and a Ford Foundation grant. From its Santa Monica base it became the archetypal Cold War think tank: its analysts developed systems analysis for weapons selection, the mathematics of nuclear strategy (with figures such as Bernard Brodie, Herman Kahn and Albert Wohlstetter), the Delphi method of expert forecasting, and — in Paul Baran's studies of survivable communications — foundational ideas for packet-switched networks that fed into the ARPANET.

Work and influence

RAND research shaped game theory through early work associated with the RAND mathematics department (the prisoner's dilemma was framed there in 1950), influenced social policy from health insurance (the RAND Health Insurance Experiment) to education and criminal justice, and advised governments on counterterrorism, Chinese and Russian military developments and homeland security. Its graduate school, the Pardee RAND Graduate School, awards doctorates in policy analysis. RAND operates offices across the United States and internationally, including RAND Europe in Cambridge and Brussels.

Criticism

Critics have charged RAND with militarising social science, with optimism about quantitative methods, and — through Daniel Ellsberg's photocopying of the Pentagon Papers, which he had worked on at RAND — the institute sits at the centre of one of the most famous leaks in American history; RAND's classified studies of the Vietnam War informed the scepticism that produced the papers' conclusions.
