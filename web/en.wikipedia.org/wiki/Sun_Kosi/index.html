Sun Kosi

The Sun Kosi, also spelled Sun Koshi, is a trans-boundary river that flows through Tibet and Nepal and forms part of the Kosi river system of the Himalayas. Known in Tibet as the Matter of the upper course, the river originates in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China and flows southeast into Nepal, where it is regarded as one of the country's premier whitewater rafting rivers.

Course

The Sun Kosi rises in the Zhangzangbo Glacier region of Tibet and enters Nepal near the border town of Kodari, following the Araniko Highway corridor. It flows generally eastward through the hills of central Nepal, gathering tributaries including the Bhotekoshi, Indravati, and Dudh Kosi, before joining the Arun and Tamor rivers at the tri-junction known as Tribeni to form the Saptakosi (Kosi River), which ultimately drains into the Ganges in India.

Tourism and hydropower

The river is internationally renowned for multi-day rafting and kayaking expeditions, commonly run from Dolalghat to Chatara over eight to ten days during the autumn season. Hydropower projects and the proposed Sunkoshi–Marin diversion scheme, intended to irrigate the Terai plains, have made the river significant in Nepal's water-resource planning.
