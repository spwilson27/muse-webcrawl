Isle of Harris

Harris

Gaelic name: Na Hearadh

Location: Outer Hebrides, Scotland

Main settlement: Tarbert

Highest point: Clisham (An Cliseam), 799 m

Known for: Harris Tweed, Luskentyre beach

The Isle of Harris (Scottish Gaelic: Na Hearadh) is the southern part of the largest island in Scotland's Outer Hebrides, the island of Lewis and Harris. Although Harris and Lewis share one landmass, they are treated as separate islands, divided by a range of hills; Harris is the smaller and more mountainous southern portion. The main village and ferry port is Tarbert.

Geography

Harris is famed for the contrast between its rugged, rocky east coast — cut by sea lochs and dotted with small crofting townships along the "Golden Road" — and the white shell-sand beaches and fertile machair of its west coast, including Luskentyre, Seilebost and Scarista, regularly ranked among the finest beaches in Britain. North Harris contains Clisham, at 799 metres the highest mountain in the Outer Hebrides. The island is strongly Gaelic-speaking, and its religious culture is predominantly Protestant (Free Church and Church of Scotland).

Harris Tweed

Harris is the home of Harris Tweed, the handwoven woollen cloth that by law may bear the name and the Orb trademark only if produced by islanders in the Outer Hebrides from pure virgin wool dyed and spun in the islands and handwoven at the islanders' homes — a definition protected by the Harris Tweed Act 1993 and overseen by the Harris Tweed Authority. Popularised in the 19th century by the Countess of Dunmore and later by aristocratic and sporting fashion, the industry survives through mills at Shawbost and Carloway on Lewis and small producers across Harris.

History and culture

The island was historically held by the MacLeods of Harris and Dunvegan. The 16th-century St Clement's Church at Rodel contains the tomb of Alexander MacLeod ("Alasdair Crotach"). In the 20th century the North Harris estate was bought by the community in a landmark buyout in 2003. The Isle of Harris Distillery, opened in Tarbert in 2015, produces gin and single malt whisky. The island is connected by ferry from Tarbert to Uig on Skye, and by the Sound of Harris ferry from Leverburgh to Berneray and North Uist.
