Fetterman Fight

Fetterman Fight
Date: 21 December 1866
Location: near Fort Phil Kearny, Wyoming Territory
Result: Lakota, Cheyenne, and Arapaho victory
U.S. losses: 81 killed (entire command)

The Fetterman Fight, also known as the Fetterman Massacre, was a battle of Red Cloud's War fought on 21 December 1866 near Fort Phil Kearny in present-day Wyoming. A force of Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors ambushed and annihilated a detachment of 81 U.S. Army soldiers under Captain William J. Fetterman. It was the worst defeat suffered by the U.S. Army on the Great Plains until the Battle of the Little Bighorn ten years later.

Background

Fort Phil Kearny had been built in 1866 to protect emigrants on the Bozeman Trail, which crossed hunting grounds guaranteed to the Lakota by treaty. Sioux leader Red Cloud's warriors repeatedly attacked wood-cutting parties supplying the fort. Captain Fetterman, a Civil War veteran newly arrived at the post, reportedly boasted that with 80 men he could "ride through the whole Sioux nation" — a claim whose wording is disputed but which captured his disdain for his opponents and his friction with the fort's commander, Colonel Henry B. Carrington, who had ordered officers not to pursue raiders beyond Lodge Trail Ridge.

The battle

On 21 December 1866 a wood train was attacked, and Fetterman led a relief force of 81 men — infantry, cavalry, and two civilian scouts. A decoy party of warriors, in which the young Crazy Horse is traditionally said to have taken part, taunted the soldiers and drew them over Lodge Trail Ridge, out of sight of the fort and beyond their orders. On the far side, an estimated 1,000 or more warriors lay in ambush along the Peno Creek valley. Within about half an hour, Fetterman's entire command was killed.

Aftermath

News of the disaster shocked the United States and led to Carrington's removal, though a court of inquiry largely exonerated him. The fight strengthened the determination of Red Cloud's coalition; under the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868) the United States abandoned the Bozeman Trail forts, including Fort Phil Kearny, which the Lakota burned. The site is preserved today as the Fetterman Battlefield, part of Fort Phil Kearny State Historic Site.
