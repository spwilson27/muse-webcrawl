Sensory system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A sensory system is the part of the nervous system that detects stimuli from the environment and the body and conveys them to the Brain for perception. It consists of sensory receptors, neural pathways and the brain regions that process them. The classical five senses are sight, Hearing, Smell, taste and touch, but physiologists recognise several more.

Beyond five senses

Additional senses include balance (the vestibular system), body position (proprioception), pain (nociception), temperature (thermoception), and internal senses monitoring blood chemistry, stretch and pressure in organs. Receptors are classified by the energy they transduce: photoreceptors (light), mechanoreceptors (force), chemoreceptors (molecules), thermoreceptors and nociceptors.

Processing

Signals are encoded by the pattern of action potentials in sensory neurons and relayed, usually through the thalamus (smell is a partial exception), to dedicated cortical areas. Perception is an active construction: the brain combines senses, fills gaps and is subject to illusions — facts exploited by stage magic and revealed by neurology.
