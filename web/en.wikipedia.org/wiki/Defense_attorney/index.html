Defense attorney

A defense attorney (defence lawyer in Commonwealth spelling; also criminal defense lawyer when practising criminal law) is a lawyer representing an accused person or defendant in legal proceedings, protecting their rights and presenting their defence against the prosecution or claimant.

Role

Defense counsel advise clients on charges and pleas, investigate facts, interview witnesses, file motions (e.g. to suppress unlawfully obtained evidence), negotiate plea bargains, cross-examine prosecution witnesses, and argue for acquittal or lenient sentencing. They owe duties of confidentiality, loyalty and zealous—within-the-law—advocacy, even for unpopular clients, a cornerstone of adversarial justice.

Right to counsel

In the United States the Sixth Amendment guarantees assistance of counsel; Gideon v. Wainwright (1963) required states to provide attorneys to indigent felony defendants, extended to misdemeanours carrying imprisonment in Argersinger v. Hamlin. Public defenders, assigned counsel and private retained lawyers form the defence system; ineffective assistance can be grounds for appeal under Strickland v. Washington. Other jurisdictions provide legal aid schemes with duty solicitors (England and Wales) or similar.

Civil defence

In civil cases, defence attorneys—often paid by insurers—represent defendants against claims for damages or injunctions, seeking dismissal, settlement or a favourable verdict.
