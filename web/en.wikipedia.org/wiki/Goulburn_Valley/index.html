Goulburn Valley

LocationVictoria, Australia
RiverGoulburn River
Main townsShepparton, Echuca, Seymour, Kyabram, Tatura
IndustryIrrigated agriculture, food processing, dairy

The Goulburn Valley is a region of Victoria, Australia, centred on the floodplain of the Goulburn River, a major tributary of the Murray River. It is one of Australia's most productive agricultural regions and is often described as the "food bowl" of Victoria.

Geography and economy

The valley stretches from the region around Seymour in the south to the Murray River near Echuca in the north. Major centres include Shepparton, Mooroopna, Kyabram, Tatura, and Cobram. Extensive irrigation schemes, supplied by Lake Eildon and Goulburn Weir, support dairy farming, orchards (peaches, pears, apples, and tomatoes for processing), and mixed agriculture. The area hosts major food processors, including SPC, whose cannery at Shepparton has operated since 1917.

History

The valley is the traditional land of peoples including the Yorta Yorta. European pastoral settlement began in the 19th century, and closer settlement and soldier-settler schemes after the World Wars expanded irrigation agriculture. The Goulburn Valley wine region, including Nagambie and the Strathbogie Ranges, is known for Marsanne and Shiraz.
