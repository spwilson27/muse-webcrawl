Water hole (radio)

The water hole lies between the hydrogen line at 1420 MHz and hydroxyl lines near 1666 MHz.

The water hole is a band of radio frequencies in the microwave region of the electromagnetic spectrum, between the spectral lines of hydrogen and hydroxyl, approximately 1420 to 1666 megahertz. It is regarded as a particularly promising range for the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI), because it is a relatively quiet region of the spectrum and because its bounding frequencies arise from the constituents of water.

Origin of the concept

The lower boundary is the hydrogen line (the 21-centimeter line, 1420.4 MHz), emitted by neutral hydrogen, the most abundant element in the universe; the upper boundary is set by hydroxyl radical emission lines around 1666 MHz. Hydrogen (H) and hydroxyl (OH) together make water, hence the name. The term was coined in 1971 by Bernard M. Oliver of Hewlett-Packard, who reasoned that just as different species gather at water holes on Earth, independently evolved technological civilizations might naturally "meet" on this band, since it would be known to any civilization with radio astronomy.

Role in SETI

Because cosmic noise and atmospheric absorption are low in this window, many SETI programs have concentrated their searches on or near the water hole, listening for narrowband signals that no known natural process produces. The band overlaps the protected 1400–1427 MHz passive band used by radio astronomers for hydrogen-line observations.
