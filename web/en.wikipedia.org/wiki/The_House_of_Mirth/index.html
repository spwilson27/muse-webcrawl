The House of Mirth

The House of Mirth is a novel by American author Edith Wharton, published in 1905. A tragedy of manners set in the glittering, ruthless world of New York high society at the turn of the 20th century, it follows the beautiful but impoverished Lily Bart as she seeks a wealthy husband and instead descends step by step into disgrace and poverty. The title is taken from Ecclesiastes 7:4: "The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning; but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth."

Plot

Lily Bart, 29 and orphaned, lives with her wealthy aunt while pursuing a rich marriage. Her near-match with the dull but wealthy Percy Gryce collapses through her own carelessness; debts from bridge losses push her to accept money from Gus Trenor, who expects sexual repayment. A Mediterranean cruise with the Dorsets ends in scandal engineered by Bertha Dorset. Cast out by society and disinherited by her aunt, Lily declines through work as a secretary and in a milliner's shop; she refuses chances to restore herself by blackmailing Bertha with love letters, and dies of an overdose of chloral — ambiguous between accident and suicide — after honourably repaying her debt to Trenor. The lawyer Lawrence Selden, whom she truly loved, arrives too late.

Reception and adaptation

The novel was a bestseller and established Wharton's reputation. It has been adapted for stage and screen, notably Terence Davies's 2000 film starring Gillian Anderson as Lily Bart.
