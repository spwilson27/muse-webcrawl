Schule Schloss Salem is a private boarding school in Salem, Baden-Württemberg, Germany, founded in 1920 by the educator Kurt Hahn together with Prince Maximilian of Baden and Karl Reinhardt. Housed originally in Salem Castle, a former Cistercian abbey, it is one of Germany's best-known boarding schools and the founding school of the Round Square network and an early model for the United World Colleges movement.

History

Hahn's philosophy emphasized character, service, outdoor challenge, and responsibility — principles he summarized in the "Seven Laws of Salem." After the Nazi rise to power, Hahn, who was Jewish, was briefly imprisoned and emigrated to Scotland, where he founded Gordonstoun. Salem continued through the century, expanding to campuses at Salem, Hohenfels, and Spetzgart (Überlingen), and today educates roughly 600 students, many completing the International Baccalaureate.

Educational philosophy

Students take part in services (such as the school fire brigade, technical relief, and social services), expeditions, sports, and a system of student offices ("guilds") that gives pupils real responsibility for school life.
