Shaktism (Sanskrit: शाक्त, Śākta, "doctrine of energy/power") is one of the major denominations of Hinduism, centred on the worship of Shakti or Devi — the Hindu Goddess — as the Supreme Being. Shaktas regard the Goddess as the ultimate reality: the active, dynamic energy (shakti) that creates, sustains and dissolves the universe, alongside and often above the male deities, who are considered inert without her.

Beliefs

Shakti is worshipped in many forms: the gentle goddesses Parvati, Lakshmi and Saraswati, and the fierce forms Durga and Kali, as well as in groups such as the ten Mahavidyas of Tantric tradition. Shakta theology ranges from devotional monotheism focused on Devi to the non-dual metaphysics of some Tantric schools, in which Shakti and Shiva are inseparable aspects of one reality. The Devi Mahatmyam (c. 5th–6th century CE), part of the Markandeya Purana, is the tradition's foundational text, narrating the Goddess's victories over the demons Shumbha-Nishumbha, Chanda-Munda, Raktabija and Mahishasura.

History

Goddess worship in South Asia has roots in prehistoric figurines and in the Indus Valley and folk traditions; hymns to goddesses such as Ushas and Vac appear already in the Rigveda, and the Devi Sukta celebrates the Goddess as the power behind all gods. Shaktism crystallised as a distinct tradition in the first millennium CE alongside the rise of the Puranas and Tantra. By the early medieval period, networks of goddess temples — the Shakti Pithas, sites where parts of the goddess Sati's body were said to have fallen — mapped sacred geography across the subcontinent.

Practices

Major festivals include Navaratri and Durga Puja (especially in West Bengal), Kali Puja, and regional celebrations such as Ambubachi at the Kamakhya temple in Assam. Practice ranges from temple worship and recitation of the Devi Mahatmyam to Tantric sadhana involving mantra, yantra (notably the Sri Yantra), and kundalini yoga. Historically, animal sacrifice formed part of some Shakta rites, a practice now declining or reinterpreted.

Relation to other traditions

Shaktism is one of the four major sampradayas of Hinduism alongside Vaishnavism, Shaivism and the Smarta tradition; in Smarta practice the Goddess is one of the five deities of the panchayatana. Shakta ideas strongly influenced Tantric Buddhism and Jainism, and goddess-centred devotion remains one of the most widespread forms of Hindu religiosity, particularly in eastern India and Nepal.
