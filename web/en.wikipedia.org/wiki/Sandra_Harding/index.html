Sandra Harding

Sandra G. Harding
Born: March 29, 1935, San Francisco, California; died 2025
Field: Philosophy of science, feminist theory
Known for: Standpoint theory, strong objectivity

Sandra G. Harding (1935–2025) was an American philosopher of science best known for her contributions to feminist epistemology, standpoint theory, and science and technology studies. Her work argued that the perspectives of marginalised groups can provide a starting point for more objective knowledge of the natural and social worlds.

Life and career

Harding earned her doctorate in philosophy from New York University in 1973. She taught at the University of Delaware, where she directed the Women's Studies Program, and later at the University of California, Los Angeles, where she was a professor of education and gender studies and directed the Center for the Study of Women.

Work

In The Science Question in Feminism (1986), Harding distinguished three feminist approaches to science: feminist empiricism, feminist standpoint theory, and feminist postmodernism. In Whose Science? Whose Knowledge? (1991) she developed the notion of "strong objectivity", arguing that inquiry which begins from the lives of those outside dominant institutions can achieve greater objectivity than the conventional "weak objectivity" of value-neutral method. Her later work, including Is Science Multicultural? (1998) and Sciences from Below (2008), examined postcolonial science studies and the relations between modern science and non-Western knowledge traditions.
