Companions of Saint Nicholas

Companions of Saint Nicholas
RegionCentral and Northern Europe
FestivalSaint Nicholas Day, 5–6 December
Notable figuresKrampus, Knecht Ruprecht, Zwarte Piet, Père Fouettard

The companions of Saint Nicholas are a group of folk figures who accompany Saint Nicholas in winter traditions across Europe during the season of Saint Nicholas Day (5 or 6 December). In contrast to the benevolent gift-giving saint, the companions are often dark, frightening figures who threaten to punish naughty children, while the saint rewards the good.

Overview

The pairing of a generous saint with a menacing servant is widespread in Alpine, Germanic, and Low Country folklore. The companion typically carries chains, bells, switches, or a sack said to be for carrying away disobedient children, and may blacken his face with soot from chimneys.

Regional figures

Krampus

In Austria, southern Germany, and neighbouring Alpine regions, Krampus is a horned, demonic figure who accompanies Saint Nicholas on Krampusnacht (5 December), with processions of masked Krampusse known as Krampuslauf.

Knecht Ruprecht

In northern and central Germany, Knecht Ruprecht is a servant figure dressed in dark clothing who questions children about their prayers and behaviour.

Zwarte Piet

In the Netherlands and Belgium, Zwarte Piet (Black Pete) assists Sinterklaas, traditionally portrayed with blackface — a practice that has become the subject of public controversy and gradual change in recent decades.

Père Fouettard

In France and Wallonia, Père Fouettard ("Father Whipper") carries a whip and is said to spank children who have misbehaved.
