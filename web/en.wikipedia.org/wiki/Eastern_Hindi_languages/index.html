Eastern Hindi languages

The Eastern Hindi languages are a group of Indo-Aryan languages spoken in north-central India, principally in the states of Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh, and in adjoining areas. In the classification of George Grierson's Linguistic Survey of India, Eastern Hindi forms a subgroup intermediate between Western Hindi and the Bihari languages.

Languages

The principal Eastern Hindi languages are:

  • Awadhi — spoken in the Awadh region of central Uttar Pradesh, famous as the language of Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas.
  • Bagheli — spoken in the Baghelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh and southern Uttar Pradesh, closely related to Awadhi.
  • Chhattisgarhi — the principal language of Chhattisgarh, sometimes regarded as a dialect of Hindi.

Fiji Hindi, spoken by Indo-Fijians, developed largely from Awadhi and Bhojpuri spoken by indentured labourers, with Awadhi providing much of its base.

Characteristics

Eastern Hindi languages descend from Ardhamagadhi Prakrit and share features distinguishing them from Western Hindi, which derives from Shauraseni Prakrit. They are written in the Devanagari script. Although often grouped under the umbrella of "Hindi" in the Indian census, Awadhi, Bagheli and Chhattisgarhi have their own literary traditions and are sometimes claimed as separate languages by their speakers.
