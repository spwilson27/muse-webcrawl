Serra da Capivara National Park

Serra da Capivara National Park
Location: Piauí, Brazil
Established: 1979
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1991

Serra da Capivara National Park is a national park in the northeastern Brazilian state of Piauí, famous for its dense concentration of prehistoric archaeological sites and rock paintings, some claimed to date back more than 25,000 years. It was created in 1979 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991.

Archaeology

Archaeologist Niède Guidon, who led excavations from the 1970s, argued that sites such as Pedra Furada indicate human habitation far earlier than the traditional Clovis-first model of the peopling of the Americas — a claim that remains debated. The park contains over a thousand recorded sites with paintings depicting hunting, rituals, dances and animals.

Landscape

The park protects caatinga vegetation and dramatic sandstone formations carved by wind and water.
