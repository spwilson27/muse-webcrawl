Cullinan Diamond

The Cullinan Diamond is the largest gem-quality rough diamond ever found, weighing 3,106 carats (621.20 g), discovered at the Premier No. 2 mine in Cullinan, South Africa, on 26 January 1905.

Cullinan Diamond

Weight: 3,106 carats rough
Discovered: 26 January 1905, South Africa
Named for: Thomas Cullinan

History

It was named after mine chairman Thomas Cullinan and found by Frederick Wells. The Transvaal government bought it and presented it to King Edward VII. It was cut by Joseph Asscher in Amsterdam into 105 stones, the largest being Cullinan I, the Great Star of Africa (530.4 carats), set in the Sovereign's Sceptre, and Cullinan II (317.4 carats) in the Imperial State Crown — part of the Crown Jewels.

Cutting

Asscher studied the stone for months; legend says he fainted after the first cleaving blow. The major stones are numbered Cullinan I–IX.
