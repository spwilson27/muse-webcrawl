Falun Gong (Chinese: 法輪功, lit. "Dharma Wheel Practice" or "Law Wheel Practice") or Falun Dafa is a new religious movement based on the Chinese qigong tradition. Founded by Li Hongzhi, who introduced it publicly in Changchun in 1992, the practice combines slow meditative exercises with a moral philosophy centred on the principles of truthfulness, compassion, and forbearance (真善忍). It emerged during China's "qigong boom" of the 1980s and 1990s and spread rapidly; by the late 1990s the Chinese government itself estimated some 70 million practitioners.

Beliefs and practice

Li's teachings, set out principally in the book Zhuan Falun (1995), present Falun Gong as a form of self-cultivation rooted in Buddhist and Daoist traditions. Practitioners perform five gentle exercises — four standing meditations and one sitting meditation — and study Li's writings, aiming to assimilate to the principles of truthfulness, compassion, and forbearance and to advance spiritually. Li teaches that the practice installs a falun (dharma wheel) in the lower abdomen and can purify the body and open supernormal abilities; the teachings also include conservative moral views and elements of cosmology involving higher beings and morality decline in the modern age. The practice is taught free of charge and has no formal membership, clergy, or rituals of worship.

History

Falun Gong was initially endorsed within China's state-run qigong system, winning prizes at Oriental Health Expos in 1992 and 1993. As tensions grew over state control of qigong organisations, Li withdrew the group from the China Qigong Scientific Research Association in 1996 and moved to the United States in 1998. State media criticism intensified; on 25 April 1999, over 10,000 practitioners gathered silently near the Zhongnanhai leadership compound in Beijing to petition for recognition and an end to harassment — the largest protest in the capital since 1989.

Persecution in China

On 20 July 1999, the Chinese Communist Party leadership under Jiang Zemin launched a nationwide campaign to eradicate Falun Gong, banning the group, arresting its adherents, and creating an extrajudicial body, the 610 Office, to coordinate suppression. Human rights organisations have documented large-scale arbitrary detention, forced labour, torture, and deaths in custody; practitioners and some investigators have also alleged systematic forced organ harvesting from prisoners of conscience, claims assessed by a 2019 London-based people's tribunal (the China Tribunal) which concluded that forced organ harvesting had occurred on a significant scale — findings the Chinese government denies. Falun Gong is legal and practised openly in most other countries.

Outside China

Overseas adherents have built a substantial media and cultural presence, including the newspaper The Epoch Times, New Tang Dynasty Television, and the Shen Yun Performing Arts dance company, which tours internationally presenting classical Chinese dance alongside depictions of the persecution and spiritual themes. Practitioners maintain protest vigils at Chinese embassies and tourist sites, and the movement's political profile — including its media's editorial stances in Western politics — has drawn both support and criticism.
