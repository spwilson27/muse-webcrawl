Kitzbühel

Kitzbühel is a medieval town in the Austrian state of Tyrol, renowned as one of the world's most famous ski resorts. It lies in the Kitzbühel Alps between the Hahnenkamm and Kitzbüheler Horn mountains and has a population of around 8,000.

History

Settled since the late Bronze Age and later a copper and silver mining town, Kitzbühel received its town charter in the thirteenth century. When mining declined, tourism took its place from the late nineteenth century, when skiing pioneers popularised the slopes above the town.

Skiing

Kitzbühel hosts the annual Hahnenkamm Races, among the most prestigious events in the Alpine Ski World Cup; its Streif downhill course is regarded as one of the most demanding and dangerous on the circuit. The town is also known for the Generali Open tennis tournament and its upscale hotels and boutiques.
