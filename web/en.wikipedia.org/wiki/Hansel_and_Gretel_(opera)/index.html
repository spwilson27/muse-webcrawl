Hansel and Gretel (opera)

ComposerEngelbert Humperdinck
LibrettistAdelheid Wette (the composer's sister)
LanguageGerman
Based on"Hansel and Gretel" from Grimm's Fairy Tales
Premiere23 December 1893, Hoftheater, Weimar; conducted by Richard Strauss

Hansel and Gretel (German: Hänsel und Gretel) is an opera in three acts by the German composer Engelbert Humperdinck, with a libretto by his sister Adelheid Wette, based on the fairy tale by the Brothers Grimm. First performed in Weimar in December 1893, it has become one of the most frequently performed operas in the world and a traditional Christmas-season work in many opera houses.

History

Wette originally asked her brother for incidental music to a children's play she had adapted from the Grimms' tale. Humperdinck expanded the songs into a singspiel and then into a full-scale opera, written in a rich Wagnerian orchestral idiom that he undercut with genuine German folk tunes. Richard Strauss, then a young conductor at Weimar, admired the score and conducted the premiere; Gustav Mahler conducted the Hamburg production the following year.

Plot

Hansel and Gretel, the children of a poor broom-maker and his wife, are sent into the forest to gather strawberries. Lost as night falls, they are protected by the Sandman and pray their evening prayer ("Abends, will ich schlafen gehn") as fourteen angels descend. In the morning they find a gingerbread house belonging to a Witch, who fattens Hansel for the oven. Gretel tricks the Witch into looking into the oven and pushes her in; the house's gingerbread children come back to life, and the family is joyfully reunited.

Music and reception

The score blends sophisticated late-Romantic orchestration — Humperdinck had assisted Wagner at Bayreuth — with folklike melodies such as "Brüderchen, komm tanz mit mir." The Evening Prayer and the pantomime of the angels are among its most famous numbers. Critics have long noted the contrast between its childlike story and its full Wagnerian orchestra, a combination that has kept it in the repertory continuously since 1893.
