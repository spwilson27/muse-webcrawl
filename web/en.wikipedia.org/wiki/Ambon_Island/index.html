Ambon Island is part of the Maluku Islands of Indonesia. The island has an area of 743.37 km² (287.02 sq mi) and is mountainous, well watered, and fertile. Ambon Island consists of two peninsulas: the northern Leihitu and the southern Leitimur, joined by a narrow isthmus. The city of Ambon, the provincial capital of Maluku, is located on Leitimur.

Geography

The island lies on the north side of the Banda Sea, in a zone of complex tectonic activity near the meeting of the Australian and Pacific plates. Its highest point is Mount Salahutu, at 1,225 metres. The two peninsulas enclose Ambon Bay, a long inlet that nearly divides the island. The island is of volcanic origin, with hot springs and fumaroles, and experiences frequent earthquakes.

History

Ambon was historically important as a centre of the clove trade. The Portuguese arrived in 1513 and built a fort; they were expelled by the Dutch in 1605, and the island became a headquarters of the Dutch East India Company (VOC). Under Dutch rule, Ambon's inhabitants were compelled to grow only cloves, which the VOC sought to monopolise. The island was occupied by the British during the Napoleonic Wars and by the Japanese in the Second World War.

In 1950 Ambon was the centre of the short-lived Republic of South Maluku, whose proclamation led to fighting with Indonesian forces. Between 1999 and 2002 the island was affected by sectarian violence between Muslim and Christian communities across Maluku.

Economy and culture

The population is predominantly Malay and Papuan in origin, and the island is roughly evenly divided between Muslims and Christians. Ambonese Malay (Melayu Ambon) is widely spoken as a lingua franca. The economy is based on agriculture — including cloves, nutmeg, and sago — fishing, and the services and administration centred on Ambon city. Pattimura Airport serves the island.
