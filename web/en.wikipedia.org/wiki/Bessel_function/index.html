Bessel function

In mathematics, Bessel functions, first defined by the mathematician Daniel Bernoulli and then generalised by Friedrich Bessel, are canonical solutions y(x) of Bessel's differential equation

x² y″ + x y′ + (x² − α²) y = 0

for an arbitrary complex number α, the order of the Bessel function. Although α and −α produce the same differential equation, different Bessel functions are defined for them so that the functions are smooth in α.

Bessel functions of the first kind

The Bessel functions of the first kind, denoted Jα(x), are solutions of Bessel's equation that are finite at the origin for nonnegative integer α. They admit the series expansion

Bessel functions of the second kind

The Bessel functions of the second kind, denoted Yα(x) and also called Weber or Neumann functions, are solutions that are singular at the origin. For non-integer α they are defined as a combination of Jα and J−α. Modified Bessel functions Iα and Kα arise for imaginary arguments and are important in diffusion and heat problems.

Applications

Bessel functions appear wherever problems with cylindrical or spherical symmetry are solved by separation of variables: wave propagation and vibrations of a circular drum, heat conduction in a cylinder, diffraction, signal processing (the Bessel filter), and the modes of optical fibres. They are among the most widely used special functions in physics and engineering.
