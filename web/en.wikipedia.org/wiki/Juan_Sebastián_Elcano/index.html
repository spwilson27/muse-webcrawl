Juan Sebastián Elcano

Juan Sebastián Elcano (c. 1486 – 4 August 1526) was a Basque Spanish navigator best known for completing the first circumnavigation of the Earth. He sailed with Ferdinand Magellan's expedition of 1519 as a ship's master; after Magellan was killed in the Philippines in 1521, Elcano took command of the surviving ship Victoria and led her across the Indian Ocean and around Africa, reaching Spain in September 1522 with just 17 other survivors.

The circumnavigation

Elcano joined the expedition as master of the Concepción. He took part in the mutiny at Puerto San Julián in 1520 and was pardoned by Magellan. After the fleet crossed the Strait of Magellan and Magellan fell at Mactan in April 1521, command passed through several hands; at Tidore in the Moluccas the survivors split: the Trinidad attempted a return across the Pacific and failed, while Elcano took the Victoria westward. Avoiding Portuguese ports, he crossed the Indian Ocean, rounded the Cape of Good Hope, and — starving, with crew dying of scurvy — reached Sanlúcar de Barrameda on 6 September 1522. A single cargo of cloves paid for the entire expedition.

Honours and death

Emperor Charles V granted Elcano a coat of arms showing a globe with the motto Primus circumdedisti me and a pension. In 1525 he sailed as second-in-command of the Loaísa expedition to the Moluccas, but died of malnutrition in the Pacific in August 1526. The Spanish Navy's training ship Juan Sebastián de Elcano bears his name.
