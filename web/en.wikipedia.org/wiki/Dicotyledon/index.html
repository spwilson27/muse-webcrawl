Dicotyledon

Informal group: Dicots

Seed leaves: Two cotyledons

Status: Traditional grouping; paraphyletic in modern classification

The dicotyledons, or dicots, are flowering plants whose seeds typically contain two embryonic leaves, or cotyledons. They were long treated as one of the two great classes of angiosperms, alongside the monocotyledons (monocots).

Characteristics

Typical dicot features include net-veined leaves, flower parts in fours or fives, vascular bundles arranged in a ring in the stem, taproots, and the capacity for secondary growth producing wood. Examples range from oaks and roses to beans, sunflowers and cacti.

Classification

Molecular phylogenetics showed the traditional dicots are paraphyletic: the eudicots form a true clade marked by tricolpate pollen, while several early-diverging lineages — magnoliids and basal angiosperms — fall outside it. Modern APG systems therefore use eudicots rather than dicotyledons as a formal taxon. Compare the grass family Poaceae, which are monocots.
