The Blackburn Buccaneer is a British carrier-capable attack aircraft designed in the 1950s for the Royal Navy. Developed to counter the Soviet Navy's Sverdlov-class cruisers, it was designed to fly very fast at extremely low level beneath radar coverage to deliver nuclear or conventional weapons. It first flew in 1958 and entered Fleet Air Arm service in 1962 as the NA.39/S.1, later improved as the Spey-engined S.2.

Design

The Buccaneer used area ruling for transonic performance, a rotating bomb bay that flipped open to release stores without disturbing the airflow, boundary-layer blowing over the wings and tail for low-speed carrier approaches, and a braking parachute and arrester hook; its airframe was built exceptionally strong for sustained low-altitude flight. The two crew sat in tandem under a single jettisonable canopy.

Service

Royal Navy Buccaneers operated from the carriers Victorious, Eagle, Ark Royal and Hermes. When fixed-wing carrier aviation ended in 1978, RAF squadrons adopted the type for maritime strike and overland attack; RAF Buccaneers famously flew "flag-waving" ultra-low demonstrations in Red Flag exercises and made long-range laser-designation (Pave Spike) sorties in the 1991 Gulf War. The South African Air Force flew its Buccaneers in the Border War. The type was retired from RAF service in 1994.
