Parody music

Parody music is a genre of music in which familiar melodies or songs are reused with new, usually humorous or satirical lyrics, or in which the style of a particular performer or genre is imitated for comic effect. Song parody has roots in folk traditions of contrafactum, where new texts were set to well-known tunes.

Notable artists

Allan Sherman scored 1960s hits with comic rewrites of standards. "Weird Al" Yankovic became the genre's most successful modern exponent, with parodies such as "Like a Surgeon" and "White & Nerdy", typically seeking the original artists' permission as a courtesy, though parody is protected as fair use in the United States.

Legal status

In the US Supreme Court case Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc. (1994), the Court held that a commercial parody could qualify as fair use, strengthening legal protection for the genre.
