The Maria Pia Bridge (Portuguese: Ponte de D. Maria Pia) is a railway bridge built in 1877 over the Douro River between Porto and Vila Nova de Gaia in Portugal. Named after Queen Maria Pia of Savoy, it was designed by Théophile Seyrig of Gustave Eiffel's company, Eiffel et Cie., and at its completion its 160-metre arch was the longest single-span arch bridge in the world.

Design and construction

The bridge is a two-hinged, crescent-shaped wrought-iron arch of 160 m span, rising 42.6 m, carrying a single-track railway deck 353 m long some 60 m above the river. The arch was erected by cantilevering outward from both banks, the two halves being joined at the crown, without falsework in the river below — a celebrated feat of nineteenth-century engineering. Construction began in January 1876 and the bridge was inaugurated by King Luís I and Queen Maria Pia on 4 November 1877.

Legacy

The Maria Pia Bridge carried the main northern railway line into Porto for more than a century. Age and its single, lightly built track led to speed and weight restrictions, and it was replaced in 1991 by the nearby São João Bridge, after which it was closed. Designated a Portuguese national monument in 1982 and an international historic civil engineering landmark by the American Society of Civil Engineers, the bridge remains an icon of Porto; Seyrig went on to design the nearby Dom Luís I Bridge (1886), while Eiffel applied the lessons of the Maria Pia arch to the Garabit Viaduct in France.
