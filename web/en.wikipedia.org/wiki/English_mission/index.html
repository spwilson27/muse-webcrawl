English mission

PeriodFrom 1580
Key figuresEdmund Campion, Robert Persons

The English mission was the effort by the Catholic Church, beginning in 1580, to sustain and restore Catholicism in England after the Elizabethan Reformation established the Protestant Church of England. Seminary priests trained at colleges abroad — notably the English College at Douai, founded in 1568 — were sent secretly into England, joined from 1580 by Jesuit missionaries.

Persecution

Under Elizabeth I and her successors, Catholic missionary priests were treated as traitors: legislation of 1585 made it high treason for a seminary priest or Jesuit to be in England. Many missionaries, including Edmund Campion (executed 1581), were executed, and those who sheltered them faced severe penalties. Despite this, the mission maintained a recusant Catholic community through gentry households.

Organisation

With no bishops resident in England, the mission was governed by archpriests and later by vicars apostolic (from 1623). Internal disputes, notably the Archpriest Controversy, divided secular clergy and Jesuits. The missionary structure endured until the restoration of the English hierarchy in 1850.
