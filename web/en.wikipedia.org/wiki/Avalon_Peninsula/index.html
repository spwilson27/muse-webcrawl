The Avalon Peninsula is a large peninsula in the southeast of the island of Newfoundland, Canada. It is home to St. John's, the provincial capital and largest city of Newfoundland and Labrador, and contains roughly half of the province's population. The peninsula is joined to the rest of the island by the narrow Isthmus of Avalon.

Geography

The Avalon is a rugged, deeply indented landmass of about 9,700 square kilometres, characterized by rocky coastlines, numerous bays and coves, and low forested hills. Major bays include Conception Bay, Trinity Bay, St. Mary's Bay, Placentia Bay, and Trepassey Bay. Cape Spear, at the peninsula's eastern edge near St. John's, is the easternmost point of North America excluding Greenland. Other notable communities include Mount Pearl, Conception Bay South, Paradise, Bay Roberts, Carbonear, and Placentia.

History

The peninsula was one of the first parts of North America to be permanently settled by Europeans. Seasonal fishing stations operated from the early 1500s, and Sir Humphrey Gilbert formally claimed Newfoundland for England at St. John's in 1583. In 1610 John Guy established a colony at Cuper's Cove (now Cupids), and in 1620 George Calvert, later Lord Baltimore, received a grant for the Province of Avalon, founding the Colony of Avalon at Ferryland in 1621. During the 17th and 18th centuries the Avalon was contested between England and France, whose North American capital at Plaisance (Placentia) guarded the French Shore; the Treaty of Utrecht (1713) ceded the island to Britain.

The peninsula takes its name from the legendary island of Avalon in Arthurian tradition, reflecting Calvert's hope that the colony would be a haven. Signal Hill in St. John's received the first transatlantic wireless signal from Guglielmo Marconi in 1901, and the area played a major role in transatlantic aviation and Second World War convoy operations.

Economy

Historically based on the cod fishery, the Avalon economy is now dominated by government services, education and health care in St. John's, Memorial University of Newfoundland, offshore oil and gas support (including the Bull Arm fabrication site and supply bases for the Hibernia and other offshore fields), and tourism.
