Nahapana


Nahapana
Reign: 1st–2nd century CE
Dynasty: Western Satraps (Kshaharata)
Territory: Western India (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Malwa)

Nahapana was an important ruler of the Western Kshatrapas (Western Satraps) in northwestern India during the 1st or 2nd century CE. He belonged to the Kshaharata family and is known from his abundant silver coinage and from inscriptions of his son-in-law and minister.

Reign

Nahapana's inscriptions, dated in years 41–46 of an era usually identified with the Saka era, place his reign around AD 119–124, though some scholars have argued for an earlier date in the 1st century. His dominions extended over Gujarat, Kathiawar, parts of Rajasthan and Malwa, and northern Maharashtra. Inscriptions at Nasik, Karle, and Junnar record donations by his son-in-law Ushavadata (Rishabhadatta) and his minister Ayama, attesting to Nahapana's power in the northern Deccan.

Defeat and coinage

Nahapana was ultimately defeated — and his line extinguished — by the Satavahana king Gautamiputra Satakarni, whose victory is celebrated in a Nashik inscription of his mother Gautami Balashri. Large numbers of Nahapana's coins were restruck by Gautamiputra as a mark of conquest, most famously in the Jogalthambi hoard. The Greek-derived Periplus of the Erythraean Sea also mentions a ruler "Nambanus", generally identified with Nahapana, whose ports handled trade between India and the Roman world.
