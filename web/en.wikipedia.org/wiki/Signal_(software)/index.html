Signal (software)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Signal is an encrypted messaging service for instant messaging, voice and video calls, developed by the non-profit Signal Foundation and its subsidiary Signal Messenger. Released with its modern name in 2015 (from the earlier TextSecure and RedPhone apps), it is known for the Signal Protocol — end-to-end encryption so widely adopted that it also underpins WhatsApp's encryption — and for collecting minimal user data (essentially a phone number).

Features and governance

Signal apps are free and open-source for Android, iOS and desktop, with features including disappearing messages, group chats and stories. The Signal Foundation, launched in 2018 with funding associated with WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton, is supported by donations. Cryptographer Moxie Marlinspike founded the project; Meredith Whittaker became foundation president in 2022.

Adoption and debates

Signal's popularity has surged whenever rival services changed privacy terms, notably in 2021. Governments have debated lawful access to encrypted messages, with Signal threatening to leave markets that mandate 'backdoors'.
