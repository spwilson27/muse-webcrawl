Russian grammar

Russian grammar describes the morphology and syntax of the Russian language, a highly inflected East Slavic language written in the Cyrillic alphabet. Russian preserves much of the inflectional complexity of Proto-Slavic, with nouns declining for six cases and three genders, and verbs conjugating for person, number, tense, and aspect.

Nouns and cases

Russian nouns belong to three genders — masculine, feminine, and neuter — and decline in six cases: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, and prepositional (locative). Case endings vary by declension class and by whether the noun is animate; for animate masculine and plural nouns the accusative is identical to the genitive. Adjectives, pronouns, and numerals agree with nouns in gender, number, and case.

Verbs and aspect

The Russian verb system is dominated by the category of aspect: most verbs exist in imperfective and perfective pairs expressing ongoing versus completed action. Verbs conjugate in the present (imperfective) or perfective future, and form a past tense agreeing in gender and number. Russian has no articles, word order is relatively free and pragmatic, and the copula is typically omitted in the present tense.
