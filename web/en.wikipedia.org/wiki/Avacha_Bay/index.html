Avacha Bay (Russian: Авачинская бухта) is a large bay on the southeastern coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East, opening onto the Pacific Ocean. Regarded as one of the world's largest natural harbours capable of accommodating any ship, it shelters the city of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, Kamchatka's capital and main port, and the closed naval town of Vilyuchinsk, home to Pacific Fleet ballistic missile submarines at the Rybachy base.

Geography

The bay is a drowned volcanic crater (caldera) roughly 24 km long and up to about 14 km wide, connected to the open ocean by a narrower channel. The entrance is marked by the Tri Brata ("Three Brothers"), a trio of basalt sea stacks that are the symbol of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky. The Avacha and Paratunka rivers flow into the bay, and the volcanoes Avachinsky and Koryaksky rise nearby. The inner Avacha Inlet freezes in winter, while the great depth of the bay — up to about 26 metres in the harbour area — keeps navigation possible.

History

The bay was charted during Vitus Bering's expeditions, and Petropavlovsk was founded in 1740 as a base for the Second Kamchatka Expedition. During the Crimean War, in August–September 1854, the bay's garrison famously repelled an Anglo-French naval attack in the Siege of Petropavlovsk — the principal Pacific engagement of that war. In the Soviet period the bay became a major closed military area centred on the submarine base across the inlet at Rybachy.

Environment

Avacha Bay supports rich fisheries and marine mammal populations. In autumn 2020 the bay and nearby beaches suffered a mass die-off of marine life — sea urchins, starfish, octopuses and seals washed ashore and surfers reported eye and skin irritation — which investigations attributed primarily to a harmful algal bloom ("red tide") of Karenia algae, with possible contributions from pollution.
