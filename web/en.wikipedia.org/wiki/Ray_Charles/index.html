Ray Charles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Ray Charles at the piano in 1969, dark glasses on, grinning mid-chorus with the Raelettes behind him]

Ray Charles Robinson (23 September 1930 – 10 June 2004), known as Ray Charles, was an American singer, songwriter and pianist, a founding father of soul music. Blind from childhood (glaucoma, after witnessing his brother's drowning), he fused gospel's fervour with blues and R&B in the 1950s ('I Got a Woman', 'What'd I Say'), then crossed every border American music had — jazz, country (Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music, 1962), pop standards — while insisting the feeling was the same.

His signature recordings include 'Georgia on My Mind' (1960, later Georgia's official state song), 'Hit the Road Jack' (1961) and his version of 'America the Beautiful'. He was among the first artists to win ownership of his masters, founded his own label, and took 17 Grammy Awards across six decades.

Origins of soul

Born in Albany, Georgia, and schooled at the Florida School for the Deaf and Blind in St Augustine, Charles gigged across Florida and Seattle (his first recordings, 1949, imitate Nat King Cole and Charles Brown) before Atlantic Records signed him in 1952. His 1954 'I Got a Woman' — a gospel song re-lyricked as love song — scandalised the church and invented soul's basic transaction. 'What'd I Say' (1959), improvised on stage one night to fill time, became his first pop smash.

The Genius

At ABC-Paramount he commanded orchestras and country bands alike; his 1962 country album was a commercial thunderbolt that helped integrate American taste. Addiction cost him years (he quit heroin in 1965 after an arrest); the public never left. The film Ray (2004) and Jamie Foxx's Oscar-winning performance renewed his legend months after his death.
