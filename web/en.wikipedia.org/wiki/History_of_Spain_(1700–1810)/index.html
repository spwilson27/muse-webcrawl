History of Spain (1700–1810)

DynastyHouse of Bourbon

The history of Spain from 1700 to 1810 covers the accession of the Bourbon dynasty, Enlightenment-era reforms, and the upheaval of the Napoleonic invasion.

War of the Spanish Succession

When the childless Charles II died in 1700, his will left Spain to Philip of Anjou (Philip V), grandson of Louis XIV. Austria, Britain and the Netherlands backed Archduke Charles, sparking the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714). The Treaties of Utrecht (1713) recognised Philip but stripped Spain of its European possessions; the Nueva Planta decrees abolished the fueros of the Crown of Aragon.

Bourbon reforms

Philip V, Ferdinand VI and especially Charles III (r. 1759–1788) centralised administration, created intendancies, liberalised colonial trade (1778), expelled the Jesuits (1767) and promoted agriculture, roads and manufactures. Charles IV's reign was dominated by his minister Manuel Godoy.

Napoleonic crisis

Spain allied with France against Britain (defeated at Trafalgar, 1805). In 1808 Napoleon forced the abdications of Bayonne and installed his brother Joseph, provoking the Dos de Mayo uprising in Madrid and the Peninsular War — the crisis of the old regime that led to the Cortes of Cádiz.
