Adaptive sports

Adaptive sports (also called adapted sports or parasports) are sports played by people with disabilities, in which the rules, equipment or playing environment are modified — or the sport itself designed — so that athletes with physical, visual or intellectual impairments can participate and compete. Some adaptive sports are adapted versions of existing able-bodied sports, while others were created specifically for athletes with a disability.

Organisation

Competitive adaptive sport is organised through classification systems that group athletes by the nature and extent of their impairment so that competition is as fair as possible. The largest multi-sport events are the Paralympic Games for athletes with physical and visual impairments, the Special Olympics for athletes with intellectual disabilities, and the Deaflympics for deaf athletes. Governing bodies such as the International Paralympic Committee coordinate rules, classification and anti-doping.

Examples

Adapted forms include wheelchair basketball and tennis (played with standard rules but allowing two bounces of the ball), para-alpine and para-Nordic skiing using sit-skis or outriggers, and amputee football. Sports invented for disabled athletes include goalball and boccia for athletes with severe impairments, and sitting volleyball. Adaptive sports also encompass recreational and rehabilitative programmes, including adaptive rowing, handcycling and equine-assisted activities, widely used in the rehabilitation of injured military veterans.
