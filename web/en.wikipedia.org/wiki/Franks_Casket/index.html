Franks Casket

The Franks Casket (or the Auzon Casket) is a small Anglo-Saxon whalebone chest of the early 8th century, carved on all faces and the lid in low relief with a variety of scenes, accompanied by inscriptions, mostly in Anglo-Saxon runes. Generally thought to be of Northumbrian origin, it is of unique importance for the insights it gives into early Anglo-Saxon culture and art. The casket has usually been on display in the British Museum in London since 1867, though one panel is in the Bargello museum, Florence.

Iconography

The panels draw on a remarkable mixture of traditions: the front combines the Germanic legend of Wayland the Smith with the Adoration of the Magi; other panels show Romulus and Remus suckled by the wolf, the capture of the Temple in Jerusalem by Titus, Aeneas leaving Troy, and scenes from the otherwise obscure legend of Egil. The runic inscriptions include a riddle-like verse identifying the material as the bone of a stranded whale, and texts in a mixture of Old English, Latin, and runes that continue to be studied for their linguistic and literary interest.

History

The casket was kept for centuries at Auzon in the Auvergne, France, used by a family to store sewing implements; during the French Revolution it was discarded as a box and one panel was separated and eventually acquired by the Bargello. The main body was purchased by Sir Augustus Wollaston Franks, who donated it to the British Museum in 1867.
