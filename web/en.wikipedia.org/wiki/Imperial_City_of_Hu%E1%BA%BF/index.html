Imperial City of Huế

Imperial City of Huế
Location: Huế, Vietnam
Built: from 1804, under Emperor Gia Long
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (part of the Complex of Huế Monuments, 1993)

The Imperial City (Hoàng thành) is a walled enclosure within the Citadel of Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam during the Nguyễn dynasty. It was the seat of the Nguyễn emperors from its construction beginning in 1804 under Gia Long until the abdication of Bảo Đại in 1945.

Layout

Modelled partly on Beijing's Forbidden City, the Imperial City lies inside the larger Citadel on the north bank of the Perfume River. Within its walls stands the Purple Forbidden City (Tử Cấm Thành), reserved for the emperor and his household, with palaces, temples, pavilions, and gates such as the Ngọ Môn (Meridian Gate) used for court ceremonies.

History

The city suffered heavy damage during the First Indochina War in 1947 and again during the Battle of Huế in the 1968 Tet Offensive, when many buildings were destroyed. Restoration has proceeded since, and the Complex of Huế Monuments was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.
