YIVO

YIVO (Yiddish: ייִוואָ), originally the Yidisher Visnshaftlekher Institut (Yiddish Scientific Institute), is an organisation that preserves, studies, and teaches the cultural history of Jewish life throughout Eastern Europe, Germany, and Russia, and is the recognised standard-setter for the orthography, grammar, and lexicography of the Yiddish language. Founded in 1925 in Wilno, Poland (now Vilnius, Lithuania), its headquarters moved to New York City in 1940, where it is based at the Center for Jewish History in Manhattan.

History

Scholars including Nokhem Shtif, Max Weinreich, and Elias Tcherikower founded the institute in 1925 to study Jewish life with modern scholarly methods and in Yiddish itself. YIVO organised sections for philology, history, economics and statistics, and psychology and education, published journals, and trained researchers; its network of volunteer collectors (zamlers) amassed documents and folklore from across Jewish Eastern Europe, and its youth autobiography contests of the 1930s became a famous source for social history.

During the Holocaust, the Nazis looted YIVO's Vilnius collections for the Frankfurt Institute for the Study of the Jewish Question; Jewish slave labourers in the Vilnius Ghetto — the "Paper Brigade" — risked their lives to smuggle documents to safety. Part of the looted material was recovered by the U.S. Army and shipped to New York; other parts, long thought destroyed, were found in Vilnius after the Cold War and have been digitised in the Edward Blank YIVO Vilna Online Collections project with the Lithuanian government.

Activities

YIVO maintains a library of some 385,000 volumes and an archive of over 23 million items, runs Yiddish language classes and the Uriel Weinreich summer programme, and publishes scholarly works and exhibitions. Its orthographic standard, set out in Takanes fun yidishn oysleyg, remains the reference for standard Yiddish spelling, and its scholars (including Uriel Weinreich, author of the Modern English-Yiddish Yiddish-English Dictionary) defined modern Yiddish linguistics.
