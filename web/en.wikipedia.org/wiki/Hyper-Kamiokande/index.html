Hyper-Kamiokande (Hyper-K) is a next-generation neutrino observatory under construction in Hida, Gifu Prefecture, Japan. The successor to the Super-Kamiokande detector, it centres on an enormous underground tank of ultra-pure water instrumented with tens of thousands of photosensors that detect the faint flashes of Cherenkov radiation produced when neutrinos interact with the water.

Design

The main detector will be a cylindrical tank about 68 metres in diameter and 71 metres tall, holding some 258,000 tonnes of water (a fiducial volume of about 187,000 tonnes), roughly eight to ten times the fiducial mass of Super-Kamiokande. Around 40,000 high-sensitivity photomultiplier tubes will line the walls. The tank is being excavated in a cavern 650 metres beneath a mountain near the Mozumi mine, close to the existing Kamioka Observatory.

Scientific goals

Hyper-Kamiokande is designed to study neutrino oscillations with a high-intensity beam sent 295 km from the J-PARC accelerator in Tōkai (the T2HK long-baseline experiment), aiming to measure CP violation in the lepton sector — a possible clue to the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe. It will also search for proton decay predicted by grand unified theories, observe neutrinos from supernovae (including a relic supernova neutrino background), and study solar and atmospheric neutrinos. Construction formally began in 2020, with data taking expected to start around 2028.
