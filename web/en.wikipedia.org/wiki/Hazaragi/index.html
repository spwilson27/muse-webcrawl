Hazaragi

Hazaragi (Persian: هزارگی) is a Persian dialect spoken by the Hazara people, primarily in the Hazarajat region of central Afghanistan, as well as in large diaspora communities in Pakistan, Iran and elsewhere. It is closely related to Dari, the variety of Persian spoken in Afghanistan, but is distinguished by a substantial vocabulary of Turkic and Mongolic loanwords reflecting the Hazara's history.

Classification and features

Hazaragi belongs to the western Iranian languages and is mutually intelligible to a large degree with Dari and other Persian varieties. Its distinctive lexicon includes words borrowed from Mongolian and Turkic languages, a legacy of the Mongol armies from which many Hazara trace descent. It retains some archaic Persian features lost in other dialects.

Speakers

Speakers number in the millions. In Pakistan, Hazaragi is concentrated in Quetta, Balochistan, where a large Hazara community has lived since the nineteenth century. Considerable diaspora populations also live in Iran, Australia, Europe and North America.

Status

Hazaragi is primarily a spoken language; written Hazara literature generally uses standard Dari Persian in the Perso-Arabic script. Efforts to document its distinctive vocabulary and oral traditions have grown in recent decades.
