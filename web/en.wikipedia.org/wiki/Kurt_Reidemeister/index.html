Kurt Werner Friedrich Reidemeister (13 October 1893 – 8 July 1971) was a German mathematician, a pioneer of knot theory and a contributor to geometric group theory and the foundations of geometry. He is best known for the Reidemeister moves in knot theory and for Reidemeister torsion, an invariant of manifolds.

Life and career

Reidemeister was born in Braunschweig and studied at Freiburg, Marburg, Hamburg and Göttingen, receiving his doctorate in 1921 under Erich Hecke at Hamburg, with a thesis on algebraic number theory. In Hamburg he became interested in knot theory. He held chairs at Vienna, Königsberg, Marburg and finally Göttingen, where he spent the last two decades of his career. Dismissed from Königsberg in 1933 for perceived political unreliability under the Nazi regime, he was reinstated at Marburg. He was also a published poet and translator, associated with expressionist literary circles.

Work

In 1926 Reidemeister showed that two diagrams represent the same knot if and only if they are related by a finite sequence of three local modifications, now called the Reidemeister moves — a foundational result of knot theory published around the same time as related work by J. W. Alexander and G. B. Briggs. His 1935 introduction of Reidemeister torsion (later extended as Reidemeister–Franz torsion) gave a powerful invariant distinguishing lens spaces and foreshadowed torsion invariants in topology. His book Knotentheorie (1932) was the first monograph on knot theory. Reidemeister also wrote on the philosophy and history of mathematics, including a book on Plato's influence on mathematical thought.
