Indiana University Bloomington

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indiana University Bloomington (IU Bloomington, Indiana University, IU, or simply Indiana) is a public research university in Bloomington, Indiana, United States. It is the flagship campus of Indiana University and enrols more than 47,000 students. Founded in 1820, it is among the oldest public universities in the United States and a member of the Association of American Universities.

History

The Indiana General Assembly chartered a State Seminary in 1820; it became Indiana College in 1828 and Indiana University in 1838. Sarah Parke Morrison became its first female graduate in 1869. Under president Herman B Wells (1937–1962) the university grew dramatically, recruiting distinguished faculty, expanding international programmes, and desegregating its facilities. Wells also protected the research of sexologist Alfred Kinsey, whose institute remains at IU.

Academics

IU Bloomington comprises more than a dozen schools, including the Kelley School of Business, the Jacobs School of Music (one of the largest music schools in the United States), the Maurer School of Law, the Luddy School of Informatics, Computing, and Engineering, and the O'Neill School of Public and Environmental Affairs. The university is noted for its library system, including the limestone Lilly Library of rare books and manuscripts, and for language instruction in more than 80 languages.

Campus

The campus is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful in the United States, with buildings of locally quarried Indiana limestone set among wooded grounds and bisected by the Jordan River. Landmarks include the Sample Gates, the Indiana Memorial Union, Assembly Hall, and the Eskenazi Museum of Art designed by I. M. Pei.

Student life and athletics

Students participate in traditions such as the Little 500 bicycle race, founded in 1951 and immortalised in the film Breaking Away. The university's teams, the Indiana Hoosiers, compete in the Big Ten Conference; the men's basketball programme has won five NCAA championships.
