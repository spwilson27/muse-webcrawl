Faith

Faith
FieldReligion / philosophy of religion
Related conceptsBelief, trust, hope; fideism vs evidentialism
[Image: Candles lit in a dark cathedral as votive offerings]

Faith is confidence or trust in a person, thing or doctrine. In religion it typically means trust in God and acceptance of teachings held to be revealed; the Christian tradition's epistle to the Hebrews calls it 'the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen'. Philosophers debate its relation to evidence: evidentialists require proportioning belief to proof, while fideists and thinkers like Søren Kierkegaard — for whom faith begins precisely where thinking leaves off — defend commitment beyond or against probability.

Models

Analyses distinguish faith as belief-that (assent to propositions), faith as trust-in (affective reliance on a person), and faith as commitment expressed in action. Aquinas integrated faith with reason as assent of the intellect moved by the will and grace; modern debates ask whether religious faith is more like ordinary interpersonal trust than like hypothesis-belief.
