Rafflesia

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Malpighiales
Family:Rafflesiaceae
Genus:Rafflesia
Species:c. 40 described

Rafflesia is a genus of parasitic flowering plants native to the rainforests of Southeast Asia — Sumatra, Borneo, Java, the Malay Peninsula, and the Philippines. It contains some of the largest flowers in the world: Rafflesia arnoldii produces blooms over 1 metre (3 ft) across and weighing up to 11 kg (24 lb). The flowers exude a strong odour of decaying flesh, earning them the nickname "corpse flowers," which attracts carrion flies as pollinators.

Biology

Rafflesia is an extreme endoparasite: it has no stems, leaves, or roots visible outside its host, and lacks chlorophyll entirely. It lives inside vines of the genus Tetrastigma (in the grape family), emerging only to flower — a bud resembling a cabbage develops over many months, then opens for just a few days into five fleshy, mottled petals surrounding a central disk. The plant's genome is among the most unusual known, having absorbed large amounts of DNA from its hosts by horizontal gene transfer. A 2023 assessment warned that most Rafflesia species are critically endangered by habitat destruction, and the genus has become an icon — and a test case — for plant conservation.

Discovery and name

The genus was named after Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles, lieutenant-governor of British Sumatra, whose 1818 expedition encountered R. arnoldii (the species name honours botanist Joseph Arnold, who died shortly after the expedition). Several species are provincial or national symbols, including in Indonesia and Thailand.
