Georges Bataille (10 September 1897 – 9 July 1962) was a French writer and philosopher whose work on eroticism, transgression, sacrifice, and economics influenced generations of thinkers, including Michel Foucault and Jacques Derrida.

Life and work

Trained as a medievalist and archivist, Bataille worked as a librarian at the Bibliothèque nationale. Associated with the surrealists before breaking with André Breton, he founded the journal Documents (1929) and later the review Critique (1946), as well as the secret society Acéphale and the Collège de Sociologie with Roger Caillois and Michel Leiris.

Ideas

His erotic novel Story of the Eye (1928) appeared under a pseudonym. In The Accursed Share (1949) he developed a "general economy" centred on excess energy and unproductive expenditure — sacrifice, luxury, and eroticism — rather than scarcity. Erotism (1957) explored the link between taboo, transgression, and death.
