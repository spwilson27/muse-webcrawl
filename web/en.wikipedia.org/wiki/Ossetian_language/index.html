Ossetian
Native name: Ирон æвзаг (Iron ævzag)
Region: Caucasus (North Ossetia–Alania, South Ossetia, Kabardino-Balkaria)
Speakers: c. 450,000–600,000
Family: Indo-European → Indo-Iranian → Iranian → Eastern → North-Eastern → Ossetian
Dialects: Iron, Digor
Writing system: Cyrillic
Official status: North Ossetia–Alania (Russia); South Ossetia

Ossetian (Ossetian: Ирон æвзаг, Iron ævzag) is an Eastern Iranian language spoken in Ossetia, a region on both sides of the Greater Caucasus Mountains. It is the language of the Ossetians, who are generally held to descend from the Alans, a Sarmatian tribe of Iranian steppe nomads. Ossetian is the sole surviving descendant of the Scythian dialect continuum once spoken across the Pontic-Caspian steppe.

Dialects

The language has two principal dialects: Iron, the more widely spoken, on which the literary standard is based, and Digor, spoken in the west. The dialects differ in phonology and vocabulary; Iron has innovated under the influence of neighbouring Caucasian languages, while Digor is in some respects more archaic. A third dialect, Jassic, was spoken in medieval Hungary by the Jász people and became extinct.

Phonology and grammar

Ossetian is notable among Iranian languages for having developed ejective consonants and a rich system of uvular and glottalised sounds, features acquired through long contact with the languages of the Caucasus. It has nine cases in the noun, an agglutinative system of postpositions, and a verbal system that retains traces of older Iranian prefixes. Vowel inventory includes the distinctive letter æ, written with a single character in the Cyrillic-based alphabet.

Writing system

Ossetian has been written in several scripts: Greek-based and Georgian-based scripts were used at times historically; the first Ossetian book was printed in 1798. In 1923 a Latin-based alphabet was introduced, replaced between 1938 and 1954 by Cyrillic in the north and Georgian script in the south. Since 1954 both North and South Ossetia have used a Cyrillic alphabet containing the special letter Æ æ.

Literature

Ossetian literature centres on the Nart sagas, the heroic epic cycle of the Narts shared across the North Caucasus. The founder of modern Ossetian literary language is the poet Kosta Khetagurov (1859–1906), whose collection Iron Fændyr ("Ossetian Lyre", 1899) remains a cornerstone of Ossetian culture.

Status

Ossetian is an official language of North Ossetia–Alania within Russia and of the disputed territory of South Ossetia. UNESCO classifies it as "definitely endangered", as many younger speakers shift to Russian or Georgian.
