Lake Tahoe

Lake Tahoe's clear blue water and Sierra pines

Lake Tahoe is a large freshwater lake in the Sierra Nevada of the western United States, straddling the state line between California and Nevada. It is the largest alpine lake in North America, lying 1,897 metres above sea level, with a surface area of 495 square kilometres and a maximum depth of 501 metres — the second-deepest lake in the United States after Crater Lake.

Tahoe is famed for the purity and blueness of its water (clarity has been monitored since 1968 and is a focus of conservation) and for the ski resorts around its shores, including Palisades Tahoe (host of the 1960 Winter Olympics as Squaw Valley). The Washoe people inhabited its basin for millennia. The lake's only outlet is the Truckee River, flowing to Pyramid Lake in Nevada.
