Genetically modified crops

Genetically modified crops (GM crops) are plants used in agriculture whose DNA has been modified by genetic engineering to introduce traits such as resistance to pests, diseases or herbicides, tolerance of environmental stress, or improved nutritional content. The first commercial GM crop, the delayed-ripening Flavr Savr tomato, was approved in the United States in 1994.

Common traits

The most widely planted GM crops are soybean, maize, cotton and canola engineered for herbicide tolerance (such as glyphosate resistance) or insect resistance via genes from Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt crops). Later developments include virus-resistant papaya, drought-tolerant maize, and biofortified Golden Rice producing vitamin A precursor beta-carotene.

Regulation and debate

GM crops are regulated differently around the world: they are widely grown in the Americas, while the European Union applies stricter approval and labeling rules. Scientific consensus assessments have found approved GM foods no riskier than conventional counterparts, but debate continues over environmental effects, corporate control of seed, herbicide use and labeling. Gene-edited crops made with tools such as CRISPR are prompting further regulatory revision.
