Masa

Masa (Spanish for "dough") is a dough made from ground nixtamalized corn, used to make tortillas, tamales, pupusas, and other foods of Latin American cuisine. It is distinguished from ordinary cornmeal by the nixtamalization process, in which dried corn kernels are soaked and cooked in an alkaline solution, usually calcium hydroxide (slaked lime), before being ground.

Preparation

Traditionally, corn is nixtamalized, washed, and ground on a metate into a fine, pliable dough. Commercially, masa is sold either fresh, as instant masa harina (dehydrated flour to which water is added), or as dried tortilla flour. Masa harina should not be confused with cornmeal: the lime treatment changes the corn's chemistry, making niacin bioavailable and improving protein quality, and giving masa its characteristic flavor and texture.

Uses

Masa is the basis of corn tortillas, the staple flatbread of Mexico and Central America; of tamales, where it is steamed in corn husks; of pupusas in El Salvador; of gorditas, sopes, and huaraches. White, yellow, and blue corn each produce masa of different color and flavor.
