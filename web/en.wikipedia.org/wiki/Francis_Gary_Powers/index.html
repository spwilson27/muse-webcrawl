Francis Gary Powers (August 17, 1929 – August 1, 1977) was an American pilot whose CIA Lockheed U-2 spy plane was shot down by Soviet air defences while he was flying a reconnaissance mission over Soviet territory on 1 May 1960, causing the 1960 U-2 incident and wrecking the Paris summit between Eisenhower and Khrushchev.

Incident and captivity

Powers took off from Peshawar, Pakistan, to photograph ICBM sites. His aircraft was hit by an S-75 (SA-2) surface-to-air missile near Sverdlovsk (Yekaterinburg); Powers parachuted safely but did not use his suicide pin, and was captured. He was tried in Moscow for espionage in August 1960, sentenced to ten years, and held for nearly two years before being exchanged on 10 February 1962, on Berlin's Glienicke Bridge, for Soviet intelligence officer Rudolf Abel — the first famous spy swap of the Cold War.

Later life

Criticized in the U.S. for not destroying the aircraft or taking his own life, Powers was later exonerated by a Senate inquiry and posthumously awarded the Distinguished Flying Cross, Prisoner of War Medal, and (in 2012) the Silver Star. He worked as a test pilot for Lockheed and then as a helicopter traffic reporter for KNBC in Los Angeles. He died in a helicopter crash on 1 August 1977 while returning from covering brush fires.
