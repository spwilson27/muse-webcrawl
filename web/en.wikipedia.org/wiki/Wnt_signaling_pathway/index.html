Wnt signaling pathway

The Wnt signaling pathways are a group of signal transduction pathways made of proteins that pass signals into a cell through cell surface receptors. The name Wnt is a portmanteau of Wingless (in Drosophila) and Int-1 (a mouse proto-oncogene). These pathways regulate cell fate, proliferation, migration and embryonic patterning in animals.

Canonical pathway

In the canonical Wnt/β-catenin pathway, Wnt ligands bind Frizzled receptors and LRP5/6 co-receptors, disabling the β-catenin destruction complex (including APC, Axin and GSK3β). Stabilised β-catenin enters the nucleus and activates TCF/LEF target genes. In the absence of Wnt, β-catenin is phosphorylated and degraded.

Non-canonical pathways

The planar cell polarity pathway and the Wnt/calcium pathway act independently of β-catenin, controlling tissue polarity, cytoskeletal dynamics and intracellular calcium.

Clinical significance

Mutations in pathway components, notably loss of the tumour suppressor APC, drive most colorectal cancers. Dysregulated Wnt signaling is also implicated in other cancers, bone diseases such as osteoporosis, and developmental disorders.
