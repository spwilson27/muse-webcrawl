Docosahexaenoic acid

Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) is an omega-3 fatty acid that is a primary structural component of the human brain, cerebral cortex, skin, and retina. In physiological literature, it is given the designation 22:6(n-3) — a 22-carbon chain with six cis double bonds.

Docosahexaenoic acid
Formula: C₂₂H₃₂O₂
Designation: 22:6(n-3)
Sources: Fatty fish, algae oil

Sources and synthesis

DHA is obtained from eating fatty fish (which accumulate it from microalgae) or directly from algal oil supplements. Humans can convert alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) to DHA only inefficiently. DHA is abundant in breast milk and is added to many infant formulas.

Biological roles

DHA makes up a large share of the polyunsaturated fatty acids in neuronal membranes and photoreceptor outer segments, influencing membrane fluidity and signalling. It is the precursor of docosanoids including resolvins and neuroprotectin D1, involved in resolving inflammation.

Health research

DHA intake is associated with fetal and infant brain and eye development. Evidence for benefits in cardiovascular disease, cognition and dementia is mixed; health authorities recommend regular oily fish consumption, while routine fish-oil supplementation shows limited benefit in large trials.
