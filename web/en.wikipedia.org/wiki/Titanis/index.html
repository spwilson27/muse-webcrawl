Titanis

Titanis walleri is an extinct genus of giant flightless predatory bird of the family Phorusrhacidae — the "terror birds" — that lived in North America during the Pliocene and early Pleistocene, roughly 5 to 1.8 million years ago. It is the only known phorusrhacid from North America north of Mexico.

Discovery and description

Titanis was named in 1963 from a distal tarsometatarsus found in Florida; further material comes from Texas and Florida river deposits. It stood an estimated 1.4–1.9 metres tall, with a massive hooked beak typical of terror birds, reduced wings and powerful running legs. Its ancestors are thought to have reached North America from South America before or during the Great American Interchange, possibly island-hopping.

Extinction

Titanis disappeared in the early Pleistocene; proposed causes include competition with placental carnivores and climate change, though the evidence is debated.
