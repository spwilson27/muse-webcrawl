Jelly beans

Jelly beans are small, bean-shaped sugar candies with a soft, chewy interior and a thin, hard outer shell. They come in a wide variety of colours and flavours, from traditional fruit flavours to novelty tastes.

History

Jelly beans developed in the nineteenth century from Turkish delight and the sugar-panning process used for Jordan almonds. They became popular in the United States in the early twentieth century and are traditionally associated with Easter.

Production

Jelly beans are made by cooking sugar, corn syrup, and starch into a gelled centre, then tumbling the centres in pans with successive coatings of sugar syrup until a shell forms. Well-known brands include Jelly Belly, famous for its many flavours. See also candy.
