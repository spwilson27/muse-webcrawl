Information privacy

Information privacy, also known as data privacy, is the relationship between the collection and dissemination of data, technology, the public expectation of privacy, and the surrounding political and legal issues. It concerns an individual's ability to determine what information about them is collected, by whom, and how it is used.

Legal frameworks

The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, in force 2018) is the most comprehensive modern framework, granting individuals rights of access, rectification, erasure ("the right to be forgotten"), portability and objection, and imposing heavy fines. Other regimes include California's CCPA/CPRA, Brazil's LGPD, and sectoral US laws covering health (HIPAA) and financial data. The OECD privacy guidelines and the Fair Information Practice Principles historically underpin many of these laws.

Concerns

Key concerns include mass surveillance by governments, behavioural advertising and data brokerage by platforms, data breaches exposing personal records, and re-identification of supposedly anonymised datasets. Privacy-enhancing technologies — encryption, differential privacy, anonymisation and privacy by design — attempt to reconcile data use with individual rights.

History

The modern debate traces to Warren and Brandeis's 1890 article "The Right to Privacy" and expanded with computerised databases in the 1960s–70s, leading to early data protection laws such as Sweden's Data Act (1973) and the German Federal Data Protection Act.
