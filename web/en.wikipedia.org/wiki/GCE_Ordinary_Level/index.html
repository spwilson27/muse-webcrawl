GCE Ordinary Level

The General Certificate of Education Ordinary Level, commonly called the O level, is a subject-based school qualification that was introduced in England, Wales and Northern Ireland in 1951 and was replaced there by the GCSE in 1988. It continues to be offered internationally by examination boards such as Cambridge Assessment International Education.

History

O levels were typically taken at age 16 and graded from A to E (pass grades) with U for unclassified. They complemented the more advanced A levels (Advanced Level), taken at 18.

International use

Cambridge O Levels remain popular in countries including Singapore (in modified form), Pakistan, Bangladesh, Mauritius, and Zimbabwe, covering subjects from mathematics and sciences to languages and humanities.

Grading

Modern Cambridge O Levels are graded A* to E. The qualification is recognised as equivalent to the GCSE for many purposes.
