Jin Ping Mei

Jin Ping Mei (Chinese: 金瓶梅; pinyin: Jīn Píng Méi), sometimes translated as The Plum in the Golden Vase or The Golden Lotus, is a Chinese naturalistic novel composed in vernacular Chinese during the late Ming dynasty. Traditionally attributed to Lanling Xiaoxiao Sheng (蘭陵笑笑生, "The Scoffing Scholar of Lanling"), a pseudonym, it is one of the Four Great Classical Novels outside the traditional canon, and is often considered the fifth.

Plot

The novel centres on Ximen Qing, a wealthy, corrupt cloth merchant in Qinghe, Shandong, his wives and concubines — including Pan Jinlian, Li Ping'er, and Pang Chunmei, whose names supply the title's three characters — and his rise and fall. Beginning as an offshoot of Water Margin, in which Ximen and Pan Jinlian murder Wu Dalang, it expands into a detailed portrait of domestic life, sexuality, corruption, and social manners in late Ming China.

Ximen bribes officials, accumulates wealth and women, and pursues pleasure relentlessly until illness and excess lead to his death. The household then disperses, providing a moral and karmic counterpoint to his ascent.

Authorship and editions

The author's identity is unknown; proposals have included scholars and officials of the Wanli era. Two main recensions survive: the Wanli edition (1617) and a later Chongzhen edition that revised the opening and style. For centuries the book circulated under bans for obscenity in China.

Literary significance

Unlike earlier Chinese novels focused on heroes and history, Jin Ping Mei pioneered realistic domestic fiction, influencing Dream of the Red Chamber. Its explicit sexual content led to censorship, but scholars praise its psychological depth, narrative structure, and social observation.

Translations

Clement Egerton's The Golden Lotus (1939) rendered erotic passages in Latin. David Tod Roy's five-volume The Plum in the Golden Vase (1993–2013) is the first complete, annotated English translation.
