Transfer functions in imaging

Transfer functions in imaging are mathematical functions describing how light is converted between scene, electrical signal, and displayed image in cameras and displays. The best-known are the opto-electronic transfer function (OETF), which converts scene light into a signal, and the electro-optical transfer function (EOTF), which converts a signal back into displayed light.

Standard functions

Traditional television used a power-law "gamma" curve of roughly 0.45 for the OETF and about 2.2–2.4 for the display EOTF, standardized for standard dynamic range in ITU-R BT.601 and BT.709, with sRGB sharing a similar piecewise curve. For high dynamic range (HDR) imaging, ITU-R BT.2100 specifies two new EOTFs: Perceptual Quantizer (PQ), an absolute curve based on the sensitivity of human vision up to 10,000 cd/m², and Hybrid Log-Gamma (HLG), a scene-referred curve co-developed by the BBC and NHK that remains compatible with SDR displays.

Purpose

Transfer functions serve several purposes: perceptual encoding (allocating more code values to brightness levels the eye distinguishes most finely), noise control, and compatibility between capture and display devices. Applying the inverse of the capture function at the display restores an intended tonal response, with an additional system gamma often applied deliberately for pleasing reproduction in typical viewing environments.
