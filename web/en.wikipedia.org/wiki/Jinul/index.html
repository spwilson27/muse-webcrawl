Jinul

Born: 1158, Goryeo

Died: 1210 (aged 51–52)

Tradition: Korean Seon (Zen)

Founder of: Jogye Order

Jinul (Korean: 지눌; Hanja: 知訥; 1158–1210), often known by his posthumous title of National Teacher Bojo (보조국사), was a Korean Buddhist monk of the Goryeo dynasty. He is widely regarded as the most influential figure in the history of Korean Buddhism, credited with founding the Jogye Order and unifying the various schools of Korean Seon (Zen) meditation.

Life

Jinul was born in 1158 into the Jeong clan of Dongju (now Seosan). He took ordination as a boy and studied widely in the doctrinal schools of Buddhism before turning to meditation practice. Dissatisfied with the corruption and worldliness of the established sangha, he withdrew to mountain hermitages and eventually founded the Samādhi and Prajñā Society (Jeonghye Gyeolsa), a reform movement dedicated to disciplined meditation and study.

In 1200 he established the community at Gilsangsa, later renamed Songgwangsa, on Mount Jogyesan. This monastery became the centre of his movement and remains one of the Three Jewels Temples of Korea.

Teachings

Jinul's central doctrinal contribution was his synthesis of meditation (Seon) and scriptural study (Gyo), arguing that enlightenment (don-o) must be followed by gradual cultivation (jeom-su). His most famous works include the Excerpts from the Dharma Collection and Special Practice Record with Personal Notes and treatises on the Hwaeom (Avatamsaka) teaching and on the "tracing back the radiance" (hoegwang banjo) method of meditation.

He promoted the kanhwa Seon method of investigating the hwadu (critical phrase), which became the dominant meditation technique in Korean Buddhism.

Legacy

The Jogye Order, which traces its lineage to Jinul, is today the largest Buddhist order in South Korea. His collected works are preserved in the Korean Buddhist Canon.
