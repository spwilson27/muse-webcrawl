Necklace of Harmonia

The Necklace of Harmonia was a legendary object in Greek mythology. Though it supposedly brought great beauty to its wearer, it was cursed and brought misfortune or death to all who possessed it. It plays a central role in the tragic cycle of myths surrounding the city of Thebes.

Origin

According to myth, the necklace was crafted by Hephaestus and given as a wedding gift to Harmonia, daughter of Ares and Aphrodite, on her marriage to Cadmus, founder of Thebes. Because Hephaestus had discovered Aphrodite's infidelity with Ares, he is said to have cursed the gift so that it would blight Harmonia's descendants. In some accounts the necklace was a gift from Zeus or Europa instead.

The curse

The necklace passed down through the Theban royal line, bringing ruin: it was associated with the house of Oedipus and the doom of his sons. Its most famous episode concerns Eriphyle, wife of the seer Amphiaraus, who was bribed with the necklace by Polynices to persuade her husband to join the expedition of the Seven against Thebes — an expedition Amphiaraus foresaw would kill him. Eriphyle was later murdered by her own son Alcmaeon in revenge, and the cursed necklace continued to pass through bloodshed until it was finally dedicated at Delphi, where it was reportedly still displayed in the temple of Athena Pronaea in historical times.

In literature

The story is told or alluded to by Apollodorus, Hyginus, Statius (in the Thebaid) and other ancient authors. A gold necklace discovered in an Etruscan tomb and once exhibited as the "Necklace of Harmonia" is now regarded as a much later artefact.
