Leaning Tower of Pisa

LocationPiazza dei Miracoli, Pisa, Italy
Construction1173 – c. 1372
Height56.67 m (low side) to 57.86 m (high side)
LeanAbout 3.97° (after restoration)
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site

The Leaning Tower of Pisa (Italian: Torre pendente di Pisa) is the freestanding bell tower, or campanile, of Pisa Cathedral, famous worldwide for its unintended tilt. It stands in the Piazza dei Miracoli ("Square of Miracles") alongside the cathedral and baptistery.

Construction

Building began in 1173, but the tower started to lean after only a few storeys because its foundations, just three metres deep, were set on soft, unstable alluvial soil. Construction halted for nearly a century — long pauses that allowed the soil to settle and probably saved the tower from collapse — and successive builders tried to compensate by making one side taller than the other, giving the tower a slight banana-like curve. It was completed around 1372 with the addition of the bell chamber; it has seven bells.

Stabilisation

By 1990 the tilt had reached about 5.5 degrees and the tower was closed to the public. An international restoration project (1990–2001), led by engineers including Michele Jamiolkowski and John Burland, used soil extraction beneath the high side, temporary lead counterweights and steel cables to reduce the lean by about 45 cm, to roughly its 1838 inclination. Engineers estimate the tower should now remain stable for at least 200 years.
