Mount Erciyes (Turkish: Erciyes Dağı; ancient Greek: Argaeus) is a massive stratovolcano located about 25 km south of Kayseri in central Turkey. At 3,917 metres it is the highest mountain in central Anatolia and the fifth-highest in Turkey.

Geology and history

Erciyes is a large stratovolcano built up over roughly 900,000 years of eruptions, producing extensive lava flows and surrounding domes. In antiquity it was known as Mount Argaeus and appeared on the coinage of Cappadocia; the geographer Strabo reported that its summit, from which both the Black Sea and the Mediterranean were said to be visible, was never free of snow. Its last eruptions are thought to date to the early Holocene or possibly to Roman times.

Tourism

The Erciyes ski resort on the northern slopes is one of Turkey's principal winter sports centres, with dozens of kilometres of pistes served by modern lifts. The mountain is also a popular mountaineering objective in summer.
