Dionysius II (c. 397 BC – 343 BC), also called Dionysius the Younger, was a Greek politician who ruled Syracuse, Sicily from 367 BC to 357 BC and again from 346 BC to 343 BC. He was the son and successor of Dionysius I, the formidable tyrant who had made Syracuse the dominant Greek power in the western Mediterranean.

First reign

Unlike his father, Dionysius II was raised in luxury and lacked experience of command. His uncle Dion, a friend and admirer of Plato, hoped to shape the young tyrant into a philosopher-king, and Plato was persuaded to visit Syracuse twice (in 366 and 361 BC) to tutor him. The experiment failed amid court intrigue; Plato's visits, recounted in the Seventh Letter, ended in disappointment, and Dion was banished.

In 357 BC Dion returned with a small force and overthrew Dionysius, who fled to Locri in southern Italy, where he ruled as tyrant.

Second reign and death

A decade of turmoil followed in Syracuse, and in 346 BC Dionysius was able to return and regain power. His position remained precarious, and when the Corinthian general Timoleon arrived in Sicily, Dionysius surrendered the citadel of Ortygia in 343 BC and was allowed to retire to Corinth, where he lived in reduced circumstances until his death.
