The DeLorean (often called the DMC DeLorean) is a rear-engine, two-seat sports car manufactured and marketed by John DeLorean's DeLorean Motor Company for the American market from 1981 until 1983. It is remembered for its unpainted stainless-steel body panels and distinctive gull-wing doors, and immortalised as the time machine in the Back to the Future film trilogy.

History

John DeLorean, a former General Motors executive, founded the company in 1975. With more than £100 million of British government backing, a factory was built at Dunmurry outside Belfast, Northern Ireland. Production began in January 1981, but the car was underpowered, expensive (US$25,000 at launch), and arrived during a recession in the key US market. The company fell into receivership in 1982; DeLorean was arrested in an FBI sting over an alleged cocaine-trafficking deal intended to save the firm, and was acquitted in 1984. About 9,000 cars were built.

Design

Designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro, the car has a fibreglass underbody with brushed stainless-steel outer panels, a rear-mounted PRV V6 engine producing about 130 hp (US specification), and a five-speed manual or three-speed automatic transmission.

Cultural impact

Cast as Doc Brown's time machine in Back to the Future (1985), the DeLorean became one of the most recognisable cars in the world; surviving examples are prized by collectors, and a Texas-based successor company supplies parts and refurbished cars.
