Spoiler (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, a spoiler (sometimes called a lift spoiler or lift dumper) is a device which intentionally reduces the lift component of an airfoil in a controlled way. Most often, spoilers are plates on the top surface of a wing that can be extended upward into the airflow to spoil the smooth flow over the wing, creating a controlled stall and a large wake of turbulence behind the plate. By doing so, the spoiler creates drag as well as destroying lift.

Uses

Spoilers serve several functions on aircraft. Ground spoilers deploy fully on touchdown to dump lift, transferring the aircraft's weight onto its wheels so that wheel braking and (on some types) reverse thrust become effective, dramatically shortening the landing roll. Flight spoilers are used in flight to increase the rate of descent without increasing airspeed. Roll spoilers, or spoilerons, rise differentially on one wing to assist or replace ailerons in roll control, particularly on large airliners at high speed; they also counteract adverse yaw. Gliders use spoilers or airbrakes extensively to control their descent path on approach, since they have no engine thrust to modulate.

Design considerations

Spoiler panels are typically hinged at their forward edge and raised by hydraulic actuators. Because deployment disrupts airflow, poorly designed spoilers can cause buffeting of the tail surfaces; careful placement and perforated or slotted designs are used to manage the wake. On modern fly-by-wire airliners, multiple spoiler panels across each wing are scheduled automatically by the flight control computers for roll, speedbrake and ground functions simultaneously.
