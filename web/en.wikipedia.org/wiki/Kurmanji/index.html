Kurmanji

Kurmanji (Kurdish: کورمانجی / Kurmancî), also termed Northern Kurdish, is the northernmost of the Kurdish languages, spoken predominantly in southeastern Turkey, northwestern and northeastern Iran, northern Iraq, northern Syria, and parts of the Caucasus and Khorasan. It is the most widely spoken Kurdish variety, with estimates of 14–16 million speakers, and is written mainly in a Latin-based alphabet in Turkey and Syria and in Perso-Arabic script in Iraq and Iran.

Geographic distribution

Kurmanji forms a dialect continuum across "Northern Kurdistan": the Turkish provinces of the southeast, the Syrian Jazira and Afrin regions, the Bahdinan area of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq (Dohuk and northern Erbil), and the West Azerbaijan and North Khorasan provinces of Iran, with additional enclaves in Armenia, Georgia and Turkmenistan dating from deportations and earlier settlement. A large diaspora speaks it in Western Europe, especially Germany.

Grammar

Like other Kurdish varieties, Kurmanji retains grammatical gender (masculine/feminine) and a case system with an oblique case, and it marks past transitive constructions with split ergativity. Nouns are linked by the ezafe construction. The vocabulary shows strong influence from Turkish and Arabic alongside its Iranian core.

Writing and literature

The classical Kurdish literary tradition was largely written in Kurmanji, including Ehmedê Xanî's epic Mem û Zîn (1692) and the poetry of Melayê Cizîrî. In the 1930s the brothers Celadet and Kamuran Bedir Khan developed the Latin-based Hawar alphabet in French-mandate Syria. Kurmanji publishing and broadcasting in Turkey were long restricted but expanded from the 1990s onward; Kurmanji is also the principal language of the Kurdish Wikipedia edition with the largest article count among Kurdish editions.
