Methanol (CH₃OH), also called wood alcohol or methyl alcohol, is the simplest alcohol: a colourless, flammable liquid with a mild odour, once obtained by destructive distillation of wood and now made on a huge scale from synthesis gas (CO + H₂) over catalysts.

Methanol is a major industrial feedstock (formaldehyde, acetic acid, MTBE, biodiesel) and an emerging fuel and hydrogen carrier. It is dangerously toxic: the body converts it to formaldehyde and formic acid, which can blind or kill — the basis of tragedies from adulterated spirits; ethanol is a competitive antidote.

[Image: A bottle of methanol and a methanol fuel flame, pale blue and hard to see]

Properties

Boiling point 64.7 °C; miscible with water; burns with a nearly invisible pale flame, a hazard in racing-fuel fires. 'Methylated spirits' is ethanol deliberately contaminated with methanol and dyes to deter drinking.

Future fuel

Green methanol — from captured CO₂ and renewable hydrogen or from biomass — is being adopted in shipping (large container vessels ordered from 2021) as a lower-carbon fuel.
