Mollweide's formulas, also known as Mollweide's equations, are a pair of relationships between the sides and angles of a triangle in trigonometry. Named after the German mathematician and astronomer Karl Mollweide (1774–1825), they state that for a triangle with sides a, b, c and opposite angles A, B, C: (a + b)/c = cos((A − B)/2) / sin(C/2), and (a − b)/c = sin((A − B)/2) / cos(C/2).

Use as a check

Because Mollweide's formulas involve all three sides and all three angles of a triangle, they are traditionally used as a check in the solution of oblique triangles: a proposed solution that fails to satisfy the equations must contain an error. The formulas can be derived from the law of sines and the sum-to-product identities.

They are analogous to the law of tangents and to Newton's formulas, and stand alongside the laws of sines and cosines as classical tools of plane trigonometry.
