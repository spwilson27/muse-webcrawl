Hartmannswillerkopf

Hartmannswillerkopf, also known as Vieil Armand in French, is a pyramidal rocky peak in the Vosges mountains of north-eastern France, rising to 956 metres (3,136 ft) above the Alsatian plain. During the First World War it was one of the most bitterly contested positions on the Western Front, changing hands repeatedly between French and German forces.

Battlefield

The summit overlooked the Rhine plain and the transport routes around Mulhouse and Cernay, making it strategically valuable to both sides. Fighting began in late 1914 and intensified through 1915, when the peak changed hands several times amid artillery bombardments, gas attacks and fighting in an elaborate network of trenches and tunnels cut into the rock. An estimated 30,000 French and German soldiers died on the mountain, which French troops nicknamed the "man-eater" (mangeuse d'hommes).

Memorial

After the war the battlefield was preserved as a national memorial. The site includes a large necropolis, a crypt and altar of the fatherland inaugurated in 1932, and extensive remains of trenches and fortifications. In 2017 a Franco-German history museum, the Historial, was opened at the site by the presidents of France and Germany as a symbol of reconciliation.
