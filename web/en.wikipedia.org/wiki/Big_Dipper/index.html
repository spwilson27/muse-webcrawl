The Big Dipper (US, Canada) or the Plough (UK, Ireland) is a prominent asterism in the northern sky, consisting of the seven brightest stars of the constellation Ursa Major. Its ladle shape—four stars forming a bowl and three forming a handle—makes it one of the most recognizable patterns in the night sky and a useful finding tool for other stars.

[Image: The seven bright stars of the Big Dipper asterism in Ursa Major]

Stars

The bowl is formed by Dubhe (α), Merak (β), Phecda (γ), and Megrez (δ); the handle by Alioth (ε), Mizar (ζ), and Alkaid (η). Mizar has a famous companion, Alcor, long used as a test of eyesight. Five of the seven stars belong to the Ursa Major Moving Group, sharing a common origin and motion through space. Dubhe and Merak, the "Pointers," indicate the direction of Polaris, the North Star.

Mythology and culture

Cultures worldwide have interpreted the pattern: as a bear followed by hunters in Greek and many Native American traditions, as a plough in Britain, as the Seven Sages (Saptarishi) in Hindu astronomy, and as a dipper in China associated with the imperial bureaucracy. Enslaved people in the United States knew it as the "Drinking Gourd," a marker pointing north toward freedom on the Underground Railroad.
