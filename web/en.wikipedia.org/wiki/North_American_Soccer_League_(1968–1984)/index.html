The North American Soccer League (NASL) was the top-level major professional soccer league in the United States and Canada that operated from 1968 to 1984. It is best known for attracting international stars such as Pelé, Franz Beckenbauer, Johan Cruyff and George Best in the 1970s, and for briefly making soccer a major spectator sport in the United States before collapsing under expansion and overspending.

History

The NASL formed in 1968 from the merger of two rival leagues, the United Soccer Association and the National Professional Soccer League, both launched in 1967. The early league struggled with low attendance and nearly folded in 1969, shrinking to five teams. It survived under commissioner Phil Woosnam and grew steadily through the early 1970s.

The turning point came in 1975, when the New York Cosmos signed Brazilian legend Pelé, then the most famous footballer in the world. Pelé's arrival drew huge crowds — over 77,000 saw his farewell match in 1977 — and television interest followed. Other clubs signed aging world stars: Beckenbauer and Carlos Alberto (Cosmos), Cruyff (Los Angeles Aztecs, Washington Diplomats), Best (Los Angeles, Fort Lauderdale, San Jose), Eusébio, Bobby Moore, Gordon Banks and Johan Neeskens.

Rules and character

To appeal to American audiences, the NASL adopted distinctive rules, including a 35-yard offside line, a shootout to decide drawn matches, and a points system awarding bonus points for goals scored. Critics argued the gimmicks alienated purists, while supporters credited them with entertaining, high-scoring games.

Decline and collapse

Rapid expansion to 24 teams, inflated salaries chasing the Cosmos' model, weak television revenue and the recession of the early 1980s doomed the league. Teams folded in waves; the Cosmos themselves withdrew after 1984 to play exhibition matches, and the NASL suspended operations in 1985 with only a handful of clubs remaining. Its indoor offshoot survived briefly as the MISL absorbed several clubs.

Legacy

The NASL is credited with popularizing soccer in North America, inspiring a generation of American players and coaches, and laying the cultural groundwork for the 1994 FIFA World Cup in the United States and the founding of Major League Soccer in 1996. A new league using the NASL name operated at the second-division level from 2011 to 2017.
