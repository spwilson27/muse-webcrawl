High-occupancy toll lane

A high-occupancy toll lane (HOT lane) is a type of traffic lane or roadway available to high-occupancy vehicles and other exempt vehicles without charge; other vehicles are required to pay a toll that varies by demand, a form of congestion pricing. Unlike a normal toll lane, which charges a fixed fee, HOT lane pricing is adjusted — often dynamically — to keep traffic in the lane flowing freely.

Operation

Carpools, buses and motorcycles typically travel free or at a discount after declaring their occupancy, often via a switchable transponder. Solo drivers may buy their way into the spare capacity of what would otherwise be a high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lane. The first HOT lanes in the United States opened on State Route 91 in Orange County, California, in 1995, followed by Interstate 15 in San Diego and facilities in Houston, Minneapolis, Denver, Atlanta, the Washington, D.C., region and elsewhere.

Criticism

Critics dub HOT lanes "Lexus lanes", arguing they let wealthy drivers buy faster travel; supporters respond that they give all drivers an option, fund transit, and use existing HOV capacity that would otherwise go unused.
