Curtis LeMay


General Curtis E. LeMay, U.S. Air Force Chief of Staff

Curtis Emerson LeMay (November 15, 1906 – October 1, 1990) was a US Air Force general who implemented an effective but controversial strategic bombing campaign in the Pacific theater of World War II. He later served as Chief of Staff of the United States Air Force from 1961 to 1965 and was the vice-presidential running mate of George Wallace in the 1968 presidential election.

World War II

LeMay first commanded B-17 groups of the Eighth Air Force over Europe, where he developed formation and bombing tactics and personally led difficult missions, including the Regensburg raid of 1943. Transferred to the Pacific in 1944, he took command of XXI Bomber Command in the Marianas in January 1945. Judging high-altitude precision bombing of Japan ineffective, he switched the B-29s to low-altitude night incendiary attacks; the firebombing of Tokyo on the night of 9–10 March 1945 killed on the order of 100,000 people and was followed by fire raids on dozens of Japanese cities, and by aerial mining of Japanese harbours (Operation Starvation).

Cold War

After the war LeMay organised the Berlin Airlift's American airlift task force, then built the Strategic Air Command (1948–1957) into the core of America's nuclear deterrent, instituting rigorous training, standardisation, and airborne alert. As Air Force Chief of Staff under Presidents Kennedy and Johnson, he clashed with civilian leaders over strategy during the Cuban Missile Crisis and the early Vietnam War. His advocacy of aggressive bombing and remarks such as the "bomb them back into the Stone Age" line (which he disavowed) made him a symbol of Cold War hard-line thinking and a model for characters in Dr. Strangelove.
