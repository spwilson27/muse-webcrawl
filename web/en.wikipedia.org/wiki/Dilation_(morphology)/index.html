Dilation (morphology)

Dilation (usually represented by ⊕) is one of the basic operations in mathematical morphology. Originally developed for binary images, it has been expanded first to grayscale images and then to complete lattices. The dilation operation usually uses a small shape or template called a structuring element to probe and expand the shapes contained in the input image.

Binary dilation

The dilation of a set A by a structuring element B is the set of all points z such that B, when translated to z, overlaps A in at least one point. Equivalently, it is the Minkowski sum of A with the reflected structuring element. Intuitively, dilation grows or thickens objects: holes and gaps smaller than the structuring element are filled, and sharp corners are rounded.

Grayscale and applications

For grayscale images, dilation acts as a local maximum filter defined by the structuring element, brightening the image and expanding bright regions. Dilation is dual to erosion under complementation, and combinations of the two define the opening and closing operations used for noise removal, shape analysis, feature extraction and image segmentation.
