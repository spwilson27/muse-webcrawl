Black-necked grebe

Scientific namePodiceps nigricollis
FamilyPodicipedidae (grebes)
RangeEurope, Asia, Africa, the Americas
ConservationLeast Concern (IUCN)

The black-necked grebe (Podiceps nigricollis), known in North America as the eared grebe, is a member of the grebe family of water birds. It is one of the most abundant grebes in the world, breeding on freshwater lakes across temperate regions of Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas.

Description

In breeding plumage the bird is unmistakable: black head and neck, a fan of golden feathers behind the red eye, and dark upperparts. In winter it becomes much plainer, grey-brown and white, resembling other small grebes. It builds a floating nest of vegetation anchored to reeds at the water's edge.

Behaviour

Black-necked grebes are highly gregarious outside the breeding season, gathering in huge flocks on saline lakes such as Great Salt Lake and Mono Lake in North America, where they feed on brine shrimp. They undergo a simultaneous moult there, becoming flightless for a period while doubling in weight before migration. They feed mainly on aquatic insects and small crustaceans, taken in short dives.
