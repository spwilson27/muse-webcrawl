Karl Ernst Adolf Anderssen (6 July 1818 – 13 March 1879) was a German chess master, generally regarded as the world's leading chess player from 1851 to 1858 and again from 1860 to 1866. He is famous for his brilliant sacrificial attacking play, exemplified by the "Immortal Game" (1851) and the "Evergreen Game" (1852).

Career

Anderssen, a mathematics teacher in Breslau (now Wrocław), won the first international chess tournament, held in London in 1851, defeating leading players of the day. He lost a famous match to the American Paul Morphy in Paris in 1858 but regained dominance after Morphy's retirement, winning the London 1862 tournament. In 1866 he lost a close match to Wilhelm Steinitz, whose more positional approach heralded modern chess.

Legacy

Anderssen's games epitomize the Romantic era of chess, prizing rapid development and daring king attacks. The opening Anderssen's Opening (1.a3) bears his name, and several of his combinations remain staples of chess literature.
