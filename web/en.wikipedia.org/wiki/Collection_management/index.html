Collection management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Collection management is the systematic care, development and oversight of the holdings of a museum, library, archive or similar institution. It covers the policies and procedures by which objects are acquired, documented, preserved, stored, lent, exhibited and, when necessary, removed from the collection (deaccessioned).

Scope

Collection management typically includes:

  • Collection development and acquisition — deciding what to collect, in line with a written collections policy, and acquiring items by purchase, donation, bequest, transfer or field collection.
  • Documentation and registration — accessioning new items, assigning inventory numbers, and maintaining catalogue records describing each object and its provenance.
  • Collections care — preventive conservation, environmental monitoring, integrated pest management, handling and storage standards intended to slow deterioration.
  • Loans and access — administering incoming and outgoing loans, insurance and transport, and making the collection available for exhibition, research and education.
  • Deaccessioning and disposal — the formal removal of items, governed by ethical codes that usually restrict the use of any proceeds to new acquisitions or direct collection care.

Standards and ethics

Professional bodies such as the International Council of Museums (ICOM), the American Alliance of Museums and national museum associations publish codes of ethics and standards for collection management. Key ethical concerns include the provenance of acquisitions (particularly antiquities and material removed during colonial periods), repatriation claims, and transparency in deaccessioning.

Digital tools

Institutions increasingly manage their holdings with collections management systems — databases that track object records, locations, condition reports, images and loan histories — and publish selected records through online collection portals.
