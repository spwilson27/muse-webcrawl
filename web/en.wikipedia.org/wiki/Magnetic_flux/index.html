Magnetic flux

SymbolΦB
SI unitWeber (Wb)
DefinitionSurface integral of the magnetic field B over an area
Related lawFaraday's law of induction

Magnetic flux is a measure of the total magnetic field passing through a given surface. Formally it is the surface integral of the normal component of the magnetic field B over that surface, written ΦB = ∫ B · dA. For a uniform field perpendicular to a flat area A, it reduces to the product B·A. The SI unit is the weber (Wb), equal to one tesla times one square metre; the older cgs unit is the maxwell.

Physical significance

Magnetic flux is central to electromagnetism because its change produces electric effects. Faraday's law of induction states that the electromotive force induced around a closed loop equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop — the principle behind electric generators, transformers and induction motors. Conversely, Gauss's law for magnetism states that the total magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero, expressing the absence of magnetic monopoles: field lines have no beginnings or ends, so as many enter a closed volume as leave it.

Flux linkage and quantisation

In a coil of N turns, the relevant quantity is the flux linkage, N times the flux through one turn, which together with current defines inductance. In superconductors, magnetic flux through a ring is quantised in units of the flux quantum Φ₀ = h/2e ≈ 2.07 × 10⁻¹⁵ Wb — a macroscopic quantum effect exploited in SQUID magnetometers, the most sensitive magnetic sensors known.
