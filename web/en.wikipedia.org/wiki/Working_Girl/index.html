Working Girl

Working Girl is a 1988 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Mike Nichols and written by Kevin Wade. It stars Melanie Griffith as Tess McGill, an ambitious Staten Island secretary with a head for business, Harrison Ford as mergers specialist Jack Trainer, and Sigourney Weaver as Tess's polished, two-faced boss Katharine Parker, who steals Tess's idea for a merger. The film follows Tess as she seizes her chance — while Katharine is away with a skiing injury — to pass herself off as an executive and put the deal together herself.

Plot

Tess McGill, a secretary taking night classes in business, is given a new post assisting Katharine Parker, a young mergers-and-acquisitions executive. When Tess proposes a plan for their client Trask Industries to acquire a radio network instead of a television company, Katharine pretends to champion it while intending to claim it as her own. After Katharine breaks her leg skiing in Europe, Tess discovers the deception and, with encouragement from her friend Cynthia (Joan Cusack), impersonates an executive: she cuts her hair, borrows Katharine's wardrobe, and pitches the deal to Jack Trainer — with whom she also falls in love, unaware he is Katharine's boyfriend. The scheme nearly collapses when Katharine returns, but at the Trask wedding Tess proves the idea was hers — details only its originator could know — and is hired into an executive post, while Katharine is dismissed.

Reception and awards

The film was a commercial and critical success, grossing about $103 million against a modest budget, and is remembered as a defining portrait of 1980s corporate ambition, shoulder pads and big hair — and of the class divide between Staten Island and Manhattan, dramatised by Tess's daily Staten Island Ferry commute. It received six Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actress (Griffith) and two for Best Supporting Actress (Weaver and Cusack); Carly Simon's theme song "Let the River Run" won the Oscar for Best Original Song, as well as the Golden Globe and Grammy.
