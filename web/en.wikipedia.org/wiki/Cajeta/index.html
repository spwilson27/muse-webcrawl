Cajeta

Cajeta is a Mexican confection of thickened, caramelized goat's milk, closely related to the broader Latin American dulce de leche but distinguished by its traditional use of goat's milk and its origin in the Bajío region, particularly the city of Celaya, Guanajuato. Its name comes from the small wooden boxes (cajetes) in which it was originally sold.

Preparation and varieties

Cajeta is made by simmering goat's milk with sugar and a little baking soda for hours until it reduces to a deep golden, pourable caramel with a slightly tangy depth from the goat's milk. Varieties include cajeta quemada ("burnt", darkest), cajeta envinada (with a touch of wine or alcohol), and vanilla-flavoured versions. It is eaten as a dessert on its own, spread on bread, drizzled over ice cream and crepes, or used in candies such as the oblea sandwiches called morelianas.

Cultural notes

Celaya's cajeta is so emblematic that during the Mexican Revolution it was issued to troops as a durable ration, and the city's name is popularly linked to the sweet. In 2010 cajeta was named among the Bicentennial flavours celebrating 200 years of Mexican independence.
