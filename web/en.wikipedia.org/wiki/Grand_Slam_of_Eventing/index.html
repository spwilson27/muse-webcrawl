Grand Slam of Eventing

[Image placeholder: Cross-country phase at Badminton Horse Trials]

The Grand Slam of Eventing, sponsored by Rolex, is a championship in the equestrian sport of eventing awarded to a rider who consecutively wins the three five-star (CCI5*-L) three-day events regarded as the world's most prestigious: the Kentucky Three-Day Event (United States), the Badminton Horse Trials (England) and the Burghley Horse Trials (England). A substantial cash prize is offered to any rider who completes the sweep, in any order, with the three wins held at the same time.

History

Rolex established the Grand Slam prize in 1998, putting up a bonus (eventually US$350,000) for the rider who could hold all three titles simultaneously. Because the events fall in spring (Kentucky, then Badminton) and autumn (Burghley), a campaign typically spans two calendar years.

Winners

Only two riders have won the Grand Slam. Pippa Funnell of Great Britain became the first in 2003, winning Kentucky on Primmore's Pride, Badminton on Supreme Rock and Burghley on Primmore's Pride. Germany's Michael Jung completed the second sweep in 2016, having won Burghley in 2015 and Kentucky and Badminton in 2016, riding La Biosthetique Sam FBW and FischerRocana FST. Several riders, including William Fox-Pitt and Andrew Nicholson, came within one event of the prize.

The three events

  • Kentucky Three-Day Event — held at the Kentucky Horse Park, Lexington, in late April.
  • Badminton Horse Trials — held on the Badminton Estate, Gloucestershire, in early May.
  • Burghley Horse Trials — held at Burghley House, Lincolnshire, in early September.
