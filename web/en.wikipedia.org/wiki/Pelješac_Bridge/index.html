Pelješac Bridge

LocationDubrovnik-Neretva County, Croatia
Total length2,404 m (7,887 ft)
TypeCable-stayed bridge

The Pelješac Bridge (Croatian: Pelješki most) is a cable-stayed bridge in Dubrovnik-Neretva County, Croatia. The bridge provides a fixed link from the southeastern part of the Croatian mainland near Komarna, across the Mali Ston Bay to the Pelješac peninsula near Brijesta, thereby connecting the southern exclave of Dubrovnik with the rest of the country without crossing the Neum corridor of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Background

After Croatian independence, traffic between Dubrovnik and the rest of Croatia had to pass twice through Bosnia and Herzegovina's short coastline at Neum, with border checks. Plans for a bridge were politically contentious — Bosnia and Herzegovina raised concerns about access to the open sea for the port of Neum — and a first construction start in the 2000s was abandoned.

Construction

The project was revived with European Union funding covering most of the cost (about €357 million of a total project value of roughly €526 million including access roads). The contract was awarded in 2018 to the China Road and Bridge Corporation, a choice that attracted debate within the EU. The 2,404-metre multi-span cable-stayed bridge, designed by Marjan Pipenbaher, has a main span arrangement allowing tall ships to pass toward Neum.

Opening

The bridge opened to traffic on 26 July 2022, shortly before Croatia joined the Schengen Area, ending the exclave's reliance on transit through Bosnia and Herzegovina. Access roads, including tunnels and the Ston bypass, completed the route in the following months.
