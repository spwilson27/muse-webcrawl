The Deccan Plateau is a vast plateau in peninsular India, bounded by the Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats and Satpura ranges, covering much of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.

Geology

It is largely formed of basalt from the Deccan Traps, enormous volcanic eruptions around 66 million years ago near the Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary, associated with the mass extinction of non-avian dinosaurs.

Climate and rivers

The Western Ghats cast a rain shadow, making much of the plateau semi-arid. East-flowing rivers Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri cross it to the Bay of Bengal. Its black regur soils suit cotton cultivation.
