Active1909–1921 (partnership)

Purcell and Elmslie was an American architectural partnership of William Gray Purcell and George Grant Elmslie, one of the most productive firms of the Prairie School movement. Elmslie had been Louis Sullivan's chief draughtsman, and the firm's work carried Sullivan's ornamental approach into houses, banks and public buildings across the Midwest.

Works

Noted works include the Purcell–Cutts House (1913) in Minneapolis, a masterpiece of progressive domestic design, and a series of small Midwestern bank buildings and courthouses rich in terra-cotta ornament, art glass and integrated furniture. George Feick Jr. was an earlier partner (Purcell, Feick & Elmslie).

Legacy

The partnership dissolved in 1921, but its extensive archives preserved at the University of Minnesota make it one of the best-documented Prairie School practices.
