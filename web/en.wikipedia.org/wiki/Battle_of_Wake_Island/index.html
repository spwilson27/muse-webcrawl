Battle of Wake Island

The Battle of Wake Island was fought from 8 to 23 December 1941 between United States and Imperial Japanese forces for the Wake Island atoll in the North Pacific, in the opening days of the Pacific War. The small American garrison repulsed a first Japanese landing attempt — one of the few bright spots for the United States in December 1941 — before being overwhelmed by a second, much stronger assault.

Background

Wake was defended by about 450 U.S. Marines of the 1st Defense Battalion, a detachment of Marine Fighting Squadron VMF-211 with twelve F4F Wildcat fighters, naval personnel, and some 1,100 civilian contractors building an airbase. Japanese air raids began on 8 December, destroying most of the Wildcats on the ground.

First assault repulsed

On 11 December a Japanese invasion force under Rear Admiral Kajioka attempted to land. Marine shore batteries held their fire until the ships were close, then sank the destroyer Hayate; the surviving Wildcats sank the destroyer Kisaragi. Kajioka withdrew — the first Japanese surface force of the war to be beaten off.

Second assault and surrender

The Japanese returned on 23 December reinforced by two aircraft carriers (Sōryū and Hiryū, diverted from the Pearl Harbor strike force) and 1,500 naval infantry. A U.S. relief expedition was recalled before reaching the island. After heavy fighting ashore, Commander Cunningham ordered the surrender. The defenders' resistance became a rallying symbol in the United States — "Wake Island" entered American memory alongside the (probably apocryphal) message "Send us more Japs". Most of the prisoners survived the war, though 98 civilian contractors kept on the island were executed by the Japanese garrison in October 1943. Japanese forces held Wake until their surrender in September 1945.
