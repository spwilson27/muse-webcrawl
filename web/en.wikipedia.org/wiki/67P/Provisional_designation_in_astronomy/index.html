Provisional designation in astronomy

Provisional designations are naming conventions applied to astronomical objects immediately following their discovery, before the object receives a permanent name or catalog number. Different schemes are used for minor planets, comets, planetary satellites, and exoplanets, and are administered largely by the Minor Planet Center and the International Astronomical Union.

Minor planets

The modern minor-planet provisional designation combines the year of discovery, a letter indicating the half-month of discovery, and a second letter plus subscript number cycling through discoveries within that half-month. For example, the designation 2015 TG387 encodes discovery in the first half of October 2015. Once an orbit is well determined, the object receives a permanent number, such as (433) Eros.

The system was adopted in 1925, replacing a variety of older schemes; designations from earlier eras have been converted to the modern format.

Comets

Comet provisional designations begin with a prefix describing the comet's nature — P/ for periodic, C/ for non-periodic, D/ for lost or disintegrated, X/ for uncertain orbits, and I/ for interstellar objects — followed by the discovery year, a half-month letter, and a sequence number. The interstellar visitor 1I/ʻOumuamua and comet 2I/Borisov were named under this scheme.

Satellites

Natural satellites receive designations such as S/2003 J 2, giving the year, the parent planet's initial, and a discovery sequence number, until the IAU assigns a name and Roman numeral.
