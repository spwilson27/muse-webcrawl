67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko is a Jupiter-family comet with an orbital period of 6.44 years. It was discovered in 1969 by Klim Ivanovich Churyumov and Svetlana Ivanovna Gerasimenko in the Soviet Union. It became famous as the target of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, which rendezvoused with the comet in August 2014 and deployed the Philae lander on its surface that November — the first soft landing on a comet nucleus.

Shape and surface

Rosetta revealed a distinctive two-lobed "rubber duck" shape, likely formed by the gentle merger of two separate bodies. The larger lobe measures roughly 4.1 × 3.3 × 1.8 km and the smaller about 2.6 × 2.3 × 1.8 km, joined by a narrow neck. The nucleus has a very low density (about 0.53 g/cm³) and high porosity, and its surface is dominated by dust-covered terrain, pits, cliffs, and smooth plains, with water-ice exposed in patches. As the comet approached perihelion in August 2015, jets of water vapor, carbon dioxide, and other gases erupted from its surface.

Rosetta mission findings

Instruments aboard Rosetta measured the comet's water and found its deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio to be much higher than that of Earth's oceans — evidence that Jupiter-family comets like 67P were probably not the main source of Earth's water. In 2016 researchers announced the detection of the amino acid glycine and phosphorus in the comet's coma, key ingredients for life. The mission ended on September 30, 2016, when the orbiter was deliberately set down on the comet.
