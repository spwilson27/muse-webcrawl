Philae (spacecraft)

Artist's impression of Philae on the nucleus of comet 67P.

Philae is a robotic lander built by a European consortium led by the German Aerospace Center (DLR) for the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission. On 12 November 2014 it became the first spacecraft to make a soft landing on the nucleus of a comet, 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko.

Landing

Philae separated from Rosetta and descended over seven hours toward the landing site Agilkia. Its anchoring harpoons failed to fire and the lander bounced twice, drifting for about two hours before coming to rest roughly a kilometer away in a shadowed spot later named Abydos, tilted against a cliff. In about 64 hours on its primary battery it nevertheless completed nearly all of its planned first science sequence, with instruments including the CIVA cameras, the SD2 drill, the COSAC and Ptolemy analysers, and the CONSERT radar sounding experiment conducted jointly with the orbiter.

Aftermath

Deprived of sunlight, Philae fell silent on 15 November 2014. It briefly re-established contact in June and July 2015 as the comet neared perihelion, but remained quiet thereafter. On 2 September 2016, less than a month before Rosetta's own end of mission, the orbiter's camera finally imaged Philae wedged in a crevice, confirming its resting place. The lander is named after the island of Philae in the Nile, where an obelisk bearing a bilingual inscription helped unlock the Rosetta Stone's decipherment of hieroglyphs.
