Galileo is a global navigation satellite system (GNSS) created by the European Union through the European Space Agency (ESA) and operated by the European Union Agency for the Space Programme (EUSPA). It is Europe's independent, civilian-controlled alternative to the United States' GPS, Russia's GLONASS and China's BeiDou. Galileo began offering Initial Services on 15 December 2016, becoming the third global navigation system in operation.

History

The programme was approved in the early 2000s, after Europe concluded that relying on GPS — a military system that could be degraded or denied in a crisis — posed strategic risks. Two experimental satellites, GIOVE-A (2005) and GIOVE-B (2008), secured Galileo's allocated frequencies and tested its atomic clocks. Four in-orbit validation satellites followed in 2011–2012, and full operational capability satellites have been launched since 2014, principally by Soyuz and Ariane rockets from Kourou, with batches later also flown on Falcon 9. The system is named after the Italian astronomer Galileo Galilei.

Services

Galileo provides several free and specialised services. The Open Service gives free positioning accurate to about one metre for dual-frequency receivers — better than standard GPS in most conditions. The High Accuracy Service, declared operational in January 2023, provides free corrections delivering horizontal accuracy of about 20 centimetres. Other services include a Public Regulated Service for government users, a contribution to the international COSPAS-SARSAT search and rescue system that can acknowledge distress beacons, and an Open Service Navigation Message Authentication feature to protect against spoofing.

Constellation

The full constellation consists of 30 satellites — 24 operational plus spares — in three planes of medium Earth orbit at an altitude of 23,222 km, inclined at 56 degrees to the equator. Each satellite carries passive hydrogen maser and rubidium atomic clocks, among the most accurate ever flown, and the ground segment is anchored by control centres in Oberpfaffenhofen, Germany, and Fucino, Italy, with a worldwide network of sensor stations.
