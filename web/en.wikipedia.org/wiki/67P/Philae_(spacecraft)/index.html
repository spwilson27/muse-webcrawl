Philae is a robotic European Space Agency lander that accompanied the Rosetta spacecraft to comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko and, on 12 November 2014, became the first spacecraft to make a soft landing on a comet nucleus. It was named after the island of Philae in the Nile, where an obelisk used in deciphering Egyptian hieroglyphs was found.

Landing

After separating from Rosetta, Philae descended for about seven hours toward the landing site Agilkia. Its landing harpoons and ice screws failed to secure it, and the lander bounced twice — spending nearly two hours in flight after the first bounce — before coming to rest in a shadowed spot dubbed Abydos, tilted against a cliff. Despite the unplanned landing, it carried out most of its planned science program on battery power before entering hibernation on 15 November 2014.

Reawakening and final images

As the comet approached the Sun, Philae briefly re-established contact in June and July 2015, but communications could not be sustained. On September 2, 2016, Rosetta's cameras finally located the lander wedged in a dark crevice, resolving its position. The orbiter itself was crash-landed on the comet on September 30, 2016, ending the mission. Philae's instruments detected organic compounds on the surface, including the first discovery of an oxygen-rich environment with molecular oxygen in the coma by Rosetta, and provided close-up images of the comet's fractured, primordial terrain.
