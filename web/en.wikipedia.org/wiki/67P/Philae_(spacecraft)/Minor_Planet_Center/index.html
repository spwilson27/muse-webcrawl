The Minor Planet Center (MPC) is the official body for observing and reporting on minor planets under the auspices of the International Astronomical Union (IAU). Founded in 1947, it operates at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory in Cambridge, Massachusetts, as part of the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian, and is largely funded by a grant from NASA's Near-Earth Object Observations Program.

Functions

The MPC collects, computes, checks and disseminates astrometric observations and orbits of asteroids and comets submitted by observatories and amateur astronomers worldwide. It publishes its results in the Minor Planet Circulars, the Minor Planet Electronic Circulars (MPECs) — which announce discoveries of near-Earth objects, comets and unusual minor planets — and periodic supplements. It maintains the definitive catalogues of minor planet orbits, assigns provisional designations to newly discovered objects, and coordinates the numbering and naming of minor planets once their orbits are secure.

Notable episodes handled through the MPC include the announcement of the first interstellar object, ʻOumuamua, via MPEC 2017-U181, and of the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov in 2019. The Center also runs the Near-Earth Object Confirmation Page, which lets observers follow up candidate fast-moving objects — such as newly spotted comets like 67P follow-up observations — before they are lost.

History

The MPC was established at Cincinnati Observatory in 1947 under Paul Herget, consolidating minor planet work that had been centred before the Second World War at the Astronomisches Rechen-Institut in Germany. It moved to the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory in 1978 under director Brian G. Marsden, who led it until 2006, followed by Timothy Spahr and later Matthew Holman. The Center's databases now hold well over a million designated minor planets and hundreds of millions of individual observations.
