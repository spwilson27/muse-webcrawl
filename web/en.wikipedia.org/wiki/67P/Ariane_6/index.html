Ariane 6 is a European expendable launch system developed and operated by Arianespace for the European Space Agency (ESA). The rocket, built by ArianeGroup, replaced the Ariane 5 as Europe's heavy-lift launcher and is intended to provide Europe with autonomous access to space at roughly half the recurring cost of its predecessor. It flies from the purpose-built ELA-4 launch complex at the Guiana Space Centre near Kourou, French Guiana.

Design

Ariane 6 comes in two variants sharing a common core: the Ariane 62, with two P120C solid rocket boosters, and the Ariane 64, with four. The central core stage (LLPM) is powered by a Vulcain 2.1 engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen, derived from the Ariane 5's Vulcain 2. The upper stage (ULPM) uses the restartable Vinci engine, also cryogenic, whose multiple-restart capability allows deployment of payloads into several different orbits on a single mission — a key requirement for satellite constellations such as Galileo. The P120C boosters are shared with the Vega C light launcher.

Development and flights

ESA member states approved Ariane 6 development in December 2014. The programme suffered several years of delay, and four ESA missions were moved to other launch providers during the gap between Ariane 5's retirement in July 2023 and Ariane 6's debut. The inaugural flight took place on 9 July 2024, carrying a mass simulator and a set of small satellites and experiments; the main objectives were achieved, although the upper stage's auxiliary power unit anomaly prevented a planned final deorbit burn. The first commercial flight followed in March 2025, launching the French CSO-3 reconnaissance satellite.
