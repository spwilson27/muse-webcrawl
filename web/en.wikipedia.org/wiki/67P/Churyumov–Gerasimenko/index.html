67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko

67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko (often abbreviated 67P/C–G, or simply "Chury") is a Jupiter-family comet with an orbital period of about 6.45 years. It was discovered in 1969 by Soviet astronomers Klim Churyumov and Svetlana Gerasimenko. The comet is the destination of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, which rendezvoused with it in August 2014 and deployed the Philae lander on its surface on 12 November 2014 — the first soft landing on a cometary nucleus.

Shape and structure

Rosetta revealed a strikingly bilobed nucleus, frequently compared to a rubber duck, consisting of two lobes joined by a narrow neck. The shape is thought to be the result of a gentle merger of two separate bodies, or of severe erosion of an originally single body. The comet's density is only about 0.53 g/cm³, implying a highly porous interior, and its surface is covered with dust, pits, cliffs and smooth plains of fallback material.

Rosetta mission

Rosetta was originally intended to visit comet 46P/Wirtanen, but a launch delay led to retargeting to 67P. After a ten-year journey including a hibernation period, Rosetta entered orbit around the comet in September 2014. The Philae landing did not go as planned — the lander bounced twice before settling in the shadow of a cliff — but it returned about 60 hours of data before its batteries ran down. Philae was finally located in images in September 2016. Rosetta's own mission ended on 30 September 2016 with a controlled impact onto the nucleus.

Among Rosetta's findings were that the comet's water has a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio quite different from Earth's oceans — arguing against Jupiter-family comets as the main source of Earth's water — and the detection of molecular oxygen and of the amino acid glycine and phosphorus in the comet's coma.

Orbit

Before 1959, 67P had a much larger perihelion distance; a close encounter with Jupiter in that year reduced its perihelion to about 1.3 AU, bringing it into the inner Solar System and making it accessible to spacecraft.
