Jupiter-family comet

Jupiter-family comets move in flat orbits strongly controlled by Jupiter.

A Jupiter-family comet (JFC) is a short-period comet whose orbit is strongly influenced by Jupiter. By the usual definition, JFCs have orbital periods shorter than 20 years and Tisserand parameters with respect to Jupiter between 2 and 3, with low to moderate inclinations close to the plane of the planets.

Origin and dynamics

Jupiter-family comets are supplied from the Kuiper belt and its scattered disc, bodies that leak inward through the centaur population until Jupiter captures them into short-period orbits. Their dynamical lifetimes are short — typically a few hundred thousand years — before they are ejected from the Solar System, collide with the Sun or a planet, or become dormant. Close encounters with Jupiter continually reshape their orbits, so individual JFCs can shift their perihelia substantially between apparitions.

Examples and exploration

Well-known members include 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, studied in orbit by ESA's Rosetta; 81P/Wild, whose dust was returned to Earth by Stardust; 9P/Tempel, struck by Deep Impact; 103P/Hartley; and 73P/Schwassmann–Wachmann, observed breaking apart. Jupiter-family nuclei are typically a few kilometers wide, dark, and rubble-like, with low densities and surfaces depleted in volatiles compared with fresh Oort-cloud comets.
