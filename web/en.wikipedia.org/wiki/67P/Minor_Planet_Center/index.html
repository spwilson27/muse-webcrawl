Minor Planet Center

The Minor Planet Center operates from the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

The Minor Planet Center (MPC) is the worldwide clearinghouse for observations of minor planets, comets, and outer irregular satellites of the major planets. It operates at the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory in Cambridge, Massachusetts, under the auspices of the International Astronomical Union, and is principally funded by NASA's Near-Earth Object Observations Program.

Functions

The MPC collects astrometric observations submitted by professional and amateur observatories around the world, computes orbits, assigns provisional designations and permanent numbers, and disseminates the results — in particular through the Minor Planet Circulars, the daily Minor Planet Electronic Circulars announcing new discoveries (including urgent near-Earth objects), and public databases of orbital elements such as MPCORB. It also manages the naming of minor planets, vetting discoverers' proposals. For comets, the MPC works alongside the Central Bureau for Astronomical Telegrams, which announces discoveries and names.

History

Founded in 1947 at the Cincinnati Observatory under director Paul Herget, the Center moved to the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory in 1978, where it was led for decades by Brian G. Marsden and then Gareth V. Williams, with Matthew Holman becoming director in 2015. The volume of data has grown enormously in the survey era: the catalogues now hold orbits for well over a million minor planets and hundreds of millions of observations.
