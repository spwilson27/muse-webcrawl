The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA /ˈnæsə/) is an independent agency of the United States federal government responsible for the civilian space programme, as well as for aeronautics and space research. NASA was established in 1958, succeeding the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), in response to the Soviet Union's launch of Sputnik 1 and the ensuing Space Race.

Since its founding, NASA has led most of the United States' space exploration efforts, including the Mercury, Gemini and Apollo programmes — which landed the first humans on the Moon in 1969 — the Space Shuttle, and the American contribution to the International Space Station. Its robotic missions, from Voyager and Viking to the Hubble and James Webb space telescopes and the Mars rovers, have transformed humanity's knowledge of the Solar System and the universe.

History

President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the National Aeronautics and Space Act on 29 July 1958. Under its first administrator, T. Keith Glennan, NASA absorbed NACA's laboratories and the Army's rocket team led by Wernher von Braun. After Yuri Gagarin's flight in April 1961, President John F. Kennedy committed the United States to landing a man on the Moon before the end of the decade, a goal achieved by Apollo 11 on 20 July 1969. Subsequent decades saw the long-running Space Shuttle programme (1981–2011), permanently crewed low Earth orbit aboard the ISS from 2000 onward, and, in the 2010s and 2020s, the Artemis programme aiming to return astronauts to the lunar surface with commercial and international partners.

Facilities

NASA operates its headquarters in Washington, D.C., and ten major field centres, including Johnson Space Center in Houston (mission control and astronaut training), Kennedy Space Center in Florida (launch operations), the Jet Propulsion Laboratory in California (robotic missions), Marshall Space Flight Center in Alabama (propulsion), Goddard Space Flight Center in Maryland (science), and research centres at Ames, Glenn, Langley and Armstrong. Its work is overseen from the small-body astronomy community in part through data services such as the Minor Planet Center under the International Astronomical Union.

Programmes

NASA's portfolio spans human spaceflight (Artemis, Commercial Crew), planetary science (Mars rovers, Europa Clipper, Dragonfly), astrophysics (Webb, Hubble, Roman), heliophysics, Earth science (climate monitoring satellites), and aeronautics research (quiet supersonic and electric aircraft demonstrators). NASA partners with commercial companies such as SpaceX and Boeing to deliver cargo and crew to the ISS, and with ESA, JAXA, CSA and other agencies on joint missions — including support of ESA missions such as Rosetta to comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko.
