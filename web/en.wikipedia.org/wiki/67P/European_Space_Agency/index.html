The European Space Agency (ESA) is an intergovernmental organization of 23 member states dedicated to the exploration of space. Established in 1975, it has its headquarters in Paris, France, and major centres across Europe. ESA's fleet includes missions for Earth observation, planetary science, astronomy, human spaceflight, and satellite navigation and communications.

Rosetta and comet 67P

ESA is closely associated with comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko through its Rosetta mission. Launched in 2004, Rosetta travelled for ten years before rendezvousing with the comet in August 2014, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit a comet nucleus. In November 2014 its Philae lander touched down on the comet's surface, the first soft landing on a comet. The mission ended in September 2016 when the orbiter was deliberately set down on the comet. Rosetta's measurements reshaped understanding of cometary composition and the early Solar System.

Programmes

ESA operates the Copernicus Earth-observation programme in partnership with the European Union, the Galileo satellite navigation system, and the Ariane and Vega launchers flown from the Guiana Space Centre in French Guiana. Its astronauts fly to the International Space Station, and its science missions have included Gaia, Planck, Herschel, and the Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer (JUICE).
