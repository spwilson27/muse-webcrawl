Philae (spacecraft)

OperatorESA / DLR
Launched2 March 2004 (aboard Rosetta)
Landed12 November 2014, comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko
Massabout 100 kg

Philae is a robotic European Space Agency lander that accompanied the Rosetta spacecraft until it landed on comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko on 12 November 2014 — the first soft landing on a comet nucleus in history.

Landing

Released about 22 kilometres above the comet, Philae descended for seven hours. Its harpoons failed to fire, and the lander bounced twice over roughly two hours before coming to rest at a site later named Abydos, tilted against a cliff and largely in shadow. Despite the awkward position, Philae operated on battery power for about 57 hours and returned data from nearly all of its ten instruments.

Later contact and discovery

As the comet neared the Sun in mid-2015, Philae briefly re-established contact several times. In September 2016, less than a month before Rosetta's mission ended, the lander was finally located in images, wedged in a dark crack. Philae's measurements of surface hardness, organic molecules, and the comet's interior informed models of cometary structure and the early Solar System.
