67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko

Designation67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko
Orbital period6.45 years
Discovered1969, by Klim Churyumov and Svetlana Gerasimenko
DimensionsAbout 4.1 × 3.3 × 1.8 km (large lobe)

67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko is a Jupiter-family comet with an orbital period of 6.45 years, discovered in 1969 on photographic plates taken in Alma-Ata. It became the target of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, the first spacecraft to orbit a comet nucleus and to deploy a lander on its surface.

Rosetta mission

Launched in 2004, Rosetta rendezvoused with the comet in August 2014 after a ten-year journey. Its Philae lander touched down on 12 November 2014, bouncing before settling in a shadowed site. The orbiter accompanied the comet through perihelion in August 2015 and ended its mission with a controlled impact in September 2016.

Characteristics

The nucleus has a distinctive two-lobed "rubber duck" shape, probably formed by the gentle merger of two cometesimals. Rosetta found the comet's water to have a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio quite different from Earth's oceans, arguing against Jupiter-family comets as the main source of Earth's water, and detected molecular oxygen and numerous organic compounds.
