67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko (often abbreviated 67P/C–G or simply 67P) is a Jupiter-family comet with an orbital period of 6.45 years. It was discovered in 1969 by Klim Ivanovich Churyumov and Svetlana Ivanovna Gerasimenko in Kiev and Alma-Ata. It is famous as the destination of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, which rendezvoused with the comet in August 2014 and deployed the Philae lander onto its surface — the first soft landing on a cometary nucleus.

Shape and properties

Rosetta's images revealed a striking "rubber duck" shape: two lobes joined by a narrow neck, thought to result from the gentle merger of two separate bodies early in the Solar System's history. The comet is about 4.3 kilometres (2.7 miles) at its longest and is extremely dark and porous, with a density of only about 0.53 g/cm³ — less than cork — indicating a fluffy, loosely consolidated interior. Its surface is divided into regions of smooth dust-covered plains, brittle cliffs and fields of boulders, and it loses material through jets of gas and dust as it approaches the Sun.

Rosetta mission

Rosetta was launched in March 2004 and, after a ten-year journey including gravity assists at Earth and Mars and flybys of asteroids Šteins and Lutetia, arrived at 67P in August 2014. On 12 November 2014 the Philae lander touched down on the comet; its anchoring harpoons failed to fire, and it bounced before settling in a shadowed location, where it operated on battery power for about 57 hours. Rosetta continued orbiting the comet through its perihelion in August 2015, and the mission ended on 30 September 2016 when the orbiter was deliberately impacted onto the comet in the Sais region.

Rosetta's instruments found that the water in 67P's coma has a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio about three times that of Earth's oceans, arguing against Jupiter-family comets as the main source of Earth's water. The mission also detected molecular oxygen and the amino acid glycine in the coma.
