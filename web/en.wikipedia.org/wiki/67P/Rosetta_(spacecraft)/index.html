Rosetta was a space probe built by the European Space Agency and launched on 2 March 2004. Along with the Philae lander module, it performed a detailed study of comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit a comet nucleus (August 2014) and to deploy a lander on one (November 2014). The spacecraft was named after the Rosetta Stone, in the expectation that it would help decode the mysteries of the early Solar System.

Journey

Rosetta's ten-year cruise covered more than 6 billion kilometres. It used three gravity assists at Earth and one at Mars, flew past the asteroids 2867 Šteins (2008) and 21 Lutetia (2010), and spent 957 days in deep-space hibernation beyond the orbit of Jupiter before waking on 20 January 2014. In August 2014 it began its slow triangular approach and entered orbit around the comet's two-lobed nucleus.

Findings and end of mission

Rosetta found that the comet's water has a deuterium/hydrogen ratio quite unlike Earth's oceans, that its coma contains abundant molecular oxygen, and that organic compounds — including the amino acid glycine — are present. After more than two years at the comet, and with the spacecraft's solar power dwindling as it receded from the Sun, mission controllers guided Rosetta to a controlled impact in the Sais region of the comet on 30 September 2016, taking close-up images during its final descent.
