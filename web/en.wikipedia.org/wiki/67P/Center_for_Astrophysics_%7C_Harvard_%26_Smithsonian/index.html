The Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian (CfA) is an astrophysics research institute jointly operated by Harvard University and the Smithsonian Institution. Formed in 1973 by the merger of the Harvard College Observatory and the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, it is headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

With several hundred scientists and engineers, the CfA is among the largest astronomical research organizations in the world. Its researchers study topics ranging from the cosmic microwave background and the formation of galaxies to exoplanets, black holes, and the physics of stars and interstellar matter.

History

The Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory was founded in 1890 and moved from Washington, D.C., to Cambridge in 1955 to affiliate with Harvard. In 1973 the two observatories formally combined their research programs under a single director, creating the modern Center for Astrophysics.

Facilities

The CfA operates or partners in a number of major observing facilities, including the Fred Lawrence Whipple Observatory in Arizona, the Submillimeter Array in Hawaii, and the Chandra X-ray Observatory science center. It also played a leading role in the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration, which produced the first images of black holes.

Research

Research divisions cover atomic and molecular physics, high-energy astrophysics, optical and infrared astronomy, radio and geoastronomy, solar and stellar physics, theoretical astrophysics, and the study of exoplanets.
