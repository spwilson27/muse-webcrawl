81P/Wild, also known as Wild 2, is a periodic comet discovered by Swiss astronomer Paul Wild in 1978. It is famous as the target of NASA's Stardust mission, which flew through its coma in 2004 and returned captured dust grains to Earth — the first sample return from a comet.

Orbit and history

Before 1974, Wild 2 had a much wider orbit; a close encounter with Jupiter that year deflected it into its present 6.4-year orbit reaching into the inner Solar System. Because it had made only a few inner-system passages before Stardust arrived, its surface was expected to be relatively fresh, though images revealed a rugged landscape of craters, pinnacles, and active jets.

Stardust findings

The returned grains contained high-temperature minerals such as olivine and calcium-aluminium-rich inclusion material, showing that material from the hot inner Solar System had been transported outward to where comets formed. The samples also contained the amino acid glycine, supporting the idea that comets delivered organic building blocks to the early Earth.
