Minor planet

A minor planet is an astronomical object in direct orbit around the Sun (or, more broadly, around any star) that is neither a major planet nor exclusively classified as a comet. The term has been used since the 19th century, when the first asteroids were discovered and initially counted as planets before being reclassified.

The Minor Planet Center, operated by the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory under the auspices of the International Astronomical Union, catalogs the orbits of all known minor planets and comets.

Populations

Minor planets include the asteroids of the main belt between Mars and Jupiter, the centaurs and trans-Neptunian objects of the outer Solar System, near-Earth objects, and the dwarf planets. By the 2020s, well over a million minor planets had been cataloged, the vast majority of them asteroids.

Discovery and naming

The first minor planet, Ceres, was discovered by Giuseppe Piazzi on 1 January 1801. Newly discovered objects receive a provisional designation based on their discovery date; once their orbits are secure, they receive a permanent number, and the discoverer may propose a name for approval by the IAU.

Classification

In 2006 the IAU introduced the dwarf planet category for the largest minor planets — those massive enough to be rounded by their own gravity but not to have cleared their orbital neighborhoods. Recognized dwarf planets include Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres.
