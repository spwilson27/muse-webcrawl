67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko (abbreviated 67P/C–G or simply 67P) is a Jupiter-family comet with an orbital period of 6.45 years. It was the destination of the European Space Agency's Rosetta mission, which rendezvoused with the comet in August 2014 and deployed the Philae lander to its surface on 12 November 2014 — the first soft landing on a comet nucleus.

Discovery

The comet was discovered in 1969 by Klim Ivanovich Churyumov on photographic plates taken by Svetlana Ivanovna Gerasimenko at the Alma-Ata Observatory in Kazakhstan. A close encounter with Jupiter in 1959 had drastically changed its orbit, bringing its perihelion in to about 1.24 AU.

Shape and surface

Rosetta revealed a bizarre "rubber duck" shape: two lobes joined by a narrow neck, probably the result of a gentle merger of two bodies. The surface is dark, dusty, and rich in cliffs, boulders, and smooth dust plains; activity is driven mainly by water-ice sublimation near perihelion, with measurable outgassing of oxygen and complex organic compounds.

Rosetta mission

Launched in 2004, Rosetta flew by Earth, Mars, and two asteroids before meeting 67P in August 2014. Philae's landing went awry — its harpoons failed and it bounced twice before coming to rest in shadow — but it returned several days of data. Rosetta orbited for two years and its mission ended with a controlled impact on the comet on 30 September 2016.

Composition

Rosetta found that the comet's water has a deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio very different from Earth's oceans, weakening the hypothesis that Jupiter-family comets delivered most of Earth's water. Instruments also detected the amino acid glycine and phosphorus, key ingredients for life, in the comet's coma.
