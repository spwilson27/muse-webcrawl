Near-Earth object

A near-Earth object (NEO) is any small Solar System body whose orbit brings it into proximity with Earth. By convention, an object is a NEO if its closest approach to the Sun (perihelion) is less than 1.3 astronomical units (AU). Near-Earth objects include asteroids and comets, and a small number of orbiting spacecraft and meteoroids large enough to be tracked in space.

Near-Earth asteroids

Most NEOs are asteroids. They are divided into groups by their orbits: the Atens and Apollos cross Earth's orbit, the Amors approach it from outside, and the Atiras orbit entirely within it. Tens of thousands of near-Earth asteroids have been cataloged, and surveys such as Pan-STARRS and the Catalina Sky Survey continue to discover more.

Potentially hazardous objects

NEOs whose orbits bring them within 0.05 AU of Earth and which are at least about 140 metres across are designated potentially hazardous objects (PHOs). Space agencies track these objects to assess impact risk, and none known currently threatens Earth in the coming century.

Impacts and defense

Historic impacts such as the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor airburst have motivated planetary defense programs. In 2022, NASA's DART spacecraft deliberately collided with the asteroid moonlet Dimorphos, demonstrating that a kinetic impactor can measurably alter an asteroid's orbit.
