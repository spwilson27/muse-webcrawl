The International Astronomical Union (IAU; French: Union astronomique internationale) is an international non-governmental organization of professional astronomers founded in 1919. Its mission is to promote and safeguard astronomy in all its aspects — research, communication, education, and development — through international cooperation.

The IAU is the internationally recognized authority for assigning official names and designations to celestial bodies and the features on them, including stars, planets, asteroids, and their surface features.

History

The union was founded in Brussels in 1919 in the aftermath of the First World War, growing out of earlier international projects such as the Carte du Ciel. Its first General Assembly was held in Rome in 1922.

General Assemblies and structure

The IAU holds a triennial General Assembly that sets policy and adopts resolutions, including the controversial 2006 resolution that reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet and formalized a definition of "planet" for the Solar System. Scientific work is organized through divisions, commissions, and working groups covering fields from fundamental astronomy to astroinformatics.

Names and designations

Through bodies such as the Minor Planet Center and the Working Group on Star Names, the IAU maintains official catalogs of names for minor planets, comets, planetary surface features, and stars, including approved proper names for bright stars and exoplanets.
