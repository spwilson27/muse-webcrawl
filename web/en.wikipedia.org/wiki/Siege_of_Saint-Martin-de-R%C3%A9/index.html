Siege of Saint-Martin-de-Ré

DateJuly – October 1627
LocationÎle de Ré, France
ResultFrench victory

The Siege of Saint-Martin-de-Ré (1627) was fought when an English expedition under George Villiers, 1st Duke of Buckingham, landed on the Île de Ré off western France to aid the Huguenots of La Rochelle, besieging the royal citadel of Saint-Martin-de-Ré held by the comte de Toiras.

Course of the siege

Buckingham's roughly 7,000 troops failed to take the citadel before French reinforcements were ferried to the island; a disastrous assault and a chaotic re-embarkation at Loix cost the English nearly half the expedition. The episode forms part of the Anglo-French War of 1627–1629.

Legacy

The failure deepened Buckingham's unpopularity in England and left La Rochelle to face the great royal siege of 1627–1628 alone. The campaign features in Alexandre Dumas's The Three Musketeers.
