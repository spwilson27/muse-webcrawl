Indentation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Indentation (from Latin in- + dentare, "to tooth" → "a dent in") is the insertion of a physical depression (a notch, recess or gouge) into an object. In materials science, indentation testing is one of the principal methods for measuring material hardness: a known load forces an indenter of defined geometry into the material surface, and the resulting permanent impression reveals its resistance to plastic deformation.

Usage

The term applies widely: paragraph indentation in text, code indentation in programming, indentation in footwear (tooth marks), and the denting of surfaces under impact. In engineering, "indent" also describes a smooth, shallow depression or cavity.

Indentation hardness testing

In indentation hardness testing, the hardness is calculated from the applied force divided by the contact area of the residual impression — for example the Vickers method divides the load by the surface area of the square pyramidal impression, while the Rockwell method measures penetration depth rather than area. Indentation tests can be performed at loads from kg-level down to the nanoindentation range used in materials research.

  • Vickers hardness test
  • Brinell scale
