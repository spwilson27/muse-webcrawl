Strasbourg astronomical clock

LocationStrasbourg Cathedral, France
Current clock1843 (third clock)
DesignerJean-Baptiste Schwilgué
Heightapprox. 18 m (case)

The Strasbourg astronomical clock is a large Renaissance astronomical clock located in Strasbourg Cathedral in Alsace, France. The present clock is the third on the site: the first, the Three Kings clock, was built in the 14th century; the second, built in the 16th century by Conrad Dasypodius and the Habrecht brothers, stopped working around the turn of the 19th century. The current mechanism, built by Jean-Baptiste Schwilgué between 1838 and 1843, reuses the old case and many of the figures.

Features

The clock displays solar time, sidereal time, the phases of the Moon, the positions of the planets then known, a perpetual calendar including movable feasts, and a computation of Easter. It is regarded as one of the first mechanisms to perform the ecclesiastical computus mechanically. Painted panels by Tobias Stimmer adorn the case of the second clock.

Automata

At various hours, automated figures perform: the ages of man parade before Death, the apostles pass before Christ, and a cock crows and flaps its wings — the present cock is the oldest such automaton figure preserved. The daily main performance takes place at solar noon, around 12:30.
