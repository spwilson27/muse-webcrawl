Poudre B

Poudre B (from French poudre blanche, "white powder") was the first practical smokeless gunpowder, developed in 1884 by the French chemist Paul Vieille. Made from gelatinised nitrocellulose, it revolutionised warfare and firearms design in the late 19th century.

Development

Vieille gelatinised nitrocellulose (guncotton) with a mixture of ether and alcohol, then rolled the resulting colloid into thin sheets and cut it into flakes. Unlike black powder, the new propellant burned progressively, produced far less smoke and fouling, and generated much higher gas pressures, giving bullets far greater velocity.

Adoption

France adopted Poudre B in 1886 for the new Lebel Model 1886 rifle and its 8×50mmR Lebel cartridge — the first military smokeless-powder cartridge — gaining a temporary but significant advantage that spurred every other major power to develop its own smokeless propellant, such as British cordite and American pyrocellulose powders.

Properties and issues

Poudre B was roughly three times as powerful as black powder by weight. Early lots could deteriorate in storage, and instability of aged powder was implicated in the catastrophic magazine explosions that destroyed the French battleships Iéna (1907) and Liberté (1911), prompting reforms in French powder manufacture and storage.
