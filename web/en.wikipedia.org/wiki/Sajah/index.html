Sajah bint al-Harith (Arabic: سجاح بنت الحارث) was an Arab Christian soothsayer and self-proclaimed prophetess of the Banu Taghlib who rose to prominence during the Ridda Wars that followed the death of Muhammad in 632 CE. She is one of a handful of rival claimants to prophecy — alongside Musaylima, Tulayha and al-Aswad al-Ansi — against whom the first caliph, Abu Bakr, fought to reunify Arabia under Islam.

Rise

Born to a Tamimi father and a Taghlibi Christian mother, Sajah proclaimed herself a prophet after Muhammad's death, delivering oracles in rhymed prose (sajʿ) in the manner of the pre-Islamic soothsayers (kāhin). Backed by her mother's tribe and elements of the Banu Tamim, she gathered a following in the Yamama region and marched on Medina.

Alliance with Musaylima

On the way, Sajah met the rival prophet Musaylima, leader of the Banu Hanifa. Rather than fighting, the two negotiated a famous alliance sealed by marriage: Musaylima presented a "revelation" exempting her followers from two daily prayers, and Sajah returned home with her army, leaving her share of the Yamama campaign to him. Early Muslim historians record the encounter with mockery, but it spared her forces from the slaughter that destroyed Musaylima's army at the Battle of Aqraba (633).

Later life

After the Ridda Wars Sajah disappears from hostile accounts; later tradition reports that she eventually embraced Islam, moved to Basra, and died there during the caliphate of Mu'awiya I, with the funeral prayer led by the governor Samura ibn Jundab. Modern historians read her career as evidence of the fluid religious and political landscape of Arabia in the 630s.
