Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg

Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg
Born: Countess Sophie Chotek, 1 March 1868, Stuttgart
Died: 28 June 1914, Sarajevo
Spouse: Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria
Known for: Assassinated with her husband, triggering the July Crisis

Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg (née Countess Sophie Marie Josephine Albina Chotek von Chotkow und Wognin; 1 March 1868 – 28 June 1914) was the wife of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria, heir presumptive to the Austro-Hungarian throne. Their assassination in Sarajevo precipitated the First World War.

Life and marriage

Sophie was born in Stuttgart into the Bohemian aristocratic Chotek family and served as a lady-in-waiting to Archduchess Isabella. Her relationship with Franz Ferdinand, discovered around 1898, caused a court scandal: although noble, she was not of a reigning dynasty, so Emperor Franz Joseph I permitted only a morganatic marriage (1900). Franz Ferdinand renounced their future children's rights to the throne, and Sophie — created Princess, later Duchess, of Hohenberg — was repeatedly slighted in court precedence.

Assassination

On 28 June 1914, during a visit to Sarajevo, Sophie and Franz Ferdinand were shot by the Bosnian Serb nationalist Gavrilo Princip, a member of Young Bosnia backed by the Black Hand. Sophie was struck in the abdomen and died within minutes, shortly before her husband. Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia set off the July Crisis and the outbreak of the First World War.
