En no Gyōja (役行者, "En the Ascetic"; born En no Ozunu, 634 – c. 700–707) was a Japanese ascetic and mystic, traditionally regarded as the founder of Shugendō, the mountain ascetic tradition blending Shinto, Buddhism, and folk religion. He is often depicted flanked by two demons, Zenki and Goki, whom he is said to have subdued and made his attendants.

Life and legend

According to early accounts including the Nihon Ryōiki, En was born in the Katsuragi mountains of present-day Nara Prefecture. He practiced austerities on Mount Katsuragi and Mount Ōmine, gathered herbs, and gained a reputation for supernatural powers, including flight and command over spirits. In 699 he was exiled to Izu Ōshima after a disciple (or the deity Hitokotonushi, in legend) accused him of sorcery and plotting against the court; he was later pardoned and, in tradition, ascended to heaven as a living sage.

Legacy

En no Gyōja is venerated by yamabushi practitioners of Shugendō, who follow pilgrimage routes on Mount Ōmine attributed to him. He received the posthumous title Jinben Daibosatsu in the Edo period. Statues of him, typically holding a scroll and staff with his two demon attendants, stand at temples across Japan.
