Kaieteur Falls

LocationPotaro-Siparuni, Guyana
Total height251 m (822 ft)
Number of drops1
Average width113 m (370 ft)
WatercoursePotaro River

Kaieteur Falls is the world's largest single-drop waterfall by volume of water flowing over it, located on the Potaro River in Kaieteur National Park, in the Essequibo territory of central Guyana. It is one of the most powerful waterfalls in the world, combining a height of 251 metres (822 ft) — including a single uninterrupted plunge of 226 metres — with an average flow of about 663 cubic metres per second.

Description

The falls lie where the Potaro River drops off the edge of the Pakaraima Mountains escarpment through a gorge of ancient sandstone and conglomerate. Below the plunge pool, the river continues through a steep-sided gorge for several kilometres.

History and legend

According to Patamona legend, Kaieteur ("Old Man's Falls") is named for Kai, a chief who sacrificed himself by paddling his canoe over the falls to save his people and appease the great spirit Makonaima. The falls were reported to Europeans in 1870 by the geologist Charles Barrington Brown.

Tourism and ecology

Kaieteur is Guyana's premier tourist attraction, reached mainly by light aircraft from Georgetown. The surrounding rainforest hosts the golden rocket frog, which lives in giant tank bromeliads, as well as Guianan cock-of-the-rock and large flocks of swifts nesting behind the curtain of water.
