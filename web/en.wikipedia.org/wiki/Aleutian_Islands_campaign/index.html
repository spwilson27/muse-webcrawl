The Aleutian Islands campaign was a military campaign fought from 3 June 1942 to 15 August 1943 in the Aleutian Islands, part of the Territory of Alaska, in the American and Pacific theaters of the Second World War. Japanese forces occupied the islands of Attu and Kiska — the only incorporated American territory seized by an enemy in the war — and Allied forces fought for more than a year to expel them, in weather so severe that it claimed more casualties than combat in some phases.

Background and occupation

The campaign began in June 1942, concurrently with the Battle of Midway, when Japanese carrier aircraft bombed Dutch Harbor on Unalaska Island on 3–4 June. Japanese troops then landed on Kiska (6 June) and Attu (7 June), meeting no resistance; the villagers of Attu were taken to Japan as prisoners. Historians debate whether the operation was a diversion for Midway or intended to extend Japan's defensive perimeter and block a possible U.S. route to Japan via the Aleutians.

Allied response

American forces built airfields at Adak and Amchitka to bring bombers within range, and harassed the Japanese garrisons through the autumn and winter. In January 1943 U.S. forces landed unopposed on Amchitka. In March 1943, the naval Battle of the Komandorski Islands cut off Japanese surface resupply to the garrisons. The campaign also forced the evacuation of Aleut villagers by the United States to camps in Southeast Alaska.

Retaking Attu and Kiska

On 11 May 1943, the U.S. 7th Infantry Division landed on Attu. After nearly three weeks of brutal fighting in fog, mud and snow, the remaining Japanese force launched one of the largest banzai charges of the war on 29 May and was annihilated; of some 2,600 defenders, only 28 were taken prisoner, while American losses exceeded 1,600, including hundreds to exposure and disease. Anticipating a similar battle for Kiska, Allied forces — including Canadian troops — landed there on 15 August 1943, only to find the Japanese had secretly evacuated the island under cover of fog on 28 July. Friendly fire and accidents during the Kiska landing nonetheless caused over 300 Allied casualties.

Aftermath

The recovery of Attu and Kiska ended the only battle fought on incorporated American soil during the war. The Aleutians thereafter served as a base for air raids against the Kuril Islands. Attu and Kiska are preserved today within the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge, and the Attu battlefield is a National Historic Landmark.
