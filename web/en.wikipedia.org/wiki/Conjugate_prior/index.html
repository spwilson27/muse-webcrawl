Conjugate prior

The beta distribution is the conjugate prior for the Bernoulli likelihood.

In Bayesian statistics, a conjugate prior is a prior probability distribution such that, when it is combined with a given likelihood function, the resulting posterior distribution belongs to the same probability distribution family as the prior. If the prior and posterior belong to the same family, the prior and posterior are called conjugate distributions, and the prior is called a conjugate prior for the likelihood function.

For example, the Gaussian family is conjugate to itself with respect to a Gaussian likelihood: if the likelihood is Gaussian, choosing a Gaussian prior gives a Gaussian posterior. Other standard pairs include the beta distribution with the Bernoulli and binomial likelihoods, the gamma distribution with the Poisson likelihood, and the Dirichlet distribution with the multinomial likelihood.

Motivation

Conjugate priors are convenient because the posterior can be computed in closed form by simply updating the prior's hyperparameters with sufficient statistics from the data, avoiding numerical integration. The hyperparameters can often be interpreted as pseudo-observations: for instance, a Beta(α, β) prior behaves like α prior successes and β prior failures. The concept was introduced by Howard Raiffa and Robert Schlaifer in 1961.
