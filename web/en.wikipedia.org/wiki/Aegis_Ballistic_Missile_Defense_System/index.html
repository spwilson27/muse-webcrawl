The Aegis Ballistic Missile Defense System (Aegis BMD or ABMD) is a sea-based missile defence programme of the United States Missile Defense Agency and the United States Navy, built around the Aegis Combat System installed on cruisers and destroyers. It intercepts ballistic missiles in their midcourse and terminal phases using Standard Missile 3 (SM-3) and Standard Missile 6 (SM-6) interceptors launched from the Mark 41 Vertical Launching System.

Components

The system combines the AN/SPY-1 phased-array radar (and, on newer ships, the AN/SPY-6), the Aegis weapon system with BMD-specific software baselines, and exo-atmospheric SM-3 interceptors carrying a kinetic warhead — the Lightweight Exo-Atmospheric Projectile (LEAP) — that destroys targets by collision at closing speeds of several kilometres per second. The SM-3 Block IIA, co-developed with Japan, has a larger rocket motor and an enlarged kinetic warhead, extending defended area against medium- and intermediate-range missiles.

Deployments

Aegis BMD ships of the U.S. Navy and the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (Kongō- and Atago-class destroyers) patrol in the Pacific, the Mediterranean, and other regions; Aegis BMD capability is also incorporated into South Korean, Australian, Spanish, and Norwegian warship programmes to varying degrees. The land-based derivative, Aegis Ashore, uses the same radar, combat system, and SM-3 interceptors in fixed sites, operational in Romania (Deveselu, from 2016) and in Poland (Redzikowo), forming part of the U.S. European Phased Adaptive Approach.

Tests and operations

Aegis BMD has conducted dozens of live-fire intercept tests since the early 2000s, including the 2008 Operation Burnt Frost shoot-down of the malfunctioning USA-193 satellite and the November 2020 FTM-44 test, in which an SM-3 Block IIA intercepted an ICBM-representative target — the first demonstration of that capability. The system's effectiveness and cost remain subjects of debate among defence analysts.
