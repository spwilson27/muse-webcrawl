Snow White (Disney character)

Snow White is a fictional character and the protagonist of Walt Disney Animation Studios' first animated feature film, Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937). Voiced by Adriana Caselotti, she is the first Disney Princess and the first female lead of an animated feature. Her design — "skin white as snow, lips red as blood, and hair black as ebony" — and her blue-bodiced, yellow-skirted gown with a red bow are among the most recognisable images in animation.

Creation and design

Snow White derives from the Brothers Grimm fairy tale "Snow White" (1812). Walt Disney softened the tale for the screen: the heroine was aged to about 14, made gentle and cheerful, and given a signature singing voice. Animators used live-action reference footage of dancer Marge Champion, and Caselotti — a trained singer chosen from some 150 auditions — supplied a distinctive light soprano, performing songs including "I'm Wishing", "Whistle While You Work", "Heigh-Ho" (with the dwarfs) and "Someday My Prince Will Come".

Character and story

Persecuted by her vain stepmother, the Evil Queen, who consults a Magic Mirror about who is "the fairest of them all", Snow White flees into the forest and is taken in by the seven dwarfs — Doc, Grumpy, Happy, Sleepy, Bashful, Sneezy and Dopey — whose cottage she keeps while singing and befriending the woodland animals. Deceived by the disguised Queen, she bites a poisoned apple and falls into a death-like sleep, from which she is awakened by the Prince's kiss; the couple depart to his castle.

Reception and legacy

Snow White defined the Disney princess template and remains central to the studio's identity: she appears at Disney parks worldwide, leads the Disney Princess franchise, and her film's success — the highest-grossing film of its time — established feature animation as a major art form. Modern critics have debated the character's passivity, while historians credit her cheerfulness and optimism as keys to the film's emotional appeal; new generations have revisited her in live-action adaptations, including Disney's 2025 Snow White starring Rachel Zegler.
