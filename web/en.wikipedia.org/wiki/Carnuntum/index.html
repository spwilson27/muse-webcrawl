Carnuntum was a Roman legionary fortress and city on the Danube frontier in present-day Austria, near the villages of Petronell-Carnuntum and Bad Deutsch-Altenburg. It grew into the capital of the province of Pannonia Superior and one of the largest Roman settlements on the northern frontier.

History

Originally a Celtic settlement, Carnuntum became a Roman military base under Tiberius and a permanent legionary fortress from around AD 50, home at various times to Legio XV Apollinaris and Legio XIV Gemina. The civilian town received municipal status under Hadrian and colonial rank under Septimius Severus, who was proclaimed emperor by his troops at Carnuntum in AD 193. Marcus Aurelius wrote part of his Meditations while campaigning from Carnuntum during the Marcomannic Wars, and in AD 308 Diocletian held a conference of emperors there.

Decline and archaeology

The city declined after invasions in the 4th century and was largely abandoned by the 5th. Excavations since the 19th century have uncovered the legionary fortress, a large civilian city, two amphitheatres, temples and the surviving Heidentor ("Heathens' Gate"), a monumental arch. Today the Archaeological Park Carnuntum presents excavated and reconstructed Roman houses, including functioning underfloor heating and a rebuilt villa.
