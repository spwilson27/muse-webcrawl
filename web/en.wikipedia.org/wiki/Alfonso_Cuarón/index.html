Alfonso Cuarón Orozco (born 28 November 1961) is a Mexican filmmaker. Regarded as one of the leading directors of his generation — and, with Guillermo del Toro and Alejandro González Iñárritu, one of the "Three Amigos" of Mexican cinema — he is known for long-take cinematography, genre range and films that move between Mexican and international production. He has won five Academy Awards, including Best Director for Gravity (2013) and Roma (2018).

Early career

Cuarón studied philosophy and film in Mexico City and worked in Mexican television and film before his feature debut, the comedy Sólo con tu pareja (1991). Hired in Hollywood, he directed A Little Princess (1995) and Great Expectations (1998).

International breakthrough

Y tu mamá también (2001), a Mexican road movie with Gael García Bernal and Diego Luna, won wide acclaim and an Oscar nomination for its screenplay, co-written with his brother Carlos. Cuarón then directed Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban (2004), often cited as the strongest entry in that series, and the dystopian Children of Men (2006), celebrated for its extended single-shot sequences.

Gravity and Roma

Gravity (2013), a space-survival thriller with Sandra Bullock developed with his son Jonás Cuarón and cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki, grossed over $700 million and won seven Academy Awards, making Cuarón the first Mexican winner of Best Director. Roma (2018), a black-and-white, semi-autobiographical drama set in 1970s Mexico City which he also photographed himself, won the Golden Lion at Venice and three Academy Awards, including Best Director and Best Cinematography. He later directed the Apple TV+ limited series Disclaimer (2024).

Style

Cuarón's films favour fluid, unbroken shots, deep backgrounds dense with social detail, and themes of class, memory, parenthood and mortality. He frequently collaborates with cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki.
