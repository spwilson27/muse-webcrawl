Jazz guitar

Jazz guitar refers to the guitar — and the styles of playing it — in jazz music. Early jazz guitarists played acoustic archtop guitars in rhythm sections; from the 1930s, amplified archtops allowed the guitar to function as a solo instrument.

History

Eddie Lang was a pioneering virtuoso of the 1920s. Charlie Christian, playing an amplified Gibson ES-250 with Benny Goodman's band from 1939, established the electric guitar as a single-note solo voice comparable to a horn. Django Reinhardt developed a distinct European gypsy jazz style on acoustic Selmer guitars.

Styles and players

Later masters include Wes Montgomery, known for his thumb-picked octaves and chord solos; Joe Pass, a virtuoso of solo chord-melody playing; Jim Hall; Pat Metheny; and John McLaughlin in jazz fusion. Big-band rhythm guitar in the Freddie Green style — sparse four-to-the-bar chord voicings — remains a foundation of the idiom. Modern jazz guitarists use archtop, semi-hollow and solid-body instruments.
