Guangyun

The Guangyun (廣韻; "Broad Rimes") is a Chinese rhyme dictionary compiled from 1007 to 1011 during the Northern Song dynasty under Emperor Zhenzong, and completed under the direction of Chen Pengnian and Qiu Yong. It is the largest and most influential of the surviving rhyme dictionaries of Middle Chinese.

Contents

The Guangyun expanded the earlier Qieyun of 601 (via the Tang dynasty Tangyun), organizing some 26,000 characters into 206 rhyme groups arranged under the four tones. Each entry gives a fanqie spelling—using two characters to indicate the pronunciation—along with definitions and variant forms. Because it preserves the phonological distinctions of its predecessors, the Guangyun is a primary source for the reconstruction of Middle Chinese pronunciation.

Legacy

The dictionary remained authoritative for literary examinations for centuries. Its rhyme groups, slightly merged, form the basis of the 106 "Pingshui" rhymes later used in regulated verse composition.
