River Derwent, Derbyshire

The Derwent flowing through the Peak District

The River Derwent is a river in Derbyshire, England. It is 66 miles (106 km) long and is a tributary of the River Trent, which it joins south of Derby. The river rises in the Peak District on Howden Moor, flows through the Upper Derwent Valley, where it feeds the great reservoirs of Howden, Derwent and Ladybower, and then runs south through Matlock, Belper and Derby to its confluence with the Trent.

Course

From its source on the gritstone moors of Bleaklow, the Derwent flows south through a deep valley in the Derwent Valley Mills corridor, passing Chatsworth House, Darley Dale, Matlock Bath — where it cuts through a limestone gorge — Cromford, Belper and Duffield, before reaching the city of Derby. Below Derby it continues past Borrowash and joins the Trent near Derwent Mouth.

Industrial heritage

The Derwent valley played a pioneering role in the Industrial Revolution. The fast-flowing river powered the water-driven cotton mills of Richard Arkwright at Cromford and the silk mill at Derby, and the stretch of mills between Derby and Matlock Bath is now the Derwent Valley Mills World Heritage Site, inscribed by UNESCO in 2001. The dams of the upper valley, built in the early twentieth century, supplied water to the growing cities of Sheffield, Nottingham, Derby and Leicester; the Derwent Dam is also famous as a training site for the RAF's 617 Squadron, the "Dambusters", before their 1943 raids.

Tributaries and ecology

Principal tributaries include the River Noe, the River Ashop, the River Wye (joining at Rowsley), the Amber and the Ecclesbourne. The river supports populations of brown trout, grayling and barbel, and its banks are home to a range of birdlife including dippers and kingfishers.
