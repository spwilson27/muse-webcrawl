Emancipation of minors

Emancipation of minors is a legal mechanism by which a minor (a person below the age of majority) is freed from control by their parents or guardians, and the parents or guardians are freed from responsibility for the child. An emancipated minor is generally given many of the rights of an adult, including the ability to enter contracts, sue and be sued, and make medical and financial decisions.

Ways emancipation occurs

Depending on the jurisdiction, emancipation may occur by court order, by marriage, by military service, or automatically when the minor reaches the age of majority. Court-ordered emancipation usually requires the minor to demonstrate financial self-sufficiency, stable housing, and maturity. In the United States, emancipation statutes exist in most states; California, for example, allows minors aged 14 or older to petition, subject to parental notification and judicial approval.

Effects and limits

Emancipation does not generally grant every adult right: emancipated minors may still be subject to compulsory school attendance laws and cannot usually vote, purchase alcohol, or consent to certain activities restricted by age. Parental support obligations commonly end upon emancipation.

History

The concept descends from Roman law, where emancipatio released a child from the father's patria potestas. In modern entertainment industries, emancipation has been sought by a number of child performers seeking control over their earnings and careers.
