Liberal Democratic Party

This article lists parties named "Liberal Democratic Party" in various countries. For the British party, see Liberal Democrats (UK); for the Japanese party, see Liberal Democratic Party (Japan).

Several parties worldwide share the name

Liberal Democratic Party is the name of numerous political parties in different countries, generally combining elements of liberalism and democracy in their platforms. Because the same name is used by parties of widely differing characters — from Japan's dominant conservative party to small liberal parties elsewhere — the name alone does not indicate a common ideology or affiliation.

Notable parties

  • Japan: The Liberal Democratic Party (自由民主党, Jiyū-Minshutō), founded in 1955, has governed Japan almost continuously since its creation. Despite its name it is a broad conservative party, the dominant force in postwar Japanese politics.
  • United Kingdom: The Liberal Democrats, formed in 1988 by the merger of the Liberal Party and the Social Democratic Party, are sometimes informally called the Liberal Democratic Party; their official short name is "Lib Dems."
  • Russia: The Liberal Democratic Party of Russia (LDPR), founded in 1989–91 under Vladimir Zhirinovsky, is an ultranationalist party despite its name.
  • Australia: The Liberal Democratic Party, founded in 2001, is a libertarian party that has elected representatives to state and federal parliaments.
  • Germany: The historical Liberal Democratic Party of Germany (LDPD) was a bloc party in East Germany; the West German liberals are the Free Democratic Party (FDP).

Other countries

Parties with the name or a close translation have also existed in Belarus, Lithuania, Malaysia, North Macedonia, Turkey, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, and several other states, as well as a European Parliament grouping predecessor, the Liberal and Democratic Reformist Group.
