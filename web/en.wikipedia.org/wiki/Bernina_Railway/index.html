The Bernina Railway is a single-track metre-gauge railway line operated by the Rhaetian Railway in the Swiss canton of Graubünden and the Italian province of Sondrio. It links St. Moritz in the Engadin valley with Tirano in the Valtellina, crossing the Bernina Pass without any rack assistance — making it one of the steepest adhesion railways in the world, with gradients of up to 7 per cent — and reaching 2,253 m at Ospizio Bernina, the highest point of any trans-Alpine railway in Europe.

Route

From St. Moritz the line runs via Pontresina and Morteratsch, climbing past the Bernina glaciers to Ospizio Bernina beside the Lago Bianco. It descends past Alp Grüm, with views of the Palü Glacier, through the Val Poschiavo — crossing the famous circular Brusio spiral viaduct — to Tirano in Italy. Together with the Albula Railway, the line was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008 as "Rhaetian Railway in the Albula / Bernina Landscapes".

History

The line was built by the Bernina-Bahngesellschaft and opened in stages between 1908 and 1910. It was electrified from the outset at 1,000 V DC (later raised to 1,000 V DC operation as retained today), unusual for its time. Financial difficulties led to its takeover by the Rhaetian Railway in 1943. The route is served by regional trains and by the panoramic Bernina Express, which runs between Chur/St. Moritz and Tirano.
