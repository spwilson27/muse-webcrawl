Kutch District

Kutch district, officially spelled Kachchh, is a district of Gujarat state in western India, with its headquarters at the city of Bhuj. Covering 45,674 km², it is the largest district in India. The district is bounded by the Gulf of Kutch and the Arabian Sea to the south and west, and by Pakistan and Rajasthan to the north.

Geography

Much of the district consists of the Rann of Kutch, a vast seasonal salt marsh divided into the Great Rann and Little Rann, which floods during the monsoon and dries into a white salt desert. South of the Rann lies a more fertile mainland tract. The region is known for the wild ass sanctuary in the Little Rann and for flamingo breeding grounds.

History

Kutch was formerly a princely state ruled by the Jadeja Rajputs, famously associated with Maharao Lakhpatji in the 18th century. It acceded to India in 1948, became a state, and was merged into Gujarat in 1960. The district was devastated by the 2001 Gujarat earthquake, whose epicentre was near Bhuj, killing thousands and prompting large-scale reconstruction.

Culture and economy

Kutch is renowned for its embroidery, block printing, bandhani textiles and the Rann Utsav festival. Major ports include Kandla and Mundra; industries include salt production, cement, chemicals and lignite mining. The language Kutchi, closely related to Sindhi, is widely spoken alongside Gujarati.
