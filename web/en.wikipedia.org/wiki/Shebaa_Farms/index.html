Shebaa Farms

The Shebaa Farms (Arabic: مزارع شبعا, Mazāriʿ Šibʿā) is a small area of land at the junction of Lebanon, Syria and the Israeli-occupied Golan Heights. Its status is disputed: Lebanon claims the area, while Israel and the United Nations have treated it as Syrian territory occupied by Israel since the Six-Day War of 1967.

Background

The area, named after the nearby Lebanese village of Shebaa, comprises a group of farms on the western slopes of Mount Hermon. Residents historically paid taxes to Lebanon and held Lebanese documents, but maps from the mandate period and later generally placed the farms within Syria.

Dispute

When Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon in 2000, the UN verified the withdrawal along the Blue Line, which left the Shebaa Farms on the Israeli-held side. Lebanon disputed this, and Hezbollah has cited continued occupation of the farms as justification for its armed activities. Syria has verbally supported Lebanon's claim but has not formally demarcated the border.
