Wooden churches of Maramureș

LocationMaramureș, northern Romania
NumberAround 100 surviving churches
BuiltMainly 17th–18th centuries (oldest from the 16th)
UNESCOEight churches inscribed in 1999

The wooden churches of Maramureș are a group of roughly one hundred Orthodox and Greek-Catholic churches in the Maramureș region of northern Romania, built mostly during the 17th and 18th centuries. They are notable for their tall, slender bell towers, steep shingled roofs, and masterful log construction. Eight of them were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999.

Characteristics

The churches are built of horizontal oak or fir logs joined without metal nails, raised on stone foundations. Above the roof rises a characteristically tall, slim spire — some exceeding 50 metres including the tower — often flanked by four small corner turrets. Interiors are frequently covered with naive-style paintings of biblical scenes executed by local artists in the 18th and early 19th centuries. Because Orthodox churches in Transylvania could not be built of stone under Habsburg rule, timber construction reached an exceptional level of refinement in the region.

The UNESCO churches

The eight inscribed churches are those of Bârsana, Budești, Desești, Ieud, Plopiș, Poienile Izei, Rogoz and Șurdești. The church at Șurdești, built in 1721, has a tower about 54 metres tall and was long considered the tallest wooden church in Europe; the church of Saint Parascheva in Poienile Izei is known for its vivid depiction of hell in the porch paintings.

Preservation

Many churches remain in active parish use. Threats include weather damage to shingles, depopulation of villages, and the construction of new masonry churches beside the old ones. Conservation programs by Romanian and international bodies have documented and restored many of the structures.
