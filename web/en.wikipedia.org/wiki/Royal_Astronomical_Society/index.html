Royal Astronomical Society

The Royal Astronomical Society (RAS) is a learned society and charity based in London that promotes the study of astronomy, solar-system science, geophysics and closely related branches of science. It was founded in 1820 as the Astronomical Society of London and received its royal charter in 1831 under William IV.

Activities

The RAS has more than 4,000 members (Fellows) worldwide. It is based at Burlington House on Piccadilly, London, where it maintains a major specialist library and archive. Its principal journals are Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (MNRAS, published since 1827), Geophysical Journal International and Astronomy & Geophysics. The society holds regular scientific meetings, public lectures and an annual National Astronomy Meeting.

Honours

The society's premier award is the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society, awarded since 1824 jointly for astronomy and geophysics. Other distinctions include the Eddington, Chapman, Fowler and Herschel medals and prizes, and named lectures including the George Darwin Lecture.
