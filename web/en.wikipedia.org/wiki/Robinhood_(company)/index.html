Robinhood (company)

Robinhood Markets, Inc. is an American financial services company headquartered in Menlo Park, California. It operates a mobile app and website offering commission-free trading of stocks, exchange-traded funds, options, and cryptocurrencies, and is credited with popularising zero-commission retail investing in the United States. The company was founded in April 2013 by Vladimir Tenev and Baiju Bhatt, who had previously built high-frequency trading platforms for financial institutions in New York.

History

The Robinhood app launched in 2015 with a waiting list of hundreds of thousands. The company introduced cryptocurrency trading in 2018 and grew rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic as new retail investors entered the market. Robinhood went public on the Nasdaq under the ticker HOOD in July 2021.

Controversies

The company has faced repeated regulatory scrutiny. In December 2020 it paid $65 million to settle SEC charges that it misled customers about its payment-for-order-flow revenue. In January 2021, during the GameStop short squeeze, Robinhood restricted purchases of certain meme stocks, provoking widespread anger, congressional hearings, and lawsuits; the FINRA fine of $70 million that followed in June 2021 was then the largest in the regulator's history. Critics have also accused its gamified design of encouraging risky behaviour by inexperienced traders.
