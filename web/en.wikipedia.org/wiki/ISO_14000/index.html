ISO 14000 is a family of standards developed by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) that provides organizations with practical tools for environmental management. The family addresses environmental management systems, auditing, labelling, performance evaluation, life-cycle assessment, and greenhouse-gas accounting. It grew out of ISO's work following the 1992 Rio Earth Summit and was first published in 1996.

ISO 14001

The best-known member of the family, ISO 14001, specifies the requirements for an environmental management system (EMS) that an organization can certify against. It follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle and, since the 2015 revision, the "Annex SL" high-level structure shared with ISO 9001 (quality) and ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety), making integrated management systems easier. ISO 14001 does not itself set environmental performance targets; instead it requires a policy, identification of environmental aspects and compliance obligations, objectives, operational control, monitoring, and continual improvement. Several hundred thousand certificates are in force worldwide, across manufacturing, services, and the public sector.

Related standards

Other standards in the family include ISO 14004 (implementation guidance), ISO 14006 (ecodesign), ISO 14020–14025 (environmental labels and declarations, including Type III product declarations), ISO 14031 (environmental performance evaluation), ISO 14040/14044 (life-cycle assessment), ISO 14064 and 14067 (greenhouse-gas quantification and product carbon footprints), and ISO 19011 (auditing guidelines).
