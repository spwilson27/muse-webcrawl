European Stability Mechanism

Image: ESM headquarters, Luxembourg (placeholder)

The European Stability Mechanism (ESM) is an intergovernmental organisation established by the member states of the euro area in 2012 to provide financial assistance to euro-area countries experiencing severe financing problems. Headquartered in Luxembourg, it replaced the temporary European Financial Stability Facility (EFSF) and European Financial Stabilisation Mechanism created during the euro-area sovereign debt crisis.

Operations

The ESM has a maximum lending capacity of €500 billion, funded by paid-in and callable capital subscribed by euro-area states, with Germany the largest shareholder. Assistance — loans, precautionary credit lines, and bank recapitalisation support — is granted only under strict economic policy conditionality agreed with the requesting state. The ESM participated in programmes for Spain (bank recapitalisation, 2012), Cyprus (2013), and Greece (2015–2018).

Reform

A 2021 agreement on ESM reform gave it a backstop role for the Single Resolution Fund for failing banks and a stronger role in programme design and debt-sustainability analysis, though ratification of the amended treaty was delayed for several years in Italy.
