The economy of Jamaica is a mixed economy built largely on services, above all tourism, together with mining — historically bauxite and alumina — agriculture, and a large flow of remittances from the Jamaican diaspora. Jamaica is an upper-middle-income country, but growth has been weak for decades and public debt, though sharply reduced from very high levels, remains a central policy concern.

Overview

Services account for roughly 70 percent of GDP. Tourism is the largest single earner of foreign exchange, centred on resort areas such as Montego Bay, Ocho Rios and Negril. Remittances from Jamaicans living in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom are comparable in size to tourism earnings. Jamaica's population is about 2.8 million, and GDP per capita is in the region of US$5,000–6,000.

Mining

Jamaica was long one of the world's leading producers of bauxite and alumina, the ore from which aluminium is made. The industry, developed from the 1950s by North American aluminium companies, has declined from its peak but remains a significant export earner; red mud disposal and rehabilitation of mined lands are persistent environmental issues.

Agriculture

Traditional export crops include sugar, bananas, coffee — the prized Blue Mountain coffee — cocoa, citrus and pimento (allspice), of which Jamaica is a leading producer. Agriculture employs a substantial share of the labour force, much of it in smallholder farming, and also supplies the domestic food market.

Public debt and reform

For decades Jamaica carried one of the highest public debt burdens in the world, exceeding 140 percent of GDP at its peak in the early 2010s. Under successive agreements with the International Monetary Fund, and through sustained primary fiscal surpluses among the largest in the world, the debt ratio was driven down toward roughly 70 percent of GDP by the mid-2020s, a widely cited case of fiscal consolidation.

Other sectors

Kingston is a major Caribbean transshipment port. Business process outsourcing (call centres and back-office services) has grown rapidly. Jamaica is also known for music, rum, and a small manufacturing sector serving regional markets. Energy costs are high because the island imports most of its petroleum, though liquefied natural gas and renewables have been expanded.
