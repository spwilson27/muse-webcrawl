Polybutadiene

Polybutadiene is a synthetic rubber formed by the polymerisation of the monomer 1,3-butadiene. It is the second-largest volume synthetic rubber produced worldwide, after styrene-butadiene rubber, and is valued for its outstanding resilience, abrasion resistance and low-temperature flexibility.

Structure and grades

Butadiene can link in cis-1,4, trans-1,4 and 1,2 (vinyl) configurations, and catalyst choice controls the microstructure. High-cis polybutadiene, made with neodymium, cobalt, nickel or titanium catalysts, has a very low glass transition temperature (around −100 °C) and excellent fatigue resistance. High-vinyl grades, made by anionic polymerisation, are used where wet grip matters more.

Uses

About 70 per cent of polybutadiene goes into tyre treads and sidewalls, usually blended with natural rubber or SBR to improve wear and reduce rolling resistance. It is also used as an impact modifier in plastics — high-impact polystyrene (HIPS) and ABS contain dispersed polybutadiene particles that toughen the material — and as the core of golf balls, in conveyor belts, hoses and footwear soles.
