Tres

The tres is a three-course stringed instrument (three pairs of strings) of Cuban origin, central to the son cubano tradition. Its name means "three" in Spanish. The player of the tres is called a tresero (or tresista).

The instrument resembles a small guitar, with a body somewhat smaller than a standard guitar and six strings grouped in three courses of two. Tunings vary: a common tuning is based on the notes of a C major chord (G–C–E or C–F–A variants in different traditions), often with octave pairs in one or more courses, giving the tres its bright, chiming sound.

Role in son cubano

In the son ensemble, the tres plays rhythmic-harmonic figures called guajeos — repetitive, syncopated ostinato patterns that interlock with the claves, bass tumbao and percussion. Arsenio Rodríguez, the influential blind tresero and bandleader of the 1940s, greatly expanded the instrument's role, developing its tumbao style and enlarging the son conjunto format that fed into modern salsa.

Related instruments

A smaller cousin, the Puerto Rican cuatro, serves an analogous role in Puerto Rican music. The tres itself appears throughout Afro-Cuban music, changüí, nengón and contemporary salsa and timba.
