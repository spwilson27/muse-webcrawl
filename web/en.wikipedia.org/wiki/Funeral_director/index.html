Funeral director

A funeral director, also known as an undertaker (British English) or mortician (American English), is a professional who manages funeral arrangements and the care of the deceased. Funeral directors work in funeral homes and are responsible for coordinating the funeral service, preparing the body, and assisting bereaved families with legal and practical matters.

Duties

Typical duties include consulting with the family of the deceased, arranging transportation of the body, embalming or other preparation, coordinating burial or cremation, obtaining death certificates and permits, placing obituary notices, and directing the funeral ceremony itself. Directors also help families choose coffins, urns, flowers, and memorial products.

History

The occupation grew out of the trades of carpentry and undertaking in the 19th century, when specialists began to take over tasks previously handled by families, clergy, and local tradespeople. Professional associations and licensing regimes developed in the late 19th and 20th centuries; in most jurisdictions funeral directors must complete formal education in mortuary science and pass licensing examinations.

Training and regulation

In the United States, funeral directors typically complete an associate or bachelor's degree in mortuary science, serve an apprenticeship, and hold a state license. In the United Kingdom, qualifications are offered through professional bodies such as the British Institute of Funeral Directors, though statutory licensing historically was not required.
