Naxos

Naxos (Greek: Νάξος) is a Greek island in the Aegean Sea, the largest of the Cyclades island group, with an area of about 430 square kilometres (166 sq mi). Its capital and largest town is Naxos town (Chora). In Greek mythology Naxos was associated with the god Dionysus and with Theseus's abandonment of Ariadne. The island is known for its marble, its emery mines, the temple gateway known as the Portara, and its fertile agricultural interior, unusual among the Cyclades.

History

Naxos was a centre of the Cycladic culture of the Bronze Age; its marble was exported across the Aegean and early Cycladic figurines were carved from it. In the Archaic period the island was wealthy and powerful: Naxian sculptors produced monumental marble statues, and the island's sanctuary stood in the harbour where work on a colossal temple of Apollo began around 530 BC under the tyrant Lygdamis. The temple was never finished; its massive marble doorway, the Portara, survives as the island's emblem.

Naxos was attacked by Persia in 499 BC (an assault that failed) and again in 490 BC, when the city was sacked before the Battle of Marathon. It later joined the Delian League, revolted against Athens around 469 BC, and was reduced to subject status — an early example of Athenian imperial coercion. In the medieval period the island became the centre of the Duchy of the Archipelago, founded by Marco Sanudo after the Fourth Crusade (1207), and Venetian Catholic nobility ruled from the castle (Kastro) of Naxos town. Ottoman rule followed from 1566, and the island joined the modern Greek state in the 19th century.

Geography

Naxos is the most fertile island of the Cyclades, with a mountainous interior rising to Mount Zas (Zeus), at 1,004 m the highest peak in the Cyclades. Unlike most of its neighbours it has substantial water supplies, supporting potatoes, olives, citrus, and livestock — Naxian graviera cheese and potatoes are noted products. The island has also long exported emery, a dark abrasive rock mined near the villages of Apeiranthos and Koronos.

Main sights
