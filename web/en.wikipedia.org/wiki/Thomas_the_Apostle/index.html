Thomas the Apostle

Thomas the Apostle (Greek: Θωμᾶς; Aramaic: ܬܐܘܡܐ), also known as Didymus ("the Twin"), was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus according to the New Testament. He is famously known as "Doubting Thomas" for his initial refusal to believe in the resurrection of Jesus until he could see and touch Christ's wounds, as recounted in the Gospel of John.

In the Gospels

Thomas appears in all four Gospels' lists of the apostles but speaks only in the Gospel of John. There he urges the disciples to accompany Jesus to Judea ("Let us also go, that we may die with him", John 11:16), asks the way at the Last Supper ("Lord, we do not know where you are going", John 14:5), and, after the resurrection, refuses to believe until he sees the nail marks. When Jesus appears and invites him to touch his wounds, Thomas confesses "My Lord and my God" (John 20:28).

Mission to India and martyrdom

According to the apocryphal Acts of Thomas and the tradition of the Saint Thomas Christians of Kerala, Thomas travelled to India in AD 52, founded churches along the Malabar Coast, and was martyred at Mylapore (near modern Chennai) in AD 72, where the San Thome Basilica now stands over his traditional tomb. His relics were later said to be translated to Edessa and eventually to Ortona, Italy.

Legacy

Thomas is the patron saint of India and of architects. The phrase "doubting Thomas" has entered many languages as a term for a sceptic who refuses to believe without direct personal evidence. The non-canonical Gospel of Thomas, a sayings gospel discovered at Nag Hammadi, is attributed to him but was not written by the apostle.
