Quezon City

Quezon City (Filipino: Lungsod Quezon), abbreviated as QC, is the most populous city in the Philippines and a highly urbanized city in Metro Manila. It was the national capital from 1948 to 1976, when the designation was returned to Manila. The city was founded in 1939 and named after Manuel L. Quezon, the second president of the Philippines, whose vision was a planned capital city to replace congested Manila.

The Quezon Memorial Shrine, the city's tallest landmark

History

Quezon City was created on October 12, 1939, through Commonwealth Act No. 502, carved out of land from surrounding towns in Rizal province. President Quezon's master plan envisioned government buildings, wide avenues, and residential districts; the Batasang Pambansa (National Assembly) complex and many national agencies were eventually built there. The Quezon Memorial Circle, containing a 66-metre shrine and mausoleum of President Quezon and his wife Aurora, marks the city centre.

Geography and economy

The city covers some 172 square kilometres in the northeast of Metro Manila and is divided into 142 barangays across six congressional districts. It hosts the main campuses of the University of the Philippines Diliman and Ateneo de Manila University, major shopping and business districts along EDSA and in Cubao and Novaliches, film and television studios, and the headquarters of many national government agencies.
