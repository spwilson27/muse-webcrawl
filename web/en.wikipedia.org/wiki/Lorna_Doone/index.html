Lorna Doone: A Romance of Exmoor is a historical romance novel by the English author R. D. Blackmore, published in 1869. Set in the late seventeenth century on Exmoor, on the borders of Devon and Somerset, it is based on a group of historical characters and infused with the author's celebrated descriptions of the West Country landscape.

Plot

The novel is narrated by John Ridd, a stolid yeoman farmer, who falls in love with Lorna, a girl held by the Doones — a notorious clan of outlaws and robbers who murdered John's father and terrorise the district. Lorna is eventually revealed to be of noble birth, the stolen heiress of a great family, and after the Doones' defeat and a dramatic attempt on her life at her wedding, the two are united. The story unfolds against real events including the Monmouth Rebellion and the Bloody Assizes of Judge Jeffreys.

Reception

Initially slow to sell, the novel's reputation grew steadily, aided by a one-volume edition in 1870; it came to be regarded as a classic and helped popularise the romance revival in English fiction. Blackmore himself rated it below his other works. "Doone Country" around the Badgworthy valley on Exmoor remains a tourist attraction, and the novel has been adapted many times for film and television, including a notable BBC adaptation in 2000.
