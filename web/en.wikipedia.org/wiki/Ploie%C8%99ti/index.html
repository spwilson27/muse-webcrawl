Ploiești is a city and county seat of Prahova County, Romania, situated in the historical region of Wallachia, about 56 kilometres north of Bucharest. It is best known as a centre of the petroleum industry; one of the world's first oil refineries was opened near the city in 1856–1857, and Ploiești became a major source of oil in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

History

The town rose to importance in the era of Michael the Brave in the late sixteenth century. The surrounding Prahova Valley oilfields made Ploiești strategically vital during the Second World War: the refineries supplied a large share of Nazi Germany's oil, and the city was heavily bombed by the United States Army Air Forces, notably in Operation Tidal Wave on 1 August 1943, a low-level B-24 raid that inflicted significant losses on the attacking force.

Economy

Today Ploiești remains an industrial city with refineries and petrochemical plants, although output has declined from its peak. It hosts the Petroleum-Gas University of Ploiești, founded in 1948 and specialising in petroleum engineering.

Culture

Sights include the Clock Museum, the Ion Luca Caragiale memorial sites, and the Ploiești Philharmonic. The writer Ion Luca Caragiale was born in the nearby village of Haimanale.
