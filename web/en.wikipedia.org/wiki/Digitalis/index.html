Digitalis

Digitalis is a genus of about 20 species of herbaceous perennial plants, shrubs, and biennials, commonly called foxgloves. The genus is native to western and southwestern Europe, western and central Asia and northwestern Africa. The flowers are tubular, produced on a tall spike, and vary in colour from purple to pink, white, and yellow. The genus is in the family Plantaginaceae.

Medicinal use and toxicity

The best-known species, Digitalis purpurea, yields powerful cardiac glycosides — including digitoxin and digoxin — that increase the force of heart muscle contraction and slow the heart rate. William Withering published his classic account of foxglove's use in treating dropsy in 1785. Digoxin remains in clinical use for heart failure and atrial fibrillation, but the drugs have a narrow therapeutic index: overdose causes dangerous arrhythmias, nausea and characteristic visual disturbances.

Cultivation

Foxgloves are popular garden plants for their striking flowering spikes and tolerance of shade, and numerous cultivars of D. purpurea have been selected in colours from white to deep purple.
