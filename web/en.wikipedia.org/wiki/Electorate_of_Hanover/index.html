The Electorate of Hanover (German: Kurfürstentum Hannover), formally the Electorate of Brunswick-Lüneburg, was an electorate of the Holy Roman Empire located in northwestern Germany. It was created in 1692 when Emperor Leopold I granted the electoral dignity to Ernest Augustus, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, of the House of Welf.

History

The electorate grew out of the Principality of Calenberg, one of the subdivisions of the Duchy of Brunswick-Lüneburg. In 1705 it was united with the Principality of Lüneburg. In 1714 the Elector George Louis ascended the British throne as George I, beginning the personal union between Hanover and Great Britain that lasted until 1837.

During the Seven Years' War the electorate was occupied by France but defended by an allied army. In the War of the First Coalition and the Napoleonic Wars, Hanover was occupied by Prussia and then France; by the Convention of Artlenburg in 1803 its army was disbanded. In 1807 most of its territory was incorporated into the Kingdom of Westphalia.

Aftermath

After Napoleon's defeat, the Congress of Vienna in 1814 re-established Hanover as a kingdom, the Kingdom of Hanover, enlarged with East Frisia and other territories. The personal union with Britain ended in 1837, when Queen Victoria could not inherit Hanover under Salic law and Ernest Augustus I became king.
