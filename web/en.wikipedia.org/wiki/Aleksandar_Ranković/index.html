Aleksandar Ranković

Aleksandar Ranković (28 November 1909 – 20 August 1983) was a Yugoslav communist politician of Serbian origin, considered the third most powerful man in Yugoslavia after Josip Broz Tito and Edvard Kardelj. A veteran of the Partisan resistance, he founded and directed the Yugoslav security apparatus — the wartime OZNA and its successor the UDBA — and served as minister of the interior and later vice-president of Yugoslavia.

Fall from power

Ranković was associated with a centralist, hard-line faction that favoured a unitary state and was resented by non-Serb republics and by Kosovo Albanians, who experienced harsh repression under his security police. In 1966, after it emerged that the UDBA had bugged even Tito's own residence, Ranković was stripped of all offices at the Brioni Plenum and expelled from the League of Communists. His downfall opened the way to the liberalising and decentralising reforms that culminated in the 1974 Yugoslav constitution.

Legacy

Ranković lived quietly in Dubrovnik until his death in 1983; despite his disgrace, some 30,000 people reportedly attended his funeral in Belgrade, an indication of his standing among many Serbs. Historians continue to debate his role: architect of a repressive secret police, but also a figure around whom narratives of Serbian national grievance later formed.
