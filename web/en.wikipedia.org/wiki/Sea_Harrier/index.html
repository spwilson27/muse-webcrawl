The British Aerospace Sea Harrier is a naval short take-off and vertical landing (STOVL) jet fighter, reconnaissance and strike aircraft. It is a development of the Hawker Siddeley Harrier and entered service with the Royal Navy in 1980, operating from the Invincible-class aircraft carriers.

Development

The Sea Harrier FRS1 adapted the Harrier GR3 for naval use with a raised cockpit, a Blue Fox radar in a lengthened nose, corrosion protection, and the ability to fire Sea Eagle anti-ship missiles and AIM-9 Sidewinder air-to-air missiles. A mid-life upgrade produced the FRS2, later FA2, with the more capable Blue Vixen pulse-Doppler radar and AIM-120 AMRAAM missiles.

Operational history

Sea Harriers achieved fame in the 1982 Falklands War, where, operating from HMS Hermes and HMS Invincible, they flew air defence and ground attack missions and were credited with 20 Argentine aircraft destroyed in air-to-air combat without a single air-to-air loss. They also saw service in the Persian Gulf, the Balkans with NATO, and Sierra Leone. The type was retired from Royal Navy service in 2006; the Indian Navy, which operated the Sea Harrier from INS Viraat, retired its aircraft in 2016.
