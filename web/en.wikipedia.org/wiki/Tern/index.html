Terns are seabirds in the subfamily Sterninae of the family Laridae, which also includes the gulls and skimmers. They are found worldwide, mostly near the sea, rivers or wetlands. Terns are generally slender, lightly built birds with long, pointed wings, deeply forked tails, short legs and sharp bills adapted for spearing fish, which they catch by plunge-diving.

Common tern (Sterna hirundo)

Description

Most terns are grey above and white below, with a black cap in breeding plumage. They range in size from the little tern, at about 23 cm, to the Caspian tern, the largest, at around 57 cm. Unlike gulls they rarely soar and do not usually swim for long periods, though they rest on the water and on floating debris.

Behaviour and migration

Terns breed in colonies on coasts and islands, and inland on freshwater marshes and lakes, nesting in a simple scrape on the ground. Many are long-distance migrants: the Arctic tern (Sterna paradisaea) migrates from its Arctic breeding grounds to the Antarctic and back each year — a round trip of up to 70,000 km, the longest known migration of any animal, giving it more daylight than any other creature.

Terns feed on small fish and marine invertebrates. Their loud, harsh calls and aggressive defence of their nests — they dive at intruders, including humans — make breeding colonies conspicuous.
