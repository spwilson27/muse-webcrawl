The M142 HIMARS (High Mobility Artillery Rocket System) is a light, wheeled multiple launch rocket system developed in the late 1990s for the United States Army and produced by Lockheed Martin Missiles and Fire Control. Mounted on a standard US Army Family of Medium Tactical Vehicles (FMTV) truck chassis, it carries one launch pod containing either six Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) rockets or one Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS) missile.

Design

HIMARS was designed to be air-transportable by the C-130 Hercules, a key requirement that distinguished it from the heavier tracked M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System, which carries two pods. The wheeled chassis gives HIMARS greater road mobility and lower operating cost, at the expense of some cross-country performance. The system can fire its full load of six rockets in under a minute and relocate immediately afterwards, reducing vulnerability to counter-battery fire.

Munitions

The launcher fires the family of MLRS munitions, including the GPS-guided GMLRS unitary and alternative-warhead rockets with a range of roughly 70–84 km (43–52 miles), extended-range variants reaching about 150 km (93 miles), and the ATACMS surface-to-surface missile with a range of up to 300 km (190 miles). The Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) was developed as a successor to ATACMS for both HIMARS and the M270.

Operational history

HIMARS entered service with the US Army in 2005 and was used in Iraq and Afghanistan. Export operators include Romania, Poland, Jordan, Singapore, and the United Arab Emirates, among others. The system gained wide public attention after deliveries to Ukraine beginning in mid-2022, where GMLRS strikes were used against Russian command posts, ammunition depots, and logistics targets during the Russo-Ukrainian War.
