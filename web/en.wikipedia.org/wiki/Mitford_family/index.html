Mitford family

The Mitford family is an English aristocratic family best known for the six daughters of David Freeman-Mitford, 2nd Baron Redesdale, and his wife Sydney Bowles. The Mitford sisters became famous — and in some cases notorious — for their sharply divergent political views and glamorous, scandal-marked lives in the 1930s and 1940s.

The sisters

  • Nancy Mitford (1904–1973), novelist, author of The Pursuit of Love and Love in a Cold Climate.
  • Pamela Mitford (1907–1994), who lived a largely rural, private life.
  • Diana Mitford (1910–2003), who married Sir Oswald Mosley, leader of the British Union of Fascists, and was imprisoned during the Second World War.
  • Unity Mitford (1914–1948), an admirer of Adolf Hitler who shot herself when Britain declared war on Germany.
  • Jessica Mitford (1917–1996), a communist and later a noted American investigative journalist, author of The American Way of Death.
  • Deborah Mitford (1920–2014), who became Duchess of Devonshire and helped restore Chatsworth House.

Their only brother, Tom Mitford, was killed in action in Burma in 1945.

Legacy

The sisters' lives have been the subject of numerous biographies, letters collections, plays, and television dramatizations, and they remain enduring figures in accounts of interwar British society.
