Bartoli indole synthesis

The Bartoli indole synthesis, also called the Bartoli reaction, is a chemical reaction in which substituted indoles are formed from ortho-substituted nitroarenes and vinyl Grignard reagents. The reaction was discovered by Giuseppe Bartoli and co-workers in 1989.

Reaction

Treatment of an ortho-substituted nitrobenzene with three equivalents of a vinylmagnesium bromide at low temperature (typically −40 °C) gives, after work-up, a 7-substituted indole. The ortho substituent is essential: without it, the reaction stops at a nitroso intermediate or gives poor yields. The method is especially valuable because 7-substituted indoles are difficult to prepare by other routes such as the Fischer indole synthesis.

Mechanism

The mechanism involves addition of two equivalents of the Grignard reagent to the nitro group, producing a nitrosoarene intermediate which undergoes a [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement analogous to that in the Fischer synthesis. A third equivalent of vinyl Grignard reagent then attacks, and cyclisation with loss of water furnishes the indole ring.

Scope

The reaction tolerates a range of ortho substituents including alkyl, aryl, halogen, alkoxy and silyl groups, and has been applied in the synthesis of indole alkaloids and pharmaceutical intermediates.
