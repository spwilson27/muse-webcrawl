Edmond Nicolas Laguerre (9 April 1834 – 14 August 1886) was a French mathematician best remembered for the Laguerre polynomials named after him. Educated at the École Polytechnique, he served as an artillery officer before teaching at the École Polytechnique and holding the chair of mathematical physics at the Collège de France from 1885 until his early death. He was elected to the Académie des Sciences in 1885.

Work

Laguerre's most lasting contribution arose in approximation theory and the study of entire functions, where the orthogonal polynomials now bearing his name solve the Laguerre differential equation and appear throughout mathematical physics, including the radial part of the hydrogen-atom wave functions. In geometry he developed the theory of oriented spheres and "cyclides", founding what is now called Laguerre geometry — one of the classical geometries of circles and spheres, later systematised in the Lie sphere geometry. He also worked on continued fractions, the location of polynomial roots (including results related to the Gauss–Lucas theorem), and the geometry of algebraic curves. His collected works were published in two volumes (1898–1905) under the editorship of Charles Hermite and others.
