Vishu (Malayalam: വിഷു) is a Hindu festival celebrating the Malayali New Year in the Indian state of Kerala, the Tulu Nadu region of Karnataka, and the Malayali diaspora. Falling on the first day of the month of Medam in the Malayalam calendar — the astronomical new year when the Sun enters the sidereal Aries — it usually falls on 14 or 15 April of the Gregorian calendar. Vishu marks the spring equinox period and the beginning of the agricultural cycle.

Vishukkani

The centrepiece of the festival is the Vishukkani ("that which is seen first on Vishu"), an arrangement prepared the night before in the prayer room: rice, golden cucumber, jackfruit and other fruits, coconut halves, yellow konna (Indian laburnum) flowers, betel leaves, coins, a metal mirror (valkannadi), holy texts and an uruli vessel, set before an image of Krishna and lit by oil lamps. Family members are led in with eyes closed at dawn to behold the arrangement first, ensuring prosperity for the year.

Other customs

Elders give money, Vishukkaineetam, to younger family members and dependents — often considered the first earnings of the year. A traditional feast, the sadya, is served on banana leaves; children burst firecrackers; new clothes (puthukodi) are worn; and devotees visit temples such as Sabarimala and Guruvayur. Vishu is also associated with the flowering of the golden shower tree (kanikonna), whose blossoms are integral to the kani. Comparable mid-April new-year festivals are celebrated across South and Southeast Asia on the same astronomical basis.
