Jones–Shafroth Act

The Jones–Shafroth Act, also known as the Jones Act of Puerto Rico, is a United States federal law enacted on March 2, 1917. It granted United States citizenship to the people of Puerto Rico and restructured the island's civil government, creating a bill of rights and an elected Senate.

Provisions

The Act separated the island's government into executive, judicial, and legislative branches, provided for an elected bicameral legislature, and retained a governor appointed by the President of the United States. It also granted Puerto Ricans statutory U.S. citizenship, though they could decline it within six months; the great majority accepted.

Significance

Coming shortly before the United States entered World War I, the Act made Puerto Ricans eligible for the military draft. It remained the fundamental organic law of Puerto Rico until the adoption of the Commonwealth constitution in 1952.
