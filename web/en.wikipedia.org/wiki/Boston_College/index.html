Boston College

Boston College (BC) is a private Jesuit research university in Chestnut Hill, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1863 by the Society of Jesus, it enrols roughly 15,000 students, including about 9,500 undergraduates. Despite its name, it is a university, not a liberal arts college, and it is located just outside Boston proper.

History

John McElroy, SJ, founded the college to educate Boston's largely Irish Catholic immigrant community; it opened in the South End in 1864. In 1909 the college began building a new Gothic Revival campus in Chestnut Hill, whose centrepiece, Gasson Hall, was completed in 1913. Women were admitted fully in the 1970s, and the college became coeducational in all programmes.

Academics

Boston College comprises eight schools and colleges, including the Morrissey College of Arts and Sciences, the Carroll School of Management, Boston College Law School, and the Lynch School of Education and Human Development. Its core curriculum reflects the Jesuit tradition of education in the liberal arts and sciences.

Athletics

The Boston College Eagles compete in NCAA Division I, principally in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The men's ice hockey team is a perennial national power, and Doug Flutie's famous "Hail Flutie" touchdown pass in 1984 remains an iconic college football moment.
