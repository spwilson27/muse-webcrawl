Social determinants of health

The social determinants of health (SDH) are the non-medical conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age that shape health outcomes. They include income and social status, education, employment and working conditions, housing, neighbourhood environments, food security, social support networks and access to health services. The World Health Organization describes them as mostly responsible for health inequities — the unfair and avoidable differences in health status seen within and between countries.

Evidence

The British Whitehall Studies of civil servants, led by Michael Marmot, showed a steep social gradient in mortality and morbidity even among people all in employment with access to healthcare. The WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health reported in 2008 with the report Closing the Gap in a Generation, urging action on the conditions of daily life and on the distribution of money, power and resources.

Policy responses

Responses range from "health in all policies" approaches and proportionate universalism — action across the whole population, scaled to need — to targeted interventions on early childhood development, housing quality and income support. Frameworks commonly used by health agencies group the determinants into economic stability, education access and quality, health care access and quality, neighbourhood and built environment, and social and community context.
