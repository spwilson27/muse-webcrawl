The SRY gene lies on the short arm of the Y chromosome

SRY (sex-determining region Y) is a gene found on the Y chromosomes of therian mammals, including humans, that encodes a transcription factor belonging to the SOX (SRY-like box) family of proteins. The SRY protein — the long-sought testis-determining factor — initiates the development of the testes and thus male sex determination in the early embryo.

Function

Around the seventh week of human development, SRY is transiently expressed in the supporting-cell precursors of the bipotential gonadal ridge. Its HMG-box domain binds and bends DNA, upregulating the gene SOX9 and driving differentiation of Sertoli cells and testis formation. The testes then secrete anti-Müllerian hormone and testosterone, directing male internal and external development. In the absence of SRY, the gonad typically develops as an ovary.

Discovery

The location of the testis-determining factor was narrowed to the Y chromosome's short arm through study of XX males and XY females; the gene itself was isolated in 1990 by Andrew Sinclair, Peter Goodfellow and co-workers. Inserting the mouse homologue Sry into XX mouse embryos produced male mice, confirming its role.

Clinical significance

Mutations or deletions of SRY cause XY gonadal dysgenesis (Swyer syndrome), in which individuals with a Y chromosome develop as females with non-functional streak gonads. Translocation of SRY onto an X chromosome during paternal meiosis can produce XX testicular or ovotesticular differences of sex development. SRY mutations have occasionally been raised in the context of sex verification in sport, though SRY screening of athletes (used 1992–1999) was abandoned as unreliable and unfair.

Evolution

SRY evolved from the X-linked gene SOX3 in an early mammal ancestor roughly 160–180 million years ago. It is one of the fastest-evolving genes, conserved mainly in its HMG box; some rodent lineages, such as certain mole voles and spiny rats, have lost SRY entirely and use alternative sex-determining mechanisms.
