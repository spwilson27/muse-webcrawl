António Sebastião Ribeiro de Spínola (1910–1996) was a Portuguese military officer and politician who served briefly as President of Portugal in 1974, following the Carnation Revolution.

Military career

A cavalry officer, Spínola commanded Portuguese forces in Guinea-Bissau during the colonial wars. His 1974 book Portugal and the Future argued that the colonial wars could not be won militarily and helped precipitate the crisis of the Estado Novo regime.

Presidency

After the coup of 25 April 1974, Spínola headed the Junta of National Salvation and became president. A conservative, he clashed with the radicalising Armed Forces Movement and resigned in September 1974. After a failed counter-coup in March 1975 he fled into exile, later returning to Portugal.
