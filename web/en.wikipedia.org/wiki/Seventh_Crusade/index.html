Seventh Crusade
Date: 1248–1254
Leader: Louis IX of France
Result: Ayyubid/Mamluk victory; Louis captured at Fariskur

The Seventh Crusade (1248–1254) was the first of the two crusades led by King Louis IX of France. Its objective was to recover the Holy Land by defeating Egypt, the centre of Ayyubid power, beginning with the capture of Damietta.

Campaign

Louis sailed from Aigues-Mortes in 1248, wintered in Cyprus, and took Damietta almost unopposed in June 1249. Advancing toward Cairo, the crusaders were defeated at the Battle of Al Mansurah in February 1250, where Robert of Artois was killed, and the army, ravaged by disease and cut off, surrendered at Fariskur in April; Louis himself was captured.

Aftermath

Louis was ransomed for 800,000 bezants and Damietta. He stayed four years in the Levant fortifying Acre, Jaffa and Sidon, while in Egypt the last Ayyubid sultan was murdered and the Mamluk regime began — the sultan Qutuz would soon face the Mongols at Ain Jalut. Louis returned to France in 1254.
