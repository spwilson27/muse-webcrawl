Natalie Wood (born Natalia Nikolaevna Zakharenko; July 20, 1938 – November 29, 1981) was an American actress who began her career as a child star and successfully transitioned to adult leading roles. She received three Academy Award nominations and won a Golden Globe Award.

Career

Born in San Francisco to Russian immigrant parents, Wood made her film debut at age four and achieved stardom as a child in Miracle on 34th Street (1947). As a teenager she earned an Academy Award nomination for Rebel Without a Cause (1955), playing opposite James Dean. Her adult career included celebrated performances in Splendor in the Grass (1961), for which she received another Oscar nomination, West Side Story (1961), Gypsy (1962), and Love with the Proper Stranger (1963), which brought a third Oscar nomination. Later films included Bob & Carol & Ted & Alice (1969).

Personal life and death

Wood married actor Robert Wagner twice—first from 1957 to 1962, and again from 1972 until her death. She had two daughters, Natasha Gregson Wagner and Courtney Wagner.

On November 29, 1981, during a break in filming Brainstorm, Wood drowned at age 43 near Santa Catalina Island, California, where she had been aboard the yacht Splendour with Wagner, co-star Christopher Walken, and the boat's captain. The circumstances of how she entered the water were never conclusively established. Her death certificate was amended in 2012 to change the cause from accidental drowning to "drowning and other undetermined factors," and the case was subsequently re-examined by Los Angeles County authorities.

Legacy

Wood remains one of the most admired actresses of her generation, noted for her emotional directness and for navigating the transition from child star to adult leading lady, an uncommon feat in Hollywood.
