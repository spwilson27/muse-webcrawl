The Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI; English: World Air Sports Federation) is the world governing body for air sports, founded in Paris in 1905. It sets standards, certifies world records in aeronautics and astronautics, and oversees international competitions across disciplines including aerobatics, ballooning, gliding, parachuting, aeromodelling, and drones.

History

The FAI was founded by representatives of eight countries at a conference convened during the early years of powered flight. It standardised the rules by which aviation records—altitude, speed, and distance—could be recognised, work that began with ratifying the first recognised flights of the Wright brothers era and Alberto Santos-Dumont.

Definitions and records

The FAI defines the boundary of space as the Kármán line at 100 km altitude, used in its astronautical records. It maintains record classes for aeroplanes, helicopters, balloons, airships, gliders, spacecraft, and model aircraft.

Activities

The federation organises the World Air Games and world championships through its air sport commissions, promotes aviation safety and education, and awards honours such as the FAI Gold Air Medal.
