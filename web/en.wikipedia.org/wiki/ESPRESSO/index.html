ESPRESSO

ESPRESSO (Echelle Spectrograph for Rocky Exoplanet and Stable Spectroscopic Observations) is a third-generation, fiber-fed, cross-dispersed echelle spectrograph mounted on the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope (VLT) at Cerro Paranal in Chile. It achieved first light in late 2017 and began scientific operations in 2018.

Capabilities

ESPRESSO can operate with a single 8.2 m Unit Telescope or coherently combine the light of all four, effectively forming a 16 m telescope for spectroscopy. It covers the wavelength range 380–788 nm at resolving powers of about 70,000 (high resolution), 140,000 (ultra-high resolution) and 190,000 in its single-object modes. Its radial-velocity precision reaches about 10 cm/s, and in ideal conditions better, improving on the HARPS spectrograph it succeeds.

Science

The instrument's main goals include searching for Earth-like exoplanets in the habitable zones of nearby stars using the radial-velocity method, measuring possible variations of fundamental physical constants such as the fine-structure constant using quasar absorption spectra, and determining the chemical composition of stars. Notable results include measurements related to the exoplanet Proxima Centauri b and the detection of heavy elements in exoplanet atmospheres.
