Vikram Ambalal Sarabhai (12 August 1919 – 30 December 1971) was an Indian physicist and astronomer who initiated space research in India and helped develop nuclear power. Widely regarded as the father of the Indian space programme, he founded the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) in Ahmedabad in 1947 and was instrumental in establishing the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).

Early life and education

Sarabhai was born into the wealthy Sarabhai industrialist family of Ahmedabad. He studied at Gujarat College and then at St John's College, Cambridge, where he took the Natural Sciences Tripos. During the Second World War he returned to India and worked at the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore under C. V. Raman, studying cosmic rays. He returned to Cambridge after the war and completed his PhD in 1947 with a thesis on cosmic ray investigations in tropical latitudes.

Career

In 1947 Sarabhai founded the Physical Research Laboratory in Ahmedabad, which became a cradle of space science in India. He also played a major role in building institutions such as the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad and the Community Science Centre in Ahmedabad, which now bears his name.

Working closely with Homi Bhabha, Sarabhai set up the first rocket launching station at Thumba near Thiruvananthapuram, where the first sounding rocket was launched in 1963. He chaired the Indian National Committee for Space Research and, after Bhabha's death, also led the Atomic Energy Commission. Under his direction the plan for India's first satellite, Aryabhata, took shape.

Death and legacy

Sarabhai died suddenly on 30 December 1971 at Kovalam, Kerala, where he had gone to witness a rocket launch and review the Thumba station. The lunar crater Sarabhai is named in his honour, as is the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre in Thiruvananthapuram. He was awarded the Padma Bhushan in 1966 and posthumously the Padma Vibhushan in 1972.
