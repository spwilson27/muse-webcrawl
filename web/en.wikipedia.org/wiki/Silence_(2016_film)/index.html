Silence (2016 film)

Silence is a 2016 epic historical drama film directed by Martin Scorsese, based on the 1966 novel of the same name by Shūsaku Endō. Set in 17th-century Japan, it follows two Portuguese Jesuit priests who travel in search of their missing mentor amid the persecution of Japanese Christians. The film stars Andrew Garfield, Adam Driver and Liam Neeson, with Tadanobu Asano and Ciarán Hinds in supporting roles. A long-standing passion project for Scorsese, who had wanted to film the novel since the late 1980s, it premiered at the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome and at the Vatican in November 2016 before a limited release by Paramount Pictures in December 2016.

Plot

In 1633, Portuguese Jesuits Sebastião Rodrigues (Garfield) and Francisco Garupe (Driver) learn that their mentor, Father Cristóvão Ferreira (Neeson), is reported to have apostatised under torture in Japan. Travelling via Macao with the drunken guide Kichijirō, they are smuggled ashore and sheltered by hidden Christians (kakure kirishitan) in villages near Nagasaki. As the authorities hunt them, villagers who shelter the priests are tortured and executed; Garupe is drowned attempting to save a prisoner, and Rodrigues is captured. The inquisitor Inoue forces Rodrigues to watch Christians tortured in pits, telling him they will suffer until he apostasises by trampling on a fumie (an image of Christ). Rodrigues finally meets Ferreira, who has indeed apostatised, taken a Japanese name and wife, and writes against Christianity. Tormented by God's apparent silence, Rodrigues at last hears what he takes to be the voice of Christ urging him to trample; he steps on the fumie. He lives out his life in Japan under a Japanese name, employed in examining imported goods for Christian imagery, periodically trampling the fumie and receiving absolution through the repeatedly apostatising and repenting Kichijirō. In death he is cremated in Buddhist fashion — but the final shot reveals a small crucifix hidden in his hands.

Production

Scorsese read Endō's novel in 1988 and secured the rights soon after; financing difficulties delayed the film for over two decades. Principal photography took place in Taiwan from January to May 2015, standing in for Japan. Cinematographer Rodrigo Prieto shot largely with natural light; the score by Kim Allen and Kathryn Kluge is minimalist, incorporating ambient natural sound. Prieto was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Cinematography.

Reception

The film grossed about $23 million worldwide against a reported $46–50 million budget, but was strongly praised by critics for its direction, performances and unflinching treatment of faith, doubt and martyrdom, appearing on many year-end top-ten lists; it was named one of the top ten films of 2016 by the American Film Institute. Some commentators debated its length and its portrayal of apostasy, while many religious viewers and critics regarded it as a major work on the "silence of God". An earlier Japanese adaptation, Chinmoku, was directed by Masahiro Shinoda in 1971.
