Roots-type supercharger

The Roots-type supercharger or Roots blower is a positive displacement lobe pump which operates by pumping a fluid with a pair of meshing lobed rotors resembling a set of stretched gears. Air is trapped in pockets surrounding the lobes and carried from the intake side to the exhaust. When used as a supercharger on an internal combustion engine, it is driven directly from the engine's crankshaft via a belt or gears.

History

The principle was patented in 1860 by Philander and Francis Marion Roots of Connersville, Indiana, originally as an air pump for mining and industrial use. The design was later adapted for many purposes, including ventilation, pneumatic conveying, and scavenging two-stroke diesel engines.

Applications

Roots blowers became iconic on drag racing engines, where the large polished blower mounted atop the engine is a hallmark of Top Fuel and hot rod cars. They have also been used on production vehicles and extensively in industry for moving large volumes of air at modest pressure ratios. Because the Roots device does not compress air internally, its efficiency falls at high pressure ratios compared with screw-type superchargers.
