Gabe Logan Newell (born November 3, 1962), nicknamed Gaben, is an American video game developer and businessman. He is the co-founder of the video game company Valve Corporation and its president. With Valve, Newell helped develop the video game series Half-Life, Portal, Team Fortress, and Left 4 Dead, as well as the digital distribution service Steam.

Born in Colorado, Newell attended Harvard University but dropped out in 1983 to join Microsoft. There, he helped create the first versions of the Windows operating system. In 1996, he and Mike Harrington left Microsoft to found Valve and fund the development of their first game, Half-Life (1998), alongside its GoldSrc engine.

Early life and education

Newell was born on November 3, 1962, in Colorado, and grew up in Davis, California. He attended Davis Senior High School and Harvard University from 1980 until dropping out in 1983. He suffered from Fuchs' dystrophy, a congenital disease which affects the cornea, but was cured by two cornea transplants in 2006 and 2007.

Career

After dropping out of Harvard, Newell spent thirteen years at Microsoft as a producer of the first three releases of the Windows operating systems. Inspired by Michael Abrash's decision to leave Microsoft to work on the computer game Quake at id Software, Newell and Mike Harrington left Microsoft in 1996 to found Valve, L.L.C. Newell and Harrington used their money to fund Valve through the development of Half-Life and the GoldSrc game engine.

During the production of Half-Life 2, Newell spent several months developing Steam, a digital distribution platform. By 2011, Steam controlled 50–70% of the market for downloaded PC games. In December 2010, Forbes named Newell as "A Name You Should Know" largely for his work on Steam. In March 2013, he received the BAFTA Fellowship award for his contributions to the video game industry.

Views on game development

Newell has been critical of game development for Xbox Live, the PlayStation 3 console, and closed software and hardware platforms generally. His comments on the PS3 — calling it "a total disaster on so many levels" — attracted wide attention; he later apologised and became more positive about Sony's platforms.

Personal life

Newell is married to Lisa Mennet Newell and has two sons. Among his favourite games are Super Mario 64, Doom, and a Star Trek game he played on a Burroughs mainframe. He has a large collection of knives. Following the COVID-19 pandemic, Newell spent an extended period in New Zealand, where he organised the free "We Love Aotearoa" event.
