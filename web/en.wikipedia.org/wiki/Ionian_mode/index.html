Ionian mode

The Ionian mode is a musical mode or diatonic scale. It is the first of the modes of the diatonic scale and is identical to the modern major scale. The Ionian mode is built from the pattern of whole and half steps W–W–H–W–W–W–H; for example, C Ionian consists of the notes C D E F G A B C.

History

Unlike the older church modes inherited from medieval theory, the Ionian mode (together with the Aeolian mode) was formally added to the modal system in the sixteenth century, most notably in Heinrich Glarean's treatise Dodecachordon (1547), which expanded the eight traditional church modes to twelve. Glarean named it after the Ionian Greeks. As tonal harmony developed in the seventeenth century, the Ionian mode became the foundation of the major key in the major–minor system.

Structure

The Ionian scale has a major third and a major seventh above the tonic, giving it a bright, resolved character. Its triads are I (major), ii (minor), iii (minor), IV (major), V (major), vi (minor), and vii° (diminished). In modern modal jazz and popular music pedagogy, Ionian is taught as the parent mode from which the Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, and Locrian modes are derived by rotation.
