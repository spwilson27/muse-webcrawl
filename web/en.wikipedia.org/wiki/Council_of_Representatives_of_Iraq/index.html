The Council of Representatives (Arabic: مجلس النواب, Majlis an-Nuwaab) is the principal legislature of the Republic of Iraq under the Constitution of 2005. It is elected for four-year terms and is responsible for enacting federal legislation, electing the President of the Republic, approving the appointment of the Prime Minister and cabinet, ratifying treaties, and overseeing the executive. The constitution envisages a second chamber, the Federation Council, but it has never been established, leaving the Council of Representatives as Iraq's de facto unicameral parliament.

Composition and elections

The Council has 329 members, apportioned among Iraq's governorates roughly in proportion to population (the constitution specifies one seat per 100,000 inhabitants). Elections use a modified Sainte-Laguë proportional representation system; following protests in 2019–2020, an amended election law divided the country into 83 multi-member districts for the October 2021 election, which encouraged local candidates. Nine seats are reserved for minorities — five for Christians and one each for Mandaeans, Yazidis, Shabaks and Feyli Kurds — and at least 25 per cent of seats must be held by women.

Powers and government formation

After each election the Council elects its Speaker, then elects the President of the Republic by a two-thirds vote. The President charges the nominee of the Council's largest bloc with forming a government; the Prime Minister-designate presents a cabinet and programme for a vote of confidence. By political convention since 2005 (the muhasasa system of apportionment), the premiership goes to a Shia Arab, the speakership to a Sunni Arab, and the presidency to a Kurd — an arrangement frequently criticised for entrenching sectarian and party patronage.

History

The first Council was elected in December 2005, succeeding the transitional National Assembly. Subsequent elections were held in 2010, 2014, 2018 and 2021. Parliament has repeatedly been at the centre of political crisis: government formation took 289 days after the 2021 election, and in July 2022 supporters of Muqtada al-Sadr stormed and occupied the parliament building amid disputes over the nomination of a prime minister.
