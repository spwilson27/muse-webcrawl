Saint Deiniol (Welsh: Deiniol; Latin: Danielus; died c. 572) was traditionally the first Bishop of Bangor in north-west Wales and the founder of Bangor Cathedral. He is venerated as a saint in Wales and Brittany.

Life

Deiniol is said to have been a descendant of the northern British royal house of Coel Hen and a son of Dunawd. Around 525 he founded a monastery at Bangor (Bangor Fawr) in Gwynedd under the patronage of Maelgwn Gwynedd, on land beside the Menai Strait. The monastic community there reportedly grew to hundreds of monks. He also founded the church at Llanddeiniol in Ceredigion and is associated with the island monastery of Bardsey (Ynys Enlli).

Tradition holds that Deiniol attended, with Saint David and Saint Dyfrig (Dubricius), the Synod of Llanddewi Brefi around 545, and that he and David were consecrated bishops by Dyfrig. Deiniol died about 572 and was buried on Bardsey Island, the "island of 20,000 saints".

Legacy

Bangor Cathedral stands on the site of Deiniol's monastery and remains dedicated to him. His feast day is 11 September. The Deiniol's Library (formerly part of Gladstone's Library in Hawarden) bears his name, and numerous Welsh churches are dedicated to him under the name Daniel in its Latinised form.
