The German Wirehaired Pointer (German: Deutsch Drahthaar) is a breed of medium to large gundog developed in Germany in the late nineteenth century as a versatile hunting dog able to search, point and retrieve game on land and in water, in any terrain and weather. It is distinguished from the German Shorthaired Pointer and the German Longhaired Pointer by its harsh, wiry double coat and its furnished face, with a prominent beard, moustache and eyebrows.

History

The breed was created by German sportsmen, notably under the direction of Baron Sigismund von Zedlitz und Neukirch ("Hegewald"), who sought an all-purpose dog for the average hunter who hunted on foot over varied ground. Breeding combined the German Shorthaired Pointer with rough-coated pointing breeds and griffons, and probably the Poodlepointer and Stichelhaar. The Verein Deutsch-Drahthaar was founded in 1902 and has maintained a performance-based breeding system centred on the Hegewald and other hunting-aptitude tests. The breed was recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1959.

Characteristics

The German Wirehaired Pointer is a strongly built, athletic dog with a harsh, water-repellent outer coat lying flat over a dense undercoat, protecting it in cold water and dense cover. Colours are liver and white (ticked, spotted or roan), black and white, or solid liver. The breed is intelligent, energetic and eager, with a strong desire to work with its handler, but can be reserved with strangers and requires substantial exercise and mental stimulation.

Use

As a versatile (vielseitig) gundog, the breed is expected to point upland game, retrieve shot waterfowl from water, track wounded game by scent, and dispatch vermin. In North America it is popular with foot hunters for pheasant, grouse and ducks.
