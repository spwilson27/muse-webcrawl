Air cargo

Air cargo is any property carried or to be carried in an aircraft. Air cargo comprises air freight, air express and airmail. Although representing a small fraction of world trade by weight (under 1%), air cargo carries roughly a third of global trade by value, because it is used disproportionately for high-value, time-sensitive goods such as electronics, pharmaceuticals, perishables, and e-commerce shipments.

Carriage

Cargo is carried either in dedicated freighter aircraft or in the belly holds of passenger aircraft ("belly cargo"). Freighters include converted passenger aircraft and purpose-built models such as the Boeing 747-8F and 777 Freighter; the largest cargo operators include FedEx Express, UPS Airlines, and Qatar Airways Cargo. Memphis International Airport and Hong Kong International Airport have long ranked among the world's busiest cargo airports.

Handling and documentation

Freight is typically consolidated into unit load devices (ULDs) — aluminium containers and pallets shaped to fit aircraft holds. Shipments move under an air waybill, and the industry is coordinated through IATA standards, including the Cargo-XML messaging standard and e-freight initiatives. Freight forwarders consolidate shipments from individual shippers and purchase capacity from airlines.

History

Airmail in the 1910s–1930s was the first regular air cargo service. Dedicated freight operations expanded after the Second World War, grew with the wide-body era, and were transformed by the overnight express networks pioneered by Federal Express from 1973. Demand surged during the COVID-19 pandemic, when the loss of belly capacity drove up freighter utilization and rates, and again with the growth of cross-border e-commerce.
