Wayne Woodrow "Woody" Hayes (February 14, 1913 – March 12, 1987) was an American football coach and player. He served as head coach at Denison University (1946–1948), Miami University (1949–1950), and Ohio State University (1951–1978), compiling a career college football record of 238–72–10. Hayes won five national championships and 13 Big Ten titles at Ohio State.

Coaching philosophy

Hayes was famous for his conservative, run-first offense, often described as "three yards and a cloud of dust." He believed passing was risky — in his oft-quoted words, three things can happen when you pass, and two of them are bad. His teams emphasised the fullback dive, the quarterback option, conditioning, and discipline.

Rivalry with Michigan

Hayes's tenure included the "Ten-Year War" (1969–1978) against Michigan, coached by his former assistant Bo Schembechler. The Hayes–Schembechler rivalry is considered one of the greatest in college football history.

End of career

Hayes's career ended after the 1978 Gator Bowl, when he punched Clemson player Charlie Bauman, who had intercepted a pass in front of the Ohio State bench. He was fired the next day. Despite the abrupt ending, Hayes remained a beloved figure in Columbus, frequently visiting the football program and the university. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1983.

Legacy

The Ohio State athletic facility (the Woody Hayes Athletic Center) and a drive on campus bear his name. His former players and assistants, including Earle Bruce and John Cooper, succeeded him at Ohio State.
