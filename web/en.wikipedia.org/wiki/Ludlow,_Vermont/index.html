Ludlow is a town in Windsor County, Vermont, United States. It lies in the Black River valley in the Green Mountains and is best known as the home of Okemo Mountain Resort, one of Vermont's major ski areas. The town includes the village of Ludlow and the former village of Tyson.

History

Ludlow was chartered in 1761 under the New Hampshire Grants. In the nineteenth century the Black River powered woolen mills and machine shops, and the town became a modest manufacturing centre; the Black River Academy, founded in 1835, counted Calvin Coolidge among its students — the future president was born in nearby Plymouth Notch and attended the academy in Ludlow. Coolidge was in Ludlow when he learned of President Harding's death, before returning to Plymouth Notch to take the oath of office.

Geography and economy

Okemo Mountain rises to 3,344 feet west of the village, and its ski resort, founded in 1955, drives the modern tourism economy alongside Lake Rescue and Lake Pauline to the north. Vermont Route 100 and Route 103 intersect in the village centre.

Notable people

  • Calvin Coolidge, 30th U.S. president (educated in Ludlow)
  • John Garibaldi Sargent, U.S. Attorney General
