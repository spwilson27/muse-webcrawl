Jackson, Mississippi

Jackson is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Mississippi. Located on the Pearl River in Hinds County, it had a population of about 153,000 in 2020, with roughly 590,000 people in its metropolitan area. The city is named after Andrew Jackson, who was a general at the time of its founding and later became president.

History

Jackson was founded in 1821 as the site for the new state capital, on land that had been a trading post called LeFleur's Bluff. It was occupied and burned several times during the Civil War; General William Tecumseh Sherman's forces captured it in 1863. In the 20th century the discovery of nearby natural gas fields fuelled growth, and the city was a major site of the civil rights movement, including the 1961 Freedom Rides and the 1963 assassination of NAACP leader Medgar Evers.

Recent issues

Jackson has faced deep fiscal and infrastructure problems, most visibly a prolonged water crisis: in 2022 flooding of the Pearl River disabled its main treatment plant, leaving residents without safe drinking water and prompting a federal emergency declaration and intervention.
