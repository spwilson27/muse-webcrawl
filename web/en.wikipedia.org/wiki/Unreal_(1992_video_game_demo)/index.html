Unreal (1992 video game demo)

Unreal's famous 3D vector world sequence.

DeveloperFuture Crew
Released1992
PlatformMS-DOS (PC)
EventSecond place, The Party 1992 demo competition

Unreal is a demo created by the Finnish demogroup Future Crew for the PC, released at The Party 1992 in Denmark, where it placed second in the demo competition. It is considered one of the most influential productions of the early PC demoscene, demonstrating that the IBM PC — long dismissed beside the Amiga — could deliver smooth, music-synchronized graphics effects.

Content

The demo runs for about twelve minutes and is divided into themed parts: a fractals and plasma introduction, a bouncing-ball and vector-ball sequence, and its celebrated centerpiece — a real-time 3D vector "world" in which the camera flies through a landscape of mountains, water, and a castle, years before such scenes were routine in games. The soundtrack, composed with Scream Tracker by Purple Motion and Skaven, was synchronized to the visuals and became famous in its own right as tracked module music.

Legacy

Unreal helped launch the PC demoscene and set up Future Crew's follow-up triumph, Second Reality (1993). Members of Future Crew went on to found companies including Remedy Entertainment and Futuremark. In 2015 the original source code of Unreal was released publicly. It has no connection to Epic Games' 1998 first-person shooter Unreal.
