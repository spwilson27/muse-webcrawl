Electronic money

Electronic money (also e-money) is a digital representation of fiat currency stored electronically — on a chip card, a mobile phone or a server-based account — and issued on receipt of funds for use as a means of payment. Unlike bank deposits held in a conventional account, e-money is typically issued by dedicated electronic money institutions as well as banks, under specific regulatory regimes.

Forms

Hardware-based e-money includes stored-value cards such as transit cards (for example Hong Kong's Octopus and London's Oyster) and prepaid chip cards. Software- and network-based forms include online wallets and mobile money services such as M-Pesa in Kenya, which brought payment and savings services to millions without bank accounts. In the European Union, the Electronic Money Directive defines e-money and requires issuers to safeguard funds and redeem them at par; similar licensing frameworks operate in the United Kingdom, Singapore and elsewhere.

Relation to other digital value

Electronic money is denominated in a state currency and is a claim on its issuer, distinguishing it from cryptocurrencies, which are not issued against fiat funds, and from central bank digital currencies, which are direct liabilities of a central bank. E-money balances are widely used for e-commerce, person-to-person transfers and micropayments.
