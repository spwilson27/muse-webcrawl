Ironman mode

Ironman mode is an optional gameplay setting in video games, most closely associated with the grand strategy games of Paradox Interactive, in which the game is automatically saved continuously to a single save file and manual saving and reloading are disabled. The player therefore cannot "save-scum" — replaying a section until a favourable random outcome occurs — and must live with the consequences of every decision. The name alludes to the endurance and single-attempt stakes of the Ironman Triathlon.

Implementation

In Paradox titles such as Europa Universalis IV, Crusader Kings II and III, Hearts of Iron IV, and Stellaris, ironman mode is required to earn achievements, which would otherwise be trivialised by reloading. The game autosaves at regular intervals (typically monthly in-game) and on exit, and the save is protected by checksums so that modifying it disables achievement eligibility. Similar modes appear in other genres: XCOM: Enemy Unknown offered an "Ironman" option, roguelikes enforce permanent consequences by default, and "hardcore mode" in role-playing games such as Diablo adds permanent character death on top of a single save.

Reception

Players and designers credit ironman mode with heightening tension, attachment to characters and campaigns, and the emergent storytelling that arises from irreversible setbacks. Critics note that bugs or misclicks can ruin long campaigns, and that frequent autosaving can cause performance hitches in large strategy games.
