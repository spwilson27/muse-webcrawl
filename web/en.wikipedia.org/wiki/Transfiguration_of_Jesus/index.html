The Transfiguration of Jesus is an episode in the New Testament in which Jesus is transfigured and becomes radiant in glory upon a mountain. The Synoptic Gospels (Matthew 17:1–8, Mark 9:2–8, Luke 9:28–36) recount that Jesus took his disciples Peter, James and John up a high mountain, where his face shone like the sun and his clothes became white as light. Moses and Elijah appeared and spoke with him, and a voice from a bright cloud declared, "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him."

Significance

In Christian theology the Transfiguration is a pivotal moment in which Jesus' divine nature is revealed to the disciples, bridging his earthly ministry and his glory. It is one of the five major milestones of Jesus' life in the Gospel narrative, alongside baptism, crucifixion, resurrection and ascension. In Eastern Orthodox theology, the uncreated light seen at the Transfiguration is central to the doctrine of theosis and the hesychast tradition defended by Gregory Palamas.

Location

None of the accounts identifies the mountain by name. Since the time of Origen, Mount Tabor in Lower Galilee has been the traditional site, crowned today by the Franciscan Church of the Transfiguration, designed by Antonio Barluzzi and completed in 1924. Some scholars favour the much higher Mount Hermon, closer to Caesarea Philippi where the preceding events occurred.

In art

The Transfiguration is a major subject of Christian art, most famously in Raphael's final painting, The Transfiguration (1516–1520), now in the Vatican Museums, and in Byzantine icons placed at the centre of the iconostasis feast tier.
