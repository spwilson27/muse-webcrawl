Bitcoin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Bitcoin logo, an orange circle with a white B symbol]

Bitcoin (abbreviation BTC) is a cryptocurrency and digital payment system invented by an unknown individual or group named Satoshi Nakamoto. It was released as open-source software in 2009 and operates without a central bank or single administrator: transactions are verified by network nodes through cryptography and recorded in a public distributed ledger called a blockchain.

New bitcoins are created by mining — computers competing to solve proof-of-work puzzles — with rewards halving roughly every four years, capping total supply at 21 million coins (expected around 2140).

History and market

The genesis block was mined on 3 January 2009, embedding a newspaper headline about bank bailouts. Early milestones include the 2010 'pizza transaction' (10,000 BTC for two pizzas), exchange booms and busts, El Salvador's adoption as legal tender (2021, later scaled back), and US approval of spot bitcoin ETFs (January 2024). Its price is famously volatile, with multiple drawdowns exceeding 70%.

Nakamoto's identity remains unknown; their estimated million mined coins have never moved.

Criticism and use

Bitcoin is used for speculation, remittances, and (contentiously) illicit payments given its pseudonymity. Critics cite energy consumption of mining, price volatility unsuited to a unit of account, and limited throughput; supporters call it 'digital gold' — a scarce, censorship-resistant asset outside state control.
