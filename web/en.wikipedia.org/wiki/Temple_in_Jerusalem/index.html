The Temple in Jerusalem was a series of structures on the Temple Mount in the Old City of Jerusalem that served as the central place of Israelite and Jewish worship. According to the Hebrew Bible, the First Temple was built by King Solomon in the tenth century BC and housed the Ark of the Covenant in its innermost sanctuary, the Holy of Holies.

First and Second Temples

Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon destroyed Solomon's Temple in 586 BC and exiled Jerusalem's population. After the Persian conquest, returnees under Zerubbabel built the Second Temple, dedicated in 515 BC. Herod the Great, beginning around 20–19 BC, rebuilt it on a vastly enlarged scale, expanding the Temple Mount platform with great retaining walls — of which the Western Wall survives as Judaism's holiest accessible prayer site — and adorning the sanctuary in marble and gold.

Worship and destruction

The Temple was the destination of pilgrimage festivals (Passover, Shavuot, Sukkot), the site of daily and festival sacrifices by priests (kohanim), and the only place where sacrificial worship could be offered. During the First Jewish–Roman War, Roman forces under Titus destroyed the Temple in AD 70, an event commemorated by Jews on Tisha B'Av and depicted on the Arch of Titus in Rome. Its destruction ended sacrificial Judaism and shifted religious life to synagogue, prayer and study.

Legacy

The Temple Mount is today occupied by the Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque, making it sacred in Islam as well and one of the most contested religious sites in the world. Expectations of a future Third Temple figure in Jewish liturgy and in some Christian eschatology.
