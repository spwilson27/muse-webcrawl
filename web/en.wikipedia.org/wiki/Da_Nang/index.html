Da Nang

Da Nang (Vietnamese: Đà Nẵng) is a coastal city in central Vietnam and the largest city on the country's central coast, with a population of roughly 1.2 million. A major port on the Han River near its mouth on the South China Sea, Da Nang is one of Vietnam's five centrally governed municipalities and an important commercial and educational centre for the region.

History

The area was originally settled by the Cham people; the nearby ancient port of the Champa kingdom grew into a trading post known to Europeans as Tourane. In 1858, French and Spanish forces attacked Da Nang, beginning France's colonisation of Vietnam. During the Vietnam War, Da Nang hosted a major American air base; in March 1965 it was the landing site of the first U.S. Marine Corps combat troops deployed to South Vietnam, and it fell to North Vietnamese forces in March 1975.

Geography and landmarks

The city is framed by mountains to the west and the sea to the east. Notable landmarks include the Dragon Bridge (Cầu Rồng), a steel arch bridge that breathes fire and water on weekend nights; the Marble Mountains (Ngũ Hành Sơn), a cluster of limestone hills with temples and caves; the Ba Na Hills resort with its Golden Bridge held by giant stone hands; and long sandy beaches such as My Khe. The ancient town of Hoi An and the ruins of My Son lie within easy reach to the south.

Economy and transport

Tourism, services, and light industry drive the economy. Da Nang International Airport and the deep-water Tien Sa Port serve the city, which sits near the midpoint of Vietnam's north–south highway and railway.
