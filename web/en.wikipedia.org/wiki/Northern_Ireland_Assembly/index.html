The Northern Ireland Assembly (Irish: Tionól Thuaisceart Éireann; Ulster Scots: Norlin Airlan Assemblie) is the devolved, unicameral legislature of Northern Ireland. It has power to legislate in a wide range of areas not explicitly reserved to the Parliament of the United Kingdom, and to appoint the Northern Ireland Executive, the devolved government. The Assembly was established by the Northern Ireland Act 1998 as part of the Good Friday Agreement.

Composition and elections

The Assembly has 90 members (reduced from 108 in 2017), known as Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs), five elected from each of Northern Ireland's 18 Westminster constituencies by the single transferable vote. Members designate themselves as "unionist", "nationalist" or "other". It sits at Parliament Buildings in the Stormont Estate in Belfast.

Power-sharing

Under the consociational arrangements of the Good Friday Agreement, the Executive is formed through mandatory coalition using the d'Hondt method, and the First Minister and deputy First Minister — a joint office of equals — come from the largest parties of the two largest designations. Certain "key decisions" require cross-community support, and members may lodge a petition of concern. The institutions have been suspended or collapsed several times, including 2017–2020 and 2022–2024, during which Northern Ireland was run by civil servants and Westminster.
