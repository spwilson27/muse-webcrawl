Kīlauea is an active shield volcano in the Hawaiian Islands, located along the southeastern shore of the island of Hawaiʻi. Historically, it is the most active of the five volcanoes that form the island, and one of the most active volcanoes on Earth. In Hawaiian, Kīlauea means "spewing" or "much spreading", referring to its frequent lava flows.

Geology

Kīlauea is the product of the Hawaiian hotspot. It is the second-youngest of the island's volcanoes and the current eruptive centre of the Hawaiian–Emperor seamount chain. Its summit caldera, about 2 by 3 miles across, contains the Halemaʻumaʻu crater, home in Native Hawaiian tradition to Pele, the goddess of fire and volcanoes.

Recent eruptions

From 1983 to 2018 Kīlauea erupted nearly continuously from the Puʻu ʻŌʻō vent on its east rift zone, one of the longest-lived eruptions in recorded history. In 2018 a major lower east rift zone eruption destroyed hundreds of homes in the Puna district while the summit collapsed dramatically. Activity since 2020 has centred on lava lakes and episodic fountaining within Halemaʻumaʻu, monitored by the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory. Most of the volcano lies within Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park.
