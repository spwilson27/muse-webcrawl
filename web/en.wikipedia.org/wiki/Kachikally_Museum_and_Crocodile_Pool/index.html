Kachikally Museum and Crocodile Pool

LocationBakau, The Gambia
TypeSacred crocodile pool and museum
PopulationAround 80 Nile crocodiles

The Kachikally Museum and Crocodile Pool is a sacred site and tourist attraction in Bakau, near the Atlantic coast of The Gambia. It centres on a freshwater pool inhabited by dozens of Nile crocodiles, tended by the Bojang family, who are regarded as its traditional custodians.

Sacred significance

Local tradition holds that the pool is a place of spiritual power associated with fertility: women seeking to conceive have long come to be ritually washed with the pool's water. The crocodiles are considered sacred and are not hunted; they are fed fish and are famously docile, allowing supervised visitors to approach and even touch them.

Museum

The adjoining small ethnographic museum, opened in the 2000s, displays Gambian cultural artefacts across several round huts — musical instruments such as the kora and balafon, masks, traditional medicine objects, and exhibits on circumcision rites, wrestling, and the history of the area. The site also includes a nature trail through forest where monkeys and birds can be seen, and a craft shop.

Conservation and tourism

Kachikally is one of three sacred crocodile pools in The Gambia, alongside those at Berending and Kartong. Entrance fees support the custodians, crocodile feeding, and local employment, making the site one of the most visited attractions in the Greater Banjul area.
