Weak signal

A weak signal is a signal whose strength is close to or below the noise floor of the receiving system, making it difficult to detect or decode. The term is used in radio communications, signal processing, and in futures studies and strategic management, where it denotes early, fragmentary indications of emerging change.

In radio communications

Amateur radio operators pursue weak-signal work using highly directional antennas, low-noise preamplifiers, and digital modes such as WSPR, FT8, and JT65, which can decode signals well below audibility by integrating over time and applying forward error correction. Earth–Moon–Earth ("moonbounce") communication is a classic weak-signal discipline.

In foresight

In futures research, a weak signal is an early indicator of a potentially significant development — a small event, innovation, or anomaly that, amplified, could become a major trend. Scanning for weak signals is part of horizon scanning and environmental scanning methods.
