Make Poverty History

Make Poverty History was an international campaign coalition of charities, religious groups, trade unions, and campaigning organisations that mobilised in 2005 to press governments — particularly those of the G8 — for action on global poverty. The British and Irish coalition formed the largest national arm of the global movement, alongside the international Global Call to Action Against Poverty.

Demands

The campaign advanced three main demands: trade justice (fairer rules for developing countries and an end to forced liberalisation), drop the debt (cancellation of unpayable debts owed by the poorest countries), and more and better aid (meeting the long-standing target of 0.7% of national income in development assistance, delivered more effectively).

Events of 2005

The campaign's symbol was a white wristband, worn by millions, including many public figures. On 2 July 2005, an estimated 225,000 people marched through Edinburgh ahead of the 31st G8 summit at Gleneagles, Scotland. The Live 8 concerts, organised by Bob Geldof and others on the same weekend, took place in cities around the world and amplified the campaign's message. Later that year the movement targeted the World Trade Organization ministerial conference in Hong Kong.

Outcomes and legacy

At Gleneagles the G8 agreed to cancel debts owed by heavily indebted poor countries to the IMF, World Bank, and African Development Fund, and pledged to increase aid by some $50 billion a year by 2010. Critics debated how much of the aid pledge represented new money and how fully it was delivered, but the campaign is widely credited with putting debt cancellation permanently into effect and reshaping public engagement with development issues.
