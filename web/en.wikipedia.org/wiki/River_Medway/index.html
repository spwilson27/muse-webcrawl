The River Medway is a river in South East England. It rises in the High Weald, East Sussex and flows through Tonbridge, Maidstone and the Medway conurbation in Kent to meet the River Thames at Sheerness, a total distance of about 113 kilometres (70 mi).

Course

The Medway flows through the North Downs and the Weald. Its main tributaries include the River Eden, the River Teise, the River Beult, the River Loose and the River Len. The river is tidal as far as Allington Lock near Maidstone. The Medway towns of Rochester, Chatham and Gillingham grew around the Royal Navy dockyard at Chatham, established in the 16th century.

History

The Medway was the site of the Raid on the Medway in 1667, during the Second Anglo-Dutch War, when the Dutch fleet sailed up the river and burned or captured much of the English fleet at anchor — one of the worst defeats in the history of the Royal Navy. In 43 AD the Battle of the Medway was fought between the Romans and the British tribes during the Roman conquest of Britain.

Navigation

The river is navigable to large vessels as far as Rochester, and to smaller craft as far as Tonbridge. The Medway Navigation was historically important for carrying agricultural produce, timber and iron from the Weald.
