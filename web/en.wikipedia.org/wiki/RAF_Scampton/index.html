RAF Scampton

Image placeholder: RAF Scampton, wartime home of No. 617 Squadron, the "Dambusters".

Royal Air Force Scampton, commonly abbreviated RAF Scampton, was a Royal Air Force station near Scampton in Lincolnshire, England. It is best known as the wartime base of No. 617 Squadron RAF, which launched the famous Dambusters raid (Operation Chastise) from the station in May 1943, and for many years afterwards as the home of the Red Arrows aerobatic team.

History

Opened in 1916 as a Home Defence night landing ground, the station was rebuilt in the 1930s expansion period. During the Second World War it hosted Hampden, Manchester and Lancaster bombers; the Dambusters' commanding officer Guy Gibson had his office in No. 2 hangar, and his dog's grave stands outside it. In the Cold War, Scampton operated Vulcan bombers of the V force carrying Blue Steel stand-off missiles.

Later use and closure

The Red Arrows were based at Scampton from 1983 (with an interval in the 1990s) until their move to RAF Waddington in 2022. The station closed in 2023. Plans were subsequently announced for the site to house asylum seekers, which attracted local controversy and legal challenge.
