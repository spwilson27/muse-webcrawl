Economics of climate change

The economics of climate change concerns the economic aspects of climate change; this can inform policies that governments might consider in response. A number of factors make this and other approaches to climate change mitigation difficult. Climate change is a long-term process that affects the whole world and involves complicated scientific, economic, political and ethical issues.

Scope

Climate change economics covers the costs of climate change impacts, the costs and benefits of mitigation (reducing greenhouse gas emissions), and the costs and benefits of adaptation. The field draws on integrated assessment models that combine climate science with economic modelling.

Costs of impacts

Impacts include sea level rise, changes in agricultural productivity, extreme weather events, effects on human health, and ecosystem loss. Estimates of the social cost of carbon—the damage caused by emitting one additional tonne of carbon dioxide—vary widely depending on assumptions about discount rates and future damages.

Mitigation and adaptation

Policy instruments discussed by economists include carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, subsidies for low-carbon technology, and regulation. The Stern Review of 2006 argued that the benefits of strong early action on climate change far outweigh the costs of inaction.
