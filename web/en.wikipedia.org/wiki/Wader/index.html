Wader

Waders (called shorebirds in North America) are birds of the order Charadriiformes commonly found wading along shorelines and mudflats to forage for invertebrates in mud and sand. They include plovers, sandpipers, snipes, avocets, stilts, oystercatchers, curlews and phalaropes.

Characteristics

Waders typically have long legs for walking in shallow water and bills of varied length and shape adapted to probing, sweeping or picking prey. Many species are highly migratory, breeding in Arctic and temperate regions and wintering on coasts worldwide; some undertake among the longest migrations of any birds, tracked along flyways such as the East Atlantic and East Asian–Australasian Flyways.

Taxonomy

The group belongs mainly to the suborder Charadrii and Scolopaci within Charadriiformes, encompassing families such as Charadriidae (plovers and lapwings), Scolopacidae (sandpipers and snipes), Recurvirostridae (avocets and stilts) and Haematopodidae (oystercatchers).

Conservation

Many wader populations are declining through loss of coastal wetlands, reclamation of tidal flats (notably around the Yellow Sea), disturbance and climate change affecting Arctic breeding grounds.
