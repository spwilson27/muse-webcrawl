Orvieto

Orvieto Cathedral (Duomo), begun in 1290.

Orvieto is a city and comune in the province of Terni, in southwestern Umbria, Italy. It sits dramatically on the flat top of a large butte of volcanic tuff, surrounded by defensive walls built of the same stone. The site was a major Etruscan city and later a refuge of medieval popes.

Sights

The city's masterpiece is its cathedral, the Duomo, a gem of Italian Gothic architecture begun in 1290 to house the relic of the Miracle of Bolsena. Its facade is decorated with mosaics and sculpture, and inside, the San Brizio Chapel contains Luca Signorelli's celebrated fresco cycle of the Last Judgment (1499–1504), which influenced Michelangelo. Other landmarks include the Pozzo di San Patrizio (St Patrick's Well, 1527–1537), a double-helix well engineered by Antonio da Sangallo the Younger, the Albornoz fortress, and the underground network of Etruscan and medieval caves beneath the town.

Wine

Orvieto is also the name of a well-known white wine from the surrounding hills, traditionally a blend dominated by Grechetto and Trebbiano grapes, produced in both dry (secco) and sweet (abboccato) styles.
