Den Helder (Dutch pronunciation: [dɛn ˈɦɛldər]) is a municipality and a city in the Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. Den Helder occupies the northernmost point of the North Holland peninsula and is home to the country's main naval base.

The Texel ferry leaves from Den Helder, carrying passengers and vehicles across the Marsdiep strait to the island of Texel, the largest of the West Frisian Islands.

History

The area around Den Helder was settled early; Huisduinen, the oldest part of the town, dates to around 1500. The town grew in importance because of its strategic position at the entrance to the Zuiderzee. During the Dutch Golden Age, ships from Amsterdam passed Den Helder on their way to the open sea.

In 1811 Napoleon visited the town and ordered the construction of a major naval base, recognising its strategic value; he called Den Helder the "Gibraltar of the North". Fortifications including Fort Kijkduin were built in this period. The New Diep harbour and the North Holland Canal (opened 1824) strengthened its role as a naval and commercial port.

During the Second World War Den Helder, as a key naval installation, was heavily bombed and much of the population was evacuated. The town was rebuilt after the war.

Naval base

Den Helder is the principal base of the Royal Netherlands Navy. Facilities include the naval harbour, the Royal Netherlands Naval College (Koninklijk Instituut voor de Marine), and the Navy Museum (Marinemuseum), whose exhibits include the submarine HNLMS Tonijn and the ram ship Schorpioen.

Geography and sights

The municipality includes the town of Den Helder, the village of Huisduinen, and the hamlet of Friese Buurt. To the south lie extensive dune areas and bulb fields. Lange Jaap, a 63.45-metre cast-iron lighthouse at Huisduinen built in 1877, is one of the tallest traditional lighthouses in the world. Fort Kijkduin, a Napoleonic fortress, houses an aquarium and museum.

Transport

Den Helder has two railway stations, Den Helder and Den Helder Zuid, with services to Alkmaar, Amsterdam and beyond. The TESO ferry to Texel departs from the ferry terminal at 't Horntje road.
