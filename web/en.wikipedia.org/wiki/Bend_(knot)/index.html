Bend (knot)


Two ropes joined by a bend

A bend is a type of knot used to join two lengths of rope together. Bends are used in a variety of situations, including climbing, sailing, and securing loads. Because a join of two ropes is intrinsically weaker than a single unbroken length — typically retaining only part of the rope's full strength — bends must be chosen and tied with care, especially in safety-critical applications.

Characteristics

Bends are classified by the structure by which the two working ends interlock. A good bend holds under load, resists slipping or capsizing into an insecure form, and ideally can be untied after loading. Some bends work best in ropes of equal diameter; others, such as the sheet bend, are intended precisely for joining ropes of unequal size. Many bends are the "interlocked" counterparts of loops or hitches built from the same geometry.

List of bends

  • Sheet bend — the classic bend for joining ropes of different diameters; the double sheet bend adds security.
  • Fisherman's knot and double fisherman's knot — compact and strong, popular with climbers and anglers, but hard to untie after loading.
  • Flemish bend (figure-eight bend) — a strong, easily inspected bend traced from a figure-eight knot.
  • Carrick bend — a decorative, symmetrical bend that does not jam.
  • Butterfly bend — based on the alpine butterfly, effective in ropes of similar size.
  • Zeppelin bend — a secure, jam-resistant bend rediscovered in the 20th century.
  • Blood knot — used by anglers to join fishing lines of similar diameter.
