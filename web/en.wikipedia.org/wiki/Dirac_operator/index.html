Dirac operator

In mathematics and quantum mechanics, a Dirac operator is a differential operator that is a formal square root of a second-order operator such as a Laplacian — that is, an operator D such that D² is (up to lower-order terms) the Laplacian. The original example, introduced by Paul Dirac in 1928 in deriving his relativistic wave equation for the electron, acts on spinor fields and uses the gamma matrices of a Clifford algebra.

Formal definition

In general, a Dirac operator is a first-order differential operator D acting on sections of a vector bundle over a Riemannian manifold such that D² is a generalised Laplacian — a second-order operator whose principal symbol agrees with that of the Laplacian. On a spin manifold, the Dirac operator acts between spaces of spinors and is fundamental to spin geometry.

Applications

Dirac operators are central to the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, to Seiberg–Witten theory, and to noncommutative geometry, where a spectral triple's Dirac operator encodes the geometry of a space. In physics they describe fermions, and the spectrum of the Dirac operator in lattice gauge theory is used to study chiral symmetry and the quark condensate of QCD.
