Al-Ma'mun

Abū al-ʿAbbās ʿAbd Allāh ibn Hārūn al-Rashīd (13 September 786 – 9 August 833), better known by his regnal name al-Maʾmūn, was the seventh Abbasid caliph, reigning from 813 until his death. He succeeded his brother al-Amin after a civil war, the Fourth Fitna, in which his forces under Tahir ibn Husayn besieged and took Baghdad. His reign is celebrated in Islamic history for its patronage of learning and the translation movement.

Early life and civil war

Al-Ma'mun was the son of Harun al-Rashid by a Persian concubine, Marajil. Harun designated his elder son al-Amin as first heir and al-Ma'mun as second heir and governor of Khurasan. After Harun's death in 809, relations between the brothers broke down; war began in 811. Al-Ma'mun's general Tahir defeated al-Amin's armies and besieged Baghdad, which fell in 813, al-Amin being killed. Al-Ma'mun initially remained in Merv, governing through his vizier al-Fadl ibn Sahl, but disorders in Iraq led him to enter Baghdad in 819.

Patronage of learning

Al-Ma'mun is associated with the Bayt al-Ḥikma (House of Wisdom) in Baghdad and with state sponsorship of translating Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic. Scholars such as al-Khwarizmi, the Banu Musa brothers, al-Kindi and Hunayn ibn Ishaq flourished under his patronage. He sponsored astronomical observatories in Baghdad and Damascus and a programme to measure the Earth's circumference. His court is often portrayed as the high point of the early Abbasid translation movement.

Religious policy

Al-Ma'mun briefly designated the Alid imam Ali al-Rida as his successor in 817, which provoked severe backlash in Iraq, though the policy lapsed after al-Rida's death. Towards the end of his reign he adopted the Mu'tazilite doctrine of the createdness of the Qur'an and in 833 instituted the miḥna, an inquisition requiring judges and scholars to affirm it; the policy was enforced by his successors before being abandoned under al-Mutawakkil.

Later reign and death

Al-Ma'mun led campaigns in person against the Byzantine Empire in Anatolia and suppressed revolts in Egypt and elsewhere. He died in 833 near Tarsus while on campaign and was succeeded by his half-brother al-Mu'tasim.
