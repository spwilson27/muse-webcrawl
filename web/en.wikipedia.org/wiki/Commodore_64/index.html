Commodore 64

Manufacturer: Commodore International
Released: August 1982
CPU: MOS Technology 6510 @ ~1 MHz
Memory: 64 KB RAM
Graphics/sound: VIC-II / SID
Units sold: ~12.5–17 million (best-selling single computer model)

The Commodore 64, also known as the C64, is an 8-bit home computer introduced by Commodore International in January 1982 (released August 1982) at an introductory price of US$595. It is listed in Guinness World Records as the best-selling single computer model of all time, with estimates of 12.5 to 17 million units.

Hardware

The C64 used a MOS 6510 processor, 64 KB of RAM — exceptional for its price — the VIC-II graphics chip capable of sprites and smooth scrolling, and the SID (MOS 6581) sound chip, whose distinctive sound made it central to chiptune music and the demoscene. It connected to televisions, the 1541 floppy disk drive, datasette tape drives, cartridges and joysticks.

Software and impact

Preceded by the VIC-20 and succeeded in part by the Commodore 128, the C64 dominated the low-end home computer market in the mid-1980s, especially in the United States and Europe, with a vast library of games, educational and productivity software, and built-in Commodore BASIC.

Legacy

The C64 remains a focus of retrocomputing, emulation and new homebrew releases, and later inspired replica machines such as THEC64.
