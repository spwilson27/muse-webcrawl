Duke of Alba

The Duke of Alba (Spanish: Duque de Alba) is a Spanish hereditary title of nobility, accompanied by the dignity of Grandee of Spain. It was created in 1472 by King Henry IV of Castile, who elevated the county of Alba de Tormes into a dukedom for García Álvarez de Toledo, 2nd Count of Alba. The title is held by the House of Alba, one of the most prominent aristocratic families of Spain, whose principal seat is the Liria Palace in Madrid.

History

The Álvarez de Toledo family rose to power in the fifteenth century. The most famous holder of the title was Fernando Álvarez de Toledo, 3rd Duke of Alba (1507–1582), the celebrated general of Charles V and Philip II, whose governorship of the Spanish Netherlands (1567–1573) and establishment of the Council of Troubles made his name a byword for severity in Dutch and English memory. In later centuries the dukedom passed by inheritance and marriage through several houses, including the House of Silva and the FitzJames-Stuart family, descendants of James FitzJames, 1st Duke of Berwick, an illegitimate son of James II of England.

The 18th Duchess, Cayetana Fitz-James Stuart (1926–2014), was widely reported to hold more titles than any other aristocrat in the world. Her son, Carlos Fitz-James Stuart, became the 19th Duke of Alba in 2014.
