Fossil fuels are hydrocarbon-containing materials formed in the Earth's crust from the fossilised remains of dead plants and animals over millions of years. The main types are coal, petroleum and natural gas; they are extracted and burned for electricity, heat, transport and as chemical feedstocks.

Climate and transition

Fossil fuels powered industrialisation from the steam engine onward and still supply most world energy, but burning them is the largest source of greenhouse gas emissions driving climate change — hence global efforts to substitute renewables such as Solar energy and wind and to electrify transport and heating.
