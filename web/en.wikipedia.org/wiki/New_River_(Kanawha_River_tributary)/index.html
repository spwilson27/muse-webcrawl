New River (Kanawha River tributary)

Location: North Carolina, Virginia, West Virginia, USA
Length: about 320 mi (515 km)
Source: Blue Ridge Mountains, Ashe County, North Carolina
Mouth: Kanawha River at Gauley Bridge, West Virginia
Notable feature: New River Gorge

The New River is a river in the southeastern United States, flowing about 320 miles (515 km) from the Blue Ridge Mountains of North Carolina northwestward through Virginia and West Virginia, where it joins the Gauley River to form the Kanawha River at Gauley Bridge. Despite its name, the New River is considered one of the oldest rivers in North America geologically — some estimates place its course at tens of millions of years old — because it predates the uplift of the Appalachian Mountains through which it cuts.

Course

The river begins at the confluence of its North and South Forks in Ashe County, North Carolina, flows north into Virginia past Galax and Radford, is impounded by Claytor Lake, and continues past the city of Radford and Pearisburg before entering West Virginia. There it passes through the Bluestone Lake reservoir and the town of Hinton, then enters the New River Gorge, a deep canyon of whitewater rapids spanned by the New River Gorge Bridge. The gorge and surrounding lands became the New River Gorge National Park and Preserve in 2020, the United States' 63rd national park.

History

The river was known to Native Americans for millennia. European explorers of the 17th century, including the 1671 Batts and Fallam expedition, reached it from the east; because it flowed westward and had escaped earlier mapping, names given to it included "Wood's River" (after Abraham Wood). The name "New River" is traditionally said to derive from its appearing on later maps as a "new" river not shown earlier. The valley was an important coal-mining and railroad region in the 19th and 20th centuries, served by the Chesapeake and Ohio Railway; towns such as Thurmond boomed with the coal trade.

Recreation

The lower New River is famous for whitewater rafting and kayaking, with Class III–V rapids in the gorge, while the upper river in North Carolina and Virginia offers gentler canoeing. The gorge is also a renowned rock-climbing area and hosts "Bridge Day" each October, when BASE jumping from the New River Gorge Bridge is permitted.
