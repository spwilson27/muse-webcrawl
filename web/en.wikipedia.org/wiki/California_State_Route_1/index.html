State Route 1 (SR 1) is a major north–south state highway that runs along most of the Pacific coastline of the U.S. state of California. At 656 miles (1,056 km), it is the longest state route in California, and the second-longest in the United States after Montana Highway 200. SR 1 has several portions designated as either Pacific Coast Highway (commonly referred to as PCH), Cabrillo Highway, Shoreline Highway, or Coast Highway.

The highway is designated as an All-American Road for much of its length and is famous worldwide for running along some of the most beautiful coastlines in the United States, leading to its designation as an All-American Road. It is part of the California Freeway and Expressway System south of San Francisco, although much of it is a two-lane rural road.

Route description

SR 1 begins at Interstate 5 in Dana Point in Orange County and runs north along the coast through the Los Angeles metropolitan area — where it follows, variously, the Pacific Coast Highway through Malibu and Santa Monica — then through Ventura and Santa Barbara. From San Luis Obispo the road becomes the Cabrillo Highway and follows the dramatic Big Sur coast of Monterey County, crossing 33 bridges, most famously the Bixby Creek Bridge, one of the tallest single-span concrete bridges in the world. It passes through Monterey and Santa Cruz, then follows the San Mateo coast through Half Moon Bay before entering San Francisco, where it crosses the Golden Gate Bridge together with US 101.

North of the Golden Gate, SR 1 (now the Shoreline Highway) hugs the Marin County coast past Stinson Beach and Point Reyes, continues through Sonoma and Mendocino counties past Mendocino and Fort Bragg, and ends at US 101 in Leggett.

History

The route was built piecemeal beginning in the 1910s; the rugged Big Sur section, constructed largely by convict labour between 1919 and 1937, was the last major link, opened on June 27, 1937. The road received the State Route 1 designation in 1934 and was officially named in various segments in honour of explorer Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo and as the Pacific Coast Highway. In 1964, California's great highway renumbering systematised the route, and portions in Southern California were removed from or added to the state highway system over the following decades.

SR 1 is vulnerable to closures from landslides along the Big Sur coast; notable closures followed heavy rains and slides in 1983, 1998, 2017 (the Mud Creek slide, one of the largest in California history, which closed the highway for over a year) and 2023–2024.

Major intersections

  • I-5 in Dana Point (southern terminus)
  • I-405 in Seal Beach and near Los Angeles International Airport
  • I-10 in Santa Monica
  • US 101 (concurrent at various points, including Santa Barbara, San Luis Obispo, and the Golden Gate Bridge)
  • SR 68 in Monterey
  • SR 17 in Santa Cruz
  • I-280 in Daly City and San Francisco
  • US 101 in Leggett (northern terminus)

In popular culture

The Pacific Coast Highway is one of the most iconic drives in the world and features in countless films, television programmes, songs and advertisements, symbolising the California lifestyle and the freedom of the open road. The Malibu and Big Sur stretches are especially frequently depicted.
