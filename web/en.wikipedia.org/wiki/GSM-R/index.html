GSM-R (Global System for Mobile Communications – Railway) is an international wireless communications standard for railway communication and applications. A sub-system of the European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS), it carries signalling data between trains and control centres for the European Train Control System (ETCS) and provides voice communication between drivers, signallers and other railway staff.

Features

GSM-R is built on the commercial GSM standard but uses dedicated frequencies near 900 MHz and adds railway-specific functions: functional addressing (calling a driver by train number rather than telephone number), location-dependent addressing, group calls, voice broadcast, and priority and pre-emption for emergency railway calls. Data transmission over GSM-R supports ETCS Level 2 and 3 operation, in which movement authorities are sent to the train by radio.

Deployment

The standard was specified in the 1990s under the EIRENE project of the International Union of Railways (UIC). It has since been deployed across most of Europe's rail networks and in countries including China, India, Australia and parts of Africa and the Middle East, covering hundreds of thousands of kilometres of track.

Future

Because GSM technology is ageing, the UIC is developing a successor, the Future Railway Mobile Communication System (FRMCS), based on 5G. Migration from GSM-R is expected to take place gradually from the late 2020s into the 2030s, with the two systems operating side by side during the transition.
