Interest rate risk

Interest rate risk is the risk that changes in market interest rates will adversely affect the value of an investment or an institution's earnings and capital. It is a central concern for banks, insurers, pension funds, and bond investors.

Mechanisms

When interest rates rise, the market value of existing fixed-rate bonds falls, because their coupons are less attractive than newly issued debt; the longer the duration of the bond, the greater the price change. For banks, risk arises from mismatches between the repricing of assets and liabilities — for example, funding long-term fixed-rate loans with short-term deposits, so that net interest income shrinks when rates rise.

Measurement and management

Common measures include duration and convexity, value at risk, and repricing gap analysis. Interest rate risk is managed by matching the maturities of assets and liabilities, and by using derivatives such as interest rate swaps, futures, and options to hedge.

Regulation

Banking supervisors, under the Basel framework, require banks to measure and hold capital against interest rate risk in the banking book, and stress tests examine resilience to large rate movements.
