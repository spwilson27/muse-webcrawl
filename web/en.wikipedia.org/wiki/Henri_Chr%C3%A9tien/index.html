Henri Jacques Chrétien (1 February 1879 – 6 February 1956) was a French astronomer and optical inventor. He is best known as the co-inventor, with George Willis Ritchey, of the Ritchey–Chrétien telescope design used in most modern professional telescopes, and as the inventor of the anamorphic lens system that led to CinemaScope.

Career

Chrétien worked at the Nice Observatory, where he met Ritchey in 1910. Together they developed the aplanatic two-mirror telescope design bearing their names, which corrects both spherical aberration and coma; the Hubble Space Telescope is a Ritchey–Chrétien design. He later founded the Institut d'Optique in Paris and taught at the Sorbonne.

Anamorphic lens

In the 1920s Chrétien developed the Hypergonar, an anamorphic lens that squeezed a wide image horizontally onto standard film. In 1952, 20th Century Fox acquired the rights to develop it into CinemaScope, and Chrétien received an Academy Award in 1954 for his invention.
