Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSK) is a cancer treatment and research institution in New York City, among the oldest and largest private cancer centres in the world. It comprises Memorial Hospital, a 498-bed specialist hospital, and the Sloan Kettering Institute, a research arm whose scientists pursue basic, translational and clinical investigation into the causes and treatment of cancer. MSK is designated a comprehensive cancer center by the US National Cancer Institute.

History

The institution began in 1884 as the New York Cancer Hospital, founded with a gift from John Jacob Astor III and others on Manhattan's Upper West Side — one of the first hospitals in the United States devoted exclusively to cancer. In 1910 it became the James Ewing Hospital society's Memorial Hospital. In 1945, industrialist Alfred P. Sloan and inventor Charles F. Kettering, through the General Motors research tradition, endowed the Sloan Kettering Institute for cancer research; hospital and institute were united under one governance in 1960 and renamed Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in 1980. The centre moved to its York Avenue site on the Upper East Side in the 1930s–40s.

Research and treatment

MSK is known for pioneering work in chemotherapy, immunotherapy (including early development of checkpoint inhibitors and CAR T-cell therapies), cancer genetics — notably the MSK-IMPACT genomic sequencing panel — bone marrow transplantation and precision oncology. It trains large numbers of oncologists and researchers through fellowship and graduate programmes, including the Gerstner Sloan Kettering Graduate School of Biomedical Sciences.

Facilities

Besides the main York Avenue campus, MSK operates regional outpatient sites across the New York metropolitan area, including in New Jersey, Westchester and Long Island, and the David H. Koch Center for Cancer Care (opened 2020). It consistently ranks among the top cancer hospitals in US surveys.
