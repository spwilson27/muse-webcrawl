Little Rann of Kutch

The Little Rann of Kutch is a salt marsh in the Kutch district of Gujarat, India, forming the smaller south-eastern part of the Rann of Kutch. Covering roughly 5,000 square kilometres when combined with its protected sanctuary, it is a seasonal wetland: flooded by monsoon waters and river inflow for part of the year, then drying into a cracked salt desert.

Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary

Most of the Little Rann is protected as the Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary, established in 1973 and one of the largest wildlife sanctuaries in India. It is the last refuge of the Indian wild ass (Equus hemionus khur), whose population has recovered from under 400 animals in the 1960s to several thousand. The sanctuary also supports nilgai, chinkara, desert fox, wolf, and large wintering flocks of flamingos and other waterbirds.

Salt production

The Little Rann is a major source of inland salt. Agariya salt workers pump subsoil brine into shallow pans and harvest the crystallised salt over an eight-month season, enduring extreme conditions; salt farming families supply a substantial share of India's inland salt output.
