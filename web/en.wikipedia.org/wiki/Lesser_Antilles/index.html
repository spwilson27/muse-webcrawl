Overview

The Lesser Antilles is a long arc of small islands in the Caribbean Sea, forming the eastern boundary between the Caribbean and the Atlantic Ocean. It runs from the Virgin Islands in the north to Grenada in the south (the Windward and Leeward Islands), with the Leeward Antilles (Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao) along the Venezuelan coast. The islands sit above the subduction zone where Atlantic crust sinks beneath the Caribbean Plate, and many are active or dormant volcanoes.

Volcanism and nations

The 1902 eruption of Mount Pelée on Martinique destroyed Saint-Pierre, killing some 28,000–30,000 people; the Soufrière Hills eruption on Montserrat (from 1995) buried its capital, Plymouth. Island nations and territories here include Barbados, Saint Lucia, Antigua and Barbuda, Dominica, Grenada, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Saint Kitts and Nevis, plus French, Dutch, British and US territories.
