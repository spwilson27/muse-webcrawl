Province of South Carolina

The Province of South Carolina, originally known as Clarendon Province, was a province of the Kingdom of Great Britain that existed in North America between 1712 and 1776. It was one of the five Southern colonies and one of the thirteen American colonies that later declared independence from Great Britain in 1776, becoming the U.S. state of South Carolina.

History

Carolina was chartered in 1663 to eight Lords Proprietors, and permanent English settlement began with the founding of Charles Town in 1670. Differences between the northern and southern settlements led to a de facto division in 1712, formalised when the Crown bought out the proprietors in 1729. The southern province, South Carolina, developed a plantation economy based on rice and indigo, worked by enslaved Africans, who by the early 18th century formed a majority of the population — the only mainland colony with a Black majority.

Government and society

The province was governed by a royal governor, an appointed council, and an elected Commons House of Assembly dominated by wealthy Lowcountry planters. Charles Town (renamed Charleston in 1783) was one of the largest and richest cities in British North America and the main port of entry for enslaved Africans. The colony's history was marked by conflicts including the Yamasee War (1715–1717) and the Stono Rebellion (1739), the largest slave uprising in the mainland colonies before the Revolution.

Revolution

South Carolina played a major role in the American Revolution: its delegates signed the Declaration of Independence in 1776, the province adopted a state constitution that year, and the southern theatre of the war saw key battles at Charleston, Camden, Cowpens and Eutaw Springs. The province ceased to exist as the State of South Carolina took its place.
