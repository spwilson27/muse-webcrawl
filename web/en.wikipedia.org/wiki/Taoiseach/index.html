Taoiseach

The Taoiseach (pronounced roughly "TEE-shock") is the head of government of Ireland and the leader of the cabinet. The office was established by the Constitution of Ireland in 1937, replacing the earlier title of President of the Executive Council used under the Irish Free State.

Appointment and role

The Taoiseach is nominated by Dáil Éireann (the lower house of the Oireachtas) and appointed by the President of Ireland. The Taoiseach appoints the other members of the government and the Tánaiste (deputy prime minister), may advise the President to dissolve the Dáil and call a general election, and represents Ireland at the European Council.

History of the office

The first Taoiseach was Éamon de Valera, who dominated Irish politics for decades; later holders include Seán Lemass, Jack Lynch, Charles Haughey, John Bruton, Bertie Ahern, Brian Cowen, Enda Kenny, Leo Varadkar — the youngest person and first of Indian heritage to hold the office — and Micheál Martin. The word taoiseach is an old Irish word for a chieftain or leader, revived for the constitution.

Department and residence

The Department of the Taoiseach supports the office from Government Buildings on Merrion Street, Dublin. Unlike many heads of government, the Taoiseach has no large official residence; Steward's Lodge at Farmleigh is available for use.
