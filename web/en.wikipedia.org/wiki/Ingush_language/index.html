Ingush language

Ingush (ГӀалгӀай мотт, Ğalğai mott) is a Northeast Caucasian language spoken by the Ingush people, primarily in the Republic of Ingushetia and Chechnya in the North Caucasus of Russia. Together with Chechen and Bats it forms the Nakh branch of the Northeast Caucasian family. Ingush and Chechen are closely related but not fully mutually intelligible. The language has on the order of a few hundred thousand speakers.

Classification and features

Like Chechen, Ingush is an ergative–absolutive language with a large consonant inventory including ejective and pharyngeal consonants, a system of noun classes (genders) marked by prefixes on verbs and adjectives, and complex verb morphology. Vowel systems feature long and short vowels and umlaut phenomena.

Writing system

Ingush was first written in an Arabic-based script in the 19th and early 20th centuries, then in a Latin alphabet from the 1920s, and since 1938 in a Cyrillic-based alphabet with the additional letter Ӏ (palochka) used to write ejective consonants and the glottal stop.

Status

Ingush is an official language of Ingushetia alongside Russian and is used in local media and education, though Russian dominates urban and administrative life. UNESCO classifies Ingush as vulnerable.
