Paraproteinemia

SpecialtyHematology
SynonymsMonoclonal gammopathy
ICD-10D47.2

Paraproteinemia, also known as monoclonal gammopathy, is the presence of excessive amounts of a single monoclonal immunoglobulin, called a paraprotein (or M protein), in the blood. The condition is caused by an underlying clonal proliferation of plasma cells or B lymphocytes that produce the abnormal protein.

Paraproteinemias range from the common and usually benign monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) to malignant conditions such as multiple myeloma, Waldenström macroglobulinemia, and amyloidosis.

Classification

Internationally agreed criteria classify paraproteinemias according to the type of immunoglobulin involved and the presence or absence of end-organ damage:

  • Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS)
  • Smoldering multiple myeloma
  • Multiple myeloma
  • Waldenström macroglobulinemia (IgM paraprotein)
  • AL amyloidosis
  • Monoclonal gammopathy of renal significance (MGRS)

Diagnosis

Paraproteins are most commonly detected by serum protein electrophoresis, which reveals a discrete band (M spike) in the gamma region, followed by immunofixation electrophoresis to identify the heavy and light chain type. Free light chain assays and bone marrow examination help determine the underlying disorder. Quantification of the paraprotein over time is used to monitor disease progression and response to treatment.

Associated conditions

Paraproteins may be associated with neuropathies, cryoglobulinemia, cold agglutinin disease, and POEMS syndrome. Even small paraproteins can cause significant harm when they deposit in tissues, as in AL amyloidosis, or exert autoantibody activity.
