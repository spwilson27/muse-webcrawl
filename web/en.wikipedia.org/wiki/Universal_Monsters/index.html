The Universal Monsters (also known as Universal Studios Monsters or Universal Classic Monsters) are a series of horror film characters and a media franchise originating in the horror films produced by Universal Pictures between the 1920s and the 1950s. The franchise rejuvenated the horror genre in American cinema and its principal characters — Dracula, Frankenstein's monster, the Mummy, the Invisible Man, the Bride of Frankenstein, the Wolf Man and the Creature from the Black Lagoon — remain icons of popular culture.

History

Universal's horror cycle began in the silent era with Lon Chaney's performances in The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1923) and The Phantom of the Opera (1925). The golden age opened with Dracula (1931), starring Bela Lugosi, followed the same year by Frankenstein, directed by James Whale, with Boris Karloff as the Monster and Jack Pierce's landmark makeup design. The Mummy (1932), The Invisible Man (1933) and Whale's Bride of Frankenstein (1935) consolidated the cycle.

A second wave in the 1940s, after the Laemmle family lost control of the studio, brought The Wolf Man (1941) with Lon Chaney Jr., numerous sequels, and multi-monster "monster rally" pictures such as Frankenstein Meets the Wolf Man (1943) and House of Frankenstein (1944). The cycle wound down with Creature from the Black Lagoon (1954), the comedy Abbott and Costello Meet Frankenstein (1948) having already heralded its popular afterlife in syndication and merchandising.

Legacy

The 1957 "Shock Theater" television package introduced the monsters to a new generation, inspiring Famous Monsters of Filmland magazine and an enduring merchandising line. Universal has repeatedly attempted revivals, from The Mummy (1999) to the short-lived "Dark Universe" launched by The Mummy (2017) and the standalone The Invisible Man (2020). The original films are preserved in the United States National Film Registry.
