Adygea

The Republic of Adygea (Russian: Республика Адыгея; Adyghe: Адыгэ Республик), also known as the Adyghe Republic, is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe. It is an enclave entirely surrounded by Krasnodar Krai. Its capital and largest city is Maykop. The republic covers about 7,800 square kilometres and has a population of roughly half a million.

Area: ~7,800 km²

Geography

Adygea stretches from the Kuban River plain in the north to the foothills of the Greater Caucasus in the south. The Belaya River, a tributary of the Kuban, is its principal waterway. Part of the Caucasus Nature Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage site, lies within the republic, and its forests cover a large share of the territory.

History

The region is the historic homeland of the Circassians (Adyghe). Following the Russian conquest of Circassia in the 19th century, much of the population was expelled or emigrated to the Ottoman Empire. In 1922 the area became the Adyghe Autonomous Oblast within the Russian SFSR; it was renamed the Adyghe Autonomous Oblast of Krasnodar Krai and, in July 1991, raised to the status of a republic within the Russian Federation.

Demographics

Ethnic Russians form a majority of the population (around 60%), while the Adyghe (Circassians) make up roughly a quarter. Both Russian and Adyghe, a Northwest Caucasian language, are official. Islam (chiefly Sunni) and Orthodox Christianity are the main religions.

Economy

Agriculture and food processing dominate the economy, including the famous Adyghe cheese; timber, oil and natural gas extraction, and tourism in the Caucasus foothills also contribute.
