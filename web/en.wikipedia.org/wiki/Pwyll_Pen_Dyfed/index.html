Pwyll Pen Dyfed

Pwyll Pen Dyfed ("Pwyll, Head of Dyfed") is the First Branch of the Mabinogi, the collection of medieval Welsh prose tales known as the Four Branches of the Mabinogi. It tells of Pwyll, prince of Dyfed in southwest Wales: his year-long exchange of shape and realm with Arawn, lord of Annwn (the Otherworld); his winning of the beautiful Rhiannon; and the mysterious birth, disappearance and recovery of his son Pryderi.

Summary

Hunting in Glyn Cuch, Pwyll offends Arawn, king of Annwn, by driving off Arawn's hounds and setting his own on a stag. To make amends, Pwyll agrees to exchange shapes and kingdoms for a year and a day, and to slay Arawn's enemy Hafgan — striking him only once, since a second blow would revive him. Pwyll rules Annwn wisely, defeats Hafgan with a single blow, and famously refrains from sleeping with Arawn's queen, earning Arawn's lasting friendship and the title "Head of Annwn" (Pen Annwfn).

Later, seated on the mound of Gorsedd Arberth, where wonders or blows are promised to whoever sits, Pwyll sees the lady Rhiannon riding past on a horse no rider can overtake, though it seems to amble. She stops for him of her own accord and chooses him over her unwanted betrothed, Gwawl. By her instructions, Pwyll wins her at a feast a year later, outwitting Gwawl with a small bag that cannot be filled and trapping him inside it — the game of "badger-in-the-bag" — until he surrenders his claim.

On the night their son is born, the child vanishes while his six attendant ladies sleep; fearing blame, they smear the sleeping Rhiannon with puppy's blood and accuse her of eating the baby. As penance Rhiannon must sit at the horse-block outside the court and offer to carry guests on her back. Meanwhile Teyrnon, lord of Gwent Is Coed, finds a baby left at his stable on May Eve — the night his mare's foals always disappear — after cutting off a monstrous claw reaching through the window. He and his wife raise the boy as Gwri Golden-Hair. Recognising his likeness to Pwyll, they restore him to Dyfed; Rhiannon is freed from her penance, and the boy is named Pryderi (from pryder, "care/anxiety").

Themes and legacy

The tale combines Otherworld sojourn, magical marriage and the "calumniated wife" motif. Rhiannon — whose name derives from *Rigantona, "great queen" — is often regarded as a euhemerised goddess figure. The First Branch sets up the dynastic line of Dyfed followed through the remaining branches, in which Pryderi is the only character to appear in all four.
