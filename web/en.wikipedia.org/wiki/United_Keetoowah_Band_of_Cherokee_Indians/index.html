United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians

The United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians in Oklahoma (UKB) is a federally recognized Native American tribe based in Tahlequah, Oklahoma. It is one of three federally recognized Cherokee tribes, alongside the Cherokee Nation and the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians. The UKB represents descendants of Cherokee people—particularly the “Old Settlers” and traditionalist communities—who moved west before and during the era of forced Cherokee removal.

History

The name Keetoowah derives from Kituwah, an ancient Cherokee mother town and a word used for Cherokee people and their original language and traditions. In the nineteenth century, Keetoowah societies preserved ceremonial grounds, language and traditional governance in Indian Territory. After allotment and Oklahoma statehood weakened tribal institutions, Cherokee traditionalists organized the United Keetoowah Band. It was federally recognized in 1950 under the Oklahoma Indian Welfare Act.

Government and citizenship

The tribe is governed by an elected chief, assistant chief, secretary, treasurer and a council representing districts in northeastern Oklahoma. Its headquarters and tribal complex are near Tahlequah. Citizenship is governed by tribal law and is tied to descent from the UKB base roll and a required degree of Cherokee Indian blood, making its enrollment rules distinct from those of the Cherokee Nation.

Culture and services

The UKB works to preserve the Cherokee language, stomp grounds and traditional crafts. It operates tribal programs in areas such as housing, education, health support, historic preservation and economic development, and runs gaming and hospitality enterprises in Oklahoma. Its cultural institutions emphasize the continuity of Keetoowah religious societies and the experiences of Cherokee communities that settled in the West.
