Casabe

Casabe is a thin, crisp flatbread made from cassava (yuca) root, originating with the Taíno and other Indigenous peoples of the Caribbean and northern South America. It remains a staple in Venezuela, the Dominican Republic, Cuba, and other Caribbean countries.

Preparation

Bitter cassava is peeled, grated, and pressed — traditionally in a woven sebucán sleeve — to expel its toxic juice (which contains cyanogenic compounds). The dry pulp is sieved into flour (catibía) and spread in a thin round on a hot griddle (budare) or clay plate, cooking into a large white disc. Sun-dried casabe keeps for months without refrigeration.

Use

Casabe is eaten as a bread substitute, dipped in soups and stews, topped with cheese, garlic, or avocado, or toasted with olive oil. In 2024 UNESCO inscribed the traditional production and consumption of casabe on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, in a multinational nomination led by Venezuela with Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Haiti, and Honduras.
