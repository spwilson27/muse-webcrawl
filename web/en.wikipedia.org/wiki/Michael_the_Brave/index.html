Michael the Brave (Romanian: Mihai Viteazul [miˈhaj viˈte̯azul]; 1558 – 19 August 1601), born Mihai Pătrașcu, was the Prince of Wallachia (as Michael II, 1593–1601), Prince of Transylvania (1599–1600) and Prince of Moldavia (1600). Under his rule, the three principalities that form present-day Romania, together with parts of today's Moldova, came under a single ruler for the first time in 1600, a union later celebrated as a precursor of the modern Romanian state.

Early life and rise to power

Michael is traditionally considered the son of the Wallachian prince Pătrașcu the Good, though this parentage has been debated. He rose through the ranks of the boyar nobility, serving as ban of Mehedinți, stolnic and postelnic before becoming Ban of Craiova. In 1593, with Ottoman support, he ascended the Wallachian throne.

War against the Ottomans

Soon after taking power Michael turned against his Ottoman overlords. In 1594 he joined the Holy League of Pope Clement VIII and began a revolt, massacring Ottoman creditors in Bucharest and attacking fortresses along the Danube. His most famous victory came at the Battle of Călugăreni (23 August 1595), where his forces checked the much larger army of Sinan Pasha, though the Ottomans briefly occupied Bucharest and Târgoviște before a counteroffensive, aided by Sigismund Báthory of Transylvania, drove them back across the Danube.

Union of the three principalities

In October 1599 Michael defeated Andrew Báthory at the Battle of Șelimbăr and entered Alba Iulia, becoming ruler of Transylvania as imperial governor on behalf of Emperor Rudolf II. In May 1600 he invaded Moldavia and expelled Ieremia Movilă, uniting Wallachia, Transylvania and Moldavia under his personal rule. The union lasted only a few months: Transylvanian nobles revolted, and with Polish intervention in Moldavia and Wallachia, Michael's position collapsed in the autumn of 1600.

Death and legacy

Michael travelled to Vienna and Prague to regain imperial favour, and in 1601 returned to Transylvania alongside the imperial general Giorgio Basta, defeating the forces of Sigismund Báthory at Guruslău on 3 August 1601. Days later, on 19 August 1601, he was assassinated at Câmpia Turzii on Basta's orders by Walloon mercenaries. In the nineteenth century Romanian national historiography elevated Michael the Brave into a national hero, the first unifier of the Romanian lands.
