Tafamidis

Trade names: Vyndaqel, Vyndamax

Class: Transthyretin stabilizer

Routes: Oral

Tafamidis, sold under the brand names Vyndaqel and Vyndamax, is a medication used to treat transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR), a disease in which misfolded transthyretin protein deposits as amyloid in nerves and the heart. It is used for the cardiomyopathy of wild-type or hereditary ATTR in adults, and in some jurisdictions for early-stage transthyretin familial amyloid polyneuropathy.

Mechanism of action

Tafamidis is a kinetic stabilizer of transthyretin. It binds selectively to the thyroxine-binding sites of the transthyretin tetramer, slowing its dissociation into monomers — the rate-limiting step in transthyretin amyloid formation — and thereby slowing disease progression.

History and approvals

The drug was developed from academic research on transthyretin chemistry, principally by the laboratory of Jeffery Kelly at Scripps Research, and commercialised by FoldRx, which Pfizer acquired in 2010. Tafamidis was approved in the European Union in 2011 (as Vyndaqel) for familial amyloid polyneuropathy, and by the US Food and Drug Administration in May 2019 for ATTR cardiomyopathy. A large trial (ATTR-ACT) showed it reduced all-cause mortality and cardiovascular hospitalisations in patients with transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy. It has been noted as one of the most expensive cardiovascular drugs, prompting debate about pricing.

Common side effects are generally mild and include urinary tract infections and diarrhoea.
