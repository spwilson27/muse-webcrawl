The Isle of Man TT (Tourist Trophy) is an annual motorcycle racing event held on the Isle of Man in late May and early June. First run in 1907, it is among the oldest and most famous motorcycle races in the world, contested over the Snaefell Mountain Course, a 37.73-mile circuit of closed public roads.

History

Racing on the island began because motor racing on public roads was prohibited in mainland Britain. The first TT in 1907 used the shorter St John's course; the Mountain Course followed from 1911, with the Senior TT as its premier race. The event formed part of the motorcycle World Championship from 1949 until 1976, when its Grand Prix status was transferred to mainland Britain because of safety concerns. The TT nonetheless flourished as a specialist road race, with time-trial starts, riders setting off at intervals and racing the clock over multiple laps; the fastest lap average speeds exceed 136 mph.

Riders and records

Joey Dunlop, with 26 victories between 1977 and 2000, remains the most successful TT rider; his nephew Michael Dunlop and John McGuinness are among the leading winners of the modern era. The races span classes from sidecars to 1000 cc superbikes, and since 2010 have included electric machines (the TT Zero and its successors).

Danger

The Mountain Course is widely regarded as one of the most dangerous circuits in motorsport: more than 260 competitors and officials have been killed in the TT and the Manx Grand Prix since 1907. The event's future is debated periodically, but it remains a cornerstone of the island's economy and identity.
