Mikhail Skobelev

Mikhail Dmitriyevich Skobelev (Russian: Михаил Дмитриевич Скобелев; 29 September 1843 – 7 July 1882) was a Russian general famous for his conquests of Central Asia and his heroism in the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78. Dressed in a white uniform and riding a white horse, he became known to his men as the "White General" and to his Turkmen opponents as "Bloody Eyes" for the ferocity of his campaigns.

Career

Skobelev came from a military family and rose rapidly in the campaigns that annexed the khanates of Central Asia: he participated in the Khivan campaign of 1873, crushed the Kokand rebellion of 1875–76, and became the first governor of the Fergana Valley. In the Russo-Turkish War he won fame at the Third Battle of Plevna and in the winter crossing of the Balkan Mountains at Shipka–Sheinovo, which opened the way to Constantinople and the Treaty of San Stefano.

His final campaign was the conquest of Turkmenistan, where in January 1881 he stormed the fortress of Geok-Tepe after a siege — a victory achieved with heavy civilian loss that remains controversial. Lionized at home as a pan-Slavic hero and mentioned as a possible political rival to the establishment, Skobelev died suddenly of a heart attack in a Moscow hotel in 1882, aged 38.

Legacy

Streets, squares, and the Bulgarian city of Skobelev's namesake commemorations honored him across Russia and Bulgaria; the city of Fergana (founded as Novy Margelan) was renamed Skobelev in his lifetime. Much of his monumental commemoration was removed after the Russian Revolution, though his reputation in Bulgaria as a liberator endures.
