Independence Hall

Independence Hall is a historic civic building in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, where the United States Declaration of Independence was adopted on 4 July 1776 and the United States Constitution was debated and signed in 1787. Completed in 1753 as the Pennsylvania State House, the red-brick Georgian building stands on Chestnut Street between 5th and 6th Streets, at the heart of Independence National Historical Park. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is depicted on the reverse of the US $100 bill.

Construction

The Pennsylvania colonial assembly authorised the building in 1732; construction, supervised by lawyer Andrew Hamilton and built by master carpenter Edmund Woolley, proceeded slowly and the State House was substantially completed in 1753. The original bell tower's wooden steeple rotted and was removed in 1781; the brick tower housed the bell now known as the Liberty Bell, which had been ordered in 1751 for the building and cracked early in its use. In 1828 the city hired William Strickland to restore a steeple in the original style, giving the building its present silhouette.

Revolutionary era

From May 1775 the Second Continental Congress met in the Assembly Room. There it appointed George Washington commander of the Continental Army, adopted the Declaration of Independence on 4 July 1776, adopted the Articles of Confederation, and — after Philadelphia's occupation by the British in 1777–78 and a later mutiny by unpaid soldiers drove Congress away in 1783 — the room hosted the Constitutional Convention of 1787, which drafted and signed the US Constitution on 17 September 1787. The building served as Pennsylvania's state house until the capital moved to Lancaster in 1799.

Later history

The City of Philadelphia purchased the square and buildings in 1816, preserving them from demolition. The hall hosted historic moments including Abraham Lincoln's speech and flag-raising in February 1861 on his way to his inauguration; after his assassination, his body lay in state there in April 1865. In 1948 the site became the core of Independence National Historical Park, administered by the National Park Service, and the interior was restored to its 18th-century appearance. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979.

The Assembly Room

The ground-floor Assembly Room, where both the Declaration and the Constitution were signed, is furnished as it would have appeared in the 18th century, with the original "Rising Sun" chair used by George Washington while presiding over the Constitutional Convention — the chair Benjamin Franklin mused upon at the signing, saying he had often wondered whether the sun carved behind the President was rising or setting, and now knew it was a rising sun. An original printed copy of the Declaration is among the documents displayed in the hall.

Legacy

Independence Hall is one of the most recognisable symbols of the United States; full-scale replicas have been built in the United States and abroad, and its image appears on the back of the $100 bill. The Liberty Bell, moved across the street to the Liberty Bell Center in 2003, remains paired with the hall in the public imagination as an emblem of American independence.
