Unidentified flying object (UFO)
Also known asUAP (unidentified anomalous phenomenon); flying saucer
Term coined1953, US Air Force (popularised by Donald Keyhoe)
First modern report1947, Kenneth Arnold sighting

An unidentified flying object (UFO), or unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP), is any perceived airborne, submerged or transmedium phenomenon that cannot be immediately identified or explained. Upon investigation, most UFOs are identified as known objects or atmospheric phenomena, while a small number remain unexplained.

While unusual sightings in the sky have been reported throughout history, UFOs became culturally prominent after World War II, escalating during the Space Age. The term was coined by the United States Air Force in 1953. Studies and investigations — including Project Blue Book in the United States — have concluded that the majority of reports have mundane explanations, and no scientific consensus exists that any UFO is of extraterrestrial origin.

History

In June 1947, private pilot Kenneth Arnold reported seeing nine crescent-shaped objects flying at high speed near Mount Rainier in Washington state. Press coverage described them as "flying saucers", a phrase that entered popular culture, and the ensuing wave of reports included the Roswell incident in New Mexico that July. The US Air Force investigated sightings through Project Sign (1948), Project Grudge, and Project Blue Book (1952–1969), which catalogued more than 12,000 reports. The 1968 Condon Report concluded that further scientific study of UFOs was unlikely to be productive.

Modern investigations

Interest revived in the 2010s and 2020s with the release of US Navy cockpit videos and the establishment of official US government offices to study UAP, including the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO). NASA convened an independent study team in 2022. Official reports have attributed most resolved cases to balloons, drones, birds, aircraft, satellites, and sensor artefacts, while noting that some cases lack sufficient data for a conclusion.

Explanations

Prosaic explanations for reports include misidentified aircraft, astronomical objects (such as Venus), weather balloons, atmospheric optical phenomena, and hoaxes. Hypotheses advanced for the unexplained residue include the extraterrestrial hypothesis and various psychosocial explanations. UFOs are also a major theme in popular culture, including claims of alien abduction and conspiracy theories about government secrecy.
