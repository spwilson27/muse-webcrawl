Stonehaven

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stonehaven (Scots: Steenhive; Scottish Gaelic: Baile nan Cloch) is a town in Aberdeenshire, Scotland. It lies on Scotland's northeast coast, about 24 kilometres (15 mi) south of Aberdeen, and had a population of roughly 11,000 at the 2011 census. The town grew around an Iron Age fishing village and later around its harbour, and is the historic county town of Kincardineshire.

History

The area around Stonehaven has been settled since prehistory; the nearby Fetteresso Castle and Spurryhillock are associated with early settlement, and Dunnottar Castle, on a dramatic headland a short distance south of the town, was a key stronghold in Scottish history, famously sheltering the Honours of Scotland during Cromwell's invasion in the 17th century. Stonehaven's old tolbooth, built in the late 16th century, served as a courthouse and prison and is now a museum. The town developed as a herring port in the 19th century.

Landmarks and culture

The town is known for its Hogmanay fireballs ceremony, in which townspeople parade along the High Street at midnight swinging flaming balls of rags and paraffin. Stonehaven has an open-air heated seawater swimming pool, built in the 1930s, and the ruins of the medieval Chapel of St Mary and St Nathalan at Kirkton of Fetteresso. Robert William Thomson, inventor of the pneumatic tyre, was born in Stonehaven.

Transport

Stonehaven railway station lies on the line between Aberdeen and Dundee and Edinburgh. In August 2020 a passenger train derailed near Carmont, west of the town, after striking a landslip, killing three people.
