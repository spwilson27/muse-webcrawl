Ewell

District: Epsom and Ewell

County: Surrey, England

Population: approx. 34,000

Ewell is a town in the borough of Epsom and Ewell in Surrey, England, at the head of the Hogsmill River about 13 miles south-west of central London. It is a suburban town with a historic village core, bordered by Nonsuch Park to the east and adjoining Epsom to the south. Its name derives from Old English æwell, meaning a spring or river source, referring to the spring line where the chalk of the North Downs meets the London Clay — a source of the Hogsmill.

History

Settlement at the springs is ancient; Roman material has been found along nearby Stane Street. Ewell appears in Domesday Book (1086) as Etwell. In the sixteenth century the town was overshadowed by Henry VIII's Nonsuch Palace, built from 1538 just to the east and demolished in the 1680s; Nonsuch Mansion and its park survive. In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries Ewell was known for gunpowder mills on the Hogsmill and for its market. The railways arrived in 1847 (Ewell West station) and 1859 (Ewell East), turning the village into a commuter suburb, and it merged administratively with Epsom in 1937.

Landmarks and education

St Mary's Church, designed with a surviving medieval tower, stands in the old village centre near Bourne Hall, a striking circular 1970 building housing a library, museum and theatre in Bourne Park. The town is home to the North East Surrey College of Technology (NESCOT). Ewell Castle School occupies a mock-castle house of 1814.
