Turkish Armenians are the ethnic Armenian citizens of the Republic of Turkey. They are one of the country's oldest indigenous communities, descended from the Armenian population of Anatolia, whose presence in the region stretches back more than two thousand years. Today the community is concentrated overwhelmingly in Istanbul, where the Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople has its seat.

History

Armenians formed a substantial part of the population of the Ottoman Empire, particularly in the eastern provinces (the "six vilayets"), Cilicia, and the capital. During the First World War, the Ottoman government carried out deportations and massacres — the Armenian genocide of 1915–1917 — which destroyed the Armenian population of Anatolia almost entirely. Survivors fled abroad or to Istanbul, which was largely spared.

Under the 1923 Treaty of Lausanne, Armenians were recognized as a non-Muslim minority in the new Turkish Republic. Nevertheless, discriminatory measures such as the 1942 Wealth Tax (Varlık Vergisi) and the Istanbul pogrom of 6–7 September 1955 drove further emigration. The community also suffered from the assassination of the journalist Hrant Dink in 2007, which sparked large public demonstrations.

Community life

The community maintains churches, schools, hospitals (such as Surp Pırgiç), foundations, and Armenian-language newspapers including Agos, Jamanak, and Marmara. The village of Vakıflı in Hatay Province is Turkey's last remaining ethnic Armenian village. Western Armenian is classified by UNESCO as an endangered language in Turkey.
