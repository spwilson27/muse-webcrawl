Jacob Coxey

Jacob Sechler Coxey Sr.

Born: April 16, 1854, Selinsgrove, Pennsylvania

Died: May 18, 1951, Massillon, Ohio

Known for: Leading Coxey's Army (1894)

Offices: Mayor of Massillon, Ohio; candidate for Congress and the U.S. Senate

Jacob Sechler Coxey Sr. (April 16, 1854 – May 18, 1951) was an American politician and populist activist who led "Coxey's Army," a march of unemployed workers on Washington, D.C., in 1894 — the first significant protest march on the capital in United States history.

Early life

Born in Pennsylvania, Coxey moved to Ohio, worked in iron mills and quarries, and eventually owned a sandstone quarry and a horse-breeding farm in Massillon. A Greenback and later Populist sympathizer, he developed a plan to put the unemployed to work building roads, financed by government-issued bonds and legal-tender notes.

Coxey's Army

During the severe depression following the Panic of 1893, Coxey proposed a "Good Roads" bill and organized a "petition in boots": about one hundred men left Massillon on Easter Sunday, March 25, 1894, marching to Washington to present his program to Congress. Joined along the way, roughly 500 marchers reached the capital on May 1, 1894, amid enormous press attention. When Coxey attempted to speak on the Capitol steps, he was arrested for walking on the grass and carrying a banner, and the march dispersed without achieving its legislation.

Later life

Coxey ran repeatedly for office — Congress, the Senate, and governor of Ohio — on populist and later Republican tickets, and served as mayor of Massillon in the early 1930s. His ideas anticipated later public-works programs: federal road building and deficit-financed employment became mainstream policy during the New Deal. On May 1, 1944, fifty years after the march, the 90-year-old Coxey finally delivered his speech from the Capitol steps, having been granted permission by Congress.
