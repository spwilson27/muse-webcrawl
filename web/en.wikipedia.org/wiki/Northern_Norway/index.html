Northern Norway (Norwegian: Nord-Norge) is the geographical region of Norway comprising the three northernmost counties: Nordland, Troms and Finnmark. It covers roughly a third of the Norwegian mainland and is known for its dramatic coastal scenery, fjords, the Lofoten Islands, the midnight sun and the northern lights.

Geography

The region stretches from Helgeland in the south to the Barents Sea coast and the Russian border in the east. Much of the area lies above the Arctic Circle. The landscape is dominated by steep mountains rising directly from the sea, deep fjords such as Saltfjorden and Porsangerfjorden, and the large islands of Senja, Sørøya and Magerøya, on which the North Cape (Nordkapp) is located.

Population and economy

Northern Norway is sparsely populated, with about 480,000 inhabitants. Tromsø is the largest city and a major centre for Arctic research and education. Fishing has historically been the backbone of the economy, especially cod fisheries around Lofoten, alongside aquaculture, tourism, petroleum-related activity in the Barents Sea and the port of Hammerfest.

Culture

The region is home to a significant Sámi population, and Sámi languages and culture are prominent, particularly in Finnmark and inner Troms. Coastal culture, stave churches, and Viking Age history, including the Lofotr Viking Museum, are also notable features.
