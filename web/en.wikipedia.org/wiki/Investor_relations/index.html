Investor relations


Field: Corporate finance, communications
Abbreviation: IR

Investor relations (IR) is a strategic management responsibility that integrates finance, communication, marketing, and securities law compliance to enable the most effective two-way communication between a company, the financial community, and other constituencies, which ultimately contributes to a company's securities achieving fair valuation. The function is usually housed in a dedicated department of a publicly traded company and is headed by an investor relations officer reporting to the chief financial officer or chief executive.

Activities

IR responsibilities include organising earnings calls and investor conferences, preparing annual reports and regulatory filings support, arranging roadshows and one-to-one meetings with analysts and institutional investors, monitoring analyst reports and market perception, and communicating strategy, performance, and governance to shareholders. IR teams must comply with disclosure rules such as Regulation Fair Disclosure in the United States, which prohibits selective disclosure of material non-public information.

History

The profession grew in the United States from the 1950s and 1960s as share ownership widened; General Electric is often credited with one of the earliest formal programmes. Professional bodies such as the National Investor Relations Institute (founded 1969) in the US and the Investor Relations Society in the UK represent practitioners.
