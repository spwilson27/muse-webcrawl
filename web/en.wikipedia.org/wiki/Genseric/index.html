Genseric (c. 389 – 25 January 477), also spelled Gaiseric, was king of the Vandals and Alans from 428 to 477. One of the most formidable figures of the Migration Period, he led his people from Iberia into North Africa, seized Carthage, and built a Vandal kingdom that raided across the Mediterranean — most famously in the sack of Rome in 455.

Genseric led the Vandals across the Strait of Gibraltar in 429.

Conquest of Africa

Genseric succeeded his half-brother Gunderic in 428. In 429 he ferried his entire people — ancient sources say some 80,000 — across to Roman Africa, traditionally at the invitation of the Roman governor Bonifatius, who was in revolt against Ravenna. Advancing eastward in a brutal campaign, Genseric besieged Augustine of Hippo in his city during the siege of 430, concluded a treaty in 435 that gave him much of Numidia and Mauretania, and then broke it to seize Carthage in October 439, capturing the Roman fleet in its harbour.

The Vandal kingdom and the sack of Rome

From Carthage, Genseric took Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, and the Balearics, and his fleets dominated the western Mediterranean. Treaties with both Roman empires recognised his gains in 442 and 474. In 455, after the murder of the emperor Valentinian III, Genseric sailed to Italy and sacked Rome — a systematic two-week plundering, remembered as more methodical than the Visigothic sack of 410, which nonetheless gave "vandalism" its later meaning. He repelled major Roman attempts to recover Africa, destroying a great Eastern Roman armada at the Battle of Cape Bon in 468. An Arian Christian, he persecuted the Nicene clergy of Africa, expropriating churches. He died in 477, having founded a kingdom that survived until the Byzantine reconquest of 533–534.
