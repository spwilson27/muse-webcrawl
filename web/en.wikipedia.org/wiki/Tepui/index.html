Tepui

Tepui
Location: Guiana Highlands, Venezuela / Guyana / Brazil
Rock: Precambrian quartz sandstone
Highest: Mount Roraima (2,810 m)
Notable: Auyán-tepui (Angel Falls)

A tepui is a table-top mountain found in the Guiana Highlands of South America, especially Venezuela. The word means "house of the gods" in the Pemón language. Tepuis are isolated remnants of a vast sandstone plateau, with sheer cliffs up to 1,000 metres high.

Ecology

Their summits — cool, wet, nutrient-poor and isolated for millions of years — host highly endemic flora and fauna, including carnivorous plants and unique frogs: "islands in the sky".

Notable tepuis

Mount Roraima inspired Conan Doyle's The Lost World. Auyán-tepui is the source of Angel Falls, the world's tallest waterfall.
