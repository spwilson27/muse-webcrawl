Affine plane

Affine plane
Field: Geometry
Characterised by: Playfair's axiom (parallel postulate)
Examples: Euclidean plane, finite affine planes of order n

In geometry, an affine plane is a system of points and lines satisfying axioms of incidence together with a parallel postulate: given a line and a point not on it, exactly one line through the point is parallel to the given line.

Axioms and examples

An affine plane requires that any two distinct points lie on a unique line, that parallels exist uniquely, and that there exist at least four points, no three collinear. The ordinary Euclidean plane is the prototypical example. Finite affine planes exist for every prime-power order; an affine plane of order n has n² points and n(n+1) lines.

Relation to projective planes

Removing a line and all its points from a projective plane yields an affine plane, and conversely every affine plane can be completed by adding a line at infinity.
