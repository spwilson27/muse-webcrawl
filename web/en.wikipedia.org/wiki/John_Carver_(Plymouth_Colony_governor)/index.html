John Carver (Plymouth Colony governor)

Born: before 1584, possibly Worksop, Nottinghamshire, England

Died: April 1621, Plymouth Colony

Office: 1st Governor of Plymouth Colony (1620–1621)

Known for: First signer of the Mayflower Compact

John Carver (before 1584 – April 1621) was one of the Pilgrims who sailed on the Mayflower in 1620 and the first governor of Plymouth Colony.

Life

Carver was a member of the English Separatist congregation that lived in exile in Leiden, Netherlands. A prosperous merchant, he acted as the congregation's agent in chartering the Mayflower and organising the voyage to America. When the settlers drew up the Mayflower Compact in November 1620, Carver was the first to sign it, and he was elected the colony's first governor.

Governorship and death

During the settlers' desperate first winter, Carver helped negotiate a peace treaty with the Wampanoag leader Massasoit in March 1621, an agreement that lasted for decades. Weeks later, in April 1621, Carver fell ill after working in the fields — possibly from sunstroke — and died within days; his wife Katherine died a few weeks later. He was succeeded as governor by William Bradford.
