The alkaline earth metals are the elements of group 2 of the periodic table: beryllium (Be), magnesium (Mg), calcium (Ca), strontium (Sr), barium (Ba) and radium (Ra). They are silvery metals with two outer electrons, forming +2 ions and oxides and hydroxides that are alkaline but less caustic than the alkali metals'.

The name 'earths' is an old term for their heat-resistant oxides, known long before the metals themselves were isolated (mostly by Davy's electrolysis in 1808). Reactivity increases down the group; radium is intensely radioactive.

[Image: Group 2 metal samples and their flame colours: red, crimson and green]

Chemistry and uses

Calcium and magnesium are major industrial and biological elements: limestone, cement and hard water (Ca), chlorophyll and light alloys (Mg). Strontium gives crimson fireworks and was used in colour-TV tubes; barium sulfate is swallowed as a 'barium meal' for X-rays because it is opaque yet insoluble and non-toxic.

Flame colours

Group 2 flame tests are classics: calcium brick-red, strontium crimson, barium apple-green (magnesium burns bright white but gives no flame colour in a Bunsen test).
