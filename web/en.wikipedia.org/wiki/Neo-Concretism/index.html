Neo-Concretism
Years active1959 – mid-1960s
CountryBrazil
Key artistsLygia Clark, Hélio Oiticica, Lygia Pape, Ferreira Gullar

Neo-Concretism (Portuguese: Neoconcretismo) was a Brazilian art movement launched in Rio de Janeiro in 1959 with the publication of the Neo-Concrete Manifesto, written by the poet Ferreira Gullar and signed by artists including Lygia Clark, Hélio Oiticica, Lygia Pape, Amilcar de Castro, and Franz Weissmann. It emerged as a reaction against the rationalism and geometric dogmatism of the São Paulo-based Concrete art movement (Grupo Ruptura).

Ideas

Drawing on phenomenology, especially Maurice Merleau-Ponty's Phenomenology of Perception, Gullar's manifesto and his "Theory of the Non-Object" (1959) argued that the artwork should be neither a machine-like object nor a representation, but a living "non-object" experienced by the body in time and space. Neo-Concrete works therefore invited touch, movement, and participation: Clark's hinged metal Bichos ("Critters") could be rearranged by the viewer, and Oiticica's Parangolés were capes and banners meant to be worn and danced in.

Legacy

Neo-Concretism lasted only a few years as a formal movement, but it laid the groundwork for the participatory and installation practices of Brazilian contemporary art and has been widely exhibited and studied internationally, influencing discussions of spectator participation in art worldwide.
