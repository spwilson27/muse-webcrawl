Nico Nico Douga (ニコニコ動画, Niko Niko Dōga) is a Japanese video-sharing website operated by Dwango, a subsidiary of Kadokawa Corporation. The name "Nico Nico" comes from the Japanese ideophone for smiling. The service is best known for its distinctive feature allowing viewer comments to scroll directly across the video screen in real time, a system that strongly influenced later danmaku-style comment culture on other platforms such as Bilibili in China.

History

Nico Nico Douga launched in December 2006, initially relying on content hosted on YouTube before moving to its own hosting infrastructure after access was blocked. The site grew rapidly during 2007 and became one of Japan's most visited websites. It played a central role in the spread of Vocaloid music, user-created remixes, and the "utaite" (online cover singer) phenomenon. An English-language version, Niconico, was launched in 2012 but was later merged back into the main service. In June 2024 the service suffered a major cyberattack attributed to the BlackSuit ransomware group, taking it offline for roughly two months.

Features

Users can upload, view, and share videos, and can overlay comments that scroll across the screen at the moment they were posted. Videos are tagged rather than placed in formal categories, and users can create "mylists" of favourite videos. The site also hosts live broadcasts (Nico Nico Namahousou), user-generated games and music, and the annual Nico Nico Chokaigi fan convention.

Cultural impact

The platform was instrumental in popularising Vocaloid characters such as Hatsune Miku, whose early songs spread virally through the site. Many Japanese internet memes, remix genres, and fan-animation styles originated in its community.
