Lütjens-class destroyer

The Lütjens class (Type 103) was a class of three guided-missile destroyers built in the United States for the West German Navy. They were modified versions of the American Charles F. Adams class and were the last steam-powered warships of the German Navy.

Ships

The class comprised Lütjens (D185), named for Admiral Günther Lütjens; Mölders (D186), named for Luftwaffe ace Werner Mölders; and Rommel (D187), named for Field Marshal Erwin Rommel. All three were built by Bath Iron Works in Maine and commissioned between 1969 and 1970.

Service

Armed with Tartar (later SM-1) surface-to-air missiles, an ASROC launcher, two 5-inch guns and torpedo tubes, the destroyers served as the air-defence backbone of the Bundesmarine's destroyer flotilla throughout the Cold War and were extensively modernised in the 1970s and 1990s. After more than three decades of service, Rommel was decommissioned in 1998, Lütjens in 2003, and Mölders in 2003; Mölders is preserved as a museum ship at the German Naval Museum in Wilhelmshaven.
