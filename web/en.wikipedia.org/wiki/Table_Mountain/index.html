Table Mountain is a flat-topped mountain forming the dramatic backdrop to Cape Town, South Africa, and one of the most recognisable urban mountains on Earth. Its level plateau, about 3 km from side to side and edged by Devil's Peak and Lion's Head, is the eroded remnant of hard sandstone laid down some 300+ million years ago; when moisture-laden south-east winds pile cloud over the summit, the white "tablecloth" spills down its face. The mountain and its national park (proclaimed 1998) enclose an astonishing share of the Cape Floristic Region — more plant species (about 2,200, including fynbos endemics) than the entire United Kingdom.

The mountain and the city

Indigenous Khoi peoples knew it as Hoerikwaggo, "mountain in the sea"; Portuguese navigators named it Taboa do Cabo; in 1503 António de Saldanha made the first recorded European ascent, and a stone cross path and Maclear's survey beacon (1838–65, for the Cape's geodetic survey) mark its summit plateau. Climbers take Platteklip Gorge, hikers the flowering slopes above Kirstenbosch botanical garden, and everyone else the aerial cableway. The mountain survived the city fires crisis of January 2021 (which gutted parts of the University of Cape Town below its slopes) as it has every Cape fire season; its Dassies (rock hyraxes), caracals and, until their removal, non-native Himalayan tahrs share the slopes with millions of visitors a year to Cape Town.
