ParticipantsStephen Hawking, Kip Thorne, John Preskill
Date1997
SubjectInformation loss in black holes
OutcomeHawking conceded in 2004

The Thorne–Hawking–Preskill bet was a public wager between theoretical physicists Stephen Hawking and Kip Thorne on one side and John Preskill on the other, made in 1997, concerning the black hole information paradox. Hawking and Thorne bet that information swallowed by a black hole is lost forever, while Preskill bet that information is eventually released in Hawking radiation, as quantum mechanics seemed to require.

Background

The paradox arises because Hawking's 1970s calculations suggested black holes evaporate into featureless thermal radiation, seemingly destroying information and violating the unitarity of quantum mechanics. The wager's stake was an encyclopedia of the loser's choice, "from which information can be retrieved at will."

Resolution

In July 2004 Hawking announced that he had changed his mind, presenting calculations suggesting that information does escape, and conceded the bet by presenting Preskill with an encyclopedia of baseball. Thorne, however, did not concede. The bet is often discussed alongside the earlier Thorne–Hawking bet on naked singularities, which Hawking also conceded.
