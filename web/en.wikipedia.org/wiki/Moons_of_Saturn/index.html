Moons of Saturn

Saturn's moons
[Image: Saturn and its largest moon Titan]
Known moons: Several hundred (274 confirmed with orbits announced by 2025)
Largest: Titan (5,150 km diameter)
First discovered: Titan, by Christiaan Huygens in 1655

The moons of Saturn are numerous and diverse, ranging from tiny moonlets only tens of metres across to Titan, the second-largest moon in the Solar System and larger than the planet Mercury. Saturn has the most known moons of any planet: as of 2025, 274 moons with confirmed orbits had been announced, following the recognition of 128 additional moons. These do not include the thousands of embedded moonlets within Saturn's ring system.

Groups

Saturn's moons are divided into regular moons, which orbit close to the planet's equatorial plane, and irregular moons, which follow distant, inclined, often retrograde orbits and are thought to be captured objects. The irregulars fall into families — the Inuit, Gallic, and Norse groups — based on their orbital characteristics and presumed common origins in collisions.

Exploration

The Cassini–Huygens mission (2004–2017) transformed knowledge of the system: the Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, and Cassini discovered the plumes of Enceladus, seas on Titan, and several small moons. NASA's planned Dragonfly rotorcraft mission is intended to explore Titan's surface in the 2030s.
