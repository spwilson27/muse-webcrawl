In Greek mythology, Amphiaraus (Ancient Greek: Ἀμφιάραος) was the king of Argos and a renowned seer, a descendant of the prophet Melampus. He was one of the Seven against Thebes, and is famous for having foreseen his own death in the expedition yet being compelled to join it.

Myth

Amphiaraus had quarrelled with his cousin Adrastus, king of Argos, but they were reconciled and Adrastus gave him his sister Eriphyle in marriage, swearing that in future disputes Eriphyle's decision would be final. When Adrastus organised the expedition of the Seven to restore Polynices to the throne of Thebes, Amphiaraus — knowing through his prophetic gift that all the champions except Adrastus would die — refused to go and hid. But Polynices bribed Eriphyle with the cursed necklace of Harmonia, and she, exercising her agreed authority, ordered her husband to march.

Before departing, Amphiaraus made his sons swear to avenge his death by killing their mother. In the battle at Thebes, as Amphiaraus fled from the spear of Periclymenus, Zeus split the earth with a thunderbolt and the seer was swallowed alive, chariot and all, before he could be killed in disgrace. His son Alcmaeon later killed Eriphyle and led the second expedition, the Epigoni, against Thebes.

Cult

After his disappearance Amphiaraus was worshipped as a healing and oracular hero. His great sanctuary, the Amphiareion at Oropos on the Attic–Boeotian border, featured a temple, a sacred spring into which the healed threw coins, and an incubation chamber where consultants slept to receive dream cures — a cult comparable to that of Asclepius. Amphiaraus also appears on the François Vase and was a subject of lost tragedies by Sophocles.
