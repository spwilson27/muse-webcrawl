The 1975 Australian federal election was held on 13 December 1975, following the dismissal of the Whitlam Labor government by Governor-General Sir John Kerr on 11 November 1975 — the most dramatic constitutional crisis in Australian history. The Liberal–National Country Coalition, led by caretaker prime minister Malcolm Fraser, won a landslide victory: the largest federal majority in terms of seats since Federation at that time.

Background

Gough Whitlam's government, elected in 1972 and re-elected at a double dissolution in 1974, had been battered by economic turmoil and scandals, most notably the Loans Affair. In October 1975 the opposition-controlled Senate deferred supply bills, seeking to force an election. Whitlam refused to resign or advise a general election. On 11 November, Kerr dismissed Whitlam and commissioned Fraser as caretaker prime minister on condition that he secure supply and immediately advise a double dissolution election. Whitlam's famous speech on the steps of Parliament House — "Well may we say 'God save the Queen', because nothing will save the Governor-General" — opened the campaign.

Campaign and result

The Coalition campaigned on economic management and the slogan "Turn on the lights, Australia" themes of stability, while Labor campaigned under "Shame Fraser, Shame" and defended its dismissal as a constitutional outrage. The Coalition won 91 of 127 House seats and a Senate majority, with a two-party-preferred vote of about 55.7 per cent.

Aftermath

Fraser's government governed until its defeat by Bob Hawke's Labor Party in 1983. The 1975 election and the dismissal remain deeply contested in Australian public life, shaping debates about the powers of the Governor-General and the Senate for decades afterwards.
