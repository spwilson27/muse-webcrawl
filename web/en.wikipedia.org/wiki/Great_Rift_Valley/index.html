Great Rift Valley

The Great Rift Valley is a series of contiguous geographic depressions running from Lebanon's Beqaa Valley in southwestern Asia to Mozambique in southeastern Africa, thousands of kilometres long, formed by the divergence of tectonic plates (the Arabian Plate and the two new African plates, Nubian and Somali).

In East Africa the valley splits into eastern and western branches, holding the African Great Lakes, volcanoes including Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya, and the Afar Triangle, where the crust is literally splitting apart and new ocean may one day form. The valley's lake sediments preserved the fossil record of human evolution — Olduvai Gorge, Lake Turkana, Hadar (where 'Lucy', Australopithecus afarensis, was found in 1974) — earning it the title 'cradle of humankind'.
