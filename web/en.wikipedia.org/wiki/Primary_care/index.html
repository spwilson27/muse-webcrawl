Primary care

Primary care is the day-to-day healthcare given by a healthcare provider, typically acting as the first point of consultation for patients and providing continuing, comprehensive care. Primary care practitioners include family physicians, general practitioners, internists, paediatricians, and increasingly nurse practitioners and physician assistants.

Scope

Primary care covers health promotion, disease prevention, diagnosis and treatment of acute and chronic illnesses, counselling, and coordination of referrals to specialists. Its defining characteristics are often summarised as first contact, continuity, comprehensiveness, and coordination.

Evidence and policy

Health systems with strong primary care orientation are associated with better population health outcomes and lower costs, a finding emphasised by Barbara Starfield's research and the World Health Organization's 1978 Alma-Ata Declaration. Many countries face shortages of primary care clinicians driven by income gaps with specialties and administrative burden.
