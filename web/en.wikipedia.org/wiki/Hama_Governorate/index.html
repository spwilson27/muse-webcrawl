Hama Governorate

Hama Governorate
Country: Syria
Capital: Hama
Area: about 8,883 km²
Population: about 1.6 million (2011 estimate)
Districts: Hama, Masyaf, Mahardah, Salamiyah, Al-Suqaylabiyah

Hama Governorate (Arabic: محافظة حماة, Muḥāfaẓat Ḥamā) is one of the fourteen governorates (provinces) of Syria. It lies in the west-central part of the country, bordered by Idlib and Aleppo to the north, Raqqa to the northeast, Homs to the south, and Tartus and Latakia to the west along the coastal mountains. Its capital is the city of Hama, on the Orontes River, famous for its great wooden norias (water wheels).

Geography

The governorate extends from the fertile Orontes valley and the al-Ghab plain in the west — an intensively farmed lowland reclaimed from marshland in the mid-20th century — eastward into the Syrian steppe and desert. The western districts around Masyaf lie in the foothills of the coastal mountain range (Jabal Ansariyah) and include the medieval castle of Masyaf, once a stronghold of the Nizari Ismailis. Agriculture is the main economic activity: wheat, cotton, potatoes, olives and pistachios are major crops, and the region is one of Syria's most important agricultural producers.

Districts and population

The governorate is divided into five districts (mintaqah): Hama, Masyaf, Mahardah, Salamiyah and Al-Suqaylabiyah. Its population was about 1.6 million in the last pre-war estimates, and is religiously diverse, including Sunni and Alawite Muslims, Ismailis (centred on Salamiyah), and long-established Christian communities in towns such as Mahardah and Al-Suqaylabiyah.

History

The region's history stretches back to ancient Hamath, a city-state mentioned in the Bible and in Assyrian records. In the modern era the governorate was the scene of the 1982 Hama massacre, in which government forces destroyed much of the old city while suppressing a Muslim Brotherhood uprising, killing many thousands. During the Syrian civil war beginning in 2011, Hama city and the surrounding countryside saw repeated fighting, displacement and shifting front lines between government and opposition forces. In December 2024, opposition forces captured Hama during the offensive that led to the fall of the Assad government.
