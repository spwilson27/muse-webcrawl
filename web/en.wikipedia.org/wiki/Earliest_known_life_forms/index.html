Earliest known life forms

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The earliest known life forms on Earth are putative fossilised microorganisms and chemical signatures found in rocks dating to the Archaean eon. Evidence places life on Earth by at least 3.7 billion years ago, during the Eoarchaean era after a geological crust had formed following the molten Hadean, and possibly as early as 4.1–4.4 billion years ago.

Evidence

Several lines of evidence are studied. Stromatolites — layered structures built by microbial mats — are known from rocks about 3.48 billion years old in the Pilbara of Western Australia, and structures interpreted as stromatolites in 3.7-billion-year-old metasediments from the Isua Greenstone Belt of Greenland were reported in 2016. Microfossils claimed from the 3.465-billion-year-old Apex chert of Australia have been debated for decades; analyses have detected carbonaceous material consistent with biological origin, though abiotic explanations persist. Carbon isotope signatures depleted in carbon-13 — a hallmark of biological carbon fixation — occur in 3.8-billion-year-old Isua sediments, and a graphite inclusion in a 4.1-billion-year-old zircon from Jack Hills, Australia, shows a similar signature. In 2017, researchers reported putative microfossils in hydrothermal vent precipitates of the Nuvvuagittuq Belt, Quebec, dated between 3.77 and 4.28 billion years old, which would make them the oldest known — a claim that remains contested.

Interpretation

Because rocks of this age have been heavily metamorphosed, every very early claim is controversial, and "biosignatures" must exclude abiotic processes that can mimic them. Phylogenetic reconstructions of the last universal common ancestor (LUCA), informed by comparative genomics, suggest life diversified remarkably early, with LUCA living perhaps around 4.2 billion years ago, consistent with a rapid origin of life once conditions allowed.

Context

The early record informs debates about abiogenesis, the late heavy bombardment's effect on habitability, and the search for life on Mars and icy moons, where similar hydrothermal environments may have existed.
