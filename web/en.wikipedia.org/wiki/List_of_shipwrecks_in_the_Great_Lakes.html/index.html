List of shipwrecks in the Great Lakes

Great Lakes shipwrecks
Lakes: Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, Ontario
Estimated wrecks: over 6,000
Famous losses: Edmund Fitzgerald, Lady Elgin, Eastland

This is a list of shipwrecks in the Great Lakes of North America, organised by lake. The five lakes — Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario — together form the largest group of freshwater lakes on Earth, and their sudden violent storms, especially the November gales, have sunk thousands of commercial vessels since the 1600s.

By lake

Lake Erie, the shallowest lake, holds hundreds of wrecks including the brig Nancy and many 19th-century schooners. Lake Huron suffered heavily in the Great Lakes Storm of 1913, which sank ships such as the Charles S. Price and the John A. McGean, and its Thunder Bay is known as "Shipwreck Alley". Lake Michigan losses include Le Griffon (1679), the Lady Elgin (1860), the Rouse Simmons "Christmas Tree Ship" (1912) and the car ferry Milwaukee (1929). Lake Ontario holds warships from the War of 1812, such as HMS Ontario (1780) and the schooners Hamilton and Scourge (1813). Lake Superior, the largest and coldest, received the Edmund Fitzgerald in 1975, the last major commercial vessel lost on the lakes; earlier losses include the John M. Osborn (1884), the Madeira (1905) and the Henry Chisholm (1898).
