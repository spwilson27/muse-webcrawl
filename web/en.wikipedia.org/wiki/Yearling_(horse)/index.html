Yearling (horse)

A yearling Thoroughbred at a sales preparation farm

A yearling is a young horse, of either sex, between the ages of one and two years. The term is used across many horse breeds and equestrian disciplines to describe the developmental stage that follows the weanling stage and precedes the age of two, at which point many horses begin formal training. In the Northern Hemisphere Thoroughbred industry, every horse officially turns one year old on January 1 (August 1 in the Southern Hemisphere), regardless of its actual foaling date, and is thereafter classed as a yearling until its second official birthday.

Development

Yearlings are typically weaned from their dams at between four and seven months of age and spend the remainder of their first year growing rapidly. By the time they reach their first official birthday, most yearlings stand at roughly 90 percent of their mature height, although they continue to gain body mass and skeletal maturity for several more years. Growth during this period is carefully managed through nutrition and turnout, since both underfeeding and overly rapid growth can contribute to developmental orthopaedic diseases such as osteochondrosis and physitis.

Yearling sales

Yearlings form the backbone of the commercial bloodstock market. Major auctions, such as the Keeneland September Yearling Sale in Kentucky and the Tattersalls October Yearling Sale in Newmarket, England, sell thousands of young Thoroughbreds each year. Prices vary enormously depending on pedigree, physical conformation, and veterinary reports; elite yearlings by fashionable stallions out of successful mares regularly sell for more than one million dollars, while the median price is far lower. Buyers include pinhookers, who resell the horses as two-year-olds in training, racing owners, and breeding operations.

Preparation and handling

Sales yearlings undergo a period of preparation, often six to ten weeks, involving controlled exercise, grooming, balanced feeding, and handling to accustom them to being led, stood up for inspection, and examined by prospective buyers and veterinarians. Away from the sales ring, young horses kept for riding or sport are typically handled regularly, taught to lead, stand for the farrier, and load into a trailer, building a foundation for later training under saddle, which in most disciplines does not begin until the horse is two or three years old.
