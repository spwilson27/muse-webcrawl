Vättern

Location: South-central Sweden

Surface area: approx. 1,900 km²

Max. depth: approx. 128 m

Notable town: Jönköping

Vättern is the second largest lake in Sweden, after Vänern, and the sixth largest lake in Europe. Located in south-central Sweden between the provinces of Östergötland, Västergötland, Närke and Småland, it covers about 1,900 km² and reaches a depth of about 128 metres.

Characteristics

The lake is known for its exceptionally clear, cold water, which is used as drinking water by several municipalities and is famous for its population of Arctic char. Vättern lies in a graben — a tectonic rift valley — and drains eastward via the Motala ström into the Baltic Sea. The Göta Canal passes through the lake, connecting it to both coasts of Sweden.

Surroundings

Jönköping, at the lake's southern tip, is its largest town; other lakeside towns include Vadstena, Hjo, Karlsborg and Askersund. The island of Visingsö in the southern part of the lake is associated with the medieval counts of the House of Brahe and the ruins of Visingsborg castle. Each June, the Vätternrundan cycling event takes thousands of riders on a 315 km circuit around the lake.
