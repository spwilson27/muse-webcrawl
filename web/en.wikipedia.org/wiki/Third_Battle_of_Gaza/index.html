The Third Battle of Gaza (1–7 November 1917) was fought in southern Palestine during the First World War, as part of the Sinai and Palestine Campaign. Coming after two failed frontal assaults on Gaza earlier in 1917, General Edmund Allenby's Egyptian Expeditionary Force broke the Ottoman Gaza–Beersheba defensive line by a surprise attack on its inland flank at Beersheba, then turned and rolled up the line, forcing the evacuation of Gaza itself.

Background

The First and Second Battles of Gaza (March and April 1917) had ended in costly British failures against strong Ottoman positions anchored on the coast. Allenby, taking command in June, rebuilt his army's morale and logistics, extended the railway and water pipeline across Sinai, and devised a plan of deception: feints and demonstrations fixed Ottoman attention on Gaza while the main blow fell 45 kilometres inland at Beersheba, where water supplies were essential.

The battle

On 31 October 1917 the Battle of Beersheba opened the offensive; the town fell by nightfall, crowned by the famous charge of the Australian 4th Light Horse Brigade — often described as the last great cavalry charge in history. From 1 November, while the Desert Mounted Corps pressed northwards, XX Corps attacked the Gaza defences in a deliberate set-piece assault beginning on the night of 1–2 November. Ottoman forces, their flank turned and water threatened, withdrew from Gaza on the night of 6–7 November, and British troops entered the ruined town on 7 November.

Aftermath

The breakthrough unhinged the whole Ottoman position in southern Palestine. In the ensuing pursuit, Allenby's forces won the Battle of Mughar Ridge, took Junction Station, and advanced on Jerusalem, which surrendered on 9 December 1917. The Third Battle of Gaza thus marked the turning point of the Palestine campaign.
