Lars Olsson Smith

Lars Olsson Smith (12 October 1836 – 9 December 1913), often known as L. O. Smith, was a Swedish spirits manufacturer and politician, remembered as the "King of Brännvin" (King of Vodka) and as the founder of the business that created Absolut Vodka.

Career

Born in Kiaby, Scania, Smith entered the spirits trade as a boy and by the 1870s controlled a large share of Sweden's brännvin (aquavit/vodka) production. In 1879 his company launched Absolut Rent Brännvin ("Absolutely Pure Vodka"), produced using a new continuous distillation rectification process at Åhus that removed fusel oils and other impurities; it is the direct ancestor of Absolut Vodka, and Smith's portrait medallion still appears on every bottle. When the city of Stockholm asserted its municipal spirits monopoly, Smith famously sold his product from the island of Reimersholme just outside the city's jurisdiction — providing free boat trips to customers — in a celebrated standoff known as the "vodka war" with the Stockholm establishment.

Smith also promoted temperance-oriented reform in the form of the "Gothenburg system" of public-house companies, served in the Swedish parliament (the Riksdag), and was one of Sweden's richest men, though his fortune was badly damaged by business losses late in life.
