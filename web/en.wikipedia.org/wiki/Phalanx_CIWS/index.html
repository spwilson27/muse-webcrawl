Phalanx CIWS

Type: Close-in weapon system (CIWS)

Manufacturer: Raytheon (originally General Dynamics)

In service: 1980–present

Armament: 20 mm M61 Vulcan rotary cannon

Rate of fire: Up to 4,500 rounds/min

The Phalanx CIWS (pronounced "sea-wiz") is a close-in weapon system developed by the United States for defending warships against incoming anti-ship missiles and aircraft that have penetrated outer defences. Built by General Dynamics' Pomona Division and now produced by Raytheon, the system mounts a radar-guided 20 mm M61 Vulcan rotary cannon in a distinctive white radome that has earned it the nickname "R2-D2" among sailors.

Design and operation

The Phalanx is a self-contained system: its Ku-band search-and-track radar, gun, and ammunition are integrated on a single mount. It automatically detects, tracks, and evaluates targets, and can engage them autonomously, firing 20 mm armour-piercing discarding-sabot tungsten rounds at 3,000 rounds per minute in early blocks (4,500 in later blocks) with an effective range on the order of 1.5–2 km. Later Block 1B upgrades added an infrared sensor and surface mode to engage small boats, helicopters, and slow aircraft, plus optimized gun barrels.

Service

Phalanx entered U.S. Navy service in 1980 aboard the carrier USS Coral Sea and has been fitted to virtually all classes of U.S. surface combatants, as well as to the navies of numerous allied nations. A land-based variant, the LPWS within the C-RAM (Counter Rocket, Artillery, and Mortar) system, was deployed by the U.S. Army to bases in Iraq and Afghanistan to intercept incoming rockets and mortar rounds.

Related systems

Comparable systems include the Dutch Goalkeeper CIWS, the Russian AK-630, and the missile-based RIM-116 Rolling Airframe Missile, whose launcher combined with the Phalanx mount forms the SeaRAM system.
