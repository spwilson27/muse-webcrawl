Will Keith Kellogg

W. K. Kellogg (1860–1951), founder of the Kellogg Company.

Will Keith Kellogg (7 April 1860 – 6 October 1951), usually referred to as W. K. Kellogg, was an American industrialist in food manufacturing, best known as the founder of the Kellogg Company, which produces a wide variety of breakfast cereals.

Early career and corn flakes

Kellogg was born in Battle Creek, Michigan. He worked as business manager at the Battle Creek Sanitarium run by his brother, Dr. John Harvey Kellogg. There, around 1894, the brothers developed a process for making flaked cereal from cooked wheat — and then corn — as a wholesome vegetarian food for patients. A bitter and prolonged dispute followed over the commercial rights to the flakes.

The Kellogg Company

In 1906 Kellogg founded the Battle Creek Toasted Corn Flake Company, marketing Kellogg's Corn Flakes with his signature on every box as a mark of authenticity. An energetic marketer, he built the firm into a global enterprise, added products such as Rice Krispies, and established overseas factories beginning with Canada and England.

Philanthropy

In 1930 Kellogg established the W. K. Kellogg Foundation, one of the world's largest philanthropic foundations, endowed with much of his fortune. Noted for his love of Arabian horses, he founded the Kellogg ranch in Pomona, California, now part of California State Polytechnic University. He died in Battle Creek in 1951, aged 91.
