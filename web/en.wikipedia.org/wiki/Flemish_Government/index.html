The Flemish Government (Dutch: Vlaamse Regering) is the executive branch of both the Flemish Community and the Flemish Region of Belgium. Unlike in the French Community and the Walloon Region, where community and regional institutions remain separate, Flanders merged its community and regional governments from the very beginning, so that a single government and a single parliament exercise both sets of competences.

The government is headed by the Minister-President of Flanders and consists of a maximum of eleven ministers. It is accountable to the Flemish Parliament, whose 124 members are directly elected. The seat of the government is on the Martelaarsplein (Martyrs' Square) in Brussels, which is also the capital of the Flemish Community even though the city itself forms a separate region.

History

The Flemish executive emerged from the Belgian state reforms of 1980, which created the Flemish Community and the Flemish Region. The first Flemish Executive, chaired by Gaston Geens, took office in December 1981. From the outset the Flemish chose to merge the community and regional executives, exercising cultural, educational and person-related matters alongside territorial matters such as economy, environment, public works and housing.

Since the reforms of 1993 Belgium has been a formal federal state, and the Flemish Government became one of the federated executives alongside the federal government, the Walloon Government, the Government of the French Community and the Government of the Brussels-Capital Region.

Competences

The Flemish Government is responsible for, among other things:

  • Education (except for federal aspects such as compulsory schooling age)
  • Culture, language use, media and sport
  • Welfare, public health policy and family policy
  • Economy, energy, employment and innovation
  • Environment, water policy, agriculture and nature conservation
  • Housing, spatial planning, mobility and public works
  • Local government supervision and foreign trade

Composition

The government is formed after elections to the Flemish Parliament, usually as a coalition of Flemish parties. The Minister-President is sworn in by the King of the Belgians. Ministers head departments known as policy areas (beleidsdomeinen), each supported by the Flemish administration.

Well-known former minister-presidents include Gaston Geens, Luc Van den Brande, Patrick Dewael, Yves Leterme, Kris Peeters, Geert Bourgeois and Jan Jambon.
