Valençay

Valençay is a commune in the Indre department in central France, in the Centre-Val de Loire region. It is best known for the Château de Valençay, once the residence of the French foreign minister Charles-Maurice de Talleyrand-Périgord, and for its pyramid-shaped goat's cheese, Valençay cheese, protected by an Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) designation.

History

The town grew around its château, built in the early 16th century by the d'Estampes family in a style mixing French Renaissance and classical elements, with a large corner donjon and ornate towers. In 1803 Napoleon ordered his foreign minister Talleyrand to purchase the estate, and Valençay became a centre of European diplomacy. During the Peninsular War, the Spanish king Ferdinand VII and his brother were held in comfortable captivity at the château between 1808 and 1813 under the Treaty of Valençay arrangements. Talleyrand is buried in the town.

Château de Valençay

The Château de Valençay is one of the great châteaux of the Loire. It preserves Talleyrand's furnishings, a park laid out in the English style, and a small theatre. The estate is open to visitors and contains an automobile museum and collections relating to Talleyrand's era.

Cheese

Valençay cheese is an unpasteurised goat's milk cheese made in the town and surrounding area. It is formed as a truncated pyramid coated in ash. Legend holds that the top was cut off so the cheese would not recall the pyramids of Egypt after Napoleon's defeated Egyptian campaign, or to avoid offending Talleyrand. The cheese received AOC status in 1998.
