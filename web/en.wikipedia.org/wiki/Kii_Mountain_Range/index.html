Kii Mountain Range

The Kii Mountain Range (Japanese: 紀伊山地, Kii-sanchi) is a mountainous region occupying most of the Kii Peninsula in Honshu, Japan, spanning Mie, Nara and Wakayama prefectures. Densely forested and among the wettest places in Japan, the range has been sacred for over a thousand years: its shrines, temples and pilgrimage routes were inscribed in 2004 as the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range".

Sacred sites

Three great sacred complexes anchor the range: Yoshino and Ōmine in the north, centres of the Shugendō mountain ascetic tradition around Mount Ōmine and the cherry-blossom mountain Yoshino; Kumano Sanzan in the south, the three grand shrines of Kumano (Hongu, Hayatama and Nachi), reached by the network of pilgrimage roads called the Kumano Kodō; and Kōyasan (Mount Kōya) in the northwest, the monastic city founded by Kūkai as the headquarters of Shingon Buddhism. Pilgrims, retired emperors and commoners have walked these routes since the Heian period.

Geography and nature

The range's steep, rain-drenched slopes are covered with ancient cedar and cypress forests, many planted as managed forestry over centuries, interspersed with sacred old-growth stands. It is home to the Nachi Falls, one of Japan's tallest waterfalls, and forms the watershed between the Pacific and the rivers flowing north to the Nara basin.
