FIA World Championship

An FIA World Championship is a motor racing series awarded world-championship status by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), the governing body of world motorsport. The FIA awards world titles to drivers and, in most series, to manufacturers or teams, under the International Sporting Code.

Current championships

The FIA currently sanctions several world championships, including the Formula One World Championship (established 1950), the World Rally Championship (1973), the World Endurance Championship (2012, successor to earlier sports-car world championships), the World Rallycross Championship, Formula E (which gained world-championship status in 2020–21), and the World Touring Car Cup's successor series arrangements, along with karting world championships.

Requirements

To hold world-championship status, a series must generally meet criteria in the FIA's regulations, including racing on multiple continents, a minimum number of events and entrants, and manufacturer participation requirements that vary by discipline.

History

The oldest FIA world championship is Formula One, whose inaugural drivers' title was won by Giuseppe Farina in 1950. Earlier world championships included the World Manufacturers' Championship of the 1920s and the World Sportscar Championship (1953–1992).
