Wilfred Edward Salter Owen MC (18 March 1893 – 4 November 1918) was an English poet and soldier. He is widely regarded as the greatest poet of the First World War, known for his stark, realistic verse about the horrors of trench and gas warfare, which stood in deliberate contrast to the patriotic public perception of war at the time.

Life and war service

Owen was born in Oswestry, Shropshire, and worked as a lay assistant to a vicar and as a private tutor in France before enlisting in 1915. Commissioned into the Manchester Regiment, he suffered shell shock after traumatic experiences at the front in 1917 and was sent to Craiglockhart War Hospital in Edinburgh, where he met Siegfried Sassoon. Sassoon's mentorship transformed Owen's poetic style, and most of his mature work was written between August 1917 and September 1918.

Death and legacy

Owen returned to the front, won the Military Cross for gallantry, and was killed in action on 4 November 1918 during the crossing of the Sambre–Oise Canal — exactly one week before the Armistice. News of his death reached his family as the church bells rang on Armistice Day. Only five of his poems were published in his lifetime; posthumous collections, and Benjamin Britten's War Requiem (1962), which sets his verse, secured his reputation.
