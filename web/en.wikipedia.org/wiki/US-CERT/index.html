United States Computer Emergency Readiness Team

The United States Computer Emergency Readiness Team (US-CERT) was a partnership between the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and the public and private sectors of the United States, created in 2003 to coordinate the nation's response to cyber threats. Its functions were later folded into the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), where the US-CERT name continued in use for alerts and publications for some years.

History

US-CERT was established in 2003 within the DHS National Cyber Security Division, building on the earlier Federal Computer Incident Response Capability (FedCIRC) and working closely with the CERT Coordination Center at Carnegie Mellon University's Software Engineering Institute. It served as the federal civilian government's focal point for analysing and responding to computer security incidents.

Activities

US-CERT published vulnerability notes, technical alerts, and current activity reports, operated a national alert system, and coordinated incident handling with federal agencies, state and local governments, industry, and international CERT partners. With the creation of CISA in 2018, its roles were consolidated into CISA's Cybersecurity Division and the National Risk Management Center.
