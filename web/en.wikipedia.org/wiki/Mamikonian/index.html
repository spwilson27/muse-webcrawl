The Mamikonian (or Mamikonean) family was one of the most powerful noble houses of late antique and early medieval Armenia. From the reign of Tiridates III in the early 4th century until the 8th century, they held the hereditary office of sparapet, commander-in-chief of the Armenian army, and at times effectively ruled the country.

History

The family's stronghold lay in the district of Taron, west of Lake Van. Mamikonians led Armenian forces in the wars against Sasanian Persia, most famously Vardan Mamikonian, who fell at the Battle of Avarayr in 451 fighting for the Christian faith and is venerated as a saint of the Armenian Church. His nephew Vahan Mamikonian later secured religious autonomy for Armenia as marzpan under the terms of the Treaty of Nvarsak (484).

Later history

After the fall of the Arsacid monarchy, the Mamikonians remained leading princes under Persian and later Byzantine and Arab rule. Branches of the family entered Byzantine service, and some genealogists link them to later Byzantine dynasties. The house faded from prominence in the 8th century after failed revolts against the Abbasid Caliphate.
