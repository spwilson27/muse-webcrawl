Eccentric training

Eccentric training
TypeResistance training method
FocusMuscle lengthening under load (eccentric contraction)
Also calledNegative training

Eccentric training is a form of resistance training that emphasises the eccentric phase of a movement, in which the active muscle lengthens while producing force — for example, slowly lowering a dumbbell after a biceps curl, or decelerating the body during a downhill run. It is contrasted with concentric training, in which the muscle shortens, and is sometimes called "negative" training.

Characteristics

Muscles can produce substantially greater force eccentrically than concentrically — often 20–60 per cent more — while consuming less oxygen and metabolic energy. Eccentric loading produces distinctive adaptations, including increases in fascicle length and particularly effective strengthening of tendons. Unaccustomed eccentric exercise is also the main cause of delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS), through microscopic disruption of muscle fibres; the "repeated bout effect" means soreness is greatly reduced when the same exercise is performed again.

Applications

Eccentric programmes are widely used in rehabilitation, notably for tendinopathies such as Achilles and patellar tendinopathy, where heavy slow eccentric heel-drop and squat protocols are standard treatments. Eccentric strengthening of the hamstrings (e.g. the Nordic hamstring exercise) is used to reduce hamstring injury rates in sport. In older adults and clinical populations, eccentric cycling and stepping have been studied as ways to gain strength with a low cardiorespiratory cost.
