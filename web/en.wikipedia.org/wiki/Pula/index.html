Pula

Country: Croatia
County: Istria
Population: about 52,000 (2021, city proper)
Known for: Pula Arena (Roman amphitheatre)

Pula (Italian: Pola) is the largest city in Istria County, Croatia, situated at the southern tip of the Istrian peninsula on the Adriatic Sea. A port and shipbuilding centre with a large natural harbour, it is best known for its remarkably preserved Roman monuments, above all the Pula Arena, one of the largest surviving Roman amphitheatres.

History

The site was inhabited from prehistoric times and was an Illyrian settlement before Rome founded the colony of Colonia Pietas Iulia Pola in the first century BC. Under Augustus the city flourished: the amphitheatre (built between 27 BC and AD 68), the Temple of Augustus, the Arch of the Sergii and twin city gates date from this period. After Rome's fall Pula passed through Ostrogothic, Byzantine, Frankish, Venetian and French hands. Under Venice it declined to a small town, but from 1856 it became the principal naval base of the Austrian Empire, and its population grew rapidly around the arsenal and shipyards.

After the First World War Pula and Istria went to Italy; after the Second World War, in 1947, they were ceded to Yugoslavia, prompting the exodus of much of the Italian population. Within Croatia since 1991, the city has remained a centre of shipbuilding (the Uljanik yard, founded 1856, whose collapse in 2019 was a national industrial crisis) and of tourism.

Sights and culture

The Arena, seating some 23,000 in antiquity, now hosts concerts and the Pula Film Festival, founded in 1954. Other sights include the Temple of Augustus, the Forum, the Venetian fortress (Kaštel) and the Zerostrasse tunnels. The writer James Joyce taught English in Pula in 1904–05; a statue and café commemorate his stay. The city's population is ethnically mixed, and under the city statute Croatian and Italian are in official use.
