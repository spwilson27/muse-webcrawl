Hopkinson's law

Hopkinson's law is a counterpart of Ohm's law used in magnetism. It is named after John Hopkinson and relates magnetomotive force (mmf) to magnetic flux through the reluctance of a magnetic circuit:

F = Φ · Rm

where F is the magnetomotive force (in ampere-turns), Φ is the magnetic flux (in webers), and Rm is the reluctance of the circuit (in ampere-turns per weber). Compare Ohm's law, V = IR: mmf corresponds to voltage, flux to current, and reluctance to electrical resistance.

Reluctance

For a uniform magnetic path of length l, cross-sectional area A, and absolute permeability μ, the reluctance is Rm = l / (μA). Unlike electrical resistance, reluctance in ferromagnetic materials is generally nonlinear because permeability varies with flux density and saturates. The law is therefore most useful for approximate magnetic-circuit calculations in the design of transformers, motors, and other electromagnetic devices, in series and parallel reluctance combinations analogous to resistor networks.
