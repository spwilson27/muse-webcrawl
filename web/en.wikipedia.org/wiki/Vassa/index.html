Vassa


Monks during the rains retreat

Vassa (Pali: vasso, Sanskrit: varṣa), also known in English as the Rains Retreat, is the three-month annual retreat observed by Theravada Buddhist monastics during the wet season. It typically lasts from the full moon of July (the day after Asalha Puja) until the full moon of October, corresponding to the South Asian monsoon.

Observance

During vassa, monks and nuns remain within the boundaries of a single monastery or temporary dwelling and intensify their meditation and study. The practice dates to the time of the Buddha: wandering ascetics traditionally suspended their travels during the rains both because monsoon travel was difficult and to avoid harming crops and small creatures washed onto the paths. The Buddha made the retreat obligatory for his monastic community, and a monk's seniority is traditionally counted by the number of vassas he has completed.

Lay supporters commonly offer food, robes and other requisites to the community during the retreat, and some laypeople undertake stricter observance of precepts. The end of vassa is marked by the Pavarana ceremony, at which monks invite admonition for faults seen, heard or suspected during the retreat, followed by the Kathina ceremony, at which lay donors present cloth for new robes.

Related observances

Similar rains retreats exist in other Buddhist traditions: in East Asian Mahayana Buddhism, the summer retreat derived from the same Indian practice is observed in China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam, and Tibetan Buddhism maintains both summer and winter retreat periods (yarne and dbyar).
