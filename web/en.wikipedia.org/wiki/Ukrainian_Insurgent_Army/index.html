The Ukrainian Insurgent Army (Ukrainian: Українська повстанська армія, Ukrainska povstanska armiia, abbreviated UPA) was a Ukrainian nationalist guerrilla army formed in 1942 during the Second World War. It was the military wing of the Bandera faction of the Organisation of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN-B) and fought for an independent Ukrainian state, at various times against Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union, and Polish forces and civilians.

History

The UPA emerged in Volhynia in late 1942, absorbing smaller nationalist partisan units. Under commanders Dmytro Klyachkivsky and, from 1943, Roman Shukhevych, it grew into a force numbering tens of thousands, controlling significant rural areas of north-western Ukraine. In 1943–1944 UPA units carried out massacres of Polish civilians in Volhynia and Eastern Galicia, in which tens of thousands of Poles were killed—events that remain a major point of Polish–Ukrainian historical dispute.

After the Red Army retook Ukraine, the UPA waged a guerrilla war against Soviet rule, assassinating officials and resisting collectivization. Soviet counter-insurgency operations, mass deportations, and infiltration gradually destroyed the organization; Shukhevych was killed in 1950, and organized resistance had effectively ended by the mid-1950s, although the last commander, Vasyl Kuk, was captured in 1954.

Legacy

The UPA's legacy is deeply contested. In Ukraine it is widely commemorated as a liberation movement, and in 2015 Ukrainian law recognized its fighters as veterans struggling for independence; in Poland and elsewhere it is remembered chiefly for the Volhynia massacres and episodes of collaboration with Nazi Germany. Historians continue to debate its ideology, conduct, and place in national memory.
