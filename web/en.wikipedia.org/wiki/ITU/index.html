International Telecommunication Union

ITU
Founded: 17 May 1865
Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland
Parent: United Nations
Membership: 194 member states

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for matters related to information and communication technologies. Founded in Paris on 17 May 1865 as the International Telegraph Union, it is the oldest UN agency and one of the oldest international organisations in the world.

Work

The ITU coordinates the shared global use of the radio spectrum, assigns satellite orbits, promotes international cooperation in communication networks, and develops technical standards (ITU-T Recommendations). It works through three sectors: Radiocommunication (ITU-R), Telecommunication Standardization (ITU-T) and Telecommunication Development (ITU-D). Well-known ITU standards include the G-series for transmission systems and the H.264/H.265 video coding standards developed with ISO/IEC.

Structure

The Union is governed by a Plenipotentiary Conference held every four years and a Council; its Secretary-General is elected by member states. Membership includes 194 member states and hundreds of private-sector members such as carriers and manufacturers.
