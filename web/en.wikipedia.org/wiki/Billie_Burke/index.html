Mary William Ethelbert Appleton Burke (August 7, 1884 – May 14, 1970), better known as Billie Burke, was an American actress who was famous on Broadway and radio, and in early silent and later sound films. She is best known to modern audiences as Glinda the Good Witch of the North in the MGM musical film The Wizard of Oz (1939), and for her role in the Topper film series.

Burke was the wife of theatrical producer Florenz Ziegfeld Jr., founder of the Ziegfeld Follies. She was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her performance in Merrily We Live (1938).

Early life

Burke was born in Washington, D.C., the daughter of Blanche Beatty and William Ethelbert Burke, a former clown with the Barnum & Bailey Circus. Her family travelled widely in Europe, and she was educated in France and England. She made her stage debut in London in 1903 and her Broadway debut in 1907, quickly becoming one of the most celebrated stage comediennes of her era, known for her high, fluttering voice and red hair.

Career

Burke made her film debut in Peggy (1916). After her husband's death in 1932 left her with debts, she returned to Hollywood and became a prolific character actress, playing scatterbrained, fluttery society women in films such as Dinner at Eight (1933), Topper (1937), and The Man Who Came to Dinner (1942). Her portrayal of Glinda in The Wizard of Oz made her a beloved figure for generations of viewers.

Later years and death

Burke starred in her own radio and television sitcom, The Billie Burke Show / At Home with Billie Burke, in the early 1950s. She retired from acting in 1960. Burke died of natural causes in Los Angeles in 1970 at the age of 85. She has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
