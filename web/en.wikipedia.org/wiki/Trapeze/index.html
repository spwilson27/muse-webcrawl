Trapeze

A trapeze is a short horizontal bar hung by ropes or metal straps from a ceiling or frame, used by acrobats in circus and aerial arts. Performers swing, pose, and execute tricks on the bar, often high above a safety net.

Types

The flying trapeze, invented by French acrobat Jules Léotard in the 1850s, involves two platforms and a catcher: the flyer launches from one trapeze, performs somersaults in mid-air, and is caught by the hands by a partner swinging from a second trapeze. The static trapeze hangs without swinging significantly, supporting poses and balances, while the dance trapeze uses a single mounting point allowing spins close to the ground. Washington trapeze and swinging trapeze are further variants.

History

Léotard first performed his act in Paris in 1859 and popularized the one-piece costume that bears his name. The flying trapeze became a signature circus spectacle, celebrated in the 1934 song "The Daring Young Man on the Flying Trapeze." Famous flyers include Antoinette Concello and the Flying Concellos, and later Miguel Vazquez, who completed a quadruple somersault in 1982.

Technique

Flying acts depend on timing: the flyer swings to build height, releases at the peak, rotates according to the trick, and presents hands for the catch. Tricks are graded by difficulty, from the simple knee hang and split to multiple somersaults and twists. Recreational trapeze schools now teach the flying trapeze to the public with safety lines.
