Marcantonio Bragadin (21 April 1523 – 17 August 1571) was a Venetian lawyer and military officer who commanded the garrison of Famagusta in Cyprus during its siege by the Ottoman Empire in 1570–1571. His defence of the city, and his flaying alive after its surrender, made him a celebrated martyr in Venice and throughout Christian Europe.

Career

Born into the Venetian patriciate, Bragadin trained in law and served in various civil and naval posts, gaining a reputation for severity — he was said to have dealt harshly with mutinous soldiers and rebellious Cretan sailors under his command. In 1569 he was appointed capitano (military governor) of Famagusta, the principal Venetian fortress in eastern Cyprus, arriving just before the Ottoman invasion of the island in 1570.

Siege of Famagusta

After the fall of Nicosia in September 1570 — where the Ottoman commander Lala Mustafa Pasha's forces massacred much of the population — Bragadin held Famagusta with a few thousand Italian troops and local militia against an army numbering in the tens of thousands. The siege lasted some ten months (September 1570 – August 1571), during which the garrison repelled repeated assaults and conducted sorties, while Ottoman artillery and mines progressively destroyed the walls. A Venetian relief expedition failed to break through. By late July 1571, with food and gunpowder exhausted and the walls breached, Bragadin negotiated surrender on terms guaranteeing the garrison and inhabitants safe passage to Crete.

Death

When Bragadin came to Mustafa's camp on 1 August 1571 to hand over the keys of the city, he was seized — the pasha, enraged by losses and accusing the Venetians of having killed Ottoman prisoners during the truce, ordered his ears and nose cut off and had him imprisoned. After days of public humiliation, Bragadin was flayed alive in the main square of Famagusta on 17 August 1571 before a jeering crowd, dying — by Venetian accounts — defiantly professing his faith. His skin was stuffed with straw, dressed in his uniform, paraded, and sent to Constantinople as a trophy.

Legacy

In 1580 Venetian agents stole Bragadin's skin from the arsenal in Constantinople and brought it home; it was eventually placed in an urn in the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo in Venice, where his tomb can still be seen. His heroism was commemorated in paintings, pamphlets, and later monuments in Famagusta and Venice, and he remains the emblematic hero of the Venetian defence of Cyprus, overshadowed in fame only by the Christian victory at Lepanto in October of the same year.
