44 Parachute Brigade was the principal airborne formation of the South African Army during the late apartheid era. Established in April 1981 at Tempe, Bloemfontein, it consolidated the Army's parachute units under a single brigade headquarters built around 1 Parachute Battalion, the original South African paratroop unit founded in 1961.

History

South Africa's airborne capability began with 1 Parachute Battalion, raised in 1961 and modelled in part on British airborne forces. Through the South African Border War the battalion supplied company-sized groups for operations in Namibia and Angola. The brigade's best-known action was the attack on Cassinga in Angola on 4 May 1978 (Operation Reindeer), when paratroops of 2 and 3 Parachute Battalions under the pre-brigade parachute battalion group jumped onto the SWAPO base at Cassinga in one of the largest airborne operations since the Second World War; the raid remains controversial because of the large number of civilian deaths reported among the camp's inhabitants.

Organisation

At full strength the brigade comprised the full-time 1 Parachute Battalion, the Citizen Force (reserve) 2 and 3 Parachute Battalions, the 44 Pathfinder Company for reconnaissance and target marking, an anti-tank and mortar capability, engineers, signals, and the 44 Parachute Brigade training unit which ran selection and the basic parachute course — the notorious vasbyt endurance phase being a rite of passage for South African paratroops, known as "Parabats".

Restructuring

Following the end of the Border War and the integration of the armed forces after 1994, the brigade was reduced in 1999 to 44 Parachute Regiment, retaining 1 Parachute Battalion as its core unit, which remains South Africa's airborne rapid-deployment capability within the modern SANDF.
