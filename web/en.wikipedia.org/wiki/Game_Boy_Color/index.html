Game Boy Color

The Game Boy Color (commonly abbreviated as GBC) is a handheld game console manufactured by Nintendo, released on October 21, 1998 in Japan and the following month internationally. It is the successor to the original Game Boy and is part of the Game Boy line of consoles. Its defining feature is a colour screen, answering competition from colour handhelds such as the Atari Lynx and Sega Game Gear while retaining the Game Boy's long battery life.

Hardware

The Game Boy Color uses a Sharp LR35902-based custom CPU running at up to 8 MHz — twice the speed of the original Game Boy, a mode it engages when running GBC-optimised cartridges — with 32 KB of RAM and 16 KB of video RAM. The 160 × 144 pixel screen can display up to 32,768 colours, with up to 56 colours on screen simultaneously in native GBC games. The system is backward compatible with the entire original Game Boy library, applying automatic colour palettes to older monochrome games. It also added an infrared port used by a small number of titles, and ran for up to about 30 hours on two AA batteries.

Games

The system launched with titles such as Tetris DX and was driven by major releases including Pokémon Gold and Silver and Pokémon Crystal, The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening DX, and the twin Oracle of Seasons and Oracle of Ages. Pokémon Gold and Silver alone sold over 23 million copies. Combined, the original Game Boy and Game Boy Color sold 118.69 million units, making the platform one of the best-selling console systems of all time.

Legacy

The Game Boy Color was produced until 2003, outliving its 2001 successor's launch by two years. Its translucent "Atomic Purple" variant became an emblem of late-1990s consumer design.
