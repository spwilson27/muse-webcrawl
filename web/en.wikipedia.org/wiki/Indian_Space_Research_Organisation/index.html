The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is the national space agency of India, headquartered in Bengaluru. It operates under the Department of Space, which reports directly to the prime minister of India. ISRO is one of the six government space agencies in the world with full launch capabilities, including the ability to deploy cryogenic engines and to undertake extraterrestrial missions.

History

Organised space research in India began in 1962 with the Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR), championed by physicist Vikram Sarabhai. ISRO superseded INCOSPAR in 1969. India's first satellite, Aryabhata, was launched by the Soviet Union in 1975, and the Rohini satellite of 1980 made India the sixth nation to launch a satellite on its own rocket (the SLV-3, led by A. P. J. Abdul Kalam).

Launch vehicles

ISRO's workhorse launchers are the PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle), famed for its reliability and for the 2017 PSLV-C37 mission that deployed a record 104 satellites in one flight, and the heavier LVM3 (formerly GSLV Mk III), which launches India's large communication satellites and human spaceflight missions. The SSLV small launcher entered service in the 2020s.

Notable missions

Chandrayaan-1 (2008) confirmed water molecules on the Moon. The Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan, 2014) made India the first country to reach Mars orbit on its maiden attempt. Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar south pole in August 2023, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon. The Aditya-L1 solar observatory reached the Sun–Earth L1 point in 2024, and the Gaganyaan programme aims to fly Indian astronauts on an Indian spacecraft.
