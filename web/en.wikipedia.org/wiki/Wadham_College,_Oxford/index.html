Wadham College is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford in the United Kingdom. It is located in the centre of Oxford, at the intersection of Broad Street and Parks Road. Wadham College was founded in 1610 by Nicholas and Dorothy Wadham, wealthy Somerset landowners, during the reign of King James I. The central buildings, a notable example of Jacobean architecture, were built between 1610 and 1613 to designs by William Arnold.

History

Nicholas Wadham died in 1609 leaving his fortune to endow a college at Oxford; his widow Dorothy, then 75, directed the project to completion. Under the statutes, the college consisted of a warden, 15 fellows, 15 scholars and a number of commoners. The college admitted women as full members from 1974, among the first of the formerly men's colleges to do so.

Wadham has a reputation for liberalism and informality. In the 17th century it was the intellectual home of the group of experimental natural philosophers that included Christopher Wren, Robert Hooke, John Wilkins and Seth Ward, whose meetings contributed to the founding of the Royal Society.

Buildings and grounds

The main site centres on the original Jacobean quadrangle, with its hall and chapel featuring a fine east window of stained glass by Bernard van Linge. Later additions include the Bowra Building, the Ferdinand Mount Building and modern accommodation at the Dorothy Wadham Building on Iffley Road. The college gardens include the Fellows' Garden beside the River Cherwell.

Notable alumni include Christopher Wren, John Wilkins, the poet and satirist John Wilmot, Earl of Rochester, the writer Thomas Beecham-era figures, and more recently the epidemiologist and politician. See also: University of Oxford, Christopher Wren, Colleges of the University of Oxford.
