The Local Supercluster (LSC or LS), also commonly known as the Virgo Supercluster (VSC or VS), is a galaxy supercluster which contains the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies, as part of the Local Group, along with some 47,000 other galaxies. The Local Supercluster is one of about 10 million superclusters in the observable universe and is now known to be a subsidiary of the larger Laniakea Supercluster.

It is elongated in shape, with a diameter of about 33 megaparsecs (107 million light-years). Its centre is the Virgo Cluster of galaxies, and it contains roughly 100 galaxy groups and clusters. Its mass has been estimated at around 1015 solar masses.

History

Beginning with the first large sample of nebulae published by William and Caroline Herschel in 1863, it has been known that there is a marked excess of nebular fields in the constellation Virgo, near the north galactic pole. In the 1950s, French-American astronomer Gérard Henri de Vaucouleurs first argued that this excess represented a large-scale galaxy-like structure, coining the term "Local Supergalaxy" in 1953, which he changed to "Local Supercluster" in 1958. Debate continued during the 1960s and 1970s as to whether the Local Supercluster (LS) was actually a structure or a possible alignment of galaxies.

The issue was resolved with the large redshift surveys of the late 1970s and early 1980s, which convincingly showed the flattened concentration of galaxies along the supergalactic plane. In 2014 it was announced that the Local Supercluster is only a lobe of a greater supercluster, Laniakea, centred on the Great Attractor.

Structure

The Local Supercluster has a flattened, pancake-like shape, roughly 60 percent of its galaxies concentrated in a thin plane. It is divided into two components: a disk component and a halo component. The disk contains luminous spiral and irregular galaxies; the halo contains luminous elliptical galaxies.

The Local Group is located in a small filament on the outskirts of the supercluster, in the Local Sheet. The Local Supercluster's main subgroups include the Virgo Cluster, the Ursa Major Cluster, and the Fornax Cluster.
