The Waverley novels are a long series of novels by Sir Walter Scott (1771–1832). For nearly a century they were among the most popular and widely read novels in Europe, and their success effectively founded the genre of the historical novel.

The series takes its name from Scott's first novel, Waverley (1814), which was published anonymously. Later volumes were issued as being "by the Author of Waverley", and Scott's authorship became an open secret long before he publicly acknowledged it in 1827. The novels include The Antiquary, The Heart of Mid-Lothian, The Bride of Lammermoor, Ivanhoe, Kenilworth, Quentin Durward and The Talisman.

Influence

The Waverley novels combined romance with careful historical detail and realistic depiction of manners and speech, influencing writers across Europe and America, including Balzac, Pushkin, Tolstoy and James Fenimore Cooper. Although their popularity faded in the 20th century, critical interest has revived, particularly in the Scottish novels.
