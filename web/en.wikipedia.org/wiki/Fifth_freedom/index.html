Fifth freedom

Fifth freedom
CategoryFreedoms of the air
DefinitionRight to carry passengers/cargo between two foreign countries on a flight originating or ending in the carrier's home country
Legal basisBilateral and multilateral air service agreements; Chicago Convention framework

The fifth freedom of the air is the right of an airline to carry revenue passengers and cargo between two countries other than its own, provided the flight originates or terminates in the airline's home country. It is one of the nine "freedoms of the air" recognised in international aviation law.

Definition and scope

Under a fifth freedom right, for example, an airline based in the United Arab Emirates may operate Dubai–Bangkok–Hong Kong and sell seats on the Bangkok–Hong Kong sector even though neither city is in its home country. Such rights are granted through air service agreements between the states concerned and were historically important when aircraft lacked the range for non-stop long-haul flights. They remain commercially significant on routes such as Singapore Airlines' Singapore–Frankfurt–New York service and Emirates' services linking Australia and New Zealand or North and South America via intermediate points.

Policy debates

Fifth freedom operations can be controversial because they compete directly with home carriers of the countries served. Supporters argue they increase connectivity and consumer choice; critics in some markets have sought to restrict them. Within the European Union, member-state carriers enjoy even broader seventh freedom-style rights under the single aviation market.

Related freedoms

The first and second freedoms cover overflight and technical stops; the third and fourth cover carriage to and from the home country; freedoms six through nine cover combinations of connecting traffic and cabotage.
