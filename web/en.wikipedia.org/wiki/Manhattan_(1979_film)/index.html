Manhattan is a 1979 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Woody Allen and produced by Charles H. Joffe. The screenplay, by Allen and Marshall Brickman, follows twice-divorced 42-year-old comedy writer Isaac Davis (Allen), who dates a 17-year-old girl (Mariel Hemingway) but falls in love with his best friend's mistress (Diane Keaton). Meryl Streep and Anne Byrne also star.

Manhattan was Allen's first film shot in black-and-white and in the widescreen 2.39:1 aspect ratio. It features a score of music by George Gershwin, including Rhapsody in Blue, performed by the New York Philharmonic under Zubin Mehta and the Buffalo Philharmonic under Michael Tilson Thomas.

Plot

The film opens with Isaac dictating the first lines of a novel about his love for New York City. Isaac is dating Tracy, a 17-year-old high school student he does not consider a serious prospect. His best friend Yale is having an affair with journalist Mary Wilkie while married to Emily. Isaac meets Mary and initially dislikes her pretentiousness, but after Yale ends the affair, Isaac and Mary begin a relationship. As the story unfolds, loyalties shift; Yale resumes his relationship with Mary, and Isaac, realising his feelings for Tracy, runs across the city to reach her before she leaves for London — where she urges him to have "a little faith in people."

Reception

Manhattan was a critical and commercial success and was nominated for two Academy Awards: Best Supporting Actress (Hemingway) and Best Original Screenplay. It is widely regarded as one of Allen's finest films and a landmark portrait of New York City. In 2001 the United States Library of Congress selected it for preservation in the National Film Registry.
