Illinois Waterway

The Illinois Waterway (ILWW) is a system of rivers, lakes, and canals in the U.S. state of Illinois that provides a navigable connection between the Great Lakes and the Mississippi River. It stretches about 336 miles (541 km) from Lake Michigan at Chicago to the confluence of the Illinois River with the Mississippi at Grafton, Illinois.

Composition

The waterway comprises the Chicago River, the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal, the Des Plaines River, and the Illinois River. A series of locks and dams, operated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, maintains a nine-foot navigation channel for commercial barge traffic carrying grain, coal, petroleum, and other bulk commodities.

History

The route follows the historic Chicago Portage used by Native Americans and French explorers, and later the Illinois and Michigan Canal (opened 1848). The modern waterway was completed in 1933, incorporating the Sanitary and Ship Canal built at the turn of the twentieth century, which reversed the flow of the Chicago River.

Ecology

The waterway is a conduit of concern for invasive species, notably Asian carp moving toward Lake Michigan; electric barriers near Romeoville, Illinois, have been installed to deter their passage.
