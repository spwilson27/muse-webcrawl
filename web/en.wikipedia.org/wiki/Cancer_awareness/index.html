Cancer awareness refers to public efforts to increase knowledge about cancer — its symptoms, risk factors, screening methods and treatments — and to raise funds for research and patient support. Awareness activity is organised by charities, health agencies and patient groups, and is often concentrated in designated awareness days, weeks and months.

Awareness months and symbols

Different cancer types have designated awareness periods: October is widely observed as Breast Cancer Awareness Month, symbolised by the pink ribbon; November hosts campaigns such as Movember (prostate and men's health) and lung cancer awareness, symbolised by a white or pearl ribbon; February includes World Cancer Day (4 February). A large table of ribbon colours associates particular colours with particular cancers and causes.

Campaigns and goals

Campaigns aim to encourage early detection (for example through screening programmes for breast, cervical and colorectal cancers), promote prevention (smoking cessation, vaccination against HPV and hepatitis B, sun safety), reduce stigma, and fund research. Common activities include sponsored runs and walks, media campaigns, workplace events and the lighting of landmarks in campaign colours.

Criticism

Awareness campaigns have been criticised for "pinkwashing" — the use of cancer branding for marketing — for emphasising individual behaviour over environmental and occupational causes, and for raising awareness without equivalent increases in funding for treatment or research. Researchers also debate whether awareness alone changes outcomes compared with investment in screening infrastructure and healthcare access.
