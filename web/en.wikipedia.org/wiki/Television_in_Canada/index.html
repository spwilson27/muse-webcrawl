Television in Canada

Television in Canada officially began with the sign-on of the nation's first television stations in Montreal and Toronto in 1952: CBFT in Montreal on 6 September 1952 and CBLT in Toronto on 8 September 1952. Television has long been a major medium in Canada, although proximity to the United States means American programming has always played a large role in Canadian schedules.

Networks

The Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC) operates the public English-language CBC Television network, while its French counterpart is Ici Radio-Canada Télé. The main private English networks are CTV, owned by Bell Media, and Global, owned by Corus Entertainment; Citytv is a smaller network owned by Rogers. In Quebec, TVA is the leading French-language private network. Aboriginal and educational broadcasters, such as APTN and TVOntario, also serve audiences nationwide and provincially.

Regulation and Canadian content

Broadcasting is regulated by the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC). To protect domestic production, Canadian broadcasters are subject to Canadian content ("CanCon") quotas, and cable and satellite distributors must apply simultaneous substitution, replacing the signal of a US station with the Canadian station airing the same programme — a practice that has famously affected Super Bowl advertising.

Cable and specialty channels

Canada has one of the world's highest rates of cable and satellite penetration. Specialty channels include TSN and Sportsnet in sports, CTV News Channel and CBC News Network in news, and Much in music and youth programming. Major distributors include Rogers Cable, Shaw, Vidéotron and Bell Satellite TV.

History

Early broadcasts relied heavily on American imports and CBC productions such as Hockey Night in Canada, which remains a national institution. The 1960s and 1970s saw the launch of CTV (1961) and Global (1974), the expansion of French-language television, and the growth of cable. The transition to digital terrestrial television was completed in major markets in 2011.
