Jyotisha

Jyotisha (Sanskrit: ज्योतिष, romanized Jyotiṣa) is the traditional Hindu system of astrology, also known as Hindu astrology, and more recently as Vedic astrology. It is one of the six auxiliary disciplines (Vedangas) connected with the study of the Vedas.

The earliest use of the term Jyotisha is in the sense of a science of tracking the movements of heavenly bodies in order to keep time and determine the auspicious moments for performing Vedic rituals. The earliest surviving text dedicated to the topic is the Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa, attributed to the sage Lagadha and dating to roughly the first millennium BCE in its core. It describes rules for tracking the motions of the Sun and the Moon for the purposes of timekeeping and the sacrificial calendar.

History

Jyotisha developed over many centuries through contact with Babylonian and Hellenistic astrology. By the classical period, Indian astrologers had absorbed the twelve-sign zodiac, the concept of birth charts (horoscopy), and omen literature from the West, fusing these with indigenous calendrical and ritual traditions. Important classical texts include the Bṛhat Saṃhitā of Varāhamihira (6th century CE), which covers mundane astrology, omens, and weather forecasting, and the works of commentators such as Utpala.

Later medieval compendia, including the Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra attributed to the sage Parashara, systematized natal astrology: the interpretation of a birth chart divided into twelve houses (bhāvas) and partitioned by the lunar mansions (nakṣatras), the planetary periods known as daśās — most famously the Vimśottarī daśā — and an elaborate scheme of planetary strengths and yogas.

Branches

Traditionally Jyotisha is divided into three branches (skandhas):

  • Siddhānta — mathematical astronomy, the computation of planetary positions.
  • Saṃhitā — mundane astrology: omens, natural phenomena, weather, and events affecting nations.
  • Horā — predictive astrology, including natal charts (jātaka), electional astrology (muhūrta, choosing auspicious times), and horary astrology (praśna, answering a specific question).

Elements of the chart

A Jyotisha chart records the positions of the nine grahas ("seizers"): the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, and the two lunar nodes, Rāhu and Ketu. Unlike Western tropical astrology, Jyotisha uses a sidereal zodiac corrected for the precession of the equinoxes by an offset called the ayanāṃśa. The chart is drawn in regional styles — the North Indian diamond chart, the South Indian square chart, and the East Indian chart.

Modern practice

Jyotisha remains widely practised in India and the Hindu diaspora, consulted for marriage compatibility (matching of birth charts by nakṣatra), career decisions, and the timing of major undertakings. It is taught in some Indian universities, and its historical astronomical component is studied as part of the history of science. Critics note the absence of empirical support for its predictive claims, while practitioners regard it as a traditional discipline integrating cosmology, ritual, and psychology.
