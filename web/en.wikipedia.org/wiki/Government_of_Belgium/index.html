The government of Belgium operates under a federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy. Belgium is divided between a federal government, three language-based Communities (Flemish, French and German-speaking) and three Regions (Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels-Capital), each with its own government and parliament — a structure created by successive state reforms between 1970 and 1993.

Federal government

The federal executive is headed by the Prime Minister, appointed by the King after elections, and consists of ministers and secretaries of state. By constitutional requirement the Council of Ministers (apart from the Prime Minister) comprises equal numbers of Dutch-speaking and French-speaking ministers. Federal authority covers defence, justice, finance, social security, foreign affairs and public health, among other matters.

Formation

Because of Belgium's fragmented party system — parties are split along linguistic lines — coalition negotiations (formations) are famously lengthy. After the 2010 election, Belgium set a modern record of 541 days without an elected government before a coalition was formed under Elio Di Rupo.

Monarchy and legislature

The King is head of state, formally appointing the government and sanctioning legislation, while executive power is exercised by the ministers. The bicameral Federal Parliament consists of the directly elected Chamber of Representatives (150 members) and the Senate, largely composed of representatives of the community and regional parliaments.
