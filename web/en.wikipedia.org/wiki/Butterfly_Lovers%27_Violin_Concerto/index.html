The Butterfly Lovers' Violin Concerto is one of the most famous works of Chinese orchestral music. Composed in 1958–1959 by He Zhanhao and Chen Gang, then students at the Shanghai Conservatory of Music, the concerto adapts the Chinese legend of the Butterfly Lovers — the tragic romance of Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai — into a single-movement concerto for violin and orchestra.

Music

The work blends Western concerto form with pentatonic melodies and techniques drawn from Chinese opera and folk music; the solo violin imitates the inflections of the erhu and pipa in places. Its three broad sections depict the lovers' meeting and happy days at school, their resistance to Zhu's arranged marriage, and their deaths and transformation into butterflies.

Reception

Premiered in Shanghai in May 1959 by the young violinist Yu Lina, the concerto was suppressed during the Cultural Revolution but revived thereafter and has become a staple of Chinese concert life and of violinists worldwide.
