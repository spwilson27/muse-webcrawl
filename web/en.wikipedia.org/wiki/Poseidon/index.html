Poseidon is a god of ancient Greek religion and mythology, one of the twelve Olympians and brother of Zeus and Hades. He is god of the sea, of earthquakes ("Earth-Shaker") and of horses; his Roman equivalent is Neptune.

Mythology

Swallowed at birth by his father Cronus, Poseidon was freed when Zeus forced Cronus to disgorge his children, and he fought beside his brothers against the Titans. When the three brothers divided the cosmos by lot, Zeus received the sky, Hades the underworld and Poseidon the sea. His temperament matched his element: moody and vengeful, he persecuted Odysseus for blinding his son, the Cyclops Polyphemus; contested Athens with Athena and lost; and sent storms and sea monsters at offences against his honour. His wife was the sea-goddess Amphitrite; his children include the hero Theseus (in one tradition), the winged horse Pegasus's sire-line legends, and numerous monsters.

Cult and legacy

Poseidon was worshipped at coastal sanctuaries across Greece — at Isthmia near Corinth, where the Isthmian Games were held in his honour, and at Cape Sounion, whose temple still stands over the Aegean. Horses and bulls were sacrificed to him, and his trident, dolphins and storms remain the standard iconography of the sea's power; the planet Neptune bears his Roman name.
