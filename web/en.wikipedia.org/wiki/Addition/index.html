Addition

Addition (usually signified by the plus symbol, +) is one of the four basic operations of arithmetic, the other three being subtraction, multiplication, and division. The addition of two whole numbers results in the total amount or sum of those values combined. For example, the adjacent image shows two columns of three apples and two apples respectively, totaling at five apples. This observation is equivalent to the mathematical expression "3 + 2 = 5" (that is, "3 plus 2 is equal to 5").

Notation and terminology

Addition is written using the plus sign "+" between the terms; that is, in infix notation. The result is expressed with an equals sign. For example, 1 + 2 = 3. The numbers being added are called addends or summands, and the result is called the sum. Addition can also be defined on other objects: vectors, matrices, and elements of abelian groups are added componentwise or by a group operation.

Properties

  • Commutativity: a + b = b + a. The order of the addends does not change the sum.
  • Associativity: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c). Grouping the addends does not change the sum.
  • Identity element: adding zero leaves a number unchanged: a + 0 = a.
  • Successor: in the Peano axioms, addition is defined recursively from the successor function.

Performing addition

Children typically learn addition first by counting on their fingers and then by memorizing basic number facts (the "addition table") for single digits. Adding multi-digit numbers in a positional numeral system uses an algorithm in which digits are added column by column from right to left, with carrying when a column sum exceeds the base. Addition of fractions requires a common denominator, while addition of decimals aligns the decimal points. Mechanical and electronic calculators perform addition in binary using adder circuits.

Extensions

Addition generalizes far beyond natural numbers: to integers, rational and real numbers, complex numbers, and abstract algebraic structures. Repeated addition defines multiplication by natural numbers. Infinite addition leads to the theory of series. In set theory, addition of cardinal and ordinal numbers extends the operation to infinite quantities, where commutativity may hold for cardinals but fails for ordinals.
