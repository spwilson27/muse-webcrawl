The Narrow Corridor

AuthorsDaron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson
SubjectPolitical economy, liberty, state capacity
PublisherPenguin Press
Published2019

The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty is a 2019 book by economists Daron Acemoglu and James A. Robinson, a sequel to their 2012 bestseller Why Nations Fail. It argues that liberty arises when a strong, capable state is kept in balance by a strong, mobilized society, and that this balance — the "narrow corridor" between despotism and anarchy — is fragile and must be constantly contested.

Argument

The authors use the metaphor of the Leviathan, after Thomas Hobbes, for the state. A Despotic Leviathan (such as modern China) dominates society and suppresses liberty; an Absent Leviathan (such as Yemen or pre-colonial stateless societies dominated by the "cage of norms") leaves people unprotected from violence; a Shackled Leviathan — constrained by an assertive citizenry, as in the United States, Switzerland, or post-medieval Europe — creates conditions for liberty to flourish. The "Red Queen effect," borrowed from evolutionary biology, describes how state and society must run ever faster together to stay in the corridor.

Case studies

The book surveys evidence from ancient Athens and Zapotec Monte Albán to medieval Iceland, the Glorious Revolution, Meiji Japan, Saudi Arabia, India and its caste system, and contemporary Colombia and Lebanon, arguing that entering and remaining in the corridor depends on contingent struggle rather than culture or geography alone.

Reception

The book was a New York Times bestseller and a Financial Times and McKinsey Business Book of the Year finalist. Reviewers praised its scope while some historians questioned its compression of complex histories into a single state–society balance. It continues Acemoglu and Robinson's "institutionalist" account of development; they later extended the argument to technology in Power and Progress (2023), and Acemoglu shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Economics for work on institutions.
