Pendulum

A pendulum is a device made of a weight suspended from a pivot so that it can swing freely. When displaced sideways from its resting equilibrium position, it is subject to a restoring force due to gravity that accelerates it back toward equilibrium, producing periodic oscillation.

Period

For small amplitudes, the period T ≈ 2π√(L/g), where L is length and g gravitational acceleration — approximately independent of mass and amplitude (isochronism, noted by Galileo).

History

Galileo Galilei studied pendulum motion in the early 1600s. Christiaan Huygens built the first pendulum clock in 1656, greatly improving timekeeping. Foucault's pendulum (1851) demonstrated Earth's rotation.

Applications

Pendulums have been used in clocks, seismometers, metronomes, and as gravimeters. See resonance and Hooke's law for related harmonic motion.
