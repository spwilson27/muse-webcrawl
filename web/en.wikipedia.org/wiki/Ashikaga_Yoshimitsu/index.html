Ashikaga Yoshimitsu

Ashikaga Yoshimitsu (Japanese: 足利 義満, September 25, 1358 – May 31, 1408) was the third shōgun of the Ashikaga shogunate, ruling from 1368 to 1394 during the Muromachi period of Japan.

Shōgun1368–1394
Born25 September 1358, Kyoto
Died31 May 1408, Kyoto

Rule

Yoshimitsu reunified the Northern and Southern Courts in 1392, ending the Nanboku-chō period, and strengthened shogunal authority. He established trade and tributary relations with Ming China, accepting the title "King of Japan" in that system, and suppressed piracy.

Culture

After abdication in favour of his son Yoshimochi, he built the Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion) as part of his Kitayama villa in Kyoto, centrepiece of Kitayama culture blending court, samurai and Zen aesthetics. He patronised Noh theatre and Zeami.
