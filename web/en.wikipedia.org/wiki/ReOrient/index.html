AuthorAndre Gunder Frank
Published1998
PublisherUniversity of California Press
SubjectWorld history, economic history

ReOrient: Global Economy in the Asian Age is a 1998 book by the German-American historian and political economist Andre Gunder Frank. It argues that Asia, and particularly China, was the centre of the world economy until around 1800, and that Europe's subsequent rise was late, brief, and contingent — made possible in part by American silver, which Europeans used to buy their way into Asian markets. The book is a major work of world-systems theory and a challenge to Eurocentric accounts of the Industrial Revolution and the "rise of the West."

ReOrient (1998), Andre Gunder Frank's revisionist account of the early modern world economy.

Argument

Frank rejected the idea of successive, self-contained modes of production in favour of a single world system with a five-thousand-year history. In ReOrient he contended that until about 1800 the global economy was centred on Asia: China absorbed a large share of the world's silver, exported manufactures such as silk, porcelain, and tea, and ran persistent trade surpluses. Europe, by contrast, was a marginal periphery that produced little Asian consumers wanted and compensated with silver looted or mined in the Americas.

On this account, the "decline of the East" and the "rise of the West" were simultaneous, recent, and cyclical phenomena rather than the result of long-standing European superiority in rationality, institutions, or technology. Frank predicted that Asia — above all China — would resume its central place in the world economy, a process he described as a "re-orienting" of the global system.

Reception

The book received wide attention and mixed-to-hostile reviews from some economic historians, who criticised its use of trade data and its dismissal of institutional explanations, while others praised it as a landmark of global history. It is frequently discussed alongside Kenneth Pomeranz's The Great Divergence (2000), which reached related conclusions by a different route.
