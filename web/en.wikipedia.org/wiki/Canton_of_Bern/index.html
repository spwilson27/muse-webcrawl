Canton of Bern

The Canton of Bern (German: Kanton Bern; French: canton de Berne) is the second-largest of the 26 cantons of Switzerland by both surface area and population. Its capital and largest city is Bern, which is also the de facto capital of Switzerland and seat of the federal authorities.

Geography

The canton stretches from the Jura Mountains in the northwest, across the Swiss Plateau, to the high Bernese Alps in the south, which include famous peaks such as the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau and the canton's highest mountain, the Finsteraarhorn. Major lakes include Lake Thun, Lake Brienz, and Lake Biel. The Bernese Oberland in the south is one of Switzerland's principal tourist regions, centred on resorts such as Interlaken, Grindelwald, and Gstaad.

History

The city of Bern, founded in 1191, joined the Swiss Confederation in 1353 and grew into one of its most powerful members, acquiring extensive subject territories including the Vaud in 1536. The canton was a centre of the Swiss Reformation under the influence of Huldrych Zwingli's successors. In 1979 the French-speaking northern districts seceded to form the canton of Jura, the most recent change to Switzerland's cantonal map; a residual "Jura question" over the town of Moutier was settled decades later when voters chose to transfer it to Jura.

Government and language

Bern is officially bilingual: German predominates, with Bernese German dialects spoken widely, while French is the majority language in the Bernese Jura region and in bilingual Biel/Bienne. The cantonal government consists of a seven-member Executive Council and a 160-member Grand Council legislature.
