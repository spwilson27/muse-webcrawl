Félix Houphouët-Boigny

Félix Houphouët-Boigny (18 October 1905 – 7 December 1993), affectionately called Le Vieux ("the Old One"), was the first president of Ivory Coast (Côte d'Ivoire), serving from independence in 1960 until his death in 1993 — one of Africa's longest-serving leaders.


In office: 1960–1993
Capital projects: Yamoussoukro Basilica
Party: PDCI

Early career

A Baoulé chief's heir trained as a medical assistant, he became a wealthy planter, founded an African farmers' union, and was elected to the French National Assembly in 1945, serving in French governments.

Presidency

Houphouët-Boigny pursued pro-Western, capitalist policies and close ties with France ("Françafrique"), producing the "Ivorian miracle" of cocoa- and coffee-fuelled growth in the 1960s–70s. He moved the capital to his home village Yamoussoukro and built there the Basilica of Our Lady of Peace, among the world's largest churches. Economic decline and one-party rule drew criticism in later years. Compare Frederick Chiluba.
