Tracy–Widom distribution

The Tracy–Widom distributions are probability distributions arising in random matrix theory as the limiting laws of the largest eigenvalue of large random Hermitian matrices. They were derived by Craig Tracy and Harold Widom in the 1990s.

Definition

For the Gaussian unitary ensemble (β = 2), the centred and scaled largest eigenvalue converges to a distribution whose cumulative distribution function can be written in terms of the Hastings–McLeod solution of the Painlevé II equation. Analogous laws hold for the Gaussian orthogonal (β = 1) and symplectic (β = 4) ensembles, with related formulas. The distributions are skewed, with a left tail decaying roughly like exp(−|x|³/12) and a right tail like exp(−2x^(3/2)/3).

Universality and applications

Tracy–Widom laws exemplify KPZ universality: the same β = 1 and β = 2 distributions appear in the fluctuations of growing interfaces, directed polymers, traffic and queuing models, and the longest increasing subsequence problem (where the β = 2 law emerged via the Baik–Deift–Johansson theorem). They have also been applied to data analysis problems such as detecting signals in large covariance matrices.
