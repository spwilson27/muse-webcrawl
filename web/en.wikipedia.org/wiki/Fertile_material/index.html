Fertile material

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fertile material is a material that, although not itself fissile (capable of sustaining a nuclear chain reaction with thermal neutrons), can be converted into fissile material by neutron absorption and subsequent nuclear decay. The two naturally occurring fertile materials are uranium-238, which makes up about 99.3% of natural uranium, and thorium-232, which is roughly three to four times more abundant in the Earth's crust than uranium. Converting fertile material into fissile fuel — "breeding" — underlies breeder reactor technology and greatly extends the energy extractable from mined uranium and thorium.

Conversion chains

When uranium-238 absorbs a neutron it becomes uranium-239, which beta-decays with a half-life of about 23 minutes to neptunium-239, which in turn beta-decays (half-life 2.36 days) to plutonium-239, a fissile isotope:

²³⁸U + n → ²³⁹U → ²³⁹Np → ²³⁹Pu

Similarly, thorium-232 absorbs a neutron to become thorium-233, decaying through protactinium-233 to fissile uranium-233:

²³²Th + n → ²³³Th → ²³³Pa → ²³³U

Breeding

In an ordinary power reactor, conversion of U-238 to Pu-239 happens incidentally and supplies a substantial fraction of the reactor's energy over its fuel cycle; conventional reactors have a conversion ratio of about 0.5–0.65. A breeder reactor is designed with more neutrons available so that its conversion (breeding) ratio exceeds 1 — it produces more fissile material than it consumes, typically either in a fast-neutron uranium–plutonium cycle or a thermal thorium–uranium-233 cycle. Because fertile material is so much more abundant than fissile uranium-235, full use of breeding could extend nuclear fuel resources from centuries to many thousands of years.

Related concepts

A layer of fertile material surrounding a reactor core to capture leaking neutrons is called a breeding blanket. Fertile isotopes differ from merely fissionable ones: U-238 can be fissioned by fast neutrons but cannot sustain a chain reaction, hence the separate "fertile" category reserved for isotopes convertible to fissile material. Depleted uranium — mostly U-238 left over from enrichment — is stockpiled in large quantities as a potential future fertile resource.
