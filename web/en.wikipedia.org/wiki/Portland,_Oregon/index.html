Portland, Oregon

Portland is a port city in the Pacific Northwest and the seat of Multnomah County, Oregon, United States. Named after Portland, Maine, the city sits at the confluence of the Willamette and Columbia rivers and is known for its bridges, parks, microbreweries, coffee culture, and strong land-use planning. Portland is the most populous city in Oregon, with a population of roughly 635,000 in the city proper and about 2.5 million in the metropolitan area.

History

The area was inhabited by Chinookan peoples, including the Multnomah, for thousands of years before European settlement. The site became a trading clearing on the Willamette River in the 1830s and 1840s. In 1845, Asa Lovejoy and Francis Pettygrove flipped a coin to name the new town after one of their Maine hometowns; Pettygrove's Portland won over Lovejoy's Boston. The famous "Portland Penny" is on display at the Oregon Historical Society. Portland incorporated in 1851 and grew as a mercantile and maritime hub, later becoming an important lumber and shipbuilding center, particularly during World War II with the Kaiser shipyards and the planned city of Vanport, destroyed by a flood in 1948.

Geography and climate

Portland lies on the Willamette River near its confluence with the Columbia, within sight of Mount Hood to the east. The city has a warm-summer Mediterranean climate with wet winters and dry summers. Forest Park, in the west hills, is one of the largest urban forests in the United States at over 5,200 acres.

Culture

Portland is famous for its roses — the International Rose Test Garden was established in 1917 — and the annual Portland Rose Festival. The city is home to Powell's City of Books, often called the world's largest independent bookstore, as well as a celebrated food-cart scene, craft brewing, and alternative culture popularized by the slogan "Keep Portland Weird." Major sports teams include the Portland Thorns FC and the Portland Timbers of the Timbers–Sounders rivalry. The television series Portlandia satirized the city's culture, and its name was borrowed from the downtown statue Portlandia.

Government

Portland long used a commission form of government unique among large American cities, but voters approved a charter change in 2022 adopting a mayor–council system with ranked-choice voting and four multi-member districts, effective in 2025. The broader region is served by Metro, an elected regional government that manages the urban growth boundary.
