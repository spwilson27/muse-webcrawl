The 2030 United States census, known as Census 2030, will be the twenty-fifth decennial United States census. As required by the United States Constitution, a census of the population has been conducted every ten years since 1790. National Census Day for the 2030 count is April 1, 2030. The results will be used to reapportion seats in the United States House of Representatives among the states and to redraw congressional and state legislative districts.

Background

Article One of the United States Constitution requires an enumeration of the population every ten years so that seats in the House of Representatives can be apportioned among the several states according to their respective numbers. The Census Bureau began early design and testing for the 2030 census in the years following the completion of the 2020 census, including research into internet self-response, address canvassing, and the counting of group quarters and hard-to-reach populations.

Questionnaire

The short-form questionnaire is expected to ask about the number of people living in a housing unit, tenure (whether the home is owned or rented), age, sex, race, Hispanic or Latino origin, and the relationship of each person to the householder. Proposals debated during the planning phase included the possible addition of a combined race and ethnicity question and, following litigation over the 2020 census, a question on citizenship; the inclusion of any such question requires approval through the federal statistical standards process and notification to Congress.

Reapportionment

Apportionment counts are scheduled to be delivered to the President by the statutory deadline, with redistricting data files following for the states. Population shifts observed in the 2020 census and subsequent estimates suggested continued seat gains for states in the South and West and losses in parts of the Northeast and Midwest.
