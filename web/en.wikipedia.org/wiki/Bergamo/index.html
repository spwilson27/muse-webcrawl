Bergamo (Italian: [ˈbɛrɡamo]; Bergamasque: Bèrghem) is a city in Lombardy, northern Italy, about 40 kilometres northeast of Milan, at the southern edge of the Bergamo Alps. It is the capital of the province of Bergamo and the fourth-largest city in Lombardy, with about 120,000 inhabitants.

Geography and layout

The city is famously divided in two. The Città Alta ("Upper City") is a medieval hilltop town enclosed by Venetian walls built in the sixteenth century — inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2017 as part of the Venetian Works of Defence — while the Città Bassa ("Lower City") spreads across the plain below in modern districts. Two funiculars connect the parts. The old town contains the Piazza Vecchia, the Palazzo della Ragione, the cathedral, the baptistery, and the Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore with the Colleoni Chapel, burial place of the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni.

History

Bergamo began as the Orobian and then Celtic settlement of Bergomum, became a Roman municipium, and was destroyed by Attila. It was a Lombard duchy, then a free commune, and from 1428 until 1797 belonged to the Republic of Venice, which fortified it as a frontier stronghold against Milan. It passed to Austria, joined united Italy in 1859–60 — Garibaldi recruited many of his "Thousand" in the area, earning Bergamo the title Città dei Mille ("City of the Thousand").

In March 2020 Bergamo became a symbol of the COVID-19 pandemic in Italy when its hospitals were overwhelmed; images of army trucks carrying coffins to other cities circulated worldwide, and the province recorded one of the highest excess death rates in Europe.

Culture and economy

Bergamo is the birthplace of the composer Gaetano Donizetti, honoured by a museum and theatre, and of the commedia dell'arte mask Arlecchino (Harlequin), traditionally a servant from Bergamo. The Accademia Carrara is a notable picture gallery. The city's economy rests on manufacturing, services and logistics; Il Caravaggio International Airport at Orio al Serio, marketed as Milan Bergamo, is one of Italy's busiest low-cost hubs. The local football club, Atalanta, has achieved European success, winning the UEFA Europa League in 2024.
