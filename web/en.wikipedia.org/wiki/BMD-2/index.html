The BMD-2 gives Russian airborne troops a fully tracked, amphibious fighting vehicle that can be dropped by parachute.

The BMD-2 (Boyevaya Mashina Desanta, "airborne combat vehicle") is a Soviet airborne infantry fighting vehicle introduced in 1985 as the successor to the BMD-1. Like its predecessor it equips the Russian Airborne Forces (VDV) and is designed to be parachute-dropped from transport aircraft with much of its crew inside, giving paratroopers immediate armoured mobility on landing.

Design

The BMD-2's principal improvement over the BMD-1 is its armament: a new single-crew turret mounting the stabilised 30 mm 2A42 autocannon — the same gun fitted to the BMP-2 — in place of the earlier 73 mm low-pressure gun, together with a roof-mounted launcher for 9M113 Konkurs (AT-5 Spandrel) anti-tank guided missiles and a coaxial 7.62 mm machine gun. Two further machine guns are mounted in the hull bow. The welded aluminium hull protects against small-arms fire and shell splinters; the vehicle is fully amphibious, propelled in water by two water jets, and retains the BMD family's adjustable hydropneumatic suspension, which lets it be lowered for air transport and parachute dropping. The engine is the 5D20 6-cylinder diesel of 240 hp, giving a road speed of about 60 km/h.

Service

The BMD-2 entered service with Soviet airborne divisions in 1985 and remained in production at the Volgograd Tractor Plant into the 1990s. It saw action in Afghanistan in the final years of the Soviet presence, in the post-Soviet conflicts of the 1990s, in Georgia in 2008, and extensively in the Russo-Ukrainian War from 2014, where both Russian airborne units and captured Ukrainian-operated examples have been used, with significant losses recorded. Variants include the BMD-2K command vehicle; the design was succeeded in production by the larger BMD-3 and BMD-4 families.
