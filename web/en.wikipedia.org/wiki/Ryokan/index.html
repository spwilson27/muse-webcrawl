Ryokan

CountryJapan
TypeTraditional inn
FeaturesTatami rooms, futon bedding, communal baths, kaiseki meals

A ryokan (Japanese: 旅館) is a traditional Japanese inn featuring tatami-matted rooms, communal baths and other public areas where visitors wear yukata. Ryokan originated in the Edo period (1603–1868), when they served travellers along Japan's highways; some famous establishments, such as Hoshi Ryokan in Ishikawa Prefecture (founded 718), claim to be among the oldest hotels in the world.

Experience

Guests sleep on futon laid over tatami, are attended by an okami (proprietress) and room attendants, and are typically served elaborate multi-course kaiseki dinners and Japanese breakfasts in their room or a dining area. Many ryokan are located at hot-spring (onsen) resorts and offer indoor and outdoor baths; guests customarily change into yukata for the duration of their stay and remove their shoes at the entrance, wearing slippers inside.

Types and economics

Ryokan range from small family-run inns to large resort properties, and are often priced per person including two meals. The more modest minshuku (family-run lodgings) offer a similar but simpler experience. Staying at a ryokan is considered a quintessential part of domestic tourism in Japan.
