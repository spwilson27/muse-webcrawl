Holmenkollen

LocationOslo, Norway
Elevation371 m (1,217 ft) approx.
Known forHolmenkollbakken ski jump; Holmenkollen Ski Festival

Holmenkollen is a neighbourhood and recreational area in the borough of Vestre Aker in Oslo, Norway. Situated on a hill overlooking the city and the Oslofjord, it is best known as a historic venue for Nordic skiing, centred on the Holmenkollbakken ski jumping hill, one of the most famous ski jumps in the world.

Ski jumping and the festival

Ski jumping competitions have been held at Holmenkollen since 1892, when the first event took place at Husebyrennet before moving to the present site. The annual Holmenkollen Ski Festival, held each March, is one of the oldest and most prestigious events in cross-country skiing and ski jumping, and forms part of the FIS World Cup. The ski jump has been rebuilt many times; the current steel structure, opened in 2010 ahead of the 2011 FIS Nordic World Ski Championships, is a landmark visible across Oslo, its cantilevered design rising above the hillside. Norway hosted the Nordic World Ski Championships at Holmenkollen in 1930, 1966, 1982 and 2011, as well as the ski jumping and Nordic events of the 1952 Winter Olympics.

Ski museum and area

The Holmenkollen Ski Museum, founded in 1923 and located beneath the jump, is the oldest museum in the world devoted to skiing, displaying artefacts spanning some 4,000 years of skiing history, including skis belonging to Fridtjof Nansen and members of the Norwegian royal family. Nearby are the Midtstubakken normal hill, cross-country trails of the Nordmarka forest, the Holmenkollen Chapel, and the former Holmenkollen Park Hotel. The area is served by the Holmenkollen Line (Line 1) of the Oslo Metro, and is a popular starting point for hiking and skiing trips into Nordmarka.
