Holzmaden

Holzmaden is a municipality in the district of Esslingen in Baden-Württemberg, Germany, at the foot of the Swabian Jura. It is world-famous among palaeontologists for the exceptionally preserved fossils of the Posidonia Shale (Posidonienschiefer), a Lower Jurassic (Toarcian, about 180 million years old) Lagerstätte worked in the slate quarries around the village.

Fossils

The black shales were deposited in a stagnant marine basin whose oxygen-poor bottom waters prevented decay and scavenging, yielding articulated skeletons and even soft-tissue outlines. Holzmaden is celebrated for its ichthyosaurs — including specimens of Stenopterygius preserved with embryos and skin impressions showing dorsal fins and tail shape — as well as plesiosaurs, pterosaurs, the marine crocodilian Steneosaurus, fishes, crinoids, and ammonites.

Museum Hauff

The Urwelt-Museum Hauff, founded by the Hauff family of quarry owners and fossil preparators, displays many of the finest Holzmaden finds, including one of the world's largest fossil crinoid colonies mounted on a fossilised log. The quarries continue to yield scientifically important material.
