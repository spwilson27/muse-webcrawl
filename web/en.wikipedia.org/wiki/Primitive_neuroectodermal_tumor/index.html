Primitive neuroectodermal tumor

Primitive neuroectodermal tumor
Specialty: Oncology, neuropathology
Classification: Small round blue cell tumors
Related: Ewing sarcoma family of tumors

Primitive neuroectodermal tumor (PNET) is a malignant neural crest tumor composed of small, round, poorly differentiated cells. These tumors were historically divided into central PNETs, arising in the brain and spinal cord, and peripheral PNETs, arising in bone and soft tissue, which overlap heavily with the Ewing sarcoma family of tumors.

Classification

Modern molecular classification has largely retired the term "central PNET" in favour of more precisely defined embryonal tumor types based on genetic alterations. Peripheral PNETs share the characteristic EWSR1 gene rearrangements of Ewing sarcoma and are treated within the same family.

Presentation and diagnosis

Symptoms depend on the site of the tumor and may include a growing mass, pain, or neurological deficits. Under the microscope the tumor is a "small round blue cell tumor"; diagnosis is confirmed by immunohistochemistry and molecular testing for gene fusions.

Treatment

Treatment typically combines surgery, multi-agent chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Prognosis depends on tumor site, size, metastatic spread, and response to chemotherapy.
