Sexual maturation

Sexual maturation
The hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis drives sexual maturation
FieldDevelopmental biology, endocrinology
Human processPuberty
Key hormonesGnRH, LH, FSH, sex steroids (testosterone, oestrogens)

Sexual maturation is the process by which an organism develops the anatomical and physiological capacity to reproduce. In animals it involves the maturation of the gonads, the development of secondary sexual characteristics, and the behavioural changes associated with reproductive competence. In humans, sexual maturation occurs during puberty.

Mechanism

Sexual maturation is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis. Pulsatile release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus stimulates the pituitary to secrete luteinising hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which act on the gonads to produce sex steroids — principally testosterone in males and oestrogens in females — and to initiate gametogenesis. The timing of maturation reflects genetic, nutritional and environmental influences; kisspeptin signalling is a key trigger of GnRH release.

In humans

In humans, maturation typically begins between ages 8–13 in girls and 9–14 in boys. It is clinically staged using the Tanner scale, from stage 1 (prepubertal) to stage 5 (adult). Maturation that is abnormally early (precocious puberty) or delayed may indicate endocrine disorders requiring investigation.

In other animals

Across species, maturation may be triggered by body size, photoperiod, temperature, or social cues. Some animals, such as many fish, can change sex during maturation, while others undergo dramatic metamorphosis. In plants, the analogous transition is the change from vegetative to reproductive (flowering) growth.
