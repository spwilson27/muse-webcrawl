Mindset

A mindset is an established set of attitudes held by someone — a person's worldview, beliefs, and characteristic way of interpreting events — which predisposes them to respond in particular ways. In psychology, the term is most associated with the research of Carol Dweck, who distinguishes between a fixed mindset, in which abilities are seen as innate and unchangeable, and a growth mindset, in which abilities are seen as developable through effort, strategy, and learning.

Fixed and growth mindsets

According to Dweck's research, students who believe intelligence can be developed tend to embrace challenges, persist through difficulty, and treat failure as information, whereas those who believe intelligence is fixed tend to avoid risk and interpret setbacks as evidence of low ability. Dweck popularized the theory in her 2006 book Mindset: The New Psychology of Success. Interventions teaching a growth mindset have been widely adopted in education, though meta-analyses find their average effects on achievement are small and context-dependent, and researchers caution against treating mindset as a panacea.

Other uses

The term is also used more broadly for collective dispositions — for example, an "entrepreneurial mindset" in business, or a "security mindset" in engineering, meaning habits of thinking oriented toward threats and defenses. In cognitive psychology, related concepts include mental sets (Einstellung), schemas, and cognitive styles.
