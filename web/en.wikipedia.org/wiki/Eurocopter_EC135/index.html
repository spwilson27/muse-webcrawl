The Airbus Helicopters H135, formerly known as the Eurocopter EC135, is a twin-engine civil light utility helicopter produced by Airbus Helicopters (formerly Eurocopter). It is capable of flight under instrument flight rules and is typically outfitted with a digital automatic flight control system. The type entered service in 1996; well over a thousand have been delivered to operators worldwide.

Development

The EC135 originated as a technology demonstrator developed by Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm in Germany during the 1980s. After the formation of Eurocopter, the design was developed into a production aircraft with a new fuselage, a bearingless main rotor, and a shrouded fenestron anti-torque device, which contributes to the helicopter's low noise signature.

Operational use

The EC135 is widely used for helicopter emergency medical services, police and law enforcement, corporate transport, and offshore operations. A military variant, the EC635 (later H135M), has been adopted by several armed forces.

Specifications

The EC135 has a maximum take-off weight of about 2,980 kg, seats up to seven passengers plus a pilot, and has a maximum cruise speed of roughly 254 km/h with a range of about 635 km, depending on variant and configuration.
