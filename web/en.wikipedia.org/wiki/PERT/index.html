The program evaluation and review technique (PERT) is a statistical tool used in project management, which was designed to analyze and represent the tasks involved in completing a given project. It is commonly used in conjunction with the critical path method (CPM), which was developed at roughly the same time.

History

PERT was developed in 1957–1958 by the United States Navy's Special Projects Office, working with the management consulting firm Booz Allen Hamilton and the Lockheed Missile and Space Company, to manage the Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile program, which involved thousands of contractors and deeply uncertain research-and-development schedules. The technique is credited with helping bring Polaris into service ahead of schedule, and it rapidly spread through the defense, aerospace and construction industries.

Technique

A PERT chart models a project as a network of events (nodes or milestones) connected by activities (arrows) with precedence relationships — which tasks must finish before others can start. For each activity, planners supply three time estimates:

  • Optimistic time (o) — the minimum possible time, assuming everything proceeds better than expected.
  • Most likely time (m) — the best estimate under normal conditions.
  • Pessimistic time (p) — the maximum possible time, assuming difficulties.

The expected duration is computed as a weighted average, te = (o + 4m + p) / 6, an approximation of a beta distribution, with standard deviation approximated by (p − o) / 6. Summing expected durations along the longest path gives the critical path and the expected project completion time; activities off the critical path have slack (float). Using the path's variance, managers can estimate the probability of meeting a given deadline.

Criticism and practice

Later research observed systematic optimism bias in three-point estimates and noted that uncertainty on near-critical paths can make the "expected" schedule misleading (the so-called merge bias). Nevertheless, PERT remains a standard teaching and planning tool in project management, and PERT charts — network diagrams of milestones on a timescale — are produced by most project-management software.
