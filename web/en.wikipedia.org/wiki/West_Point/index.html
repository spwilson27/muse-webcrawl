West Point

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

West Point is a United States Army installation and census-designated place on the western bank of the Hudson River in Orange County, New York, about 60 miles (97 km) north of New York City. It is the site of the United States Military Academy, founded in 1802, the oldest of the five American service academies.

History

The site's strategic value was recognized during the American Revolutionary War: West Point guards a sharp S-curve in the Hudson, and fortifications were built there in 1778 under the supervision of Tadeusz Kościuszko. A great iron chain was stretched across the river to prevent British ships from passing. In 1780, the American commander of the fort, Benedict Arnold, conspired to surrender West Point to the British, a plot that was uncovered and made his name synonymous with treason.

President Thomas Jefferson signed legislation establishing the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1802. Under Superintendent Sylvanus Thayer (1817–1833), the academy developed the rigorous engineering-focused curriculum for which it became known.

The academy

The academy educates and trains the Corps of Cadets, about 4,400 strong, for commissioning as officers in the U.S. Army. Its motto is "Duty, Honor, Country". The campus, largely built in a military Gothic style, is a National Historic Landmark and a popular tourist destination, with landmarks including Trophy Point, the Cadet Chapel and the West Point Museum.
