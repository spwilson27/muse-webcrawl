The Buggles


Trevor Horn and Geoff Downes of the Buggles

The Buggles were an English new wave band formed in London in 1977 by singer and bassist Trevor Horn and keyboardist Geoff Downes. They are best known for their 1979 debut single "Video Killed the Radio Star", which topped the charts in 16 countries and whose music video was the first ever broadcast on MTV in the United States, at 12:01 a.m. on 1 August 1981.

Career

Horn and Downes came together after working in Tina Charles's backing band. Drawing on influences from Kraftwerk and 10cc, the Buggles' debut album The Age of Plastic (1980) explored themes of nostalgia for earlier media in a slick, technology-saturated present. The album also produced the hits "Living in the Plastic Age", "Clean Clean" and "Elstree".

In 1980, to widespread surprise, Horn and Downes joined the progressive rock band Yes, recording the album Drama. After Yes split, Downes co-founded the supergroup Asia, while Horn became one of the most successful record producers of the 1980s, working with ABC, Frankie Goes to Hollywood, Seal and the Pet Shop Boys, and co-founding the ZTT label and the group Art of Noise. A second Buggles album, Adventures in Modern Recording, appeared in 1981, largely as a Horn solo effort.

Legacy and reunions

"Video Killed the Radio Star" has become a cultural landmark marking the dawn of the music-video age. The Buggles reunited for occasional performances, including a full performance of The Age of Plastic in London in 2010, and Horn and Downes have continued to perform the material in later projects.
