Port of registry

A port of registry (also called a home port) is the port at which a ship is registered, as recorded in the vessel's official documentation. Under the law of the sea, a ship sails under the flag of its state of registry and is subject to that state's jurisdiction and maritime law. The port of registry is traditionally painted on the stern of the vessel, together with the ship's name.

Registration

Registration establishes a ship's nationality and ownership record. The port of registry need not be a port the vessel ever visits: under "open registry" systems — commonly known as flags of convenience — shipowners may register vessels at ports in countries such as Panama, Liberia or the Marshall Islands, which offer low fees and lighter regulation. A large proportion of the world's merchant tonnage is registered in such jurisdictions.

Legal significance

The registry state is responsible for certifying the vessel's seaworthiness and compliance with international conventions on safety, crewing and pollution, and its law governs matters such as mortgages over the ship. Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, there should be a "genuine link" between a ship and its flag state, though in practice this requirement is weakly enforced.
