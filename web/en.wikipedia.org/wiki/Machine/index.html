Machine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of exposed gears and linkages in an industrial machine]

A machine is a physical system that uses power to apply forces and control movement to perform a task. Machines range from simple devices — the classical simple machines of lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge and screw — to engines, industrial robots and the abstract “machines” of computing.

Simple machines and mechanisms

Simple machines change the direction or magnitude of a force, trading distance for force. Real machines combine elements such as gears, cams, linkages and bearings, and their study forms part of mechanical engineering. Efficiency measures how much input work becomes useful output, the rest being lost mainly as heat through friction.

History

Machines developed from hand tools through water- and wind-driven mills to the steam engines of the Industrial Revolution and modern automated machinery. The word also survives figuratively and in computing, e.g. the Turing machine, a theoretical model of computation.
