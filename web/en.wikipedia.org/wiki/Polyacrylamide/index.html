Polyacrylamide

Polyacrylamide (abbreviated PAM) is a water-soluble polymer formed from acrylamide subunits, with the formula (C3H5NO)n. It can be synthesised as a simple linear chain or cross-linked, most commonly with N,N′-methylenebisacrylamide, and is produced in nonionic, anionic and cationic forms by copolymerisation.

Uses

Because it dramatically increases the viscosity of water and promotes flocculation, polyacrylamide is used in large quantities as a flocculant in water and wastewater treatment, in pulp and paper manufacture, in mining and mineral processing, and in enhanced oil recovery, where polymer flooding improves sweep efficiency. Anionic polyacrylamide is also applied as a soil conditioner to reduce erosion.

In the laboratory, cross-linked polyacrylamide forms the gel matrix used for polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) of proteins and nucleic acids. Related formulations (superabsorbent hydrogels) are used in agriculture and hygiene products.

Safety

Polyacrylamide itself is not considered toxic, but commercial products may contain residual acrylamide monomer, a neurotoxin and probable carcinogen, so monomer levels are regulated in applications involving drinking water and food contact. Environmental concerns focus on the mechanical degradation of the polymer back to monomer under some conditions.
