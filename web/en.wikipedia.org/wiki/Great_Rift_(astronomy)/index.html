Great Rift (astronomy)

In astronomy, the Great Rift (sometimes called the Dark Rift or less commonly the Dark River) is a dark band across the bright expanse of the Milky Way. It is caused by a series of overlapping, non-luminous molecular dust clouds in the plane of the galaxy that lie between the Solar System and the Sagittarius Arm, obscuring the light of the stars behind them.

Appearance

The Great Rift begins near the bright star Deneb in the constellation Cygnus — where its most prominent section is known as the Cygnus Rift or the Northern Coalsack — and extends southward through Aquila and Ophiuchus to Sagittarius, where it hides the Galactic Center from optical view. To the naked eye under dark skies it appears to split the Milky Way lengthwise into two roughly parallel streams.

Constituent clouds

Prominent dark nebulae along the Rift include the Coalsack Nebula, the Dark Doodad, and the Pipe Nebula. Because the obscuring dust blocks visible light, the inner galaxy behind the Rift is studied primarily at infrared and radio wavelengths, which penetrate the dust.

In culture

The dark band featured in the astronomy and calendrical traditions of several pre-Columbian Mesoamerican cultures, in which the Rift was associated with a road or river in the sky; popular claims linking it to the Maya calendar's 2012 phenomenon are not supported by scholars.
