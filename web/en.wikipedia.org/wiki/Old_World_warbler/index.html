Old World warbler

Old World warblers are a large group of small, mostly insectivorous passerine birds. They were formerly united in the family Sylviidae in a broad sense, which at its height contained over 400 species and served as a "wastebasket taxon" for many hard-to-classify songbirds. Molecular studies have since split the old Sylviidae into several families, including Sylviidae proper (the typical warblers, such as the blackcap and whitethroats) and the families Phylloscopidae (leaf warblers), Acrocephalidae (reed and tree warblers), Locustellidae (grass warblers), Cettiidae and Scotocercidae.

Description

Most Old World warblers are small birds, often with drab greenish, brown or grey plumage, though some — particularly the typical warblers of the genus Sylvia — are more boldly patterned, with black or chestnut caps and white throats. Many species are renowned for their rich, varied songs, and many are long-distance migrants between Eurasian or African breeding grounds and wintering areas in Africa or southern Asia.

Distribution

The group is found throughout Europe, Asia and Africa, with a few species reaching Australasia and one, the Arctic warbler, breeding in Alaska.
