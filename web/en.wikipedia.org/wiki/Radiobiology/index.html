Radiobiology

Radiobiology (or radiation biology) is the study of the action of ionizing radiation on living things, including the health effects of radiation on humans and other organisms. It underpins radiation therapy for cancer, radiation protection standards, and understanding of radiation accidents.

Mechanisms

Ionizing radiation damages cells mainly by breaking chemical bonds in DNA, directly or via free radicals produced in water. Consequences include mutations, cell death, and carcinogenesis. Effects are broadly classed as deterministic (tissue reactions with dose thresholds, e.g. radiation burns) and stochastic (probabilistic effects such as cancer, modelled without a safe threshold in protection frameworks).

Key principles

The law of Bergonié and Tribondeau (1906) states that radiosensitivity is greatest in rapidly dividing, undifferentiated cells. Dose, dose rate, fractionation, and linear energy transfer (LET) all modify biological effect; relative biological effectiveness (RBE) compares radiation types.

Applications

Radiobiology guides radiotherapy fractionation schedules, occupational and public dose limits (through bodies such as the ICRP), spaceflight health, and the management of radiological emergencies.
