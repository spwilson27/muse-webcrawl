Draught horse


A Belgian draught horse in harness.

A draught horse (UK spelling; in North America usually spelled draft horse), also called a dray horse, carthorse or heavy horse, is a large horse bred to perform heavy work such as ploughing, hauling and other farm labour. Draught horses are recognisable by their tall stature, extremely muscular build, heavy bone and the abundant feathering of long hair around their lower legs and hooves.

Characteristics

Most draught breeds stand between 16 and 19 hands (162–193 cm) and weigh from about 640 to over 900 kilograms. They were selected for strength, patience and a calm, docile temperament suited to working in close harness with humans. Their conformation typically features a short, powerful back, strong hindquarters and large, broad hooves that distribute their weight on soft ground.

History

Heavy horses descend in part from the large war horses of the Middle Ages, which needed strength to carry an armoured knight. With the growth of agriculture, mining, canal transport and urban haulage in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, specialised draught breeds were developed across Europe and North America. Railways and, later, the tractor and motor lorry displaced them during the twentieth century, causing steep declines in population, though many breeds survive through showing, logging, ceremonial work and leisure driving.

Breeds

Well-known draught breeds include the Shire, Clydesdale, Suffolk Punch and British Percheron from Britain; the Belgian Draught; the French Percheron and Breton; the German Rhineland Heavy Draught; the American Belgian and the American Cream Draft; and the Australian Draught Horse. The Shire stallion Samson, foaled in 1846 and later renamed Mammoth, stood over 21 hands and remains one of the largest horses reliably recorded.

Uses

Today draught horses are used in horse shows and pulling competitions, traditional farming, forestry where machinery is impractical, brewery drays and promotional work, mounted police and ceremonial units, and crossbreeding to produce sport horses and heavy hunters.
