Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer

The Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer is a multilateral environmental agreement adopted in 1985 and in force from 1988. It established the framework of international cooperation on protecting the stratospheric ozone layer, within which the Montreal Protocol later set binding controls on ozone-depleting substances. See Ozone depletion.

The Convention itself contains no binding reduction targets; it commits parties to research, monitoring and information exchange on the effects of human activities on the ozone layer. It was among the first treaties to achieve universal ratification.

Role and legacy

Negotiated under the United Nations Environment Programme, the Convention created the legal and scientific scaffolding — conferences of the parties, assessment panels and reporting duties — that allowed the much stronger Montreal Protocol to be agreed only two years later, once the Antarctic ozone hole had been confirmed.

Its framework-plus-protocol model became a template for later environmental law, including climate agreements.
