Bursary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A bursary is a monetary award made by any educational institution or funding authority to individuals or groups. It is usually awarded to enable a student to attend school, university or college when they might not be able to, otherwise. Some awards are aimed at encouraging specific groups or individuals into study.

Characteristics

Bursaries are typically need-based, in contrast to scholarships, which are usually merit-based, although many awards combine both criteria. Bursaries may cover tuition fees in whole or in part, and may also contribute to living expenses, books, or travel. They do not normally have to be repaid.

By country

In the United Kingdom, bursaries are a common form of student support provided by universities, schools, and charitable trusts, including means-tested awards for independent school places. In Canada and South Africa, bursaries are widely used in higher education to assist students from low-income households. In some institutions the office that administers student finance is itself called the bursary, overseen by a bursar.
