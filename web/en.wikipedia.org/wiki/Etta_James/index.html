Etta James (born Jamesetta Hawkins; January 25, 1938 – January 20, 2012) was an American singer whose powerful, earthy voice bridged rhythm and blues, soul, gospel, jazz, and rock and roll. She is best known for her recording of the ballad "At Last" (1961), as well as "Tell Mama" and "I'd Rather Go Blind."

Life and career

Raised in Los Angeles, Hawkins began singing in church and was discovered as a teenager by bandleader Johnny Otis, who reversed her name to "Etta James." Her first hit, "The Wallflower" (1955) — better known as "Roll with Me, Henry" — was an answer record to Hank Ballard's "Work with Me, Annie."

Signing with Chess Records in 1960, James recorded a string of classics for the Argo/Cadet labels, including "All I Could Do Was Cry," "At Last," "Something's Got a Hold on Me," and the Muscle Shoals-recorded "Tell Mama" and "I'd Rather Go Blind" (1967–68). Her career was repeatedly disrupted by heroin addiction, but she staged notable comebacks, including the Grammy-winning album Mystery Lady: Songs of Billie Holiday (1994).

Legacy

James won six Grammy Awards and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1993, the Blues Hall of Fame in 2001, and the Grammy Hall of Fame (for "At Last," 1999). She died in 2012 of complications of leukemia. Beyoncé portrayed her in the 2008 film Cadillac Records.
