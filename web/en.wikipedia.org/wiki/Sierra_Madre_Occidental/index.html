Sierra Madre Occidental

The Sierra Madre Occidental is a major mountain range system of the North American Cordillera, running northwest–southeast through northwestern and western Mexico. It extends roughly 1,500 km (930 mi) from Arizona in the north through the Mexican states of Sonora, Chihuahua, Durango, Sinaloa, Nayarit, Zacatecas, Aguascalientes and Jalisco, where it joins the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt.

Geography

The range's highest peak is generally considered to be Cerro el Potosí, though elevations vary by source; the high tableland averages around 2,250 m (7,380 ft). The range is famous for its dramatic canyons, including Copper Canyon (Barranca del Cobre), a system of gorges larger and in places deeper than the Grand Canyon.

Geology and ecology

The mountains were formed largely by extensive silicic ignimbrite volcanism in the mid-Tertiary. They host pine–oak forests of great biodiversity and are home to the Rarámuri (Tarahumara) people.
