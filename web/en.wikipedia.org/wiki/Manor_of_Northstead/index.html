Manor of Northstead

The Manor of Northstead is a former manor house and estate in North Yorkshire, England, near Scarborough. Since the 19th century its stewardship has been used as a legal device to allow a Member of Parliament of the United Kingdom to resign from the House of Commons.

Resignation from the Commons

MPs cannot technically resign their seats. Under a rule dating to 1624, a member who accepts an "office of profit under the Crown" is disqualified from sitting. Two nominal offices are used for this purpose: Steward and Bailiff of the three Chiltern Hundreds, and Steward and Bailiff of the Manor of Northstead. The Chancellor of the Exchequer appoints the resigning MP to one of these unpaid sinecures, vacating the seat and triggering a by-election; the appointments alternate between the two offices where successive resignations occur.

History

The manor once belonged to the Crown and the manor house stood near Scalby; the site is now within the Scarborough area and no substantial building survives. The stewardship was first used for a resignation in 1842, by Patrick Chalmers, MP for Montrose Burghs. The office remains in regular use: recent holders include MPs resigning to take other posts or to leave politics mid-parliament.
