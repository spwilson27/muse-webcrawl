The Peace of Augsburg, signed on 25 September 1555, was a treaty between Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor, and the forces of the Lutheran Schmalkaldic League, concluding the religious struggle in Germany for a generation. Negotiated by Charles's brother Ferdinand, it made Lutheranism a legally recognised confession within the Empire.

Terms

Its central principle was cuius regio, eius religio — "whose realm, his religion": each prince could choose either Catholicism or Lutheranism as the official religion of his territory, and subjects who dissented could emigrate. The "ecclesiastical reservation" required a prince-bishop who converted to resign his see. Calvinism and other faiths were not recognised, a limitation that contributed to later conflict.

Significance

The peace ended the first wave of the German Reformation wars, partitioned Christendom in the Empire de facto, and lasted until the outbreak of the Thirty Years' War in 1618.
