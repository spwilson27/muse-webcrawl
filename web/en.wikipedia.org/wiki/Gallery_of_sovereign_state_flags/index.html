Gallery of sovereign state flags

This gallery of sovereign state flags shows the national flags of the member and observer states of the United Nations, together with the flags of other widely recognised sovereign states. Flags are arranged alphabetically by country name.

About national flags

A national flag is the principal visual symbol of a sovereign state. Most modern flags are rectangular, commonly in proportions of 2:3 or 3:5. Two sovereign states — Nepal and, in its state flag, Iran uses a rectangular banner, while Nepal's flag is famously non-rectangular, being formed of two stacked pennants and bearing the sun and crescent moon. Switzerland and Vatican City use square flags.

Recurring design families include:

  • Tricolours and tribands, such as France, Italy, Germany and Russia.
  • Nordic crosses, used by Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland.
  • Pan-African colours (green, yellow and red), derived from the flag of Ethiopia.
  • Pan-Arab colours (black, white, green and red), used across the Middle East and North Africa.
  • Pan-Slavic colours (white, blue and red).
  • Flags bearing the Union Jack in the canton, including Australia and New Zealand.
  • Flags with stars and crescents, common in Muslim-majority states such as Turkey, Pakistan and Malaysia.

Organisation of the gallery

Entries in the full gallery are presented from Afghanistan to Zimbabwe, each showing the flag image, the country name, and a link to the dedicated article on that flag (for example, Flag of France or Flag of Japan). Several states with limited recognition, such as Kosovo, Northern Cyprus, Somaliland and Transnistria, are listed separately at the end of the gallery.
