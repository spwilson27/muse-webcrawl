Dative shift

Dative shift is a grammatical alternation in which a verb phrase appears in two different configurations, one with a prepositional phrase and one with two bare noun phrases. In English, a sentence such as "John gave a book to Mary" (the prepositional dative) alternates with "John gave Mary a book" (the double-object construction).

Properties

Not all verbs allow both forms: verbs of Latinate origin such as donate or explain typically resist the double-object construction ("*explain me the problem"), while short Germanic verbs like give, send and show alternate freely. The two constructions also differ subtly in meaning and information structure: the double-object form usually presents the recipient as more affected or as coming to possess the theme, and favours given, pronominal recipients.

Analysis

The alternation has been a central case study in syntax and in language acquisition research. Children generally master both constructions, and the constraints on which verbs shift are an example in debates about how much of grammar is learned from generalisation versus memorised item by item. Related alternations exist in many languages, sometimes marked by case or applicative morphology rather than word order.
