De Rham cohomology

[Image: Diagram of the de Rham complex on a smooth manifold]

In mathematics, de Rham cohomology (named after Georges de Rham) is a tool belonging both to algebraic topology and to differential topology, capable of expressing basic topological information about smooth manifolds in a form particularly adapted to computation and the concrete representation of cohomology classes. It is a cohomology theory based on the existence of differential forms with prescribed properties.

Definition

The de Rham complex is the cochain complex of differential forms on a smooth manifold M, with the exterior derivative as the differential. The k-th de Rham cohomology group HkdR(M) is the quotient of the vector space of closed k-forms (those annihilated by the exterior derivative d) by the subspace of exact k-forms (those of the form dω for some (k−1)-form ω).

On any smooth manifold, the de Rham cohomology groups are finite-dimensional real vector spaces when the manifold is compact, and their dimensions are the Betti numbers of the manifold.

De Rham's theorem

Stokes' theorem asserts that integration of forms gives a well-defined pairing between de Rham cohomology and singular homology. De Rham's theorem, proved by Georges de Rham in 1931, states that this integration map induces an isomorphism between de Rham cohomology with real coefficients and singular cohomology with real coefficients. Wedge product of forms corresponds to the cup product of singular cohomology, making the direct sum of the de Rham groups a graded ring isomorphic to the singular cohomology ring.

Examples

For the n-sphere Sn, the only nontrivial de Rham cohomology groups are H0 and Hn, each one-dimensional. For the torus, the first cohomology group is two-dimensional, generated by the standard angle forms. A manifold is orientable if and only if its top de Rham cohomology (in degree equal to its dimension, for connected compact manifolds) is one-dimensional.
