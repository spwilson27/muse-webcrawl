Standby

Standby generally denotes a state of readiness in which a person, device or system is not actively working but can be brought into action immediately. This article covers the main usages of the term.

Electronics

In electronics, standby mode is a low-power state in which a device, such as a television, computer monitor or games console, appears to be switched off but remains ready to resume full operation quickly when it receives a signal, typically from a remote control. The residual power consumed in this state is called standby power (also "vampire power" or phantom load); although small per device — typically a fraction of a watt to several watts under modern regulations — it accounts for a measurable share of household electricity consumption worldwide, and regulators in the European Union, the United States and elsewhere have progressively tightened limits on standby consumption. On computers, standby is closely related to sleep mode, in which the state of the system is preserved in memory while most components are powered down, contrasted with hibernation, in which the state is written to disk.

Travel

In air travel, a standby passenger is one who waits at the airport for an available seat on a flight for which they do not hold a confirmed reservation, for example because their original flight was cancelled or because they are travelling on a pass (such as airline employees) or hoping to take an earlier departure. Seats are assigned by priority rules shortly before departure; standby travel was historically associated with cheap "standby fares" sold to young travellers in the early decades of jet travel.

Other uses

  • Standby duty — a state in which employees, medical staff or military units remain available to be called into service at short notice; in some labour-law systems "on standby" time may count partly as working time.
  • Standby (theatre) — in musical theatre, a performer who covers a leading role but, unlike an understudy, does not normally perform in the ensemble.
  • Standby letter of credit — in banking, a guarantee by which a bank promises to pay a beneficiary if its customer defaults on an obligation.
  • Standby arrangement — an IMF facility under which a member country may draw funds subject to agreed conditions.
