Archaeological site of Atapuerca

The archaeological site of Atapuerca is a complex of prehistoric cave sites in the Sierra de Atapuerca near Burgos, in northern Spain. It contains one of the richest records of early human occupation in Europe and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

Location: Burgos Province, Spain
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2000)
Key sites: Gran Dolina, Sima de los Huesos, Sima del Elefante, Galería

Discoveries

The Sima del Elefante has yielded some of the oldest hominin fossils in Western Europe, over 1.2 million years old. At Gran Dolina, fossils around 800,000 years old were assigned to a proposed species, Homo antecessor. The Sima de los Huesos ("Pit of the Bones") contains thousands of fossils of at least 28 individuals dating to about 430,000 years ago, attributed to early Neanderthal relatives, and has produced some of the oldest known hominin DNA.

Significance

Atapuerca provides an exceptional, continuous record of human evolution, technology, and behaviour in Europe over more than a million years.
