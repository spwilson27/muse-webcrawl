Ceres (mythology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Roman religion, Ceres was a goddess of agriculture, grain crops, fertility and motherly relationships. She was originally the central deity of Rome's so-called plebeian or Aventine Triad, later supplanted by a Greek-derived mythology in which Ceres was identified with the Greek goddess Demeter. She was the daughter of Saturn and Ops, the sister of Jupiter, and the mother of Proserpina.

Myths

The central myth of Ceres concerns the abduction of her daughter Proserpina (Greek Persephone) by Pluto, god of the underworld. In her grief and anger, Ceres neglected the earth, causing famine, until Jupiter brokered an arrangement by which Proserpina would spend part of each year below the earth — explaining the cycle of the seasons.

Cult and festivals

Ceres' main festival, the Cerealia, was held in mid-April and involved games (the ludi Ceriales) and the sacrifice of sows. As goddess of the plebeians, her temple on the Aventine Hill, shared with Liber and Libera, served as a plebeian religious and political centre, housing the archives of the plebeian aediles. Women-only rites in her honour were held in secrecy.

Legacy

Ceres' name survives in the word "cereal". The dwarf planet Ceres, discovered in 1801, was named for her, as was the element cerium.
