Robert Barro

Robert J. Barro
Born: 28 September 1944, New York City
Institution: Harvard University
Field: Macroeconomics, economic growth

Robert Joseph Barro (born 28 September 1944) is an American macroeconomist and the Paul M. Warburg Professor of Economics at Harvard University. He is known for reviving Ricardian equivalence, for contributions to endogenous growth theory, and for empirical research on the determinants of economic growth.

Contributions

Barro's 1974 paper "Are Government Bonds Net Wealth?" argued that government debt is offset by private saving in anticipation of future taxes — the Ricardian equivalence proposition. With Robert King and others he helped develop real business cycle and new classical macroeconomics, building on his work with rational expectations on monetary policy ineffectiveness. His textbook Macroeconomics and, with Xavier Sala-i-Martin, Economic Growth are widely used.

Growth empirics

His cross-country growth regressions identified factors such as initial schooling, life expectancy, rule of law and openness as correlates of growth, an influential but debated research programme. He has also studied religion and economics, rare macroeconomic disasters, and the effects of fiscal stimulus, writing columns for the Wall Street Journal and BusinessWeek.
