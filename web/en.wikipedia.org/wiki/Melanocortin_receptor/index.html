Melanocortin receptor

Melanocortin receptors are a family of G protein-coupled receptors activated by melanocortin peptides, which are derived from the precursor protein proopiomelanocortin (POMC) and include the melanocyte-stimulating hormones (α-, β-, and γ-MSH) and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH). There are five known melanocortin receptors, designated MC1R through MC5R. They are involved in a wide range of physiological processes, including pigmentation, energy homeostasis, inflammation, steroidogenesis, and sexual function. The receptors are unusual among GPCRs in having endogenous antagonists and inverse agonists, the agouti signalling protein (ASIP) and agouti-related protein (AgRP).


Melanocortin receptors are seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptors.

Clinical significance

Variants of MC1R are associated with red hair, fair skin, freckling, and increased risk of melanoma. Loss-of-function mutations in MC4R are the commonest known monogenic cause of severe early-onset obesity, and the MC4R agonist setmelanotide is approved for certain genetic obesity syndromes. The MC2R is the receptor for ACTH, and its dysfunction causes familial glucocorticoid deficiency. Other melanocortin drugs include afamelanotide, an MC1R agonist used in erythropoietic protoporphyria, and bremelanotide, used for hypoactive sexual desire disorder.
