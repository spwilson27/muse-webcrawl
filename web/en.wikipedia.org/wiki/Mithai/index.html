Mithai (confectionery)

TypeConfectionery
RegionSouth Asia
Main ingredientsMilk, sugar, flour, nuts, ghee

Mithai are sweets and desserts originating from the Indian subcontinent. Made principally from milk, sugar, flour, nuts and ghee, mithai are central to celebrations, festivals, religious offerings and hospitality across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka, and are sold by specialist sweet makers known as halwai.

Varieties

Well-known mithai include milk-based sweets such as gulab jamun, rasgulla, sandesh, peda, burfi and kalakand; syrup-soaked fried sweets such as jalebi and imarti; nut-based sweets such as kaju katli; and flour-based sweets such as laddu and mysore pak. Many varieties are garnished with edible silver leaf (varak) or pistachios.

Cultural role

Mithai are exchanged as gifts during festivals such as Diwali and Eid, distributed to celebrate good news, and offered in temples as prasad. Regional traditions differ widely, from Bengal's chhena-based sweets to the ghee-rich confections of the south.
