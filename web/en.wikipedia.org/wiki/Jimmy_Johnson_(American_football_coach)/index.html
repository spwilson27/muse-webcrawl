Jimmy Johnson (American football coach)

James William Johnson (born July 16, 1943) is an American former football coach and broadcaster. He served as head coach of the Dallas Cowboys from 1989 to 1993, leading them to victories in Super Bowls XXVII and XXVIII, and of the Miami Dolphins from 1996 to 1999. He is the first head coach to win both a college football national championship (with the University of Miami in 1987) and a Super Bowl.

Johnson during his tenure with the Dallas Cowboys.

College career

Johnson played defensive line at the University of Arkansas, where he was a teammate of future Cowboys owner Jerry Jones. After assistant-coaching jobs, he became head coach at Oklahoma State (1979–1983) and then at Miami (1984–1988), where his Hurricanes went 52–9 and won the 1987 national championship, known for a brash, aggressive style.

NFL career

Hired by Jones in 1989 after the Cowboys were sold, Johnson rebuilt Dallas from a 1–15 team into a dynasty, drafting stars such as Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith and Michael Irvin and engineering the famous Herschel Walker trade. The Cowboys won back-to-back Super Bowls in the 1992 and 1993 seasons. Johnson left Dallas in 1994 after a falling-out with Jones, coached the Miami Dolphins for four seasons, and retired in 2000.

Broadcasting and honours

Johnson became a long-serving studio analyst on Fox NFL Sunday. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2020 and the College Football Hall of Fame in 2012.
