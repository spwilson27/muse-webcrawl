Silver mining in Mexico

Silver mining in Mexico has been a central feature of the country's economy for nearly five centuries. Mexico has historically been the world's largest silver producer, and in recent years has again ranked first in global mine production of the metal.

Colonial era

Silver mining began in earnest after the Spanish conquest, with major strikes in the 1540s at Zacatecas and later at Guanajuato, San Luis Potosí, Taxco, Pachuca, and Fresnillo. The introduction of the patio process (amalgamation with mercury) in the 1550s allowed low-grade ores to be refined profitably. Mexican silver — minted into pesos ("pieces of eight") — became a global currency, flowing to Europe and across the Pacific to China via the Manila galleons. Mining revenues underpinned the viceregal economy of New Spain, though production relied heavily on coerced and poorly paid indigenous labour.

Independence and modern era

Output declined during the wars of independence but revived in the later nineteenth century with foreign investment and new technology. In the twentieth century, mining was progressively Mexicanised; today major producers include Fresnillo plc and Industrias Peñoles, with the Fresnillo and Saucito mines in Zacatecas among the largest silver mines in the world.
