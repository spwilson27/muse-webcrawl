The Millennium Dome, now part of The O2, is a vast dome-shaped building on the Greenwich Peninsula in south-east London, built to house the Millennium Experience, the United Kingdom's principal exhibition celebrating the start of the third millennium. Designed by Richard Rogers' practice with structural engineer Buro Happold, its white Teflon-coated glass-fibre roof is supported by twelve 100-metre yellow masts — one for each month of the year — over a circle 365 metres across. It is one of the largest single-roofed structures in the world.

The Millennium Experience

Opened by Queen Elizabeth II on the night of 31 December 1999, the exhibition inside was organised into themed "zones" — Body, Mind, Faith, Work, Play and others — around a central show staged by Peter Gabriel and Mark Fisher. Funded largely by the National Lottery at a cost of about £789 million, it attracted roughly 6.5 million visitors during 2000, well short of the 12 million forecast, and became a byword for expensive public-project failure. It closed at the end of 2000.

Afterlife as The O2

After years of controversy over the empty dome's upkeep, the site was redeveloped by Anschutz Entertainment Group and reopened in 2007 as The O2, an entertainment district built inside the dome shell, containing a 20,000-seat arena — one of the world's busiest music venues — plus cinemas, restaurants and exhibition space. The dome structure itself is a protected landmark of the London skyline, and visitors can climb its roof on the "Up at The O2" attraction.
