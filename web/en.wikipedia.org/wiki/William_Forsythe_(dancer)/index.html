William Forsythe (dancer)

Born: 30 December 1949, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Choreographer, dancer
Known for: Ballett Frankfurt; The Forsythe Company; deconstructing classical ballet

William Forsythe (born December 30, 1949) is an American choreographer and dancer. He is recognised internationally for his reworking of the ballet vocabulary and for dance works, installations, and digital projects that treat choreography as a broad field of organisation.

Career

Born in New York, Forsythe trained at Jacksonville University and the Joffrey Ballet School, then danced with the Stuttgart Ballet, where he began choreographing in 1976. In 1984 he became director of the Ballett Frankfurt, leading the company for twenty years and creating works such as Artifact, Impressing the Czar, Limb's Theorem, and The Loss of Small Detail.

The Forsythe Company and later work

After Ballett Frankfurt closed in 2004, Forsythe founded The Forsythe Company (2005–2015), based in Dresden and Frankfurt. His "Choreographic Objects" — installations and environments exhibited in museums worldwide — extend his ideas beyond the stage. He developed the digital dance archive and learning tool Synchronous Objects with Ohio State University.

Style

Forsythe's work extends classical lines off-balance, multiplies centres of coordination, and uses improvisation technologies that let dancers generate movement from shared cues — an approach that profoundly influenced contemporary ballet worldwide.
