Rail safety

Rail safety encompasses the systems, rules, and technologies used to prevent accidents on railways, including collisions, derailments, and incidents at level crossings. Rail is statistically among the safest modes of land transport per passenger-kilometre, but individual accidents can be severe, and safety regulation has historically been driven by accident investigation and lessons learned.

Safety measures include signalling systems that enforce separation between trains; automatic train protection (such as ETCS in Europe, PTC in the United States, and TPWS in Britain) that intervenes if a driver passes a signal at danger; interlocking of points and signals; level-crossing protection; crashworthiness standards for rolling stock; and track and vehicle maintenance regimes.

Causes of accidents

Major accident causes include signals passed at danger, excessive speed, broken rails and other track defects, level-crossing collisions (the largest source of fatalities in many countries), trespass, and human error. Independent accident investigation bodies, such as the RAIB in the United Kingdom and the NTSB in the United States, investigate serious incidents and issue safety recommendations.
