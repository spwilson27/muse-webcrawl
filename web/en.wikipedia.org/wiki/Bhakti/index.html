Bhakti

Bhakti (Sanskrit: भक्ति) is a term found in Indian religions meaning devotion, devotional love, and a path to spiritual liberation centred on a personal god or representation of God. One of the three major paths in Hinduism, alongside Jnana (knowledge) and Karma (action), it is emphasised in the Bhagavad Gita.

History

The Bhakti movement swept India from around the 6th–7th centuries CE, beginning with the Tamil Alvars (devotees of Vishnu) and Nayanars (devotees of Shiva), and later producing poet-saints such as Kabir, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Tukaram and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. It often challenged caste barriers and ritualism, composing in vernacular languages rather than Sanskrit.

Practice

Forms include kirtan and bhajan singing, japa (repetition of a divine name), puja, pilgrimage, and surrender (prapatti). Bhakti also appears in Sikhism, Buddhism and Jainism in adapted forms.
