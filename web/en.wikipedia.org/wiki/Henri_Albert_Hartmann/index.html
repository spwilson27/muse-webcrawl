Henri Albert Hartmann
Born: 16 June 1860, Paris
Died: 1 January 1952, Paris
Occupation: Surgeon

Henri Albert Hartmann (16 June 1860 – 1 January 1952) was a French surgeon who is best remembered for Hartmann's operation (Hartmann's procedure), a surgical technique for treating cancer or severe inflammation of the sigmoid colon and upper rectum that he described in 1921. He is regarded as one of the founders of modern colorectal and digestive surgery in France.

Career

Hartmann studied medicine in Paris, became professeur agrégé in 1895, and held the chair of clinical surgery at the Hôtel-Dieu hospital, where he developed a renowned surgical school. He published extensively on diseases of the digestive tract, including works on gastric and colorectal cancer, and was elected to the Académie de médecine and the Académie des sciences.

Hartmann's procedure

In Hartmann's operation, the diseased segment of sigmoid colon (or upper rectum) is resected, the rectal stump is closed and left in the pelvis, and the proximal colon is brought out as an end colostomy. The procedure avoids a risky anastomosis in obstructed, perforated or inflamed bowel and remains widely used in emergency colorectal surgery, with the option of later reversal restoring bowel continuity. Related eponyms include Hartmann's pouch — both the anatomical pouch of the gallbladder neck and the residual rectal stump after his operation.
