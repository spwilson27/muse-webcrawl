Mortlockese (also called Mortlock or Nomoi) is a Micronesian language spoken on the Mortlock Islands (Nomoi) of Chuuk State in the Federated States of Micronesia, including the atolls of Satawan, Lukunor, Ettal, Namo, Oneop, and Moch, as well as by diaspora communities on Chuuk Lagoon, Pohnpei, Guam, and in the United States. It belongs to the Chuukic (Trukic) continuum of closely related languages and is closely related to Satawalese and, more distantly, to Chuukese (Trukese), the dominant language of Chuuk State.

Classification and dialects

Mortlockese forms part of the Chuukic dialect chain stretching from Sonsorol and Tobi in the west to the Mortlocks in the east. Its varieties — including the dialects of Satawan, Lukunor, and Ettal — are largely mutually intelligible, though small differences in pronunciation and vocabulary distinguish the islands. Because of the chain's structure, Mortlockese sits between the languages of the Chuuk Lagoon area and more easterly varieties such as Puluwatese.

Features

Like other Chuukic languages, Mortlockese has a relatively small consonant inventory with contrastive consonant gemination, vowel-length distinctions, and a system of possessive classifiers that distinguish, for example, general possession, food, and drink. It has a numeral classifier system inherited from Proto-Micronesian, and verbs frequently incorporate initial consonant mutations and reduplication to mark aspect and plurality of action.

Status

Mortlockese is used in daily life, church, and local schooling in the Mortlocks, and a Latin-based orthography is in use. It is nevertheless under pressure from Chuukese and English through schooling, media, and migration; documentation and dictionary work by linguists and community members aims to record and sustain the language.
