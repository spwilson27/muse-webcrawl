Operation Weserübung

Date9 April – 10 June 1940
LocationDenmark and Norway
ResultGerman victory; occupation of Denmark and Norway
BelligerentsGermany; Denmark, Norway, United Kingdom, France, Poland

Operation Weserübung (German: Unternehmen Weserübung) was the code name for Germany's assault on Denmark and Norway during the Second World War and the opening operation of the Norwegian Campaign. The attacking forces were divided into six naval groups which landed troops simultaneously at Narvik, Trondheim, Bergen, Kristiansand, and Oslo in Norway, alongside the invasion of Denmark.

Background

Germany depended on Swedish iron ore shipped through the Norwegian port of Narvik, and both Germany and the Allies made plans to intervene in Norway. The British mining of Norwegian waters (Operation Wilfred) and the Altmark incident of February 1940 accelerated German preparations. Hitler ordered the operation under the command of General Nikolaus von Falkenhorst.

Invasion

On 9 April 1940, German forces invaded Denmark, which capitulated within hours, and landed in Norway. Norwegian resistance, aided by British, French, and Polish forces, continued until June; the campaign saw the sinking of the German heavy cruiser Blücher in the Oslofjord and heavy Kriegsmarine losses at Narvik, but Germany secured both countries, which remained occupied until May 1945.
