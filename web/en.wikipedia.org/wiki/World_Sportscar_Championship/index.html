The World Sportscar Championship was the world championship series for sports car endurance racing, sanctioned by the FIA and run from 1953 to 1992. For forty seasons it crowned manufacturers (and, in later years, drivers and teams) across a calendar of long-distance races that included many of the most famous events in motorsport, among them the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the 1000 km Nürburgring, the 12 Hours of Sebring, the Targa Florio and the Mille Miglia in its early years.

History

The championship was created by the FIA in 1953 as a manufacturers' title contested over the great road and endurance races of the day, in which marques such as Ferrari, Jaguar, Mercedes-Benz, Aston Martin and Maserati competed. In the 1960s the series became the stage for the famous Ford–Ferrari rivalry at Le Mans. The championship's commercial peak came with the Group C formula (1982–1993), whose fuel-consumption-based rules produced spectacular, relatively affordable prototype racing dominated in turn by Porsche (notably the 956/962), Jaguar and the Sauber-Mercedes team that trained future Formula One champion Michael Schumacher.

Decline and cancellation

At the end of the 1980s the FIA attempted to align Group C with Formula One by mandating 3.5-litre F1-style engines from 1991, intending to draw in manufacturers such as Peugeot and Toyota. Costs exploded, privateer entries collapsed and television and spectator interest waned; after a thin 1992 season won by Peugeot, the championship was cancelled, ending top-level world sports car racing for two decades. The FIA revived the concept in 2012 with the FIA World Endurance Championship, again centred on Le Mans.

Champions

Ferrari was the most successful manufacturer of the early decades, while Porsche accumulated the most titles overall across the series' many formats. A drivers' championship, formalised in the 1980s, was won by such figures as Jacky Ickx, Derek Bell, Jean-Louis Schlesser and Mauro Baldi. The final champions of 1992 were Peugeot (teams) and Yannick Dalmas and Derek Warwick (drivers).
