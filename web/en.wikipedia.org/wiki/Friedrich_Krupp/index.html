Friedrich Krupp

Born: 17 July 1787, Essen
Died: 8 October 1826 (aged 39), Essen
Occupation: Industrialist
Known for: Founder of the Krupp steel works

Friedrich Carl Krupp (17 July 1787 – 8 October 1826) was a German steel manufacturer and founder of the Krupp family industrial empire, which grew into one of the largest steel and armaments companies in the world (today part of thyssenkrupp).

Life

Krupp was born in Essen, then in the County of Mark within the Holy Roman Empire, into a family of merchants that had been established in the city since the 16th century. His father died when he was nine, and he was raised by his grandmother Helene Amalie Krupp, who had bought the Sterkrade ironworks in 1800. He worked in that enterprise before setting out to realise his ambition of producing high-quality cast steel (crucible steel) in Germany, a material then imported largely from England.

The cast steel factory

In 1811 Krupp founded a cast steel factory in Essen with partners, and in 1816 he moved production to a new site that became the nucleus of the great Krupp works beside the family's original home. The business struggled financially for years: cast steel was expensive to make and demand was initially limited to dies for mints, rolls and tools. Krupp's health deteriorated under the strain, and he died in 1826 at the age of 39, leaving the works heavily indebted and employing only a handful of workers.

Legacy

On his death, his widow Therese Krupp took over the firm and, crucially, Friedrich's son Alfred Krupp — who had left school at fourteen to work in the business — built it into a vast industrial concern supplying rails, tyres and artillery to states across Europe. Friedrich's founding of the works and his insistence on the quality of cast steel laid the foundation for the enterprise that bore his family name for two centuries.
