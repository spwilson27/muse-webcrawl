Suriname River

The Suriname River (Dutch: Surinamerivier) is a river of about 480 kilometres in the South American country of Suriname, from which the country takes its name. It is the country's most important river, draining a large part of the interior and flowing north to the Atlantic Ocean near the capital, Paramaribo.

Suriname River
Length: approx. 480 km
Source: Guiana Highlands
Mouth: Atlantic Ocean, near Paramaribo
Major structure: Afobaka Dam / Brokopondo Reservoir

Course

The river rises in the Guiana Highlands in the south of Suriname, between the Wilhelmina and Eilerts de Haan mountain ranges, and flows northward through dense rainforest and savanna. It passes the settlements of Pokigron and Brokopondo, receives tributaries, and eventually reaches the coastal plain. Near Paramaribo the river is about a kilometre wide; its estuary opens into the Atlantic by Nieuw Amsterdam, opposite the capital. The Commewijne River joins the Suriname near its mouth.

Brokopondo Reservoir

The Afobaka Dam, completed in the 1960s to supply hydroelectric power to an alumina refinery and Paramaribo, impounds the river to form the Brokopondo Reservoir (Brokopondomeer), one of the largest reservoirs in the world, covering roughly 1,560 km2. Its creation flooded forest and displaced Saramaka Maroon villages, an enduring social and environmental issue.

Transport and landmarks

The lower river is navigable by ocean-going ships to Paramaribo, Suriname's principal port. The Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge (opened 2000), about 1.5 kilometres long, crosses the river connecting Paramaribo with Meerzorg in the Commewijne District; before it, traffic relied on ferries. Plantations, historic forts (such as Fort Zeelandia), and riverine villages line the banks.
