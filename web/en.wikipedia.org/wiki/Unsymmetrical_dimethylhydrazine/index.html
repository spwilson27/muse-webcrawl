Unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine

Structure of UDMH

Unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine (UDMH; 1,1-dimethylhydrazine) is a chemical compound with the formula H2N–N(CH3)2. It is a colourless liquid with a sharp, fishy, ammoniacal smell typical of organic amines. UDMH is best known as a component of rocket propellants, where it is used as a hypergolic fuel in combination with oxidizers such as nitrogen tetroxide (NTO) or red fuming nitric acid.

Properties

Samples of UDMH turn yellowish on exposure to air and absorb oxygen and carbon dioxide. It is miscible with water, ethanol, and most petroleum fuels. Despite its useful properties as a storable rocket fuel, UDMH is highly toxic, carcinogenic, and environmentally hazardous; its use has declined in newer launch vehicles in favour of less toxic alternatives, though it remains in service in vehicles such as the Russian Proton rocket and Chinese Long March rockets, and in spacecraft propulsion systems.

Production

UDMH is produced industrially by methods including the catalytic hydrogenation of dimethylnitrosamine with excess hydrogen, and by chloramination of dimethylamine followed by treatment with base.
