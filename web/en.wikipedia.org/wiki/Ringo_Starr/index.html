Ringo Starr

Born: Richard Starkey, 7 July 1940, Liverpool, England
Occupations: Musician, singer, songwriter, actor
Instruments: Drums, vocals
Years active: 1957–present

Sir Richard Starkey (born 7 July 1940), known professionally as Ringo Starr, is an English musician and actor who achieved international fame as the drummer of the Beatles. He joined the band in August 1962, replacing Pete Best, and sang lead on songs including "Yellow Submarine" and "With a Little Help from My Friends", and wrote "Octopus's Garden" and "Don't Pass Me By".

Solo career

After the Beatles split in 1970, Starr scored solo hits in the early 1970s with Ringo (1973) and singles "Photograph", "You're Sixteen" and "It Don't Come Easy". Since 1989 he has toured with successive editions of his All-Starr Band. He was knighted in 2018.

Drumming

Left-handed but playing a right-handed kit, Starr is praised for his steady timekeeping, distinctive fills and musical feel, and is credited with raising the profile of drummers in rock music.
