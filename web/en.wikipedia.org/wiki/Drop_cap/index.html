Drop cap

A drop cap (also called a dropped capital or initial) is a large capital letter at the beginning of a paragraph or section that extends down over several lines of the following text. The word or phrase following it continues in ordinary type, with the lines of text wrapped around the enlarged letter.

History

Drop caps descend from the enlarged and decorated initials of medieval illuminated manuscripts, where scribes left space for a rubricator or illuminator to add an elaborate initial, often historiated with scenes or figures. With the arrival of printing, early books imitated the practice: spaces were left blank for hand-drawn initials, and later printers cast large decorative type sorts. The device remained common in fine book typography and appears today in novels, magazines, and newspapers at the openings of chapters and articles.

Styles

A drop cap may sit on the baseline of the first line and rise above it (a "raised cap" or stick-up initial), or descend through several lines with its top aligned with the cap height of the first line — the classic dropped form. The letter is usually two to four lines deep. In CSS, drop caps on the web are commonly implemented with the ::first-letter pseudo-element combined with floating and font-size adjustments, and the CSS Inline Layout specification defines an initial-letter property for the purpose.

Usage considerations

Typographers advise that drop caps should be used sparingly, aligned optically with the text lines they span, and followed by small caps or a slightly letterspaced run-in for the next few words in formal settings. Poorly aligned drop caps are a common design error.
