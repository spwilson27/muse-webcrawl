Porrentruy

Porrentruy (German: Pruntrut) is a Swiss municipality and the capital of the district of the same name in the canton of Jura in northwestern Switzerland. It is the second-largest town in the canton, with a population of around 6,600, and is the cultural and economic centre of the Ajoie region near the French border.

History

Porrentruy was first mentioned in 1136. From 1528, when the prince-bishops of Basel were driven from their city by the Reformation, until the end of the 18th century, the town served as their residence, and the imposing castle above the old town dates largely from this period. After revolutionary upheavals and a period under French rule, the town passed to the canton of Bern in 1815, and then to the newly created canton of Jura in 1979.

Geography

The town lies on the Allaine river in the rolling limestone country of the Ajoie, close to the French frontier. It is connected by rail to Delémont and to Belfort in France, and the A16 motorway (the Transjurane) passes nearby.

Sights

Landmarks include the Château de Porrentruy, the old town with its fountains (including the Fountain of the Samaritan Woman and the Fountain of the Boar), the churches of Saint-Pierre and Saint-Germain, and the botanical garden, one of the oldest in Switzerland, founded in 1794.
