Fox squirrel

Scientific nameSciurus niger
FamilySciuridae
Length45–70 cm (incl. tail)
Mass0.5–1.4 kg
ConservationLeast Concern (IUCN)

The fox squirrel (Sciurus niger), also known as the eastern fox squirrel or Bryant's fox squirrel, is the largest species of tree squirrel native to North America. Despite its name, its coat colour varies widely, from grizzled orange-brown ("foxy") upperparts with rusty-orange underparts to black and patterned phases in parts of its range.

Distribution and habitat

The species is native to the eastern and central United States, southern Canada, and northeastern Mexico, favouring open woodland, oak–hickory forest edge, and hedgerows rather than dense forest. It has been introduced to many western cities and is common in parks and suburbs. Several subspecies are recognized, including the Delmarva fox squirrel of the Delmarva Peninsula, formerly listed as endangered, and the mangrove fox squirrel of southern Florida.

Behaviour and ecology

Fox squirrels are diurnal and spend more time foraging on the ground than grey squirrels. They feed chiefly on tree seeds and nuts — acorns, hickory nuts, walnuts — as well as buds, fruit, fungi, and occasionally insects and bird eggs, and they scatter-hoard nuts for winter. They build leaf nests (dreys) and use tree cavities for shelter and rearing young. Main predators include hawks, owls, foxes, bobcats, and snakes.
