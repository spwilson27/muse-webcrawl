Baja California peninsula

LocationNorth-western Mexico
Length~1,200 km
StatesBaja California, Baja California Sur
[Image: Desert and the Sea of Cortés on Baja]
Desert and the Sea of Cortés on Baja

The Baja California peninsula is a long, narrow peninsula in north-western Mexico, separating the Pacific Ocean from the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortés), which Jacques Cousteau called "the world's aquarium". It comprises the states of Baja California and Baja California Sur.

Character

Some 1,200 km long and rarely 200 km wide, the peninsula is desert mountain spine and cactus plain — boojum trees, cardón forests and mission towns from 1697 — flanked by whale lagoons (gray whales calve at Ojo de Liebre and San Ignacio) and islands full of endemic reptiles. At its tip, Los Cabos concentrates resort wealth; the East Cape and Bahía de los Ángeles remain quiet. The Transpeninsular Highway (1973) first joined the peninsula by road. Its gulf is among the most productive seas on Earth, with island reserves inscribed by UNESCO.
