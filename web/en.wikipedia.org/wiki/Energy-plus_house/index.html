Energy-plus house

An energy-plus house (German: Plusenergiehaus) is a building that, over the course of a year, produces more energy from renewable sources than it consumes. It extends the concepts of the low-energy house and the passive house by adding on-site generation, usually photovoltaics, so that the annual energy balance is positive — the surplus being fed into the grid.

Design principles

An energy-plus house first minimises demand through excellent insulation, airtight construction, heat-recovery ventilation, efficient appliances and passive solar design. It then covers the remaining demand — heating, hot water, lighting, ventilation and household electricity — with renewable generation, typically roof-mounted solar panels, sometimes combined with a heat pump and small wind turbine. Battery storage and smart controls increasingly shift surplus production to times of demand.

History and examples

The concept was popularised by architect Rolf Disch, whose Heliotrope in Freiburg, Germany (1994) rotates to track the sun and generates several times its own energy use; his later Solar Settlement in Freiburg applied the standard to a whole neighbourhood. Energy-plus buildings are now included in several national building programmes and demonstrations, including solar decathlon entries.

Related concepts

A zero-energy building balances consumption and generation to net zero over a year, while an energy-plus building goes further and shows a credit. Definitions vary as to whether primary energy or site energy is counted and whether embodied energy of materials is included.
