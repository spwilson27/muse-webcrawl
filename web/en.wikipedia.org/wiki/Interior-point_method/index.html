Interior-point method

Interior-point methods (also referred to as barrier methods) are a class of algorithms to solve linear and nonlinear convex optimization problems. Unlike the simplex method, which traverses the boundary of the feasible region, interior-point methods move through the interior of the feasible set.

History

Although barrier methods were studied in the 1960s, the field was revolutionized in 1984 by Narendra Karmarkar, who introduced a polynomial-time algorithm for linear programming that proved highly effective in practice. Subsequent work produced primal-dual methods, which are the basis of most modern implementations.

Barrier functions

Interior-point methods keep iterates strictly inside the feasible region by adding a barrier function — typically a logarithmic barrier — to the objective. The barrier grows to infinity as the boundary is approached, preventing the iterates from crossing it. A sequence of problems with decreasing barrier parameter is solved, converging to the optimum.

Applications

Interior-point methods are used in large-scale linear programming, semidefinite programming, and many engineering and machine-learning problems, and are implemented in solvers such as MOSEK, Gurobi, and CPLEX.
