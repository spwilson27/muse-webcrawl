Haute-Vienne is a department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of west-central France, named after the river Vienne, which crosses it from east to west. Its prefecture and largest city is Limoges, historically the western part of the old province of the Limousin. The department number 87 is used in postal codes and vehicle registrations.

History

Haute-Vienne was created on 4 March 1790 during the French Revolution from parts of the provinces of Limousin, Marche, Poitou, Angoumois and Berry. The area is known for the massacre at Oradour-sur-Glane on 10 June 1944, when Waffen-SS troops murdered 642 inhabitants; the ruined village is preserved as a memorial. During the war the maquis of the Limousin, led locally by Georges Guingouin, was among the most active resistance movements in France.

Geography

The department is a land of rolling granite uplands on the northwestern edge of the Massif Central, rising to about 795 m in the Monts de Blond and the Ambazac hills, and drained by the Vienne and its tributaries including the Gorre, Briance and Taurion. It is largely rural, with cattle farming (the red Limousin breed), chestnut and oak woods, and kaolin and former uranium mining. The Périgord-Limousin Regional Natural Park covers part of the southwest.

Economy and culture

Limoges is famed for its hard-paste porcelain — made possible by kaolin deposits discovered near Saint-Yrieix in 1768 — and for medieval enamelwork. The department's economy rests on public services, ceramics, paper, leather (the glove-making town of Saint-Junien), and agriculture.
