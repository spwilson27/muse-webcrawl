Ballistics

Ballistics is the field of mechanics concerned with the launching, flight behaviour and impact effects of projectiles, especially munitions such as bullets, artillery shells, rockets and bombs. The word comes from the Greek βάλλειν (ballein), "to throw." Ballistics is conventionally divided into four phases of a projectile's life.

A bullet in flight with its shock wave — a classic ballistics image

Branches

  • Internal ballistics — the behaviour of the projectile within the weapon: propellant combustion, chamber pressure, acceleration down the barrel.
  • Transition ballistics — the brief phase as the projectile exits the muzzle and propellant gases overtake it.
  • External ballistics — flight through the air, governed by gravity, drag, spin drift, the Coriolis effect and aerodynamic stability.
  • Terminal ballistics — the behaviour and effects of the projectile when it strikes its target, including penetration and wound ballistics.

History

Niccolò Tartaglia's Nova Scientia (1537) was the first printed study; Galileo established the parabolic trajectory of projectiles under gravity, and Newton supplied the drag laws that, with modern ballistic coefficients, still underpin firing tables and sniper calculations. The ballistic pendulum (1742) first measured bullet velocity accurately.

Forensic ballistics

In forensic science, "ballistics" loosely denotes the examination of firearms evidence — comparing rifling marks on bullets and cartridge cases to a suspect weapon, for example through databases such as NIBIN. Strictly, specialists distinguish this firearms identification work from ballistics proper.
