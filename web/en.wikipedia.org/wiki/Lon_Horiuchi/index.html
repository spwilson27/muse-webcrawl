Lon Horiuchi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lon Tomohisa Horiuchi (born June 9, 1954) is an American former Federal Bureau of Investigation agent and sniper with the FBI's Hostage Rescue Team (HRT). He became a controversial public figure because of his role in two of the most criticised federal law-enforcement operations of the 1990s: the 1992 siege at Ruby Ridge, Idaho, where his shot killed Vicki Weaver, and the 1993 siege of the Branch Davidian compound near Waco, Texas, where he was also deployed as a sniper.

Background

Horiuchi, a Japanese-American from Hawaii, graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point and served as an infantry officer in the US Army before joining the FBI in the 1980s. He qualified as an HRT sniper and was among the agents deployed to Ruby Ridge in August 1992, where federal marshals were attempting to arrest fugitive Randy Weaver.

Ruby Ridge

On 22 August 1992, operating under special rules of engagement that authorised deadly force against armed adult males, Horiuchi fired two shots. The first wounded Randy Weaver; the second, fired as the group fled back into their cabin, struck Kevin Harris and killed Vicki Weaver, who was standing behind the cabin door holding her infant daughter. The killings, and the rules of engagement themselves, became the focus of Senate hearings and public outrage. In 1997 Boundary County, Idaho, charged Horiuchi with involuntary manslaughter; the case was removed to federal court and dismissed on supremacy-clause immunity grounds, a dismissal that was briefly reversed by an en banc Ninth Circuit panel before the county dropped the charges in 2001. Horiuchi was also present with the HRT at Waco the following year, a fact later cited by conspiracy theorists—including Oklahoma City bomber Timothy McVeigh, who cited both incidents as motives—though no evidence linked him to wrongdoing there.

Horiuchi retired from the FBI in 2006. His name remains a byword in debates over federal use-of-force policy, and the Weaver family received a $3.1 million government settlement in 1995.
