Felipe González Márquez (born 5 March 1942) is a Spanish lawyer and politician who served as Prime Minister of Spain from 1982 to 1996. Leader of the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) from 1974 to 1997, he is the longest-serving prime minister of democratic Spain and oversaw the country's consolidation as a modern European democracy after the Franco dictatorship.

Early life and rise

González was born in Seville and studied law at the University of Seville, joining the then-clandestine PSOE in 1964. Working as a labour lawyer, he rose through the party under the pseudonym "Isidoro" and was elected secretary-general at the Suresnes congress of 1974. After the death of Franco and Spain's transition to democracy, he led the PSOE into opposition in 1977 and resigned briefly in 1979 over the party's Marxist definition before being re-elected once it was dropped.

Prime minister

The PSOE won a landslide victory in the 1982 general election. González's governments modernised the Spanish economy, expanded the welfare state and public infrastructure, and steered Spain into the European Community in 1986. A referendum the same year confirmed Spain's membership of NATO. His later years in office were marred by corruption scandals and the GAL affair, in which state-linked death squads targeted ETA members. The PSOE lost power to José María Aznar's People's Party in 1996.

Later life

González stepped down as PSOE leader in 1997 and left parliament in 2004. He has remained an influential elder statesman, active in European and Latin American affairs, and founded the Fundación Felipe González to preserve and study his archive.
