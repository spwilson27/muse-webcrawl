Frederick Handley Page

BornNovember 15, 1885, Cheltenham, England
DiedApril 21, 1962 (aged 76), Westminster, London, England
OccupationAircraft manufacturer
Known forFounder of Handley Page Limited

Sir Frederick Handley Page (15 November 1885 – 21 April 1962) was an English industrialist who founded the aircraft company Handley Page Limited in 1909, one of the world's first aircraft manufacturers. He was a pioneer of large multi-engined aircraft and an inventor of the leading-edge slot.

Career

Handley Page trained as an electrical engineer before turning to aviation. His company, based at Cricklewood in London, produced some of the first British bombers of the First World War, notably the Handley Page Type O (O/100 and O/400), among the largest aircraft of the war and used for strategic bombing of German targets.

Between the wars the firm built airliners such as the Hannibal class and the Heracles for Imperial Airways. In the Second World War it produced the Hampden medium bomber and, most importantly, the Halifax, one of the RAF's principal four-engined heavy bombers. After the war came the Hermes airliner, the Victor V-bomber — one of the three aircraft of Britain's nuclear deterrent — and the Herald and Jetstream airliners.

The leading-edge slot

Handley Page developed and patented the slotted wing — an automatic leading-edge slat that delays the stall and improves low-speed control — an invention that earned him substantial royalties and influenced aircraft design worldwide.

Honors

Handley Page was knighted in 1942 and served as president of the Royal Aeronautical Society and the Society of British Aircraft Constructors. He died in 1962; his company, unable to compete after government pressure to merge the industry, went into liquidation in 1970.
