Brown, Boveri & Cie (BBC) was a Swiss group of electrical engineering companies founded in Baden, Switzerland, in 1891 by Charles Eugene Lancelot Brown and Walter Boveri. It became one of the world's leading manufacturers of electrical equipment, including generators, turbines, locomotives and power transmission systems.

History

Brown had previously worked for Maschinenfabrik Oerlikon, where he developed innovative rotating electric machines, while Boveri was a manager there. Their new firm quickly won major contracts, notably equipment for early hydroelectric and alternating-current transmission projects across Europe.

BBC developed the first practical high-voltage direct current transmission links, built turbo-generators for power stations, and produced electric locomotives for the Swiss railways. In 1988 BBC merged with the Swedish company ASEA to form ABB (Asea Brown Boveri), creating one of the largest electrical engineering groups in the world.
