Taxicab

A New York City yellow cab, among the world's most recognisable taxis

A taxicab, also called a taxi or cab, is a type of vehicle for hire with a driver, used by a single passenger or small group, often for a non-shared ride. Most taxicabs are distinguished from other for-hire transport by special licensing, a taximeter that computes the fare from distance travelled and waiting time, and the ability to be hailed on the street, hired at a taxi rank, or booked by phone or app. The word derives from "taximeter cabriolet", combining the fare-measuring taximeter with the earlier horse-drawn cab.

History

Horse-drawn hackney carriages for hire operated in London and Paris from the early 17th century, and the two-wheeled hansom cab became the standard London taxi of the Victorian era. Motorised taxicabs appeared in the late 1890s: electric taxis ran in London and New York from 1897, and petrol-powered taxis with taximeters spread rapidly after 1907, when large fleets were introduced in New York and other cities. The familiar yellow colour of New York cabs traces to the Yellow Cab Company founded by John D. Hertz in Chicago in 1915.

Regulation and the medallion system

Most cities regulate taxis through licensing of drivers and vehicles, fixed metered fares, and limits on numbers. New York's medallion system, introduced in 1937, capped the number of licensed cabs; medallions traded at prices exceeding one million dollars at their 2013 peak before collapsing in value with the rise of app-based ride-hailing services such as Uber and Lyft, which from the 2010s transformed urban for-hire transport worldwide and prompted widespread regulatory reform.

Vehicles and types

Taxis are commonly saloon cars painted in a distinctive local colour — yellow in New York, black in London (where purpose-built "black cabs" such as the LEVC TX carry up to five passengers), white, green or multi-coloured in other cities. Variants include wheelchair-accessible taxis, minivan taxis, airport taxis, water taxis, and motorcycle taxis common in parts of Asia and Africa. London taxi drivers are famous for "the Knowledge", the memorisation of the city's streets required for licensing.
