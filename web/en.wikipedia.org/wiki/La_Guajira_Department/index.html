La Guajira Department

La Guajira (Spanish pronunciation: [la ɣwaˈxiɾa]) is a department of Colombia in the Caribbean Region, occupying most of the Guajira Peninsula in the northeast of the country, on the Caribbean Sea and bordering Venezuela to the east. Its capital is Riohacha. With an area of about 20,848 km² (8,049 sq mi) and a population of roughly one million, it is one of Colombia's poorest departments but one of its richest in natural resources and indigenous culture.

Geography and population

Much of the department is arid or semi-arid desert and xeric scrubland, especially in Alta Guajira, while the south (Media and Baja Guajira) is greener, watered by the Ranchería and Cesar rivers descending from the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Serranía del Perijá. La Guajira is the homeland of the Wayuu, Colombia's largest indigenous people, who also live across the border in Venezuela and maintain their own language (Wayuunaiki), clan system, and matrilineal traditions; many municipalities have Wayuu-majority populations.

Economy

The department hosts Cerrejón, one of the world's largest open-pit coal mines, operated for decades by multinational partners and now owned by Glencore, as well as natural gas fields (Chuchupa and Ballena) offshore. Salt is produced at the Manaure salt flats, Colombia's largest. These extractive industries contrast with chronic poverty, water scarcity, and child malnutrition among rural Wayuu communities, which have drawn national and international attention and court-ordered interventions.

Tourism and culture

Destinations include the beaches of Cabo de la Vela and Punta Gallinas — the northernmost point of South America — the flamingo sanctuary at Los Flamencos, and Macuira National Natural Park, a cloud-forested mountain oasis in the desert. Riohacha hosts the Festival Francisco el Hombre, celebrating vallenato music; the department features prominently in Gabriel García Márquez's writings, whose family came from the region.
