Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET), also called fluorescence resonance energy transfer, is a mechanism of energy transfer between two light-sensitive molecules (chromophores) in which an excited donor transfers energy to an acceptor through non-radiative dipole–dipole coupling, without the emission and reabsorption of a photon. Because its efficiency depends extremely steeply on the distance between donor and acceptor, FRET is widely used as a "spectroscopic ruler" for measuring distances and detecting interactions between biomolecules on the scale of 1 to 10 nanometres.

Theory

The theory was developed by Theodor Förster in the 1940s. The transfer efficiency E depends on the inverse sixth power of the donor–acceptor separation r:

E = 1 / (1 + (r/R0)6)

where R0, the Förster radius, is the distance at which transfer is 50% efficient. R0 depends on the overlap between the donor's emission spectrum and the acceptor's absorption spectrum, the donor quantum yield, the refractive index of the medium, and the relative orientation of the two transition dipoles, usually captured by the orientation factor κ² (often approximated as 2/3 for freely rotating dyes).

Requirements

Efficient FRET requires that the donor emission spectrum overlap the acceptor absorption spectrum, that the two chromophores be close in space (typically within about 10 nm), and that their transition dipoles not be perpendicular. The process competes with donor fluorescence, so it both decreases donor emission intensity and lifetime and, if the acceptor is fluorescent, increases acceptor emission.

Applications

FRET is used to study protein–protein interactions, conformational changes, enzyme activity and nucleic acid structure. Common pairs include the fluorescent proteins CFP and YFP, and dyes such as Cy3 and Cy5. Genetically encoded biosensors exploit FRET changes to report calcium (cameleons), kinase activity and membrane voltage in living cells. Variants include time-resolved FRET (TR-FRET), acceptor photobleaching, and fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM-FRET), which measures the donor lifetime and is largely independent of concentration.
