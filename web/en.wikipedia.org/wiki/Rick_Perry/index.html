Rick Perry

James Richard "Rick" Perry (born March 4, 1950) is an American politician who served as the 47th governor of Texas from 2000 to 2015 and as United States Secretary of Energy from 2017 to 2019. A Republican, he is the longest-serving governor in Texas history.

Early life and career

Perry was born in Paint Creek, Texas, graduated from Texas A&M University, and served as a captain in the U.S. Air Force. He was elected to the Texas House of Representatives in 1984 as a Democrat, switched to the Republican Party in 1989, and served as Texas Agriculture Commissioner in the 1990s and as lieutenant governor under George W. Bush from 1999. He succeeded Bush as governor when Bush resigned to become president.

Governorship and later roles

Perry won full terms in 2002, 2006, and 2010, presiding over the "Texas Miracle" period of job growth while championing low taxes, tort reform, and socially conservative positions. He sought the Republican presidential nomination in 2012 and 2016. His 2012 campaign is remembered for an "oops" moment in a debate when he could not recall the third federal agency he proposed eliminating. As energy secretary under President Donald Trump, he oversaw the national laboratories and nuclear weapons complex. In 2014 he was indicted on abuse-of-power charges, which were ultimately dismissed.
