Anisocoria

Anisocoria is a condition in which the pupils of the two eyes are unequal in size. A small difference, often up to about 1 millimetre, may be physiological and present in a substantial minority of people. Larger or newly developing differences can indicate disease of the iris, autonomic nervous system or visual pathways.

Causes

Causes include Horner syndrome, third cranial nerve palsy, tonic pupil, iris trauma, inflammation, pharmacological exposure to dilating or constricting agents, and acute angle-closure glaucoma. The clinical pattern depends on whether the difference is greater in bright light or darkness and whether ptosis, eye-movement abnormalities or pain are present.

Evaluation

Assessment compares pupil size and reactions in light and dark, examines eyelid position and ocular motility, and may use pharmacological drops or neuroimaging. Sudden anisocoria with headache, ptosis or a dilated pupil can be an emergency because it may signal an aneurysm compressing the third cranial nerve.
