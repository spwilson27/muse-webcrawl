George I (24 December 1845 – 18 March 1913) was King of Greece from 1863 until his assassination in 1913. Born Prince William of Denmark, the second son of King Christian IX, he was elected king by the Greek National Assembly at the age of seventeen and reigned for nearly fifty years, the longest reign of any modern Greek monarch.

Reign

Under George, Greece adopted the democratic constitution of 1864 and gradually expanded its territory: the Ionian Islands were ceded by Britain in 1864, Thessaly and part of Epirus were gained from the Ottoman Empire in 1881, and Crete achieved autonomy after 1898. Athens hosted the first modern Olympic Games in 1896. During the First Balkan War of 1912–13, Greek forces liberated Thessaloniki, where George was assassinated by Alexandros Schinas a few months later.

Family

He married Grand Duchess Olga Constantinovna of Russia in 1867; their eldest son succeeded him as Constantine I. George was the brother of Queen Alexandra of the United Kingdom and Empress Maria Feodorovna of Russia.
