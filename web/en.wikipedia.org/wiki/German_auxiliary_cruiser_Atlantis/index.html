The German auxiliary cruiser Atlantis (HSK 2), known to the Kriegsmarine as Schiff 16 and to the Royal Navy as Raider-C, was a merchant raider of Nazi Germany's navy in the Second World War. Commanded by Kapitän zur See Bernhard Rogge, she conducted one of the most successful raiding cruises of the war, lasting 602 days.

Career

Converted from the motor vessel Goldenfels, Atlantis sailed in March 1940 disguised as neutral shipping. Using a succession of false identities, flags and silhouettes, she operated in the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific oceans, sinking or capturing 22 merchant ships totalling roughly 146,000 gross register tons. In May 1941 she captured the Egyptian liner Zamzam, whose passengers included American journalists; photographs from the incident, published in Life magazine, helped the Royal Navy identify the raider.

Sinking

On 22 November 1941, while refuelling the submarine U-126 in the South Atlantic, Atlantis was surprised by the British heavy cruiser HMS Devonshire, which opened fire from beyond the raider's effective range. Atlantis was sunk; most of her crew were rescued by U-126 and German supply ships and eventually returned to Germany. Rogge was among the few German raider captains decorated and later reached flag rank in the post-war West German navy.

Legacy

Atlantis's cruise became the subject of books and the 1960 film Under Ten Flags. Rogge's comparatively humane treatment of prisoners was frequently noted in accounts of the raider war.
