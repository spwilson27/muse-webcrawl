Vespula germanica

Vespula germanica, the German wasp or European wasp, is a species of social wasp native to Europe, North Africa and temperate Asia. It has been introduced to North and South America, South Africa, Australia and New Zealand, where it is an invasive pest.

Description

Workers are about 13 mm long, with the black-and-yellow abdominal markings typical of social wasps, three small black dots on the face distinguishing the species from the common wasp Vespula vulgaris. Colonies, founded by a single queen in spring, are built of papery nests — usually underground in old rodent burrows, but also in wall cavities and roofs — and may contain several thousand workers by late summer. Workers forage for insects to feed larvae and for sugary foods for themselves, which brings them into conflict with people at outdoor meals; they can sting repeatedly.

Invasiveness

In New Zealand beech forests, where abundant honeydew allows colonies to survive the winter and grow unusually large, German wasps are a major ecological problem, competing with native birds and insects. Control programmes use poisoned protein baits.
