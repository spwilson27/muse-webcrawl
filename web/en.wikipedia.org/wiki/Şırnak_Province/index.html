Şırnak Province (Turkish: Şırnak ili) is a province of Turkey in Southeastern Anatolia, bordering Iraq and Syria. Created in 1990 out of parts of Siirt, Hakkâri and Mardin provinces, its capital is the city of Şırnak. The province lies in the rugged Taurus foothills and includes Mount Cudi, which local tradition associates with the landing place of Noah's Ark.

The population is predominantly Kurdish. Districts include Cizre — an ancient town on the Tigris, home of the medieval scholar and inventor al-Jazari and the reputed tomb of Noah — Silopi, with the Habur border gate to Iraq that carries much of Turkey's trade with Iraqi Kurdistan, İdil, Beytüşşebap, Güçlükonak and Uludere.

History and economy

Şırnak was heavily affected by the Kurdish–Turkish conflict; many villages were evacuated in the 1990s, and the provincial capital and Cizre saw severe fighting and lengthy curfews in 2015–2016, after which large districts were rebuilt. The economy rests on agriculture, livestock, cross-border trade and public employment, and the province has some of Turkey's highest unemployment rates. Near Şırnak, asphaltite (a solid petroleum) has long been mined, and the region has seen growing interest in oil exploration.
