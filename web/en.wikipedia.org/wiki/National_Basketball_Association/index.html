The National Basketball Association (NBA) is a professional basketball league in North America composed of 30 teams (29 in the United States and 1 in Canada). It is one of the major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada and is considered the premier professional basketball league in the world. The league's headquarters are in New York City.

The NBA was created on 6 June 1946 as the Basketball Association of America (BAA) and adopted its present name in 1949 after merging with the rival National Basketball League (NBL). Teams play an 82-game regular season, followed by playoffs culminating in the NBA Finals, contested by the champions of the Eastern and Western Conferences for the Larry O'Brien Trophy.

History

The league's early era was dominated by the Minneapolis Lakers of George Mikan, then by the Boston Celtics, who won 11 championships in 13 seasons (1957–1969) with Bill Russell. The 1980s rivalry between Magic Johnson's Los Angeles Lakers and Larry Bird's Celtics revived the league's popularity; Michael Jordan's Chicago Bulls then won six titles in the 1990s and globalised the game. Recent eras have featured LeBron James, Stephen Curry's Golden State Warriors (four titles, 2015–2022) and the increasing dominance of international stars such as Giannis Antetokounmpo, Nikola Jokić and Luka Dončić.

Records

The Boston Celtics have the most championships (18, most recently 2024). Kareem Abdul-Jabbar's scoring record stood for 38 years until LeBron James passed him in February 2023; James also passed 40,000 career regular-season points in 2024. Wilt Chamberlain's 100-point game (2 March 1962) remains the single-game record.
