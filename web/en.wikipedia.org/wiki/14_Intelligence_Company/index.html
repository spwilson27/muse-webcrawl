14 Intelligence Company

The 14 Intelligence Company was a British Army special forces intelligence unit that operated in Northern Ireland during the Troubles. Officially titled the 4 Field Survey Troop and later part of the cover structure known as the "Det", the unit conducted covert surveillance, close target reconnaissance and intelligence gathering against paramilitary organisations. It is generally regarded as a forerunner of the Special Reconnaissance Regiment.

History

The unit grew out of early undercover surveillance experiments in Northern Ireland, including the Military Reaction Force, which was disbanded amid controversy. Reorganised under tighter control in the mid-1970s, detachments of the company — known colloquially as "Dets" — were established across the province, including in Belfast, Derry and rural border areas. Operators were selected from across the armed forces and underwent demanding training in surveillance, photography, weapons handling and close-quarter battle, including instruction by the Special Air Service.

Operations

Members of 14 Intelligence Company worked in plain clothes, often in high-risk environments, watching suspected members of the Provisional IRA and loyalist groups and supporting arrests and interceptions. Several operators were killed on duty, including Captain Robert Nairac, whose abduction and murder in 1977 became one of the most notorious episodes of the conflict. The unit's existence was long officially unacknowledged. In 2005 its role was absorbed into the newly formed Special Reconnaissance Regiment, which took over the Army's covert surveillance mission worldwide.
