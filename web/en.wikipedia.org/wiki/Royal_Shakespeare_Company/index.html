The Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC) is a major British theatre company dedicated to the plays of William Shakespeare, his contemporaries and new writing. Based in Shakespeare's birthplace, Stratford-upon-Avon, it is one of the United Kingdom's flagship publicly funded arts organisations, staging around 20 productions a year seen by over a million people in Stratford, London, on tour and in cinemas worldwide.

History

The company descends from the Shakespeare Memorial Theatre (opened 1879). In 1961 director Peter Hall reorganised it as the Royal Shakespeare Company, establishing a permanent ensemble, expanding into contemporary drama, and opening a London base at the Aldwych Theatre (later the Barbican). Hall's landmark Wars of the Roses cycle (1963) set the company's style. Successive artistic directors include Trevor Nunn, Terry Hands, Adrian Noble, Michael Boyd, Gregory Doran, and Daniel Evans and Tamara Harvey (from 2023). Famous productions include Peter Brook's A Midsummer Night's Dream (1970) and the stage adaptation of Les Misérables (1985, co-produced with Cameron Mackintosh).

Venues and work

In Stratford the RSC performs in the 1,000-seat Royal Shakespeare Theatre (redeveloped 2007–2010 with a thrust stage), the Swan Theatre (for Jacobean and contemporary work) and the studio theatre The Other Place. The company also runs extensive education programmes, touring productions and live cinema broadcasts ("Live from Stratford-upon-Avon").
