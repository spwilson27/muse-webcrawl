Whiddy Island

Whiddy Island (Irish: Faoide) is an island near the head of Bantry Bay, Ireland, about 5.5 km (3.4 mi) long by 2.4 km (1.5 mi) wide. It has a permanent population of around twenty people and is linked to Bantry by ferry.

Oil terminal

In the late 1960s Gulf Oil built a major oil terminal on the island, with jetties able to take the largest supertankers of the day. On 8 January 1979 the French tanker Betelgeuse exploded while discharging its cargo, killing 50 people and wrecking the terminal's jetty. The disaster remains one of Ireland's worst. The terminal was later acquired by the Irish government and is operated by Zenith Energy as a strategic oil storage facility.

History and tourism

The island has Napoleonic-era fortifications built against a feared French invasion, a former pilchard fishery, and the ruins of a castle of the O'Sullivan Beare clan. Today it attracts walkers and cyclists, and has a pub, the Bank House.
