The Calderone glacier (Italian: Ghiacciaio del Calderone) is the only glacier in the Apennines and one of the southernmost glaciers in Europe. It lies in a cirque on the northern side of the Corno Grande, the highest peak of the Gran Sasso massif in Abruzzo, Italy, within the Gran Sasso e Monti della Laga National Park.

Description

The glacier survives because of its sheltered, north-facing position in a deep cirque that accumulates wind-blown and avalanche snow and is shaded for much of the day. Its surface is largely covered by rock debris, which insulates the ice beneath. In 2000 the shrinking glacier split into two separate ice bodies, an upper and a lower part.

Retreat

Like most small Mediterranean glaciers, the Calderone has retreated dramatically since the end of the Little Ice Age, losing much of its area and thickness through the 20th and early 21st centuries. It is monitored by glaciologists as an indicator of climate change in the central Mediterranean and is sometimes described as Europe's southernmost glacier, a distinction it shares in debate with small ice bodies in Spain's Sierra Nevada and the Balkans.
