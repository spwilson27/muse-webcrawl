Air fryer

An air fryer is a small countertop convection oven designed to simulate deep frying without submerging food in oil. A fan circulates hot air at high speed, producing a crisp layer via the Maillard reaction. Foods are typically cooked in a perforated basket, and a small amount of oil may be applied to the food.

History

Convection ovens have been used since the mid-20th century. The modern air fryer was developed by Dutch inventor Fred van der Weij and introduced by Philips at the Internationale Funkausstellung in Berlin in 2010 under the Airfryer name, using Rapid Air technology. Air fryers became widely popular in many countries during the 2010s and 2020s.

Cooking

Air fryers are used for foods such as french fries, chicken wings, and frozen snacks. Compared with deep frying, they use substantially less oil, though results differ in flavour and texture from food cooked in oil. Compared with a full-size convection oven, an air fryer's smaller chamber heats quickly and cooks faster, but has limited capacity.

Types

Common types include basket-style pod air fryers, air fryer ovens with shelves, and multifunction pressure cookers with air-frying lids.
