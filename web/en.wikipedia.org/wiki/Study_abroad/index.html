Study abroad

Students on an exchange programme at a host university abroad

Study abroad is the act of a student pursuing educational opportunities in a country other than one's own. This can include primary, secondary and post-secondary students. Study abroad programmes range from short summer or faculty-led trips of a few weeks, through semester and year-long exchanges, to entire degrees taken overseas; the number of students studying abroad represents about 1% of all students enrolled at institutions of higher education in the United States, and worldwide mobile students number in the millions.

Programmes

The European Union's Erasmus Programme, founded in 1987, is the largest organised exchange scheme, sending millions of students between European universities. In the United States, popular destinations include the United Kingdom, Italy, Spain and France, while the largest sources of international students worldwide include China and India. The Junior Year in Munich (founded 1931) and similar language-immersion programmes are among the oldest continuously running schemes for American students.

Motivations and outcomes

Students commonly cite language acquisition, cultural immersion, academic specialisation and career advantages as motivations. Research associates study abroad with improved intercultural competence and language fluency, though costs, credit transfer and safety are recurring concerns, and participation remains skewed toward wealthier students.
