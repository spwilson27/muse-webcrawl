Carrier air wing

A carrier air wing (abbreviated CVW) is an operational naval aviation organisation composed of several aircraft squadrons and detachments of various types of fixed-wing and rotary-wing aircraft. Assigned to an aircraft carrier, the wing is the ship's principal striking arm; a modern United States Navy carrier air wing consists of roughly 60–65 aircraft and some 2,500 personnel organised into seven to nine squadrons.

Composition

A typical modern U.S. CVW includes four strike-fighter squadrons flying the F/A-18E/F Super Hornet (increasingly supplemented by the F-35C), an electronic-attack squadron of EA-18G Growlers, an airborne early warning squadron of E-2D Hawkeyes, a fleet logistics detachment of C-2 Greyhounds or CMV-22 Ospreys, and helicopter sea combat and maritime strike squadrons flying the MH-60S and MH-60R Seahawk.

History

The concept evolved from the pre-1945 carrier air group. The designation system CVW-1 through CVW-17 was adopted in 1963, and air wings became independent commands under a captain (the "CAG", from the earlier title Commander, Air Group). Since the Cold War, wings are no longer permanently tied to a single carrier hull, allowing flexible assignment within the fleet.
