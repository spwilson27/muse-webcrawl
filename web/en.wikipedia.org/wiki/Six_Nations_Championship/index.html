The Six Nations Championship is an annual international rugby union competition between England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland and Wales, played over five weekends in February and March. It descends from the Home Championship of 1883, the oldest international rugby tournament, became the Five Nations when France joined in 1910, and took its current form when Italy was admitted in 2000.

A team winning all five matches achieves a "Grand Slam"; victories over all three other home nations constitute a "Triple Crown". England has the most titles overall, but the modern Six Nations era has been defined by Ireland — Grand Slams in 2018 and 2023 and back-to-back titles in 2023–2024 under Andy Farrell — and by France, champions in 2022 (Grand Slam) and 2025, led by scrum-half Antoine Dupont, widely regarded as the world's best player. Italy, long the weakest side, has improved markedly, beating Scotland and drawing with France in 2024. Matches fill stadiums such as Twickenham, the Aviva Stadium, Murrayfield and the Stade de France.
