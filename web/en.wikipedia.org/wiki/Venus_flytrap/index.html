Venus flytrap

The Venus flytrap (Dionaea muscipula) is a carnivorous plant native to subtropical wetlands of the East Coast of the United States, in a small region around Wilmington, North Carolina. It catches insects and spiders with a snap trap formed by the terminal portion of each leaf, supplementing the poor mineral nutrition available in its acidic, nutrient-poor soils.

Trap mechanism

Each trap is a hinged pair of lobes fringed with stiff spines (cilia) and bearing three to four sensitive trigger hairs on each inner surface. When an insect touches two hairs within about twenty seconds, the lobes snap shut in roughly a tenth of a second, driven by changes in turgor pressure and release of elastic strain in the curved lobes. Continued struggling stimulates the plant to seal the trap and secrete digestive enzymes; digestion takes five to twelve days, after which the trap reopens. Each trap catches only a few meals before it withers.

Cultivation and conservation

The plant is widely cultivated as a curiosity but is vulnerable in the wild from poaching and habitat loss, and is listed as vulnerable. Its mechanism inspired Charles Darwin, who called it "one of the most wonderful plants in the world."
