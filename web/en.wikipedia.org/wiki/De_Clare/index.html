De Clare

OriginNormandy / Clare, Suffolk
TitlesEarls of Hertford, Gloucester, Pembroke
Prominence12th–14th centuries

The de Clare family was a prominent Anglo-Norman noble family that held lands in England, Wales and Ireland from the Norman Conquest until the early fourteenth century. Descended from Richard fitz Gilbert, a companion of William the Conqueror who was granted the lordship of Clare in Suffolk, the family produced earls of Hertford, Gloucester and Pembroke.

Origins and rise

Richard fitz Gilbert received extensive estates recorded in Domesday Book, centred on Clare and Tonbridge. His descendants married into the Marshal and other great families, and branches of the family became central figures in the Norman invasion of Ireland under Richard de Clare, 2nd Earl of Pembroke, known as "Strongbow".

Height of power

The senior line's earls of Hertford and Gloucester were among the richest magnates in England. Gilbert de Clare, 7th Earl of Gloucester, was a leading baron of Edward I's reign and built the great castle at Caerphilly in Wales.

Decline

The male line ended when Gilbert de Clare, 8th Earl of Gloucester, was killed at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314; his vast estates were divided among his three sisters, one of the largest inheritances of medieval England.
