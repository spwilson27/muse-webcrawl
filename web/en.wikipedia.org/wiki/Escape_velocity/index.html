Escape velocity

Escape velocity is the minimum speed needed for an object to break free from a body's gravitational pull without further propulsion. For Earth it is about 11.2 km/s.

In celestial mechanics, escape velocity or escape speed is the minimum speed needed for a free, non-propelled object to escape from the gravitational influence of a massive body, thus reaching an infinite distance from it. It is calculated by equating the object's kinetic energy with the magnitude of its gravitational potential energy. For a spherically symmetric body of mass M at distance r from its centre, the escape velocity is ve = √(2GM/r), where G is the gravitational constant.

Values

Escape velocity at a body's surface falls with size and density of the body. The Moon's is 2.38 km/s, Earth's 11.19 km/s (about 40,300 km/h), Jupiter's 59.5 km/s and the Sun's roughly 618 km/s. From Earth's surface, a spacecraft must reach 11.2 km/s to leave Earth's gravity altogether — though practical missions exploit Earth's rotation, staging and gravity assists, and escape from the Solar System from Earth's orbit requires a heliocentric speed of about 42 km/s, much of which is supplied by Earth's own orbital motion. Escape velocity is a scalar: any direction away from the body that does not intersect it will do. Note that "velocity" is a misnomer inherited from older usage; the concept also ignores atmospheric drag and assumes no further thrust.

Relation to orbits

Circular orbital velocity at a given altitude is smaller than escape velocity there by a factor of √2: an object in orbit that increases its speed by about 41 per cent will escape. A trajectory at exactly escape velocity is a parabola; faster, a hyperbola; slower, an ellipse. Escape velocity from the event horizon of a black hole would exceed the speed of light — an observation, made in the eighteenth century by John Michell and Laplace with their "dark stars", that foreshadows relativity. Real rockets never impart escape velocity instantaneously at the surface; they accelerate gradually, and continuously powered craft could in principle leave at any speed.
