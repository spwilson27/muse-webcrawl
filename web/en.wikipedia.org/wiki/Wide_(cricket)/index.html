Wide (cricket)

In cricket, a wide is a delivery bowled too wide or too high for the striker to play a normal cricket shot, judged under Law 22. A wide does not count as one of the over's legal deliveries and adds one run (a wide run) to the batting side's total, plus any further runs taken.

Judging

Umpires consider the striker's position and reach. In limited-overs cricket, guidelines mark return crease lines — any ball passing outside them on the off side is usually a wide — and leg-side bowling is judged strictly. A ball passing over head height may be called wide; in Tests a bouncer above head height can be called wide under Law 22 interpretations.

Scoring and consequences

Wides are recorded as extras against the bowler's figures. A batter cannot be out bowled, caught, lbw or hit wicket off a wide, but can be stumped, run out, or out hit the ball twice or obstructing the field. A wide that reaches the boundary scores five wide runs in total (the wide plus four).
