Navy Pier is a 3,300-foot-long (1,010 m) pier on the shoreline of Lake Michigan, located in the Streeterville neighborhood of the Near North Side community area in Chicago, Illinois, United States. Built between 1914 and 1916 as a municipal pier for shipping and public recreation, it is one of the most visited tourist attractions in the American Midwest, drawing roughly nine million visitors a year.

History

The pier opened in 1916 as Municipal Pier No. 2, part of Daniel Burnham's 1909 Plan of Chicago. It served shipping, warehousing, and mass public entertainment. During the Second World War it was taken over by the US Navy as a training facility for pilots and was renamed Navy Pier in 1927 in honour of naval veterans of the First World War. From 1946 to 1965 it housed the Chicago campus of the University of Illinois. After a period of decline, a major redevelopment in the 1990s transformed it into a festival marketplace and cultural venue; a further renovation for its centennial in 2016 added the 196-foot Centennial Wheel.

Attractions

Navy Pier includes the Chicago Children's Museum, the Chicago Shakespeare Theater, an IMAX/AMC cinema, gardens, exhibition halls, restaurants, and excursion-boat docks. Fireworks displays are held over the lake during the summer season.
