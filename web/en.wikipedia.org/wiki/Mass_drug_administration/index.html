Mass drug administration

Community-directed distributors administering antiparasitic drugs during a mass drug administration campaign.

Mass drug administration (MDA) is the administration of medication to an entire at-risk population, regardless of the infection status of the individual recipients. It is a central strategy in the control and elimination of neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), and is also used against malaria and other infectious diseases. Because treatment is given presumptively to everyone in a defined area, MDA is sometimes called preventive chemotherapy when applied to helminth infections.

Rationale

Many parasitic infections are widespread, underdiagnosed, and cheap to treat with safe, donated drugs. Individual diagnosis would cost more than the treatment itself, and light infections still sustain transmission. Treating the whole population at regular intervals reduces the community parasite burden, interrupts transmission, and protects even untreated individuals. MDA programmes typically aim for coverage of at least 65–80% of the target population, repeated annually or biannually for several years.

Diseases targeted

  • Lymphatic filariasis — treated with albendazole combined with ivermectin or diethylcarbamazine; the Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis, launched in 2000, has delivered billions of treatments.
  • Onchocerciasis (river blindness) — annual ivermectin through community-directed treatment.
  • Soil-transmitted helminthiasis — albendazole or mebendazole, often delivered through schools.
  • Schistosomiasis — praziquantel.
  • Trachoma — azithromycin, as part of the SAFE strategy.
  • Malaria — mass administration of artemisinin-based combinations has been trialled in elimination settings and in emergencies such as the West African Ebola epidemic.

Implementation

Campaigns are usually run by national ministries of health with support from the World Health Organization and pharmaceutical donation programmes — among the largest public–private partnerships in global health, with companies donating ivermectin, albendazole, praziquantel, and azithromycin. Drugs are distributed house-to-house by community volunteers or at fixed points such as schools.

Concerns

Critics raise the risk of accelerating drug resistance — a serious worry for malaria and a theoretical one for helminths — as well as adverse events in people with certain co-infections (notably severe reactions to ivermectin in individuals with high Loa loa loads). There is also debate about whether repeated MDA alone can achieve elimination without improvements in water, sanitation, and vector control.
