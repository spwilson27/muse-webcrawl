Continuity of care

Seeing the same clinician over time is the classic form of relational continuity.

Continuity of care is the process by which a patient and their physician-led care team are cooperatively involved in ongoing health care management toward the shared goal of high-quality, cost-effective medical care. It is a central principle of primary care and family medicine, and is associated with improved patient satisfaction, better uptake of preventive care, fewer hospital admissions and lower mortality.

Types of continuity

Researchers commonly distinguish three types. Relational (or interpersonal) continuity is an ongoing therapeutic relationship between a patient and one or more providers. Informational continuity is the use of information on past events and personal circumstances to make current care appropriate for the individual. Management continuity is a consistent and coherent approach to the management of a health condition that is responsive to a patient's changing needs, often delivered by a team following shared plans and protocols.

Evidence

Systematic reviews have found that greater continuity with a regular doctor is associated with significantly lower mortality rates, reduced emergency hospital admissions, and better medication adherence. Continuity is thought to work through accumulated knowledge of the patient, trust, and a sense of responsibility on the part of the clinician.

Challenges

Continuity has declined in many health systems because of larger practice sizes, part-time working, subspecialisation, and models of care that prioritise rapid access over seeing a known clinician. Health systems seek to balance timely access with continuity, for example through personal lists, small team-based "micro-teams", and better sharing of electronic health records to preserve informational continuity even when relational continuity is not possible.
