Food steamer

A food steamer or steam cooker is a kitchen appliance used to cook food with steam generated by heated water. The food is held in a closed vessel above or around the boiling water so that it cooks in the moist heat of the steam without being immersed in liquid.

Design

Traditional steamers, used for centuries in East Asian cooking, consist of stacked bamboo trays placed over a wok or pot of boiling water. Modern stovetop steamers are usually made of stainless steel or aluminium and consist of a pot, one or more perforated insert trays, and a lid. Electric food steamers generate steam with their own heating element and often include a timer and several stacking tiers so different foods can cook simultaneously.

Use

Steaming is widely used for vegetables, fish and seafood, rice dishes, dumplings such as dim sum, buns, puddings and reheating leftovers. Because the food does not sit in water, steaming tends to retain colour, texture and water-soluble nutrients better than boiling, and requires no added fat.

History

Steam cooking has ancient origins in China, where bamboo and earthenware steamers were used to prepare rice and filled buns. Commercial steam ovens and combi steamers became common in professional kitchens during the twentieth century.
