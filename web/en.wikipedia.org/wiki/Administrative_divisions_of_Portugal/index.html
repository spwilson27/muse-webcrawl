Administrative divisions of Portugal

The administrative divisions of Portugal organise the country into a hierarchy of territorial units. For statistical purposes Portugal uses the European Union's NUTS classification, while constitutionally its mainland comprises districts, municipalities (concelhos) and civil parishes (freguesias), plus the two autonomous regions of the Azores and Madeira, which have their own governments and legislatures.

Structure

Mainland Portugal is divided into 18 districts (Aveiro, Beja, Braga, Bragança, Castelo Branco, Coimbra, Évora, Faro, Guarda, Leiria, Lisbon, Portalegre, Porto, Santarém, Setúbal, Viana do Castelo, Vila Real and Viseu), which are administrative areas of the central state rather than elected tiers of government. Below them, 308 municipalities administer local services through elected municipal chambers and assemblies, and municipalities are subdivided into civil parishes, the smallest local government units, each with its own parish assembly and executive (junta de freguesia). In 2013 a controversial reform reduced the number of parishes from 4,259 to 3,092 by amalgamation.

Autonomous regions

The Atlantic archipelagos of the Azores and Madeira became autonomous regions under the 1976 Constitution, with regional legislative assemblies, governments and presidents, and wide legislative powers.

Statistical regions

For EU statistics Portugal is divided into NUTS II regions — Norte, Centro, Lisboa, Alentejo, Algarve, Azores and Madeira — revised over time, with municipalities forming the local administrative unit (LAU) level. Attempts to create elected administrative regions on the mainland were rejected in a 1998 referendum, so districts and the statistical regions remain the principal intermediate framework.
