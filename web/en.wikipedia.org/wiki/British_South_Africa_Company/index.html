British South Africa Company

Founded: 1889
Founder: Cecil Rhodes
Headquarters: London
Administered: Rhodesia (to 1923/24)

The British South Africa Company (BSAC) was chartered in 1889 under Cecil Rhodes, modelled on the old East India Company, with power to administer territory, maintain police and make treaties in south-central Africa north of the Transvaal.

Expansion

Armed with the Rudd Concession extracted from Lobengula, king of the Ndebele, the Company's Pioneer Column occupied Mashonaland in 1890 and founded Fort Salisbury. War with the Ndebele (1893–94) and the suppression of the Shona and Ndebele risings of 1896–97 (the First Chimurenga) consolidated control over what became Rhodesia — present-day Zimbabwe and Zambia.

Jameson Raid and later years

The failed Jameson Raid of 1895–96, launched by Company administrator Leander Starr Jameson into the Transvaal, disgraced Rhodes and increased government oversight. The BSAC administered Southern Rhodesia until 1923 and Northern Rhodesia until 1924, when the British government took over, but retained lucrative mineral rights — in Northern Rhodesia until Zambian independence in 1964.
