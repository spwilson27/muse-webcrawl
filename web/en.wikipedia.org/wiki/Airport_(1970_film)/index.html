Airport (1970 film)

Airport is a 1970 American drama film written and directed by George Seaton and based on Arthur Hailey's 1968 novel of the same name. An ensemble production produced by Ross Hunter for Universal Pictures, it stars Burt Lancaster and Dean Martin, with Jean Seberg, Jacqueline Bisset, George Kennedy, Helen Hayes, and Van Heflin in supporting roles. The film follows the staff of a major metropolitan airport through a single snowbound night, culminating in an attempt by a desperate passenger to blow up an airliner in flight for insurance money.

Airport launched the 1970s disaster film cycle.

Plot

At the fictional Lincoln International Airport near Chicago, manager Mel Bakersfeld (Lancaster) struggles to keep the airport open during a crippling snowstorm while fighting plans to close it and fending off a stalled airliner blocking a runway. His brother-in-law, pilot Vernon Demerest (Martin), is at odds with him over airport policy and is conducting an evaluation flight to Rome — unaware that passenger D.O. Guerrero (Heflin), ruined and despairing, has boarded with a bomb in an attaché case, intending to destroy the aircraft so his wife can collect on a flight insurance policy he has just purchased. When the bomb detonates, the damaged Boeing 707 must turn back and land on the very runway Bakersfeld has been struggling to clear. Running storylines include a resourceful elderly stowaway, Ada Quonsett (Hayes), and the captain's relationship with a pregnant stewardess (Bisset).

Production and reception

The film was shot largely at Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport, standing in for the fictional Chicago-area airport, with a Boeing 707 leased from Flying Tiger Line. It was one of the last films shot in the 70 mm Todd-AO process and featured the final score of veteran composer Alfred Newman. Made for about $10 million, it grossed over $100 million worldwide, becoming one of the biggest hits of 1970.

Critics were divided, but the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences rewarded it with ten Oscar nominations; Helen Hayes won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her performance as the stowaway. Together with the success of the novel, the film is credited with popularising — indeed, effectively creating — the all-star disaster film genre that dominated 1970s popular cinema.

Sequels and legacy

Universal followed it with three sequels: Airport 1975 (1974), Airport '77 (1977), and The Concorde... Airport '79 (1979), with George Kennedy's character Joe Patroni appearing in all four films. The series was later affectionately parodied by the comedy Airplane! (1980), which borrowed its plot structure and melodramatic style.
