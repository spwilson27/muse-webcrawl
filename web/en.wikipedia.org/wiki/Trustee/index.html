Trustee

Trustee
Area of law:Trust law, equity
Counterparties:Settlor, beneficiary
Key duties:Loyalty, prudence, account

A trustee is a person or firm that holds and administers property or assets for the benefit of a third party under the terms of a trust. The trustee holds legal title to the trust property but must manage it solely for the beneficiaries, who hold the equitable (beneficial) interest. Trustees may be individuals, or professional or corporate fiduciaries such as banks and trust companies.

Appointment and types

A trustee is appointed by the settlor in the trust instrument, or by a court where necessary. Related offices include bankruptcy trustees, who administer insolvent estates for creditors; charitable trustees, who govern charities and sit on boards of universities, museums and foundations; and pension trustees, who oversee retirement scheme assets. A trustee in bankruptcy in the United States is appointed under the Bankruptcy Code to marshal and liquidate or reorganise a debtor's estate.

Duties

Trustees are fiduciaries subject to demanding duties: the duty of loyalty (no conflicts of interest or unauthorised profit), the duty to act prudently in investing trust assets, the duty to keep and render accounts, the duty to act impartially among beneficiaries (subject to the trust's terms), and the duty to obey the trust instrument and the law. Breach of trust may result in personal liability, removal, and orders to restore losses to the trust fund. Trustees are usually entitled to reimbursement of properly incurred expenses and, if the deed provides, to remuneration.
