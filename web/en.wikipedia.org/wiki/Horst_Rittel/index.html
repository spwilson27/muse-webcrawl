Horst Wilhelm Jakob Rittel (14 July 1930 – 9 July 1990) was a German-born design theorist and university professor, best known for coining the concept of wicked problems and for developing the Issue-Based Information System (IBIS) approach to structuring design and planning deliberations.

Career

Rittel studied mathematics and theoretical physics in Hamburg, then taught at the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm), a successor to the Bauhaus, where he headed the methodology group. From 1963 he was a professor at the University of California, Berkeley, in the College of Environmental Design, holding appointments in architecture and in city and regional planning. In 1973 he returned part-time to Germany, founding an institute for design methodology at the University of Stuttgart while continuing at Berkeley.

Wicked problems

In the 1973 paper "Dilemmas in a General Theory of Planning", written with Melvin M. Webber, Rittel argued that problems of social policy cannot be definitively described, have no stopping rule, and admit no true-or-false solutions — only better or worse ones. Such "wicked problems" contrast with the "tame" problems of science and engineering, and the concept has been widely adopted in design, planning, public policy, and software development.

IBIS and argumentation

Rittel proposed that design is fundamentally an argumentative process in which participants raise and debate questions, positions, and arguments. His IBIS notation — issues, positions, arguments — underlies later argument-mapping and design-rationale tools such as gIBIS and the Compendium approach.
