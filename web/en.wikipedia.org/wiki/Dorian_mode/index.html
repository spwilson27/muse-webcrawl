Dorian mode

The Dorian mode (or Dorian scale) is a musical scale, or mode, that can be represented as the white keys of the piano from D to the D an octave higher, or equivalently as a natural minor scale with a raised sixth degree. Its interval pattern is whole–half–whole–whole–whole–half–whole.


The D Dorian scale: D E F G A B C D.

Characteristics

Because it differs from the natural minor (Aeolian) scale only by its raised sixth, Dorian is often described as a minor scale with a brighter, more hopeful colour. The raised sixth gives Dorian harmony its characteristic major IV chord in an otherwise minor key.

Usage

The mode takes its name from the Dorian Greeks, though the medieval church mode called Dorian corresponds only loosely to ancient Greek practice. Dorian is common in folk music, Gregorian chant, modal jazz (for example Miles Davis's "So What", built on D and E♭ Dorian chords), and rock songs such as "Scarborough Fair", "Billie Jean" (verse), and "Eleanor Rigby".
