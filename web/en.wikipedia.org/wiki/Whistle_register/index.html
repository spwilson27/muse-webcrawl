The whistle register (also called the flageolet register) is the highest register of the human voice, lying above the modal register and falsetto/head register. It produces flute-like pitches — in trained singers beginning around the sixth octave (C6) and extending, in exceptional cases, above C7. The register is used most often by coloratura sopranos and by popular singers as an effect.

Physiology

In whistle phonation only a small rear portion of the vocal folds vibrates, or, on some accounts, the folds cease to vibrate fully and turbulence in the airstream generates the pitch, whence the name. Laryngoscopic studies show the folds shortened, tensed, and adducted with a small triangular opening. Because the mechanism is incompletely understood and difficult to teach systematically, pedagogues disagree on whether all voices can access it, though children and women produce it most readily.

Use in music

Classical coloratura repertoire occasionally reaches into the register (the Queen of the Night's high F in The Magic Flute lies near its lower boundary). In popular music the register became famous through singers such as Yma Sumac, Minnie Riperton ("Lovin' You", 1975), and Mariah Carey, whose whistle notes became a signature of 1990s pop and R&B, and it is used extensively by contemporary artists such as Ariana Grande.
