The Mother of All Demos

DateDecember 9, 1968
VenueCivic Auditorium, San Francisco
PresenterDouglas Engelbart
OrganizationAugmentation Research Center, SRI International

"The Mother of All Demos" is a name given retroactively to a landmark computer demonstration, live computing's most famous demo, presented at the ACM/IEEE Fall Joint Computer Conference in San Francisco on December 9, 1968, by Douglas Engelbart and his team at the Augmentation Research Center (ARC) of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI).

The demonstration

In a 90-minute live presentation before about 1,000 computer professionals, Engelbart publicly demonstrated the oN-Line System (NLS) for the first time. Features shown included the computer mouse, video conferencing, teleconferencing, hypertext, word processing, hypermedia, object addressing and dynamic file linking, revision control, and a collaborative real-time editor. Engelbart sat on stage wearing a headset, manipulating a console in Menlo Park some 30 miles away through a custom microwave link, while a large video projector displayed the shared screens to the audience.

Significance

The demonstration presented, in embryonic form, many of the elements of modern personal computing — windows, hypertext, graphics, and video communication — decades before they became commonplace. Journalist Steven Levy later gave it the name "the Mother of All Demos," and Andries van Dam called it "the most significant computer demonstration" he had ever seen. Engelbart's vision of augmenting human intellect influenced the researchers at Xerox PARC who developed the Alto and the graphical user interface, which in turn shaped the Apple Macintosh and Microsoft Windows. NLS was the second system ever connected to ARPANET in October 1969.

Legacy

Engelbart received the National Medal of Technology in 2000 for his work. On the demo's 30th and 40th anniversaries, commemorations were held, and the original film of the demo is preserved and widely viewed as a foundational document of computing history.
