The Hong Kong Jockey Club (HKJC; Chinese: 香港賽馬會) is a non-profit organisation founded in 1884 that holds a government-granted monopoly on horse racing betting, football betting and the Mark Six lottery in Hong Kong. It is one of the territory's largest employers and taxpayers, and one of the world's largest single charitable donors through the Hong Kong Jockey Club Charities Trust.

History

Horse racing was introduced to Hong Kong by the British in the 1840s, and the Jockey Club was founded in 1884 to organise the sport; it became the Royal Hong Kong Jockey Club in 1959, reverting to its present name after the 1997 handover. Racing was long centred on Happy Valley Racecourse; the much larger Sha Tin Racecourse opened in 1978. The club was authorised to run the Mark Six lottery in 1975 and football betting in 2003.

Operations

The club conducts about 88 race meetings a season at Happy Valley and Sha Tin, including major international races such as the Hong Kong International Races in December, and operates off-course betting branches and telephone and online betting. It trains horses at the Conghua Racecourse in Guangdong, opened in 2018. Betting duty paid by the club makes it Hong Kong's largest single taxpayer, and its Charities Trust donates several billion Hong Kong dollars annually to community projects in education, medical services, sport and the arts; the club also built and funded major venues including the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology's early development and the Hong Kong Academy for Performing Arts.
