East Riding of Yorkshire

The East Riding of Yorkshire, often abbreviated to the East Riding, is a ceremonial county and unitary authority area in Yorkshire and the Humber, England. It is named after the historic East Riding, one of the three ridings (subdivisions) of the historic county of Yorkshire.

The unitary authority's administrative centre is Beverley, a historic market town with a famous minster. The largest settlement in the ceremonial county is the city of Kingston upon Hull, which is a separate unitary authority. Other towns include Bridlington, Driffield, Hornsea, and Goole, an inland port on the River Ouse.

Geography

The landscape includes the chalk hills of the Yorkshire Wolds, the low-lying marshy Holderness plain between the Wolds and the North Sea, and the flat Vale of York. The coast features the chalk headland of Flamborough Head and the rapidly eroding cliffs of Holderness, as well as Spurn Head, a narrow spit at the mouth of the Humber Estuary.
