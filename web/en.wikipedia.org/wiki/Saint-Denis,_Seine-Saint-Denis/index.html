Saint-Denis is a commune in the northern suburbs of Paris, France, in the department of Seine-Saint-Denis, of which it is a subprefecture. It lies about 9 kilometres north of the centre of Paris. The city is famous for the Basilica of Saint-Denis, the necropolis of French kings, and for the Stade de France, the country's national stadium.

History

Saint-Denis takes its name from Saint Denis, the first bishop of Paris and patron saint of France, martyred in the 3rd century. According to legend he carried his severed head from Montmartre to the site of the present basilica. A church was built over his tomb, and under King Dagobert I in the 7th century it became a royal abbey and the burial place of French monarchs — nearly every king from the 10th century to 1789 is buried there. The basilica, rebuilt under Abbot Suger in the 1140s, is regarded as the first great building of Gothic architecture.

During the French Revolution the town was renamed Franciade and the royal tombs were desecrated. In the 19th and 20th centuries Saint-Denis became a heavily industrialised working-class suburb and a stronghold of the French Communist Party. On 1 January 2025, Saint-Denis merged with neighbouring Pierrefitte-sur-Seine to form an enlarged commune.

Sport

The Stade de France, built for the 1998 FIFA World Cup (won by France at the stadium), has a capacity of about 81,000 and hosts the French national football and rugby teams, as well as major athletics events. Saint-Denis was a principal venue for the Paris 2024 Olympic Games, hosting the Olympic Aquatics Centre and forming part of the Olympic Village.

Transport

The commune is served by Paris Métro lines 12 and 13, RER lines B and D, tramways, and the future Grand Paris Express lines 16 and 17 at Saint-Denis–Pleyel, a major new interchange station.
