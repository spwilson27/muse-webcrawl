Multi-armed bandit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The problem is named for a gambler choosing among slot machines ("one-armed bandits").

The multi-armed bandit problem is a classic problem in probability theory, statistics, and machine learning that models the trade-off between exploration and exploitation. A gambler must choose repeatedly among K slot machines ("one-armed bandits"), each with an unknown reward distribution, and aims to maximize total reward. Playing an arm reveals information about its payoff (exploration) but may yield less than the currently best-looking arm (exploitation). The problem was formalized by Herbert Robbins in 1952 and has become a foundational model for sequential decision-making under uncertainty.

Formulation

In the stochastic bandit, each arm i yields rewards drawn independently from a fixed unknown distribution with mean μi. A strategy's performance is measured by its regret: the expected difference between the reward obtainable by always playing the best arm and the reward the strategy actually accumulates. Good strategies achieve regret growing only logarithmically with the number of rounds, which is provably optimal.

Algorithms

Well-known strategies include ε-greedy (play the empirical best arm except for a small random fraction of explorations), softmax/Boltzmann exploration, upper confidence bound methods (UCB1 and its descendants, which play the arm with the highest optimistic estimate), and Thompson sampling, a Bayesian method dating to 1933 that samples each arm's mean from its posterior distribution. Gittins indices give an optimal solution for the discounted infinite-horizon version of the problem.

Variants and applications

Important variants include contextual bandits (each round presents side information, as in personalized recommendation), adversarial bandits (rewards chosen by an adversary, addressed by the EXP3 algorithm), restless bandits, and bandits with many or infinitely many arms. Applications range from clinical trial design and A/B testing of websites to online advertising, recommender systems, dynamic pricing, and hyperparameter tuning.
