François Édouard Anatole Lucas (4 April 1842 – 3 October 1891) was a French mathematician best known for his study of the Fibonacci sequence and the related Lucas sequences, and for devising methods of testing whether Mersenne numbers are prime.

Work

Lucas studied generalisations of the Fibonacci sequence, the families now called Lucas sequences Un(P,Q) and Vn(P,Q), and identified the sequence of Lucas numbers. In 1876 he proved by hand that the Mersenne number 2127 − 1 is prime — still the largest prime ever verified without computers — using a primality test later refined into the Lucas–Lehmer test, which remains fundamental to the search for large primes. He also worked on symbolic logic in arithmetic and discovered a neat test for Fibonacci divisibility properties.

Recreational mathematics

Lucas popularised the Tower of Hanoi puzzle, which he introduced in 1883 under the anagrammatic pseudonym "N. Claus de Siam", and invented or described other recreations, including the Dots and Boxes game (la pipopipette). His multi-volume Récréations mathématiques became a classic of the genre.

Death

Lucas died in unusual circumstances in 1891, days after a dropped plate at a banquet cut his cheek; the wound developed into erysipelas and he died within days, aged 49.
