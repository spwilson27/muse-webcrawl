Canadian Multiculturalism Act

The Canadian Multiculturalism Act (French: Loi sur le multiculturalisme canadien) is a federal statute of Canada, assented to in 1988, that gives legal expression to the government's policy of multiculturalism. Canada was the first country in the world to enact national multiculturalism legislation.

Background

Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau announced a policy of multiculturalism within a bilingual framework on October 8, 1971, responding to the report of the Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism. The policy recognized that, while Canada had two official languages, no single culture held official status. The Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms later directed, in section 27, that the Charter be interpreted in a manner consistent with the preservation and enhancement of the multicultural heritage of Canadians.

Provisions

The Act declares it to be government policy to recognize and promote the understanding that multiculturalism reflects the cultural and racial diversity of Canadian society and acknowledges the freedom of all members of Canadian society to preserve, enhance, and share their cultural heritage. It directs federal institutions to provide equal employment opportunities, enhance the ability of individuals and communities of all origins to contribute to Canada, and promote interaction among communities of different origins.

The Act also commits the federal government to facilitate the acquisition, retention, and use of all languages that contribute to the cultural diversity of Canada, while strengthening the status and use of the two official languages.

Implementation and debate

The Act is administered by the Minister of Canadian Heritage, who must table an annual report on its operation. While broadly supported, the legislation has been periodically debated, including over funding for ethnocultural organizations and, in Quebec, over the relationship between federal multiculturalism and Quebec's own policy of interculturalism.
