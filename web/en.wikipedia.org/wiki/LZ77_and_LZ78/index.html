LZ77 and LZ78

LZ77 and LZ78 are the two lossless data compression algorithms published in papers by Abraham Lempel and Jacob Ziv in 1977 and 1978. They are also known as LZ1 and LZ2 respectively. These two algorithms form the basis for many variations including LZW, LZSS, LZMA, and others. Besides their academic influence, these algorithms formed the basis of several ubiquitous compression schemes, including GIF and the DEFLATE algorithm used in PNG and ZIP.


LZ77's sliding-window principle

LZ77

LZ77 is a dictionary coder that replaces repeated occurrences of data with references to an earlier copy within a sliding window of already-encoded text. Each output is a triple (offset, length, next character): copy length bytes starting offset bytes back, then output the given literal. Variants of LZ77 dominate practical compression: LZSS substitutes a flag to distinguish literals from references, and DEFLATE (used in ZIP, gzip, and PNG) combines LZ77 with Huffman coding.

LZ78

LZ78 instead builds an explicit dictionary of phrases encountered in the input, outputting (dictionary index, new character) pairs. Its best-known descendant is LZW (Lempel–Ziv–Welch, 1984), which powers the GIF image format and the historical Unix compress utility; the LZW patent held by Unisys until 2003–2004 famously constrained GIF software. Other relatives include LZAP and the Lempel–Ziv parsing schemes underlying LZMA.
