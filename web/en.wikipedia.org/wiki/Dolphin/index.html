Dolphin

Bottlenose dolphins are strong, social swimmers.

Dolphins are marine mammals of the families Delphinidae (oceanic dolphins, ~40 species) and the river dolphin groups. Intelligent, social and acrobatic, they are among the best-known ocean animals. The bottlenose dolphin is the most studied species.

Biology

Dolphins are toothed whales (odontocetes) that hunt fish and squid using echolocation — clicks focused by the fatty melon, returning echoes reveal prey in darkness. They breathe voluntarily through a blowhole and sleep with one brain hemisphere at a time. Pods have complex social lives: alliances, signature whistles functioning like names, cooperative herding of fish, and cultural transmission of tool use (sponging in Shark Bay, Australia).

Intelligence and conservation

Dolphins rank high in studies of Animal intelligence: mirror self-recognition, understanding of symbols and pointing, and problem-solving. Threats include bycatch in fishing nets, captivity controversies, noise and pollution; several river dolphins are critically endangered, and the baiji is considered extinct since the early 2000s.
