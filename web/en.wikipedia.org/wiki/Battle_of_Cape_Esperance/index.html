Battle of Cape Esperance

The Battle of Cape Esperance, also known as the Second Battle of Savo Island in Japanese records, was a naval engagement fought on the night of 11–12 October 1942 between the Imperial Japanese Navy and the United States Navy near Cape Esperance on Guadalcanal, in the Solomon Islands. It was one of the series of night surface actions fought over control of the waters around Guadalcanal during the Pacific War.

Night action off Cape Esperance, October 1942.

The battle

A US task force of four cruisers and five destroyers under Rear Admiral Norman Scott intercepted a Japanese bombardment group of three heavy cruisers and two destroyers under Rear Admiral Aritomo Gotō, which was en route to shell Henderson Field, while a separate Japanese group landed reinforcements. Scott's ships, using radar, crossed the Japanese T shortly before midnight. In the confused night action, the Japanese cruiser Furutaka and destroyer Fubuki were sunk and the cruiser Aoba badly damaged; Gotō was mortally wounded. The Americans lost the destroyer Duncan, and the cruiser Boise was heavily damaged. Friendly fire incidents struck both sides in the darkness.

Significance

Cape Esperance was the US Navy's first clear victory in a night surface action against the Japanese, achieved through radar-directed gunnery and Scott's battle plan. However, the Japanese reinforcement convoy completed its mission, and two nights later Japanese battleships bombarded Henderson Field almost unopposed, so the battle's strategic effect was limited.
