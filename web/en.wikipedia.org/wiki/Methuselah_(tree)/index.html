Methuselah is a 4,850-year-old Great Basin bristlecone pine (Pinus longaeva) tree growing high in the White Mountains of Inyo County in eastern California. It is regarded as one of the oldest known non-clonal living organisms on Earth. Methuselah's exact location within the Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest is kept secret by the United States Forest Service to protect it from vandalism.

Discovery and dating

Methuselah was dated in 1957 by Edmund Schulman and Tom L. Harlan of the Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research at the University of Arizona, who determined from increment core samples that the tree germinated around 2833 BC. Schulman named it after Methuselah, the biblical figure said to have lived 969 years.

Setting

The tree grows at roughly 2,900–3,000 m (9,500–9,800 ft) elevation in dolomitic soil with little rainfall — conditions that produce slow growth, dense decay-resistant wood, and the gnarled, mostly-barkless appearance typical of ancient bristlecones. Only a thin ribbon of living bark sustains the tree.

Other ancient trees

Methuselah was long considered the oldest living non-clonal organism. A neighbouring bristlecone, confirmed by Tom Harlan shortly before his death, is reportedly older still (over 5,000 years), though the claim has not been independently published. In 2012 a core known as WPN-114 was reported to date a bristlecone to more than 5,060 years, which would surpass Methuselah if confirmed.
