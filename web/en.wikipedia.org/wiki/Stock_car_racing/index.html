Stock car racing

Stock car racing is a form of automobile racing run principally on oval tracks, featuring cars that originally resembled ordinary production ("stock") automobiles. It is most strongly associated with the United States and with NASCAR, whose top-level Cup Series is one of the country's most popular spectator sports, but stock car racing is also held in Canada, Mexico, Brazil, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand and elsewhere.

History

The sport's roots lie in the Prohibition era, when bootleggers in the American South modified cars to outrun revenue agents and began racing each other informally. Organised races on beaches and dirt tracks grew through the 1930s and 1940s. In December 1947 Bill France Sr. gathered promoters and drivers in Daytona Beach, Florida, to found NASCAR (the National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing), imposing standard rules; the first "Strictly Stock" season followed in 1949. The opening of Daytona International Speedway (1959) and other superspeedways, full live television coverage of the 1979 Daytona 500, and the rise of stars such as Richard Petty, Dale Earnhardt and Jeff Gordon carried the sport to national prominence.

The cars

Modern top-level stock cars are purpose-built racing machines: a tube-frame chassis wearing a steel and composite body that echoes a showroom model, powered by a pushrod V8 engine of about 358 cubic inches producing roughly 670–750 horsepower. Because the cars are closely matched, races feature large packs, frequent overtaking, drafting at superspeedways and heavy contact. Safety advances — SAFER barriers, roof flaps, head-and-neck restraints and the "Car of Tomorrow" redesign of 2007, followed by the Next Gen car of 2022 — followed fatal accidents, most notably that of Dale Earnhardt in 2001.

Forms and series

Below NASCAR's three national series (Cup, Xfinity and Trucks) sits a vast pyramid of regional and local racing: late models, modifieds and street stocks on short paved and dirt ovals, where most drivers begin. Internationally, series such as the NASCAR Euro Series, the Brazilian Stock Car Pro Series and Australia's former AUSCAR adapt the formula to local conditions and road courses.
