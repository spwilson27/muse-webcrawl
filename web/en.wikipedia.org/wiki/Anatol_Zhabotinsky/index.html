Anatol Zhabotinsky, Soviet and American biophysicist.

Anatol Markovich Zhabotinsky (Russian: Анатолий Маркович Жаботинский; 17 January 1938 – 16 September 2008) was a Soviet and American biophysicist known for his work on oscillating chemical reactions. He is one of the two scientists after whom the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction is named.

Life and work

Zhabotinsky was born in Moscow and studied physics at Moscow State University. In the 1960s, working in the laboratory of Simon Shnoll, he took up the oscillating reaction first reported by Boris Belousov, whose findings had been largely dismissed by the Soviet chemical establishment. Zhabotinsky systematically investigated the reaction, identified its key components — a bromate oxidizer, malonic acid, and a metal-ion catalyst such as cerium or ferroin — and developed a quantitative description of its behaviour. His work transformed the Belousov reaction from a curiosity into a cornerstone of nonlinear chemical dynamics and the study of self-organization.

In 1991 Zhabotinsky emigrated to the United States and joined Brandeis University, where he continued research on chemical waves, pattern formation, and excitable media, including work related to cardiac dynamics. He received the Lenin Prize in 1980, together with Belousov and others, for the discovery and investigation of the reaction.
