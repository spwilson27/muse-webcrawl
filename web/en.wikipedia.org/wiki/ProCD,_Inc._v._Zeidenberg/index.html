ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg

ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg, 86 F.3d 1447 (7th Cir. 1996), is a United States Court of Appeals decision holding that shrinkwrap licences — terms presented inside or on software packaging, accepted by opening or using the product — can be enforceable contracts. The opinion was written by Judge Frank Easterbrook.

Background

ProCD sold SelectPhone, a CD-ROM database of telephone listings, at a lower price to consumers than to commercial users; its licence restricted use to non-commercial purposes. Matthew Zeidenberg bought a consumer copy, formed a company, and resold the data online through his Silken Mountain Products website. ProCD sued for breach of the licence and copyright infringement; the district court held the shrinkwrap licence unenforceable.

Holding

The Seventh Circuit reversed. Applying the Uniform Commercial Code, Easterbrook reasoned that a buyer accepts terms by keeping and using the software after having a chance to read the licence and return the product; contracts formed in this "pay now, terms later" manner are valid, as with insurance policies and airline tickets. The court also held the licence claim was not preempted by federal copyright law because a contract binds only its parties. The decision is a leading authority on standard-form software licensing and has been widely discussed, both followed and criticised, in later cases.
