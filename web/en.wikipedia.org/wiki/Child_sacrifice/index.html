Child sacrifice is the ritualistic killing of children in order to please or appease a deity, supernatural beings, or sacred social order, as part of human sacrifice practices attested in various cultures in antiquity and, in a small number of documented cases, in more recent times. The victims were typically considered especially pure or valuable offerings, and the rites were often performed to avert disasters such as drought, famine, or defeat in war.

Ancient Near East and the Mediterranean

The Hebrew Bible repeatedly condemns the sacrifice of children to the god Moloch in the Valley of Hinnom (Gehenna) outside Jerusalem, and the binding of Isaac is interpreted by scholars as a narrative marking a rejection of child sacrifice. Greco-Roman authors accused the Carthaginians of sacrificing infants to Baal Hammon and Tanit at sacred precincts called tophets, where urns containing cremated infant remains have been excavated; the scale and interpretation of the Carthage tophet remain debated among archaeologists — whether it was a sacrificial site or a special infant cemetery.

The Americas

In Mesoamerica, the Aztecs sacrificed children to the rain god Tlaloc, whose cult held that children's tears ensured rain; remains of dozens of children were excavated at the Great Temple of Tenochtitlan. The Maya also performed child sacrifice, including at the Sacred Cenote of Chichén Itzá. In the Andes, the Inca performed the capacocha rite, in which carefully chosen children were sacrificed at shrines on high mountain summits; the remarkably preserved frozen mummies of three children found in 1999 at the summit of Llullaillaco volcano in Argentina are the most famous evidence of the practice. The Moche of Peru also practised sacrifice including of adolescents.

Later reports and modern crimes

Sporadic reports of sacrificial killings of children have occurred in the colonial and modern eras, often sensationalised or as part of moral panics; verified cases are rare, criminal acts rather than institutional rites. The topic has also been distorted by blood libel accusations against religious minorities, which are baseless fabrications.
