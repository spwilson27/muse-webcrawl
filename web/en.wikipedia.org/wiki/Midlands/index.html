Midlands

The Midlands
Country: England
Regions: West Midlands, East Midlands
Largest city: Birmingham
Historic kingdom: Mercia

The Midlands is the central part of England, bordered by Northern England to the north, Southern England to the south, Wales to the west and the North Sea to the east. It broadly corresponds to the statistical regions of the West Midlands and the East Midlands, together encompassing counties such as Staffordshire, Warwickshire, Worcestershire, Derbyshire, Nottinghamshire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire and Shropshire.

History

The area corresponds roughly to the early medieval kingdom of Mercia. The Midlands lay at the heart of the Industrial Revolution: Birmingham became a great manufacturing centre known as the "workshop of the world", the Staffordshire Potteries dominated ceramics, and the region's coalfields, canals and later motorways made it a transport hub. The phrase "the Black Country" refers to the industrial districts west of Birmingham blackened by coal smoke.

Geography and cities

Major cities include Birmingham (England's second-largest city), Coventry, Leicester, Nottingham, Derby, Lincoln, Stoke-on-Trent and Wolverhampton. The terrain is mostly lowland, drained by the Trent and Severn river systems, with uplands in the Peak District in the north. The Midlands is sometimes described as the dividing line between the cultures and accents of northern and southern England.
