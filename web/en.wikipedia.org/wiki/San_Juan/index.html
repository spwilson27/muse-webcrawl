Overview

San Juan is the capital and most populous municipality of Puerto Rico, on the island's northern coast. Founded in 1521 by Spanish settlers under Juan Ponce de León, it is among the oldest European-founded cities in the Americas; the settlement was moved from nearby Caparra to the defensible islet at the harbour mouth where Old San Juan now stands.

Spain fortified the port with the castles of San Felipe del Morro and San Cristóbal and a city wall, and San Juan repelled attacks by Drake in 1595 and the Dutch in 1625. The old city, with its blue cobblestone streets and pastel colonial buildings, is a US National Historic Site and UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Modern city

Greater San Juan — including Bayamón, Carolina and Guaynabo — is Puerto Rico's economic centre, with the Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport, the Condado and Isla Verde hotel districts, and the Plaza Las Américas shopping centre, among the largest in the Caribbean.
