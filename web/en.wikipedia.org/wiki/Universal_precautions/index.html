Universal precautions

FieldInfection control
Introduced1985–1988 (CDC)
SuccessorStandard precautions (1996)

Universal precautions are a set of infection-control practices introduced by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in the mid-1980s, largely in response to the HIV/AIDS epidemic. They require healthcare workers to treat all human blood and certain body fluids as if they were known to be infectious for HIV, hepatitis B, and other bloodborne pathogens.

Practices

Universal precautions call for the use of barrier protection such as gloves, gowns, masks, and eye protection when exposure to blood or body fluids is anticipated, careful handling and disposal of needles and other sharps, and hand hygiene after glove removal.

Standard precautions

In 1996 the CDC merged universal precautions with body-substance isolation into the broader framework of standard precautions, which apply to all patients regardless of suspected infection status and extend protection to all body fluids, secretions, and excretions except sweat, as well as non-intact skin and mucous membranes.
