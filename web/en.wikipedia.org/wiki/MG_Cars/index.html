MG Cars

MG is a British automotive marque founded by Cecil Kimber in the 1920s, best known for its open two-seater sports cars. The initials derive from Morris Garages, the Oxford dealership of William Morris that began modifying and selling tuned Morris cars. The octagonal MG badge became one of the most recognisable in motoring. Today the name is owned by the Chinese state-owned automaker SAIC Motor, which sells a range of cars, including electric models, under the MG brand through MG Motor.

History

Morris Garages began producing its own modified cars in the early 1920s under manager Cecil Kimber, whose sporting specials created the MG identity. The Abingdon factory, opened in 1929, became synonymous with the marque. Pre-war cars such as the MG Midget and the record-breaking EX-series speed cars built MG's reputation, and after 1945 the MG TC introduced American servicemen to the British sports car, helping launch the sports car boom in the United States. The MGA (1955), the Midget (1961) and above all the MGB (1962–1980) — of which more than 500,000 were built — defined the classic MG.

MG passed through a succession of British corporate owners: the Nuffield Organisation, BMC, British Leyland and the Rover Group. The Abingdon factory closed in 1980. The badge was revived on sporting Metro, Maestro and Montego models in the 1980s, and on the mid-engined MG F roadster in 1995 under BMW ownership. MG Rover collapsed in 2005; the assets were bought by China's Nanjing Automobile, which was itself absorbed into SAIC. Production restarted at Longbridge in the UK and expanded in China, Thailand and India.

Modern MG

Under SAIC, MG has been repositioned as a value-oriented mainstream brand. Models such as the MG3 hatchback, the ZS and HS SUVs and especially the electric MG4 EV (2022) — praised for its handling and pricing — have made MG one of the fastest-growing car brands in Britain and Europe, recapturing a significant market share. The Cyberster electric roadster (2024) was launched as a deliberate echo of the classic MG roadsters for the brand's centenary.

Motorsport

MG has a long competition history, from 1930s land-speed records at Brooklands and Utah to modern touring car racing, including the British Touring Car Championship with the MG6 in the 2010s.
