Chinese orchestra

A Chinese orchestra (Chinese: 華樂團 / 中乐团) is a large ensemble of traditional Chinese musical instruments, modelled on the Western symphony orchestra. It typically comprises four sections — bowed strings, plucked strings, winds, and percussion — and performs both traditional repertoire and newly composed works.

Instrumentation

The bowed-string section is led by the erhu family (erhu, gaohu, zhonghu) supplemented by cellos and double basses adapted from the Western orchestra. Plucked strings include the pipa, liuqin, ruan, guzheng, and yangqin. Winds feature the dizi, xiao, sheng, and suona, while percussion ranges from dagu drums to bianzhong-style bells and gongs.

History

The modern Chinese orchestra emerged in the 1920s–1950s, when musicians such as Liu Tianhua reformed traditional instruments and ensemble practice along Western lines. Professional ensembles were established in Shanghai, Beijing, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Taipei, and the form remains central to Chinese musical life and education.

Repertoire

Standard works include "Dance of the Yao People," "Moon Reflected on Second Spring" (arranged from the erhu solo of Abing), and "Awa Mountain" suites, alongside folk-song arrangements and contemporary compositions.
