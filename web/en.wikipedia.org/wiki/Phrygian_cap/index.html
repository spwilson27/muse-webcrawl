Phrygian cap

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A Phrygian cap.

The Phrygian cap is a soft conical cap with the apex bent over, associated in antiquity with several peoples of Anatolia, including the Phrygians. In the Western imagination it came, by a long iconographic tradition, to signify freedom and the pursuit of liberty.

History

In Greek and Roman art, the cap marked figures from the east: Trojans such as Paris, the god Mithras, and Amazons wore it. In Rome the felt cap called a pileus was worn by freed slaves, so a similar cap became a symbol of emancipation. The two forms — pileus and Phrygian cap — merged in later depictions.

The cap figured prominently in the iconography of the American and French revolutions. It crowns Liberty in Eugène Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People, is worn by Marianne, the personification of the French Republic, and appears on numerous coats of arms in Latin America and on the seal of the United States Senate. During the French Revolution the red cap, the bonnet rouge, was the emblem of the sans-culottes.

In popular culture

The cap is worn by the Smurfs in their distinctive white form, and a Phrygian cap named Phryge served as the mascot of the Paris 2024 Olympic and Paralympic Games.
