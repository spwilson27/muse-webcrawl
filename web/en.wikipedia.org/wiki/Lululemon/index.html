Lululemon Athletica Inc., commonly known simply as lululemon, is a Canadian-American multinational athletic apparel retailer headquartered in Vancouver, British Columbia. Founded in 1998 by Chip Wilson as a design studio by day and yoga studio by night, it became a standalone store in 2000 and grew into one of the world's largest athletic apparel companies, credited with popularising the "athleisure" trend.

History

Wilson opened the first store in Vancouver's Kitsilano neighbourhood in 2000, combining retail space with community yoga classes. The company's yoga pants made from proprietary fabrics such as Luon, and later Nulu and Align materials, became its signature products. Lululemon went public on NASDAQ in 2007. It expanded internationally across the United States, Asia, Europe, and Australia, and broadened beyond women's yoga apparel into men's apparel, running, training, footwear, and accessories.

In 2020 the company acquired the at-home fitness company Mirror for about US$500 million, later discontinuing the hardware. In 2024, long-serving CEO Calvin McDonald, who had led the company since 2018, oversaw continued growth; in late 2025 the company announced McDonald would step down at the end of January 2026.

Products and marketing

Best known products include Align and Wunder Train leggings, the Define jacket, ABC men's trousers, and the Everywhere Belt Bag. Rather than traditional advertising, lululemon built its brand through local store "ambassadors" (yoga instructors and fitness professionals), free in-store classes and community events, and premium pricing — a model that cultivated a loyal customer base.

Controversies

The company recalled its signature yoga pants in 2013 after customers found them too sheer, an episode that cost an estimated $67 million in revenue; founder Chip Wilson drew criticism for comments about the recall and resigned as chairman. Lululemon has also faced scrutiny over labour practices in its supply chain and disputes over the origin of the company name.
