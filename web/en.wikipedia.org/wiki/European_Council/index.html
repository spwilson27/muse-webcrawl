European Council

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Council is a collective body (not to be confused with the Council of the European Union, the ministerial legislature, or the Council of Europe, an unrelated organisation) that comprises the heads of state or government of the EU member states, along with the President of the European Council and the President of the European Commission. It defines the European Union's overall political direction and priorities, but does not pass laws.

Role

Meeting at least four times a year (the Brussels "summits"), the European Council takes the Union's hardest decisions: treaty change, enlargement, multi-year budgets, foreign-policy lines, and crisis management (the euro crisis, migration, pandemic recovery fund, and war in Ukraine all ran through it). It elects its own President (for a renewable two-and-a-half-year term), proposes the Commission President, and appoints the High Representative and the ECB's leadership. Decisions are usually by consensus. Critics describe a drift of power to the European Council ("intergovernmentalism", crisis-driven presidentialism of the big states); defenders see legitimate democratic anchoring through elected national leaders. Its distinction from the Council of Ministers — similar name, different body — is a perennial examination trap in EU studies.
