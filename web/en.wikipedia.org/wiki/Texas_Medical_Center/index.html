Texas Medical Center

Founded: 1945

Location: Houston, Texas, United States

Area: about 2.1 square miles (total campus footprint)

Member institutions: 60+

The Texas Medical Center (TMC) is the largest medical complex in the world, located in south-central Houston, Texas, adjacent to Hermann Park and the Rice University area. Founded in 1945, it comprises more than 60 member institutions — hospitals, medical schools, research institutes, and support organizations — employing well over 100,000 people and receiving millions of patient visits each year.

History

The center grew from a 1943 gift by businessman Monroe Dunaway Anderson, whose M. D. Anderson Foundation purchased the site and funded the first institutions. Baylor College of Medicine relocated from Dallas in 1943, and the M. D. Anderson Hospital (now MD Anderson Cancer Center) was among the founding members. Anchored by institutions such as Houston Methodist Hospital, Texas Children's Hospital, Memorial Hermann–Texas Medical Center, Baylor College of Medicine, and the University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston, the TMC expanded into a city-within-a-city.

Scope

Member institutions perform some of the world's most complex care, including the highest volumes of heart and transplant surgeries in the United States, and the center hosts the world's largest children's hospital and largest cancer hospital. Its TMC Innovation campus and TMC3 collaborative research development extend its role into biotechnology commercialization.
