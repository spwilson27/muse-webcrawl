Aesthetic group gymnastics

Aesthetic group gymnastics (AGG) is a discipline of gymnastics developed from Finnish women's gymnastics. In AGG, a team of gymnasts — typically six to ten — performs a choreographed routine accompanied by music, without any hand apparatus. The sport emphasizes natural, flowing whole-body movement, expressiveness, and unity of the group.

Characteristics

Routines combine body waves, swings, balances, jumps and leaps, dance steps, and lifts, all performed in unison and in continuous flow from one movement to the next. Required body movements include total body waves, swings, bends, twists, contractions, and lunges. Teams are judged on technical value, artistic impression — composition, music, and expression — and execution, including synchronization and uniformity.

History and organization

The sport grew out of the Finnish tradition of women's group gymnastics dating to the early 20th century. The International Federation of Aesthetic Group Gymnastics (IFAGG) was founded in 2003, and World Championships have been held annually since 2000. AGG is especially popular in Finland and Estonia, whose teams have dominated the world championships, with strong programmes also in Russia, Bulgaria, and other countries.
