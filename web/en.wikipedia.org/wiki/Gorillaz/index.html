Gorillaz

Gorillaz are an English virtual band created in 1998 by musician Damon Albarn and artist Jamie Hewlett. The band consists of four fictional members: vocalist and keyboardist 2-D, bassist Murdoc Niccals, drummer Russel Hobbs and guitarist Noodle. Their fictional universe is presented in music videos, interviews, comics and short animations, while their music — a shifting blend of alternative rock, hip hop, electronica and pop — is written and performed by Albarn with a rotating cast of collaborators. Gorillaz are cited by Guinness World Records as the world's "most successful virtual band".

Career

The self-titled debut album Gorillaz (2001) introduced the band with singles "Clint Eastwood" and "19-2000", selling several million copies. The follow-up, Demon Days (2005), produced by Danger Mouse, was a darker and more ambitious work; its single "Feel Good Inc." became the band's biggest hit and won a Grammy Award. Plastic Beach (2010) took environmental themes and orchestral and guest-heavy arrangements, and The Fall (2010) was recorded largely on an iPad during a tour.

After a hiatus, the band returned with Humanz (2017), followed by The Now Now (2018), the collaborative Song Machine, Season One: Strange Timez (2020) and Cracker Island (2023). Live performances have evolved from shows in which the real musicians played behind a screen of animation to full stage productions mixing live performers with their animated personas.

Style and influence

Gorillaz' sound is deliberately genre-fluid, drawing on Britpop, dub, Latin, Afrobeat and contemporary rap, with guest vocalists ranging from De La Soul and Bobby Womack to Lou Reed and Bad Bunny. The project is widely credited with pioneering the modern virtual act and blurring the boundaries between band, art project and multimedia franchise.
