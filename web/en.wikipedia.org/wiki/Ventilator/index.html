Ventilator

A ventilator is a machine that provides mechanical ventilation by moving breathable air into and out of the lungs, to deliver breaths to a patient who is physically unable to breathe, or breathing insufficiently. Modern ventilators are computer-controlled microprocessor devices, though patients can also be ventilated with a simple hand-operated bag valve mask.

Function

Ventilators are used chiefly in intensive care medicine, home care, emergency medicine and anesthesiology. They deliver a set tidal volume or pressure at a controlled rate, often adding positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) to keep alveoli open, and allow adjustment of the fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2).

Modes

Common modes include volume-controlled ventilation, pressure-controlled ventilation, assist-control, synchronized intermittent mandatory ventilation (SIMV) and pressure support ventilation, which supports spontaneous breathing during weaning.

History

Negative-pressure devices such as the iron lung dominated during the polio epidemics of the mid-20th century. The 1952 Copenhagen polio epidemic accelerated the shift to positive-pressure ventilation through tracheostomy, giving rise to modern intensive care units. During the COVID-19 pandemic, ventilator availability became a central concern of health systems worldwide.

Risks

Prolonged ventilation carries risks including ventilator-associated pneumonia, barotrauma and ventilator-induced lung injury; care bundles and daily weaning assessments reduce these complications.
