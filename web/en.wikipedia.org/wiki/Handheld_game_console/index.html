Handheld game console

TypePortable video game console
First modelMicrovision (1979)
Best-sellingNintendo DS family (154 million units)

A handheld game console is a small, portable, self-contained video game console with a built-in display, game controls, speakers, and battery power. Unlike earlier handheld electronic games, which were dedicated to a single game, handheld consoles play interchangeable software, usually distributed on cartridges, optical discs, or by download.

History

The Milton Bradley Microvision (1979) was the first handheld with interchangeable cartridges, but the category was defined by Nintendo's Game Boy (1989), whose long battery life, rugged design, and pack-in game Tetris carried it past colour competitors such as the Sega Game Gear and Atari Lynx to sales of over 118 million units including its Color successor. Nintendo's dominance continued with the Game Boy Advance and the dual-screen, touchscreen Nintendo DS (2004), the best-selling handheld line ever.

Sony entered the market with the PlayStation Portable (2004), which emphasised multimedia and near-console graphics, followed by the PlayStation Vita (2011). The spread of smartphones after 2007 squeezed the dedicated handheld market, but Nintendo's hybrid Nintendo Switch (2017), usable both as a home console and a handheld, sold more than 140 million units, and PC-based handhelds such as Valve's Steam Deck (2022) opened a new segment.

Design

Handhelds balance processing power against battery life, heat, size, and cost. Controls typically combine a directional pad or analogue sticks with face buttons and shoulder triggers; later systems added touchscreens, cameras, motion sensors, and wireless networking for multiplayer and digital distribution. Because they are carried and dropped, durability and repairability are significant design concerns.
