The Saône (French: [soːn]; Arpitan: Sona) is a river in eastern France. At 473 kilometres it is the longest river lying entirely within France that does not reach the sea directly, being the chief right-bank tributary of the Rhône, which it joins at Lyon. Its Latin name was Arar.

Course

The Saône rises at Vioménil at the foot of the Vosges Mountains, at about 405 metres elevation, and flows generally southwest through Haute-Saône, past Gray, Auxonne and Chalon-sur-Saône, then south past Tournus and Mâcon to Lyon, where it converges with the Rhône at the southern tip of the Presqu'île. Its major tributaries include the Doubs, the Lanterne, the Seille and the Azergues. The river is famous for its very gentle gradient — it drops only about 180 metres over its whole course — giving it a characteristically slow, placid current.

Navigation

The Saône is navigable from Corre, north of its source region, to Lyon, and forms part of the grand canal network linking the Rhône and the Mediterranean to the Rhine, Seine and Loire basins via the Canal de l'Est, the Canal du Rhône au Rhin, the Canal du Centre and other connecting waterways. Pleasure boating and barge traffic use the river today, and its broad floodplain, the Val de Saône, is noted for its wetlands and market gardens.
