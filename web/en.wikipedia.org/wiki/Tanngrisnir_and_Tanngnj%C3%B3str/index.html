Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr

MythologyNorse
Names meaning"Teeth grinder" and "teeth gnasher"
RoleGoats that pull Thor's chariot

Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr (Old Norse "teeth-barer, snarler" and "teeth-grinder") are the two goats that pull the chariot of the Norse god Thor in the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson.

Mythology

According to the Prose Edda's Skáldskaparmál, Thor could slaughter and eat the goats each evening and then restore them to life the following morning by blessing their intact bones and skins with his hammer Mjölnir. When the farmer's son Þjálfi cracked one of the leg bones to suck out the marrow, the resurrected goat came up lame, and Þjálfi and his sister Röskva became Thor's servants in compensation.

Interpretations

Scholars have connected the goats with fertility, storms, and the sacrificial feasts of Norse religion, and the motif of animals restored from their bones appears in other folklore traditions.
