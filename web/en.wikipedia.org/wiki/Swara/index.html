Swara

The seven swaras form the basis of the raga system

Swara (Sanskrit: svara) is the concept of a musical note in Indian classical music. Unlike the fixed pitches of Western notation, a swara is a relative scale degree defined within an octave and intoned in relation to a tonic drone. The seven basic swaras are the foundation of both Hindustani and Carnatic music, and are sung with the solfa syllables sa re ga ma pa dha ni.

The seven swaras

The full names are Shadja (Sa), Rishabha (Re), Gandhara (Ga), Madhyama (Ma), Panchama (Pa), Dhaivata (Dha), and Nishada (Ni) — often likened to the Western solfège do re mi fa sol la ti, with the mnemonic derived from the sounds of animals in tradition. Sa and Pa are fixed (prakriti swaras), while the others admit lowered (komal) or raised (tivra) variants, giving twelve pitch positions (swarasthanas) in the octave. The ancient Natya Shastra and later texts such as the Sangita Ratnakara further subdivide the octave into 22 microtonal shrutis.

Role in raga

A raga selects and orders swaras into ascending (aroha) and descending (avaroha) patterns, emphasises a king note (vadi) and its consonant (samvadi), and prescribes characteristic phrases and ornamentation (gamaka) around the notes. Because swaras are relative, the performer chooses a comfortable tonic, usually set against the tanpura drone. Improvisation consists of elaborating raga grammar on this scaffold of swaras.
