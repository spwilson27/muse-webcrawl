Hanjin Group (Korean: 한진 그룹) is a South Korean chaebol (conglomerate) founded in 1945 by Cho Choong-hoon. It grew from a trucking company into one of the world's largest logistics groups, controlling Korean Air and, until its collapse, Hanjin Shipping, once the seventh-largest container shipping line in the world.

History

Hanjin expanded during the Korean War by transporting supplies for the US military, and later built a major presence in Vietnam War logistics. It acquired Korea's national airline in 1969, renaming it Korean Air, and entered container shipping in 1977. The Cho family retained control through cross-shareholdings typical of chaebol structures.

Hanjin Shipping bankruptcy

In August 2016, Hanjin Shipping filed for court receivership after creditors withdrew support, in what was then the largest bankruptcy in shipping history. Dozens of vessels were stranded at sea, ports refused to handle its ships for fear of unpaid fees, and global supply chains were disrupted in the run-up to the holiday season. The company was declared bankrupt by a Seoul court in February 2017; its assets were sold piecemeal to rivals including Maersk Line, Hyundai Merchant Marine and MSC.
