Cameroon line

The Cameroon line (French: Ligne du Cameroun) is a 1,600 km (990 mi) long chain of volcanoes and volcanic highlands running from the Atlantic Ocean island of Annobón in the southwest, through the Gulf of Guinea islands and the Cameroon Highlands, to the border region near Lake Chad in the northeast. The chain includes the islands of Annobón, São Tomé, Príncipe and Bioko, and on the continent Mount Cameroon and the highlands of western Cameroon and eastern Nigeria.

Geology

Unlike most volcanic chains, the Cameroon line shows no simple age progression along its length, and it straddles the boundary between oceanic and continental crust at an angle of about 30 degrees to the coast. Volcanism along the line has been active from about 42 million years ago to the present, and similar-aged rocks occur at both ends of the chain. Proposed explanations include a mantle plume, reactivation of ancient fractures at the margin of the Congo craton, and edge-driven convection beneath the lithosphere; no single hypothesis is universally accepted.

Major features

  • Mount Cameroon (Mount Fako), an active shield volcano about 4,040 m high, is the highest peak in sub-Saharan West and Central Africa.
  • Bioko, Príncipe, São Tomé and Annobón form the oceanic section of the line in the Gulf of Guinea.
  • The Cameroon Highlands include the Bambouto massif, Mount Oku and the Adamawa Plateau.
  • Crater lakes along the line include Lake Nyos and Lake Monoun, both noted for limnic eruptions of dissolved carbon dioxide.

Biodiversity

The montane forests of the Cameroon line are a recognised centre of endemism, with many plant, amphibian and bird species found nowhere else, including on the Gulf of Guinea islands.
