Lake Tanganyika is an African Great Lake in the western arm of the East African Rift. It is the longest freshwater lake in the world (673 km) and the second-largest by volume and second-deepest after Lake Baikal. Its waters are shared by Tanzania, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Burundi and Zambia.

Geography and limnology

The lake fills a deep graben between mountainous rift shoulders. Its main inflows are the Ruzizi River from Lake Kivu and the Malagarasi River; its only outflow is the Lukuga River into the Congo system, which flows intermittently. The deep water is anoxic and permanently stratified (meromictic): below about 100–200 m the water contains no dissolved oxygen, so fish are confined to the upper layers. The lake is estimated to be 9–12 million years old, one of the oldest lakes on Earth.

Biology

Tanganyika is a celebrated centre of evolution, home to some 250 species of cichlid fish, nearly all endemic, plus endemic crabs, shrimps, jellyfish and molluscs — a marine-like fauna in fresh water. Its fisheries for sardine-like dagaa (Stolothrissa and Limnothrissa) and Lates perches support millions of people. The shores include Gombe Stream and Mahale Mountains national parks in Tanzania, famous for chimpanzee research begun by Jane Goodall at Gombe in 1960.

Transport and history

The MV Liemba, originally the German ship Graf Goetzen (scuttled in 1916 and later raised), still ferries passengers along the Tanzanian shore from Kigoma, the lake's main port, linked by rail to Dar es Salaam. Explorers Burton and Speke reached the lake in 1858, and Stanley and Livingstone met at Ujiji on its shore in 1871.
