Lake Victoria

Lake Victoria is one of the African Great Lakes. With a surface area of approximately 59,947 km² (23,146 sq mi), it is Africa's largest lake by area, the world's largest tropical lake, and the world's second-largest fresh water lake by surface area after Lake Superior in North America. The lake is shared by Tanzania, Uganda and Kenya, and its waters eventually drain into the Nile.

Despite its size, Lake Victoria is relatively shallow, with a mean depth of about 40 metres. Its largest inflow is the Kagera River, and its only outlet is the Victoria Nile at Jinja, Uganda, regulated by the Nalubaale and Kiira hydroelectric dams. The lake contains more than 3,000 islands, including the Ssese Islands in the northwest.

History

The lake was named for Queen Victoria by the British explorer John Hanning Speke, who reached its southern shore in 1858 and identified it as the source of the Nile, a claim confirmed by Henry Morton Stanley's circumnavigation in 1875. Geological evidence suggests the lake basin is young — the lake may have completely dried up about 17,300 years ago and refilled roughly 14,700 years ago.

Ecology

Lake Victoria formerly supported an extraordinary radiation of more than 500 species of haplochromine cichlid fish, most of them endemic. The introduction of the Nile perch in the 1950s, combined with eutrophication and overfishing, drove many native species to extinction or near-extinction, transforming both the lake's ecology and its export fishery. Water hyacinth invasion and pollution from lakeside cities remain major concerns.

Transport and economy

Ferries link the lakeside ports of Mwanza, Bukoba and Musoma (Tanzania), Jinja, Entebbe and Port Bell (Uganda), and Kisumu (Kenya). The lake basin supports one of the most densely populated rural regions in Africa, with tens of millions of people depending on it for fish, water and transport.
