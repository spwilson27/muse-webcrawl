Lake Kivu is one of the African Great Lakes, lying on the border between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda in the Albertine Rift. It drains southward through the Ruzizi River into Lake Tanganyika. Its shores include the cities of Goma and Bukavu (DRC) and Gisenyi, Kibuye, and Cyangugu (Rwanda), and it contains Idjwi, one of the largest inland islands in the world.

Dissolved gases

Kivu is one of only three known "exploding lakes," along with Lake Nyos and Lake Monoun in Cameroon. Its deep waters hold enormous quantities of dissolved carbon dioxide (about 300 km³) and methane (about 60 km³), accumulated from volcanic activity below. Scientists fear a limnic eruption could devastate the two million people living around its shores. Since 2016 the KivuWatt project in Rwanda has extracted methane from the lake to generate electricity, which also gradually reduces the gas hazard.

History and ecology

The lake was first reported by Europeans in 1894 by the German explorer Gustav Adolf von Götzen. Its fish fauna includes the introduced Tanganyika sardine (Limnothrissa miodon), locally called sambaza, which supports an important fishery. In 1994 the shores were the scene of refugee camps during the aftermath of the Rwandan genocide.
