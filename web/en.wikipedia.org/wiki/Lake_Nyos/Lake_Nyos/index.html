Lake Nyos

Lake Nyos is a crater lake in the Northwest Region of Cameroon, located about 315 km (196 mi) northwest of Yaoundé. Nyos is a deep lake high on the flank of an inactive volcano on the Cameroon line of volcanic activity. A volcanic dam impounds the lake waters. A pocket of magma lies beneath the lake and leaks carbon dioxide (CO2) into the water, changing it into carbonic acid. Nyos is one of only three lakes known to be saturated with carbon dioxide in this way — the others are Lake Monoun, also in Cameroon, and Lake Kivu on the border of the Democratic Republic of Congo and Rwanda.

The 1986 disaster

On 21 August 1986, possibly triggered by a landslide, the lake suddenly released a huge cloud of carbon dioxide in a limnic eruption, which suffocated 1,746 people and 3,500 livestock in nearby towns and villages — one of the deadliest natural disasters in Cameroonian history. The gas cloud, denser than air, flowed down two valleys and displaced all the air in low-lying areas, asphyxiating people up to 25 km away. Nearly all the victims were found at home, many still in their beds. A similar but smaller eruption at Lake Monoun in 1984 had killed 37 people and first alerted scientists to the phenomenon.

Degassing and safety

Since 2001, self-siphoning degassing columns have vented CO2 from the lake's deep water, with additional pipes installed in 2011, reducing the risk of a repeat disaster. Scientists also monitor the natural dam of volcanic rock that holds back the lake, whose erosion or collapse could flood lower valleys; reinforcement works have been undertaken with international support.
