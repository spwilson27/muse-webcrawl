African Great Lakes
Region: East Africa, Great Rift Valley
Principal lakes: Victoria, Tanganyika, Malawi (Nyasa), Turkana, Albert, Kivu, Edward
Countries: Burundi, DR Congo, Ethiopia, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia

The African Great Lakes (Swahili: Maziwa Makuu) are a series of lakes constituting the part of the Rift Valley lakes in and around the East African Rift. They include Lake Victoria, the second-largest freshwater lake in the world by area; Lake Tanganyika, the world's second-largest freshwater lake by volume and depth; Lake Malawi; and Lake Turkana, the world's largest permanent desert lake.

The lakes

The principal lakes, in rough order of size, are Lake Victoria (68,800 km²), Lake Tanganyika (32,900 km²), Lake Malawi (29,600 km²), Lake Turkana (6,405 km²), Lake Albert (5,300 km²), Lake Kivu (2,700 km²) and Lake Edward (2,325 km²). Lakes Victoria, Albert and Edward drain into the White Nile; Tanganyika and Kivu drain via the Congo River system to the Atlantic; Malawi drains through the Shire River to the Zambezi.

Ecology

The lakes are among the most biologically diverse freshwater systems on Earth. The rift lakes, especially Malawi, Tanganyika and Victoria, are famous for their explosive radiations of cichlid fish, with hundreds of endemic species in each lake. The introduction of the Nile perch into Lake Victoria in the 1950s, together with eutrophication and overfishing, drove many native cichlids to extinction and transformed the ecosystem.

People and economy

The African Great Lakes region is one of the most densely populated parts of Africa. The lakes support major artisanal and commercial fisheries, transport routes and lakeside cities such as Kisumu, Mwanza, Bukoba, Kigoma, Bujumbura and Kalemie. The region's kingdoms — Buganda, Rwanda, Burundi, Bunyoro and others — rose around the lakes' northern and western shores.
