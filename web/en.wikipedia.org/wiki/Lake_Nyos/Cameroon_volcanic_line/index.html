Cameroon Volcanic Line

Cameroon Volcanic Line
Location: Gulf of Guinea and western Cameroon / eastern Nigeria
Length: approx. 1,600 km
Highest point: Mount Cameroon, 4,040 m
Notable features: Mount Cameroon, Lake Nyos, Lake Monoun, Adamawa Plateau, São Tomé, Príncipe, Bioko, Annobón

The Cameroon Volcanic Line (CVL) is a 1,600 km (1,000 mi) long chain of volcanoes and volcanic highlands running from the island of Annobón in the Atlantic Ocean northeast through São Tomé, Príncipe and Bioko, then onto the African mainland through Mount Cameroon and the Cameroon highlands toward the Adamawa Plateau and the border regions of Nigeria and Chad.

Geology

The line has been volcanically active for roughly 80 million years, yet shows no simple age progression of the kind expected from a classic mantle hotspot track; volcanoes at both ends and in the middle have erupted in the recent geological past. Explanations proposed include a hotspot, reactivated Precambrian fracture zones, and edge effects along the boundary between thick continental craton and thinner lithosphere beneath the Congo Basin and the Benue Trough. The line roughly follows the Foumban shear zone and other ancient structures.

Volcanism and hazards

Mount Cameroon, an active shield volcano and one of Africa's largest, erupted most recently in 2012. The CVL's crater lakes are of special concern: in 1984 Lake Monoun released a cloud of carbon dioxide that killed 37 people, and on 21 August 1986 Lake Nyos released a far larger limnic eruption that asphyxiated about 1,746 people and thousands of livestock. Since 2001 and 2003 respectively, degassing pipes installed in Lake Nyos and Lake Monoun have slowly vented dissolved CO₂ to reduce the risk of recurrence.
