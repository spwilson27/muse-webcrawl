Lake Nyos

Lake Nyos is a crater lake in the Northwest Region of Cameroon, formed in a volcanic maar on the flank of an inactive volcano in the Oku volcanic plain of the Cameroon volcanic line. The lake is notorious for the limnic eruption of 21 August 1986, which suddenly released a huge cloud of carbon dioxide that asphyxiated 1,746 people and thousands of livestock in nearby villages. Siphon pipes have since been installed to degas the lake continuously and reduce the risk of a recurrence.

Geology

Lake Nyos occupies a maar created by a phreatomagmatic eruption roughly four centuries ago. Magma beneath the lake leaks carbon dioxide into the bottom water. Because the lake is deep and thermally stratified (meromictic), the CO₂-laden deep water normally stays isolated at the bottom, dissolving the gas under pressure — a natural soda bottle. A natural dam of volcanic debris at the lake's outlet is a secondary concern, as its failure could flood downstream valleys.

The 1986 disaster

On the night of 21 August 1986, the lake overturned — possibly triggered by a landslide, heavy rain, or the saturated water reaching a tipping point — releasing an estimated 100,000 to 300,000 tonnes (some estimates up to 1.6 million tonnes) of CO₂. The dense gas, up to about 50 m thick, hugged the ground and flowed down surrounding valleys at speeds reported up to 20–50 km/h, suffocating people and animals up to 25 km away. The villages of Nyos, Kam, Cha and Subum were devastated: 1,746 people died. Two years earlier a similar, smaller event at Lake Monoun had killed 37 people and provided the interpretive key to Nyos.

Degassing and monitoring

Beginning in 2001, scientists installed self-siphoning pipes that draw CO₂-rich water from the lake bottom and fountain it at the surface, safely venting the gas; additional pipes were added in 2011, and the deep-water CO₂ level has been progressively reduced toward safety. The natural dam has been assessed and reinforced in planning, and resettled populations around the lake remain under monitoring. Lake Nyos, with Lake Monoun and Lake Kivu in Rwanda/DR Congo, is one of three known CO₂-saturated lakes of this type.
