Idjwi

Idjwi Island
Location: Lake Kivu, DR Congo
Coordinates: 2°07′S 29°03′E
Area: approx. 285 km²
Length: approx. 70 km
Population: approx. 300,000 (estimates vary)
Main town: Bugarula

Idjwi is an island in Lake Kivu, belonging to the Democratic Republic of the Congo. At about 285 km² it is the second-largest inland island in Africa and the tenth-largest lake island in the world. It lies roughly midway between the Congolese shore at Goma and Bukavu and the Rwandan shore.

Geography and people

The island is mountainous, rising to over 2,000 m, and its rich volcanic soils support dense smallholder agriculture: coffee, beans, cassava, bananas and pineapples. The population, mainly Havu (Bany'Idjwi) people, has grown rapidly, and estimates range from about 250,000 to over 300,000, making Idjwi one of the most densely populated rural areas of the Congo. Notably, Idjwi remained comparatively peaceful during the Congo wars of the 1990s and 2000s, and received refugees from the mainland.

History

Historically Idjwi formed a small kingdom under a mwami. The island is also known for its population of Bambuti pygmies, among the earliest inhabitants of the region. Long isolated by a lack of electricity, roads and ferry links, Idjwi has attracted attention from development agencies; coffee cooperatives there have gained a reputation for high-quality arabica exported through Bukavu and Goma.
