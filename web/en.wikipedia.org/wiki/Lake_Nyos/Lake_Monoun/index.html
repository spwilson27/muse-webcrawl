Lake Monoun is a crater lake in the West Region of Cameroon, formed in a volcanic maar of the Cameroon volcanic line. It is one of three lakes in the world known to be saturated with carbon dioxide at depth, along with Lake Nyos (also in Cameroon) and Lake Kivu.

The 1984 disaster

On 15 August 1984, Lake Monoun underwent a limnic eruption: a sudden release of dissolved carbon dioxide from its deep waters. The invisible, heavier-than-air gas cloud flowed into nearby low-lying areas and suffocated 37 people. Two years before the far deadlier Lake Nyos disaster of 1986, the Monoun event was at first attributed to other causes; scientific investigation of both lakes established the limnic-eruption mechanism.

Degassing

Since 2003, a degassing column has been installed at Lake Monoun to vent CO₂ from the deep water in a controlled fountain, reducing the risk of a recurrence; similar systems operate at Lake Nyos.
