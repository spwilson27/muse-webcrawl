Juan Manuel Puig Delledonne (28 December 1932 – 22 July 1990), commonly known as Manuel Puig, was an Argentine author. He is best known for his novel El beso de la mujer araña (Kiss of the Spider Woman, 1976), which was adapted into an Academy Award-winning film and a Broadway musical.

Life

Puig was born in General Villegas, in the Argentine pampas, a small town that reappears in his fiction as "Coronel Vallejos." As a child he immersed himself in Hollywood films at the local cinema, an experience that shaped his narrative style. He studied architecture and philosophy in Buenos Aires and in 1956 went to Rome to study film direction at Cinecittà, later working as an assistant director and writing screenplays in Italy, London, and New York. Finding his true medium in prose fiction, he published his first novel in 1968.

Work

La traición de Rita Hayworth (Betrayed by Rita Hayworth, 1968) broke with the conventions of the Latin American Boom: written largely in dialogue and interior monologue, it explored provincial life through the consciousness of a film-loving child. Boquitas pintadas (1969), published in English as Heartbreak Tango, presented a melodrama of romantic betrayal through letters, diaries, and tango lyrics, and became a bestseller.

His most famous novel, Kiss of the Spider Woman (1976), consists almost entirely of conversation in a Buenos Aires prison cell between a gay window dresser, Molina, and a Marxist revolutionary, Valentín. Banned in Argentina, it was written during Puig's exile. Under threat from the military regime, Puig left Argentina in 1973 and lived in New York, Rio de Janeiro, and finally Cuernavaca, Mexico, where he died of a heart attack after surgery in 1990.

Style

Puig built his novels from popular culture — Hollywood melodrama, tangos, radio serials, detective fiction — which he juxtaposed with psychoanalytic and political discourse. He avoided conventional narration, constructing stories from documents, footnotes, transcripts, and streams of consciousness. His later novels include Pubis angelical (1979) and Cae la noche tropical (1988).
