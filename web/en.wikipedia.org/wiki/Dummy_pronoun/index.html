Dummy pronoun

A dummy pronoun, also called an expletive pronoun or pleonastic pronoun, is a pronoun used in a sentence where it serves a grammatical function but contributes no semantic meaning. Dummy pronouns appear because syntax requires a subject (or other argument) even when no referent exists.

Examples

In English, the most common dummy pronoun is it in weather expressions such as "It is raining", in time expressions such as "It is five o'clock", and in cleft constructions such as "It was John who called". The pronoun there in existential sentences like "There are three apples on the table" is also analysed as a dummy element.

Across languages

Languages differ in whether they require dummy subjects. So-called "pro-drop" languages, such as Spanish and Italian, can omit subject pronouns entirely, while non-pro-drop languages like English and French require an overt subject, using a dummy pronoun where no semantic subject is available. German uses es and Dutch uses het or er in comparable roles.
