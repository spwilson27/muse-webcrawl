Khabarovsk War Crime Trials

The Khabarovsk War Crime Trials were a series of hearings held in the Soviet city of Khabarovsk between 25 and 31 December 1949, in which twelve members of the Japanese Kwantung Army were tried as war criminals for manufacturing and using biological weapons during the Second Sino-Japanese War and World War II.

The accused

The defendants included General Yamada Otozō, the last commander of the Kwantung Army, and senior officers and medical personnel associated with Unit 731 and Unit 100, the Imperial Japanese Army's biological warfare units. Among them were Lieutenant General Kajitsuka Ryūji, head of the Kwantung Army's medical administration, and Takahashi Takaatsu, a veterinary officer of Unit 100.

Proceedings and verdicts

All twelve defendants pleaded guilty and were convicted. In their testimony they described human experimentation, the cultivation of plague, anthrax, cholera and other pathogens, and field attacks in which plague-infected fleas were scattered over Chinese cities. The sentences ranged from two to twenty-five years' imprisonment in labour camps—far lighter than the death sentences handed down at other war crimes trials, since the Soviet Union had abolished capital punishment at the time. Most of the convicted men were repatriated to Japan by 1956 under a Soviet–Japanese settlement.

Legacy

The trial materials were published by the Soviet Union in 1950 as Materials on the Trial of Former Servicemen of the Japanese Army Charged with Manufacturing and Employing Bacteriological Weapons, in several languages. In the West the proceedings were long dismissed as Communist propaganda, partly because the United States had granted immunity to the leaders of Unit 731 in exchange for their experimental data and never prosecuted them at the Tokyo Trial. Later scholarship, based on archival evidence and the confessions recorded at Khabarovsk, has confirmed the essential accuracy of the trial's account of the Japanese biological warfare programme.
