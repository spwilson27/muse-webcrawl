Origin of birds

Archaeopteryx, a transitional form between dinosaurs and birds

The scientific question of the origin of birds — which animals they descend from and how flight evolved — was actively debated from the beginnings of evolutionary biology. The modern consensus, supported by an extensive fossil record, is that birds are a group of maniraptoran theropod dinosaurs that originated in the Late Jurassic period, around 160 million years ago. Birds are therefore regarded by many biologists as the only living dinosaurs.

History of the debate

Thomas Henry Huxley noted striking anatomical similarities between birds and small carnivorous dinosaurs in the 1860s, shortly after the discovery of Archaeopteryx in the Solnhofen Limestone in 1861. The idea lost favour in the early 20th century, when Gerhard Heilmann proposed descent from more primitive archosaurs, but was revived in the 1970s by John Ostrom, whose work on the raptor Deinonychus re-established the theropod hypothesis.

Fossil evidence

Since the 1990s, the Liaoning deposits of China have yielded thousands of fossils of feathered dinosaurs — including Sinosauropteryx, Caudipteryx, four-winged forms such as Microraptor, and early birds such as Confuciusornis. These document the stepwise evolution of feathers (which first served insulation or display), wishbones, hollow bones, beaks, and powered flight. Debate continues over whether flight evolved "ground up" through running or "trees down" through gliding.

Modern birds

Crown-group birds (Neornithes) diversified before the end of the Cretaceous and were among the few dinosaur lineages to survive the Cretaceous–Palaeogene extinction event 66 million years ago, radiating into the roughly 11,000 species alive today.
