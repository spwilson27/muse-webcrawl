Faculty (division)

A faculty is a division within a university or college comprising one subject area or a group of related subject areas, possibly also delimited by level (for example, an undergraduate faculty). In American usage, the word is also used for the academic staff of an institution, but this article concerns the organisational division common in British, Commonwealth and many European universities.

History

The medieval University of Paris, which served as a model for most of the later medieval universities, had four faculties: the higher Faculties of Theology, Law and Medicine, and the lower Faculty of Arts, where students first studied the liberal arts before progressing to a higher faculty. This structure persisted for centuries; the traditional faculties of arts, law, medicine and theology (or divinity) remain recognisable in many older universities.

Organisation

A faculty is typically headed by a dean and is subdivided into departments, schools or institutes. Faculties group related departments — for example, a Faculty of Science may contain departments of physics, chemistry and biology, while a Faculty of Humanities groups history, languages and philosophy. Since the late 20th century many universities have merged or renamed faculties (for example into "colleges" or "schools") for administrative or marketing reasons, but the term remains standard in much of Europe, Latin America, and the Commonwealth.
