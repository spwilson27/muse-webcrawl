Agis II (Greek: Ἆγις; died c. 401/400 BC) was a Eurypontid king of Sparta who reigned from about 427 BC, succeeding his father Archidamus II, through most of the Peloponnesian War against Athens.

Career

Agis led the invasions of Attica in the later Archidamian War and negotiated in the run-up to the Peace of Nicias (421 BC), which many Spartans blamed him for accepting on unfavourable terms. When the peace broke down he redeemed his reputation with a decisive victory over Argos, Mantinea and Athens at the Battle of Mantinea in 418 BC, the largest land battle of the war, which restored Spartan prestige in the Peloponnese.

In 413 BC, on the advice of the exiled Athenian Alcibiades, Agis fortified Decelea in Attica, maintaining a permanent garrison that strangled Athens' agriculture and silver mines for the rest of the war; he remained based there for long periods, effectively commanding Sparta's war effort. He later campaigned in Elis (c. 402–400 BC), forcing it to liberate its subject towns, but fell out with the ephors over the spoils.

Family and succession

Agis's wife was Timaea; Alcibiades was rumoured (by hostile sources) to be the father of her son Leotychidas, whose legitimacy Agis doubted. On Agis's death Leotychidas was set aside and the throne passed to Agis's half-brother Agesilaus II, backed by Lysander.
