Zeno of Citium (c. 334 – c. 262 BC) was a Hellenistic philosopher from Cyprus who founded the Stoic school in Athens around 300 BC. A Phoenician-Greek merchant who turned to philosophy after a shipwreck — remarking, tradition says, that he had made a prosperous voyage when he was wrecked — he taught in the Painted Porch (Stoa Poikile), from which Stoicism takes its name.

Teaching

Zeno divided philosophy into logic, physics and ethics (compared to an egg, or a garden's fence, soil and fruit). He taught that the universe is a rational, providential organism governed by the divine logos, and that happiness is a 'good flow of life' achieved by living in agreement with nature. Little of his writing survives; his doctrines were preserved and developed by successors such as Cleanthes and Chrysippus.
