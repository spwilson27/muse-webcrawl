Multitrack recording


A multitrack tape recorder.

Multitrack recording (also known as multitracking or tracking) is a method of sound recording that allows for the separate recording and editing of multiple sound sources or of sound sources recorded at different times to create a cohesive whole. Each audio track is recorded to a separate channel of a recording medium such as magnetic tape or a digital audio workstation.

History

The technique was pioneered in the 1940s–50s: guitarist Les Paul experimented with sound-on-sound overdubbing, and in 1955 Ampex developed Sel-Sync (selective synchronous) recording, allowing individual tracks of an 8-track tape machine to be monitored in sync while recording others. Ross Snyder and Ampex built the first 8-track "Octopus" for Les Paul.

Multitrack machines expanded to 16 and 24 tracks on 2-inch tape, transforming popular music: the Beatles and producer George Martin exploited four- and eight-track recording at Abbey Road, and artists could record parts at different times and places. From the 1990s, non-linear digital recording in digital audio workstations made effectively unlimited tracks and non-destructive editing standard.

Process

Recording proceeds by laying down tracks (often a rhythm section first), overdubbing additional parts, and finally mixing down all tracks through a mixing console to a stereo master, balancing levels, equalisation, panning and effects.
