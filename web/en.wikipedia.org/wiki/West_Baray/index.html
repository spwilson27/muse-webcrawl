West Baray
Location:Angkor, Siem Reap Province, Cambodia
Built:11th century (begun under Suryavarman I, completed under Udayadityavarman II)

The West Baray (Khmer: បារាយណ៍ខាងលិច) is a baray, or artificial reservoir, at Angkor, Cambodia, oriented east–west and located just west of the walled city Angkor Thom. It is the largest of the Angkorian reservoirs and among the largest hand-excavated water bodies in the world.

Construction

Construction probably began under Suryavarman I in the 11th century and was completed under Udayadityavarman II. Rather than being dug out, the reservoir was formed by massive earthen dikes enclosing an area some 8 km by 2.1 km, fed by diverting the Siem Reap River. Its scale required an enormous organised labour force.

Function

Scholars long debated whether the barays served irrigation, flood control, ritual symbolism — representing the Hindu cosmic ocean around Mount Meru — or all of these. The precision of Angkor's hydraulic network supported the rice agriculture that fed a city of perhaps a million people; later research suggests failures of this water system under climatic extremes contributed to Angkor's decline.

West Mebon

In the centre of the baray stands the West Mebon, an island temple of Udayadityavarman II. A famous bronze statue of the reclining Vishnu, found there in 1936, is now in the National Museum in Phnom Penh — the largest bronze sculpture surviving from Angkor.

Modern use

The West Baray still holds water in its western half and is used for fishing, irrigation and recreation; boat trips visit the West Mebon when water levels allow.
