Daffy Duck is an animated cartoon character created by Warner Bros. for the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series. An anthropomorphic black duck with a white neck ring and an orange bill, Daffy first appeared in Tex Avery's Porky's Duck Hunt (1937) as a manic, hyperactive trickster who bounced around the screen crying "Woo-hoo!" In later cartoons his personality evolved into that of a vain, scheming, self-serving foil — greedy, jealous of Bugs Bunny's success, and forever pronouncing himself "despicable" in the eyes of others.

History

Directors Bob Clampett, Friz Freleng and Chuck Jones each shaped the character. Jones's "Hunting Trilogy" with Bugs Bunny and Elmer Fudd (Rabbit Fire, Rabbit Seasoning, Duck! Rabbit, Duck!, 1951–1953) and Duck Amuck (1953) — in which Daffy is tormented by his own animator — are considered classics; Duck Amuck was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry. Jones also cast Daffy as the hapless space hero Duck Dodgers, a parody of Buck Rogers.

Characterisation

Mel Blanc's voice for Daffy — a lateral lisp produced by speaking with the tongue against the teeth — became one of the most recognisable in animation. Daffy ranked number 14 on TV Guide's list of the 50 greatest cartoon characters. See also Bugs Bunny, Looney Tunes, Porky Pig, and Duck Dodgers.
