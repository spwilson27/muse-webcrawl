University of Iowa

The University of Iowa (UI, U of I, UIowa, or simply Iowa) is a public research university in Iowa City, Iowa, founded in 1847. It is the state's oldest university and a member of the Big Ten Conference; its Hawkeyes teams compete in NCAA Division I.

University of Iowa
Established: February 25, 1847
Location: Iowa City, Iowa
Type: Public research university
Mascot: Herky the Hawk

History

Founded just 59 days after Iowa became a state, the university opened in 1855 and was among the first American universities to admit men and women on an equal basis. The Old Capitol, once Iowa's statehouse, remains its symbol.

Academics

Iowa is renowned for the Iowa Writers' Workshop (founded 1936), the first creative-writing MFA program, whose alumni include many Pulitzer winners. It also excels in medicine, law, space physics (James Van Allen), and speech pathology. The University of Iowa Hospitals and Clinics is a major academic medical centre.

Campus

The campus straddles the Iowa River, divided by it into east and west sides joined by bridges.
