Fatigue

SymptomsTiredness, exhaustion, reduced energy and motivation
CausesExertion, sleep loss, illness, anaemia, thyroid disease, depression, medications
SpecialtyInternal medicine, primary care

Fatigue is a feeling of tiredness, exhaustion, or lack of energy that is not proportional to recent exertion and is not fully relieved by rest. It is one of the most common symptoms in medicine and can be physical, mental, or both.

Classification

Clinicians distinguish acute fatigue, a normal response to exertion, sleep deprivation, or intercurrent illness, from chronic fatigue persisting for months. Fatigue may be peripheral (arising in the muscles) or central (perceived by the brain, which reduces the drive to exert effort). When severe, unexplained fatigue persists for six months or more with other characteristic symptoms, it may meet criteria for myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS).

Causes

The differential diagnosis is broad: infections, anaemia, hypothyroidism, diabetes, heart failure, sleep apnoea, depression and anxiety, autoimmune disease, cancer, and the side effects of many drugs. Lifestyle factors — poor sleep, inactivity, alcohol, and overwork — are frequent contributors. Evaluation usually begins with history, examination, and basic blood tests rather than extensive imaging.

Management

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause. Sleep hygiene, graded increases in activity, treatment of mood disorders, and review of medication form the usual foundations of care.
