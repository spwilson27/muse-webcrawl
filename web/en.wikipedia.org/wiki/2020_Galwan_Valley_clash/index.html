The 2020 Galwan Valley clash was a violent confrontation between Indian and Chinese troops in the disputed Galwan Valley in Ladakh on the night of 15–16 June 2020, during the wider 2020–2021 China–India skirmishes. Fought with stones, clubs and improvised weapons rather than firearms, it was the first fatal clash along the Line of Actual Control since 1975.

Background

Indian and Chinese troops had been in a standoff at several points along the LAC since May 2020, amid Indian road construction near the border and mutual accusations of transgression. A disengagement agreement for the Galwan area had been reached at corps commander talks on 6 June.

The clash

On 15 June, an Indian patrol led by Colonel B. Santosh Babu went to verify the removal of a Chinese observation post near Patrol Point 14. A brawl escalated into hours of hand-to-hand fighting on steep terrain in darkness; soldiers fell into the Galwan River. Twenty Indian soldiers, including Colonel Babu, died. China later stated that four of its soldiers were killed.

Aftermath

The clash sharply worsened India–China relations. India banned numerous Chinese mobile apps and increased border deployments; both sides engaged in months of military and diplomatic talks, with phased disengagement at Pangong Tso and other friction points beginning in February 2021.
