Basilica of Superga

The Basilica of Superga (Italian: Basilica di Superga) is a Baroque church built on the hill of Superga overlooking Turin, Italy. It was commissioned by Victor Amadeus II of Savoy in fulfilment of a vow made during the siege of Turin in 1706, and designed by the architect Filippo Juvarra. The basilica houses the royal crypt of the House of Savoy.

History

During the French siege of Turin in 1706, Duke Victor Amadeus II climbed the Superga hill to survey the battlefield and vowed to build a church to the Virgin Mary if the city was saved. After the Piedmontese victory he kept his promise. Construction began in 1717 and the church was consecrated in 1731. Juvarra's design combines a classical temple portico with a tall dome, deliberately echoing Roman models.

Royal crypt and the Superga air disaster

Beneath the church lies the mausoleum of the House of Savoy, containing the tombs of Sardinian and Italian royalty including Victor Amadeus II and Charles Albert. The hill is also remembered for the 1949 Superga air disaster, in which a plane carrying the Grande Torino football team crashed into the retaining wall behind the basilica, killing all 31 people on board; a memorial stands at the site.
