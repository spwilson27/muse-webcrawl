Edict of Nantes

Edict of Nantes
Signed: April 1598
Issued by: Henry IV of France
Revoked: 1685, Edict of Fontainebleau

The Edict of Nantes was signed in April 1598 by King Henry IV and granted the Calvinist Protestants of France, also known as Huguenots, substantial rights in the nation, which was largely Catholic, ending the French Wars of Religion.

Provisions

The edict granted freedom of conscience, limited freedom of worship, civil rights including holding public office, and control of fortified places de sûreté such as La Rochelle, with royal funding for garrisons and pastors.

Revocation and legacy

Under Louis XIII and Richelieu the Huguenots' military privileges were removed after the siege of La Rochelle (1628). Louis XIV revoked the edict with the Edict of Fontainebleau in 1685, prompting mass emigration of Huguenots to England, the Netherlands, Prussia and America, harming the French economy. It remains a landmark in the history of religious toleration. See also Huguenots.
