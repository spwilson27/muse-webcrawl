Radboud University Nijmegen

Established: 1923
Type: Public (originally Roman Catholic)
Location: Nijmegen, Gelderland, Netherlands
Students: about 25,000

Radboud University (Dutch: Radboud Universiteit, abbreviated RU; formerly Catholic University of Nijmegen) is a public research university located in Nijmegen, the Netherlands. It is considered one of the best traditional, general universities in the Netherlands.

History

The university was founded on 17 October 1923 as the Catholic University of Nijmegen, an initiative of the Dutch Catholic community to establish its own university; it began with 189 students and 27 professors. It was named after Saint Radboud of Utrecht, a bishop known for his devotion to learning, in 2004, to emphasise that it is more than a denominational institution. In 2020 the Dutch bishops withdrew the university's "Catholic" designation following a governance dispute, though the university retains its historical identity.

Profile

Radboud has seven faculties — philosophy, theology and religious studies; arts; law; science; management; social sciences; and medical sciences (Radboudumc) — and hosts major research institutes including the Donders Institute for Brain, Cognition and Behaviour and the High Field Magnet Laboratory. Alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates Andre Geim and Konstantin Novoselov (graphene), and Dutch prime minister Dries van Agt.
