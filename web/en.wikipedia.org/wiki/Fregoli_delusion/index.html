Fregoli delusion

The Fregoli delusion (or Fregoli syndrome) is a rare disorder in which a person holds a delusional belief that different people are in fact a single person who changes appearance or is in disguise. The syndrome may be related to a brain lesion and is often of a paranoid nature, with the delusional person believing themselves persecuted by the person they believe is in disguise.

History

The condition is named after the Italian actor Leopoldo Fregoli, who was renowned for his ability to make quick changes of appearance during his stage act. It was first described in a 1927 paper by Courbon and Fail, who discussed the case of a 27-year-old woman who believed two actors she often saw at the theatre were pursuing her in the guise of people she knew.

Relation to other syndromes

Fregoli syndrome is classified as a delusional misidentification syndrome, along with the Capgras delusion (in which a familiar person is believed to have been replaced by an impostor), intermetamorphosis, and subjective doubles.
