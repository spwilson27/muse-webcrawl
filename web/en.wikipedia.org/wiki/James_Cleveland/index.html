The Reverend James Edward Cleveland (December 5, 1931 – February 9, 1991) was an American gospel singer, musician, and composer, widely regarded as the "King of Gospel Music." He is credited with creating the modern gospel sound by blending traditional gospel with soul, pop, and jazz elements, and with pioneering the mass-choir format that dominates contemporary gospel.

Career

Raised in Chicago, Cleveland sang as a boy soprano at Pilgrim Baptist Church under the influence of gospel pioneer Thomas A. Dorsey, and developed his distinctive gravelly baritone after his voice changed. He worked as pianist and arranger for groups including the Caravans, working alongside Albertina Walker and a young Aretha Franklin. In 1963 his recording of "Peace Be Still" with the Angelic Choir of Nutley, New Jersey, became a landmark live gospel album. Settling in Los Angeles, he founded the Cornerstone Institutional Baptist Church and recorded prolifically for Savoy Records, producing dozens of albums.

Gospel Music Workshop of America

In 1968 Cleveland founded the Gospel Music Workshop of America (GMWA), an annual convention that trained and showcased gospel choirs, singers, and musicians and became the genre's principal institution. He won multiple Grammy Awards and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, the first gospel artist so honoured.
