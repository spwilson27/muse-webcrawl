Venice Biennale of Architecture

The Venice Biennale of Architecture (Italian: Mostra Internazionale di Architettura) is an international exhibition of architecture held every two years in Venice, Italy, alternating with the Venice Biennale art exhibition. Founded in 1980, it is the most prestigious architectural exhibition in the world, combining a thematic central exhibition curated by an appointed director with national pavilions in the Giardini and across the city, plus the vast spaces of the Arsenale.

History

Architecture first appeared within the art Biennale in the 1970s; the first independent architecture exhibition, directed by Paolo Portoghesi in 1980 under the theme "The Presence of the Past", launched the postmodern debate with its Strada Novissima of façades by leading architects. Subsequent directors have included Aldo Rossi, Massimiliano Fuksas, Rem Koolhaas (2014, "Fundamentals"), Alejandro Aravena (2016, "Reporting from the Front"), Yvonne Farrell and Shelley McNamara of Grafton Architects (2018), Hashim Sarkis (2021) and Lesley Lokko (2023, "The Laboratory of the Future"), the first edition curated by an African-born director.

Awards

An international jury awards the Golden Lion for best national participation and for the best participant in the central exhibition, a Silver Lion for a promising young participant, and a Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement.

Format

Each edition is organised around a theme set by its curator. National pavilions — many in permanent buildings in the Giardini dating from the early 20th century — present each country's response, while collateral events spread throughout Venice. Attendance runs to several hundred thousand visitors over the roughly six-month run, from late spring to November.
