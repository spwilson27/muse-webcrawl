Amanullah Khan

Amanullah Khan (1 June 1892 – 25 April 1960) was the ruler of Afghanistan from 1919 to 1929, first as emir and, from 1926, as king. He led Afghanistan to victory in the Third Anglo-Afghan War in 1919, winning complete independence from the United Kingdom in foreign affairs, and then embarked on an ambitious program of modernization and social reform. His reforms — including Afghanistan's first constitution, secular education for girls, and measures against the veil and polygamy — provoked conservative and tribal backlash that culminated in civil war and his abdication in 1929.

Amanullah came to power after the assassination of his father, Emir Habibullah Khan, in February 1919, prevailing over his uncle Nasrullah and brother Inayatullah. Rejecting the subsidy relationship with British India, he attacked British forces in May 1919; the resulting Treaty of Rawalpindi (August 1919) recognized Afghan independence, which is still celebrated as the country's independence day.

Accession and the Third Anglo-Afghan War

Though the war was militarily inconclusive, British exhaustion after the First World War and unrest in India made London willing to concede control of Afghan foreign policy. Amanullah followed up with treaties with the Soviet Union, Turkey, Persia and European states, presenting Afghanistan as a modern sovereign state; in 1921 he concluded a friendship treaty with Soviet Russia.

Reforms

Influenced by his father-in-law Mahmud Tarzi and by the example of Atatürk's Turkey, Amanullah promulgated a constitution in 1923, codified civil and criminal law, abolished slavery, promoted secular schools (including for girls), introduced a national budget and currency, and encouraged Western dress in Kabul. With his queen, Soraya Tarzi, he campaigned publicly against purdah. After an eight-month world tour in 1927–28 he returned with accelerated and more radical proposals.

Downfall and exile

Reforms, taxation and conscription alienated tribal and religious leaders; revolts beginning in late 1928 spread, and in January 1929 Amanullah abdicated in favour of his brother Inayatullah, who was himself overthrown within days by the Tajik bandit leader Habibullah Kalakani. Amanullah's attempt to retake Kabul failed, and he fled to Italy, dying in Zurich in 1960. His body was returned to Afghanistan and buried in Jalalabad.

Legacy

Amanullah is remembered as Afghanistan's independence hero and its first great modernizer; his fate is often cited in Afghan and Western debate about the pace of reform in a conservative, tribal society. The mausoleum in Jalalabad and his image on currency have kept him prominent in national memory.
