Warmblood

A warmblood is a type of middle-weight horse, originally developed by crossing heavy "cold-blooded" draft breeds with lighter, more spirited "hot-blooded" breeds such as the Arabian and Thoroughbred. The term describes a temperament and build classification rather than a single breed.

Breeding and registries

Most modern warmbloods were developed in continental Europe, especially Germany and the Netherlands, in the 19th and 20th centuries, as cavalry and carriage horses were progressively refined into sport horses. Famous registries and breeds include the Hanoverian, Oldenburg, Westphalian, Holsteiner, Trakehner, Dutch Warmblood (KWPN) and Selle Français. Registration is by inspection: horses are evaluated for conformation, movement and performance, and only approved animals enter the studbook — an "open book" system that permits ongoing improvement.

Uses

Warmbloods dominate the Olympic equestrian disciplines of dressage and show jumping, and are also prominent in eventing and combined driving, combining the power of draft stock with the athleticism and sensitivity of hot-blooded breeds.
