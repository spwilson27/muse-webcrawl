The Ligurian Republic (Italian: Repubblica Ligure) was a French client republic formed by Napoleon Bonaparte on 14 June 1797 out of the old Republic of Genoa. It took its name from the ancient Ligurian people of the region, and its territory corresponded roughly to the modern Italian region of Liguria.

History

After Bonaparte's victorious Italian campaign of 1796–97, the aristocratic Republic of Genoa was forced to accept a new democratic constitution modelled on that of France. The old ruling families were displaced, and a provisional government gave way to the Ligurian Republic, whose constitution followed the French Directory model, with power shared among executive and legislative bodies that were in practice dominated by pro-French factions.

The republic served as a French satellite throughout the Revolutionary Wars. In 1805, following Napoleon's assumption of the imperial title and the creation of the Kingdom of Italy, the Ligurian Republic petitioned for — and received — annexation to the French Empire, its territory being divided among the departments of the Apennins, Montenotte and Gênes.

Restoration and end

After Napoleon's first abdication in 1814, the republic was briefly re-established between 28 April and 28 July 1814. The Congress of Vienna, however, awarded Genoa and Liguria to the Kingdom of Sardinia, and the territory was annexed in January 1815, ending Genoese independence for good.
