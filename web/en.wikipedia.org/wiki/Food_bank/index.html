Food bank

TypeNon-profit / charitable organisation
PurposeCollecting and distributing food to people in need
First modern food bankSt. Mary's Food Bank, Phoenix, Arizona (1967)

A food bank is a non-profit, charitable organisation that distributes food to those who have difficulty purchasing enough to avoid hunger, usually through intermediaries such as food pantries, soup kitchens and community groups.

History and model

The modern food bank concept is credited to John van Hengel, who founded St. Mary's Food Bank in Phoenix, Arizona, in 1967, and went on to help establish the network that became Feeding America. Food banks typically collect surplus and donated food from farmers, manufacturers, retailers and the public, store it in warehouses, and supply front-line agencies rather than handing food directly to individuals — though some operate direct distribution and mobile pantries. In Europe, the European Food Banks Federation coordinates national networks, and food banking expanded widely after the 2008 financial crisis and during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Discussion

Supporters see food banks as an efficient way to rescue surplus food and relieve immediate need; critics argue they can let governments retreat from welfare provision and note problems such as the nutritional quality of donated food and the dignity of users. Many food banks also campaign on the causes of food insecurity.
