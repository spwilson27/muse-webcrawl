Antwerp (Dutch: Antwerpen [ˈɑntʋɛrpə(n)]; French: Anvers) is a city and municipality in Belgium and the capital of the province of Antwerp in the Flemish Region. With a population of roughly 530,000, it is the largest municipality in Belgium by population and the second-largest metropolitan region after Brussels.

The Port of Antwerp, on the river Scheldt about 88 kilometres inland, is one of Europe's largest seaports — by cargo tonnage second only to Rotterdam. Antwerp is also the world's leading centre for the trade in diamonds, with the majority of the world's rough diamonds passing through its diamond district.

History

Antwerp grew from a small settlement on the Scheldt into a major trading city in the late Middle Ages. By the mid-16th century it was the richest trading city in Europe, handling goods from across the continent and the new Atlantic trade. Its golden age ended with the Dutch Revolt: the Spanish Fury of 1576 and the Fall of Antwerp in 1585 led many merchants to flee to Amsterdam, and the closure of the Scheldt to navigation stifled trade for two centuries.

The port revived in the 19th century under Belgian independence (1830). Antwerp hosted the 1920 Summer Olympics. During the Second World War the city suffered heavy V-weapon bombardment after its liberation in September 1944.

Culture

Antwerp is closely associated with the painter Peter Paul Rubens, whose house, the Rubenshuis, is now a museum, and with the printer Christophe Plantin; the Plantin-Moretus Museum is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The Cathedral of Our Lady, completed in 1521, is the tallest church in the Low Countries. In fashion, the city is known for the "Antwerp Six" designers who emerged from its Royal Academy of Fine Arts in the 1980s.

Economy

Besides the port and diamond trade, Antwerp hosts one of the world's largest integrated chemical and petrochemical clusters. The Antwerp Zoo, founded in 1843, is one of the oldest in the world.
