GCHQ

Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) is an intelligence and security organisation responsible for providing signals intelligence (SIGINT) and information assurance (IA) to the government and armed forces of the United Kingdom. Primarily based in "The Doughnut" in the suburbs of Cheltenham, GCHQ is the responsibility of the country's Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs, but it is not a part of the Foreign Office and its director ranks as a Permanent Secretary.

History

GCHQ was established on 1 November 1919 as the Government Code and Cypher School (GC&CS). During the Second World War, its codebreakers at Bletchley Park — including Alan Turing — famously decrypted German Enigma and Lorenz ciphers. After the war the organisation moved to Eastcote and then to Cheltenham in the 1950s.

GCHQ operates as part of the Five Eyes alliance with the US NSA and counterparts in Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. Edward Snowden's 2013 disclosures revealed programmes such as Tempora involving bulk interception of communications, generating sustained public and legal debate.

Roles

GCHQ's work includes signals intelligence for national security, military support, and counter-terrorism. Its cyber-security arm, the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), founded in 2016, provides guidance to government, industry and the public.
