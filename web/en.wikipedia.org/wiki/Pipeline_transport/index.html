Pipeline transport

Pipeline transport is the long-distance movement of liquids or gases through a system of pipes — a pipeline — typically to a market area for consumption. Pipelines carry crude oil, refined petroleum products, natural gas, water, and other commodities, and are generally the most economical way to move large volumes over land.

Oil and gas pipelines

Transmission pipelines of large diameter move oil and gas from producing fields and import terminals to refineries and distribution networks, with compressor and pumping stations maintaining flow. Notable systems include the Trans-Alaska Pipeline, Russia's Druzhba network, and the Keystone system in North America. Inspection devices known as "pigs" are sent through lines to clean and examine them. Pipelines are regulated for safety, since leaks and ruptures can cause serious environmental damage, as in major spills that have shaped industry standards.

Other uses

Pipelines also deliver drinking water and remove sewage; district heating systems distribute hot water; and slurry pipelines move coal or mineral concentrates mixed with water. Pneumatic tube systems transport capsules carrying documents or small goods in hospitals, banks, and industry. Experimental and limited networks have been proposed or built for hydrogen, carbon dioxide for sequestration, and even foodstuffs such as beer.
