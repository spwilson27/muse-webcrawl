Queen dowager

A queen dowager or dowager queen is a title reserved for a widowed queen consort, or the widow of a king. The term describes the widow of a monarch who retains her title after her husband's death while the successor (often her child or stepchild) reigns.

Role and precedence

A queen dowager typically retains the style and precedence of a queen but no longer performs the role of consort. She may receive a dower — property and income settled for her widowhood. In some monarchies a dowager has exercised significant political influence, occasionally serving as regent for a minor heir. The equivalent for an emperor's widow is empress dowager, a position of great power in imperial China, where figures such as Empress Dowager Cixi effectively ruled the country.

Distinction from queen mother

A queen dowager who is also the mother of the reigning sovereign may be styled queen mother. Not every dowager is the sovereign's mother, and not every queen mother is a dowager, though the terms often overlap.
