In Christianity, Jesus is the central figure: the incarnate Son of God, the second Person of the Trinity, and the saviour of humanity. Christians believe Jesus is the promised Messiah (Christ) of the Old Testament prophecies.

Core beliefs

As summarised in the Nicene Creed, Christians hold that Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary; that he suffered crucifixion under Pontius Pilate; that he rose from the dead on the third day; and that he ascended into heaven, from where he will return in judgment.

Christology

His death is understood as an atoning sacrifice for the sins of humanity. The doctrine of the hypostatic union — that Christ is fully God and fully man in one person — was defined at the Council of Chalcedon (451). Christians also regard Jesus's teachings, especially the Sermon on the Mount, as the foundation of Christian ethics, and they celebrate his birth at Christmas and his resurrection at Easter.
