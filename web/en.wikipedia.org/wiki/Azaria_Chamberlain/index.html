Azaria Chantel Loren Chamberlain (11 June 1980 – 17 August 1980) was an Australian baby girl who disappeared during a family camping trip near Uluru in the Northern Territory. Her parents, Lindy and Michael Chamberlain, maintained that she had been taken by a dingo; her body was never found.

Disappearance and trials

The case became one of Australia's most publicised legal dramas. Despite initial inquest findings supporting the dingo account, Lindy Chamberlain was convicted of murder in 1982 and sentenced to life imprisonment. After new evidence — the discovery of Azaria's matinee jacket near a dingo lair in 1986 — she was released and the convictions were quashed in 1988.

Coronial resolution

After further inquests, a fourth inquest in 2012 formally recorded that a dingo had taken Azaria and caused her death, and an amended death certificate was issued. The phrase "a dingo's got my baby" became a widely known cultural reference, and the story was dramatised in the film Evil Angels (released internationally as A Cry in the Dark).
