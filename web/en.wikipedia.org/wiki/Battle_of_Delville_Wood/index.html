Battle of Delville Wood

The Battle of Delville Wood (14–20+ July 1916) was an action of the Battle of the Somme in the First World War, fought between the German Empire and the British Empire in Delville Wood (Bois d'Elville), near Longueval in northern France. The fighting is especially remembered for the stand of the 1st South African Brigade, which held the wood at great cost.

Course of the battle

As part of the Somme offensive, British forces attacked on 14 July 1916 to capture the German second position around Longueval. Delville Wood, anchoring the right of the attack, was assigned to the 9th (Scottish) Division, with the attached 1st South African Brigade under Brigadier-General Henry Lukin ordered to take and hold the wood "at all costs".

The South Africans entered the wood on 15 July and cleared most of it, but German artillery and repeated counter-attacks reduced the brigade steadily. Shellfire obliterated the trees, leaving tangled roots and craters. When the remnants were relieved on 20 July, only about 140–750 men of the original brigade answered roll calls at various counts; total brigade casualties for the period were around 2,500. British troops continued fighting in and around the wood until it was finally secured in late August/early September.

Legacy

Delville Wood became a central place of South African remembrance of the First World War. The South African National Memorial stands in the wood, and the site is maintained as a memorial park; a single hornbeam tree that survived the battle — the "Last Tree" — is preserved, with plantings grown from its seeds.
