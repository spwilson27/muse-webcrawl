Getz/Gilberto

Released: March 1964
Recorded: 18–19 March 1963, New York City
Genre: Bossa nova, Latin jazz
Label: Verve
Artists: Stan Getz, Charlie Byrd, João Gilberto, Antônio Carlos Jobim

Getz/Gilberto is an album by American saxophonist Stan Getz and Brazilian guitarist João Gilberto, featuring pianist and composer Antônio Carlos Jobim, who composed many of the tracks. Released by Verve Records in March 1964, it is widely regarded as the album that popularised bossa nova worldwide and remains one of the best-selling jazz albums of all time.

Background and recording

Following the success of Getz's Jazz Samba (1962) with Charlie Byrd, producer Creed Taylor brought Getz together with Gilberto, one of the founders of the bossa nova style, along with Jobim on piano, Sebastião Neto on bass, and Milton Banana on drums. João Gilberto's wife, Astrud Gilberto — who had never sung professionally — sang the English lyrics on "The Girl from Ipanema" and "Corcovado" at the sessions, launching her own celebrated career.

Reception and legacy

The album won the Grammy Award for Album of the Year in 1965 — the first jazz album to do so — as well as Best Jazz Instrumental Album and Best Engineered Recording. "The Girl from Ipanema" won Record of the Year and became one of the most recorded songs in popular music history. Getz/Gilberto spent 96 weeks on the charts and is credited with igniting the global bossa nova craze of the mid-1960s.
