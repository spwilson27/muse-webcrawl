Decebalus (sometimes written Dekebalos) was the last king of Dacia, reigning from 87 to 106 AD. He is famous for fighting three wars against the Roman Empire under emperors Domitian and Trajan, repeatedly defeating Roman armies before his kingdom was finally conquered and annexed as the Roman province of Dacia.

Wars with Rome

Decebalus came to power after Dacian forces under Diurpaneus ravaged the Roman province of Moesia. Domitian's armies suffered defeats including the destruction of a legion, and in 89 AD Rome settled for a peace that recognised Decebalus as a client king and supplied him with subsidies and engineers—resources he used to strengthen his fortresses.

Trajan, rejecting the settlement, invaded in 101–102 and again in 105–106. In the second war, Roman forces built Apollodorus's great bridge over the Danube, stormed the Dacian capital Sarmizegetusa, and drove Decebalus into the mountains. Rather than be captured, Decebalus took his own life in 106; his head was sent to Rome and displayed during Trajan's triumph. His campaigns are depicted on Trajan's Column in Rome.

Legacy

Decebalus is a national hero in Romania. Between 1994 and 2004 a colossal rock sculpture of his face—at 55 metres high, the tallest rock relief in Europe—was carved into the cliff above the Danube at the Iron Gates, near Orșova, bearing the inscription "Decebalus Rex — Dragan Fecit".
