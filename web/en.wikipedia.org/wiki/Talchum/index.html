Talchum

OriginKorea
TypeMask dance drama
Hangul탈춤
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2022)

Talchum (Korean: 탈춤), also known as talnori, is a traditional Korean mask dance drama performed by dancers wearing masks, accompanied by music, dialogue, mime, and vigorous dance. Performances traditionally took place outdoors in village marketplaces during festivals such as Daeboreum and Dano.

Content

Talchum plays satirize the corrupt yangban aristocracy, apostate monks, and social hypocrisy through stock characters including the lecherous nobleman, the flirtatious young woman (gaksi), the monk, and commoners who mock their betters. Regional variants include Hahoe, Bongsan, and Yangju byeolsandae mask dances.

Heritage status

Many regional forms of talchum are designated Important Intangible Cultural Properties of South Korea, and in 2022 "Talchum, mask dance drama in the Republic of Korea" was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
