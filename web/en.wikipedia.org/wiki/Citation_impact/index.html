Citation impact

Citation impact is a measure of how often a scholarly work — such as a journal article, book, or conference paper — is cited by other works in the academic literature. It is one of the most widely used indicators in bibliometrics and scientometrics for assessing the influence, visibility, and quality of research, researchers, journals, institutions, and countries.

Measures

The simplest measure of citation impact is the raw citation count: the total number of times a work has been cited. Because citation practices differ greatly between fields, raw counts are often normalised. Field-normalised citation impact compares a publication's citation count with the average for publications of the same type, year, and subject area; a value of 1.0 represents world average performance. Related indicators include the journal impact factor, the h-index, and percentile-based measures that rank papers within their field.

Uses

Citation impact is used in research assessment exercises, university rankings, funding decisions, and hiring and promotion cases. Services such as Web of Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar compile citation data and compute impact indicators.

Criticism

Critics argue that citations do not necessarily indicate quality: papers may be cited negatively, citation behaviour varies by field and language, and metrics can be gamed through self-citation and citation cartels. The San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA) and the Leiden Manifesto recommend that citation metrics support, rather than replace, expert judgement.
