The Name of the Rose

The Name of the Rose (Italian: Il nome della rosa) is the 1980 debut novel by Italian author and semiotician Umberto Eco. A historical murder mystery set in an Italian monastery in 1327, it sold more than fifty million copies worldwide.

Plot

Franciscan friar William of Baskerville, accompanied by the novice Adso of Melk, arrives at a wealthy abbey to investigate a suspicious death. Over seven days, as more monks die, William applies logic and semiotics to unravel a mystery centred on the abbey's labyrinthine library and a lost book on comedy attributed to Aristotle. The narrative intertwines detective fiction with debates on theology, heresy, and the poverty of Christ.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a 1986 film starring Sean Connery as William, and into a 2019 television series starring John Turturro. Eco published a Postscript (1983) reflecting on the novel's writing and reception.
