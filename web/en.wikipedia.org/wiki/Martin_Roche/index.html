Martin Roche (1853–1927) was an American architect based in Chicago, best known as a partner, with William Holabird, in the firm of Holabird & Roche, one of the leading practices of the Chicago School of architecture. The firm designed many of the early steel-frame skyscrapers that defined downtown Chicago, as well as hotels, department stores and office buildings across the United States.

Career

Roche was born in Cleveland, Ohio, in 1853 and moved to Chicago, where he worked in the office of William Le Baron Jenney, the engineer-architect credited with the first skyscraper. There he met William Holabird, who had also trained in Jenney's office. In 1881 the two formed their own partnership, Holabird & Roche.

The firm became a dominant force in Chicago commercial architecture. Notable works include the Tacoma Building (1889, demolished), an early skeletal-frame tower; the Marquette Building (1895), celebrated for its light court and Tiffany mosaics; the Chicago Building (1904–05); the Republic Building; and large hotels such as the Palmer House. Roche was generally credited with much of the firm's design work, while Holabird handled engineering and business matters.

After Holabird's death in 1923, the firm continued with the next generation — John Wellborn Root Jr. and John Augur Holabird — and was reorganised as Holabird & Root in 1927, the year of Roche's death.
