World Professional Association for Transgender Health

The World Professional Association for Transgender Health (WPATH), formerly the Harry Benjamin International Gender Dysphoria Association (HBIGDA), is a professional organization devoted to the understanding and treatment of gender identity and gender dysphoria, and the health of transgender and gender-diverse people. Founded in 1979, it is best known for publishing the Standards of Care for the Health of Transgender and Gender Diverse People (SOC), which have guided clinical practice internationally.

Standards of Care

The first version of the Standards of Care appeared in 1979, drawing on the work of Harry Benjamin. Subsequent editions progressively revised recommendations on assessment, hormone therapy, surgery, and the care of adolescents and children. Version 7 was published in 2011, and Version 8 (SOC8) was published in 2022 in the International Journal of Transgender Health. The standards address areas including assessment, mental health, hormone therapy, reproductive health, surgery, voice and communication, and care in institutional environments.

Activities and criticism

WPATH organizes a biennial scientific symposium and supports regional associations (such as USPATH and EPATH). The organization and its guidelines have been influential in shaping insurance coverage and clinical protocols, but have also drawn criticism from multiple directions—including debates over the evidence base for youth treatments and, historically, over requirements such as real-life experience and gatekeeping by mental-health professionals.
