Castile

Castile is a historical region of Spain, occupying much of the central Meseta plateau. Originating as a frontier county of the Kingdom of León in the 9th–10th centuries — named for its many castles — it grew into the Kingdom of Castile and then the Crown of Castile, the dominant power in the Iberian Peninsula and the core of the later Spanish monarchy.

History

Castile became a kingdom in 1065 under Ferdinand I. Through the Reconquista it expanded southward, taking Toledo in 1085 and, after the victory at Las Navas de Tolosa in 1212, most of al-Andalus except Granada. In 1230 Ferdinand III permanently united Castile and León. The marriage of Isabella I of Castile to Ferdinand II of Aragon in 1469 laid the foundation of Spain, though the crowns remained legally distinct for centuries.

Legacy

Castilian became the standard Spanish language, and Castile's institutions, law and overseas empire — from the Americas to the Philippines — shaped the early modern world. Today the name survives in two autonomous communities, Castile and León and Castilla–La Mancha, plus the Community of Madrid and parts of neighbouring regions.
