Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again is a 2018 jukebox musical romantic comedy film written and directed by Ol Parker, from a story by Parker, Catherine Johnson, and Richard Curtis. It is a follow-up to the 2008 film Mamma Mia!, which in turn is based on the West End and Broadway musical of the same name using the music of Swedish pop group ABBA. The film functions as both a sequel and a prequel, interweaving the present-day story with flashbacks to 1979.

Plot

Five years after the events of the first film, Sophie Sheridan prepares for the grand reopening of her mother Donna's hotel on the Greek island of Kalokairi, following Donna's death a year earlier. As Sophie struggles with the reopening, her husband Sky's absence in New York, and storms threatening the celebration, the narrative flashes back to 1979, tracing young Donna's graduation from Oxford, her travels through Europe and Greece, and her romances with the young Harry, Bill, and Sam — the three possible fathers of Sophie. In the present, the celebration is rescued by the arrival of friends and family, including Sophie's grandmother Ruby, Donna's estranged mother, played by Cher. Sophie also discovers she is pregnant, echoing her mother's story.

Musical numbers

The soundtrack features ABBA songs not used in the first film alongside reprises, including "When I Kissed the Teacher", "Waterloo", "Why Did It Have to Be Me?", "I Have a Dream", "Fernando", "The Day Before You Came", "Angel Eyes", "Dancing Queen", and a finale performance of "Super Trouper" by the full cast. The soundtrack album reached number one in the United Kingdom.

Production

Principal photography took place from August to December 2017 on the island of Vis in Croatia, which stood in for the fictional Kalokairi, as well as at Shepperton Studios in England. Björn Ulvaeus and Benny Andersson of ABBA served as executive producers and appear in cameo roles.

Reception

The film received generally positive reviews, with critics praising the performances of Lily James and Cher and the emotional weight of the prequel storyline, and it grossed over $402 million worldwide against a $75 million budget, making it a commercial success.
