Paul Robin Krugman (born 28 February 1953) is an American economist known for founding the "new trade theory" and the new economic geography, and later as a prominent newspaper columnist. He received the 2008 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his analysis of trade patterns and the location of economic activity.

Contributions

Krugman's late-1970s models showed how economies of scale and consumers' love of variety explain why similar countries trade similar goods (intra-industry trade) — overturning the pure comparative advantage story — and why economic activity concentrates geographically. He helped formalise currency-crisis models and, writing for general readers, became a leading Keynesian advocate for fiscal stimulus in the liquidity-trap conditions after 2008, notably in The Return of Depression Economics. From 2000 to 2024 he wrote an op-ed column for The New York Times, making him one of the most widely read economists in the world; he taught at MIT, Stanford, Yale and Princeton, and later at the City University of New York.
