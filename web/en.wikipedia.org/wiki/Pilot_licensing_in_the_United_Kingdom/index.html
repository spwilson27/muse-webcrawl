Pilot licensing in the United Kingdom

Pilot licensing in the United Kingdom is regulated by the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA). The United Kingdom was among the first countries to license pilots: the Royal Aero Club issued its first aviator's certificates in 1910. Following the UK's departure from the European Union, the CAA took over licensing functions formerly exercised under EASA rules, retaining a structure closely modelled on Part-FCL.

Licences

UK licences include the Light Aircraft Pilot Licence (LAPL), Private Pilot Licence (PPL), Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL), and Airline Transport Pilot Licence (ATPL), for aeroplanes, helicopters, and other categories. The older UK national PPL and the NPPL (National Private Pilot Licence) remain in use for non-Part-21 aircraft such as microlights and vintage types.

Training and testing

Training is delivered by approved training organizations and declared training organizations. Applicants pass theoretical knowledge examinations, complete prescribed flight instruction, and pass a skill test with a CAA-authorized examiner. Medical certification ranges from a self-declaration (for some light-aircraft privileges) to the Class 1 medical required of professional pilots.

Ratings

Class and type ratings, instrument ratings, and instructor and examiner certificates are added to licences and periodically revalidated or renewed.
