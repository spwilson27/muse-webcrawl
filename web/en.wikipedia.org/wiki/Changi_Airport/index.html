Changi Airport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Singapore Changi Airport (IATA: SIN, ICAO: WSSS) is the primary civilian airport serving Singapore and one of the largest transportation hubs in Asia. Located in Changi, about 17 kilometres northeast of the city centre, it is the home base of Singapore Airlines and a major hub for passenger and cargo traffic in Southeast Asia.

History

Changi opened on 1 July 1981, replacing Paya Lebar Airport as Singapore's main airport. Built largely on reclaimed land, it has been expanded repeatedly: Terminal 2 opened in 1990, Terminal 3 in 2008, the budget-oriented Terminal 4 in 2017, and the mixed-use Jewel Changi Airport complex, with its Rain Vortex indoor waterfall, in 2019.

Facilities and operations

The airport has four passenger terminals served by the Skytrain people-mover, two main runways (with a third under development as part of the Changi East expansion), and capacity for roughly 85 million passengers a year. Before the COVID-19 pandemic it handled a record 68.3 million passengers in 2019.

Recognition

Changi has frequently been named the world's best airport in the Skytrax awards, praised for its gardens, leisure facilities and efficiency. It also consistently ranks among the busiest airports in the world by international passenger traffic.
