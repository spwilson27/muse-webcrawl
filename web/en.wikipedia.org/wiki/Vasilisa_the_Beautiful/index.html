Vasilisa the Beautiful

Vasilisa the Beautiful (Russian: Василиса Прекрасная) is a Russian fairy tale collected by Alexander Afanasyev in his Narodnye russkie skazki (Russian Folk Tales). It is one of the best-known tales featuring the witch Baba Yaga, and is classified in the Aarne–Thompson–Uther index among tales of the persecuted heroine helped by a magical gift.

Plot

Vasilisa is the daughter of a merchant. On her deathbed, her mother gives her a small wooden doll and tells her to feed it, keep it secret, and ask its help in times of trouble. After her father remarries, her cruel stepmother and stepsisters burden her with work and eventually extinguish the household fire, sending her to the hut of Baba Yaga to fetch light, in the hope that the witch will eat her.

In the forest Vasilisa passes three mysterious riders — white, red, and black — representing day, sun, and night. At Baba Yaga's hut, which stands on chicken legs and is fenced with human bones topped by skulls, the witch sets her a series of impossible tasks, which the doll completes. Vasilisa is finally given a skull lantern whose eyes burn with fire. Returning home, the skull's burning gaze incinerates her stepmother and stepsisters, leaving Vasilisa free. In the tale's conclusion Vasilisa becomes a skilled weaver, and her cloth comes to the attention of the Tsar, whom she marries.

Analysis and adaptations

The tale is a staple of Slavic folklore studies and has been widely illustrated, notably by Ivan Bilibin in his celebrated 1899 edition. It has inspired adaptations in film, theatre, and literature, and the psychoanalyst Clarissa Pinkola Estés popularized a reading of the story as an initiation narrative in Women Who Run with the Wolves.
