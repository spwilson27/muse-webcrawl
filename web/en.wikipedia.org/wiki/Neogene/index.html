Neogene

The Neogene is a geologic period and system that spans 20.45 million years from the end of the Paleogene Period 23.03 million years ago (Ma) to the beginning of the present Quaternary Period 2.58 Ma. It is divided into the Miocene (23.03–5.333 Ma) and Pliocene (5.333–2.58 Ma) epochs. During the Neogene, mammals and birds evolved considerably, continents continued drifting toward their modern positions, and the global climate cooled toward the ice ages of the Quaternary.

Climate and geography

The Neogene saw a long cooling trend from the warm early Miocene climatic optimum. The Isthmus of Panama formed by the late Pliocene (around 3 Ma), joining North and South America, enabling the Great American Interchange of fauna and reshaping ocean circulation. The Mediterranean Sea nearly dried out during the Messinian salinity crisis (5.96–5.33 Ma). Grasslands expanded at the expense of forests in many regions, favouring grazing animals, and Antarctica remained glaciated while northern ice sheets grew toward the end of the period.

Life

Mammals diversified into recognisably modern families: horses, elephants, apes, and whales all evolved substantially. The first hominins appeared in Africa in the later Neogene. Marine life included the giant shark megalodon, which lived through much of the period, and kelp forest ecosystems took shape in the Pacific.

Terminology

The term "Neogene" was coined by Moritz Hörnes in 1853. Older schemes included the Pliocene in a "Tertiary" period and sometimes extended the Neogene into the Pleistocene; the current International Chronostratigraphic Chart places the Neogene/Quaternary boundary at 2.58 Ma.
