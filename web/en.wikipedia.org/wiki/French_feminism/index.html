French feminism

French feminism comprises the theories and movements for women's rights developed in France, from the revolutionary era — when Olympe de Gouges published her Declaration of the Rights of Woman (1791) — through the suffrage campaigns of the early 20th century (French women voted from 1945) to the post-1968 Mouvement de libération des femmes (MLF).

Key figures and ideas

Simone de Beauvoir's The Second Sex (1949) was a foundational existentialist analysis of women's situation. In the 1970s, theorists associated with "French feminism" in Anglophone scholarship — Hélène Cixous, Luce Irigaray and Julia Kristeva — developed accounts of écriture féminine and sexual difference drawing on psychoanalysis and post-structuralism, although the extent to which they form a single school is disputed within France itself. Materialist feminists such as Christine Delphy critiqued this tendency.
