Wetzlar (German pronunciation: [ˈvɛtslaːɐ̯]) is a city in the state of Hesse, Germany, on the River Lahn about 50 kilometres north of Frankfurt am Main. It is the seat of the Lahn-Dill district and a historic centre of the optical and precision-engineering industries, home to Leica Camera and the former Leitz works.

History

Settlements in the area are documented from the 9th century; Wetzlar became a free imperial city in 1180. From 1689 to 1806 it hosted the Reichskammergericht, the Imperial Chamber Court of the Holy Roman Empire, which brought jurists and litigants from across the empire. The young Goethe worked as a legal intern at the court in 1772; his unhappy love for Charlotte Buff, who lived in Wetzlar, inspired The Sorrows of Young Werther (1774), making the town a place of literary pilgrimage. The Lottehaus and the Jerusalemhaus — where Karl Wilhelm Jerusalem, model for Werther's suicide, shot himself — survive as museums.

Wetzlar Cathedral (Wetzlarer Dom), begun in the Romanesque style and continued in Gothic, remains famously unfinished, its two dissimilar towers a symbol of the city.

Industry

In the 19th and 20th centuries Wetzlar grew into a centre of optics and precision mechanics. Ernst Leitz founded his optical works here in 1869; the Leica, the first successful 35 mm camera, was developed in Wetzlar in the 1920s. Today Leica Camera AG, Leica Microsystems, and other optical firms (including Minox and Zeiss suppliers) maintain the tradition, alongside the iron and steel works of the Lahn valley.
