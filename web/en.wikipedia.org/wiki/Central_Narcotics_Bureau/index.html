Central Narcotics Bureau

The Central Narcotics Bureau (CNB; Malay: Biro Narkotik Pusat; Chinese: 中央肃毒局) is a department under the Ministry of Home Affairs of Singapore responsible for tackling the drug problem in the country. Established in 1971, it is the primary drug enforcement agency of Singapore, empowered to investigate offences under the Misuse of Drugs Act and the Criminal Law (Temporary Provisions) Act.

Functions

The CNB conducts enforcement operations against drug traffickers, importers and abusers; supervises drug abusers in Drug Rehabilitation Centres and community-based programmes; and runs preventive drug education aimed especially at young people. Its officers are granted powers of investigation, search, arrest without warrant for drug offences, and urine and hair testing.

Singapore's drug policy context

Singapore maintains one of the world's strictest drug regimes, including the mandatory death penalty for trafficking above statutory thresholds (for example 15 grams of diamorphine or 500 grams of cannabis). The CNB is the operational arm of this "zero tolerance" approach, which Singaporean authorities credit for low rates of drug abuse; the policy attracts sustained criticism from human rights organisations internationally. The Bureau also represents Singapore in regional and international counter-narcotics cooperation, including ASEAN drug enforcement coordination.
