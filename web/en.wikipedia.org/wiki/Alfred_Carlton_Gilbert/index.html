Alfred Carlton Gilbert (February 13, 1884 – January 24, 1961) was an American inventor, athlete, magician, and businessman. He is best known as the inventor of the Erector Set and the founder of the A. C. Gilbert Company of New Haven, Connecticut, a major manufacturer of educational toys, chemistry sets, and microscopes.

Athletic career

Gilbert attended Pacific University and Yale University, where he excelled in gymnastics and track and field. At the 1908 Summer Olympics in London he won the gold medal in the pole vault, sharing the title after clearing 3.71 metres. He set several world records in the event and helped finance his education by performing as a professional magician.

Toy company

Gilbert began by manufacturing magic tricks, then in 1913 introduced the Erector Set, a construction toy of metal beams, bolts, and pulleys inspired by the steel girders he saw erected for railroad electrification near New York. The sets were marketed as educational and became a staple of American childhood. Gilbert famously lobbied the Council of National Defense in 1918 to exempt toys from a wartime ban on non-essential manufacture, earning him the press nickname "the man who saved Christmas."

His company also produced chemistry sets — later controversially including the Gilbert U-238 Atomic Energy Laboratory with radioactive samples — American Flyer model trains, and science kits. Gilbert wrote an autobiography, The Man Who Lives in Paradise (1954). The company declined after his death and was sold in 1966.

Legacy

The A. C. Gilbert's Discovery Village children's museum in Salem, Oregon, preserves his childhood home and celebrates his work. Gilbert is a member of the National Toy Hall of Fame's roster of influential inventors, and his Erector Set was inducted into the hall in 1998.
