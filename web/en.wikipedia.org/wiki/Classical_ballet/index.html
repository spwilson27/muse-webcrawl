Classical ballet is any of the traditional, formal styles of ballet that exclusively employ classical ballet technique. It is known for its aesthetics and rigorous technique (such as pointe work, turnout of the legs, and high extensions), its flowing, precise movements, and its ethereal qualities. The classical repertory reached its peak in late 19th-century Russia with the works of choreographer Marius Petipa and composer Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky.

History

Ballet originated in the Italian Renaissance courts of the 15th century and was developed into a concert dance form in France under Louis XIV, who founded the Académie Royale de Danse in 1661. In the 19th century, the Romantic ballet — exemplified by La Sylphide (1832) and Giselle (1841) — emphasized ethereal female dancers en pointe. The art form's center then shifted to Imperial Russia, where Petipa's grand spectacles for the Imperial Ballet in St. Petersburg defined the classical style: multi-act story ballets with divertissements, pas de deux, and corps de ballet formations.

Technique

Classical technique is built on five basic positions of the feet, turnout (outward rotation of the legs from the hips), and a codified vocabulary of steps mostly named in French. Major training systems include the Vaganova method (Russian), the Cecchetti method (Italian), the Bournonville method (Danish), and the system of the Royal Academy of Dance (English). Female dancers perform en pointe, wearing specially reinforced pointe shoes; both sexes train in jumps, turns, lifts, and partnering.

Structure of a classical ballet

A full-length classical ballet typically unfolds in two to four acts telling a story through mime and dance. Set pieces follow a conventional hierarchy: ensemble dances for the corps de ballet, small-group dances, pas de deux for the principal couple (with an entrée, adagio, variations for each dancer, and a coda), and virtuosic solos. Productions are presented by major companies including the Bolshoi Ballet, Mariinsky Ballet, Royal Ballet, Paris Opera Ballet, and American Ballet Theatre.
