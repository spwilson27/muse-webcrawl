Satay

Satay (Indonesian and Malaysian spelling: sate), is a dish of seasoned, skewered and grilled meat, served with a sauce. The earliest preparations of satay is believed to have originated in Javanese cuisine, but has spread throughout almost all of Indonesia, where it has become a national dish. It is also popular in many other Southeast Asian countries including Brunei, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. It is also recognized and popular in Suriname and the Netherlands.

Satay may consist of diced or sliced chicken, goat, mutton, beef, pork, fish, other meats, or tofu; bamboo skewers are often used, while rustic style of preparations use skewers from the midrib of the coconut palm frond. These are grilled or barbecued over a wood or charcoal fire, then served with various spicy seasonings. Satay can be served in various sauces; most commonly combination of soy and peanut sauce, hence it is often served with peanut sauce. However, other sauces are also used.

Origins

According to a popular theory, satay was developed by Javanese street vendors as an adaptation of Indian kebabs, introduced by Muslim traders from India and the Arab world. Another theory states that satay originated in Java, where grilled skewered meat was already known as a snack sold by street vendors as early as the 19th century. From Java, satay spread throughout the Malay Archipelago and, as a consequence, many variations of the dish have evolved.

Ingredients and preparation

Turmeric is an essential ingredient in satay marinade, giving the dish its characteristic yellow colour. Meats commonly used include chicken, beef, lamb, goat, pork, rabbit and offal. The meat is marinated, threaded onto skewers — traditionally made from bamboo, or in some regions from coconut palm frond midribs — and grilled over a charcoal fire. It is frequently accompanied by lontong (compressed rice cakes), ketupat, cucumber and onion.

Regional variations

Indonesia has been described as the home of satay, and the dish is widely available across the archipelago. Famous variants include sate Madura from Madura island, sate Padang from West Sumatra (served with a thick spicy curry-like sauce), sate lilit from Bali (minced meat wrapped around lemongrass stalks), sate buntel from Solo, and sate Ponorogo. In Malaysia and Singapore, satay is commonly served with peanut sauce, ketupat, cucumber and onions; Kajang in Selangor is renowned as a satay town. The Netherlands adopted satay (as saté) through its colonial ties with Indonesia, where peanut sauce is a common accompaniment to fries and grilled meats.
