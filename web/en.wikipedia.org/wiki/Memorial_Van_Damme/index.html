Memorial Van Damme

The Memorial Van Damme is an annual track and field athletics meeting held at the King Baudouin Stadium in Brussels, Belgium. First staged in 1977, it was created in memory of Ivo Van Damme, the Belgian middle-distance runner who won silver medals in the 800 metres and 1500 metres at the 1976 Montreal Olympics and was killed in a car accident later that year, aged 22.

History

Organised initially by a group of journalists shortly after Van Damme's death, the meeting grew into one of the world's leading one-day athletics events. It was part of the IAAF Golden League from the competition's inception in 1998 until 2009, and since 2010 the Memorial Van Damme has traditionally hosted the final of the Diamond League season, at which many annual Diamond Race winners are decided.

Notable performances

The meeting has witnessed numerous world records. Among the most famous: Sebastian Coe's 800 m record in 1981, and on 26 August 2005 Kenenisa Bekele set the 10,000 metres world record of 26:17.53, which stood for fifteen years. In 2020, Sifan Hassan and Mo Farah set world records in the rarely contested one-hour run at a specially staged edition. The meeting is also famous for its enthusiastic crowds, post-meeting concerts, and fireworks display that close the evening.
