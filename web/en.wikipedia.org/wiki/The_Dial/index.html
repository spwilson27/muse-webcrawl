The Dial was an American magazine published under the same title in two distinct periods. In its first incarnation (1840–1844) it was the journal of the Transcendentalists, edited successively by Margaret Fuller and Ralph Waldo Emerson and publishing Henry David Thoreau, among others.

Modernist revival

Revived in 1880 as a political and literary review and relaunched in 1920 under Scofield Thayer and James Sibley Watson, The Dial became one of the leading modernist magazines in English. It published T. S. Eliot — including the first American appearance of The Waste Land in 1922, which won the magazine's Dial Award — as well as Ezra Pound, William Butler Yeats, D. H. Lawrence, Thomas Mann, and E. E. Cummings, and reproduced works by modern artists such as Picasso. Marianne Moore served as editor from 1925 until the magazine ceased publication in 1929.
