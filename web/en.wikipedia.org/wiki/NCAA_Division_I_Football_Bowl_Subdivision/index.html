The NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) is the highest level of college football in the United States. The FBS consists of the largest schools in Division I, whose football programmes are generally the biggest sources of revenue for their universities' athletic departments. Unlike other NCAA divisions, the FBS champion is not determined by an NCAA-run event; instead, the champion emerges from the College Football Playoff, a system of bowl games and a national championship game administered separately from the NCAA.

FBS football features the largest stadiums and budgets in college sport.

Overview

FBS programmes must meet minimum attendance requirements (an average of 15,000 in actual or paid attendance over a rolling two-year period) and may award up to 85 full football scholarships. Teams play a 12-game regular season, usually within one of ten conferences: the five historically wealthiest "Power" conferences — the SEC, Big Ten, ACC, Big 12, and (much diminished after 2024 realignment) the Pac-12 — and the "Group of Five" conferences. Notre Dame competes as an independent in football. A postseason of more than 40 bowl games culminates the season.

College Football Playoff

From 2014 the FBS title was decided by a four-team College Football Playoff selected by a committee; rankings from the Associated Press and Coaches polls, and previously the BCS (1998–2013), structured the debate before it. The playoff expanded to 12 teams beginning with the 2024 season, with byes for the highest-ranked conference champions in the initial format and automatic bids for conference champions in later revisions. The championship game is among the most-watched broadcasts in American television.

History

Division I football was split into I-A and I-AA in 1978, renamed FBS and FCS in 2006. Television money transformed the subdivision from the 1984 Supreme Court decision in NCAA v. Board of Regents, which freed schools and conferences to negotiate their own TV deals. A series of conference realignments — accelerating in the 2010s and again in 2023–24, when most of the Pac-12 departed for the Big Ten, Big 12, and ACC — concentrated media revenue in the SEC and Big Ten. The arrival of NIL compensation and the House revenue-sharing settlement in the mid-2020s pushed FBS football further toward a professionalised model.
