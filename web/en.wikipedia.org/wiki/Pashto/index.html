Pashto (پښتو‎) is an Eastern Iranian language in the Indo-European language family, natively spoken in north-western Pakistan and southern and eastern Afghanistan. It is the first language of the Pashtun people and one of the two official languages of Afghanistan, alongside Dari. In Pakistan it is a provincial language of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and northern Balochistan. Estimates of the number of speakers vary widely, from around 40 million to as many as 60 million.

History

Pashto descends from an Eastern Iranian language closely related to ancient tongues of the region such as Avestan and Bactrian, though its precise origins remain debated among linguists. Literary Pashto emerged in the early modern period; the poet Khushal Khan Khattak (1613–1689) is regarded as a foundational figure of Pashto literature, and the warrior-poet Rahman Baba (c. 1632–1708) remains its most beloved classical author. The Bayazid Ansari (Pir Rokhan) movement of the 16th century produced some of the earliest surviving Pashto prose.

Dialects

Pashto dialects are conventionally divided into two main groups: the "soft" southern dialects centred on Kandahar (including the prestige Kandahari dialect) and the "hard" northern dialects centred on Peshawar and Kabul. The division is marked most visibly by the pronunciation of the letters ښ and ږ, which differ between the two standards. The language has official status in Afghanistan with a standard based largely on the Kandahari dialect, while Pakistani Pashto media use northern varieties.

Grammar

Pashto is a subject–object–verb language with split ergativity: in past-tense transitive clauses the verb agrees with the object and the subject appears in the oblique case. Nouns are masculine or feminine and inflect for two cases (direct and oblique) and number. The language has a rich system of verbal aspect and a retroflex consonant series inherited from its Iranian roots, combined with sounds absorbed from neighbouring Indo-Aryan languages.

Writing system

Pashto is written in a modified Perso-Arabic script of 44 letters, including characters unique to Pashto such as ټ, ډ, ړ, ږ, ښ, ڼ, and ې. It is written from right to left. A Latin-based orthography has been proposed at various times but never adopted for general use.
