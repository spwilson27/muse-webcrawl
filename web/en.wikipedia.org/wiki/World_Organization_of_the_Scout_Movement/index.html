The World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM) is the largest international Scouting organization, with member organizations in more than 170 countries and tens of millions of members, predominantly young people. It was established in 1922 as the international coordinating body of national Scout associations and is headquartered in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, with the World Scout Bureau as its secretariat. Its female counterpart is the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS).

Structure

WOSM's governing bodies are the World Scout Conference, which meets every three years and elects the World Scout Committee, and the World Scout Bureau, headed by the Secretary General. The organization works through six regions: Africa, Arab, Asia-Pacific, Eurasia, Europe and Interamerica. It generally recognises only one member organization per country, which in many countries is a federation of associations, some of them coeducational and some working alongside separate Guide associations.

Activities

WOSM organizes the World Scout Jamboree, held every four years since 1920, as well as the World Scout Moot for older members and the annual Jamboree on the Air and Jamboree on the Internet. It coordinates global programmes on the environment, peace education and community service, and in the 21st century aligned its work with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals through initiatives such as Scouts for SDGs.

History

Scouting was founded by Robert Baden-Powell in 1907 and spread rapidly around the world. An international bureau was set up in London in 1920 following the first World Scout Jamboree, and the International Conference of Scout associations was formally constituted in Paris in 1922, adopting the name World Organization of the Scout Movement in 1959. The World Scout Bureau moved from London to Ottawa in 1959, then to Geneva in 1968, and the secretariat relocated to Kuala Lumpur in 2013–14.
