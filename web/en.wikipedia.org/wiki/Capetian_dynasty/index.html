Capetian dynasty

The Capetian dynasty is a dynasty of Frankish origin, founded by Hugh Capet, that ruled the Kingdom of France from 987 to 1328 in the direct line, and continued through cadet branches — Valois, Bourbon and Orléans — until 1848, with restorations. It is one of Europe's oldest and largest royal houses.

Origins

The Capetians descended from the Robertians, counts of Paris. Hugh Capet was elected king in 987 after the Carolingians died out. Early Capetians secured hereditary succession by crowning sons in their own lifetimes.

Direct Capetians

Kings such as Philip II Augustus, Louis IX (Saint Louis) and Philip IV expanded royal power and territory. The direct line ended when the three sons of Philip IV died without surviving male heirs (1316–1328), triggering the succession that led to the Valois and, in dispute with England, the Hundred Years' War.

Cadet branches

Capetian branches ruled Burgundy, Naples, Sicily, Hungary, Poland, Navarre, Portugal and Spain; the Bourbon branch still reigns in Spain and Luxembourg's grand ducal family descends from it.
