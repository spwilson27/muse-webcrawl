John III Sobieski

John III Sobieski (Polish: Jan III Sobieski; 17 August 1629 – 17 June 1696) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1674 until his death. A renowned military commander, he is famous for defeating the Ottoman Empire at the Battle of Vienna in 1683, leading the largest cavalry charge in history and halting Ottoman expansion into Europe.

Early life and military career

Born at Olesko Castle into a noble family, Sobieski was educated in Kraków and travelled in Western Europe. He fought against Cossacks, Swedes during the Deluge, Tatars, and Ottomans, becoming Grand Hetman of the Crown in 1668. His victory at Khotyn (1673) strengthened his election as king in 1674.

Reign and Vienna

As king he sought to strengthen royal power and reform the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth amid magnate opposition. In 1683 he allied with the Holy Roman Empire in the Holy League; at Vienna on 12 September 1683 his relief army, with Polish winged hussars, routed Kara Mustafa's Ottomans. Pope Innocent XI hailed him as saviour of Christendom.

Later campaigns in Moldavia were less successful. He died at Wilanów Palace, which he had built for his beloved wife Marie Casimire, to whom his famous letters were written.
