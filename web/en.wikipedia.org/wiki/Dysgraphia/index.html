Dysgraphia
SpecialtyNeuropsychology; pediatrics
SymptomsIllegible handwriting, poor spelling, difficulty expressing thoughts in writing
Usual onsetWhen handwriting is first taught
TypesDyslexic, motor, spatial (and others)

Dysgraphia is a neurological disorder and learning disability characterised by impaired handwriting and, in many cases, difficulty with spelling and putting thoughts on paper. It is classified in the DSM-5 under specific learning disorder with impairment in written expression. Dysgraphia commonly occurs alongside dyslexia and ADHD but is a distinct condition.

Signs and symptoms

People with dysgraphia typically produce handwriting that is illegible or inconsistent, with irregular letter sizes and shapes, a mixture of print and cursive, awkward pencil grip and hand pain or cramping while writing. Writing is often slow and laborious, spacing within and between words is uneven, and spelling errors are frequent. Because transcription is not automatic, the effort of forming letters consumes working memory, so written work may fall far short of the person's oral expression and general intelligence.

Subtypes and causes

Clinicians distinguish, among other forms, dyslexic dysgraphia (poor spelling and free writing with relatively preserved copying), motor dysgraphia (poor fine-motor control affecting both writing and copying), and spatial dysgraphia (problems with spacing and using the page). Developmental dysgraphia arises as part of neurodevelopment; acquired dysgraphia (agraphia) results from brain injury or disease affecting writing-related areas.

Diagnosis and support

Diagnosis is made through educational and neuropsychological assessment of handwriting, spelling and fine-motor skills, ruling out lack of instruction or other conditions. Management includes occupational therapy for motor skills, explicit handwriting and spelling instruction, and accommodations such as extra time, reduced copying demands and access to keyboards and speech-to-text tools, which allow students to demonstrate knowledge without the transcription bottleneck.
