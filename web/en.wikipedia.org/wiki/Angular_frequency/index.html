Angular frequency

In physics, angular frequency (symbol ω), also called angular speed and angular rate, is a scalar measure of the angle through which a rotating or oscillating object turns per unit of time, or equivalently the rate of change of the phase of a sinusoidal waveform.

Angular frequency is the rate of change of phase angle
Angular frequency
Symbolω
SI unitradian per second (rad/s)
Relation to frequencyω = 2πf
DimensionT−1

Definition

For a periodic phenomenon of ordinary frequency f (cycles per second, in hertz) and period T, the angular frequency is

ω = 2πf = 2π / T.

One complete cycle corresponds to an angle of 2π radians, so angular frequency measures how many radians of phase are swept per unit time. For a point in uniform circular motion, ω is the rate of change of the polar angle θ: ω = dθ/dt.

Examples

The Earth's rotation has an angular frequency of about 7.29 × 10−5 rad/s. The second hand of a clock rotates at ω = 2π/60 ≈ 0.105 rad/s. In alternating-current circuits, mains electricity at 50 Hz corresponds to ω ≈ 314 rad/s (377 rad/s at 60 Hz).

Applications

Angular frequency is used throughout physics and engineering because it simplifies the mathematics of oscillation and waves: the equation of a harmonic oscillator is written ω0 = √(k/m) for its natural (resonant) angular frequency, and waves satisfy ω = 2πc/λ. In spring–mass systems, LC circuits and signal processing, using ω avoids repeated factors of 2π in differential equations and Fourier transforms. The related concept of angular velocity is a vector quantity directed along the axis of rotation by the right-hand rule.
