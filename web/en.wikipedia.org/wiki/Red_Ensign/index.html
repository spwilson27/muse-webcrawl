Red Ensign

Red Ensign
Design: Red field with the Union Jack in the canton
Use: Civil ensign of the United Kingdom
Adopted in present form: 1801 (earlier versions from the 17th century)

The Red Ensign, sometimes called the "Red Duster", is the civil ensign of the United Kingdom: a red field with the Union Jack in the canton. It is flown by British merchant ships and by private British vessels, and forms the basis of many ensigns of British colonies, former colonies, and overseas territories.

History

The Red Ensign originated in the early 17th century as a flag of English ships. In 1674 Charles II proclaimed it the ensign of English merchantmen. Originally the canton bore the cross of St George; after the Union of 1707 it bore the combined crosses of St George and St Andrew, and after 1801 the modern Union Jack, including the saltire of St Patrick. The Merchant Shipping Act 1894 confirmed it as the national colours for British merchant vessels. Until 1864 the Red Ensign was also flown by the Royal Navy's Red Squadron; after the 1864 reorganisation the White Ensign became the naval ensign, the Blue Ensign the government ensign, and the Red Ensign was reserved to the merchant service.

Defaced ensigns

The plain ensign may not be used by all organisations: yacht clubs with Admiralty warrants, government departments, and Crown dependencies fly defaced Red Ensigns bearing a badge in the fly. Colonial and Commonwealth flags derived from it include the former Canadian Red Ensign (Canada's unofficial national flag until 1965), the Australian Red Ensign, and the flags of Bermuda, Gibraltar, and other territories, as well as the ensigns of Manitoba and Ontario.
