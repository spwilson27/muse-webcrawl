Economic sector

An economic sector is a category used to group together areas of an economy according to the type of activity performed. The best-known scheme is the three-sector model, which divides economic activity into primary, secondary and tertiary sectors. Economists and statisticians use sector classifications to measure output, employment and structural change.

The three-sector model

The primary sector extracts and harvests natural resources: agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining and quarrying. The secondary sector transforms raw materials into finished goods through manufacturing, construction and utilities. The tertiary sector provides services — retail, transport, finance, health care, education, entertainment and government — rather than physical products.

Later writers added a quaternary sector covering knowledge-based activities such as research, information technology and consulting, and sometimes a quinary sector for the highest levels of decision-making and human services.

Structural change

The three-sector model, developed in the 1930s and 1940s by economists including Allan Fisher, Colin Clark and Jean Fourastié, holds that as economies develop, labour and output shift from agriculture to manufacturing and then to services. This pattern of structural change is visible in the long-run statistics of most industrialised countries, where services now account for the large majority of employment.

Other classifications

Statistical agencies use detailed industry classifications such as the International Standard Industrial Classification (ISIC) and the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS). A separate distinction divides the economy into the public sector, the private sector and the voluntary or third sector.
