Citrate

A citrate is a salt or ester of citric acid. Citric acid is a tribasic acid, so citrates can carry charges of −1, −2 or −3 depending on the degree of deprotonation; the fully deprotonated citrate ion has the formula C6H5O73−.

Biochemistry

Citrate is a key intermediate of the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle): it is formed in mitochondria by citrate synthase from oxaloacetate and acetyl-CoA, and is then isomerised to isocitrate by aconitase. Cytosolic citrate also serves as a building block for fatty acid synthesis and as a regulatory signal that inhibits the glycolytic enzyme phosphofructokinase.

Uses

Sodium citrate is used as a food additive (E331) for acidity regulation and emulsification, and as an anticoagulant in stored blood, where it binds calcium ions. Potassium citrate is used medically to prevent certain kidney stones by making the urine less acidic. Ferric citrate and other metal citrates are used as mineral supplements and in photography and blueprinting processes.
