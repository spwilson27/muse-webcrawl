Juggling

Juggling is a physical skill in which objects are repeatedly thrown into the air and caught, keeping more objects in motion than the performer has hands. The most recognizable form is toss juggling with balls, clubs, or rings.

History

Juggling is ancient: tomb paintings at Beni Hasan in Egypt (c. 1994–1781 BC) depict jugglers, and the skill was practised in China, India, Greece, and Rome. In medieval Europe jugglers were itinerant entertainers, and the modern juggler emerged in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century variety theatre and circus. Juggling enjoyed a revival from the 1980s, with conventions, the International Jugglers' Association (founded 1947), and mathematical analysis through siteswap notation.

Techniques

The basic three-ball pattern is the cascade; the reverse cascade, shower, columns, and Mills' mess are common variations. Jugglers also practise club passing in pairs and groups, contact juggling (rolling balls on the body), bounce juggling, and manipulation of diabolo, devil sticks, cigar boxes, and poi.

Records and culture

Competitive juggling includes endurance and numbers records — the most balls successfully juggled is fourteen for a brief flash. Juggling is also used in education, therapy, and recreational communities worldwide.
