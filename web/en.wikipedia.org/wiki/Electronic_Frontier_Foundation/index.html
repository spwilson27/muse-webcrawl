Founders: Kapor, Gilmore, Barlow

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) is an international non-profit digital rights group based in San Francisco, California. The foundation was formed on July 10, 1990, by Mitch Kapor, John Gilmore, and John Perry Barlow to promote Internet civil liberties. It provides funds for legal defense in court, presents amicus curiae briefs, defends individuals and new technologies from what it considers abusive legal threats, works to expose government malfeasance, provides guidance to courts, and organizes political action.

History

The EFF was founded in response to a series of actions by American law enforcement agencies, most notably the 1990 Secret Service raids associated with Operation Sundevil, which the founders saw as threatening free expression and due process online. One early case involved Steve Jackson Games, whose offices were raided and whose electronic mail was seized; the resulting lawsuit helped establish legal protections for email privacy.

In 1995, the EFF supported the plaintiffs in Bernstein v. United States, challenging export restrictions that treated cryptographic source code as a munition; the courts ultimately recognized code as protected speech. John Perry Barlow's 1996 essay "A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace", written at Davos, became an influential statement of early Internet libertarianism.

Activities

The EFF engages in litigation, policy analysis, grassroots activism, and technology development. Its projects have included the HTTPS Everywhere browser extension (developed with the Tor Project), the Certbot ACME client for obtaining free TLS certificates (in support of Let's Encrypt), Privacy Badger, and the Panopticlick/ Cover Your Tracks browser-fingerprinting test. It publishes reports on government surveillance, maintains the Coders' Rights Project, and runs campaigns against mass surveillance programs disclosed in 2013.

Funding and structure

The EFF is supported primarily by donations and is a United States 501(c)(3) non-profit organization. Its staff includes lawyers, technologists, and activists, and it is governed by a board of directors.
