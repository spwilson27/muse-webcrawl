Lotus Cars Limited is a British manufacturer of sports cars and racing cars, headquartered in Hethel, Norfolk, England. Founded by engineer Colin Chapman, Lotus became famous for a philosophy of extreme light weight and fine handling — Chapman summarized it as "Simplify, then add lightness" — and for a Formula One team, Team Lotus, that ranked among the most innovative and successful of the 1960s and 1970s.

History

Chapman built his first trials car, the Mark 1, in 1948, modifying an Austin 7 in a London garage. Lotus Engineering Company was formally established in 1952, and early successes included the featherweight Seven (1957), which survives today in derivative form as the Caterham 7. The glassfibre monocoque Elite (1957) and the mid-engined Europa (1966) established Lotus's road-car reputation, followed by the Elan, Esprit (1976, immortalized in the James Bond film The Spy Who Loved Me), and the front-engined Excel and Eclat. In Formula One, Team Lotus won seven Constructors' Championships between 1963 and 1978 and pioneered the monocoque chassis, ground-effect aerodynamics, and commercial sponsorship liveries, with world titles for Jim Clark, Graham Hill, Jochen Rindt, Emerson Fittipaldi, and Mario Andretti.

Ownership changes

After Chapman's sudden death in 1982, the company passed through several owners: General Motors (1986), Romano Artioli's ACBN Holdings (1993, which launched the Elise, named for Artioli's granddaughter), and Malaysia's Proton (1996). In 2017 China's Geely acquired a majority stake and funded a major expansion, including an all-electric future built around new platforms and production in both England and China.

Modern models

The Elise (1996), Exige, and Evora carried the lightweight philosophy into the 21st century, all using bonded aluminium chassis. The Emira (2021) was launched as Lotus's last internal-combustion sports car. The electric Evija hypercar, targeted at nearly 2,000 horsepower, became the flagship of the marque's electrification, followed by the Eletre electric SUV and Emeya saloon built on the company's Electric Premium Architecture. Lotus Engineering continues to provide consultancy to other manufacturers.

Motorsport legacy

Team Lotus competed in Formula One from 1958 to 1994, winning 74 Grands Prix and pioneering innovations — the monocoque Lotus 25, the wedge-shaped Lotus 72, and the ground-effect Lotus 78 and 79 — that reshaped racing car design. Ayrton Senna's early Grand Prix wins came with the team in the mid-1980s. The Lotus name later returned to Formula One in various forms through licensing agreements.
