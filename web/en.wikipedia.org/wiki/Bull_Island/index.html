Bull Island (Irish: Oileán an Tairbh) is a low-lying island in Dublin Bay, Ireland, about 5 kilometres long, lying parallel to the shore from Clontarf to Sutton. It is an artificial island: it grew from a sandbank behind the North Bull Wall, a sea wall completed in the 1820s to keep Dublin's shipping channel clear of silt. The island is a noted nature reserve and recreational area, containing Dollymount Strand, two golf courses, and extensive dune, saltmarsh, and mudflat habitats of international importance for birds.

Formation

Ships entering Dublin Port were long troubled by silting at the river mouth. After surveys by Captain William Bligh and others, the North Bull Wall was built between 1820 and 1825. The wall scoured the channel deeper, and the expelled sand accumulated behind it, creating the island, which has continued to grow slowly seaward. The island is joined to the mainland by a wooden bridge at Clontarf and a causeway built in 1964.

Nature reserve

Bull Island is protected as a nature reserve and forms part of the Dublin Bay Biosphere Reserve designated by UNESCO. Its mudflats and saltmarsh support tens of thousands of wintering waders and wildfowl, including light-bellied brent geese that migrate from Arctic Canada. The dunes hold rare plants and an important population of Irish hares. An interpretive centre explains the island's ecology and history.

Recreation

Dollymount Strand, the island's long sandy beach, is popular for walking, swimming, and kitesurfing. The island hosts two golf clubs, including the Royal Dublin Golf Club, founded in 1885. St Anne's Park on the adjacent mainland was formerly part of the Guinness family's estate connected with the island's development.
