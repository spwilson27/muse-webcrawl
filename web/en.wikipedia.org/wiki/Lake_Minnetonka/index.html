Lake Minnetonka is the ninth-largest lake in Minnesota, a 14,528-acre (5,879 ha) lake in Hennepin and Carver counties west of Minneapolis. It is formed of a complex system of bays, channels and islands — some 125 miles (200 km) of shoreline in all — and drains eastward through Minnehaha Creek to the Mississippi River at Minneapolis. Its name comes from the Dakota phrase Mní iá Tháŋka, "water big speakers", commonly rendered "big water".

History

The lake was sacred to the Dakota people, and its shores were ceded by the Dakota in the Treaty of Traverse des Sioux in 1851; the first European to describe it in print was Joseph Nicollet's expedition mapping of the region. After the American Civil War the lake became one of the Midwest's great resort areas: excursion steamers such as the side-wheeler City of Saint Louis carried thousands of visitors, grand hotels rose at Excelsior, Wayzata and Spring Park, and James J. Hill's streetcar-boat system linked the lake to Minneapolis. Big Island hosted an amusement park from 1906 to 1911.

The grand-hotel era faded by the 1920s, and the lakeshore became predominantly wealthy residential suburbs. The Tonka Toys company took its name from the lake.

Geography and ecology

The lake is divided conventionally into an Upper Lake and Lower Lake with bays including Wayzata Bay, Smithtown Bay, St. Louis Bay, Crystal Bay and Carson's Bay; Big Island is its largest island. It is a popular fishery for walleye, northern pike, muskellunge and bass. Eurasian watermilfoil, zebra mussels and other invasive species are managed by the Lake Minnetonka Conservation District, the body regulating lake use.

Communities

Shore cities include Excelsior, Wayzata, Deephaven, Shorewood, Minnetonka Beach, Spring Park, Mound, Orono, Tonka Bay and Greenwood.

In culture

The musician Prince, a Minneapolis native, popularised the lake in the 1984 film Purple Rain, in which his character tells Apollonia to "purify yourself in the waters of Lake Minnetonka" — a scene actually filmed at a different lake, but one that made Minnetonka a pop-culture reference.
