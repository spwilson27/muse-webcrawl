Pinball

Pinball is a type of arcade game in which a ball rolls and is propelled and manipulated on a specially designed table by the player, scoring points by striking various targets, usually at the instigation of timed play. The primary objective is to score as many points as possible by hitting targets and making various shots with flippers, before the ball is lost (drains).

History

Pinball evolved from outdoor rolling games such as bowls and ground billiards. Tabletop bagatelle games in 18th- and 19th-century France and Britain added pins and spring plungers. Coin-operated machines appeared in the 1930s; the addition of electrically powered flippers in 1947 (with Gottlieb's Humpty Dumpty) transformed pinball into a game of skill rather than chance, helping to overturn bans that had treated it as gambling.

Gameplay

The player launches the ball with a spring-loaded plunger and controls flippers with buttons on the cabinet sides. The ball interacts with bumpers, ramps, targets, spinners, and magnets. Modern solid-state machines add electronic displays, multiball modes, and licensed themes from films, music, and comics.

Manufacturers

Historic manufacturers include Gottlieb, Bally, and Williams. Stern Pinball is the oldest surviving major manufacturer, joined in the 2010s by new companies such as Jersey Jack Pinball, reflecting a revival of interest in competitive and home pinball.

Competition

Competitive pinball is organized by bodies such as the International Flipper Pinball Association, which maintains world rankings, and the Professional and Amateur Pinball Association.
