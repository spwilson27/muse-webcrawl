Australian Industrial Relations Commission

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A commission hearing room in Melbourne

The Australian Industrial Relations Commission (AIRC), known between 1956 and 1988 as the Commonwealth Conciliation and Arbitration Commission, was the national industrial relations tribunal of Australia from 1988 until 2009. It was the descendant of the Commonwealth Court of Conciliation and Arbitration established in 1904 under the conciliation and arbitration power of the Australian Constitution, and played a central role in Australia's distinctive system of compulsory conciliation and arbitration of industrial disputes.

History

The original Court, whose first president was H. B. Higgins, set the 1907 Harvester judgment establishing a "fair and reasonable" basic wage for an unskilled labourer supporting a family of five—an enduring landmark of Australian social policy. Following the Boilermakers' case of 1956, which held that a federal tribunal could not exercise both judicial and arbitral functions, the court's functions were split between the Commonwealth Industrial Court and the Conciliation and Arbitration Commission. The Industrial Relations Act 1988 renamed the body the Australian Industrial Relations Commission.

Functions

The Commission's members—presidential members, senior deputy presidents, deputy presidents, and commissioners—resolved industrial disputes by conciliation and, where necessary, arbitration; made and varied industrial awards setting minimum wages and conditions; certified enterprise agreements; heard unfair dismissal claims; and dealt with union registration and demarcation disputes. Its national wage cases and safety net reviews periodically adjusted award wages across the economy.

Decline and replacement

The Workplace Relations Act 1996 reduced the Commission's award-making powers to a set of "allowable matters" and introduced statutory individual agreements. The Howard government's WorkChoices legislation of 2006 transferred wage-setting to the Australian Fair Pay Commission and stripped the AIRC of much of its role, including most unfair dismissal jurisdiction. Under the Fair Work Act 2009 of the Rudd government, the Commission was abolished at the end of 2009 and its functions absorbed into Fair Work Australia (since 2013, the Fair Work Commission).
