Theodora (wife of Justinian I)
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

Theodora (c. 500 – 28 June 548) was Byzantine empress as the wife of Justinian I, and one of the most powerful women of the ancient/medieval world. Born, according to Procopius's hostile Secret History, into the world of the Hippodrome as the daughter of a bear-keeper and herself an actress, she became Justinian's wife in 525 after he changed the law to permit the marriage.

During the Nika riots of 532, when Justinian was urged to flee, Theodora reportedly insisted that 'purple makes a fine shroud' — the court stayed, and the revolt was crushed. She influenced legislation improving women's position (against forced prostitution, on divorce and property) and protected Miaphysite Christians despite Justinian's Chalcedonian policy. She died in 548, possibly of cancer, predeceasing Justinian by 17 years, and is depicted beside him in the San Vitale mosaics at Ravenna.
