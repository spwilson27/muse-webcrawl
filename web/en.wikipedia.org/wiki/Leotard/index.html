Leotard

A leotard is a close-fitting one-piece garment that covers the torso but leaves the legs exposed, worn by dancers, gymnasts, acrobats, and athletes. It is named after the French acrobat Jules Léotard (1838–1870), the pioneering flying trapeze artist who popularised the garment in his performances and called it his maillot.

History

Léotard's knitted one-piece costume allowed freedom of movement and showed the performer's musculature; it became standard dress for circus and acrobatic performers in the later 19th century. The word "leotard" itself only became common in English in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In the 20th century, leotards became the standard practice and performance clothing in ballet and gymnastics, and from the 1970s, with the fitness and aerobics boom and fabrics such as Lycra, they entered mainstream fashion and exercise wear.

Styles and use

Leotards vary in sleeve length (sleeveless, short, or long-sleeved), neckline, and leg cut, and are made in cotton, velvet, or stretch synthetics, often decorated for competition with sequins and mesh panels. In artistic gymnastics, competition leotards are regulated by the sport's governing bodies. Related garments include the unitard, which also covers the legs, and the biketard.
