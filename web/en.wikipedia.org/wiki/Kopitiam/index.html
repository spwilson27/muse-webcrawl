Kopitiam
RegionMalaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Brunei, Southern Thailand
TypeCoffee shop / food court
Main itemsKopi (coffee), teh (tea), kaya toast, soft-boiled eggs, noodles

A kopitiam is a traditional coffee shop mostly found in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei and Southern Thailand patronised for meals and beverages, originally owned by the local Chinese community. The word kopi is an Indonesian and Malay term for coffee, while tiam is the Hokkien term for shop (店). Hence, a kopitiam is literally a "coffee shop".

Traditional kopitiams are known for their rustic ambience, often with marble-topped round tables and wooden chairs. Food in a kopitiam is usually offered by a few stalls, each specialising in a single or a few types of food, and the coffee shop itself usually operates the drinks stall, serving coffee, tea and soft drinks.

History

The kopitiam culture developed alongside waves of Chinese immigration into British Malaya and the Dutch East Indies in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Hainanese immigrants, who often arrived later than other Chinese dialect groups, found work as cooks and domestic servants in British households and later opened coffee shops of their own. The Hainanese kopitiam became a fixture of towns across the Malay Peninsula and the archipelago.

Kopitiams served as social gathering places where news was exchanged and business conducted informally. During the colonial era they were among the few public spaces where people of different ethnic backgrounds mixed freely.

Food and drink

The archetypal kopitiam breakfast consists of kaya toast — grilled or toasted bread spread with kaya, a coconut and egg jam, and butter — served with soft-boiled eggs seasoned with soy sauce and white pepper, and a cup of kopi.

Kopi in a kopitiam is traditionally made by pouring boiling water through coffee grounds held in a cloth filter known as a "sock". The coffee itself is often roasted with sugar and margarine, giving it a distinctive caramelised flavour. Kopi is ordered using a localised vocabulary: kopi o is black coffee with sugar, kopi c is coffee with evaporated milk and sugar, and kopi peng is iced coffee. Teh tarik, "pulled tea", is made by pouring sweetened tea with condensed milk back and forth between two vessels to create a frothy top.

Other common stalls in a kopitiam sell nasi lemak, char kway teow, laksa, wantan mee, roti canai, economic rice and various noodle soups.

Modern kopitiams

From the late 20th century, modernised kopitiam chains emerged, combining the traditional menu with air-conditioned premises and standardised decor. Chains such as OldTown White Coffee, Ya Kun Kaya Toast, Killiney Kopitiam and Toast Box have expanded regionally and internationally, popularising kopitiam fare well beyond Southeast Asia. At the same time, traditional family-run kopitiams face pressure from rising rents and changing consumer habits, prompting heritage-preservation efforts in cities such as George Town and Singapore.
