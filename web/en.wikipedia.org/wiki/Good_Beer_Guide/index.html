The Good Beer Guide is an annual guidebook to pubs and breweries in the United Kingdom, published by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) since 1974. It lists around 4,500 pubs selected by CAMRA's local branch members for the quality of their cask-conditioned ("real") ale, together with a comprehensive directory of British breweries.

Selection

Entries are chosen by CAMRA's roughly 200 local branches, whose members survey and score pubs on beer quality throughout the year; inclusion is regarded as a mark of prestige in the pub trade, and the guide also recognizes a Pub of the Year competition culminating in a national winner. Unlike commercial guides, inclusion cannot be bought.

History

The first edition appeared in 1974, shortly after CAMRA's founding in 1971, at a time when traditional cask ale was being displaced by keg beer from large national brewers. Early editions were famously outspoken — the 1975 edition's blunt advice about one major brewer's products had to be revised before printing. The guide has since become the standard reference for British beer and pubs, spawning a companion Good Bottled Beer Guide and an app edition.
