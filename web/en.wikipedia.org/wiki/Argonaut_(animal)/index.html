The argonauts (genus Argonauta), also called paper nautiluses, are a group of pelagic octopuses. Females secrete a delicate, paper-thin calcareous egg case — often mistaken for a true shell — in which they brood their eggs and which they can use as a flotation device by trapping air at the surface.

Description

Argonauts are strongly sexually dimorphic: females grow far larger than the tiny males, which lack the egg case. The female's "shell" is secreted by two specialized dorsal arms with expanded webs; unlike the shell of a true nautilus, it is not attached to the body and is carried by the arms. The case is ribbed, laterally compressed, and planispiral.

Species and behavior

Recognized species include the greater argonaut Argonauta argo, the winged argonaut A. hians, and the knobby argonaut A. nodosa. Argonauts live in surface waters of tropical and temperate seas worldwide, feeding on small crustaceans, mollusks, and fish. Males transfer sperm with a detachable hectocotylus arm that remains in the female's mantle cavity — early naturalists famously mistook this arm for a parasitic worm.
