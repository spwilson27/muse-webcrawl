The Suwannee River (also spelled Suwanee) is a major river that runs through south Georgia southward into Florida in the southern United States. It is a wild blackwater river, about 246 miles (396 km) long, famed in American popular culture as the "Swanee" of Stephen Foster's song "Old Folks at Home".

The Suwannee near White Springs, Florida

Course

The river rises in the Okefenokee Swamp in southeastern Georgia, flows southwest into Florida, and is joined by the Alapaha and Withlacoochee rivers before turning south past high limestone banks and numerous springs. Near its lower course it receives the Santa Fe River and empties into the Gulf of Mexico near the town of Suwannee. Much of its Florida corridor is protected within the Suwannee River State Park and associated water-management lands, and it is noted for Gulf sturgeon and its spring-fed tributaries.

In culture

Stephen Foster made the river famous in 1851 with "Old Folks at Home" ("Way down upon the Swanee River"), choosing the name for its metre though he never visited it; the song became Florida's state song in 1935 (with lyrics revised in 2008). George Gershwin's "Swanee" (1919) reinforced the shortened name in popular usage.
