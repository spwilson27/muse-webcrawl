Max Bense (7 February 1910 – 29 April 1990) was a German philosopher, writer and publicist, known for his work in the philosophy of science, logic, aesthetics and semiotics, and for his pioneering role in computer art and generative aesthetics.

Life and work

Bense studied mathematics, physics, philosophy and geology at the universities of Bonn, Cologne and Basel. From 1949 he was professor at the Technical University of Stuttgart, where he taught philosophy and the theory of science until 1978. In Stuttgart he built an influential intellectual circle around the journal Augenblick and the gallery he founded, attracting writers and artists of the post-war avant-garde.

Information aesthetics

In his four-volume Aesthetica (1954–1960), Bense applied information theory, following Claude Shannon, and Charles Sanders Peirce's semiotics to art and literature, proposing that aesthetic states could be analysed as distributions of signs and measured in terms of information. His "generative aesthetics" anticipated computer-generated art: in 1965 the Stuttgart gallery hosted one of the first exhibitions of computer graphics, by Georg Nees and Frieder Nake. Bense also promoted concrete poetry, notably that of the Brazilian Noigandres group, and influenced early digital art and media theory in Germany.
