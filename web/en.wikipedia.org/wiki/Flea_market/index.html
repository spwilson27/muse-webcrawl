Flea market

A flea market (or swap meet) is a type of street market that provides space for vendors to sell previously-owned (second-hand) merchandise. Flea markets are often held outdoors, in locations such as parking lots or fields, although some, such as the indoor markets of the United Kingdom, are held indoors year-round.

Vendors at an outdoor flea market

Etymology

The origin of the term "flea market" is disputed. One theory holds that it is a literal translation of the French marché aux puces, the name given to a market in Paris that specialised in shabby second-hand goods of the kind that might contain fleas. Another theory traces the term to the Dutch colonial settlement of New Amsterdam, where a market called the Vlaie (valley) market was held at the foot of Maiden Lane; "Vlaie" was pronounced "vlea" by settlers, giving rise to the English rendering.

Characteristics

Flea markets typically feature numerous vendors selling a wide variety of goods, including antiques, collectibles, clothing, furniture, household items, tools, books, toys, and crafts. Prices are often negotiable through haggling. Some markets are permanent fixtures open every weekend, while others are seasonal or one-time events. Larger markets may charge admission fees or parking fees, and vendors usually rent stall space from the market operators.

Well-known flea markets include the Rose Bowl Flea Market in Pasadena, California; the Marché aux Puces de Saint-Ouen in Paris, one of the largest antique markets in the world; and Portobello Road Market in London.

Regulation and counterfeit goods

Flea markets have occasionally been criticised as venues for the sale of counterfeit and pirated goods, and market operators in many jurisdictions face legal obligations to police the merchandise sold by their vendors.
