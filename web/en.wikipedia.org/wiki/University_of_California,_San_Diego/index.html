The University of California, San Diego (UC San Diego or UCSD) is a public land-grant research university in San Diego, California. Established in 1960 near the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego is the southernmost campus of the University of California system and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity". It is consistently ranked among the top public universities in the United States and the world.

History and structure

The campus grew from the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, founded in 1903; oceanographer Roger Revelle championed the creation of a general campus, which admitted its first undergraduates in 1964. UC San Diego is organized into eight undergraduate residential colleges (Revelle, John Muir, Thurgood Marshall, Earl Warren, Eleanor Roosevelt, Sixth, Seventh, and Eighth), modeled loosely on the Oxford–Cambridge collegiate system, each with its own general-education requirements and traditions. Its striking central library, renamed the Geisel Library in 1995 after Theodor Seuss Geisel ("Dr. Seuss") and his wife Audrey, is a landmark of brutalist architecture.

Academics and research

The university comprises schools of medicine, engineering (Jacobs School), management (Rady School), global policy (School of Global Policy and Strategy), and the Scripps Institution, a leading center for ocean and earth science. UC San Diego faculty and alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, and the campus is a major center for research in neuroscience, bioengineering, computer science, and climate science. Its athletic teams, the Tritons, compete in NCAA Division I.
