Jeroboam I (reigned c. 931–910 BC) was the first king of the northern Kingdom of Israel following the division of the United Monarchy after the death of Solomon. Not to be confused with the much later king Jeroboam II.

Rise to power

An Ephraimite official under Solomon, Jeroboam fled to Egypt after a failed revolt encouraged by the prophet Ahijah. When Solomon's son Rehoboam refused to lighten the people's burdens, the ten northern tribes seceded and made Jeroboam king.

Religious reforms

To keep his subjects from worshipping at Jerusalem, Jeroboam established rival sanctuaries with golden calves at Bethel and Dan, appointing non-Levitical priests and inventing a new festival calendar. In the biblical narrative this becomes the archetypal "sin of Jeroboam", the standard against which all later northern kings are condemned — a contrast to the Jerusalem-centred piety associated with the prophet Jeremiah's era centuries later.
