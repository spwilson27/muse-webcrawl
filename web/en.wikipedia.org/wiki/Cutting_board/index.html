Cutting board

A wooden cutting board in a kitchen.

A cutting board (known as a chopping board in some regions) is a durable board on which to place material for cutting. The kitchen cutting board is commonly used in preparing food; other types exist for cutting raw materials such as leather or plastic. Kitchen cutting boards are often made of wood or plastic and come in various widths and sizes. There are also cutting boards made of glass, steel, or marble, which can be harder on knife blades.

Materials

Wooden boards, especially end-grain butcher blocks, are gentle on knife edges and have long been traditional; they require oiling and hand washing. Plastic boards, often colour-coded in professional kitchens to prevent cross-contamination between raw meat, fish, and vegetables, are lightweight and dishwasher-safe. Glass and stone boards are hygienic and easy to clean but dull knives quickly and are noisy in use.

Hygiene

Food safety guidance generally recommends separate boards for raw meat and ready-to-eat foods, thorough washing after use, and replacing boards that develop deep grooves, since knife scars can harbour bacteria. Research comparing wood and plastic has found that both can be safe when properly cleaned, though plastic boards are easier to sanitise in a dishwasher.
