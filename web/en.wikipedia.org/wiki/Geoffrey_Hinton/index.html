Geoffrey Hinton

Born: 6 December 1947, Wimbledon, London, England

Known for: Backpropagation, Boltzmann machine, deep learning

Awards: Turing Award (2018), Nobel Prize in Physics (2024)

Geoffrey Everest Hinton (born 6 December 1947) is a British-Canadian computer scientist and cognitive psychologist, most noted for his work on artificial neural networks. He is often called one of the "Godfathers of AI" and the "Godfather of Deep Learning". In 2024 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with John Hopfield "for foundational discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks".

Early life and education

Hinton was born in Wimbledon, London, and educated at King's College, Cambridge, graduating with a degree in experimental psychology in 1970. He received his PhD in artificial intelligence from the University of Edinburgh in 1978, supervised by Christopher Longuet-Higgins. He is the great-great-grandson of George Boole, the founder of Boolean logic.

Career

After positions at Sussex University, Carnegie Mellon University, and University College London, Hinton moved to the University of Toronto in 1987, where he held a professorship for most of his career. In 1986 he co-authored, with David Rumelhart and Ronald Williams, the landmark paper that popularised the backpropagation algorithm for training multilayer neural networks. He also co-invented the Boltzmann machine with Terrence Sejnowski.

In 2012, Hinton and his students Alex Krizhevsky and Ilya Sutskever created AlexNet, a deep convolutional neural network that dramatically won the ImageNet competition and ignited the deep learning revolution. Google acquired their company, DNNresearch, in 2013, and Hinton split his time between Toronto and Google Brain.

Views on AI risk

In May 2023, Hinton left Google, saying he wanted to speak freely about the dangers of artificial intelligence. He has warned that advanced AI systems could surpass human intelligence, be misused by bad actors, and pose long-term existential risks, while also acknowledging their potential benefits in medicine and science.

Honours

Hinton shared the 2018 ACM A.M. Turing Award with Yoshua Bengio and Yann LeCun for conceptual and engineering breakthroughs in deep neural networks. He was made a Companion of the Order of Canada and is a Fellow of the Royal Society.
