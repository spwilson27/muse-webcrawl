Hull number

A hull number is a designation given to a ship by a navy or coast guard, combining a hull classification symbol (letters indicating the vessel's type) with a sequential number. Hull numbers are painted on the hull, typically on the bow and stern, and used together with the ship's name to identify it.

United States system

The United States Navy introduced standardised hull classification symbols in 1920. Examples include CVN for nuclear-powered aircraft carriers, DDG for guided-missile destroyers, SSBN for ballistic-missile submarines, and LCS for littoral combat ships; thus USS Enterprise was CVN-65. Numbers are assigned sequentially within each type series and are rarely reused.

Other systems

Other navies use related schemes, such as pennant numbers in the Royal Navy and Commonwealth fleets. Merchant vessels are instead identified by IMO numbers and official numbers, while recreational craft carry hull identification numbers (HIN) moulded into the transom.
