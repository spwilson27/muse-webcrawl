Safavid Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Shah Mosque on Naqsh-e Jahan Square in Isfahan, with its blue-tiled dome]

The Safavid Empire (1501–1736) was one of the great gunpowder empires of the Islamic world, ruling Persia and at its height parts of the Caucasus, Iraq and Central Asia. Founded by Shah Ismail I, a fourteen-year-old Sufi order leader who proclaimed Twelver Shi'a Islam the state religion, the Safavids made Iran a Shi'a state — the decisive event in forming modern Iran's identity — and fought century-long wars with the Ottoman Empire in the west and Uzbeks and Mughals in the east. Under Shah Abbas I (r. 1588–1629) the empire reached its cultural and commercial zenith, with Isfahan rebuilt as its splendid capital.

Rise and the Shi'a state

The Safavid order, a Sufi brotherhood turned militant movement, rallied Turkmen tribesmen (the Qizilbash, 'red heads'). Ismail took Tabriz in 1501 and was crowned shah, imposing Shi'ism on a mostly Sunni population by persuasion and force. Defeat by the Ottomans at Chaldiran (1514) checked expansion westward but not the dynasty. Shah Tahmasp's long reign consolidated the state amid Ottoman and Uzbek pressure.

Abbas the Great and decline

Abbas I, with English adventurers Robert and Anthony Sherley, built a standing army of musketeers and ghulams to counter the Qizilbash, retook Baghdad and the Caucasus, humbled the Uzbeks, expelled the Portuguese from Hormuz (1622), and made Isfahan — 'half the world', in the Persian saying — a centre of silk trade, carpet weaving and architecture (the Shah Mosque, the Naqsh-e Jahan square). After his death the empire slowly declined under weak shahs and court eunuchs until Afghan rebels besieged Isfahan and ended effective Safavid rule in 1722; the dynasty's last remnants ended in 1736.
