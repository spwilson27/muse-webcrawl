Humphead wrasse


A humphead wrasse with its prominent forehead hump.

The humphead wrasse (Cheilinus undulatus) is a large species of wrasse mainly found on coral reefs in the Indo-Pacific region. It is also known as the Māori wrasse, Napoleon wrasse, and Napoleon fish. It is the largest living member of the family Labridae, with males reaching up to 2 metres (6.6 ft) in length and females up to 1 metre (3.3 ft).

Biology

Adults are identified by their thick lips and the prominent hump on the forehead, which grows with age. The species is a protogynous hermaphrodite: some fish are born female and change into males later in life. Humphead wrasse feed on molluscs, echinoderms (including the toxic crown-of-thorns starfish), crustaceans, and other invertebrates, crushing them with powerful pharyngeal teeth. Individuals may live for more than 30 years.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List and is included in CITES Appendix II, mainly because of intense exploitation for the luxury live reef food fish trade in Southeast Asia and China. Its slow growth, late maturity, and sex-changing life history make it especially vulnerable to overfishing.
