Bicycle messenger

A bicycle messenger (or bike courier) is a person who works for a courier company delivering items by bicycle, most often in dense city centres where bikes can move faster than cars through traffic. Messengers typically carry documents, packages, and small goods between businesses on tight deadlines.

Work and equipment

Messengers are dispatched by radio, phone, or app, ride fixed-gear or single-speed bicycles for simplicity and durability in some subcultures, and carry large messenger bags or backpacks. Pay is often by commission per delivery, and many messengers work as independent contractors.

Culture

Bicycle messengers developed a distinctive subculture, celebrated in events such as "alleycat" races — informal urban races replicating messenger work — and the Cycle Messenger World Championships, held since 1993. The occupation has featured in films such as Quicksilver (1986) and Premium Rush (2012).

Decline and evolution

Email and electronic document transfer sharply reduced document deliveries from the late 1990s, while the rise of app-based food and parcel delivery in the 2010s created a new, larger gig-economy courier workforce using bicycles and e-bikes.
