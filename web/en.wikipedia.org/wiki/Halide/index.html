Halide

Halide
Examples: Fluoride, chloride, bromide, iodide
Common salt: Sodium chloride (NaCl)

A halide is a binary phase or chemical compound consisting of a halogen atom and another element or radical, in which the halogen carries a negative charge (X−). The halide anions are fluoride (F−), chloride (Cl−), bromide (Br−), iodide (I−) and astatide.

Classes

Metal halides range from highly ionic salts such as sodium chloride to covalent compounds such as titanium tetrachloride. In organic chemistry, compounds containing a halogen bonded to carbon — alkyl halides and aryl halides — are central reagents; silver halides are the light-sensitive materials of traditional photography.

Tests and uses

Halides are commonly detected by precipitation with silver nitrate, giving characteristic silver halide precipitates. Halide salts are used in lighting (metal-halide lamps), photography, synthesis, and as electrolytes.
