Aerial warfare is the use of military aircraft and other flying machines in warfare, including the fighting between aircraft, attacks on ground and naval targets, air transport of troops and materiel, aerial reconnaissance and surveillance, and the defence of airspace against enemy air attack. It became a decisive dimension of war during the 20th century and is conducted mainly by air forces, supplemented by naval aviation and army aviation.

History

Balloons were used for observation from the 1790s and during the American Civil War, but the armed aeroplane arrived with the Italo-Turkish War of 1911, when an Italian pilot dropped the first bombs from an aircraft. The First World War saw rapid development of fighters, bombers and reconnaissance types, as well as the first strategic bombing raids by Zeppelins and Gotha bombers. In the interwar period theorists such as Giulio Douhet, Hugh Trenchard and Billy Mitchell argued that air power could be independently decisive. The Second World War confirmed air warfare's centrality: the Battle of Britain, carrier battles in the Pacific, massive strategic bombing campaigns in Europe and Asia, airborne assaults and, at its close, the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

Cold War to present

Jet fighters, surface-to-air missiles, airborne radar and precision-guided munitions transformed air combat in Korea, Vietnam and the Middle East. Since the 1991 Gulf War, campaigns have emphasised stealth, satellite surveillance, suppression of enemy air defences and networked precision strike. In the 21st century unmanned aerial vehicles (drones) — from large armed platforms to small loitering munitions — have become pervasive, a trend starkly visible in the war in Ukraine.

Concepts

Core concepts include command of the air (air supremacy, superiority), the OODA loop in air combat, close air support, interdiction, strategic bombing, and air mobility and tanker support that give air forces global reach.
