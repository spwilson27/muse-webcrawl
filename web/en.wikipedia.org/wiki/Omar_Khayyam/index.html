Omar Khayyam

Omar Khayyam
Born: 18 May 1048, Nishapur, Persia
Died: 4 December 1131, Nishapur
Known for: Rubaiyat, algebra, calendar reform

Omar Khayyam (18 May 1048 – 4 December 1131) was a Persian polymath, known for his contributions to mathematics, astronomy, philosophy, and poetry. He was born in Nishapur, in northeastern Iran, and spent most of his life there and in Samarkand and Isfahan.

Mathematics

Khayyam made major contributions to algebra. In his Treatise on Demonstration of Problems of Algebra he gave a systematic method for solving cubic equations by intersecting conic sections, and he classified equations of degree up to three. He also worked on the parallel postulate and contributed to the theory of proportions and irrational numbers.

Astronomy

Working at the observatory in Isfahan under the patronage of Sultan Malik-Shah I, Khayyam led a group of astronomers who produced the Jalali calendar, a solar calendar of remarkable accuracy, introduced in 1079. His measurement of the length of the year was highly precise.

Poetry

In the West, Khayyam is best known for the Rubaiyat, a collection of quatrains (rubāʿiyāt) attributed to him, famously rendered into English by Edward FitzGerald in 1859. The poems reflect on the transience of life, fate, wine, and love, and became enormously popular in the English-speaking world.
