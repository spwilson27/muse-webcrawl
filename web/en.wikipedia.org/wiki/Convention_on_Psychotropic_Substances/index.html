Signed21 February 1971, Vienna, Austria

The Convention on Psychotropic Substances of 1971 is a United Nations treaty designed to control psychoactive drugs such as amphetamine-type stimulants, barbiturates, benzodiazepines, and psychedelics. It was signed in Vienna on 21 February 1971 and entered into force on 16 August 1976. Together with the Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs of 1961 and the Convention Against Illicit Traffic of 1988, it forms the core of the international drug control system.

The treaty is administered from Vienna by the INCB and UNODC.

Schedules

The Convention places substances into four schedules of decreasing strictness, based on dependence potential, abuse liability, and therapeutic value:

Schedule I covers substances presenting a high risk of abuse and constituting an especially serious threat to public health with very limited therapeutic value, such as LSD, MDMA, and mescaline. Schedule II includes amphetamine, methamphetamine, and secobarbital. Schedule III includes certain barbiturates and buprenorphine (later moved), and Schedule IV includes most benzodiazepines such as diazepam, along with substances like phenobarbital (in Schedule IV under this Convention's listing practice).

History

Drug control treaties before 1971 focused on plant-based drugs (opium, coca, cannabis). The proliferation of synthetic stimulants, sedatives, and hallucinogens in the 1960s exposed a gap that the 1971 Convention filled. Scheduling decisions are made by the UN Commission on Narcotic Drugs, acting on recommendations of the World Health Organization.

Implementation and criticism

Parties must license manufacture and trade, require medical prescriptions for scheduled substances, and report statistics to the INCB. Critics have faulted the Convention for scheduling substances such as MDMA and psychedelics in ways said to impede medical research, while others note the relatively looser controls compared with the 1961 Convention. Unlike the Single Convention, the 1971 treaty does not impose an estimates system on production.
