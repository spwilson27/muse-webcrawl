White Tiger (mythology)

The White Tiger (Chinese: 白虎, Bái Hǔ) is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations. It represents the west and the autumn season, and is one of the four great mythological creatures of Chinese astronomy and cosmology, alongside the Azure Dragon of the east, the Vermilion Bird of the south and the Black Tortoise of the north.

Astronomy

In Chinese astronomy, the White Tiger presides over the seven lunar mansions of the western quadrant of the sky. The tiger's body stretches across this region of stars, and the mansions were used to track the movement of the moon and the seasons.

Cultural significance

The tiger was associated with metal in the five-elements system, with warfare and with the direction west. In military symbolism, the tiger appeared on banners and tallies; in feng shui, the White Tiger is one of the four surrounding landforms, ideally a lower, gentler hill to the west of a site, balanced by the Azure Dragon to the east.

The White Tiger also spread to Korea (as Baekho) and Japan (as Byakko), where it appears in temple art, tomb murals and popular culture. In Korean mythology, the appearance of a white tiger was sometimes taken as an omen that a ruler governed virtuously.
