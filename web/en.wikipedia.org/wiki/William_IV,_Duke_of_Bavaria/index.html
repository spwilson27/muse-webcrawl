William IV (German: Wilhelm IV.; 13 November 1493 – 7 March 1550) was Duke of Bavaria from 1508 to 1550, ruling at first jointly with his younger brother Louis X. His reign saw Bavaria established as a stronghold of the Catholic Counter-Reformation in the Holy Roman Empire.

Reign

William inherited the duchy from his father Albert IV, whose primogeniture decree ended centuries of Bavarian partition. Although Louis X received the districts of Landshut and Straubing by the Treaty of Ratisbon (1514), the brothers cooperated closely and the duchy remained effectively unified. William suppressed the peasant revolts of 1525 in his territories and resisted Protestant reform, inviting the Jesuits to Ingolstadt and supporting the Catholic University there.

The Reinheitsgebot

In 1516, in Ingolstadt, William promulgated the Reinheitsgebot, the famous Bavarian Beer Purity Law, decreeing that beer could be brewed only from water, barley and hops. It is the oldest food-quality regulation still invoked today and a lasting symbol of Bavarian brewing.

Patronage and death

William and Louis were notable patrons of Renaissance art and music; composers such as Ludwig Senfl served at their Munich court, and the dukes amassed a celebrated collection of paintings. William died in Munich in 1550 and was succeeded by his son Albert V.
