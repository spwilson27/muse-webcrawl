Rudolf Viest

Rudolf Viest
Born: 24 September 1890, Revúca, Austria-Hungary
Died: 1945 (presumed), Germany
Allegiance: Czechoslovakia
Rank: General
Commands: Slovak Insurgent Army (1944)

Rudolf Viest (1890–1945) was a Slovak general and commander of the insurgent forces during the Slovak National Uprising of 1944. He was the highest-ranking Slovak officer in the Czechoslovak armed forces and one of only two ethnic Slovak generals in the First Czechoslovak Republic.

Career

Viest served as an officer in the Austro-Hungarian Army in the First World War, was captured on the Eastern Front, and joined the Czechoslovak Legions in Russia. After 1918 he pursued a career in the Czechoslovak Army, studied in France, and held diplomatic and senior staff posts, reaching the rank of division general.

After the dismemberment of Czechoslovakia in 1939, Viest left for exile and joined the resistance abroad; he was a member of the Czechoslovak government-in-exile structures in London and served in the Middle East. In August 1944 he was flown into liberated Banská Bystrica to take command of the 1st Czechoslovak Army in Slovakia, the armed force of the Slovak National Uprising against the Nazi-aligned Slovak state and German invasion.

Death

After the collapse of the uprising in October 1944, Viest and General Ján Golian refused exile and were captured by German forces in November 1944. They were taken to Berlin, interrogated, and are believed to have been executed in 1945, probably at Flossenbürg concentration camp, although the exact circumstances of their deaths remain unclear. Both generals were posthumously honoured by Slovakia and the Czech Republic.
