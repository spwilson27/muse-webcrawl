Location: Southern Sweden

Max. depth: 128 m
Surface elevation: 88 m

Vättern (Swedish: [ˈvɛ̌tːɛɳ]) is the second largest lake in Sweden by surface area, after Vänern, and the sixth largest lake in Europe. It is a long, finger-shaped lake in southern Sweden, lying in the counties of Östergötland, Jönköping, Västra Götaland and Örebro.

Geography

The lake is about 120 km long and up to 30 km wide, with a surface area of approximately 1,912 km² and a maximum depth of 128 metres. Its best-known feature is the exceptional clarity of its water, which gives visibility to depths of 15–20 metres; the water is so pure that the lake serves as a drinking-water source for several municipalities around its shores. The island of Visingsö lies in the southern part of the lake, reached by ferry from Gränna.

Towns on its shores include Jönköping and Huskvarna at the southern tip, Askersund in the north, Motala and Vadstena in the east, and Hjo and Karlsborg in the west. The lake drains eastward through the Motala ström into the Baltic Sea, and it is connected westward to Vänern by the Göta Canal.

Geology and ecology

Vättern occupies a graben — a rift valley formed by tectonic movement some 800–870 million years ago, later scoured by Ice Age glaciers. The cold, nutrient-poor water supports a distinctive fauna including a relict population of Arctic char and several species that survived from glacial times. Around 30 fish species are found in the lake.

Recreation

The lake is famous as the setting of Vätternrundan, the world's largest recreational cycling event, in which around 20,000 participants ride roughly 300–315 km around the lake each June. Sailing, swimming and fishing are also popular, and the lake's beaches, such as that at Gränna — famous for its polkagris peppermint candy — draw many visitors in summer.
