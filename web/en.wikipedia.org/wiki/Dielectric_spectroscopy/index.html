Dielectric spectroscopy

Dielectric spectroscopy, also known as impedance spectroscopy in its electrochemical form, measures the dielectric properties of a material as a function of frequency. A sample is subjected to an alternating electric field and the complex permittivity (or impedance) is recorded over frequencies that can span from microhertz to terahertz.

Principles

Different molecular processes — dipolar reorientation, ionic conduction, interfacial (Maxwell–Wagner) polarisation and electronic and atomic polarisation — respond over characteristic frequency ranges, so the spectrum reveals relaxation times and charge-transport mechanisms. Data are often modelled with equivalent circuits or functions such as the Debye, Cole–Cole and Havriliak–Negami models.

Applications

The technique is used in materials science, electrochemistry (electrochemical impedance spectroscopy for batteries, fuel cells and corrosion), polymer physics, biophysics and food and pharmaceutical analysis.
