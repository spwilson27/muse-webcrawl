Expansion pack

An expansion pack, or simply expansion, is an addition to an existing game — most commonly a video game, but also a role-playing game, board game, or collectible card game — that extends it with new content such as levels, campaigns, characters, items, or rules. An expansion typically requires the original base game to function and is sold separately, usually at a lower price than the base product.

In video games

Expansion packs emerged as a distribution model in the early 1990s, when PC games were sold in boxed retail copies. Because an expansion could reuse the base game's engine and assets, it could be produced more cheaply and quickly than a full sequel while still offering substantial content. Prominent early examples include the mission packs for Doom and Quake, and the campaign expansions of real-time strategy games such as Warcraft II: Beyond the Dark Portal (1996) and StarCraft: Brood War (1998), the latter widely regarded as a landmark expansion that significantly rebalanced and extended its base game.

The practice was especially characteristic of certain genres: strategy games (the Age of Empires and Civilization series), role-playing games (Diablo II: Lord of Destruction, Baldur's Gate II: Throne of Bhaal), simulations (The Sims received a long line of expansion packs, making the series one of the best-selling in PC history), and massively multiplayer online games, where large paid expansions such as World of Warcraft: The Burning Crusade (2007) add new continents, character levels, and classes on a roughly biennial cycle.

Standalone expansions

A standalone expansion is sold as a separate product that does not require the original game, often using the same engine and mechanics with a new campaign; examples include Company of Heroes: Opposing Fronts and Half-Life: Blue Shift. Standalone expansions blur the line between expansion and sequel, sometimes being marketed as full games in their own right.

Relationship to downloadable content

With the spread of digital distribution in the 2000s, the expansion pack model was partly displaced by downloadable content (DLC) — smaller, individually priced additions ranging from cosmetic items to full story chapters — and later by season passes, free updates supported by microtransactions, and live-service models. The term "expansion" is now commonly reserved for the largest DLC releases, which add a substantial amount of gameplay comparable to the classical retail expansions.

In other games

Tabletop role-playing games have long sold expansion books (rulebooks, settings, and adventures) supplementing a core rulebook. Board games such as Catan and Carcassonne have numerous expansions adding tiles, pieces, and mechanics, and collectible card games are organised around expansion sets released in rotation.
