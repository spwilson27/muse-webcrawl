Felipe Calderón

Felipe de Jesús Calderón Hinojosa (born 18 August 1962) is a Mexican politician who served as the 56th president of Mexico from 2006 to 2012. A member of the National Action Party (PAN), his presidency was dominated by the declaration of an aggressive military campaign against drug cartels, which began the ongoing Mexican drug war.

Early career

Calderón was born in Morelia, the son of PAN co-founder Luis Calderón Vega. Trained as a lawyer with postgraduate studies in economics and public administration, he rose through the PAN as a federal deputy, party president (1996–1999), and director of the development bank Banobras, before serving as Secretary of Energy under President Vicente Fox — a post he resigned in 2004 to pursue the presidency.

Presidency

Calderón won the closely contested 2006 election over Andrés Manuel López Obrador by less than one percentage point, amid protests and allegations of fraud. Days after taking office he launched Operation Michoacán, deploying the military against drug cartels; the ensuing drug war saw a dramatic rise in homicides and remains his most debated legacy. His government also expanded health coverage through Seguro Popular, pursued infrastructure spending, weathered the 2008 global financial crisis and the 2009 H1N1 flu pandemic (which began in Mexico), and hosted the 2010 Cancún climate conference. After leaving office he founded the Global Commission for the Economy and Climate and has taught and written on energy and climate policy; in 2018 he resigned from the PAN after nearly four decades.
