Stokes' law

Stokes' law is a formula for the frictional force, or drag, exerted on a small spherical object moving through a viscous fluid. It was derived by George Gabriel Stokes in 1851 and states that the drag force is:

Fd = 6πηrv

where η is the dynamic viscosity of the fluid, r the radius of the sphere, and v the flow velocity relative to the object.

Terminal velocity

A small sphere falling under gravity through a fluid reaches a terminal velocity when drag and buoyancy balance its weight. This principle underlies the falling-ball viscometer and the sedimentation of particles in liquids and gases.

Applications

Stokes' law is used in the Millikan oil-drop experiment, in meteorology for the settling of cloud droplets, and in geology and chemistry for sedimentation and centrifugation analysis. It holds for low Reynolds numbers (Re < 1), the laminar or "creeping flow" regime.
