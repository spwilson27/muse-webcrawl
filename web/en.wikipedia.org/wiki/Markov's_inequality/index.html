Markov's inequality

In probability theory, Markov's inequality gives an upper bound on the probability that a non-negative random variable is greater than or equal to some positive constant, in terms of its expected value. It is named after the Russian mathematician Andrey Markov.

Statement

If X is a nonnegative random variable and a > 0, then Pr(X ≥ a) ≤ E(X)/a. The bound requires only knowledge of the mean, and holds for any distribution.

Applications

Though often loose, Markov's inequality is fundamental: it is used to prove Chebyshev's inequality and other concentration inequalities, and it underlies counting arguments such as the probabilistic method in combinatorics.
