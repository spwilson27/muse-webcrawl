The Atlas Cheetah is a South African fighter aircraft developed and produced by the Atlas Aviation Corporation (later Denel Aviation) of South Africa. It was developed as a major upgrade of the Dassault Mirage III, and was the result of South Africa's inability to acquire modern fighters because of the international arms embargo imposed during the apartheid era.

Development

During the 1980s, the South African Air Force's Mirage III fleet was increasingly outclassed, notably by Soviet-built aircraft operated by neighbouring Angola. Unable to buy new fighters abroad, South Africa launched a comprehensive upgrade programme. The Cheetah incorporated canards, an upgraded Atar 9K50 engine, new avionics partly derived from Israel's Kfir programme, an in-flight refuelling probe and a modernised cockpit.

Variants and service

Three variants were produced: the single-seat Cheetah C, the two-seat Cheetah D, and the Cheetah E interim single-seat model. The type equipped No. 2 Squadron SAAF and saw service during the final years of the Border War period, though it arrived too late for extensive combat use. The Cheetah was retired from South African service in 2008, replaced by the Saab JAS 39 Gripen.

Exports

Ecuador acquired Cheetah C and D aircraft, and a number of retired South African airframes were later sold to the US company Draken International for use as adversary ("aggressor") training aircraft.
