Solimões River

The Solimões River (Portuguese: Rio Solimões) is the name given to the upper stretches of the Amazon River in Brazil, from the country's western border with Peru and Colombia to the confluence with the Rio Negro near Manaus. Above the tri-border area the river continues into Peru as the Marañón and Ucayali systems; in many international usages the entire main stem is simply called the Amazon, but in Brazil the Solimões designation is standard for this reach.

Course

The Solimões flows generally eastward through the state of Amazonas, passing river towns such as Tabatinga, São Paulo de Olivença, Santo Antônio do Içá, Tonantins, Jutaí, Fonte Boa, Tefé, Coari, Codajás, Anori, and Manacapuru. It receives major tributaries including the Juruá, Japurá, Içá (Putumayo), Tonantins, Badajós, and Purus. Like the Amazon generally, it is a sediment-rich "whitewater" river: its pale, café-au-lait colour comes from Andean sediment eroded far upstream.

Meeting of the Waters

Near Manaus the dark, acidic waters of the Rio Negro meet the pale Solimões at the famous Encontro das Águas (Meeting of the Waters). Because of differences in temperature, density, and flow speed, the two rivers run side by side for more than 6 km without mixing — a celebrated natural spectacle. Below the confluence the river is called the Amazon proper in Brazilian usage.

Transport and settlement

With few roads in the region, the Solimões is the principal transport artery of western Amazonas; passenger boats and cargo vessels link Manaus with Tefé, Tabatinga, and the Peruvian port of Iquitos. Seasonal flooding of the várzea floodplains governs agriculture and settlement along its banks.
