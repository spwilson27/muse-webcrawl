Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis

The hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis (HPG axis) is a neuroendocrine system made up of the hypothalamus, the pituitary gland and the gonads (testes or ovaries). Together these glands control reproduction, sexual development and the production of sex hormones through a cascade of hormonal signals with feedback loops.

HPG axis
ComponentsHypothalamus, pituitary gland, gonads
Key hormonesGnRH, LH, FSH, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone
FunctionReproduction and sexual maturation

Mechanism

Neurons in the hypothalamus release gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) in pulses into the portal circulation. GnRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to secrete luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). These gonadotropins act on the gonads: in males LH drives testosterone production by Leydig cells and FSH supports spermatogenesis; in females they control follicular development, ovulation and the production of oestrogen and progesterone.

Feedback

Sex steroids and inhibin feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary to regulate GnRH, LH and FSH secretion. In females, high oestrogen switches to positive feedback that triggers the mid-cycle LH surge.

Life cycle and disorders

The axis is active briefly in infancy, quiescent through childhood, and reactivated at puberty. Disruption at any level can cause hypogonadism, infertility, delayed or precocious puberty, and conditions such as Kallmann syndrome, in which GnRH neurons fail to develop.
