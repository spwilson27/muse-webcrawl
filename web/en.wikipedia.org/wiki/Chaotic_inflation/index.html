Chaotic inflation

Chaotic inflation is a model of cosmic inflation proposed by Andrei Linde in 1983. In this scenario, inflation is driven by a scalar field (the inflaton) displaced far from the minimum of a simple potential, for example a mass term V(φ) = ½m²φ² or a quartic potential. As the field slowly rolls and oscillates toward its minimum, the universe expands quasi-exponentially. The model is called "chaotic" because inflation can begin from very general, disordered initial conditions at the Planck scale, without the need for thermal equilibrium or a finely tuned starting state.

The inflaton field slowly rolling in a simple quadratic potential

Mechanism

If in some region of the early universe the inflaton field takes a sufficiently large value and varies slowly in space, the friction of the expanding universe makes the field roll very slowly, so its potential energy acts like an effective cosmological constant and drives inflation. Quantum fluctuations of the field during this phase seed the density perturbations that later grow into galaxies, with a nearly scale-invariant spectrum. Inflation ends when the field approaches the minimum and begins to oscillate; its energy is then converted into ordinary particles in the process of reheating.

Eternal inflation

Chaotic inflation generically leads to eternal inflation: quantum fluctuations of the field during inflation can, in some regions, push the field uphill as fast as it rolls down, so those regions keep inflating and spawn an unbounded number of thermalized "pocket universes." The global structure is thus a multiverse in which inflation never ends everywhere at once. This picture strongly influenced later discussions of the string theory landscape and anthropic selection of cosmological parameters.

Observational status

The simplest monomial potentials predict a relatively large tensor-to-scalar ratio. Measurements of the cosmic microwave background by the Planck satellite and BICEP/Keck experiments have increasingly favoured plateau-like potentials over the pure quadratic model, though chaotic inflation remains a foundational and widely studied framework, and modern variants (such as α-attractors) retain its chaotic initial-condition philosophy while matching the data.
