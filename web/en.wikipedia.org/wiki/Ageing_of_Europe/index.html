Ageing of Europe

The ageing of Europe, also known as the greying of Europe, is a demographic phenomenon characterized by a rising median age and a growing share of older people, driven by declining fertility rates, increased life expectancy, and in some countries emigration of the young. Europe is the oldest continent, with a median age above 40 in most countries.

Causes

Fertility in most European countries has been below the replacement level of about 2.1 children per woman since the 1970s–1990s, falling as low as around 1.2–1.4 in parts of Southern and Eastern Europe. Simultaneously, life expectancy has risen steadily, so larger cohorts survive into old age. The result is a rising old-age dependency ratio: in the European Union there were roughly three working-age people per person aged 65+ in the early 2020s, with projections approaching two by mid-century.

Consequences

Population ageing strains pension and healthcare systems, tightens labor markets, and shifts political priorities, while also creating a growing "silver economy" of goods and services for older consumers. Policy responses include raising retirement ages, encouraging higher labor-force participation, pro-natalist incentives, and immigration, none of which individually reverses the trend.
