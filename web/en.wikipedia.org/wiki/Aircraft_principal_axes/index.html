Aircraft principal axes

An aircraft in flight can rotate in three dimensions about its centre of gravity along three mutually perpendicular principal axes. Rotation about these axes defines the aircraft's attitude and is controlled by the pilot through the primary flight controls.

Principal axes
Longitudinal (roll) axisRuns nose to tail; controlled by ailerons
Lateral (pitch) axisRuns wingtip to wingtip; controlled by elevators
Normal (yaw) axisRuns vertically through the centre of gravity; controlled by the rudder

The three axes

Roll is rotation about the longitudinal axis, raising one wing and lowering the other; it is produced mainly by the ailerons. Pitch is rotation about the lateral (transverse) axis, raising or lowering the nose; it is controlled by the elevator (or stabilator). Yaw is rotation about the normal (vertical) axis, swinging the nose left or right; it is controlled by the rudder and is used, among other things, to counteract adverse yaw in turns.

Conventions

The axes form a right-handed coordinate system fixed to the aircraft body. In engineering conventions the x-axis points forward (longitudinal), the y-axis to the right (lateral), and the z-axis downward (normal), though sign conventions vary by discipline. The rotation rates about the axes are conventionally denoted p (roll rate), q (pitch rate), and r (yaw rate). Attitude is commonly expressed in Euler angles — roll (bank), pitch, and heading.
