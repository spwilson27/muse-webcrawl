Galois extension


A Galois extension is an algebraic field extension that is normal and separable.

In mathematics, a Galois extension is an algebraic field extension E/F that is normal and separable; equivalently, E is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over F. Galois extensions are the central objects of Galois theory, named after Évariste Galois.

Characterizations

For an algebraic extension E/F, the following are equivalent: E/F is Galois; E is the splitting field of a separable polynomial with coefficients in F; the fixed field of the automorphism group Aut(E/F) is exactly F; and the number of F-automorphisms of E equals the degree [E : F]. The group Aut(E/F) is then called the Galois group Gal(E/F).

Examples

The field ℚ(√2) is Galois over ℚ with group of order 2. Cyclotomic extensions ℚ(ζn), obtained by adjoining a primitive n-th root of unity, are Galois with group isomorphic to (ℤ/nℤ)×. Finite extensions of finite fields are Galois. By contrast, ℚ(∛2) is not Galois over ℚ because it is not normal — it does not contain all the roots of x³ − 2; its Galois closure is ℚ(∛2, ω), with Galois group S3.

Fundamental theorem

The fundamental theorem of Galois theory gives a bijection between intermediate fields of a Galois extension and subgroups of its Galois group, under which normal subextensions correspond to normal subgroups. This correspondence translates questions about fields into questions about groups, and underlies the proof that general polynomial equations of degree five or higher are not solvable by radicals.
