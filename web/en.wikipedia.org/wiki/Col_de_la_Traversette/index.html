Col de la Traversette

Col de la Traversette
Elevation: 2,950 m (9,678 ft)
Range: Cottian Alps
Location: France–Italy border

The Col de la Traversette is a high mountain pass in the Cottian Alps on the border between France and Italy, at an elevation of about 2,950 metres above sea level. It lies near Monte Viso and has been used since antiquity as a crossing between Piedmont and the French Queyras valley.

History

The pass is famous for the Buco di Viso, a pedestrian tunnel bored through the rock just below the summit in the late 15th century under the Marquis of Saluzzo. Considered one of the earliest Alpine tunnels, it shortened the route for merchants carrying salt and other goods between France and Italy and avoided the exposed, dangerous ridge crossing.

Hannibal's crossing

The Traversette is among the passes proposed by historians and scientists as the route taken by Hannibal when he crossed the Alps with his army and elephants in 218 BC during the Second Punic War. Studies of soils and sediments near the pass have been advanced in support of this theory, although the question remains debated.

Access

The pass is reachable today only on foot, by paths from the Italian side near Crissolo and from the French Queyras. It forms part of long-distance hiking routes around Monte Viso.
