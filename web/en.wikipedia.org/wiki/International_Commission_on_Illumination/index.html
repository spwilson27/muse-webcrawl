International Commission on Illumination

CIE
French: Commission internationale de l'éclairage
Formation: 1913
Headquarters: Vienna, Austria

The International Commission on Illumination (CIE, from its French name Commission internationale de l'éclairage) is the international authority on light and lighting, colour and vision, photobiology and image technology. Founded in 1913, it is devoted to worldwide cooperation and the exchange of information on all matters relating to the science and art of light and lighting.

Colour science

The CIE is best known for the CIE 1931 colour spaces, the first mathematically defined links between the physical distribution of wavelengths and perceived colour, created from the colour matching experiments of Wright and Guild. The CIE standard illuminants, such as D65, and systems including CIELAB (1976) remain foundations of colour management in imaging, printing and display industries.

Organisation

The commission works through technical committees organised in divisions covering vision and colour, photometry and radiation measurement, lighting applications and other topics, publishing standards and technical reports used worldwide.
