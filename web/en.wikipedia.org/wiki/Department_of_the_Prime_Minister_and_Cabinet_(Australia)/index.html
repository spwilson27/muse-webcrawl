Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (Australia)

The Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet (PM&C) is an Australian Government public service central department whose role is to support the Prime Minister of Australia and the Cabinet. It is the senior department of the Australian Public Service and is responsible for coordinating policy across government, supporting the machinery of government, and advising on national security, economic, social, and international matters.

History and role

The department traces its origins to the Prime Minister's Department created in 1911, taking its modern form in 1971 when it merged with the Cabinet Office functions. PM&C supports the Prime Minister as head of government, services Cabinet and its committees, coordinates intergovernmental relations through the National Cabinet and the Council for the Australian Federation, and houses functions such as the Office for Women and, at various times, Indigenous affairs policy coordination.

Structure

The department is headed by a Secretary, who is the senior public servant advising the Prime Minister. Its responsibilities have shifted over time as machinery-of-government changes moved portfolios such as the public service commission and official establishments in and out; the Australian Public Service Commission and the Office of National Intelligence have been associated agencies within the portfolio.
