Heathrow Airport

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An aerial view of Heathrow terminals and runways west of London]
Heathrow Airport from the air.

Heathrow Airport (IATA: LHR) is the main international airport serving London, England, and one of the world\'s busiest international airports. Located 23 km west of central London, it has two runways and four passenger terminals (2, 3, 4 and 5), serving around 80 million passengers a year in its busiest years. It is the primary hub of British Airways and a base for Virgin Atlantic.

History

Heathrow began as a small grass airfield in 1929 near the hamlet of Heath Row. Requisitioned in the Second World War, it opened for civil flights on 1 January 1946. Terminal 5 opened in 2008 after a famously troubled baggage-system start; Terminal 1 closed in 2015.

Operations

With only two runways, Heathrow operates at near capacity, and its expansion — a proposed third runway northwest of the airport — has been debated for decades, approved and challenged repeatedly. Night flights are restricted, and its \'slot\' pairs are among the most valuable in world aviation.

Connections

The airport links to London via the Piccadilly line, Heathrow Express to Paddington, and the Elizabeth line. Around 80 airlines serve some 180 destinations; before COVID-19 it was Europe\'s busiest airport by passengers, a title contested with Istanbul and Paris Charles de Gaulle in recent years.
