Motorways of Switzerland

The motorways of Switzerland (Autobahnen / autoroutes / autostrade) form a dense national network of controlled-access highways, unusual for its scale in a mountainous country of Switzerland's size. The network is famous for major Alpine crossings, including the Gotthard Road Tunnel — at 16.9 km one of the longest road tunnels in the world — beneath the Gotthard Pass.

History and structure

The first Swiss motorway, between Lucerne and Horw, opened in 1955, and the federal network plan of 1960 launched sustained construction. Motorways are numbered with the prefix "A" (for example A1 Geneva–St. Gallen and A2 Basel–Chiasso). Because Swiss motorways traverse the Alps, they include exceptionally long tunnels and high viaducts.

Use and tolls

Switzerland levies no distance-based tolls on ordinary cars; instead, use of motorways requires an annual vignette (sticker) displayed on the windscreen. The general speed limit is 120 km/h. Heavy goods vehicles pay a distance-related fee and are subject to strict Alpine transit rules.

Notes

Cantonal expressways supplement the federal network, and Switzerland's motorway density per capita is among the highest in Europe.
