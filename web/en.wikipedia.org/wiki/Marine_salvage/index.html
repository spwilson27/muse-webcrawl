Marine salvage

See also Maritime salvage for the legal doctrine of pure salvage.

Marine salvage is the process of recovering a ship and its cargo after a shipwreck or other maritime casualty. Salvage may encompass towing, refloating a grounded or sunken vessel, patching and pumping to prevent sinking, firefighting, and the recovery of cargo and equipment. Modern salvage is carried out by professional salvors, often under the Lloyd's Open Form contract, and ranges from emergency response to wreck removal and deep-ocean recovery.

Types of salvage

Contract salvage is performed under a prior agreement fixing the terms and payment, regardless of success. Pure (merit) salvage arises when no prior agreement exists: a volunteer who successfully saves maritime property acquires a claim for a salvage award against the saved property, assessed on the value saved, the danger, and the salvor's skill — the principle of "no cure, no pay". Life salvage, saving human life at sea, is generally rewarded out of property awards, as there is usually no claim against the person saved. Other categories include naval salvage, harbour clearance, cargo salvage, equipment salvage, and environmental salvage aimed at preventing pollution.

Law

Salvage law has ancient roots in Rhodian sea law and is codified today in the International Convention on Salvage 1989, which introduced special compensation for salvors who prevent environmental damage even when little property value is saved. Awards are determined by maritime courts or arbitrators, in London traditionally under Lloyd's Open Form with arbitration under Lloyd's Salvage Arbitration Clauses. A salvor acquires a maritime lien on the salvaged property but not ownership; abandoned wrecks and treasure trove are governed by distinct rules, including laws of finds in some jurisdictions.

Notable operations

Famous salvage operations include the clearing of Suez Canal blockages, the refloating of the capsized cruise ship Costa Concordia (2012–2014, then the largest wreck removal in history), the recovery of gold from HMS Edinburgh in the Barents Sea, the raising of the sunken Russian submarine Kursk in 2001, and the unblocking of the Ever Given in the Suez Canal in 2021.
