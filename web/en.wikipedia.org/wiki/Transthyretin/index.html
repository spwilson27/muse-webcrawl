Transthyretin

The transthyretin tetramer (formerly called prealbumin).

Transthyretin (TTR or TBPA) is a transport protein in the plasma and cerebrospinal fluid that transports the thyroid hormone thyroxine (T4) and retinol (vitamin A) bound to retinol-binding protein. This is how transthyretin gained its name: it transports thyroxine and retinol. The protein was formerly called prealbumin because it migrated ahead of albumin on electrophoresis gels.

Structure and function

TTR is a 55 kDa homotetramer synthesised mainly in the liver and the choroid plexus of the brain. Its central channel contains two thyroxine-binding sites, although normally only a small fraction of circulating T4 is carried by TTR in humans.

Clinical significance

Misfolding and aggregation of transthyretin causes amyloid diseases. Mutations in the TTR gene (more than 200 are known, most famously Val122Ile and Val30Met) cause hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis, leading to polyneuropathy and cardiomyopathy; the wild-type protein can also deposit with ageing, causing wild-type (senile) cardiac amyloidosis. Treatments include tetramer stabilisers such as tafamidis, and gene-silencing RNA therapies such as patisiran and inotersen. Serum transthyretin is also used, cautiously, as a marker of nutritional status.
