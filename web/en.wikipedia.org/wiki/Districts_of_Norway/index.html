Districts of Norway

The districts of Norway are traditional geographical regions of the country. Unlike counties and municipalities, districts have no official administrative role, but they correspond to historical divisions and are widely used in everyday speech, media weather forecasts, and regional identity. There are roughly 90 traditional districts.

Overview

Many districts descend from pre-Christian petty kingdoms and medieval jurisdictions (such as the syssel and len systems). Their borders often follow natural features such as fjords, mountain ranges, and valleys.

Examples

Well-known districts include Romerike, Hedmarken, and Hadeland in Eastern Norway; Sogn, Hardanger, Voss, and Nordhordland in the west; Trøndelag's districts such as Fosen and Innherred in central Norway; Sørlandet's Setesdal; and northern districts such as Helgeland, Salten, Lofoten, Vesterålen, and Finnmark's coastal regions.

Relation to counties

Districts may span parts of one or more counties, and a single county usually contains several districts. Courts, police, and health authorities sometimes organize their regions along district lines even though the districts themselves hold no formal status.
