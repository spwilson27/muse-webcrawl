Contactor

A contactor is an electrically controlled switch used for switching an electric power circuit. Unlike a relay, a contactor is designed to be directly connected to high-current load devices, and is used where higher current capacity is required. Contactors range from small devices with a breaking current of a few amperes to large vacuum or air-break units switching thousands of amperes and many kilovolts.

Construction and operation

A contactor consists of a coil (electromagnet), a movable armature carrying main power contacts, fixed contacts, an enclosure, and often auxiliary contacts for control circuits. When the coil is energised by a control voltage (commonly 24 V DC or 120/230 V AC), the electromagnet pulls the armature against spring pressure, closing the main contacts and completing the power circuit. When the coil is de-energised, springs open the contacts. Contacts are usually "normally open" in motor-control service, though normally closed and latching designs exist. Arc chutes, magnetic blowouts or vacuum interrupters extinguish the arc drawn when contacts open under load.

Applications

The most common use is in electric motor starting: a contactor combined with a thermal overload relay forms a motor starter, switching motors ranging from fractional-horsepower machines to compressors and pumps of hundreds of kilowatts. Contactors also control lighting circuits, heating loads, capacitor banks for power-factor correction, and the traction supply in electric vehicles, where sealed DC contactors switch battery packs of several hundred volts.

Ratings

Contactors are rated by maximum load current, operational voltage, and utilisation category (IEC 60947 categories such as AC-1 for resistive loads and AC-3 for squirrel-cage motor starting), and by the number of operations they can perform over their electrical and mechanical life. In North America, NEMA sizes standardise ratings for motor control.
