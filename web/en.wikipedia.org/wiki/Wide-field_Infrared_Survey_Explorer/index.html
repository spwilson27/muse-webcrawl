The Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (WISE, observatory code C51) is a NASA infrared astronomy space telescope launched in December 2009. It performed an all-sky survey in four infrared bands, imaging the entire sky one and a half times before its coolant was exhausted. In 2013 the spacecraft was reactivated as NEOWISE to hunt for near-Earth objects, a role it continued until 2024.

Mission

WISE was an Explorer-class mission managed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, with the spacecraft built by Ball Aerospace. Launched from Vandenberg Air Force Base on a Delta II rocket on 14 December 2009, it entered a Sun-synchronous polar orbit about 525 km above Earth. Its 40 cm telescope, cooled by solid hydrogen cryogen, observed in four bands centred at 3.4, 4.6, 12 and 22 micrometres, reaching sensitivities hundreds to hundreds of thousands of times greater than earlier infrared surveys such as IRAS. It was expected to detect the coolest brown dwarfs, the most luminous infrared galaxies, and millions of asteroids.

Primary survey results

During its cryogenic mission (January–October 2010) WISE took more than 1.5 million images and catalogued over 560 million objects. Highlights included the discovery of the first Earth trojan asteroid (2010 TK7), tens of thousands of new asteroids, numerous comets, and nearby ultra-cool brown dwarfs, including members of the Y spectral class — among the coldest known stellar-like objects. After the coolant ran out, the telescope continued a warm mission (NEOWISE Post-Cryogenic) using its two shortest-wavelength detectors, surveying the main asteroid belt before hibernation in February 2011.

NEOWISE reactivation

In September 2013 NASA reactivated the spacecraft as NEOWISE, dedicated to identifying near-Earth objects (NEOs), including potentially hazardous asteroids and comets, and measuring their sizes and albedos. Over the following decade it observed tens of thousands of solar system objects and discovered hundreds of previously unknown NEOs and comets — most famously comet C/2020 F3 (NEOWISE), which became a bright naked-eye object in July 2020. Its data have been used to estimate the population and physical properties of near-Earth asteroids for planetary defence.

End of mission

With the Sun's increasing activity dragging the spacecraft down from its orbit, NASA ended the NEOWISE mission on 31 July 2024; the transmitter was switched off on 8 August 2024, and the spacecraft re-entered Earth's atmosphere in late 2024. Its successor surveyor, NEO Surveyor, is planned for launch later in the decade.
