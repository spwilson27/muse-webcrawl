Caniformia is a suborder within the order Carnivora consisting of "dog-like" carnivorans. It includes dogs (wolves, foxes, etc.), bears, raccoons, mustelids (weasels, otters, etc.), skunks, the red panda, and the pinnipeds (seals, sea lions, and walruses). The centre of diversification for the Caniformia is North America and northern Eurasia.

Caniformia stands in contrast to the other suborder of Carnivora, the Feliformia ("cat-like" carnivorans), whose centre of diversification was in Africa and southern Asia.

Characteristics

Most caniforms have non-retractile claws and tend to be plantigrade or semi-plantigrade, in contrast to the digitigrade feliforms. The auditory bulla in most caniforms is single-chambered or partially divided, formed mainly by the entotympanic bone, whereas in feliforms it is double-chambered. Many caniforms are omnivorous rather than strictly carnivorous.

Evolution

Caniforms first appeared as tree-climbing, superficially marten-like carnivores in the Eocene, around 42 million years ago. The group diversified extensively, and the pinnipeds — seals and their relatives — returned to the sea, probably from bear-like ancestors on the North Pacific coast.
