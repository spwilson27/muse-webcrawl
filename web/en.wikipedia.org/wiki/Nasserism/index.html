Nasserism

Nasserism
Ideology: Arab nationalism, Arab socialism, anti-imperialism
Key figure: Gamal Abdel Nasser
Region: Egypt and the Arab world
Era: 1950s–1970s

Nasserism is an Arab nationalist and socialist political ideology based on the thinking of Gamal Abdel Nasser, president of Egypt from 1956 until his death in 1970. It combined pan-Arabism, state-led socialist economic development, and anti-imperialist foreign policy, and it inspired political movements across the Arab world.

Principles

Nasserism rested on several pillars: the unity and independence of the Arab nation; the rejection of Western imperialism and alignment with neither superpower bloc during the Cold War (Nasser was a co-founder of the Non-Aligned Movement); the nationalization of key industries and land reform to break the power of the old landed elite; free education and healthcare; and a one-party political order—in Egypt, the Arab Socialist Union—justified as necessary for national development and unity.

Historical development

The ideology emerged after the Egyptian revolution of 1952, in which the Free Officers Movement overthrew the monarchy. Nasser's defiance of Britain, France, and Israel during the Suez Crisis of 1956 made him a hero across the Arab world. In 1958 Egypt and Syria merged as the United Arab Republic, the high point of pan-Arabism, though the union collapsed in 1961. Egypt's defeat in the Six-Day War of 1967 badly damaged Nasser's prestige; after his death in 1970, his successor Anwar Sadat dismantled much of the Nasserist system through the infitah (opening) economic liberalization and the "Corrective Revolution."

Legacy

Nasserist parties and movements persisted in Egypt and other Arab countries, and Nasser remains a potent symbol of Arab dignity and resistance to foreign domination; his image reappeared in the protests of the Arab Spring in 2011.
