Asiatic cheetah

Asiatic cheetah
Scientific nameAcinonyx jubatus venaticus
Conservation statusCritically Endangered (IUCN)
Present rangeIran (central plateau)
Former rangeArabian Peninsula to India

The Asiatic cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus venaticus) is a critically endangered cheetah subspecies surviving today only in Iran. It once ranged from the Arabian Peninsula and the Caspian region through Iran to the Indian subcontinent, where it was famously tamed by Mughal emperors for coursing game; it was declared extinct in India in 1952.

Description

The Asiatic cheetah is slightly smaller and paler than its African relatives, with a thicker winter coat adapted to the cold nights of the Iranian plateau. Its coat is fawn to cream with black spots, and the distinctive black "tear marks" run from the eyes to the mouth.

Population and threats

Surveys in the 2010s estimated fewer than 50 individuals remained, confined to sub-populations in protected areas such as Miandasht, Touran and Bafq. Threats include depletion of wild prey (gazelles and wild sheep), poaching, roadkill on highways crossing its habitat, herding dogs, and drought. Iran's Department of Environment and the Conservation of Asiatic Cheetah Project, run with the UNDP, have led protection efforts; as the country's most celebrated endangered species, the cheetah appeared on the Iranian national football team's 2014 World Cup jersey.

Reintroduction in India

In September 2022, African cheetahs from Namibia (with others later from South Africa) were released in India's Kuno National Park under Project Cheetah — a reintroduction of the species to India, though using the African subspecies rather than the Asiatic one.
