Arithmetic series

Arithmetic series
DefinitionSum of terms of an arithmetic progression
Sum formulaSn = n/2 · (2a + (n − 1)d)
EquivalentlySn = n/2 · (a + l)

An arithmetic series is the sum of the terms of an arithmetic progression, a sequence in which consecutive terms differ by a constant common difference d. If the first term is a and there are n terms, the sum is:

Sn = (n/2)(2a + (n − 1)d) = (n/2)(a + l)

where l = a + (n − 1)d is the last term.

Derivation

The formula follows by writing the series forwards and in reverse and adding: each of the n column pairs sums to (a + l), so 2Sn = n(a + l). A famous anecdote tells that the young Carl Friedrich Gauss used this pairing trick to sum the integers 1 to 100 almost instantly, obtaining 5050.

Examples and properties

The sum of the first n positive integers is n(n + 1)/2 (the triangular numbers); the sum of the first n odd numbers is n². Unlike a geometric series, an arithmetic series with nonzero common difference always diverges as n → ∞. Arithmetic series appear in finance (e.g., sums of arithmetic-gradient cash flows) and in the analysis of algorithms with linearly growing costs.
