Pratt & Whitney PW1000G

The Pratt & Whitney PW1000G, marketed as the GTF (geared turbofan), is a family of high-bypass geared turbofan engines produced by Pratt & Whitney. Its defining feature is a reduction gearbox between the fan and the low-pressure spool, allowing the large fan to rotate more slowly than the compressor and turbine, which raises bypass ratio and efficiency while reducing noise. Variants power the Airbus A320neo family (PW1100G), the Airbus A220 (PW1500G), and the Embraer E-Jets E2 (PW1700G/PW1900G), and it was selected for the Irkut MC-21.

Design

A star gearbox rated at roughly 30,000 horsepower decouples the fan from the low-pressure turbine, letting each run near its optimal speed. The result is a bypass ratio of about 12:1 on the A320neo variant, fuel burn reductions of roughly 16 percent per seat versus previous-generation engines, and a substantially smaller noise footprint. The family was originally called the Geared Turbofan and was developed from the earlier PW6000 core technology and geared-fan demonstrators of the 1990s–2000s.

Service history

The PW1100G entered service with Lufthansa's A320neo in January 2016, after early teething problems with start times and combustor durability were addressed. A far more serious disruption came in 2023, when Pratt & Whitney announced that powder-metal contamination in parts made between 2015 and 2021 required accelerated inspections of some 1,200 PW1100G engines, grounding hundreds of A320neo-family aircraft in rotation through the mid-2020s and costing parent company RTX billions of dollars in compensation and reserves.
