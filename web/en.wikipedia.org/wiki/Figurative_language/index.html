Figurative language

Figurative language is language that uses words or expressions with a meaning different from their literal interpretation. Writers and speakers use figurative devices—such as metaphor, simile, personification, and hyperbole—to create imagery, emphasise ideas, and evoke emotion in ways that plain literal language cannot.

Common figures of speech

A metaphor states that one thing is another ("time is a thief"), while a simile makes a comparison using "like" or "as" ("brave as a lion"). Personification attributes human qualities to non-human things. Hyperbole is deliberate exaggeration for effect, and understatement minimises. Other devices include irony, metonymy, synecdoche, alliteration, onomatopoeia, oxymoron, and idiom.

Use in literature

Figurative language is fundamental to poetry and prose, from the metaphors of Shakespeare to modern song lyrics. It allows abstract ideas to be expressed concretely and adds layers of meaning that reward interpretation. Understanding figurative language is a key part of literacy education.
