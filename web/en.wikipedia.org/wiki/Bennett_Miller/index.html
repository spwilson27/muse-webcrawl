Bennett Miller (born 30 December 1966) is an American film director known for a small body of critically acclaimed dramatic films, all of which received Academy Award nominations for their lead actors. He began his career with the documentary The Cruise (1998), a portrait of New York tour guide Timothy "Speed" Levitch.

Feature films

His narrative debut, Capote (2005), starred Philip Seymour Hoffman as Truman Capote during the writing of In Cold Blood; Hoffman won the Academy Award for Best Actor and Miller was nominated for Best Director. Moneyball (2011), starring Brad Pitt as baseball executive Billy Beane, received six Oscar nominations including Best Picture. Foxcatcher (2014), the true-crime story of John du Pont and wrestlers Mark and Dave Schultz, won Miller the Best Director award at the Cannes Film Festival and five Academy Award nominations.

Style

Miller is known for austere, restrained storytelling, long production periods, and performances drawn from quiet observation rather than theatrical display. He is a childhood friend of actor Dan Futterman, who wrote Capote, and was a classmate of Philip Seymour Hoffman.
