Novel food

Novel food is defined, in food law, as food that was not consumed to a significant degree by humans within a jurisdiction before a certain date, or food produced by new processes or from new sources. Such foods generally require pre-market safety assessment and authorisation before they can be sold.

European Union

In the European Union, the original Novel Foods Regulation (EC) No 258/97 applied to foods not consumed significantly before 15 May 1997. It was replaced by Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, which centralised authorisation through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and created a notification route for traditional foods from third countries. Examples of authorised novel foods include chia seeds, krill oil, UV-treated products, and various insect species authorised in the 2020s.

Elsewhere

Canada regulates "novel foods" under Division 28 of its Food and Drug Regulations, covering genetically modified foods and new processing methods. Other jurisdictions, including Singapore, the United Kingdom (post-Brexit), Australia and New Zealand, operate similar frameworks for products such as cell-cultured meat and new protein sources.
