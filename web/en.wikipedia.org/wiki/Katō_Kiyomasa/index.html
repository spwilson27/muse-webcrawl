Katō Kiyomasa

Katō Kiyomasa (加藤 清正, 25 July 1562 – 2 August 1611) was a Japanese daimyō of the Azuchi–Momoyama and early Edo periods. One of Toyotomi Hideyoshi's most trusted generals, he was counted among the Seven Spears of Shizugatake and commanded Japanese forces in the invasions of Korea. He built Kumamoto Castle and ruled the northern half of Higo Province from Kumamoto.

Career

Kiyomasa was born in Nakamura, Owari — the same village as Hideyoshi — and entered his service as a boy. At the Battle of Shizugatake in 1583 he distinguished himself as one of the "Seven Spears". He later took part in the Kyushu and Odawara campaigns, and in 1588 was granted northern Higo Province.

In the first invasion of Korea (1592–93), Kiyomasa commanded the Second Division, racing Konishi Yukinaga's First Division to Seoul and fighting his way north to the Tumen River, where his men fought Jurchen forces — the source of later legends that he wrestled a tiger. A devout follower of Nichiren Buddhism, he clashed with the Christian Konishi, and their rivalry persisted through the second invasion (1597–98) and the succession struggle after Hideyoshi's death.

During the Sekigahara campaign of 1600 Kiyomasa sided with Tokugawa Ieyasu and defended Kyushu against Western Army forces, which secured his position in Higo after the Tokugawa victory. Between 1601 and 1607 he built Kumamoto Castle, regarded as one of Japan's finest fortresses, and promoted flood control and rice cultivation in his domain. He died in 1611, shortly after mediating a meeting between Ieyasu and Toyotomi Hideyori; suspicions of poisoning have never been substantiated.

Legacy

Kiyomasa is worshipped as a deified guardian of Kumamoto, where festivals honour him, and Kumamoto Castle's reconstruction after the 2016 earthquakes restored his great keep. In Korea he is remembered as a brutal invader, while Japanese tradition celebrates his martial prowess and administrative works, including the irrigation channels of the Shirakawa river.
