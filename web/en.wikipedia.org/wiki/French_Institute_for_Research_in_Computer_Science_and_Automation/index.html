The French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation (French: Institut national de recherche en informatique et en automatique, Inria) is a French national research institution focused on computer science and applied mathematics. It was founded in 1967 as IRIA (Institut de recherche en informatique et en automatique), as part of the government's Plan Calcul, and took the name Inria in 1979.

Research and organisation

Inria employs several thousand researchers organised into project teams, many jointly run with universities, across research centres at sites including Rocquencourt (near Paris), Sophia Antipolis, Rennes, Grenoble, Nancy, Bordeaux, Lille, Saclay, Lyon, and Montpellier. Its research spans algorithms, programming languages, networks, robotics, artificial intelligence, scientific computing, and formal methods.

Notable work

Inria teams developed the OCaml programming language, the Coq proof assistant, the Scilab numerical software, contributions to internet protocols under researchers such as Louis Pouzin (whose CYCLADES network influenced TCP/IP), and the open-source CGAL geometry library. It plays a central role in French and European digital research policy and technology transfer through startups and industrial partnerships.
