Tethys (Titaness)

Greek: Τηθύς
Parents: Uranus and Gaia
Consort: Oceanus
Children: The Oceanids and river gods

In Greek mythology, Tethys (Greek: Τηθύς, romanized: Tēthýs) was a Titan daughter of Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth), and the sister and wife of the Titan Oceanus, god of the sea. Together they were the parents of the great rivers of the world — including the Nile, Alpheus, and Maeander — and of some three thousand daughters, the Oceanids. As a primordial sea goddess she embodied the fresh waters that nourished the earth.

Mythology

Tethys took no active part in most myths and, like the other Titans, did not join the Titanomachy against Zeus — for which reason she and Oceanus were left in peace after the war. In the Iliad, Hera claims she is traveling to visit Oceanus and Tethys, who raised her during the war against the Titans, and a passage in Book 14 calls Oceanus "origin of the gods" and Tethys their mother. In some traditions Tethys also fostered Hera, and the goddess's constellation lore — the circumpolar stars that never set — was explained by Tethys refusing to receive them at Hera's request.

Legacy

Tethys's name survives in the Tethys Ocean, the ancient ocean named by geologist Eduard Suess in 1893, and in Tethys, a moon of Saturn discovered in 1684.
