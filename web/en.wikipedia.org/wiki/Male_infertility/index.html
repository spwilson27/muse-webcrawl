Male infertility

Male infertility refers to a sexually mature male's inability to cause pregnancy in a fertile female. It accounts for about 40–50% of cases of infertility in humans, affecting roughly 7% of all men. Male infertility is commonly due to deficiencies in the semen, and semen quality is used as a surrogate measure of male fecundity.

Causes

Pre-testicular and hormonal

Disorders of the hypothalamus or pituitary gland can reduce secretion of gonadotropins, impairing testosterone production and spermatogenesis. Genetic conditions such as Kallmann syndrome and pituitary tumours are examples; anabolic steroid use can suppress the axis as well.

Testicular

Testicular causes include genetic abnormalities such as Klinefelter syndrome and Y-chromosome microdeletions, cryptorchidism (undescended testes), varicocele, infections such as mumps orchitis, gonadotoxic drugs and radiation, chemotherapy, and testicular torsion or trauma. Varicocele — an enlargement of the veins within the scrotum — is the most common correctable cause of male infertility.

Post-testicular

Obstructions of the sperm transport tract (including congenital absence of the vas deferens, associated with cystic fibrosis gene mutations, and obstruction after vasectomy or infection) and disorders of ejaculation or erectile function can prevent sperm from reaching the egg despite normal production.

Diagnosis

Evaluation centres on semen analysis, which assesses sperm concentration, motility, and morphology against World Health Organization reference values. Azoospermia (no sperm in the ejaculate) and oligospermia (low sperm count) are key findings. Physical examination, hormonal testing, genetic testing, and imaging may follow depending on the results.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause: surgical repair of varicocele or ductal obstruction, hormonal therapy for endocrine disorders, and surgical sperm retrieval combined with assisted reproductive technologies such as in vitro fertilisation with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), which can achieve fertilisation with a single sperm and has transformed the prognosis for severe male-factor infertility. Lifestyle factors — smoking, obesity, alcohol, and heat exposure — are commonly addressed.
