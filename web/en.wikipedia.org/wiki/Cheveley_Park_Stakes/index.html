The Cheveley Park Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to two-year-old fillies. It is run on the Rowley Mile at Newmarket Racecourse over a distance of six furlongs, usually in late September as part of the Cambridgeshire Meeting.

History

The race is named after Cheveley Park, an estate near Newmarket once owned by the race's founder, Harry McCalmont. It was established in 1899 and has long ranked among the most prestigious tests for juvenile fillies in Europe, often serving as a trial for the following season's 1000 Guineas.

Notable winners

Winners that went on to classic success include Pretty Polly, and more recently fillies such as Special Duty and Clemmie. The race's sponsor history includes the Duchess of Cambridge Stakes being its principal trial at the July Meeting.
