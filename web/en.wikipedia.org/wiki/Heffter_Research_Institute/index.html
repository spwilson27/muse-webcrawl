Heffter Research Institute

The Heffter Research Institute is a non-profit organization founded in 1993 in Santa Fe, New Mexico, United States, dedicated to the scientific study of psychedelic substances. The institute promotes research using psychedelics to study the mind and brain and to explore their potential for the treatment of addiction and other disorders, particularly in areas where current treatments are otherwise insufficient.

Heffter Research Institute

History

The institute is named after Dr. Arthur Heffter (1859–1925), a German chemist and pharmacologist who was the first to isolate a pure psychedelic compound — mescaline, from the peyote cactus — in 1897. The Heffter Research Institute was founded by a group of scientists including David E. Nichols, a medicinal chemist at Purdue University, together with colleagues such as Mark Geyer, Charles Grob, Dennis McKenna, Pablo Sanchez-Ramos, George Greer, and Phil Wolfson.

Research

The institute provides scientific and financial support for studies of psychedelic compounds, particularly psilocybin, at research institutions including Johns Hopkins University, New York University, the University of New Mexico, and Imperial College London. Areas of investigation supported by the institute have included psilocybin-assisted therapy for tobacco and alcohol addiction, psilocybin for anxiety and depression in patients with life-threatening cancer, the effects of ayahuasca, and basic neuroscience studies of how psychedelics affect the brain.
