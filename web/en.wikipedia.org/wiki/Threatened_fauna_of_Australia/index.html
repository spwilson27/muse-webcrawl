Threatened fauna of Australia
LegislationEnvironment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999
Key threatsInvasive species, habitat loss, altered fire regimes, climate change
Notable speciesKoala, numbat, northern quoll, greater bilby

The threatened fauna of Australia comprise the animal species native to Australia that face a high risk of extinction. Australia has one of the worst mammal extinction records in the world: more than thirty endemic mammal species have become extinct since European settlement in 1788, and many more are listed as threatened under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (EPBC Act).

Causes

The main drivers of decline are predation by introduced species — especially feral cats and red foxes — competition and habitat change caused by rabbits and livestock, land clearing for agriculture, altered fire regimes, and, increasingly, climate change. Many small-to-medium-sized ground mammals in the "critical weight range" of 35 grams to 5.5 kilograms have been especially affected.

Conservation

Conservation responses include feral predator control, fenced havens and island refuges free of cats and foxes, captive breeding, and reintroductions of species such as the greater bilby, numbat, and western quoll. Listed species receive legal protection under the EPBC Act, and threat abatement plans address key processes such as predation by feral cats.
