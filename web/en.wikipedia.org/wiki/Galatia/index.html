Galatia

Galatia was an ancient region in the highlands of central Anatolia, in modern Turkey, settled in the 3rd century BC by migrating Celtic tribes — the Galatians. Its main centres were Ancyra (modern Ankara), Pessinus and Tavium.


Location: Central Anatolia
Peoples: Galatian Celts
Roman province from: 25 BC

History

Celtic groups crossed into Asia Minor in 278 BC, raiding widely before settling. Their three tribes — Tectosages, Tolistobogii and Trocmi — formed a tribal federation. Galatia became a Roman client kingdom and, on the death of King Amyntas in 25 BC, a Roman province under Augustus.

Later history

Paul the Apostle addressed his Epistle to the Galatians to churches in the region. The Galatians gradually assimilated into Greek-speaking Anatolian culture, though the Galatian language survived for centuries — St Jerome noted its similarity to the speech of the Treveri.
