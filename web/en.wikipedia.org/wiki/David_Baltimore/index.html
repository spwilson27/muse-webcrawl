David Baltimore (7 March 1938 – 6 September 2025) was an American biologist, university administrator and Nobel laureate. In 1975 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Howard Temin and Renato Dulbecco "for their discoveries concerning the interaction between tumour viruses and the genetic material of the cell".

Reverse transcriptase

Working at MIT, Baltimore in 1970 independently discovered reverse transcriptase — an enzyme in retroviruses that synthesises DNA from an RNA template — at the same time as Temin. The discovery overturned a simplistic reading of the central dogma and proved essential to understanding retroviruses such as HIV, as well as to modern molecular biology techniques. He also devised the Baltimore classification, the standard system grouping viruses by their genome type and replication strategy, and his laboratory made major contributions to immunology, including the discovery of the recombination-activating genes (RAG) and work on the NF-κB transcription factor.

Institutional leadership and controversies

Baltimore was a founding director of the Whitehead Institute and president of Rockefeller University (1990–1991); his tenure there was overshadowed by the "Baltimore affair", a protracted dispute over alleged fraud in a 1986 paper he had co-authored — his co-author Thereza Imanishi-Kari was ultimately cleared, and Baltimore, who had defended her, apologised and resigned. He rebounded to serve as president of Caltech from 1997 to 2006, raising its profile and endowment. In 1975 he had been a key organiser of the Asilomar conference on recombinant DNA safety. Baltimore died in September 2025, aged 87.
