The Japanese Grand Prix is a motor racing event in the Formula One World Championship, held almost every year since 1976. It has been staged at two venues: Fuji Speedway (1976–77 and 2007–08) and, most famously, the Honda-owned Suzuka Circuit, which has hosted the race since 1987.

History

The first two Formula One Japanese Grands Prix were held at Fuji in 1976 and 1977; the 1976 race, run in torrential rain, decided the championship when James Hunt finished third after Niki Lauda withdrew, judging the conditions too dangerous. After a hiatus, the race returned at Suzuka in 1987 and became renowned for dramatic title deciders, including the collisions between Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost in 1989 and 1990.

Suzuka

Suzuka's figure-of-eight layout, with corners such as the Degners, Spoon Curve and 130R, is widely considered one of the most demanding circuits in Formula One. The race has traditionally been held in autumn, though it moved to a spring slot on the calendar in 2024. Michael Schumacher is the most successful driver at the event with six victories.
