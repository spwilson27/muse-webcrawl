Philmont Scout Ranch
Location: Near Cimarron, Colfax County, New Mexico, US
Area: 140,177 acres (219 sq mi)
Owner: Scouting America (Boy Scouts of America)
Donated: 1938 and 1941, by Waite Phillips
Known for: High-adventure backpacking treks

Philmont Scout Ranch is a large ranch in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains of northeastern New Mexico, owned and operated by Scouting America (the Boy Scouts of America) as a national high-adventure base. Covering some 140,000 acres, it is one of the largest youth camps in the world, hosting tens of thousands of Scouts and leaders each summer for backpacking treks.

History

The land was part of the Maxwell Land Grant and later the ranch of oilman Waite Phillips, who built the Villa Philmonte as his summer home. Phillips donated 35,857 acres in 1938 (originally called the Philturn Rockymountain Scoutcamp) and a further large tract, including the Tooth of Time ridge, in 1941, renaming the property by combining his name with "mountain". The ranch also operated as a working cattle ranch, and its history includes stretches of the Santa Fe Trail and the 19th-century mining town of Baldy Town.

Program

Philmont is best known for its 12-day (and shorter) backcountry treks, in which crews of Scouts hike between staffed camps offering programs such as rock climbing, horseback riding, black-powder rifle shooting, gold panning, mountain biking and conservation work. Signature landmarks include the Tooth of Time, Baldy Mountain (12,441 ft) and the Rayado country. Other programs include the Philmont Training Center for Scouting families and leaders, winter programs, and the Order of the Arrow Trail Crew.

Wildfires

The ranch has been affected by major wildfires, notably the Ponil Complex fire of 2002 and the Ute Park Fire of 2018, the latter of which forced the cancellation of much of that summer's trek season — the first full-season closure in Philmont's history.
