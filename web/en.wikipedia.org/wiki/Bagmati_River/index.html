Bagmati River

The Bagmati River (Nepali: बागमती नदी) flows through the Kathmandu Valley of Nepal, separating the cities of Kathmandu and Patan, before crossing into the Indian state of Bihar and joining the Kamala River, ultimately flowing into the Kosi. It rises at Bagdwar in the Shivapuri hills north of Kathmandu and runs for about 597 kilometres in total.

Religious significance

The river is considered holy by both Hindus and Buddhists. On its banks in Kathmandu stands the Pashupatinath Temple, one of the most sacred Hindu shrines dedicated to Shiva, where cremation ghats line the river. Numerous other temples stand along its course in the valley, and the annual Rishi Panchami rituals traditionally involve bathing in its waters.

Pollution

The Bagmati is severely polluted by untreated sewage, industrial effluent and solid waste from the rapidly growing Kathmandu metropolis, and its condition has been a major public concern. Government and citizen campaigns, including weekly volunteer clean-ups begun in 2013, have sought to restore the river, and a long-running Bagmati River Basin Improvement Project has worked on sewerage infrastructure.
