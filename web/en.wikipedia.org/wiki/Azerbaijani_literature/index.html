Azerbaijani literature

Language: Azerbaijani
Key figures: Nizami Ganjavi, Fuzuli, Imadaddin Nasimi
Classics: Book of Dede Korkut; Leyli and Majnun

Azerbaijani literature is the written and oral literature produced in the Azerbaijani language, one of the oldest literary languages of the Turkic world. Its heritage spans epic tradition, classical poetry in the Persianate mould, ashik folk poetry, and modern prose and drama.

Classical period

The epic Book of Dede Korkut preserves early Oghuz heroic tales. In the classical era, poets such as Imadaddin Nasimi, the founder of Azerbaijani divan poetry, and Muhammad Fuzuli, author of the celebrated Leyli and Majnun, wrote in Azerbaijani alongside Persian and Arabic. Nizami Ganjavi, though writing in Persian, remains central to the region's literary identity.

Modern period

The 19th century saw the rise of realism and theatre with Mirza Fatali Akhundov; the early 20th century brought satirical journalism around the magazine Molla Nasraddin. Soviet and post-Soviet literature includes poets such as Samad Vurgun and novelists such as Anar.
