Synchronized swimming, officially renamed artistic swimming in 2017, is a hybrid form of swimming, dance, and gymnastics in which swimmers perform a synchronized routine of elaborate moves in the water, accompanied by music. Competitors perform in solos, duets, trios, and teams, judged on execution, artistic impression, and difficulty.

History

The sport grew out of water ballet and ornamental swimming displays in Canada and the United States in the early 20th century. Annette Kellerman's glass-tank performances and Esther Williams's film aquamusicals popularized it. It became an Olympic sport at the 1984 Los Angeles Games.

Competition

Routines require exceptional breath control, flexibility, and strength; swimmers use eggbeater kick to stay elevated and perform lifts, spins, and figures, often upside down. Russia (and the Soviet Union before it) long dominated Olympic competition, followed by strong programs in China, Spain, Japan, and Ukraine. Men were admitted to World Championships mixed duets in 2015 and to the Olympic team event in 2024.
