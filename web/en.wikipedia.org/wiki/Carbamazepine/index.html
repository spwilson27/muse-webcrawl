Carbamazepine, sold under the trade name Tegretol among others, is an anticonvulsant medication used primarily in the treatment of epilepsy and neuropathic pain. It is first-line for trigeminal neuralgia and is also used in bipolar disorder. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Carbamazepine is effective for focal and tonic-clonic seizures but may worsen absence and myoclonic seizures. It is the drug of choice for trigeminal neuralgia and is used off-label for other neuropathic pain and as a mood stabiliser.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dizziness, drowsiness, nausea, and unsteadiness. Serious effects include hyponatraemia (via SIADH), blood dyscrasias such as aplastic anaemia and agranulocytosis, and severe skin reactions including Stevens–Johnson syndrome — a risk strongly associated with the HLA-B*1502 allele, common in some Asian populations, for which screening is recommended. Carbamazepine induces its own metabolism and that of many other drugs, causing numerous interactions, and is teratogenic, associated with neural tube defects.

History

Carbamazepine was discovered by chemist Walter Schindler at Geigy in 1953 and first marketed in Switzerland in 1962, initially for trigeminal neuralgia and later for epilepsy.
