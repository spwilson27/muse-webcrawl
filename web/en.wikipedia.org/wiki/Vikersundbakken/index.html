Vikersundbakken is a ski flying hill at Vikersund in Modum, Norway. It is the largest ski jumping hill in the world and the only ski flying hill in northern Europe. After several rebuilds, the hill now has a hill size of HS240 and a K-point of K-200.

The hill was built in 1935 and opened in 1936. It has been renovated on several occasions, most recently in 2010, when the inrun was lengthened and the profile redesigned to allow longer flights. On 25 February 2011, Vikersundbakken hosted the first official ski flying competition in which a jump exceeded 240 metres.

History

Ski jumping in Vikersund dates to the late 19th century, but the modern ski flying hill was constructed in 1935 by a local committee led by Kristian Hovde. The first record on the hill was set by Birger Ruud, who jumped 76 metres in 1936. Over the following decades the hill was repeatedly enlarged to keep pace with the development of ski flying elsewhere in Europe, particularly at Planica in Slovenia and Oberstdorf in Germany.

The major reconstruction of 2010, costing around 50 million Norwegian kroner, moved the takeoff table and steepened the landing slope, making the hill capable of hosting jumps in excess of 250 metres. In 2015, Anders Fannemel became the first jumper to exceed 250 metres at Vikersund, landing at 251.5 metres. Two years later, Stefan Kraft of Austria set the current world record of 253.5 metres on the hill.

Events

Vikersundbakken regularly hosts FIS Ski Flying World Cup competitions and has hosted the FIS Ski Flying World Championships on several occasions, including 1977, 1990, 2000, 2012 and 2022. The annual Raw Air tournament, established in 2017, includes a stop at Vikersund.
