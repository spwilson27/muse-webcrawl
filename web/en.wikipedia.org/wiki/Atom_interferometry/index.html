Atom interferometry

FieldQuantum physics, metrology

Atom interferometry uses the wave-like nature of atoms, as described by quantum mechanics, to perform interferometric measurements with very high precision. Like optical interferometers, atom interferometers split a matter wave into paths and recombine them; the resulting interference pattern is sensitive to accelerations, rotations, gravity and fundamental constants.

Techniques

Early demonstrations in 1991 used mechanical gratings; modern devices use laser pulses as beam splitters and mirrors for cold atoms, often in a Mach–Zehnder configuration, following methods developed by groups including Mark Kasevich's at Stanford.

Applications

Applications include gravimeters and gyroscopes, tests of the equivalence principle and general relativity, measurements of the fine-structure constant, searches for dark matter and gravitational waves with proposed large-baseline detectors, and inertial navigation.
