At sign

The @ character, indispensable to email addresses and social-media handles.
At sign
Character@
UnicodeU+0040 (Commercial At)
Traditional meaning"at the rate of" in commercial bookkeeping

The at sign, @, is a logogram originally meaning "at the rate of" in accounting — as in "7 widgets @ $2" — that became globally familiar as the separator in email addresses and the prefix of social-media user names.

History

The symbol's origins are debated: it may derive from a scribal ligature of the Latin ad ("at, toward"), from the French à, or from the abbreviation of the Greek amphora measure; a 1536 letter by the Florentine merchant Francesco Lapi uses @ for amphorae of wine. It survived into the typewriter era as a commercial key, present on the standard keyboard but little used by the general public.

Email and computing

In 1971, Ray Tomlinson of BBN chose @ to separate user name from host name in networked email because it was available on the keyboard, meant "at," and appeared in no names. Social networks later adopted @ for mentions and handles. In programming, @ appears in decorators (Python), annotations (Java), directives and several languages' syntax; in everyday writing it is also used to gender-neutralise words in Spanish ("latinx"-style spellings such as amig@s). Nicknames for the symbol in many languages are whimsical — "monkey tail," "snail," "little mouse," "strudel."
