New Atheism

Period2004–present (peak prominence late 2000s)

New Atheism is a term coined in 2006 for a group of contemporary writers and public intellectuals who advocate the view that religion should not simply be tolerated but should be countered, criticised and exposed by rational argument wherever its influence arises.

Origins

The movement is commonly dated to a cluster of bestselling books published between 2004 and 2007: Sam Harris's The End of Faith (2004), Richard Dawkins's The God Delusion (2006), Daniel Dennett's Breaking the Spell (2006) and Christopher Hitchens's God Is Not Great (2007).

The four authors were dubbed the 'Four Horsemen' after a filmed discussion among them in 2007.

Ideas

New Atheist writers argue that religious belief is unsupported by evidence, that faith can motivate harmful behaviour, and that the deference traditionally granted to religion in public life should be withdrawn.

They also promoted science education and secular ethics, and some associated themselves with broader movements for secularism and skepticism.

Criticism

Critics, including some atheists and philosophers, have accused New Atheism of philosophical naivety, of conflating fundamentalist religion with all religion, and of hostility toward believers. Others objected to statements by some figures about Islam and to positions taken in debates over gender and race.

By the mid-2010s the label was less frequently used, though the books and debates of the period continued to influence public discussion of religion.
