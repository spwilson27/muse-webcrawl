Alpha blocker

Alpha blockers, also called alpha-adrenergic antagonists, are a class of pharmacological agents that act as antagonists at alpha-adrenergic receptors. By blocking these receptors they inhibit the effects of adrenaline (epinephrine) and noradrenaline (norepinephrine) on blood vessels and other tissues.

Classification

Alpha-1 blockers, such as prazosin, doxazosin, terazosin, tamsulosin, and alfuzosin, relax smooth muscle in blood vessels and in the prostate and bladder neck. Alpha-2 blockers, such as yohimbine, have more limited clinical use. Some agents, like phenoxybenzamine and phentolamine, are non-selective.

Medical uses

Alpha-1 blockers are used to treat benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), improving urinary flow, and were historically used for hypertension, though they are no longer first-line because of heart-failure risk shown in the ALLHAT trial. They are also used for ureteral stones, pheochromocytoma management, and symptoms of PTSD-related nightmares (prazosin).

Side effects

Common adverse effects include orthostatic hypotension (especially a "first-dose effect"), dizziness, headache, and retrograde ejaculation with uroselective agents such as tamsulosin.
