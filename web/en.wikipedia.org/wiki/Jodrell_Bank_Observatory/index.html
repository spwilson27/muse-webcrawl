Jodrell Bank Observatory

Jodrell Bank Observatory in Cheshire, England, hosts a number of radio telescopes as part of the Jodrell Bank Centre for Astrophysics at the University of Manchester. Established in 1945 by Bernard Lovell, it is best known for the Lovell Telescope, one of the world's largest fully steerable radio telescopes. The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

History

Bernard Lovell used surplus radar equipment to study cosmic rays and meteors at the site, then a university botany field station. The 250-foot (76 m) Lovell Telescope, completed in 1957, famously tracked the carrier rocket of Sputnik 1 and later contributed to pulsar, quasar, and gravitational lens research, as well as space probe communications.

Facilities

The observatory is the hub of e-MERLIN, a network of radio telescopes across England operated as a national facility. The Mark II telescope (1964) and smaller instruments support research and engineering. A visitor centre and the annual bluedot festival engage the public; the site is headquarters of the Square Kilometre Array Observatory.
