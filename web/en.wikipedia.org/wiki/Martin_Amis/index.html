Martin Amis

Sir Martin Louis Amis (25 August 1949 – 19 May 2023) was an English novelist, essayist, memoirist, and screenwriter. He is best known for the novels Money (1984), London Fields (1989), and The Information (1995), and for his memoir Experience (2000). His father was the novelist Kingsley Amis.

Career

Educated at Exeter College, Oxford, Amis worked at the Times Literary Supplement and as literary editor of the New Statesman before his first novel, The Rachel Papers (1973), won the Somerset Maugham Award. Money, a satire of greed and self-destruction in 1980s London and New York, established him as a leading stylist of his generation. Later works include Time's Arrow (1991), narrated in reverse; The Zone of Interest (2014), set in Auschwitz; and Inside Story (2020), a novelised autobiography.

Style and reputation

Amis was celebrated — and sometimes criticised — for his virtuoso prose: Nabokovian wordplay, pyrotechnic sentences, and a mimic's ear for dialogue. A prominent public intellectual, he wrote essays on politics, literature, and language, and feuded and reconciled publicly with his close friend Christopher Hitchens. He was knighted in 2023, shortly before his death from oesophageal cancer at his home in Florida.
