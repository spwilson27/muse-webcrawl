Joel Salatin (born February 24, 1957) is an American farmer, author, and lecturer known for his advocacy of sustainable, pasture-based livestock farming at Polyface Farm in Swoope, Virginia, in the Shenandoah Valley. He gained wide recognition through Michael Pollan's book The Omnivore's Dilemma (2006) and the documentary Food, Inc. (2008).

Polyface Farm and methods

Salatin's family purchased the Polyface farm in 1961, and Joel took over its management in the early 1980s. The farm raises cattle, pigs, chickens, rabbits, and turkeys using intensive rotational grazing: cattle are moved daily to fresh pasture in portable fencing, followed by laying hens in mobile "eggmobile" shelters that spread manure and control pests, mimicking the relationships of wild herbivore herds and birds. Chickens are raised in movable floorless pens ("chicken tractors") and processed on-farm. Salatin markets directly to local customers, restaurants, and buying clubs, refusing to ship food long distances or sell through conventional supermarket channels.

Writing and advocacy

Salatin has written numerous books on farming and food policy, including Pastured Poultry Profits (1993), You Can Farm (1998), Salad Bar Beef, Everything I Want to Do Is Illegal (2007), and The Sheer Ecstasy of Being a Lunatic Farmer (2010). A self-described "Christian libertarian environmentalist capitalist lunatic farmer," he is a prominent critic of industrial agriculture and of food-safety regulations he argues are biased toward large processors, and a frequent speaker at sustainable agriculture conferences.
