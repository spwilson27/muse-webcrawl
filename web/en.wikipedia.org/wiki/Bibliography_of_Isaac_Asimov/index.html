Isaac Asimov bibliography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Isaac Asimov (1920–1992) was an American writer of science fiction and popular science. According to UNESCO's Index Translationum, Asimov is the world's 24th most-translated author. He wrote or edited more than 500 books — counting individual volumes, short stories and essays — and roughly 90,000 letters and postcards. His bibliography is one of the largest of any 20th-century author.

Science fiction

Foundation series

The core trilogy — Foundation (1951), Foundation and Empire (1952) and Second Foundation (1953) — grew from stories published in Astounding Science Fiction in the 1940s. Asimov later added sequels Foundation's Edge (1982) and Foundation and Earth (1986) and prequels Prelude to Foundation (1988) and Forward the Foundation (1993, posthumous).

Robot series

Asimov's robot stories, introducing the Three Laws of Robotics, were collected in I, Robot (1950), and continued in the Elijah Baley novels The Caves of Steel (1954), The Naked Sun (1957), The Robots of Dawn (1983) and Robots and Empire (1985).

Galactic Empire and other novels

Empire novels include The Stars, Like Dust (1951), The Currents of Space (1952) and Pebble in the Sky (1950). Standalone novels include The End of Eternity (1955), The Gods Themselves (1972, Hugo and Nebula Award winner) and Fantastic Voyage (1966).

Popular science

Asimov wrote science essays monthly for The Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction from 1958 until his death — 399 consecutive columns — collected in dozens of volumes. His books include The Intelligent Man's Guide to Science (1960), Asimov's Guide to the Bible (1968–69), Asimov's Chronology of the World (1991) and the children's Lucky Starr series (written as Paul French).

Cataloguing

Asimov himself organised his books by the Dewey Decimal Classification in his autobiographies, and prepared a self-bibliography, Opus 100 (1969), Opus 200 (1979) and Opus 300 (1984), marking each hundred-book milestone.
