The Laurentian Codex (Russian: Лаврентьевская летопись, Lavrentyevskaya letopis) is a collection of chronicles copied in 1377 by the monk Lavrenty (Laurence) for Prince Dmitry of Suzdal. It is one of the most important sources for the early history of the East Slavs.

Contents

The codex contains the oldest surviving copy of the Primary Chronicle (The Tale of Bygone Years), extended to 1305, together with a series of chronicles relating the history of the Vladimir-Suzdal lands. It also includes the Instruction (Pouchenie) of Vladimir Monomakh — his testament and autobiographical writings — a text preserved only in this manuscript tradition.

History

Named after its scribe, the codex was acquired by Count Aleksey Musin-Pushkin (who also discovered the Tale of Igor's Campaign) in the late 18th century and presented to Alexander I; it is now held by the Russian National Library in Saint Petersburg. Together with the later Hypatian Codex, it is one of the two principal witnesses to the text of the Primary Chronicle.
