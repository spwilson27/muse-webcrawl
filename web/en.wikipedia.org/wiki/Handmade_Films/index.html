Handmade Films

Handmade Films is a British film production company founded by musician and actor George Harrison and his business partner Denis O'Brien in 1978. It was created to finance the Monty Python film Life of Brian (1979) after the original backers, EMI Films, withdrew at the last moment; Harrison famously mortgaged his house to fund the production, which Python member Eric Idle called "the most expensive cinema ticket ever bought".

Productions

The company went on to produce a string of notable British films of the 1980s, including Time Bandits (1981), The Long Good Friday (1980), Mona Lisa (1986), Withnail and I (1987), A Private Function (1984), and Shanghai Surprise (1986), whose failure contributed to the company's decline.

Later history

Financial difficulties and litigation with O'Brien led Harrison to wind down involvement in the early 1990s, and the company's library was sold. Handmade was later revived as a public company and continued to license its catalogue.
