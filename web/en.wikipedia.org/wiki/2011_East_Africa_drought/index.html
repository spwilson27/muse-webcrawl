2011 East Africa drought

Event details
Period2011 (worst mid-2011)
Regions affectedSomalia, Ethiopia, Kenya, Djibouti, Eritrea

Primary causesFailed rains, La Niña, conflict, food prices

The 2011 East Africa drought was a severe drought affecting the Horn of Africa region, regarded as the worst in the area in some 60 years. Two consecutive failed rainy seasons, driven in part by a strong La Niña event, caused widespread crop failure and livestock deaths across Somalia, Ethiopia and Kenya. In July 2011 the United Nations declared a famine in parts of southern Somalia — the first formal famine declaration of the 21st century — and more than 13 million people across the region required humanitarian assistance.

Causes

The drought followed the failure of the October–December 2010 deyr rains and the poor April–June 2011 gu rains, producing the driest period in the eastern Horn since 1950–51. High global food and fuel prices, reduced remittances and, in Somalia, two decades of civil conflict and the presence of the militant group al-Shabaab compounded the effects, restricting both food production and humanitarian access.

Humanitarian impact

Hundreds of thousands of Somalis fled to refugee camps in Kenya and Ethiopia, notably Dadaab, which became one of the largest refugee complexes in the world, and Dollo Ado in Ethiopia. Malnutrition rates among children in southern Somalia exceeded emergency thresholds by wide margins, and measles and cholera outbreaks followed. Later studies estimated that roughly 258,000 people died in Somalia between October 2010 and April 2012, about half of them children under five.

Response

The UN appealed for billions of dollars in aid; donors including the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union pledged large sums, though funding arrived slowly and access to al-Shabaab-controlled areas remained severely limited. The famine declaration for southern Somalia was lifted in February 2012 after improved rains and aid deliveries, but the crisis continued in various forms, and the region faced renewed drought emergencies in later years, including the severe multi-season drought of 2020–2023.
