Xian

Xian (Chinese: 仙; pinyin: xiān; Wade–Giles: hsien) is a Chinese word for a transcendent person or an immortal being, used in Taoism and Chinese mythology. Xian are often translated as "immortals" or "transcendents," and are associated with longevity, supernatural abilities, and harmonious alignment with the Tao.

Etymology and meaning

The character 仙 combines the radicals for "person" and "mountain," suggesting a mountain-dwelling sage or hermit. In early texts, xian were depicted as beings who had transcended mortality through spiritual cultivation, alchemy, diet, breath control, or other practices, sometimes shown with feathers and the ability to fly.

In Taoism

Within religious Taoism, attaining xian-hood became a central goal. Practices included external alchemy (waidan), the compounding of elixirs — notoriously including cinnabar preparations — and later internal alchemy (neidan), meditation, gymnastics (daoyin), and dietary regimens such as grain avoidance (bigu). Famous legendary immortals include the Eight Immortals (Baxian), each with distinctive attributes and stories.

Depictions

Xian appear throughout Chinese literature, art and folklore, dwelling in paradises such as Mount Kunlun, the Isles of the Blessed (Penglai), or celestial bureaus, and are a recurring subject in poetry from the Han dynasty onward. The concept also influenced Korean, Japanese and Vietnamese religious culture.
