Shelby Cobra

The Shelby Cobra (sold in the United States as the Shelby Cobra and in the United Kingdom as the AC Cobra) is a sports car manufactured by British company AC Cars, with a Ford V8 engine. It was produced intermittently in both the United Kingdom and later the United States from 1962 to 1967.

History

Retired racing driver Carroll Shelby approached AC Cars with a proposal to build a lightweight sports car powered by an American V8 engine. AC, whose Ace roadster was losing its engine supply, agreed, and Ford provided its new small-block V8. The first cars, powered by the 260 cu in (4.3 L) engine and later the 289 cu in (4.7 L), began production in 1962.

Cobra 427

In 1965 Shelby introduced the big-block Cobra 427, built around a new chassis with coil-spring suspension and powered by Ford's 427 cu in (7.0 L) side-oiler V8, producing around 425 horsepower in street trim. A competition version was rated at roughly 485 horsepower. The 427 became famous as one of the fastest production cars of its era.

Racing

Cobras were highly successful in American sports car racing, and the enclosed Shelby Daytona Coupe variant won the FIA International Championship for GT Manufacturers in 1965, defeating Ferrari. The small-block Cobra won numerous SCCA championships, while the big-block cars dominated in drag and road racing.

Legacy

Original Cobras are among the most valuable collector cars in the world, and numerous replica and continuation cars have been built. Counterfeit and replica vehicles are so numerous that genuine cars are carefully documented in the Shelby American registry.
