Schechter luminosity function

The Schechter function: a power law with an exponential cut-off at high luminosities

The Schechter luminosity function is an analytic formula describing the distribution of galaxy luminosities in a volume of space. It was proposed by Paul Schechter in 1976 and remains the standard parameterisation of the galaxy luminosity function.

Formula

The function gives the number density of galaxies with luminosities between L and L + dL:

φ(L) dL = φ* (L/L*)α exp(−L/L*) (dL/L*)

It is characterised by three parameters:

  • φ* — the normalisation (number density of galaxies);
  • L* — the characteristic luminosity at which the power law breaks and the exponential cut-off begins;
  • α — the faint-end slope, typically around −1 to −1.3 for field galaxies.

Interpretation

At luminosities below L*, the distribution follows a power law: faint galaxies are far more numerous than bright ones. Above L*, the exponential term suppresses the abundance sharply, reflecting the rarity of very luminous galaxies. The form is thought to arise from the physics of galaxy formation, in which the halo mass function is truncated by feedback processes such as supernova and active galactic nucleus heating.

Applications

Fitting the Schechter function to survey data allows astronomers to compute the total luminosity density of the universe, track the evolution of the galaxy population with redshift, and compare observations with cosmological simulations.
