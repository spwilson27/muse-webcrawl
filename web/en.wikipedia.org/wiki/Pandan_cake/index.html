Pandan cake

Pandan cake is a light, fluffy green sponge or chiffon cake flavoured with the juice of pandan leaves (Pandanus amaryllifolius), popular across Southeast Asia, particularly in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and the Philippines. The cake's characteristic green colour and fragrant, vanilla-like aroma come naturally from the pandan leaf extract.

Origins and varieties

The cake blends European chiffon and sponge techniques, introduced through colonial contact, with local pandan flavouring; it is sometimes called pandan chiffon. Singapore's Bengawan Solo bakery is credited with popularising a celebrated version in the 1970s, and pandan cake was named Singapore's national cake by CNN in 2017. Variations include Indonesian bolu pandan, steamed versions, and the Filipino buko pandan family of desserts, which pair pandan with young coconut.

Preparation

A typical pandan chiffon is made with egg yolks, flour, sugar, oil, and coconut milk or water blended with pandan juice, folded into stiffly beaten egg whites and baked in an ungreased tube pan, which the cake climbs as it rises and in which it cools inverted to preserve its airy texture.
