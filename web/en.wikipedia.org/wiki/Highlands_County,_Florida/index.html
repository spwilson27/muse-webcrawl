Highlands County is a county in the Florida Heartland region of the US state of Florida. Its county seat is Sebring. The county was created in 1921 from DeSoto County and named for its rolling terrain, which is high by Florida standards, sitting astride the Lake Wales Ridge. At the 2020 census its population was 101,235.

Geography and economy

The county covers about 2,865 km² (1,106 sq mi) of ridge, scrub and lake country in central peninsular Florida, including large lakes such as Lake Istokpoga, one of Florida's largest. Agriculture — especially citrus, cattle and caladium bulbs (centred on Lake Placid, which calls itself the "Caladium Capital of the World") — underpins the economy, together with tourism and retirement communities. Sebring is internationally known for the Sebring International Raceway, host of the 12 Hours of Sebring endurance race since 1952.

Communities

The incorporated municipalities are Sebring, Avon Park and Lake Placid; other communities include Lorida, Venus and Placid Lakes. Highlands Hammock State Park, one of Florida's first state parks, lies near Sebring.
