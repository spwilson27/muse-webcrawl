OODA loop

The OODA loop is a decision-making model developed by United States Air Force Colonel John Boyd. The acronym stands for the four stages of the cycle: observe, orient, decide, and act. Boyd originally developed the concept from his experience as a fighter pilot in the Korean War and in air combat manoeuvring theory, then extended it into a general theory of strategy and competitive decision-making.

The four stages

To observe is to collect data from the environment and oneself. To orient is to analyse and synthesize that data through the filters of cultural traditions, genetic heritage, previous experience, and analysis — Boyd regarded orientation as the most important stage, since it shapes how the other stages are performed. To decide is to generate and select a course of action, and to act is to implement it, whereupon the cycle repeats with new observations.

Strategic use

Boyd argued that a combatant who can cycle through the loop faster than an adversary gains an advantage: operating "inside" the opponent's decision cycle generates confusion and disorder, causing the opponent's orientation to fall increasingly out of step with unfolding reality until their system collapses. The concept has been adopted in the doctrine of manoeuvre warfare, and versions of it are widely used in business strategy, law enforcement, cyber security, and litigation.

Criticism

Critics note that the simple four-step diagram is a simplification of Boyd's own much richer sketch, which included feedback paths, implicit guidance, and unfolding interaction, and that the model describes rather than prescribes decision-making.
