Baldwin V (c. 1177 – 1186) was king of Jerusalem from 1185 until his death in 1186. The son of Sibylla of Jerusalem and her first husband, William of Montferrat, he was crowned co-king during the lifetime of his uncle, the leper king Baldwin IV, and succeeded as sole king as a small child. His brief reign was dominated by the regency of Count Raymond III of Tripoli and by factional struggle at court.

Background

Because Baldwin IV's leprosy made a direct succession impossible, the kingdom's future turned on the marriage of his sister Sibylla. Her son by William Longsword of Montferrat, the young Baldwin, was the natural heir. In November 1183 the dying king had the five-year-old crowned co-king to forestall the ambitions of Sibylla's unpopular second husband, Guy of Lusignan, and Count Raymond of Tripoli became regent.

Reign and death

Baldwin IV died in 1185, and the child king reigned under Raymond's regency, during which a truce with Saladin was maintained. In the summer of 1186, aged about nine, Baldwin V died at Acre. The succession settlement — that a committee including the pope and western monarchs should decide between Sibylla and her half-sister Isabella — was ignored: Sibylla was crowned and crowned Guy of Lusignan as king. The resulting resentment among the baronage deepened the divisions that crippled the kingdom on the eve of Saladin's invasion and the disaster at Hattin in 1187.
