The Comeragh Mountains (Irish: Sléibhte an Chomaraigh) are a glaciated mountain range in County Waterford, in the south-east of Ireland. The range runs roughly north–south between the towns of Clonmel, Dungarvan, Rathgormack and Kilmacthomas, and forms one of the principal uplands of Munster.

Geology and landscape

The mountains are formed largely of Devonian Old Red Sandstone and conglomerates. Glacial action sculpted deep corries (coums) into the eastern face, many occupied by small lakes — Coumshingaun Lough, set in a dramatic horseshoe corrie, is the best known. The highest summit is Fauscoum at 792 metres (2,598 ft); other notable peaks include Knockanaffrin (755 m) and the Sgilloges. The Mahon Falls, an 80-metre waterfall on the River Mahon, is a popular visitor attraction, and the range is noted for its "Magic Road", a gravity hill.

Recreation

The Comeraghs are popular with hillwalkers, offering plateau walking, scrambling in the corries and views over the Waterford coast and the plains of Tipperary. The range's bogs and heaths support red grouse and other upland wildlife, and parts are designated for nature conservation.
