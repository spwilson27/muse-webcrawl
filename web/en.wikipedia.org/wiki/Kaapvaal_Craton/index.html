Kaapvaal Craton

The Kaapvaal Craton is an ancient, stable block of the Earth's crust in southern Africa, covering much of South Africa's Northern Cape, North West, Limpopo and Mpumalanga provinces and parts of Botswana and Eswatini. Formed and stabilised between about 3.7 and 2.6 billion years ago, it is one of the world's best-preserved pieces of early continental crust.

Its basement consists of Archaean granite-greenstone terrains, including the Barberton Greenstone Belt, which contains some of the oldest known evidence of life on Earth. The craton hosts the Witwatersrand Basin — the source of roughly a third of all gold ever mined — and the Bushveld Igneous Complex, the world's largest layered intrusion and a major source of platinum-group metals, chromium and vanadium.

The Vredefort impact structure, the world's largest verified meteorite impact crater, lies within the craton.
