Claudia Goldin

Claudia Dale Goldin (born May 14, 1946) is an American economic historian and labour economist who is the Henry Lee Professor of Economics at Harvard University. In 2023 she received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences "for having advanced our understanding of women's labour market outcomes", becoming the third woman — and the first solo female recipient — to win the economics prize. She is best known for her historical work on women in the American economy, the gender pay gap, income inequality, and the economics of education.

Career

Goldin was born in New York City and grew up in the Bronx, attending the Bronx High School of Science. She studied economics at Cornell University and earned her PhD from the University of Chicago in 1972, where she was influenced by Gary Becker and Robert Fogel. She taught at Princeton and the University of Pennsylvania before joining Harvard in 1990, becoming the first woman to be tenured in Harvard's economics department. She served as president of the American Economic Association in 2013–14 and directed the National Bureau of Economic Research's Development of the American Economy program for three decades.

Research

Goldin's book Understanding the Gender Gap: An Economic History of American Women (1990) traced two centuries of female labour-force participation, showing that its rise was not steady but U-shaped, and that much of the historical gap reflected the changing structure of work rather than discrimination alone. Her later research showed that the remaining gender earnings gap arises largely within occupations — particularly from pay structures that disproportionately reward long, continuous, and on-call hours ("greedy work") — rather than from women choosing different fields. She documented the transformative role of the birth-control pill in delaying marriage and enabling women's professional education, and, with Lawrence Katz, wrote The Race between Education and Technology (2008), a major account of American education and inequality. Her 2021 book Career and Family: Women's Century-Long Journey toward Equity synthesised her work for a general audience.

Honours

Besides the Nobel Prize, Goldin has received the IZA Prize in Labor Economics (2016), the Erwin Plein Nemmers Prize in Economics, and the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award. She is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
