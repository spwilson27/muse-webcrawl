Smallholding

Smallholding
Typical sizeUnder 50 acres (20 ha) in UK definitions; often far smaller
ActivitiesMixed livestock, horticulture, market gardening, self-sufficiency

A smallholding or smallholder is a small farm operating under a small-scale agriculture model. Definitions vary widely between contexts and countries: in the United Kingdom a smallholding is generally a holding of less than 50 acres (20 hectares), often run part-time or for self-sufficiency rather than as a full commercial farm. Worldwide, smallholders — farmers working a few hectares or less — produce a large share of the food supply in developing countries.

History in Britain

British smallholdings were promoted by legislation from the Small Holdings and Allotments Act 1908 onward, and after the First World War the Land Settlement (Facilities) Act 1919 created council smallholdings to settle returning servicemen. County council estates still let small farms to tenants today. The post-1970s self-sufficiency movement, popularised by John Seymour's The Complete Book of Self-Sufficiency and the television series The Good Life, revived interest in smallholding as a lifestyle.

Global smallholders

The FAO estimates there are some 500 million smallholder farms worldwide, supporting around two billion people. Smallholders typically practise mixed, low-input farming and face challenges of land tenure, credit access, and climate vulnerability, but are central to food security and agrobiodiversity.
