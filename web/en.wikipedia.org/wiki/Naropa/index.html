Naropa

Naropa (Sanskrit; Tibetan: ནཱ་རོ་པ་, 1016–1100 CE), also known as Abhayakirti, was an Indian Buddhist mahasiddha associated with the Nalanda and Vikramashila monastic universities and a key figure in the transmission of Buddhism to Tibet. He is famous for the Six Yogas (or Six Dharmas) of Naropa, which became central to the Kagyu school of Tibetan Buddhism.

Life

Born into an upper-class family in Bengal, Naropa was a renowned scholar at Nalanda, where he was appointed a gatekeeper (scholar of the four directions) under the abbot Atisha. Dissatisfied with mere learning, he sought the siddha Tilopa, who subjected him to twelve major and twelve minor hardships before accepting him as a disciple and transmitting the mahamudra lineage.

Teachings

The Six Yogas of Naropa are: tummo (inner heat), illusory body, dream yoga, luminosity (clear light), phowa (consciousness transference) and bardo (intermediate state). Through his student Marpa, the Tibetan translator, they entered the Kagyu lineage and became core completion-stage practices. Naropa University in Boulder, Colorado — founded in 1974 by Chögyam Trungpa — is named for him.
