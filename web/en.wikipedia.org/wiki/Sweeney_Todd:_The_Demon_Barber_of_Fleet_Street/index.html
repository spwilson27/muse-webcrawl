Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street

Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street is a 1979 musical thriller with music and lyrics by Stephen Sondheim and a book by Hugh Wheeler. It is based on Christopher Bond's 1973 play of the same name, which drew on the Victorian penny dreadful character Sweeney Todd. The story follows Todd — formerly Benjamin Barker, a barber transported to Australia on false charges by the corrupt Judge Turpin — who returns to London bent on revenge, murders his customers with a cut-throat razor, and has his accomplice Mrs. Lovett bake their bodies into meat pies. The original Broadway production won eight Tony Awards, including Best Musical, and the work is considered one of Sondheim's masterpieces.

Plot

Barber Benjamin Barker returns to London under the name Sweeney Todd after fifteen years' exile, learning from pie-shop owner Mrs. Lovett that his wife Lucy was raped by Judge Turpin and poisoned herself, and that his daughter Johanna is the judge's ward. Todd reopens his barber shop above Mrs. Lovett's establishment; when a rival barber threatens to expose him, Todd kills him, and Mrs. Lovett hits on baking the body into pies — a venture that makes her shop famous. Todd's revenge escalates into indiscriminate killing until, in the climax, he murders a beggar woman who proves to be Lucy herself, then kills the judge, and is in turn killed by the young Tobias.

Music and reception

The score — including "The Ballad of Sweeney Todd," "Johanna," "Pretty Women," "A Little Priest," and "Not While I'm Around" — is nearly continuous and operatic in scope, using the Dies Irae chant as a recurring motif. The original production starred Len Cariou and Angela Lansbury. Tim Burton's 2007 film adaptation starred Johnny Depp and Helena Bonham Carter. Major revivals, including the intimate 2017 Off-Broadway production staged in a working pie shop, have confirmed the musical's status as a modern classic.
