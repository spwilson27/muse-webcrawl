Nativity play

A nativity play or Christmas pageant is a play whose subject is the Nativity of Jesus, recounting the story of Jesus's birth as told in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke. Nativity plays are performed at Christmas in churches, schools and theatres across the Christian world, and are especially associated with children's performances in Britain, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and the United States.

History

The tradition descends from medieval liturgical drama and the mystery plays, which dramatised biblical episodes in churches and town squares. St Francis of Assisi is credited with creating the first living nativity scene at Greccio in 1223, using real people and animals to make the Christmas story vivid to ordinary worshippers. Liturgical plays such as the Officium Pastorum (Play of the Shepherds) dramatised the visits of the shepherds and the Magi to the infant Jesus.

Typical content

A typical school nativity play includes Mary and Joseph's journey to Bethlehem, the search for lodging and the innkeeper, the birth in a stable, the annunciation to the shepherds, the visit of the three wise men following the star, and often King Herod. Traditional carols are sung between scenes, and a narrator frequently links the episodes. Costumes are homemade, and productions range from simple tableaux for very young children to elaborate musical versions such as those performed in churches and community theatres.

Variations

In some traditions the pageant includes additional elements: in Mexico, Las Posadas re-enacts Mary and Joseph's search for shelter over nine nights; in parts of Europe, Epiphany plays follow the Magi. Modern schools sometimes adapt the format with contemporary or humorous scripts, and debates occasionally arise about religious content in state schools.
