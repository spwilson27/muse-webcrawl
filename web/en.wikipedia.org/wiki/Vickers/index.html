Vickers

Vickers was a famous British engineering conglomerate, founded in Sheffield in 1828 as a steel foundry and dissolved as a group in 1999. The name is associated with armaments, shipbuilding and aircraft.

History

The family firm of Vickers, Sons & Company expanded from steel into armour plate, artillery and warships, merging with Armstrong Whitworth's rival interests in part: in 1927 its armaments and shipbuilding assets combined with Armstrong Whitworth to form Vickers-Armstrongs, while the steel interests went elsewhere. Vickers produced the Vickers machine gun, used by the British Army for over fifty years, tanks, and aircraft including the Vickers Vimy (which made the first non-stop transatlantic flight in 1919), the Wellington bomber, the Viscount airliner and the VC10.

Post-war nationalisations stripped away aircraft (into BAC) and shipbuilding; the remaining Vickers plc diversified into engineering and medical equipment before being acquired by Rolls-Royce plc in 1999, whose defence interests were later sold on. The name survives in Vickers hardness testing and in historic product names.
