Watermill

TypeMill powered by a waterwheel
UsesGrinding grain, sawing, fulling, powering machinery

A watermill or water mill is a mill that uses hydropower. It is a structure that uses a water wheel or water turbine to drive a mechanical process such as milling (gristmilling), rolling, or hammering. Such processes are needed in the production of many material goods, including flour, lumber, paper, textiles, and many metal products.

History

Water-powered mills are known from the ancient world; the Greek geographer Strabo described a water mill at Cabeira in the 1st century BC, and the Barbegal complex in Roman Gaul used sixteen overshot wheels to mill grain on an industrial scale. Watermills spread widely in medieval Europe: the Domesday Book of 1086 records more than 5,600 mills in England alone.

Types

Wheels may be undershot (driven by the flow striking the bottom of the wheel), breastshot, or overshot (driven by water falling over the top, the most efficient arrangement). A mill race or leat channels water from a river or millpond to the wheel, and gearing converts the wheel's slow rotation into faster motion for millstones or machinery.
