Prehistory of Thailand


Ban Chiang painted pottery

The prehistory of Thailand spans from the earliest evidence of human habitation, hundreds of thousands of years ago, to the emergence of historic kingdoms in the first millennium AD. The region was home to some of Southeast Asia's earliest farming and bronze-working communities.

Palaeolithic and Hoabinhian

Stone tools attributed to early humans are known from sites such as Mae Tha in Lampang Province. From roughly the late Pleistocene, Hoabinhian hunter-gatherers occupied caves and rock shelters throughout the region, leaving distinctive pebble-tool industries and shell middens.

Neolithic and Bronze Age

By about 2000 BC, sites such as Ban Chiang and Non Nok Tha in the north-east show settled rice farming, domesticated animals and sophisticated painted pottery. Ban Chiang's bronze artefacts were once claimed to be the world's earliest; current dating places its bronze working in the second millennium BC, still among the earliest in Southeast Asia. Iron working and elaborate burials followed in the late first millennium BC.
