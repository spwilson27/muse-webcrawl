The Sandamuni Pagoda (Burmese: စန္ဒာမုနိဘုရား) is a Buddhist stupa located to the southeast of Mandalay Hill in Mandalay, Myanmar (Burma). It was built in 1874 by King Mindon in memory of his younger brother, Crown Prince Kanaung, who was assassinated along with three other princes during the Myingun Prince rebellion of 1866.

The pagoda complex is famous for its collection of 1,774 marble slabs inscribed with commentaries on the Tipitaka (the Pali Canon), each housed in its own small whitewashed stupa. This makes Sandamuni a companion site to the nearby Kuthodaw Pagoda, which holds the text of the Tipitaka itself on 729 slabs and is often described as "the world's largest book".

History

King Mindon, founder of Mandalay as the Burmese capital in 1857, was a devout Buddhist who convened the Fifth Buddhist Council in 1871, during which the Tipitaka was recited, edited and inscribed on the marble slabs at Kuthodaw. Following the assassination of Crown Prince Kanaung in 1866, Mindon had the remains of the slain princes interred at the site that became Sandamuni.

In 1874, the commentaries (Atthakatha) and sub-commentaries on the Tipitaka were inscribed on 1,774 marble slabs and installed around a central gilded stupa at Sandamuni. The central shrine also came to house a large iron Buddha image, the Sandamuni image, which had originally been cast in the 18th century and moved from Amarapura when Mandalay became the capital.

Layout

The complex is arranged around a central golden stupa in the Burmese style. Radiating around it in concentric arrangements are hundreds of small white stupas (known locally as kyauksa gu), each containing a single upright marble slab carved with dense Pali text in Burmese script. Shaded walkways run between the rows, and the site lies within sight of Mandalay Hill and the Kuthodaw Pagoda to the north.

Together, Kuthodaw and Sandamuni preserve in stone the entire Tipitaka and its principal commentaries — an extraordinary undertaking in Buddhist publishing history.
