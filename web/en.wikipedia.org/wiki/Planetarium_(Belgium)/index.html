Planetarium (Belgium)


The Planetarium building at Heysel, Brussels

The Planetarium of the Royal Observatory of Belgium (French: Planétarium de l'Observatoire royal de Belgique; Dutch: Planetarium van de Koninklijke Sterrenwacht van België) is a planetarium in the Heysel/Heizel district of Brussels, Belgium, near the Atomium. It is part of the Royal Observatory of Belgium and is one of the largest planetariums in Europe, with a dome 23 metres in diameter.

History

A first planetarium was built for the Brussels International Exposition of 1935 (the Alberteum). The present building opened in 1976. The dome is equipped to project thousands of stars onto its 840-square-metre screen, and the planetarium underwent a major modernization of its projection system in the 2000s, replacing the original opto-mechanical Zeiss projector setup with digital projection.

Activities

The planetarium presents astronomy shows for schools and the general public in French and Dutch, and hosts exhibitions, lectures, and educational activities run in association with the Royal Observatory. It forms part of Belgium's federal scientific institutions located on the Heysel plateau and in nearby Uccle.
