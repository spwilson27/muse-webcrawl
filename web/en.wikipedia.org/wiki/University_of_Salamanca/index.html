University of Salamanca

The University of Salamanca (Spanish: Universidad de Salamanca) is a Spanish public university in Salamanca, founded in 1218 by King Alfonso IX of León. It is the oldest university in the Hispanic world and one of the oldest in Europe still operating.

History

Granted a charter by Alfonso X in 1254 and recognized by the papacy, Salamanca became a center of scholasticism. The School of Salamanca, with Francisco de Vitoria and others, shaped international law and economics. Christopher Columbus's proposals were examined by its scholars, and Fray Luis de León famously resumed lectures after imprisonment with 'As we were saying yesterday…'

Today

The university enrolls around 30,000 students, is famed for Spanish language teaching, and its historic Escuelas Mayores facade is a Plateresque landmark where visitors search for a carved frog said to bring luck.
