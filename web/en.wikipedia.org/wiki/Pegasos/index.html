Pegasos

The Pegasos II motherboard.

Pegasos is a MicroATX motherboard and computer platform based on PowerPC processors, developed by bPlan GmbH and later Genesi. Released in the early 2000s, the Pegasos was designed to run MorphOS, AmigaOS 4, and several Linux distributions, and was marketed particularly to the Amiga community after the decline of Commodore.

Hardware

The original Pegasos (2002) used IBM and Motorola/Freescale PowerPC G3 processors on cards, with the ArticiaS northbridge. The revised Pegasos II (2003) supported G3 and G4 processors at higher clock speeds, DDR SDRAM, Gigabit Ethernet, and standard expansion including AGP and PCI slots, in a conventional MicroATX tower case. The platform used Open Firmware for booting.

Software

The machine shipped with MorphOS, an Amiga-compatible operating system, and also supported AmigaOS 4.1, various Linux distributions such as Debian and Yellow Dog Linux, and other PowerPC operating systems. Its combination of standard PC peripherals and PowerPC architecture made it a notable niche platform in the post-Amiga market.

Successors

Genesi later produced related designs including the Efika, a smaller MPC5200B-based board. Consumer production of the Pegasos ended in the mid-2000s as Genesi shifted toward embedded and ARM-based products.
