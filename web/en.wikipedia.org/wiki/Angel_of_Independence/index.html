The Angel of Independence (Spanish: El Ángel de la Independencia), commonly known as El Ángel, is a victory column on a roundabout of the Paseo de la Reforma in downtown Mexico City. Built to commemorate the centenary of Mexico's War of Independence, it was inaugurated on 16 September 1910 by President Porfirio Díaz and is one of the most recognisable landmarks of the Mexican capital.

Description

The monument consists of a stepped base decorated with bronze figures representing Law, War, Justice and Peace, a quadrangular pedestal bearing statues of heroes of the independence struggle, and a Corinthian column of pink quarry stone rising to a capital surmounted by a 6.7-metre gilded bronze Winged Victory — the "Angel" — holding a laurel crown and a broken chain. The figure was modelled by the Italian sculptor Enrique Alciati; the column was designed by architect Antonio Rivas Mercado.

History

Construction began in 1902; the first foundation sank into the soft lakebed soil and had to be demolished and redesigned on a steel-and-concrete footing. Since 1925 the base has served as a mausoleum containing the remains of independence heroes including Miguel Hidalgo, José María Morelos, Ignacio Allende and Vicente Guerrero. During the earthquake of 28 July 1957 the Angel fell and shattered; it was restored and re-erected within a year. The monument survived the 1985 and 2017 earthquakes.

El Ángel is the traditional site of national celebration — football victories and political demonstrations alike converge there — and since 2019, following feminist protests in which the base was graffitied, it has also become a focal point of debate over monuments and public memory.
