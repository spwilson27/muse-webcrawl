La Fortaleza

La Fortaleza (The Fortress), also known as the Palacio de Santa Catalina, is the official residence of the governor of Puerto Rico, located in Old San Juan. Built between 1533 and 1540 to defend the harbor of San Juan, it is the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas and forms part of the San Juan National Historic Site, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

La Fortaleza was the first defensive fortification built for the city of San Juan, originally consisting of a circular tower and four stone walls. It proved militarily inadequate — it was captured twice, by the Earl of Cumberland in 1598 and by the Dutch under Boudewijn Hendricksz in 1625 — and was soon superseded by larger forts such as El Morro.

In 1649 the structure was converted into the residence of the Spanish colonial governors. A major reconstruction in 1846 transformed the fortress into a palatial residence with marble facades, gardens, and a chapel. It has served as the governor's mansion ever since, under both Spanish and, after 1898, United States administration.

Siege of 1950

During the Puerto Rican Nationalist Party revolts of 1950, nationalist gunmen attacked La Fortaleza in an attempt to assassinate Governor Luis Muñoz Marín; the assault was repelled by police, and the governor was unharmed.
