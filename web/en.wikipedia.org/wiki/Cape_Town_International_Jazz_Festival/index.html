Cape Town International Jazz Festival

Genre: Jazz and related music
Location: Cape Town International Convention Centre, Cape Town, South Africa
Years active: 2000–present
Attendance: Around 35,000–37,000
Nickname: "Africa's Grandest Gathering"

The Cape Town International Jazz Festival is an annual music festival held in Cape Town, South Africa. First held in 2000, it has grown into the largest music event in sub-Saharan Africa and is often ranked among the leading jazz festivals in the world, earning it the nickname "Africa's Grandest Gathering".

The festival

The two-day event takes place at the Cape Town International Convention Centre (CTICC), where several indoor stages — named Kippies, Moses Molelekwa, Basil "Manenberg" Coetzee, the Rosies and the Top Billing stage in various years — host simultaneous performances. The line-up typically features around 40 international and African acts, spanning straight-ahead jazz, fusion, Afro-jazz, soul, funk and hip hop.

International headliners over the years have included Herbie Hancock, Chick Corea, Jill Scott, Erykah Badu, Hugh Masekela, Abdullah Ibrahim and many others; the festival has been an important platform for South African jazz and for emerging African artists.

Development programmes

Beyond the main concerts, the festival runs a substantial outreach programme, including free concerts on Greenmarket Square, music workshops, master classes, a schools' band programme and training in arts journalism, photography and event management aimed at young people from disadvantaged communities. A free community concert in the city centre traditionally precedes the festival weekend.

Impact

Studies commissioned by the organisers estimate the festival contributes hundreds of millions of rand annually to the Western Cape economy and supports several thousand jobs, drawing visitors from across Africa and overseas. After interruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic, the festival returned as an in-person event.
