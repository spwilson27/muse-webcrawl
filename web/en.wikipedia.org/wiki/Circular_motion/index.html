Circular motion

In physics, circular motion is a movement of an object along the circumference of a circle or rotation along a circular arc. It can be uniform, with constant angular rate of rotation and constant speed, or non-uniform, with a changing rate of rotation.

Uniform circular motion

In uniform circular motion the speed v is constant but the velocity vector changes direction continuously, so the particle experiences a centripetal acceleration a = v²/r directed toward the centre of the circle, where r is the radius. In SI units: ac = v²/r = ω²r, with angular velocity ω. Examples include a satellite in a circular orbit and a ball whirled on a string.

Non-uniform circular motion

When the angular velocity changes, the particle also experiences a tangential acceleration, at = rα, where α is the angular acceleration. The net acceleration is the vector sum of the centripetal and tangential components.

Polar coordinates and equations

Circular motion is described naturally in polar coordinates: position is given by radius r and angle θ(t), with ω = dθ/dt and α = dω/dt. Newton's second law in rotating frames introduces the fictitious centrifugal and Coriolis forces, which are needed when analysing circular motion from within a rotating reference frame.
