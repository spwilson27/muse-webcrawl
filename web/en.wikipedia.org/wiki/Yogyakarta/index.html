Yogyakarta (also spelled Jogjakarta and often called Jogja) is the capital city of the Special Region of Yogyakarta in Indonesia, on the island of Java. It is renowned as a centre of Javanese culture, education, and the arts, and is one of Indonesia's most popular tourist destinations, in part because of its proximity to the temples of Borobudur and Prambanan.

History

The city was founded in 1755–1756 by Sultan Hamengkubuwono I following the Treaty of Giyanti (1755), which divided the Mataram Sultanate; the sultan built his palace, the Kraton, around which the city grew. Yogyakarta played a central role in Indonesia's struggle for independence: after the Dutch occupied Jakarta, the republican capital moved to Yogyakarta in 1946, and it served as the capital of the Republic of Indonesia until 1949. Uniquely, the Sultan of Yogyakarta serves as the region's governor, and the region retains special autonomous status within Indonesia.

Culture and education

Yogyakarta is a major centre of classical and contemporary Javanese arts, including gamelan music, wayang puppet theatre, batik, and silverwork. It is also a leading educational city, home to Gadjah Mada University, one of Indonesia's largest and most prestigious universities, and many other institutions. The main commercial street, Jalan Malioboro, is lined with shops and street vendors and is a focal point for visitors.

Geography

The city lies in south-central Java, about 30 km south of the active volcano Mount Merapi, whose 2010 eruption affected the region, and some 25 km north of the Indian Ocean coast.
