The economy of Bangladesh is a developing, market-based mixed economy and one of the fastest-growing in the world over the past two decades, with GDP growth frequently between 6 and 8 percent a year before the COVID-19 pandemic. Classified by the World Bank as a lower-middle-income country, Bangladesh is scheduled to graduate from the United Nations' least-developed-country category in 2026. Its transformation has been driven by the ready-made garment industry, remittances from a large diaspora workforce, and a resilient agricultural sector.

Sectors

Garments and textiles

Bangladesh is the world's second-largest garment exporter after China, supplying brands such as H&M, Zara (Inditex), and Walmart; the sector employs around four million workers, the majority women, and generates roughly four-fifths of export earnings. Workplace safety became a global concern after the 2013 Rana Plaza factory collapse killed more than 1,100 workers, prompting the Accord on Fire and Building Safety and widespread factory remediation.

Agriculture and other industries

Agriculture employs the largest share of workers and Bangladesh is among the world's top producers of rice and jute — historically its "golden fibre." Pharmaceuticals are a growing export, supplying a large majority of domestic demand and exporting generics widely; shipbuilding, leather goods, footwear, and information technology services are also expanding. Services account for just over half of GDP.

Challenges

Despite rapid progress in poverty reduction — extreme poverty fell from roughly a third of the population in the 1990s to under a tenth by the late 2010s — Bangladesh faces challenges including banking-sector weakness, energy import dependence, inflation pressures in the early 2020s, climate vulnerability in its low-lying delta, and the need to diversify exports beyond garments as LDC trade preferences end.
