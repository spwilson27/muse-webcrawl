Wu (吳語) is a major group of Sinitic languages spoken in Shanghai, southern Jiangsu and Zhejiang provinces in eastern China, by roughly 80 million people. Its best-known varieties include Shanghainese, Suzhounese and Wenzhounese. Wu is not mutually intelligible with Mandarin.

Features

Wu is notable among Chinese varieties for preserving the voiced (murmured) initial consonants of Middle Chinese, giving it a three-way contrast of voiceless unaspirated, voiceless aspirated and voiced initials. Many Wu dialects have rich vowel systems and complex tone sandhi, in which the tones of syllables change across a word or phrase. Historically, Suzhou dialect was the prestige form; since the 20th century Shanghainese has been the most influential.

Status

Wu has a long literary and operatic tradition (including Suzhou pingtan storytelling and Yue opera), but its use has declined under the promotion of Standard Mandarin in education and media; preservation movements have grown in response.
