Arab Spring

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Arab Spring was a series of anti-government protests, uprisings and armed rebellions that spread across much of the Arab world in the early 2010s, beginning in Tunisia in December 2010 with the self-immolation of the street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi (17 December 2010) and the flight of President Ben Ali on 14 January 2011. Protests brought down rulers in Egypt (Mubarak, February 2011), Libya (Gaddafi, killed October 2011 after NATO-backed rebellion) and Yemen (Saleh's negotiated exit, 2011–12), forced reforms or repression elsewhere (Morocco's constitutional amendments; Bahrain's uprising crushed with Gulf intervention), and in Syria escalated into a civil war of catastrophic scale.

Outcomes diverged bitterly from the hopes of 2011: Egypt's elected Muslim Brotherhood government was overthrown by the military in 2013 and a more thorough authoritarianism installed; Libya and Yemen fragmented into civil wars; Syria's regime survived with Russian and Iranian support at the cost of hundreds of thousands of deaths and millions displaced (its collapse only in December 2024, when Bashar al-Assad fell to a rebel offensive, reopened Syria's transition a decade on); Tunisia, long the sole democratic success, underwent presidential power-grabs from 2021 that dismantled much of its democratic order. A second wave of protests (Sudan, Algeria, Iraq, Lebanon, 2018–19) repeated the pattern of mobilisation without consolidation. The Spring's causes (youth unemployment, corruption, food prices, dignity — karama) and its media ecology (satellite television and social media) are a permanent case study in revolution's promises and the resilience of security states.
