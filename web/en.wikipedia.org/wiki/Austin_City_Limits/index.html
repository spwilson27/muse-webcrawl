Austin City Limits

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Austin City Limits is a television program that has been produced by PBS in Austin, Texas since 1974. It is the longest-running music series in American television history. It presents live music performances by artists in a variety of genres, recorded before a studio audience. The program helped establish Austin's reputation as the "Live Music Capital of the World."

History

The pilot episode was taped on October 17, 1974, and featured Willie Nelson. Created by Bruce Scafe, directed by Charles Ramirez-Berg, and produced by Paul Bosner, the show grew out of Austin's progressive country and cosmic cowboy scene at the Armadillo World Headquarters. In 1976 the program began national distribution on PBS.

Over the decades the series broadened from country and folk to rock, blues, soul, indie and hip-hop, hosting performers including Stevie Ray Vaughan, Johnny Cash, B. B. King, Radiohead, Kendrick Lamar and Billie Eilish. In 2003 it received the National Medal of Arts, the only television series so honoured, and in 2011 the original studio was designated a Rock and Roll Hall of Fame landmark.

Austin City Limits Music Festival

In 2002 the program lent its name to the Austin City Limits Music Festival, an annual multi-day event held in Zilker Park each October, which grew into one of the largest music festivals in the United States. The television series moved in 2011 to the purpose-built Moody Theater in downtown Austin, whose terrace displays a statue of Willie Nelson.
