Howard Gardner

Howard Earl Gardner (born July 11, 1943) is an American developmental psychologist, the John H. and Elisabeth A. Hobbs Professor of Cognition and Education at the Harvard Graduate School of Education. He is best known for his theory of multiple intelligences, proposed in his 1983 book Frames of Mind.

Multiple intelligences

Gardner argued that the conventional notion of intelligence, based on IQ tests, is too narrow, and proposed that humans possess several relatively independent intelligences — originally seven: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, interpersonal and intrapersonal — to which he later added naturalistic intelligence, and considered an existential intelligence. The theory has been highly influential in education, encouraging broader approaches to teaching and assessment, though it has been criticised by some psychologists for lacking empirical support.

Other work

Gardner was a co-founder of Harvard Project Zero, a research group on human cognition and education, and has written extensively on creativity, leadership and ethics, including Creating Minds (1993) and the "GoodWork" project on professional ethics. He has received numerous honours, including a MacArthur Fellowship in 1981.
