Santa Cruz massacre

Date12 November 1991
LocationSanta Cruz cemetery, Dili, East Timor
PerpetratorsIndonesian Army (TNI)
DeathsAt least 250 (estimates vary)

The Santa Cruz massacre (also known as the Dili massacre) was the mass shooting of at least 250 East Timorese pro-independence demonstrators in the Santa Cruz cemetery in Dili, East Timor, on 12 November 1991, during the Indonesian occupation of East Timor. It is considered a turning point in East Timor's struggle for independence, because the killings were filmed and photographed by foreign journalists and broadcast worldwide.

Memorial at Santa Cruz cemetery, Dili

Background and events

The demonstration began as a memorial march for Sebastião Gomes, a pro-independence youth killed by Indonesian forces weeks earlier. Thousands marched from the Motael Church to the Santa Cruz cemetery, unfurling banners supporting independence and the resistance leader Xanana Gusmão. At the cemetery, Indonesian soldiers opened fire on the crowd. British journalist Max Stahl filmed the massacre from within the cemetery; his footage, smuggled out of the country, was shown internationally, together with the testimony of American journalists Amy Goodman and Allan Nairn, who were beaten by soldiers.

Aftermath

The massacre galvanized international opposition to the occupation: the United States Congress moved to restrict military aid and training to Indonesia, solidarity movements grew, and East Timor's cause gained sustained diplomatic attention. Following the fall of Suharto in 1998, East Timor voted for independence in a 1999 referendum and became fully independent in 2002. The events of 12 November are commemorated in Timor-Leste as National Youth Day.
