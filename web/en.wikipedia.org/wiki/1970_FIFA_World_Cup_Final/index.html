The 1970 FIFA World Cup Final was held on 21 June 1970 at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, contested by Brazil and Italy. Brazil won the match 4–1, claiming their third World Cup title and earning the right to keep the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently. The Brazilian team of 1970, captained by Carlos Alberto and starring Pelé, Jairzinho, Gérson, Tostão and Rivelino, is widely regarded as one of the greatest football teams of all time.

Brazil's victory in Mexico City secured their third World Cup and permanent possession of the Jules Rimet Trophy.

Match summary

Pelé opened the scoring in the 18th minute with a towering header from Rivelino's cross — the 100th goal scored by Brazil in World Cup history. Italy equalised before half-time through Roberto Boninsegna, who capitalised on a defensive error. In the second half Brazil took control: Gérson restored the lead with a powerful shot in the 66th minute, Jairzinho added a third three minutes later — becoming the only player to score in every match of a World Cup finals tournament — and captain Carlos Alberto completed the scoring in the 86th minute with a thunderous strike at the end of a flowing team move involving eight outfield players, frequently cited as one of the greatest goals in World Cup history.

Aftermath

Under coach Mário Zagallo, Brazil became the first nation to win three World Cups, and Pelé — the only player to have won three World Cups — cemented his status as the sport's pre-eminent figure. A new trophy, the FIFA World Cup Trophy, was introduced for the 1974 tournament.
