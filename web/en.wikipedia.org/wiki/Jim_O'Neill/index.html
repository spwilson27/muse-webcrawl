Jim O'Neill

Terence James O'Neill, Baron O'Neill of Gatley (born 17 March 1957), commonly known as Jim O'Neill, is a British economist best known for coining the acronym BRIC — for Brazil, Russia, India and China — in a 2001 Goldman Sachs paper arguing that the four economies would reshape the global economy in the twenty-first century. The grouping later became a diplomatic bloc (BRICS, with South Africa) and founded institutions including the New Development Bank.

Early life and education

O'Neill was born in Manchester, England, and grew up in Gatley, Stockport. He studied economics at the University of Sheffield, graduating in 1978, and earned a PhD from the University of Surrey in 1982. He began his career in finance at Bank of America and later worked at Swiss Bank Corporation, where he became head of global research.

Career at Goldman Sachs

O'Neill joined Goldman Sachs in 1995 and rose to head of global economic research. There he led the work that produced the 2001 paper "Building Better Global Economic BRICs", the 2003 follow-up "Dreaming with BRICs: The Path to 2050", and the identification of the "Next Eleven" growth markets. He became chairman of Goldman Sachs Asset Management in 2010, overseeing roughly $800 billion in assets, before retiring from the firm in 2013. His BRICs thesis — that economic and political governance in the big emerging markets would drive a shift in global power — proved both influential and controversial as the four economies' fortunes diverged.

Government and later work

In 2014 O'Neill chaired the UK government's Review on Antimicrobial Resistance, whose reports warned that drug-resistant infections could cause 10 million deaths a year by 2050 and cost up to $100 trillion in lost output, and which prompted UN-level attention to the issue. In 2015 he became Commercial Secretary to the Treasury in David Cameron's government and was created a life peer, Baron O'Neill of Gatley. He resigned from the government in September 2016, reportedly over policy toward China and the Northern Powerhouse project, becoming the first minister to leave Theresa May's administration. He later chaired the think tank Chatham House (2018–2021) and advised on economic policy and the Northern Powerhouse.

Views and other interests

O'Neill is a prominent advocate of reforming global economic governance to give emerging economies weight commensurate with their size, and a critic of short-termism in Western policymaking revealed by the 2008 financial crisis. A lifelong Manchester United supporter, he was involved in the "Red Knights" consortium that explored a bid for the club in 2010 and served on its board as a non-executive director in the years around its IPO.
