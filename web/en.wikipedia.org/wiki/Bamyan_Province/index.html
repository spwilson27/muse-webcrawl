Bamyan Province (also spelled Bamiyan) is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the central highlands of the Hazarajat region. Its capital is the town of Bamyan. The province is predominantly populated by the Hazara people and is among the poorest but historically most significant regions of the country.

History and heritage

Bamyan lay on the Silk Road, and its valley was a major centre of Buddhism for centuries. Two monumental statues of the Buddha, 55 and 38 metres tall, carved into the cliffs in the 6th–7th centuries, were destroyed by the Taliban in March 2001, an act widely condemned internationally. The cultural landscape of the valley is a UNESCO World Heritage Site listed in danger.

Geography and tourism

The province encompasses part of the Hindu Kush mountains and contains Band-e Amir National Park, Afghanistan's first national park, famous for its chain of deep blue travertine lakes. Bamyan has been promoted as a centre for tourism and winter sports in Afghanistan, including an annual ski challenge, though security conditions after the Taliban's return to power in 2021 have limited development.
