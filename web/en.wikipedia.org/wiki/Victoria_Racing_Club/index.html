The Victoria Racing Club (VRC) is an Australian thoroughbred horse racing club, founded in 1864, which conducts racing at Flemington Racecourse in Melbourne, Victoria. It is best known as the organiser of the Melbourne Cup and the four-day Melbourne Cup Carnival.

Flemington Racecourse on Melbourne Cup Day.

History

The VRC was formed in 1864 from the merger of the Victoria Turf Club and the Victoria Jockey Club, and took over the management of racing at Flemington. The Melbourne Cup, first run in 1861, became the club's flagship event, growing into "the race that stops a nation". The club developed the spring carnival around the Cup, including Victoria Derby Day, Oaks Day, and Stakes Day, which together draw hundreds of thousands of racegoers.

Role

For much of its history the VRC also acted as the governing body for racing in Victoria, setting the rules and registering horses and participants, until regulatory functions passed to Racing Victoria in 2001. The club remains the principal metropolitan racing club in the state and custodian of Flemington, including its heritage-listed grandstands and famous rose gardens.
