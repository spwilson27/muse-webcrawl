Juan Pujol García

Juan Pujol García (14 February 1912 – 10 October 1988), also known by the codenames Garbo (to the British) and Alaric (to the Germans), was a Spanish double agent during the Second World War who played a key role in the success of Operation Fortitude, the deception that protected the D-Day landings.

Background

Born in Barcelona, Pujol developed a hatred of both fascism and communism during the Spanish Civil War. Determined to act against Nazi Germany, he offered his services to the British, was initially rebuffed, and then fabricated a spy network from Lisbon, feeding invented reports to the Abwehr, which accepted him as an agent.

Agent Garbo

Eventually recruited by MI5, Pujol moved to Britain in 1942 and, with his case officer Tomás Harris, built an entirely fictitious network of sub-agents whose "reports" mixed genuine low-grade information with deception. He convinced German intelligence that the Normandy landings of June 1944 were a diversion and that the main invasion would come at the Pas-de-Calais, causing German forces to be held in place for crucial weeks.

Honours and later life

Pujol is one of very few people to receive decorations from both sides: he was appointed MBE by Britain and awarded the Iron Cross by Germany. After the war he faked his death in Angola and lived quietly in Venezuela, where he ran a bookshop. He returned to public attention in the 1980s and died in Caracas in 1988.
