Aircraft registration country prefix

An aircraft registration country prefix is the one- or two-character (occasionally longer) code at the start of an aircraft's civil registration that identifies the country in which the aircraft is registered. The prefixes are allocated by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) from its table of radio call sign prefixes, and the system is administered internationally under the framework of the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). A registration such as N12345 denotes a United States aircraft, while G-ABCD is British and D-ABCD is German.

Tail registrations begin with the prefix of the state of registry.

Assignment and format

Under the 1944 Chicago Convention, every civil aircraft must bear the nationality and registration marks of its state of registry. Prefixes may be followed by a suffix of letters, digits or both, usually separated by a hyphen (except in the United States, where the "N" number carries no hyphen). Some countries reserve particular suffix blocks for gliders, helicopters, historic aircraft or government fleets.

History

The first international allocation of nationality letters was agreed at the Paris Convention of 1919, drawing on wireless call signs assigned at the London radiotelegraph conference of 1912. The present system descends from the ITU allocations of 1927 and was carried into the Chicago Convention regime after the Second World War. Colonial and successor states inherited or subdivided earlier blocks, which explains some shared letters among neighbouring countries. See also aircraft registration and ICAO.
