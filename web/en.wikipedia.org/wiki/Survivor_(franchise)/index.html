Survivor (franchise)

Survivor is an international reality television franchise based on the Swedish programme Expedition Robinson, created by Charlie Parsons and first broadcast in 1997. Contestants are marooned in an isolated location, must provide their own food and shelter, compete in challenges, and progressively vote one another out until a sole survivor wins a cash prize.

Format

Castaways are divided into tribes that compete for rewards and for immunity from elimination. Periodically the tribe that loses an immunity challenge attends Tribal Council, where one member is voted out. Tribes merge midway through the game into a single tribe, after which challenges are individual; eliminated players then form a jury which votes at the final Tribal Council to choose the winner from among the finalists. Hidden immunity idols, advantages and twists have been layered onto the basic format over the years.

Versions

The American version, hosted by Jeff Probst and broadcast by CBS since 2000, is the franchise's flagship: its first season in Borneo became a cultural phenomenon and helped launch the reality-TV boom, and the show has run for more than 45 seasons. Other long-running versions include Australian Survivor, South Africa's Survivor South Africa, the French Koh-Lanta, and editions in the United Kingdom, the Philippines, Israel, and dozens of other markets; versions have been produced in more than 50 territories in total.

Impact

Survivor is regarded as the progenitor of modern competition reality television, popularising the confessional, the alliance and the ceremonial elimination vote. Its vocabulary — "the tribe has spoken", "outwit, outplay, outlast" — entered popular culture, and formats worldwide adapted its social-strategy gameplay.
