Prenatal testing


An obstetric ultrasound examination

Prenatal testing (or prenatal screening) consists of tests performed during pregnancy to assess the health of the mother and the fetus and to detect conditions such as chromosomal abnormalities, neural tube defects and infections before birth.

Screening and diagnosis

Screening tests estimate risk and are non-invasive: they include first-trimester combined screening (nuchal translucency ultrasound plus blood tests), the second-trimester quadruple test, and non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) analysing cell-free fetal DNA in maternal blood. Diagnostic tests — chorionic villus sampling (CVS) and amniocentesis — can confirm chromosomal and genetic conditions but carry a small risk of miscarriage. Routine ultrasound scans also identify structural anomalies and monitor growth.

Ethics

Prenatal testing raises ethical questions about informed consent, disability rights and selective termination, addressed in differing legal frameworks worldwide.
