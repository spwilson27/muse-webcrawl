Beefsteak tomato

Beefsteak tomato
SpeciesSolanum lycopersicum
TypeLarge slicing tomato
Typical weight200–450 g (some over 1 kg)
Well-known cultivarsBrandywine, Big Beef, Mortgage Lifter, Cherokee Purple

Beefsteak tomatoes are the largest varieties of cultivated tomato, grown for their meaty, solid flesh and relatively few seeds. Individual fruits commonly weigh 200 to 450 grams (7 to 16 oz), and prize specimens can exceed one kilogram (2.2 lb). Their size makes them the slicing tomato of choice for sandwiches and burgers, where a single slice can cover the bread.

Characteristics

Beefsteak fruits are typically flattened or irregular in shape, often ribbed at the shoulders, with thick walls and small seed cavities embedded in abundant gel. Most beefsteak cultivars are indeterminate — the vines grow and fruit continuously until frost — and require staking or caging. They generally need a long growing season, often 80 or more days from transplant to first ripe fruit.

Notable cultivars

Heirloom beefsteaks include Brandywine, an Amish heirloom famed for rich flavor; Cherokee Purple, a dusky purple-brown variety from Tennessee; and Mortgage Lifter, developed in West Virginia during the Great Depression. Modern hybrids such as Big Beef combine heirloom-style size and flavor with disease resistance and higher yields.

Cultivation

Beefsteaks require full sun, fertile well-drained soil, consistent watering (to prevent cracking and blossom-end rot), and support for their heavy vines and fruit. Because of their size, fruits may suffer catfacing and splitting when temperatures fluctuate during pollination. Gardeners in short-season climates start seeds indoors six to eight weeks before the last frost.
