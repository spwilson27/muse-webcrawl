Raphael Semmes (27 September 1809 – 30 August 1877) was an officer in the United States Navy from 1826 to 1860 and in the Confederate States Navy during the American Civil War. As captain of the commerce raider CSS Alabama, he became the most successful commerce raider in naval history, capturing or destroying dozens of Union merchant vessels before his ship was sunk off Cherbourg, France, in 1864.

Early career

Born in Maryland and orphaned young, Semmes was raised in Georgetown, District of Columbia, and entered the Navy as a midshipman at sixteen. He studied law and was admitted to the bar during a period of shore duty. In the Mexican–American War he commanded the brig USS Somers, wrecked off Veracruz in 1846 with heavy loss of life, though a court of inquiry cleared him. He settled in Alabama, practised law, and wrote a memoir of his war service.

Civil War

Resigning his U.S. commission in 1861, Semmes first took the raider CSS Sumter through the Union blockade, taking 18 prizes before the ship was trapped at Gibraltar. He then took command of the newly built Alabama in the Azores in August 1862. Over 22 months Alabama ranged the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and South China Sea, taking some 65 prizes and sinking the warship USS Hatteras. On 19 June 1864 the USS Kearsarge sank Alabama in the English Channel off Cherbourg; Semmes was rescued by a British yacht. Promoted to rear admiral and also commissioned a brigadier general in the closing weeks of the war, he was briefly arrested for treason after the surrender but released without trial. He spent his last years in Mobile as a lawyer, teacher, and author.
