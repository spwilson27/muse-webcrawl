Green Mountain Boys

The Green Mountain Boys were a militia organisation formed in the 1770s in the territory between the British provinces of New York and New Hampshire, known as the New Hampshire Grants (modern Vermont). Led by Ethan Allen, they defended settlers' land claims against New York's attempts to govern the territory, and later fought in the American Revolutionary War.

Origins

Settlers holding New Hampshire land grants resisted New York's claims, which the Crown had upheld in 1764. When New York declared Allen and his associates outlaws, the settlers organised the Green Mountain Boys, named after Vermont's Green Mountains, with Allen as colonel commandant. They intimidated New York surveyors and sheriffs and controlled the disputed territory in practice.

Revolutionary War

On 10 May 1775 the Green Mountain Boys, together with Benedict Arnold's men, seized Fort Ticonderoga in a dawn raid — the first offensive American victory of the war. A regiment under Seth Warner later fought at Hubbardton and Bennington in 1777 during the Saratoga campaign.

Legacy

The Boys' resistance underpinned the creation of the independent Vermont Republic (1777–1791), which became the 14th US state. "Green Mountain Boys" remains a nickname associated with Vermonters, and the Vermont National Guard's 172nd Infantry Regiment carries the tradition.
