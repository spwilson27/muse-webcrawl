Indigenous religion

Scope: Traditional belief systems of indigenous peoples worldwide

Common features: Oral tradition, animism, shamanism, ancestor veneration

An indigenous religion is a religion or belief system native to a particular ethnic or cultural group, typically developed within that community over generations rather than introduced from outside. Such religions are usually closely tied to a specific land, language, and way of life.

Characteristics

Indigenous religions are commonly transmitted orally rather than through scripture, and often feature animism — the belief that natural phenomena possess spirits — shamanic specialists who mediate with the spirit world, and veneration of ancestors. Ritual life is frequently bound to seasonal cycles, hunting, agriculture, and the local landscape.

Distribution

Examples include Native American religions, traditional African religions, Aboriginal Australian spirituality, Sámi religion, Shinto's folk roots, and numerous traditions across Asia, Oceania, and the Arctic.

Modern context

Many indigenous religions declined under colonization and missionary activity, but revitalization movements and legal recognition of indigenous rights have supported their continuation and recovery.
