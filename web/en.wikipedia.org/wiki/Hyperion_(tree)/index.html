Hyperion (tree)

Hyperion is a coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) in California that is the world's tallest known living tree, measured at 116.07 metres (380.8 ft) in height. It was discovered in Redwood National Park on August 25, 2006, by naturalists Chris Atkins and Michael Taylor, and its height was verified by climbing and direct tape measurement by a team including Humboldt State University ecologist Stephen Sillett.

Discovery and measurement

Hyperion surpassed the previous record holder, the Stratosphere Giant in Humboldt Redwoods State Park, which had itself been the tallest known tree until 2006. Laser-based survey work by Taylor, Atkins, and colleagues in the early 2000s found several redwoods exceeding 113 m and led to the systematic search that found Hyperion on a remote hillside. Researchers estimated the tree contains about 502 cubic metres (17,700 cu ft) of wood.

Protection

The exact location of Hyperion has never been publicly disclosed by the National Park Service, in order to protect the tree and its ecosystem from foot traffic, which compacts soil and damages shallow redwood roots. Nevertheless, off-trail visitors located the tree, and in 2022 the park closed the surrounding area, warning that visitors who approach Hyperion face fines of up to $5,000 and six months in jail. Park officials have noted that heavy visitation caused erosion, vegetation damage, and litter at the site, and have pointed out that the tree is not visible from any viewpoint and lacks the spectacle many visitors expect. The world's tallest tropical tree and tallest flowering plant are individuals of Shorea faguetiana (Menara, in Sabah) and mountain ash respectively, both somewhat shorter than Hyperion.
