Base of the pyramid

The base of the pyramid (BoP), also called the bottom of the pyramid, is a term in economics and business strategy referring to the largest but poorest socio-economic group in the world: the roughly four to five billion people who live on a few dollars a day. The concept was popularised by the management scholar C. K. Prahalad.

The concept

In his 2004 book The Fortune at the Bottom of the Pyramid, Prahalad argued that multinational companies could profitably serve poor consumers while also helping to alleviate poverty, if they adapted their products, packaging, pricing, and distribution to low-income markets — for example through single-serve sachets, microcredit partnerships, and mobile-phone-based services. The "pyramid" metaphor comes from a global income distribution chart in which a small wealthy tier sits atop a vast poor base.

Criticism

Critics, notably Aneel Karnani, have argued that the size and purchasing power of the BoP market are overstated, that the poor are better served as producers than as consumers, and that selling to the poor can shade into exploitation. Others have developed "BoP 2.0" approaches emphasising co-creation of businesses with local communities rather than merely selling to them.
