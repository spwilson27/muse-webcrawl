Weak derivative

In mathematics, a weak derivative is a generalization of the concept of the derivative of a function (strong derivative) for functions not assumed differentiable pointwise, but only integrable, i.e., elements of the Lp space L1([a, b]).

The method of integration by parts motivates the definition: a locally integrable function v is the weak derivative of u if, for every infinitely differentiable test function φ with compact support, ∫u φ′ = −∫v φ. The definition extends to higher dimensions using multi-indices and partial derivatives, and underlies the theory of Sobolev spaces.

Definition

Suppose u is a locally integrable function on an open set U ⊆ Rn. If there exists a locally integrable function v such that ∫U u ∂φ/∂xi = −∫U v φ for all test functions φ ∈ Cc∞(U), then v is called the weak partial derivative of u with respect to xi. Weak derivatives, when they exist, are unique up to sets of measure zero.

Examples

The absolute value function u(x) = |x| is not differentiable at 0, but it has a weak derivative given by the sign function: v(x) = −1 for x < 0 and v(x) = 1 for x > 0. By contrast, a function with a jump discontinuity, such as the Heaviside step function, has no weak derivative in the L1 sense — its derivative in the distributional sense is the Dirac delta, which is not a function.

Properties

If a function is continuously differentiable, its weak derivative coincides with its classical derivative almost everywhere. Weak differentiability is the basis of the weak formulation of partial differential equations and of variational methods in analysis and mathematical physics.
