Hunter S. Thompson (1937–2005) was an American journalist and author who created 'gonzo journalism' — first-person, participatory, deliberately subjective reporting — in works such as Hell's Angels (1967), Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (1971) and Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72, becoming a countercultural icon (and the model for Doonesbury's Uncle Duke).

Career

Thompson's Kentucky Derby piece for Scanlan's (1970) launched gonzo; his Rolling Stone political coverage and his battles with editors and deadlines are legend. He died by suicide at his Colorado home in 2005; his ashes were fired from a cannon at a memorial he had planned.
