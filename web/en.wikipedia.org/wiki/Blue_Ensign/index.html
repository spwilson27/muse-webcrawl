Blue Ensign

[Hero image: the British Blue Ensign — a blue field with the Union Jack in the canton]

The Blue Ensign is a flag, one of several British ensigns, consisting of a blue field with the Union Jack in the upper hoist canton. In its plain (undefaced) form it is the ensign of the Royal Navy's Blue Squadron historically, and in modern use it is flown by certain government vessels and departments. When "defaced" with a badge or emblem in the fly, the Blue Ensign forms the basis of the flags of many British colonies and former colonies, and of numerous government and institutional ensigns.

History

From 1650 the English fleet was divided into Red, White and Blue squadrons, each flying its coloured ensign. After the 1864 reorganisation of naval flags, the plain Blue Ensign was assigned to the naval reserve and to vessels commanded by officers of the Royal Naval Reserve, while the White Ensign became the Royal Navy's sole battle ensign and the Red Ensign went to the merchant fleet. Defaced blue ensigns were authorised for the government services of colonies from 1865 onwards, when the Colonial Office directed that colonial government vessels fly the Blue Ensign with the colony's badge in the fly.

Current uses

Plain blue ensigns are flown by certain British government departments and by ships of the Royal Fleet Auxiliary (as the "defaced" RFA ensign with its badge). Defaced blue ensigns serve as national flags for countries such as Australia and New Zealand (both defaced with stars), and as state and territorial flags elsewhere. Many British Overseas Territories fly blue ensigns bearing their arms. Numerous clubs and institutions — for example yacht clubs with warrants — fly special defaced blue ensigns.

Related flags

The "sky-blue ensign" of the Royal Air Force and the civil air ensign are separate designs derived from the Blue Ensign pattern. The Royal Navy's submarine service famously flies a Jolly Roger rather than an ensign when returning from patrol.
