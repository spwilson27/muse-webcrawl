Stade Geoffroy-Guichard

Location: Saint-Étienne, France
Opened: 1931
Capacity: 41,965
Tenants: AS Saint-Étienne

The Stade Geoffroy-Guichard is a multi-purpose stadium in Saint-Étienne, France, and the home ground of the football club AS Saint-Étienne. It is nicknamed le Chaudron ("the Cauldron") for the intense atmosphere generated by its supporters, and also "the green cauldron" after the club's colours.

History

The stadium is named after Geoffroy Guichard, founder of the Casino grocery retail chain, who bought the land on which it was built. Construction began in 1930 and the stadium opened on 13 September 1931 with a match against FAC Nice. The original ground was built around an athletics track and held only a few thousand spectators.

The stadium was substantially rebuilt for the 1998 FIFA World Cup, when it hosted six matches, and again renovated ahead of UEFA Euro 2016, when it hosted four matches. The renovations removed the athletics track and brought the stands closer to the pitch in an English-style rectangular configuration, increasing capacity to just under 42,000. It also hosted matches during the 1984 European Championship and the 2003 FIFA Confederations Cup, as well as rugby union fixtures during the 2023 Rugby World Cup.

Atmosphere

The two kops behind the goals are home to the club's principal ultra groups, and the stadium is renowned in French football for its noise and choreographed displays. A record crowd of more than 47,000 attended a match against Nantes in 1985, before all-seater requirements reduced capacity.
