Right to education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The right to education has been recognized as a human right in a number of international conventions, most notably the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, which recognizes a right to free, compulsory primary education for all, an obligation to develop secondary education generally available and accessible to all, and an obligation to develop equal access to higher education. The right is also enshrined in Article 26 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) and Articles 28 and 29 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989).

Content of the right

UNESCO and the UN Special Rapporteur on the right to education describe the right through the "4As" framework developed by Katarina Tomaševski: education must be available, accessible (without discrimination, physically and economically), acceptable (of good quality and culturally appropriate) and adaptable to learners' needs. The right includes freedom to establish educational institutions, subject to minimum state standards, and parents' liberty to choose schools other than public ones.

Implementation

Most states provide free compulsory schooling for a number of years, but global monitoring by UNESCO shows persistent gaps: hundreds of millions of children and youth remain out of school, and many more leave without basic literacy and numeracy. Sustainable Development Goal 4 commits governments to inclusive, equitable quality education and lifelong learning for all by 2030.

Case law

Regional and national courts have enforced the right in various settings, including the European Court of Human Rights' judgments under Article 2 of Protocol No. 1 to the European Convention, and constitutional litigation in countries such as India, whose Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act 2009 implemented a constitutional guarantee.
