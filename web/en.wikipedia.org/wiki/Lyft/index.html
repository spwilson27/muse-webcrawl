Lyft, Inc. is an American company offering ride-hailing, vehicles for hire, motorized scooters, a bicycle-sharing system, rental cars, and food delivery in the United States and selected cities in Canada. Lyft is the second-largest ridesharing company in the United States after Uber. Most of its operations are accessed through the Lyft mobile app, which connects riders with drivers who provide transportation in their own vehicles.

History

Lyft was launched in the summer of 2012 by computer programmers Logan Green and John Zimmer as a service of Zimride, a long-distance intercity carpooling company they had founded in 2007. The Lyft service was distinguished in its early years by a large pink furry mustache ("carstache") that drivers fixed to the front of their cars, and by encouraging riders to sit in the front seat and greet drivers with a fist bump; the mustache was later replaced by a smaller dashboard emblem called the Glowstache, and then by the Amp device. In May 2013 the company officially changed its name from Zimride to Lyft and sold the Zimride service.

Lyft expanded rapidly across the United States and entered Canada in 2017 with service in the Toronto area. It acquired the bike-share operator Motivate in 2018. Lyft became the first ridesharing company to go public, listing on the Nasdaq in March 2019 with an initial public offering that raised about $2.3 billion. In 2023, former Amazon executive David Risher became CEO, succeeding Logan Green, and the company undertook cost-cutting measures including layoffs.

Services

Through its app, Lyft offers several ride tiers, including standard rides, larger vehicles (Lyft XL), priority pickups, shared rides (introduced as Lyft Line in 2014, suspended during the COVID-19 pandemic and later relaunched), and premium options. The company also runs Lyft Rentals, scooter and bike networks in various cities, and has partnered on autonomous vehicle pilots, including with Motional and Waymo.

Criticism and regulation

Like other gig-economy platforms, Lyft has faced disputes over the classification of drivers as independent contractors rather than employees, most prominently in California, where Assembly Bill 5 (2019) was followed by the voter-approved Proposition 22 (2020), which Lyft co-sponsored with other app-based companies. Lyft has also faced litigation and debate over passenger and driver safety, background checks, accessibility for wheelchair users, and its effect on traffic congestion and public transit.
