Emilio Azcárraga Milmo

Emilio Azcárraga Milmo (September 6, 1930, in San Antonio, Texas – April 16, 1997, in Miami, Florida) was a Mexican businessman and the son of Emilio Azcárraga Vidaurreta, founder of the Mexican broadcasting giant Televisa. Known as "El Tigre" ("The Tiger") for his aggressive business style, he was one of the most powerful media figures in Latin America.

Career

After studying in the United States, Azcárraga Milmo joined the family businesses of his father, which included the XEW radio network and early Mexican television stations. In 1973, following his father's death, he merged his family's Telesistema Mexicano with competitors to form Televisa, becoming its president and principal owner. Under his leadership, Televisa became the world's largest producer of Spanish-language television programming, famous for its telenovelas, and expanded internationally through Univision in the United States and exports to Latin America, Europe and Asia.

Azcárraga Milmo was a close ally of Mexico's ruling Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) and openly described Televisa as supportive of the party and the president. He was also involved in football as owner of Club América and promoted the 1986 FIFA World Cup in Mexico. He died on his yacht near Miami in 1997 and was succeeded at Televisa by his son, Emilio Azcárraga Jean.
