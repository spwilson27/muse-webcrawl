Sports betting in the United States

Sports betting in the United States has expanded rapidly since 2018, when the Supreme Court struck down the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act (PASPA), which had effectively banned single-game sports wagering outside Nevada. Since the decision, most states have legalised sports betting in some form, at retail sportsbooks, online, or both.

History

Betting on horse racing has long been legal under pari-mutuel rules, but wagers on team sports were largely driven underground during the twentieth century, associated with organised crime and scandals such as the 1919 Black Sox. Nevada legalised sportsbooks in 1949 and for decades held a near-monopoly. PASPA, passed in 1992, froze the status quo until New Jersey's legal challenge succeeded in Murphy v. NCAA (2018).

Modern market

Following legalisation, major operators including DraftKings and FanDuel expanded nationwide, and professional leagues that once opposed gambling embraced sponsorship and data partnerships. Annual legal wagering handle in the United States has exceeded one hundred billion dollars, generating significant state tax revenue alongside debate about problem gambling and advertising.
