Threnody to the Victims of Hiroshima, originally 8'37", is a musical composition for 52 string instruments composed in 1960 by Polish composer Krzysztof Penderecki. Dedicated to the victims of the Hiroshima atomic bombing, it won the UNESCO Tribune Internationale des Compositeurs prize in 1961 and became one of the most famous examples of sonorism and extended string technique.

Composition and title

Penderecki originally titled the piece simply by its duration, 8'37", in the spirit of an abstract sound study. On hearing its first performance, he was struck by its emotional charge and decided to dedicate it: "May the Threnody express my firm belief that the sacrifice of Hiroshima will never be forgotten and lost."

Style and notation

The music is built from tone clusters, glissandi and sounds produced by playing behind the bridge, on the tailpiece, and striking the soundboard — effects notated with graphic symbols and proportional (time-based) notation rather than conventional pitches and rhythms. Its searing sonorities have made it a staple of film and television soundtracks, including excerpts in The Exorcist and Children of Men.
