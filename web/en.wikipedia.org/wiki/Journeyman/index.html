Journeyman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A journeyman is a worker, skilled in a given building trade or craft, who has successfully completed an official apprenticeship qualification. Journeymen are considered competent and authorized to work in that field as fully qualified employees. They earn their license by education, supervised experience, and examination. Although journeymen have completed a trade certificate and are allowed to work as employees, they may not yet work as self-employed master craftsmen.

Etymology

The word "journeyman" derives from the French journée, meaning "a day" — a reference to the system under which a qualified tradesman was entitled to charge a fee for each day's work. The term should not be confused with the later Germanic tradition of the "journeyman years" (Wanderjahre), although the two are historically linked through the custom of traveling to work under different masters.

Historical guild system

In the medieval guild system, advancement had three grades: apprentice, journeyman, and master. After serving an apprenticeship of several years, a young craftsman was released as a journeyman, free to sell his labor for wages. To become a master, he typically had to produce a "masterpiece" judged by the guild, accumulate capital to open his own workshop, and receive the guild's and the town's approval. In Germany, Austria, and German-speaking Switzerland, journeymen traditionally undertook years of travel — the Walz — working for masters in different towns while wearing distinctive costume and forbidden from returning within a set distance of home. Remarkably, this tradition of traveling journeymen (Wandergesellen) survives in Germany to the present day.

Modern usage

In modern North America and Australia, journeyman status (for example, journeyman electrician or journeyman plumber) is a formal licensing grade obtained after completing an apprenticeship and passing examinations administered by a state, provincial, or union authority. Under a journeyman's supervision, apprentices may work; above the journeyman grade stands the master license, which is required to pull permits, contract work independently, and employ other tradespeople. Unions such as the IBEW and the building trades organize training through joint apprenticeship committees.

Other senses

By extension, "journeyman" describes a reliable but unspectacular professional in other fields: in sports, a journeyman athlete is one who plays competently for many different teams without becoming a star, and in acting and music the term denotes a seasoned, dependable performer. The word also appears in phrases such as "journeyman boxer," whose role is often to provide experienced opposition for rising prospects.
