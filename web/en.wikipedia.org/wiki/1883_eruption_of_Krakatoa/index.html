The 1883 eruption of Krakatoa (Indonesian: Letusan Krakatau 1883) in the Sunda Strait occurred from 20 May until 21 October 1883. The climax was on Monday, 27 August, when more than 70 percent of the island of Krakatoa and its surrounding archipelago were destroyed as the volcano collapsed into a caldera. The eruption is one of the deadliest and most destructive volcanic events in recorded history, killing an estimated 36,417 people in the resulting tsunamis and pyroclastic flows.

The eruption

Activity began with eruptions from the Perboewatan cone in May 1883, drawing excursion boats of sightseers from Batavia (Jakarta). Explosions intensified through the summer. On the afternoon of 26 August a series of enormous explosions began; by the morning of 27 August four colossal blasts — at 5:30, 6:44, 10:02, and 10:41 a.m. — culminated in the collapse of the island. The 10:02 explosion is estimated to have released energy equivalent to about 200 megatons of TNT and is considered the loudest sound in recorded history: it was heard nearly 4,800 km (3,000 mi) away, on Rodrigues Island in the Indian Ocean and in Perth, Australia.

Tsunamis and casualties

The collapse of the caldera generated tsunamis with waves up to about 46 metres (151 ft) that destroyed 295 towns and villages along the coasts of Java and Sumatra, drowning most of the victims. The Dutch colonial authorities put the official death toll at 36,417. Pyroclastic flows crossed the strait and killed people on the Sumatran coast some 40 km away.

Global effects

The eruption injected enormous quantities of ash and sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, lowering average global temperatures by as much as 1.2 °C (2.2 °F) in the following years and producing vivid red sunsets worldwide — phenomena famously captured in paintings of the era and possibly in the sky of Edvard Munch's The Scream. Atmospheric pressure waves circled the globe multiple times and were recorded by barographs everywhere.

Anak Krakatau

In 1927 a new island, Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa"), emerged from the caldera and has grown since. Its flank collapse on 22 December 2018 generated a tsunami that killed more than 400 people, a reminder of the continuing hazard.
