Employee stock ownership

In an ESOP, a trust holds shares for employees, who receive allocations over time.

Employee stock ownership refers to arrangements in which employees hold shares in the company that employs them, giving them a direct ownership stake in the business. It takes many forms worldwide, from individual share purchases and broad stock-option plans to collective trust structures — most notably the employee stock ownership plan (ESOP) in the United States, in which a tax-qualified retirement trust borrows money to buy company stock and allocates shares to employees' accounts as the loan is repaid.

History

The modern ESOP was designed in the 1950s by American lawyer and economist Louis O. Kelso, who argued that broadening capital ownership was essential in an economy where capital produced a growing share of income. Senator Russell Long championed the concept in Congress, and the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA) gave ESOPs a statutory framework and tax advantages. In Britain, related models include the John Lewis Partnership's trust ownership (dating to 1929) and, since 2014, the Employee Ownership Trust (EOT), which allows owners to sell a controlling stake to an employee trust with tax relief.

Scale and effects

In the United States there are roughly 6,500 ESOPs covering some 14 million participants, concentrated in privately held companies, where the ESOP often provides a business-succession mechanism: a selling owner gets a market for shares and tax benefits while employees gain a retirement asset. Research by the National Center for Employee Ownership and Rutgers scholars generally finds that employee-owned firms show modestly higher productivity and employment stability, particularly when ownership is combined with participatory management practices, though employees also bear concentration risk when their savings are tied to their employer's stock — a danger infamous from the collapse of Enron's stock-heavy retirement plans.

Forms

  • ESOP — leveraged or non-leveraged trust plan (U.S.)
  • Employee Ownership Trust — indirect collective ownership (U.K.)
  • Stock options and RSUs — individual equity compensation, common in technology firms
  • Worker cooperatives — democratic ownership in which shares and control are linked
  • ESPP — employee stock purchase plans offering shares at a discount
