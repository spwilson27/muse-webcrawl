Zellweger spectrum disorders

Type: Peroxisome biogenesis disorders

Inheritance: Autosomal recessive

Genes: PEX genes (e.g. PEX1, PEX6, PEX12)

Zellweger spectrum disorders are a group of rare inherited metabolic conditions caused by defects in the assembly of peroxisomes. The spectrum ranges from the severe Zellweger syndrome through neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy to the milder infantile Refsum disease.

Cause

The disorders result from mutations in any of more than a dozen PEX genes encoding peroxins required for peroxisome formation. Without functional peroxisomes, very-long-chain fatty acids and other metabolites accumulate and damage the brain, liver and other organs.

Clinical features

Severely affected infants show hypotonia, feeding difficulties, distinctive facial features, seizures, liver dysfunction and skeletal abnormalities, and often die in the first year of life. Milder forms present later with developmental delay, vision and hearing loss, and leukodystrophy. There is no cure; treatment is supportive.
