Soho Foundry

Soho Foundry is a factory in Smethwick, near Birmingham, England, built in 1795 by the partnership of Matthew Boulton and James Watt and their sons. It was created specifically for the manufacture of steam engines by the firm of Boulton and Watt, and is notable as one of the earliest purpose-built factories of the Industrial Revolution, integrating under one roof the casting, machining, fitting, and assembly of complete engines.

History

Before 1795, Boulton and Watt's engines were assembled from parts made by subcontractors, with only key components made at the original Soho Manufactory. The new foundry, sited beside the Birmingham Canal at Smethwick, brought production in-house: it included a foundry for castings, a smithy, and machine shops equipped with some of the most advanced machine tools of the day. William Murdoch, the firm's celebrated engineer, was closely involved in its work.

The foundry produced engines for factories, mines, waterworks, and later marine use. After the Boulton and Watt partnership dissolved, the works continued under James Watt & Co. and was eventually acquired by W & T Avery Ltd in 1895, becoming part of their weighing-machine works. Remarkably, parts of the site remain in industrial use, and it is recognised as a scheduled monument and listed buildings survive on the site.

Legacy

Soho Foundry exemplified the factory system: division of labour, standardised interchangeable parts, and planned layout. It is frequently cited, along with the nearby Soho Manufactory and Soho Mint, as a birthplace of modern manufacturing.
