Canid hybrid

A canid hybrid is an offspring resulting from the interbreeding of members of different species within the family Canidae, the group that includes domestic dogs, wolves, coyotes, jackals and foxes. Because most canids share a similar number of chromosomes (typically 78 in the genus Canis), many species can produce viable and sometimes fertile offspring when crossed, either in the wild or in captivity.

Types of hybrid

Well-known canid hybrids include the coydog (coyote × dog), the coywolf (coyote × wolf, occurring naturally in eastern North America), the wolfdog (wolf × dog), and crosses between dogs and jackals. In the genus Canis, fertile hybrids are common; hybrids between more distantly related canids, such as dog × fox crosses, are generally not viable because of differing chromosome numbers.

Fertility and genetics

Unlike many other animal hybrids, canid hybrids within Canis are usually fertile in both sexes, because the parent species diverged relatively recently and retain homologous chromosomes. Genetic studies have shown that the eastern coyote of North America carries a mixture of coyote, wolf and domestic dog ancestry, the product of historical hybridisation events.

Legal and ethical issues

Wolfdogs and other high-content hybrids are restricted or banned in some jurisdictions because of concerns about unpredictable behaviour and animal welfare. Conservationists also worry that hybridisation between endangered wolves and domestic dogs or coyotes can dilute the gene pool of threatened populations.
