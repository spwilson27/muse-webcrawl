Oswald Veblen

Oswald Veblen (June 24, 1880 – August 10, 1960) was an American mathematician, geometer and topologist whose work found applications in atomic physics and the theory of relativity. A grandson-in-law's family connection aside, he was the nephew of the economist Thorstein Veblen.

Born in Decorah, Iowa, Veblen studied at the University of Iowa and Harvard, and received his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1903 with a dissertation on a rigorous axiomatic treatment of geometry. He taught at Princeton University from 1905, where he helped build a leading research school in mathematics, and in 1932 he became one of the first professors at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, playing a central role in selecting its faculty, including Albert Einstein and John von Neumann.

Veblen made fundamental contributions to projective geometry, topology (including work on the Jordan curve theorem) and differential geometry. During both world wars he worked on ballistics. The Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry of the American Mathematical Society is named in his honour. He was also an early conservation advocate in the Princeton area.
