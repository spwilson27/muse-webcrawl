Mebendazole (MBZ) is a medication used to treat a number of parasitic worm infestations, including ascariasis, pinworm infection, hookworm infections, guinea worm infections, hydatid disease, and giardia, among others. It is taken by mouth.

Medical uses

Mebendazole is a broad-spectrum anthelmintic effective against many nematodes and some other parasites. It is commonly used for pinworm (enterobiasis), whipworm, roundworm, and hookworm infections. For hydatid disease caused by Echinococcus, it may be used when surgery is not possible, although albendazole is often preferred. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism of action

Mebendazole works by binding to tubulin in the parasite's intestinal cells and preventing the polymerization of microtubules. This blocks glucose uptake and depletes the worm's energy stores, leading to immobilization and death of the parasite. It is poorly absorbed from the human gastrointestinal tract, which concentrates its action in the gut while limiting systemic exposure.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, vomiting, and ringing in the ears. At high doses or with prolonged use it may cause suppression of the bone marrow and liver problems. It is not recommended during pregnancy, as it has been shown to cause harm in animal studies.

History

Mebendazole was developed by Janssen Pharmaceutica in Belgium and came into medical use in 1971. It is available as a generic medication and is widely used in mass deworming programs in regions where soil-transmitted helminths are endemic.
