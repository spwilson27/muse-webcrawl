Qissa Khwani Bazaar, Peshawar.

The Qissa Khwani Bazaar massacre took place on 23 April 1930 in the Qissa Khwani Bazaar of Peshawar, in the North-West Frontier Province of British India. Troops of the British Indian Army opened fire on unarmed demonstrators of the Khudai Khidmatgar ("Servants of God") movement, a nonviolent Pashtun organisation led by Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan. It was one of the defining moments of the civil disobedience movement in the North-West Frontier and a landmark in the history of nonviolent resistance.

Background

In April 1930, as Gandhi's Salt March inspired protests across India, the colonial authorities arrested leaders of the Khudai Khidmatgar in Peshawar, including Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan. Large crowds gathered in protest. Soldiers of the Royal Garhwal Rifles were ordered to fire on the crowd; in a celebrated act of defiance, a platoon under Chandra Singh Garhwali refused the order and was subsequently court-martialled.

The massacre

Armoured cars were driven into the bazaar, killing several people, and troops repeatedly fired machine guns into the unarmed crowds over several hours. The demonstrators, committed to nonviolence, reportedly offered themselves to the bullets without retaliating. Official British figures put the death toll at around twenty, while Indian sources estimated several hundred dead and wounded.

Aftermath

The massacre and the Garhwali refusal attracted worldwide attention and intensified international criticism of British rule. Ghaffar Khan's movement grew in stature, and the events in Peshawar became a powerful symbol of disciplined nonviolent resistance. The city remained under martial law for weeks afterwards.
