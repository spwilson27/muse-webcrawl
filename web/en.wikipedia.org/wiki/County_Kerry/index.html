County Kerry (Irish: Contae Chiarraí, "land of Ciar", an ancient tribal name) is a county in the province of Munster in south-western Ireland. Known as "the Kingdom", it is one of Ireland's most celebrated tourist regions, famed for its mountain scenery, Atlantic peninsulas and lakes. Its county town is Tralee; other major towns are Killarney, Listowel, Kenmare and Dingle (An Daingean).

Geography

Kerry's coastline is deeply indented by the Iveragh, Dingle and Beara peninsulas. The county contains Ireland's highest mountain, Carrauntoohil (1,038 m), in the MacGillycuddy's Reeks, and the Lakes of Killarney within Killarney National Park. Offshore lie the Skellig Islands — the monastic island of Skellig Michael is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and the Blasket Islands. The Ring of Kerry touring route circles the Iveragh Peninsula.

History and culture

Parts of Kerry, including Corca Dhuibhne (the Dingle Peninsula), are Gaeltacht areas where Irish is spoken daily. The county has a rich literary tradition — Peig Sayers and Tomás Ó Criomhthain of the Great Blasket are iconic figures — and Listowel hosts a famous writers' week. Kerry is the most successful county in Gaelic football, having won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship more times than any other county, and its green-and-gold colours are known throughout Ireland.

Economy

Tourism, agriculture (especially dairy farming) and fishing are the pillars of the Kerry economy, with technology employers in Tralee and Killarney. The Rose of Tralee international festival, held each August, is one of Ireland's best-known cultural events.
