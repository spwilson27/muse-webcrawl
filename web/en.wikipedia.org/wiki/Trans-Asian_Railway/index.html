Trans-Asian Railway

The Trans-Asian Railway (TAR) is a project to create an integrated freight railway network across Europe and Asia, a project of the United Nations Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific (UNESCAP). The TAR network agreement was signed in 2006 and came into force in 2009.

The network is intended to provide a continuous railway link of roughly 117,000 km connecting Singapore to Istanbul, and beyond into Europe, enabling land transport of containers between Asia and Europe as an alternative to sea routes. It is sometimes described as the "Iron Silk Road".

Network structure

The TAR is organized into four major corridors:

  • Northern Corridor — links Europe and Northeast Asia via Russia's Trans-Siberian Railway, Mongolia and China.
  • Southern Corridor — connects Southeast Asia through India, Pakistan and Iran to Turkey and Europe.
  • Southeast Asian network — the Singapore–Kunming rail link and associated ASEAN connections.
  • North–South Corridor — links northern Europe through Russia and the Caucasus/Caspian region to the Persian Gulf.

Challenges

The principal obstacles include breaks of gauge — the network spans standard gauge (1,435 mm), Russian broad gauge (1,520 mm), Indian broad gauge (1,676 mm) and metre gauge lines — as well as missing links, notably between Myanmar and Thailand and within Southeast Asia, and differing customs and signalling systems.

Development

The concept dates to the 1950s, when railways also began to be promoted as a means of providing access to landlocked Asian countries. The Intergovernmental Agreement on the Trans-Asian Railway Network was adopted in 2005 and signed by 18 Asian nations in November 2006 in Busan, South Korea; it entered into force on 11 June 2009.
