Guy Lombardo

Guy Lombardo (1902–1977)
Born: London, Ontario, Canada
Occupation: Bandleader, violinist
Band: The Royal Canadians

Gaetano Alberto "Guy" Lombardo (19 June 1902 – 5 November 1977) was a Canadian and American bandleader and violinist. He formed The Royal Canadians in 1924 with his brothers Carmen, Lebert, and Victor, and the band became famous for playing "the sweetest music this side of Heaven". Lombardo is best remembered for his nearly fifty years of New Year's Eve broadcasts, which made his rendition of "Auld Lang Syne" a fixture of the American celebration.

Career

Born in London, Ontario, to Italian immigrant parents, Lombardo learned violin from his father. The Royal Canadians gained fame at Chicago's Granada Cafe, then moved to New York's Roosevelt Hotel in 1929, beginning a residency that lasted decades. Their smooth, danceable style — featuring Carmen Lombardo's saxophone and vocals — sold an estimated 100 to 300 million records, including hits such as "Charmaine" and "Coquette".

New Year's Eve tradition

Lombardo's band first broadcast on New Year's Eve in 1928, and the shows continued on radio and then television from Times Square and the Waldorf-Astoria until 1976. His role as "Mr. New Year's Eve" outlasted his life: recordings of the Royal Canadians playing "Auld Lang Syne" at midnight were still used by television coverage for years after his death in 1977.

Other pursuits

An avid powerboat racer, Lombardo won national championships in hydroplanes such as Tempo VI. He also ran the Jones Beach Marine Theater on Long Island, staging popular musical productions in the 1960s and 1970s.
