The Unitarian Universalist Association (UUA) is a liberal religious association in the United States, formed in 1961 by the consolidation of the American Unitarian Association (founded 1825) and the Universalist Church of America (founded 1793). Its member congregations practise Unitarian Universalism, a non-creedal faith that draws on many religious and philosophical traditions and is governed by shared principles and congregational polity rather than a common doctrine.

History

American Unitarianism grew out of liberal New England Congregationalism in the late 18th and early 19th centuries — William Ellery Channing's 1819 sermon "Unitarian Christianity" is a landmark — while Universalism, preaching universal salvation, spread through rural New England and the frontier. After decades of cooperation the two bodies merged in 1961. The UUA has been prominent in social-justice causes, including the civil rights movement (during which the Unitarian minister James Reeb was murdered at Selma in 1965), LGBTQ rights and same-sex marriage, and environmental advocacy.

Beliefs and organisation

The UUA has no creed; its congregations covenant to affirm seven Principles, including the inherent worth and dignity of every person and respect for the interdependent web of all existence, and draw from six Sources including world religions and humanist teachings. Each congregation is self-governing and owns its property. The association's president is elected by delegates at the annual General Assembly. In 2017 the Reverend Susan Frederick-Gray became the first woman elected president of the UUA.
