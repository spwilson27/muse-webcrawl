Bendigo

Bendigo is a city in Victoria, Australia, located in the Bendigo Valley about 150 kilometres (93 mi) north-west of the state capital, Melbourne. As of 2022, Bendigo had a population of 103,034 in its urban area, making it the fourth most populous city in Victoria.

History

The traditional owners of the area are the Dja Dja Wurrung and Taungurung peoples of the Kulin nation. Bendigo Creek was the site of one of the world's richest shallow alluvial gold rushes after gold was discovered in 1851. The town grew rapidly during the Victorian gold rush, becoming a major financial and administrative centre. The name Bendigo is believed to derive from a local shepherd and bare-knuckle boxer nicknamed after William Abednego "Bendigo" Thompson.

Geography and economy

Bendigo lies amid ironbark forests and farmland. Its economy today centres on health, education, finance, tourism, food production and mining, with Bendigo and Adelaide Bank headquartered in the city. Landmarks include the Alexandra Fountain, Sacred Heart Cathedral — one of Australia's largest churches — the Central Deborah Gold Mine, and a well-preserved Victorian streetscape including Pall Mall.

Culture

The Bendigo Art Gallery is one of Australia's largest regional galleries. The city is served by Bendigo railway station on the Melbourne–Bendigo line and by La Trobe University's Bendigo campus.
