Moto2 is the intermediate class of the FIM Grand Prix motorcycle racing world championship, sitting between the lightweight Moto3 class and the premier MotoGP class. The category replaced the 250cc two-stroke class in 2010 and is a one-make engine formula: all riders use identical engines, with competition centred on chassis design and rider skill.

History

When Moto2 debuted in 2010, the spec engine was a 600 cc four-stroke inline-four derived from the Honda CBR600RR, producing around 140 horsepower. Chassis were open to manufacturers such as Suter, Kalex, Moriwaki, and FTR, with Kalex becoming dominant from the mid-2010s onward. From 2019, Triumph became the sole engine supplier with a 765 cc three-cylinder engine based on the Street Triple RS unit, raising output and torque substantially.

Regulations

Moto2 machines are prototypes not based on production motorcycles, unlike the Superbike categories. Bikes weigh a minimum of 140 kg (217 kg combined minimum with rider). Electronics were standardized with a spec ECU introduced in 2019 along with the Triumph engine. Riders must be at least 18 years old. The class serves as the principal feeder series for MotoGP, and nearly all modern MotoGP riders passed through Moto2.
