Arcachon

The Bay of Arcachon on the Atlantic coast of southwestern France

Arcachon (French pronunciation: [aʁkaʃɔ̃]) is a commune in the Gironde department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It is a popular seaside resort town on the Atlantic coast, some 55 km southwest of Bordeaux, on the southern shore of the Arcachon Basin (Bassin d'Arcachon).

Geography

The town occupies the southern shore of the Bassin d'Arcachon, a large lagoon-like bay opening onto the Atlantic Ocean. Across the basin lies the Cap Ferret peninsula. The bay is famous for its oyster farming, its tidal flats, and the nearby Dune of Pilat, the tallest sand dune in Europe, located just south of the town in La Teste-de-Buch.

History

Arcachon developed in the 19th century as a health and seaside resort, aided by the arrival of the railway from Bordeaux in 1857, the same year it was officially established as a commune. Its mild climate and pine forests made it a favoured winter resort, and the Ville d'Hiver (Winter Town) district retains many elaborate 19th-century villas.

Economy

Tourism and oyster farming dominate the local economy. Arcachon oysters are renowned throughout France, and the town's four ports include a fishing port, a marina, and oyster-farming harbours.
