Underwater rugby

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Underwater rugby is a team sport played in a swimming pool, in which two teams of six players compete to place a negatively buoyant ball into the opposing team's goal — a heavy metal basket fixed on the floor at each end of the pool. Players wear mask, snorkel, fins, and water polo caps, and play entirely underwater on breath-hold, surfacing to breathe as play continues. Despite the name, it owes little to rugby union beyond physical tackling of the ball carrier.

Gameplay

The game is played in a pool 3.5 to 5 metres deep, typically 12–22 metres long and 8–12 metres wide. The ball is filled with salt water so that it sinks slowly, and can be passed in any direction — the sport is fully three-dimensional, with attacks coming from above, below, and the sides. Only the player holding the ball may be tackled, and only the ball holder may tackle. Matches consist of two 15-minute halves; there are two referees in the water and one on deck, who signal fouls with buzzers audible underwater. Speed, breath management, and spatial awareness are decisive.

History

The sport originated in Germany in the 1960s, when Ludwig von Bersuda of the German Underwater Club in Mülheim adapted a fitness game involving a sand-filled ball and a bucket. The first official game was played in 1964 and national championships followed; the first World Championships were held in Mülheim in 1980. The sport is governed internationally by CMAS, the World Underwater Federation, and is strongest in Germany, Norway, Sweden, Colombia, and Australia, with world titles contested by men's and women's teams.
