Revolutions of 1848

Revolutions of 1848
Also known as: Springtime of the Peoples
Date: 1848–1849
Location: Europe

The Revolutions of 1848, known in some countries as the Springtime of the Peoples or the Springtime of Nations, were a series of revolutions throughout Europe over the course of more than a year, from 1848 to 1849. They remain the most widespread revolutionary wave in European history to date.

Causes

The revolutions arose from widespread dissatisfaction with political leadership, demands for more participation in government and democracy, demands for freedom of the press, other demands made by the working class for economic rights, and the rise of nationalism. They were fuelled by economic crisis, poor harvests in 1846–47, and the spread of liberal and nationalist ideas since the French Revolution.

Course

The wave began in Sicily in January 1848 and ignited in France in February, when the July Monarchy of Louis Philippe fell and the French Second Republic was proclaimed. Revolutions spread to the German states, the Austrian Empire, Hungary, Italy, Denmark, Poland and elsewhere. In Vienna, Chancellor Metternich — architect of the post-1815 Concert of Europe — was forced to resign and flee.

Failure and legacy

Most revolutions were suppressed by 1849 by conservative forces, often with Russian military assistance in Hungary. Tens of thousands were killed and many exiles fled, including to the United States. Yet the revolutions left lasting effects: serfdom was abolished in Austria and Hungary, the Danish monarchy became constitutional, and the events shaped later movements for national unification in Italy and Germany, as well as the development of liberalism and socialism.
