Numerology

Numerology is the belief in an occult, divine or mystical relationship between a number and one or more coinciding events. It also denotes the study of the numerical value of the letters in words, names and ideas, and is often associated with astrology and similar divinatory arts. Numerology is regarded by modern science as a pseudoscience, though it has deep roots in the history of mathematics and religion.

History

Number symbolism appears in ancient Babylonian, Chinese, Egyptian and Indian traditions. In the Greek world, the followers of Pythagoras (6th century BCE) held that numbers underlay the structure of the universe ("all is number"). Jewish tradition developed gematria, assigning numerical values to Hebrew letters to interpret scripture — the method behind readings of the number of the beast. Christian, Islamic (abjad numerals) and Chinese systems (with lucky 8 and unlucky 4) each developed their own lore. Modern Western numerology was popularised in the early 20th century by writers such as L. Dow Balliett and Cheiro.

Methods

Most modern systems map letters to digits 1–9 (the Pythagorean or Chaldean tables), then reduce sums by repeated addition to a single digit — except the "master numbers" 11, 22 and sometimes 33. Core calculations include the Life Path number (from the birth date), the Destiny/Expression number (from the full birth name), the Soul Urge and Personality numbers (from vowels and consonants), each assigned traits and meanings. Chinese numerology instead rests on homophones between number names and auspicious or inauspicious words.

Criticism

Scientists note that numerological claims are unfalsifiable, that digit-reduction works in any base, and that apparent "hits" arise from confirmation bias and the flexibility of interpretation. Nevertheless numerology remains popular in popular culture, publishing and online media, and number superstitions influence behaviour such as the avoidance of 13 in Western buildings or of 4 in East Asian contexts (tetraphobia).
