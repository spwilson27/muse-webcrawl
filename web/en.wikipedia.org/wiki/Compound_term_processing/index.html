Compound term processing

Compound term processing, in information retrieval, is the splitting or decompounding of compound words into their constituent parts so that documents and queries using different forms can be matched. It is especially important for languages with productive compounding, such as German, Dutch, Finnish, and Swedish.

Motivation

In compounding languages, a concept may appear as part of many different compound words. If a search engine indexes only whole tokens, a query for one constituent will miss documents where the word appears inside a compound, hurting recall.

Techniques

Decompounders use dictionaries, morphological rules, and statistical or corpus-based methods to split words at plausible boundaries, then index both the whole compound and its parts. Ambiguities — multiple possible splits — are a central challenge.

Applications

Compound term processing is used in cross-language and monolingual retrieval evaluations such as CLEF, and in commercial search engines serving Germanic-language markets.
