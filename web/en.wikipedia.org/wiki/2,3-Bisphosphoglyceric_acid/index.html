2,3-Bisphosphoglyceric acid
Abbreviation2,3-BPG; 2,3-DPG
FormulaC3H8O10P2
RoleAllosteric regulator of hemoglobin
PathwayRapoport–Luebering shunt of glycolysis

2,3-Bisphosphoglyceric acid (2,3-BPG), also called 2,3-diphosphoglycerate, is a three-carbon isomer of the glycolytic intermediate 1,3-bisphosphoglyceric acid. It is present in high concentrations in human red blood cells, where it binds to hemoglobin and regulates the release of oxygen to the tissues.

Function

2,3-BPG binds in the central cavity of deoxyhemoglobin, between the two beta subunits, stabilizing the T (tense) state and thereby lowering hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen. This promotes oxygen unloading in the tissues. A single molecule of 2,3-BPG binds per hemoglobin tetramer. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF), which contains gamma instead of beta chains, binds 2,3-BPG weakly, helping the fetus draw oxygen across the placenta from maternal blood.

Synthesis

In erythrocytes, 2,3-BPG is produced and degraded in the Rapoport–Luebering shunt, a side pathway of glycolysis: the enzyme bisphosphoglycerate mutase converts 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to 2,3-BPG, and a phosphatase activity hydrolyses it to 3-phosphoglycerate. Its concentration rises at high altitude and in chronic hypoxia, shifting the oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve to the right.

Clinical significance

Levels fall in stored blood, temporarily reducing oxygen delivery after transfusion. Rare enzyme deficiencies affecting the shunt alter red-cell 2,3-BPG and hemoglobin oxygen affinity.
