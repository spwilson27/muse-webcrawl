Kingdom of Sikkim

Existed: 1642 – 16 May 1975
Monarchy: Chogyal (Namgyal dynasty)
First Chogyal: Phuntsog Namgyal
Last Chogyal: Palden Thondup Namgyal
Capital: Yuksom, later Gangtok
Status: British protectorate, then Indian protectorate (1950–1975)

The Kingdom of Sikkim (Classical Tibetan and Sikkimese: འབྲས་མོ་གཤོངས་མཛད་; Wylie: Dremoshong) was a hereditary monarchy in the Eastern Himalayas that existed from 1642 to 1975. It was ruled by the Chogyals of the Namgyal dynasty and was wedged between Nepal, Bhutan, Tibet and British India. In 1975, following a referendum, the kingdom was dissolved and Sikkim became the 22nd state of India.

Founding

The kingdom was founded in 1642, when three revered Tibetan lamas consecrated Phuntsog Namgyal as the first Chogyal (dharma king) at Yuksom in western Sikkim. The Namgyal dynasty traced its descent to Guru Tashi, a prince from the Kham region of Tibet, and to the legendary Khye Bumsa, who had formed a blood brotherhood with the Lepcha chief Thekong Tek in the 13th century.

History

Over the following centuries Sikkim lost much territory to its neighbours: the Limbuana and Morang regions to Nepal, the Chumbi Valley area to Tibet, and the Darjeeling district to the British, to whom it was ceded in 1835. After the Anglo-Sikkimese Treaty of Tumlong (1861), Sikkim became a de facto British protectorate, administered through a Political Officer stationed at Gangtok.

After Indian independence, a treaty in 1950 made Sikkim a protectorate of India, which controlled its defence, external affairs and communications while the Chogyal retained internal authority. The last Chogyal, Palden Thondup Namgyal, succeeded in 1963; his marriage to the American Hope Cooke, who became queen (Gyalmo), drew international attention to the kingdom. Growing anti-monarchy agitation and ethnic tensions between the Bhutia-Lepcha elite and the Nepali-speaking majority led to unrest in 1973. In April 1975, following a disputed referendum on abolishing the monarchy, Indian forces disarmed the palace guard, and on 16 May 1975 Sikkim formally became a state of India, ending 333 years of the Namgyal monarchy.
