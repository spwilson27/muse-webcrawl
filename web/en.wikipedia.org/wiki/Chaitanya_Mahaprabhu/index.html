Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (Bengali: চৈতন্য মহাপ্রভু; 18 February 1486 – 14 June 1534) was an Indian saint regarded by his devotees as the combined avatar of Radha and Krishna. He is the founder of Gaudiya Vaishnavism and is credited with popularising the public chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra through sankirtana (congregational chanting). His philosophy, systematised by his followers, is known as Achintya Bheda Abheda — "inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference".

Life

Chaitanya was born in Nabadwip (Navadvipa), Bengal, to Jagannath Mishra and Sachi Devi, and was named Vishvambhara; he was also affectionately called Nimai and Gauranga ("the golden one") for his fair complexion. A brilliant scholar of Sanskrit grammar and logic in his youth, he married Lakshmipriya and, after her death, Vishnupriya.

While on a pilgrimage to Gaya, he met the ascetic Ishvara Puri, from whom he received initiation and underwent a profound spiritual transformation. Returning to Nabadwip, he began leading ecstatic public chanting processions, defying local opposition. At the age of 24 he took sannyasa (renounced life) from Keshava Bharati and received the name Krishna Chaitanya.

He travelled through South India and to Vrindavan, and finally settled in Puri, Odisha, home of the Jagannath temple, where he spent the last 18 years of his life. He died in Puri in 1534; the circumstances of his death are described differently in various hagiographies.

Teachings

Chaitanya's own written work consists of the eight verses known as the Shikshashtakam. His theology was elaborated by the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan, his chosen disciples, in an extensive Sanskrit literature. Central teachings include:

  • Devotion (bhakti) to Krishna as the supreme reality, attained especially through chanting the holy names.
  • The chanting of the Hare Krishna mantra as the prescribed spiritual practice for the Kali Yuga.
  • The doctrine of Achintya Bheda Abheda, that God is simultaneously one with and different from his energies and devotees, in a way inconceivable to the mind.

Legacy

Chaitanya's movement transformed the religious life of Bengal and Odisha and inspired a rich tradition of Bengali devotional literature, biography (such as the Chaitanya Bhagavata and Chaitanya Charitamrita), and music. In the 20th century, Gaudiya Vaishnavism was carried worldwide by the Gaudiya Math and by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, founder of ISKCON (the Hare Krishna movement). Chaitanya is also remembered for his socially levelling appeal, welcoming devotees across caste boundaries.
