The Collège de France is a higher education and research establishment in Paris, founded in 1530 by King Francis I as the Collège des lecteurs royaux. It occupies a unique place in French intellectual life: its professors are elected to chairs in fields across the sciences and humanities, and their lectures are free and open to all, without enrolment, degrees or examinations.

Principles

The Collège's mission is famously described as teaching "knowledge in the making" (le savoir en train de se faire): professors must present their current research rather than settled doctrine, and chairs are regularly refounded in new disciplines. It has about fifty chairs at any time.

Notable professors

Past holders include the historian Jules Michelet, the philosopher Henri Bergson, the sociologist Pierre Bourdieu and the philosopher Michel Foucault, whose lecture courses at the Collège became major works.
