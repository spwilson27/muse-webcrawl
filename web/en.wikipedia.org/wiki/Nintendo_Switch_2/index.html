The Nintendo Switch 2 is a video game console developed by Nintendo and released worldwide on 5 June 2025. The successor to the Nintendo Switch, it retains the hybrid design that can be used as a handheld, in tabletop mode, or docked to a television, while substantially upgrading the hardware: a larger 7.9-inch LCD screen at 1080p with HDR and up to 120 Hz, support for 4K output when docked, a custom Nvidia processor with DLSS upscaling and ray tracing, 12 GB of RAM, and 256 GB of internal storage expandable with microSD Express cards.

Hardware and controllers

The new Joy-Con 2 controllers attach magnetically to the console's sides and can be used as computer mice on flat surfaces; they include larger sticks and buttons and an additional "C" button for the GameChat voice and video feature, which works with an optional camera accessory. A new Nintendo Switch 2 Pro Controller was released alongside. The system is backward compatible with most Nintendo Switch game cards and digital purchases, and Nintendo Switch Online continues on the platform, with an Expansion Pack tier adding Nintendo GameCube games.

Release and sales

Nintendo formally revealed the console in January 2025 and detailed it in a Nintendo Direct in April. Launch software included Mario Kart World, the first open-world entry in the series and a pack-in option in a hardware bundle, and Nintendo Switch 2 Welcome Tour, with Donkey Kong Bananza following in July 2025. The console became the fastest-selling in Nintendo's history, selling more than 3.5 million units in its first four days and passing 10 million within its first months on the market, despite debate over game pricing, including $80 flagship titles and "game-key card" physical releases that download their content.

Reception

Reviewers praised the performance leap, build quality, and backward compatibility while criticizing battery life in handheld mode, the LCD (rather than OLED) display, and the rising cost of games and accessories.
