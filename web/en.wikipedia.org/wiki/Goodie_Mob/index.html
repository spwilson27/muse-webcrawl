Goodie Mob

Goodie Mob is an American hip hop group based in Atlanta, Georgia, consisting of Big Gipp, Khujo, T-Mo and CeeLo Green. The group's name is often explained as an acronym for "the Good Die Mostly Over Bullshit". A founding act of the Dungeon Family collective alongside Outkast, Goodie Mob helped define Southern hip hop in the 1990s, pairing street-level reportage with spiritual and socially conscious themes; the title of their debut album popularised the phrase "Dirty South".

Career

The group emerged from Atlanta's Dungeon Family scene, appearing on Outkast's 1994 debut Southernplayalisticadillacmuzik. Their own debut, Soul Food (1995), produced by Organized Noize, is widely regarded as a Southern classic, featuring the singles "Cell Therapy" and "Soul Food". The follow-up Still Standing (1998) continued their critical acclaim.

World Party (1999) pursued a lighter, more commercial sound and divided fans; CeeLo Green left the group afterwards to pursue a solo career, later achieving international fame with Gnarls Barkley and as a solo artist. The remaining trio released One Monkey Don't Stop No Show (2004). The original quartet reunited for Age Against the Machine (2013) and released Survival Kit in 2020.

Legacy

Goodie Mob's influence lies in demonstrating that Southern rap could carry the thematic weight of its East and West Coast counterparts — addressing poverty, policing, faith and Black Southern identity over soulful, live-instrument production. Their work laid groundwork for Atlanta's eventual dominance of mainstream hip hop.
