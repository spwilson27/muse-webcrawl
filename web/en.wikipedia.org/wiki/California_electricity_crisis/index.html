California electricity crisis

The California electricity crisis, also known as the Western U.S. energy crisis of 2000 and 2001, was a period of severe electricity shortages, extreme wholesale price spikes and rolling blackouts in California and neighbouring western states. Between mid-2000 and mid-2001 the state experienced repeated Stage 3 emergency alerts, rolling blackouts affecting hundreds of thousands of customers at a time, the bankruptcy of its largest utility, Pacific Gas and Electric, and costs to the state estimated in the tens of billions of dollars.

Causes

The crisis followed California's partial deregulation of its electricity market under Assembly Bill 1890 of 1996. The restructuring separated generation from retail supply, required the investor-owned utilities to sell much of their generating capacity, pushed them to buy power on volatile spot markets through the California Power Exchange, and simultaneously froze the retail rates they could charge customers. When wholesale prices soared in 2000 — driven by rising natural gas prices, drought that reduced Pacific Northwest hydroelectric output, growing demand, and inadequate generating capacity — the utilities were forced to buy high and sell low, accumulating ruinous debts.

Subsequent investigations showed that energy trading companies, most notoriously Enron, had manipulated the market through strategies nicknamed “Death Star”, “Fat Boy” and “Get Shorty”, and that some generators deliberately withheld capacity to raise prices. Enron collapsed in 2001 amid accounting scandal, several traders were prosecuted, and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission eventually ordered billions of dollars in refunds and settlements.

Consequences

The state government intervened in early 2001, authorising the Department of Water Resources to buy power on long-term contracts and issuing some $11 billion in revenue bonds. Governor Gray Davis's handling of the crisis badly damaged his popularity and contributed to his recall in 2003, when he was replaced by Arnold Schwarzenegger. The crisis discredited electricity deregulation schemes across the United States for a generation.
