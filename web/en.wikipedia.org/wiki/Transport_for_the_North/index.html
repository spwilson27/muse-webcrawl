Transport for the North

Abbreviation: TfN

Formation: 2014 (as a partnership); statutory body from 2018

Type: Sub-national transport body

Headquarters: Manchester, England

Region served: Northern England

Transport for the North (TfN) is a sub-national transport body in England. It brings together local transport authorities and local enterprise partnerships from across the North of England to advise the government on the region's transport investment priorities. It became England's first statutory sub-national transport body in April 2018.

History

Transport for the North was established in 2014 as a partnership of city region authorities, following the model of Transport for London. In 2016 the government announced its intention to give TfN statutory status, and regulations laid in 2017 made it a statutory body from 1 April 2018. Its board includes the leaders of the combined authorities and county councils of the North, along with business representatives.

Role and strategy

TfN's principal role is to develop a long-term strategic transport plan for the North and to advise the Secretary of State for Transport on investment. Its Strategic Transport Plan, published in 2019, set out priorities including Northern Powerhouse Rail, a proposed network of new and upgraded rail lines linking Liverpool, Manchester, Leeds, Sheffield, Hull and Newcastle. TfN also works on strategic road studies, integrated ticketing and the development of major rail and road schemes in partnership with Network Rail, Highways England and the Department for Transport.

Northern Powerhouse Rail

Northern Powerhouse Rail (later rebranded as part of the Integrated Rail Plan) was TfN's flagship programme, intended to transform east-west rail connectivity across the Pennines. The government's Integrated Rail Plan of November 2021 scaled back several of TfN's preferred options, a decision criticised by northern leaders.
