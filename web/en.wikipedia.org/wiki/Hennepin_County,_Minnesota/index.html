Hennepin County is a county in the U.S. state of Minnesota. It is the state's most populous county and one of the most populous counties in the Upper Midwest. Its county seat and largest city is Minneapolis. The county is named for the 17th-century French explorer Father Louis Hennepin, who described the Falls of St. Anthony.

History

Hennepin County was established by the Minnesota territorial legislature in 1852 from land west of the Mississippi River. Minneapolis and its twin city, Saint Paul in Ramsey County, anchor the Minneapolis–Saint Paul metropolitan area. The county grew with milling, railroading and manufacturing in the 19th century and became a centre of finance, medicine and education in the 20th.

Geography and communities

The county covers about 607 square miles, including numerous lakes such as Lake Minnetonka in the west. Besides Minneapolis, its cities include Bloomington, Plymouth, Minnetonka, Eden Prairie, Maple Grove, Edina and Brooklyn Park. The Hennepin County Government Center in downtown Minneapolis houses county administration and courts.

Government and services

The county is governed by an elected board of commissioners from seven districts. It operates the Hennepin County Library system, the regional Hennepin Healthcare system centred on HCMC, major road and transit networks, and the county sheriff's office. It is consistently ranked among the wealthiest and most educated counties in Minnesota, while also containing the state's largest concentration of urban poverty.
