Xenophobia

Xenophobia (from Ancient Greek xénos, "strange, foreign, alien", and phóbos, "fear") is the fear or dislike of anything that is perceived as foreign or strange. It is an expression based on the perception that a conflict exists between an in-group and an out-group, and it may manifest in suspicion of the activities of others and a desire to eliminate their presence to secure a presumed purity; it may also relate to a fear of losing national, ethnic, or racial identity.

Definitions and distinctions

International bodies distinguish two forms: hostility or fear directed at groups perceived as outsiders within a society, and rejection of cultural diversity or foreign cultural influences. Xenophobia overlaps with, but is distinct from, racism: racism is based on perceived racial difference, whereas xenophobia targets perceived foreignness and can operate between groups of the same "race". Dictionary definitions generally describe it as fear and hatred of strangers or foreigners, or of anything strange or foreign.

Manifestations

Xenophobia has manifested historically in discriminatory immigration laws (such as the Chinese Exclusion Act in the United States), pogroms and violence against immigrant communities, exclusionary nationalist movements, and everyday discrimination in housing, employment, and policing. It frequently intensifies during economic downturns, wars, epidemics, and rapid demographic change, and it is a recurrent theme in the politics of migration and asylum.
