Elder abuse (also called elder mistreatment) is harm inflicted on an older adult, typically by someone in a position of trust such as a family member, caregiver or institution. The World Health Organization defines it as "a single, or repeated act, or lack of appropriate action, occurring within any relationship where there is an expectation of trust which causes harm or distress to an older person."

Types

Commonly recognised forms include: physical abuse (inflicting pain or injury); psychological or emotional abuse (threats, humiliation, isolation); sexual abuse; financial abuse (theft, fraud, coercion over money or property — the most commonly reported form in many studies); neglect (failure to provide food, medication, hygiene or care); and abandonment. Self-neglect by an older person who cannot care for themselves is often tracked alongside abuse, though it involves no perpetrator.

Prevalence and risk factors

The WHO estimates that around one in six people aged 60 and over in community settings experienced some form of abuse in the past month, with higher rates in care institutions. Abuse is substantially under-reported, owing to victims' dependence on the abuser, cognitive impairment, shame, or fear. Risk factors include dementia, social isolation, shared living arrangements, caregiver stress and substance abuse by the perpetrator. Elder abuse is associated with increased mortality, hospitalisation and depression.

Responses

Many jurisdictions have Adult Protective Services agencies empowered to investigate reports, and some impose mandatory reporting duties on professionals. Interventions range from legal action and guardianship to respite care, caregiver support and financial safeguards. The United Nations recognises June 15 as World Elder Abuse Awareness Day, first observed in 2006 and formally recognised by the UN General Assembly in 2011.
