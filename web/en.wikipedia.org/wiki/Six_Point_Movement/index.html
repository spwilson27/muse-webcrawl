Six Point Movement

The Six Point Movement (Bengali: ছয় দফা আন্দোলন, romanized: Chhôẏ dôfa andôlon) was a Bengali nationalist movement in East Pakistan spearheaded by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and the Awami League. The movement reached its peak in 1966. It remains a milestone in the history of Bangladesh, representing the first time that the Bengalis of East Pakistan demanded autonomy from the central government of Pakistan in West Pakistan. The six points are widely seen as a stepping stone to the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971.

Background

Following the partition of India in 1947, East Bengal became a province of Pakistan despite being separated from West Pakistan by over 1,600 km of Indian territory and differing in language and culture. Ruling elites in West Pakistan dominated the military and bureaucracy, and Bengali grievances over economic disparity, political under-representation, and the status of the Bengali language (addressed by the Language Movement of 1952) deepened through the 1950s and 1960s.

The six points

Sheikh Mujibur Rahman announced the six points in February 1966, in Lahore and then at a press conference in Dhaka. The demands were:

  1. A federal state of Pakistan based on the Lahore Resolution, with a parliamentary government elected by universal adult franchise.
  2. The federal government to deal only with defense and foreign affairs; all other powers to reside in the constituent units.
  3. Two separate but freely convertible currencies, or a single currency with safeguards against capital flight from East to West Pakistan.
  4. Taxation and revenue collection to rest with the federating units; the federal centre to receive a share to meet its responsibilities.
  5. Separate foreign-exchange accounts for the two wings, with earnings of each wing under its own control.
  6. The raising of a militia or paramilitary force for East Pakistan for its own security.

Movement and aftermath

The programme ignited mass mobilization across East Pakistan. The government of Ayub Khan denounced it as secessionist; Mujib was arrested in May 1966 and later tried in the Agartala Conspiracy Case. A general strike on 7 June 1966 was met with police firing, and the date is still commemorated in Bangladesh as Six-Point Day. The mass uprising of 1969 forced Mujib's release, and the Awami League's victory in the 1970 general election — fought largely on the six points — set the stage for the crisis that culminated in Bangladeshi independence in 1971.
