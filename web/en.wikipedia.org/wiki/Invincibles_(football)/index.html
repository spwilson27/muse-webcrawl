Invincibles (football)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Arsenal's 2003–04 "Invincibles" with the Premier League trophy

The Invincibles is a nickname given to football teams that complete an entire league season undefeated. The term is most closely associated with two English sides: Preston North End of 1888–89 and Arsenal of 2003–04.

Preston North End (1888–89)

Preston North End won the inaugural Football League championship in 1888–89 without losing a match — 18 wins and 4 draws from 22 games — and also won the FA Cup that season without conceding a goal, completing "the Double". Managed by William Sudell and featuring players such as John Goodall and Jimmy Ross, the team earned the original "Invincibles" epithet.

Arsenal (2003–04)

Arsène Wenger's Arsenal won the 2003–04 FA Premier League with 26 wins and 12 draws from 38 matches, the first — and still the only — team to go unbeaten through a 38-game English top-flight season. Captained by Patrick Vieira and featuring Thierry Henry, Robert Pires, Sol Campbell and Jens Lehmann, the side extended its unbeaten run to a record 49 league matches before losing at Manchester United in October 2004. The Premier League awarded the club a unique gold replica trophy to mark the achievement.

Other unbeaten teams

Undefeated league seasons have also been achieved in other major leagues, including AC Milan (1991–92 Serie A), Ajax (1994–95 Eredivisie), Juventus (2011–12 Serie A), Celtic (2016–17 Scottish Premiership) and Bayer Leverkusen, who completed the 2023–24 Bundesliga season unbeaten — the first such season in German top-flight history — as part of a record European unbeaten run across all competitions.
