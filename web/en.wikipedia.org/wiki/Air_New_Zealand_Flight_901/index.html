Air New Zealand Flight 901

Date: 28 November 1979
Site: Mount Erebus, Ross Island, Antarctica
Aircraft: McDonnell Douglas DC-10-30
Fatalities: 257 (all aboard)

Air New Zealand Flight 901 (TE901) was a scheduled Antarctic sightseeing flight that crashed into Mount Erebus on Ross Island, Antarctica, on 28 November 1979, killing all 237 passengers and 20 crew. It remains New Zealand's deadliest disaster and one of the deadliest in aviation history.

Accident

Air New Zealand had operated Antarctic sightseeing flights from Auckland since 1977, flying a wide loop over McMurdo Sound with commentary by experienced guides. Unknown to the crew, the company's flight-planning section had altered the navigation coordinates for the flight path the night before, shifting the track about 27 nautical miles east — directly toward Mount Erebus — without informing the pilots. In whiteout conditions, with a flat light phenomenon known as sector whiteout hiding the mountain, the DC-10 flew into the volcano's lower slopes at about 1,500 feet.

Investigations and controversy

The official accident report by Chief Inspector of Air Accidents Ron Chippindale attributed the crash primarily to pilot error for descending below the authorised minimum altitude. A Royal Commission headed by Justice Peter Mahon reached a very different conclusion, finding that the route change and the airline's failure to inform the crew were the dominant causes, and famously describing an "orchestrated litany of lies" by airline executives. The Privy Council later ruled that Mahon's conspiracy finding breached natural justice, though his core factual findings about the navigational error stood. Recovery of bodies and wreckage in extreme conditions (Operation Overdue) became a major undertaking for New Zealand police and mountaineers, and a memorial stands near Scott Base.
