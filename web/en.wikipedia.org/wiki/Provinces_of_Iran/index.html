Provinces of Iran

Placeholder image — map of Iran's provinces

Iran is subdivided into thirty-one provinces (Persian: استان, ostān), each governed from a local centre, usually the largest local city, which is called the capital (مرکز, markaz) of that province. The provincial authority is headed by a governor-general (استاندار, ostāndār), who is appointed by the Minister of the Interior subject to approval of the cabinet.

History

Modern provincial administration dates to reforms under the Qajar and Pahlavi dynasties. In 1937 Iran was divided into ten provinces; the number grew over the following decades as larger provinces were split. The most recent change came in 2010, when the province of Alborz was created from part of Tehran Province, bringing the total to thirty-one.

Structure

Provinces are further divided into counties (shahrestan), districts (bakhsh), cities (shahr) and rural districts. Below the provinces, local government includes elected city and village councils, though governors-general and most executive officials are appointed by the central government in Tehran.
