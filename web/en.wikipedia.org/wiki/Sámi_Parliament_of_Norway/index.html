The Sámi Parliament of Norway (Norwegian: Sametinget; Northern Sámi: Sámediggi) is the representative body for the Sámi people in Norway. It was established under the Sámi Act of 1987 and held its first session in 1989, opened by King Olav V. The parliament sits in Karasjok, in a distinctive building whose plenary chamber is shaped like a traditional Sámi lavvu tent.

Functions

The Sámi Parliament is an advisory and administrative body dealing with matters affecting the Sámi as an Indigenous people: language, culture, education, reindeer husbandry, land and resource questions, and the administration of cultural heritage. It distributes state funding to Sámi purposes and is consulted by the Norwegian government on issues affecting Sámi interests. Its authority was strengthened by the Finnmark Act of 2005 and by Norway's consultation agreements with the parliament.

Elections

Thirty-nine representatives are elected every four years, at the same time as Norwegian parliamentary elections, from seven constituencies covering the whole country. Voters must be registered in the Sámi electoral roll, which requires self-identification as Sámi and Sámi language or ancestry criteria. Political organisation combines national parties' Sámi lists with independent Sámi associations such as the Norwegian Sámi Association (NSR) and the Sámi People's Party.
