Military academy

A military academy or service academy is an educational institution which prepares candidates for service in the officer corps of the armed forces. It normally provides education in a military environment, the exact definition depending on the country concerned. Military academies may offer secondary-level education for adolescents, degree-level education for officer cadets, or postgraduate and staff training for serving officers.

Types

Pre-college institutions train school-age pupils in a military-style environment, sometimes as preparation for later officer training. University-level academies provide bachelor's degrees and commission their graduates as officers; examples include the United States Military Academy at West Point, the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in the United Kingdom, Saint-Cyr in France, and the National Defence Academy in India. Postgraduate institutions, such as staff colleges and war colleges, train mid-career and senior officers in strategy, command, and joint operations.

History

The first modern military academies appeared in Europe in the eighteenth century, as warfare became more technical — particularly in artillery and engineering — and states sought a professional, educated officer corps in place of commissions purchased or held by birth. The Royal Military Academy at Woolwich (1741) and the French École Militaire (1750) were among the early foundations; the United States Military Academy followed in 1802.

Training

Cadets typically follow an academic curriculum alongside military instruction in drill, tactics, leadership, and physical training, and are subject to a disciplinary code and honor system. On graduation, students are commissioned as junior officers and usually owe a period of active service in return for their education.
