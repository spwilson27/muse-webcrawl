Republican People's Party

The Republican People's Party (Turkish: Cumhuriyet Halk Partisi, CHP) is a social democratic political party in Turkey. Founded on 9 September 1923 by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk as the People's Party, it is the oldest political party in Turkey and the party that established the Turkish Republic. The CHP is associated with Kemalism, summed up in the "Six Arrows" of its emblem: republicanism, populism, nationalism, laicism (secularism), statism and revolutionism/reformism.

History

The CHP governed Turkey as a single-party regime from 1923 until the transition to multi-party democracy in 1946–50, under Atatürk and his successor İsmet İnönü, carrying through the sweeping Atatürk reforms: abolition of the caliphate, adoption of the Latin alphabet, secular law codes and women's suffrage. In opposition and government at various times after 1950, it shifted towards the centre-left under Bülent Ecevit in the 1970s. The party was banned after the 1980 military coup and refounded in 1992.

Modern era

From 2002 the CHP became the main opposition to the governing Justice and Development Party (AKP). Under Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu (leader 2010–2023) it built broad opposition alliances, winning the Istanbul and Ankara mayoralties in the 2019 local elections through figures such as Ekrem İmamoğlu. Özgür Özel became leader in November 2023, and in the 2024 local elections the CHP finished first nationwide in the popular vote for the first time in decades, retaining the major metropolitan municipalities.

Ideology

The party defends the secular, unitary republic founded by Atatürk and has repositioned itself as a social democratic party emphasising rule of law, social welfare and closer ties with Europe. It is a member of the Socialist International and an associate of the Party of European Socialists.
