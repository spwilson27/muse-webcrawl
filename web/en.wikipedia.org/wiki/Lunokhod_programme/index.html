Lunokhod programme

The Lunokhod programme (Russian: Лунохо́д, "Moonwalker") was a series of Soviet robotic lunar rovers designed to land on the Moon between 1969 and 1977. Its two successful vehicles, Lunokhod 1 (1970) and Lunokhod 2 (1973), were the first roving remote-controlled robots to land on another celestial body, and they remain among the most distant-traveled rovers ever built.

Design

Each Lunokhod was a tub-like magnesium-alloy pressure vessel with a large convex lid that opened during the lunar day to expose solar cells and closed at night to conserve heat. The rover rode on eight independently powered wire-mesh wheels, each with titanium spokes, and carried a conical antenna, a highly directional helical antenna, television cameras for navigation, and a soil mechanics tester. A team of five controllers on Earth drove the rover in near-real time despite the roughly 2.6-second round-trip signal delay.

Missions

Lunokhod 1 landed in the Sea of Rains aboard Luna 17 on 17 November 1970 and operated for about 322 Earth days, travelling roughly 10.5 km and returning more than 20,000 television images and 206 high-resolution panoramas. Lunokhod 2 landed in Le Monnier crater aboard Luna 21 in January 1973 and travelled about 39 km — a record for off-Earth roving that stood until NASA's Opportunity rover surpassed it in 2014. A third rover, Lunokhod 3, was built for a 1977 mission that was cancelled; it now resides in the Lavochkin museum.
