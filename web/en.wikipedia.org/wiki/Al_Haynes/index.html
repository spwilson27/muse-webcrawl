Al Haynes

Alfred Clair Haynes (August 31, 1931 – August 25, 2019) was an American pilot who flew for United Airlines for 35 years. He became widely known as the captain of United Airlines Flight 232, which crash-landed at Sioux City, Iowa, in 1989 after total hydraulic failure; his crew's management of an almost unflyable aircraft saved 184 of the 296 people on board and became a landmark case study in crew resource management.

Early life and career

Haynes was born in Paris, Texas, and raised in Dallas. After attending Texas A&M University he joined the US Marine Corps, serving as a pilot and flight instructor, then joined United Airlines in 1956. He was based in Seattle and Chicago during his career and had logged some 30,000 flying hours by 1989.

Flight 232

On 19 July 1989 the McDonnell Douglas DC-10 Haynes was commanding from Denver to Chicago suffered an uncontained failure of its tail-mounted engine, which severed all three hydraulic systems and left the aircraft without functioning flight controls. Haynes and his crew — first officer William Records, flight engineer Dudley Dvorak and off-duty training captain Denny Fitch, who joined them in the cockpit — steered the jet for 45 minutes using only differential engine thrust, reaching the runway at Sioux Gateway Airport. The aircraft cartwheeled on touchdown and broke apart, killing 112 people, but 184 survived, an outcome investigators considered remarkable given the circumstances. Haynes frequently deflected praise, crediting teamwork and luck, and spent his retirement speaking on aviation safety and crisis leadership.
