Sir John Soane's Museum is a historic house museum in Lincoln's Inn Fields in the London Borough of Camden. It was formerly the home and studio of the Neoclassical architect John Soane (1753–1837). It holds many drawings and architectural models of Soane's projects and a large collection of paintings, sculptures, drawings, and antiquities that he assembled over his career.

History

Soane demolished and rebuilt three houses on the north side of Lincoln's Inn Fields between 1792 and 1824, progressively extending them to house his growing collection and to serve as a setting for architectural experimentation with space and light — top-lit rooms, domed ceilings, coloured glass, and mirrors creating surprising spatial effects within a modest footprint. In 1833 Soane obtained a private Act of Parliament that preserved the house and collection, exactly as they were arranged at his death, for the benefit of "amateurs and students" in architecture and the arts; the museum opened after his death in 1837, and admission remains free.

Collection

Highlights include the Egyptian sarcophagus of Seti I in the basement Sepulchral Chamber, Hogarth's original paintings for A Rake's Progress and An Election in the Picture Room — whose hinged wall panels open to reveal further pictures and a view into the Monk's Parlour — works by Canaletto, Turner, and Reynolds, and the chained library of architectural books and drawings, among them the drawings collection of Robert and James Adam. The museum also preserves the curator's spaces, including the studio and the model room.

Today

The museum underwent a major restoration programme, "Opening up the Soane", completed in 2016, which restored rooms lost to later alterations and improved access. It is frequently described as one of the most atmospheric small museums in the world, and its candlelit evening openings are a long-standing tradition.
