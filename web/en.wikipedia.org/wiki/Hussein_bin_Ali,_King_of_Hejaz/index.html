Hussein bin Ali, King of Hejaz

Bornc. 1853, Istanbul, Ottoman Empire
Died4 June 1931, Amman, Transjordan
ReignKing of Hejaz: 1916–1924; Sharif of Mecca: 1908–1924
DynastyHashemite
IssueAli, Abdullah, Faisal, Zeid

Hussein bin Ali (Arabic: الحسين بن علي, romanized: Al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī; c. 1853 – 4 June 1931) was the Sharif and Emir of Mecca from 1908 and the King of Hejaz from 1916 until 1924. A member of the Hashemite family claiming descent from the Prophet Muhammad, he launched the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War, encouraged by British promises of Arab independence recorded in the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence. His sons Faisal and Abdullah became the first kings of Iraq and Jordan.

Early life and Sharifate

Hussein was born in Istanbul, where his father lived in exile, and spent years in the Ottoman capital before returning to the Hejaz. In 1908, following the Young Turk revolution, he was appointed Sharif of Mecca, the traditional Hashemite custodianship of the holy cities of Mecca and Medina. He manoeuvred between the Ottoman authorities and local rivals, gradually consolidating his position while quietly cultivating contacts with Arab nationalist societies and the British in Egypt.

Arab Revolt

In 1915–1916 Hussein exchanged a series of letters with Sir Henry McMahon, British High Commissioner in Egypt, negotiating the terms on which the Arabs would rise against the Ottomans in exchange for recognition of Arab independence. In June 1916 he proclaimed the Arab Revolt in Mecca. Hashemite forces, advised by British officers including T. E. Lawrence and supplied with British gold and weapons, seized Mecca, Jeddah, and Taif, then advanced along the Hejaz railway and into Syria, entering Damascus in October 1918. In October 1916 Hussein was proclaimed King of the Arab Lands, though the Allies recognized him only as King of Hejaz.

Downfall and exile

After the war Hussein felt betrayed by the Sykes–Picot Agreement and the Balfour Declaration and refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles or accept the mandate system. In March 1924, after the abolition of the Ottoman Caliphate, he proclaimed himself Caliph, a claim that alienated many Muslims and his British patrons. His old rival Abdulaziz Ibn Saud, whose Ikhwan forces had already seized much of Arabia, invaded the Hejaz in 1924; Hussein abdicated in favour of his son Ali in October 1924 and went into exile in Cyprus. The Saudis took Mecca and Jeddah, ending Hashemite rule in Arabia. Hussein died in Amman in 1931 and was buried in Jerusalem, at the al-Aqsa Mosque compound.
