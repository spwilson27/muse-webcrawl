Le Morte d'Arthur ("The Death of Arthur") is a work of prose by Sir Thomas Malory, first published in 1485 by William Caxton. It retells and unifies the legends of King Arthur, Guinevere, Lancelot, Merlin and the Knights of the Round Table, drawing mainly on French and English sources. It is the best-known work of English-language Arthurian literature and the source for nearly all later retellings.

Contents

Malory's book traces Arthur's birth and rise to the throne, the founding of the Round Table, the quests of its knights — including the Grail quest of Galahad, Percival and Bors — the love affair of Lancelot and Guinevere, and the final civil war with Mordred that ends with Arthur's death and departure to Avalon. Caxton's edition divided the text into 21 books and 507 chapters.

The author and manuscripts

Malory completed the work around 1469–1470, apparently while imprisoned. For centuries it was known only through Caxton's printed edition; in 1934 a manuscript version, the Winchester Manuscript, was discovered, differing in some respects and apparently closer to Malory's original.

Influence

The work shaped later Arthurian literature, from Tennyson's Idylls of the King to T. H. White's The Once and Future King and modern films and novels.
