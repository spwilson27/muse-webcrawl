Montesinho Natural Park

LocationBragança District, north-eastern Portugal
Areaabout 742 km² (74,200 ha)
Established1979
Nearest cityBragança

Montesinho Natural Park (Portuguese: Parque Natural de Montesinho) is a protected area in the municipalities of Bragança and Vinhais, in the Trás-os-Montes region of north-eastern Portugal, along the Spanish border. Covering some 74,000 hectares, it is one of the largest natural parks in the country, taking its name from the Serra de Montesinho, whose highest ground rises to about 1,486 metres.

Landscape and wildlife

The park's landscape is a mosaic of rolling schist and granite plateaus, river valleys, and gentle mountains clothed in one of the largest continuous stands of Pyrenean oak in Portugal, along with chestnut groves, holm oak, and riparian alder and ash. Its fauna includes the Iberian wolf, wild boar, red deer, roe deer, otter, and the Iberian hare, and a rich birdlife including golden eagles and black storks; its streams support trout and endangered freshwater pearl mussels.

Villages and culture

Within the park lie some ninety small villages, many built of schist and granite with communal traditions still visible in shared threshing floors, forges, and mills. Rio de Onor, a village straddling the Spanish border, is famed for its community-based way of life. Depopulation has left many settlements with only a handful of elderly residents, and the park authorities promote rural tourism and local products such as chestnuts, honey, and cured meats to sustain the communities.
