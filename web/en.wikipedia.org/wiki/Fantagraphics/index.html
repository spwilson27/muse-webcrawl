Fantagraphics

Fantagraphics (previously Fantagraphics Books) is an American publisher of alternative comics, classic comic strip anthologies, magazines, graphic novels, and the erotic Eros Comix imprint. Founded in 1976 by Gary Groth and Mike Catron, the company is based in Seattle, Washington, and is widely regarded as one of the most important publishers in the history of alternative comics.

History

Groth and Catron founded Fantagraphics in 1976, initially publishing the comics news and criticism magazine The Comics Journal, which became famous for its long, in-depth interviews and sharp critical stance toward the mainstream comics industry. In the early 1980s the company began publishing comics, most notably Love and Rockets by the Hernandez brothers (Gilbert, Jaime, and Mario Hernandez), which became a landmark of the alternative comics movement.

Other notable Fantagraphics creators and series include Peter Bagge's Hate, Daniel Clowes's Eightball (and its collection Ghost World), Jim Woodring's Frank, and work by Chris Ware, Joe Sacco, and many others.

Classic reprints

Fantagraphics became a leading publisher of archival reprints of classic newspaper comic strips and cartoonists, including complete editions of Charles M. Schulz's Peanuts, Walt Kelly's Pogo, George Herriman's Krazy Kat, E. C. Segar's Popeye, and the complete Carl Barks and Floyd Gottfredson Disney libraries.

The Comics Journal

The Comics Journal, published by Fantagraphics from 1976, was one of the first magazines devoted to serious comics criticism. Its combative editorial voice and extensive interviews made it an influential, if controversial, presence in the field.
