Fidesz – Hungarian Civic Alliance (Hungarian: Fidesz – Magyar Polgári Szövetség) is a national-conservative political party in Hungary. Founded in 1988 as the Federation of Young Democrats (Fiatal Demokraták Szövetsége, from which the acronym Fidesz derives), it has been led for most of its history by Viktor Orbán and has governed Hungary since 2010.

History

Fidesz began as an anti-communist youth movement with an age limit of 35, playing a prominent role in Hungary's transition to democracy; Orbán's 1989 speech demanding the withdrawal of Soviet troops made him nationally known. After entering parliament in 1990 as a liberal party, Fidesz shifted to the right in the mid-1990s. It first governed in coalition from 1998 to 2002, then spent eight years in opposition.

In government since 2010

Fidesz won a two-thirds parliamentary supermajority in 2010 in alliance with the Christian Democratic People's Party (KDNP), and repeated its victories in 2014, 2018 and 2022. The Orbán governments adopted a new constitution, centralised media ownership among government allies, and pursued "illiberal democracy", drawing criticism and rule-of-law proceedings from the European Union. Fidesz was a member of the European People's Party until 2021, when its MEPs left the EPP group; in 2024 it co-founded the Patriots for Europe group in the European Parliament.

Ideology and support

The party combines national conservatism, economic nationalism, opposition to immigration and socially conservative positions with state intervention in parts of the economy. Its electoral strongholds are rural areas and small towns.
