City of Westminster

The City of Westminster is a London borough with city status in Greater London, England. It is the site of the United Kingdom's Houses of Parliament and much of the British government. Westminster, a historic area within the borough, was the historic center of government; the borough lies on the north bank of the River Thames, west of the ancient City of London.

Districts and landmarks

The borough contains many of London's most famous districts and attractions: the West End with its theatres, Soho, Mayfair, Belgravia, Pimlico, Marylebone, Paddington, and much of the royal parks including Hyde Park and Green Park. Landmarks include the Palace of Westminster, Westminster Abbey, Buckingham Palace, Trafalgar Square, Piccadilly Circus, Oxford Street, Leicester Square, and the National Gallery. Westminster Abbey and the Palace of Westminster form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Westminster grew up around the royal palace and abbey founded in the 11th century; Edward the Confessor built his palace beside the abbey he rebuilt, and the site became the permanent seat of royal government. The modern borough was created in 1965 by merging the former metropolitan boroughs of Westminster, Paddington, and St Marylebone.
