Law enforcement in South Africa

Law enforcement in South Africa is principally the responsibility of the South African Police Service (SAPS), the national police force, which operates under the Department of Police. Municipal police services in the largest cities and specialised units complement its work.

Structure

The SAPS was established after the end of apartheid by amalgamating the police forces of the former republic and the homelands into a single service under the 1995 South African Police Service Act. It is organised nationally and provincially, with more than a thousand police stations across the country. Specialised directorates include the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation, known as the Hawks, which handles organised crime and corruption.

Oversight and challenges

Civilian oversight is exercised through bodies such as the Independent Police Investigative Directorate, which investigates deaths and serious complaints involving police. South African policing faces persistent challenges including high levels of violent crime, resource constraints, and public concern over corruption and brutality, issues that remain central to national debate.
