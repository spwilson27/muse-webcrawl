Chromism

A chromic material before and after exposure to a stimulus

In chemistry, chromism is a process that induces a change, often reversible, in the colours of compounds. In most cases, chromism is based on a change in the electron states of molecules, especially the π- or d-electron state, induced by an external stimulus which can alter the electron density of substances. Many natural and synthetic compounds exhibit chromism in response to stimuli such as light, heat, electricity, pressure, or chemical environment.

Types

Photochromism is a reversible colour change induced by light. It is exploited in photochromic lenses, which darken in sunlight, and in optical data storage research. Well-known photochromic materials include spiropyrans, diarylethenes, and silver halide glasses.

Thermochromism is a colour change induced by temperature. Examples include mood rings, battery test strips, and thermochromic inks used on packaging. Liquid crystals and leuco dyes are common thermochromic systems.

Electrochromism is a colour change caused by an electric current or applied voltage. It is used in smart windows that modulate light transmission, in rear-view mirrors that dim automatically, and in low-power displays. Tungsten oxide is a widely studied electrochromic material.

Solvatochromism is a colour change depending on the polarity of the solvent in which a compound is dissolved, arising from differential solvation of the ground and excited states of the chromophore. Ionochromism refers to colour changes triggered by ions, and halochromism to those triggered by changes in pH, as seen in acid–base indicators.

Mechanochromism describes colour changes under mechanical force such as grinding, stretching, or pressure, while piezochromism is the specific case of change under pressure. Magnetochromism is a change induced by a magnetic field.

Applications

Chromic materials find applications in sensors, smart windows, anti-counterfeiting inks, novelty items, rewritable media, and displays. Because the colour change is often reversible and requires no continuous power input in some systems, chromic materials are of interest for energy-efficient building glazing and indicators.
