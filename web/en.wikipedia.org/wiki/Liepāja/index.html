Liepāja

Liepāja (pronounced [liepaːja]; German: Libau) is a state city in western Latvia, on the shore of the Baltic Sea. With a population of roughly 68,000, it is Latvia's third-largest city and an important ice-free port. Its motto is "the city where the wind is born" (pilsēta, kurā piedzimst vējš), a reference to the sea breeze and to the Latvian rock music scene that flourished there.

History

Originating as a Curonian fishing village, Liepāja was chartered in 1625 and grew as a trading port under the Duchy of Courland and later the Russian Empire, which developed the naval base of Karosta in the 1890s. During the Latvian War of Independence it briefly served as the seat of the provisional government in 1919. The city suffered heavily under the Soviet and Nazi occupations, including the massacre of its Jewish population in 1941.

Today

Modern Liepāja combines port industries, metalworking and tourism. Landmarks include the Art Nouveau and wooden architecture of the centre, the Holy Trinity Cathedral with one of the world's largest mechanical organs, the Karosta prison and breakwater, and the Great Amber (Lielais dzintars) concert hall, opened in 2015.
