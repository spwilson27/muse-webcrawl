Reluctance motor

TypeElectric motor
Torque sourceVariation of magnetic reluctance
VariantsSynchronous reluctance, switched reluctance, variable reluctance stepper

A reluctance motor is a type of electric motor that induces non-permanent magnetic poles on the ferromagnetic rotor. The rotor carries no windings or permanent magnets; torque is generated because the rotor moves to align itself with the stator's magnetic field in the position of minimum magnetic reluctance.

Types

The main types include the synchronous reluctance motor, which runs at synchronous speed and requires a variable-frequency drive; the switched reluctance motor, in which stator windings are energised in sequence by electronic switching; and the variable reluctance stepper motor, used for precise position control.

Advantages and disadvantages

Reluctance motors have simple, robust rotors without magnets or brushes, making them cheap and able to operate at high temperatures and speeds. Their disadvantages include torque ripple, acoustic noise (particularly in switched reluctance designs), and the need for electronic control.
