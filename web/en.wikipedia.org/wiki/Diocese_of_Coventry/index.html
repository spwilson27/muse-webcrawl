The Diocese of Coventry is a Church of England diocese covering Coventry, most of Warwickshire, and parts of the West Midlands in England. Its seat is Coventry Cathedral, dedicated to St Michael, and the diocesan bishop is the Bishop of Coventry.

History

A medieval diocese of Coventry and Lichfield existed from the early Middle Ages, but the modern diocese was created in 1918 from part of the Diocese of Worcester, reflecting Coventry's growth as an industrial city. Coventry had previously been home to a cathedral — St Mary's Priory — destroyed at the Dissolution of the Monasteries, and to St Michael's parish church, which became the cathedral in 1918.

The cathedral

St Michael's was largely destroyed in the Coventry Blitz of 14 November 1940. The ruins were preserved as a memorial, and the new cathedral, designed by Basil Spence and consecrated in 1962, was built alongside, becoming internationally known for its ministry of reconciliation.

Organisation

The diocese is divided into archdeaconries and deaneries, and is served by the Bishop of Coventry and the suffragan Bishop of Warwick. It lies within the Province of Canterbury.
