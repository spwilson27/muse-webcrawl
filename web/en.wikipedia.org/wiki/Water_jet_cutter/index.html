Water jet cutter

A water jet cutter, or waterjet, cuts materials using a very high-pressure jet of water, often mixed with abrasive.

A water jet cutter, also known as a water jet or waterjet, is an industrial tool capable of cutting a wide variety of materials using an extremely high-pressure jet of water, or a mixture of water and an abrasive substance. The term "abrasive jet" refers specifically to systems using a water–abrasive mixture to cut hard materials such as metal, stone or glass, while a pure water jet without abrasive cuts softer materials such as food, rubber and foam.

Principle

A high-pressure pump drives water at pressures of up to about 600 MPa (90,000 psi) through a tiny jewel orifice, producing a jet travelling at several times the speed of sound. For abrasive cutting, garnet abrasive is drawn into the jet in a mixing chamber. The jet erodes the material along the cut line, producing a narrow kerf with no heat-affected zone, since the process is essentially one of supersonic erosion rather than melting or burning.

Applications

Waterjets are used in machine shops and manufacturing to cut metals, composites, stone, tile, glass and food products. Advantages include the ability to cut without heating or hardening the material, minimal mechanical stress, and the capacity to cut intricate shapes under CNC control. Limitations include relatively slow cutting in thick hard materials and operating costs from abrasives and high-pressure components.
