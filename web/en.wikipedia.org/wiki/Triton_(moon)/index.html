Triton (moon)

Discovery: William Lassell, 1846
Diameter: 2,710 km
Orbital period: 5.877 days (retrograde)
Surface temp.: ~38 K

Triton is the largest natural satellite of the planet Neptune and the seventh-largest moon in the Solar System. It was discovered by the English astronomer William Lassell on 10 October 1846, just 17 days after Neptune itself. Triton is unique among large moons in orbiting its planet in a retrograde direction, which strongly suggests it is a captured Kuiper belt object similar to Pluto.

Surface and atmosphere

Triton's surface is remarkably young and geologically active. Voyager 2, the only spacecraft to visit (in 1989), imaged the "cantaloupe terrain" of the western hemisphere, vast plains of water-ice volcanism, and nitrogen geysers erupting plumes up to 8 km high that leave dark streaks across the southern polar cap. A thin nitrogen atmosphere gives a surface pressure of about 1.4 Pa, and the surface temperature of about 38 K makes Triton one of the coldest measured bodies in the Solar System.

Future

Because of tidal deceleration, Triton's orbit is slowly decaying; in roughly 3.6 billion years it is expected to pass within Neptune's Roche limit and be torn apart, forming a ring system.
