Homicide

For other uses, see Homicide (disambiguation). Homicide is the killing of one human being by another.


Homicide investigation at a crime scene.

Homicide is an act in which an individual causes the death of another human being. Not all homicide is criminal: killings committed with legal justification, such as in self-defence or in lawful war, are classified as non-criminal, while killings lacking justification or excuse are criminal homicides.

Types

Criminal homicide

Murder is the unlawful killing of another with malice aforethought — an intent to kill, intent to cause grievous bodily harm, or extreme recklessness. Many jurisdictions divide murder into degrees: first-degree (premeditated) and second-degree. Manslaughter covers unlawful killings without malice: voluntary manslaughter involves killings in the "heat of passion" after provocation, while involuntary manslaughter arises from negligence or an unlawful act not amounting to murder. Some jurisdictions also recognise felony murder, where a death during a serious felony is treated as murder regardless of intent.

Non-criminal homicide

Justifiable homicide includes killings in self-defence or defence of others, by law-enforcement officers in the line of duty, and lawful executions. Excusable homicide covers killings by accident or misadventure without negligence. Killing in war by combatants acting lawfully is generally not treated as murder under the laws of armed conflict.

Statistics

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime estimated around 458,000 intentional homicides worldwide in 2021, a rate of about 5.8 per 100,000 people. Rates vary enormously by region: the Americas and parts of Africa record the highest rates, while Europe and East Asia record the lowest. Firearms account for a large share of homicides in the Americas. Men make up roughly 80 percent of victims and the great majority of perpetrators globally.

Investigation and law

Homicide investigation typically involves forensic pathology (autopsy to determine cause and manner of death), crime-scene examination, and detective work. Legal definitions and punishments vary across jurisdictions, from life imprisonment to (in a minority of countries) the death penalty.
