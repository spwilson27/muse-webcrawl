The Little Mermaid (Danish: Den lille Havfrue) is a bronze statue by Edvard Eriksen, depicting a mermaid becoming human. Sitting on a rock by the waterside at the Langelinie promenade in Copenhagen, Denmark, it is based on the 1837 fairy tale by Hans Christian Andersen. Small and unimposing — only 1.25 metres (4.1 ft) tall — the statue is nevertheless an icon of Copenhagen and one of Denmark's most famous tourist attractions, drawing over a million visitors a year.

History

The statue was commissioned in 1909 by Carl Jacobsen, founder of the Carlsberg brewery, who had been captivated by a ballet performance of Andersen's tale at the Royal Danish Theatre. Jacobsen asked the sculptor Edvard Eriksen to create the figure; the ballerina Ellen Price, who had danced the title role, declined to model nude, so Eriksen's wife Eline modelled for the body while Price's likeness informed the head. The statue was unveiled on 23 August 1913.

Vandalism

The statue has been repeatedly damaged and defaced, so often that it is sometimes described as Denmark's most abused artwork. Her head was sawn off in 1964 and never recovered (a new head was cast from the original moulds); an arm was sawn off in 1984; in 1998 the head was removed again but returned; in 2003 the statue was knocked off its rock with explosives; and it has repeatedly been doused in paint, including in 2017 and 2020. Each time, the statue has been restored and returned to her rock.

Copies and travels

Authorised and unauthorised copies stand in several countries. In 2010 the original statue travelled for the first (and so far only) time, exhibited in the Danish pavilion at Expo 2010 in Shanghai, while a video installation by Ai Weiwei occupied her place in Copenhagen harbour.
