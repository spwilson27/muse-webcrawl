Bartolomeo Berucci

Bartolomeo Berucci was an Italian goldsmith and medalist of the Renaissance period, active in Florence in the sixteenth century. He is remembered primarily for his association with the circle of Benvenuto Cellini and for a notorious dispute over a valuable gem that became entangled with the patronage politics of the Medici court.

Life and career

Berucci was among the goldsmiths working in Florence during the reign of Duke Cosimo I de' Medici, when the city was a leading centre of luxury metalwork, hardstone carving, and jewelled objects for princely collections. Goldsmiths of the period supplied the court with engraved gems, medals, and plate, and rivalry between workshops was intense. Cellini's autobiography, the Vita, mentions several contemporary Florentine goldsmiths and jewellers with whom he competed for commissions.

Berucci's name survives in archival records of the Florentine court concerning precious stones and goldsmiths' work for the Medici guardaroba. Like many artisan figures of the period, few securely attributed works by his hand are known today, and his reputation rests mainly on documentary traces.
