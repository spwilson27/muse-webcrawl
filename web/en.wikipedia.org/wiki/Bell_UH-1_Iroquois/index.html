Bell UH-1 Iroquois

The Bell UH-1 Iroquois is a utility military helicopter designed and produced by the American manufacturer Bell Helicopter. Universally known by its nickname "Huey" — derived from its original designation HU-1 — it became the iconic aircraft of the Vietnam War and one of the most successful helicopters in history, with more than 16,000 built. It first flew on 20 October 1956 and entered U.S. Army service in 1959.

Development and design

The UH-1 was developed to meet a U.S. Army requirement for a medical evacuation and general utility helicopter powered by the new Lycoming T53 turboshaft engine. It is a single-rotor helicopter with a two-bladed main rotor and tail rotor, skid landing gear, and room for a pilot, crew and up to about 14 troops in later stretched variants such as the UH-1H. The twin-engine UH-1N Twin Huey followed for the U.S. Air Force and Marine Corps.

Operational history

In Vietnam the Huey transformed warfare: it served in troop transport, medical evacuation ("Dustoff"), command and control, and gunship roles, and some 7,000 were deployed there, of which more than 3,000 were lost. Armed variants led directly to the purpose-built AH-1 Cobra attack helicopter. UH-1s have served with the armed forces of dozens of countries and in countless civilian roles — firefighting, search and rescue, and transport. Although largely retired from U.S. front-line service in favour of the UH-60 Black Hawk, upgraded Hueys such as the UH-1Y Venom remain in production and service.
