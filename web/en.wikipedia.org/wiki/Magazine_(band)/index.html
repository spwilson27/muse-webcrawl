Magazine are an English rock band formed in 1977 in Manchester by vocalist Howard Devoto after he left the punk band Buzzcocks, seeking a more ambitious, keyboard- and guitar-driven sound. Magazine were among the originators of post-punk, and their debut single "Shot by Both Sides" (1978) and first album Real Life set out a template of literate lyrics, angular guitar from John McGeoch, and prominent synthesizers from Dave Formula.

History

The band released the albums Secondhand Daylight (1979), The Correct Use of Soap (1980) — containing their cover of Sly Stone's "Thank You (Falettinme Be Mice Elf Agin)" — and Magic, Murder and the Weather (1981) before Devoto disbanded the group in 1981. McGeoch went on to acclaim with Siouxsie and the Banshees; bassist Barry Adamson later joined Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds. Magazine reunited in 2009 for concerts and released the comeback album No Thyself in 2011. Their influence on post-punk and alternative rock, including bands such as Radiohead and Pulp, is widely acknowledged.
