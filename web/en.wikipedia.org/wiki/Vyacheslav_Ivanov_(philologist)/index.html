Vyacheslav Ivanov (philologist)

Vyacheslav Vsevolodovich Ivanov (21 August 1929 – 7 October 2017) was a Russian philologist, linguist, and semiotician, one of the leading figures of the Tartu–Moscow Semiotic School and a specialist in Indo-European studies.

Life and work

The son of writer Vsevolod Ivanov, he studied at Moscow State University and became a prominent scholar of Hittite and Tocharian, historical linguistics, and poetics. With Vladimir Toporov he reconstructed aspects of Balto-Slavic mythology, and with Tamaz Gamkrelidze he co-authored the monumental Indo-European and the Indo-Europeans (1984), which proposed the controversial glottalic theory and an Armenian-highland homeland for Proto-Indo-European.

Career

Dismissed from Moscow University in 1958 for publicly supporting Boris Pasternak and Roman Jakobson, Ivanov was later rehabilitated, directed the Russian State Library, and from 1989 taught at UCLA while remaining active in Russian academic life. He was a member of the Russian Academy of Sciences and a deputy in the final Soviet Congress of People's Deputies.
