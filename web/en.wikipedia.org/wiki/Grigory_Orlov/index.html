Grigory Orlov

Count Grigory Grigoryevich Orlov (17 October 1734 – 24 April 1783) was a Russian statesman, soldier, and the favourite of Empress Catherine the Great. With his brothers, he organized the coup d'état of 1762 that deposed Peter III and placed Catherine on the throne, and he remained her close adviser and lover for more than a decade.

Early life and the coup

Orlov was born in the village of Lyutkino in Tver Governorate, the second of five brothers of a noble family. He served in the Seven Years' War, was wounded at Zorndorf, and rose in the Guards in St. Petersburg, where he became Catherine's lover around 1759–1760. In June 1762 the Orlov brothers' conspiracy overthrew Peter III, who died shortly afterward in custody.

Favourite of Catherine II

Rewarded with the title of count, estates, and high office, Orlov was Catherine's chief favourite until about 1772. He took part in the Legislative Commission of 1767, directed the response to the Moscow plague riot of 1771 (for which a triumphal arch was raised to him at Tsarskoye Selo), and led Russian forces in the early part of the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774. He and Catherine had a son, Alexei Bobrinsky.

Later life

Eclipsed by Grigory Potemkin after 1774, Orlov travelled abroad, married his young cousin Ekaterina Zinovieva in 1777, and fell into mental decline after her death in 1782. He died in Moscow in 1783. The town of Orlov and the Orlov diamond — once set in the Imperial Sceptre — preserve his name.
