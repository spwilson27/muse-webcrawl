Bartholomew I of Constantinople

Bartholomew I (born Dimitrios Arhondonis, 29 February 1940) is the 270th Archbishop of Constantinople and Ecumenical Patriarch, the spiritual leader of the world's Eastern Orthodox Christians, since 2 November 1991. He is the longest-serving Ecumenical Patriarch in the office's history.

Life and ministry

Born on the island of Imbros to a Greek family, Bartholomew studied theology at Halki Seminary in Istanbul, then in Rome, Bossey, and Munich, earning a doctorate in canon law. Fluent in many languages, he served as Metropolitan of Philadelphia and of Chalcedon before his election in 1991. Based at the Patriarchal Church of St George in Istanbul's Fener district, he operates under Turkish restrictions including the closed Halki Seminary, whose reopening he has long sought.

Initiatives

Nicknamed the "Green Patriarch", Bartholomew made environmental protection a religious duty, convening symposia on water and climate and influencing Orthodox and wider Christian ecological teaching. He has championed interfaith dialogue with Roman Catholics, Jews, and Muslims, met successive popes in Jerusalem and Istanbul, and in 2019 granted autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine — a decision that ruptured relations with the Moscow Patriarchate.
