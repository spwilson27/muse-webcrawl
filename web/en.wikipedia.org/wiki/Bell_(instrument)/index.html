Bell (instrument)

Bell

ClassificationPercussion (struck idiophone)
MaterialUsually bell metal (bronze)

A bell is a directly struck idiophone percussion instrument. Most bells have the shape of a hollow cup that resonates when struck, with its sides forming an acoustic resonator. Bells are usually cast from bell metal, a hard bronze alloy, and are sounded by an internal clapper or an external hammer.

History

Bells were made in China more than 3,500 years ago and are among the oldest musical instruments. In Europe, the craft of bell founding developed through the medieval period, and bells became central to church life, marking the hours, calling worshippers, and warning of danger. Famous bells include the Liberty Bell in Philadelphia, Big Ben (the Great Bell of the Palace of Westminster, London), and the Tsar Bell in Moscow, the largest bell in the world.

Acoustics and tuning

A bell's sound is inharmonic: its strongest partials include the hum tone (an octave below the strike tone), the prime, the tierce (a minor third) and the quint. Bell founders tune bells by removing metal on a lathe to bring the principal partials into harmonic relationship. In Western churches sets of bells are rung in methods of change ringing, while tuned sets played from a keyboard form a carillon. The study of bells is called campanology.
