Prince Philip Movement

The Prince Philip Movement is a religious movement followed by some villagers of Yaohnanen and nearby villages on Tanna island in Vanuatu, who believe that Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, consort of Queen Elizabeth II, was a divine being, the pale-skinned son of a mountain spirit and brother of the culture hero John Frum.

Origins

The belief developed in the mid-20th century, interacting with the John Frum cargo movement. According to village tradition, the spirit's son travelled across the seas to marry a powerful lady, a story later attached to Prince Philip after his marriage to Elizabeth and reinforced by the royal couple's visit to the New Hebrides in 1974, during which residents saw Philip in his white naval uniform.

Relations with the Prince

Village leaders exchanged gifts and photographs with Prince Philip over decades, and a delegation met him at Windsor Castle in 2007 for the Channel 4 programme Meet the Natives. Signed photographs sent by the prince were treasured relics in Yaohnanen.

After Philip's death

Prince Philip died on 9 April 2021. The movement mourned him formally, and followers discussed whether the belief would pass to his son, then Prince Charles (King Charles III), who had himself visited Vanuatu and been accorded a chiefly title.
