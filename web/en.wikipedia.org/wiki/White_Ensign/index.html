White Ensign

The White Ensign, at one time called the St George's Ensign, is an ensign flown on British Royal Navy ships and shore establishments. It consists of a red St George's Cross on a white field, with the Union Jack in the upper canton.

History

The Royal Navy originally used three squadronal ensigns — red, white and blue — corresponding to the squadrons into which the fleet was divided. In 1864 an Admiralty order reorganised the system: the White Ensign was reserved to the Royal Navy, the Red Ensign to merchant ships, and the Blue Ensign to government vessels. Since then the White Ensign has been the distinctive flag of the Royal Navy.

Use

The White Ensign is flown by Royal Navy warships, submarines and commissioned shore establishments, and by vessels of Commonwealth navies that have adopted it, sometimes with their own national emblem added. It may also be flown by yachts of the Royal Yacht Squadron, the only civilian vessels permitted to do so. Because of its association with the Navy, flying the White Ensign without authority is an offence under British law.
