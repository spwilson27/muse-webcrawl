Sariah

Sariah is a figure in the Book of Mormon, a scripture of the Latter Day Saint movement. She is the wife of the prophet Lehi and the mother of Laman, Lemuel, Sam, Nephi, Jacob, and Joseph.

Narrative

According to the Book of Mormon, Sariah left Jerusalem with her family around 600 BCE, following Lehi's prophetic warning of the city's destruction. In the wilderness she complained against Lehi when her sons returned late from an errand to obtain the brass plates, fearing they had died, but rejoiced at their safe return. She later travelled with the family across the ocean to the Americas. One notable episode recounts her suffering during a storm at sea when she fell ill and nearly died.

Significance

Sariah is one of only a few named women in the Book of Mormon and is frequently cited in Latter-day Saint teaching as an example of faith and maternal devotion. Her name does not appear in the Bible; some researchers have noted similar names attested in ancient Egyptian and Hebrew sources, while critics regard the name as an invention of Joseph Smith.
