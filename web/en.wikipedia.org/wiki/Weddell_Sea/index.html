Weddell Sea

The Weddell Sea is part of the Southern Ocean off the coast of Antarctica, bounded by the Antarctic Peninsula to the west and Coats Land to the east. It is named after the Scottish sailor James Weddell, who reached far south into the sea in 1823. Much of its southern shore is the edge of the Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf.

Oceanography

The Weddell Sea is a major site of deep-water formation: cold, salty water produced under its ice shelves sinks to form Antarctic Bottom Water, which spreads through the world ocean. Its waters are among the clearest of any sea, and its sea ice is notoriously heavy — the sea was the scene of Ernest Shackleton's Endurance expedition (1914–1917), in which the ship was trapped and crushed by ice.

Wildlife

The sea supports Weddell seals (named after it), emperor and Adélie penguins, orcas, and rich krill populations, and has been proposed as a marine protected area.
