Transverse Ranges

The Transverse Ranges are a group of mountain ranges of Southern California, in the Pacific Coast Ranges physiographic region. They are unusual among American ranges in running predominantly east–west rather than north–south, a result of the bend in the San Andreas Fault system where the Pacific Plate grinds against the North American Plate.

Geography

The ranges extend from the Santa Ynez Mountains in Santa Barbara County eastward through the San Gabriel and San Bernardino Mountains to the Little San Bernardino Mountains east of the Coachella Valley, and include the Santa Monica Mountains, the Topatopa Mountains and the Channel Islands' northern chain. The highest peak is Mount San Gorgonio (3,506 m / 11,503 ft) in the San Bernardino Mountains. The Los Angeles Basin lies to the south of the ranges, and the Mojave Desert to the north.

Geology

The ranges are being actively uplifted by compressional forces at the restraining bend of the San Andreas Fault, where the fault's strike changes and the plates converge. Faults such as the San Andreas, San Jacinto and Sierra Madre faults accommodate the shortening, producing frequent earthquakes and some of the steepest mountain fronts in the United States. The ranges create distinctive ecological transitions between coastal, valley, montane and desert environments within short distances.
