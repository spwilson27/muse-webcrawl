Abydos King List

The Abydos King List, also known as the Abydos Table, is a list of the names of 76 kings of ancient Egypt carved on a wall of the Temple of Seti I at Abydos. Seti I and his son, the future Ramesses II, are shown making offerings to the cartouches of their royal ancestors. Together with the Karnak, Saqqara and Turin lists, it is one of the principal sources for reconstructing the sequence of pharaohs, especially for the earliest dynasties.

Contents

The list proceeds in historical order through the Old Kingdom, the First Intermediate and Middle Kingdom periods, and the New Kingdom up to Seti I himself. For the Old Kingdom in particular it provides a fuller and more reliable sequence than the Karnak list, preserving names in order for dynasties 1 through 8 and confirming the succession of the Twelfth Dynasty.

Omissions

The list is a ritual and ideological document, not a neutral chronicle: it systematically omits rulers Seti's court considered illegitimate. Absent are the Hyksos kings of the Second Intermediate Period, Hatshepsut, and the Amarna pharaohs — Akhenaten, Smenkhkare, Tutankhamun and Ay — who are passed over as the succession jumps from Amenhotep III to Horemheb. These omissions themselves are historically informative about New Kingdom royal ideology, but mean the list must be cross-checked against the Turin Canon and Manetho's history.

Discovery and significance

The temple was cleared and the list recorded in the 19th century; a second, fragmentary copy of a similar list from the neighbouring temple of Ramesses II is now in the British Museum. Because the Abydos list gives cartouche spellings and an unbroken order for many early kings, it remains a cornerstone for Egyptological chronology and for identifying royal names found elsewhere.
