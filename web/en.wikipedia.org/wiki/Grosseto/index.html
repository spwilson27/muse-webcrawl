Grosseto (Italian pronunciation: [ɡrosˈseːto]) is a city and comune in the central Italian region of Tuscany, the capital of the province of Grosseto. The city lies 14 kilometres (9 miles) from the Tyrrhenian Sea, in the Maremma, at the centre of an alluvial plain on the Ombrone river. It is the most populous city in the Maremma, with around 82,000 inhabitants.

History

The origins of Grosseto can be traced to the High Middle Ages. It was first mentioned in 803 as a fiefdom of the Counts Aldobrandeschi. The city grew in importance following the decline of the nearby Etruscan-Roman centre of Roselle (Rusellae), whose bishopric was transferred to Grosseto in 1138. In 1336 the city finally submitted to Siena after resisting numerous sieges, and it later passed to Florence and the Grand Duchy of Tuscany under the Medici.

The surrounding Maremma marshes were notorious for malaria well into the modern era. Extensive reclamation (bonifica) projects under the Lorraine grand dukes and again in the 20th century drained the marshes and transformed the region into fertile farmland. The city was heavily damaged by Allied bombing in 1943.

Main sights

Grosseto's historic centre is enclosed by well-preserved hexagonal Medici walls (1574–1593). Notable buildings include the Romanesque-Gothic Cathedral of San Lorenzo (begun 1294), the Church of San Francesco with a crucifix attributed to Duccio di Buoninsegna, the Palazzo Aldobrandeschi in Piazza Dante, and the Museo archeologico e d'arte della Maremma, which houses Etruscan artefacts from Roselle and Vetulonia.

Transport

The city is served by the Pisa–Rome railway and by Grosseto Airport, a joint civil and military airfield that hosts the Italian Air Force's 4th Wing with Eurofighter Typhoons.
