Nuclear weapon

Mushroom cloud from a nuclear test.

A nuclear weapon is an explosive device that derives its destructive force from nuclear reactions, either fission or a combination of fission and fusion. Even small nuclear weapons are vastly more powerful than conventional explosives; a single weapon can destroy a city.

History

Nuclear weapons were first developed by the United States during the Manhattan Project in World War II and used against the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. The Soviet Union tested its first weapon in 1949, beginning an arms race that produced tens of thousands of warheads.

Types and effects

Fission weapons ("atomic bombs") split heavy nuclei such as uranium-235 or plutonium-239; thermonuclear weapons ("hydrogen bombs") add a fusion stage. Their effects include a blast wave, thermal radiation, ionising radiation and radioactive fallout. Nine states are believed to possess nuclear weapons today, and treaties such as the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty seek to limit their spread.
