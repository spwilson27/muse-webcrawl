Japanese Shorthorn

Japanese Shorthorn
Country of origin: Japan
Use: Beef
Traits: Red coat, medium frame

The Japanese Shorthorn (Japanese: 日本短角種, Nihon Tankaku-shu) is a breed of small beef cattle developed in northern Japan. It is one of the four native Japanese Wagyu breeds, alongside the Japanese Black, Japanese Brown and Japanese Polled.

History

The breed was developed during the Meiji era in the Tōhoku region of Honshu, principally in Iwate, Aomori and Akita prefectures. Local cattle were cross-bred with imported Shorthorn stock, and later with Devon and other breeds, to improve size and milk production for draught and dairy use. The breed was officially recognised in 1957.

Characteristics

Japanese Shorthorn cattle are solid red in colour, of medium size, and noted for hardiness in the cold, snowy climate of northern Japan. They are traditionally raised by a summer-grazing and winter-housing system, grazing on mountain pasture in summer. Their beef is leaner than that of the Japanese Black, with a coarser marbling but a strong flavour.

Population

The breed is the second most numerous of the Wagyu breeds but still accounts for a small share of Japan's beef production, concentrated in Iwate Prefecture.
