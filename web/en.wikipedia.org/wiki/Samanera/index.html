Samanera

A sāmaṇera (Pali); Sanskrit śrāmaṇera, is a novice male monastic in the Buddhist tradition. A sāmaṇerī (Sanskrit śrāmaṇerī) is a novice nun. A samanera has taken the "going forth" (pabbajja) ordination but not yet the higher ordination (upasampada) that makes a fully ordained monk (bhikkhu) or nun (bhikkhuni).

Ordination and precepts

A candidate as young as seven years old—traditionally old enough to "scare away crows"—may be admitted as a samanera with the permission of his parents. Instead of the 227 rules of the bhikkhu Patimokkha, the novice observes the Ten Precepts: abstaining from killing, stealing, sexual activity, lying, intoxicants, eating after noon, entertainment and adornment, luxurious beds, and handling money. The novice shaves his head, wears the ochre or saffron robes, and lives under the guidance of a preceptor.

Role

Samaneras may remain novices for life or proceed to full ordination at the age of twenty. In countries such as Thailand and Myanmar, temporary ordination as a novice is a common rite of passage for schoolboys during holidays, regarded as a way of making merit for oneself and one's family. The Buddha's own son, Rāhula, is traditionally regarded as the first samanera.
