Ross-shire

Ross-shire (Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Rois) is a historic county in the Scottish Highlands, bounded by Sutherland to the north, Cromartyshire and Inverness-shire to the south, and the North Sea and Moray Firth to the east. Its county town was Dingwall. In 1889 Ross-shire was merged with Cromartyshire to form the county of Ross and Cromarty, which functioned as a local government area until 1975. Today the area lies within the Highland council area, and "Ross and Cromarty" survives as a lieutenancy area and registration county.

Geography

Ross-shire divides into two contrasting parts. Easter Ross, in the east, is a low-lying agricultural region along the shores of the Moray, Cromarty and Dornoch firths, containing the towns of Tain, Invergordon, Alness and Dingwall, and the Black Isle peninsula (part of which historically lay in Ross-shire). Wester Ross, by contrast, is a rugged mountainous district on the Atlantic coast, famed for peaks such as Liathach and Beinn Eighe in Torridon, deep sea lochs including Loch Maree and Loch Torridon, and scattered settlements such as Gairloch, Ullapool (founded as a fishing village in 1788) and Applecross.

History

The province of Ross emerged as a mormaerdom and later earldom in the medieval period, ruled successively by the O'Beolan earls and, after the forfeiture of the Lord of the Isles' earldom of Ross in 1476, passing through various hands. The sheriffdom of Ross developed around the royal burgh of Dingwall and the burgh of Tain, one of Scotland's oldest royal burghs. The county was strongly affected by the Highland Clearances in the 19th century, particularly in Strathconon and the west, and crofting agitation in the 1880s contributed to the Crofters' Holdings (Scotland) Act 1886.

The Local Government (Scotland) Act 1889 united Ross-shire with Cromartyshire — which consisted of a number of detached enclaves within Ross-shire plus the town of Cromarty — creating Ross and Cromarty. The Ross and Cromarty county council was based in Dingwall. Local government reorganisation in 1975 transferred the area to the Highland Region, and since 1996 it has formed part of the unitary Highland Council.

Culture

Ross-shire retains a significant Gaelic-speaking heritage, especially in Wester Ross. The county's name is preserved in the Ross-shire place-names, the former Ross-shire constituency, and in sporting institutions such as the shinty club at Rosemarkie and Fortrose on the Black Isle.
