Eadbald of Kent

Eadbald (Old English: Ēadbald) was King of Kent from 616 until his death in 640. He was the son of King Æthelberht and his wife Bertha, a daughter of the Merovingian king Charibert.

Eadbald of Kent
Reign: 616-640
Predecessor: Æthelberht
Successor: Eorcenberht
Father: Æthelberht of Kent

Reign

On succeeding his father, the first Christian king in England, Eadbald initially rejected Christianity and, according to Bede, married his stepmother, causing the Canterbury mission to falter. He later converted, influenced by Archbishop Laurentius or Justus.

Later reign

Eadbald married Emma, a Frankish princess, in 618. He founded churches, helped restore Canterbury's influence, and his sister Æthelburg married King Edwin of Northumbria, spreading Christianity north. He was buried in St Mary's Church, Canterbury.
