Cortona

Cortona (Italian: [korˈtoːna]) is a town and comune in the province of Arezzo, in Tuscany, Italy. It is the main cultural and artistic centre of the Val di Chiana after Arezzo. The town, situated on a hill about 600 metres above sea level overlooking Lake Trasimeno, has a population of about 22,000.

History

Cortona was one of the twelve cities of the Etruscan League (known to the Etruscans as Curtun). Substantial stretches of its Cyclopean walls survive, as does the Etruscan Academy museum (MAEC) with finds including the famous bronze chandelier. The city came under Roman influence, was sacked and rebuilt several times, and in the Middle Ages became a free commune before being sold to Florence in 1411. The Renaissance artist Luca Signorelli and the Baroque painter Pietro da Cortona were born here, as was the composer Francesco Mancini. The painter Gino Severini was also a native.

In popular culture

Cortona gained international fame as the setting of Frances Mayes's memoir Under the Tuscan Sun (1996) and its 2003 film adaptation, drawing many visitors to the town and its Bramasole villa.

Main sights

  • Piazza della Repubblica and the Palazzo Comunale (13th century)
  • Cortona Cathedral (Duomo)
  • MAEC – Museum of the Etruscan Academy and of the City of Cortona
  • Girifalco Fortress, above the town
  • The Hermitage Le Celle, founded by St Francis of Assisi
