Swiss National Park

The Swiss National Park (German: Schweizerischer Nationalpark; Romansh: Parc Naziunal Svizzer) is located in the western Rhaetian Alps, in the canton of Graubünden in eastern Switzerland, within the Engadin valley region. Founded on 1 August 1914, it was Switzerland's first national park and for a century its only one. It covers about 170 km² (some 65 square miles), making it the largest protected area in the country, and forms part of the UNESCO Val Müstair–Parc Naziunal biosphere reserve.

Protection and wildlife

The park is classified as a strict nature reserve (IUCN Category Ia): visitors must stay on the roughly 80 km of marked trails, and hunting, fishing, camping and off-trail walking are prohibited, so that ecosystems develop without human interference. Wildlife includes red deer, chamois, ibex (reintroduced in the 1920s), marmots, and golden eagles; bearded vultures were reintroduced from 1991, and brown bears from Italy's Trentino population occasionally wander into the park. The park centre is in Zernez, where a visitor centre opened in 2008.
