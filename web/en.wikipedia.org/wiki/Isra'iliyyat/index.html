Isra'iliyyat

Isra'iliyyat (Arabic: إسرائيليات, "of the Israelites") is a genre in Quranic exegesis (tafsir) and Islamic literature consisting of narratives and traditions derived from Jewish (and sometimes Christian) sources — the Bible, rabbinic literature and folklore — that were incorporated into Islamic accounts of the prophets and biblical figures.

Early tafsir works drew on Jewish and Christian lore.

History

Such material entered Islamic tradition through converts and storytellers such as Ka'b al-Ahbar and Wahb ibn Munabbih, and appears in early commentaries including that of al-Tabari. A hadith permitting the narration of stories from the Children of Israel was cited in its favour. Later scholars, notably Ibn Kathir and Ibn Taymiyyah, criticised Isra'iliyyat as unreliable, sorting such reports into those consistent with Islam, those contradicting it, and those neither confirmed nor denied.
