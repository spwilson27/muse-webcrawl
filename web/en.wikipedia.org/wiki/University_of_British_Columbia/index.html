University of British Columbia

The University of British Columbia (UBC) is a public research university with campuses in Vancouver and Kelowna (UBC Okanagan), British Columbia, Canada. Founded in 1908, it is British Columbia's oldest university and one of Canada's leading research institutions, consistently ranked among the top three universities in the country and the top fifty worldwide. Its motto is Tuum Est ("It is yours").

History

UBC was established by an act of the provincial legislature in 1908 and opened in 1915 at a temporary site in Fairview before moving to its Point Grey campus in 1925. After the Second World War, enrolment expanded dramatically with returning veterans. In 2005 the university acquired the Okanagan campus in Kelowna, formerly Okanagan University College.

Academics and research

The university comprises numerous faculties, including Arts, Science, Applied Science (engineering), Medicine, Law (the Peter A. Allard School of Law), and the Sauder School of Business. UBC's research output is among the largest in Canada, supported by facilities such as TRIUMF, Canada's national particle accelerator centre, located on the Vancouver campus. Alumni and faculty include eight Nobel laureates and three prime ministers of Canada, including Justin Trudeau.

Campus

The Vancouver campus occupies the western tip of the Point Grey peninsula, surrounded by forest and ocean beaches, about 10 km from downtown Vancouver. Notable features include the Museum of Anthropology, designed by Arthur Erickson, the Beaty Biodiversity Museum, and the Chan Centre for the Performing Arts. The university's athletic teams, the Thunderbirds, compete in U Sports; UBC also fields teams in the NAIA.
