Flat-four engine

A flat-four engine, also known as a horizontally opposed-four or boxer-four, is a four-cylinder piston engine with two banks of two cylinders lying on opposite sides of a common crankshaft, the cylinders firing in a pattern reminiscent of a boxer's punches.

Characteristics

The configuration gives a low center of gravity and good primary balance, with a distinctive exhaust note in some applications.

Applications

Famous users include the original Volkswagen Beetle and Type 2, many Porsche models (356, 912, and the modern 718), and most Subaru cars, which pair flat-four engines with all-wheel drive. Aircraft engines by Lycoming and Continental also use the layout at larger displacements.
