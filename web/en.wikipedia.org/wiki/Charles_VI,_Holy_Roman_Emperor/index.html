Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor

Charles VI (German: Karl VI.; 1 October 1685 – 20 October 1740) was Holy Roman Emperor and ruler of the Austrian Habsburg monarchy from 1711 until his death, succeeding his elder brother Joseph I. He first claimed the Spanish throne as Charles III during the War of the Spanish Succession. His reign is best remembered for the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713, intended to secure the Habsburg succession for his daughter Maria Theresa.

Spanish succession claim

The second son of Emperor Leopold I, Charles was proclaimed king of Spain in Vienna in 1703 and landed in Spain during the War of the Spanish Succession, holding a court at Barcelona. Allied defeats and the death of Joseph I in 1711 — which made Charles emperor and raised British fears of a reunited Habsburg empire — led the allies to abandon his claim; the Peace of Utrecht (1713) recognized Philip V in Spain while awarding Austria the Spanish Netherlands, Milan, Naples, and Sardinia.

Reign

As emperor, Charles fought successful wars against the Ottoman Empire under Prince Eugene of Savoy (1716–1718) but lost ground in the War of the Polish Succession (1733–1735) and the Russo-Austrian–Turkish War (1737–1739). Domestically he promoted trade through the Ostend Company, founded the imperial court library's great home in Vienna, and was a devoted patron of music — himself a capable harpsichordist and composer.

Pragmatic Sanction and death

Having no surviving son, Charles spent much of his reign securing European acceptance of the Pragmatic Sanction, which allowed his daughter Maria Theresa to inherit the Habsburg lands undivided. He died suddenly in October 1740; despite the guarantees, Prussia's invasion of Silesia that December opened the War of the Austrian Succession.
