Transitions lenses

Transitions is a brand of photochromic lenses produced by Transitions Optical, a company founded in 1990 as a joint venture between PPG Industries and Essilor. The lenses darken on exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light and return to clear indoors, providing eyeglasses that double as sunglasses.

Technology

Photochromic lenses contain molecules — historically silver halide crystals in glass, and in modern plastic lenses organic photochromic dyes such as naphthopyrans — that undergo a reversible chemical change when struck by UV radiation, absorbing visible light. Successive generations of Transitions lenses (marketed as Signature, XTRActive, and Gen 8/Gen S) have improved darkening speed, darkness outdoors, clarity indoors, and performance behind car windshields, which block much UV; XTRActive variants also respond to visible light. The lenses block 100% of UVA and UVB rays when activated.

History and market

Photochromic glass was developed by Corning in the 1960s. Transitions Optical, based in Pinellas Park, Florida, commercialized plastic photochromic lenses from 1991. In 2014 Essilor acquired PPG's stake to take full ownership. Competing photochromic brands include Zeiss PhotoFusion and Hoya Sensity.
