Mental status examination
AbbreviationMSE
FieldPsychiatry, clinical psychology, neurology
PurposeStructured assessment of psychological functioning

The mental status examination (MSE) is an important part of the clinical assessment process in psychiatry and neurology. It is a structured way of observing and describing a patient's psychological functioning at a given point in time, under the domains of appearance, attitude, behaviour, mood and affect, speech, thought process, thought content, perception, cognition, insight and judgment.

Components

The examiner records appearance and behaviour (including eye contact and psychomotor activity), speech (rate, rhythm and volume), mood (the patient's stated feeling) and affect (the observed emotional expression), the form and content of thought, perceptual disturbances such as hallucinations, and cognitive functions including orientation, attention, memory and abstract reasoning.

Use

The MSE complements the history of the presenting illness and past psychiatric history, and changes in the MSE over time help clinicians track the course of illness and response to treatment. Brief cognitive screens such as the Mini-Mental State Examination are related but distinct instruments.
