Crunchie is a brand of chocolate bar manufactured by Cadbury, consisting of a honeycomb toffee (known in several countries as sponge toffee or "hokey pokey") centre coated in milk chocolate. It was launched in 1929 by J. S. Fry & Sons of Bristol, England, and became part of the Cadbury range when Fry's merged into Cadbury in the following decades.

Crunchie is sold widely in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa. For many years its advertising centred on the slogan "Get that Friday feeling," with memorable rail-themed commercials in which a steamroller of excitement marked the end of the working week.

History

Fry's introduced the Crunchie in 1929. The bar's honeycomb sugar-glass centre — made by reacting sugar syrup with bicarbonate of soda to trap gas as it sets — gave it a novel texture among chocolate bars of the era. Production for the UK long centred on the Somerdale factory at Keynsham (the historic Fry's plant); after Somerdale's closure in 2011, UK Crunchie production moved to the Cadbury plant in Skarbimierz, Poland, while manufacture for Australasian markets takes place in Australia and New Zealand (and the brand was carried in the US at times under arrangements with Hershey — where a similar product is not standard in the Hershey line).

Production

The sponge toffee is cooked as a slab, cut into fingers, and enrobed in milk chocolate. Air holes and texture are controlled by the soda reaction and syrup composition; the finished centre shatters crisply and dissolves with a characteristic "crunch," then melts. Bars are produced in standard, multipack, bite-size, and sharing formats, and the honeycomb pieces also appear in ice cream and dessert products under licence.

Variants and related products

Variants have included limited-edition flavours and seasonal shapes (such as Easter eggs with honeycomb pieces), Crunchie Rocks (clusters) in some markets, and ice cream bars. Competing or sibling honeycomb bars include Nestlé's Violet Crumble in Australia — a long-running trans-Tasman rivalry in New Zealand — and various own-brand "honeycomb crunch" bars.

Advertising

The "Friday feeling" campaign, running from the 1980s in the UK, is among British advertising's most durable confectionery properties, featuring the song of the same phrase and themes of weekend release. In New Zealand, a famous 1980s–90s campaign promised "the great train robbery" style adventure with the slogan "get a taste of the Crunchie feeling," and the Cadbury "Crunchie train" became a Kiwi pop-culture reference.
