Guwahati

Guwahati (Assamese: গুৱাহাটী)
State: Assam, India
Location: Banks of the Brahmaputra River
Population (2011): about 1 million

Guwahati is the largest city in the Indian state of Assam and the largest metropolis in northeastern India. It lies on the south bank of the Brahmaputra River and serves as the region's principal commercial, educational, and transport hub — often called the "Gateway to North-East India". The state capital, Dispur, is a suburb within the city.

History

Guwahati is an ancient city, identified with Pragjyotishpura, the legendary capital of King Naraka and of the Kamarupa kingdom. The Ahom kingdom fought repeatedly with Mughal forces for control of the city, which changed hands several times in the 17th century; it passed to the British after the Treaty of Yandabo in 1826. It became the capital of Assam in 1874 (moving to Shillong later that year) and again hosted the capital after Indian independence until Dispur was designated in 1973.

Geography and climate

The city is ringed by hills and wetlands, including the Deepor Beel Ramsar site. It has a humid subtropical climate with heavy monsoon rainfall from June to September, and the Brahmaputra frequently floods surrounding lowlands. Saraighat Bridge (1962) and the New Saraighat Bridge link the city to North Guwahati across the river.

Culture and landmarks

The Kamakhya Temple on Nilachal Hill is one of Hinduism's most important Shakti shrines, drawing pilgrims for the Ambubachi Mela. Umananda Temple stands on Peacock Island, reputedly the world's smallest inhabited river island. Institutions include IIT Guwahati, Gauhati University, and Assam Medical College; the city also hosts the Assam State Zoo and a major tea auction centre reflecting Assam's tea industry.
