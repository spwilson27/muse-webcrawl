Sindhudurg Fort (Marathi: सिंधुदुर्ग किल्ला, "sea fort") is a historical sea fort located on a rocky island in the Arabian Sea, about one kilometre off the coast of Malvan in Sindhudurg district, Maharashtra, India. It was built by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, founder of the Maratha Empire, as the headquarters of his nascent navy and remains one of the most celebrated monuments of Maratha military architecture.

Construction

Construction began on 25 November 1664 on the island of Kurte and was completed in about three years, under the supervision of the architect Hiroji Indulkar. Tradition records that more than one hundred Portuguese architects and three thousand labourers worked on the project, and that the foundation stones were laid in molten lead to resist the sea. The fort's walls, roughly 4 km in perimeter and over 9 m high in places, enclose some 48 acres with 42 large bastions. Well concealed entrances — including a tortuous main gate invisible at a distance — and freshwater wells within the sea-girt walls were notable features.

History

Sindhudurg served as the chief naval base of the Maratha fleet, supporting Shivaji's challenge to the Siddis of Janjira, the Portuguese and the English East India Company along the Konkan coast. It passed through the hands of the Kolhapur branch of the Maratha royal family, and was ceded to the British in 1812. Inside the fort stand temples dedicated to Shivaji (unusually depicting him without a beard), Hanuman and Bhavani.

Tourism

The fort is reached by ferry from Malvan and is a major tourist attraction of the Konkan; the surrounding waters are also known for scuba diving and snorkelling. The district of Sindhudurg, created in 1981, is named after the fort.
