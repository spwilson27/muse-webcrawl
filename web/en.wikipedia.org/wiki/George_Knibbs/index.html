George Knibbs

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sir George Handley Knibbs
Born: 13 June 1858, Sydney, New South Wales
Died: 30 May 1929 (aged 70), Camberwell, Melbourne, Victoria
Occupation: Statistician, public servant
Known for: First Commonwealth Statistician of Australia; first director of the Commonwealth Institute of Science and Industry

Sir George Handley Knibbs CMG (13 June 1858 – 30 May 1929) was an Australian statistician and public servant who, as the first Commonwealth Statistician (1906–1921), established the Commonwealth Bureau of Census and Statistics and laid the foundations of official statistics in Australia. He later became the first director of the Commonwealth Institute of Science and Industry, the forerunner of the CSIRO.

Early life and career in New South Wales

Knibbs was born in Sydney and trained as a licensed surveyor, joining the New South Wales public service in the lands and survey departments. Largely self-educated in mathematics, physics, and philosophy, he built a formidable reputation as a polymath: he lectured in geodesy and astronomy at the University of Sydney, served as president of the Royal Society of New South Wales, and wrote on topics from demography to the philosophy of science. He also acted as NSW government statistician and helped organise the statistical work for the state's 1901 census.

Commonwealth Statistician

Following Federation, Knibbs was appointed in 1906 to head the new Commonwealth Bureau of Census and Statistics under the Census and Statistics Act 1905. He designed and directed the first national census of Australia in 1911 and built up the Bureau's year books and statistical series — population, trade, production, finance — into a system praised internationally for its rigour and coherence. His massive official report on the 1911 census (many volumes) remains a monument of early demography. He was appointed CMG in 1911 and knighted in 1923.

Science administration and later work

In 1921 Knibbs left the Bureau to become the first director of the Commonwealth Institute of Science and Industry, the body that evolved through the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (1926) into today's CSIRO. He wrote widely, including The Shadow of the World's Future (1928), an early and anxious analysis of world population growth and resource limits. He died in Melbourne in 1929.
