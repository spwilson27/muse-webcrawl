Aaro Olavi Pajari (17 July 1897 – 14 October 1949) was a Finnish major general and one of the most celebrated Finnish field commanders of the Second World War. He is chiefly remembered for leading the counterattack that won the Battle of Tolvajärvi in December 1939, the first major Finnish victory of the Winter War, and he was one of only a handful of officers to receive the Mannerheim Cross twice nominated honours — he was appointed a Knight of the Mannerheim Cross, 2nd class, in 1941.

Early life and career

Pajari was born in Asikkala, the son of a parish vicar. As a young man he fought in the Finnish Civil War of 1918 on the White side and remained in the army, graduating from the Cadet School and the War College. During the interwar years he held regimental and staff appointments, becoming known as a demanding trainer and a student of offensive tactics.

Winter War

When the Soviet Union attacked Finland on 30 November 1939, Lieutenant Colonel Pajari commanded Infantry Regiment 16 north of Lake Ladoga. Retreating Soviet pressure threatened the whole Ladoga Karelia front, and Pajari was given command of a detached battle group ("Group Pajari"). On 12 December 1939 his troops counterattacked across the ice of Lake Tolvajärvi and in days of confused fighting destroyed the Soviet 139th Rifle Division piecemeal, advancing as far as Ägläjärvi by Christmas. The victory — won by ski troops against a much larger motorized force — was a sensation in Finland and abroad, and it secured the northern flank of the Finnish IV Corps.

Continuation and Lapland Wars

Promoted to major general, Pajari commanded the 18th Division in the Continuation War (1941–1944), leading it in the Finnish offensive into East Karelia and the capture of Petrozavodsk, and later the 3rd Division. In the Lapland War of 1944–45 his troops fought the retreating German forces in northern Finland, including the amphibious landing at Tornio. He was twice decorated with the Mannerheim Cross nominations being among the few commanders so honoured, and he remained in service after the war as an inspector of infantry.

Death and legacy

Pajari died of a heart attack on 14 October 1949 while on an inspection tour in Kokkola. His name remains closely associated in Finnish memory with bold offensive leadership and the Winter War victory at Tolvajärvi.
