Phloroglucinol

Phloroglucinol
IUPAC name: Benzene-1,3,5-triol
Formula: C6H6O3
Molar mass: 126.11 g/mol
Appearance: White to beige crystalline solid
Melting point: about 219 °C
CAS number: 108-73-6

Phloroglucinol is an organic compound with the formula C6H3(OH)3, systematically benzene-1,3,5-triol. It is one of three isomeric benzenetriols (the others being hydroxyquinol and pyrogallol) and exists in equilibrium with a triketo tautomer. It takes its name from phloretin, from which it was first obtained by hydrolysis.

Occurrence and synthesis

Phloroglucinol and its derivatives (phlorotannins and hyperforin among them) occur widely in plants, algae and bacteria, where the ring is assembled by polyketide synthases. Industrially it has been prepared by several routes from 1,3,5-trinitrobenzene or triaminobenzene, and it is a product of the alkaline fusion of resorcinol-type materials.

Reactions and uses

The compound behaves both as a phenol and, through its keto tautomer, as a 1,3,5-triketone, undergoing electrophilic substitution, condensation with aldehydes, and characteristic colour reactions. It is used as a coupling agent in the manufacture of azo dyes and pharmaceuticals, as an antispasmodic drug (for smooth-muscle spasm of the digestive and urinary tracts), and in the laboratory: with hydrochloric acid it gives a red colour with lignin (the Wiesner test), and in the Tollens-type phloroglucinol test it detects pentoses. It is also a building block in the synthesis of explosives such as TATB.
