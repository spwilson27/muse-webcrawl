Welwyn Garden City is a town in Hertfordshire, England, about 20 miles (32 km) north of London. It was the second garden city in England, founded in 1920 by Sir Ebenezer Howard, the originator of the garden city movement, following Letchworth Garden City. Its name is usually abbreviated by residents to "Welwyn" (pronounced "well-in"), although it is a distinct settlement from the older village of Welwyn.

History

Howard purchased 1,458 acres of land in 1919 and formed Welwyn Garden City Limited in 1920. The town was planned by Louis de Soissons, with a neo-Georgian town centre arranged around the wide, tree-lined boulevard of Parkway and Howardsgate, and residential neighbourhoods separated from industry, all to be surrounded by a permanent agricultural green belt. In 1948 the town was designated a New Town under the New Towns Act 1946, and development passed to a development corporation.

Economy and transport

Early industries included the Shredded Wheat factory (whose silos are a landmark), Roche Pharmaceuticals and the Welgar garden district. The town centre's Campus West, Gosling Sports Park and the Howard Centre serve the wider area. Welwyn Garden City station, on the East Coast Main Line, provides fast trains to London King's Cross, and the town lies just west of the A1(M) motorway.
