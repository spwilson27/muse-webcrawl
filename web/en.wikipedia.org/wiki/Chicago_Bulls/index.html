The Chicago Bulls are an American professional basketball team based in Chicago, Illinois, competing in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Eastern Conference Central Division. Founded in 1966, the team plays its home games at the United Center.

The Jordan era

The Bulls are best known for their dynasty of the 1990s, led by Michael Jordan, Scottie Pippen, and coach Phil Jackson. The team won six NBA championships in two "three-peats" (1991–1993 and 1996–1998). The 1995–96 Bulls set a then-record 72–10 regular season, and Jordan's final shot in the 1998 Finals — the "Last Shot" — sealed the sixth title and cemented the team's global popularity, which helped internationalise the NBA.

Later years

After the dynasty broke up in 1998, the Bulls rebuilt around Derrick Rose, who in 2011 became the youngest NBA MVP in history, before injuries curtailed his prime. The franchise has retired four numbers: Jerry Sloan (4), Bob Love (10), Michael Jordan (23), and Scottie Pippen (33). Its mascot, Benny the Bull, is one of the most recognisable in sports.
