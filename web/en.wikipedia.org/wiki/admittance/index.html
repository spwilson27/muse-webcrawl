Admittance

In electrical engineering, admittance is a measure of how easily a circuit or device will allow a current to flow. It is defined as the reciprocal of impedance, analogous to how conductance and resistance are reciprocals. The SI unit of admittance is the siemens (symbol S). Oliver Heaviside coined the term admittance in December 1887.

Definition

Admittance Y is defined as the ratio of the phasor current through a component to the phasor voltage across it: Y = I/V = 1/Z, where Z is the impedance. Like impedance, admittance is generally a complex quantity: Y = G + jB, where G is the conductance (the real part) and B is the susceptance (the imaginary part).

Conversion from impedance

Given an impedance Z = R + jX, the admittance is Y = 1/(R + jX). Separating real and imaginary parts gives G = R/(R2 + X2) and B = −X/(R2 + X2). The magnitude of admittance is |Y| = 1/|Z|.

Use in circuit analysis

Admittances are particularly convenient for analysing parallel circuits, because the admittances of parallel branches simply add, whereas impedances add directly only in series. The nodal analysis method in network theory is formulated in terms of an admittance matrix relating node voltages to injected currents, and admittance parameters (Y-parameters) are one of the standard two-port network descriptions.
