San Quentin State Prison, renamed the San Quentin Rehabilitation Center in 2023, is a California state prison located on Point Quentin in Marin County, overlooking San Francisco Bay. Opened in 1852, it is the oldest prison in California and was for decades the site of the state's only gas chamber and death row for male condemned inmates. The facility occupies some 275 acres of valuable waterfront land north of San Francisco.

History

California's first prisoners were held aboard the prison ship Waban anchored near Point Quentin. The state purchased the point in 1852, and inmates built the first cell blocks from bricks made on site; the oldest dungeon-like cells date to 1854. San Quentin long held California's execution chamber: hangings gave way to the gas chamber in 1938, and after lethal injection was adopted, the gas chamber was converted for that method. Notable inmates over the decades included stagecoach robber Black Bart, murderer Charles Manson, and serial killer Richard Ramirez.

Death row and reforms

San Quentin historically housed the largest death row in the United States. California has not carried out an execution since 2006, and in 2019 the governor imposed a moratorium and ordered the execution chamber closed. Condemned men were subsequently transferred to other prisons as the state moved to dissolve death row. In 2023 Governor Gavin Newsom announced a plan to transform San Quentin into a rehabilitation-focused facility modeled partly on Scandinavian correctional systems, accompanied by the name change.

In popular culture

Johnny Cash performed famous concerts at San Quentin in 1959 and 1969; the 1969 performance produced the hit live album At San Quentin and the song "San Quentin". The prison has been referenced in numerous films, novels, and songs.
