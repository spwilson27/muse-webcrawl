1975 Haicheng earthquake

Date4 February 1975
LocationHaicheng, Liaoning, China
Magnitude7.3 M
Deaths~1,328

The 1975 Haicheng earthquake struck Haicheng, Liaoning, China, on 4 February 1975 with a magnitude of 7.3. It is famous as one of the few major earthquakes to have been successfully predicted in advance, allowing a large-scale evacuation hours before the shock.

Prediction and evacuation

Seismologists noted a sequence of foreshocks and other anomalies. On the morning of 4 February, authorities ordered residents to leave buildings and stay outdoors despite the winter cold. The main shock struck at 19:36 local time, devastating the city but killing far fewer people than expected.

Impact

Some 90% of structures in Haicheng were damaged or destroyed, yet deaths were around 1,328 — remarkably low for a quake of this size in a populated area. The successful prediction was celebrated, though the failure to predict the devastating Tangshan earthquake the following year showed the limits of the methods used.
