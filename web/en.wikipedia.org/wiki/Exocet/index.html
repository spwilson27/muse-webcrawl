Exocet

The Exocet (French for "flying fish") is a French-built anti-ship missile whose various versions can be launched from surface vessels, submarines, helicopters, and fixed-wing aircraft. Developed by Nord Aviation (later Aérospatiale, now MBDA) beginning in 1967, it entered service in the mid-1970s. The missile flies a sea-skimming trajectory at subsonic speed (about Mach 0.9) and homes on its target with active radar in the terminal phase; its warhead weighs 165 kg.

Combat use

The Exocet gained worldwide notoriety during the Falklands War of 1982, when Argentine Navy Super Étendard aircraft fired air-launched AM39 Exocets that sank the British destroyer HMS Sheffield and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor; a land-launched MM38 also damaged HMS Glamorgan. During the Iran–Iraq War, an Iraqi Mirage F1 fired two Exocets at the US frigate USS Stark in 1987, killing 37 American sailors.

Variants

Principal variants are the ship-launched MM38 and MM40 (the Block 3 version has a range exceeding 180 km and a turbojet engine), the air-launched AM39, and the submarine-launched SM39. The Exocet has been exported to dozens of navies and remains in production with MBDA.
