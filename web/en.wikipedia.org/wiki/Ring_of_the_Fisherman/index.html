Ring of the Fisherman

The Ring of the Fisherman (Latin: Anulus Piscatoris; Italian: Anello Piscatorio), also known as the Piscatory Ring, is an official part of the regalia worn by the Pope, who in Catholic tradition is the successor of Saint Peter, a fisherman by trade.

Description and history

The ring features a bas-relief of Peter fishing from a boat or casting a net, with the reigning pope's name around it. Mentioned as early as a letter of Clement IV in 1265, it was historically used to seal papal briefs, the ring being pressed into wax as the pope's signet.

Ceremonial use

A new ring is made for each pope and presented at his inauguration. On a pope's death or resignation, the ring is traditionally defaced or broken by the Camerlengo to prevent forgery during the sede vacante. Benedict XVI's ring was defaced with a cross after his 2013 resignation rather than fully destroyed. Modern popes have worn simplified versions for daily use.
