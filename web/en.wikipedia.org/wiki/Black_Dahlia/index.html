Elizabeth Short (July 29, 1924 – c. January 15, 1947), known posthumously as the Black Dahlia, was an American woman found murdered in the Leimert Park neighborhood of Los Angeles, California, on January 15, 1947. Her case became notorious because of the graphic nature of the crime, in which her body had been severely mutilated and bisected at the waist. The case remains unsolved and is one of the oldest open cases in Los Angeles history.

Background

Short was born in Boston and raised in Medford, Massachusetts. She moved between Florida and California as a young adult, working waitressing jobs and aspiring to an acting career in Hollywood. She lived in various rooming houses and hotels in Los Angeles during the last months of her life.

Discovery and investigation

A local resident discovered Short's body in a vacant lot on Norton Avenue. The Los Angeles Police Department investigation became one of the largest in the city's history, with hundreds of officers assigned and numerous suspects interviewed; dozens of people falsely confessed. The press dubbed her the "Black Dahlia", a name said to derive from a contemporary film noir, The Blue Dahlia (1946), and from the black clothing she reportedly favoured. A person claiming to be the killer mailed items belonging to Short to a newspaper, but no suspect was ever charged.

Legacy

The case has inspired numerous books, films and television dramatisations, including James Ellroy's 1987 novel The Black Dahlia and its 2006 film adaptation. Retired detective Steve Hodel has publicly argued that his father, George Hodel — a person of interest in the original investigation — was the killer, a claim that remains contested.
