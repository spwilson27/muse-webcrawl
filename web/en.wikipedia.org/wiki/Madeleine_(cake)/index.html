Madeleine (cake)

A madeleine is a small sponge cake from the Lorraine region of northeastern France, distinguished by its distinctive shell-like shape, obtained from being baked in pans with shell-shaped depressions. Madeleines are made from a génoise batter, traditionally flavoured with lemon zest, and have a light, buttery crumb with a characteristic hump on the back.

History

The madeleine is associated with the towns of Commercy and Liverdun in Lorraine. Legend links it to a young servant named Madeleine Paulmier, said to have baked the cakes for Stanisław Leszczyński, the exiled king of Poland and duke of Lorraine, in the eighteenth century. The cakes were popularised in Paris in the nineteenth century, sold at railway stations and by the bakery trade.

In literature

The madeleine is famous in world literature through Marcel Proust's novel In Search of Lost Time, in which the taste of a madeleine dipped in tea triggers the narrator's flood of involuntary memory. The phrase "Proustian moment" descends from this episode.

Preparation

The batter of flour, eggs, sugar, melted butter and lemon zest is rested and chilled, then piped into buttered madeleine moulds and baked briefly at high heat, producing the cakes' golden colour and signature bump.
