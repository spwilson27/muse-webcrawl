Amoghasiddhi

Amoghasiddhi (Sanskrit: अमोघसिद्धि, "he whose accomplishment is not in vain") is one of the Five Tathāgatas (Five Wisdom Buddhas) of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. He presides over the north and embodies the wisdom of all-accomplishing action (kṛtyānuṣṭhāna-jñāna), the transmutation of envy and jealousy into effective, fearless activity. His name signifies unerring success in spiritual practice and the removal of obstacles.

Iconography

Amoghasiddhi is depicted green in colour, seated in the north of the mandala of the Five Buddhas. His right hand is raised in abhaya mudra, the gesture of fearlessness, peace and protection, while his left hand rests in his lap in meditation, sometimes holding an alms bowl. His emblem is the viśvavajra, the crossed double thunderbolt, and he is associated with the karma (action) family, the element of air or wind, the season of summer, and the aggregate (skandha) of formations (saṃskāra). His seed syllable is Āḥ and his consort is Green Tārā (Śyāmatārā).

Role in practice

In Vajrayana visualisation and mandala practice, Amoghasiddhi's wisdom overcomes envy and paralysing fear, converting them into the capacity for effective action. He figures prominently in the mandalas of tantras such as the Tattvasaṃgraha, and his image appears in temple art across Tibet, Nepal, China and Japan, where he is known as Fukūjōju Nyorai.
