Frontier thesis

The Frontier thesis, or Turner's thesis, is the argument advanced by historian Frederick Jackson Turner in his 1893 essay "The Significance of the Frontier in American History" that the moving American frontier — free land and westward expansion — shaped American democracy, individualism and character.


Author: Frederick Jackson Turner
Presented: 1893, Chicago

Argument

Turner held that the frontier repeatedly stripped away European customs and fostered self-reliance, egalitarianism and innovation, and he noted the 1890 census declaration that a frontier line no longer existed.

Criticism

Later historians criticised the thesis for neglecting Native American displacement, slavery, women, cities and the environment, and for romanticising conquest. The New Western History reframed the West as a place of conquest and continuity rather than a closing frontier. Related: Fugitive Slave Act of 1850.
