Group analysis

Group analysis is a method of group psychotherapy originated by S. H. Foulkes in the 1940s in Britain. It combines psychoanalytic insights with an understanding of social systems, holding that the individual is fundamentally social and that psychological disturbance is best understood—and treated—within the network of relationships in which it arises.

Principles

In group-analytic therapy, a group of usually seven to nine members meets regularly with a conductor (therapist). Communication in the group is understood at several levels at once: the individual, the interpersonal, and the group-as-a-whole. Foulkes's key concepts include the group matrix—the web of communication and relationships shared by the members—resonance, mirroring, and exchange. Symptoms are interpreted as distorted forms of communication that can be examined and transformed within the group.

Development

Foulkes founded the Group Analytic Society in London in 1952, and the Institute of Group Analysis in 1971. Group analysis is practiced widely in Europe in clinical, organizational, and educational settings, and large-group formats have been used to explore social and cultural conflicts.
