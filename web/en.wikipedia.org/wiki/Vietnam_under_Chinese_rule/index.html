Vietnam under Chinese rule

Vietnam under Chinese rule refers to the roughly thousand years (111 BC – AD 939), divided by historians into four periods of domination, during which northern Vietnam was governed by successive Chinese dynasties, interspersed with brief independent interludes.

Periods

Han general Zhao Tuo's kingdom of Nanyue was annexed by Han China in 111 BC. The Trưng Sisters' revolt (AD 40–43) briefly restored independence; later risings included Lý Bí's Early Lý dynasty (544–602). Chinese administration brought Confucian bureaucracy, Chinese characters (chữ Hán), Mahayana Buddhism and agricultural techniques, while Vietnamese identity and language persisted.

Independence

Ngô Quyền's victory over the Southern Han fleet at the Battle of Bạch Đằng (938), using tide-driven stakes, ended permanent Chinese rule; he proclaimed himself king in 939. A later Ming occupation (1407–1427) was expelled by Lê Lợi's Lam Sơn uprising.

Legacy

The millennium left deep Sinitic influence on Vietnamese government, law, literature and vocabulary, coexisting with a strong tradition of resistance celebrated in Vietnamese historiography.
