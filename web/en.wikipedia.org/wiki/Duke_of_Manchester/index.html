Duke of Manchester is a title in the Peerage of Great Britain, created in 1719 for Charles Montagu, 4th Earl of Manchester. The dukes descend from the Montagu family, whose historic seat was Kimbolton Castle in Huntingdonshire (now Cambridgeshire), where Catherine of Aragon died in 1536.

History

The first Duke served as ambassador to Venice and France. Later holders included George Montagu, 4th Duke, a politician and diplomat, and William Montagu, 5th Duke, Governor of Jamaica. The 8th Duke's wife, Consuelo Yznaga, was a Cuban-American heiress whose marriage exemplified the era of American heiresses marrying into the British aristocracy. Consuelo Vanderbilt married the 9th Duke in 1895, a notably unhappy union that ended in divorce.

The current holder is Alexander Montagu, 13th Duke of Manchester, who has lived largely outside Britain and has been the subject of considerable controversy.
