The Domus Augustana is the modern name given to the residential wing of the vast Palace of Domitian on the Palatine Hill in Rome. Completed in AD 92, it served as a principal imperial residence for several centuries. The name is modern: in antiquity the whole palace was known as the Domus Augustana or Palatium — the origin of the word "palace".

Description

The residence descends the south-eastern slope of the Palatine in terraces facing the Circus Maximus. Its most striking surviving feature is a great sunken peristyle garden, lying some 15 metres below the upper court, with rooms arranged around it on two levels and a curved, island-like exedra in its fountain court. Upper floors contained reception rooms, while lower levels housed private apartments thought to include the emperor's own quarters.

History

Built by the emperor Domitian as part of his palace complex (with the ceremonial Domus Flavia and a stadium-shaped garden), the residence remained in use by later emperors. Its brick-faced concrete structures still dominate the Palatine's southern side, though marble revetments and furnishings were stripped in later centuries.
