Energy-efficient driving

Energy-efficient driving comprises techniques that drivers can use to reduce the fuel consumption of their vehicles. Often called ecodriving, these techniques include gentle acceleration, anticipating traffic flow to avoid unnecessary braking, maintaining steady speeds, reducing idling, keeping tyres properly inflated and removing excess weight and aerodynamic drag.

Real-time fuel-economy feedback helps drivers adopt efficient habits.

Techniques

Key techniques include driving at moderate, steady speeds (fuel consumption rises steeply at high speed due to aerodynamic drag), shifting up early in manual-transmission vehicles, coasting to decelerate rather than braking late, and planning routes to avoid congestion. Advanced techniques used by "hypermilers" can achieve consumption well below official ratings, though some extreme methods raise safety concerns.

Benefits

Ecodriving typically saves 5–15% of fuel for average drivers, reducing costs and carbon dioxide emissions, and is promoted by governments and motoring organisations as a low-cost climate measure.
