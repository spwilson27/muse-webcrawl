Agrobiodiversity

Agrobiodiversity, or agricultural biodiversity, is the variety and variability of animals, plants, and microorganisms that are used directly or indirectly for food and agriculture, including crops, livestock, forestry, and fisheries. It encompasses diversity within species (genetic resources), between species, and of the ecosystems in which agriculture operates, as well as the diversity of supporting organisms such as pollinators, soil microbes, and natural enemies of pests. Agrobiodiversity is a subset of biodiversity shaped by thousands of years of human selection and management.

Components

  • Crop diversity: the roughly 7,000 plant species cultivated by humans, their landraces, modern varieties, and wild relatives—though just a handful (rice, wheat, maize, potato) supply most calories.
  • Livestock diversity: thousands of breeds of cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, chickens, and other domesticated animals adapted to local conditions.
  • Aquatic and forest species used for food and livelihoods.
  • Associated biodiversity: pollinators, soil organisms, and other species that support production through ecosystem services.

Importance

Genetic diversity within crops and livestock underpins breeding for yield, disease resistance, and climate adaptation. Diverse farming systems can be more resilient to pests, diseases, and weather extremes, and dietary diversity improves nutrition. The Irish potato famine of the 1840s is a classic demonstration of the danger of genetic uniformity: reliance on a few closely related potato varieties allowed late blight to devastate the crop.

Threats and conservation

The FAO has estimated that some 75% of crop genetic diversity was lost during the twentieth century as farmers adopted uniform modern varieties, and many livestock breeds are at risk of extinction. Conservation strategies include ex situ storage in seed banks and gene banks—most famously the Svalbard Global Seed Vault—and in situ/on-farm conservation of landraces and traditional farming systems. International frameworks include the Convention on Biological Diversity and the FAO's International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture (2001).
