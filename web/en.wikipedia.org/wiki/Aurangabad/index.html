Aurangabad is a city in the Indian state of Maharashtra. It is the administrative headquarters of Aurangabad district and is the largest city in the Marathwada region. In 2023 the Government of Maharashtra approved renaming the city Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, in honour of Sambhaji, the second Chhatrapati of the Maratha Empire. Located on a hilly upland terrain in the Deccan plateau, Aurangabad is a major industrial and educational centre and a tourism hub, visited for the nearby Ajanta and Ellora caves, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

History

The settlement was founded in 1610 by Malik Ambar, the Ethiopian-born prime minister of the Ahmadnagar Sultanate, under the name Khadki. Under his successor Fateh Khan it was called Fatehnagar. In 1653 the Mughal prince Aurangzeb was appointed viceroy of the Deccan and made the city his capital, renaming it Aurangabad. Aurangzeb later built a wall around the city and several gates—the city came to be known as the "City of Gates"—and his son Azam Shah commissioned the Bibi Ka Maqbara (1678), a mausoleum for Aurangzeb's wife Dilras Banu Begum often compared to the Taj Mahal. After Aurangzeb's death in 1707 the city passed to the Nizam of Hyderabad, under whose dominion it remained until Hyderabad's accession to India in 1948. In 1956 it became part of Maharashtra.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the Kham River in a drought-prone region of the Deccan, at an elevation of about 513 metres. Its economy rests on manufacturing (automotive and pharmaceutical plants in the Waluj and Shendra industrial areas), cotton and silk textiles—including the traditional Himroo shawls and Paithani sarees—and tourism. Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Marathwada University, founded in 1958, anchors its higher-education sector.

Landmarks

  • Bibi Ka Maqbara – 17th-century Mughal mausoleum.
  • Aurangabad Caves – Buddhist rock-cut caves on the city's northern edge.
  • Panchakki – a water mill powered by an artificial waterfall, part of a Sufi shrine complex.
  • Daulatabad Fort – the formidable hill fortress northwest of the city.
  • Grishneshwar Temple near Ellora, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas.
