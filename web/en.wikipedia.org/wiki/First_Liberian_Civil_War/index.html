First Liberian Civil War

The First Liberian Civil War was the first of two civil wars in the West African nation of Liberia, lasting from 1989 to 1997. The conflict killed around 200,000 people and eventually led to the involvement of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and the United Nations. The peace did not last, and in 1999 the Second Liberian Civil War broke out.

Background

In 1980, Master Sergeant Samuel Doe seized power in a coup, ending the long dominance of the Americo-Liberian elite but inaugurating a repressive regime favouring his Krahn ethnic group. Doe survived an attempted coup by Thomas Quiwonkpa in 1985, after which government forces carried out reprisals against the Gio and Mano peoples of Nimba County — the recruiting ground of the coming rebellion.

Course of the war

On 24 December 1989, Charles Taylor's National Patriotic Front of Liberia (NPFL), a force of about 100 rebels trained in Libya and backed by Burkina Faso and Côte d'Ivoire, crossed from Côte d'Ivoire into Nimba County. It rapidly gained support among Gio and Mano communities and advanced toward Monrovia. In 1990 a splinter faction under Prince Yormie Johnson (INPFL) broke away; ECOWAS deployed its monitoring group ECOMOG to Monrovia in August 1990, and in September Johnson's men captured and brutally killed President Doe — a murder recorded on videotape.

ECOMOG installed an interim government, but Taylor controlled most of the country ("Greater Liberia") and its timber, rubber, and diamond revenues. The war fragmented into factional and ethnic fighting: ULIMO (and its ULIMO-J and ULIMO-K splits), the Lofa Defense Force, and government remnants contested territory, while ECOMOG itself became a belligerent at times, notably during Taylor's failed assault on Monrovia, "Operation Octopus" (October 1992). Child soldiers were widely used by all factions. A series of peace agreements — Cotonou (1993), Akosombo, Accra — collapsed until the Abuja Accords of 1995–96, supplemented after the savage April 1996 battle for Monrovia, finally held.

Outcome

Under the Abuja framework the factions disarmed under ECOMOG and UNOMIL supervision, and elections were held in July 1997. Charles Taylor won the presidency with about 75 percent of the vote — an outcome widely attributed to voters' fear that he would restart the war if he lost. The UN estimated the war killed some 200,000 Liberians, displaced perhaps half the population, and destroyed most of the country's infrastructure and formal economy. Taylor's presidency, and continued instability, led to renewed war in 1999.
