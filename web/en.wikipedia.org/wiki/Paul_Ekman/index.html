Paul Ekman

Paul Ekman (born 15 March 1934) is an American psychologist and professor emeritus at the University of California, San Francisco, known as a pioneer in the study of emotions and their relation to facial expressions.

Research

Ekman's cross-cultural fieldwork, including among the Fore people of Papua New Guinea, argued that a set of basic emotions — commonly anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise (with contempt sometimes added) — is recognised universally from facial expressions, challenging purely cultural accounts. With Wallace Friesen he developed the Facial Action Coding System (FACS, 1978), a taxonomy of facial movements, and later studied micro-expressions and deception, popularised in his book Telling Lies (1985).

Influence and debate

His work influenced the animated film Inside Out (2015), on which he consulted, and the television series Lie to Me. Some later researchers have questioned the strength of universality claims, prompting ongoing debate.
