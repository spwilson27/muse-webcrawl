Amsler grid

The Amsler grid is a grid of horizontal and vertical lines used to monitor a person's central visual field. It was developed by Swiss ophthalmologist Marc Amsler and has been used since the mid-20th century to detect visual disturbances caused by changes in the retina, particularly the macula, as well as the optic nerve and visual pathway.

InventorMarc Amsler
UsesScreening for macular degeneration and central visual field defects

Use

The patient, wearing reading correction, holds the chart at normal reading distance, covers one eye, and fixates the central dot, noting any areas where lines appear wavy, distorted (metamorphopsia), blurred, missing or dark. Testing is repeated for the other eye.

Clinical significance

Distortions can be an early sign of age-related macular degeneration, macular edema, epiretinal membrane or other macular disease, prompting ophthalmologic examination and imaging such as optical coherence tomography. The test is simple for home monitoring but does not replace professional eye examination, and more sensitive variants and preferential hyperacuity perimetry have been developed.
