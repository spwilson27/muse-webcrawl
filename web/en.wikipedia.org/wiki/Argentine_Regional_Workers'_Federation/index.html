The Argentine Regional Workers' Federation (Spanish: Federación Obrera Regional Argentina, abbreviated FORA) is an Argentine anarcho-syndicalist trade union federation founded in 1901. It was for decades one of the most important labor organizations in Argentina and a major force in the history of anarchism in the Americas.

A FORA demonstration in Buenos Aires in the early 20th century

History

FORA was founded on 25 May 1901 as the Argentine Workers' Federation, initially uniting socialist and anarchist unions. As anarchist influence grew, it adopted the name FORA in 1904. At its fifth congress in 1905, the federation explicitly adopted anarcho-communism as its goal, becoming known as FORA V after a 1915 split in which syndicalist unions that rejected the ideological commitment formed a rival FORA IX.

Heyday and repression

FORA led major strikes in the port of Buenos Aires and among tenant farmers and rural workers. It played a central role in the events surrounding the Tragic Week of January 1919 and supported the Patagonia rebelde strikes of 1920–1922, which were crushed by the army. The federation suffered severe repression under the dictatorship of José Félix Uriburu after 1930 and declined with the rise of Peronism in the 1940s, which absorbed much of the labor movement into state-aligned unions.

Modern activity

FORA continues to exist as a small anarcho-syndicalist federation and is affiliated with the International Workers' Association (AIT). Its newspaper La Protesta, founded in 1897, is one of the longest-running anarchist publications in the world, though it now appears irregularly.
