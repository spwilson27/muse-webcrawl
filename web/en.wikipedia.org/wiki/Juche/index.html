Juche (Korean: 주체; lit. 'subject') is the official ideology of North Korea, described by the regime as a "creative application" of Marxism–Leninism developed by Kim Il Sung and systematised by Kim Jong Il. It holds that the popular masses are the masters of revolution and construction, and that a nation must pursue independence and self-reliance in ideology, politics, the economy and defence.

Principles

Official doctrine organises Juche around the idea that "man is the master of everything and decides everything". Its applications are conventionally summarised as ideological autonomy, political independence, economic self-sufficiency and self-reliance in defence. In practice it underpins the leader-centred political system, in which loyalty to the supreme leader is presented as the practical expression of the ideology.

History

The term emerged in a 1955 speech by Kim Il Sung criticising dogmatism and "flunkeyism" toward the Soviet Union and China. During the 1960s–70s Juche displaced explicit references to Marxism–Leninism in North Korean texts; the 1972 constitution made it the state's guiding ideology, and in 1982 Kim Jong Il's treatise On the Juche Idea gave it a systematic form. The Juche calendar, counting years from Kim Il Sung's birth in 1912, was adopted in 1997. The 150-metre Tower of the Juche Idea in Pyongyang commemorates the doctrine.

Assessment

Outside North Korea, scholars commonly analyse Juche as a legitimating ideology for hereditary leadership and national autarky, noting the gap between its claims of self-reliance and the country's dependence on trade and aid. North Korean sources maintain that it is an original philosophy centred on human independence.
