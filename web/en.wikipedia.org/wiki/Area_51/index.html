Area 51 is the common name of a highly classified United States Air Force facility within the Nevada National Security Site, located at Groom Lake, a dry lake bed in southern Nevada about 83 miles (134 km) north-northwest of Las Vegas. The base has long been used to develop and test experimental aircraft and weapons systems, and the intense secrecy surrounding it has made it a focus of conspiracy theories involving unidentified flying objects and extraterrestrials.

History

The site was selected in 1955 as the test location for the Lockheed U-2 spy plane, developed under CIA auspices by Lockheed's Skunk Works under Kelly Johnson. The designation "Area 51" comes from the grid numbering used on Atomic Energy Commission maps of the Nevada Test Site. The base subsequently supported the Lockheed A-12 OXCART and SR-71 Blackbird reconnaissance aircraft, the D-21 drone, and the F-117 Nighthawk stealth attack aircraft, which was secretly flown from the Tonopah Test Range and Groom Lake for years before its existence was revealed in 1988. Captured Soviet aircraft were also evaluated there by units such as the 4477th Test and Evaluation Squadron ("Red Eagles").

Secrecy and acknowledgment

For decades the United States government neither confirmed nor denied the base's existence, though its runways were plainly visible in satellite imagery and its restricted airspace — the so-called "Groom Box" — was marked on aeronautical charts. Workers were flown in daily from Las Vegas on unmarked aircraft. In 2013, following a Freedom of Information Act request, the CIA released a declassified history of the U-2 programme that explicitly acknowledged Area 51, explaining many UFO sightings as tests of high-altitude reconnaissance aircraft flying far above the performance of contemporary airliners.

In popular culture

Claims by Bob Lazar in 1989 that he had worked on reverse-engineering alien spacecraft near the base cemented Area 51's place in UFO folklore, and it features in films such as Independence Day. The Nevada State Route 375 was officially named the "Extraterrestrial Highway", and the nearby town of Rachel trades on the legend. In 2019 a satirical Facebook event, "Storm Area 51", attracted millions of online sign-ups and a small real-world gathering, prompting warnings from the Air Force.
