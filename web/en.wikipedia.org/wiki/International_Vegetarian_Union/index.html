The International Vegetarian Union (IVU) is a non-profit organisation whose purpose is to promote vegetarianism worldwide. Founded in 1908 at its first World Vegetarian Congress in Dresden, Germany, it is an umbrella body uniting national and regional vegetarian and vegan societies.

History

The idea of an international union was discussed at early gatherings of the British and German vegetarian movements; at the Dresden congress of 18 August 1908 the IVU was formally established, with the French and other European societies joining. World congresses have been held regularly ever since, interrupted only by the world wars, in venues across Europe, India, North and South America, Asia, Africa and Australasia. The 1975 congress in Orono, Maine, led to the founding of the North American Vegetarian Society. Figures associated with the IVU include its long-serving general secretary and later president Gordon Latto, and author and campaigner Geoffrey Rudd, who edited IVU publications for many years.

Activities

The IVU organises the World Vegetarian Congress, held every two years in recent decades, and supports member societies through regional groups covering Africa, Asia-Pacific, Europe, Latin America, North America and the Middle East. It maintains an extensive online archive of vegetarian history, recipes and resources, promotes World Vegetarian Day (1 October) alongside member organisations, and works with bodies such as the European Vegetarian Union on labelling standards for vegetarian and vegan products. In recent years the IVU has increasingly embraced veganism within its remit, reflecting the direction of the wider movement.

Definitions

For membership and labelling purposes, the IVU describes vegetarian food as that which does not consist of, or contain, meat, fish, molluscs, crustaceans or slaughterhouse by-products; vegan food additionally excludes eggs, milk, dairy products and honey.
