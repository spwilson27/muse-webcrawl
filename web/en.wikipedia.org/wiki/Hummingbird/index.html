Hummingbirds are birds of the family Trochilidae, native to the Americas. They are among the smallest of birds, most species measuring 7.5–13 cm, and are known for their ability to hover in mid-air by rapidly flapping their wings — 12 to 80 times per second depending on species — producing the humming sound from which they take their name.

Biology

Hummingbirds have the highest mass-specific metabolic rate of any homeothermic animal and feed primarily on flower nectar, which they lap with long, specialised tongues; they also catch insects for protein. Their iridescent plumage results from structural coloration in the feathers. Many species enter torpor at night to conserve energy.

Evolution and distribution

More than 360 species are recognised, with the greatest diversity in Ecuador and Colombia. Molecular evidence suggests hummingbirds diverged from swifts about 42 million years ago, with the modern radiation occurring in South America around 22 million years ago.
