Inferior frontal gyrus

The inferior frontal gyrus is a gyrus of the frontal lobe of the human brain, situated below the inferior frontal sulcus. Its posterior portion on the language-dominant hemisphere contains Broca's area, a region central to speech production and language processing.

Subdivisions

The inferior frontal gyrus is divided by the ascending and horizontal rami of the lateral sulcus into three parts:

  • Pars opercularis (Brodmann area 44), between the ascending ramus and the precentral sulcus.
  • Pars triangularis (Brodmann area 45), between the ascending and horizontal rami.
  • Pars orbitalis (Brodmann area 47), below the horizontal ramus, continuous with the orbital surface of the frontal lobe.

Function

The left inferior frontal gyrus is essential for language production, syntax processing and verbal working memory; damage causes Broca's aphasia, characterised by effortful, non-fluent speech with relatively preserved comprehension. The region — particularly on the right — also participates in response inhibition and cognitive control, and it forms part of the putative mirror neuron system and the ventral attention network involved in action understanding and social cognition.
