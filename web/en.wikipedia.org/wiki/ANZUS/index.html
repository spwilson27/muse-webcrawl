The Australia, New Zealand, United States Security Treaty (ANZUS or the ANZUS Treaty) is a collective security agreement between Australia, New Zealand and the United States, signed in San Francisco on 1 September 1951 and entering into force on 29 April 1952. It binds the parties to consult on security threats in the Pacific and to act to meet a common danger in accordance with their constitutional processes.

History

The treaty grew out of Australian and New Zealand anxiety about a resurgent Japan and communist expansion in Asia in the early Cold War; in exchange for accepting a lenient peace treaty with Japan, the two Dominions sought a formal American security guarantee. Unlike NATO, ANZUS created no integrated command structure or standing forces.

The New Zealand dispute

In 1984 the New Zealand government of David Lange adopted a policy banning nuclear-armed and nuclear-powered ships from its ports. When the United States refused to confirm or deny whether the destroyer USS Buchanan carried nuclear weapons in 1985, New Zealand refused it entry. The US responded by suspending its treaty obligations toward New Zealand in 1986, and ANZUS has since operated effectively as two bilateral relationships (Australia–US and Australia–New Zealand). Relations between Washington and Wellington later thawed — notably with the 2010 Wellington Declaration — without a formal restoration of the US–NZ treaty leg.

Invocations

Australia invoked ANZUS for the first time after the September 11 attacks in 2001, and the alliance has underpinned Australian participation in operations in Afghanistan and Iraq, as well as growing US force posture cooperation in northern Australia.
