Karl Lachmann

Karl Konrad Friedrich Wilhelm Lachmann (4 March 1793 – 13 March 1851) was a German philologist and critic, regarded as a founder of modern textual criticism. He taught at the universities of Königsberg and Berlin.

Work

Lachmann produced critical editions of classical authors including Propertius, Catullus, Tibullus, Lucretius, and the New Testament, as well as editions of medieval German literature such as the Nibelungenlied, Hartmann von Aue's Iwein, and the works of Wolfram von Eschenbach and Walther von der Vogelweide.

Lachmann's method

His name is attached to the genealogical or stemmatic method of textual criticism: the systematic comparison of surviving manuscripts to eliminate copies of copies (eliminatio codicum descriptorum), classify witnesses by their shared errors, construct a family tree (stemma codicum), and thereby reconstruct the archetype from which all surviving copies derive. Although later scholars — notably Joseph Bédier — criticised mechanical application of the method, it remains foundational to scholarly editing.
