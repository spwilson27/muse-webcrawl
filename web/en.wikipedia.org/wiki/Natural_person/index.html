Natural person

Field: Law
Definition: A human being recognised as a legal person
Contrast: Juridical (legal) person — a corporation or other entity

In law, a natural person is a human being who has legal personality — the capacity to hold rights and duties — as distinct from a juridical person (also called a legal or artificial person), such as a corporation, company or state body, which the law treats as a person for certain purposes although it is not a human being.

Legal capacity

A natural person's legal personality generally begins at birth and ends at death. Natural persons can own property, enter contracts, sue and be sued, and hold human and civil rights that cannot be held by juridical persons, such as the right to vote, marry or hold public office; conversely, only natural persons can be imprisoned. The extent of a person's capacity to act may be limited, for example for minors or persons under guardianship, without removing their status as rights-holders.

Usage

The term is especially common in civil law jurisdictions (French personne physique, German natürliche Person) and in statutes on taxation, banking and data protection, where "natural person" identifies the individual human to whom rules apply, in contrast to companies and other entities. Modern legal systems also debate borderline cases, such as whether animals, rivers or ecosystems should receive legal personhood of a kind.
