Launeddas

Launeddas, the traditional triple-pipe instrument of Sardinia

The launeddas (also spelled launaddas or lionedas) is a traditional Sardinian woodwind instrument consisting of three reed pipes played simultaneously by a single musician. It is one of the oldest continuously used instruments in Europe, with origins traced to the ancient Nuragic civilisation of Sardinia; bronze figurines from around the 8th century BC appear to depict players of a similar triple pipe.

Construction and playing technique

The instrument comprises three tubes of cane (Arundo donax), each fitted with a single idioglot reed. Two longer pipes, the tumbu and the mancosa manna, are bound together and held in the left hand, while the shorter melodic pipe, the mancosedda, is held in the right hand. The player uses circular breathing — inhaling through the nose while expelling air stored in the cheeks — to produce an unbroken stream of sound, accompanying melodies with drone notes.

Music and use

Launeddas music is central to Sardinian folk tradition, accompanying dances such as the ballu tundu and religious processions and festivals. The repertoire is organised into intricate rhythmic and melodic patterns called nodas. Instruments are made in numerous tunings, each suited to particular dances and occasions. Renowned twentieth-century masters included Efisio Melis and Antonio Lara.
