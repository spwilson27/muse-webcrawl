The East Midlands is one of nine official regions of England. It comprises the counties of Derbyshire, Leicestershire, most of Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire, Nottinghamshire and Rutland. Its largest settlements are Nottingham, Leicester, Derby, Lincoln and Northampton. The region covers 15,627 square kilometres and has a population of roughly 4.9 million, making it one of the less densely populated English regions.

Geography

The region stretches from the Peak District in the north-west — including the southern Pennines and the region's highest ground at Kinder Scout — to the flat fenlands and North Sea coast of Lincolnshire in the east. Major rivers include the Trent, the Derwent, the Soar, the Welland and the Witham. The National Forest spans parts of Derbyshire, Leicestershire and Staffordshire. East Midlands Airport, near Castle Donington, is a major freight hub, and the M1 motorway runs the length of the region.

History and economy

Much of the region corresponds to the territory of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw — Derby, Leicester, Lincoln, Nottingham and Stamford. Historically its industries included framework knitting and lace in Nottingham, hosiery and footwear in Leicester and Northampton, coal mining in the Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire coalfield, and engineering in Derby, long a centre of railway works and home to Rolls-Royce aero-engine production. The economy today mixes logistics and distribution (the "Golden Triangle" of warehouses near the M1), food production in Lincolnshire, universities in Nottingham, Leicester and Loughborough, and tourism in the Peak District.

Governance

Regional government offices were abolished in the 2010s; in 2024 an East Midlands Combined County Authority was established with an elected mayor covering Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire. Lincolnshire has pursued separate devolution arrangements.
