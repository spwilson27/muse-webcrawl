JPEG 2000

JPEG 2000 (JP2) is an image compression standard and coding system created by the Joint Photographic Experts Group in 2000 to supersede the original JPEG standard. Based on the discrete wavelet transform rather than the discrete cosine transform, it offers superior compression at low bit rates, lossless and lossy compression in a single codestream, and progressive decoding by resolution or quality.

Technical features

JPEG 2000 applies a multi-level discrete wavelet transform (the reversible Le Gall 5/3 or the irreversible Daubechies 9/7 wavelet) followed by quantization and EBCOT (Embedded Block Coding with Optimized Truncation) arithmetic coding. Features include region-of-interest coding, very large image support, transparency, and a single decompression architecture. At very low bit rates it avoids JPEG's 8×8 blocking artifacts, producing instead a smoother, blur-like degradation.

Adoption

Despite its technical advantages, JPEG 2000 saw limited consumer adoption, partly due to patent concerns and computational cost. It became established in digital cinema (the DCI specification), medical imaging (DICOM), geospatial imagery, archival digitization (libraries and the Internet Archive), and satellite imagery.
