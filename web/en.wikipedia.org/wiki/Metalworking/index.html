Metalworking

Metalworking is the process of shaping and reshaping metals to create useful objects, parts, assemblies and large-scale structures. As a term it covers a wide and varied range of processes, skills and tools for producing objects on every scale, from enormous ships and bridges down to precise engine parts and delicate jewellery.

History

Metalworking began in prehistory with the hammering of native copper and gold, and advanced through the Bronze Age and Iron Age with smelting, alloying and casting. Industrialisation brought machine tools, mass production and precision measurement, while modern practice adds computer numerical control (CNC) machining and additive manufacturing.

Processes

Metalworking processes are generally divided into forming (forging, rolling, extrusion, bending), cutting (milling, turning, drilling, grinding), and joining (welding, brazing, soldering, riveting). Casting — pouring molten metal into a mould — is sometimes treated as its own category. Supporting trades include heat treatment, surface finishing and metrology.
