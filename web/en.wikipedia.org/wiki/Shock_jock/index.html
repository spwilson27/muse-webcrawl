Shock jock

A shock jock is a radio broadcaster or disc jockey who entertains listeners with humor that a significant portion of the audience considers offensive. Most shock jocks use material that is sexual, racist, scatological, or otherwise taboo, often deliberately pushing the boundaries of broadcast decency standards to attract attention.

History and style

The style emerged in American radio in the 1970s and 1980s. Petey Greene is often cited as an early pioneer. The format reached its commercial peak in the 1990s and 2000s with personalities such as Howard Stern, whose New York morning show was syndicated nationally, and the duo Opie and Anthony. Stern's transition to satellite radio (Sirius) in 2006 was motivated in part by freedom from Federal Communications Commission (FCC) indecency regulation, which applies only to terrestrial broadcasts.

Regulation and controversy

Shock jocks have frequently been fined by the FCC and dismissed by stations after controversial stunts; notable examples include Opie and Anthony's 2002 "Sex for Sam" contest broadcast from St. Patrick's Cathedral in New York. Critics argue the format trades in bigotry and cruelty, while defenders frame it as satire and free expression.
