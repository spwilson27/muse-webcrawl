Penjing

Penjing (Chinese: 盆景, "tray scenery") is the ancient Chinese art of creating miniature landscapes in trays or pots, using artistically shaped trees, rocks, soil, and water. It is the ancestor of the Japanese art of bonsai and of Vietnamese hòn non bộ.

Styles

Penjing is traditionally divided into tree penjing (shumu penjing), the equivalent of bonsai; landscape penjing (shanshui penjing), which recreates mountain-and-water scenery with rocks and small trees; and water-and-land penjing (shuihan penjing), combining trees, rocks, and water to evoke shorelines. Regional schools include Yangzhou, Sichuan, Guangdong (Lingnan), Suzhou, and Shanghai styles, each with characteristic techniques of pruning and wiring.

History

Miniature potted landscapes appear in Chinese art from at least the Tang dynasty, and tomb figures suggest even earlier origins; the practice was refined by scholar-gentlemen and Buddhist and Taoist aesthetics of harmony with nature. Notable collections are held at the Suzhou Museum and the Shanghai Botanical Garden.
