Leosthenes (Greek: Λεωσθένης; died 323 BC) was an Athenian general who commanded the Greek forces in the Lamian War (323–322 BC), the revolt of Athens and its allies against Macedonian rule following the death of Alexander the Great.

Career

Before 323 BC, Leosthenes had gathered and commanded the thousands of Greek mercenaries discharged from Alexander's armies in Asia, who had assembled at Taenarum in Laconia. Secretly commissioned by the Athenian assembly — influenced by the anti-Macedonian orators Demosthenes and Hypereides — he recruited these veterans as Athens prepared to revolt when news of Alexander's death in Babylon arrived.

Lamian War

As commander-in-chief, Leosthenes led the combined Greek army north, won over the Thessalians, and defeated the Macedonian viceroy Antipater in battle, penning him up in the fortress town of Lamia over the winter of 323/322 BC. During the siege Leosthenes was struck on the head by a stone — some accounts say slung or thrown from the walls — and died three days later. Hypereides delivered his funeral oration. His death was a heavy blow: though the Greeks initially held the advantage, Antipater was eventually relieved, and the Greeks were crushed at the Battle of Crannon in 322 BC, ending the revolt and, effectively, Athenian independence.
