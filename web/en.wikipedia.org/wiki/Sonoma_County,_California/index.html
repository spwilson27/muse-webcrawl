Sonoma County is a county located in the northwestern part of the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, its population was 488,863. Its county seat and largest city is Santa Rosa. The county lies on the Pacific coast north of San Francisco Bay and forms part of California's Wine Country, alongside Napa, Mendocino, and Lake counties.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Pomo, Coast Miwok, and Wappo peoples. Spanish missionaries founded Mission San Francisco Solano in 1823, the northernmost of the California missions. In the 1830s Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo established the town of Sonoma under Mexican rule. On June 14, 1846, American settlers proclaimed the short-lived California Republic at Sonoma in the Bear Flag Revolt, before U.S. forces took control during the Mexican–American War. Sonoma County was one of California's original counties, created in 1850 at statehood.

Geography

Sonoma County borders the Pacific Ocean to the west, Mendocino County to the north, Lake and Napa counties to the east, and Marin County and San Pablo Bay to the south. The Russian River flows through the county to the sea at Jenner. Terrain ranges from coastal bluffs and redwood forests to the Mayacamas Mountains in the east.

Wine industry

Sonoma County is one of California's premier wine-growing regions, with more than 425 wineries and 18 American Viticultural Areas (AVAs), including the Russian River Valley, Alexander Valley, Dry Creek Valley, and Sonoma Valley. Grapes are the county's largest agricultural product, and wine tourism is a major part of the economy.

Cities

Incorporated cities include Santa Rosa, Petaluma, Rohnert Park, Windsor, Healdsburg, Sonoma, Cloverdale, Cotati, and Sebastopol.
