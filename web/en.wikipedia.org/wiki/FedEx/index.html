FedEx Corporation, formerly Federal Express Corporation and later FDX Corporation, is an American multinational conglomerate holding company specialising in transportation, e-commerce and business services. Headquartered in Memphis, Tennessee, the company is named from a syllabic abbreviation of its original air division, Federal Express.

History

FedEx was founded as Federal Express in 1971 by Frederick W. Smith, a Yale graduate whose 1965 undergraduate economics paper had outlined an overnight air-freight system built on a hub-and-spoke model. Operations began on 17 April 1973, delivering 186 packages to 25 US cities using Dassault Falcon jets flying through Memphis. The company pioneered package tracking, barcode scanning and the money-back-guaranteed overnight letter.

After airline deregulation in 1977, FedEx acquired larger aircraft and expanded rapidly. It purchased the European courier TNT Express in 2016 for €4.4 billion, consolidating its international network. Chairman Fred Smith stepped down as CEO in 2022, succeeded by Raj Subramaniam. In 2023 FedEx announced its DRIVE consolidation programme, merging the Express and Ground networks.

Operations

FedEx Express operates one of the world's largest cargo airlines, centred on the Memphis "SuperHub", with further hubs at Indianapolis, Anchorage, Paris Charles de Gaulle, Cologne and Guangzhou. FedEx Ground handles residential and commercial ground parcels in North America, FedEx Freight is a major less-than-truckload carrier, and FedEx Office (formerly Kinko's) provides retail print and shipping services. Company-wide, FedEx handles on the order of 16 million shipments per business day across more than 220 countries and territories.

Branding

The FedEx wordmark, designed by Lindon Leader in 1994, is frequently cited in design literature for the white-space arrow formed between the "E" and "x". The company's purple-and-orange colour scheme differs per division ("Ex" in orange for Express, green for Ground, red for Freight).
