Dielectric mirror

A dielectric mirror, also known as a Bragg mirror, is a type of mirror composed of multiple thin layers of dielectric material, typically deposited on a substrate of glass or another optical material. By careful choice of the materials and thicknesses, reflectivity can exceed 99.999% over a designed wavelength range.

Principle

Each layer boundary reflects part of the incident light. When the optical thickness of each layer equals a quarter of the design wavelength, the reflections interfere constructively, producing very high reflectance within a stop band. Alternating high- and low-refractive-index materials such as titanium dioxide and silicon dioxide is common.

Applications

Dielectric mirrors are used in lasers, interferometers, telescopes, and optical instruments, as laser line mirrors, dichroic filters, and the end mirrors of resonant cavities. Unlike metal mirrors they can have extremely low absorption losses.
