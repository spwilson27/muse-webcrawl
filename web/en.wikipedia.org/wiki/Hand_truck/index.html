Hand truck

A hand truck, also known as a dolly, sack truck, sack barrow, or two-wheeler, is an L-shaped hand cart with two wheels at the base and a small flat toe plate (or "nose") at the bottom. Loads are stacked on the toe plate and the truck is tilted back so the weight rests over the axle, allowing one person to move loads far heavier than they could lift unaided.

Design and use

Typical hand trucks have a tubular steel or aluminium frame, solid or pneumatic wheels, and a straight or looped handle; common variants include convertible trucks that can be laid flat as four-wheeled carts, stair-climbing models with tri-wheel clusters, and appliance trucks with straps and larger capacities. Typical capacities range from about 70 kg for light domestic models to 300 kg or more for industrial versions.

Hand trucks are ubiquitous in warehousing, delivery, removals and retail stock handling, where they reduce lifting injuries by keeping loads at axle height. Safe use emphasises loading heavy items at the bottom, tilting back to balance the load, and pushing rather than pulling where possible.
