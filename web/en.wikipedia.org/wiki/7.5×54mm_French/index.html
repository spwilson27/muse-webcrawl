7.5×54mm French

7.5×54mm French
Type: rifle cartridge
Country of origin: France
Used in MAS-36, MAS-49 and other French arms.

The 7.5×54mm French, also known as the 7.5×54mm MAS or 7.5 French, is a rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridge adopted by France in the interwar period. It replaced older French service ammunition concepts after the First World War and became the standard rifle and machine-gun cartridge for French forces through the Second World War and into the postwar decades.

Development

France sought a modern cartridge suited to bolt-action rifles, semi-automatic rifles and light machine guns. An earlier 7.5×58mm version was superseded by the slightly shorter 7.5×54mm case in 1929, in part to avoid confusion and feeding problems with similar foreign cartridges. The resulting round offered performance broadly comparable to other contemporary full-power service cartridges.

Weapons and service

The cartridge chambered the MAS-36 bolt-action rifle, the FM 24/29 light machine gun, the MAS-49 and MAS-49/56 semi-automatic rifles, and other French weapons. It remained in use during the Second World War, the First Indochina War and the Algerian War. As NATO standardisation favoured 7.62×51mm and later 5.56×45mm, the 7.5 French gradually left front-line French service, though it survived in some roles and in former French-influenced arsenals.

Characteristics

The case is 54 mm long with an overall cartridge length of about 78 mm. Typical military ball loadings fired a bullet of roughly 9–12 g at velocities suitable for a full-power rifle cartridge. Because its dimensions are close to some other cartridges but not interchangeable with them, positive identification matters for collectors and shooters.
