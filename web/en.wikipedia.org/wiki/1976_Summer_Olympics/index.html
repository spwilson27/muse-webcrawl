The 1976 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXI Olympiad, were held from 17 July to 1 August 1976 in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. They are best remembered for the flawless gymnastics of Romania's Nadia Comăneci, who scored the first perfect 10.0 in Olympic history, and for a boycott by most African nations protesting New Zealand's sporting contacts with apartheid South Africa. The Games left Montreal with a debt of about C$1.6 billion that took thirty years to pay off.

Background and boycott

Montreal won the Games over Moscow and Los Angeles in 1970 under mayor Jean Drapeau, who famously declared that the Olympics "can no more have a deficit than a man can have a baby". Construction strikes and corruption drove costs far beyond estimates, and the showpiece Olympic Stadium — designed by Roger Taillibert with a retractable roof suspended from the world's tallest inclined tower — was unfinished at the opening (the tower and roof were completed only in 1987).

Days before the opening, 29 African nations (and Iraq and Guyana in solidarity) withdrew because the IOC refused to ban New Zealand, whose All Blacks rugby team had toured South Africa. Taiwan also withdrew after Canada refused to let its athletes compete under the name "Republic of China".

Highlights

Fourteen-year-old Comăneci received seven perfect 10s and won three gold medals; the scoreboard, programmed only for three digits, displayed her perfect mark as "1.00". The Soviet Union topped the medal table with 49 golds, ahead of East Germany (40) — whose success was later linked to its state doping programme — and the United States (34). Other stars included Finland's Lasse Virén, who repeated his 5,000 m / 10,000 m double; Cuba's Alberto Juantorena, the only man to win the 400 m and 800 m at one Games; American decathlete Bruce Jenner; and Japanese gymnast Shun Fujimoto, who performed his still-rings dismount on a broken knee to secure team gold. Women's rowing and team handball and basketball made their Olympic debuts. Host Canada failed to win a gold medal — the first host nation of a Summer Games ever to do so — though it collected eleven medals in total.

Legacy

The "Big Owe" — as Montrealers nicknamed the stadium — became a byword for Olympic cost overruns; the city's Olympic debt was finally discharged in 2006. Montreal's experience discouraged bidding cities for years and pushed the IOC towards the commercially driven model adopted for Los Angeles 1984.
