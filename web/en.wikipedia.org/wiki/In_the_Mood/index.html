"In the Mood" is a popular big band-era jazz standard recorded by American bandleader Glenn Miller. Released in 1939, it topped the charts for 13 straight weeks in 1940 in the United States and became Miller's signature tune and an emblem of the swing era and the Second World War generation.

Origins

The song was composed by Joe Garland and based on an existing theme by Wingy Manone ("Tar Paper Stomp", recorded by Edgar Hayes in 1930). After passing through the hands of several bands, it was arranged for Miller — with contributions variously credited to Eddie Durham and others — and built around a hypnotic saxophone riff repeated with rising intensity, punctuated by the famous false ending: the band drops to a whisper, then swells back for three final choruses.

Legacy

Miller's recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1983 and selected for the Library of Congress National Recording Registry. The tune remains a staple of swing dancing and has been covered by artists from the Andrews Sisters to Ernie Fields, whose rock-and-roll version charted in 1959. The enduring popularity of "In the Mood" helped define the sound of the big band era for later generations.
