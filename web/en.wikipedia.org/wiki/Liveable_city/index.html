Liveable city

A liveable city (also spelled livable city) is an urban area assessed as offering a high quality of life for its residents. The concept is operationalised in several widely reported rankings — the Economist Intelligence Unit's Global Liveability Index, Mercer's Quality of Living Survey and Monocle's Quality of Life Survey — which score cities on stability, healthcare, education, infrastructure, environment, culture and personal safety.

Rankings

Vienna, Copenhagen, Zurich, Melbourne and Sydney have repeatedly headed the EIU index in recent years; Vienna has been ranked the world's most liveable city in multiple consecutive surveys. Canadian and Australian cities historically dominated the top ten, alongside cities in Switzerland, Denmark, the Netherlands, New Zealand and Japan.

Criticism

The rankings have been criticised for reflecting the preferences of expatriate executives rather than ordinary residents, for undervaluing affordability (housing costs in top-ranked cities are often extreme), and for producing a narrow, Western- and northern-European-centric picture of urban quality. Urbanists argue that liveability should also encompass housing affordability, social equity, walkability, public space and carbon emissions. Concepts such as the "15-minute city" and Jan Gehl's human-scale urban design are frequently invoked in liveability debates.
