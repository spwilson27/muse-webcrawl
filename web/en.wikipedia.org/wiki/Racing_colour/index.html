Racing colours

Image placeholder: jockey silks in various registered racing colours.

Racing colours, also called silks, are the coloured and patterned jackets and caps worn by jockeys in horse racing. The colours are registered to the owner of the horse (or the partnership or racing club running it) and identify the horse's connections during a race. Standard patterns — plain, hooped, striped, quartered, halved, with spots, stars, sashes, epaulettes or crosses — are combined with colours from an approved palette so that every set is distinguishable.

History

Distinctive colours were in use in English racing by the 18th century; the Jockey Club was founded partly to settle disputes between owners, and registration of colours became formalised by the 19th century. Famous sets include the royal colours (purple, scarlet and gold), the rose-pink silks associated with the Aga Khan's emerald green, and the dark blue of Godolphin. In the United States colours are registered with The Jockey Club, while in Australia and other jurisdictions they are administered by state racing authorities.

Usage

A jockey wears the registered silks of the owner of the horse being ridden in each race; where one owner has several runners in the same race, the jockeys wear distinguishing caps. Colours are published in racecards and are used by commentators and punters to follow horses during a race.

Motor racing

The term is distinct from national racing colours in motor sport, such as British racing green, Italian rosso corsa, French blue and German silver/white, which originated in early international competition.
