Great Seal of Scotland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Seal of Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Seala Mòr na h-Alba) is the seal used to authenticate official documents of the highest importance in Scotland. Historically the principal seal of the Scottish monarch, it is today held by the Keeper of the Great Seal of Scotland — an office held ex officio by the First Minister of Scotland since devolution in 1999 — and is administered by Registers of Scotland.

History

Scottish kings used great seals from at least the reign of Edgar (r. 1097–1107). As elsewhere in medieval Europe, the seal's two faces conventionally showed the monarch enthroned in majesty on one side and mounted as a warrior on the other, later replaced by the royal arms of Scotland. The seal authenticated charters, treaties, and commissions, and custody of it was a great office of state. During the Wars of Independence the seal became a potent symbol of sovereignty: Edward I of England had the Scottish seal matrices broken up in 1296, and later Scottish governments repeatedly cut new seals.

The Union and after

Under the Treaty of Union of 1707, a single Great Seal was created for Great Britain, but a separate seal — the Seal appointed by the Treaty of Union to be used in place of the Great Seal of Scotland — continued for documents relating exclusively to Scotland, such as Scottish judicial appointments and charters of Scottish land. This union seal remains in use for many purposes. Since 1999 the separate Great Seal of Scotland proper has been used for the royal assent of Acts of the Scottish Parliament and for the most senior Scottish appointments, and its custody by the First Minister as Keeper symbolises the continuity of the Scottish Crown's legal personality.

Design

The modern seal bears the reigning monarch's Scottish royal arms — the lion rampant within a double tressure flory-counter-flory — together with the mottoes Nemo me impune lacessit and the Order of the Thistle's In defens. Impressions are traditionally made in red sealing wax within a protective silver skippet.
