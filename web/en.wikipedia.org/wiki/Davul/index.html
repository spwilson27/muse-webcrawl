Davul

Davul
Classification: Membranophone
Other names: Tupan, daf, tabl baladi
Region: Balkans, Turkey, Middle East, Caucasus

The davul is a large double-headed drum with a deep wooden shell, played throughout the Balkans, Turkey, Armenia, the Middle East and the Caucasus. In Turkey it is traditionally paired with the zurna, a loud double-reed wind instrument, and the davul–zurna duo remains the classic outdoor music for weddings, circumcision celebrations, wrestling matches and folk dance.

Construction and playing

The shell is usually made of walnut, mulberry or other hardwood, and the two heads are of animal skin — typically a thicker skin on one side and a thinner skin on the other. The drum is slung over the player's shoulder and struck with two different beaters: a heavy stick (tokmak) in one hand produces deep bass strokes on the thick head, while a thin switch (çubuk) in the other hand plays rapid, bright patterns on the thin head, allowing a single player to produce a complete rhythmic accompaniment.

Variants

Related instruments include the Bulgarian and Macedonian tupan, the Serbian goč, the Assyrian dawoola, and the Arabic tabl baladi. Sizes vary by region and purpose, from smaller dance drums to very large ceremonial instruments. The davul also held a military role in Ottoman mehter (Janissary) bands, where giant versions called kös were played from horseback or camelback.
