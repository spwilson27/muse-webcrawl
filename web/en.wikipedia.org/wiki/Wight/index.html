Wight

A wight is a creature or living being, a word rooted in Old English wiht, meaning a thing, creature, or being. In its original sense it was neutral — any living entity could be a wight — but in later usage, especially through folklore and fantasy literature, the word came to denote an undead or supernatural creature.

Etymology and usage

The word survives in the phrase "not a wight" (no one at all) and is cognate with German Wicht and Scandinavian words for beings. In translations of Old Norse sagas, wight is used for various beings, including the undead draugr.

In fantasy

J. R. R. Tolkien popularized the sinister sense with the barrow-wights in The Fellowship of the Ring (1954). Wights subsequently became stock undead monsters in fantasy works, including the tabletop game Dungeons & Dragons and George R. R. Martin's A Song of Ice and Fire and its television adaptation Game of Thrones, where wights are corpses reanimated by the White Walkers.
