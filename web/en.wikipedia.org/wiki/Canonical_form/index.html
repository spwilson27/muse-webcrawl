Canonical form

Canonical form
FieldMathematics, computer science, logic
Also calledNormal form, standard form

In mathematics and computer science, a canonical form, normal form, or standard form of a mathematical object is a standard way of presenting that object as a mathematical expression. Often, it is one which provides the simplest representation of an object and allows it to be identified in a unique way.

Purpose

Canonical forms make it possible to decide whether two objects are equal by simply comparing their canonical representations as strings or structures. They also simplify computation: algorithms generally work on canonical inputs, and classification theorems in mathematics frequently state that every object of a class can be put into a canonical form.

Examples

Familiar examples include the Jordan normal form and row echelon form of matrices; the disjunctive and conjunctive normal forms of Boolean formulas in logic; the reduced form of a fraction (with numerator and denominator coprime); the standard form of a differential equation; and in computing, canonicalisation of data — such as normalising URLs, Unicode strings, or XML documents — so that equivalent data has a single representation. In graph theory, a canonical labelling assigns each graph a canonical form to solve the graph isomorphism problem in practice.

Not every class of objects admits a useful canonical form: for some problems (such as canonical forms of general program behaviour) computing one is undecidable.
