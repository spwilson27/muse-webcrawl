David Lewin

David Benjamin Lewin (July 2, 1933 – May 5, 2003) was an American music theorist, music critic and composer, and a leading figure in twentieth-century music theory. His book Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations (1987) founded transformational theory, an influential approach modeling musical relations as transformations within mathematical groups.

Career

Lewin studied mathematics at Princeton University and composition with Milton Babbitt, Edward Cone, and Earl Kim. He taught at the University of California, Berkeley, SUNY Stony Brook, Yale University, and from 1985 at Harvard University, where he was the Walter W. Naumburg Professor of Music.

Work

Besides his theoretical writings, Lewin composed works for voice and chamber ensembles and wrote analytical studies of composers from Mozart and Schubert to Webern and Carter. He was a founding figure of the Society for Music Theory.
