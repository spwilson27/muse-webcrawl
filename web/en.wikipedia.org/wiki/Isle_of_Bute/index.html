The Isle of Bute (Scottish Gaelic: Eilean Bhòid), is an island in the Firth of Clyde in Scotland, in the unitary council area of Argyll and Bute. It is divided into highland and lowland areas by the Highland Boundary Fault.

Formerly a constituent island of the larger County of Bute, it is now part of Argyll and Bute. In 2011 its usually resident population was around 6,500. The island's main town is Rothesay, a Victorian seaside resort linked by ferry to Wemyss Bay on the mainland, with a second ferry from Portavadie to the island's north at Rhubodach.

Geography

Bute lies between the Cowal peninsula and the Isle of Arran, separated from Cowal by the narrow Kyles of Bute. The north is hilly and wooded; the south and west are more fertile lowlands with freshwater lochs including Loch Quien, Loch Ascog, and Loch Fad. Notable beaches include Ettrick Bay, West Bay, and Scalpsie Bay with its seal colony.

History

Bute has been inhabited since prehistoric times, with standing stones and chambered cairns surviving. St Blane's Chapel and Kingarth church recall the early medieval church; the island lay in the Norse-influenced Kingdom of the Isles before passing to Scotland in the 13th century. Rothesay Castle, with its unusual circular curtain wall, was a royal Stewart castle, and the title Duke of Rothesay derives from it.

Mount Stuart

Mount Stuart House, seat of the Stuart family (Marquesses of Bute), is a spectacular Victorian Gothic palace built by the 3rd Marquess of Bute, one of Britain's wealthiest men, after a fire in 1877 destroyed the earlier house. It is noted as one of the first Scottish homes with an indoor heated swimming pool and electric lighting, and stands in extensive gardens open to the public.
