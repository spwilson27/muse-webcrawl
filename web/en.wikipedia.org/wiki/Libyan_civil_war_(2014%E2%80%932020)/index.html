Libyan civil war (2014–2020)
Date: 2014–2020
Location: Libya
Status: Ended by ceasefire of 23 October 2020

The Libyan civil war of 2014–2020, also known as the Second Libyan Civil War, was an armed conflict between rival Libyan administrations and armed groups competing for control of the country after the collapse of central authority that followed the 2011 revolution.

Origins

Disputes between the General National Congress in Tripoli and the newly elected House of Representatives in Tobruk, combined with General Khalifa Haftar's Operation Dignity campaign in Benghazi beginning in May 2014, ignited open warfare. Rival governments, each backed by shifting militia coalitions and foreign sponsors, claimed legitimacy.

Major phases

The war saw fighting over Tripoli's airport in 2014, the siege and capture of Benghazi by the LNA (2014–2017), campaigns against the Islamic State's enclave in Sirte (2016), and the LNA's 2019–2020 offensive against the UN-backed Government of National Accord in Tripoli, which failed after Turkish intervention. Front lines froze around Sirte and Jufra in mid-2020.

Aftermath

The October 2020 Geneva ceasefire, followed by the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum's selection of a Government of National Unity in 2021, formally ended the war, though the country remained politically divided.
