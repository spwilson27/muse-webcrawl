Enthalpy

Enthalpy
SymbolH
DefinitionH = U + pV
SI unitJoule (J)
DimensionEnergy

Enthalpy (symbol H) is a property of a thermodynamic system defined as the sum of the system's internal energy (U) and the product of its pressure (p) and volume (V): H = U + pV. It is a state function used in many measurements in chemical, biological and physical systems at constant pressure.

Definition and change

For a process at constant pressure, the change in enthalpy ΔH equals the heat absorbed or released by the system: ΔH = qp. Exothermic reactions have ΔH < 0 and endothermic reactions ΔH > 0.

Applications

Standard enthalpies of formation, combustion and reaction (via Hess's law) underpin thermochemistry. Enthalpy changes accompany phase transitions (fusion, vaporisation) and mixing. In engineering, enthalpy is central to steam tables and the analysis of turbines, compressors and refrigeration cycles.

Relation to other potentials

Enthalpy is the Legendre transform of internal energy appropriate when pressure, rather than volume, is the natural variable. Its differential is dH = TdS + Vdp for a closed system at equilibrium composition.
