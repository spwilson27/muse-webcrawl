Chinese mathematics

Counting rods and the Nine Chapters tradition

Chinese mathematics developed largely independently of other traditions and produced major achievements in arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and number theory over more than two millennia. Chinese mathematicians used a decimal place-value system with counting rods, worked with negative numbers by the Han dynasty, and developed methods for solving systems of linear equations, extracting roots, and computing π to high precision.

Early period

The foundational text is The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art (Jiuzhang Suanshu), compiled by the Han dynasty (around the 1st century CE), which presents 246 practical problems covering agriculture, engineering, surveying, and taxation, together with procedures including Gaussian-like elimination for linear systems and the rule for right triangles (gougu, the Pythagorean relation). Liu Hui's 263 CE commentary gave proofs and computed π to 3.14159; Zu Chongzhi (5th century) improved this to seven digits, a record that stood for nearly a thousand years.

Song and Yuan peak

During the Song and Yuan dynasties, Chinese algebra reached its classical zenith: Qin Jiushao's Mathematical Treatise in Nine Sections (1247) presented the Chinese remainder theorem and a method equivalent to Horner's for polynomial equations; Yang Hui, Li Ye, and Zhu Shijie (whose "four unknowns" method handled systems of polynomial equations) extended these techniques. The magic squares and combinatorial results in Yang Hui's works include the triangle known in the West as Pascal's triangle, described in China centuries earlier.

Later period

From the 17th century, Jesuit missionaries introduced European mathematics, and Chinese scholars such as Mei Wending synthesized the two traditions. In the 20th century, China reintegrated with international mathematics, producing notable figures including Hua Luogeng and Shiing-Shen Chern.
