Mount Huang (Chinese: 黄山; pinyin: Huángshān), known in English as the Yellow Mountains, is a granite mountain range in southern Anhui province, eastern China. Renowned for its scenery of jagged peaks, oddly shaped pines, hot springs and "sea of clouds", it is one of China's most celebrated landscapes and a frequent subject of traditional Chinese painting and literature.

Geography

The range comprises many peaks, 77 exceeding 1,000 metres; the three highest are Lotus Peak (1,864 m), Bright Summit and Celestial Capital Peak. The mountains were formed about 100 million years ago and were sculpted by glaciers during the Quaternary period, producing the characteristic pillar-like peaks.

Culture and tourism

Originally called Mount Yishan, it was renamed for the legendary Yellow Emperor (Huangdi), who is said to have ascended to heaven from its slopes. Mount Huang inspired generations of artists and the Shanshui school of painting. Cable cars, thousands of stone steps and cliffside hotels now serve millions of visitors annually; the area was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1990.
