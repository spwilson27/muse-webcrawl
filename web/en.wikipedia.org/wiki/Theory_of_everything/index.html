Theory of everything

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Collage-style illustration of equations from relativity and quantum mechanics converging]
A theory of everything would unify relativity and quantum theory.

A theory of everything (TOE) is a hypothetical physical theory that fully explains and links together all aspects of the universe. Finding such a theory is one of the major unsolved problems in physics: the two pillars of modern physics, general relativity (governing the very large) and quantum mechanics with the Standard Model (governing the very small), are mutually incompatible in their present forms.

A candidate theory of everything must quantise gravity (or otherwise merge the frameworks), reproduce the Standard Model's particle spectrum and couplings, and make testable predictions. String theory is the most developed candidate; Loop quantum gravity addresses quantised gravity without unification into the other forces. Einstein spent his last three decades seeking a classical unified field theory, in vain; the phrase 'theory of everything' popularised in the 1980s has been used ironically by sceptics (Stephen Hawking himself, who championed the quest in A Brief History of Time, later argued Gödel's theorem may forbid a finite complete formulation). Whether a TOE, if found, would explain everything in the everyday sense — chemistry, biology, consciousness — is emphatically doubted even by its seekers: it would be a theory of the fundamental laws, not of their emergent consequences.

Requirements and status

Any TOE must explain the ~19 free parameters of the Standard Model (masses, couplings, mixing angles) or show them as environmental; it must resolve black-hole information and the Big Bang singularity; and it must survive contact with experiment — the discipline that has so far eliminated every concrete proposal except the structurally flexible string framework and the conservative loop quantisation. The problem remains open, a century after it was posed.
