Lungfish

Scientific classification
KingdomAnimalia
ClassSarcopterygii
SubclassDipnoi
Living generaNeoceratodus, Protopterus, Lepidosiren

Lungfish are freshwater lobe-finned fishes belonging to the subclass Dipnoi. They are best known for retaining primitive traits, including the ability to breathe air via lungs, and fleshy paired fins. Six species survive in Africa, South America and Australia.

Evolution

Lungfish were widespread in the Devonian. Today the Australian lungfish (Neoceratodus forsteri) retains large overlapping scales and a single lung, while African (Protopterus) and South American (Lepidosiren paradoxa) species have paired lungs and thread-like fins. Genomic studies show lungfish are the closest living fish relatives of tetrapods.

Behaviour

African lungfish can survive seasonal drought by aestivating in mud cocoons for months, breathing air. Lungfish are omnivorous, eating molluscs, crustaceans and plant matter.
