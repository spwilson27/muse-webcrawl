Convolution

In mathematics (in particular functional analysis), convolution is an operation on two functions f and g that produces a third function expressing how the shape of one is modified by the other. For functions defined on the real line it is the integral

(f ∗ g)(t) = ∫ f(τ) g(t − τ) dτ,

an integral over all shifts, which can be pictured as flipping one function, sliding it across the other, and accumulating the area of overlap. The discrete analogue replaces the integral by a sum and underlies polynomial multiplication and digital filtering.

Properties

Convolution is commutative, associative and distributive over addition, and the Dirac delta acts as its identity. The convolution theorem states that the Fourier transform of a convolution is the pointwise product of the Fourier transforms, allowing convolution to be computed efficiently via the fast Fourier transform.

Applications

Convolution describes the response of linear time-invariant systems to inputs, the distribution of sums of independent random variables in probability, blurring and edge detection in image processing, and the receptive-field layers of convolutional neural networks in machine learning.
