Ferenc Puskás, captain of Hungary's "Golden Team"

Ferenc Puskás (1 April 1927 – 17 November 2006) was a Hungarian footballer and manager, widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. A prolific forward, he scored 84 goals in 85 international matches for Hungary and later played for Spain. He was the captain and star of Hungary's celebrated "Mighty Magyars" team of the 1950s.

Career

Puskás began his career at Budapest Honvéd, where he won five Hungarian league titles. Hungary's national team, built around Honvéd players, went unbeaten for four years, won the gold medal at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, and famously defeated England 6–3 at Wembley in 1953 and 7–1 in Budapest in 1954. At the 1954 World Cup, Hungary reached the final but lost 3–2 to West Germany in the "Miracle of Bern"; Puskás, playing with an ankle injury, scored in the final and had a late equaliser disallowed.

After the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, Puskás refused to return home and eventually joined Real Madrid in 1958, aged 31. With Alfredo Di Stéfano he formed one of the most feared forward lines in history, winning three European Cups and five La Liga titles. In the 1960 European Cup Final against Eintracht Frankfurt he scored four goals in a 7–3 victory.

Later life and legacy

After retiring, Puskás coached clubs around the world, most notably leading Panathinaikos to the 1971 European Cup Final. He returned to Hungary after the fall of communism and died in Budapest in 2006. FIFA's Puskás Award, given annually for the most beautiful goal, and Budapest's Puskás Aréna are named in his honour.
