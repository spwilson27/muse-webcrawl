Type 90 (90式戦車)
Type: Main battle tank
Place of origin: Japan
In service: 1990–present
Designer/Manufacturer: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries
Produced: 1990–2009 (341 built)
Main armament: 120 mm L/44 smoothbore gun

The Type 90 (90式戦車, Kyūmarushiki sensha) is a main battle tank of the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF). Developed and built by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries in cooperation with the Japanese Defense Agency's Technical Research and Development Institute, it entered service in 1990 as the successor to the Type 74. At the time of its introduction it was among the most advanced and most expensive production tanks in the world.

Design

The Type 90 is armed with a licence-produced Rheinmetall 120 mm L/44 smoothbore gun, the same weapon fitted to later versions of the Leopard 2 and the M1A1 Abrams. A bustle-mounted automatic loader developed by Mitsubishi allowed the crew to be reduced to three men and the turret to be made smaller. The tank uses modular composite armour over the turret and hull front, a Mitsubishi 10ZG diesel engine of 1,500 horsepower, and a hydropneumatic suspension inherited in concept from the Type 74. Its fire-control system included a laser rangefinder, thermal imaging, and automatic target tracking for the gunner.

Service

Production ran from 1990 to 2009, with 341 vehicles completed. Because of its weight of about 50 tonnes and Japan's road and bridge limits, the Type 90 was deployed mainly with units in Hokkaido and at the Fuji School, rather than throughout the country; this constraint was a major reason for developing the lighter Type 10. The Type 90 remains in service alongside the Type 10, and formed the basis of the Type 90 armoured recovery vehicle.
