Banana slug

GenusAriolimax
FamilyAriolimacidae
RangePacific coastal forests of North America, from Alaska to California
LengthUp to about 25 cm (9.8 in)

Banana slugs are North American terrestrial slugs of the genus Ariolimax, named for their resemblance to a ripe banana. They are among the largest land slugs in the world, reaching lengths of up to about 25 cm, and are commonly bright yellow, though green, brown and white individuals occur. Banana slugs inhabit the moist forest floors of the Pacific coast from southeastern Alaska to central California.

Biology

Banana slugs are detritivores, feeding on fallen leaves, animal droppings, fungi and carrion, and they play an important role in nutrient cycling and in dispersing the seeds and spores of forest plants. Their mucus serves both for locomotion and defence: it can numb the tongues of predators, and its consistency changes with water content. The slugs breathe through a pneumostome on the right side of the mantle. Like other land slugs they are simultaneous hermaphrodites; mating sometimes ends in apophallation, in which one partner gnaws off the other's penis.

Species

Recognised species include Ariolimax californicus (California banana slug), A. columbianus (Pacific banana slug) and A. dolichophallus (slender banana slug).

In culture

The banana slug is the mascot of the University of California, Santa Cruz, where "Sammy the Banana Slug" is a well-known symbol of the campus's unconventional character.
