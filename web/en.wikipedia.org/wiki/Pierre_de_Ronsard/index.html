Pierre de Ronsard

Pierre de Ronsard (11 September 1524 – 27 December 1585) was a French poet, the leader of the Pléiade group and the most celebrated French poet of the Renaissance. He was called the "prince of poets" in his lifetime, and his love sonnets — above all the famous injunction "Cueillez dès aujourd'hui les roses de la vie" ("Gather today the roses of life") — remain staples of French literature.

Life

Ronsard was born at the manor of La Possonnière in the Vendômois. He served as a page at court and travelled to Scotland and elsewhere in the train of French diplomacy, but increasing deafness in the early 1540s ended hopes of a courtier's career, and he turned to study and poetry. Under the Hellenist Jean Dorat at the Collège de Coqueret in Paris, he joined the circle of young poets — including Joachim du Bellay — who in 1549 formed the Pléiade, pledged to enrich the French language by imitating Greek, Latin and Italian models. Du Bellay's manifesto, La Défense et illustration de la langue française (1549), announced the programme.

Works

Ronsard's Odes (1550), modelled on Pindar and Horace, were followed by the Amours (1552), a sonnet sequence inspired by Cassandre Salviati in the manner of Petrarch. He became court poet to a succession of Valois kings, wrote the Hymnes (1555–56), and during the Wars of Religion defended the Catholic royal cause in his Discours (1562–63). His projected national epic, La Franciade (1572), was left unfinished and is generally judged a failure. His last great collection, the Sonnets pour Hélène (1578), written for Hélène de Surgères, contains the celebrated sonnet "Quand vous serez bien vieille".

Reputation

Ronsard's fame faded in the seventeenth century, when Malherbe and the classicists censured his exuberance, but the Romantics revived him in the nineteenth century, and he is now recognised as the central French poet of the sixteenth century.
