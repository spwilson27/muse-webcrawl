Roxana

Roxana (Greek: Ῥωξάνη, from Old Iranian Raušnā, "shining, radiant"; died c. 310 BC) was a Bactrian or Sogdian princess, the daughter of the nobleman Oxyartes, and the first wife of Alexander the Great.

Life

Alexander captured the Sogdian Rock in 327 BC, where Roxana was among the prisoners; according to the sources he fell in love with her and married her, a union also serving to reconcile the local aristocracy to Macedonian rule. She accompanied Alexander on his Indian campaign and bore him a posthumous son, Alexander IV, born after the king's death at Babylon in 323 BC.

Death

During the Wars of the Diadochi, Roxana and her son were placed under the protection of Cassander, who eventually had them murdered at Amphipolis around 310 BC, eliminating a rival claim to the Macedonian throne. Her story inspired Handel's opera Alessandro and Daniel Defoe's novel Roxana (1724), whose heroine takes her name.
