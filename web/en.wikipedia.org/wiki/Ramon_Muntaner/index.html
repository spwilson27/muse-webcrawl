Ramon Muntaner

Born1265, Peralada, Crown of Aragon
Died1336, Ibiza
OccupationSoldier, administrator, chronicler
Notable workCrònica (Chronicle)

Ramon Muntaner (1265–1336) was a Catalan soldier and writer from Peralada in the Crown of Aragon. He served as an administrator and soldier with the Catalan Company, the mercenary army of almogàvers led first by Roger de Flor, in its campaigns in Sicily and the Byzantine Empire.

The Chronicle

Muntaner is best known for his Crònica, written in Catalan between 1325 and 1328, one of the four great chronicles of medieval Catalan literature (together with those of James I, Bernat Desclot and Peter IV). It recounts the history of the Crown of Aragon from the birth of James I to the coronation of Alfonso IV, and gives an eyewitness account of the Catalan Company's expedition to the east, including the conquest of the duchies of Athens and Neopatras.

Life

After years of service in the Mediterranean, Muntaner settled on Ibiza, where he served as a jurat (municipal officer) and died in 1336.
