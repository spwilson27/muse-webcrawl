Electric blues

Electric blues
Stylistic originsDelta blues, Chicago blues, jump blues
Cultural origins1940s, United States
InstrumentsElectric guitar, harmonica, bass, drums

Electric blues is any type of blues music distinguished by the use of electric amplification for musical instruments. Pioneered in Texas and Chicago in the 1940s, it transformed acoustic Delta and country blues into louder urban styles that laid foundations for rhythm and blues and rock and roll.

History

T-Bone Walker was an early innovator of the amplified guitar solo. After World War II, migrants to Chicago such as Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, and Little Walter electrified Delta blues at Chess Records, adding amplified harmonica, piano, bass, and drums, in a style known as Chicago blues.

British blues boom

In the 1960s British bands — the Rolling Stones, Yardbirds, Led Zeppelin, and guitarists such as Eric Clapton — popularized electric blues internationally, feeding back into American blues rock.

Notable artists

B.B. King, Freddie King, Albert King, Buddy Guy, Stevie Ray Vaughan, and Robert Cray represent later generations of electric blues guitarists.
