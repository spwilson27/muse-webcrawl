Kanagawa Prefecture

Kanagawa Prefecture (Japanese: 神奈川県, Kanagawa-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Kantō region on the island of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Yokohama. With a population of over nine million, it is the second most populous prefecture in Japan and forms part of the Greater Tokyo Area, bordering Tokyo to the northeast, Shizuoka to the west, and Yamanashi to the northwest.

Geography

Kanagawa's coast runs along Tokyo Bay and Sagami Bay on the Pacific. The western part of the prefecture is mountainous, including the Tanzawa range and Hakone, a famous volcanic hot-spring resort area within Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park. The Miura Peninsula separates the two bays, and Enoshima and Kamakura lie along the Shōnan coast. Major cities include Yokohama, Kawasaki, Sagamihara, Yokosuka, Fujisawa, Kamakura, and Odawara.

History

The region corresponds largely to the former Sagami Province and part of Musashi Province. Kamakura was Japan's de facto capital under the Kamakura shogunate (1185–1333). In 1854 Commodore Matthew Perry concluded the Convention of Kanagawa at Yokohama, opening Japan to American trade; Yokohama grew into the country's principal treaty port. The prefecture was also the scene of Hokusai's famous woodblock print The Great Wave off Kanagawa, showing boats off the prefectural coast with Mount Fuji behind.

Economy and transport

Kanagawa is a major industrial and research zone — the Keihin industrial belt along Tokyo Bay combines steel, chemicals, electronics, and automotive manufacturing — and a commuter region for Tokyo, linked by the Tōkaidō Shinkansen and dense private railway networks. It hosted sailing events of the 2020 Tokyo Olympics at Enoshima and several matches of the 2019 Rugby World Cup in Yokohama.
