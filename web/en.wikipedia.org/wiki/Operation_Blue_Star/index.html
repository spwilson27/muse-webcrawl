Operation Blue Star

Operation Blue Star was the code name of a military operation by the Indian Army, conducted between 1 and 10 June 1984, to remove Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale and his armed followers from the Golden Temple complex (Harmandir Sahib) in Amritsar, Punjab. Bhindranwale, a Sikh religious leader, had occupied and fortified the complex, including the Akal Takht, with armed militants.

Background

The operation took place against the backdrop of the Punjab insurgency and the Dharam Yudh Morcha movement, which demanded greater autonomy for Punjab and implementation of the Anandpur Sahib Resolution. Tensions between the Indian government under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi and militant groups escalated through the early 1980s.

The operation

Indian Army troops assaulted the temple complex beginning on the night of 5 June, encountering fierce resistance from well-armed defenders. Heavy fighting caused extensive damage to the Akal Takht and other buildings in the sacred complex, and coincided with a major religious festival that drew many pilgrims, contributing to a high civilian death toll. Bhindranwale and many of his followers were killed. Casualty figures remain disputed, with official and unofficial estimates varying widely.

Aftermath

The operation provoked outrage among many Sikhs worldwide. On 31 October 1984, Indira Gandhi was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, which in turn triggered anti-Sikh riots in Delhi and elsewhere in which thousands of Sikhs were killed. Operation Blue Star remains one of the most controversial episodes in modern Indian history and a lasting source of grievance in Sikh politics.
