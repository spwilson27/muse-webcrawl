Legal drama


A courtroom confrontation

A legal drama, also called a courtroom drama, is a subgenre of dramatic fiction in film, television, theatre, and literature that generally focuses on narratives regarding legal practice and the justice system. The dramatic core of the genre is frequently a trial, in which the conflict between prosecution and defence plays out before a judge or jury, though legal dramas also follow lawyers' work outside the courtroom — investigation, negotiation, and ethical dilemmas.

Characteristics

Legal dramas commonly depict courtroom procedure in stylised form: opening statements, examination and cross-examination of witnesses, dramatic revelations of evidence, and summing up to a verdict. The genre has long served as a vehicle for exploring social and moral questions, from capital punishment to civil rights, through the adversarial contest.

Notable examples

In film, classic examples include 12 Angry Men (1957), To Kill a Mockingbird (1962), and A Few Good Men (1992). On television, series such as Perry Mason, L.A. Law, Law & Order, and The Practice defined the genre for successive generations. In literature, the novels of John Grisham popularised the legal thriller, a closely related form.

Accuracy

Real trials are often criticised as far less dramatic than their fictional counterparts; commentators note the so-called "CSI effect" and the tendency of legal drama to compress procedure and overstate the frequency of surprise witnesses and confessions on the stand.
