Kennel club

Kennel clubs maintain breed standards and run conformation shows such as Crufts.

A kennel club (known as a kennel council or canine council in some countries) is an organisation for canine affairs concerned with the breeding, showing and promotion of purebred dogs. Kennel clubs typically maintain a stud book and pedigree registry, publish breed standards, license dog shows and trials, and set rules for their sport.

History

The first such body, The Kennel Club of the United Kingdom, was founded in 1873 and organised the first stud book and the shows that became Crufts. The American Kennel Club followed in 1884, the Canadian Kennel Club in 1888, and many other national clubs thereafter. Most national clubs outside the United States belong to the international Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI), founded in 1911, which coordinates mutual recognition of pedigrees and standards.

Activities and criticism

Clubs register litters, recognise breeds (grouping them as sporting, working, terrier, toy and so on, or by the FCI's ten groups), and promote responsible breeding. They have also been criticised — notably in the 2008 documentary Pedigree Dogs Exposed — for breed standards that reward extreme conformation at the expense of health and for tolerating inbreeding, prompting reforms in several countries.
