Interference fit

An interference fit, also known as a press fit or friction fit, is a form of fastening between two tight-fitting mating parts that produces a joint held together by friction after the parts are pushed together, rather than by a separate fastener.

Description

In an interference fit, the external dimension of one part (such as a shaft) is deliberately made slightly larger than the internal dimension of the mating part (such as a hole or hub). Assembling the parts requires force, heating of the outer part, cooling of the inner part, or a combination; when assembled, the elastic deformation of both parts creates high contact pressure and hence friction that resists relative motion.

Applications

Interference fits are used for mounting bearings on shafts, fixing gear wheels and pulleys to axles, assembling railway wheels onto axles, and in shrink-fitting applications. The amount of interference must be chosen carefully: too little allows slippage, while too much can overstress or crack the components.
