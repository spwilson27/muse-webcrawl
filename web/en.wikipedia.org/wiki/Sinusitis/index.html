Sinusitis

Sinusitis, also known as rhinosinusitis, is inflammation of the mucous membranes lining the paranasal sinuses and nasal cavity. Symptoms include a blocked or runny nose, facial pain or pressure, reduced sense of smell, thick nasal discharge, cough and fever.

Causes and classification

Sinusitis is classified as acute (under 4 weeks), subacute, chronic (over 12 weeks) or recurrent. Most acute cases follow a viral upper respiratory infection; a minority develop secondary bacterial infection. Chronic sinusitis is associated with allergies, nasal polyps, asthma and anatomical factors.

Treatment

Viral sinusitis usually resolves without antibiotics; supportive care includes saline irrigation, intranasal corticosteroids and analgesics. Antibiotics are reserved for severe, worsening or prolonged bacterial cases. Surgery (functional endoscopic sinus surgery) may be used for chronic disease that does not respond to medical therapy. Rare complications include orbital and intracranial spread of infection.
