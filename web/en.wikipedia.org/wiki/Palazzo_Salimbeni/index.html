Palazzo Salimbeni

Palazzo Salimbeni is a historic Gothic palace in Siena, Tuscany, Italy, overlooking the Piazza Salimbeni. It is the historic headquarters of the Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena, the world's oldest surviving bank, founded in 1472.

LocationPiazza Salimbeni, Siena, Italy
StyleSienese Gothic, with neo-Gothic restoration
Built14th century; restored 19th century
OccupantBanca Monte dei Paschi di Siena

History

The palace was built in the 14th century on the foundations of earlier structures belonging to the Salimbeni family, one of Siena's great merchant-banking families, incorporating an earlier castle-like stronghold at the rear. In 1472 the Monte di Pietà — the origin of the Monte dei Paschi bank — was established in the building complex.

Architecture

The facade facing Piazza Salimbeni is notable for its neo-Gothic restoration carried out in the 19th century by the architect Giuseppe Partini, who regularised the crenellations and triple-light windows in the Sienese Gothic manner. In the square stands a statue of the economist and priest Sallustio Bandini, carved in 1882. The palace houses an important collection of Sienese art and the bank's historic archive and museum.
