Kanpur (Hindi: कानपुर), historically spelled Cawnpore, is a major industrial city in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh, situated on the banks of the Ganges. It is one of the largest cities of northern India and an important centre for the leather, textile and chemical industries, long nicknamed the "Leather City of India" and, in its industrial heyday, the "Manchester of the East".

History

Kanpur grew around a British garrison town established after the area came under East India Company control in 1801. It became infamous in the Indian Rebellion of 1857, when the besieged British garrison under General Wheeler surrendered to Nana Sahib's forces and many were killed at Satichaura Ghat and the Bibighar — events commemorated by the Memorial Church and memorial well. The city later industrialised rapidly with cotton mills, woollen mills and tanneries.

Economy and education

The leather industry, centred on tanneries along the Ganges, is a major employer and exporter, though it has faced sustained scrutiny over river pollution. Kanpur is home to IIT Kanpur, one of India's premier engineering institutes (established 1959), along with CSJM University, the Harcourt Butler Technical University, and research institutes such as the National Sugar Institute. The Kanpur Metro opened in 2021.

Geography

The city lies on the southern bank of the Ganges, about 90 km southwest of Lucknow, with the Pandu River to its south. Its climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers, a monsoon season, and cool, foggy winters; air quality in winter ranks among India's poorest.
