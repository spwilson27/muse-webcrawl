Rwanda

Rwanda, officially the Republic of Rwanda, is a landlocked country in the Great Rift Valley of East Africa, where the African Great Lakes region and Southeast Africa converge. Known as the 'land of a thousand hills', it has a population of about 14 million — one of Africa's densest — and its capital is Kigali.

Rwanda is known above all for the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, in which about 800,000 people were killed in 100 days while the world failed to intervene. Since then, under President Paul Kagame, Rwanda has achieved notable development, order and anti-corruption results — and is criticised for political repression and for backing the M23 rebellion in neighbouring DRC (which it denies). Mountain gorilla trekking in Volcanoes National Park is its signature tourism draw; Kigali is noted as one of Africa's cleanest capitals, with monthly community cleaning (umuganda) and a ban on plastic bags.
