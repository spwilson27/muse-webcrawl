Fugitive

A fugitive or runaway is a person fleeing from custody, whether it be from private slavery, a government arrest, government or non-government questioning, vigilante violence, or outraged private individuals. A fugitive from justice, also known as a wanted person, is a person either convicted or accused of a crime and hiding from law enforcement in the state or taking refuge in a different country in order to avoid arrest.

Terminology and legal status

In the United States, a person who crosses state lines to avoid prosecution may be charged under federal law with unlawful flight to avoid prosecution, and the FBI maintains lists such as the Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, first published in 1950. Bounty hunters and bail enforcement agents may pursue fugitives who have skipped bail in jurisdictions that permit the practice. Internationally, fugitives may seek asylum, and their return is usually governed by extradition treaties between states.

Fugitives from slavery

Historically, the term was applied to people escaping enslavement. In the United States, the Fugitive Slave Acts of 1793 and 1850 required the return of escaped enslaved people to their enslavers, a policy resisted by abolitionists and by the network known as the Underground Railroad. The struggle over fugitive slaves was one of the central political conflicts preceding the American Civil War.

In popular culture

The figure of the fugitive is a staple of film and television, notably The Fugitive (1963–1967 television series; 1993 film), in which Dr. Richard Kimble hunts the "one-armed man" while evading capture for a murder he did not commit.
