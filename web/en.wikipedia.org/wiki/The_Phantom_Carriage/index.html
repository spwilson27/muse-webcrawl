The Phantom Carriage

The Phantom Carriage (Swedish: Körkarlen, literally "The Wagoner") is a 1921 Swedish silent fantasy film directed by and starring Victor Sjöström, based on the 1912 novel Thy Soul Shall Bear Witness! by Selma Lagerlöf.

Plot

According to legend, the last person to die each year must drive Death's phantom carriage for the following year, collecting the souls of the dead. On New Year's Eve, the drunken, violent David Holm is struck dead in a brawl at the stroke of midnight. The carriage driver — his old friend Georges, whose own corruption led Holm astray — forces Holm's soul to review his ruined life in a nested series of flashbacks: his descent into alcoholism, his cruelty to his wife and children, and the selfless Salvation Army sister Edit, dying of tuberculosis contracted in his company, who still hopes for his redemption. Confronted with Edit's death and his family's despair, Holm repents, is restored to life, and vows to change.

Technique and influence

The film is celebrated for its complex flashback-within-flashback structure and its pioneering special effects, notably the double-exposure photography that renders the ghostly carriage and transparent souls. It is a cornerstone of Swedish silent cinema and was a profound influence on Ingmar Bergman, who cast Sjöström in Wild Strawberries (1957) and paid homage to the film in The Seventh Seal; the scene of a man axing through a door was echoed in The Shining.

Legacy

The Phantom Carriage has been ranked among the greatest Swedish films ever made and appears in reference works on the thousand essential films. It has been restored several times and screened with new scores, including electronic and orchestral accompaniments.
