The Siege of Jerusalem (587 BC) was the final Babylonian campaign against the Kingdom of Judah. King Zedekiah, installed by Nebuchadnezzar II after the earlier siege of 597 BC, rebelled against Babylon and sought Egyptian support. Nebuchadnezzar's army besieged Jerusalem for about eighteen months, beginning in 589 BC, reducing the city by famine.

Fall of the city

In the summer of 587 BC (dated by some scholars to 586 BC) the Babylonians breached the walls. Zedekiah fled toward Jericho but was captured; he was forced to watch the execution of his sons, then blinded and taken in chains to Babylon. About a month later, the Babylonian captain Nebuzaradan burned the city, destroyed Solomon's Temple, carried off its treasures and vessels, demolished the walls, and deported much of the population — officials, craftsmen, and the poor who remained were left under a governor, Gedaliah, at Mizpah.

Significance

The destruction of the First Temple and the exile to Babylon are among the defining catastrophes of Jewish history, commemorated by the fast of Tisha B'Av. The Babylonian captivity profoundly shaped the development of Judaism and the editing of the Hebrew Bible. Judah ceased to exist as a kingdom, becoming the Babylonian province of Yehud.
