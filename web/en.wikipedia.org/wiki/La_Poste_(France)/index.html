La Poste (France)

La Poste is a French company providing postal services, banking, insurance, and digital services. It is the successor to the state PTT administration and France's designated universal postal service provider. Through subsidiaries including GeoPost/DPDgroup (parcels) and La Banque Postale, the La Poste group is one of France's largest employers.

History

Royal postal relays were established in France under Louis XI in 1477, and a state postal service for the public followed in 1576. The administration of Posts, Telegraphs and Telephones (PTT) ran the service for much of the 20th century. In the 1991 reforms the PTT was split into France Télécom and La Poste, which became an autonomous public operator. In 2010 La Poste became a public limited company, and in 2020 the Caisse des dépôts et consignations became its majority shareholder in a deal that also brought CNP Assurances into the group.

Activities

La Poste operates roughly 17,000 postal contact points and delivers mail six days a week across France. Its banking arm, La Banque Postale, was created in 2006 from the postal financial services. The parcel arm GeoPost, operating under the DPD, Chronopost, and Seur brands, is one of Europe's largest parcel networks. Facing secular decline in letter volumes, the group has expanded into local services, digital identity, health logistics, and e-commerce.
