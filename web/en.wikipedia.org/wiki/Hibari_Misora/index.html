Hibari Misora

Hibari Misora (美空 ひばり, Misora Hibari, born Kazue Katō, 29 May 1937 – 24 June 1989) was a Japanese singer, actress and cultural icon. Widely regarded as one of the greatest Japanese singers of all time, she recorded some 1,200 songs, sold an estimated 68 million records, and appeared in 166 films.

Career

Born in Yokohama, Misora displayed remarkable vocal talent as a child and made her singing debut in 1945 at the age of eight. She became a sensation with her film debut in Nodojiman-kyō jidai (1949) and the hit song "Kappa Boogie Woogie". Through the 1950s she was one of Japan's biggest film stars, appearing in the Sannin Musume ("Three Daughters") series with Chiemi Eri and Yukio Hashi-era contemporaries. Her signature songs include "Ringo Oiwake" (1952), "Yawara" (1964), which won the Japan Record Award, and "Kawa no Nagare no Yō ni" (1989), her final recording, frequently ranked the greatest Japanese song of all time.

Legacy

Misora died of pneumonia in 1989 at age 52. She was the first woman to receive the People's Honour Award posthumously, and her voice was widely mourned as a symbol of postwar Japan. In 2019, an AI recreation of her voice was used for a "comeback" performance on NHK's Kōhaku Uta Gassen, sparking debate about digital resurrection of performers.
