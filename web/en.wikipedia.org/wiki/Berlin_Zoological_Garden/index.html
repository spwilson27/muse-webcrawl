Berlin Zoological Garden

The Elephant Gate (Elefantentor), the zoo's ornate main entrance.

The Berlin Zoological Garden (German: Zoologischer Garten Berlin) is the oldest surviving zoo in Germany and one of the most visited in Europe. Opened in 1844 in what is now the Tiergarten district, it presents one of the most comprehensive collections of species of any zoo in the world, and is paired with the Aquarium Berlin, which shares its grounds.

History

The zoo grew out of the royal menageries of the Prussian kings. On the initiative of the explorer Alexander von Humboldt and the zoologist Martin Lichtenstein, King Frederick William IV donated animals from the menagerie on Pfaueninsel and the pheasantry in the Tiergarten, and the zoo opened on 1 August 1844 as the first zoo in Germany. Under director Heinrich Bodinus and, above all, Ludwig Heck (director 1888–1931), it acquired famous showpiece houses in exotic styles, of which the Elephant Gate and the antelope house survive.

The zoo was devastated in the Second World War: of some 3,700 animals in 1939, only 91 survived bombing and the Battle of Berlin. Under Katharina Heinroth, the first woman to direct a German zoo, and Heinz-Georg Klös, it was rebuilt into a landscape-style zoo and regained international standing. During Berlin's division it was the zoo of West Berlin, while the Tierpark was built in the east; since reunification the two have cooperated closely.

Collection and pandas

The zoo is home to roughly 20,000 animals of about 1,300 species across 33 hectares, including hippopotamuses, gorillas and polar bears — Knut, the polar bear cub hand-raised at the zoo in 2006–07, became a worldwide sensation. In 2017 the zoo received the giant pandas Meng Meng and Jiao Qing on loan from China; their twin cubs, born in 2019, were the first giant pandas born in Germany, and further twins were born in 2024. The Aquarium Berlin, opened in 1913, displays fish, reptiles, amphibians and invertebrates.
