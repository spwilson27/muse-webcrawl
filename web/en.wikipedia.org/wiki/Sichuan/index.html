Sichuan

Sichuan (Chinese: 四川; pinyin: Sìchuān) is a landlocked province in southwestern China, occupying most of the Sichuan Basin and the easternmost part of the Tibetan Plateau. Its capital and largest city is Chengdu. With a population of roughly 84 million, Sichuan is among China's most populous provinces, and it is famed for its spicy cuisine, its giant panda sanctuaries, and its long history as a rich agricultural heartland nicknamed the "Land of Abundance" (天府之国, Tiānfǔ zhī Guó).

History

The region was home to the ancient kingdoms of Shu and Ba. The remarkable Bronze Age sites of Sanxingdui and Jinsha near Chengdu have revealed a sophisticated bronze-casting culture distinct from that of the Yellow River valley. The Qin state conquered Shu and Ba in 316 BCE, and in 256 BCE the Qin engineer Li Bing built the Dujiangyan irrigation system, which still waters the Chengdu Plain today. During periods of division, Sichuan repeatedly hosted independent regimes, including Shu Han in the Three Kingdoms era.

Geography

Western Sichuan rises into the Hengduan Mountains and the edge of the Tibetan Plateau, with peaks such as Mount Gongga (7,556 m), the province's highest point. The east is dominated by the fertile, densely populated Sichuan Basin, ringed by mountains. The Yangtze River and its tributaries, including the Min and Jialing, drain the province. Sichuan was struck by a devastating magnitude-8.0 earthquake on 12 May 2008, centred near Wenchuan, which killed tens of thousands.

Culture and cuisine

Sichuan cuisine is one of the great regional cuisines of China, celebrated for the numbing-spicy (málà) flavour of Sichuan pepper combined with chillies, in dishes such as mapo tofu, kung pao chicken, and hot pot. Chengdu was named a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2010. The province is also the natural home of the giant panda; the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding and the Wolong reserves are major centres of panda conservation. Sichuan opera, famous for its face-changing (bian lian) art, remains a living tradition.

Economy

Sichuan is a major producer of rice, grain, silk, and medicinal herbs, and a growing centre for electronics, aerospace, and software, much of it concentrated in Chengdu and the Chengdu–Chongqing economic circle. Chongqing was administered as part of Sichuan until it became a separate direct-controlled municipality in 1997.
