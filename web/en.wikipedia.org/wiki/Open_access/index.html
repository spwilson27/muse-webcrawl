Open access (OA) is a set of principles and practices through which research outputs — chiefly peer-reviewed journal articles — are distributed online free of access charges and most copyright restrictions. It grew out of the scholarly community's frustration with rising subscription prices in academic publishing.

Models

Gold OA makes the published version free immediately, sometimes for an author fee; green OA relies on authors depositing manuscripts in repositories such as arXiv; diamond OA charges neither readers nor authors. The 2002 Budapest Open Access Initiative gave the movement its founding definition, and funder mandates such as Plan S have pushed OA toward a majority of new papers, all vetted through peer review as before.
