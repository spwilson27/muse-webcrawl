Rama I

Rama I
King of Siam
Reign: 6 April 1782 – 7 September 1809
Born: 20 March 1737, Ayutthaya
Died: 7 September 1809, Bangkok
House: Chakri dynasty

Rama I (20 March 1737 – 7 September 1809), born Thongduang, was the founder of the Chakri dynasty and the first monarch of the Rattanakosin Kingdom of Siam (now Thailand). He came to power following the deposition of King Taksin of Thonburi and founded the new capital city of Bangkok in 1782.

Early life

Thongduang was born in the Ayutthaya Kingdom to a family of Mon descent. He entered the service of King Taksin after the fall of Ayutthaya to the Burmese in 1767 and rose to become one of Taksin's leading generals, receiving the title Chao Phraya Chakri. He led successful campaigns that reunified Siam and expanded its influence into Laos, Cambodia and the Malay Peninsula.

Accession and reign

In 1782, after a revolt deposed Taksin, Thongduang was proclaimed king and took the regnal name Ramathibodi, later known posthumously as Rama I. He moved the capital across the Chao Phraya River to Bangkok, where he built the Grand Palace and the Temple of the Emerald Buddha (Wat Phra Kaew). His reign saw a major revival of Thai literature and law: he commissioned the recension of the Ramakien, the Thai version of the Ramayana, oversaw the compilation of legal codes, and sponsored the first complete revision of the Pali Buddhist canon, the Tripitaka, in 1788.

Wars and foreign relations

Rama I successfully defended Siam against the Burmese invasion of 1785–1786, known as the Nine Armies' War. Siamese influence over Cambodia and the Lao kingdoms was consolidated, and relations were maintained with Qing China through the tributary system.

Death and legacy

Rama I died on 7 September 1809 and was succeeded by his son, Rama II. He is remembered as the founder of modern Bangkok and of the ruling Chakri dynasty, whose current monarch is Rama X.
