The Camargue is a natural region in the Rhône delta in southern France, between the Mediterranean Sea and the two arms of the river Rhône — the Petit Rhône to the west and the Grand Rhône to the east. It is one of the largest river deltas in Europe and is famous for its marshes, lagoons (étangs), salt flats, and distinctive wildlife and traditions.

Flora and fauna

The wetland supports greater flamingos, which breed in one of Europe's few colonies here, along with hundreds of other bird species. The region is home to the Camargue horse, an ancient breed of white horse ridden by the gardians (cowboys), and to black Camargue bulls raised for the course camarguaise, a bloodless form of bull game in which ribbons and rosettes are snatched from the bull's horns.

Economy and culture

Salt has been produced around Aigues-Mortes and Salin-de-Giraud since Roman times; the pink salt pans remain a major industry. Rice has been grown in the northern delta since the 1940s. The Camargue is also a place of pilgrimage: Romani people gather each May at Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer for the feast of Saint Sarah.

Protection

Much of the delta forms the Camargue Regional Natural Park (established 1970) and a UNESCO biosphere reserve; the central wetland around the Étang de Vaccarès is a strict nature reserve.
