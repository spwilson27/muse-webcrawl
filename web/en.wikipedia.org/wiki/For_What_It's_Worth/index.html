For What It's Worth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"For What It's Worth" is a song written by Stephen Stills and recorded by the American rock band Buffalo Springfield. Released as a single in December 1966 and added to later pressings of the band's debut album, it reached No. 7 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became one of the defining songs of the 1960s counterculture era.

Background

Stills wrote the song after witnessing the Sunset Strip curfew riots in Los Angeles in November 1966, when clashes broke out between police and young people protesting a curfew and the planned closure of the Pandora's Box nightclub. The opening lines — "There's something happening here / What it is ain't exactly clear" — and the refrain "I think it's time we stop, children, what's that sound / Everybody look what's going down" gave the song a generality that led many listeners to take it as an anti–Vietnam War protest anthem, though Stills wrote it about the Strip disturbances.

Legacy

The song has been covered by dozens of artists, sampled in hip hop (notably by Public Enemy in "He Got Game"), and used in films such as Forrest Gump. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and is frequently ranked among the greatest songs of the era. Its title phrase comes from a remark of Stills' when presenting the song to his record label: he reportedly already had a title — "For what it's worth."
