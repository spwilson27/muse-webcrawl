Free tekno

A sound system wall at a teknival.

Free tekno, also known as tekno, freetekno or hardtek, is the music predominantly played at free parties in Europe. It is a fast, hard form of electronic dance music characterised by heavy, distorted kick drums, tempos typically between 150 and 185 beats per minute, and repetitive, often industrial or acid-influenced sounds. The spelling "tekno" with a "k" distinguishes the free-party scene from commercial techno.

History

The scene emerged in the early 1990s around travelling sound systems such as the British collective Spiral Tribe, who helped spread free parties — unlicensed gatherings often held in warehouses, fields, and squatted spaces — across Europe after crackdowns in the United Kingdom, including following the famous Castlemorton Common Festival of 1992 and the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994. Large multi-day gatherings, known as teknivals, became a fixture of the movement, particularly in France, the Czech Republic (CzechTek), Italy, and Spain.

Culture

Free tekno culture emphasises autonomy, DIY ethics, and non-commercial organisation: events are typically free or donation-based, locations are spread by word of mouth, and sound systems operate as semi-nomadic collectives. The movement has frequently clashed with authorities over noise, licensing, and land use, and several teknivals have been shut down by police.
