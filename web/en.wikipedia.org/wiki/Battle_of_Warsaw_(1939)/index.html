The Battle of Warsaw was fought between the Polish garrison and German forces for control of the Polish capital during the invasion of Poland in September 1939. After repelling the first German assault on 8–9 September, Warsaw endured a three-week siege of artillery bombardment and heavy air raids before capitulating on 28 September. The defence, led militarily by General Walerian Czuma and symbolised by Mayor Stefan Starzyński's radio broadcasts, became an emblem of Polish resistance.

Background

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939. German armoured spearheads of the 4th Panzer Division reached Warsaw's outskirts on 8 September, expecting the capital to fall quickly; Polish defences around Ochota and the western suburbs stopped the attack with significant German losses in tanks.

The siege

Reinforced by armies converging from the north and east, and joined by retreating Polish field forces, the city was encircled after the Battle of the Bzura ended. From mid-September the Luftwaffe and German artillery subjected Warsaw to intense bombardment — the heaviest raids came on 25 September ("Black Monday") — destroying waterworks, hospitals and whole districts and killing tens of thousands of civilians. With food, water and ammunition failing, and no relief possible after the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland on 17 September, the garrison negotiated surrender.

Aftermath

Warsaw capitulated on 28 September and German troops entered on 1 October. The city's ordeal under five years of occupation — the Ghetto Uprising of 1943 and the Warsaw Uprising of 1944, after which the Germans systematically destroyed the city — began with this battle.
