Path integration

Placeholder image

Path integration is the method thought to be used by animals for dead reckoning: continuously updating an internal representation of position relative to a starting point by integrating self-motion cues, without reference to external landmarks.

Mechanism

By summing the directions and distances traveled — using vestibular signals, proprioception, optic flow, and in some species compasses based on the sun or polarized light — an animal can compute the direct route back to its starting point, such as a nest or burrow. Desert ants of the genus Cataglyphis are a classic study subject: after a winding foraging trip, they run almost straight home.

Charles Darwin proposed the process in 1873, and it has since been demonstrated in many invertebrates and vertebrates, including humans, though errors accumulate over long or complex paths. The underlying neural machinery involves head-direction cells, grid cells, and place cells in the mammalian brain. See also dead reckoning and animal navigation.
