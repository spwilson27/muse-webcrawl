Maximum life span

Maximum life span (or maximum reported age at death) is a measure of the maximum amount of time one or more members of a population have been observed to survive. It is distinct from life expectancy and mean life span: maximum life span is an extreme value, typically the age of the oldest known individual of a species.

Humans

The longest verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who died at age 122 years and 164 days. The oldest verified man was Jiroemon Kimura of Japan (1897–2013), who died at 116 years and 54 days. Whether there is a fixed biological limit to human life is debated; some researchers argue for a limit near 115–125 years, while others contend observed plateaus in mortality at extreme ages leave the question open.

Other species

Maximum life spans vary enormously across life: certain tortoises and Greenland sharks live for centuries (the Greenland shark may exceed 300–500 years), ocean quahog clams have surpassed 500 years, and some trees, such as bristlecone pines, live for nearly 5,000 years. By contrast, many insects live only weeks. In laboratory organisms like the nematode C. elegans and mice, genetic and dietary interventions can extend maximum life span, a central topic of biogerontology.
