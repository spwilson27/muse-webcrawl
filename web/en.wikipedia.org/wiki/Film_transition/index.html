Film transition

A film transition is a technique used in the post-production process of film and video editing by which scenes or shots are combined. Most often this is through a normal cut to the next shot, but editors may instead join shots with dissolves, fades, wipes, and other devices that carry narrative or emotional meaning.

Common transitions

The cut is an instantaneous transition; variations include the cutaway, cross cut, jump cut, match cut, and the J- and L-cuts that offset sound from picture. A dissolve overlaps the end of one shot with the start of the next and often signals the passage of time. Fades to or from black conventionally open and close sequences. A wipe replaces one image with another along a moving edge — famously used in the Star Wars films — and other historical devices include iris shots and push transitions.

Use and style

Transitions were once made with optical printers and are now applied digitally in editing software. Contemporary practice favours the simple cut, reserving conspicuous transitions for deliberate stylistic or narrative effect — marking changes of location, time, point of view, or tone.
