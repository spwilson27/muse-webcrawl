Adventure (1980 video game)

Image placeholder: gameplay from Adventure

Adventure is an action-adventure video game developed by Warren Robinett and published by Atari, Inc. for the Atari 2600. Released in 1980, it is considered one of the first action-adventure games and the first console game in the fantasy genre, adapting the text adventure Colossal Cave Adventure into a graphical world.

Developer: Atari, Inc. (Warren Robinett)
Publisher: Atari, Inc.
Release: 1980
Platform: Atari 2600

Gameplay

The player controls a square avatar exploring a kingdom of castles and mazes to find an enchanted chalice and return it to the Golden Castle. Three dragons — Yorgle (yellow), Grundle (green) and the faster, more dangerous Rhindle (red) — hunt the player, while a bat steals and swaps carried items. Keys, a sword, a bridge and a magnet help solve the labyrinth across three game variations of increasing difficulty and randomness.

The Easter egg

Because Atari did not credit its programmers, Robinett hid a secret room containing the text "Created by Warren Robinett", reachable by carrying an invisible one-pixel "dot" to a specific wall. It is regarded as one of the earliest and most famous video game Easter eggs, and the search for it forms a central plot element of Ernest Cline's novel Ready Player One.

Legacy

Adventure sold about a million copies and influenced later action-adventure design, including the conventions of explorable worlds, inventory items and boss-like enemies. It is frequently included in lists of the greatest video games of all time.
