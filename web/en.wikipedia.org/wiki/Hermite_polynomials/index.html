Hermite polynomials

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Hermite polynomials are a classical orthogonal polynomial sequence named after the French mathematician Charles Hermite. They arise in probability (in connection with Gaussian distributions and Edgeworth series), in combinatorics, in numerical analysis (Gauss–Hermite quadrature), and in physics, where they give the eigenfunctions of the quantum harmonic oscillator.

Definitions

Two conventions are in use. The "probabilists' Hermite polynomials" are defined by Hen(x) = (-1)n ex²/2 (dn/dxn) e-x²/2, while the "physicists' Hermite polynomials" use Hn(x) = (-1)n ex² (dn/dxn) e-x². The first few physicists' polynomials are H0(x) = 1, H1(x) = 2x, H2(x) = 4x² − 2, H3(x) = 8x³ − 12x.

Properties

The Hermite polynomials form an orthogonal system with respect to the Gaussian weight function, satisfy a three-term recurrence relation and a second-order differential equation, and possess an explicit generating function. In quantum mechanics the stationary states of the harmonic oscillator are products of Hermite polynomials and a Gaussian factor.
