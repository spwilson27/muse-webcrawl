La Malinche

La Malinche (c. 1500 – c. 1529), also known as Malinalli, Malintzin or Doña Marina, was a Nahua woman from the Mexican Gulf Coast who became the interpreter, adviser and intermediary of the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire (1519–1521).

Life

Sold or given into slavery by her family, Malinalli passed to the Maya and in 1519 was given to Cortés at Potonchán. Speaking Nahuatl and Chontal Maya, she translated via Gerónimo de Aguilar (Maya–Spanish), then learned Spanish herself, becoming indispensable and bearing Cortés a son, Martín, often called one of the first mestizos.

Legacy

Post-independence Mexican nationalism cast her as a traitor — malinchismo denotes preference for the foreign — while Chicana feminists and modern historians have reframed her as a survivor and a mother of Mexican identity.
