Gibson Les Paul

The Gibson Les Paul is a solid-body electric guitar that was first sold by the Gibson Guitar Corporation in 1952. Designed in collaboration with popular guitarist Les Paul, it was intended to compete with Fender's Telecaster and became one of the most famous and enduring guitar models in popular music.

History

The first model, the Les Paul Goldtop (1952), featured a gold finish and two P-90 pickups. The Custom appeared in 1954, and the Standard (initially called simply the Les Paul Model) adopted a sunburst finish in 1958. In 1957 the model gained the newly invented humbucking pickup, designed by Seth Lover, whose warm, powerful tone became the signature Les Paul sound. Sales of the traditional model declined in the early 1960s and it was discontinued in favour of the redesigned SG shape, but demand from rock guitarists led to its reissue in 1968.

Influence

The late-1950s sunburst Standard — the "Burst" — is among the most collectible guitars ever made, associated with players such as Eric Clapton, Jimmy Page, Billy Gibbons, and Slash. The Les Paul's thick, sustaining tone has shaped blues rock, hard rock, and heavy metal.

Models

Principal variants include the Standard, Custom, Junior, Special, Deluxe, Studio, and Traditional, along with numerous signature editions.
