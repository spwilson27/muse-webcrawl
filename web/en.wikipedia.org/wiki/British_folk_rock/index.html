British folk rock

British folk rock is a genre of music that developed in the United Kingdom in the mid-to-late 1960s, fusing traditional British folk music with rock instrumentation and arrangements. Its most commercially and critically significant exponents were Fairport Convention, Pentangle and Steeleye Span.

Folk rock instrumentation mixes fiddle and electric guitars.

Origins

The genre emerged from the British folk revival and the influence of American folk rock acts such as the Byrds and Bob Dylan. London-based Fairport Convention turned decisively to native material after 1969, when, following the departure of singer Sandy Denny and the addition of fiddler Dave Swarbrick, they recorded Liege & Lief — widely regarded as the founding album of British folk rock, built from traditional ballads such as "Tam Lin" and "Matty Groves" researched in Cecil Sharp's collections.

Development

Steeleye Span, formed by Fairport bassist Ashley Hutchings, took a more overtly rock approach and had chart success with "All Around My Hat" (1975). Pentangle blended folk with jazz and blues. The Albion Band, Mr Fox and the Incredible String Band explored related territory, while Richard Thompson's solo career grew out of the Fairport circle. The genre fed into Celtic rock in Scotland and Ireland and remains sustained by festivals such as Fairport's annual Cropredy Convention.
