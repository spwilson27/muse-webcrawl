Latin honors

Latin honors are a system of Latin phrases used in some colleges and universities to indicate the level of distinction with which an academic degree has been earned. The system is used primarily in the United States and is also employed in some European countries with varying levels of detail. Many institutions either use their own customs or do not use Latin honors at all (for example, the University of Oxford and most British universities classify degrees instead). The three main levels, in ascending order, are cum laude, magna cum laude, and summa cum laude.

Latin honors are printed on the diploma and noted in the commencement programme.

Levels

Latin phraseLiteral meaningTypical usage
cum laude"with praise" / "with honor"The lowest of the three honors levels, commonly the top 20–30% of a class or a GPA threshold
magna cum laude"with great praise"The middle level, commonly the top 10–15%
summa cum laude"with highest praise"The highest level, commonly the top 1–5% of a class

Some institutions use additional or variant phrases. Yale has historically used egregia cum laude and related forms; a few schools award maxima cum laude. In the Philippines, the system also includes summa, magna, and cum laude honours determined by general weighted average. In several European countries, such as the Netherlands and Italy, the top distinction (for example the Italian 110 e lode) may be rendered as cum laude on the degree.

Criteria

Schools determine honors by grade point average, class rank, completion of an honors thesis or special programme, faculty recommendation, or a combination of these. Harvard College awards summa cum laude to roughly the top 5 per cent of the graduating class and magna cum laude on the basis of both GPA and field of study. Because criteria vary widely between institutions, Latin honors are not directly comparable across schools; grade inflation has also led some commentators to question how discriminating the distinctions remain at highly selective universities, where a large fraction of graduates may receive honors.

History

Harvard University was the first institution in the United States to use Latin honors, introducing them in the 1860s–1870s; Amherst College had operated an early honors system from 1821. The practice spread during the late nineteenth century and became the dominant American scheme, displacing older class-rank style graduations. It derives loosely from the medieval universities' custom of granting degrees "with praise."
