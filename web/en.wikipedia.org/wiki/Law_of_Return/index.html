Law of Return

The Law of Return (Hebrew: חוק השבות, Ḥok ha-Shvut) is an Israeli law passed on 5 July 1950 that gives Jews, persons with Jewish ancestry, and their spouses the right to immigrate to Israel and acquire Israeli citizenship. It enacts in law a central tenet of Zionism: that Israel is the homeland of the Jewish people and open to Jewish immigration (aliyah).

Provisions

The law declares that every Jew has the right to come to Israel as an immigrant (oleh). A 1970 amendment extended the right to the children and grandchildren of Jews, and to the spouses of Jews and of their descendants, whether or not the person is considered Jewish under religious law. Citizenship is granted automatically on arrival unless exceptions apply, such as a criminal past or activity deemed dangerous to public health or state security.

Debate

The law has been debated over the definition of "who is a Jew", with disputes between religious and secular interpretations producing landmark Israeli Supreme Court cases, and over the law's broader place in Israel's character as both a Jewish and a democratic state.
