Edward Verne Roberts (January 23, 1939 – March 14, 1995) was an American activist and a pioneering leader of the disability rights movement. He is often called the father of the independent living movement. Paralysed from the neck down by polio, Roberts used a wheelchair and an iron lung and became the first severely disabled student to attend the University of California, Berkeley.

Early life and education

Roberts contracted polio at age 14 in 1953, leaving him paralysed and dependent on a respirator. Initially discouraged by school officials, he fought to complete high school and then, after advocacy and litigation, was admitted to UC Berkeley in 1962. There he and other disabled students — the self-styled "Rolling Quads" — pressed the university for accessible housing and support services, leading to the Physically Disabled Students Program, the first of its kind in the United States.

Activism and career

In 1972 Roberts co-founded the Center for Independent Living in Berkeley, the first organisation run by and for people with disabilities providing peer support, advocacy, and services; it became the model for hundreds of independent living centres worldwide. In 1975 Governor Jerry Brown appointed him director of the California Department of Vocational Rehabilitation — the same agency that had once deemed him too disabled to work. He later co-founded the World Institute on Disability. Roberts died in 1995; California marks January 23 as Ed Roberts Day.
