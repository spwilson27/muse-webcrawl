DVD+R

DVD+R ("DVD plus R") is a recordable optical disc format developed by the DVD+RW Alliance, a consortium including Philips, Sony, Hewlett-Packard, and others. Introduced in 2002, it allows a single write (record-once) of up to 4.7 GB of data, and competed with the rival DVD-R format promoted by the DVD Forum.

DVD+R
Media type: Recordable optical disc
Capacity: 4.7 GB (single layer); 8.5 GB (dual layer, DVD+R DL)
Developed by: DVD+RW Alliance
Released: 2002

Technical features

DVD+R uses a wobbled groove with ADIP (Address In Pregroove) encoding for positioning, supporting lossless linking when recording incrementally, which reduced errors compared with early DVD-R writing. Its book type field can be set ("bitsetting") so that players recognize the disc as a pressed DVD-ROM, improving compatibility with older set-top players.

Format war and aftermath

The rivalry between DVD+R and DVD-R was a mild echo of earlier format wars; most drives soon supported both ("DVD±R"), and the distinction faded in practical importance. Dual-layer DVD+R DL discs store about 8.5 GB. Recordable DVDs were widely used for video recording, backup, and software distribution in the 2000s before USB flash drives, external hard disks, and streaming displaced them.
