The Skeleton Dance

The Skeleton Dance is a 1929 American animated short film produced by Walt Disney and directed by Ub Iwerks. It was the first entry in Disney's Silly Symphony series and is widely regarded as a landmark in animation history for its close synchronisation of music and movement.

Plot

On a dark, stormy night in a graveyard, four human skeletons rise from their graves and dance together to eerie music. They cavort among the tombstones, use one another's bones as xylophones and percussion instruments, and scatter as a rooster crows at dawn, reassembling themselves and tumbling back into a single grave.

Production

The film's music, arranged by Carl Stalling, adapts Edvard Grieg's "March of the Dwarfs" and incorporates the "Fossils" movement from Camille Saint-Saëns's The Carnival of the Animals. Animator Ub Iwerks animated most of the picture. Although distributor Pat Powers initially struggled to sell the novelty cartoon, it found an enthusiastic audience after opening at the Carthay Circle Theatre in Los Angeles and went on to influence the direction of the Disney studio, demonstrating the commercial viability of cartoons built entirely around music.

Legacy

The Skeleton Dance is frequently cited among the greatest animated shorts ever made, and its imagery of dancing skeletons has been widely imitated. The film was selected for preservation and is celebrated each Halloween season as a classic of early sound animation.
