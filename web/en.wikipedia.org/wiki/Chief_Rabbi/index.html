Chief Rabbi

ReligionJudaism
RoleSenior rabbinic authority of a country or community
ExamplesChief Rabbinate of Israel; Chief Rabbi of the United Hebrew Congregations (UK)

Chief Rabbi is a title given in several countries to the recognized religious leader of that country's Jewish community, or to a rabbinic leader appointed by secular authorities.

History

The office developed in medieval and early modern Europe, where governments preferred a single Jewish interlocutor. Prominent chief rabbinates include that of the British United Hebrew Congregations, established in the 19th century, and the Chief Rabbinate of Israel, founded in 1921, which comprises an Ashkenazi and a Sephardi Chief Rabbi.

Functions

Depending on the country, a Chief Rabbi may oversee kosher certification, religious courts, conversions and marriage registration, and may represent the Jewish community to the state.
