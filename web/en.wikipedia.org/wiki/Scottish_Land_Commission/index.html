The Scottish Land Commission (Scottish Gaelic: Coimisean Fearainn na h-Alba) is a public body of the Scottish Government established under the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016. It began work on 1 April 2017 and is tasked with driving progress on land reform in Scotland, reviewing the effectiveness of laws and policies relating to land and making recommendations to Scottish Ministers.

Functions

The Commission works to create a fairer and more productive system of land ownership and use in Scotland, where land ownership is historically highly concentrated — a frequently cited statistic holds that a few hundred owners hold around half of Scotland's privately owned rural land. Its work covers land ownership, land rights and responsibilities, land use decision-making, and land markets. It publishes research and guidance, including protocols under the Scottish Government's Land Rights and Responsibilities Statement.

Tenant Farming Commissioner

The Act also created the role of Tenant Farming Commissioner, housed within the Commission, who promotes good relations between agricultural landlords and tenants, prepares codes of practice, and can investigate alleged breaches of those codes.

Structure

The Commission consists of six Land Commissioners (including its chair) appointed by Scottish Ministers, plus the Tenant Farming Commissioner, and is based in Inverness. Andrew Thin was appointed its first chair.
