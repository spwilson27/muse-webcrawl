Lapping

Lapping is a machining process in which two surfaces are rubbed together with an abrasive between them, by hand or by machine. It produces very flat, smooth surfaces and is used for finishing gauge blocks, optical components, valves, seals and precision metal parts.

Process

In machine lapping, the workpiece is held against a rotating lap plate — often cast iron charged with abrasive — with a slurry of abrasive grains in oil or water. The loose abrasive rolls and cuts microscopically at both surfaces, removing material slowly but very uniformly. Two-plate (double-sided) lapping machines finish both faces of components such as pump gears and silicon wafers simultaneously. Hand lapping uses a flat plate and figure-of-eight strokes to avoid grooving.

Results

Lapping can achieve flatness measured in fractions of a wavelength of light and surface roughness values of a few nanometres, at the cost of slow material removal. It is distinguished from honing, which uses bonded abrasives on tools of defined shape, typically for bores.
