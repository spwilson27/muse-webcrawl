The Pietà (Italian: [pjeˈta]; "the Pity") is a Renaissance sculpture by Michelangelo Buonarroti, housed in St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City. Completed in 1499, when the artist was only 24 years old, it depicts the Virgin Mary cradling the dead body of Jesus Christ after the Crucifixion. It is the only work Michelangelo ever signed.

Commission and creation

The sculpture was commissioned by the French cardinal Jean de Bilhères for his funerary chapel in St. Peter's. Carved from a single block of Carrara marble, the work was completed in under two years and was immediately celebrated for its beauty and technical mastery. Michelangelo famously signed the sash across Mary's chest after overhearing visitors attribute the work to another sculptor.

Description

The composition is pyramidal, with Mary's serene, youthful face at the apex. Her body is enlarged and her draperies deeply carved to support the body of Christ, giving the group stability and an impression of effortlessness. In keeping with Renaissance ideals, Mary is shown far younger than a woman with a 33-year-old son would be; Michelangelo defended this choice on theological grounds.

Damage and restoration

On 21 May 1972, the Hungarian-born geologist Laszlo Toth attacked the sculpture with a geologist's hammer, striking it fifteen times and severing Mary's arm and damaging her face. The work was painstakingly restored over the following years and is now displayed behind bulletproof glass.

Legacy

The Pietà remains one of the most admired sculptures of the Renaissance and the most famous of Michelangelo's early works, celebrated for its combination of classical balance, naturalism, and devotional emotion.
