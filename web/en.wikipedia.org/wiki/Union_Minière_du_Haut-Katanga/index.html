Union Minière du Haut-Katanga

The Union Minière du Haut-Katanga (UMHK) was a Belgian mining company, founded in 1906, that exploited the copper belt of Katanga in the Congo Free State and Belgian Congo until its assets were nationalized in 1967.

The Union Minière du Haut-Katanga (UMHK) was a Belgian mining company — a joint venture of the Société Générale de Belgique, the Congo Free State's Comité Spécial du Katanga, and British interests associated with Tanganyika Concessions — formed in 1906 to develop the exceptionally rich copper deposits of southern Katanga in what is now the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Colonial operations

From its first copper production at Lubumbashi (Élisabethville) in 1911, UMHK became one of the world's largest copper producers, also mining cobalt, zinc, tin, and uranium. Its Shinkolobwe mine supplied the uranium used by the Manhattan Project in the Second World War. The company was a pillar of the colonial economy: it built railways, towns, hospitals, and schools, and developed a paternalist labour system that stabilized its workforce — while relying in its early decades on coercive recruitment and harsh conditions that drew international criticism.

Independence, secession, and nationalization

At Congolese independence in 1960, UMHK backed Moise Tshombe's secession of Katanga (1960–63), financing and supplying the breakaway state; its payments and loyalties were a central issue of the Congo Crisis. Under President Mobutu, the company's Congolese assets were nationalized on 1 January 1967 and transferred to the state company that became Gécamines, after bitter disputes over compensation. The parent company refocused on metallurgy in Belgium and eventually evolved into the materials group Umicore.
