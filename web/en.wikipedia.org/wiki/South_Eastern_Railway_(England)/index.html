South Eastern Railway (England)

South Eastern Railway
Incorporated: 1836
Headquarters: London
Successor: Southern Railway (1923)
Region: Kent and East Sussex

The South Eastern Railway (SER) was a railway company in south-eastern England, incorporated in 1836 to build a line from London to Dover. It grew to serve much of Kent and East Sussex, with London termini at London Bridge, Charing Cross, and Cannon Street.

History

The SER's main line reached Dover via Redhill, Tunbridge Wells, and Ashford — a circuitous route dictated by its early sharing of the London and Brighton Railway's line out of London. It developed the cross-Channel traffic through Folkestone and Dover. From 1899 the SER worked in a controversial management union with its rival, the London, Chatham and Dover Railway, as the South Eastern and Chatham Railway (SECR).

Grouping

Under the Railways Act 1921, the SECR companies became part of the Southern Railway in 1923. Today most former SER routes form part of the Southeastern franchise network in Network Rail's Southern region.
