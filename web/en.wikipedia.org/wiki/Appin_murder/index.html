Appin murder

Date14 May 1752
LocationLettermore wood, Appin, Argyll, Scotland
VictimColin Roy Campbell of Glenure
ConvictedJames Stewart of the Glens

The Appin murder was the assassination on 14 May 1752 of Colin Roy Campbell of Glenure, a government factor managing forfeited Jacobite estates in Appin, Argyll, Scotland. Campbell was shot in the back while riding through Lettermore wood. In the political aftermath of the Jacobite rising of 1745, suspicion fell on the local Stewart clan.

Trial and execution

Although the suspected gunman, Allan Breck Stewart, fled to France, James Stewart of the Glens ("James of the Glens") was arrested and tried at Inveraray for being an accessory to the murder. The court was presided over by the Duke of Argyll, chief of Clan Campbell, and much of the jury was Campbells. James Stewart was convicted and hanged at Cnap a' Chaolais, Ballachulish, on 8 November 1752, protesting his innocence on the scaffold. His body remained in chains on the gibbet for eighteen months.

In culture

The case is widely regarded as a miscarriage of justice and remains one of Scotland's most famous unsolved murders — the identity of the actual marksman is still debated. It forms a central episode of Robert Louis Stevenson's novel Kidnapped (1886), and a memorial cairn marks the site of Stewart's execution.
