A gas is one of the four fundamental states of matter, in which particles move freely at high speed, far apart, filling any container and exerting pressure on its walls. Unlike liquids and solids, gases are highly compressible and mix completely with one another.

Gas behaviour is described by the gas laws: Boyle's law (pressure × volume constant at fixed temperature), Charles's law (volume proportional to absolute temperature) and the combined ideal gas law, PV = nRT — a good model for most gases at ordinary conditions and the basis of the kinetic theory of gases.

Kinetic theory

The kinetic theory explains pressure as countless molecular collisions and temperature as average molecular kinetic energy; it predicts diffusion and effusion rates (Graham's law) and was a historic proof of the reality of molecules.

Real gases

At high pressures and low temperatures gases deviate from ideality (van der Waals corrections) and liquefy — the basis of industrial gas separation, refrigeration and LPG. Critical temperature marks the limit above which no pressure can liquefy a gas.
