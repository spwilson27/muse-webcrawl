Utilization review

Utilization review assesses the medical necessity of health services

Utilization review (UR) is the process used by health insurers and health care organisations to evaluate the medical necessity, appropriateness, and efficiency of health care services, procedures, and facilities. It is a key component of managed care in the United States, intended to control costs and ensure that care provided is clinically justified.

Methods

Utilization review can occur prospectively (before care is given, as prior authorisation), concurrently (during a hospital stay, reviewing the need for continued admission), or retrospectively (after care has been delivered, when claims are examined). Reviews are performed against evidence-based criteria by nurses and physicians employed by insurers or by independent review organisations.

Criticism

Physicians and patient advocates criticise utilization review — especially prior authorisation — for delaying care, creating administrative burden, and substituting insurer judgement for that of treating clinicians; insurers argue it curbs unnecessary and low-value treatment.
