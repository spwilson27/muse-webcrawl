Arma Christi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Arma Christi displayed around the Man of Sorrows, a common late medieval image.

Arma Christi ("weapons of Christ") or the Instruments of the Passion are the objects associated with the Passion of Jesus Christ in Christian symbolism and art. They are treated as potent devotional symbols and, in heraldic usage, as the "arms" or coat of arms of Christ, displayed much like the heraldic devices of a knight.

The instruments

The exact list varies, but the most frequently depicted instruments include:

  • The Cross and the nails of the Crucifixion
  • The Crown of Thorns
  • The pillar of the Flagellation, and the scourges or whips
  • The Holy Lance that pierced Christ's side
  • The sponge on a reed, offered with vinegar
  • The ladder used at the Deposition, and the pincers used to remove the nails
  • The hammer, the chalice of the Agony in the Garden, and the thirty pieces of silver
  • The rooster that crowed at Peter's denial, the lantern of the arrest, the sword that cut off Malchus's ear, and Veronica's veil

Devotional and artistic use

Images of the Arma Christi flourished in late medieval devotion, especially in association with the Mass of Saint Gregory, the Man of Sorrows, indulgenced prayers, and Books of Hours. In such images the instruments, detached from narrative scenes, are arranged heraldically around Christ or on a shield, functioning as objects of meditation on the Passion and as protective symbols. Many relics claimed to be Instruments of the Passion circulated in medieval Europe; the most famous, the Crown of Thorns, was acquired by Louis IX of France and housed in the Sainte-Chapelle in Paris.
