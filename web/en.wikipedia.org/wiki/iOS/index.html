iOS

iOS (formerly iPhone OS) is a mobile operating system developed by Apple exclusively for its smartphones. It was unveiled in January 2007 for the first-generation iPhone, which was released in June of that year. It is the operating system that powers many of the company's mobile devices, and the global market share of iOS is around 28 percent.

DeveloperApple Inc.
Initial release29 June 2007
Written inC, C++, Objective-C, Swift, assembly
PlatformsiPhone (and formerly iPad, iPod touch)

History

iOS was derived from Mac OS X (now macOS) and shares its Darwin foundation. The App Store, launched in 2008 with iPhone OS 2, transformed software distribution. Major versions are released annually, announced at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference.

Features

The interface is based on direct manipulation using multi-touch gestures. Features include the Home screen with app icons, Control Center, Notification Center, Siri voice assistant, Face ID, and deep integration with Apple services such as iCloud, iMessage, and Apple Pay.

Related systems

iOS is the basis for Apple's other platforms, including iPadOS, watchOS, and tvOS.
