Mantı

TypeDumpling
RegionTurkey, Central Asia, Caucasus
Main ingredientsFlour, minced meat (lamb or beef), onion; served with yogurt, garlic, spiced butter

Mantı (Turkish: mantı) is a type of dumpling popular in Turkish cuisine, as well as in the cuisines of Central Asia, the South Caucasus and the Balkans. It consists of small pieces of spiced minced meat wrapped in thin sheets of dough, which are boiled or steamed and traditionally served with a garlic yogurt sauce and melted butter infused with red pepper flakes or dried mint and sumac.

Preparation

The dough is made from flour, water, egg and salt, rolled very thin and cut into small squares. Each square receives a small amount of filling, typically ground lamb or beef mixed with onion, salt and black pepper, and the corners are pinched together to seal the dumpling. Turkish mantı are notably small; a well-known saying holds that a skilled cook should be able to fit forty pieces in a single spoon. The Kayseri region of central Anatolia is especially famous for its tiny mantı.

Varieties and related dishes

Variations include hingel in the eastern provinces, filled with potato, and baked or fried versions. Related dumplings spread across Asia along Turkic and Mongol routes of migration: Uzbek and Kazakh manti are larger and steamed, often served with sour cream; Armenian manti are boat-shaped and baked before being served in broth. The dish is generally linked to the wider family of filled dumplings that includes Chinese jiaozi and Korean mandu.
