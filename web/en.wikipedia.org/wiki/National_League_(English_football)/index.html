National League (English football)

Founded: 1979 (as Alliance Premier League)
Country: England
Level: 5th tier (with North/South at tier 6)
Promotion to: EFL League Two

The National League is an association football league in England consisting of three divisions — the National League, National League North and National League South — forming the fifth and sixth tiers of the English football league system.

History

The top division was founded in 1979 as the Alliance Premier League, the first national league below the Football League, uniting leading clubs from the Southern League and Northern Premier League. It was later known as the Conference before adopting the National League name in 2015. Automatic promotion to the Football League began in 1987, ending the old re-election system.

Competition

The champions of the National League are promoted to EFL League Two along with the winners of a six-team play-off, while relegated clubs drop to the North or South divisions. The league's clubs also compete in the FA Cup and the FA Trophy, and the top flight is notable for professional clubs and large crowds by non-League standards, with Wrexham's 2023 promotion drawing global attention.
