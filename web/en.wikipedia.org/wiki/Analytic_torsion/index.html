Analytic torsion

FieldDifferential geometry, topology
Introduced byDavid B. Ray and Isadore M. Singer (1971)

Analytic torsion, also called Ray–Singer torsion, is an invariant of a compact Riemannian manifold defined using the regularised determinants of Laplacian operators acting on differential forms. It was introduced by D. B. Ray and I. M. Singer in 1971, who conjectured that it equals the older combinatorial Reidemeister torsion; this was proved independently by Werner Müller and Jeff Cheeger, so analytic torsion is a topological invariant despite its analytic definition.

Definition

Given a flat vector bundle over a closed Riemannian manifold, one forms the Laplacians on bundle-valued forms. The zeta-regularised product of their determinants, weighted by degree, defines the analytic torsion. When the bundle's cohomology vanishes the invariant is a positive real number; in general it is an element of the determinant line of the cohomology.

Significance

The equality of analytic and Reidemeister torsion provided one of the first bridges between spectral geometry and algebraic topology. Generalisations include the work of Jean-Michel Bismut and collaborators on families of Dirac operators, and analytic torsion plays a role in index theory and arithmetic geometry.
