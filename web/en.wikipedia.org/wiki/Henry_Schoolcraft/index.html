Overview

Henry Rowe Schoolcraft (1793–1864) was an American geographer, geologist and ethnologist noted for his expedition to and identification of the true headwaters lake of the Mississippi River (Lake Itasca, 1832 — the name he coined from Latin veritas caput, "true head") and for his voluminous studies of Native American peoples as a long-serving US Indian agent in Michigan (from 1822, at Sault Ste. Marie and Mackinac).

Work and legacy

Schoolcraft married Jane Johnston (Bamewawagezhikaquay), of Ojibwe and Irish descent, whose family's stories informed his collections of Ojibwe oral literature — material Henry Wadsworth Longfellow drew on for The Song of Hiawatha (1855). His six-volume Historical and Statistical Information Respecting the Indian Tribes (1851–57), commissioned by Congress, is monumental and flawed — valuable records mixed with the assimilationist assumptions of his era and office; he helped negotiate the 1836 Treaty of Washington ceding vast Ojibwe and Odawa lands. Modern scholarship reads him ambivalently, and Jane Johnston Schoolcraft is now recognised as one of the first Native American writers.
