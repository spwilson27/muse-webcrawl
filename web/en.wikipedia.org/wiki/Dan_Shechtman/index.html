Dan Shechtman

Dan Shechtman (Hebrew: דן שכטמן‎; born 24 January 1941) is an Israeli materials scientist, the Philip Tobias Professor of Materials Science at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, and a professor at Iowa State University. He won the 2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of quasicrystals.

Early life and education

Shechtman was born in Tel Aviv in 1941. He received his B.Sc. in mechanical engineering in 1966, and his M.Sc. and Ph.D. in materials engineering in 1968 and 1972 respectively, all from the Technion in Haifa.

Discovery of quasicrystals

On 8 April 1982, while a visiting researcher at the U.S. National Bureau of Standards in Washington, D.C., Shechtman examined a rapidly cooled aluminium–manganese alloy in a transmission electron microscope. Its diffraction pattern showed sharp spots arranged with tenfold (icosahedral) symmetry — a symmetry that classical crystallography held to be impossible, since crystals were defined as periodic structures and fivefold symmetry cannot fill space periodically.

Shechtman's claim was met with ridicule; he was told to reread his textbook and was asked to leave his research group. The double Nobel laureate Linus Pauling was a prominent and vocal critic, insisting there were "no quasicrystals, only quasi-scientists", and arguing the pattern came from multiple twinning. The paper was eventually published in 1984 (Shechtman, Blech, Gratias and Cahn, Physical Review Letters). The finding forced a redefinition: in 1991 the International Union of Crystallography broadened the definition of a crystal to include aperiodic structures with discrete diffraction patterns.

Awards and later life

Quasicrystals — solids with ordered but non-repeating atomic structure — proved to exist both in the laboratory and in nature. Shechtman received many honours, culminating in the 2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, awarded to him alone for the discovery. In 2014 he ran unsuccessfully for President of Israel. He has also promoted entrepreneurship education and science teaching for young children.
