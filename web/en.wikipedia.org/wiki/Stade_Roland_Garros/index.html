The Stade Roland Garros is a tennis stadium complex in the 16th arrondissement of Paris, France, beside the Bois de Boulogne, and the home of the French Open, the second Grand Slam tournament of the year. Built in 1928 to host France's defence of the Davis Cup, it is named after Roland Garros, a French pioneer aviator and First World War fighter pilot. The complex's red clay courts — crushed brick over limestone — give the French Open its distinctive slow, demanding character.

Courts and redevelopment

The main stadium, Court Philippe-Chatrier, was rebuilt and given a retractable roof in 2020, allowing night sessions from 2021. Court Suzanne-Lenglen honours the great French champion of the 1920s, while Court Simonne-Mathieu, opened in 2019 within the neighbouring botanical gardens' greenhouses, is regarded as one of the most beautiful courts in tennis. A statue of Rafael Nadal, the tournament's 14-time champion, was unveiled outside the main court in 2021. The venue also hosted the tennis and boxing events of the Paris 2024 Olympic Games.
