Virtual production

Virtual production is an area of media production that combines conventional filmmaking with computer graphics techniques to allow visual effects to be created and captured in real time on set, rather than exclusively in post-production. Its best-known form uses large LED video walls — an "LED volume" — displaying real-time 3D environments rendered by a game engine, so actors are photographed inside digital locations with correct lighting and parallax.

Techniques

Key components include real-time rendering engines (notably Unreal Engine), camera tracking, in-camera visual effects (ICVFX), simulcam for animated characters, motion capture, and virtual scouting and previsualisation in VR. The LED wall both lights the performers naturally and provides reflections on vehicles and sets that would otherwise require green screens and extensive compositing.

History and adoption

Precursors include rear projection and the motion-capture-driven processes of Avatar (2009). The approach was dramatically popularised by The Mandalorian (2019), produced by Lucasfilm with Industrial Light & Magic's StageCraft volume in Manhattan Beach, California. Studios have since built permanent volumes worldwide, and the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated adoption by reducing location travel. Advantages include creative iteration on set and "final pixel" shots; challenges include up-front asset costs, moiré and colour limitations of LED panels, and the need for new crew roles.
