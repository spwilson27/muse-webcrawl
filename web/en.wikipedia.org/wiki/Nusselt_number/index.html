Nusselt number

In thermal fluid dynamics, the Nusselt number (symbol Nu, after the German engineer Wilhelm Nusselt) is the ratio of convective to conductive heat transfer across a boundary in a fluid. It is a dimensionless number defined as

NuL = hL / k

where h is the convective heat transfer coefficient, L is a characteristic length, and k is the thermal conductivity of the fluid. A Nusselt number of 1 represents heat transfer by pure conduction; larger values indicate increasingly effective convection. The Nusselt number can equivalently be viewed as a dimensionless temperature gradient at the surface.

Interpretation

The conduction component is measured under the same conditions as the convection but for a hypothetically stagnant (motionless) fluid. Because convection enhances heat transfer relative to that conductive baseline, the Nusselt number quantifies the enhancement. In practice, a flowing fluid typically produces Nusselt numbers from about 100 up to values in the thousands or higher for turbulent flows.

Correlations

Engineers estimate the Nusselt number from empirical correlations expressed in terms of other dimensionless groups, chiefly the Reynolds number (Re) and the Prandtl number (Pr). For turbulent flow inside a smooth tube, the widely used Dittus–Boelter correlation gives Nu ≈ 0.023 Re0.8 Prn, with n = 0.4 when the fluid is being heated and 0.3 when it is being cooled. For laminar flow in a tube with constant wall temperature, the Nusselt number approaches a constant value of about 3.66 for a circular cross-section. In natural (free) convection, correlations are instead written in terms of the Grashof number or the Rayleigh number.

Applications

Nusselt number correlations underpin the design of heat exchangers, boilers, radiators, electronic cooling systems, and building insulation performance, wherever the convective heat transfer coefficient must be predicted rather than measured directly.
