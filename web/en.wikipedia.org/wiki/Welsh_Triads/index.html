The Welsh Triads (Welsh: Trioedd Ynys Prydein, "Triads of the Island of Britain") are a group of related texts in medieval Welsh manuscripts which preserve fragments of Welsh folklore, mythology and traditional history in groups of three. The triad form was a rhetorical and mnemonic device used by Welsh bards to organise their material and aid its memorisation.

Content

The triads typically give a heading such as "Three X of the Island of Britain" followed by three names or items, sometimes with brief explanatory glosses. They record, among other things, the "Three Generous Men", the "Three Futile Battles", and numerous groupings concerning King Arthur and his court, legendary kings, saints, horses and treasures. Many figures named in the triads also appear in the tales of the Mabinogion and in the poem cycles associated with Taliesin and Myrddin.

Manuscripts and study

The earliest surviving collection is found in the Peniarth 16 manuscript (13th century), and the most complete in the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1400). The standard modern edition is Rachel Bromwich's Trioedd Ynys Prydein (first published 1961, with revised editions), which arranges the triads with extensive commentary. Bromwich demonstrated that the triads preserve genuinely old traditional material, while a number of "triads" published by the 18th-century forger Iolo Morganwg were fabrications.
