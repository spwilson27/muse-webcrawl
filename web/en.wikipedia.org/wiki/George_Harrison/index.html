George Harrison

George Harrison MBE
Born: February 25, 1943, Liverpool, England
Died: November 29, 2001, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Musician, singer-songwriter, producer
Years active: 1958–2001

George Harrison (February 25, 1943 – November 29, 2001) was an English musician, singer and songwriter who achieved international fame as the lead guitarist of the Beatles. Sometimes called "the quiet Beatle", Harrison embraced Indian culture and Hindu spirituality, helped broaden the scope of popular music through his interest in the sitar and Indian instrumentation, and contributed songs including "Taxman", "While My Guitar Gently Weeps", "Something" and "Here Comes the Sun".

With the Beatles

Born in Liverpool, Harrison joined John Lennon and Paul McCartney's skiffle group the Quarrymen while still at school, and became the Beatles' lead guitarist. Although his songwriting was initially overshadowed by the Lennon–McCartney partnership, his compositions grew in stature through the 1960s; "Something", from Abbey Road (1969), is among the most covered songs of the era. His encounter with Indian music and Ravi Shankar in 1965–66, and the band's study of Transcendental Meditation, shaped both his writing and the Beatles' sound.

Solo career

After the Beatles broke up in 1970, Harrison released the triple album All Things Must Pass, his best-selling solo work, containing "My Sweet Lord" and "What Is Life". In 1971 he organised the Concert for Bangladesh, a pioneering charity rock event. Later successes included Living in the Material World (1973), Cloud Nine (1987) — with his number-one cover of "Got My Mind Set on You" — and membership in the supergroup the Traveling Wilburys alongside Bob Dylan, Roy Orbison, Tom Petty and Jeff Lynne. He also founded the film company HandMade Films, which backed Monty Python's Life of Brian and other British films.

Death and legacy

Harrison died of lung cancer on November 29, 2001, aged 58; his final album, Brainwashed, was released posthumously in 2002. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice: as a member of the Beatles in 1988 and as a solo artist in 2004.
