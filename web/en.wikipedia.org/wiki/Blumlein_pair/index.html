Blumlein pair

The crossed figure-of-eight configuration of a Blumlein pair

The Blumlein pair is a stereo recording technique invented by Alan Blumlein for the creation of recordings that, upon playback through headphones or loudspeakers, recreate the spatial properties of the recorded signal. It is a coincident-pair technique using two microphones with bidirectional (figure-of-eight) polar patterns, positioned as close together as possible and angled at 90 degrees to each other.

Technique

The two figure-of-eight microphones are arranged so that the positive lobe of the left microphone faces 45 degrees to the left of the sound source and its rear lobe faces 45 degrees to the right of the rear, with the right microphone mirrored. Because the microphones are coincident, sounds arrive at both at the same time, so stereo imaging is produced purely by level differences — as in Blumlein's original binaural patents of the early 1930s.

Characteristics

The technique is prized for accurate, natural stereo imaging and a strong sense of the recording space, since the rear lobes capture the hall's ambience. Its weaknesses include poor mono compatibility relative to some alternatives, sensitivity to room acoustics, and weak low-frequency directivity. It remains popular for recording classical ensembles, choirs, and jazz groups in good acoustic spaces.
