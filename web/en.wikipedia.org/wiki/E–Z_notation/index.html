E–Z notation

E–Z notation, or the entgegen–zusammen convention, is a method in organic chemistry for describing the absolute stereochemistry of double bonds. It is an extension of cis–trans isomer notation that works for all cases, including double bonds bearing four different substituents, and follows IUPAC rules.

Assignment

For each carbon of the double bond, the two attached groups are ranked by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules. If the two higher-priority groups are on opposite sides of the double bond, the configuration is E (from German entgegen, "opposite"); if they are on the same side, it is Z (from zusammen, "together"). The descriptor is written in italics in parentheses before the name, as in (E)-but-2-ene.

Worked example

In but-2-ene the methyl groups take priority on both carbons, so trans-but-2-ene is the (E)-isomer and cis-but-2-ene is the (Z)-isomer. Where substituents are more complex — for example in oximes, imines and cumulenes — the priority rules are applied atom by atom. Note that E/Z does not always correspond to trans/cis, and does not by itself indicate the direction of optical rotation.
