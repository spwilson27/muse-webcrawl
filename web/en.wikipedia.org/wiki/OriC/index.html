OriC

Organisation of the E. coli origin of replication, oriC.

oriC is the origin of replication of the chromosome of the bacterium Escherichia coli and many related bacteria. It is the specific DNA sequence at which replication is initiated, and the site where the initiator protein DnaA assembles the machinery that unwinds the double helix and loads the replicative helicase. The minimal E. coli oriC spans approximately 245 base pairs and is one of the best-characterised origins of replication in any organism.

Structure

oriC contains several characteristic sequence elements. Multiple DnaA boxes — nine-base-pair consensus sequences (TTATNCACA) — are distributed through the region and bind DnaA with varying affinities. Adjacent to these lies the DNA unwinding element (DUE), an AT-rich region of about 60 base pairs containing three 13-mer repeats, which is melted open when DnaA assembles at the origin. Binding sites for the proteins IHF and Fis modulate the architecture of the initiation complex, and a cluster of GATC sequences is methylated by the Dam methyltransferase, a modification that helps regulate the timing of initiation.

Initiation of replication

Initiation begins when DnaA in its ATP-bound form occupies the DnaA boxes of oriC and wraps the DNA around a helical protein core, causing the strands of the DUE to separate. DnaA then recruits the DnaB helicase, delivered by its loader DnaC, onto each exposed single strand. The helicases in turn recruit primase (DnaG) and the DNA polymerase III holoenzyme, establishing two replication forks that travel in opposite directions around the circular chromosome.

Regulation

Because rapidly growing E. coli can initiate new rounds of replication before previous rounds have finished, oriC firing is tightly controlled so that each origin fires once and only once per cell cycle. After initiation, the newly replicated oriC is temporarily sequestered by the SeqA protein at the membrane, and ATP hydrolysis by DnaA (stimulated by the Hda protein and the sliding clamp) converts DnaA–ATP to inactive DnaA–ADP, preventing premature re-initiation.

Conservation

Origins analogous to oriC, with DnaA boxes and an AT-rich unwinding element, are found across the bacterial domain, and oriC sequences are used as landmarks in bacterial genome annotation, since the origin is typically located near the dnaA gene.
