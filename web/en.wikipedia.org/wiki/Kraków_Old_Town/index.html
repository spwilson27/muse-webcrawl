Kraków Old Town

Kraków Old Town (Stare Miasto)
LocationKraków, Lesser Poland, Poland
UNESCO inscription1978 (one of the first World Heritage Sites)
Key landmarksMain Market Square, St. Mary's Basilica, Cloth Hall, Wawel Castle, Barbican, Planty Park

The Kraków Old Town (Polish: Stare Miasto) is the historic central district of Kraków, Poland. Laid out after the city's 1257 charter, it contains the largest medieval market square in Europe and an exceptional concentration of Gothic, Renaissance, and Baroque monuments. In 1978 it was inscribed—together with Wawel and Kazimierz—among the very first UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Layout and landmarks

The Old Town is organised around the Main Market Square (c. 200 × 200 m), with the Cloth Hall and the Town Hall Tower at its centre and St. Mary's Basilica, famous for its wooden altarpiece by Veit Stoss and the hourly hejnał trumpet call, on its eastern side. Streets run in a regular grid from the square, broken by the Royal Road, the coronation route from St. Florian's Gate through the Barbican to Wawel Castle and Cathedral at the southern end, where Polish kings were crowned and buried. The medieval walls were largely demolished in the 19th century and replaced by the Planty, a ring of gardens encircling the district.

History

Kraków was the capital of the Kingdom of Poland until 1596 and survived the Second World War largely undamaged, unlike Warsaw; it served as the seat of the German General Government during the occupation. The Old Town's university quarter preserves the Jagiellonian University, founded in 1364, where Nicolaus Copernicus studied. Today the district is Poland's premier tourist destination, dense with churches, museums, cafés, and cellar venues.
