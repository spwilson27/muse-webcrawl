Operator-precedence parser

An operator-precedence parser is a simple shift-reduce parser for expressions in a programming language or mathematical notation. It interprets a precedence table that specifies the binding strength and associativity of operators, allowing expressions such as a + b * c to be parsed so that multiplication binds more tightly than addition.

Method

The parser alternates between reading operands and operators. When a new operator arrives, its precedence is compared with that of the operator on top of the parse stack: if the incoming operator binds more tightly, parsing shifts to consume more input; otherwise the pending operation is reduced. Variants include the shunting-yard algorithm of Edsger Dijkstra and Pratt parsing, introduced by Vaughan Pratt in 1973.

Applications

Operator-precedence parsing is widely used in compilers and interpreters for the expression sub-language of a grammar, often combined with a recursive-descent parser for statements. Its simplicity and speed make it a standard textbook technique in compiler construction.
