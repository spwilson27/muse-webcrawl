Stevedore

A stevedore, also called a longshoreman, a docker or a wharfie, is a waterfront manual laborer who is involved in loading and unloading ships, trucks, trains or airplanes. The word derives from the Spanish estibador, "one who stows", from estibar, to stow or pack tightly.

Work

Stevedoring involves stowing cargo so that a vessel remains stable and cargo is protected, and discharging it in port. Before containerisation, gangs of stevedores loaded break-bulk cargo item by item using hooks, slings and nets—a skilled but dangerous trade organised through union hiring halls. Although a stevedore traditionally refers to the individual worker, in many ports a "stevedore" is also the company contracted by a shipowner or terminal operator to load and discharge vessels.

Containerisation

The spread of the standard shipping container from the 1960s transformed the occupation: cranes replaced much manual labour, employment on the docks fell dramatically, and the surviving jobs became centred on operating machinery and lashing containers. Major waterfront disputes, such as the 1971 and 1998 Australian waterfront disputes and the long history of the International Longshoremen's Association on the US East Coast and the ILWU on the West Coast, shaped the politics of the trade.
