United Grand Lodge of England

The United Grand Lodge of England (UGLE) is the governing Masonic lodge for the majority of Freemasons in England, Wales, and several overseas districts. Tracing its origin to the first Grand Lodge, founded in London in 1717, the UGLE in its present form dates from 1813, when the rival "Premier" and "Antient" Grand Lodges united. Its headquarters are at Freemasons' Hall, Great Queen Street, London.

Freemasons' Hall, London, headquarters of the UGLE.

History

On 24 June 1717, four London lodges met at the Goose and Gridiron tavern and formed the first Grand Lodge, electing Anthony Sayer as Grand Master. In 1723 the Rev. James Anderson published The Constitutions of the Free-Masons. A rival body, the Antient Grand Lodge, was formed in 1751, largely by Irish masons in London who considered the original body had departed from tradition. The two rivals coexisted — often acrimoniously — until 27 December 1813, when they united under the Duke of Sussex (for the "Moderns") and the Duke of Kent (for the "Antients") to form the United Grand Lodge of England.

Structure and recognition

The UGLE governs thousands of private lodges organised into Metropolitan and Provincial Grand Lodges in England and Wales and District Grand Lodges overseas. Its Grand Master has traditionally been a member of the royal family; since 1967 the office has been held by Prince Edward, Duke of Kent. The UGLE is the reference point for "regular" Freemasonry worldwide, maintaining relations of mutual recognition with many other grand lodges. Its basic principles were published in 1929, and a 1989 statement reaffirmed them.

Charity and public profile

Freemasons under the UGLE are prominent charitable donors through bodies such as the Masonic Charitable Foundation. The UGLE celebrated its tercentenary in 2017 with events including a service at Canterbury Cathedral and an exhibition at the Library and Museum of Freemasonry.
