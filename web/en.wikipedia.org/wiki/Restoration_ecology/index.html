Restoration ecology

Restoration ecology is the scientific study supporting the practice of ecological restoration, which is the practice of renewing and restoring degraded, damaged, or destroyed ecosystems and habitats in the environment by active human interruption and action. Effective restoration requires an explicit goal or policy, preferably an unambiguous one that is articulated, accepted socially and sanctioned politically.

History

Early restoration work included the establishment of Curtis Prairie at the University of Wisconsin–Madison Arboretum in the 1930s, one of the first attempts to restore a tallgrass prairie. The field developed rapidly from the 1980s, with the founding of the Society for Ecological Restoration (1988) and the journal Restoration Ecology (1993). The United Nations declared 2021–2030 the Decade on Ecosystem Restoration.

Practice

Restoration projects may involve removing invasive species, reintroducing native species, replanting vegetation, rewetting drained wetlands, removing dams or other barriers to river connectivity, and remediating contaminated soils. Practitioners use reference ecosystems to set targets, though debate continues over how faithful restored systems must be to historical states given climate change and novel ecosystems. Related practices include reforestation, habitat conservation and rewilding, and restoration is now a major tool in biodiversity and climate policy, for example under the Bonn Challenge and national wetland mitigation programmes.
