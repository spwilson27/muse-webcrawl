Sri Lankan civil war

The Sri Lankan civil war was a civil war fought in Sri Lanka from 1983 to 2009. Beginning on 23 July 1983 with an intermittent insurgency by the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE, also known as the Tamil Tigers), the conflict ended on 19 May 2009 with the Sri Lankan military's defeat of the LTTE in the Battle of Mullivaikkal. The LTTE had fought to create an independent Tamil state called Tamil Eelam in the north and east of the island.

Origins

The origins of the civil war lie in the ethnic conflict between the majority Sinhalese and the minority Tamils. After independence from Britain in 1948, successive Sinhalese-majority governments passed measures — notably the Sinhala Only Act of 1956 — that Tamils regarded as discriminatory. In 1976 the Tamil United Liberation Front adopted the Vaddukoddai Resolution calling for an independent Tamil state, Tamil Eelam. Militant Tamil groups, of which the LTTE under Velupillai Prabhakaran became the most powerful, began armed attacks on police, soldiers and politicians.

Course of the war

Eelam War I (1983–1987)

The war is conventionally dated from 23 July 1983, when the LTTE ambushed an army patrol at Thirunelveli, Jaffna, killing thirteen soldiers. The ambush triggered the anti-Tamil pogrom known as Black July, in which hundreds and possibly thousands of Tamils were killed and many fled abroad, seeding a large Tamil diaspora. The LTTE and other militant groups grew rapidly, fighting the Sri Lankan armed forces across the north and east.

Indian intervention (1987–1990)

Under the Indo-Sri Lanka Accord of July 1987, India deployed the Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF) to disarm the militants. The LTTE refused to disarm and fought the IPKF in a costly two-year war; the Indians withdrew in March 1990 having failed to defeat the Tigers.

Eelam Wars II and III (1990–2001)

Fighting resumed in 1990. The LTTE assassinated Indian prime minister Rajiv Gandhi in 1991 and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa in 1993. The Tigers developed a navy (the Sea Tigers), pioneered suicide bombing through the Black Tiger unit, and by the late 1990s controlled much of the north-east as a de facto state, while also mounting devastating attacks in Colombo, including the bombing of the Central Bank and the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic.

Ceasefire and final offensive (2002–2009)

A Norwegian-brokered ceasefire in 2002 brought several years of uneasy peace, but talks collapsed and full-scale war resumed in 2006 ("Eelam War IV"). The government of Mahinda Rajapaksa abrogated the ceasefire in January 2008 and launched a massive offensive. The army captured the LTTE's de facto capital Kilinochchi in January 2009 and drove the Tigers and tens of thousands of Tamil civilians into a shrinking coastal strip at Mullivaikkal. On 18–19 May 2009 the army announced the death of Prabhakaran and the complete defeat of the LTTE.

Human cost and aftermath

Estimates of the death toll range from 80,000 to over 100,000; tens of thousands of civilians are believed to have died in the final months alone. A UN panel of experts later found credible allegations of war crimes by both sides in the final phase, including government shelling of "no fire zones" and LTTE use of civilians as human shields. Nearly 300,000 Tamils were displaced and interned in camps after the war. Calls for an international investigation and for a political settlement of Tamil grievances have continued since 2009.
