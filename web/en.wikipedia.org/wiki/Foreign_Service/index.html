Foreign Service

A foreign service is a body of career diplomats and other officials who represent their country abroad, staff its embassies and consulates, and implement its foreign policy. The term is most commonly associated with the United States Foreign Service, the diplomatic corps of the U.S. Department of State, but analogous services exist in most countries.

United States Foreign Service

The U.S. Foreign Service was created by the Rogers Act of 1924, which merged the diplomatic and consular services into a single professional corps, and was reconstituted by the Foreign Service Act of 1980. Its members — Foreign Service Officers and Specialists — serve at more than 270 embassies, consulates and diplomatic missions worldwide, in career tracks including political, economic, consular, public diplomacy and management work.

Functions

Under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961), diplomatic services represent the sending state, protect its nationals, negotiate with host governments, report on conditions, and promote friendly relations. Consular officers additionally issue visas and passports and assist citizens abroad.
