Mount Whitney

Mount Whitney is the highest summit in the contiguous United States, with an elevation of 4,421 metres. It is in California, on the boundary between Inyo and Tulare counties, in the Sierra Nevada, about 135 kilometres west-northwest of Death Valley's Badwater Basin, the lowest point in North America — the greatest elevation contrast in the contiguous US within a short distance.

Whitney is the most frequently climbed mountain peak in the Sierra Nevada; the trail from Whitney Portal gains about 1,900 metres over 17.7 kilometres, and permits are allocated by lottery because of demand. The mountain was named for geologist Josiah Whitney. Its sheer east face offers classic big-wall and mountaineering routes.
