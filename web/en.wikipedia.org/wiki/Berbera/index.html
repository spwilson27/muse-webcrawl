Berbera (Somali: Berbera; Arabic: بربرة) is the capital of the Sahil region of Somaliland and its principal seaport. It sits on the southern coast of the Gulf of Aden, roughly 160 kilometres northwest of Hargeisa, and has served for centuries as one of the Horn of Africa's most important trading harbours.

History

Berbera corresponds to the ancient market town described in the first-century Periplus of the Erythraean Sea as Malao, a roadstead trading in myrrh, frankincense, cinnamon and slaves. In the medieval period it was part of the Adal Sultanate and hosted a famous annual fair drawing merchants from across the Indian Ocean world. The Ottoman Empire held the port in the sixteenth century, and in the nineteenth century it came under Egyptian and then British control; it became the first capital of British Somaliland.

During the Cold War the port was developed with Soviet assistance and later used by the United States. After the collapse of the Somali state in 1991, Berbera became the main commercial gateway of the self-declared Republic of Somaliland, exporting livestock to the Arabian Peninsula.

Port and economy

The Port of Berbera handles most of Somaliland's imports and a large share of landlocked Ethiopia's trade. In 2016 the Dubai-based operator DP World agreed to invest in and manage the port, and a major expansion, including a new container terminal, was inaugurated in 2021. The Berbera corridor road to the Ethiopian border at Wajaale is a key regional trade route.

Climate

Berbera has a hot desert climate, with summer temperatures among the highest recorded anywhere, frequently exceeding 40 °C between June and September, moderated by the seasonal monsoon winds that historically governed the sailing calendar.
