Gwendolyn Elizabeth Brooks (June 7, 1917 – December 3, 2000) was an American poet, author, and teacher. Her work often dealt with the personal celebrations and struggles of ordinary people in the Black community of Chicago's South Side. In 1950 she became the first African American to receive a Pulitzer Prize, for her book Annie Allen.

Life and career

Brooks was born in Topeka, Kansas, and raised in Chicago. She published her first poem at thirteen. Her first book, A Street in Bronzeville (1945), portrayed life in the Bronzeville neighborhood and won wide critical praise. Annie Allen (1949), centered on the coming of age of a Black girl in Chicago, won the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry in 1950. Her only novel, Maud Martha, appeared in 1953.

One of her most famous poems, "We Real Cool," was published in The Bean Eaters (1960). After attending a conference of Black writers at Fisk University in 1967, Brooks embraced the Black Arts Movement, leaving her major publisher for Black-owned presses such as Haki Madhubuti's Third World Press and Dudley Randall's Broadside Press.

Honors

Brooks was appointed Poet Laureate of Illinois in 1968 and served for 32 years. In 1985–86 she was Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress (now Poet Laureate of the United States). She died in Chicago on December 3, 2000. The Gwendolyn Brooks Cultural Center at Western Illinois University and numerous schools are named in her honor.
