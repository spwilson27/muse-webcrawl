Spanking

Spanking is a form of physical punishment involving striking the buttocks of another person, typically with an open hand, though implements such as paddles, straps, or switches have historically been used. It has most commonly been applied to children by parents or teachers as a disciplinary measure, and it also appears historically in judicial and institutional settings.

Child discipline

Spanking of children has been widespread across cultures and eras, but its prevalence and acceptability have declined in many countries over the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Sweden became the first country to ban all corporal punishment of children in 1979; since then more than sixty countries have prohibited it in the home, and many more have banned it in schools. School corporal punishment remains legal in some jurisdictions, including parts of the United States, where it is usually administered with a wooden paddle.

Research and debate

Large bodies of research, including meta-analyses of studies of hundreds of thousands of children, associate spanking with increased aggression, antisocial behaviour, and mental health problems, and find little evidence of long-term benefits compared with non-physical discipline. Major medical and psychological bodies, including the American Academy of Pediatrics, advise against spanking. Critics of these conclusions argue that studies often conflate mild spanking with harsher punishment and that effects are confounded by other factors; the topic remains a subject of public and scholarly debate, strongly patterned by culture, religion, and region.
