Eurodac

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type: Fingerprint database

Established: 15 January 2003

Operator: eu-LISA

Purpose: Supporting the Dublin Regulation on asylum responsibility

Eurodac is the European Union's fingerprint database for asylum seekers and certain other third-country nationals. Established by Council Regulation in 2000 and operational since 15 January 2003, it allows EU member states (and associated countries including Norway, Iceland, Switzerland and Liechtenstein) to check whether a person has previously applied for asylum or been apprehended irregularly crossing a border in another participating state.

Operation

Member states take the fingerprints of asylum applicants aged 14 and over, and of persons caught irregularly entering, and transmit them to a central unit, managed since 2012 by the EU agency eu-LISA. Matches ("hits") support application of the Dublin Regulation, under which normally the first country of entry is responsible for examining an asylum claim. Data on asylum seekers are retained for ten years; data on irregular border-crossers for shorter periods, after which records are erased or blocked.

Development and debate

The 2013 recast regulation permitted, under strict conditions, searches by law-enforcement authorities and Europol for the prevention and investigation of terrorism and serious crime. As part of the EU's New Pact on Migration and Asylum, a further recast adopted in 2024 expands Eurodac to additional categories of people, lowers the fingerprinting age to six, and adds facial images. Civil-liberties groups have criticised the system's expansion and the fingerprinting of children, while governments argue it is essential to a functioning common asylum system and to preventing multiple applications.
