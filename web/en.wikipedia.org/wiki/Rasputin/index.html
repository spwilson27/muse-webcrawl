Grigori Yefimovich Rasputin (21 January 1869 – 30 December 1916) was a Russian mystic and faith healer who became an influential figure in the court of Tsar Nicholas II and Tsarina Alexandra in the final years of the Russian Empire. His apparent ability to ease the suffering of the haemophiliac heir, Alexei Nikolaevich, gave him extraordinary access to the imperial family, while his scandalous reputation and perceived influence over state appointments made him a symbol of corruption and decay in wartime Russia. He was murdered by a group of aristocrats in December 1916.

Early life

Rasputin was born a peasant in the Siberian village of Pokrovskoye. After a religious conversion in his late twenties — following a pilgrimage to the St Nicholas Monastery at Verkhoturye — he travelled widely as a strannik (holy wanderer), reaching Mount Athos and Jerusalem, and acquired a reputation as a starets and healer. By 1903 he had arrived in St Petersburg, where his rough charisma found an eager audience among spiritually hungry aristocrats.

At court

Introduced to the imperial family in 1905, Rasputin was summoned in 1907 to the bedside of the Tsarevich Alexei during a haemorrhage, and the boy's recovery — possibly connected with the withdrawal of aspirin — convinced Alexandra that Rasputin was a man of God. Thereafter his influence grew; during the First World War, with Nicholas at the front and Alexandra effectively governing, Rasputin's interventions in ministerial appointments (the "ministerial leapfrog") fuelled rumours that he was a German agent or the Tsarina's lover, neither of which has ever been substantiated.

Assassination

On the night of 29–30 December 1916, Prince Felix Yusupov, Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich and the politician Vladimir Purishkevich lured Rasputin to the Yusupov Palace. Yusupov's famous account — poisoned cakes and wine that seemed not to work, then gunshots — is partly embellished; a post-mortem indicated Rasputin was shot, and his body was thrown from a bridge into the Malaya Nevka River. He was buried at Tsarskoye Selo; after the February Revolution his corpse was exhumed and burned. Within weeks of his death the monarchy itself fell.

Legacy

Rasputin's life and death have generated an enormous mythology, from Boney M.'s disco hit "Rasputin" to countless films and novels. Historians generally regard his direct political influence as exaggerated but see his presence at court as a potent accelerant of public disillusion with the Romanov regime.
