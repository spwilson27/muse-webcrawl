INSEAD (Institut Européen d'Administration des Affaires) is a non-profit graduate business school with campuses in Europe (Fontainebleau, France), Asia (Singapore), the Middle East (Abu Dhabi) and North America (San Francisco). Founded in 1957, it is widely regarded as one of the world's leading and largest graduate business schools, known for its international outlook and its one-year MBA programme.

History

INSEAD was founded in 1957 by venture capitalist and Harvard Business School professor Georges Doriot, together with Claude Janssen and Olivier Giscard d'Estaing, three years after Doriot first raised the idea of a European business school. It was the first business school in Europe to offer an MBA taught in English, and the first to require students to be multilingual. The first MBA class of about 60 participants began in 1959 at the Château de Fontainebleau.

The school opened its Asia campus in Singapore in 2000, making it the first business school with two full campuses offering the same MBA programme. A campus in Abu Dhabi followed in 2010, and a hub in San Francisco opened in 2020.

Programmes

INSEAD's flagship is its 10-month MBA programme, with two intakes per year starting in January and August. Students rotate between the Fontainebleau and Singapore campuses. Other programmes include the Executive MBA (GEMBA), Master in Management, executive education courses, and a PhD programme. INSEAD also offers online programmes and, from 2024, a Master in Finance.

Rankings and reputation

The INSEAD MBA has repeatedly ranked at or near the top of the Financial Times Global MBA ranking, placing first in 2016, 2017 and 2021. The school is consistently ranked among the top business schools worldwide for alumni outcomes, international mobility and diversity; roughly 90 nationalities are represented in a typical MBA class, and no single nationality dominates.

Alumni

Notable alumni include business leaders across finance, consulting, technology and industry, as well as entrepreneurs; INSEAD alumni have founded companies such as Nubank and have led firms including L'Oréal, Unilever and numerous private-equity houses.
