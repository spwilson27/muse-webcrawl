Thomas Frognall Dibdin

Thomas Frognall Dibdin
Born: 31 August 1776, Calcutta, India
Died: 18 November 1847, Kensington, London
Occupation: Bibliographer, clergyman

Thomas Frognall Dibdin (31 August 1776 – 18 November 1847) was an English bibliographer and clergyman, remembered for his lavishly produced works on the history of books and book collecting. He popularised the term "bibliomania" and was a founding spirit of the Roxburghe Club, the first of the book-collecting societies.

Life

Dibdin was born in Calcutta, the son of a naval captain, and was brought to England as a child after the death of both parents. He was educated at St John's College, Oxford, took holy orders, and held several livings. His great passion, however, was books. Under the patronage of Earl Spencer, whose magnificent library at Althorp he catalogued, Dibdin became the best-known bibliographer of his day. He was librarian to Earl Spencer and was involved in the famous sale of the Duke of Roxburghe's library in 1812, at which the Valdarfer Boccaccio fetched the then-record price of £2,260 — an event commemorated by the founding of the Roxburghe Club.

Works

Dibdin's best-known publications include The Bibliomania; or Book Madness (1809), a playful dialogue on the "disease" of book collecting; Bibliotheca Spenceriana (1814–15), the catalogue of Earl Spencer's library; The Bibliographical Decameron (1817); A Bibliographical, Antiquarian and Picturesque Tour in France and Germany (1821), based on a book-buying journey for Earl Spencer; and Reminiscences of a Literary Life (1836). His works were famed for their copious woodcut illustrations and exuberant, digressive style, though later scholars have criticised their inaccuracies.

Legacy

Although his scholarship was often careless, Dibdin did more than anyone to make bibliography and the history of printing popular subjects in Britain, and his accounts remain valuable sources for the history of book collecting in the Romantic era.
