Sexual identity

Sexual identity refers to how one thinks of oneself in terms of to whom one is romantically or sexually attracted — that is, the label or labels a person applies to their own sexuality, such as heterosexual, gay, lesbian, bisexual, pansexual or asexual. Sexual identity is one component of a person's overall identity and is related to, but distinct from, sexual orientation (the underlying pattern of attraction) and sexual behaviour.

Identity, attraction and behaviour

Identity, attraction and behaviour do not always align: a person may experience same-sex attractions but identify as heterosexual, or identify as bisexual while behaving monogamously. Survey research therefore often measures the three dimensions separately. Sexual identity may also change over the life course, a phenomenon studied under the heading of sexual fluidity, particularly in longitudinal research on women.

Development

Models of homosexual identity development, such as the Cass identity model, describe stages from identity confusion through comparison, tolerance, acceptance and pride to synthesis. Realising and disclosing a minority sexual identity is commonly termed "coming out". Minority stress theory links the stigma attached to minority identities with disparities in mental-health outcomes.

Measurement in research

Large surveys increasingly record sexual identity directly. The UK Office for National Statistics and the US National Health Interview Survey, among others, ask respondents to select an identity category, and the proportions identifying as lesbian, gay or bisexual have risen in younger cohorts.
