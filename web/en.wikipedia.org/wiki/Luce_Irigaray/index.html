Luce Irigaray

Born: 3 May 1930, Blaton, Belgium
Occupation: Philosopher, psychoanalyst, linguist, feminist theorist
Known for: Speculum of the Other Woman; This Sex Which Is Not One

Luce Irigaray (born 3 May 1930) is a Belgian-born French feminist philosopher, psychoanalyst and linguist. She is one of the most influential figures in contemporary French feminist theory, best known for her critique of phallocentrism in Western philosophy and psychoanalysis and for her concept of sexual difference.

Life and career

Irigaray studied at the University of Louvain and later in Paris, where she trained as a psychoanalyst and was for a time a member of Jacques Lacan's École Freudienne de Paris. Her 1974 doctoral thesis, Speculum of the Other Woman (Speculum de l'autre femme), a critical reading of Plato, Freud and other canonical thinkers, caused a rupture with the Lacanian school and cost her teaching position at the University of Vincennes. She subsequently held research posts at the Centre national de la recherche scientifique (CNRS) in Paris.

Work

In This Sex Which Is Not One (1977), Irigaray argued that Western culture is structured by a masculine symbolic order in which the feminine is represented only as the negative or mirror of the masculine. She developed notions such as mimesis, the "two lips", and a culture of sexual difference in which women and men relate as two irreducible subjects. Later works, including An Ethics of Sexual Difference (1984), I Love to You (1990) and To Be Two (1994), explore language, love, law and civil rights between the sexes, as well as Eastern thought and breathing practices drawn from yoga.

Reception

Irigaray's writing has been praised for its originality and criticised for essentialism and obscurity; her work remains central to debates in feminist philosophy, literary theory and psychoanalysis, alongside contemporaries such as Hélène Cixous and Julia Kristeva.
