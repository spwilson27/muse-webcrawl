Waterloo Cup

The Waterloo Cup was England's premier hare coursing event, held from 1836 to 2005.

First held: 1836
Location: Altcar, Lancashire
Last held: 2005

The Waterloo Cup was the premier event in English hare coursing, held annually at Great Altcar in Lancashire from 1836 until 2005. Named after the Waterloo Hotel in Liverpool, whose proprietor organised the first meeting, the competition grew from a small stake for eight greyhounds into a famous 64-dog knockout tournament that drew large crowds and heavy betting.

Format

In the cup, pairs of greyhounds were slipped after a hare on the Altcar estate; a mounted judge awarded points for speed, turning, and the "go-bye", with the winning dog advancing through successive rounds until a champion emerged. Famous winners included Master McGrath, the Irish greyhound who won three times in the late 1860s and became a national celebrity, and Fullerton, who won four consecutive cups (1889–1892).

End

Coursing became increasingly controversial on animal-welfare grounds. The Hunting Act 2004 banned hare coursing in England and Wales, and the final Waterloo Cup was held in February 2005, just before the ban took effect.
