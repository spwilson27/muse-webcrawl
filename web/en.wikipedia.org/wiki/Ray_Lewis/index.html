Ray Anthony Lewis Jr. (born 15 May 1975) is an American former professional football linebacker who spent his entire 17-year career with the Baltimore Ravens of the National Football League (NFL). He is widely regarded as one of the greatest linebackers of all time.

Career

Lewis played college football at the University of Miami and was drafted by the Ravens in the first round of the 1996 NFL draft, the franchise's inaugural season. He led the Ravens' record-setting defence to victory in Super Bowl XXXV (2001), where he was named Super Bowl MVP, and won a second championship in Super Bowl XLVII in his final season (2012). A 13-time Pro Bowler and two-time NFL Defensive Player of the Year (2000, 2003), he holds Ravens records for tackles and played a franchise-record 17 seasons.

Legacy

Lewis was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2018. His pregame "squirrel dance" entrance and emotional leadership made him the defining figure of the Ravens franchise. In 2000 he pleaded guilty to obstruction of justice in connection with a double homicide in Atlanta, having originally been indicted on murder charges that were dropped in exchange for his testimony against two companions.
