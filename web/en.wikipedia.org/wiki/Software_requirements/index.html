Software requirements

Software requirements are the descriptions of what a software system should do — the functions it must perform and the constraints under which it must operate. They form the foundation of requirements engineering, the branch of software engineering concerned with eliciting, analysing, specifying, validating, and managing the needs of stakeholders.

Types of requirements

Functional requirements specify behaviours or functions of the system, such as calculations, data processing, and user interactions. Non-functional requirements specify criteria for judging the operation of the system — including performance, security, reliability, usability, and scalability — rather than specific behaviours. Requirements are further categorised as business requirements, user requirements, and system requirements.

Documentation

Requirements are traditionally recorded in a Software Requirements Specification (SRS), described by standards such as IEEE 830 and its successor ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148. In agile development, requirements are often expressed as user stories and acceptance criteria maintained in a product backlog. Good requirements are unambiguous, testable, consistent, complete, and traceable to stakeholder needs.

Process

Requirements engineering proceeds through elicitation (interviews, workshops, observation), analysis and negotiation, specification, validation, and ongoing requirements management as needs change over the project's life.
