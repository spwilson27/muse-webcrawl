Bolsena is a town and comune in the province of Viterbo, in the Italian region of Lazio, situated on the northeastern shore of Lake Bolsena, the largest volcanic lake in Europe. The town is known for the medieval Rocca Monaldeschi castle, its Romanesque churches, and its connection with the Eucharistic Miracle of Bolsena.

History

The area was settled in Etruscan and Roman times; the nearby archaeological site of Volsinii Novi preserves Roman remains. In 1263, according to tradition, a Bohemian priest celebrating Mass in the church of Santa Cristina witnessed a consecrated host bleeding onto the altar cloth. The stained corporal was taken to nearby Orvieto, and the event is commemorated by the institution of the Feast of Corpus Christi by Pope Urban IV in 1264 and by the Cappella del Corporale in Orvieto Cathedral.

Main sights

Notable sights include the Collegiate Church of Santa Cristina, with early Christian catacombs, the Rocca Monaldeschi della Cervara fortress housing a museum of the lake, the Palazzo del Drago, and the lakeside promenade. The town's economy is based largely on tourism, fishing, and agriculture, including wine and olive oil production.
