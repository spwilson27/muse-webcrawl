RecQ helicase

RecQ helicase is a family of helicase enzymes important in genome maintenance. They function in the unwinding of DNA, using energy from ATP hydrolysis to separate the strands of a duplex. The family is named after RecQ, the founding member found in Escherichia coli, where it initiates homologous recombination in one repair pathway.

Human RecQ helicases

Humans possess five RecQ-like helicases: RECQ1, BLM, WRN, RECQ4 (RTS) and RECQ5. Mutations in three of them are associated with heritable disorders characterised by genomic instability, cancer predisposition and features of premature ageing:

  • BLM — mutations cause Bloom syndrome.
  • WRN — mutations cause Werner syndrome, an adult progeroid syndrome.
  • RECQ4 — mutations cause Rothmund–Thomson syndrome, Baller–Gerold syndrome and RAPADILINO syndrome.

Function

RecQ helicases act in DNA replication, recombination and repair. They can unwind unusual DNA structures such as G-quadruplexes and Holliday junctions, dissolve recombination intermediates, and stabilise stalled replication forks. Their loss leads to elevated sister-chromatid exchanges — the cellular hallmark of Bloom syndrome — and to chromosomal instability.
