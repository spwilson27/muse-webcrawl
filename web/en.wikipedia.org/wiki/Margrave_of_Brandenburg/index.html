Margrave of Brandenburg

The Margraves of Brandenburg were the rulers of the Margraviate of Brandenburg (German: Markgrafschaft Brandenburg), one of the major principalities of the Holy Roman Empire from 1157 to 1806. Its rulers, the margraves, belonged to the seven prince-electors of the Empire and thus also bore the title of Elector of Brandenburg. The margraviate was founded by Albert the Bear in 1157 and was ruled successively by the houses of Ascania, Wittelsbach, Luxembourg, and, from 1415, Hohenzollern.

History

In 1157, Albert the Bear, already Margrave of the Northern March, definitively conquered Brandenburg from the Slavic prince Jaxa of Köpenick and founded the Margraviate of Brandenburg. The Ascanian margraves expanded the territory eastward, founding towns and promoting German settlement during the Ostsiedlung. By the Golden Bull of 1356, Brandenburg was confirmed as one of the seven electorates of the Holy Roman Empire.

After the Ascanians died out in 1320, Brandenburg passed to the Wittelsbach dynasty and then to the Luxembourgs. In 1415, Emperor Sigismund enfeoffed Frederick VI, Burgrave of Nuremberg, of the House of Hohenzollern, with the margraviate; as Frederick I he became the first Hohenzollern Elector of Brandenburg. His descendants ruled Brandenburg for five centuries.

Later development

In 1618 the Hohenzollern margraves inherited the Duchy of Prussia, and in 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia as Frederick I, creating the composite state of Brandenburg-Prussia. The margraviate remained a province of the Prussian kingdom until the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806.
