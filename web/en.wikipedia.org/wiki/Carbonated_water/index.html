Carbonated water is water containing dissolved carbon dioxide gas, either artificially injected under pressure or occurring naturally above underground formations. The dissolved gas forms small bubbles and a weak acid, carbonic acid, giving the water a slightly tart taste and effervescence. It is also known as sparkling water, soda water, seltzer water or (especially in the United States) fizzy water.

History

In 1767 the English chemist Joseph Priestley discovered a method of infusing water with carbon dioxide by suspending a bowl of water above a beer vat at a brewery in Leeds, producing the first artificially carbonated water. Commercial production followed in the 1780s, notably by Jacob Schweppe in Geneva and London. Soda fountains became widespread in the United States in the 19th century, and seltzer was delivered to homes in distinctive siphon bottles.

Types

Sparkling mineral water is naturally carbonated at source and contains dissolved minerals; seltzer is artificially carbonated water without added minerals; club soda has added mineral salts for flavour; and tonic water contains quinine and sweeteners.

Health aspects

Carbonated water is generally considered as hydrating as still water. Its mild acidity has raised questions about dental erosion, but studies indicate the effect is far smaller than that of sugary soft drinks.
