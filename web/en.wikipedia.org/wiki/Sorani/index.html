Sorani

Sorani (Sorani Kurdish: سۆرانی, Soranî), also called Central Kurdish (کوردیی ناوەندی), is one of the main dialects of the Kurdish language, spoken primarily in Iraqi Kurdistan and western Iran. It is the most widely written form of Kurdish and, alongside Kurmanji, one of the two official languages of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. Sorani is usually written in a modified Perso-Arabic script, unlike Kurmanji, which generally uses the Latin alphabet.

Geographical distribution

Sorani is spoken around the cities of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Halabja and Kirkuk in Iraq, and across the Iranian provinces of Kurdistan and West Azerbaijan, including Mahabad, Sanandaj and Bokan. The dialect takes its name from the former Soran Emirate, centred on Rawandiz.

Grammar features

Unlike Kurmanji, Sorani has lost grammatical gender and case distinctions in nouns, but it retains a distinctive system of clitic pronouns and uses the izafe construction to link nouns and modifiers. It employs split-ergative alignment in past-tense transitive clauses, a feature inherited from older Iranian languages. Its vocabulary includes substantial loanwords from Arabic, Persian and Turkish.

Literature and media

Sorani became the dominant literary Kurdish language in the 20th century, used by poets such as Piramerd and Goran, and is the language of most Kurdish satellite media broadcasting from the Kurdistan Region.
