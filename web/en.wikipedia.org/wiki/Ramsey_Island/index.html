Ramsey Island

Ramsey Island (Welsh: Ynys Dewi)
Location: Pembrokeshire, Wales
Area: 261 hectares
Highest point: Carnysgubor, 136 m
Managed by: RSPB

Ramsey Island (Welsh: Ynys Dewi) is an island about 1 km off the coast of the St Davids peninsula in Pembrokeshire, Wales, in the community of St Davids and the Cathedral Close. It is 261 hectares in size and rises to 136 m at Carnysgubor. The island is an RSPB nature reserve and a National Nature Reserve and Site of Special Scientific Interest.

Wildlife

Ramsey is an important breeding site for grey seals, which pup on its beaches in autumn, and for seabirds including Manx shearwaters, razorbills, guillemots and kittiwakes. It also has a notable population of choughs and peregrine falcons, and red deer were introduced in the 1970s. Rats were eradicated from the island to protect ground-nesting birds.

History and access

The island is associated with St Justinian and St David, and its Welsh name Ynys Dewi means "David's Island". It was farmed for centuries; the last permanent farming family left in the 1960s. Today the island is staffed by RSPB wardens, and visitor boats run from St Justinian in season. The waters of Ramsey Sound are known for strong tidal races and the reef called the Bitches.
