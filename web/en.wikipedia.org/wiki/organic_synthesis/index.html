Organic synthesis

FieldOrganic chemistry
GoalConstruction of organic compounds
Key methodRetrosynthetic analysis

Organic synthesis is a special branch of chemical synthesis dealing with the intentional construction of organic compounds. Organic molecules are often more complex than purely inorganic compounds, and their synthesis has developed into one of the most important branches of organic chemistry.

Approaches

In total synthesis, chemists build a complex target, often a natural product, from simple commercially available precursors. In semisynthesis, compounds isolated from natural sources are modified into new products. Divergent and convergent strategies describe whether intermediates branch toward several targets or are joined from parallel routes.

Retrosynthetic analysis

E. J. Corey's retrosynthetic analysis works backward from the target, breaking bonds on paper (disconnections) to reveal simpler synthons and ultimately starting materials. Protecting groups temporarily mask reactive functionality during multistep sequences.

Landmarks

Famous syntheses include Woodward's strychnine and vitamin B12 syntheses, Kishi's palytoxin work, and modern catalytic asymmetric methods recognized by the 2001 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Green chemistry

Contemporary synthesis emphasizes atom economy, safer solvents, catalysis over stoichiometric reagents, and waste reduction.
