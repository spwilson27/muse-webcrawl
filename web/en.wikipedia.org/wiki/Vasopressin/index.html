Vasopressin, also called antidiuretic hormone (ADH) and argipressin, is a peptide hormone synthesised in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary gland. Its two primary functions are to conserve water by reducing urine output and to constrict blood vessels, raising blood pressure.

Structure and synthesis

Vasopressin is a nonapeptide with a disulfide bridge between two cysteine residues. It is produced as part of a precursor protein in magnocellular neurons of the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus, transported down axons to the posterior pituitary, and released into the bloodstream. In most mammals the eighth residue is arginine (arginine vasopressin); in pigs and some marsupials it is lysine.

Physiological effects

Antidiuretic action

Acting on V2 receptors in the collecting ducts of the kidney, vasopressin triggers insertion of aquaporin-2 water channels into cell membranes, increasing water reabsorption and concentrating the urine. Release is stimulated primarily by increased plasma osmolality, detected by hypothalamic osmoreceptors, and by significant falls in blood volume or pressure.

Vasoconstriction

At higher concentrations, vasopressin acts on V1a receptors in vascular smooth muscle to cause vasoconstriction — the source of the name "vasopressin". It also acts in the brain, where it influences social behaviour, pair bonding, and circadian rhythms in some species, and in the anterior pituitary (via V1b receptors) to modulate ACTH release.

Clinical significance

ConditionVasopressin problemConsequence
Central diabetes insipidusDeficient secretionLarge volumes of dilute urine
Nephrogenic diabetes insipidusKidney unresponsive (V2/aquaporin defect)Same, despite normal ADH
SIADHExcess secretionWater retention, hyponatraemia

The synthetic analogue desmopressin (DDAVP), selective for V2 receptors, is used to treat central diabetes insipidus and bedwetting. Vasopressin itself is used in vasodilatory shock and historically in cardiac arrest protocols. Receptor antagonists (vaptans) are used for hyponatraemia in SIADH.
