Wildlife of Iran

The wildlife of Iran includes the flora and fauna of the country and their natural habitats. Iran's varied climates and terrain — Caspian forests, Zagros and Alborz mountains, central deserts and Persian Gulf coasts — support a rich biodiversity at the junction of several biogeographic regions.

Mammals

Iran is one of the last strongholds of the critically endangered Asiatic cheetah, and also hosts the Persian leopard, Eurasian lynx, caracal, striped hyena, grey wolf, golden jackal, urial, wild goat (bezoar ibex), goitered gazelle and the endangered Persian fallow deer. The Caspian tiger became extinct in the mid-20th century.

Birds and other fauna

More than 500 bird species have been recorded, including Siberian cranes wintering in the past, ground jays and flamingos in wetlands such as the Anzali Lagoon. Reptiles include the Persian horned viper; the Caspian Sea hosts the Caspian seal and sturgeons prized for caviar.

Conservation

Protected areas cover roughly a tenth of the country, administered by the Department of Environment, including Golestan, Kavir and Touran national parks. Threats include habitat loss, drought, poaching and road mortality, notably for the cheetah, of which only a few dozen survive.
