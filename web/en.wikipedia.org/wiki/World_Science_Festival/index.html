The World Science Festival is an annual science festival held in New York City, produced by the World Science Foundation. Founded in 2008 by Columbia University physicist Brian Greene and his wife, the former television producer Tracy Day, it presents science to general audiences through panel discussions, demonstrations, performances and outdoor events, with the aim of conveying the drama and wonder of scientific discovery in the manner of an arts festival.

History and format

The inaugural festival ran from 28 May to 1 June 2008 across venues including New York University, the Metropolitan Museum of Art and Lincoln Center, and drew tens of thousands of attendees; a street fair for families closed the event. Subsequent festivals have typically taken place over five days in late spring, combining ticketed main-stage programmes — on subjects such as cosmology, neuroscience, artificial intelligence and climate — with free events, youth and family programming, and the Big Ideas series of conversations with Nobel laureates and other prominent researchers. Panels have featured figures such as Stephen Hawking (by video), E. O. Wilson, Oliver Sacks and numerous Nobel Prize winners.

Related initiatives

The World Science Foundation also produces the World Science Scholars programme for exceptionally talented high-school mathematics and science students, the Kavli Prizes-related programming, and the annual Brian Greene-hosted lecture series. Since 2016 a sister event, World Science Festival Brisbane, has been staged in Australia in partnership with the Queensland Museum, the first long-term international licence of the festival. Festival programmes are extensively archived online, and its videos of talks on subjects such as string theory and black holes have reached large global audiences.

Reception

The festival has been widely praised for treating science as culture and for the scale of its audiences, and is credited with inspiring a wave of similar public science festivals in the United States and abroad. It has also received support from major foundations and cultural institutions in New York.
