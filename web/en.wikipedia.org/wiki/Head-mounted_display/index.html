A head-mounted display (HMD) is a display device worn on the head or as part of a helmet, with a small display optic in front of one eye (monocular) or each eye (binocular). HMDs are the primary hardware interface for virtual reality and much of augmented reality, combining miniature high-resolution displays, lenses, and motion tracking so that the imagery responds to head movement.

History

Computer scientist Ivan Sutherland built the first tracked HMD in 1968; it was so heavy it had to be suspended from the ceiling, earning the nickname "the Sword of Damocles". Military aviation drove development for decades — helmet-mounted sights let pilots target weapons by looking — and NASA and research labs pursued virtual-environment systems through the 1980s and 1990s. Consumer VR arrived with the Oculus Rift Kickstarter in 2012, followed by HTC Vive, PlayStation VR, and standalone headsets such as the Meta Quest line; mixed-reality devices include Microsoft HoloLens and Apple Vision Pro (2024).

Technology

Key parameters include field of view, resolution per eye, refresh rate, and motion-to-photon latency, which must be kept very low to avoid simulator sickness. Tracking may be outside-in (external cameras or base stations) or inside-out (onboard cameras performing simultaneous localisation and mapping). Optical see-through HMDs superimpose graphics on the real world, while video-passthrough designs mediate the view with cameras.
