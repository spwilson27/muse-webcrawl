Yttrium(III) oxide

Yttrium(III) oxide, also known as yttria, is the chemical compound with the formula Y2O3. It is an air-stable, white solid oxide of the rare-earth metal yttrium and the most important yttrium compound in commerce, serving as the starting material for most other yttrium compounds and as a valued material in its own right.

Structure and properties

Yttria adopts a cubic bixbyite (C-type rare-earth oxide) crystal structure at room temperature, with yttrium ions in two distinct six-coordinate environments. It is a wide-bandgap semiconductor-like insulator with a dielectric constant of about 14–18, and it begins to sublime or decompose only at very high temperatures. Thin films can be deposited by pulsed laser deposition, sputtering and atomic layer deposition.

Applications

Yttria is the precursor of the red phosphor Y2O3:Eu3+ used in colour television tubes, fluorescent lamps and LEDs, and of yttrium iron garnet (YIG) used in microwave devices. It is the host material of the Nd:YAG laser when combined with alumina in yttrium aluminium garnet, and it stabilises zirconia in the ceramic yttria-stabilised zirconia (YSZ), used in thermal barrier coatings, oxygen sensors and solid-oxide fuel cells. Transparent yttria ceramics have been developed as laser hosts and infrared windows. Historically, yttria was also the active material in the Nernst lamp glower.

Production

Yttria is obtained from rare-earth ores such as xenotime and monazite by solvent extraction and ion-exchange separation of the chemically similar lanthanides, followed by precipitation of yttrium oxalate or hydroxide and calcination to the oxide.
