Solar architecture

Solar architecture is the integration of solar technology into modern building techniques in response to rising concerns about environmental sustainability and energy consumption. The goal of solar architecture is to design buildings that use the sun's energy for heating, cooling, lighting, and electricity generation, thereby reducing dependence on fossil fuels and minimising the building's overall environmental impact.

History

The use of solar energy in building design dates back to ancient times. The Greeks and Romans oriented their houses to capture the winter sun, and the cliff dwellings of the Ancestral Puebloans in the American Southwest were positioned to take advantage of solar gain in winter while providing shade in summer. In the twentieth century, interest in solar architecture grew during the energy crises of the 1970s, which spurred experimentation with passive solar design, solar water heating, and early photovoltaic systems. Since then, advances in materials, glazing, and building-integrated photovoltaics have made solar design a mainstream element of sustainable architecture.

Passive solar design

Passive solar design uses the building's orientation, materials, and layout to collect, store, and distribute solar energy without mechanical systems. Key strategies include orienting the long axis of the building east–west, placing most windows on the sun-facing side (south-facing in the Northern Hemisphere), using thermal mass such as concrete or brick floors and walls to store heat, and designing overhangs that admit low winter sun while blocking high summer sun. Proper insulation and airtight construction complement these measures.

Active solar systems

Active solar systems use mechanical or electrical equipment to convert sunlight into usable energy. These include photovoltaic (PV) panels that generate electricity, solar thermal collectors that heat water or air, and solar-assisted heat pumps. Building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) replace conventional building materials in parts of the building envelope, such as roofs, skylights, or facades, serving both as the outer skin of the structure and as an electricity generator.

Daylighting

Daylighting is the practice of designing buildings to make maximum use of natural light, reducing the need for artificial lighting. Techniques include light shelves, clerestory windows, atria, reflective surfaces, and careful window placement to bring daylight deep into interior spaces while controlling glare and heat gain.
