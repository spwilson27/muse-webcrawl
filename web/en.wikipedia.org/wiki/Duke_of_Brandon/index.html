The title Duke of Brandon is a title in the Peerage of Great Britain, held by the Dukes of Hamilton since its creation in 1711. Brandon is a village in Suffolk, and the title refers to Brandon Hall in that county.

History

The title was created on 10 September 1711 for James Hamilton, 4th Duke of Hamilton, a Scottish peer. The creation was politically controversial: the House of Lords initially resolved that Scottish peers created peers of Great Britain after the Union of 1707 could not sit in the Lords under their new titles, a position reversed in 1782 when the 9th Duke of Hamilton took his seat as Duke of Brandon.

The Dukes of Hamilton and Brandon are also hereditary Keepers of Holyroodhouse and bear the Crown of Scotland in procession. The current holder is Alexander Douglas-Hamilton, 16th Duke of Hamilton and 13th Duke of Brandon.
