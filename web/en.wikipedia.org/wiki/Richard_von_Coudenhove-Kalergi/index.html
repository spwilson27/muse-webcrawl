Count Richard Nikolaus Eijiro von Coudenhove-Kalergi (17 November 1894 – 27 July 1972) was an Austrian-Japanese politician, philosopher and writer, founder of the Paneuropean Union and one of the earliest and most persistent advocates of a politically united Europe. He is considered a founding figure of the modern European integration project.

Background

Coudenhove-Kalergi was born in Tokyo, where his father, an Austro-Hungarian diplomat, was stationed; his mother, Mitsuko Aoyama, came from a Japanese merchant and samurai-descended family. He grew up on the family estate at Ronsperg (Poběžovice) in Bohemia and studied philosophy and history in Vienna, taking his doctorate in 1917.

The Paneuropean movement

Shocked by the First World War, he concluded that Europe's nation-states would destroy the continent unless they united. In 1922 he founded the Paneuropean Union, and in 1923 published his manifesto Pan-Europa, proposing a political and economic union of European states (initially excluding Britain and Soviet Russia). The movement attracted prominent supporters, including Aristide Briand, who served as its honorary president, and figures such as Albert Einstein, Thomas Mann and Konrad Adenauer.

His books were burned by the Nazis and he fled Austria after the Anschluss in 1938, eventually reaching the United States, where he taught at New York University. After the war he returned to Europe, founded the European Parliamentary Union in 1947, and campaigned for the Council of Europe and successive integration projects. In 1950 he became the first recipient of the Charlemagne Prize of Aachen for services to European unity.

Ideas

Coudenhove-Kalergi envisioned five great world blocs — Pan-Europe, Pan-America, a British Commonwealth bloc, the Soviet bloc and Pan-Asia — and saw European federation as the only means for the continent to remain a world power. His writings also contain reflections on the future demographic and cultural mixing of Europe that have been quoted, often polemically and out of context, in modern debates about the origins of the European Union.
