Shareholder activism

Shareholder activism comprises efforts by shareholders to influence a corporation's behaviour or management by exercising their rights as partial owners. Activist shareholders range from large institutional investors and specialised hedge funds to individual investors and public-interest groups.

Methods

Activists may file shareholder resolutions, wage proxy fights to elect directors, run public campaigns, negotiate privately with management, or litigate. Hedge-fund activists typically seek strategic or financial changes — such as selling divisions, returning cash to shareholders or replacing executives — while social and governance activists focus on issues such as climate risk, executive pay or board diversity.

Debate

Supporters argue activism improves accountability and long-term value; critics contend some campaigns promote short-term gains at the expense of investment and other stakeholders.
