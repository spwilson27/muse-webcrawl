Shimon bar Yochai (Aramaic: רשב״י, Rashbi) was a 2nd-century tannaitic sage in ancient Israel, said to be active after the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE. A leading disciple of Rabbi Akiva, he is a central figure in rabbinic literature, with many teachings recorded in the Mishnah, and is traditionally regarded as the author of the Zohar, the foundational work of Kabbalah.

Life and legend

According to rabbinic tradition, Shimon spoke critically of Roman rule and was condemned to death, fleeing with his son Eleazar to hide in a cave in the Galilee. Sustained by a carob tree and a spring, they are said to have remained in hiding for thirteen years, devoting themselves to study. Upon emerging, Shimon performed miracles and became a celebrated wonder-worker. He was among the five disciples ordained by Judah ben Baba after the Bar Kokhba revolt, and tradition holds that he was later sent to Rome, where his exorcism of a demon from the emperor's daughter won the annulment of anti-Jewish decrees.

Teachings

More than 700 teachings of Shimon bar Yochai are preserved in rabbinic sources. The homiletic midrash Sifre on Numbers and Deuteronomy is traditionally attributed to his school. He is quoted as teaching that there are three crowns — the crown of Torah, the crown of priesthood, and the crown of kingship — but that the crown of a good name surpasses them all (Mishnah Avot 4:13, attributed in some versions).

The Zohar

The Zohar, a mystical commentary on the Torah written in Aramaic, presents itself as the teachings of Shimon and his circle. It surfaced in 13th-century Castile through Moses de León. Most academic scholars, following Gershom Scholem, regard it as a medieval composition, while traditional Kabbalists maintain its ancient authorship by Shimon bar Yochai.

Lag BaOmer and Meron

The anniversary of Shimon's death, on Lag BaOmer (the 33rd day of the Omer count, 18 Iyar), is celebrated as a joyous festival, with mass pilgrimages to his tomb at Meron, bonfires, and music. The gathering at Meron is one of the largest annual Jewish events in Israel. In April 2021 a crowd crush at the site killed 45 people, the deadliest civilian disaster in Israel's history.
