Spearman's rank correlation coefficient

In statistics, Spearman's rank correlation coefficient, named after Charles Spearman and often denoted by the Greek letter ρ (rho) or rs, is a nonparametric measure of rank correlation: the statistical dependence between the rankings of two variables.

Definition

Spearman's coefficient is the Pearson correlation coefficient computed between the rank values of the variables rather than the raw data. For a sample of size n with no tied ranks, it can be computed as ρ = 1 − (6 Σ di2) / (n(n2 − 1)), where di is the difference between the two ranks of each observation.

Interpretation

The coefficient ranges from −1 to +1. It assesses how well the relationship between two variables can be described by a monotonic function, unlike Pearson's coefficient, which measures only linear association. It is therefore robust to outliers and appropriate for ordinal data. Significance can be tested using a t-approximation with n − 2 degrees of freedom.

History

Spearman introduced the coefficient in 1904 in work on the correlation of mental traits, making it one of the earliest nonparametric statistics.
