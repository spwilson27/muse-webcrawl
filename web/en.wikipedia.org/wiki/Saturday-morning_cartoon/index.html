Saturday-morning cartoon

Saturday-morning cartoon is a colloquial term for the animated television programming that was typically scheduled on Saturday mornings in the United States on the major broadcast networks, aimed primarily at children. The block was a fixture of American television from the mid-1960s through the late 1990s.

History

When television animation developed in the 1950s, early shows were often broadcast in prime time. In the mid-1960s the major networks, led by CBS programming executive Fred Silverman, concentrated children's animation on Saturday mornings, where it attracted large young audiences and heavy advertising from toy and cereal manufacturers. Producers such as Hanna-Barbera, Filmation, and DePatie–Freleng supplied hours of limited-animation series, including Scooby-Doo, Where Are You! and The Bugs Bunny Show.

Regulation and criticism

Advocacy groups such as Action for Children's Television criticised the programming's violence and advertising content, leading to Federal Communications Commission guidelines in the 1970s and, later, the Children's Television Act of 1990, which required educational programming.

Decline

The tradition eroded from the 1990s as cable channels such as Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network offered children's programming all week, home video and streaming changed viewing habits, and the networks progressively replaced cartoons with news, sports, and educational or live-action programming. By 2014 no major broadcast network still aired a traditional Saturday-morning cartoon block.
