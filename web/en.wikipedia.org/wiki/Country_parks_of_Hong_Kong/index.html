Country parks of Hong Kong

24 country parks covering about 44,300 hectares.

The country parks of Hong Kong are protected areas designated under the Country Parks Ordinance of 1976 for conservation, recreation, and education. Despite Hong Kong's dense urban development, the parks cover roughly 40 per cent of its land area—about 44,300 hectares—encompassing hills, woodland, reservoirs, and coastline in the New Territories and on outlying islands.

History

Concerns about protecting Hong Kong's water-gathering grounds and countryside led to the Country Parks Ordinance (Cap. 208) in 1976; the first parks, including Shing Mun and Lantau South, were designated in 1977. The Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department manages the system, maintaining hiking trails, campsites, barbecue areas, and visitor centres, and carrying out large-scale tree planting that has transformed formerly eroded hillsides into secondary woodland.

Major parks

The largest park is Lantau South Country Park, covering much of southern Lantau Island including Lantau Peak and Sunset Peak. Other notable parks include Tai Mo Shan (containing Hong Kong's highest peak), Sai Kung East and Sai Kung West (known for beaches and the High Island Reservoir), Plover Cove, Tai Lam, Ma On Shan, Pat Sin Leng, and the Aberdeen and Pok Fu Lam parks on Hong Kong Island. Long-distance trails—the MacLehose, Wilson, Hong Kong, and Lantau trails—cross many of the parks.

Flora, fauna, and use

The parks support species such as barking deer, porcupines, macaques, wild boar, and a rich bird and butterfly fauna, alongside native woodland planted with species like Schima and Acacia. Tens of millions of visits are made annually for hiking, camping, picnicking, and trail running. Activities such as lighting fires outside designated sites, littering, and unlicensed camping are regulated by permit and by-law.
