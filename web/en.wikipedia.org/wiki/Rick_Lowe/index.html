Rick Lowe (born 1961) is an American artist and community organiser, widely regarded as a pioneering figure in social practice art. He is the founder of Project Row Houses in Houston's Third Ward, a celebrated model of art-driven community development, and the recipient of a 2014 MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship.

Early life and education

Lowe was born in rural Russell County, Alabama, and studied at Columbus State University in Georgia before moving to Houston in 1985, where he became involved in community activism through art, creating politically charged paintings and installations addressing issues such as homelessness and police violence. A turning point came when a young student challenged him to move beyond depicting problems to creating solutions.

Project Row Houses

In 1993, Lowe and a group of fellow African American artists — including Jesse Lott, Bert Samples and Terry Adkins — acquired 22 derelict shotgun houses on a two-block site in Houston's historic Third Ward. Rather than demolishing them, they renovated the houses into a hybrid of art spaces, housing for young single mothers, and community services. Project Row Houses (PRH) grew to encompass dozens of properties, programmes in arts education, housing for artists in residence, and the spin-off Row House CDC for affordable housing. It became an internationally cited model of "social sculpture" — a term Lowe borrowed from Joseph Beuys — in which the artwork is the sustained transformation of a neighbourhood, balancing revitalisation against displacement of existing residents.

Other projects

Lowe has applied the model elsewhere: the Watts House Project in Los Angeles (2009), the Victoria Square Project in Athens, Greece (for documenta 14, 2017), and work in New Orleans after Hurricane Katrina. Since 2013 he has been a professor of art at the University of Houston. In the 2020s he also returned to painting, producing large abstract canvases meditating on maps and domino games, exhibited by Gagosian.

Recognition

Among Lowe's honours are the MacArthur Fellowship (2014), the Heinz Award in the Arts and Humanities, a Loeb Fellowship at Harvard's Graduate School of Design, and honorary doctorates. Project Row Houses received the National Trust for Historic Preservation's Rudy Bruner Award and other planning and arts honours.
