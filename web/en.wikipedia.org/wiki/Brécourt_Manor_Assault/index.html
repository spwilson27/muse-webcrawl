The Brécourt Manor Assault was a successful assault by United States Army paratroopers of Easy Company, 2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division, on a German artillery battery during the early hours of D-Day, 6 June 1944, in the Normandy landings of the Second World War. The battery of four 105 mm howitzers, dug in and camouflaged in a field at Brécourt Manor near Sainte-Marie-du-Mont, was firing on American troops landing at Utah Beach.

The assault

First Lieutenant Richard Winters, having assumed command of Easy Company after its commander was killed in the jump, assembled a scratch force of roughly a dozen men from his company and others. Reconnoitring the position, Winters devised a plan exploiting the trench system connecting the four gun pits: a small assault team would attack one gun at a time along the trenches while a base of fire with machine guns suppressed the others. In about two to three hours of fighting, the paratroopers destroyed all four guns using TNT charges placed down the barrels, killed or drove off the German defenders, and captured a map showing German gun positions across the Cotentin Peninsula.

Legacy

The action is still taught at the United States Military Academy at West Point as a model small-unit assault on a defended position. Winters received the Distinguished Service Cross, and other participants received Silver and Bronze Stars. The assault was dramatised in the second episode ("Day of Days") of the 2001 miniseries Band of Brothers.
