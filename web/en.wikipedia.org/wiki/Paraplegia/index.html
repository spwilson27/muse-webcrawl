Paraplegia

Paraplegia is an impairment in motor or sensory function of the lower extremities. It usually results from injury to the spinal cord below the first thoracic spinal nerve, or from congenital conditions such as spina bifida. Loss of function affecting all four limbs is called quadriplegia (tetraplegia).

Causes

The most common causes are traumatic injuries such as vehicle collisions, falls, and violence, as well as tumours, infections, vascular disorders, and degenerative diseases affecting the spinal cord.

Management

There is no cure for spinal cord injury, but rehabilitation, mobility aids such as wheelchairs, and management of complications (pressure sores, urinary infections, spasticity) greatly improve independence and quality of life. Research into neuroprosthetics and regenerative therapies is ongoing.
