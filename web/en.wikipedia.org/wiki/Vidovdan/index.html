Vidovdan

Vidovdan
Observed bySerbs; Serbian Orthodox Church
Date28 June (Gregorian); 15 June (Julian)
SignificanceFeast of St. Vitus; commemoration of the Battle of Kosovo (1389)

Vidovdan (Serbian Cyrillic: Видовдан, "St. Vitus Day") is a Serbian national and religious holiday, a slava (feast day) celebrated on 28 June. It commemorates Saint Vitus and, above all, the Battle of Kosovo of 1389, in which Prince Lazar and his army fought the invading Ottomans. Few dates carry comparable weight in Serbian historical memory.

The Gazimestan monument near the site of the 1389 Battle of Kosovo, where Vidovdan commemorations are held.

Historical associations

The date has repeatedly coincided with pivotal events in Serbian and European history. On Vidovdan 1914, Gavrilo Princip assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo, precipitating the First World War. The Treaty of Versailles was signed on the same date in 1919, and the Yugoslav Vidovdan Constitution was adopted in 1921. On 28 June 1989, Slobodan Milošević delivered the Gazimestan speech before a huge crowd at the battlefield's 600th anniversary, an event widely seen as a harbinger of the Yugoslav wars. In 2001, Milošević was transferred to The Hague tribunal on Vidovdan.

Observance

The Serbian Orthodox Church commemorates Prince Lazar, canonized as a martyr, whose relics rest at Ravanica Monastery. Liturgies and memorial services (parastos) for fallen soldiers are held across Serbia and the diaspora, and the day functions as a general day of remembrance for Serbian war dead.
