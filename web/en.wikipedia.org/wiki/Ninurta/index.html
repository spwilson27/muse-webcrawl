Ninurta

Ninurta (Sumerian: 𒀭𒊩𒌆𒁺, "lord of the earth/plough") was a Mesopotamian god associated with farming, healing, hunting, law and war. Originally worshipped as a god of agriculture and thunderstorms in Sumer — where as Ninĝirsu he was patron of Girsu — he became in Akkadian and Assyrian tradition a warrior god, son of Enlil, with his chief shrine at Nippur and later a temple at Kalhu (Nimrud).

Mythology

Ninurta stars in several Sumerian and Akkadian myths: his battle with the demon Asag to restore the waters of the Tigris; the Anzû myth, in which he recovers the stolen Tablet of Destinies from the great bird Anzû; and Lugal-e, recounting his ordering of stones and rivers. He was credited with teaching agriculture to mankind and was husband of the healing goddess Gula (Bau).

Worship

Cult centres included Nippur's Ešumeša temple and Lagash/Girsu in his form Ninĝirsu. Under the Neo-Assyrian empire his warlike aspect was emphasised, and Ashurnasirpal II built him a major temple at the capital Kalhu.
