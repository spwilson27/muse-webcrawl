Foundation (engineering)

Foundations transfer the loads of a structure safely to the ground.

In engineering, a foundation is the element of a structure that connects it to the ground and transfers loads from the structure to the earth. Foundations are designed so that the soil or rock beneath can bear the loads without excessive settlement or failure, and they are generally divided into shallow foundations, which transfer loads near the surface, and deep foundations, which reach down to stronger strata.

Shallow foundations

Shallow (spread) foundations include isolated pad footings under columns, strip footings under walls, combined footings, and raft (mat) foundations that spread the load of a whole building over a large slab — useful on weak soils or where loads are heavy and closely spaced. They are placed below the depth of frost action and seasonal moisture change, and are economical where competent soil exists near the surface.

Deep foundations

Deep foundations include driven piles (timber, steel or precast concrete), bored piles and drilled shafts formed in place, caissons, and micropiles. They transfer loads by end bearing on a hard stratum, by friction along the shaft, or both. Deep foundations are used for high-rise buildings, bridges, offshore platforms and wind turbines, and wherever surface soils are soft, compressible or subject to scour or liquefaction.

Design considerations

Geotechnical investigation — boreholes, sampling and testing — establishes soil strength, bearing capacity and groundwater conditions. Designers check ultimate limit states (bearing failure, sliding, overturning) and serviceability (total and differential settlement, which cracks buildings), following codes such as Eurocode 7 or national equivalents. Karl Terzaghi, founder of soil mechanics, developed the classic bearing-capacity theory in the 1940s.

Problems and remediation

Foundation failures arise from expansive clays, subsidence, erosion, nearby excavation, and earthquake liquefaction. The Leaning Tower of Pisa is a famous case of differential settlement, stabilised in 1990–2001 by soil extraction. Remediation techniques include underpinning with new piles or piers, jet grouting, resin injection, and structural jacking. Retaining structures and basement waterproofing form related parts of foundation engineering.
