Acid dissociation constant

An acid dissociation constant, Ka, (also known as acidity constant, or acid-ionization constant; denoted by the symbol Ka) is a quantitative measure of the strength of an acid in solution. It is the equilibrium constant for a chemical reaction known as dissociation in the context of acid–base reactions. For a generic acid HA, the dissociation can be written as:

HA ⇌ A− + H+

where A− is the conjugate base of the acid. The equilibrium constant is:

Ka = [A−][H+] / [HA]

pKa

Because the values of Ka span many orders of magnitude, they are usually expressed as a logarithmic value, pKa = −log10 Ka. A smaller pKa value indicates a stronger acid. For example, acetic acid has a pKa of about 4.76, while hydrochloric acid has a pKa of roughly −7.

Polyprotic acids

Acids that can donate more than one proton, such as sulfuric acid or phosphoric acid, have multiple dissociation constants, Ka1, Ka2, and so on, corresponding to the successive loss of each proton. Successive values generally decrease because it is harder to remove a positively charged proton from an increasingly negative species.

Equilibrium constant

Strictly, the dissociation constant should be defined in terms of activities rather than concentrations, but in dilute solutions the two are approximately equal. The value of Ka depends on temperature and on the solvent; most tabulated values are for water at 25 °C.

The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

The relationship between pH, pKa, and the ratio of conjugate base to acid is given by the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log10([A−]/[HA]). This equation is central to the design of buffer solutions, which resist changes in pH near the pKa of the weak acid used.
