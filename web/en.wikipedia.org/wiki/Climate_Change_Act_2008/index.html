Climate Change Act 2008

The Climate Change Act 2008 (c. 27) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. The Act makes it the duty of the Secretary of State to ensure that the net UK carbon account for all six greenhouse gases for the year 2050 is at least 100% lower than the 1990 baseline, toward avoiding dangerous climate change. The Act aims to enable the United Kingdom to become a low-carbon economy and gives ministers powers to introduce the necessary measures to achieve that aim.

Background

The Act was the world's first legally binding national framework for cutting greenhouse gas emissions. It followed the Stern Review on the economics of climate change (2006) and a campaign led by Friends of the Earth ("The Big Ask") for a climate change bill. The bill was introduced by the Labour government of Gordon Brown and passed with near-unanimous cross-party support, with only five MPs voting against at its third reading.

Provisions

Key provisions include:

  • A legally binding target to reduce emissions. As originally enacted this was at least 80% by 2050 against a 1990 baseline; in June 2019 the target was amended to 100% ("net zero") by secondary legislation.
  • A system of five-year carbon budgets, set at least twelve years in advance, capping total emissions over each period.
  • The establishment of the independent Committee on Climate Change to advise the government on budgets and report annually to Parliament on progress.
  • Requirements for government reporting on the risks of climate change and a programme of adaptation.

Influence

The Act has served as a model for climate framework legislation in other countries, and its carbon budgeting system became a template for subsequent laws elsewhere in Europe and beyond.
