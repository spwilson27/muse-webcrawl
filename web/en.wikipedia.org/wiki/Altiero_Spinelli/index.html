Altiero Spinelli

Altiero Spinelli (1907–1986) was an Italian politician and a founding father of European federalism. A former communist imprisoned by Mussolini's Fascist regime, he became one of the most influential advocates of a federal Europe.

The Ventotene Manifesto

While confined on the island of Ventotene in 1941, Spinelli co-authored with Ernesto Rossi (and with input from Eugenio Colorni) the Ventotene Manifesto, "For a Free and United Europe", which argued that the nation-state system had caused Europe's wars and called for a European federation. Smuggled to the mainland, it became the founding document of the European Federalist Movement, which Spinelli established in Milan in 1943.

Institutions

After the war Spinelli advised Alcide De Gasperi, served as a European Commissioner (1970–1976), and was elected to the Italian parliament and then the European Parliament. There he led the "Crocodile Club" of federalist MEPs and drafted the Draft Treaty establishing the European Union, adopted by the Parliament in 1984; though not enacted as such, it influenced the Single European Act and later treaties. A main building of the European Parliament in Brussels bears his name. He died in Rome in 1986.
