F. W. Woolworth Company

The F. W. Woolworth Company (often referred to as Woolworth's or simply Woolworth) was a retail company and one of the pioneers of the five-and-dime store. It was among the most successful American and international five-and-dime businesses, setting trends and creating a model that other retailers adopted. Founded by Frank Winfield Woolworth, the first store opened on 22 February 1879 in Utica, New York; after it failed, a second store opened in Lancaster, Pennsylvania on 21 June 1879 and became a success.

Woolworth's sold general merchandise at fixed low prices — originally five and ten cents — undercutting local merchants who bargained with customers. The company grew rapidly, incorporated in 1905, and in 1913 built the Woolworth Building in New York City, then the tallest building in the world, paid for in cash. By 1924 it operated more than 2,000 stores in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Germany.

Civil rights history

Woolworth's lunch counters were the site of the Greensboro sit-ins of 1960, when four Black students from North Carolina A&T sat at the segregated counter in Greensboro, North Carolina, sparking a nationwide protest movement that led the company to desegregate its counters later that year.

Decline and transformation

Faced with competition from discount chains, the U.S. Woolworth variety stores closed in 1997. The corporation refocused on sporting goods and footwear, principally Foot Locker, and renamed itself Venator Group in 1998 and Foot Locker, Inc. in 2001. Separate Woolworth businesses survived longer abroad: the British Woolworths chain collapsed in 2008–2009, while Woolworths in Australia and South Africa are unrelated companies, and Woolworth GmbH continues in Germany.
