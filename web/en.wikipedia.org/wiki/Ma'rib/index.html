Ma'rib

Ma'rib (Arabic: مأرب) is a city in Yemen and the capital of Ma'rib Governorate, east of Sanaa. In antiquity it was the capital of the kingdom of Saba (Sheba), famous for the Great Dam of Ma'rib, one of the engineering wonders of the ancient world, which irrigated the surrounding oasis for more than a millennium.

Ancient Ma'rib

The ancient city was a major stop on the Incense Route, trading frankincense and myrrh northward to the Mediterranean. The Great Dam, built in stages from around the 8th century BCE, eventually stood some 580 metres long. Its final breach in about 570 CE, mentioned in the Qur'an, is traditionally associated with the decline of the South Arabian kingdoms and large migrations northward. Nearby ruins include the Awwam Temple (Mahram Bilqis), associated with the Queen of Sheba, and the Barran Temple.

Modern era

The modern dam, completed in 1986 with funding from the United Arab Emirates, was built a few kilometres upstream. During the Yemeni Civil War, Ma'rib became a strategically vital stronghold of the internationally recognised government, hosting large numbers of displaced people and defending rich oil and gas fields nearby; it withstood a major Houthi offensive in 2021. The ancient landmarks of Ma'rib were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023.
