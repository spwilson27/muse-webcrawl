Detmold

Detmold (German pronunciation: [ˈdɛtmɔlt]) is a town in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, with a population of approximately 75,000. It was the capital of the small Principality of Lippe from 1468 until 1918 and then of the Free State of Lippe until 1947. Today it is the administrative centre of the district of Lippe and of the Detmold government region.

History

Detmold was first mentioned in 783 as Theotmalli, near the site where the Saxons under Charlemagne fought in the Saxon Wars. The town received its charter in the 13th century. From 1468 the counts (later princes) of Lippe resided in the castle, making Detmold a small residential capital notable for its court theatre and music tradition.

Sights

  • The Princely Residence Castle (Fürstliches Residenzschloss), a Renaissance palace still occupied by the princely family.
  • The Hermannsdenkmal, a 53-metre monument to Arminius (Hermann), victor of the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest, completed in 1875 on the Grotenburg hill nearby.
  • The LWL Open-Air Museum Detmold, one of the largest open-air museums in Germany.
  • The Hochschule für Musik Detmold, a renowned music conservatory.

Culture

The composer Johannes Brahms spent formative years at the Lippe court in the 1850s. Detmold's theatre, the Landestheater, is one of the largest touring theatres in Germany.
