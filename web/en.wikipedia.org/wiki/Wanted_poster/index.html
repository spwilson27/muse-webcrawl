Wanted poster

A wanted poster is a poster, traditionally distributed in public places, identifying a person whom authorities wish to apprehend, usually a fugitive from justice or a suspect in a crime. Wanted posters typically include a photograph or likeness, physical description, details of the alleged offence, and contact information; many offer a reward. The words "WANTED" appear in large letters at the top, and the phrase "dead or alive" is famously associated with posters of the American Old West, though it was rare on real historical examples.

A wanted poster in the classic American style

History

Public notices seeking fugitives date to antiquity, but the printed wanted poster flourished in 19th-century America, where law enforcement agencies, railroads, and private firms such as the Pinkerton Detective Agency circulated handbills for outlaws including Jesse James and Billy the Kid. After the assassination of Abraham Lincoln in 1865, the U.S. War Department issued a famous poster offering rewards for John Wilkes Booth and his accomplices — among the first to use photographs.

Modern use

The FBI's Ten Most Wanted Fugitives list, launched in 1950, is the modern descendant of the wanted poster; posters are now distributed primarily online and through media rather than nailed to posts. Similar notices, such as Interpol red notices, operate internationally. Wanted posters remain a fixture of popular culture depictions of the Wild West.
