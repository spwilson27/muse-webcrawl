Guilloché

Guilloché is a decorative technique in which a very precise, intricate and repetitive pattern is mechanically engraved into a material with fine detail. Traditionally executed on a rose-engine lathe, the interlaced bands of the pattern form designs that are extremely difficult to forge — a quality exploited on banknotes, passports, certificates and other security documents.

The technique flourished in fine watchmaking from the 18th century, notably on the dials and cases of Abraham-Louis Breguet, and in the enamelled gold work of Fabergé. Guilloché patterns on paper currency are generated by geometric lathes and, in modern design, by mathematical software producing equivalent lathe-like curves.

Related visual patterns arise from spirograph drawing tools and from the interference effects known as moiré.
