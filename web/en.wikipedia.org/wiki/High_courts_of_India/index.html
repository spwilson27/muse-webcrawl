High courts of India

The high courts of India are the highest courts of appellate and original jurisdiction in each state and union territory of India. There are 25 high courts in the country, each functioning as the principal civil and criminal court of appeal for its territorial jurisdiction, and exercising original civil and criminal jurisdiction when a case involves a substantial question of law or specified subject matter. Below the high courts are district and subordinate courts, and above them stands the Supreme Court of India.

The work of most high courts primarily consists of appeals from lower courts and writ petitions under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution of India. Writ jurisdiction is also an original jurisdiction of the high court.

History

The first high courts in India were established by letters patent issued by Queen Victoria in 1862 under the Indian High Courts Act 1861, at Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras. The Calcutta High Court, formally opened on 1 July 1862, is the oldest high court in the country. A fourth high court was established at Allahabad in 1866. Over time each province and later each state acquired its own high court; the newest is the Andhra Pradesh High Court at Amaravati, which began functioning in 2019 after the bifurcation of the former common high court at Hyderabad.

Composition and appointment

Each high court consists of a chief justice and such other judges as the President of India determines. Judges are appointed by the President after consultation through the collegium system, under Articles 217 and 222 of the Constitution. High court judges serve until the age of 62 and can only be removed by impeachment by Parliament on grounds of proven misbehaviour or incapacity.

Jurisdiction and powers

  • Original jurisdiction — in matters such as enforcement of fundamental rights (through writs under Article 226), company law, and certain revenue and admiralty matters in the presidency courts.
  • Appellate jurisdiction — appeals against decisions of district and subordinate courts in civil and criminal cases.
  • Supervisory jurisdiction — under Article 227, high courts exercise superintendence over all courts and tribunals in their territory.
  • Court of record — under Article 215, every high court is a court of record with power to punish for contempt of itself.

List of high courts

India's 25 high courts include, in order of establishment: Calcutta (1862), Bombay (1862), Madras (1862), Allahabad (1866), Karnataka (1884), Patna (1916), Madhya Pradesh (1936), Punjab and Haryana (1947), Orissa (1948), Rajasthan (1949), Kerala (1956), Gujarat (1960), Delhi (1966), Himachal Pradesh (1971), Sikkim (1975), Gauhati (1948/1972), Chhattisgarh (2000), Uttarakhand (2000), Jharkhand (2000), Tripura (2013), Manipur (2013), Meghalaya (2013), Telangana (2019), Andhra Pradesh (2019), and Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh (1928, as the common court for the two union territories).
