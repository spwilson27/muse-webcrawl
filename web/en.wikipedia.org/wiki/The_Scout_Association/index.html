The Scout Association

The Scout Association, which also uses the name Scouts, is the largest Scouting organisation in the United Kingdom and is the UK's recognised member of the World Organization of the Scout Movement. It was founded in 1910 as the Boy Scouts Association by Robert Baden-Powell, following his experimental camp on Brownsea Island in 1907 and the publication of Scouting for Boys in 1908.

Structure and sections

The association is organised into sections by age: Squirrels (ages 4–6), Beavers (6–8), Cubs (8–10½), Scouts (10½–14), Explorer Scouts (14–18) and the Scout Network (18–25). Local Scout Groups are supported by Districts, Counties/Areas, and national headquarters at Gilwell Park in London. The association has several hundred thousand members and is led by a Chief Scout; the adventurer Bear Grylls has served in that role since 2009.

History

Scouting spread rapidly across Britain and the Empire after 1908. Girls were formally admitted on an equal basis following reforms in the 1970s, and the association became fully co-educational in all sections by 2007, its centenary year. The Scout Promise and Law were modernised in the 1960s Advance Party Report reforms.
