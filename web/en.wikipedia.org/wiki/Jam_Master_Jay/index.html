Jam Master Jay

Jason William Mizell (January 21, 1965 – October 30, 2002), better known by his stage name Jam Master Jay, was an American musician and DJ. He was the DJ of the hip hop group Run-DMC, whose rise in the 1980s established hip hop as a dominant commercial and cultural force.

Career

Mizell was born in Brooklyn and raised in Hollis, Queens, New York. He played several instruments before turning to the turntables, and in 1983 joined school friends Joseph "Run" Simmons and Darryl "D.M.C." McDaniels to form Run-DMC. Behind his scratching and production, the group released landmark albums including King of Rock and Raising Hell, the latter featuring the rock-rap crossover hit Walk This Way with Aerosmith.

In the 1990s Mizell founded JMJ Records, which signed Onyx and helped launch the career of 50 Cent, and opened the Scratch DJ Academy in Manhattan to train aspiring DJs.

Death

On October 30, 2002, Mizell was shot and killed at his recording studio in Jamaica, Queens. The murder remained unsolved for nearly two decades; in 2020 two men were indicted, and in 2024 both were convicted of murder in connection with a drug-related dispute, though one conviction was later overturned on appeal proceedings. Run-DMC retired after his death, and the group was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009.
