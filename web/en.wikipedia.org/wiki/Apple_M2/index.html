The Apple M2 is a system on a chip (SoC) designed by Apple Inc. as the successor to the Apple M1, part of the Apple silicon family based on the ARM architecture. It was announced on June 6, 2022, at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference, and first shipped in the redesigned MacBook Air and the 13-inch MacBook Pro in July 2022. The M2 later appeared in the Mac mini, iPad Pro, and as the standard processor option in the Apple Vision Pro headset.

The M2 integrates CPU, GPU, Neural Engine, and unified memory controllers on one die.

Design

Built on TSMC's second-generation 5-nanometre process with 20 billion transistors — 25 percent more than M1 — the M2 combines four high-performance "Avalanche" CPU cores and four energy-efficient "Blizzard" cores, delivering, by Apple's figures, up to 18 percent faster multithreaded CPU performance than M1 at the same power. The integrated GPU has up to 10 cores (two more than M1) and is up to 35 percent faster at maximum power. The 16-core Neural Engine processes 15.8 trillion operations per second, about 40 percent more than M1. Unified memory bandwidth increased to 100 GB/s with support for up to 24 GB of LPDDR5. The media engine adds hardware acceleration for H.264, HEVC, ProRes, and ProRes RAW, including a dedicated ProRes encode/decode engine in higher configurations.

The M2 Pro and M2 Max debuted in the 14- and 16-inch MacBook Pro and Mac mini in January 2023. The M2 Ultra, fusing two M2 Max dies with Apple's UltraFusion interconnect, powered the Mac Studio and the final generation of the Mac Pro in June 2023, completing the Mac's transition away from Intel processors.

Reception

Reviewers praised the M2's performance per watt, particularly in the fanless MacBook Air, while noting modest generational gains over M1, thermal throttling under sustained load in passively cooled machines, and slower base-model SSD speeds in some configurations due to a single NAND chip design.
