Pi-system

A π-system (or pi system) in chemistry is a set of atoms connected by conjugated π bonds, in which p orbitals overlap along a chain or ring of atoms. The π electrons are delocalised over the whole system rather than confined between individual atom pairs.

Conjugation

In a conjugated system, alternating single and double bonds allow the p orbitals on adjacent atoms to overlap side by side. Delocalisation lowers the energy of the system, shortens and equalises bond lengths, and produces characteristic ultraviolet–visible absorption. Common examples include butadiene, benzene, and polyenes such as β-carotene.

Effects

Extended π-systems are responsible for the colours of many dyes and pigments, the conductivity of conducting polymers when doped, and the light-absorbing behaviour of photosynthetic pigments such as chlorophyll.
