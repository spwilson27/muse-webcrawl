Mount Toubkal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Toubkal
Elevation: 4,167 m — highest peak in North Africa
Range: High Atlas, Morocco
First ascent: 1923 (European recorded); long known to local Amazigh

Mount Toubkal (Jbel Toubkal) is the highest peak in the Atlas Mountains and in North Africa, at 4,167 metres, in Toubkal National Park in Morocco, about 60 km south of Marrakech. It is the highest point of the Maghreb and of the Arab world west of Arabia.

The massif is a dome of ancient volcanic and metamorphic rock lifted with the High Atlas as Africa pressed into Europe — the range that continues, across Gibraltar, into the Baetic mountains of the Iberian Peninsula. The standard route from the village of Imlil climbs through the Mizane valley to refuges at 3,200 m and on to a summit marked by a triangular metal frame, with views — on clear days — from the Atlantic to the Sahara.

Toubkal's villages are Amazigh (Berber); the region was at the epicentre of the destructive Al Haouz earthquake of 8 September 2023, which killed nearly three thousand people in the High Atlas and Marrakech, devastating the very valleys from which the climb begins. Recovery of the mountain communities has become a measure of Morocco's promises to its rural south.
