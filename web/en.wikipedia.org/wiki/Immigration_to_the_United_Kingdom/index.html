Immigration to the United Kingdom

Immigration to the United Kingdom is the process by which people migrate to the United Kingdom. Since 1945, immigration has been a major focus of political discussion. According to the 2021 census, about 16 per cent of the population of England and Wales was born outside the UK, and the foreign-born population of the whole United Kingdom numbers around ten million people. London is the most diverse region, with over 40 per cent of its residents born abroad.

History

Britain has received migrants for centuries, including Huguenot refugees from France in the 17th century, Irish migrants throughout the 19th century, and Jewish refugees fleeing pogroms and later Nazi persecution. The British Nationality Act 1948 confirmed that all Commonwealth citizens were British subjects with the right to enter and settle in the UK. The arrival of the HMT Empire Windrush at Tilbury in 1948, carrying several hundred Caribbean migrants, became the symbolic start of post-war mass migration from the Commonwealth.

Successive Commonwealth Immigrants Acts (1962, 1968) and the Immigration Act 1971 restricted primary settlement, while the British Nationality Act 1981 redefined citizenship. From the late 1990s, asylum applications and then, after 2004, migration from new EU member states in Central and Eastern Europe increased substantially; free movement under European Union law continued until the end of the Brexit transition period in 2020, after which a points-based system—first introduced for non-EU migrants in 2008—was extended to all nationalities.

Modern policy

Most work and study migrants are admitted under a points-based system requiring sponsorship by a licensed employer or educational institution. Major routes include the Skilled Worker visa, the Health and Care Worker visa, the Graduate route, and family visas. The "hostile environment" policies of the 2010s aimed to make life difficult for people without lawful status, and led to the Windrush scandal, in which long-resident Commonwealth citizens were wrongly detained, denied services, and in some cases deported.

New visa schemes were created for Hong Kong British National (Overseas) passport holders in 2021, and for Ukrainians fleeing the Russian invasion in 2022. Net migration reached record levels in the early 2020s, driven by work, study, and humanitarian routes, and remained a central issue in British politics.

Demographics

The largest foreign-born groups have historically been born in India, Poland, Pakistan, Ireland, and Germany, with recent growth in arrivals from Nigeria, China, and other countries. Immigrants and their descendants have reshaped British culture, cuisine, music, and sport.
