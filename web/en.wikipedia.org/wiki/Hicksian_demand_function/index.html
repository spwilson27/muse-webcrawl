Hicksian demand function

In microeconomics, a Hicksian demand function (or compensated demand function) gives the quantity of a good demanded by a consumer who minimises expenditure while achieving a fixed level of utility. It is named after the economist John Hicks and is the solution to the expenditure minimization problem.

Definition

Formally, the Hicksian demand for good i, written hi(p, u), depends on the price vector p and the target utility level u. Unlike Marshallian (uncompensated) demand, which holds income constant and therefore mixes substitution and income effects, Hicksian demand holds utility constant, so it isolates the pure substitution effect of a price change.

Properties

Hicksian demand is homogeneous of degree zero in prices, and by Shephard's lemma it equals the partial derivative of the expenditure function with respect to the good's price. The relationship between Hicksian and Marshallian demand is expressed by the Slutsky equation, a cornerstone of consumer theory.
