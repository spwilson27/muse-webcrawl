Passing (racial identity)

Racial passing occurs when a person who is classified as a member of one racial group is accepted or perceived ("passes") as a member of another. Historically, the term has been used primarily in the United States to describe a person of mixed African and European ancestry who is perceived as white, or who deliberately presents themselves as white, in a society organized around racial segregation and the one-drop rule.

History

Passing has a long history in the United States, documented from the era of slavery onward. Enslaved people who could pass as white sometimes escaped bondage entirely by leaving their communities and assuming a new identity. During the era of Jim Crow segregation, passing could mean access to better employment, education, housing, and physical safety — but often at the cost of severing ties with family and community.

The phenomenon was extensively explored in literature, most famously in Nella Larsen's 1929 novel Passing, and in works by authors such as James Weldon Johnson (The Autobiography of an Ex-White Man, 1912). Passing also appears in the biographies of figures such as Anatole Broyard, the literary critic who lived as white for most of his adult life.

Sociological analysis

Sociologists and historians note that passing exposes the constructed nature of racial categories: if a person's race can change simply because of how others perceive them, then race is partly a matter of social recognition rather than biology. Passers often described psychological costs — fear of exposure, guilt over abandoned relatives, and a sense of inauthenticity.

Related concepts

Passing is related to, but distinct from, code-switching and assimilation. The concept has also been extended to other identities, including gender passing, passing as non-disabled, and religious passing. In the 21st century, controversies over individuals who claimed a racial identity different from their ancestry — most prominently Rachel Dolezal in 2015 — renewed public debate about the term and its meaning.
