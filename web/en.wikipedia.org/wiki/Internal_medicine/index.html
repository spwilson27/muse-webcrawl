Internal medicine

Internal medicine, also known as general internal medicine in Commonwealth nations, is a medical specialty that focuses on the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases in adults, particularly those of the internal organs. Physicians who practise internal medicine are called internists (not to be confused with interns, who are doctors in their first year of postgraduate training). Internists are especially skilled in managing patients who have undifferentiated or multi-system disease processes, and in caring for hospitalised and ambulatory patients.

Scope

Internists treat a wide range of conditions affecting the heart, lungs, kidneys, gastrointestinal tract, endocrine system, blood, joints and immune system. Because their training covers many organ systems, internists frequently act as consultants to other specialists and as primary care physicians for adults. Unlike family medicine, internal medicine is confined to adult patients, and unlike general practice it traditionally emphasises hospital-based and subspecialty care, although many internists provide continuing outpatient care.

Training

In the United States, training in internal medicine consists of a three-year residency following medical school, leading to board certification by the American Board of Internal Medicine. In the United Kingdom, physicians undertake core medical training followed by specialty training in general internal medicine or a subspecialty, under the oversight of the Royal College of Physicians. The German term Innere Medizin ("internal medicine") is a historical source of the English name.

Subspecialties

Internists may undertake fellowship training in subspecialties including cardiology, gastroenterology, nephrology, pulmonology, endocrinology, haematology, oncology, rheumatology, infectious diseases and geriatric medicine. Hospital medicine — the care of hospitalised patients — has emerged as a distinct focus within the specialty, practised by physicians known as hospitalists.

History

The specialty developed in the late 19th century, particularly in Germany, where laboratory-based investigation of diseases of the internal organs was pioneered. In the United States, Sir William Osler is regarded as a founding figure; his textbook The Principles and Practice of Medicine (1892) and his establishment of the residency system at Johns Hopkins Hospital shaped the discipline. The American College of Physicians, founded in 1915, is the specialty's principal professional society in the US.
