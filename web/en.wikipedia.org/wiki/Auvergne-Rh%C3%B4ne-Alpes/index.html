Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes is an administrative region of France created by the territorial reform of 2014 from the merger of the former regions of Auvergne and Rhône-Alpes, effective 1 January 2016. Its capital is Lyon, France's third-largest city. It is the country's second most populous region and one of its most economically important.

Geography

The region spans the volcanic plateaus of the Massif Central in the west (Auvergne) and the French Alps in the east, including Mont Blanc, Western Europe's highest peak. It is bordered by Switzerland and Italy, and traversed by the Rhône and Loire rivers. Major cities include Lyon, Grenoble, Saint-Étienne, Clermont-Ferrand, Annecy, and Chambéry.

Economy

Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes is a leading region for industry, research, and tourism, with strengths in chemicals, pharmaceuticals, microelectronics (around Grenoble), mechanical engineering, winter sports, and agriculture, including AOC cheeses and wines. Lyon is a major banking and biotech center.

Departments

The region comprises the departments of Ain, Allier, Ardèche, Cantal, Drôme, Isère, Loire, Haute-Loire, Puy-de-Dôme, Rhône, Savoie, and Haute-Savoie, plus the Metropolis of Lyon.
