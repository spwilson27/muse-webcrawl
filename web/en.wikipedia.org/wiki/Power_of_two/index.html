Power of two


Labelled image placeholder: powers of two from 20 to 216

A power of two is a number of the form 2n where n is an integer; that is, the result of exponentiation with number two as the base and integer n as the exponent. Powers of two are fundamental in computer science because binary number systems represent values using powers of two.

First powers

The first few non-negative powers of two are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, and so on. Well-known larger values include 210 = 1,024 (the basis of the binary kilobyte conventions), 216 = 65,536, 232 = 4,294,967,296, and 264, a number famous from the legend of grains of wheat on a chessboard.

Properties

In binary, a power of two is written as a single 1 followed by n zeros, and integers that are one less than a power of two (Mersenne numbers, 2n − 1) are strings of ones; the prime numbers among them are the Mersenne primes. Every positive integer can be expressed uniquely as a sum of distinct powers of two, which is the basis of binary representation. A number is a power of two if and only if its binary representation contains exactly one set bit, a fact used in fast bit-manipulation tests.

Applications

Powers of two determine memory sizes, address spaces, colour depths, and data-block sizes in computing; a 32-bit address space can address 232 bytes. They also appear in tournament brackets, the doubling of bacteria, the binary search algorithm, and the folding of paper problems in exponential growth illustrations.
