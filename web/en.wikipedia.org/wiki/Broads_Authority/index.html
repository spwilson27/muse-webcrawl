Broads Authority

Formed1989 (under the Norfolk and Suffolk Broads Act 1988)
TypeNational park authority and navigation authority
HeadquartersYare House, Norwich
Area coveredThe Broads, Norfolk and Suffolk (about 303 km²)
PurposesConservation, public enjoyment, navigation

The Broads Authority is the body with overall responsibility for the Broads in Norfolk and Suffolk, England — a network of shallow lakes (broads) connected by rivers such as the Bure, Yare and Waveney. Established under the Norfolk and Suffolk Broads Act 1988 and operational from 1989, it functions as a national park authority and is also the navigation authority for the Broads waterways. Since 2015 the area has been marketed as the "Broads National Park", although its legal status differs slightly from the other national parks of England and Wales.

Duties

The Authority has three statutory purposes of equal weight: conserving and enhancing the natural beauty, wildlife and cultural heritage of the Broads; promoting opportunities for understanding and enjoyment by the public; and protecting the interests of navigation. It controls development as the local planning authority for its area, maintains some 125 miles (200 km) of navigable waterways, dredges channels, issues tolls for powered craft, and manages nature conservation in one of Britain's most important wetland landscapes, home to species such as the swallowtail butterfly, bittern, otter and the rare fen orchid.

Governance

The Authority is run by a board of members appointed by local authorities, the Secretary of State, and bodies representing navigation and other interests, and is funded by government grant, navigation tolls and planning income. It works with landowners, the Environment Agency and conservation groups on issues including water quality, flood management, climate adaptation and the management of tourism pressure.
