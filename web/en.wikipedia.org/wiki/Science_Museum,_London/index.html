The Science Museum is a major museum on Exhibition Road in South Kensington, London. It was founded in 1857 as part of the South Kensington Museum complex, and gained its independence in 1909. Today it is part of the Science Museum Group and is one of the city's most popular tourist attractions, attracting more than three million visitors annually. General admission is free.

History

The museum originated in the collections of the Royal Society of Arts and surplus items from the Great Exhibition of 1851, under the vision of Prince Albert and Henry Cole for a cultural quarter nicknamed "Albertopolis". The science collections grew to include landmark machines: Puffing Billy, the oldest surviving steam locomotive; Stephenson's Rocket; and the first jet engine. The East Block was completed in 1919, and galleries expanded through the twentieth century, with the Wellcome Wing opening in 2000.

Collections and galleries

Holdings exceed 300,000 objects spanning science, technology, engineering, medicine, and mathematics. Highlights include Charles Babbage's Difference Engine No. 1, the Apollo 10 command module, a V2 rocket, Crick and Watson's DNA model, and the Clockmakers' Museum collection. Galleries cover space exploration, flight, computing (the Information Age gallery, opened 2014), medicine, energy, and mathematics (the Winton Gallery, designed by Zaha Hadid Architects, opened 2016). The Dana Research Centre and Library supports scholarly research.

Science Museum Group

The Science Museum is the flagship of the Science Museum Group, which also includes the National Railway Museum in York, the Science and Industry Museum in Manchester, the National Science and Media Museum in Bradford, and Locomotion in Shildon.
