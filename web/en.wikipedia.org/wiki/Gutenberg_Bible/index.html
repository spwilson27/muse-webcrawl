Gutenberg Bible

Gutenberg Bible
Published: c. 1455, Mainz, Germany
Printer: Johannes Gutenberg
Language: Latin (Vulgate)
Pages: ~1,286, two volumes
Copies: ~180 printed; 49 survive substantially

The Gutenberg Bible, also called the 42-line Bible, was the first major book printed in Europe with movable metal type, produced by Johannes Gutenberg in Mainz around 1454–1455. It marks the start of the printing revolution.

Production

Gutenberg's workshop printed about 180 copies of the Latin Vulgate, about three-quarters on paper and the rest on vellum. Each page has two columns of 42 lines in a blackletter type imitating manuscript script; rubrication and illumination were added by hand.

Significance

Mass production of books lowered costs, spread literacy and scholarship, and helped enable the Renaissance, Reformation and Scientific Revolution. Surviving copies are among the world's most valuable books.

Surviving copies

Complete copies are held by institutions including the British Library, the Library of Congress, the Bibliothèque nationale de France and the Gutenberg Museum in Mainz.
