B-segment

Typical B-segment cars: small hatchbacks roughly 3.7–4.2 metres long.

The B-segment is the second-smallest category in the European system of car classification segments, ranking above the city-car A-segment and below the compact C-segment. In North American terminology, B-segment cars correspond broadly to the subcompact class, and in Britain they are often called superminis.

Characteristics

B-segment cars are typically between about 3.7 and 4.3 metres long. The dominant body style is the five-door hatchback, although saloons, estates, small MPVs (sometimes classed as a separate B-MPV sub-segment) and small crossovers (B-SUV) are also built on B-segment platforms. Examples of long-running B-segment nameplates include the Ford Fiesta, Volkswagen Polo, Renault Clio, Peugeot 208, Toyota Yaris, Opel/Vauxhall Corsa and Hyundai i20.

Market position

The B-segment has historically been one of the highest-volume segments in Europe, where fuel prices, narrow streets and taxation favour small cars. Since the 2010s, sales of conventional B-segment hatchbacks have declined in many markets as buyers shifted to B-segment crossover SUVs — models such as the Renault Captur, Volkswagen T-Cross and Ford Puma — which share platforms and mechanical parts with the hatchbacks but offer a raised driving position and SUV styling. Several manufacturers have also withdrawn from the segment or moved their entries upmarket as electrification and safety equipment raised costs.
