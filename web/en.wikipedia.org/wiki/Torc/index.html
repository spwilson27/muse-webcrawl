Torc

A torc, also spelled torq or torque, is a large rigid neck ring made of metal, worn as jewellery and a status symbol. Torcs are especially associated with the Celts of the European Iron Age, though examples are known from earlier Bronze Age cultures.

Construction

Torcs were typically made from twisted strands of gold, silver, bronze or iron, formed into a nearly closed ring with decorative terminals. Some were flexible and could be sprung open; others were hinged or fastened by their terminals.

Cultural significance

In Celtic art and mythology the torc signified nobility and divine status; gods and heroes are depicted wearing them. Famous finds include the Snettisham Hoard in Norfolk, England, which contained dozens of gold torcs dating to around the first century BC.

Later use

Torcs survived as ceremonial ornaments in some early medieval contexts, and the form has been revived in modern jewellery.
