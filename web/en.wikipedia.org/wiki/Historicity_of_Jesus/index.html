Historicity of Jesus

The historicity of Jesus concerns whether Jesus of Nazareth existed as a historical figure and how reliably the sources — principally the canonical Gospels — reflect his life. Virtually all scholars of antiquity agree that Jesus existed, was baptised by John the Baptist, and was crucified under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. The contrary view, the Christ myth theory, is regarded as a fringe position in academic scholarship.

Sources

The main sources are the letters of Paul (written in the 50s AD), which mention Jesus's crucifixion and his brother James, and the four Gospels (c. 65–100 AD). Non-Christian sources include the Jewish historian Josephus, whose Antiquities of the Jews contains two passages referring to Jesus (the partially interpolated Testimonium Flavianum and a reference to "James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ"), and the Roman historian Tacitus, who records in his Annals that "Christus" was executed under Pilate.

Historical method

Scholars of the historical Jesus apply criteria such as multiple attestation, embarrassment and coherence to the sources. There is broad agreement on a framework of events: Jesus was a Galilean Jew, active as a teacher and healer, who proclaimed the kingdom of God, gathered disciples, created a disturbance at the Jerusalem Temple at Passover, and was crucified by Roman authority around 30 or 33 AD. Details such as the details of his birth, the chronology of his ministry, and the authenticity of individual sayings remain debated.
