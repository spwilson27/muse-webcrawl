University of Texas at Austin

The University of Texas at Austin (UT Austin, UT, or Texas) is a public research university in Austin, Texas. Founded in 1883, it is the flagship institution of the University of Texas System and one of the largest universities in the United States, with an enrollment of over 50,000 students. Its colors are burnt orange and white, and its teams are the Texas Longhorns.

History

The Texas Constitution of 1876 mandated a "university of the first class"; voters chose Austin as the site in 1881, and the university opened in 1883. The Main Building's 307-foot Tower, completed in 1937, is the campus landmark; it was the scene of a mass shooting by Charles Whitman in 1966. Oil discovered on university lands in 1923 transformed the endowment, making UT Austin one of the wealthiest universities in the country.

Academics and research

UT Austin comprises 18 colleges and schools, including the McCombs School of Business, the Cockrell School of Engineering, and the Moody College of Communication. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity", operates the McDonald Observatory, and is home to the Texas Advanced Computing Center. Admissions litigation over its affirmative action policies reached the U.S. Supreme Court twice in Fisher v. University of Texas.
