Tipperary GAA

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tipperary County Board of the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), known as Tipperary GAA, governs Gaelic games in County Tipperary, Ireland. The county teams wear blue and gold and play their home games at Semple Stadium in Thurles, one of the most important venues in Irish sport and the town where the GAA was founded in 1884.

Hurling

Tipperary is one of the traditional powers of hurling, standing among the top counties in the roll of honour for the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship with more than two dozen titles, behind only Kilkenny and Cork. "The Premier County" won the inaugural All-Ireland championship in 1887 and has enjoyed celebrated eras in the 1949–51 three-in-a-row, the 1960s, and further titles in 1989, 1991, 2001, 2010, 2016 and 2019. Its rivalries with Kilkenny and Cork are among the fiercest in the game.

Gaelic football

Tipperary has also won All-Ireland Senior Football titles in the early championship era, and its 2020 Munster Senior Football Championship victory — won on the centenary weekend of Bloody Sunday, in replica 1920 jerseys — was one of the most resonant results in the county's history. The county also fields teams in camogie and ladies' football.
