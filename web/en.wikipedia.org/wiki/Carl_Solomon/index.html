Carl Solomon (March 30, 1928 – February 26, 1993) was an American writer and editor associated with the Beat Generation. He is best known as the dedicatee of Allen Ginsberg's landmark poem "Howl" (1956) and the subject of its refrain, "Carl Solomon, I'm with you in Rockland."

Life

Solomon met Ginsberg in 1949 in a New York psychiatric institute, where Solomon had voluntarily committed himself following bouts of depression. Inspired by French surrealism and Antonin Artaud, Solomon influenced Ginsberg's thinking with his surrealist gestures — famously throwing potato salad at a lecturer on Dadaism. Solomon spent periods in and out of mental institutions, including Rockland State Hospital, and underwent insulin shock and electroshock treatments.

Career

Solomon worked for his uncle A. A. Wyn at Ace Books, where he was involved in publishing early work by William S. Burroughs (Junkie, 1953). His own writings — surrealist, aphoristic prose — appeared in collections such as Mishaps, Perhaps (1966) and More Mishaps (1968), and later Emergency Messages (1989).

Legacy

Though he published little, Solomon's friendship with Ginsberg immortalized him as a symbol of the gifted, damaged figures at the heart of the Beat movement.
