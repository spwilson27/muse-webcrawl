Albatros D.V

An Albatros D.Va in German service

The Albatros D.V was a German single-seat fighter aircraft of the First World War, built by Albatros Flugzeugwerke and used by the Imperial German Army Air Service (Luftstreitkräfte) from 1917. Developed from the successful D.III, it was produced in large numbers — about 900 D.V and 1,600 of the strengthened D.Va variant — yet it disappointed in service, offering only marginal improvement over its predecessor while inheriting a dangerous structural weakness in the lower wing.

Design and problems

The D.V featured a new, more rounded oval-section plywood fuselage and a larger spinner compared with the D.III. However, like the D.III, it used a sesquiplane wing layout with a narrow-chord lower wing of single-spar construction that was prone to twisting and, in dives, to catastrophic flutter and structural failure. Several pilots were killed in wing failures, and the strengthened D.Va, which added bracing wires and a revised aileron linkage, never fully eliminated the defect.

Service

By late 1917 the D.V was outclassed by newer Allied fighters such as the S.E.5a and SPAD S.XIII, and although aces including Manfred von Richthofen flew the type and scored victories in it, Richthofen judged it obsolete. Albatros fighters nonetheless remained in front-line use until the end of the war because the superior Fokker D.VII was not available in sufficient numbers.
