Erlenmeyer flask

An Erlenmeyer flask, also known as a conical flask (British English) or a titration flask, is a type of laboratory flask with a flat bottom, a conical body, and a cylindrical neck. It is named after the German chemist Emil Erlenmeyer (1825–1909), who designed it in 1860.

Uses

The conical shape allows contents to be swirled by hand without spilling, makes the flask ideal for titration, and reduces evaporative loss compared with a beaker, while the narrow neck can be fitted with a stopper or condenser. Erlenmeyer flasks are widely used for mixing, heating, boiling, and storing liquids, and for growing microbial cultures. Because the glass is stressed unevenly under vacuum or pressure, standard Erlenmeyer flasks should not be used for vacuum work; the heavier-walled Büchner (filter) flask is a purpose-built variant for vacuum filtration.

Sizes and materials

Flasks are made in volumes from a few millilitres to several litres, usually of borosilicate glass or plastic, often with approximate volume graduations printed on the side.
