Dartmoor is an upland area in southern Devon, England, protected as a National Park since 1951. The moorland and surrounding countryside cover 954 km² (368 sq mi) and are capped with exposed granite hilltops known as tors, formed from a batholith intruded during the Carboniferous period. The highest point is High Willhays at 621 metres, the loftiest ground in southern England south of the Peak District.

Landscape and wildlife

Dartmoor's landscape of blanket bog, heather moor and valley mires is the source of many Devon rivers, including the Dart, Teign and Tavy. Semi-feral Dartmoor ponies roam the commons, where sheep and cattle also graze under commoners' rights. The park holds an exceptionally rich archaeological record: stone rows, hut circles, kistvaens and the reaves — Bronze Age field boundaries that survive across the moor.

Human history and culture

Medieval tin streaming and later mining scarred parts of the moor, and Dartmoor Prison at Princetown, built for Napoleonic prisoners of war, remains in use. Dartmoor inspired Arthur Conan Doyle's The Hound of the Baskervilles (1902), and its folklore includes pixies, the Hairy Hands of the B3212 and numerous ghost stories. Parts of the northern moor are used as a military training area. A 2023 Supreme Court case confirmed that the public right to backpack-camp on the commons, curtailed by a 2022 High Court ruling, survived in law. See also Tor, Devon, and Exmoor.
