Antidisestablishmentarianism

Antidisestablishmentarianism is a position that advocates that a state church (the "established church") should continue to receive government patronage, rather than be disestablished. In 19th-century Britain, it was used to describe those who opposed proposals to remove the Church of England's status as the state church, and parallel debates concerned the Church of Ireland (disestablished in 1871) and the Church in Wales (disestablished in 1920).

The word

At 28 letters, "antidisestablishmentarianism" is famous as one of the longest non-technical, non-coined words in the English language: anti- + dis + establishment + arian + ism. It is commonly cited as the longest word in English, though longer technical and coined words exist, such as pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis.

Context

Opponents of disestablishment argued that an established church provided national moral cohesion and that establishment did not prevent religious toleration. Supporters of disestablishment — including many Nonconformists and the Liberation Society — argued that state privilege for one denomination was unjust in a religiously plural society.
