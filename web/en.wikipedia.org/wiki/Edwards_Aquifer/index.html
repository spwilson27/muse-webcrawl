Edwards Aquifer

The Edwards Aquifer is one of the most prolific artesian aquifers in the world. Located on the eastern edge of the Edwards Plateau in the U.S. state of Texas, it is the source of drinking water for two million people, and is the primary water supply for agriculture and industry in the aquifer's region. Additionally, the Edwards Aquifer feeds the Comal and San Marcos Springs, provides base flows for several south-central Texas rivers, and is home to several unique and endangered species.

Geology and geography

The aquifer lies within the Balcones Fault Zone and consists largely of faulted and fractured Cretaceous limestone (the Edwards Group and associated formations) that store and transmit large volumes of water. It is divided into drainage, recharge, and artesian zones running in an arc from Brackettville in Kinney County eastward through San Antonio and Austin to Salado in Bell County. Streams crossing the recharge zone lose much of their flow into sinkholes, caves, and fractures in the limestone, replenishing the aquifer, which discharges at major springs such as San Marcos Springs, Comal Springs, Barton Springs, and San Antonio Springs.

Use and management

The city of San Antonio is one of the largest cities in the United States dependent solely on groundwater for its municipal supply. Because spring flows support endangered species protected under the Endangered Species Act — including the fountain darter, Texas wild rice, and the San Marcos salamander — pumping from the aquifer is regulated by the Edwards Aquifer Authority, created by the Texas Legislature in 1993, which issues withdrawal permits and imposes staged reductions during droughts. A habitat conservation plan adopted in 2013 funds measures to protect spring flows and species at Comal and San Marcos Springs.
