Flagship

A flagship is a vessel used by the commanding officer of a group of naval ships, characteristically a flag officer entitled by custom to fly a distinguishing flag. Used more loosely, it is the lead ship in a fleet of vessels, typically the first, largest, fastest, most heavily armed, or best known.

During the age of sail, the admiral's flag was flown at the mainmast of his ship, making it the most visible and important target in battle. Famous flagships include HMS Victory, Admiral Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805; USS Constitution; and the Japanese battleship Mikasa, flagship of Admiral Tōgō at Tsushima in 1905.

Modern usage

Modern flagships are often purpose-equipped with command and communications facilities so an embarked commander and staff can direct operations. Examples include the United States Navy's Blue Ridge-class command ships. Many navies also maintain ceremonial flagships, such as the Royal Navy's HMS Victory, still formally in commission as flagship of the First Sea Lord.

Metaphorical use

By extension, "flagship" is widely used in commerce for the most important or prominent product, store, or institution of an organisation: a retailer's flagship store, a car manufacturer's flagship model, or a university's flagship campus.
