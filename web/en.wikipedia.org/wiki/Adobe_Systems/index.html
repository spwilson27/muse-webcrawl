Adobe Inc. (/əˈdoʊbi/), originally called Adobe Systems Incorporated, is an American multinational computer software company headquartered in San Jose, California. Founded on the PostScript page-description language, Adobe created or popularised several foundational technologies of digital publishing — PostScript, the PDF format and its Acrobat software, and the standard creative applications Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign and Premiere Pro — and now organises its business around the Creative Cloud, Document Cloud and Experience Cloud.

[Image: Adobe's headquarters towers in downtown San Jose]

History

John Warnock and Charles Geschke left Xerox PARC in 1982 to commercialise their work on a device-independent page description language; Adobe — named after Adobe Creek, which ran behind Warnock's home in Los Altos — was founded in December 1982. PostScript, licensed for Apple's LaserWriter printer (1985), ignited the desktop publishing revolution. Adobe followed with the Type 1 font format, Illustrator (1987), Photoshop (licensed 1988, released 1990), Premiere (1991) and Acrobat with PDF (1993). The company acquired Aldus (1994, gaining PageMaker) and Macromedia (2005, gaining Flash, Dreamweaver and ColdFusion), later expanded into marketing software with Omniture (2009), Magento and Marketo (2018). Its attempt to acquire design-tool maker Figma for $20 billion, announced in 2022, was abandoned in December 2023 under regulatory pressure in the EU and UK.

Business model

From 2012–2013 Adobe shifted from perpetual licences to subscription distribution via Adobe Creative Cloud, a change that produced recurring revenue growth — annual revenue exceeded $19 billion in fiscal 2023 — while drawing criticism from users over long-term costs. PDF was released as an open ISO standard (ISO 32000) in 2008, though Adobe remains the format's most influential steward.
