Human resource management (HRM) is the strategic and coherent approach to the management of an organisation's employees — the people who individually and collectively contribute to the achievement of its objectives. The corresponding department, human resources (HR), oversees recruitment and selection, onboarding, training and development, performance appraisal, compensation and benefits, employee relations, health and safety, and compliance with employment law.

The field emerged from the older personnel administration function, itself a product of the welfare and personnel departments of early twentieth-century factories and of the human relations movement associated with Elton Mayo's Hawthorne studies. From the 1980s, writers such as Dave Ulrich redefined HR as a strategic partner in the business, contributing to organisational capability rather than merely processing paperwork.

Functions

Key activities include workforce planning and job analysis; recruiting and selecting staff; induction and training; managing performance through appraisal systems; designing pay structures and benefit plans; handling grievances, discipline, and collective bargaining with trade unions; and ensuring legal compliance in areas such as equal opportunities, working time, and workplace safety. Modern HR also handles employee engagement, diversity and inclusion, succession planning, and HR analytics.

Models and theory

Influential frameworks include the Michigan (matching) model, which stresses fitting HR systems to business strategy, and the Harvard model of Beer and colleagues, which emphasises stakeholder interests and long-term consequences. Ulrich's model assigns HR four roles: strategic partner, change agent, employee champion, and administrative expert. Debate continues over whether HR practices are universally "best" or contingent on context, and over evidence that high-performance work systems raise productivity.

Criticism

HRM has been criticised for rhetoric that outruns reality — espousing commitment and empowerment while implementing cost-cutting — and for serving management interests while presenting itself as neutral. The growth of gig work, outsourcing, and algorithmic management has challenged traditional employment-centred HR models.
