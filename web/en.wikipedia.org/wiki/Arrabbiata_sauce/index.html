Arrabbiata sauce (Italian: sugo all'arrabbiata) is a spicy sauce for pasta made from garlic, tomatoes and dried red chilli peppers cooked in olive oil. The sauce originates from the Lazio region of Italy, and particularly the city of Rome.

Name

Arrabbiata literally means "angry" in Italian (from arrabbiare, "to get angry"); the name refers to the heat of the chillies, which are said to make the diner's face flush as if in anger. In Romanesco dialect the dish is sometimes called arrabbiata with the intensifying sense of a sauce that "grabs" the palate.

Preparation and serving

Garlic is fried gently in olive oil, dried chilli (often peperoncino) is added, and tomatoes — typically canned San Marzano or fresh — are simmered until the sauce thickens. Parsley may be added at the end. The sauce is most famously served with penne as penne all'arrabbiata, a classic of Roman trattorias, and is sometimes finished with grated pecorino romano. The film director Federico Fellini and Roman popular culture helped cement the dish's fame in the mid-20th century.
