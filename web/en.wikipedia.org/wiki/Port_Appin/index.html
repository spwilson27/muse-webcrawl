Port Appin

Port Appin is a small village in Argyll and Bute, on the west coast of Scotland. It lies on the shore of Loch Linnhe near the mouth of Loch Creran, roughly 22 kilometres north of Oban, and looks across to the island of Lismore.

History

The village developed around its pier, which served steamers on the Oban–Fort William route and supported local fishing and a slate and limestone trade. Castle Stalker, a four-storey tower house of the Stewarts of Appin standing picturesquely on a tidal islet in Loch Laich just north of the village, is one of the best-known images of the Scottish Highlands.

Present day

Port Appin is now a quiet tourist and holiday destination. A passenger ferry crosses to Lismore from the pier, and the village has a hotel (the Pierhouse), a village hall, a shop and the Airds Hotel nearby. The surrounding Appin district is associated with the Appin Murder of 1752, fictionalised in Robert Louis Stevenson's Kidnapped.
