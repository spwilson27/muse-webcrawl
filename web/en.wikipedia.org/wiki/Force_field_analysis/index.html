Force field analysis

Force field analysis is a management technique developed by Kurt Lewin, a pioneer in social psychology, for diagnosing situations. It is based on the idea that situations are held in equilibrium by a balance of forces: driving forces that push for change and restraining forces that maintain the status quo. A situation changes only when the balance between these forces shifts.

Method

In a force field analysis, a group identifies the forces for and against a proposed change, then scores each force for strength. The results are drawn as opposing arrows around a central line representing the current state. Strategy then focuses either on strengthening driving forces or — as Lewin argued was usually more effective and less likely to provoke resistance — weakening the restraining forces, so that the equilibrium moves toward the desired state.

Relation to Lewin's change model

The analysis forms part of Lewin's three-stage model of change: unfreeze (destabilise the current equilibrium), change (move to a new state), and refreeze (stabilise the new state). Force field analysis is principally a tool for the unfreezing stage.

Applications

The technique is widely used in organisational development, project management, health promotion and community work as a simple, participative way to structure discussion about change.
