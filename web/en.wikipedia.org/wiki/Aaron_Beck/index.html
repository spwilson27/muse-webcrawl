Aaron Beck

Aaron Temkin Beck (18 July 1921 – 1 November 2021) was an American psychiatrist and professor at the University of Pennsylvania. He is widely regarded as the father of cognitive therapy and of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), approaches he developed in the 1960s that transformed the treatment of depression and other mental disorders. His self-report inventories, including the Beck Depression Inventory and the Beck Anxiety Inventory, are among the most widely used instruments in clinical psychology.

Early life and training

Beck was born in Providence, Rhode Island, the youngest of four children of Russian Jewish immigrants. He graduated from Brown University in 1942 and earned his medical degree from Yale in 1946. Trained as a psychoanalyst, he set out in the 1950s to test psychoanalytic theories of depression empirically — expecting dreams of depressed patients to reveal the "inverted hostility" the theory predicted. His studies instead suggested that depressed patients' conscious thoughts were characterised by negative themes about themselves, the world and the future.

Cognitive therapy

From these observations Beck developed cognitive therapy, set out in Depression: Causes and Treatment (1967) and Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders (1976). The approach holds that psychological distress is driven by distorted patterns of thinking — automatic thoughts, cognitive distortions such as all-or-nothing thinking and overgeneralisation, and underlying schemas. Therapy helps patients identify, test and correct these thoughts collaboratively and empirically.

Beck proposed the "negative cognitive triad" in depression: negative views of the self, the world and the future. His model was later extended to anxiety disorders, personality disorders, substance use, schizophrenia and suicidality. With his daughter Judith S. Beck, he co-founded the Beck Institute for Cognitive Behavior Therapy in 1994. In his final decade he developed Recovery-Oriented Cognitive Therapy (CT-R) for people with serious mental illness.

Recognition

Beck received the Albert Lasker Award for Clinical Medical Research in 2006 and was frequently described as one of the most influential psychotherapists of all time. He published more than 600 papers and 25 books, and continued working until shortly before his death at the age of 100.
