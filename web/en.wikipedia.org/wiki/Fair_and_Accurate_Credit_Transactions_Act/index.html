The Fair and Accurate Credit Transactions Act of 2003 (FACT Act or FACTA) is a United States federal law, enacted by the 108th United States Congress and signed by President George W. Bush on December 4, 2003, as an amendment to the Fair Credit Reporting Act. The Act allows consumers to request and obtain a free credit report once every 12 months from each of the three nationwide consumer credit reporting companies: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Provisions

The Act established the AnnualCreditReport.com website and toll-free telephone number through which consumers can request their free reports. It also created a number of protections against identity theft, including the right to place fraud alerts and credit freezes, requirements that credit card numbers be truncated on printed receipts, and the "Red Flags Rule" obliging financial institutions and creditors to maintain identity-theft prevention programs.

The FACT Act also directed the Federal Trade Commission and federal financial regulators to study the accuracy of credit reports and to address consumer disputes. Its disposal rule requires businesses to properly dispose of consumer information derived from credit reports.

History

The law arose from concerns that state laws protecting consumers would be preempted when provisions of the Fair Credit Reporting Act expired. Congress made the federal preemptions permanent while adding new consumer protections, including the free annual credit report provision phased in across the United States during 2004 and 2005.
