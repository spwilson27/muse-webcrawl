Torsion (algebra)

Torsion (algebra)
FieldAbstract algebra (module theory)
Key conceptsTorsion element, torsion submodule, torsion-free module
RelatedTorsion subgroup, Tor functor

In mathematics, specifically in abstract algebra, torsion refers to elements of a group or module that have finite order or are annihilated by a nonzero scalar. An element g of an abelian group is a torsion element if it has finite order, that is, if ng = 0 for some positive integer n. The set of all torsion elements of an abelian group forms a subgroup, called the torsion subgroup; a group in which every element has finite order is a torsion group, and a group with no nonzero torsion elements is torsion-free.

Torsion in modules

The notion generalises to modules over a ring R: an element m of a module M is a torsion element if there exists a nonzero (more precisely, a non-zero-divisor) r in R such that rm = 0. The torsion elements of M form a submodule, the torsion submodule tM, when R is a domain; the quotient M/tM is torsion-free. For example, in a module over the integers — that is, an abelian group — the torsion submodule is precisely the set of elements of finite order, and the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups decomposes such a group into a free part and a finite torsion part.

Examples and related ideas

In ℤ/nℤ every element is torsion; the rationals ℚ as a ℤ-module are torsion-free. Torsion appears throughout algebra and topology: the torsion subgroup of the Jacobian of a curve underlies the theory of rational points on elliptic curves (bounded by the Mazur torsion theorem), and the name of the Tor functors in homological algebra derives from the torsion product, since Tor₁ of abelian groups detects their torsion subgroups.
