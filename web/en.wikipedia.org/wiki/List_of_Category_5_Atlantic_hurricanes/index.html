List of Category 5 Atlantic hurricanes

Category 5 is the highest classification on the Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale, with sustained winds exceeding 157 mph (252 km/h). Since records began in 1851, a few dozen Atlantic hurricanes have reached this intensity, most in the peak months of August and September. Pressure and wind records below follow the Atlantic hurricane database (HURDAT).

Other Category 5 storms include Janet (1955), David (1979), Hugo (1989), Isabel (2003), Ivan (2004), Dean and Felix (2007), Matthew (2016), Michael (2018), Lee (2023) and Beryl (2024), the earliest Category 5 on record in the Atlantic, reaching the intensity in early July.

Climatology

Category 5 landfalls in the United States are rare: the 1935 Labor Day hurricane, Camille, Andrew and Michael are the principal examples. Most Category 5 storms form in the western Caribbean Sea, the Gulf of Mexico, or the open Atlantic south of 25°N, where warm water and low wind shear coincide in late summer.
