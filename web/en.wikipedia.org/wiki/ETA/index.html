ETA, an acronym for Euskadi Ta Askatasuna ("Basque Homeland and Liberty"), was a Basque separatist armed organisation founded in Spain in 1959. It sought independence for the Basque Country — comprising parts of northern Spain and southwestern France — and a socialist state. During its campaign of bombings, shootings and kidnappings, ETA killed more than 850 people, most of them members of the Spanish security forces, politicians and civil servants, but also many civilians.

History

ETA emerged from a student cultural group, Ekin, during the Franco dictatorship, when the Basque language and culture were repressed. Its first lethal attack took place in 1968. Its most famous operation was the 1973 assassination in Madrid of Admiral Luis Carrero Blanco, Franco's prime minister and designated successor. After Spain's transition to democracy, ETA continued its campaign despite Basque autonomy, and public opposition grew sharply after atrocities such as the 1987 Hipercor supermarket bombing in Barcelona, which killed 21 civilians.

Decline and dissolution

Sustained police work by Spain and France, arrests of successive leaderships, and near-universal Basque political rejection steadily weakened the group. ETA declared a "definitive cessation of armed activity" in October 2011, handed over its weapons caches in April 2017, and announced its complete dissolution in May 2018. Victims' associations and Spanish governments long demanded that ETA also acknowledge the suffering it caused and help resolve unsolved crimes.
