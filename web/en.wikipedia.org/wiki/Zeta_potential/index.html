Zeta potential

Zeta potential is the electric potential at the slipping plane of a particle suspended in a liquid — the boundary between the layer of fluid that moves with the particle and the bulk medium. It is a key indicator of the stability of colloidal dispersions and is usually expressed in millivolts (mV).

Theory

A charged particle in suspension attracts a layer of oppositely charged ions (the Stern layer), surrounded by a diffuse layer of ions; together these form the electrical double layer. As the particle moves, ions within the slipping plane travel with it, while those beyond do not. The potential difference between this slipping plane and the bulk liquid is the zeta potential. It is often estimated from measurements of electrophoretic mobility using models such as the Smoluchowski or Hückel equations.

Practical significance

The magnitude of the zeta potential indicates the degree of electrostatic repulsion between particles. Dispersions with high zeta potential (roughly beyond ±30 mV, positive or negative) are generally considered stable, resisting aggregation, whereas values near zero let particles approach and flocculate. The pH at which the zeta potential is zero is the isoelectric point, where a dispersion is least stable.

Applications

Zeta potential measurements are used in water treatment, ceramics, mineral processing, paints and coatings, pharmaceuticals and nanomedicine — for example to formulate stable suspensions, predict nanoparticle behaviour in biological fluids, and characterise surfaces, fibres and membranes via streaming potential.
