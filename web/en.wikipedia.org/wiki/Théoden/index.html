Théoden

Théoden is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. He is the seventeenth King of Rohan, the horse-riding kingdom of the Rohirrim allied to Gondor. Appearing chiefly in The Two Towers and The Return of the King, he is portrayed as an aged king sunk into premature dotage under the influence of his corrupt counsellor Gríma Wormtongue and the wizard Saruman, and restored to vigour by Gandalf.

Story

Freed from Wormtongue's influence at Edoras, Théoden leads his people to the defence of Helm's Deep against Saruman's army, and afterwards rides with six thousand horsemen to the relief of Minas Tirith. At the Battle of the Pelennor Fields he rallies his riders in one of the novel's most celebrated charges, but is mortally wounded when his horse Snowmane falls on him, struck by the Witch-king of Angmar — whom his niece Éowyn then destroys. He dies in the arms of the hobbit Meriadoc Brandybuck and is succeeded by Éomer.

Analysis

Théoden's name is Old English for "lord, king". Scholars have compared his decline and recovery with King Lear and with the Anglo-Saxon heroic tradition that Tolkien, a professor of Anglo-Saxon, drew on for Rohan's culture, including the poem Beowulf.
