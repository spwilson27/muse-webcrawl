Darling River

The Darling River is the third-longest river in Australia, at 1,472 kilometres, and a major tributary of the Murray River, with which it forms the Murray-Darling system. It rises in the Great Dividing Range near the New South Wales-Queensland border and flows southwest across the outback to join the Murray at Wentworth.

The Darling was a paddle-steamer highway from Bourke, once the world's largest inland wool port. Today its flows are heavily extracted for cotton irrigation around Cubbie Station and others, and the lower Darling (Baaka, in Barkindji language) has suffered catastrophic fish kills — millions of fish died near Menindee in 2019 and again in 2023 amid heat, low flows and algal blooms, traumatising river communities.
