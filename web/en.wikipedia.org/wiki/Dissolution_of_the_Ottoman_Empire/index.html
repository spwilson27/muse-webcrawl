Dissolution of the Ottoman Empire

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The dissolution of the Ottoman Empire (1908–1922) was a period of history in which the Ottoman Empire, following defeat in the First World War, was partitioned among the victorious Allied powers and new states emerged from its territory. The process ended with the abolition of the Ottoman sultanate on 1 November 1922 and the proclamation of the Republic of Turkey in 1923.

Background

By the early twentieth century the empire had lost nearly all its European territory in the Balkan Wars (1912–13) and faced rising Arab and Turkish national movements. The Young Turk government of the Committee of Union and Progress led the empire into the First World War on the side of the Central Powers in 1914.

War and partition

During the war the Allies made conflicting arrangements for the empire's future: the Hussein–McMahon Correspondence promised Arab independence, the secret Sykes–Picot Agreement (1916) divided the Arab provinces between Britain and France, and the Balfour Declaration (1917) promised a Jewish national home in Palestine. The empire signed the Armistice of Mudros on 30 October 1918, and Allied troops occupied Constantinople.

The Treaty of Sèvres (1920) would have reduced Turkey to a rump state in Anatolia, but it was rejected by the Turkish national movement under Mustafa Kemal. After victory in the Turkish War of Independence, the nationalists negotiated the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), which recognised the Republic of Turkey within roughly its modern borders. The Arab provinces became League of Nations mandates: Iraq, Palestine, and Transjordan under Britain, and Syria and Lebanon under France; the Arabian Peninsula consolidated under Ibn Saud.

Consequences

The dissolution redrew the map of the Middle East, created the state system that largely persists today, and left contested legacies including the borders of Iraq, Syria, and Israel/Palestine, the Kurdish question, and the memory of the Armenian, Greek, and Assyrian genocides carried out during the empire's final years.
