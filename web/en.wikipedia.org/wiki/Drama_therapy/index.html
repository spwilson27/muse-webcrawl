Drama therapy

Drama therapy is the use of theatre techniques to facilitate personal growth and promote mental health. It is a form of expressive therapy and creative arts therapy that uses role play, improvisation, storytelling, puppetry, games and other dramatic processes to help clients explore emotions, relationships and life experiences at a safe aesthetic distance.

History

The field draws on the work of Jacob L. Moreno, founder of psychodrama, and on theatre practitioners and educators in the mid-20th century. It became established as a distinct profession in Britain and the United States in the 1970s, with training programmes and professional associations including the North American Drama Therapy Association, founded in 1979.

Practice

Drama therapists work in schools, hospitals, mental health settings, prisons and private practice with individuals and groups, including people experiencing trauma, developmental disorders and dementia. Core concepts include dramatic projection, embodiment, role, distancing and the therapeutic theatre process.
