Cheilitis is a medical condition characterized by inflammation of the lips. The inflammation may include the perioral skin, the vermilion border, or the labial mucosal surface. The skin and the vermilion border are more commonly involved, as the mucosa is less affected by inflammatory and allergic reactions.

Classification

Cheilitis can be broadly divided into temporary (acute) and chronic types. Most cheilitis is caused by exogenous factors such as dry weather, frequent licking of the lips, or contact irritants and allergens in lipsticks and toothpastes. Recognised forms include:

Angular cheilitis (angular stomatitis), affecting the corners of the mouth, often associated with Candida or staphylococcal infection and with iron or B-vitamin deficiency. Actinic cheilitis, caused by chronic sun exposure, mainly affects the lower lip and is considered potentially premalignant. Exfoliative cheilitis involves continuous peeling of the vermilion. Allergic contact cheilitis results from sensitisation to ingredients in lip products, dental materials or foods. Granulomatous cheilitis causes persistent lip swelling and may be associated with Crohn's disease or Melkersson–Rosenthal syndrome.

Signs and symptoms

Common features are dryness, erythema, scaling, fissuring, crusting and swelling of one or both lips. Pain, burning and itching may occur, and secondary bacterial infection can produce golden crusts resembling impetigo.

Management

Treatment depends on the type and cause. Irritants and allergens should be identified and avoided; emollients and barrier ointments relieve simple dry cheilitis. Antifungal or antibacterial preparations are used for infected angular cheilitis, and actinic cheilitis requires sun protection and monitoring, sometimes with topical therapy or surgery. Nutritional deficiencies, when present, should be corrected.
