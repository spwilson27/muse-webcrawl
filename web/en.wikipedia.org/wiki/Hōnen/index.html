Hōnen

Hōnen (法然)
Born: 13 May 1133, Kume, Mimasaka
Died: 29 January 1212, Kyoto
Founder of: Jōdo-shū

Hōnen (法然, 1133–1212), also known as Genkū, was the religious reformer and founder of the first independent branch of Japanese Pure Land Buddhism called Jōdo-shū ('Pure Land School'). He taught that in the degenerate age of mappō, liberation was attainable by ordinary people solely through reciting the nembutsu — 'Namu Amida Butsu' — in devotion to Amida Buddha.

Life

After his father, a provincial official, was assassinated, the nine-year-old Hōnen entered a monastery and later studied Tendai Buddhism on Mount Hiei. Dissatisfied, he withdrew to study Pure Land texts; reading the Chinese master Shandao's commentary convinced him the nembutsu alone sufficed. From 1175 he preached in Kyoto, attracting followers from court nobles to peasants, fishermen and women excluded by other schools — among them Shinran, later founder of Jōdo Shinshū.

Persecution and exile

Established schools petitioned against the exclusive nembutsu teaching. In 1207 the court banned it; two disciples were executed and Hōnen was exiled to Tosa on Shikoku under the criminal name Fujii Motohiko. Pardoned in 1211, he returned to Kyoto and died the following year.

Teaching and legacy

His major work, the Senchaku Hongan Nembutsushū (1198), argued Amida's Primal Vow selected the nembutsu as the practice for all. The head temple is Chion-in, Kyoto. See Pure Land Buddhism and Shinran.
