The cinema of South India comprises the five major film industries of southern India — Telugu, Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, and Tulu cinema — based chiefly in Hyderabad, Chennai, Bengaluru, and Kochi. Together these industries produce a large share of India's total film output and are distinguished by their separate languages, star systems, and strong regional audiences, as well as increasing pan-Indian distribution.

History

South Indian filmmaking began with R. Nataraja Mudaliar's silent film Keechaka Vadham (c. 1916–17), produced in Madras. Talkies arrived in 1931 with the Telugu/Tamil bilingual Bhakta Prahlada and Kalidas. Studios in Madras such as Gemini, AVM, and Vijaya Productions dominated early production across languages. After linguistic state reorganization, the industries developed distinct identities: Hyderabad became the center of Telugu cinema, with Ramoji Film City among the world's largest studio complexes, while Chennai remained the home of Tamil cinema.

Industries

Telugu cinema is one of India's largest industries by output and box office, known for large-scale productions such as S. S. Rajamouli's Baahubali films and RRR (2022), which achieved global success. Tamil cinema has a long tradition of politically engaged and star-driven filmmaking, with figures such as M. G. Ramachandran and Kamal Haasan crossing into politics and national prominence. Malayalam cinema is acclaimed for realistic, content-driven films and periodic "new generation" movements. Kannada cinema, based in Bengaluru, gained nationwide attention with the KGF films and Kantara (2022).

Pan-Indian era

From the late 2010s, dubbed and simultaneously multilingual releases turned several South Indian productions into nationwide blockbusters, reshaping the economics of Indian cinema and elevating South Indian stars and directors across the country and abroad.
