Virginia State Capitol

The Virginia State Capitol is the seat of the Virginia General Assembly and the state government of the Commonwealth of Virginia, located on Capitol Square in Richmond. Designed by Thomas Jefferson with Charles-Louis Clérisseau and completed in 1788, it is one of the most influential buildings in American architecture — the first public building in the modern world modelled on a classical Roman temple.

Design

Jefferson, then American minister to France, based the design on the Maison Carrée, an ancient Roman temple at Nîmes, adapting it with an Ionic portico. The white-painted brick (later stuccoed) temple sits on a podium overlooking the James River. Inside the rotunda stands Jean-Antoine Houdon's life-size statue of George Washington (1796), the most prized sculpture in the building, made from life. The original House chamber was the scene of Aaron Burr's treason trial in 1807.

Later history

During the Civil War the building served as the Capitol of the Confederate States of America. In 1870 a gallery in the courtroom above the House chamber collapsed during a crowded hearing, killing 62 people. Two large wings for the House and Senate, designed in a compatible classical style, were added in 1904–1906. A major restoration and expansion, completed in 2007, added a below-ground visitors' extension. The capitol and its grounds, containing monuments including the Virginia Washington Monument, are a National Historic Landmark.
