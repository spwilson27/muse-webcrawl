Informbiro period

The Informbiro period was an era in the history of Yugoslavia following the Tito–Stalin split in 1948, lasting until reconciliation with the Soviet Union in 1955 under Khrushchev. It is named after the Cominform (Information Bureau of the Communist and Workers' Parties), which expelled Yugoslavia in June 1948.

The split

Disputes over foreign policy, the Balkan federation plans, and Tito's independence led Stalin to denounce the Yugoslav leadership. The Cominform resolution of 28 June 1948 accused the Communist Party of Yugoslavia of nationalism and deviation.

Repression

Yugoslavs suspected of supporting Stalin — “Cominformists” (ibeovci) — were purged and many imprisoned, notably on Goli Otok, a barren Adriatic island camp. Meanwhile Yugoslavia turned toward the West for aid and developed workers' self-management and later the Non-Aligned Movement.

End

After Stalin's death, Khrushchev visited Belgrade in 1955 and issued the Belgrade Declaration normalising relations, though Yugoslavia kept its independent path.
