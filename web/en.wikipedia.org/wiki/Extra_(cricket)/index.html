Extra (cricket)

In cricket, an extra (sometimes referred to as a sundry) is a run scored by, or awarded to, a batting team which is not credited to any individual batter. They are the runs scored through means other than the batter hitting the ball with the bat.

Extras are tallied separately on the scorecard and count toward the team's total but not toward individual batting statistics. The main types of extras are:

Types of extras

  • No-ball — a penalty for an illegal delivery; the batting side is awarded runs and the delivery does not count toward the over.
  • Wide — a delivery bowled too wide or too high for the batter to hit; runs are awarded and the ball must be re-bowled.
  • Bye — runs scored when the ball passes the batter without touching bat or body, and the wicket-keeper fails to gather it.
  • Leg bye — runs scored after the ball deflects off the batter's body, provided the batter attempted a shot or took evasive action.
  • Penalty runs — awarded for unfair play under the Laws of Cricket.

In limited-overs cricket, wides and no-balls are particularly costly: a no-ball in many formats also grants the batting side a "free hit" on the next legal delivery, from which the batter cannot be dismissed except by run out.
