Sociocultural evolution

Sociocultural evolution describes how cultures and societies change over time.

Sociocultural evolution is the process by which societies and cultures change over time, and the body of theory in anthropology and sociology that seeks to describe and explain those changes. Early theories treated evolution as unilinear — all societies progressing through the same stages — while most modern approaches are multilinear, holding that different societies follow different trajectories shaped by environment, technology, demography, and contact with others.

Classical theories

Nineteenth-century thinkers such as Auguste Comte, Herbert Spencer, Lewis Henry Morgan, and Edward Tylor proposed stage models: Morgan's Ancient Society (1877) divided history into savagery, barbarism, and civilization, and Karl Marx's theory of history similarly rested on successive modes of production. These schemes were criticized in the early 20th century by Franz Boas and his students, who emphasized historical particularism and diffusion and rejected rankings of cultures as ethnocentric.

Neo-evolutionism

Interest revived mid-century with Leslie White's theory that cultures evolve as they harness more energy per capita, and Julian Steward's "multilinear evolution," which traced regularities in how similar environments shape similar adaptations. Elman Service proposed the widely used sequence of band, tribe, chiefdom, and state as levels of sociopolitical integration. Later scholars have applied evolutionary frameworks from biology — including dual-inheritance (gene–culture coevolution) theory and memetics — and quantitative comparative methods using cross-cultural databases.

Contemporary views

Most anthropologists today use "evolution" in the neutral sense of cumulative change rather than progress, and debates continue over directions and causes of change: population growth, agricultural intensification, warfare, trade, and information technology are among the factors commonly invoked. Modernization theory in sociology and development studies is a related descendant of these ideas.
