Yves Henri Donat Mathieu-Saint-Laurent (1 August 1936 – 1 June 2008), referred to as Yves Saint Laurent or YSL, was a French fashion designer who, in 1961, founded his eponymous fashion label. He is regarded as one of the greatest fashion designers of the twentieth century, credited with popularising ready-to-wear, the women's tuxedo suit ("Le Smoking", 1966), and drawing inspiration from art and non-European cultures.

Early career at Dior

Born in Oran, Algeria, Saint Laurent moved to Paris as a teenager and was hired by Christian Dior after winning a design competition. Following Dior's sudden death in 1957, the 21-year-old Saint Laurent became head designer of the house; his 1958 "Trapeze" collection saved the firm from financial ruin. Conscripted into the French Army in 1960, he suffered a breakdown and was dismissed by Dior.

His own house

With his partner Pierre Bergé, Saint Laurent opened his own couture house in 1961. His designs — the Mondrian dress (1965), the women's tuxedo, safari jackets, sheer blouses, and the Rive Gauche ready-to-wear line (1966) — reshaped women's fashion and blurred gender boundaries. He was the first living fashion designer to be honoured with a retrospective at the Metropolitan Museum of Art (1983).

Later life and legacy

Saint Laurent retired from haute couture in 2002 and died of brain cancer in Paris in 2008. The Fondation Pierre Bergé – Yves Saint Laurent preserves his work, and museums dedicated to him opened in Paris and Marrakech in 2017.
