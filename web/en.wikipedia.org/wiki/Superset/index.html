Subset and superset

In mathematics, a set A is a subset of a set B if all elements of A are also elements of B; B is then a superset of A. It is possible for A and B to be equal; if they are unequal, then A is a proper subset of B. The relationship of one set being a subset of another is called inclusion or sometimes containment.

Notation

A ⊆ B denotes that A is a subset of B, and equivalently B ⊇ A denotes that B is a superset of A. Strict (proper) inclusion is written A ⊂ B or A ⊊ B, though notation varies between authors. The subset relation defines a partial order on the collection of all subsets of a given set, giving a Boolean algebra structure.

Examples

The set of natural numbers is a proper subset of the set of integers, which is itself a proper subset of the rational numbers, which in turn are a proper subset of the real numbers. Every set is a subset of itself, and the empty set, denoted ∅, is a subset of every set.
