Galician literature is the literature written in the Galician language of Galicia, in north-western Spain. It begins with one of the most brilliant lyric traditions of medieval Europe, passes through centuries of decline in which Galician survived chiefly as a spoken language, and returns in a nineteenth-century revival whose leading figure, Rosalía de Castro, is now regarded as a foundational writer of modern Spanish as well as Galician letters.

Medieval period

In the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, Galician-Portuguese was the prestige language of lyric poetry across the Christian kingdoms of Iberia. Over 1,600 cantigas survive — love songs (cantigas de amor), songs of the absent lover (cantigas de amigo, often in a female voice), and satirical cantigas de escarnio — along with the great religious collection of the Cantigas de Santa Maria, compiled under Alfonso X of Castile, who chose Galician for his verse. By about 1500, however, Galician had ceased to be a written language of administration and high culture.

The dark centuries and Rexurdimento

During the Séculos Escuros ("dark centuries", sixteenth to eighteenth), Castilian dominated print while Galician persisted in oral tradition. The revival, or Rexurdimento, gathered force in the nineteenth century, conventionally dated from the publication of Rosalía de Castro's Cantares gallegos in 1863 — the year now celebrated as Galician Literature Day. Eduardo Pondal and Manuel Curros Enríquez joined Rosalía as the movement's canonical poets, while prose, theatre and journalism in Galician gradually re-emerged.

Twentieth century to the present

The Xeración Nós generation — Vicente Risco, Ramón Otero Pedrayo and the artist-politician Castelao — gave Galician prose and thought new ambition before the Spanish Civil War. Under Franco the language was again suppressed in public life, though writers such as Álvaro Cunqueiro kept it alive. Since the Statute of Autonomy of 1981 made Galician co-official, a lively literature has developed, with poets like Manuel Rivas (also a novelist of international reach) and Chus Pato, and an active publishing industry centred on Vigo and Santiago de Compostela.
