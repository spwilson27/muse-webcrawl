The Rose Bowl Game is an annual American college football bowl game, traditionally played on January 1 (New Year's Day) at the Rose Bowl stadium in Pasadena, California. Nicknamed "The Granddaddy of Them All" by broadcaster Keith Jackson, it is the oldest currently operating bowl game and part of America's New Year celebration associated with the Tournament of Roses Parade.

History

The first game was played on January 1, 1902, when Michigan defeated Stanford 49–0 in the Tournament East–West football game; the result was so lopsided that football was replaced by chariot races and other events until 1916, when the game returned permanently. From 1947 the game matched the champions of the Pacific Coast Conference (later the Pac-12) and the Big Ten. The Rose Bowl stadium, built in 1922, seats over 90,000 spectators.

Since 2014 the game has been part of the College Football Playoff, hosting a national semifinal every third year; in non-semifinal years it traditionally keeps its Big Ten–Pac-12 matchup. The 2024 game, a playoff semifinal, saw Michigan defeat Alabama in overtime on the way to the national championship.

Traditions

The game follows the Rose Parade on the morning of New Year's Day. The participating teams' bands perform, and the sunset over the San Gabriel Mountains during the fourth quarter is a celebrated visual hallmark of the broadcast. The game is typically the highest-rated and most-watched bowl game each season.
