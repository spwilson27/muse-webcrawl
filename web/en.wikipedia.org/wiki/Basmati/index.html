Basmati (pronounced ['bɑːsməti]) is a variety of long, slender-grained aromatic rice which is traditionally grown in India, Pakistan and Nepal. As of 2019, India accounted for 65% of the international trade in basmati rice, while Pakistan accounted for the remainder. Many countries use domestically grown basmati cultivars, though true basmati is protected by geographical indication in several markets.

Cooked basmati, prized for its length, fragrance and fluffy texture.

Characteristics

The name comes from the Hindi/Urdu bāsmatī, "fragrant". Basmati grains elongate dramatically when cooked — often doubling in length — while remaining separate and fluffy rather than sticky. The distinctive popcorn-like aroma comes largely from the compound 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline, which occurs in basmati at about twelve times the concentration found in ordinary rice. Traditional cultivars include Basmati 370 and Taraori; widely grown modern hybrids include Pusa Basmati 1121, whose grains are among the longest of any rice.

Cultivation and trade

Basmati is grown mainly in the foothills and plains of Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and western Uttar Pradesh in India, and in Pakistani Punjab. The grain is a major export earner for both countries, and disputes over its geographical-indication status — and a controversial 1997 US patent granted to the company RiceTec for "basmati" lines, most claims of which were later withdrawn or cancelled — have made it a prominent case in debates over biopiracy and traditional knowledge.

In cuisine

Basmati is the rice of choice for biryani, pilaf (pulao) and other South Asian and Middle Eastern dishes where distinct, fragrant grains are desired.
