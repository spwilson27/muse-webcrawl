The Last Supper (Italian: Il Cenacolo) is a mural painting by the Italian High Renaissance artist Leonardo da Vinci, dated to c. 1495–1498. It depicts the scene of the Last Supper of Jesus with the Twelve Apostles as told in the Gospel of John — specifically the moment after Jesus announces that one of his apostles will betray him. The work was commissioned as part of a renovation of the convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan by Leonardo's patron Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan, and covers the end wall of the refectory.

Composition

Leonardo arranged the thirteen figures in four groups of three apostles flanking Christ, whose outstretched arms form a calm triangular centre. One-point perspective draws every line of the coffered ceiling and tapestried walls toward Christ's head, framed by the central window. Each apostle reacts individually to the announcement of betrayal — questioning, protesting, recoiling — while Judas, fourth from the left, withdraws into shadow clutching a small bag.

Technique and condition

Rather than painting in true fresco, Leonardo used an experimental technique of tempera and oil on dry plaster, which allowed him to work slowly but began to deteriorate within decades. The mural has survived humidity, a doorway cut through the wall in 1652, use of the refectory as a stable during the Napoleonic occupation, and the bombing of 1943, which destroyed much of the convent but spared the wall. A major restoration concluded in 1999 removed centuries of overpainting.

Legacy

The painting is one of the most famous and reproduced religious artworks in the world, and is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with the church and convent. Early full-size copies, including one attributed to Leonardo's workshop (Giampietrino), preserve much of the detail lost from the original.
