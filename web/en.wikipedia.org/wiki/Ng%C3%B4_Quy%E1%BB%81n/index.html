Ngô Quyền

Ngô Quyền (898 – 944) was a Vietnamese general and ruler who defeated the Chinese Southern Han forces at the Battle of Bạch Đằng River in 938, ending roughly a millennium of Chinese imperial rule over Vietnam. In 939 he proclaimed himself king and established his capital at Cổ Loa, laying the foundation for an independent Vietnamese state.

Rise

Ngô Quyền was a general and son-in-law of Dương Đình Nghệ, the lord who had seized control of Tĩnh Hải quân (northern Vietnam) from the Southern Han. After Dương was assassinated by the usurper Kiều Công Tiễn in 937, Ngô Quyền raised an army, defeated and killed the usurper, and prepared to meet the Southern Han expedition sent to restore Chinese rule.

Battle of Bạch Đằng

In 938 Ngô Quyền had iron-tipped wooden stakes planted in the bed of the Bạch Đằng River, hidden at high tide. Vietnamese boats lured the Southern Han fleet, commanded by Liu Hongcao, upriver at high water; as the tide fell the Chinese ships were impaled on the stakes and destroyed. The victory secured Vietnamese independence, which — brief interludes aside — endured under successive dynasties.

Reign and legacy

King Ngô ruled for about five years before dying in 944, after which his brother-in-law Dương Tam Kha usurped the throne and the realm descended into the Anarchy of the 12 Warlords. Ngô Quyền remains one of Vietnam's foremost national heroes, commemorated by temples, streets and schools throughout the country.
