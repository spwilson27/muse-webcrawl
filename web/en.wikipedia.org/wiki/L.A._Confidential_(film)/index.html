L.A. Confidential is a 1997 American neo-noir crime film directed, produced and co-written by Curtis Hanson. Adapted from James Ellroy's 1990 novel — the third in his L.A. Quartet — the film is set in 1950s Los Angeles and intertwines the stories of three very different LAPD officers investigating a mass shooting at a coffee shop.

Plot

Ambitious, by-the-book detective Edmund Exley (Guy Pearce), brutal but protective officer Bud White (Russell Crowe) and slick, fame-seeking sergeant Jack Vincennes (Kevin Spacey), technical adviser to a television police show, are drawn into the "Nite Owl" massacre case. Their investigation uncovers police corruption reaching to Captain Dudley Smith (James Cromwell), a call-girl ring of women surgically altered to resemble film stars — including Lynn Bracken (Kim Basinger) — and a struggle for control of organised crime after the fall of Mickey Cohen. The strands converge on a heroin deal and a showdown at a deserted motel.

Reception and awards

The film premiered at Cannes and was a critical triumph, often cited among the finest American films of the 1990s. Nominated for nine Academy Awards, it won two: Best Supporting Actress for Basinger and Best Adapted Screenplay for Hanson and Brian Helgeland; it lost Best Picture to Titanic. In 2015 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
