Robertson–Seymour theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Robertson–Seymour theorem is a landmark result in graph theory stating that the finite undirected graphs, partially ordered by the graph minor relation, form a well-quasi-ordering: there is no infinite antichain and no infinite strictly decreasing sequence of graphs under the minor relation.

Consequences

A corollary is that every property of graphs that is closed under taking minors — such as planarity, or embeddability on a given surface — can be characterised by a finite set of forbidden minors. For planar graphs this recovers Wagner's theorem, which gives the forbidden minors K5 and K3,3. The theorem also implies that any such minor-closed property can be tested in polynomial time, though often with impractical constants and without an explicit list of obstructions.

Proof

Neil Robertson and Paul Seymour proved the theorem across a series of twenty papers on graph minors published between 1983 and 2004, totalling some 500 pages. The work earned them the Fulkerson Prize and is regarded as one of the deepest achievements in combinatorics. A special case, Kruskal's tree theorem, had earlier foreshadowed the result for trees.
