Ear training

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ear training or aural skills is the process of developing the ability to identify, by hearing alone, the pitches, intervals, melody, chords, rhythms and other elements of music. It is a standard component of formal music education at conservatoires and universities, and a practical skill for performers, composers, producers and audio engineers.

Methods

Training typically progresses through recognising intervals (often taught with reference songs for each interval), identifying chord qualities and progressions, melodic and rhythmic dictation — writing down music as it is played — and sight-singing, in which the student sings notated music at first sight, frequently using solfège syllables or scale-degree numbers. Students may also transcribe recorded music, a central practice in jazz and popular music learning. Modern ear training uses dedicated software and apps that generate randomised exercises and track accuracy.

Concepts

Teachers distinguish absolute pitch (naming a pitch without a reference, a rare ability) from relative pitch (identifying pitches in relation to a tonic), which is the trainable foundation of most aural work. Functional approaches teach students to hear scale degrees by their tendencies and resolutions within a key, rather than as isolated intervals.

Assessment and research

Aural examinations feature in graded music exams such as those of ABRSM and in university entrance auditions. Research in music cognition indicates aural skills improve substantially with structured practice, and that training transfers to improvisation, memorisation and ensemble performance.
