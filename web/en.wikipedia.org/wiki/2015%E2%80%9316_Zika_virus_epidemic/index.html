2015–16 Zika virus epidemic

DateApril 2015 – November 2016
LocationThe Americas, especially Brazil
CauseZika virus, transmitted mainly by Aedes aegypti
Major complicationCongenital Zika syndrome, including microcephaly

The 2015–16 Zika virus epidemic was an outbreak of Zika virus disease that began in Brazil in April 2015 and spread throughout the Americas and to parts of Asia and the Pacific. In February 2016 the World Health Organization declared the cluster of associated birth defects a Public Health Emergency of International Concern — only the fourth such declaration in WHO history. The emergency was lifted in November 2016 as transmission declined.

Spread

Zika virus, a flavivirus first isolated in Uganda in 1947, is transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. Genetic analyses suggest the virus entered Brazil around 2013–2014, possibly during international sporting events. By early 2016, active transmission was reported in dozens of countries and territories in the Americas, with hundreds of thousands of suspected cases; most infections are mild or asymptomatic.

Effects on pregnancy

In late 2015, Brazilian doctors in Pernambuco noticed a sharp rise in babies born with microcephaly — an abnormally small head and underdeveloped brain. Evidence accumulated that Zika infection during pregnancy caused a spectrum of birth defects now called congenital Zika syndrome. Thousands of confirmed cases were reported in Brazil alone, and health authorities advised women in affected regions to consider delaying pregnancy.

Guillain–Barré syndrome

The epidemic also coincided with increases in Guillain–Barré syndrome, a rare autoimmune condition causing paralysis, in several countries, a link first observed during the 2013–14 French Polynesia outbreak.

Response

Responses included mosquito-control campaigns, controversial aerial spraying, travel advisories for pregnant women, and an accelerated research programme for diagnostics and vaccines. The epidemic cast a shadow over the 2016 Rio Olympic Games, though the Games proceeded without a Zika outbreak among attendees.
