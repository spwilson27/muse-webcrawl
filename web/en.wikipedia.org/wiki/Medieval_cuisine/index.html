Medieval cuisine

Medieval cuisine includes the foods, eating habits, and cooking methods of European cultures during the Middle Ages, roughly the 5th to the 15th centuries.

Common foods

Bread, pottage, and ale were staples for all classes. Grains such as wheat, rye, oats, and barley predominated; meat was costlier and more prominent in aristocratic diets, while fasting rules of the Church shaped consumption of fish and dairy. See Meat pie.

Aristocratic dining

Elite feasts featured roasted meats, elaborate pies, imported spices such as pepper, saffron, and cinnamon, and showpieces called subtleties. Cookbooks such as Le Viandier and The Forme of Cury record the cuisine of royal courts.

Drink

Wine dominated in the south, ale and later hopped beer in the north; mead and cider were regional specialties.
