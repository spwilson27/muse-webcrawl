Newgrange (Irish: Sí an Bhrú) is a prehistoric monument in County Meath, Ireland, located about 8 kilometres west of Drogheda on the north side of the River Boyne. Built around 3200 BC during the Neolithic period — making it older than Stonehenge and the Egyptian pyramids — it is the most famous of the passage tombs of the Brú na Bóinne ("Boyne palace") complex, which also includes the great mounds of Knowth and Dowth.

Description

The monument is a large circular mound about 85 metres in diameter and 13.5 metres high, covering roughly an acre, retained by 97 large kerbstones, many carved with megalithic art — spirals, lozenges and concentric circles. The most famous carved stone is the entrance stone, with its triple spirals. A single passage about 19 metres long leads to a cruciform (cross-shaped) chamber with a corbelled roof that has kept the interior dry for five thousand years. In the chamber's three recesses stand shallow stone basins, in which the cremated remains of the dead were probably placed.

Above the entrance is a special opening, the "roof box". At dawn around the winter solstice (about 19–23 December), a beam of sunlight enters through the roof box, travels down the passage and illuminates the chamber floor for about 17 minutes — one of the most celebrated examples of archaeoastronomy. Excavations led by Professor Michael J. O'Kelly of University College Cork between 1962 and 1975 uncovered the monument and reconstructed its striking facade of white quartz and granite cobbles, a reconstruction that remains debated among archaeologists. The cremated bones of at least five individuals and grave goods including pendants and beads were found inside.

Mythology and later history

In Irish mythology Newgrange is one of the sídhe, dwellings of the Tuatha Dé Danann; it is associated with the Dagda and his son Aengus (Óengus), who won the mound by a trick in some tales. The site was quarried and forgotten after the early medieval period; the entrance was rediscovered in 1699 when a local landowner's workers removed stones for road building. Today Newgrange is managed by the Office of Public Works, access is by guided tour from the Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre, and places on the winter-solstice sunrise tours are allocated by lottery, attracting tens of thousands of entries each year.
