Athos (fictional character)

Athos, Count de la Fère, is a fictional character in the novels The Three Musketeers (1844), Twenty Years After (1845) and The Vicomte de Bragelonne (1847–1850) by Alexandre Dumas, père. He is a highly fictionalised version of the historical musketeer Armand de Sillègue d'Athos d'Autevielle.

In The Three Musketeers, he and the other two musketeers, Porthos and Aramis, are friends of the novel's protagonist, d'Artagnan. Athos is the oldest and most aristocratic of the inseparables, a father figure to d'Artagnan, and is distinguished by his noble bearing, taciturn nature, and heavy drinking, which conceal a sorrowful secret.

Character

Athos's secret is gradually revealed: in his youth, as the Count de la Fère, he married a young woman who turned out to be a branded criminal — the woman later known as Milady de Winter. Believing that he had hanged her according to his seigneurial rights, he abandoned his estates and took service in the musketeers under an assumed name. His discovery that Milady lives drives much of the novel's drama, and he ultimately authorises her execution by the headsman of Lille.

In the sequels

In Twenty Years After, Athos, having resumed his title, opposes Cardinal Mazarin during the Fronde and raises his illegitimate son Raoul, the Vicomte de Bragelonne. In The Vicomte de Bragelonne, he works to restore Charles II to the English throne. He dies of grief shortly after the death of Raoul in North Africa, at the same hour as Porthos, having outlived the heroic age he embodied.

In film and television

Athos has been portrayed on screen by many actors, including Van Heflin in the 1948 film, Oliver Reed in the 1973 and 1974 films, Kiefer Sutherland (in name, as a younger-style Athos) in the 1996 Disney version, Matthew Macfadyen in the 2011 film, and Tom Burke in the BBC series The Musketeers (2014–2016).
