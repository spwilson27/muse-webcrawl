Smiles of a Summer Night (Swedish: Sommarnattens leende) is a 1955 Swedish comedy film written and directed by Ingmar Bergman. It was Bergman's first international success, winning a special prize at the 1956 Cannes Film Festival and establishing his reputation abroad after a period when his career — and Svensk Filmindustri itself — had been in doubt.

Plot

In turn-of-the-century Sweden, the middle-aged lawyer Fredrik Egerman is married to the much younger Anne, who after two years remains a virgin. Fredrik visits his former lover, the actress Desirée Armfeldt, provoking the jealousy of her current lover, the dragoon Count Carl-Magnus Malcolm, whose wife Charlotte is Anne's friend. Desirée's mother invites all parties to her country estate for a midsummer weekend, during which — over a wine said to make drinkers fall in love — the couples sort themselves out: Fredrik reunites with Desirée, the Count returns to Charlotte, and Anne runs off with Fredrik's son Henrik, a tormented theology student. The elderly Mrs. Armfeldt observes that the summer night "smiles" three times: for the young lovers, for the fools, and for the sad and dejected.

Legacy

The film inspired Stephen Sondheim's stage musical A Little Night Music (1973) — which includes the song "Send In the Clowns", written for Desirée — and its 1977 film version, as well as elements of Woody Allen's A Midsummer Night's Sex Comedy (1982). It remains one of Bergman's most beloved works and a high point of his early comedies with Harriet Andersson and Eva Dahlbeck.
