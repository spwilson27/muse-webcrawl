Prisoners' rights

The rights of prisoners are the legal and human rights retained by people who are incarcerated. Although imprisonment necessarily restricts liberty, prisoners in most legal systems retain basic rights, including freedom from cruel and unusual punishment, access to medical care, adequate food and sanitation, and access to the courts and to legal counsel.

International standards

The United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, revised in 2015 and known as the Nelson Mandela Rules, set out international norms. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights requires that prisoners be treated with humanity and respect for their inherent dignity.

National frameworks

In the United States, the Eighth Amendment prohibits cruel and unusual punishment, and the Supreme Court has recognised prisoner rights to medical care, religious practice (qualified by the First Amendment), and due process in some disciplinary contexts. In Europe, the European Convention on Human Rights and the European Committee for the Prevention of Torture provide oversight.

Debates

Contested areas include voting rights for prisoners, solitary confinement, overcrowding, and rehabilitation versus purely punitive approaches to incarceration.
