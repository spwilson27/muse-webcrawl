Panniculitis

Panniculitis is a group of disorders characterised by inflammation of the subcutaneous fat (the panniculus adiposus), the layer of fat beneath the skin. It typically presents as tender, red subcutaneous nodules, most often on the legs, and may be accompanied by fever and systemic symptoms. Panniculitides are classified according to whether inflammation is centred mainly on the fibrous septa between fat lobules (septal) or on the lobules themselves (lobular), and whether vasculitis is present.

Causes and types

The best-known form is erythema nodosum, a septal panniculitis without vasculitis that is associated with infections (such as streptococcal infection and coccidioidomycosis), sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease, pregnancy and certain drugs. Other forms include erythema induratum (nodular vasculitis, associated with tuberculosis), pancreatic panniculitis, in which circulating pancreatic enzymes digest fat and produce characteristic "ghost cells", lupus panniculitis (lupus profundus), alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency panniculitis, cold panniculitis, factitial panniculitis from injections, and subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis generally requires a deep incisional skin biopsy that includes the subcutaneous fat, since superficial samples miss the pathology. Laboratory tests seek an underlying cause. Treatment is directed at the underlying condition, with supportive measures such as rest and leg elevation, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, potassium iodide for some forms, and corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants in severe or systemic cases.
