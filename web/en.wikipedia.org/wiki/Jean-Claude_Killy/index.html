Jean-Claude Killy (born 30 August 1943) is a French former alpine ski racer. He dominated the sport in the late 1960s and won all three gold medals in alpine skiing at the 1968 Winter Olympics in Grenoble, France, becoming a national hero. He later served as a member of the International Olympic Committee from 1995 to 2014.

Early life

Killy was born in Saint-Cloud, a suburb of Paris, during the Second World War. His family moved to Val-d'Isère in the French Alps, where his father ran a ski shop, and Killy was racing on skis from early childhood. He was raised in the resort that would later name its ski area, Espace Killy, in his honour.

Skiing career

Killy rose to prominence in the mid-1960s with an aggressive, dynamic style. He won the first two overall World Cup titles, in 1967 and 1968, winning 12 of 17 races in the inaugural 1967 season. At the 1966 World Championships in Portillo, Chile, he won gold in the downhill and combined.

At the Grenoble Games in February 1968, Killy won the downhill by a narrow margin, then took gold in the giant slalom and, controversially, the slalom — after Austria's Karl Schranz was disqualified following a disputed re-run. The sweep matched the feat of Toni Sailer in 1956. Killy retired from competition shortly afterward, at the age of 24.

Later career

After racing, Killy became a successful businessman and sports administrator. He was co-president of the organising committee of the 1992 Winter Olympics in Albertville, and served on the IOC's coordination commissions for several Games. He resigned from the IOC in 2014. In 1999 he was voted France's Sportsman of the Century.
