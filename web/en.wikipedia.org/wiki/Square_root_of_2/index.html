Square root of 2

The square root of 2 (approximately 1.4142) is the positive real number that, when multiplied by itself, gives the number 2. It may be written as √2 or 21/2. It is an algebraic number of degree two and is geometrically the length of the diagonal of a square with sides of one unit.

The square root of 2 is notable for being, according to tradition, the first number proved to be irrational — that is, it cannot be expressed as a ratio of two integers. This discovery, attributed to the Pythagoreans and specifically to Hippasus of Metapontum, contradicted the Pythagorean belief that all numbers could be expressed as ratios of integers.

Numerical value

√2 ≈ 1.4142135623730951. It has been computed to trillions of decimal digits. Its continued fraction expansion is periodic: √2 = [1; 2, 2, 2, ...], which yields the sequence of best rational approximations 3/2, 7/5, 17/12, 41/29, 99/70, ..., known as side and diagonal numbers.

Proof of irrationality

The classic proof proceeds by contradiction: if √2 were equal to a fraction p/q in lowest terms, then p² = 2q², so p² is even, hence p is even; writing p = 2r gives 4r² = 2q², so q² = 2r² and q is also even, contradicting the assumption that p/q was in lowest terms. Therefore √2 is irrational.

Applications

The ratio √2 : 1 is used in the ISO 216 paper size system (A4, A5, etc.), where halving a sheet along its longer side preserves the aspect ratio. It also appears in electronics (the ratio of peak to RMS voltage in a sine wave), photography (f-stop spacing), and geometry.
