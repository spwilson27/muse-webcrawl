Mausoleum of Galla Placidia

The Mausoleum of Galla Placidia is a late antique Roman mausoleum in Ravenna, Italy, built between 425 and 450 CE. It was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996 as part of the "Early Christian Monuments of Ravenna" for its outstanding Byzantine mosaics, among the best preserved and artistically accomplished of the period.

History

The cruciform brick building is traditionally associated with Galla Placidia, daughter of Emperor Theodosius I and regent of the Western Roman Empire for her son Valentinian III. Although called her mausoleum, she in fact died in Rome in 450 and was probably buried there; the Ravenna building likely served as an oratory or chapel dedicated to Saint Lawrence, attached to the imperial palace church of Santa Croce.

Mosaics

The interior is famed for its deep-blue mosaic decoration. The central dome shows a golden cross amid a starry sky with the symbols of the four Evangelists; the lunettes depict apostles, doves drinking from fountains, and, most famously, the Good Shepherd above the entrance — Christ seated among his flock in a landscape, rendered with classical naturalism. Another lunette shows Saint Lawrence approaching a gridiron and a cabinet of the Gospels. Three large sarcophagi inside are traditionally attributed to Galla Placidia, her husband Constantius III, and her son Valentinian III.
