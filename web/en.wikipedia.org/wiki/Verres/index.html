Verres

Gaius Verres
Bornc. 120 BC
Died43 BC, Massilia (Marseille)
OfficePraetor (74 BC); governor of Sicily (73–71 BC)
Known forCorrupt governorship; prosecuted by Cicero

Gaius Verres (c. 120 – 43 BC) was a Roman magistrate, notorious for his corrupt governorship of Sicily. His prosecution by Cicero in 70 BC, documented in the speeches known as the Verrines (In Verrem), made him a byword for provincial extortion in the late Roman Republic.

Career

Verres served as quaestor in 82 BC, deserting the Marian cause for Sulla, and as legate in Cilicia, where he was already accused of plundering. Elected praetor for 74 BC, he presided over the urban court, then governed Sicily as propraetor for an unusually long three years (73–71 BC).

Governorship and trial

According to Cicero, Verres used his authority to extort money through manipulated grain tithes, shameless theft of artworks and treasures from temples and private homes, rigged court cases, and the illegal scourging and crucifixion of a Roman citizen, Publius Gavius. The Sicilians appealed to Cicero, who had served as quaestor in western Sicily in 75 BC, to prosecute.

At the trial in 70 BC, Cicero's opening speech and the witness testimony were so overwhelming that Verres' advocate, Hortensius, abandoned the defence and Verres fled into exile at Massilia before the trial concluded. He lived there on his plunder until 43 BC, when he was proscribed and killed — tradition says because Mark Antony coveted his Corinthian bronzes.

Legacy

The case was a landmark in Roman law and launched Cicero's career as Rome's leading orator. The published Verrines remain a principal source for the administration — and abuse — of Roman provincial government, and for the art collections of the ancient world.
