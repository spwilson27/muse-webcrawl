Hellenistic astrology

Hellenistic astrology is a tradition of horoscopic astrology that was developed and practised in the Mediterranean region, especially Egypt and Greece, from roughly the 2nd century BCE through the 7th century CE. It synthesised Babylonian astrology with Egyptian decanic traditions and Greek cosmology.

Development

After Alexander the Great's conquests, Babylonian techniques — the twelve-sign zodiac, planetary omens and mathematical astronomy — merged with Egyptian and Greek elements in Alexandria. Foundational texts include the works attributed to Nechepso and Petosiris, Dorotheus of Sidon's Carmen Astrologicum, Claudius Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos, and Vettius Valens' Anthology.

Techniques

Hellenistic astrologers used whole-sign houses, the ascendant and four angles, planetary sect (diurnal/nocturnal), lots (such as the Lot of Fortune), annual profections and zodiacal releasing to interpret natal charts and time predictions.

Legacy

The tradition was transmitted through translations into Middle Persian and Arabic, becoming the basis of medieval Islamic and later European astrology, and was recovered for modern study beginning in the 1990s by translation projects such as Project Hindsight.
