Universal Product Code

The Universal Product Code (UPC) is a barcode symbology that is widely used worldwide for tracking trade items in stores. It consists of 12 numerical digits that are uniquely assigned to each trade item. The UPC was developed in the early 1970s by a consortium of grocery industry companies seeking an automated checkout system.

History

In 1973 the grocery industry adopted a standard product code, and after evaluating several barcode designs it selected a proposal from IBM, designed largely by George Laurer. The first UPC-marked item scanned at a retail checkout was a 10-pack of Wrigley's Juicy Fruit gum, scanned at a Marsh supermarket in Troy, Ohio, on 26 June 1974.

Composition

A UPC-A barcode encodes 12 digits: a number system digit, a five-digit manufacturer code, a five-digit product code, and a check digit calculated from the others to catch scanning errors. The code identifies the manufacturer and the specific product, but carries no price information — prices are looked up in the retailer's database.

Variants

Variants include UPC-E, a compressed six-digit form for small packages, and the 13-digit EAN used internationally, of which UPC is a subset. Administration of company prefixes is handled by GS1.
