Phillip Cagan


Phillip D. Cagan (30 April 1927 – 15 June 2012) was an American economist and professor at Columbia University, known for his work on money demand and hyperinflation.

Phillip David Cagan (April 30, 1927 – June 15, 2012) was an American economist and author. He was a professor of economics at Columbia University from 1966 until his retirement, and previously taught at the University of Chicago and Brown University. He was also associated with the National Bureau of Economic Research.

The demand for money under hyperinflation

Cagan's fame rests on his study "The Monetary Dynamics of Hyperinflation", published in Milton Friedman's edited volume Studies in the Quantity Theory of Money (1956). Analysing seven hyperinflations of the 1920s and 1940s, Cagan modelled real money balances as a function of expected inflation, deriving a semi-elasticity of money demand with respect to expected inflation. The "Cagan model" became a foundational framework for the study of inflation dynamics, expectations formation and the fiscal roots of hyperinflation, and his definition of hyperinflation — inflation exceeding 50 per cent per month — remains in common use.

Other work

Cagan wrote on the determinants of money growth in the United States (Determinants and Effects of Changes in the Stock of Money, 1875–1960, 1965), on monetary policy, pensions, and financial regulation, including The New Federal Reserve and works on private pension funds. He served in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War and received his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1954.
