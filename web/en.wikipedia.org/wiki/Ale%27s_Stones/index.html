Ale's Stones

LocationKåseberga, Skåne, Sweden
TypeStone ship setting
LengthAbout 67 m (largest in Sweden)
MaterialSandstone boulders
BuiltEarly Iron Age (debated)

Ale's Stones (Swedish: Ales stenar) is a megalithic monument in Kåseberga, a village in the Ystad Municipality of Skåne County in southern Sweden. It consists of 59 large boulders arranged in the outline of a ship, forming the largest preserved stone ship setting in Sweden.

Description

The monument is about 67 metres long and 19 metres wide, set on a ridge above the Baltic Sea near the fishing village of Kåseberga. The stones, each weighing up to about 5 tonnes, form the prow, stern, and sides of a ship, with taller stones at the ends.

Dating and interpretation

The age of Ale's Stones has been long debated. Radiocarbon dating of material from the site has suggested dates in the Nordic Iron Age, roughly AD 500–1000, though other researchers have argued for an earlier, Bronze Age date. Interpretations include a burial monument, a grave marker for a legendary king Ale, and a solar calendar aligned to solstice sunsets and sunrises.

Tourism

Ale's Stones is one of Skåne's most visited ancient monuments and a popular walking destination, with views over the Baltic coast.
