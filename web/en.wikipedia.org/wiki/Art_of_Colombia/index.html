Art of Colombia

The art of Colombia spans more than 3,500 years, from the goldwork and ceramics of Pre-Columbian cultures through Spanish colonial painting and sculpture to the internationally celebrated modern art of figures such as Fernando Botero. Colombia's museums, above all Bogotá's Gold Museum, preserve one of the richest artistic heritages in the Americas.


The Muisca raft (Balsa Muisca), Museo del Oro, Bogotá

Pre-Columbian art

Cultures including the Muisca, Quimbaya, Tolima, San Agustín and Tierradentro produced fine goldwork in tumbaga (a gold–copper alloy), massive stone statuary and painted ceramics. The Muisca raft, depicting the El Dorado ceremony, is the most famous single piece, while the burial statuary of San Agustín forms the largest group of religious megaliths in South America.

Colonial period

Under Spanish rule (16th–18th centuries), art was dominated by religious painting and sculpture for churches, blending European Baroque models with local craftsmanship. Gregorio Vásquez de Arce y Ceballos was the leading painter of the New Kingdom of Granada.

19th and 20th centuries

After independence, portraiture and landscape predominated; costumbrismo documented regional life. In the 20th century Colombian art gained international stature through the monumental figurative painting and sculpture of Fernando Botero, the work of Alejandro Obregón, Eduardo Ramírez Villamizar and Edgar Negret, and later politically engaged artists such as Doris Salcedo, whose work addresses Colombia's armed conflict.
