Dan (rank)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The dan (段) ranking system is used by many Japanese, Okinawan, Korean and other martial arts organisations to indicate the level of a person's ability within an art. Taken together, the dan and kyū systems form a graded scale: kyū grades (typically numbered downwards) are held by students below master level, while dan grades (numbered upwards from first dan, shodan) denote advanced practitioners and masters. The character 段 means "step" or "grade".

History

The dan ranking system was invented by Kanō Jigorō (1860–1938), founder of judo, who awarded the first dan ranks in 1883, borrowing the concept from the ranking system of the board game Go. Kanō's senior students were distinguished by black belts (obi), in contrast to the white belts of kyū-grade students. The system was subsequently adopted by karate (through Funakoshi Gichin, a friend of Kanō), aikido, kendo and other Japanese arts, and spread to Korean arts such as taekwondo, where dan grades are also called dan (단).

Structure

Dan grades usually run from 1st dan to 10th dan, although the number of levels and requirements vary by art and organisation:

GradeJapaneseGeneral meaning
1st danShodan (初段)"Beginning step" – a competent practitioner who has mastered the basics
2nd–3rd danNidan, SandanAdvanced practitioner; often assistant instructor
4th–5th danYondan, GodanExpert; full instructor grades in many arts
6th–8th danRokudan–HachidanSenior master grades, often awarded for contribution as much as technique
9th–10th danKudan, JūdanHighest grades, rarely awarded; sometimes reserved for founders or heads of style

Progression through the lower dan grades normally requires examination and minimum time-in-grade; higher grades are commonly conferred honorarily by governing bodies. In many arts the black belt itself does not change colour with dan grade, though some use striped, paneled or red-and-white belts for senior grades, and a red belt for the highest ranks in some traditions.

Use outside martial arts

Dan and kyū grades originating in Go are also used in other Japanese disciplines and games, including shogi, renju and some traditional arts, and the terms have been adapted to ranking systems in fields such as Japanese flower arrangement in certain schools.
