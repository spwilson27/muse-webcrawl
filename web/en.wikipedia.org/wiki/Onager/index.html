Onager

Scientific nameEquus hemionus
FamilyEquidae
RangeDeserts and steppes of Asia (Iran, Mongolia, northern China, India)
StatusNear Threatened (species overall)

The onager (Equus hemionus), also known as the hemione or Asiatic wild ass, is a species of the family Equidae native to Asia. It is sandy- to reddish-brown in colour, with a pale belly and a dark dorsal stripe, and is larger and faster than the African wild ass, capable of running 60–70 km/h.

Subspecies and distribution

Recognised subspecies include the Mongolian wild ass (khulan), the Indian wild ass (khur), the Turkmenian kulan, the Persian onager, and the extinct Syrian wild ass. Onagers live in desert plains, semi-deserts, oases and steppes, feeding on grasses and travelling long distances between grazing and water. Historically widespread across western and central Asia, their range has shrunk dramatically through hunting and competition with livestock; the Persian onager is endangered, and reintroduction programmes operate in Israel's Negev and elsewhere.

Behaviour

Onagers are social, living in herds whose composition changes seasonally; stallions may hold territories around water sources. They were never successfully domesticated, unlike the African wild ass, the ancestor of the domestic donkey — a distinction noted in ancient texts, where "onager" also lent its name to a Roman torsion catapult that kicked like the animal when fired.
