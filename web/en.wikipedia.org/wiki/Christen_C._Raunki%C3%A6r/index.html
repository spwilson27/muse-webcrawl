Christen Christensen Raunkiær (29 March 1860 – 11 March 1938) was a Danish botanist, who pioneered the quantitative study of plant ecology. He is chiefly remembered for his plant life-form system, first published in 1904–1907, which classifies plants according to the position of their perennating (survival) buds relative to the ground during the unfavourable season.

Life-form system

Raunkiær's main classes are phanerophytes (buds high on trees and shrubs), chamaephytes (buds near the ground), hemicryptophytes (buds at the soil surface), cryptophytes or geophytes (buds underground, including bulbs and rhizomes), and therophytes (annuals surviving as seeds). By counting the proportion of each class in a flora — a "biological spectrum" — he showed that climates leave characteristic signatures: phanerophytes dominate the tropics, therophytes the deserts, hemicryptophytes temperate lands.

Career

Raunkiær worked at the Botanical Museum and Botanical Garden of the University of Copenhagen, becoming professor of botany in 1912. He also devised the widely used quadrat-based frequency method (the "Raunkiær circle") for measuring how regularly a species occurs in a plant community.
