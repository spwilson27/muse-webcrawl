Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

Capital: Schwerin
Largest city: Rostock
Area: 23,180 km²
Population: about 1.6 million

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern (MV; also known in English as Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania) is a state in the north-east of Germany. Of the country's sixteen states, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern ranks 14th in population and sixth in area, and it is the least densely populated state.

The Baltic Sea coast, including islands such as Rügen and Usedom, stretches about 2,000 km and features many resorts. Inland, the Mecklenburg Lake District contains more than a thousand lakes, the largest being Lake Müritz.

History

The state was formed after the Second World War from the historical regions of Mecklenburg and the western part of Pomerania. It was dissolved into districts in East Germany in 1952 and re-established at German reunification in 1990.

Geography and economy

Schwerin is the state capital and Rostock the largest city, a major Baltic port. Tourism, agriculture, food processing, shipbuilding and renewable energy are important industries. The state is known for brick Gothic churches, Hanseatic old towns in Wismar and Stralsund (UNESCO World Heritage Sites), and national parks including the Western Pomerania Lagoon Area and Jasmund.
