Regional jet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A small white regional jet with rear-mounted engines on an airport apron]
An Embraer-style regional jet.

A regional jet (RJ) is a jet-powered regional airliner, typically with fewer than 100 seats, used on short-to-medium routes feeding hubs and linking smaller cities. The type boomed after Bombardier\'s CRJ100/200 (1992) proved 50-seat jet economics; Embraer\'s ERJ and E-Jet families and the larger E-Jets E2, the Airbus A220 (née Bombardier CSeries) and Mitsubishi\'s cancelled SpaceJet mark its evolution. US \'scope clauses\' long capped regional-jet size at 76 seats for mainline pilot contracts.

Rise

Turboprops dominated regional flying until the CRJ showed passengers preferred jets and airlines valued speed and commonality. US majors outsourced flying to regional partners (SkyWest, Republic) under capacity-purchase agreements; the 50-seat fleet peaked in the 2000s before fuel prices favoured 70–76 seat types like the E175.

Aircraft

Bombardier exited commercial aviation (CRJ line ended 2020; CSeries became the Airbus A220). Embraer\'s E170/175/190/195 and the re-engined E2 family are the main Western regional jets; China\'s COMAC ARJ21 (C909) and Russia\'s Superjet serve home markets.

Pressures

Pilot shortages after COVID hit regional airlines hardest, accelerating retirements of 50-seaters and upgauging; electric and hybrid regional aircraft are proposed for the 2030s but not yet certified.
