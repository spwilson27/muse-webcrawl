Manner of articulation

In articulatory phonetics, the manner of articulation is the configuration and interaction of the articulators (speech organs such as the tongue, lips, and palate) when making a speech sound. The concept is used mainly for the classification of consonants, although the manner features of vowels (such as height, backness, and roundedness) are also relevant. A given manner of articulation may have several manners of phonation, which are treated as secondary features.

Overview

Manners of articulation may be divided along a continuum from complete obstruction of the airstream (plosives) through partial obstruction causing turbulence (fricatives) to relatively unimpeded airflow (approximants and vowels). Along the way are found nasals, trills, taps and flaps, and lateral consonants.

Major manners

Obstruents

  • Plosive (stop): complete closure of the vocal tract followed by release, e.g. [p], [t], [k].
  • Fricative: narrow constriction causing turbulent airflow, e.g. [s], [z], [f], [ʃ].
  • Affricate: a stop released into a fricative, e.g. [tʃ], [dʒ].

Sonorants

  • Nasal: oral closure with airflow through the nose, e.g. [m], [n], [ŋ].
  • Trill: vibration of one articulator against another, e.g. [r].
  • Tap or flap: a single brief contact, e.g. [ɾ].
  • Approximant: constriction without turbulence, e.g. [j], [w], [l].
  • Lateral: airflow along the sides of the tongue, e.g. lateral approximant [l] and lateral fricatives [ɬ], [ɮ].

Other manners

Consonants produced with a secondary airstream mechanism include ejectives, implosives, and clicks, each of which combines a manner of articulation with a glottalic or velaric airstream. Sibilants are a subclass of fricatives characterised by a high-amplitude hissing quality directed against the teeth.
