LBV 1806-20 is a candidate luminous blue variable (LBV) star located on the far side of the Milky Way, in the constellation Sagittarius. It lies within the star cluster 1806-20, which also hosts the soft gamma repeater SGR 1806-20, a magnetar. Heavily obscured by interstellar dust, the star is invisible in visible light and detectable only in the infrared.

Properties

When announced in 2004, LBV 1806-20 was claimed to be among the most luminous and most massive stars known, with an estimated luminosity possibly reaching 40 million times that of the Sun and a mass of 130–200 solar masses, rivalling or exceeding the Pistol Star. Subsequent studies revised the figures downward — multi-epoch spectroscopy suggested it may be a binary system, and luminosity estimates around 2 million L☉ with a mass of roughly 36–65 M☉ have been proposed; its distance from Earth remains uncertain, with values between about 8.7 and 15 kiloparsecs.

Like other luminous blue variables, LBV 1806-20 is expected to be a massive star in a brief, unstable phase of evolution, shedding mass through powerful stellar winds before ending as a supernova or collapsing directly into a black hole. Its host cluster, 1806-20, is one of the most massive young star clusters in the Galaxy and contains several other extremely massive stars, including Wolf–Rayet stars and the magnetar SGR 1806-20, whose 2004 giant flare was the brightest extrasolar gamma-ray event recorded.
