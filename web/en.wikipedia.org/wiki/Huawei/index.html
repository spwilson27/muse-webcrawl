Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. is a Chinese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Shenzhen, Guangdong. Founded in 1987 by Ren Zhengfei, a former People's Liberation Army engineering officer, Huawei began as a reseller of telephone switch equipment and grew into the world's largest manufacturer of telecommunications equipment and a leading maker of smartphones, semiconductors (through its HiSilicon subsidiary), and information technology services. The company says it is owned by its employees through an internal shareholding scheme.

Business

Huawei's operations span carrier networks (including 5G base stations), enterprise IT, cloud computing, and consumer devices. At its peak around 2020 it was the world's largest smartphone vendor by shipment volume, overtaking Samsung and Apple in some quarters. Its consumer brand portfolio included the P and Mate smartphone series; in November 2020 it sold its Honor sub-brand to a consortium under pressure from trade restrictions.

Sanctions and controversies

Huawei has faced sustained scrutiny from the United States and allies over alleged ties to the Chinese state and fears that its equipment could enable espionage — allegations the company denies. In May 2019 the U.S. Department of Commerce placed Huawei on the Entity List, cutting it off from U.S. technology including Google Mobile Services, which severely damaged its overseas smartphone business. The company's chief financial officer, Meng Wanzhou (Ren's daughter), was detained in Canada in December 2018 on a U.S. extradition request and released in September 2021 under a deferred prosecution agreement. Several countries restricted or banned Huawei equipment from their 5G networks.

In 2023 Huawei surprised analysts by launching the Mate 60 Pro with a 7-nanometre HiSilicon Kirin 9000S processor produced in China, signalling progress in domestic semiconductor manufacturing despite export controls.
