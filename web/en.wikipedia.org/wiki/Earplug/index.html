Earplug
Purpose: Hearing protection, water exclusion, sleep
Materials: Foam, silicone, wax

An earplug is a device inserted into the ear canal to protect the ears from loud noise, water, dust or foreign objects, or to reduce sound for sleep and concentration.

Types

Disposable foam earplugs, made of memory foam that expands in the canal, are the most common industrial hearing protectors. Pre-moulded silicone or rubber plugs are washable and reusable; custom-moulded plugs are fitted to an individual's ear. Musicians' earplugs use filters to reduce volume evenly across frequencies, preserving sound quality. Wax and silicone putty plugs seal the canal entrance and are often used for swimming and sleeping.

Effectiveness and safety

Properly fitted earplugs typically reduce noise by 15–30 decibels. Real-world protection depends heavily on fit, and occupational programmes use fit-testing. Plugs should be inserted with clean hands and replaced when soiled to reduce the risk of ear infection or wax impaction.
