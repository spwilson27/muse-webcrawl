John Wilder Tukey (16 June 1915 – 26 July 2000) was an American mathematician and statistician, best known for developing the Cooley–Tukey fast Fourier transform algorithm and the box plot, and for founding exploratory data analysis as a distinct approach to statistics.

Career and contributions

Tukey spent his career split between Princeton University and Bell Telephone Laboratories. During the Second World War he worked on fire-control problems at the Fire Control Research Office. In 1965, with James Cooley, he published the modern form of the fast Fourier transform, which revolutionised signal processing.

His 1977 book Exploratory Data Analysis championed graphical, robust methods — the box plot, stem-and-leaf display and resistant summaries — as a complement to confirmatory inference. He also contributed the jackknife, Tukey's range test, and Tukey's lemma in set theory. Tukey is credited with coining the computing terms "bit" (1947) and "software" (1958). He advised on matters from the Kinsey Reports' statistics to U-2 reconnaissance and election-night forecasting for NBC.

Honours

Tukey received the National Medal of Science in 1973 and was a founder of Princeton's statistics department, one of the first in the United States.
