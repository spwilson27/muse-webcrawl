Karl Paul Reinhold Niebuhr (1892–1971) was an American Reformed theologian and ethicist, professor at Union Theological Seminary for more than 30 years and one of the most influential public intellectuals of 20th-century America. His "Christian realism" applied the doctrines of sin and human self-interest to politics, in reaction against the optimism of liberal Protestantism and the Social Gospel.

Ideas and works

In Moral Man and Immoral Society (1932) Niebuhr argued that groups — nations, classes, races — behave more selfishly than individuals, so politics requires coercion and a balance of power rather than appeals to goodwill alone. His Gifford Lectures, published as The Nature and Destiny of Man (1941–43), are considered his theological masterpiece. He is also credited with the Serenity Prayer ("God, give us grace to accept with serenity the things that cannot be changed, courage to change the things that should be changed, and the wisdom to distinguish the one from the other"), adopted widely by Alcoholics Anonymous.

Influence

Niebuhr helped found Americans for Democratic Action, influenced Cold War liberalism and the early thought of Martin Luther King Jr., and was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1964. His blend of moral seriousness and political realism continues to be cited by politicians and ethicists across the spectrum.
