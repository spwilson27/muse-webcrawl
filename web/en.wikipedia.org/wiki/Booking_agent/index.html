Booking agent
Also called: Talent agent (in part); booker
Industry: Entertainment, live performance
Role: Securing engagements for artists
Typical commission: around 10–20% of fees

A booking agent is a person or company that arranges live engagements for performers — including concerts, tours, festival appearances, theatrical productions, comedy shows, and speaking engagements — on behalf of the artists they represent. Booking agents negotiate fees, routes, and contract terms with promoters and venues.

Role and responsibilities

In the music industry, the booking agent plans and sells an artist's tours: pitching the act to promoters, holding dates, negotiating performance fees and production requirements, issuing contracts, and coordinating with the artist's manager, record label, and tour manager. Agents usually earn a commission, commonly 10 to 20 percent of the artist's performance fee. In many jurisdictions, notably parts of the United States, agents who procure employment are regulated or licensed separately from talent managers, and in film and television the role overlaps with that of the talent agent.

Industry structure

Major entertainment agencies with large booking departments have included Creative Artists Agency (CAA), United Talent Agency (UTA) and WME (Endeavor) in the United States and their counterparts internationally, alongside numerous independent and boutique agencies specialising in particular genres or territories.
