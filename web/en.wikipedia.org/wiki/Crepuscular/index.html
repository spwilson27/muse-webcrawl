Crepuscular animal

In zoology, a crepuscular animal is one that is active primarily during the twilight period, being partly matutinal (active in the early morning) and partly vespertine (active in the evening). This activity pattern is one of several ways animals partition time in their diel (24-hour) activity cycle, alongside the better-known nocturnal, diurnal, and cathemeral patterns.

The word crepuscular derives from the Latin crepusculum, meaning "twilight" or "dusk". Crepuscular activity is often described as an adaptation to avoid predators that are strictly diurnal or nocturnal, or as a compromise that allows an animal to exploit food resources at times when temperatures are moderate and competition is low.

Adaptive significance

Crepuscular behaviour is thought to arise from several selective pressures:

  • Predator avoidance — Being active at dawn and dusk can reduce exposure to both day-hunting and night-hunting predators.
  • Thermoregulation — In hot climates, twilight activity avoids the heat of midday while still allowing some visual foraging.
  • Prey availability — Many insects and other prey are themselves crepuscular, concentrating feeding opportunities at twilight.
  • Light compromise — Intermediate light levels suit animals whose eyes are adapted neither to full daylight nor to complete darkness.

Examples

Well-known crepuscular animals include many deer, rabbits, opossums, and skunks; the domestic cat is frequently described as crepuscular, which reflects the behaviour of its wild ancestor, the African wildcat. Among birds, the common nighthawk and some owls show crepuscular tendencies; the crepuscular ray, a flowering plant pollinated by twilight-active moths, illustrates that the activity pattern extends across kingdoms. Many species of moth, beetle, and other insects are crepuscular, as are some rodents such as hamsters and chinchillas.

Matutinal and vespertine

Animals active only at dawn are termed matutinal; those active only at dusk are vespertine. Some species show a bimodal activity pattern with peaks at both dawn and dusk and rest during the middle of the day and night — a pattern especially common in desert mammals avoiding midday heat.
