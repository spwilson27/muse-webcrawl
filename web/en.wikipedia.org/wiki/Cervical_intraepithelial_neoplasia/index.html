Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), also known as cervical dysplasia, is the abnormal growth of cells on the surface of the cervix that could potentially lead to cervical cancer. It is caused in most cases by persistent infection with oncogenic types of human papillomavirus (HPV), especially HPV types 16 and 18.

Classification

CIN is graded by the depth of epithelial involvement: CIN 1 involves the lower third of the epithelium (mild dysplasia), CIN 2 the lower two-thirds (moderate), and CIN 3 more than two-thirds up to full thickness (severe dysplasia, equivalent to carcinoma in situ). In the Bethesda system for cytology, these correspond broadly to low-grade (LSIL) and high-grade (HSIL) squamous intraepithelial lesions.

Diagnosis

CIN produces no symptoms and is usually detected by Pap test screening or HPV testing. Abnormal screening results are followed by colposcopy and biopsy, which confirm the grade.

Treatment

CIN 1 frequently regresses spontaneously and is usually managed by observation. Higher grades are treated by ablative methods (cryotherapy, laser) or excisional procedures such as the loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP) or cold knife conization, which remove the transformation zone of the cervix.

Prevention

HPV vaccination prevents infection with the HPV types responsible for most cases, and regular cervical screening allows CIN to be detected and treated before invasive cancer develops.
