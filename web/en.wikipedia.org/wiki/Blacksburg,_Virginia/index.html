Blacksburg is an incorporated town in Montgomery County, Virginia, United States, in the New River Valley near the Blue Ridge Mountains. It is dominated by Virginia Tech, whose students make up a large share of the town's population of roughly 45,000.

History

The town was founded in 1798 on land donated by William Black and grew slowly as a small agricultural and trading centre. Its transformation began in 1872 with the founding of the Virginia Agricultural and Mechanical College, today's Virginia Tech, now Virginia's largest university by enrolment. The town grew around the campus through the twentieth century, developing a notable technology sector anchored by the Virginia Tech Corporate Research Center.

Virginia Tech shooting

On 16 April 2007, Blacksburg was the site of the Virginia Tech shooting, in which a student gunman killed 32 people and wounded 17 others before taking his own life; it remains one of the deadliest mass shootings in modern American history and prompted national debates about gun policy and campus security.

Life in Blacksburg

The town is consistently ranked among the best college towns and places to live in the United States, with a walkable downtown, the Huckleberry Trail for cyclists and pedestrians, and easy access to Appalachian Trail hiking and the New River. Its economy centres on education, research and spin-off technology companies.
