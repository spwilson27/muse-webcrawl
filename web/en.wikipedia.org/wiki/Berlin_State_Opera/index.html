The Berlin State Opera (German: Staatsoper Berlin), also known as the Staatsoper Unter den Linden, is a listed building on Unter den Linden in the Mitte district of Berlin and one of Germany's leading opera companies. Founded by Frederick the Great as the Royal Court Opera, it opened on 7 December 1742 with Carl Heinrich Graun's Cleopatra e Cesare; it is the oldest of Berlin's three opera houses and one of the oldest opera companies in the world.

History

The house, designed by Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff, burned down in 1843 and was rebuilt under Carl Ferdinand Langhans. Premieres there included Weber's Der Freischütz (at the Schauspielhaus company, 1821) and, at the State Opera itself, Alban Berg's Wozzeck in 1925 under Erich Kleiber. The building was destroyed twice by Allied bombing in the Second World War and rebuilt in the 1950s in East Berlin in a restored classical style. Conductors associated with the house include Richard Strauss, Otto Klemperer, and Herbert von Karajan.

Since reunification

Since 1992 the general music director has been Daniel Barenboim, who also leads the Staatskapelle Berlin, the opera's orchestra (founded 1570 as the electoral court orchestra). After an extensive renovation, the house reopened in December 2017; during the works the company performed at the Schiller Theater. In 2023 Barenboim stepped down for health reasons and Christian Thielemann was announced as his successor from 2024.
