The Gulf of Kutch (also spelled Gulf of Kachchh) is an inlet of the Arabian Sea along the west coast of India, in the state of Gujarat. It separates the Kutch region to the north from the Kathiawar (Saurashtra) peninsula to the south. The gulf is about 180 kilometres long and varies in width from roughly 16 to 65 kilometres. It opens towards the Gulf of Khambhat.

Geography

The gulf has one of the highest tidal ranges in India, reaching up to 11 metres, which drives strong tidal currents. The southern shore near Jamnagar features extensive coral reefs, mudflats, and mangrove forests, while the northern shore is characterised by sandy beaches and tidal creeks. The Marine National Park, established in 1982 in the Jamnagar district, was India's first marine national park and protects 42 islands and their coral ecosystems.

Ports and industry

Major ports on the gulf include Kandla (Deendayal Port), one of India's busiest cargo ports, Mundra, Okha, Bedi, and Salaya. The gulf hosts major oil refineries, including the Jamnagar Refinery, the largest petroleum refinery in the world, and the Vadinar refinery. Single point moorings in the gulf handle a large share of India's crude oil imports.

Ecology

The gulf supports coral reefs, seagrass beds, mangroves, and mudflats that provide habitat for marine species including dugongs, dolphins, sea turtles, and numerous bird species. Industrial development and oil handling pose continuing pollution risks to this ecosystem.
