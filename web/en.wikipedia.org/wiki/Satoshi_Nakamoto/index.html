Satoshi Nakamoto is the pseudonym used by the unknown person or group who created Bitcoin, authored its white paper, and built the first blockchain database. Nakamoto was active in developing Bitcoin from its announcement in 2008 until gradually withdrawing from the project in 2010–2011, handing control to other developers. Despite extensive journalistic investigation, the identity behind the pseudonym has never been conclusively established.

Nakamoto communicated only electronically and never revealed personal information.

Creation of Bitcoin

On 31 October 2008, Nakamoto published the white paper "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" to a cryptography mailing list, proposing a decentralised digital currency whose transactions would be secured by proof-of-work and recorded in a public chain of blocks, solving the double-spending problem without a trusted third party. The Bitcoin network launched on 3 January 2009 with the mining of the genesis block, into which Nakamoto embedded a newspaper headline about bank bailouts. Nakamoto collaborated with early developers, including Hal Finney — the recipient of the first Bitcoin transaction — before stepping back in 2011.

Identity

Numerous people have been proposed or have claimed to be Nakamoto, including Nick Szabo, Dorian Nakamoto (the subject of a 2014 Newsweek cover story, which he denied), Hal Finney, and Craig Wright, whose claim was rejected by UK courts in 2024. Approximately one million bitcoins mined in the network's first year, attributed to Nakamoto, have never moved, which would make the inventor one of the wealthiest people in the world if the keys were ever used.
