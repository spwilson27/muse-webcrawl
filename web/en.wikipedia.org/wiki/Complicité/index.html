Complicité is a British theatre company based in London, founded in 1983 by Simon McBurney, Annabel Arden, Fiona Gordon and Marcello Magni. Its original name was Théâtre de Complicité. The company is known for its devised and physical theatre work, which integrates text, design, music, and film-like visual storytelling, and tours its productions internationally. The word "complicité" is French for a kind of complicity or unspoken understanding between performers.

History and style

The company members trained in the tradition of Jacques Lecoq in Paris, and their early work drew on mime, clowning, and ensemble improvisation. Productions are created collaboratively through research and improvisation rather than from a fixed script, and are characterised by inventive staging, ensemble playing, and the use of objects and bodies to create images and landscapes.

Notable productions

  • The Street of Crocodiles (1992), adapted from the stories of Bruno Schulz.
  • The Three Lives of Lucie Cabrol (1994), adapted from John Berger.
  • Mnemonic (1999), exploring memory and the story of Ötzi the Iceman.
  • The Elephant Vanishes (2003), adapted from the short stories of Haruki Murakami, co-produced with the Setagaya Public Theatre, Tokyo.
  • A Disappearing Number (2007), about the mathematicians G. H. Hardy and Srinivasa Ramanujan.
  • The Master and Margarita (2011), adapted from Bulgakov's novel.
  • The Encounter (2015), based on Petru Popescu's Amazon Beaming, notable for its binaural sound design delivered to the audience through headphones; it transferred to Broadway in 2016.
  • Beware of Pity (2017), adapted from Stefan Zweig, co-produced with the Schaubühne, Berlin.

Recognition

Complicité has won numerous awards including Olivier Awards, Tony Awards, and international critics' prizes, and is regarded as one of the most influential British theatre companies of its generation. Simon McBurney was also co-founder of the company and has worked extensively as an actor in film and television alongside directing opera and theatre.
