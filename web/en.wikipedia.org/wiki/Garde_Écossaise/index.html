Garde Écossaise


Archers of the Garde Écossaise in the service of the King of France.

The Garde Écossaise (French for "Scots Guard") was an elite Scottish military unit founded in 1418 by the future Charles VII of France to aid in fighting the English during the Hundred Years' War. The unit was an expression of the Auld Alliance between France and Scotland. The Garde Écossaise served as the personal bodyguard of the French king and endured until 1830, making it one of the longest-serving royal guard units in European history.

History

Scottish soldiers had fought for France from the start of the Hundred Years' War. After the Scottish victory alongside the French at the Battle of Baugé in 1421, Charles formalised the guard. The Garde's premier company, the archers of the guard, held the privileged position of guarding the king's person, carrying the royal standards at coronations and standing watch at the Louvre. Scottish officers of the guard enjoyed high rank at court, and recruits continued to be drawn from Scottish families, though Frenchmen were increasingly admitted over the centuries.

Dissolution

The guard survived the French Revolution as part of the Maison militaire du Roi, was restored under the Bourbons, and was finally disbanded in 1830 following the July Revolution. Its ceremonial role left echoes in Anglo-French military tradition, including links with the Royal Company of Archers in Scotland.
