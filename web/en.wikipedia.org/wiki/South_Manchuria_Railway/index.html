The South Manchuria Railway (Japanese: 南満洲鉄道, Mantetsu) was a Japanese state-owned railway company operating in Manchuria, north-east China, from 1906 until 1945. Established after Japan's victory in the Russo-Japanese War, under the Treaty of Portsmouth Japan acquired Russia's leasehold and railway rights in southern Manchuria, including the line from Changchun to the ice-free port of Dalian (Dairen) and the Liaodong Peninsula.

Role in Manchuria

The company was far more than a railway: it administered the railway zone, ran coal mines (notably at Fushun), steelworks at Anshan, ports, schools, hospitals and public utilities, and maintained a famous research department that produced extensive intelligence on China and the Soviet Union. Its zone was guarded by the Kwantung Army, the Japanese force whose staged explosion on the line near Mukden on 18 September 1931 — the Mukden Incident — provided the pretext for the Japanese invasion of Manchuria and the creation of the puppet state of Manchukuo.

Operations

The railway's flagship service was the Asia Express, a streamlined luxury train introduced in 1934 running between Dalian and the Manchukuo capital of Hsinking (Changchun), among the fastest trains in Asia at the time. The network, originally built to Russian broad gauge, was converted to standard gauge under Japanese operation and expanded to roughly 1,100 km of main line.

Dissolution

Following Japan's defeat in 1945, the railway's assets were seized — first by the Soviet Union as war reparations and later transferred to the People's Republic of China. The company was formally dissolved under the Allied occupation of Japan.
