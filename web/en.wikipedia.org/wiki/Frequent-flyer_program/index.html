Frequent-flyer program

A frequent-flyer program (FFP) is a loyalty program offered by an airline. Members accumulate points — commonly called miles — based on the distance flown, fare paid, or amount spent, and redeem them for award flights, upgrades, and other benefits. Many programs also award elite status tiers carrying perks such as lounge access, priority boarding, and extra baggage allowance.

History

Texas International Airlines created what is generally regarded as the first mileage-based program in 1979. American Airlines launched AAdvantage on 1 May 1981, quickly followed by United's Mileage Plus and similar schemes worldwide. After airline deregulation in the United States, the programs became central marketing tools and were extended through airline alliances (Star Alliance, SkyTeam, Oneworld) and partnerships with hotels, car rental firms, retailers, and especially co-branded credit cards.

How programs work

Members earn miles or points for flights and partner transactions; many large programs have shifted from distance-based to revenue-based earning. Accumulated miles can be redeemed for flights subject to availability and dynamic pricing, cabin upgrades, merchandise, or experiences. Miles may expire after periods of inactivity. Elite (status) levels are earned by meeting annual flight or spending thresholds.

Economic significance

Loyalty programs have become major profit centres: airlines sell huge volumes of miles to banks issuing co-branded credit cards, and during the COVID-19 pandemic several carriers raised billions of dollars in financing secured by their programs, whose valuations in some cases exceeded those of the airlines themselves. Critics note that programs can encourage additional flying with associated carbon emissions, and that devaluations of miles are common.
