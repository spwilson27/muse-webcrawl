Battle of Goose Green

The Battle of Goose Green (28–29 May 1982) was an engagement of the Falklands War between British and Argentine forces on East Falkland. British paratroopers of 2 PARA (2nd Battalion, Parachute Regiment) attacked the Argentine garrison holding the settlements of Darwin and Goose Green. It was the first — and longest — land battle of the war.

Goose Green

Background

After the British landing at San Carlos on 21 May 1982, the Argentine garrison at Goose Green — with an airstrip (BAM Cóndor) and a force of over a thousand men — threatened the flank of the advance on Stanley. 2 PARA, under Lieutenant-Colonel Herbert "H" Jones, was ordered to attack, partly also to provide a visible land victory.

The battle

The attack began on the night of 27–28 May, advancing down the narrow isthmus against entrenched positions on Darwin Hill. Progress was slow in daylight against trenches, machine guns, anti-aircraft guns used in the ground role, and Pucará air attacks. Jones was killed leading an attack on an Argentine trench and was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross. Command passed to Major Chris Keeble, who reorganised the battalion and resumed the attack; by the morning of 29 May the Argentine position was untenable and Lieutenant-Colonel Ítalo Piaggi surrendered some 960 men.

Aftermath

The victory boosted British morale and removed the threat to the San Carlos beachhead's flank. The 114 Falkland Islander civilians who had been confined in the Goose Green community hall were freed. Jones's Victoria Cross and Sergeant Ian McKay's (won shortly after at Mount Longdon) became emblematic of the land campaign.
