Overseas France

Overseas France (French: France d'outre-mer) consists of the French-administered territories outside Europe, which form an integral, if legally varied, part of the French Republic. Together they are home to roughly 2.8 million people, use French-standard institutions in varying measure, and give France the second-largest exclusive economic zone in the world (over 11 million km² of maritime territory), as well as a presence on every continent and in every ocean.

Administrative categories

Overseas France is not a single legal status. Its territories fall into several categories:

Overseas departments

The five overseas departments are fully integrated into France: they elect deputies and senators to the French Parliament, are governed by the same civil and criminal law (with limited local adaptations), use the euro, and are outermost regions of the European Union. Guadeloupe and Martinique lie in the Caribbean; French Guiana, the largest department by area, is on the north-eastern coast of South America and hosts the Guiana Space Centre at Kourou, Europe's principal spaceport; Réunion lies in the Indian Ocean east of Madagascar; and Mayotte, in the Mozambique Channel, became the 101st department in 2011 following a referendum, despite a competing claim by the Comoros.

Collectivities and New Caledonia

The overseas collectivities have their own statutes of autonomy to varying degrees. French Polynesia, a vast archipelago in the South Pacific centred on Tahiti, has an assembly and its own president, while France retains defence, foreign affairs and currency. Saint Martin shares its Caribbean island with the Dutch Sint Maarten; Saint Barthélemy is a byword for luxury tourism; Saint Pierre and Miquelon, off Newfoundland, are the last remnant of France's once-vast North American empire; and Wallis and Futuna in the Pacific combine republican administration with three traditional kingdoms. New Caledonia, in the south-west Pacific, enjoys a unique status under the 1998 Nouméa Accord, with substantial powers transferred locally and nickel mining central to its economy; three referendums (2018, 2020, 2021) each returned a majority against independence, though the legitimacy of the 2021 vote is contested by Kanak pro-independence parties.

History

Overseas France is the remnant of France's two colonial empires. Most of the mainland empire was lost through the decolonisation wave after the Second World War, but certain territories — notably the "old colonies" of the Caribbean and Réunion, departmentalised in 1946 — chose (or were kept in) integration with France rather than independence. Subsequent reforms, including the constitutional revision of 2003, regularised the patchwork of statuses.

Significance

The overseas territories give France strategic depth: military bases and pre-positioned forces, control of key shipping routes, space launch facilities, vast fishing grounds, and undersea mineral resources. Their residents are French and European citizens, though unemployment and costs of living are typically much higher than in metropolitan France, and movements for greater autonomy or independence remain politically significant in several territories.
