Potentially hazardous object


Labelled image placeholder: plot of known potentially hazardous asteroid orbits

A potentially hazardous object (PHO) is a near-Earth object – either an asteroid or a comet – with an orbit that can make close approaches to the Earth and that is large enough to cause significant regional damage in the event of impact. Most PHOs are asteroids and are termed potentially hazardous asteroids (PHAs).

Definition

NASA defines a potentially hazardous asteroid as one with a minimum orbit intersection distance (MOID) with Earth of 0.05 astronomical units or less, and an absolute magnitude of 22 or brighter, corresponding to a diameter of roughly 140 metres or more. Objects meeting these criteria are considered capable of causing unprecedented regional devastation if they struck the Earth.

Numbers

As of the mid-2020s, more than 2,300 potentially hazardous asteroids are known, out of a total near-Earth asteroid population of over 30,000. Surveys such as Pan-STARRS, the Catalina Sky Survey, and ATLAS are estimated to have found the large majority of PHAs larger than one kilometre, while completeness at the 140-metre threshold remains lower. The largest known PHA is (53319) 1999 JM8, several kilometres across.

Risk assessment

No known PHO is on a collision course with Earth for the next century. Close approaches are monitored by NASA's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies, and impact probabilities are expressed on the Torino scale and the technical Palermo scale. The asteroid 99942 Apophis briefly received a Torino rating of 4 in 2004 before further observations ruled out an impact in 2029.
