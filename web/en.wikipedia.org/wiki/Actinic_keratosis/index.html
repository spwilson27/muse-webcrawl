Actinic keratosis (AK), also called solar keratosis, is a pre-cancerous area of thick, scaly, or crusty skin. These growths are more common in fair-skinned people and those who are frequently exposed to the sun. They usually appear on sun-exposed areas such as the face, ears, scalp, neck, forearms and hands.

[Image: Actinic keratosis on sun-exposed skin]

Signs and symptoms

Actinic keratoses typically present as rough, dry, scaly patches or papules, often felt more easily than seen — likened to touching sandpaper. They range in colour from skin-toned to reddish or brown, and may occasionally itch or burn. A variant, the cutaneous horn, presents as a small horn-like projection.

Cause and progression

AKs result from cumulative damage to keratinocytes by ultraviolet radiation, which causes mutations (notably in the TP53 gene). They are regarded as precancerous because a small proportion progress to squamous cell carcinoma of the skin; estimates of the annual risk per lesion vary, but the risk is the main reason treatment is recommended.

Treatment

Options include cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen, curettage, topical treatments such as fluorouracil, imiquimod or diclofenac gel, chemical peels, and photodynamic therapy. Sun protection reduces the development of new lesions.
