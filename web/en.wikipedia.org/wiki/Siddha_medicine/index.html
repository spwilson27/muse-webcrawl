Siddha medicine

Siddha medicine is a traditional system of healing that originated in South India and is chiefly practised in Tamil Nadu. It is one of the oldest medical systems in India, traditionally attributed to the Siddhars — saintly physicians and alchemists, of whom Agastya is considered the first. The Siddha system is one of the AYUSH systems officially recognised by the Government of India.

Principles

Siddha physiology holds that the human body is composed of the five elements (pancha bhoota) and governed by three humours — vatham (air), pitham (fire/bile), and kapham (water/phlegm) — whose derangement produces disease. Diagnosis traditionally relies on the eightfold examination (envagai thervu), especially pulse reading (naadi), examination of urine, eyes, tongue, complexion, voice, and stool.

Treatments

Siddha pharmaceutics is distinctive for its extensive use of minerals and metals, including preparations of mercury, sulphur, gold, and arsenic compounds, processed through elaborate purification and calcination into powders (parpam, chenduram) alongside herbal juices, decoctions, and medicated oils. The tradition is also intertwined with varmam, the manipulation of vital points, and with alchemical practices aimed at longevity (kayakalpa).

Regulation and research

In modern India, Siddha practice is regulated by the National Commission for Indian System of Medicine; undergraduate training leads to the BSMS (Bachelor of Siddha Medicine and Surgery) degree. The Central Council for Research in Siddha (CCRS), headquartered in Chennai, conducts scientific research. Critics note that some Siddha mineral preparations can contain toxic heavy metals if improperly prepared, and emphasise the need for standardisation and pharmacovigilance; supporters point to a long recorded history of use and growing pharmacological study.
