Rice University

William Marsh Rice University, commonly known as Rice University, is a private research university in Houston, Texas, United States. It sits on a 300-acre campus adjacent to the Texas Medical Center and is noted for its strong programs in science, engineering, architecture, music, and business.

History

The university was founded in 1912 with an endowment from the estate of businessman William Marsh Rice, whose murder in 1900 by his valet — at the instigation of his lawyer — nearly diverted the fortune. Albert B. Greene, the lawyer, was convicted, and the endowment was secured for the institute. Edgar Odell Lovett was the founding president.

Campus and student life

Rice operates a residential college system modelled on those of Oxford and Cambridge, with eleven colleges (including Baker, Wiess, Hanszen, and Lovett) that serve as students' social and residential communities for all four years. Enrollment is roughly 8,500 students, with a notably low student-to-faculty ratio.

Research

Rice researchers Richard Smalley and Robert Curl shared the 1996 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of fullerenes (buckminsterfullerene) at Rice. The university is also associated with the Shepherd School of Music and the Baker Institute for Public Policy.
