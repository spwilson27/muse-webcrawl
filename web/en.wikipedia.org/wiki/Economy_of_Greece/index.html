The economy of Greece is the economy of a developed country located in Southeast Europe. Greece is a member of the European Union and of the eurozone, having adopted the euro in 2001. Its economy is dominated by the service sector, which accounts for roughly four-fifths of output, with tourism and merchant shipping among the most important industries.

Greece entered a severe sovereign debt crisis in 2009, when a large budget deficit was revealed and investor confidence collapsed. A series of bailout programmes from the European Union, the European Central Bank and the International Monetary Fund were accompanied by austerity measures, and the economy contracted by more than a quarter. Greece exited its final bailout programme in August 2018 and has since returned to growth, though public debt remains among the highest in the world relative to GDP.

History

After the Second World War, Greece experienced rapid growth, sometimes called the Greek economic miracle, with annual growth rates averaging around 7 per cent between 1950 and 1973. The country joined the European Economic Community in 1981. Growth slowed in the 1980s and 1990s, and public debt rose steadily. Entry into the euro in 2001 gave Greece access to cheap borrowing, fuelling a consumption and construction boom that ended abruptly with the global financial crisis and the revelation in 2009 that the budget deficit was far larger than previously reported.

Sectors

Tourism

Tourism is a cornerstone of the Greek economy, contributing a substantial share of GDP and employment. Greece regularly receives more than 30 million visitors a year, drawn by its islands, beaches, archaeological sites and climate.

Shipping

Greek shipowners control the largest merchant fleet in the world by deadweight tonnage. Shipping earnings are a major source of foreign exchange, although much of the fleet is registered under foreign flags.

Agriculture

Agriculture employs a significant share of the workforce. Greece is a major producer of olive oil, cotton, tobacco, fruits and vegetables, and is noted for products such as feta cheese and Greek wine.

Debt crisis

The Greek government-debt crisis began in late 2009. Three rescue packages followed in 2010, 2012 and 2015, totalling more than €280 billion in loans. Private creditors accepted a large write-down of Greek bonds in 2012. Unemployment peaked above 27 per cent in 2013. In 2015 Greece held a referendum rejecting creditors' terms, but the government subsequently accepted a third programme with further conditions.
