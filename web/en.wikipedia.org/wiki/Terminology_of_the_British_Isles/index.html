Terminology of the British Isles

The terminology of the British Isles refers to the words and concepts used to describe the various political and geographical entities within the geographical grouping known as the British Isles, and the conflicts and sensitivities surrounding some of these terms. The naming dispute centres on the archipelago off the north-west coast of continental Europe, comprising the islands of Great Britain, Ireland, and over 6,000 smaller islands.

Key terms

  • British Isles — the geographical name for the archipelago. The term is not officially recognised by the government of Ireland, and "Britain and Ireland" or the "Atlantic Archipelago" are sometimes used as alternatives.
  • Great Britain — the largest island, containing England, Scotland and Wales. Geographically it excludes surrounding smaller islands; politically, "Great Britain" may include them.
  • United Kingdom — the sovereign state, formally the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, comprising Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
  • Ireland — both the island and the sovereign state (the Republic of Ireland) occupying most of it; the state is officially named "Ireland", with "Republic of Ireland" as a description.
  • British Islands — a legal term in UK law for the UK, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man collectively.

Sensitivities

Many people in Ireland regard "British Isles" as implying British ownership or identity over Ireland and dislike its use; the Irish government does not use the term and discourages its use in official contexts. In the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, the phrase "these islands" was used instead. Within the UK, similar care surrounds terms such as "Ulster" (geographically nine counties, of which six are in Northern Ireland), "the mainland", and "Éire".
