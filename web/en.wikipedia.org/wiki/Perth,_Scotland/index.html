Perth, Scotland

Perth (Scottish Gaelic: Peairt) is a centrally located city in Scotland, on the banks of the River Tay. It is the administrative centre of Perth and Kinross council area and was historically the county town of Perthshire.

History

Perth, the "Fair City" of Sir Walter Scott's The Fair Maid of Perth, was a royal burgh and effectively a capital of Scotland, with kings crowned at nearby Scone Abbey on the Stone of Scone. It was a major medieval port and market. The city suffered in the Wars of Independence, hosted the Battle of the Clans in 1396, and was occupied by Jacobites in 1715 and 1745.

Modern city

A centre for insurance (General Accident / Aviva), whisky and retail, Perth regained city status in 2012 for the Diamond Jubilee. Landmarks include St John's Kirk, Perth Museum — home of the Stone of Destiny returned in 2024 — Scone Palace and Kinnoull Hill.
