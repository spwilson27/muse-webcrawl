One thousand origami cranes (Japanese: senbazuru, "thousand cranes") is a traditional Japanese practice of folding a thousand paper cranes, often strung together on threads. According to legend, a person who folds a thousand cranes within a year will be granted a wish by the gods, or granted happiness, health, and recovery from illness.

Tradition

The crane in Japanese culture symbolizes longevity and good fortune. Strings of a thousand cranes are given as wedding gifts wishing the couple a thousand years of happiness, to newborns for a long life, and to the sick for recovery. The earliest known book on origami, the Sembazuru Orikata ("How to Fold a Thousand Cranes"), was published in 1797.

Sadako Sasaki

The practice became a worldwide symbol of peace through the story of Sadako Sasaki, a Hiroshima schoolgirl who developed leukemia after the atomic bombing and folded cranes hoping to recover. A statue of Sadako holding a crane stands in Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park, where visitors leave millions of paper cranes each year.
