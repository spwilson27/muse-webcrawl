Sukhoi

Sukhoi (Russian: Сухой, IPA: [sʊ′xoj]) is a Russian aircraft manufacturer headquartered in Moscow. Founded in the Soviet Union by Pavel Sukhoi in 1939 as the Sukhoi Design Bureau (OKB-51), it designs both military and civil aircraft. Since the 2000s the company has been part of the United Aircraft Corporation, a holding company that consolidated most of Russia's aviation industry. Sukhoi's combat aircraft, known by the "Su" prefix, form the backbone of the Russian Air Force's fighter fleet and have been widely exported.

History

Pavel Sukhoi formed a design team within the Tupolev bureau in the 1930s, producing the ANT-25's contemporaries and the Su-2 light bomber, which served in the Second World War. The independent Sukhoi OKB was dissolved by Stalin in 1949 but re-formed in 1953 after his death, going on to produce the Su-7 and Su-9/Su-11/Su-15 interceptor families during the Cold War.

The Flanker family

The fourth-generation Su-27 (NATO reporting name "Flanker"), first flown in 1977 and entering service in 1985, made Sukhoi's international reputation as a maker of large, highly manoeuvrable air-superiority fighters. It spawned a large family of derivatives, including the Su-30 multirole two-seater, the carrier-based Su-33, the Su-34 strike fighter, the Su-35 "4++ generation" fighter, and licensed and indigenous developments in China (the Shenyang J-11/J-15/J-16 series) and India (the HAL-built Su-30MKI).

Recent programmes

The Sukhoi Su-57 (Felon), first flown in 2010, is Russia's first production stealth fighter, developed under the PAK FA programme. In the civil market the company produced the Sukhoi Superjet 100 regional airliner, which entered service in 2011 as the first post-Soviet Russian airliner, marketed through Sukhoi Civil Aircraft. Sukhoi has also developed the Su-75 Checkmate light stealth fighter concept, unveiled in 2021.
