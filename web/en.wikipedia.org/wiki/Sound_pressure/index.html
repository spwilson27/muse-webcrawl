Sound pressure

Sound pressure or acoustic pressure is the local pressure deviation from the ambient (average) atmospheric pressure, caused by a sound wave. In air, sound pressure can be measured using a microphone, and in water with a hydrophone. The SI unit of sound pressure is the pascal (Pa).

Sound pressure level

Because the range of audible sound pressures is enormous — from about 20 micropascals at the threshold of hearing to pressures millions of times larger — sound pressure is usually expressed as a sound pressure level (SPL) in decibels (symbol: dB SPL), relative to the reference pressure of 20 μPa in air:

Lp = 20 log10(p / p0) dB, where p0 = 20 μPa.

Relation to other quantities

Sound pressure is related to particle velocity through the acoustic impedance of the medium; in a plane wave, p = Z·v, where Z ≈ 413 Pa·s/m for air at standard conditions. Sound intensity is the product of pressure and particle velocity. Because pressure is a scalar field quantity measurable at a point, it is the quantity most commonly used to characterize sound fields.

Distance law

For a point source in free field, sound pressure decreases inversely with distance (the inverse-distance law), so the level drops by 6 dB for each doubling of distance.
