Jökulhlaup

A jökulhlaup is a type of glacial outburst flood, a sudden and often catastrophic release of water from a glacier or an ice-dammed or subglacial lake. The term comes from Icelandic: jökull means glacier and hlaup means leap or burst. Jökulhlaups are most closely associated with Iceland, where geothermal activity beneath ice caps such as Vatnajökull periodically melts ice and produces enormous floods.

Causes

Jökulhlaups can be triggered by subglacial volcanic eruptions, by the drainage of ice-dammed lakes when rising water floats the ice barrier, or by geothermal heating that melts ice from below. The 1996 eruption beneath Vatnajökull produced a jökulhlaup on the Skeiðarársandur plain with a peak discharge estimated at 45,000–50,000 cubic metres per second, among the largest floods ever measured.

Hazards

These floods can destroy bridges, roads, and farmland within hours. In Iceland, flood warning systems monitor subglacial lakes such as Grímsvötn, and roads across the southern sandur plains are closed when a hlaup is detected. Similar outburst floods occur in Alaska, the Himalayas, and other glaciated regions.
