Winton Formation

The Winton Formation is a Cretaceous geological formation in central-western Queensland, Australia, near the town of Winton. Deposited about 100–95 million years ago (late Albian to early Turonian) in rivers and floodplains bordering the inland Eromanga Sea, it is one of Australia's most important sources of dinosaur fossils.

Palaeontology

Dinosaurs described from the formation include the giant titanosaur sauropods Diamantinasaurus matildae and Wintonotitan wattsi, the huge Australovenator wintonensis — a megaraptorid theropod nicknamed "Banjo" — and the sauropod Savannasaurus elliottorum. Many specimens were found by the Elliott family and are displayed at the Australian Age of Dinosaurs museum near Winton.

The formation also yields lungfish, turtles, crocodilians, bivalves and abundant plant fossils, indicating a lush, well-watered floodplain environment.

Geology

The unit consists mainly of sandstone, siltstone and mudstone of the Rolling Downs Group. It is famous too for opalised fossils occurring farther south in related deposits, and for dinosaur trackways such as those preserved at Lark Quarry, interpreted as a stampede site.
