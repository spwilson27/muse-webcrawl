Brown rice

Brown rice
TypeWhole grain rice
ProcessingHull (husk) removed; bran and germ retained

Brown rice is a whole grain rice with the inedible outer hull removed. This is as compared with white rice, which is the same grain further milled to remove both the husk, bran and cereal germ. Red rice, gold rice, and black rice (also called purple rice) are all types of rice that are pigmented in the outer layer and eaten unmilled, like brown rice.

Nutrition

Brown rice retains the bran and germ removed in white rice, and therefore contains substantially more dietary fiber, magnesium, manganese and B vitamins, together with small amounts of lipids concentrated in the bran. Milling rice to white removes most of the thiamine, and diets based almost entirely on polished white rice historically caused beriberi. Brown rice has a moderately low glycaemic index and a nuttier flavour and chewier texture than white rice; it also takes longer to cook, because water penetrates the bran layer slowly.

Storage

Because the bran contains oils that can turn rancid, brown rice has a shorter shelf life (about six months at room temperature) than white rice, and is often stored refrigerated or vacuum-packed. Germinated brown rice (GABA rice) is produced by soaking the grain in warm water to improve texture and increase gamma-aminobutyric acid content.
