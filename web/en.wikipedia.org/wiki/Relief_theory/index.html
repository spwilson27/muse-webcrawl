Relief theory

Relief theory is one of the classical theories of humour, alongside superiority theory and incongruity theory. It proposes that laughter results from the release of built-up nervous energy or psychological tension: humorous situations allow people to vent pent-up feelings — such as anxiety, embarrassment or taboo impulses — in a safe and socially acceptable form.

Proponents

Elements of the theory appear in Lord Shaftesbury's 1709 essay on the freedom of wit and humour. Herbert Spencer developed a physiological version in "On the Physiology of Laughter" (1860), and Sigmund Freud elaborated it in Jokes and Their Relation to the Unconscious (1905), arguing that jokes economise on the psychic energy normally used to repress forbidden thoughts. Modern critics note that relief may accompany humour without explaining why particular things are found funny.
