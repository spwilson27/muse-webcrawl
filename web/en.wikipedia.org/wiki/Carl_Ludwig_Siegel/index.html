Carl Ludwig Siegel

Carl Ludwig Siegel (31 December 1896 – 4 April 1981) was a German mathematician specialising in analytic number theory. He is known for fundamental results on Diophantine approximation, transcendence, and the theory of Siegel modular forms.

Carl Ludwig Siegel
Born: 1896, Berlin, Germany
Died: 1981, Göttingen
Fields: Number theory
Awards: Wolf Prize (1978)

Career

Siegel studied at Berlin and Göttingen and became a professor at Frankfurt in 1922 and Göttingen in 1938. Opposed to the Nazi regime, he emigrated to the United States in 1940, working at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, before returning to Göttingen in 1951.

Mathematics

Siegel proved the finiteness of integral points on elliptic curves (Siegel's theorem), advanced Thue–Siegel–Roth results on Diophantine approximation, and contributed to transcendental number theory, celestial mechanics, and several complex variables. He shared the first Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1978 with Israel Gelfand.
