Proton affinity

Definition: Negative enthalpy change for gas-phase protonation
Units: kJ/mol
Water: 691 kJ/mol
Ammonia: 854 kJ/mol

The proton affinity (PA) of a species is the negative of the enthalpy change for the gas-phase reaction in which it accepts a proton: A− + H+ → HA, or for a neutral base B + H+ → BH+. It measures the intrinsic basicity of the species in the absence of solvent.

Measurement and trends

Proton affinities are determined by mass-spectrometric equilibrium and bracketing experiments and compiled in evaluated databases. Values increase with the electron-donating character of substituents: ammonia (854 kJ/mol) exceeds water (691 kJ/mol), and amines exceed ammonia. Strong "proton sponge" bases exceed 1000 kJ/mol.

Uses

Proton affinities govern ion chemistry in mass spectrometry (including chemical ionization and proton-transfer-reaction MS), interstellar cloud chemistry, and the rationalization of gas-phase acidity and basicity scales, complementing solution-phase pKa values.
