LOINC (Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes) is a database and universal standard for identifying medical laboratory observations and other clinical measurements. It was created in 1994 by the Regenstrief Institute, a US nonprofit medical research organisation, in response to the demand for a common way to exchange laboratory and clinical results electronically between hospitals, laboratories and health information systems.

Structure

Each LOINC term has a unique numeric code with a check digit, written in the form xxxxx-x (for example, 2339-0 for glucose in blood). A fully specified name is built from six axes: the component or analyte measured, the property (such as mass concentration), the time aspect, the specimen system (such as blood or urine), the scale (quantitative, ordinal or nominal), and, where relevant, the method. The database contains on the order of 100,000 terms covering laboratory tests, vital signs, clinical documents, survey instruments and other observations.

Use

LOINC is the standard vocabulary for laboratory results in HL7 and FHIR messages and is required or recommended in national health IT regulations in the United States and many other countries. It complements SNOMED CT, which identifies clinical findings and procedures, and UCUM, used for units of measure. The Regenstrief Institute publishes new releases twice a year and provides the RELMA mapping tool to help laboratories assign codes.
