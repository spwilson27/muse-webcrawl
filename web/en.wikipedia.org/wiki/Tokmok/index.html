Tokmok (Kyrgyz: Токмок, "hammer") is a city in the Chuy Valley of northern Kyrgyzstan, east of the capital Bishkek. It is a city of regional significance within Chuy Region, although it is not the region's administrative capital. Tokmok sits on the south bank of the Chu River, which here forms part of the border with Kazakhstan.

History

The area was an important stop on the northern Silk Road. About 15 km south-west of the modern city lie the ruins of Ak-Beshim, identified with Suyab, a major Sogdian and later Turkic trading city and a capital of the Western Turkic Khaganate; nearby stands the Burana Tower, the minaret of the medieval city of Balasagun. A later settlement near the site flourished before the Mongol invasions devastated the valley.

The modern town originated as a northern military outpost of the Khanate of Kokand, built around 1825. Russian forces under Colonel Mikhail Chernyayev captured the fortress in 1864, and during the Soviet period Tokmok grew into an industrial city with glass, food-processing, and wool plants, and a significant Russian, Ukrainian, and Dungan population. After Kyrgyz independence in 1991, many industries declined and the population fell from its Soviet-era peak.

Geography and economy

Tokmok lies in the irrigated agricultural plain of the Chuy Valley at the foot of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too range, which rises to the south. The economy is based on food processing, glass production, services, and trade; the city is a rail and road junction on the route between Bishkek and Lake Issyk-Kul. The Burana Tower archaeological site is the region's principal tourist attraction.
