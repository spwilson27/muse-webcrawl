Miriam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Miriam (Hebrew: מִרְיָם, Miryām) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, described as a prophetess, the elder sister of Moses and Aaron, and the daughter of Amram and Jochebed of the tribe of Levi.

In the Bible

Miriam first appears in the Book of Exodus. Tradition identifies her with the unnamed sister who watches from a distance as the infant Moses is placed in a basket among the reeds of the Nile, and who arranges for their mother to nurse him for Pharaoh's daughter (Exodus 2). After the crossing of the Red Sea, Miriam leads the women in the victory song with timbrels and dancing: "Sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea" (Exodus 15:20–21) — among the oldest poetic passages in the Bible, and she is there explicitly called "the prophetess".

In Numbers 12, Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses over his Cushite wife and his unique prophetic authority; Miriam alone is struck with a scaly skin disease (tzaraath), often translated "leprosy", and is shut outside the camp for seven days after Aaron intercedes and Moses prays, "O God, please heal her." She dies and is buried at Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin (Numbers 20:1). The prophet Micah recalls her as one of the three leaders sent before Israel: Moses, Aaron and Miriam (Micah 6:4).

Legacy

Rabbinic tradition credits Miriam with a miraculous well that accompanied Israel in the wilderness, and the Passover seder has in modern times sometimes included a "Miriam's cup" honouring her. Her name, in its later forms (Mariam, Mary), became one of the most common female names in Jewish and Christian tradition.
