Khepri (Egyptian: ḫprj, also spelled Khepera, Kheper, Khepra or Chepri) is a scarab-faced god in ancient Egyptian religion who represents the rising or morning sun. By extension, he can also represent creation and the renewal of life, and is often associated with the scarab beetle, which was held sacred by the Egyptians. Khepri was principally worshipped at Heliopolis, the centre of the sun cult.

Etymology

The name Khepri is derived from the Egyptian verb ḫpr, meaning "to develop", "to come into being", or "to be created". The god's name therefore means roughly "he who is coming into being". This root also gives the Egyptian word for the scarab beetle, ḫprr, linking the god's identity to the insect that symbolised rebirth.

Role and symbolism

Khepri was understood as one of three aspects of the sun god across the day: Khepri at dawn, Ra at noon, and Atum at dusk. As the morning sun, Khepri was believed to roll the sun disk across the sky each day, just as the scarab beetle rolls a ball of dung along the ground. Egyptians observed the young scarabs emerging from these dung balls and concluded that the beetles were spontaneously generated — a symbol of rebirth and renewal. The scarab consequently became one of the most popular amulets in ancient Egypt.

Khepri is also associated with the blue lotus, a flower that opens each morning with the rising sun, further reinforcing his connection with daily renewal.

Depiction

Khepri is most often depicted as a scarab beetle, sometimes pushing the solar disk. In human form, he appears as a man with a scarab beetle for a head, or with a scarab above his head. He is rarely shown anthropomorphically, and most depictions of him use the beetle form, particularly in funerary contexts and on scarab seals.

In funerary religion

Because of his association with rebirth, Khepri figured in Egyptian funerary beliefs. Scarab amulets were placed over the heart of the deceased to aid their regeneration in the afterlife. In the Book of the Dead, spells invoke Khepri so that the dead might "come into being" again like the morning sun.
