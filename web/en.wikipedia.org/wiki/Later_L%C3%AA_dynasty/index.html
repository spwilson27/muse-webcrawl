Later Lê dynasty

The Later Lê dynasty (1428–1789) was the longest-ruling Vietnamese dynasty. It was founded by Lê Lợi, who led the Lam Sơn uprising that expelled the Ming Chinese and took the throne as Emperor Lê Thái Tổ in 1428, ruling the kingdom of Đại Việt.

The dynasty reached an early high point under Lê Thánh Tông (r. 1460–1497), who promulgated the Hồng Đức code, reformed administration and conquered Champa territory in 1471. After 1527 the dynasty was challenged by the Mạc dynasty, then restored (the "Lê Restoration", 1533) but with real power held by the Trịnh lords in the north and Nguyễn lords in the south, the emperors reigning largely as figureheads.

The Tây Sơn rebellion ended Lê rule in 1788–89; the last emperor, Lê Chiêu Thống, fled to China.
