Districts of India

A district (Hindi: zilā) is an administrative division of an Indian state or union territory. India has more than 780 districts (the count has risen steadily as states create new ones; there were 640 in the 2011 census). Districts are the principal unit of general administration, headed by a District Collector (also called District Magistrate or Deputy Commissioner), an officer of the Indian Administrative Service.

Administration

The District Collector combines revenue administration, executive magistracy, and coordination of development programmes. Police administration in the district is led by a Superintendent of Police. Most districts are subdivided into tehsils (talukas/mandals) and blocks, and contain hundreds of villages governed by gram panchayats, along with towns and cities run by municipalities.

Scale

Districts vary greatly: by population, the largest (such as Thane in Maharashtra, with over 11 million people in 2011) exceed many countries, while the smallest, such as Dibang Valley in Arunachal Pradesh, have fewer than 10,000 inhabitants. By area, Kutch in Gujarat is among the largest at over 45,000 km².

Formation

State governments may create, alter, or abolish districts by executive notification; the number has grown from 310 in 1951 to over 780, driven by population growth and demands for more accessible administration.
