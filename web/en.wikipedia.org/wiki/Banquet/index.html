Banquet

A banquet is a large formal meal or feast, usually attended by many guests and held to celebrate an occasion, honour a person or institution, or raise money for a cause. Banquets are typically served in multiple courses at long or round tables, with speeches, toasts, and entertainment forming part of the ceremony. The word entered English from French banquet, a diminutive of banc ("bench"), itself borrowed from Italian banchetto — a little bench or the small meal eaten upon one.

History

Feasting has been a political and social ritual since antiquity: the Greek symposium, the Roman convivium, the sacrificial feasts of ancient China, and the royal banquets of medieval Europe all used shared eating to display hierarchy, seal alliances, and reward followers. Renaissance courts codified elaborate service, and the banquet became a distinct course of sweetmeats and confections served separately — the origin of the modern dessert course. In imperial and modern China, the banquet, seated at round tables with dishes shared in the centre, remains the principal setting for business and official hospitality.

Modern forms

Today "banquet" covers wedding banquets, state banquets hosted by heads of state, corporate and charity dinners, awards ceremonies, and the banquet facilities of hotels that specialise in large catered functions. Seating plans, place cards, a head table, and a sequence of toasts remain characteristic in Western practice, while Chinese wedding and New Year banquets are distinguished by dishes chosen for auspicious symbolism, such as whole fish for abundance and longevity noodles.
