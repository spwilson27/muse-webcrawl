Rice noodle roll

Rice noodle roll
Alternative namesCheung fun, chee cheong fun
TypeDim sum
Place of originChina (Guangdong)
Main ingredientsRice noodle, filling (shrimp, beef, pork, or vegetables), sweetened soy sauce

A rice noodle roll (also translated as steamed rice roll, Cantonese: 腸粉, chèuhng fán, often called cheung fun) is a Cantonese dish originating from Guangdong Province in Southern China, commonly served either as a snack, a small meal, or as a variety of dim sum. It is a thin roll made from a wide strip of shahe fen (rice noodles), filled with shrimp, beef, pork, or vegetables, and served with a seasoned, sweetened soy sauce poured over it just before serving.

Preparation

The dish is made by spreading a thin layer of rice batter—rice flour mixed with water and small amounts of other starches such as tapioca or potato starch for elasticity—onto a cloth or perforated tray inside a steamer. The batter steams in well under a minute into a delicate sheet. The filling is scattered over the sheet, which is then rolled or folded around it with a scraper. The rolls are cut into sections, plated, and dressed with warm sweetened soy sauce, sometimes with sesame seeds or chopped scallions.

Varieties

  • Shrimp cheung fun (蝦腸粉) — whole or chopped prawns, often with chives.
  • Beef cheung fun (牛肉腸粉) — seasoned minced beef, sometimes with water chestnut and coriander.
  • Char siu cheung fun (叉燒腸粉) — barbecued pork.
  • Plain cheung fun — unfilled rolls, commonly eaten for breakfast with soy sauce, hoisin, sesame paste, and chili sauce, and sprinkled with toasted sesame seeds; a classic Hong Kong street food.
  • Zhaliang (炸兩) — a roll wrapped around youtiao (fried dough stick), giving a contrast of soft and crisp textures.
  • Vegetable cheung fun — mushrooms, lettuce, or other vegetables.

Regional versions

In Malaysia and Singapore, the dish is known as chee cheong fun (from the Cantonese name, literally "pig intestine noodle", a reference to the roll's shape) and is served with a dark sweet sauce, or in some regions a curry sauce, alongside a selection of yong tau foo items. In Vietnam, the related dish bánh cuốn is a steamed rice roll filled with minced pork and wood-ear mushrooms, served with fish sauce. A drier Chaozhou (Teochew) style omits heavy saucing and highlights the rice sheet itself.

In dim sum service

Cheung fun is a standard item in Cantonese tea houses and dim sum restaurants worldwide, where it is typically prepared to order in the kitchen or at specialised stations and brought to the table in bamboo steamers or on small plates, the soy sauce added at the last moment so the rolls stay hot and silky.
