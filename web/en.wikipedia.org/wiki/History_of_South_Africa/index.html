History of South Africa

The history of South Africa spans some of the earliest evidence of human habitation anywhere in the world through to the modern democratic republic. Southern Africa was home to San hunter-gatherers and Khoikhoi pastoralists for millennia, and Bantu-speaking farming peoples settled from the early centuries AD, founding states including Mapungubwe and Great Zimbabwe's regional sphere.

Colonisation

The Dutch East India Company founded a refreshment station at the Cape in 1652; the colony expanded with settler farming, slavery, and conflict with indigenous peoples. Britain seized the Cape during the Napoleonic Wars, and many Dutch-descended Boers trekked inland in the 1830s, founding republics. The discovery of diamonds (1867) and gold (1886) transformed the region and led to the Anglo-Boer Wars, after which the Union of South Africa was formed in 1910.

Apartheid and democracy

After 1948 the National Party government institutionalised racial segregation as apartheid, provoking decades of resistance led by the African National Congress. Nelson Mandela, released in 1990 after 27 years in prison, led negotiations that culminated in the first universal-franchise elections in 1994, when he became president of a democratic South Africa.
