Petit Palais

The Petit Palais ("small palace") is an art museum in the 8th arrondissement of Paris, France. Built for the 1900 Exposition Universelle ("universal exhibition") to the designs of architect Charles Girault, it now houses the City of Paris Museum of Fine Arts (Petit Palais, Musée des Beaux-Arts de la Ville de Paris). It stands on the Avenue Winston-Churchill, opposite its larger sibling the Grand Palais, near the Pont Alexandre III.

Architecture

The Beaux-Arts building is arranged around a semicircular courtyard garden, with an Ionic colonnade, a grand entrance porch under a dome, and rich sculptural decoration. Girault used innovative construction, including reinforced concrete concealed behind the stone façade. After the 1900 exhibition the building became a municipal museum, opening in 1902.[1]

Collections

The permanent collection, free to visit, spans antiquities, medieval and Renaissance objects, 17th- and 18th-century art, and 19th- and early 20th-century French painting and sculpture, with works by Rembrandt, Rubens, Monet, Renoir, Cézanne, Courbet, and Mary Cassatt, and Art Nouveau decorative art by René Lalique and others. Major donations include the Dutuit collection of antiquities and the Tuck collection of 18th-century decorative arts. The museum also hosts major temporary exhibitions.
