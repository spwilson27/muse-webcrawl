Ribonucleotide

A ribonucleotide is a nucleotide that contains the pentose sugar ribose. Ribonucleotides are the monomeric building blocks of ribonucleic acid (RNA), joined together by phosphodiester bonds between the 3' and 5' carbons of successive sugars.

Structure

Each ribonucleotide consists of a nitrogenous base (adenine, guanine, cytosine, or uracil), a ribose sugar, and one to three phosphate groups. The distinguishing feature of ribose, compared with the deoxyribose of DNA, is the hydroxyl group on the 2' carbon, which makes RNA more chemically reactive and less stable than DNA.

Examples and roles

The four ribonucleoside triphosphates — ATP, GTP, CTP, and UTP — serve as substrates for RNA synthesis. Beyond polymer formation, ribonucleotides such as ATP function as the cell's principal energy currency, while cyclic AMP acts as a second messenger in signal transduction. In cells, deoxyribonucleotides for DNA synthesis are produced by reduction of ribonucleotides by the enzyme ribonucleotide reductase.
