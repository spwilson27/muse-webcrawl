In Greek mythology, Amphitrite (Greek: Ἀμφιτρίτη) is a sea goddess and the queen of the sea as wife of Poseidon. She is usually counted among the Nereids, the fifty daughters of the sea god Nereus and the Oceanid Doris, though some sources make her an Oceanid herself.

Mythology

According to the most familiar story, Poseidon saw Amphitrite dancing with her sisters on the island of Naxos and fell in love with her. She fled from his advances to the far reaches of the ocean, near Atlas, but a dolphin (Delphinus) sent by Poseidon found her and persuaded her to return and marry the god; in gratitude Poseidon placed the dolphin among the stars as a constellation. As queen of the sea she rides beside Poseidon in a chariot drawn by hippocamps or other sea creatures, attended by Tritons and Nereids.

Worship and legacy

Amphitrite had no independent cult of great importance but was honoured alongside Poseidon at coastal sanctuaries. In art she is often shown beside her husband holding a trident or a fish. Her name was given to the main-belt asteroid 29 Amphitrite, discovered in 1854, and "Amphitrite" has been a popular name for naval vessels, including ships of the Royal Navy and the United States Navy.
