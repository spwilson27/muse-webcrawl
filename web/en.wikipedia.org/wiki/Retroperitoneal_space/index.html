Retroperitoneal space

The retroperitoneal space (retroperitoneum) is the anatomical space in the abdominal cavity behind the peritoneum. It has no specific delineating anatomical structures. Organs are said to be retroperitoneal if they have peritoneum on their anterior side only, while structures that were originally suspended in the abdominal cavity by mesentery but migrated posteriorly during development are called secondarily retroperitoneal.

Structures

Retroperitoneal structures include the kidneys, adrenal glands, ureters, the aorta and inferior vena cava, most of the duodenum, the pancreas (except the tail), the ascending and descending colon, and the rectum. A common mnemonic for the retroperitoneal organs is "SAD PUCKER".

Clinical significance

Bleeding into the retroperitoneal space (retroperitoneal hemorrhage) can result from trauma, ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm, or anticoagulation. The space is divided into compartments including the anterior pararenal space, perirenal space, and posterior pararenal space, which guide the interpretation of imaging findings.
