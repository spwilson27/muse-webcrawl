Learned society

A learned society (also learned academy, scholarly society, or academic association) is an organisation that exists to promote an academic discipline, a profession, or a group of related disciplines. Most learned societies are non-profit organisations, and many are professional associations whose members are scholars, researchers, and practitioners in the field. Membership may be open to all, require qualifications, or be an honour conferred by election.

History

Among the earliest learned societies were the Italian academies of the Renaissance and the seventeenth-century national academies of science: the Accademia dei Lincei (1603), the Royal Society of London (1660), and the French Académie des Sciences (1666). The eighteenth and nineteenth centuries saw an explosion of specialist societies in fields from geology and astronomy to philology and archaeology, alongside professional bodies for medicine, law, and engineering.

Activities

  • Publishing academic journals and books, often including some of the leading titles in a field.
  • Organising conferences, lectures, and prizes.
  • Setting professional standards and accrediting qualifications.
  • Advising governments and funding bodies, and representing the discipline publicly.
  • Awarding fellowships and medals recognising scholarly achievement.

Notable examples

Examples include the Royal Society, the British Academy, the American Philosophical Society, the national academies of sciences of many countries, and subject societies such as the Geological Society of London and the Modern Language Association. Umbrella bodies such as the Academy of Social Sciences coordinate learned societies within a region or sector.
