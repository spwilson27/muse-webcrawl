Belsnickel (also Belznickel, Belschnickel, and in Pennsylvania Dutch der Belznickel) is a crotchety, fur-clad Christmas gift-bringer figure in the folklore of the Palatinate region of southwestern Germany and among the Pennsylvania Dutch in the United States. His name derives from German Pelz ("fur") combined with Nickel, a familiar form of Saint Nicholas — hence "Fur Nicholas".

Description and custom

The Belsnickel appears in tattered clothing and furs, sometimes with a mask, carrying a switch (or bundle of switches) in one hand and a sack or pockets full of cakes, candies and nuts. Tradition holds that he visits homes in the weeks before Christmas, rapping on windows or doors with his switch. Children who have behaved receive treats; naughty children are threatened with a tap or rap from the switch. Unlike the jolly gift-giver Santa Claus, the Belsnickel combines reward and discipline in a single figure, testing children's behaviour and, in some accounts, their knowledge of catechism or lessons.

Origins and spread

The figure belongs to the family of frightening companions of Saint Nicholas found across German-speaking Europe, comparable to Knecht Ruprecht in northern Germany and Krampus in the Alpine regions. Palatine emigrants carried the custom to America in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, where "Belsnickeling" — masked visiting from house to house in the Christmas season — persisted in Pennsylvania Dutch communities into the twentieth century and has been revived as a heritage tradition. Related masked-visiting customs survive in parts of Maryland, Virginia's Shenandoah Valley, and in Nova Scotia, Canada, where "belsnickling" was practised in Lunenburg County.

In popular culture

The character gained wider recognition through the American television comedy The Office, in which Dwight Schrute assumes the persona of Belsnickel at a Christmas party, sorting his co-workers into "impish" and "admirable". The figure also appears in regional Christmas events and museum programming in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania.
