Ethnic dance

Ethnic dances express the traditions and identity of a particular cultural community.

Ethnic dance is an umbrella term for dance forms that originate in, and express the heritage of, a specific ethnic or cultural community. It encompasses folk dances, ceremonial and ritual dances, social dances and theatricalised presentations of traditional movement, and is studied within dance ethnology (ethnochoreology) and anthropology.

Definition and scope

Scholars use "ethnic dance" to distinguish community-rooted forms—learned informally and tied to the life of a group—from concert forms such as ballet developed for the stage. Examples include the Irish céilí dances, Indian classical and regional forms such as Kathak and Bhangra, Japanese Bon Odori, the Dabke of the Levant, Polynesian hula, West African ceremonial dances and the many regional folk dances of Europe and Latin America.

Function

Ethnic dances mark harvests, weddings, funerals, initiations, seasonal festivals and religious observances; they may re-enact myths, prepare for war or hunting, or simply cement social bonds. Movement vocabularies often encode local geography, costume, music and gender roles.

Preservation and performance

Migration and diaspora communities sustain ethnic dance as a marker of identity. Folk dance ensembles, festivals and, since 2003, UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage lists promote documentation and transmission, though staged versions typically adapt participatory dances for proscenium presentation. Critics note the term itself is debated, since it can mark non-Western forms as "ethnic" while treating Western forms as universal.
