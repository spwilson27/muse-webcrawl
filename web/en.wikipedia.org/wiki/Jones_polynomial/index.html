Jones polynomial

Field: Knot theory
Discovered by: Vaughan Jones (1984)
Type: Knot polynomial (link invariant)
Related: Alexander polynomial; HOMFLY polynomial

In the mathematical field of knot theory, the Jones polynomial is a knot polynomial discovered by Vaughan Jones in 1984. Specifically, it is an invariant of an oriented knot or link which assigns to each oriented knot or link a Laurent polynomial in the variable t1/2 with integer coefficients.

Definition by skein relation

The Jones polynomial V(L) of an oriented link L can be characterised by the initial condition V(unknot) = 1 together with the skein relation

t−1 V(L+) − t V(L−) + (t−1/2 − t1/2) V(L0) = 0,

where L+, L−, and L0 are three link diagrams identical except at one crossing, where they show a positive crossing, a negative crossing, and a smoothing, respectively. Jones originally defined the polynomial through his work on von Neumann algebras, via a representation of the braid group.

Significance

The discovery astonished knot theorists because it produced a far stronger invariant than the classical Alexander polynomial (1923) and could distinguish some knots from their mirror images—for instance, it assigns different polynomials to the right- and left-handed trefoils. It launched a revolution connecting knot theory with statistical mechanics, quantum groups, and quantum field theory; Edward Witten later gave an interpretation via Chern–Simons theory. Jones received the Fields Medal in 1990 partly for this work. Whether the Jones polynomial detects the unknot remains an open problem.
