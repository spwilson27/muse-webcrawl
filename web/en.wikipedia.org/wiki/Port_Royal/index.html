Port Royal

Port Royal is a town at the end of the Palisadoes spit at the mouth of Kingston Harbour in Jamaica. Founded by the Spanish as a fishing village and developed by the English after their capture of Jamaica in 1655, it became one of the largest and wealthiest ports in the English Americas — and notorious as a haven of privateers and pirates, "the wickedest city on earth".

The pirate city

From its base at Port Royal, buccaneers such as Henry Morgan raided Spanish shipping and settlements under letters of marque; the port's merchants grew rich on loot and trade. By the 1680s Port Royal had some 6,500–7,000 inhabitants, hundreds of ships visiting annually, and a reputation for taverns and brothels outnumbering its churches.

The 1692 earthquake

On 7 June 1692 a massive earthquake struck; liquefaction of the sandy spit caused about two-thirds of the town to sink beneath the sea within minutes, killing around 2,000 people, with thousands more dying afterwards. Survivors relocated across the harbour to found Kingston. Rebuilt Port Royal was later ravaged by fire (1703) and hurricanes, and became a Royal Navy station. The sunken city is now a unique underwater archaeological site, preserved where it fell.
