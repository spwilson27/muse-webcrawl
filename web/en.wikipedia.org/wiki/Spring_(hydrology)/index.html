Spring (hydrology)


A karst spring emerging from limestone.

A spring is a natural exit point at which groundwater flows out of an aquifer to the Earth's surface. Springs occur where the water table intersects the land surface, or where permeable rock layers, faults, or fractures allow confined groundwater to escape. Their discharge can range from a trickle to hundreds of cubic metres per second.

Formation and classification

Springs are classified by the source of their pressure (gravity or artesian), by geology — depression, contact, fracture, and karst springs — and by discharge, using a magnitude scale from first magnitude (over 2.8 m³/s) down to eighth. Karst springs in limestone regions are often the largest, draining extensive cave systems. Hot springs are heated by geothermal activity, and mineral springs carry dissolved minerals long prized for supposed medicinal properties.

Uses and significance

Springs have supplied drinking water to settlements since prehistory and are frequently sites of religious significance. Ecologically, they support specialised species assemblages and constant-temperature refuges. Over-extraction of groundwater can reduce or eliminate spring flow, as has occurred in many arid regions.
