Andrew Johnson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Andrew Johnson (29 December 1808 – 31 July 1875) was the 17th president of the United States (1865–1869), succeeding Abraham Lincoln on his assassination. A Tennessee tailor turned politician — the only Southern senator to remain loyal to the Union in 1861 — he was chosen as Lincoln's 1864 running mate to balance the ticket, and inherited the task of Reconstruction, over which he fought Congress bitterly.

Johnson favoured rapid restoration of the seceded states with minimal conditions and opposed federal protection of freedpeople's rights, vetoing civil-rights and Freedmen's Bureau legislation (Congress overrode him) and campaigning against the Fourteenth Amendment. His dismissal of Secretary of War Edwin Stanton in defiance of the Tenure of Office Act triggered his impeachment by the House in February 1868; the Senate acquitted him by a single vote (35–19). He left office in 1869, and in 1875 returned to the Senate — the only former president to do so — dying months later. Historians consistently rank him among the worst presidents for his obstruction of racial justice in Reconstruction.
