MV Goya was a Norwegian motor freighter launched in 1940 and seized by Germany during the Second World War. She is remembered as one of the ships lost in the Baltic evacuation of 1945; her sinking by a Soviet submarine on 16 April 1945 caused the deaths of an estimated 6,000 to 7,000 people, making it one of the worst maritime disasters of all time by loss of life.

Construction and early service

Goya was built by the Akers Mekaniske Verksted yard in Oslo, Norway, and launched in 1940 for the Johan Ludwig Mowinckel shipping company, intended for the South American trade. Before she could enter commercial service, Germany invaded Norway in April 1940 and the vessel was confiscated. She was subsequently employed in the Baltic, including as a target ship for U-boat training and later as a transport.

Evacuation and sinking

In early 1945, as Soviet forces advanced into East Prussia, Goya took part in Operation Hannibal, the German naval evacuation of troops and refugees westward across the Baltic Sea. On 16 April 1945, crammed with roughly 6,000–7,000 soldiers and civilian refugees bound for Swinemünde, she sailed from the Hela Peninsula in a small convoy.

Shortly before midnight, the Soviet submarine L-3, under Captain Vladimir Konovalov, fired two torpedoes that struck Goya amidships. The ship broke apart and sank within minutes in roughly 78 metres of water. Only about 180 people were rescued by the escorting vessels. Konovalov was later awarded the title Hero of the Soviet Union for the sinking.

Legacy

The wreck of Goya was located in 2002 by Polish technical divers. Along with the Wilhelm Gustloff and the Steuben, Goya is counted among the deadliest sinkings of the Hannibal evacuation.
