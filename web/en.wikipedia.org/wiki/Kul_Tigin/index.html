Kul Tigin (Old Turkic: 𐰚𐰇𐰠𐱅𐰃𐰏𐰤, Kül Tigin; Chinese: 闕特勤, Què Tèqín; 685 – 27 February 731) was a general and prince of the Second Turkic Khaganate. He was a younger son of Ilterish Qaghan, the khaganate's founder, and the brother of Bilge Qaghan, in whose reign Kul Tigin served as the empire's leading military commander.

Life

Kul Tigin was seven when his father died in 691 and was raised amid the consolidation of the khaganate under his uncle Qapghan Qaghan. From his teens he fought in the khaganate's wars: against the Türgesh at Bolchu (711), the Kyrgyz, the Tang dynasty and rebellious subject peoples. His campaigns carried Turkic arms west to the Iron Gate near Samarkand and east against the Khitan. When Qapghan was killed in 716 and a succession struggle followed, Kul Tigin led the coup that destroyed the rival claimant and placed his elder brother Bilge on the throne, after which he acted as the power behind the throne and commander of the army.

Death and memorial

Kul Tigin died in 731 at the age of 46. His brother erected for him a vast memorial complex in the Orkhon valley (the Khöshöö-Tsaidam site in present-day Mongolia), including a temple, statues and the famous Kul Tigin stele. The inscription, carved in Old Turkic runic script and Chinese and composed by Yollıg Tigin, recounts the history of the Turks and Kul Tigin's deeds; its decipherment by Vilhelm Thomsen in 1893 founded the study of Old Turkic. A stone head from the complex, found in 1957, is kept in Ulaanbaatar.

Legacy

Together with his brother's stele, the Kul Tigin inscription is the oldest substantial text in a Turkic language and a cornerstone of Turkic national heritage in Turkey, Mongolia and Central Asia.
