Amadeus (film)

Amadeus is a 1984 American period biographical drama film directed by Miloš Forman, adapted by Peter Shaffer from his 1979 stage play of the same name. Set in Vienna in the late 18th century, it is a fictionalised account of the lives of composers Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Antonio Salieri, framed as the elderly Salieri's confession of his jealousy and his plot against the younger genius. The film stars F. Murray Abraham as Salieri and Tom Hulce as Mozart.

Plot

In 1823, the aged Antonio Salieri, confined to a mental asylum after a suicide attempt, recounts to a priest how, as court composer in Vienna, he recognised the divine genius of Mozart — whose vulgarity he despised — and, cursing God for choosing such a vessel, set out to destroy him. The narrative follows Mozart's triumphs and humiliations at the court of Joseph II, his financial decline, and his death while composing the Requiem, which Salieri attempted to claim as his own.

Production

The film was shot largely in Prague, which stood in for 18th-century Vienna, including at the Estates Theatre where Mozart had premiered Don Giovanni. The music was conducted by Neville Marriner with the Academy of St Martin in the Fields. A director's cut with additional footage was released in 2002.

Reception

Amadeus was a critical and commercial success, winning eight Academy Awards from eleven nominations, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor for Abraham. It is regularly ranked among the greatest films ever made; the American Film Institute placed it 53rd on its 1997 list of the 100 greatest American films.
