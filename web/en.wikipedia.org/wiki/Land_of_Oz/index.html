Land of Oz

The Land of Oz is a fictional magical country introduced in the 1900 children's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz by L. Frank Baum. It is the best-known of Baum's fantasy lands and the setting for most of the fourteen Oz books he wrote, beginning with The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900) and continuing through Glinda of Oz (1920).

Geography

Oz is roughly rectangular and divided into four quadrants, each associated with a colour: the blue Munchkin Country in the east, the red Quadling Country in the south, the yellow Winkie Country in the west, and the purple Gillikin Country in the north. At the centre stands the green Emerald City. Oz is surrounded by the Deadly Desert and neighbouring fairy lands such as Ev and Ix.

Inhabitants and rulers

Oz is home to Munchkins, Quadlings, Winkies and Gillikins, as well as talking animals and magical beings. In Baum's books, Oz is eventually ruled by Princess Ozma, and Dorothy Gale repeatedly travels there from Kansas. Magic is common in Oz, and its inhabitants do not age or die in the ordinary way once Baum's later books establish the country's enchantment.

Legacy

The 1939 MGM film The Wizard of Oz made the land famous worldwide, though its depiction differs from the books in details such as the colour of the magic slippers. Oz has inspired many continuations by other authors, stage musicals including Wicked, and a large body of scholarship.
