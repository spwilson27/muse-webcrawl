Wind power in the European Union

Installed capacity>200 GW (EU, early 2020s)
Share of EU electricityRoughly one-sixth to one-fifth in recent years
Leading countriesGermany, Spain, France, Sweden, Italy, Netherlands

Wind power in the European Union is a major source of renewable electricity. The EU hosts some of the world's largest onshore wind fleets and is the global leader in offshore wind, particularly in the North Sea. Wind energy is central to the EU's climate policy, including the European Green Deal and the target of climate neutrality by 2050.

Development

European wind power grew from early Danish and German pioneers in the 1980s and 1990s. Feed-in tariffs in Germany and Spain drove rapid expansion in the 2000s, while auction systems later spread across member states. Capacity in the EU passed 200 gigawatts in the early 2020s, with wind supplying a substantial and rising share of electricity demand.

Offshore wind

The world's first offshore wind farm was built at Vindeby, Denmark, in 1991. The United Kingdom (while an EU member), Germany, the Netherlands, Denmark and Belgium built large North Sea fleets, and offshore wind is planned to expand sharply under intergovernmental declarations such as the Esbjerg Declaration of 2022, which targeted 120 GW of North Sea offshore wind by 2030 among participating countries.

Policy

EU policy frameworks include the Renewable Energy Directive, with a target of at least 42.5% renewable energy in final consumption by 2030, the REPowerEU plan to accelerate deployment after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, and a European Wind Power Action Plan addressing permitting, supply chains and grid investment. Permitting speed and grid connection queues remain widely cited obstacles.

By country

Germany has the largest installed fleet in Europe, followed by Spain and France. Denmark generates the highest share of its electricity from wind of any large country — often more than half in windy years. Sweden has expanded rapidly on land, while the Netherlands has combined onshore growth with major offshore zones.
