United States Navy Fighter Weapons School

Unit information
Active: 1969–1996 (as independent school); now part of NSAWC
Location: Naval Air Station Fallon, Nevada
Nickname: TOPGUN

The United States Navy Fighter Weapons School, commonly known as TOPGUN, is the United States Navy's premier school for teaching air combat maneuvering and strike-fighter tactics to selected naval aviators and naval flight officers.

History

TOPGUN was established on March 3, 1969, at Naval Air Station Miramar, California, in response to the findings of Captain Frank Ault's report on poor air-to-air performance during the Vietnam War. Navy pilots flying F-4 Phantoms had achieved an exchange ratio of barely 2.5:1 over North Vietnam, far worse than in Korea. The school taught Dissimilar Air Combat Training (DACT), using small, agile aircraft such as the A-4 Skyhawk to simulate Soviet-built MiGs.

After the school's first graduates returned to combat during Operation Linebacker in 1972, the Navy's kill ratio improved dramatically to roughly 12.5:1, while the Air Force — which had no equivalent school at the time — saw no comparable improvement.

Modern role

In 1996 TOPGUN moved to Naval Air Station Fallon, Nevada, where it became part of the Naval Strike and Air Warfare Center (NSAWC), now the Naval Aviation Warfighting Development Center. The course trains strike-fighter tactics instructors who return to fleet squadrons to teach what they learned.

In popular culture

The school achieved worldwide fame through the 1986 film Top Gun and its 2022 sequel Top Gun: Maverick.
