Opioid epidemic

The opioid epidemic, also referred to as the opioid crisis, is the rapid increase in the overuse, misuse/abuse, and overdoses attributed to opioids, beginning in the United States in the 1990s and causing hundreds of thousands of deaths.

Period1990s–present
LocationPrimarily United States and Canada
WavesPrescription opioids, heroin, fentanyl

Waves

The first wave followed aggressive marketing and overprescription of drugs such as OxyContin by Purdue Pharma. A second wave centred on heroin from about 2010, and a third on illicit synthetic fentanyl, far more potent, driving overdose deaths above 80,000 a year in the US in the early 2020s.

Response

Responses include litigation and settlements with manufacturers and distributors, prescribing guidelines, treatment with methadone and buprenorphine, naloxone distribution, and FDA and CDC action.
