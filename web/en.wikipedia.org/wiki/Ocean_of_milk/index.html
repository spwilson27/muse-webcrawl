Ocean of milk

In Hindu cosmology, the Ocean of Milk (Sanskrit: Kṣīra Sāgara) is the fifth of seven oceans that encircle the concentric continents of the world in the Puranas. It is the setting of the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk, one of the most famous episodes in Hindu mythology, and it is in this ocean that the god Vishnu reclines on the serpent Shesha.

The churning of the ocean

According to the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and the Puranas, the devas (gods), weakened by a curse, joined with their rivals the asuras (demons) to churn the ocean of milk in order to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. Mount Mandara served as the churning rod, set upon the back of Vishnu's tortoise avatar Kurma; the serpent Vasuki served as the rope, pulled alternately by gods and demons.

The churning produced a series of treasures and beings, including the goddess Lakshmi, the moon Chandra, the divine cow Kamadhenu, the elephant Airavata, the apsaras, the physician Dhanvantari bearing the pot of amrita — and first a deadly poison, halahala, which Shiva drank to save the world, turning his throat blue. Vishnu, in the form of the enchantress Mohini, finally distributed the nectar to the gods alone.

Symbolism and depictions

The churning is interpreted variously as a cosmic creation myth, an allegory of spiritual discipline (churning the mind to extract wisdom), and a dramatization of the tension between order and chaos. It is famously depicted in the great bas-relief gallery of Angkor Wat in Cambodia, in sculpture at temples across India and Southeast Asia, and in a well-known sculptural tableau at Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport.
