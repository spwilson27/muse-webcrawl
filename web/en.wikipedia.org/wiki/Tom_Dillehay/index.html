Tom Dillehay

Tom D. Dillehay (born 1947) is an American archaeologist and anthropologist, best known for his excavations at Monte Verde in southern Chile, which provided some of the earliest widely debated evidence of human settlement in the Americas before the Clovis culture.

Monte Verde

Beginning in 1977, Dillehay's team excavated Monte Verde, where waterlogged peat preserved wooden structures, plant remains, and artefacts dated to about 14,500 years ago—roughly a thousand years before the Clovis horizon that many archaeologists then held to represent the first Americans. After two decades of controversy, a 1997 site visit by a panel of specialists endorsed the site's antiquity, helping to overturn the "Clovis first" model and opening the field to coastal-migration theories of the peopling of the Americas.

Career

Dillehay has taught at the University of Kentucky and Vanderbilt University, and has conducted extensive fieldwork in Peru and Chile on early agriculture, Andean state formation, and the archaeology of the Mapuche. He received Chile's National Prize and was granted Chilean citizenship for his contributions to the country's archaeology.
