Rural City of Wangaratta

The Rural City of Wangaratta is a local government area in the Hume region of Victoria, Australia, located in the north-eastern part of the state. It covers an area of approximately 3,645 square kilometres and, at the 2021 census, had a population of about 29,800. The council's administrative centre is the regional city of Wangaratta, at the junction of the Ovens and King Rivers, about 236 kilometres north-east of Melbourne.

History

The Rural City was formed in 1994 from the amalgamation of the City of Wangaratta, the Shire of Wangaratta, the Shire of Oxley, and parts of neighbouring shires, as part of statewide local government reform in Victoria. Initially governed by state-appointed commissioners, an elected council returned in the late 1990s. In 2013 the council was dismissed by the Victorian Parliament following a report into governance failures, administrators were appointed, and elections were held again in 2016.

Geography and settlements

The municipality spans the Ovens Valley and King Valley, noted for viticulture and gourmet food production, especially around Milawa and Whitfield. Towns and localities within the Rural City include Wangaratta, Milawa, Oxley, Whitfield, Cheshunt, Eldorado, Everton, Glenrowan (famous for its association with the bushranger Ned Kelly), Moyhu, Peechelba, Springhurst, Tarrawingee and Whorouly. The Warby-Ovens National Park lies to the north-west, and the municipality abuts the Victorian Alps.

Economy

Agriculture, wine production, food manufacturing, health and education services, and tourism dominate the local economy. Wangaratta is a significant service centre for north-eastern Victoria, with a major hospital, TAFE and secondary education facilities. The King Valley is known as one of Australia's leading regions for Italian grape varieties such as prosecco, sangiovese and pinot grigio.
