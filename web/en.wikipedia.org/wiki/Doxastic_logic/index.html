Doxastic logic

Doxastic logic is a type of logic concerned with reasoning about beliefs. The term doxastic derives from the ancient Greek δόξα, doxa, meaning belief. Typically, a doxastic logic treats the operator B, meaning "It is believed that", as a modal operator. Developed in depth by Jaakko Hintikka in his 1962 work Knowledge and Belief, doxastic logic models the beliefs of an agent using possible-worlds semantics, in which a belief is true in a world if the proposition holds in all worlds compatible with the agent's beliefs.

Types of believers

Different sets of axioms characterise different idealised reasoners: a "reasonable believer" never holds contradictory beliefs and believes all tautologies; a believer with positive and negative introspection believes that they believe (or do not believe) what they in fact believe. Systems of doxastic logic differ from epistemic logic chiefly in that belief, unlike knowledge, may be false, so the axiom that what is believed is true is omitted.
