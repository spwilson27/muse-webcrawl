Adrian helmet

The Adrian helmet (casque Adrian) was a combat helmet issued to the French Army during the First World War. Designed under the direction of Intendant-General Louis Adrian and introduced in 1915, it was the first modern steel helmet produced for a major army in the war.

Made of mild steel with a distinctive crest running front-to-back and a badge on the front indicating the arm of service, the Adrian was designed to protect against falling shrapnel rather than direct bullet fire. About three million were produced in the first year alone.

The helmet was exported or copied widely, serving with Belgium, Italy, Russia, Serbia, Japan and many other armies, and updated versions (the modèle 1926) remained in French service into the Second World War.
