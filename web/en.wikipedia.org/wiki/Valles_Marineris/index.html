Valles Marineris


Location: Mars, south of the equator, east of Tharsis
Length: over 4,000 km
Depth: up to 7 km

Valles Marineris (Latin for "Mariner Valleys", named after the Mariner 9 orbiter that discovered it) is a vast system of canyons that runs along the equator of Mars, east of the Tharsis volcanic region. Stretching more than 4,000 km — roughly the width of the United States — and reaching depths of up to 7 km, it is one of the largest canyon systems in the Solar System.

Structure

The system consists of several parallel troughs (chasmata), including Noctis Labyrinthus in the west, the central Ius, Tithonium, Melas, Candor and Ophir Chasmata, and Coprates Chasma, opening eastward into chaotic terrain and outflow channels that lead toward Chryse Planitia.

Formation

The canyons are thought to have begun as rift faults caused by crustal uplift of the Tharsis region, and were later widened by erosion, landslides and possibly flowing water. Layered deposits and hydrated minerals within the chasmata suggest lakes may once have stood in some of the troughs.
