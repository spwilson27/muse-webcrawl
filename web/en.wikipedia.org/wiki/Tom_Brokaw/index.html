Thomas John Brokaw (born February 6, 1940) is an American journalist and author, anchor and managing editor of NBC Nightly News from 1982 to 2004. With Peter Jennings and Dan Rather he formed the era's 'Big Three' network anchors, and his 1998 book The Greatest Generation gave a name to the generation that lived through the Depression and won the Second World War.

Early career

Born in Webster, South Dakota, Brokaw joined NBC in 1966, reporting from Los Angeles and serving as White House correspondent during Watergate, then co-hosting Today (1976-1981).

Nightly News

He became Nightly News' sole anchor in 1983, leading NBC's coverage through the fall of the Berlin Wall (he reported its opening from the scene in 1989), the Gulf War and 9/11, keeping the broadcast at or near the top of the ratings.

Books and later work

The Greatest Generation (1998) and its sequels were bestsellers. After stepping down in 2004 (succeeded by Brian Williams) he remained an NBC special correspondent, moderated a 2008 presidential debate, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2014. His memoirs include Boom! and A Lucky Life Interrupted.
