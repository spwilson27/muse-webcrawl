County Westmeath

Contae na hIarmhí; county town Mullingar; largest town Athlone.

County Westmeath (Irish: Contae na hIarmhí) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Leinster and the Eastern and Midland Region. It is named after the historic kingdom of Meath (Mide, "the middle"), of which it formed the western part. The county town is Mullingar, and Athlone, straddling the River Shannon at the county's western edge, is its largest town.

Geography

Westmeath is a low-lying county of lakes, eskers, and farmland in the Irish midlands. Its principal lakes include Lough Ennell, Lough Owel, Lough Derravaragh, Lough Lene, and Lough Ree on the Shannon. The Hill of Uisneach, south-west of Mullingar, was in Irish mythology the symbolic centre of Ireland, where the provinces met and the festival of Bealtaine was celebrated with a great fire.

History

The territory formed part of the ancient kingdom of Mide, a fifth province of Ireland ruled in turn by branches of the Uí Néill. After the Norman invasion it passed to the de Lacy lords of Meath. County Westmeath was formally separated from Meath by an Act of the Irish Parliament in 1543. In the 1640s and the Williamite war the county saw sieges at Athlone, whose bridge over the Shannon was famously contested in 1690–91.

Economy and culture

Agriculture, particularly cattle rearing, food processing, and tourism around the lakes and the Royal Canal and Grand Canal ways, are central to the economy; Mullingar is known for its pewter and its association with the singer Joe Dolan, while the writer Jonathan Swift spent summers near Lough Owel. In sport, Westmeath won its first Leinster Senior Football Championship in 2004 and the Tailteann Cup in 2022.
