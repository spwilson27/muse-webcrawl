The American Law Institute (ALI) is a research and advocacy group of judges, lawyers, and legal scholars in the United States, founded in 1923 to promote the clarification and simplification of the law and its better adaptation to social needs. Its most influential products are the Restatements of the Law and model statutes such as the Model Penal Code.

Work

The Institute produces Restatements — authoritative summaries of common-law doctrine in areas such as contracts, torts, property, and agency — drafted by specialist reporters, debated, and approved by the membership at annual meetings. Though not binding, Restatements are highly persuasive authority in American courts. The ALI also jointly produced the Uniform Commercial Code with the National Conference of Commissioners on Uniform State Laws, and publishes Principles of the Law projects addressing areas where courts would benefit from reform-oriented guidance.

History

The ALI emerged from concern among leading jurists, including William Howard Taft and Elihu Root, that American judge-made law had become uncertain and complex. Its first director was William Draper Lewis, dean of the University of Pennsylvania Law School.
