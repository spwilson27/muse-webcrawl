Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga

Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga is a Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva, located in the ancient city of Ujjain in the state of Madhya Pradesh, India. The temple is situated on the side of the holy river Shipra. The presiding deity, Shiva in the lingam form, is believed to be swayambhu (self-manifested) and is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, the most sacred abodes of Shiva.

Mahakal is regarded as the supreme lord of time and death, and the deity is revered as the ruler of Ujjain. The temple faces south, a feature known as dakshinmukhi, unique among the twelve Jyotirlingas.

History

The temple's foundations are traditionally attributed to the ancient period, with references in the Puranas. The present structure was rebuilt over several centuries; the Maratha ruler Ranoji Scindia reconstructed the temple in 1736, and later contributions were made by the Holkar and Scindia dynasties. The temple complex features a three-storey structure with the sanctum housing the Jyotirlinga, and shrines to Omkareshwar and Nagchandreshwar on the upper levels.

Worship and festivals

The temple is famous for the Bhasma Aarti, a ritual in which the deity is offered sacred ash in the early morning. Ujjain is also one of the seven Sapta Puri pilgrimage cities and hosts the Simhastha Kumbh Mela every twelve years. Maha Shivaratri is the principal festival celebrated at the temple.
