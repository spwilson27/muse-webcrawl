The Sims

Developer: Maxis
Publisher: Electronic Arts
Released: 4 February 2000
Genre: Life simulation

The Sims is a life simulation video game developed by Maxis and designed by Will Wright, released by Electronic Arts in 2000. Players create virtual people called Sims, place them in houses, and direct their daily lives — eating, sleeping, working, socializing, and pursuing needs and wants — often described as a virtual dollhouse.

Development and gameplay

Wright conceived the game as a satire of consumer culture and an "interactive dollhouse," partly inspired by rebuilding his life after losing his home in the 1991 Oakland firestorm, and by Christopher Alexander's design theory. Sims speak the gibberish language Simlish and are governed by needs bars such as hunger, comfort, and bladder. Seven expansion packs were released, adding pets, vacations, fame, and magic.

Reception and legacy

The Sims became the best-selling PC game of its era, selling over 11 million copies, and notably attracted an unusually large female audience. It launched one of the most successful franchises in video-game history, with sequels The Sims 2 (2004), The Sims 3 (2009), and The Sims 4 (2014), and won numerous awards for its open-ended design.
