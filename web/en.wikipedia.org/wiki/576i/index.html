576i is a standard-definition (SD) video mode originally used by the PAL and SECAM analogue television systems in most of the world. The "576" represents 576 horizontal lines of vertical resolution, and the "i" stands for interlaced scanning. It operates at a field rate of 50 per second, giving 25 full frames per second — the same temporal rate as the corresponding film speed used in PAL countries — and is commonly associated with a 4:3 or 16:9 aspect ratio.

Technical details

In digital form, 576i is typically stored and transmitted as a 720 × 576 pixel raster under the ITU-R BT.601 standard, with pixel aspect ratios accounting for the difference between 4:3 and 16:9 displays. The analogue signal carries 625 total lines per frame, of which 576 carry picture information, the remainder being used for the vertical blanking interval. It is the PAL-world counterpart to the 480i mode used in NTSC regions.

Usage

576i is used in PAL/SECAM DVDs, digital terrestrial broadcasts (DVB-T), satellite and cable standard-definition channels, and the Wii and other games consoles' PAL modes. Its progressive counterpart, in which all 576 lines are drawn each frame, is 576p, classed as enhanced-definition in some markets.
