Nothing Else Matters

"Nothing Else Matters" is a song by American heavy metal band Metallica. A power ballad written by frontman James Hetfield while on the phone with his girlfriend, it was released in 1992 as the third single from their self-titled fifth album, Metallica (commonly known as the Black Album, 1991).

Composition and reception

Hetfield initially wrote the song for himself and was reluctant to record it, considering it too personal for the band; drummer Lars Ulrich persuaded him to include it. The song begins with an open E minor arpeggio — famously playable with one hand because Hetfield was holding a telephone — and builds to a full-band climax with an orchestral arrangement by Michael Kamen. Its departure from thrash metal divided some longtime fans but greatly expanded the band's mainstream audience.

The song reached the top 40 in numerous countries and has become one of Metallica's best-known recordings and a concert staple. It was performed with the San Francisco Symphony on the live album S&M (1999) and in an acoustic four-man arrangement, and has been widely covered by artists in other genres.

Music video

The accompanying music video, directed by Adam Dubin, shows documentary footage of the band recording the album in the studio, giving fans an unusually candid view of the sessions.
