Nicolò Amati

Nicolò Amati (3 September 1596 – 12 April 1684) was an Italian luthier from Cremona and the most famous member of the Amati family of violin makers. The grandson of Andrea Amati, who is credited with creating the modern violin family, Nicolò refined the instrument's design and was the teacher of Antonio Stradivari.

Nicolò Amati

Born: 3 September 1596, Cremona, Duchy of Milan

Died: 12 April 1684, Cremona

Occupation: Luthier

Known for: "Grand pattern" violins; teacher of Stradivari

Life and work

Nicolò was the son of Girolamo Amati and grandson of Andrea Amati. When plague devastated Cremona in 1630, killing his father and much of the city's population of makers, Nicolò became the leading luthier in the city. Unlike his predecessors, he took apprentices from outside the family, including Andrea Guarneri and, most famously, Antonio Stradivari.

Instruments

Around the 1640s Amati developed his "Grand Amati" pattern, a slightly larger model with graceful curves, high arching and a sweet, powerful tone prized for chamber music. His instruments, made with fine maple and spruce and finished with a rich golden-brown varnish, remain among the most sought-after string instruments in the world, played by leading soloists and held in major museum collections.
