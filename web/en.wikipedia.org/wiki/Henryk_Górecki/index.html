Henryk Mikołaj Górecki (6 December 1933 – 12 November 2010) was a Polish composer of contemporary classical music. An early figure of the Polish avant-garde after Stalinism, he later developed a spare, deeply spiritual style rooted in Polish folk song, chant, and early music. He achieved extraordinary international fame when a recording of his Symphony No. 3 became one of the best-selling classical albums of the 1990s.

Life

Górecki was born in Czernica in Silesia. He studied composition at the State Higher School of Music in Katowice, where he later taught and briefly served as rector before resigning in protest at government interference in 1979. In the late 1950s he was associated with the sonorist avant-garde of the Warsaw Autumn festival, with works such as Scontri (1960), but from the 1970s his music turned toward simplicity, modality, and religious texts.

Symphony of Sorrowful Songs

Górecki composed his Symphony No. 3, the "Symphony of Sorrowful Songs", in 1976. Each of its three slow movements sets a Polish text about a mother grieving for her child: a 15th-century lament of Mary, a prayer scratched on a Gestapo cell wall by an 18-year-old prisoner in Zakopane, and a Silesian folk song of a mother searching for her son killed in the Silesian uprisings. The work attracted little notice for years, but the 1992 Nonesuch recording by soprano Dawn Upshaw and the London Sinfonietta under David Zinman sold over a million copies and topped classical charts worldwide.

Later work

Górecki is often grouped with Arvo Pärt and John Tavener under the label "holy minimalism", a term he resisted. Other noted works include Euntes ibant et flebant, Miserere, the playful Kleines Requiem für eine Polka, three string quartets written for the Kronos Quartet, and the Symphony No. 4, left nearly complete at his death and premiered posthumously in London in 2014.
