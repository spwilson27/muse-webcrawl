London boroughs

The London boroughs are the 32 local authority districts that together with the City of London make up the administrative area of Greater London. Each is governed by a London borough council, elected every four years. The boroughs were created on 1 April 1965 by the London Government Act 1963, which amalgamated the previous county boroughs, metropolitan boroughs and urban districts of the old County of London and surrounding areas.

The 32 boroughs of Greater London and the City of London.

Functions

Borough councils provide most day-to-day local services in London: schools, social services, libraries, waste collection and recycling, local planning decisions, housing, leisure services and local roads. Strategic functions — transport, policing oversight, fire and the London Plan — rest with the Greater London Authority (Mayor of London and London Assembly). The City of London Corporation governs the historic City separately.

Inner and outer London

The boroughs are commonly divided into Inner London (those that formed the old County of London, roughly the 12 boroughs closest to the centre, plus the City) and Outer London (the remaining 20). They range in population from around 150,000 (Kensington and Chelsea) to nearly 400,000 (Croydon), and in character from dense central districts to suburban and semi-rural edges.

List
