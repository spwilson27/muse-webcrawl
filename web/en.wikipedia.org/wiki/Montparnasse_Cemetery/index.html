Montparnasse Cemetery

LocationMontparnasse, 14th arrondissement, Paris, France
Established1824
Size19 hectares (47 acres)
Intermentsabout 35,000

Montparnasse Cemetery (French: Cimetière du Montparnasse) is a cemetery in the Montparnasse quarter of Paris, in the 14th arrondissement. Created in 1824 on former farmland as the "southern cemetery" (it was also known as Le Cimetière du Sud), it was one of three major cemeteries established outside the old city limits in the early 19th century, alongside Père Lachaise (east) and Montmartre (north). It covers 19 hectares and receives roughly 1,000 burials a year.

Notable burials

The cemetery is the resting place of many figures of French intellectual and artistic life. Among those buried here are the philosophers Jean-Paul Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir (in a shared grave), the poet Charles Baudelaire, the writers Guy de Maupassant and Samuel Beckett, the playwright Eugène Ionesco, the photographers Man Ray and Brassai, the singers Serge Gainsbourg and Jane Birkin, the sculptor Constantin Brâncuși, the painter Chaïm Soutine, the composer César Franck, the mathematician Henri Poincaré, the statesman and founder of Citroën André Citroën, and former Mexican president Porfirio Díaz.

Layout

The cemetery is divided into 30 divisions by a grid of tree-lined avenues, with the main entrance on Boulevard Edgar Quinet. Notable monuments include the Genie du Sommeil Eternel and the communal graves and memorials of the Franco-Prussian War and the Paris Commune. Just outside its story stands the Paris Catacombs' old quarry district, and the Tour Montparnasse overlooks the cemetery from the north.
