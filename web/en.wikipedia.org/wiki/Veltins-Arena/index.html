The Veltins-Arena (German pronunciation: [ˈfɛltɪnsʔaˌʁeːnaː]), known as Arena AufSchalke during UEFA competitions, is a retractable roof and pitch football stadium in Gelsenkirchen, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It opened on 13 August 2001 as the new home ground of FC Schalke 04, replacing the Parkstadion.

History

Plans for a new stadium emerged in the late 1990s as Schalke sought a modern multi-purpose arena. Construction began in 1998 and the stadium was completed in 2001 at a cost of roughly €191 million. The naming rights were sold to the German brewery Veltins in 2005; the stadium was originally called Arena AufSchalke.

Design

The stadium is noted for its retractable roof and its slide-out pitch, which can be moved outside the stadium in about four hours to allow the grass to grow under natural conditions and to permit the arena to host concerts and other events without damaging the playing surface. A large video cube hangs above the centre of the pitch. The capacity is 62,271 for domestic league matches, reduced to about 54,740 for international fixtures where standing is not permitted.

Major events

The Veltins-Arena hosted the 2004 UEFA Champions League Final, in which FC Porto defeated AS Monaco 3–0. It was one of the venues of the 2006 FIFA World Cup, hosting five matches including a quarter-final. It also hosted matches at UEFA Euro 2024. Outside football, the arena has staged concerts, ice hockey, biathlon and boxing events.
