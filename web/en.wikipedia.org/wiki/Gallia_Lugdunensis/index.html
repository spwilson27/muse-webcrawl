Gallia Lugdunensis


Gallia Lugdunensis was a province of the Roman Empire in central Gaul; capital: Lugdunum (modern Lyon).

Gallia Lugdunensis (French: Gaule Lyonnaise) was a province of the Roman Empire, one of the three provinces into which Augustus divided Gaul in 27 BC (together with Gallia Aquitania and Gallia Belgica), distinct from the earlier-conquered Narbonensis in the south. It occupied a large area of central France between Aquitania and Belgica, extending to the Atlantic coast of Brittany and Normandy.

History

The province took its name from its capital, Lugdunum (modern Lyon), founded in 43 BC and one of the most important cities of the western Empire. Lugdunum hosted an imperial mint, the Sanctuary of the Three Gauls with its altar to Rome and Augustus — where delegates from sixty Gallic tribes met annually — and was the birthplace of the emperors Claudius and Caracalla. Important cities included Lutetia (Paris), Lugdunum, Autun (Augustodunum), and Rouen (Rotomagus).

Later divisions

Under Diocletian's reforms (c. AD 297), Lugdunensis was divided into smaller provinces — Lugdunensis Prima (around Lyon), Secunda (around Rouen), Tertia (around Tours), and Senonia (around Sens) — within the Diocese of Gaul. The province's territory later passed into the Frankish kingdoms after the collapse of Roman authority in the fifth century. See also Roman Gaul.
