Gaetano Donizetti

Domenico Gaetano Maria Donizetti (29 November 1797 – 8 April 1848) was an Italian composer, best known for his operas. Along with Gioachino Rossini and Vincenzo Bellini, he was a leading composer of the bel canto opera style during the first half of the nineteenth century and a probable influence on other composers such as Giuseppe Verdi.

Donizetti composed some seventy operas in a remarkably prolific career.

Life and career

Donizetti was born in Bergamo to a poor family. He was taken under the wing of the composer Simon Mayr, who admitted him to his free music school and helped launch his career. After early successes in Venice and Rome, Donizetti established himself in Naples, where Anna Bolena (1830) brought him international fame.

His most famous works include L'elisir d'amore (1832), Lucia di Lammermoor (1835) — with its celebrated "mad scene" — La fille du régiment (1840), La favorite (1840), and the comic masterpiece Don Pasquale (1843), written for Paris. He worked in Paris and Vienna in his later years while maintaining his position in Naples.

Final years

Donizetti's health collapsed in the mid-1840s, owing largely to syphilis; he was institutionalised, then returned to Bergamo, where he died in 1848. In total he wrote about 70 operas, along with symphonies, quartets, and sacred music, with extraordinary speed.
