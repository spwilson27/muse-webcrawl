Projective module

In mathematics, particularly in algebra, a module P over a ring R is projective if it has the lifting property: for every surjective module homomorphism f : N → M and every homomorphism g : P → M, there exists a homomorphism h : P → N with f ∘ h = g. Equivalently, Hom(P, –) is an exact functor.

Characterizations

Several conditions are equivalent to projectivity: every short exact sequence ending in P splits; P is a direct summand of a free module; and P has a dual basis. Free modules are projective, and over a local ring every finitely generated projective module is free. The idempotent description — modules of the form eRn for idempotent matrices — shows that projective modules correspond to vector bundles in algebraic geometry.

Examples and applications

Over a field, every module (vector space) is free, hence projective. Over a principal ideal domain, finitely generated projective modules are free. Non-free projective modules arise naturally from idempotents in rings and from line bundles: the tangent bundle of the 2-sphere gives a classic example of a projective module that is not free. Projective modules are fundamental to homological algebra — projective resolutions are used to compute derived functors such as Tor and Ext — and to algebraic K-theory, where the class group of projective modules forms K0 of a ring. The Quillen–Suslin theorem (the former Serre conjecture) established that finitely generated projective modules over polynomial rings over a field are free.
