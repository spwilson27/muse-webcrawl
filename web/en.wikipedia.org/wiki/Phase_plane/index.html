Phase plane

A phase portrait of a simple pendulum in the phase plane.

A phase plane is a graphical representation used in the study of dynamical systems, particularly in the analysis of ordinary differential equations. For a two-dimensional autonomous system, the phase plane is the plane whose coordinates are the two state variables of the system, and each point represents a complete state. Curves traced through the plane as time evolves are called trajectories or orbits, and the collection of all trajectories forms the phase portrait.

Phase plane analysis

In phase plane analysis, the direction of motion along trajectories is determined by the vector field defined by the system of equations. Points where the vector field vanishes are equilibrium points (fixed points), which may be classified as stable or unstable nodes, saddle points, foci (spirals), or centers. Straight-line solutions where one variable is held proportional to the other, called isoclines or nullclines, help in sketching trajectories by hand; the nullclines, where each derivative is zero, intersect at equilibria.

Applications

The method is classical in the study of nonlinear oscillators, predator–prey models such as the Lotka–Volterra equations, mechanical systems such as the pendulum, and electrical circuits. Closed trajectories in the phase plane correspond to periodic oscillations, and isolated closed curves called limit cycles are important in the theory of self-sustained oscillation developed by Aleksandr Andronov and others.
