Oswiu (c. 612 – 15 February 670) was King of Northumbria from 642 until his death. The younger brother of Oswald, he succeeded in the northern kingdom of Bernicia after Oswald's death at Maserfield, while Deira in the south initially passed to other rulers; Oswiu gradually reunited the Northumbrian kingdoms under his rule.

Reign

Oswiu's early reign was dominated by conflict with Penda of Mercia, who repeatedly invaded Northumbria. On 15 November 655 Oswiu destroyed Penda's army at the Battle of the Winwaed, at which Penda was killed; Oswiu then ruled Mercia directly for a time and was recognised—briefly—as overlord of much of England. Bede lists him as the seventh king to hold imperium over the English kingdoms south of the Humber.

Synod of Whitby

In 664 Oswiu presided over the Synod of Whitby, convened to settle differences between the Irish and Roman practices in the Northumbrian church. His decision in favour of the Roman method of calculating Easter and the Roman tonsure aligned Northumbria with mainstream western Christianity and is treated by Bede as a turning point in the history of the English church.

Death

Oswiu died of illness on 15 February 670 and was buried at Whitby Abbey beside Edwin of Northumbria. He was succeeded by his son Ecgfrith. His children included kings Ecgfrith and Ælfwine, and St Ælfleda, abbess of Whitby.
