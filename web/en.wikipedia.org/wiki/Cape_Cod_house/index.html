Cape Cod house

A Cape Cod house is a low, broad, single-story frame building with a moderately steep pitched roof, a large central chimney, and very little ornamentation. Originating in New England in the 17th century, the style was adapted from English half-timbered houses but built with local materials to withstand the region's stormy, stark weather.

Cape Cod house
Type: Vernacular house style
Region: New England, United States
Era: 17th century onward; revival 1930s-1950s

History

English settlers in Massachusetts built simple, symmetrical cottages suited to the cold winters and abundant timber of the Cape Cod region. The classic form is one and a half stories, with a steep roof to shed snow, cedar shingles that weather to grey, and a central chimney serving multiple fireplaces.

Characteristics

Traditional Cape Cod houses feature a symmetrical facade with a central front door, multi-paned double-hung windows with shutters, and dormers added for light and space. The Colonial Revival of the 1930s to 1950s made the Cape Cod one of the most popular suburban house styles in the United States.
