Gascon (French: gascon; Gascon: gascon) is a variety of the Occitan language traditionally spoken in Gascony in southwestern France — roughly the territory between the Garonne, the Pyrenees and the Atlantic — and, as Aranese, in the Val d'Aran in Catalonia, Spain. Many linguists treat it as a distinct dialect group within Occitan, notable for a series of phonological features influenced by the Basque–Aquitanian substratum.

Characteristics

Gascon's most distinctive trait is the change of Latin initial f- to h- (e.g. hilha "daughter", from Latin filia), a development shared with Castilian Spanish and attributed to influence from the pre-Roman Aquitanian language related to Basque. Other features include the preservation of certain consonant clusters and characteristic endings in verbs and articles.

History and status

Gascon was the language of administration and literature in medieval Gascony and Béarn, and of the troubadour lyric in its wider Occitan form. From the early modern period it declined under pressure from French, and like other regional languages of France it is now considered endangered, though it is taught optionally in some schools and enjoys cultural promotion. In the Val d'Aran, its Aranese variety is co-official and used in local administration and education.
