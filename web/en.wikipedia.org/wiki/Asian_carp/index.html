Asian carp

Asian carp
Species included: Silver, bighead, grass, and black carp
Native to: East Asia
Status in North America: Invasive

Asian carp is a grouping of several species of heavy-bodied cyprinid fishes native to East Asia — principally the silver carp, bighead carp, grass carp, and black carp. Introduced to North America in the 1970s to control algae and plankton in aquaculture and wastewater facilities, they escaped into the Mississippi River basin and became a major invasive species problem.

Ecology and spread

Silver and bighead carp are filter feeders that consume large quantities of plankton, competing directly with native fish and mussels. They reproduce prolifically in large rivers and have spread through the Mississippi, Missouri, Illinois, and Ohio river systems. Silver carp are notorious for leaping from the water when startled by boat noise, occasionally injuring boaters.

Control efforts

Preventing Asian carp from reaching the Great Lakes — where they could threaten a multibillion-dollar fishing industry — has motivated electric barrier systems in the Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal, intensive commercial harvesting, and monitoring using environmental DNA (eDNA). Management agencies have promoted harvesting the fish for food and other products, and the state of Illinois rebranded the fish as "Copi" in 2022 to encourage consumption.

Use as food

In China the four species — the "four great domestic fishes" — have been farmed in polyculture for centuries and are prized food fish; their poor reputation in the United States stems partly from the North American association of "carp" with the unrelated common carp.
