Shekhawati

Shekhawati is a semi-arid historical region in northeastern Rajasthan, India, comprising roughly the present-day districts of Jhunjhunu, Sikar and Churu. It takes its name from Rao Shekha, a 15th-century Rajput chieftain of the Shekhawat clan who established his rule there around 1471.

The painted havelis

Shekhawati is famous as an "open-air art gallery": its towns — Mandawa, Nawalgarh, Fatehpur, Jhunjhunu, Ramgarh and others — are filled with havelis (mansions) built between the late 18th and early 20th centuries by wealthy Marwari merchant families who had made fortunes in trade in Calcutta, Bombay and elsewhere, then spent lavishly at home. The walls are covered with frescoes mixing Hindu mythology (Krishna legends, episodes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata) with scenes of contemporary life — trains, motorcars, Europeans, aeroplanes and gramophones — painted in mineral and later synthetic pigments. The region also has stepwells, temples, cenotaphs (chhatris) and forts.

Economy and heritage

Many Marwari business dynasties of modern India trace their origins to Shekhawati's villages. In recent decades many havelis have decayed through neglect and divided ownership, though some have been restored as heritage hotels, and the region draws growing cultural tourism.
