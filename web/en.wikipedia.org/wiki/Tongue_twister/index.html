Tongue twister

A tongue twister is a phrase that is designed to be difficult to articulate properly, and can be used as a type of spoken (or sung) word game. Tongue twisters rely on sequences of similar but distinct phonemes, quick alternation between similar sounds, or unfamiliar combinations of syllables.

Famous English examples include "She sells seashells by the seashore", "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers", and "How much wood would a woodchuck chuck". The difficulty arises because the speech organs must switch rapidly between nearly identical positions, and the brain's planning of the phrase tends to substitute or swap sounds.

Uses

Tongue twisters are used in speech therapy and elocution training to improve articulation and clarity, by actors and broadcasters as warm-up exercises, and in children's play and language teaching. Similar constructions exist in most languages, often exploiting sounds that are especially hard for learners of that language.
