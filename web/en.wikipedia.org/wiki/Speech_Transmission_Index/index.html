Speech Transmission Index

The Speech Transmission Index (STI) is a measure that represents how well a transmission channel preserves the intelligibility of speech. It is expressed on a scale from 0 (completely unintelligible) to 1 (perfect intelligibility).

Method

STI is based on the modulation transfer function: a test signal consisting of noise in seven octave bands (125 Hz–8 kHz), each amplitude-modulated at 14 modulation frequencies, is played through the channel (a room, PA system, or electroacoustic chain). Reductions in modulation depth caused by reverberation, echoes, and background noise are measured and combined into a single index.

Scale and applications

Values below 0.3 are rated "bad", 0.45–0.6 "fair", 0.6–0.75 "good", and above 0.75 "excellent". STI (and its public-address variant STIPA) is prescribed by standards such as IEC 60268-16 and used to certify voice alarm and emergency sound systems, and to assess classrooms, stations, and places of worship.
