Choi Soon-sil

Choi Soon-sil in 2016

Choi Soon-sil (born June 23, 1956) is a South Korean woman who was at the center of the 2016 South Korean political scandal that led to the impeachment and removal of President Park Geun-hye. Choi, a longtime confidante of Park holding no official government position, was revealed to have extensive undue influence over the president, reviewing and editing official speeches, influencing policy and personnel decisions, and using her connections to extract large donations from Korean conglomerates for foundations she controlled.

Background

Choi is the daughter of Choi Tae-min, a shadowy religious figure who founded a syncretic movement and became a mentor to Park Geun-hye after the assassination of her mother in 1974. Choi Soon-sil's friendship with Park continued for four decades. Her daughter, Chung Yoo-ra, became embroiled in a related admissions scandal at Ewha Womans University.

Scandal and trial

The scandal broke in October 2016 when media reported that a tablet computer belonging to Choi contained presidential documents, including draft speeches. Mass candlelight protests drew millions of South Koreans into the streets. Park was impeached by the National Assembly in December 2016 and removed by the Constitutional Court in March 2017. In February 2018, Choi was convicted of abuse of power, bribery, and coercion and sentenced to 20 years in prison, with the sentence later adjusted through appeals and retrial to 18 years.
