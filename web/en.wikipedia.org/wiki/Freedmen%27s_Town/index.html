Founded1865
DesignationNational Register of Historic Places district (1985)

Brick streets laid by residents in Freedmen's Town

Freedmen's Town is a historic African American community in the Fourth Ward of Houston, Texas. It was founded in 1865 by formerly enslaved people immediately after emancipation in Texas was announced on Juneteenth, and grew into one of the most important Black communities in the American South.

History

Freed people settled along the southern bank of Buffalo Bayou, purchasing lots, building homes, churches, and businesses. Residents themselves paved several streets with bricks in the early twentieth century — the hand-laid brick streets of Andrews and Wilson streets survive today. Institutions such as Antioch Missionary Baptist Church, founded in 1866, anchored the community, and by the early 1900s Freedmen's Town was the centre of Black economic and cultural life in Houston.

Decline and preservation

From the mid-twentieth century, highway construction, urban renewal, and later gentrification destroyed much of the neighbourhood; the construction of Interstate 45 and the Allen Parkway Village redevelopment displaced many residents. In 1985 the Freedmen's Town Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places, and the Houston Freedmen's Town Conservancy now works with the city to preserve the remaining houses, churches, and brick streets.
