Michael E. Krauss (15 August 1934 – 11 August 2019) was an American linguist known for his work on the Indigenous languages of Alaska and for founding the Alaska Native Language Center at the University of Alaska Fairbanks in 1972. He documented the Eyak language extensively and worked with its last native speaker, Marie Smith Jones.

Career

Krauss's 1960s fieldwork produced grammars, dictionaries, and text collections for languages including Eyak, Lower Tanana, and Haida. In 1992 he published the influential paper "The world's languages in danger", which galvanized the modern language endangerment and documentation movement with its estimate that a large majority of the world's languages could cease to be learned by children within a century.

Legacy

The Alaska Native Language Archive, which Krauss helped build, preserves recordings and manuscripts for all Alaska Native languages and serves as a model for indigenous language archives worldwide.
