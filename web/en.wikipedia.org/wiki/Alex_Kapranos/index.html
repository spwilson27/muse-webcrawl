Alexander Paul Kapranos Huntley (born 20 March 1972) is a British musician, singer and songwriter best known as the lead vocalist and guitarist of the Scottish indie rock band Franz Ferdinand. With the band he won the 2004 Mercury Prize for their self-titled debut album and became one of the defining acts of the 2000s post-punk revival.

Early life

Kapranos was born in Almondsbury, near Bristol, to a Greek father and an English mother. He spent part of his childhood in Greece before the family settled in Scotland, where he grew up in Edinburgh and Glasgow. He studied English literature at the University of Aberdeen and later theology at the University of Glasgow, working a variety of jobs — including as a chef, barman and welder — while playing in Glasgow bands such as The Karelia.

Franz Ferdinand

In 2001 Kapranos formed Franz Ferdinand in Glasgow with Nick McCarthy, Bob Hardy and Paul Thomson. The band's angular, danceable art-rock sound brought them rapid success: their debut single "Darts of Pleasure" (2003) was followed by the album Franz Ferdinand (2004), which won the Mercury Prize, and the single "Take Me Out", an international hit. Subsequent albums include You Could Have It So Much Better (2005), Tonight: Franz Ferdinand (2009) and Right Thoughts, Right Words, Right Action (2013). In 2015 the band collaborated with Sparks as the supergroup FFS.

Other work

Kapranos has written about food, including the column and book Sound Bites, drawn from his experiences eating on tour. He has also produced records for other artists and presented radio documentaries. In 2024 Franz Ferdinand released the single "Audacious" ahead of the album The Human Fear (2025).
