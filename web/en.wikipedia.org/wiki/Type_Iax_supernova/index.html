Type Iax supernova

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Type Iax supernovae are a class of supernovae similar to Type Ia supernovae but with lower peak luminosity and lower ejecta velocities. The class was formally proposed in 2013, with the prototypical member SN 2002cx.

Characteristics

Type Iax events reach peak absolute magnitudes of roughly −14 to −18.5, making them substantially fainter than normal Type Ia supernovae, and their spectra show expansion velocities of only about 2,000–8,000 km/s. Dozens of members are now known, making them a significant fraction of thermonuclear supernovae.

Progenitor model

The leading explanation is a pure deflagration of a carbon–oxygen white dwarf: a subsonic burning flame consumes only part of the star and fails to unbind it completely, ejecting some material while leaving behind a bound, "puffed-up" remnant. Helium-star companion systems, with accretion of helium onto the white dwarf, are favoured progenitors.

Remnants

The surviving remnant may persist as an unusual, inflated white dwarf enriched in heavy elements; the hypervelocity white dwarf LP 40-365 and similar objects have been proposed as examples of such survivors.
