Denitrification

Denitrification is a microbially facilitated process where nitrate (NO3−) is reduced and ultimately produces molecular nitrogen (N2) through a series of intermediate gaseous nitrogen oxide products. Denitrifying bacteria are facultative anaerobic bacteria that perform denitrification as a type of respiration that reduces oxidized forms of nitrogen in response to the oxidation of an electron donor such as organic matter.

Process

The process is performed primarily by heterotrophic bacteria (such as Pseudomonas and Clostridium) in anaerobic conditions. The steps of reduction are:

NO3− → NO2− → NO → N2O → N2

Denitrification generally proceeds through some combination of the following half-reactions, with the enzyme catalyzing each step in parentheses: nitrate reductase, nitrite reductase, nitric oxide reductase, and nitrous oxide reductase.

Significance

Denitrification is an important process in the nitrogen cycle, returning fixed nitrogen to the atmosphere. In agriculture it can cause the loss of nitrogen fertilizer from soils, reducing crop yields. It is also exploited in wastewater treatment to remove nitrogen compounds that would otherwise cause eutrophication of receiving waters. However, the intermediate product nitrous oxide (N2O) is a potent greenhouse gas, and incomplete denitrification in soils is a significant source of it.

Conditions

Denitrification requires anoxic conditions, a source of nitrate, and available organic carbon as an electron donor. It occurs in waterlogged soils, sediments, groundwater, and engineered systems such as constructed wetlands and denitrifying bioreactors.
