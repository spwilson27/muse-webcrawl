Job vacancy

Job vacancy
DefinitionAn unoccupied position for which an employer is actively recruiting
Key statisticJob vacancy rate = vacancies / (vacancies + occupied posts)
Measured byJOLTS (United States); Eurostat job vacancy statistics (EU)

A job vacancy is a post, either newly created, unoccupied, or about to become vacant, for which an employer is taking active steps to find a suitable candidate from outside the enterprise. Job vacancies are a key labour-market indicator: the number and rate of vacancies measure unmet demand for labour and, together with unemployment, define the Beveridge curve.

Measurement

In the United States, the Bureau of Labor Statistics' Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS) has published monthly job openings data since 2000; a "job opening" requires that a specific position exists, work could start within 30 days, and the employer is actively recruiting. In the European Union, Eurostat collects quarterly job vacancy statistics from member states, defining the job vacancy rate as vacancies divided by the sum of occupied posts and vacancies.

Economic interpretation

High vacancy rates alongside high unemployment may indicate mismatch between workers' skills or locations and employers' needs (structural unemployment). The ratio of unemployed persons to job openings is watched as a measure of labour-market tightness and wage pressure; it fell below 1.0 in the United States in 2018 and reached historic extremes during the post-pandemic "Great Resignation" period of 2021–2022, when U.S. openings exceeded 12 million.

Filling a vacancy

Employers typically advertise a vacancy in a job posting describing duties, qualifications, and terms, then screen applicants through application review and a job interview process before making an offer.
