His Majesty's Most Loyal Opposition (United Kingdom)

His Majesty's Most Loyal Opposition, commonly known as the Official Opposition, is the main political opposition party in the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It is the largest political party in the House of Commons that is not in government, and its leader is the Leader of the Opposition.

Origin of the phrase

The expression "His Majesty's Opposition" is credited to the MP John Cam Hobhouse, who coined it in jest in Parliament in 1826; the addition of "loyal" expressed the key constitutional point that opposing the government of the day was not disloyalty to the Crown or the state. The notion of a loyal opposition is one of the foundations of the Westminster system, and it distinguishes parliamentary opposition from sedition: the opposition is expected to scrutinise, criticise, and offer itself as an alternative government.

Role and privileges

The Official Opposition forms a shadow cabinet, whose members shadow each government department and question ministers. It receives dedicated parliamentary time, including twenty "opposition days" per session in the Commons when it chooses the subject for debate, and its leader questions the prime minister at the start of Prime Minister's Questions. The Leader of the Opposition receives a statutory salary in addition to an MP's pay, a provision first made in 1937, and is usually appointed a privy counsellor. The opposition also receives public funding, known as Short money in the Commons, to support its parliamentary work.

Official status

The Official Opposition is normally simply the second-largest party in the Commons, but in unusual circumstances — such as grand coalitions or disputed standings — the Speaker may have to determine which party is entitled to the position. When the same party is not involved, smaller parties are collectively called "the opposition" but do not hold the official role or its privileges.
