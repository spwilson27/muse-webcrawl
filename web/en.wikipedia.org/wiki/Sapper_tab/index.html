Sapper tab

The Sapper tab is a military badge of the United States Army authorised for soldiers who graduate from the Sapper Leader Course at Fort Leonard Wood, Missouri. It is one of only four permanent individual skill tabs authorised for wear on the Army uniform, alongside the Ranger, Special Forces, and President's Hundred tabs.

Description

The tab is a cloth arc worn above the unit shoulder sleeve insignia on the left sleeve. The full-colour version is a red tab with white lettering reading "SAPPER"; subdued versions in olive drab and camouflage patterns are worn on field uniforms.

Sapper Leader Course

The Sapper Leader Course is a demanding 28-day course run by the 169th Engineer Battalion at the U.S. Army Engineer School. It trains combat engineers and other soldiers in small-unit tactics and combat engineering, including demolitions, breaching, field fortifications, water operations, mountaineering, and patrolling. The course traces its origins to sapper training established in the 1980s, and the tab was formally approved for wear in 2004, with its award made retroactive to earlier graduates.
