Gochujang (Korean: 고추장) is a savory, sweet, and spicy fermented chili paste used throughout Korean cuisine. It is made from gochugaru (red chili powder), glutinous rice, powdered fermented soybeans (meju), barley malt, and salt, and is traditionally fermented in large earthenware pots called onggi. Its heat comes from capsaicin in Korean red peppers, measured in Gochujang Heat Units (GHU).

History

Chili peppers reached Korea from the Americas via Japan in the late 16th century, around the time of the Imjin War. Recipes for chili paste appear in 18th-century Korean texts such as the Jeungbo Sallim Gyeongje. The Sunchang region of North Jeolla Province is especially famous for its gochujang.

Uses

Gochujang is the defining seasoning of tteokbokki (spicy rice cakes), a base for stews and marinades, the sauce for bibimbap, and a dip (with sesame oil) for grilled meats; thinned with vinegar it becomes chogochujang, served with raw fish (hoe). In modern fusion cooking it appears in pasta sauces, fried chicken glazes, and mayonnaise.

Production

Traditional production ferments the paste for months outdoors; commercial production accelerates the process. Commercial brands are graded by spiciness from mild to extreme.
