Tanbur

The tanbur (Persian: تنبور) is a family of long-necked lutes, a string instrument originating in Mesopotamia and Central Asia. It is the ancestor of many long-necked lutes, including the Turkish bağlama family, the setar, and the dutar, and the term survives in instrument names across the Balkans (tambura) and South Asia (tanpura).

History

Long-necked lutes appear in Akkadian iconography from the third millennium BCE. The tanbur is prominently mentioned in Persian literature and was regarded by the medieval theorist al-Farabi as an important instrument. In Kurdistan, especially Kermanshah province, the tanbur holds a sacred role in the Yarsan (Ahl-e Haqq) faith, where its music accompanies ritual zikr and the hymns of the kalam.

Construction and playing

The Kurdish tanbur has a small pear-shaped body carved from a single block of mulberry wood, a long slender neck with tied frets, and three metal strings usually tuned in fourths or fifths. Unlike most lutes it is played with the full hand — the fingers strike and pluck together in a distinctive rolling technique — rather than with a plectrum.
