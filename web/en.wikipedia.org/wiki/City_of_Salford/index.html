City of Salford

The City of Salford is a metropolitan borough with city status in Greater Manchester, England, named after its main settlement, Salford. The borough, created in 1974, also includes the towns of Eccles, Swinton, Walkden and Worsley, and the village of Irlam. It has a population of roughly 270,000.

History

Salford, a mill town at the western end of the Manchester conurbation, grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution as a centre of textile manufacture, stimulated by the Bridgewater Canal (1761) and the Manchester Ship Canal (1894). The historic county borough of Salford received city status in 1926; the present metropolitan borough was formed on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972.

Regeneration

Since the 2000s, Salford Quays has been redeveloped as MediaCityUK, home to BBC North and ITV Granada, alongside the Imperial War Museum North and The Lowry arts centre. The University of Salford, founded as a technical institute in 1896, is a major local institution.
