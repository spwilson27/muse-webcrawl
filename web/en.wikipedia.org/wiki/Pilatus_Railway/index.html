The Pilatus Railway (German: Pilatusbahn) is a rack railway in Switzerland running from Alpnachstad, on Lake Lucerne, to the summit station of Pilatus Kulm on Mount Pilatus. With a maximum gradient of 48% (480 per mille) it is the steepest rack railway in the world.

Locher system

Ordinary rack systems could not hold a train on such a grade, so engineer Eduard Locher designed a unique arrangement: a double rack laid horizontally between the rails, engaged by two horizontal cogwheels under the car whose flanges grip beneath the rack heads, preventing the car from lifting or derailing. Because the cogwheels are horizontal, conventional points cannot be used; the line instead uses transfer tables and track turntables to move cars between tracks.

History and operation

The line opened with steam traction on 4 June 1889 and was electrified in 1937 at 1,550 V DC. The journey of 4.6 km climbs 1,635 m and takes about 30 minutes up and 40 minutes down. The line operates seasonally, roughly May to November, since snow closes the exposed upper section in winter. Its distinctive railcars have slanted bodies stepped to the slope, and a new generation of double railcars entered service in the 2020s.
