Ossicles

Ossicles

Part ofMiddle ear
BonesMalleus, incus, stapes
FunctionTransmission and amplification of sound vibrations

The ossicles (also called the auditory ossicles) are three tiny bones in the middle ear of mammals that form a chain transmitting sound vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear. They are the malleus (hammer), the incus (anvil), and the stapes (stirrup) — the smallest bones in the human body.

Structure

The malleus, about 8 millimetres long, is attached to the inner surface of the tympanic membrane. It articulates with the incus, which in turn connects to the stapes. The base of the stapes, the footplate, sits in the oval window of the cochlea. The chain is suspended by ligaments within the tympanic cavity and is acted on by two small muscles, the tensor tympani and the stapedius, which dampen excessive vibration.

Function

Airborne sound is a poor match for the fluid-filled inner ear: without help, most energy would be reflected at the boundary. The ossicles act as a lever system, and the large area of the eardrum compared with the small stapes footplate concentrates pressure, together providing an impedance-matching gain of roughly 22 times. This allows airborne sound to drive fluid motion in the cochlea efficiently.

Development and evolution

The ossicles are among the first bones to ossify fully in the developing foetus. Evolutionarily, they are a celebrated example of structural repurposing: the malleus and incus derive from bones that formed part of the jaw joint in early tetrapods and mammal ancestors — the articular and quadrate — while the stapes derives from the hyomandibula. Fossil intermediates document the gradual migration of these jaw elements into the ear as the mammalian dentary–squamosal jaw joint took over.

Clinical significance

Damage or fixation of the ossicles causes conductive hearing loss. In otosclerosis the stapes becomes fixed to the oval window; cholesteatoma and chronic ear infection can erode the chain, most often the long process of the incus. Surgical repair, ossiculoplasty, may use the patient's own remodelled bone or prostheses of titanium or other materials.
