Polish Armed Forces in the West

The Polish Armed Forces in the West were the military formations of the Polish government-in-exile that fought alongside the Western Allies during the Second World War. Formed first in France after the fall of Poland in 1939 and re-formed in Britain after the fall of France, they grew to roughly 200,000 personnel by 1945, making them one of the largest Allied contingents.

Major formations

Polish airmen flew with the Royal Air Force, most famously No. 303 (Kościuszko) Squadron, the highest-scoring Hurricane squadron of the Battle of Britain. The Polish Navy operated destroyers and submarines under Royal Navy command. On land, the 1st Armoured Division under General Stanisław Maczek fought in Normandy and liberated Breda; the 1st Independent Parachute Brigade dropped at Arnhem; and General Władysław Anders's II Corps, formed from Poles released from Soviet captivity, captured Monte Cassino in May 1944 and fought up the Italian peninsula.

Aftermath

After the war, the Western powers recognised the Soviet-backed government in Warsaw, and most Polish servicemen in the West were unable or unwilling to return to a communist Poland. The Polish Resettlement Corps helped them settle in Britain and elsewhere; they were not invited to march in the London Victory Parade of 1946, a slight remembered with bitterness.
