British Aerospace Harrier II

The British Aerospace Harrier II was a second-generation vertical/short takeoff and landing (V/STOL) ground-attack aircraft used by the Royal Air Force and, in Sea Harrier form's successor role, operated jointly with the Royal Navy. Developed from the American-designed McDonnell Douglas AV-8B Harrier II, the British variants — GR5, GR7, GR9 and the T10/T12 trainers — featured a larger composite wing, improved avionics, and greater payload than the first-generation Harrier.

Development and service

Whereas the AV-8B was built around American requirements, the RAF's Harrier II substituted British avionics and systems and later added night-attack capability (GR7) and the integrated weapons programme, new Pegasus 107 engine and improved systems of the GR9. A planned naval variant was dropped, and from 2006 RAF and Fleet Air Arm Harrier IIs were operated as the combined Joint Force Harrier.

Harrier IIs flew combat missions over the Balkans in the 1990s, in Iraq, and in Afghanistan from Kandahar and HMS Illustrious. In the 2010 Strategic Defence and Security Review the type was retired abruptly, with the last flights on 15 December 2010; the airframes were subsequently sold to the United States Marine Corps as a source of spares for its AV-8B fleet.
