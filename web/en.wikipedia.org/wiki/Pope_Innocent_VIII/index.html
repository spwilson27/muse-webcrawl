Pope Innocent VIII

Pope Innocent VIII (Latin: Innocentius VIII; born Giovanni Battista Cybo; 1432 – 25 July 1492) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 29 August 1484 until his death. He is remembered for the papal bull Summis desiderantes affectibus (1484), which encouraged the persecution of alleged witches in Germany, and for his open acknowledgement of his children, a first among Renaissance popes.

Pontificate

Born in Genoa, Cybo rose through posts at the courts of Naples and Rome, becoming a cardinal in 1473. Elected pope after the death of Sixtus IV, Innocent pursued an inconstant foreign policy, quarrelling with King Ferdinand I of Naples and encouraging the Barons' Conspiracy against him. His bull of 1484 empowered the inquisitors Heinrich Kramer and Jacob Sprenger, whose work produced the notorious witch-hunting manual Malleus Maleficarum.

Family and court

Unlike predecessors who disguised their offspring as "nephews", Innocent openly recognised his son Franceschetto Cybo and daughter Teodorina, marrying Franceschetto to Maddalena de' Medici, daughter of Lorenzo the Magnificent — an alliance that helped bring a Medici, Giovanni (later Leo X), to a cardinal's hat at thirteen. Financially pressed, Innocent sold offices on a large scale and notoriously pawned papal treasures. His tomb in St. Peter's Basilica is the only surviving original monument from Old St. Peter's.
