Jalebi

Jalebi is a popular sweet snack of South Asia and the Middle East, made by deep-frying a fermented wheat-flour (maida) batter in pretzel or circular coils and soaking them in sugar syrup. The result is crisp, glossy, and intensely sweet, often flavoured with saffron, cardamom, or rose water.

History

The sweet derives from the Middle Eastern zalabiya and Persian zulbiya, which travelled to the Indian subcontinent with Persian-speaking merchants and rulers during the medieval period. Jalebi is mentioned in Indian cookery texts from the fifteenth century onward and has become one of the subcontinent's most ubiquitous sweets, sold by street vendors and sweet shops across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nepal.

Preparation and serving

Batter is piped through a small nozzle or cloth directly into hot oil in spiral shapes, fried until crisp, and immediately immersed in warm syrup. Jalebi is eaten fresh and warm or at room temperature. It is commonly served with rabri (thickened sweet milk), curd, or poha for breakfast in parts of India, and is a standard offering at festivals and weddings. Regional variants include imarti (made from urad dal flour), the thicker jangri of South India, and paneer or khoya jalebis.
