Portopulmonary hypertension

Portopulmonary hypertension (POPH) is a form of pulmonary arterial hypertension that occurs in association with portal hypertension, most often due to cirrhosis. It is defined by pulmonary arterial hypertension in the setting of portal hypertension, with a raised mean pulmonary artery pressure and pulmonary vascular resistance and a normal pulmonary artery wedge pressure on right heart catheterization.

Pathophysiology

Portal hypertension allows vasoactive substances that would normally be cleared by the liver to bypass it via portosystemic shunts and reach the pulmonary circulation, while a hyperdynamic circulatory state increases pulmonary blood flow and shear stress. Over time this produces obstructive remodelling of small pulmonary arteries similar to other forms of pulmonary arterial hypertension. It affects a small percentage of patients with portal hypertension and is distinct from hepatopulmonary syndrome, which involves intrapulmonary vascular dilatation and hypoxemia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Screening is by transthoracic echocardiography in liver transplant candidates, with confirmation by right heart catheterization. Treatment uses pulmonary arterial hypertension therapies (endothelin receptor antagonists, phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors, prostacyclins), and liver transplantation may be considered in carefully selected, well-controlled cases; severe POPH increases transplant risk.
