Economic, social and cultural rights

Economic, social and cultural rights (ESCR)
"Second-generation" human rights
Key treaty: ICESCR (1966)

Economic, social and cultural rights (ESCR) are human rights concerning the basic material and cultural conditions of life, including the rights to work, social security, an adequate standard of living, housing, food, health, and education, and the right to participate in cultural life.

Legal framework

ESCR are set out in Articles 22–28 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and codified in the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR, 1966), monitored by the UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. Unlike the mainly immediate obligations attached to civil and political rights, ICESCR rights are generally subject to "progressive realisation" to the maximum of a state's available resources, though non-discrimination and minimum core obligations apply immediately.

Debates

Critics have questioned whether ESCR are justiciable — capable of enforcement by courts — but many constitutions, including those of South Africa and India, make at least some socio-economic rights enforceable, and courts have issued landmark rulings on housing, water, and access to medicines.
