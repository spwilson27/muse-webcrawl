Garmo Stave Church

Garmo Stave Church (Norwegian: Garmo stavkirke) is a Norwegian stave church originally built around 1200 in the village of Garmo in Lom municipality, Oppland. When a new church replaced it in 1879, the old stave church was dismantled; its timbers were eventually acquired by Anders Sandvig and re-erected in 1921 at the Maihaugen open-air museum in Lillehammer, where it stands today.

History and architecture

The church was built in several stages, with a chancel added in the 17th century and a transept in 1690 giving it a cruciform plan. Like other stave churches, it is a timber structure raised on vertical staves, with carved portals and tarred shingle roofing. After demolition in 1879, parts of the building material were scattered; Sandvig collected the pieces over decades, and the church was reconstructed at Maihaugen and consecrated in 1921.

Today

Garmo Stave Church is one of the most accessible stave churches in Norway, forming part of Maihaugen's rural collection. It remains a consecrated church and is used for services, weddings and baptisms, including a notable font dating to the 12th century.
