Galanthus nivalis

Galanthus nivalis, the snowdrop or common snowdrop, is the best-known and most widespread of the 20 or so species in the genus Galanthus. Snowdrops are among the first bulbs to bloom in spring and can form impressive carpets of white in areas where they are native or have been naturalised. The scientific name means "milk flower of the snow".

The common snowdrop flowers from January to March in the wild.

Description

Galanthus nivalis grows to about 7–15 cm tall. The leaves are glaucous and strap-like, emerging with or before the flowers. Each bulb produces a single nodding white flower on a slender stalk; the flower has six tepals — three larger outer ones and three smaller inner ones, the inner marked with a green or greenish-yellow blotch at the tip.

Distribution and habitat

The species is native to a large area of Europe, from Spain and France eastwards to Ukraine, in damp woodlands and meadows. It has been widely introduced and is naturalised in the British Isles (where it is unlikely to be native), North America, and elsewhere.

Cultivation

Numerous cultivars have been selected, including double-flowered forms and the vigorous hybrid-like selection 'S. Arnott'. Enthusiasts of the genus are known as "galanthophiles". Snowdrops are a source of the alkaloid galantamine, used in treating Alzheimer's disease, though in this species the compound is more characteristic of its relative G. woronowii. All parts of the plant are poisonous if eaten in quantity.
