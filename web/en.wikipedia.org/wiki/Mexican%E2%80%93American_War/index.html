Mexican–American War

Date25 April 1846 – 3 February 1848
LocationTexas, northern and central Mexico, California
ResultAmerican victory; Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

The Mexican–American War (1846–1848) was an invasion of Mexico by the United States Army. It followed the 1845 American annexation of Texas, which Mexico still considered its territory because it refused to recognize the Treaties of Velasco, signed by President Antonio López de Santa Anna after the Texas Revolution a decade earlier.

President James K. Polk, a proponent of Manifest Destiny, sought to expand United States territory. After the Thornton Affair on the Rio Grande, Congress declared war in May 1846.

Course of the war

American forces under Zachary Taylor won victories at Palo Alto, Monterrey, and Buena Vista in the north, while Winfield Scott's army landed at Veracruz and captured Mexico City in September 1847.

Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

The war ended with the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), in which Mexico ceded roughly half of its territory — including California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, and parts of other present-day states — to the United States in exchange for 15 million dollars.
