Talk radio

Talk radio is a radio format consisting primarily of discussion and conversation rather than music. Typically it involves a host discussing current events, politics, sport or other topics with guests and with listeners who telephone the programme, and it may also include radio news, interviews and features. Talk radio stations are usually found on the AM band in the United States, though the format is common on FM and public radio elsewhere.

History

Phone-in programmes began in the United States in the 1940s and 1950s, pioneered by hosts such as Barry Gray in New York. The format expanded as rock-and-roll music migrated to FM in the 1970s, leaving AM stations to adopt talk programming. In the United States its modern scale dates from the FCC's repeal of the Fairness Doctrine in 1987, which freed stations from the obligation to present contrasting views, and from the national syndication of Rush Limbaugh's conservative programme from 1988. Limbaugh's success made political talk — overwhelmingly conservative in the commercial syndicated market — a dominant radio genre, followed by hosts such as Sean Hannity and Glenn Beck; liberal "Air America" ventures proved less durable, though public radio talk and sports talk flourished in parallel.

Other countries

In the United Kingdom, talk formats include BBC Radio 4's news and discussion programming, the phone-in network station LBC (founded 1973), talkSPORT and BBC Radio 5 Live. In Australia, "talkback radio" is a major political force, with influential presenters in Sydney and Melbourne. In many countries, phone-ins and discussion programmes are a staple of public-service broadcasting as well as commercial stations.

Formats and influence

Sub-formats include hot talk, sports talk, advice and counselling shows, and paranormal or overnight programmes such as Coast to Coast AM. Critics argue that talk radio rewards outrage and misinformation; supporters credit it with giving audiences a direct, unfiltered forum. Its audience-participation model anticipated later interactive media, and many prominent podcasters and streaming commentators descend from the format.
