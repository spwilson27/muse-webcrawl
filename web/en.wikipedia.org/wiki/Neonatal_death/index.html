Neonatal death

Neonatal death is the death of a newborn within the first 28 days of life. Deaths in the first seven days are termed early neonatal deaths, and those from day 7 to day 28 late neonatal deaths. The neonatal mortality rate (NMR) is the number of such deaths per 1,000 live births and is a key indicator of a population's health and quality of maternal and newborn care.

Causes

Globally, the leading causes of neonatal death are complications of preterm birth, intrapartum-related events (such as birth asphyxia), sepsis and other infections, and congenital abnormalities. Most neonatal deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, and the large majority happen in the first week of life.

Scale and trends

Worldwide neonatal deaths fell from about 5 million in 1990 to roughly 2.3–2.4 million in recent years, but progress has been slower than for older children, so newborns account for a growing share of under-five mortality — close to half. Interventions with proven impact include skilled birth attendance, antenatal corticosteroids, kangaroo mother care for preterm infants, early and exclusive breastfeeding, clean cord care and prompt treatment of infection.
