Color term

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A color term (or color name) is a word or phrase that refers to a specific color. The color term may refer to human perception of that color (which is affected by visual context) which is usually defined according to the Munsell color system, or to an underlying physical property (such as a specific wavelength of visible light). There are also numerical systems of color specification, referred to as color spaces.

Basic color terms

An important distinction in the study of color terms is between basic and non-basic terms. In their landmark 1969 work Basic Color Terms: Their Universality and Evolution, Brent Berlin and Paul Kay proposed that languages differ in how many basic color categories they distinguish, but that the categories themselves are drawn from a universal inventory and emerge in a predictable sequence. A basic color term is monolexemic (its meaning is not predictable from its parts), is not a hyponym of another color term, is not restricted to a narrow class of objects, and is psychologically salient for speakers.

English has eleven basic color terms: black, white, red, green, yellow, blue, brown, purple, pink, orange and grey. According to Berlin and Kay's hierarchy, a language with only two basic terms will have words for black and white (or dark and light); the third term to appear is always red; green and yellow follow; then blue; then brown; and finally purple, pink, orange and grey in any order.

Color naming across languages

Languages vary considerably in color vocabulary. Some languages historically lacked a separate term for blue, using the same word for blue and green (a phenomenon sometimes called "grue"). Russian, by contrast, distinguishes two basic blues: siniy (dark blue) and goluboy (light blue). The World Color Survey, which gathered naming data from speakers of 110 unwritten languages, broadly supported the universality of focal colors while documenting substantial variation in category boundaries.

Abstract and concrete terms

Color terms may be abstract (used purely as colors, like red) or derived from the names of colored objects (like orange, from the fruit, or turquoise, from the stone). Terms anchored to objects tend to be less basic and more context-dependent.
