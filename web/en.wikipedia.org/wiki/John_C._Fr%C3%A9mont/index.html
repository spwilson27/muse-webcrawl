John C. Frémont

John Charles Frémont (1813–1890) was an American explorer, military officer and politician, nicknamed "The Pathfinder" for his expeditions mapping the American West. He was the first candidate of the Republican Party for President of the United States, in 1856.

Explorations

As an officer in the U.S. Topographical Engineers, Frémont led five expeditions between 1842 and 1853, surveying the Oregon Trail, the Great Basin, the Sierra Nevada and California. His reports, written with his wife Jessie Benton Frémont, fired public imagination and guided emigration westward.

Politics and war

Frémont played a contentious role in the conquest of California during the Mexican–American War, served briefly as one of California's first U.S. senators, and ran for president in 1856 under the slogan "Free Soil, Free Men, Frémont". In the Civil War his unauthorised emancipation order in Missouri was revoked by Lincoln. He later served as territorial governor of Arizona.
