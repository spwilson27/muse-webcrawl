Hungry ghost

Hungry ghost is the English translation of the Sanskrit term preta (Sanskrit: प्रेत, literally "departed one"), a class of supernatural beings in Hinduism, Buddhism, and related traditions. Hungry ghosts are beings driven by intense emotional needs — especially greed, envy, addiction, and compulsive desire — in an animalistic and tormented state. They are traditionally depicted with enormous distended bellies but mouths the size of a needle's eye and necks too thin to swallow, so that they can never satisfy their hunger.

Origins and beliefs

The concept arose in India and spread throughout Asia with Buddhism. In Hindu tradition, the pretas are spirits of the dead who did not receive proper funeral rites (śrāddha) or whose desires went unfulfilled in life. In Buddhism, rebirth as a preta is one of the unhappy destinations (durgati) in saṃsāra, the result of karma generated by greed, stinginess, and obsession. The preta realm is sometimes counted among the six realms of rebirth and sometimes described as a condition "between" the animal and hell realms.

Chinese Buddhism adopted the figure as the èguǐ (餓鬼), and in Japanese tradition they are the gaki (餓鬼). Taoism also incorporated hungry ghosts, and offerings of food to wandering, uncared-for spirits became a major ritual focus.

Rituals and festivals

In Chinese tradition, the Ghost Festival (Zhongyuan) on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month is devoted to feeding hungry ghosts. Families burn paper money and paper goods, set out food offerings, and release floating lanterns to guide wandering spirits. In Japan, the corresponding observance is Obon, and specialized segaki ("feeding hungry ghosts") services are held at temples, in which priests offer rice and water while reciting sutras and the name of Amida Buddha. In Tibet, the practice of chöd and the offering of torma cakes and sur (burnt food offerings) serve a similar function, transferring merit (pariṇāmanā) to relieve the pretas' suffering.

In art and literature

Hungry ghosts are a popular subject of Asian art. The Japanese Gaki Zōshi ("Scroll of the Hungry Ghosts", 12th century) depicts pretas haunting graveyards, latrines, and riverbanks. In modern usage, "hungry ghost" has become a metaphor in psychology and addiction literature for insatiable craving — a comparison popularized in the West by writers such as physician Gabor Maté, whose book In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts (2008) examines addiction.
