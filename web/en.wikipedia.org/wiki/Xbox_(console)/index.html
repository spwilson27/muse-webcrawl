The Xbox is a home video game console manufactured by Microsoft, released on 15 November 2001 in North America. It was Microsoft's first foray into the gaming console market and the first console in the Xbox series. It competed with Sony's PlayStation 2, Nintendo's GameCube and Sega's Dreamcast in the sixth generation of consoles. The name derives from "DirectX Box", reflecting the console's origins in Microsoft's DirectX graphics technology.

Hardware

The Xbox was built substantially from PC components: a 733 MHz Intel Pentium III-derived processor, an Nvidia NV2A graphics chip closely related to the GeForce 3 series, 64 MB of unified DDR memory, and — uniquely among consoles at the time — a built-in hard disk drive and Ethernet port. The system was large and heavy, and its original "Duke" controller was criticised for its size, particularly in Japan; a smaller Controller S followed. Despite selling over 24 million units, the console was sold at a substantial loss as Microsoft bought its way into the market.

Software and Xbox Live

The Xbox launched with Halo: Combat Evolved, a first-person shooter that became the system's defining title and one of the most influential games of its generation; its sequel Halo 2 (2004) drove adoption of Xbox Live, the subscription online service launched in November 2002. Xbox Live standardised voice chat, unified friends lists and downloadable content for console gaming, ideas later adopted across the industry. Other notable titles included Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Project Gotham Racing, Fable and Ninja Gaiden.

Legacy

Support for the Xbox ended with the introduction of its successor, the Xbox 360, in 2005; Xbox Live for the original console was discontinued in 2010. The platform established Microsoft as a durable third force in consoles, and many original Xbox games remain playable through the backward-compatibility programmes of later Xbox systems.
