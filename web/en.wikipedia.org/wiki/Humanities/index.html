Humanities

The humanities are academic disciplines that study aspects of human society and culture. They investigate human experience, expression and values, using methods that are primarily critical, philosophical or historical, in contrast to the empirical approaches of the natural and social sciences.

History of the term

During the Renaissance, the studia humanitatis — grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry and moral philosophy — were contrasted with divinity and scholastic studies. The modern category of the humanities developed in the 19th and 20th centuries as universities organised knowledge into faculties.

Disciplines

Core humanities fields include history, philosophy, literature and literary studies, linguistics and modern/classical languages, religious studies, art history, musicology, law and classics. The digital humanities apply computational methods to these fields.

Role and debates

The humanities are defended as cultivating critical thinking, communication skills, cultural literacy and ethical reflection. They are also the subject of recurring debate about funding, enrolments and vocational relevance in higher education.
