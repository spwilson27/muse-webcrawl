FIM-43 Redeye

The FIM-43 Redeye was a man-portable surface-to-air missile system (MANPADS) developed by the United States. It was produced by General Dynamics and entered service in the 1960s as the first generation of American shoulder-fired anti-aircraft missiles. Its name derives from its infrared seeker, which homed on the heat of an aircraft's engine exhaust.

Design and operation

The Redeye was a fire-and-forget weapon for its time: the operator visually acquired the target, activated the missile's infrared seeker, and waited for a lock-on tone and light before firing. Its lead-sulfide seeker was uncooled and easily decoyed by flares or the sun, and it could effectively engage only targets flying away from the operator, limiting it to tail-chase engagements of slow, low-flying aircraft.

Service history

The Redeye served with U.S. forces from the late 1960s and was widely exported. It saw combat with Afghan mujahideen and in Central America during the 1980s. It was superseded by the far more capable FIM-92 Stinger, which added all-aspect engagement and improved counter-countermeasures; the last Redeyes were withdrawn from U.S. service by the mid-1990s.
