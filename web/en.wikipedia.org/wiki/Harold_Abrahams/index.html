Harold Abrahams

Harold Maurice Abrahams (15 December 1899 – 14 January 1978) was an English track and field athlete who won the gold medal in the 100 metres at the 1924 Olympic Games in Paris. His story, together with that of Eric Liddell, was dramatised in the film Chariots of Fire (1981), in which he was played by Ben Cross.

Early life and education

Abrahams was born in Bedford, the son of a Lithuanian Jewish immigrant who had made a fortune in finance. He was educated at Repton School and then at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, where he excelled at sprinting and the long jump and competed against Oxford in the annual varsity match.

Athletics career

After a disappointing showing at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics, Abrahams worked with coach Sam Mussabini, one of the first athletes to employ a professional trainer — a practice then frowned upon in British amateur sport. At Paris in 1924 he won the 100 metres in 10.6 seconds, defeating the American favourites, and took silver in the 4 × 100 metres relay. A leg injury ended his competitive career in 1925.

Later life

Abrahams became a barrister, a sports journalist, and a leading athletics administrator and statistician, serving as president of the Amateur Athletic Association. He wrote extensively on Olympic history and was regarded as the doyen of British athletics officials. He died in Enfield in 1978.
