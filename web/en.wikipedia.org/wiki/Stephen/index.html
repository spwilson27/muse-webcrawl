Stephen

Stephen is a masculine given name derived from the Greek name Stéphanos (Στέφανος), meaning "crown" or "wreath", and ultimately from stéphō, "that which surrounds". The name is most closely associated in Christianity with Saint Stephen, the first martyr (protomartyr) of the Christian Church, whose stoning is described in the Acts of the Apostles.

History and variants

The fame of Saint Stephen spread the name throughout Christian Europe. In England it was popular among the Normans and is famously borne by King Stephen, who ruled during the civil war known as the Anarchy (1135–1154). Hungary's founding king, Saint Stephen I, made the name István enduringly popular there. Cognates include French Étienne, Spanish and Italian Esteban and Stefano, German Stephan or Stefan, Polish Stefan or Szczepan, and Scandinavian Stefan or Steffen.

The spelling variants Stephen and Steven coexist in English. Although the names are pronounced identically (STEE-vən) in most contexts, some bearers of "Stephen" pronounce it STEF-en. The name was among the top ten boys' names in the United States in the early 1950s and remained common through the 1970s.

Religious significance

Saint Stephen's feast day is 26 December in the Western Church (Boxing Day in many Commonwealth countries) and 27 December in the Eastern Church. He is the patron saint of deacons, altar servers, stonemasons and horses, among others, and is traditionally depicted holding a palm frond and the stones of his execution.
