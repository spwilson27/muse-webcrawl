Ulmus glabra

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Ulmaceae
Genus: Ulmus
Species: U. glabra
Common names: Wych elm, Scots elm

Ulmus glabra, the wych elm or Scots elm, is a species of elm native to Europe and western Asia, from Ireland and Scandinavia east to the Caucasus and the Ural Mountains. It is the sole elm species native to Ireland and is especially associated with upland and montane woods.

Description

The wych elm is a large deciduous tree reaching 40 metres in height, with a broad, rounded crown. Unlike other elms it rarely produces root suckers, reproducing only by seed. Its leaves are large and rough to the touch, with a short stalk and strongly asymmetrical base; the winged samaras have the seed near the centre of the wing.

Ecology and disease

Wych elm favours moist, nutrient-rich soils along streams and in ravines. It proved somewhat less susceptible to the first pandemic of Dutch elm disease but has suffered severely from the later, more aggressive strain, particularly in northern Britain and Scandinavia.

Uses

The strong, water-resistant timber was used for water pipes, boat-building and furniture. The name "wych" derives from an Old English word for a pliant or bendable tree, reflecting the flexibility of its branches.
