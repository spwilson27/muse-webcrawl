Linz

Linz is the capital city of Upper Austria and the third-largest city in Austria, with a population of about 210,000. Located on the Danube about 30 kilometres south of the Czech border, it grew from the Roman fort of Lentia.

History

Linz was a provincial and local government city of the Holy Roman Empire, and an important trade centre. Johannes Kepler lived and worked there, discovering his third law of planetary motion while in the city (1618). Adolf Hitler spent much of his youth in Linz and designated it a "Führer city", planning grandiose rebuilding; the nearby Mauthausen concentration camp stands as a memorial to the victims of the Nazi era.

Culture

Modern Linz is known for the Ars Electronica Festival and Center devoted to electronic art, the Lentos Art Museum, and the Brucknerhaus concert hall named after composer Anton Bruckner, born nearby. It was European Capital of Culture in 2009. Its signature dessert is the Linzer torte, among the oldest known cake recipes in the world.
