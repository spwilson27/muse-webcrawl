IATA

International Air Transport Association

The IATA logo

The International Air Transport Association (IATA) is a trade association of the world's airlines, founded in Havana, Cuba, in April 1945. Headquartered in Montreal, Canada, with executive offices in Geneva, Switzerland, it represents around 300 airlines accounting for the great majority of international air traffic.

Functions

IATA develops industry standards and practices covering safety, security, ticketing and baggage, and runs financial settlement systems between airlines and travel agents (the Billing and Settlement Plan). It is well known to the public for its three-letter airport codes and two-letter airline designators, and for the IATA Operational Safety Audit (IOSA). Its Dangerous Goods Regulations govern the carriage of hazardous materials by air.

History

IATA succeeded the earlier International Air Traffic Association founded in 1919. In the regulated era it played a central role in coordinating international fares through its traffic conferences, a role that diminished with deregulation and competition-law changes, though fare-filing and settlement functions persist.
