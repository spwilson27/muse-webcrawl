Whirlaway

Whirlaway (April 2, 1938 – April 6, 1953) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse, best known as the fifth winner of the American Triple Crown, which he captured in 1941. Bred and owned by Calumet Farm and trained by Ben A. Jones, he was ridden in his Triple Crown races by the great jockey Eddie Arcaro.

Racing career

A handsome chestnut son of the English Derby winner Blenheim II, Whirlaway was a temperamental, eccentric runner nicknamed "Mr. Longtail" for his exceptionally long, flowing tail. As a two-year-old in 1940 he won several stakes and was voted American Champion Two-Year-Old Colt despite a habit of bearing out toward the outside rail in the stretch.

In 1941 he won the Kentucky Derby in a then-record time of 2:01 2/5, coming from far behind, then took the Preakness Stakes and the Belmont Stakes to complete the Triple Crown. He was named Horse of the Year in both 1941 and 1942, a season in which he won 12 of 22 starts against older horses, including the Travers Stakes — making him the first Triple Crown winner also to win the Travers, a feat long dubbed the "superfecta" of American racing. In total he won 32 of 60 starts and earned $561,161, a record for the time.

Later life

Retired to stud at Calumet, Whirlaway was later sold to French breeder Marcel Boussac and stood at Haras de Fresnay-le-Buffard, where he died in 1953. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1959, and is ranked among the top American racehorses of the 20th century.
