Son preference


Son preference is a cultural preference for male children over female children.

Son preference is a social and cultural preference for sons over daughters, historically widespread in parts of East, South, and West Asia and the Caucasus. It is rooted in patrilineal kinship systems in which sons carry the family name, inherit property, support parents in old age, and perform ancestral rites.

Consequences

Where son preference combines with small-family norms and access to sex-selective technology, it produces skewed sex ratios at birth. Demographers, following Amartya Sen's famous analysis, speak of tens of millions of "missing women" worldwide. Consequences also include discrimination against daughters in nutrition, healthcare, and education.

Change

South Korea, once strongly son-preferring, saw its sex ratio normalise from the 1990s with urbanisation, women's education, and legal change. Governments elsewhere have banned prenatal sex determination and run campaigns valuing daughters, with mixed results.
