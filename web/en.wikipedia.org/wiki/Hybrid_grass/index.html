Hybrid grass or reinforced natural grass is a sports playing surface composed mainly of natural grass whose rootzone is reinforced with synthetic fibres. The fibres are typically stitched vertically into the pitch with specialised machines, or the grass is grown in a synthetic carpet backing. The result plays and looks like natural grass but withstands considerably more use and recovers faster than an unreinforced surface.

Stitched systems

In stitched systems such as Desso GrassMaster (introduced in the 1990s) and SISGrass, machines inject artificial fibres some 20 cm deep, with a few centimetres protruding; grass roots intertwine with the fibres, anchoring the sward. A pitch may contain around 20 million fibres yet remains overwhelmingly natural grass by surface area (commonly quoted as about 3–5 percent synthetic).

Adoption

Hybrid pitches became standard in top-level association football: Wembley Stadium, most Premier League grounds, and FIFA World Cup stadiums (including the 2018 and 2022 tournaments) use them. They are also used in rugby league and union, American football, and at golf venues. The reinforced surface allows multi-use stadiums to host concerts and multiple teams without the turf breaking up in winter.

Advantages and criticism

Advantages include stability (fewer divots and less slipping), consistent ball roll, longer playing hours and faster regrowth. Criticisms include installation cost, the presence of microplastics/synthetic material in an otherwise natural surface, and debates over player injury profiles compared with pure grass or full artificial turf.
