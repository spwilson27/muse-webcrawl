Renga

Renga (連歌, "linked poem") is a genre of Japanese collaborative poetry in which alternating stanzas, or ku (句), of 5-7-5 and 7-7 mora (sound units), are linked in succession by multiple poets. It developed from the tanka and reached its height in the medieval and early modern periods. The opening verse of a renga sequence, the hokku, later developed into the independent haiku form.

Forms

The two most common forms are the usui-style hundred-verse sequence (hyakuin) and the thirty-six-verse kasen, named for the thirty-six poetic immortals. A renga master (sōshō) presided over sessions, judging verses and enforcing the elaborate rules of linking: each verse must link to the one immediately before it, while avoiding repetition of imagery and following prescribed patterns of seasonal themes, moon and flower verses at fixed positions.

History

Collaborative linked verse emerged in the Heian and Kamakura periods, first as short tanrenga. In the 15th century poets such as Sōgi elevated the "serious" renga (ushin) to a refined art. In the Edo period, the lighter, comic haikai no renga (later called renku) flourished under poets such as Matsuo Bashō, whose sequences are still celebrated.
