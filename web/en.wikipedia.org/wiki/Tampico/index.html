Tampico is a city and port in the southeastern corner of the Mexican state of Tamaulipas, on the north bank of the Pánuco River near its mouth on the Gulf of Mexico. Together with Ciudad Madero and Altamira it forms a major industrial-port metropolitan area.

History

The name derives from the Huastec Tam-Piko, "place of otters". An earlier town was destroyed by pirates in the 1680s and the modern city was founded in 1823. Tampico became famous during Mexico's first oil boom in the early 20th century, when fields around it made it one of the world's great petroleum ports, and it was the scene of the 1914 Tampico Affair, a diplomatic incident that led to the U.S. occupation of Veracruz. In 1829 it was the site of a major patriotic victory when Mexican forces under Santa Anna defeated a Spanish expedition attempting to reconquer Mexico.

Modern city

Today Tampico is a commercial fishing and cargo port with petrochemical industries, noted for its historic centre of early-20th-century oil-era architecture, the Plaza de Armas, and the Metropolitan Cathedral. Local traditions include the jaiba (crab) cuisine and the "Tampico" meat cuts; the city lies in a low-lying lagoon system and was famously the source of a long-running local legend about UFOs protecting the area from hurricanes.
