Chapultepec Castle (Spanish: Castillo de Chapultepec) is located on top of Chapultepec Hill in Mexico City's Chapultepec park. The castle, at 2,325 metres above sea level, was built during the Viceroyalty of New Spain as a summer house for the viceroy. It is the only royal castle in North America that actually served as a residence for royalty — the Habsburg Emperor Maximilian I and Empress Carlota lived there from 1864 until the fall of the Second Mexican Empire.

History

Construction began in 1785 under Viceroy Bernardo de Gálvez. After independence the building became the Heroic Military Academy. During the Mexican–American War it was the scene of the Battle of Chapultepec on 13 September 1847, remembered for the Niños Héroes, six teenage cadets who died in its defence. Under Maximilian and Carlota the castle was remodelled in neoclassical style with European furnishings and gardens; the couple also built the tree-lined boulevard now called Paseo de la Reforma to connect it to the city.

Presidential residence and museum

From the presidency of Porfirio Díaz until 1939, the castle served as the official presidential residence. President Lázaro Cárdenas then donated it to the nation, and since 1944 it has housed the Museo Nacional de Historia, displaying artefacts from the Spanish conquest to the Mexican Revolution, along with the preserved state rooms of Maximilian and of President Benito Juárez.
