Signal-to-interference ratio

The signal-to-interference ratio (SIR or S/I), also known as the carrier-to-interference ratio (CIR or C/I), is the quotient between the average received modulated carrier power and the average received co-channel interference power from other transmitters. It is a key measure of link quality in cellular radio systems.

Definition

SIR is usually expressed in decibels: SIRdB = 10 log10(Psignal/Pinterference). When thermal noise is also significant, the combined measure SINR (signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio) is used instead. Achieving a target SIR determines frequency reuse distances and cell planning in mobile networks.
