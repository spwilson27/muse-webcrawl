Taygetus (Greek: Ταΰγετος, Taygetos) is a mountain range on the Peloponnese peninsula in Southern Greece. The highest mountain in the range is Mount Taygetus, also known as Profitou Ilias or Prophitis Elias (Prophet Elias); its summit, at 2,404 metres (7,887 ft), is the highest point in the Peloponnese. The range extends about 115 kilometres from near Tripoli in the north to the Mani Peninsula in the south, separating Laconia and the Eurotas valley from Messenia.

Geography and nature

The massif is predominantly limestone and schist, with rugged ridges, gorges such as the Vyros Gorge, and extensive forests of Greek fir and black pine at altitude. Slopes harbour notable biodiversity, including many endemic plants. Much of the range is protected within the Natura 2000 network. Snow lies on the summit well into spring, and the peaks are prominent landmarks visible from Sparta, Kalamata and, on clear days, from Crete and Italy in extreme conditions.

History and mythology

In Greek mythology the range was named for the nymph Taygete, one of the Pleiades. In antiquity the mountains formed the western wall of Sparta's territory; according to later sources, Spartan elders inspected newborns and the unfit were exposed at a place called the Apothetae on the mountain's slopes—a story modern historians treat with caution. During the Byzantine period the range sheltered settlements and monasteries around Mystras; in the Ottoman period and the Greek War of Independence its villages and klepht bands remained largely autonomous. The Slavic-speaking communities of the mountain and the Maniots retained distinctive traditions into the modern era.
