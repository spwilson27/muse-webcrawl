Bottle Shock is a 2008 American comedy-drama film directed by Randall Miller, dramatizing the events leading up to the 1976 wine tasting known as the Judgment of Paris, in which California wines defeated French wines in a blind tasting.

Plot

British wine merchant Steven Spurrier (Alan Rickman), whose Paris shop struggles, travels to Napa Valley seeking wines for a blind tasting intended to promote his business. In California, Jim Barrett (Bill Pullman), founder of Chateau Montelena, struggles financially while clashing with his son Bo (Chris Pine). The film follows the selection of the Californian wines and their triumph in Paris, which transformed the global wine industry.

Accuracy and reception

The film took liberties with the historical record, inventing subplots and composite characters; Spurrier himself objected to his portrayal, and winemaker Mike Grgich, who made the winning Chardonnay, was largely omitted. Critics gave the film mixed-to-positive reviews, praising Rickman's performance and the Napa scenery. The title refers both to the wine-transport condition "bottle shock" and to the shock of the tasting's result.

Legacy

Together with the documentary coverage of the Judgment of Paris, the film helped popularize the story of California wine's emergence on the world stage.
