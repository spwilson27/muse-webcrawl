Firestone Tire and Rubber Company

The Firestone Tire and Rubber Company is an American tire company founded by Harvey S. Firestone in 1900 in Akron, Ohio, initially to supply pneumatic tires for wagons, buggies, and other wheeled vehicles of the period. It grew into one of the largest tire manufacturers in the world and a major supplier to the automobile industry, including an early close association with the Ford Motor Company.

History

Firestone began production with 12 employees in 1900 and by 1906 was supplying tires in large volumes to Henry Ford for the Model T. The company expanded into a global enterprise, operating vast rubber plantations in Liberia from 1926 onward. In 1988, Firestone was acquired by Japan's Bridgestone Corporation for $2.6 billion, then the largest acquisition of an American company by a Japanese firm; the Firestone brand continues under Bridgestone Americas.

Controversies

In the 1970s the Firestone 500 radial tire was recalled in one of the largest tire recalls of the era amid tread-separation failures. In 2000, Firestone tires fitted to Ford Explorer SUVs were implicated in tread separations linked to rollover accidents and more than 200 deaths in the United States, leading to a recall of millions of tires, congressional hearings, and the end of the century-old Firestone–Ford relationship.

Motorsport

Firestone has a long history in racing, including decades as a tire supplier in Indianapolis 500 competition, and is the exclusive tire supplier to the IndyCar Series.
