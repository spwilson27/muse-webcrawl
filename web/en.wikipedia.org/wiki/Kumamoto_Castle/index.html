Kumamoto Castle (熊 本 城, Kumamoto-jō) is a hilltop Japanese castle located in Chūō-ku, Kumamoto, in Kumamoto Prefecture. It was a large and well-fortified castle, regarded along with Himeji Castle and Matsumoto Castle as one of Japan's three premier castles. The castle complex measures roughly 1.6 km from east to west and 1.2 km from north to south, and the main keep is 30.3 metres high.

History

A fortification existed on the site from 1467; the present castle was constructed between 1601 and 1607 by the daimyō Katō Kiyomasa, famed as a castle architect. Its defences included 49 turrets, 18 turret gates, and 29 smaller gates, with curving stone walls (musha-gaeshi) designed to repel climbers, and wells and provisions for a long siege. The castle was later held by the Hosokawa clan for most of the Edo period.

During the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, the castle withstood a 53-day siege by Saigō Takamori's army, although the main keep burned down shortly before the siege from an undetermined cause. The siege is often said to have inspired the fictionalised events of the film The Last Samurai.

Modern era

The keep was reconstructed in reinforced concrete in 1960, and several surviving original turrets and sections of wall are designated Important Cultural Properties. The 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes severely damaged the castle, collapsing roof tiles, cracking the keep's walls, and toppling sections of stone ramparts; a long-term restoration, expected to span decades, has since returned the main keep to public view while stone-by-stone rebuilding of the walls continues.
