Helsingborg

Helsingborg (also spelled Hälsingborg in older Swedish usage) is a city in Scania (Skåne) County, Sweden, with a population of roughly 115,000 in the urban area and about 150,000 in the municipality. It sits at the narrowest part of the Øresund strait, facing the Danish city of Helsingør (Elsinore) just 4 km across the water — one of the shortest international ferry crossings in Europe.

History

Helsingborg is one of Sweden's oldest cities; it is mentioned in a letter of King Canute the Holy dated 21 May 1085. As a strategic fortress town guarding the Øresund, it was long contested between Denmark and Sweden. The keep known as Kärnan, built in the 1310s under Eric VI of Denmark, is the surviving tower of the medieval castle. By the Treaty of Roskilde (1658) the city passed permanently to Sweden, though Danish forces briefly retook it in later wars, including after the Battle of Helsingborg in 1710 — Sweden's last major battle on Scania soil.

Landmarks

Notable sights include Kärnan tower in Slottshagen park, the neo-Gothic town hall (1897) on Stortorget, St Mary's Church (Mariakyrkan), the Fredriksdal open-air museum, and Sofiero Palace, a former royal summer residence famed for its rhododendron gardens. The Dunkers kulturhus cultural centre, opened in 2002, stands on the north harbour.

Transport and economy

Ferry lines — long operated as the HH route — carry passengers and vehicles to Helsingør around the clock. The port is one of Sweden's largest, and the city hosts major Swedish companies and logistics operations; IKEA's Swedish national organisation is based in Helsingborg.
