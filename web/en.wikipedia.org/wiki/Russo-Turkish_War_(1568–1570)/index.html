Russo-Turkish War (1568–1570)

Date1568–1570
LocationAstrakhan and the lower Volga
ResultRussian victory; Astrakhan retained by Russia
BelligerentsTsardom of Russia; Ottoman Empire and Crimean Khanate

The Russo-Turkish War of 1568–1570 was the first of the many Russo-Turkish wars. It was fought over control of Astrakhan, the former khanate at the mouth of the Volga which Russia had annexed in 1556, and over Ottoman ambitions to dig a canal between the Don and the Volga linking the Black Sea to the Caspian.

Background

The conquests of Kazan (1552) and Astrakhan (1556) gave Ivan IV's Russia control of the entire Volga. The Ottoman sultan Selim II, encouraged by the Crimean khan Devlet I Giray, sought to restore Muslim rule at Astrakhan, protect pilgrim and trade routes, and challenge Russian expansion toward the Caucasus.

The Astrakhan expedition

In the summer of 1569 a large Ottoman force under Kasim Pasha, supported by Crimean Tatar cavalry, marched from Azov toward Astrakhan, while work began on a Don–Volga canal near Perevolok. The canal proved impractical, supplies failed, and storms destroyed much of the fleet carrying siege artillery in the Caspian. The besiegers failed to take the Astrakhan citadel, defended by a Russian garrison, and a relief force defeated the retreating army.

Aftermath

The Ottoman troops withdrew in the autumn of 1569 with heavy losses, and a peace concluded in 1570 confirmed Russian possession of Astrakhan. The failure ended Ottoman plans for a Volga–Don canal for the time being, though Crimean raids continued — Devlet Giray burned Moscow in 1571 during the Livonian War.
