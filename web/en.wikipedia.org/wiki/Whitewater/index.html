Whitewater

Whitewater is the foamy, turbulent water formed in a river where a steep gradient, obstructions or a constricted channel make the flow fast and aerated. Rapids are the classic whitewater feature, and whitewater rivers are the setting for sports such as whitewater kayaking, rafting and canoeing.

Formation and features

Whitewater appears where the riverbed steepens or boulders and ledges disturb the flow. Characteristic features include standing waves, "holes" (recirculating currents behind submerged rocks), eddies (calm pockets behind obstacles where the current runs upstream), and V-shaped tongues of smooth water marking the deepest channel through a rapid. The white colour comes from air mixed into the water, which also reduces its buoyancy — a real hazard for swimmers.

Classification

Rapids are graded on the International Scale of River Difficulty from Class I (easy, small waves) to Class VI (extreme, dangerous and effectively unrunnable). Famous whitewater rivers include the Colorado River through the Grand Canyon, the Zambezi below Victoria Falls, and the Gauley River in West Virginia.

Recreation

Whitewater rafting is a major adventure-tourism industry, and whitewater slalom canoeing and kayaking have been Olympic sports since 1972. Artificial whitewater courses, built for events such as the Olympics, use pumps and movable obstacles to create rapids on demand.
