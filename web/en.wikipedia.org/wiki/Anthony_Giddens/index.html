Anthony Giddens

Anthony Giddens, Baron Giddens (born 18 January 1938) is an English sociologist who is known for his theory of structuration and his holistic view of modern societies. He is considered to be one of the most prominent modern sociologists and the author of at least 34 books, published in at least 29 languages. In 2007, Giddens was listed as the fifth most-referenced author in the humanities.

Born: 18 January 1938, Edmonton, London, England
Known for: Structuration theory, Third Way, reflexive modernity
Institutions: University of Leicester, University of Cambridge, London School of Economics

Career

Giddens studied at the University of Hull, the London School of Economics, and King's College, Cambridge. He taught at the University of Leicester, where he helped establish its sociology department, and later at Cambridge, where he was a fellow of King's College. From 1997 to 2003 he served as director of the LSE. He co-founded the publisher Polity Press in 1985.

Ideas

His structuration theory, developed in The Constitution of Society (1984), holds that social structures are both the medium and the outcome of the practices they organize — the "duality of structure." Later works addressed modernity, globalization, risk (Runaway World, the 1999 Reith Lectures), and intimacy. Giddens also became an influential, if controversial, adviser associated with the "Third Way" politics of Tony Blair's New Labour, and was made a life peer in 2004.
