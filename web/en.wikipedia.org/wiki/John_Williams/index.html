John Towner Williams (born 8 February 1932) is an American composer and conductor. Widely regarded as one of the greatest film composers of all time, he has written many of the most recognisable scores in cinema history, in a career spanning seven decades.

Life and career

Born in New York and raised in Los Angeles, the son of a jazz drummer, Williams studied composition at UCLA and briefly at Juilliard. After work as a jazz pianist and studio session musician, he composed for television and films, rising to prominence in the 1970s through collaborations with Steven Spielberg — Jaws (1975), Close Encounters of the Third Kind, Raiders of the Lost Ark, E.T., Jurassic Park, Schindler's List, and Saving Private Ryan — and with George Lucas on the Star Wars saga. His other celebrated scores include Superman, Home Alone, and the first three Harry Potter films.

Style and recognition

Williams's music revived the classical Hollywood symphonic tradition, using full orchestra, leitmotifs, and memorable themes. He has won five Academy Awards (for Fiddler on the Roof, Jaws, Star Wars, E.T., and Schindler's List) and received more than fifty Oscar nominations — the most of any living person. He also served as principal conductor of the Boston Pops (1980–1993) and has written concert works and fanfares, including for Olympic Games.
