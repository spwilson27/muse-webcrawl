The Holy League (Latin: Sacra Liga; Spanish: Liga Santa) of 1571 was a coalition of Catholic Mediterranean states arranged by Pope Pius V to counter the expansion of the Ottoman Empire in the eastern Mediterranean. Its fleet, commanded by Don John of Austria, won a crushing victory over the Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto.

Background

The Ottoman conquest of Cyprus, begun in 1570, and the fall of Famagusta accompanied by atrocities against its defenders, galvanised Catholic Europe. Pius V negotiated tirelessly to unite Spain under Philip II, the Republic of Venice, Genoa, the Knights of Malta and several Italian states, despite their mutual rivalries. The treaty was signed in Rome on 25 May 1571; the League was to maintain a large standing fleet, with costs divided chiefly between Spain, Venice and the Papacy.

Lepanto

The allied fleet of over 200 galleys and six Venetian galleasses assembled at Messina and sailed east. On 7 October 1571 it met the Ottoman fleet in the Gulf of Patras off Lepanto (Naupactus). The Christians destroyed or captured most of the Ottoman fleet and freed thousands of Christian galley slaves. Miguel de Cervantes fought and was wounded in the battle. News of the victory was greeted with rejoicing across Europe, and Pius V instituted the feast of Our Lady of Victory (later Our Lady of the Rosary) to commemorate it.

Aftermath

Despite Lepanto, the League failed to exploit the victory: the Ottomans rebuilt their fleet within a year, and Venice, exhausted, made a separate peace in 1573, ceding Cyprus. The League dissolved, though Lepanto retained enormous symbolic importance as proof that Ottoman naval power could be broken.
