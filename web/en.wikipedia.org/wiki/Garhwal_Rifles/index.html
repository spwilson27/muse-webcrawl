Garhwal Rifles

The Garhwal Rifles, formerly known as the Royal Garhwal Rifles, are an infantry regiment of the Indian Army. It was originally raised in 1887 as the 39th (Garhwal) Regiment of Bengal Infantry. It has since served in the two World Wars and the wars fought after Independence. It mainly draws from the Garhwali Rajput and Brahmin communities of the Garhwal region of Uttarakhand.

Over its existence, the regiment has amassed a number of battle and theatre honours, including honours from both World Wars. Soldiers of the regiment have received numerous gallantry awards, including Victoria Crosses in the First World War and the Param Vir Chakra awarded to Rifleman Jaswant Singh Rawat for his actions during the Sino-Indian War of 1962.

History

The regiment was first raised at Almora in 1887 by Lieutenant Colonel E. P. Mainwaring, and its first battalion was formed from Garhwali soldiers previously serving in Gorkha regiments. During the First World War, battalions of the regiment fought in France, Mesopotamia, and elsewhere; Naik Darwan Singh Negi and Rifleman Gabar Singh Negi were awarded the Victoria Cross. In the Second World War the regiment served in Burma, Malaya, and the Middle East. After Indian independence in 1947, the regiment was allocated to India and has since fought in the wars of 1947–48, 1962, 1965, 1971, and the Kargil conflict of 1999.

Regimental centre and motto

The regimental centre is at Lansdowne in Pauri Garhwal district, Uttarakhand, a cantonment town named after Lord Lansdowne, Viceroy of India at the time of the regiment's founding. The regimental motto is Bajrang Bali Ki Jai ("Victory to Bajrang Bali"), and its war cry invokes Durga.
