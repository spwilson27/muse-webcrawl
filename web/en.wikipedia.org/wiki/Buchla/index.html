Buchla

Buchla refers to the electronic musical instruments designed by Donald "Don" Buchla (1937–2016) and to the companies he founded, beginning with Buchla & Associates. Working on the West Coast of the United States at the same time as Robert Moog was developing his synthesizers on the East Coast, Buchla created an alternative approach to modular synthesis that became known as the "West Coast" style, emphasising waveshaping, low-pass gates, complex oscillators and touch-controlled, keyboard-free interfaces.

History

Buchla built his first modular system, the Series 100, in 1963 for the San Francisco Tape Music Center, funded by a Rockefeller Foundation grant, working with composers Morton Subotnick and Ramon Sender. The system included one of the first voltage-controlled sequencers. The Series 200 "Electric Music Box" followed in the 1970s.

Unlike Moog, Buchla avoided the term "synthesizer" and conventional piano keyboards, preferring touch plates and capacitive controllers, reasoning that his instruments should not imitate existing instruments. His designs include the Music Easel (1973), a compact portable performance system that has been reissued, the digitally controlled 200e series, and controllers such as the Thunder, Lightning and Marimba Lumina. Morton Subotnick's Silver Apples of the Moon (1967), composed largely on a Buchla system, was one of the first electronic albums.

Legacy

The Buchla approach — building timbre by adding harmonics through waveshaping and wavefolding rather than filtering a harmonically rich wave, as in the "East Coast" Moog style — has strongly influenced modern modular synthesis, particularly the Eurorack movement. The company Buchla U.S.A. continued producing instruments after Don Buchla's death in 2016.
