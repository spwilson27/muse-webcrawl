Randall–Sundrum model
Proposed byLisa Randall; Raman Sundrum
Year1999
FieldTheoretical particle physics

The Randall–Sundrum model is an idea in theoretical physics, proposed by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum in 1999, that addresses the hierarchy problem using a warped extra dimension. It describes the universe as a five-dimensional anti-de Sitter space in which the Standard Model particles live on (or near) a four-dimensional brane.

Overview

Two variants are commonly discussed. In RS1, gravity is localised near one brane and the exponential warp factor of the metric naturally generates the large gap between the Planck scale and the weak scale. RS2 considers a single brane in an infinite extra dimension and shows that four-dimensional gravity can still be recovered.

Experimental status

The models predict Kaluza–Klein excitations of the graviton and other particles, which have been searched for at the Large Hadron Collider; no confirmed evidence has been found, placing lower bounds on the relevant mass scales.
