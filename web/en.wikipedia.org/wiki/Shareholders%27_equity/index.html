Shareholders' equity


Also called: Owners' equity, net assets
Identity: Assets − Liabilities = Equity
Reported on: Balance sheet

Shareholders' equity (or owners' equity for unincorporated businesses) is the residual interest in the assets of a company after deducting its liabilities. On the balance sheet it satisfies the accounting equation: assets equal liabilities plus equity.

Components

Equity typically comprises share capital (the par or stated value of issued shares), additional paid-in capital, retained earnings accumulated from profits, and accumulated other comprehensive income, less any treasury shares repurchased by the company.

Interpretation

Positive equity indicates that the book value of assets exceeds obligations; negative equity can signal financial distress. Return on equity relates net income to shareholders' equity and is a common profitability measure. In liquidation, shareholders are residual claimants, paid only after all creditors.
