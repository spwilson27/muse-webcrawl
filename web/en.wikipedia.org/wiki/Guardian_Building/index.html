Guardian Building

Location500 Griswold Street, Detroit, Michigan
Opened1929
Height192.6 m (632 ft), 36 stories
ArchitectWirt C. Rowland

The Guardian Building is a landmark Art Deco skyscraper in downtown Detroit, Michigan, completed in 1929. Nicknamed the "Cathedral of Finance", it was built as the headquarters of the Union Trust Company.

Architecture

Designed by Wirt C. Rowland of the firm Smith, Hinchman & Grylls, the building rises 36 stories and is clad in orange brick with coloured terra cotta, Pewabic tile and travertine. Its design blends Art Deco with Aztec and Native American motifs. The banking hall features a vaulted ceiling, a mural map of Michigan by Ezra Winter, and a Tiffany clock.

History

During the Second World War the building served as a command centre for war production. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1989 and now houses offices, including those of Wayne County.
