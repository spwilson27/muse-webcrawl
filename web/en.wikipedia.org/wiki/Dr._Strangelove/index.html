Dr. Strangelove

Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb is a 1964 political satire black comedy film directed, produced, and co-written by Stanley Kubrick. It stars Peter Sellers in three roles and also features George C. Scott. The film, financed and released by Columbia Pictures, is loosely based on Peter George's thriller novel Red Alert (1958).

The story concerns an insane United States Air Force general who orders a pre-emptive nuclear attack on the Soviet Union. It follows the President of the United States (Merkin Muffley), his advisers, the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and a Royal Air Force officer as they attempt to stop the crew of a B-52 bomber from bombing the USSR and starting a nuclear war.

Plot

Brigadier General Jack D. Ripper, the paranoid commander of Burpelson Air Force Base, orders his wing to attack the Soviet Union using Wing Attack Plan R, a retaliatory plan that allows lower-echelon commanders to retaliate after a Soviet first strike. When the attack becomes unstoppable, officials gather in the Pentagon's War Room to avert catastrophe, assisted by Dr. Strangelove, an ex-Nazi nuclear scientist in a wheelchair whose arm involuntarily gives Nazi salutes.

Reception

The film is widely considered one of the best comedies ever made and one of the greatest films of all time. In 1989 it was among the first 25 films selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
