Pine Island Glacier

Pine Island Glacier (PIG) is a large ice stream and the fastest-melting glacier in Antarctica, draining the West Antarctic Ice Sheet into Pine Island Bay of the Amundsen Sea. Together with the neighbouring Thwaites Glacier, it is one of the largest contributors to global sea-level rise from Antarctica.

Retreat and thinning

Since the 1990s the glacier has accelerated, thinned, and its grounding line — where the ice begins to float — has retreated inland by tens of kilometres. Warm ocean water reaching beneath its floating ice shelf melts the shelf from below, reducing the buttressing that slows the grounded ice. Its basin rests on bedrock below sea level that deepens inland, a geometry vulnerable to marine ice sheet instability.

Sea-level significance

The catchment of Pine Island Glacier holds enough ice to raise global sea level by roughly half a metre if fully discharged. It has produced several large calving events; in recent years icebergs such as B-46 and B-49 broke from its front. Intensive international research, including airborne radar surveys and ocean moorings, monitors its changes.
