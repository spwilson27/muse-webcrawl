Graphic design

Graphic design combines typography, imagery and layout to communicate ideas.

Graphic design is a profession and applied art whose practice involves creating visual communication intended to transmit specific messages to social groups, with specific objectives. It is an interdisciplinary branch of design whose foundations and objectives revolve around the definition of problems and the determination of objectives for decision-making, through creativity, innovation and lateral thinking along with manual or digital tools, transforming them for proper interpretation. Graphic designers arrange type, imagery and other visual elements in advertisements, publications, websites, signage and packaging.

The term "graphic design" was coined by the American book designer William Addison Dwiggins in 1922, though the practice itself dates back to the earliest printed and illustrated works and developed rapidly with industrialisation and mass printing in the nineteenth century.

History

Origins

Precursors of graphic design include illuminated manuscripts, early woodblock printing in China, and the handbills and trade cards of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The invention of lithography in 1796 and later chromolithography made colourful posters possible; artists such as Jules Chéret and Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec elevated the advertising poster to an art form in Paris in the 1890s.

The modern profession

In the early twentieth century, movements such as the Bauhaus, De Stijl, Constructivism and the Swiss Style (International Typographic Style) established the principles of grid-based layout, sans-serif typography and objective photography that still underpin much design practice. Designers such as Jan Tschichold, Josef Müller-Brockmann, Paul Rand, Saul Bass and Massimo Vignelli shaped corporate identity, film titles, signage systems and editorial design. Herbert Bayer and others carried Bauhaus ideas to the United States.

Digital era

The arrival of desktop publishing in the mid-1980s — with the Apple Macintosh, PostScript and software such as PageMaker, Illustrator and later Photoshop and InDesign — transformed the industry, merging the previously separate trades of typesetting, paste-up and retouching into a single computer-based workflow. Graphic designers subsequently moved into web design, motion graphics and user-interface design.

Applications

  • Branding and identity — logos, visual identity systems and brand guidelines.
  • Editorial design — books, magazines, newspapers and other publications.
  • Advertising — print and digital campaigns, posters and billboards.
  • Environmental and wayfinding design — signage, exhibition graphics and public information systems.
  • Packaging — the design of boxes, labels and containers.
  • Digital design — websites, apps, social-media graphics and user interfaces.
  • Typography — the design of typefaces and the arrangement of type.
  • Motion design — film titles, television graphics and animation.

Principles

Commonly taught principles of graphic design include balance, contrast, emphasis, hierarchy, alignment, repetition, proximity, white space and rhythm. Designers work with elements such as line, shape, colour, texture, and space, and typically follow a process of research, briefing, concept development, design, feedback and production.

Occupations

Graphic designers work in advertising agencies, design studios, publishing houses, in-house marketing departments and as freelancers. Related roles include art director, brand designer, illustrator, type designer and user-experience designer. Professional bodies include AIGA in the United States and the Chartered Society of Designers in the United Kingdom.
