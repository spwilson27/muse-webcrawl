The North Yungas Road (Spanish: Camino a los Yungas) is a road leading from La Paz to Coroico, 56 kilometres northeast of La Paz in the Yungas region of Bolivia. Famous — and notorious — as the "Death Road" (Camino de la Muerte), it was long considered one of the most dangerous roads in the world before a modern bypass diverted most motor traffic.

Description

The road descends roughly 3,450 metres from the high Altiplano pass of La Cumbre (4,650 m) to the subtropical Yungas valley. Much of the historic route is a single-lane dirt and gravel track as narrow as 3.2 metres, cut into near-vertical mountainsides with drops of up to 600 metres, few guardrails, and frequent fog, rain, mudslides and falling rock. Unusually for Bolivia, traffic historically kept left on the road so descending drivers could judge the edge — a rule blamed and credited in roughly equal measure in local lore.

History

The road was built in the 1930s, largely by Paraguayan prisoners captured in the Chaco War. For decades it was the only route connecting La Paz with the Amazonian north of Bolivia. The Inter-American Development Bank dubbed it the "world's most dangerous road" in 1995; estimates commonly cited put fatalities at 200–300 travellers per year in its worst period, including a 1983 bus disaster that killed over 100 people. Bolivia drives on the right generally, but the Death Road's left-hand rule for this stretch was made official.

Bypass and tourism

A paved alternative route (part of National Route 3) opened in stages, completed in 2006, and motor traffic on the old road collapsed. Since then the North Yungas Road has become primarily a tourist attraction: tens of thousands of visitors a year ride mountain bikes downhill from La Cumbre with tour agencies from La Paz. Fatalities among cyclists still occur, and memorials line the route. The road remains open to local traffic and is maintained as a gravel road.
