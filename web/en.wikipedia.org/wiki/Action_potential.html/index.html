Action potential

An action potential is a rapid, transient change in the membrane potential of an excitable cell, such as a neuron or muscle cell, that propagates along the cell membrane and serves as the basic unit of long-distance electrical signalling in the nervous system.

It is generated when depolarisation opens voltage-gated sodium channels, allowing Na+ ions to rush in and drive the membrane potential from its resting value near −70 mV to a positive peak, after which potassium channels open and K+ outflow repolarises the membrane. Action potentials are 'all-or-none': their amplitude is constant, and information is encoded in their frequency.

In myelinated axons the action potential is regenerated at the nodes of Ranvier, a saltatory conduction that greatly increases propagation speed. The refractory period following an action potential ensures one-way propagation and limits firing rates.
