Skorpios

Skorpios (Greek: Σκορπιός) is a private island in the Ionian Sea off the western coast of Greece, just east of the island of Lefkada, near the island of Meganisi and the smaller islet of Skorpidi. It covers roughly 83 hectares and is heavily forested. Administratively it belongs to the municipality of Meganisi in the regional unit of Lefkada.

Onassis era

The island is famous worldwide as the retreat of the Greek shipping magnate Aristotle Onassis, who purchased it in 1962 (reportedly for a modest sum in drachmas). Onassis extensively redeveloped the island, planting tens of thousands of trees and plants, importing sand from Salamis to create beaches, and building the "Pink House" villa, guest houses, a harbour and even a small farm and power station. He famously had fresh water, and even soil, shipped in.

On 20 October 1968, Onassis married Jacqueline Kennedy, the widow of U.S. President John F. Kennedy, in a small ceremony in the island's chapel — one of the most publicised weddings of the century. Aristotle Onassis, his son Alexander (killed in a plane crash in 1973) and his daughter Christina are all buried in the family mausoleum on Skorpios.

After Onassis

After Christina Onassis's death in 1988, control of the island passed to her daughter, Athina Onassis. In 2013, Athina agreed a long-term lease of the island — reported to run for up to 99 years — to companies associated with Ekaterina Rybolovleva, the daughter of Russian billionaire Dmitry Rybolovlev. The Rybolovlev family announced plans to develop the island into a luxury resort, the "VIP Exclusive Club", with investment reported in the hundreds of millions of euros, while preserving its natural environment and the Onassis graves.

Geography

Skorpios is densely wooded with pine and olive trees, shaped somewhat like its name ("scorpion"). It has several small beaches, including the famous shore where Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis was photographed swimming. Its highest point is about 92 metres above sea level.
