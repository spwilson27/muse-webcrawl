Music recording certification

A music recording certification is a system of certifying that a music recording has shipped, sold, or streamed a certain number of units. The certification is typically awarded by a record industry association, such as the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) in the United States or the British Phonographic Industry (BPI) in the United Kingdom. The standard awards are silver, gold, platinum, multi-platinum, and diamond.

A gold disc award, presented to mark certified sales.

History

The practice began informally when record companies presented gold-painted discs to artists — the first commonly cited presentation was RCA Victor's gold record to Glenn Miller in 1942 for "Chattanooga Choo Choo", which had sold over a million copies. The RIAA formalised its Gold Award in 1958, originally requiring $1 million in sales; the Platinum Award was introduced in 1976, the multi-platinum award in 1984, and the Diamond Award in 1999 for ten million units.

Thresholds

Thresholds vary by country, format, and era, and have often been lowered as markets changed. In the United States, current album thresholds are:

AwardUnits (U.S. album)
Gold500,000
Platinum1,000,000
Multi-platinum2,000,000+
Diamond10,000,000

In the United Kingdom, singles are certified Silver (200,000), Gold (400,000), and Platinum (600,000), while albums are Silver (60,000), Gold (100,000), and Platinum (300,000).

Streaming era

As consumption shifted to streaming, certifying bodies incorporated on-demand streams into "album-equivalent units": in the U.S., 1,500 streams of songs from an album count as one album unit, and permanent downloads of individual tracks also contribute (ten track downloads equalling one album in the TEA measure). Programme streams were added by the RIAA in 2013 for singles and in 2016 for albums.

Notable records

The Eagles' Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975) is the highest-certified album in the United States (38× platinum), ahead of Michael Jackson's Thriller. "Old Town Road" by Lil Nas X holds the highest single certification in the U.S., certified diamond many times over. Certifications are tracked internationally through national bodies coordinated by the IFPI.
