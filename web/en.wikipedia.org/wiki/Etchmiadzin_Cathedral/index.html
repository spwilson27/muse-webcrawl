Etchmiadzin Cathedral (Armenian: Էջմիածնի մայր տաճար) is the mother church of the Armenian Apostolic Church, located in the city of Vagharshapat (Etchmiadzin), Armenia. According to tradition it was built between 301 and 303 AD, immediately after Armenia adopted Christianity as a state religion, under the direction of St Gregory the Illuminator, on the site where, in Gregory's vision, Christ descended and struck the ground with a golden hammer — the name Etchmiadzin means "the Descent of the Only-Begotten". It is often described as the oldest cathedral in the world and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The original church was built over a pagan temple early in the fourth century, after King Tiridates III proclaimed Christianity the state religion of Armenia — the first nation to do so. The present core of the cathedral dates chiefly to 483/4, when the catholicos Vahan Mamikonian rebuilt it in stone with a cruciform plan after the earlier building had been damaged. The cathedral was repeatedly repaired; the dome, murals and much of its present appearance derive from restorations in the seventeenth century, including the three-tiered belfry added in 1658.

Architecture and treasures

The cathedral is a domed cruciform church of tufa stone, with later frescoes by the Hovnatanian family of painters. Its treasury and museum preserve major Armenian relics, most famously the Holy Lance (Geghard) said to have pierced Christ's side, a fragment of Noah's Ark, and the relic of the right hand of Gregory the Illuminator, with which the Catholicos blesses the holy chrism (muron) every seven years.

Role today

Etchmiadzin is the seat of the Catholicos of All Armenians, the supreme head of the Armenian Apostolic Church, and the spiritual centre of Armenians worldwide. The cathedral and the surrounding churches of Vagharshapat were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.
