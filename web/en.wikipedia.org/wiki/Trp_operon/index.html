The trp operon is a group of genes that is transcribed together, encoding the components for the production of amino acid tryptophan in bacteria. The trp operon is present in many bacteria, but was first characterised in Escherichia coli. It is a classic example of a repressible operon and of regulation by attenuation.

Structure

The operon contains five structural genes — trpE, trpD, trpC, trpB and trpA — which encode the enzymes that convert chorismate into tryptophan, preceded by a promoter, an operator and a leader sequence (trpL) containing an attenuator region.

Regulation

Repression: the trpR repressor, when bound to tryptophan (its corepressor), binds the operator and blocks transcription; when tryptophan is scarce, the inactive repressor releases the operator and the operon is expressed. Attenuation: the leader peptide of trpL contains tandem tryptophan codons; ribosome stalling at these codons when tryptophan is low determines which hairpin forms in the mRNA — an antiterminator allowing transcription to continue, or a terminator that halts it. Together, repression and attenuation adjust enzyme production over roughly a 700-fold range.

Comparison

The trp operon contrasts with the lac operon, which is inducible and controls catabolism: the trp operon controls an anabolic pathway and is switched off by its end product.
