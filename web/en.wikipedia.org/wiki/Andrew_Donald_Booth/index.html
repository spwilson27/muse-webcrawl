Andrew Donald Booth

Andrew Donald Booth (11 February 1918 – 29 November 2009) was a British electrical engineer, physicist and early computer scientist. Working at Birkbeck College, University of London, he designed a series of early electronic computers and, with his wife Kathleen Booth, developed foundational work on computer programming languages and machine translation.

Born11 February 1918, East Molesey, Surrey, England
Died29 November 2009, Sooke, British Columbia, Canada
Known forBooth's multiplication algorithm; early stored-program computers; magnetic drum memory
InstitutionBirkbeck College, University of London

Work

Booth studied mathematics at Jesus College, Cambridge, and obtained his PhD in crystallography from the University of Birmingham. At Birkbeck he built the Automatic Relay Calculator and the All Purpose Electronic Computer (APEC) series. He is credited with inventing the rotating magnetic drum memory and Booth's multiplication algorithm, still used in computer arithmetic. Together with Kathleen Booth, he wrote some of the earliest books on programming for automatic digital calculators.

Booth later emigrated to Canada, where he became president of Lakehead University in Ontario. He remained active in computing research, including machine translation of natural languages, well into his later years.
