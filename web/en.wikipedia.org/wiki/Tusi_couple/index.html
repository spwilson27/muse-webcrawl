Tusi couple

The Tusi couple (also known as the Tusi's mechanism) is a mathematical device in which a small circle rotates inside a larger circle twice its diameter, and a point on the circumference of the small circle traces a straight line — a diameter of the large circle. The device was invented by the Persian astronomer and mathematician Nasir al-Din al-Tusi in 1247, in his work Tahrir al-Majisti (Commentary on the Almagest), as a way of generating linear motion from circular motions.

Use in astronomy

Al-Tusi introduced the couple to solve a long-standing problem in Ptolemaic astronomy: the equant, a point in Ptolemy's model about which motion was uniform but which was not the centre of the deferent circle, violated the principle that celestial motions should be combinations of uniform circular motions. Using the Tusi couple, an epicycle could be made to oscillate in a straight line, allowing models — notably in al-Tusi's Maragha observatory tradition and in the later work of Ibn al-Shatir — to reproduce planetary latitudes and other phenomena without the equant.

Relation to Copernicus

A diagram identical to al-Tusi's appears in Nicolaus Copernicus's De revolutionibus orbium coelestium (1543), where the couple is used for the same purpose. Historians of science have long noted the striking similarity, including matching lettering of points in the two diagrams, and have debated the route by which the device may have been transmitted from the Islamic world to Renaissance Europe, possibly via Byzantine Greek manuscripts.

Mathematics

The curve traced by a general point on the rolling small circle is a special case of a hypocycloid with two cusps; for a point on the circumference itself, the hypocycloid degenerates into the straight diameter. The construction also underlies the geometry of the hypocyclic gear drive and is demonstrated in modern times by the spirograph-like behaviour of rolling coins and by mechanical "straight-line mechanisms." In modern terminology, the Tusi couple is the hypocycloid with parameter ratio 2:1.
