Northern pike

The northern pike (Esox lucius) is a species of carnivorous fish of the genus Esox (the pikes), found in brackish and fresh waters across the Northern Hemisphere in North America, Europe and Asia. It is known simply as "pike" in Britain and Ireland, and colloquially as the "northern", "jack" or "snot rocket" in parts of North America.

Description

The northern pike has an elongated, torpedo-shaped body, a flattened duckbill snout full of sharp backward-pointing teeth, and olive-green colouring with light bean-shaped spots along the flanks. Most individuals measure 40–55 cm (16–22 in), but large specimens exceed 1 metre and the record fish weighed over 25 kg (55 lb). Females grow larger than males.

Behaviour and diet

Pike are ambush predators of slow-moving and weedy waters — lakes, rivers, flooded marshes and brackish Baltic inlets. They lurk motionless among vegetation and accelerate explosively to seize fish, frogs, voles and even ducklings. They are notably cannibalistic, and pike reproduction occurs in early spring over flooded vegetation.

Angling

The northern pike is a popular sport fish, valued for its aggressive strikes and fighting ability, and is targeted with lures, deadbaits and fly fishing. Its flesh is tasty but very bony. Pike have been introduced outside their native range in places, where they can damage native fish populations.
