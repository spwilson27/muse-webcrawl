CHU (radio station)

Broadcast area: North America
Frequencies: 3330, 7850, 14670 kHz
Operator: National Research Council Canada
First air date: 1929 (as VE9OB); 1938 as CHU

CHU is the call sign of a shortwave time signal radio station operated by the National Research Council (NRC) in Ottawa, Ontario, Canada. It broadcasts time signals derived from the NRC's atomic clocks, providing Canada's official time signal service for shortwave listeners.

Broadcasts

CHU transmits on frequencies of 3330, 7850, and 14670 kHz using upper sideband (H3E) emission because the historic lower frequencies were originally amplitude-modulated. The signal includes tones, digital time codes, and bilingual (English and French) voice announcements of the time each minute. CHU uses atomic clocks run by the NRC Frequency and Time section, coordinated with UTC.

History

The station traces its origins to experimental time signal broadcasts by the Dominion Observatory beginning in 1929 under the call sign VE9OB. Regular service under the CHU call sign began in 1938. In 1970 the station's operation transferred to the National Research Council. The transmitters are located in Barrhaven, southwest of Ottawa. CHU's frequencies and format are similar in purpose to those of WWV and WWVH in the United States and are used by radio amateurs, astronomers, and mariners to check their clocks and radio propagation conditions.
