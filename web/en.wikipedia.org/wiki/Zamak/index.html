Zamak

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ZAMAK (or Zamak, formerly MAZAK) is a family of alloys with a base metal of zinc and alloying elements of aluminium, magnesium, and copper. The name is an acronym of the German words for the constituent metals: Zink (zinc), Aluminium, Magnesium and Kupfer (copper).

Properties and use

Developed by the New Jersey Zinc Company in the late 1920s, Zamak alloys have a low melting point (around 380–390 °C) and excellent fluidity, making them ideal for pressure die casting of intricate parts with fine detail and thin walls. Zamak 3, containing about 4% aluminium, is the most widely used grade in North America; Zamak 2 has higher copper content and greater strength and hardness.

Typical applications include automotive trim and hardware, zippers, padlocks, toys, model railroad equipment, plumbing fittings, and carburetor bodies. Earlier impure castings suffered from "zinc pest" (intergranular corrosion), a problem eliminated by the use of high-purity zinc. Zamak parts can be readily plated, painted, or chromated, and are commonly finished to imitate more expensive metals.
