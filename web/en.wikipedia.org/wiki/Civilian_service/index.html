Civilian service

Civilian service is a form of national service performed instead of military service by conscientious objectors or others, usually in social, health, environmental or civil-protection work. It is also called alternative civilian service or substitute service.

[Image: images/civilian-service-worker.jpg — Civilian service participant working in a care home]

Practice by country

Germany's Zivildienst operated from 1961 until conscription was suspended in 2011, supplying large numbers of workers to hospitals and care services; it was replaced as a volunteer option by the Federal Volunteers Service. Austria's Zivildienst continues, commonly nine months versus six months of military service. Switzerland's civilian service, introduced in 1996, lasts 1.5 times the military term. Finland, Greece, Israel (national service), South Korea (social service personnel), Norway and other conscription states have various schemes; France's service civique and the U.S. AmeriCorps are voluntary rather than substitutes for a draft.

Principles and debate

Systems differ on testing an objector's sincerity, relative length of service (longer terms are sometimes criticized as punitive), and permissible placements, including service abroad. The right to conscientious objection is recognized in the jurisprudence of the European Court of Human Rights.
