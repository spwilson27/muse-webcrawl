Theatre of the Oppressed

FounderAugusto Boal (1931–2009)
Key textTheatre of the Oppressed (1974)
Key techniquesForum theatre, image theatre, invisible theatre, legislative theatre

The Theatre of the Oppressed is a set of theatrical techniques developed by the Brazilian director Augusto Boal in the 1960s and 1970s, intended to turn spectators into active participants — "spect-actors" — who rehearse solutions to real oppression in their lives. Influenced by Paulo Freire's Pedagogy of the Oppressed, Boal argued that conventional theatre separates actor from audience and thereby reproduces passivity.

Techniques

In forum theatre, a short play depicting an unresolved oppression is performed twice; on the second run any spectator may shout "stop", take the protagonist's place and try a different course of action, while a facilitator (the "Joker") manages the discussion. Image theatre uses frozen tableaux sculpted from participants' bodies. Invisible theatre stages scripted scenes in public without announcing them as theatre, provoking real debate. Boal later extended forum methods into legislative theatre while a city councillor in Rio de Janeiro, generating actual laws from audience proposals.

Influence

Exiled from Brazil under the military regime, Boal developed the work in Paris and published Theatre of the Oppressed in 1974; the techniques are now used worldwide in education, community work, prisons and conflict resolution. The approach stands in a tradition of politically engaged theatre alongside Brecht, though Boal criticised Brecht for keeping the audience's activity intellectual rather than embodied.
