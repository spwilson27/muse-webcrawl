Forest Park (St. Louis)

Location: St. Louis, Missouri, United States
Area: 1,326 acres (537 ha)
Opened: 1876
Known for: 1904 Louisiana Purchase Exposition

Forest Park is a public park in western St. Louis, Missouri. Covering about 1,326 acres (537 hectares) — larger than New York's Central Park — it opened in 1876 and is regarded as one of the great urban parks of the United States, sometimes called the "Heart of St. Louis".

History

The park was established in 1876 on land west of the city, over the objections of critics who considered it too remote. It achieved worldwide fame as the site of the Louisiana Purchase Exposition of 1904 (the St. Louis World's Fair) and of events of the 1904 Summer Olympics, the first Olympic Games held in the United States. Most fair buildings were temporary, but the Palace of Fine Arts survived as the Saint Louis Art Museum.

Attractions

Forest Park is home to five major cultural institutions, all offering free admission: the Saint Louis Art Museum, the Missouri History Museum, the Saint Louis Science Center, the Saint Louis Zoo and The Muny, America's oldest and largest outdoor musical theatre. The park also contains the Jewel Box greenhouse, golf courses, tennis courts, lakes and waterways, and the Grand Basin at the foot of Art Hill. A major restoration guided by a 1995 master plan, funded in part by the non-profit Forest Park Forever, revitalised the park in the early 2000s.
