Digital television transition in the United States

The digital transition in the United States was the switchover from analog to exclusively digital broadcasting of terrestrial television programming. Under the Deficit Reduction Act of 2005, full-power television stations were required to end analog broadcasts; after several postponements, the final deadline was June 12, 2009, when the remaining full-power stations ceased analog transmissions.

Background

The FCC allocated a second channel to each broadcaster for digital (ATSC) broadcasting during a long simulcast period. Congress directed that the recovered spectrum — channels 52 to 69 in the 700 MHz band — be reassigned to public safety and commercial wireless services and auctioned.

Converter box programme

To assist viewers with analog sets, the National Telecommunications and Information Administration distributed coupons worth $40 toward certified converter boxes. Concern over unprepared households led Congress to delay the original February 17, 2009 deadline with the DTV Delay Act; about 700 stations nonetheless switched on the original date.

Aftermath

Low-power stations and translators were given a later transition, completed in 2021. The freed 700 MHz spectrum was auctioned for over $19 billion and became a key band for 4G mobile broadband; a later incentive auction in 2016–17 cleared further UHF spectrum, followed by a nationwide repack of television stations.
