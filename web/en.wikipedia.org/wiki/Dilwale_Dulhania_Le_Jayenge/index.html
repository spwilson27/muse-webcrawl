Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge

Directed by: Aditya Chopra
Produced by: Yash Chopra
Starring: Shah Rukh Khan, Kajol
Release date: 20 October 1995
Running time: 189 minutes

Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (Hindi: The Brave-Hearted Will Take the Bride), also known by the initialism DDLJ, is a 1995 Indian Hindi-language romantic drama film written and directed by Aditya Chopra in his directorial debut, and produced by his father Yash Chopra under Yash Raj Films. The film stars Shah Rukh Khan and Kajol as Raj and Simran, two young non-resident Indians who fall in love during a vacation through Europe with their friends.

Plot

Raj Malhotra and Simran Singh meet on a trip across Europe. Simran's strict father, Baldev Singh, has promised her hand to the son of his friend in Punjab, and takes the family back to India. Raj follows, determined to win over Simran's family rather than elope — refusing to "steal" her away and instead seeking her father's blessing. The climax, in which Baldev finally releases Simran's hand so she can board the departing train with Raj, became one of the most iconic scenes in Hindi cinema.

Reception and legacy

Released on 20 October 1995, the film became the highest-grossing Bollywood film of the year and one of the most successful Indian films of all time; adjusted for inflation it remains among the highest grossers ever. It won 10 Filmfare Awards — a record at the time — including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress, and the National Film Award for Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment. The film is credited with reshaping Bollywood romance for the diaspora audience while affirming traditional family values.

Remarkably, DDLJ has had an uninterrupted theatrical run at the Maratha Mandir cinema in Mumbai since its release — exceeding 1,000 weeks in 2014 and continuing for decades (with a brief pandemic-era interruption), making it the longest-running film in Indian cinema history.

Soundtrack

The songs, composed by Jatin–Lalit with lyrics by Anand Bakshi, including "Tujhe Dekha To", "Mere Khwabon Mein", and "Mehndi Laga Ke Rakhna", became enduring classics.
