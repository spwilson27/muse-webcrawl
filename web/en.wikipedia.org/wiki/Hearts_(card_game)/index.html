Hearts (card game)

Players: 3–7 (usually 4)

Cards: 52

Type: Trick-avoidance

Origin: United States (descended from Spanish Reversis)

Hearts is an "evasion-type" trick-taking card game for four players, although most variations can accommodate three to seven players. First recorded in America in the 1880s, it is descended from the 18th-century Spanish game Reversis and related games in which players sought to avoid taking certain penalty cards.

Rules

The game is played with a standard 52-card pack. In the standard four-player game each player receives 13 cards. Before each hand, players pass three cards to another player (the direction rotates: left, right, across, and no pass). The player holding the 2 of clubs leads it to the first trick; players must follow suit if able.

No penalty points may be scored on the first trick. A player who cannot follow suit may discard. The winner of a trick leads the next. Hearts may not be led until a heart has been "broken" by being discarded on another trick.

Scoring

Each heart taken in tricks scores one penalty point, and the queen of spades scores thirteen penalty points. Players aim for the lowest score. The game usually ends when one player reaches 100 points, at which time the player with the lowest score wins.

A player who takes all the penalty cards — all thirteen hearts and the queen of spades — is said to have "shot the moon": depending on the variation, the shooter either subtracts 26 points from their own score or adds 26 points to every opponent's score.

Computer versions

A digital version of Hearts was long bundled with Microsoft Windows (from Windows 3.1 onward), introducing the game to millions of casual players.
