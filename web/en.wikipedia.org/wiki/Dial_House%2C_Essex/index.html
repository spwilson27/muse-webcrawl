Dial House, Essex

Dial House is a farm cottage in Epping Forest, Essex, England. Since 1967 it has been the home and centre of activity of the anarchist punk band Crass and associated artists, becoming one of the best-known intentional anarchist communities in Britain.

History

The cottage was first occupied by the artist and musician Penny Rimbaud in 1967, when it was rented from a local farmer, and was later bought at auction. It developed into an open house for artists, musicians and activists following an ethos of self-sufficiency, communal living and creative freedom.

Crass and after

Dial House was the base for Crass (1977–1984), the influential anarcho-punk band and collective, and for their label Crass Records. After the band's split, Rimbaud continued to run the house as a venue for performances, workshops and publishing projects, and it remains a hub for alternative and avant-garde culture.
