Dysprosium

Dysprosium is a chemical element with the symbol Dy and atomic number 66. It is a rare-earth element in the lanthanide series with a metallic silver lustre. Dysprosium is never found in nature as a free element, though it occurs in minerals such as xenotime. It is chiefly used in high-performance permanent magnets.

History

French chemist Paul-Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran identified dysprosium spectroscopically in 1886 while analysing holmium oxide, and isolated its oxide after many laborious separations — hence its name, from the Greek dysprositos, "hard to get". Pure metal was not produced until the 1950s, with ion-exchange techniques.

Properties

Dysprosium is soft enough to cut with a knife, relatively stable in air, and strongly magnetic: it is ferromagnetic below 85 K and has one of the highest magnetic moments of any element. It has a very high thermal neutron absorption cross-section.

Applications

The most important use is as an additive to neodymium–iron–boron magnets, where dysprosium preserves magnetic strength at high temperatures — vital in electric vehicle motors, wind turbine generators and hard disk drives. It is also used in control rods' cermets for nuclear reactors, in dosimeters, lasers, and in the phosphors and halide lamps of the lighting industry. Because most supply comes from a small number of sources, it is classed as a critical raw material by the EU and US.
