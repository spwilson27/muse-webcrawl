Pew Research Center

The Pew Research Center is a nonpartisan American think tank based in Washington, D.C. It provides information on social issues, public opinion, and demographic trends shaping the United States and the world. It also conducts public opinion polling, demographic research, random sample survey research, media content analysis, and other empirical social science research.

The Pew Research Center is a subsidiary of The Pew Charitable Trusts, its primary funder. The center does not take policy positions; it is a non-profit and nonpartisan organization. Its research covers US politics and policy; journalism and media; internet, science, and technology; religion and public life; hispanic trends; global attitudes and trends; and social and demographic trends.

History

The center grew out of a research project begun in 1990 by the Times Mirror Company, which sponsored regular surveys of media consumption and public attitudes. In 1995, the Pew Charitable Trusts assumed primary sponsorship, and the project became the Pew Research Center for the People & the Press. In 2004, the Trusts established the Pew Research Center in Washington as a subsidiary to house that project and six others, covering religion and public life, Hispanic trends, global attitudes, the internet and American life, social and demographic trends, and journalism.

Research areas

  • U.S. Politics & Policy — surveys of political attitudes, voting, and partisanship.
  • Journalism & Media — studies of news consumption and the state of the news media.
  • Internet, Science & Tech — research on the social impact of the internet and technology.
  • Religion & Public Life — large-scale demographic and attitudinal studies of religion, including the Religious Landscape Studies.
  • Global Attitudes & Trends — public opinion surveys conducted in dozens of countries.
  • Social & Demographic Trends — research on family, race, gender, and economic life.
  • Hispanic Trends — research on the U.S. Hispanic and Latino population (formerly the Pew Hispanic Center).

Funding and reception

The Pew Research Center is funded primarily by The Pew Charitable Trusts, with additional support from other foundations for specific projects. It describes itself as a "fact tank," in contrast to policy-advocacy think tanks, and its surveys are widely cited by news organizations, academics, and policymakers. Its polling methods, including its use of the American Trends Panel, are published openly, and its survey data are made available to researchers after an embargo period.
