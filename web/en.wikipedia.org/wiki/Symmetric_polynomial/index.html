Symmetric polynomial

In algebra, a symmetric polynomial is a polynomial in several variables that is unchanged under any permutation of its variables. Formally, a polynomial P(X1, …, Xn) is symmetric if substituting any permutation of the variables leaves it unaltered. Symmetric polynomials arise naturally in the study of polynomial roots, Galois theory, and representation theory, where they form a bridge between the roots of a polynomial and its coefficients.

A symmetric polynomial is invariant under every permutation of its variables.

Examples

The simplest symmetric polynomials in two variables include X + Y and XY. In n variables, the elementary symmetric polynomials e1, …, en are defined as the sums of products of distinct variables taken k at a time: for instance e1 = X1 + ⋯ + Xn and en = X1⋯Xn. Other important families include the power-sum symmetric polynomials, the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials, monomial symmetric polynomials and the Schur polynomials of representation theory.

Fundamental theorem

The fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials states that every symmetric polynomial can be expressed uniquely as a polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials. This result underlies Viète's formulas, which relate the coefficients of a polynomial to sums and products of its roots: if a polynomial factors as (x − r1)⋯(x − rn), its coefficients are, up to sign, the elementary symmetric polynomials in the roots. Newton's identities give an efficient way to convert between elementary and power-sum symmetric polynomials. The theorem is also a first step in Galois theory, since any symmetric rational function of the roots is expressible in terms of the coefficients and is therefore invariant under the Galois group.

Symmetric functions

Passing to infinitely many variables and bounded degree yields the ring of symmetric functions, which plays a central role in algebraic combinatorics and the representation theory of the symmetric and general linear groups; Schur functions correspond to irreducible characters of these groups.
