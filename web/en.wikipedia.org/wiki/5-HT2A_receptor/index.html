The 5-HT2A receptor is a subtype of the 5-HT2 receptor family of serotonin receptors. It is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) that is coupled to Gq/G11 and mediates excitatory neurotransmission. The receptor is encoded by the HTR2A gene in humans. It is widely expressed in the central nervous system, particularly in the cerebral cortex, and plays roles in learning, memory, anxiety, and perception.

Function

Activation of the 5-HT2A receptor stimulates phospholipase C, increasing levels of inositol trisphosphate and diacylglycerol and raising intracellular calcium. In the cortex, the receptor is densely expressed on the apical dendrites of pyramidal cells in layer V, where its activation modulates glutamate release and cortical network activity.

Pharmacology

The 5-HT2A receptor is the principal target of classic serotonergic psychedelics such as LSD, psilocybin (via its metabolite psilocin), mescaline, and DMT, which act as agonists or partial agonists at the receptor. Conversely, many atypical antipsychotic drugs, including clozapine, risperidone, and olanzapine, act as antagonists or inverse agonists at 5-HT2A receptors, an action thought to contribute to their efficacy against schizophrenia with fewer extrapyramidal side effects. Selective antagonists include ketanserin and volinanserin.

Clinical significance

Polymorphisms in the HTR2A gene have been studied in relation to schizophrenia, mood disorders, obsessive–compulsive disorder, and responses to antidepressant and antipsychotic treatment, though findings have been inconsistent. Antagonism of 5-HT2A receptors also contributes to the antidepressant and sleep-promoting effects of drugs such as trazodone and mirtazapine.
