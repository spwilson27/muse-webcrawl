Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by an Ensemble in a Drama Series

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by an Ensemble in a Drama Series is an award given by the Screen Actors Guild (now SAG-AFTRA) to honour the finest ensemble acting achievements in dramatic television. It has been presented annually since the 1st Screen Actors Guild Awards in 1995 (for the 1994 season), making it one of the principal television categories of the ceremony.

History

The award recognises the entire regular cast of a drama series rather than individual performers, reflecting the Guild's emphasis on ensemble work. NYPD Blue won the inaugural award. ER and The West Wing dominated the early years, each winning multiple times; The Sopranos won three times across its run, and Boardwalk Empire, Downton Abbey, Game of Thrones, and The Crown have each won the award more than once. The record for the most wins in the category is shared by ER and The West Wing, with four victories each.

Superlatives

Counting total nominations, long-running ensemble dramas such as Law & Order, ER, and Grey's Anatomy are among the most frequently recognised series. The category is notable because every regular cast member of the winning series receives the Actor statuette, making it one of the largest group awards in television.
