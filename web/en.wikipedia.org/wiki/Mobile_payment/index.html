Mobile payment

Paying for goods with a smartphone at a contactless terminal

Mobile payment, also referred to as mobile money, is any payment made using a mobile device such as a smartphone or tablet. Mobile payments can be used at the point of sale in place of a physical card, within apps and websites, or to transfer money between individuals. They are regulated as a form of electronic money transfer in most jurisdictions.

Methods

At physical points of sale, mobile payments commonly use near-field communication (NFC), in which the device emulates a contactless payment card, as in Apple Pay, Google Pay and Samsung Pay. Other systems display or scan QR codes, a model dominant in China through Alipay and WeChat Pay. Person-to-person payments are made through apps linked to bank accounts or stored-value wallets.

Mobile money in developing economies

In much of sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, "mobile money" services operated by mobile network operators allow users without bank accounts to store and transfer value using basic handsets and networks of local agents. Kenya's M-Pesa, launched in 2007, is the best-known example and is credited with substantially expanding financial inclusion.

Security

Card-based mobile wallets typically use tokenisation, substituting a device-specific token for the real card number, and authenticate the user with biometrics or a passcode. Risks include device theft, phishing and fraud through compromised accounts.
