Euclid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Euclid
[Image: an imagined bust of Euclid — a bearded Greek scholar, as in the standard article's artist's impression]
BornMid-4th century BC (exact dates unknown)
DiedMid-3rd century BC
Known forElements; Euclidean geometry; Euclidean algorithm; proof of the infinitude of primes
FieldsMathematics

Euclid was an ancient Greek mathematician active as a teacher and author at Alexandria, Egypt, around 300 BC, in the reign of Ptolemy I. He has been styled the "father of geometry" for his treatise the Elements, which deduced the principles of what is now called Euclidean geometry from a small set of axioms. Very little is reliably known about his life; most details come from much later commentators such as Proclus.

The Elements

The Elements consists of thirteen books covering plane geometry, proportion, number theory (including the Euclidean algorithm for greatest common divisors and a proof that there are infinitely many prime numbers), incommensurable lengths and solid geometry culminating in the five Platonic solids. It remained the core mathematics textbook for over two thousand years, was translated into Arabic and then Latin, and, after the Bible, is often said to be among the most printed books in history. Abraham Lincoln famously studied it to learn what it meant to "demonstrate".

Other works and legacy

Surviving works include Data, On Divisions of Figures, Catoptrics, Phaenomena (spherical astronomy) and Optics. The parallel postulate of the Elements was questioned for centuries; attempts to prove it eventually led to non-Euclidean geometry in the 19th century, and hence indirectly to the geometry used in general relativity. His name attaches to Euclidean space, distance and division, and a Euclid space telescope launched by ESA in 2023 to map the geometry of the dark universe.
