The Sneetches and Other Stories

Author: Dr. Seuss (Theodor Geisel)
Published: 1961, Random House

The Sneetches and Other Stories is a collection of four rhymed children's stories by Dr. Seuss (Theodor Seuss Geisel), published in 1961: "The Sneetches," "The Zax," "Too Many Daves," and "What Was I Scared Of?" Three of the stories had first appeared in Redbook magazine in the 1950s.

The Sneetches

The title story tells of the Sneetches, some of whom bear green stars on their bellies and consider themselves superior to the Plain-Belly Sneetches. A fixer-upper named Sylvester McMonkey McBean sells both groups trips through his Star-On and Star-Off machines until nobody can remember who originally had stars, and the Sneetches conclude that "Sneetches are Sneetches." Inspired by Geisel's opposition to antisemitism, it is widely read as a satire on discrimination and conformity.

Other stories

"The Zax" depicts a North-Going and a South-Going Zax who refuse to step aside for one another, standing still while the world builds a highway around them. "Too Many Daves" tells of Mrs. McCave, who named all 23 of her sons Dave. "What Was I Scared Of?" — the tale of a pair of empty green pale pants encountered in the dark — gently addresses irrational fear.

Adaptations

The stories were adapted in the 1973 animated special Dr. Seuss on the Loose, and "The Sneetches" has been adapted for the stage and used widely in anti-bullying education.
