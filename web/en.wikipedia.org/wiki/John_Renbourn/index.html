John Renbourn (August 8, 1944 – March 26, 2015) was an English guitarist and songwriter. A leading figure of the 1960s British folk revival, he co-founded the folk-jazz band Pentangle and was celebrated for a fingerstyle technique that blended blues, jazz, early music and folk.

Career

Renbourn studied classical guitar before turning to American blues and folk, recording solo albums such as John Renbourn (1965) and Sir John Alot (1968), and two duet albums with Bert Jansch. In 1967 he formed Pentangle with Jansch, Jacqui McShee, Danny Thompson and Terry Cox; the group became one of the most influential British folk acts, touring internationally until its split in 1973.

Later work

Renbourn continued to record and tour — with the John Renbourn Group, Stefan Grossman, and in later years with guitarist Wizz Jones — while teaching and writing on guitar technique. He died of a heart attack at his home in Hawick in 2015, shortly before a scheduled tour date.
