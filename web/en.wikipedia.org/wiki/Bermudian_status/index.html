Bermudian status is the local immigration and belonging status of Bermuda, a British Overseas Territory. A person who "possesses Bermudian status" — often called a Status Bermudian — has the right to live and work in Bermuda without restriction, to vote in Bermudian elections, and to own land without the licences required of non-Bermudians. The status is defined by the Bermuda Immigration and Protection Act 1956 and is distinct from British citizenship: Bermudians are British Overseas Territories citizens and, since 2002, also full British citizens, but British citizenship alone confers no right to live in Bermuda.

Acquisition

Bermudian status is acquired principally by birth or descent — being born in Bermuda to a parent who possesses Bermudian status, or being born to a Bermudian parent — and in some cases by grant, for example to spouses of Bermudians after a qualifying period of marriage and residence, and to certain long-term residents under historical provisions. Children born in Bermuda to non-Bermudian parents do not automatically acquire Bermudian status.

Related categories

Long-term residents who do not have status may hold a Permanent Resident's Certificate (PRC), introduced by amendments in 2002, which permits indefinite residence but not voting. "Belonger status" is the generic term used across British Overseas Territories for such local statuses. Work permits, by contrast, are time-limited permissions to work for a specific employer and confer no belonging rights.
