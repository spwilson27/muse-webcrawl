Arita (Japanese: 有田町, Arita-chō) is a town in Nishimatsuura District, Saga Prefecture, Japan, on the island of Kyushu. It is famous as the birthplace of Japanese porcelain: in the early 17th century, kaolin clay was discovered nearby at Izumiyama, and Arita became the center of production of Arita ware, also known as Imari ware after the port from which it was exported.

History

Porcelain production in Japan began after the Japanese invasions of Korea (1592–1598), when Korean potters were brought to Kyushu. According to tradition, the potter Yi Sam-pyeong (Ri Sanpei, later Kanagae Sanbee) discovered porcelain stone at Izumiyama around 1616, enabling Japan's first true porcelain. Arita's kilns supplied the domestic market and, from the 1650s, exported large quantities through the Dutch East India Company from the port of Imari, after Chinese porcelain exports were disrupted by the Ming–Qing transition. Arita porcelain — in blue-and-white, Kakiemon enamel, and Nabeshima styles — became highly prized in Europe.

Sights

The town preserves streets of kilns, potteries, and merchants' houses, including the Kyushu Ceramic Museum and the Arita Porcelain Park, a recreation of a German baroque palace celebrating the historical trade connection with Meissen. The annual Arita Ceramic Fair (Arita Tōki Matsuri), held during Golden Week, attracts over a million visitors. The nearby Sueyama Shrine is notable for its porcelain torii gate.

Economy

Porcelain remains the town's main industry, with workshops producing both traditional tableware and industrial ceramics, complemented by tourism.
