The Dorians (Greek: Δωριεῖς, Dōrieîs) were one of the four major ethnic groups into which the ancient Greeks traditionally divided themselves, alongside the Aeolians, Achaeans and Ionians. They were traditionally said to have come from the mountains of Doris in central Greece and to have spread across the Peloponnese, Crete and the southern Aegean during the so-called Dorian invasion at the end of the Bronze Age.

Tradition and origins

Greek tradition, recorded by authors such as Herodotus and Thucydides, held that the Dorians, led by the descendants of Heracles (the Heracleidae), conquered the Peloponnese from the Achaeans. Modern scholars debate how far this reflects a real migration around 1100 BC, a gradual infiltration, or a later identity constructed in the Archaic period. The destruction of the Mycenaean palace centres and the appearance of new dialect areas are often associated with the tradition, but no single archaeological horizon clearly marks a Dorian invasion.

Language

The Dorians spoke the Doric dialect of Greek, characterised by features such as the retention of long ā where Attic-Ionic developed ē, and by forms such as the future in -seō. Doric was used in regions from Sparta and Corinth to Rhodes, Crete, Syracuse and Cyrene, and it was the language of choral lyric poetry, including the odes of Pindar's Doric colouring and the choruses of tragedy.

Society and states

Dorian communities were traditionally organised into three tribes — the Hylleis, Dymanes and Pamphyloi — a structure found at Sparta, Argos and many Dorian colonies. The most famous Dorian state, Sparta, built its austere military society on the subjugation of the helots, while Corinth became a great commercial and colonising power. In the Peloponnesian War the Dorian–Ionian distinction was rhetorically mapped onto the Spartan and Athenian alliances, though alliances in practice crossed ethnic lines.

Legacy

The name survives in the Doric order of classical architecture — the plainest and earliest of the Greek orders — whose proportions later writers associated with Dorian strength and simplicity.
