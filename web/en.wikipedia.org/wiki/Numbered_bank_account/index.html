Numbered bank account

A numbered bank account is a type of bank account where the name of the account holder is kept secret and identified only by a number or code word. The bank's knowledge of the client's identity is restricted to a small number of senior staff. Numbered accounts became famous in Switzerland, where they were long associated with the country's tradition of banking secrecy.

History

Swiss numbered accounts date to the early 20th century and were formalised by the Swiss Banking Law of 1934, which made it a criminal offence for bank employees to disclose client information. The accounts offered privacy to clients fleeing persecution or instability, but also attracted criticism for facilitating tax evasion and the concealment of illicit funds.

Modern restrictions

Contrary to popular belief, numbered accounts are not anonymous: the bank must know and verify the identity of the beneficial owner under know-your-customer rules. International pressure, agreements on the automatic exchange of tax information, and anti-money-laundering regulation have greatly reduced the secrecy once offered, and comparable arrangements in other jurisdictions have been similarly curtailed.
