Hove

Hove is a seaside town in East Sussex, England, which together with neighbouring Brighton forms the city of Brighton and Hove. Hove is largely residential, characterised by grand Regency and Victorian terraces and squares such as Brunswick Square and Palmeira Square.

History

Hove was a small fishing and smuggling village until the nineteenth century, when it developed as a fashionable resort alongside Brighton. The Brunswick estate, built from the 1820s, set the pattern of elegant crescents facing the sea. Hove became an urban district in 1894 and a municipal borough in 1898; in 1997 it merged with Brighton, and the combined authority gained city status in 2001.

Landmarks include Hove Museum and Art Gallery, St Andrew's Church, the Hove Lawns along the promenade, and the County Cricket Ground, home of Sussex County Cricket Club since 1872. The town is served by Hove railway station on the West Coastway line.
