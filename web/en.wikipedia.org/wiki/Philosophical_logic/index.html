Philosophical logic

Philosophical logic is the study of fundamental philosophical questions by logical methods, and the application of formal logic to concepts such as truth, existence, necessity, reference, and meaning. It contrasts with mathematical logic, which focuses on formal systems and their mathematical properties.

Topics

Philosophical logic encompasses modal logic (necessity and possibility), tense logic, deontic logic (obligation and permission), conditionals, vagueness and the sorites paradox, reference and definite descriptions in the tradition of Bertrand Russell, and theories of truth including responses to the liar paradox.

History

The field grew from the work of Frege, Russell, and Wittgenstein in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and was developed by philosophers such as Saul Kripke, whose semantics for modal logic reshaped metaphysics and philosophy of language.
