Iron man (sports streak)

"Iron man" in sports
Cal Ripken Jr. broke Lou Gehrig's consecutive-games record in 1995.
MeaningAthlete known for an exceptional streak of consecutive appearances
Notable examplesCal Ripken Jr. (MLB), Lou Gehrig (MLB), Brett Favre (NFL), Doug Jarvis (NHL)

In sports, an "iron man" (or ironman) is an athlete noted for remarkable durability — typically an exceptionally long streak of consecutive games played, races finished, or appearances made without absence through injury, rest or suspension. The term conveys toughness, reliability and longevity.

Baseball

The most famous iron-man streak in sports is Cal Ripken Jr.'s record of 2,632 consecutive games played for the Baltimore Orioles, from 30 May 1982 to 19 September 1998. On 6 September 1995 Ripken broke Lou Gehrig's longstanding record of 2,130 consecutive games (1925–1939), an achievement widely credited with helping baseball recover from the 1994 players' strike. Gehrig had himself been nicknamed "The Iron Horse".

American football

Brett Favre holds the NFL record for consecutive starts by a quarterback, with 297 (321 including playoffs) from 1992 to 2010, earning him the nickname "The Iron Man" of football. Other notable streaks include Jim Marshall's 282 consecutive games played.

Ice hockey

Doug Jarvis played 964 consecutive NHL games (1975–1987), a record later surpassed by Keith Yandle (989, ending 2022) and Phil Kessel (1,064, ending 2023). Goaltender Glenn Hall famously played 502 consecutive complete games in goal, a streak that included playing through injuries with rudimentary equipment.

Other sports

In motorsport, "Ironman" drivers are those who complete punishing endurance events; in cycling, riders who finish every stage of multiple Grand Tours in a season are sometimes described in iron-man terms. The Ironman triathlon is a separate trademarked endurance event, not a streak record.
