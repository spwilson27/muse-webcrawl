Hirakud Dam

Hirakud Dam
Location: Sambalpur, Odisha, India
River: Mahanadi
Length: 25.79 km (including dykes)
Opened: 1957

Hirakud Dam is built across the Mahanadi River, about 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from Sambalpur in the state of Odisha in India. It is the longest earthen dam in the world. Behind the dam extends a lake, Hirakud Reservoir, 55 km (34 mi) long. It was one of the first major multipurpose river valley projects started after India's independence, and was formally inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 January 1957.

History

Following devastating floods of the Mahanadi delta, engineer M. Visvesvaraya proposed a detailed investigation of storage reservoirs in the basin in 1937. After independence, the foundation stone of the main dam was laid by Governor General C. Rajagopalachari on 15 March 1948. Construction displaced more than 1,100 villages and over 100,000 people — a source of lasting controversy and protest.

Structure

The main dam is 4.8 km (3.0 mi) long, spanning between the hills Lamdungri and Chandili Dunguri. Together with its earthen dykes — the longest dykes in the world — the total length reaches 25.79 km (16.03 mi). The dam is a composite structure of earth, concrete and masonry. Two observation towers, the Gandhi Minar and the Nehru Minar, overlook the reservoir, and a revolving minaret provides panoramic views.

Purpose and benefits

The dam controls floods in the Mahanadi delta, irrigates roughly 436,000 hectares of farmland in the delta and western Odisha, and generates hydroelectric power through two powerhouse stations at Burla and Chiplima, with a combined installed capacity of around 347.5 MW. The reservoir also supports fishing and tourism, and serves as a habitat for migratory birds in winter.

Displacement and siltation

The submergence of over 240 villages and the displacement of their residents, many of whom were never fully compensated, remains a contentious legacy. Siltation has also reduced the reservoir's storage capacity over the decades.
