Electroclash is a genre of music that fuses 1980s electro, new wave and synth-pop with 1990s techno, retro-style electropop and electronic dance music. It emerged in the later 1990s and was pioneered by artists such as International Deejay Gigolo Records founder DJ Hell, Tiga, and the New York–based duo Fischerspooner. The term was coined by Larry Tee, who organized the Electroclash festival in New York in 2001 and helped popularize the associated scene.

Sound and aesthetics

Electroclash combines the cold, minimalist electronic sounds of 1980s synth-pop and electro with a deliberately theatrical, fashion-conscious performance style. Vocals are often deadpan, detached or ironic, delivered over drum-machine beats and analogue synthesizer lines. Lyrically and visually the genre drew on glam, punk and club culture, with androgynous imagery, retro-futurism and a tongue-in-cheek attitude toward celebrity and consumer culture.

History

The movement grew out of underground clubs in New York, Berlin, London and Montreal around 1997–2000. Felix da Housecat's album Kittenz and Thee Glitz (2001), featuring Miss Kittin, brought the sound to a wider audience, as did Fischerspooner's single "Emerge" and the work of Peaches and Ladytron. Mainstream pop acts soon absorbed electroclash influences, and by the mid-2000s the named scene had largely dissipated, with many of its artists moving into electro house and other styles.

Legacy

Electroclash is credited with reviving interest in 1980s electronic music and paving the way for later synth-driven pop revivals. Its DIY performance ethos and fusion of art, fashion and club music influenced a generation of electronic and pop performers in the 2000s and 2010s.
