Battle of Nicopolis

The Battle of Nicopolis on 25 September 1396 near Nicopolis on the Danube (modern Nikopol, Bulgaria) was a crushing Ottoman victory over a Franco-Hungarian crusade. Sultan Bayezid I destroyed the crusader army of King Sigismund of Hungary; the battle is often called the last great crusade of the Middle Ages.

Background

Responding to Ottoman expansion in the Balkans, a large crusading army — including the flower of French chivalry under John, son of the Duke of Burgundy — marched down the Danube and besieged Nicopolis. Bayezid raised the siege of Constantinople and force-marched to meet them.

Battle

Against Sigismund's plan, the French insisted on the honour of the first charge. They routed the Ottoman light troops and irregulars but charged uphill into stakes that dismounted them before Janissaries and sipahis; exhausted, the French were overwhelmed — decisively aided by Stefan Lazarević's Serbian heavy cavalry hitting the Hungarian flank. Sigismund escaped by boat; thousands of crusaders were killed, and many prisoners were massacred in reprisal for the crusaders' earlier slaughter at Rahovo, though wealthy nobles were ransomed.

Aftermath

Western Europe was shocked; no comparable crusade followed. Bayezid's triumph was cut short in 1402 when Timur defeated and captured him at Ankara. Nicopolis remained Ottoman for nearly five centuries.
