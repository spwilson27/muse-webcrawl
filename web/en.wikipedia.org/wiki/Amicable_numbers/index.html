Amicable numbers are two different numbers related in such a way that the sum of the proper divisors of each is equal to the other number. The smallest pair of amicable numbers is (220, 284): the proper divisors of 220 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 11, 20, 22, 44, 55 and 110, summing to 284; the proper divisors of 284 are 1, 2, 4, 71 and 142, summing to 220.

History

Amicable numbers were known to the Pythagoreans, who attributed mystical significance to them as symbols of friendship. The Arab mathematician Thābit ibn Qurra derived a formula in the 9th century that generates amicable pairs for certain values, yielding pairs including (17296, 18416), later rediscovered by Fermat, and (9363584, 9437056), found by Descartes. Euler added dozens more pairs in the 18th century, yet the second-smallest pair, (1184, 1210), escaped notice until found in 1866 by the 16-year-old B. Nicolò I. Paganini.

Properties and open questions

More than a billion amicable pairs are now known through computer searches, but it remains unknown whether infinitely many exist, whether any pair is coprime, or whether a pair can consist of one even and one odd number. Related concepts include perfect numbers (equal to the sum of their own proper divisors) and sociable numbers, which form longer cycles of the same divisor-sum relationship.
