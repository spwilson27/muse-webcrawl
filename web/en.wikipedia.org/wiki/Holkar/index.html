Holkar

Rajwada, the palace of the Holkar rulers of Indore

Holkar was a family that was part of the Maratha Empire in India. The Holkars started ruling as the Maharajas of Indore, being members of the Dhangar clan. Malhar Rao Holkar, the founder of the dynasty, rose through the ranks of the Maratha army under Peshwa Baji Rao I and was granted territory in Malwa in 1731. The Holkar state, centred on Indore and Maheshwar, became one of the most powerful members of the Maratha Confederacy.

History

Malhar Rao Holkar (1693–1766) was one of the foremost Maratha commanders in northern India, campaigning in Malwa, Gujarat, and the Doab. His adopted heir and daughter-in-law, Ahilyabai Holkar (1725–1795), succeeded to the throne in 1767 and is remembered as one of India's most revered rulers: she administered the state from Maheshwar, patronised temples across India, and promoted trade and industry. Her reign is celebrated for good governance and religious tolerance.

The Holkars fought the British East India Company in the Second and Third Anglo-Maratha Wars. After Yashwant Rao Holkar's resistance ended, the Treaty of Mandsaur (1818) made Indore State a British protectorate and princely state. The state acceded to the Dominion of India in 1948, and Indore became part of Madhya Pradesh.

Notable rulers

Notable Holkar rulers include Malhar Rao Holkar I, Ahilyabai Holkar, Tukoji Rao Holkar I, Yashwant Rao Holkar, and in the 20th century Yashwant Rao Holkar II, the last ruling Maharaja of Indore, known for his patronage of modernist art and architecture.
