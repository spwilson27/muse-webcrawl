Asian water monitor

Scientific name: Varanus salvator
Family: Varanidae
Range: South and Southeast Asia
Length: typically 1.5–2 m

The Asian water monitor is one of the world's largest lizards.

The Asian water monitor (Varanus salvator) is a large varanid lizard native to South and Southeast Asia, ranging from northeastern India and Sri Lanka through southern China to Indonesia. It is among the largest lizards in the world, commonly reaching 1.5 to 2 metres in length, and is strongly associated with wetlands, rivers, canals and mangrove swamps.

Description

The water monitor has a long, muscular body, powerful limbs with sharp claws, and a laterally compressed tail adapted for swimming. Its skin is dark grey or black with rows of yellowish spots and ocelli. Like other monitors it has a long forked tongue used to detect carrion and prey by scent, and recent research indicates that monitor lizards possess mild venom glands.

Behaviour and diet

Water monitors are excellent swimmers and semi-aquatic carnivores and scavengers. They feed on fish, frogs, rodents, birds, eggs, crabs and carrion, and in urban areas — famously in Bangkok's parks and canals — they consume food waste and even dead pets. They are generally shy of humans but may bite or lash with the tail if cornered.

Reproduction

Females lay clutches of up to about 40 eggs in burrows, rotting logs or termite mounds. The young are more brightly patterned than adults and spend much of their time in trees to avoid predators, including larger monitors.

Conservation and human use

The species is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN owing to its wide range and adaptability, though it is heavily hunted for its skin, used in the leather trade, and for meat and traditional medicine. It is listed on CITES Appendix II, regulating international trade.
