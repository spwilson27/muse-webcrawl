Eisbock (German for "ice bock") is a traditional specialty beer style from the Kulmbach region of Franconia, Germany, made by partially freezing a doppelbock and removing the ice to concentrate the flavour and alcohol content. The resulting beer is rich, malty and very strong, typically between 9% and 14% alcohol by volume.

History

According to legend, the style was created accidentally in the winter of 1890 at the Kulmbacher Reichelbräu brewery, when a barrel of bock beer was left outdoors overnight during severe frost. The water in the beer partially froze, and when the barrel was brought inside and the ice removed, the workers found the remaining liquid to be an intensely flavoured, sweet and powerful brew. The brewery later reproduced the effect deliberately and marketed the result as eisbock.

Production

Eisbock is produced by freezing a strong lager such as doppelbock and skimming or draining away the ice crystals, which are almost pure water. Because alcohol has a lower freezing point than water, the remaining beer becomes concentrated in alcohol, malt sugars and flavour compounds. The process is a form of fractional freezing, sometimes loosely called freeze distillation.

Characteristics

Eisbocks are deep copper to dark brown, with intense malt sweetness, notes of dark fruit, caramel and toast, and a warming but usually smooth alcohol character. Hop bitterness is low to moderate and mainly serves to balance the malt. Well-known examples include Kulmbacher Eisbock and Schneider Aventinus Weizen-Eisbock, the latter made from a wheat doppelbock.

Legality

Because freeze concentration involves separating alcohol from water, eisbock production has occasionally raised regulatory questions in some jurisdictions, where it may be classified with distillation for tax or licensing purposes, although in Germany and most of the European Union it is treated as beer.
