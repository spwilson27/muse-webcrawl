Śvetāmbara

Śvetāmbara
ReligionJainism
Meaning"white-clad"
Founder figureMahavira (24th Tirthankara)

Śvetāmbara ("white-clad") is one of the two main sects of Jainism, the other being Digambara ("sky-clad"). Śvetāmbara monks and nuns wear simple white robes, in contrast to Digambara monks, who practise nudity.

Beliefs and practices

Śvetāmbaras hold that women can attain liberation (moksha) and that the 19th Tirthankara, Mallinatha, was female. They accept a larger body of canonical scriptures, the Āgamas, and believe Mahavira renounced the world after marriage and that he was born through embryo transfer. Śvetāmbara ascetics carry a mouth-cloth (mukhavastrika) to avoid harming airborne organisms and use a whisk broom to clear insects from their path.

Sub-sects

The tradition subdivides into image-worshipping Mūrtipūjakas and the non-image-worshipping Sthānakavāsī and Terāpanth orders, the latter founded in the 18th century by Ācārya Bhikṣu.

History

The schism between the two sects crystallised in the early centuries CE, traditionally linked to a famine-era migration south under Bhadrabahu. Śvetāmbara communities are concentrated today in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Mumbai.
