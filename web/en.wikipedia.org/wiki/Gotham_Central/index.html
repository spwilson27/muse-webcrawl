Gotham Central is a monthly American comic book series published by DC Comics from 2003 to 2006, written by Ed Brubaker and Greg Rucka, with art principally by Michael Lark. The series focuses on the Gotham City Police Department, in particular the detectives of its Major Crimes Unit, presenting police-procedural stories set in the city of Batman — who appears only occasionally and is viewed with ambivalence by officers who resent depending on a vigilante.

Premise and characters

The series follows the MCU's day and night shifts under Captain Maggie Sawyer. Detectives include Renee Montoya — whose forced outing as a lesbian in the Harvey Award-recognised story arc "Half a Life" is among the series' best-known — her partner Crispus Allen, who is killed in the line of duty during the events surrounding Infinite Crisis, Romy Chandler, Marcus Driver, Josie MacDonald and others. Cases involve Gotham's costumed criminals, including the Joker, Two-Face and Mr. Freeze, examined from the perspective of ordinary police work: forensics, interrogation, internal affairs and departmental politics.

Reception and legacy

Gotham Central was critically acclaimed, winning the Eisner Award for Best Serialized Story for "Half a Life" (2004), but sold poorly and was cancelled with issue #40 in 2006. Its reputation has grown since; the collected editions remain in print, and the series was a major influence on the television series Gotham (2014–2019), while Renee Montoya went on to become the second Question in other DC titles. Brubaker and Rucka's realistic treatment helped define the modern crime-comics style both writers became known for.

Collected editions

The series has been collected in trade paperbacks including In the Line of Duty, Half a Life, On the Freak Beat, The Quick and the Dead and Dead Robin, and in two hardcover/omnibus volumes.
