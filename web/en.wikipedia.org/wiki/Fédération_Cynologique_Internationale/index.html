The Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) is the largest international federation of national kennel clubs. Founded on 22 May 1911 by the kennel clubs of Germany, Austria, Belgium, France and the Netherlands, it is headquartered in Thuin, Belgium. Its members undertake to mutually recognise one another's pedigrees and judges, and the FCI maintains the international standards for recognised dog breeds.

Structure and activities

The federation comprises one member organisation per country — 98 full members, associate members and contract partners in total — organised into regional sections for Europe, the Americas and the Caribbean, and Asia, Africa and Oceania. It recognises more than 350 breeds, classified into ten groups (from sheepdogs and cattle dogs through retrievers, terriers, sighthounds and companion dogs), each breed's standard being maintained by its country of origin. The FCI sanctions international dog shows, including the annual World Dog Show, and oversees international championships in conformation and in working disciplines such as obedience, agility and herding trials.

Relations with other registries

The major English-speaking kennel clubs — the American Kennel Club, the Canadian Kennel Club and The Kennel Club of the United Kingdom — are not FCI members, although they cooperate with it through agreements on pedigree recognition. Breed recognition and standards can therefore differ between FCI and non-FCI systems.
