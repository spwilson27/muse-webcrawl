480p is the shorthand name for a family of video display resolutions having a vertical resolution of 480 pixels. The p stands for progressive scan, i.e. non-interlaced: each frame draws all 480 lines in sequence, rather than splitting them into alternating fields as in 480i. The resolution was the standard of DVD-Video and of standard-definition television in NTSC countries, and is often described as enhanced-definition television (EDTV) when broadcast progressively.

Resolutions

The most common 480p variants are based on the NTSC digital sampling standards: 720×480 and 704×480, used on DVDs and in digital broadcasting, with either a 4:3 or anamorphic 16:9 display aspect ratio. In computing, 640×480 (VGA) gives square pixels in a 4:3 ratio, while 854×480 (FWVGA) approximates 16:9 with square pixels and is widely used as a low-bandwidth streaming resolution on video platforms, where it is sometimes labelled "480p" alongside 360p and 720p quality options.

Comparison with 480i

Standard-definition television in North America and Japan historically used interlaced 480i. Progressive 480p at 60 frames per second displays twice the temporal information and eliminates interline flicker and combing artefacts, which made EDTV plasma and LCD sets, progressive-scan DVD players, and the sixth generation of games consoles — many of which, such as the GameCube, PlayStation 2 and Xbox, could output 480p — noticeably sharper on compatible displays.
