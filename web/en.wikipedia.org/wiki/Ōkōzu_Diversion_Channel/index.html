Ōkōzu Diversion Channel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ōkōzu Diversion Channel (大河津分水路, Ōkōzu Bunsuiro) is a flood-control channel in Niigata Prefecture, Japan, that diverts water from the Shinano River—the longest river in Japan—directly to the Sea of Japan near Teradomari. Opened in 1922 after more than a decade of construction, the roughly 10 km channel was built to protect the low-lying Echigo Plain and the city of Niigata from the Shinano's devastating floods.

History

Calls to divert the Shinano's floodwaters dated to the Edo period, but work began only in 1909. The channel's movable weir at Ōkōzu regulates how much water enters the diversion; during major floods it can discharge enormous volumes to the sea. A catastrophic breach of the channel's levee in 1927, when the diversion's embankment collapsed, flooded the very plain it was meant to protect and led to reconstruction with stronger works, completed in the early 1930s.

Significance

The diversion is regarded as a landmark of Japanese civil engineering and a cornerstone of Niigata's transformation into a major rice-producing region. The Shinano River Ohkouzu Riverside Museum near the weir documents the project and the river's history.
