Radio drama

Radio drama (or audio drama, audio play) is a dramatised, purely acoustic performance broadcast on radio or published as audio. With no visual component, radio drama depends on dialogue, music and sound effects to convey story and setting, and has been called a "theatre of the mind".

History

Regular radio drama began in the 1920s as broadcasting spread. In the United States the 1930s and 1940s were a golden age, with series such as The Mercury Theatre on the Air — whose 1938 War of the Worlds adaptation caused public alarm — and long-running soap operas, a genre named for its detergent sponsors. In Britain, BBC radio drama grew from features and plays into institutions such as The Archers (1951– ), the world's longest-running drama.

Decline and revival

Television displaced radio drama in many countries from the 1950s, though the BBC, German public radio and others maintained strong traditions. Podcasting and streaming in the 21st century revived the form, with fiction podcasts and audio productions reaching global audiences.

Techniques

Writers and producers use stereo perspective, foley effects, silence and narration to orient listeners; actors often voice multiple roles.
