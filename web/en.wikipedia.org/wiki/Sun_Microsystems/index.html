Sun Microsystems

Sun Microsystems, Inc. was an American multinational technology company that sold computers, computer components, software, and information technology services. Headquartered in Santa Clara, California, Sun was founded on February 24, 1982, by Vinod Khosla, Andy Bechtolsheim, and Scott McNealy, with Bill Joy joining shortly afterward. The company name was derived from the Stanford University Network (SUN), the workstation project Bechtolsheim designed at Stanford.

Sun was best known for its Unix workstations and servers, the SPARC microprocessor architecture, the Solaris operating system, the NFS network file-sharing system, and the Java programming language, which was introduced in 1995 and became one of the most widely used software platforms in the world.

History

Sun's early workstations, beginning with the Sun-1, were built around the Motorola 68000 processor and ran a version of Unix. The company grew rapidly during the 1980s, becoming a leading vendor of technical workstations for engineering, finance, and academic markets. Its SPARC (Scalable Processor Architecture) RISC processors, introduced in 1987, powered generations of Sun machines, and the Solaris operating system evolved from SunOS into a leading enterprise Unix.

During the dot-com boom of the late 1990s, Sun's servers were central to the build-out of the commercial Internet, and its market capitalization peaked at over $200 billion in 2000 under the slogan "The Network Is the Computer." After the crash of 2001, the company struggled with falling hardware margins, competition from Linux on commodity x86 hardware, and the shift toward cloud computing. Sun acquired storage company StorageTek in 2005 and open-source database vendor MySQL AB in 2008.

Acquisition by Oracle

On April 20, 2009, Oracle Corporation announced it would acquire Sun for approximately $7.4 billion. The deal closed on January 27, 2010, after approval by the European Commission, and Sun became a subsidiary of Oracle. Oracle continued developing Solaris, Java, and SPARC-based systems, and Sun's former campus in Menlo Park later became associated with Facebook. Scott McNealy's famous quips and the company's engineering culture left a lasting mark on Silicon Valley.
