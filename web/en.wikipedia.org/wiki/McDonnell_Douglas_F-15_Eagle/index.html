The McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle is an American twin-engine, all-weather tactical fighter aircraft designed by McDonnell Douglas (now part of Boeing). Following reviews of proposals, the US Air Force selected McDonnell Douglas's design in 1969 to meet the service's need for a dedicated air superiority fighter. Its first flight was in July 1972, and it entered service in 1976.

Design

The F-15 was designed "not a pound for air-to-ground": a large, lightweight airframe with a broad lifting fuselage, two Pratt & Whitney F100 engines giving a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than one, a large pulse-doppler radar, and armament of AIM-7 Sparrow and AIM-9 Sidewinder missiles plus a 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon. Later models added AMRAAM missiles, improved radars (including the APG-63(V)3 AESA on some aircraft) and conformal fuel tanks.

Operational history

The F-15 is among the most successful modern fighters, with over 100 aerial combat victories and no losses in air-to-air combat — a record accumulated mainly by Israeli, US and Saudi pilots in conflicts including the 1982 Lebanon War, Operation Desert Storm and Balkan operations. The two-seat F-15E Strike Eagle, introduced in 1988, added a deep-strike, all-weather ground-attack capability while retaining full air-to-air performance. The latest derivative, the F-15EX Eagle II, entered US service in the 2020s as a single-seat multirole variant with fly-by-wire controls and the EPAWSS electronic-warfare suite. Exports have gone to Japan (built under licence by Mitsubishi), Israel, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Korea and Qatar.
