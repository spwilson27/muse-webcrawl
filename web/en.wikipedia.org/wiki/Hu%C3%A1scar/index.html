Huáscar Inca (c. 1491–1533) was Sapa Inca of the Inca Empire from 1527 to 1532. He succeeded his father, Huayna Capac, and his elder brother Ninan Cuyochi, both of whom died in an epidemic, possibly smallpox, that swept the empire shortly before the arrival of the Spanish conquistadors.

Civil war

Huáscar, based in the capital Cusco, came into conflict with his half-brother Atahualpa, who controlled the northern part of the empire from Quito. The dispute escalated into the Inca Civil War, in which Atahualpa's veteran armies defeated Huáscar's forces, culminating in the Battle of Quipaipán in 1532. Huáscar was captured and later executed on Atahualpa's orders at Andamarca in 1533, after Atahualpa himself had been taken prisoner by Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca.

Legacy

The civil war gravely weakened the Inca Empire on the eve of the Spanish conquest. Huáscar's name is preserved in the Chilean ironclad Huáscar, captured from Peru during the War of the Pacific.
