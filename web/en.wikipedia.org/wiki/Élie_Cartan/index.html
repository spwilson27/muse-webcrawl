Élie Joseph Cartan (9 April 1869 – 6 May 1951) was an influential French mathematician who did fundamental work in the theory of Lie groups, differential systems (the PDE–theoretic counterpart of differential geometry), and differential geometry. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the twentieth century.

Work

Cartan's thesis (1894) completed the classification of semisimple Lie algebras begun by Wilhelm Killing, and he later classified the simple Lie groups, symmetric spaces, and real forms of complex Lie algebras. He developed the theory of spinors in 1913 — before their role in quantum mechanics was known — and created the method of moving frames (repère mobile) and the exterior calculus of differential forms, which became the standard language of modern differential geometry.

In general relativity, the Einstein–Cartan theory extends Einstein's gravity by coupling spacetime torsion to the spin of matter, building on Cartan's generalized geometry of connections (the Cartan connection). His work on exterior differential systems, summarized in Les systèmes différentiels extérieurs et leurs applications géométriques (1945), remains foundational.

Life

Born in Dolomieu in the Isère department, the son of a blacksmith, Cartan studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at Montpellier, Lyon, Nancy, and finally the University of Paris (Sorbonne), where he spent most of his career. His son Henri Cartan was a leading mathematician of the Bourbaki generation. The lunar crater Cartan and the asteroid 17917 Cartan are named for him.
