Viral marketing

Viral marketing is a business strategy that uses existing social networks to promote a product, mainly on the Internet and through word of mouth, in a manner analogous to the spread of a biological virus or a computer virus. Its goal is to increase brand awareness or sales through self-replicating messages — today chiefly short online videos, images, and interactive content that users voluntarily share.

History

The term is often credited to a 1996 Fast Company article by Jeffrey Rayport; venture capitalist Steve Jurvetson popularised it in 1997, describing Hotmail's practice of appending a promotional line to outgoing emails as the classic example. The strategy largely displaced the older term "word-of-mouth marketing" as social media platforms made sharing measurable and near-instantaneous.

Methods and examples

Common vehicles include viral videos, shareable memes, advergames, email forwards, and incentivised referral schemes. Famous campaigns include the "Subservient Chicken" for Burger King (2004), Old Spice's "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (2010), and the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge (2014), a charity-driven example that raised over $100 million. Marketers value the high "pass-along rate," which can be quantified as a K-factor analogous to a virus's reproduction number.

Criticism

Viral campaigns are notoriously unpredictable — deliberate attempts often fail to spread — and undisclosed paid seeding or astroturfing has drawn regulatory and ethical criticism.
