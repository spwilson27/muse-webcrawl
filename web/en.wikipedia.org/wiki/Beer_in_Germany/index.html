Beer in Germany is a central part of German culture. Germany has around 1,500 breweries producing more than 5,000 brands of beer, and the country is consistently among the world's highest per-capita consumers of beer, though consumption has declined from its 1970s peak. Beer is most strongly associated with Bavaria, home of the Oktoberfest in Munich, the world's largest beer festival.

History

Brewing in the German lands dates back to early medieval monasteries, whose monks refined techniques for hopping, lagering and quality control. In 1516 the Bavarian dukes Wilhelm IV and Ludwig X issued the Reinheitsgebot (purity order), restricting beer ingredients to water, barley and hops — later extended to the whole country and widely regarded as the world's oldest food-quality regulation still influential today. Industrial lager brewing in the 19th century, aided by refrigeration and rail transport, made pilsner-style beer dominant.

Styles

German styles include Pilsner, Helles, Dunkel, Märzen/Oktoberfestbier, Bock and Doppelbock, wheat beers such as Hefeweizen and Berliner Weisse, Altbier from Düsseldorf, Kölsch from Cologne, Gose from Leipzig, and smoked Rauchbier from Bamberg. Ales and lagers coexist, with regional loyalties often strong.

Culture

Beer gardens, festival tents, the communal Maß (one-litre mug) and the toast "Prost!" are fixtures of German social life. The annual Oktoberfest draws around six million visitors. German beer is exported worldwide, and German brewing traditions have influenced brewing in North America, Latin America and Asia.
