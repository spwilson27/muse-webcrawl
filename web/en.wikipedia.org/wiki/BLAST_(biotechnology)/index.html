BLAST (biotechnology)

Original authorsStephen Altschul, Warren Gish, Webb Miller, Eugene Myers, David J. Lipman
Initial release1990
TypeBioinformatics tool
DeveloperNCBI

In bioinformatics, BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) is an algorithm and program for comparing primary biological sequence information, such as the amino-acid sequences of proteins or the nucleotides of DNA and RNA sequences. A BLAST search enables a researcher to compare a subject protein or nucleotide sequence (called a query) with a library or database of sequences, and identify database sequences that resemble the query sequence above a certain threshold.

History

BLAST was developed by Stephen Altschul, Warren Gish, Webb Miller, Eugene Myers and David J. Lipman at the U.S. National Institutes of Health and published in the Journal of Molecular Biology in 1990. It became one of the most widely used bioinformatics programs, and the original paper is among the most cited scientific papers of all time. It is hosted by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI).

Algorithm

BLAST uses a heuristic method that finds short matches (words) between the query and database sequences, then attempts to extend those matches into longer local alignments. Results are scored with a substitution matrix such as BLOSUM62, and statistical significance is expressed through an expectation value (E-value), which estimates the number of alignments of equal or better score expected by chance.

Programs

The BLAST family includes blastn (nucleotide against nucleotide), blastp (protein against protein), blastx (translated nucleotide against protein), tblastn (protein against translated nucleotide) and tblastx (translated nucleotide against translated nucleotide). Later variants such as PSI-BLAST and DELTA-BLAST use position-specific scoring to detect more distant relationships.
