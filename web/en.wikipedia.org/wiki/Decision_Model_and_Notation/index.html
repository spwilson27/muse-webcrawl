Decision Model and Notation

Decision Model and Notation (DMN) is a standard published by the Object Management Group (OMG) for modeling and executing business decisions. It provides a common notation, understandable by business users and technical developers alike, for describing decision logic in a precise, tabular, and graphical form that can be automated by decision engines.

Overview

DMN models consist of Decision Requirements Diagrams (DRDs), which show decisions, input data, business knowledge models, and knowledge sources and their dependencies, together with decision logic specified as decision tables, boxed expressions, and literal expressions. The first version, DMN 1.0, was published in 2015; subsequent versions (1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and later) refined the specification.

FEEL

DMN defines the Friendly Enough Expression Language (FEEL) for writing expressions in decision tables and boxed logic. FEEL is designed to be readable by business users while being formally executable, supporting functions, ranges, lists, and contexts.

Relationship to BPMN and CMMN

DMN is one of the OMG's family of business modeling standards alongside BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) and CMMN (Case Management Model and Notation): BPMN describes processes, CMMN describes cases, and DMN describes the decisions made within them. A BPMN business rule task typically invokes a decision modeled in DMN.
