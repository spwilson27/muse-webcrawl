Protected areas of Romania

National parks: 13
Natural parks: 14
Biosphere reserves: 3 (Danube Delta, Retezat, Pietrosul Rodnei)
Coverage: roughly 23% of territory including Natura 2000 sites

Protected areas of Romania comprise a network of national parks, natural parks, biosphere reserves, nature reserves, and Natura 2000 sites covering a substantial share of the country's territory. Romania retains some of Europe's largest remaining tracts of virgin forest and the continent's largest populations of brown bears, wolves, and lynx outside Russia.

National parks

Romania has 13 national parks, including Retezat (the first, established 1935), Piatra Craiului, Domogled-Valea Cernei, Buila-Vânturarița, and the Danube Delta, which is both a national-level biosphere reserve and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Administration

Protected areas are administered by the National Agency for Protected Natural Areas (ANANP) together with Romsilva, local councils, and non-governmental organizations. Many sites also carry overlapping Natura 2000 designations under EU nature directives.
