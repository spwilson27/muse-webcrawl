Trisomy

A trisomy is a type of chromosomal abnormality in which a cell has three copies of a particular chromosome instead of the usual two. Trisomies are a form of aneuploidy and usually arise from nondisjunction during meiosis.

Types in humans

The best-known human trisomy is Down syndrome (trisomy 21), the most common chromosomal condition compatible with survival to birth, occurring in roughly 1 in 700 births. Other autosomal trisomies seen in live births include trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome) and trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome), both associated with severe congenital abnormalities and high infant mortality. Sex-chromosome trisomies include Klinefelter syndrome (XXY), triple X syndrome (XXX), and XYY syndrome, which often have milder phenotypes.

Causes and detection

Most trisomies result from errors in chromosome segregation during gamete formation, with risk increasing with maternal age. They can be detected prenatally by karyotyping, chorionic villus sampling, amniocentesis, or non-invasive prenatal testing of cell-free fetal DNA.
