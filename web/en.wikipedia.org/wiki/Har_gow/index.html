Har gow

Har gow (also spelled ha gao, ha gow, or hakao; Chinese: 蝦餃) is a traditional Cantonese shrimp dumpling served in dim sum. The dumpling is sometimes called a shrimp bonnet for its pleated shape.

Har gow is, along with siu mai, one of the classic dishes of the dim sum tradition, and a skill benchmark for dim sum chefs: the wrapper should be thin and translucent but strong enough not to break when picked up with chopsticks.

Preparation

The wrapper is made from wheat and tapioca (or potato) starch, giving it a characteristic translucency after steaming. The filling consists of shrimp, often with bamboo shoots and pork fat, seasoned with salt, sugar, and sesame oil. Traditionally the dumpling is pleated with at least seven, and preferably ten or more, folds, and steamed in a bamboo basket.

Serving

Har gow is typically served hot with soy sauce or chilli oil and eaten as part of a yum cha meal with tea.
