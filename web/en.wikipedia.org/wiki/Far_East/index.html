Far East

The Far East is a European term for the geographical region that encompasses East and Southeast Asia, as well as parts of the Russian Far East. The term came into use in European geopolitical discourse in the 19th century, denoting the easternmost territories of the Asian continent relative to Europe.

Scope

Definitions vary, but the Far East typically includes China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia, Taiwan, and the Russian Far East, and in broader usage the Southeast Asian countries of Vietnam, Thailand, Myanmar, Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, and others. Modern usage increasingly prefers the more precise terms "East Asia" and "Southeast Asia", as "Far East" is a Eurocentric designation.

History of the term

Along with "Near East" and "Middle East", the term belongs to a family of designations measured from a European vantage point. It remains common in institutional names — such as the Far Eastern Federal District of Russia and various universities and journals — and in historical contexts such as the First Sino-Japanese War era and the Second World War's Far Eastern theatres.
