Parliament of Lebanon

The Parliament building on Place de l'Étoile, Beirut.

The Parliament of Lebanon (Arabic: Majlis an-Nuwwab, "Assembly of Deputies") is the unicameral national legislature of Lebanon. It is elected for a four-year term by universal suffrage and meets in the Place de l'Étoile building in downtown Beirut.

Composition

The parliament has 128 seats. Under Lebanon's confessional system, seats are allocated in equal halves between Christians and Muslims, and within each bloc proportionally among the recognised sects: Maronite, Greek Orthodox, Greek Catholic, Armenian Orthodox, Armenian Catholic, Protestant, Sunni, Shia, Druze, Alawite and others. This distribution was formalised by the Taif Agreement of 1989, which ended the Lebanese Civil War and replaced the earlier 6:5 Christian-to-Muslim ratio.

Functions

Parliament elects the President of the Republic, approves the government through a vote of confidence, passes legislation and the budget, and exercises oversight of the executive. Its presiding officer, the Speaker, is by convention a Shia Muslim; Nabih Berri has held the office since 1992, making him one of the longest-serving parliamentary speakers in the world.

History

A representative council existed under the French Mandate from the 1920s, and the first parliament of independent Lebanon sat after 1943 under the National Pact. Parliamentary terms have repeatedly been extended in times of crisis, including during the civil war (1975–1990) and in 2013–2017, when elections were postponed several times before being held in 2018 under a new proportional-representation electoral law.
