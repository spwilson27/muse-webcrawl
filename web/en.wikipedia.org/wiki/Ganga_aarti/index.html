Ganga aarti

Ganga aarti (ritual of offering prayers to the Ganges river) is performed daily at dusk in the three holy cities of Haridwar, Rishikesh, and Varanasi in India. It is a ceremony in which lamps with oil or ghee are offered to the goddess Ganga, the personification of the river. The aarti is performed on a stage facing the river by a group of priests, dressed in similar clothing, who move the lamps in a synchronized, choreographed motion accompanied by the chanting of bhajans and the ringing of bells.

Ceremony

In Varanasi, the aarti takes place every evening at the Dashashwamedh Ghat, where seven priests perform the ritual together. Large crowds gather on the ghats and in boats on the river to watch. The ceremony begins with the blowing of a conch shell and includes the waving of incense sticks, large flaming lamps moved in circles, and flower offerings floated on the river.

In Haridwar, the aarti is performed at Har Ki Pauri, and in Rishikesh at the Triveni Ghat and along the ghats near Parmarth Niketan. Each place has its own scale and character, but all share the offering of light to the river at sunset.

Significance

The Ganges is regarded in Hinduism as a goddess and a purifier of sins. The aarti expresses gratitude and devotion, and forms part of the daily worship (puja) offered to the river by devotees who believe that witnessing or participating in the ceremony brings blessings.
