Dame Julia Mary Walters (born 22 February 1950) is an English actress and author. One of Britain's most popular and prolific actresses, she has won numerous accolades including two BAFTA Film Awards, four BAFTA Television Awards, and an Academy Award nomination. She first came to prominence in Educating Rita (1983) and became widely known for her long collaboration with comedian Victoria Wood.

Early life

Walters was born in Edgbaston, Birmingham, the daughter of Mary Bridget (née O'Brien), a postal clerk of Irish Catholic descent, and Thomas Walters, a builder and decorator. She trained as a nurse before studying drama at Manchester Polytechnic (now Manchester Metropolitan University).

Career

After work with the Everyman Theatre in Liverpool, Walters played Rita in Willy Russell's stage play Educating Rita and reprised the role in the 1983 film opposite Michael Caine, winning the BAFTA and Golden Globe for Best Actress and receiving an Academy Award nomination. Her double act with Victoria Wood produced the sketch series Wood and Walters and Victoria Wood: As Seen on TV, featuring the beloved soap parody Acorn Antiques and its receptionist Mrs Overall.

Film roles include Personal Services (1987), Buster (1988), Stepping Out (1991), Billy Elliot (2000) — for which she was again Oscar-nominated — Calendar Girls (2003), Becoming Jane (2007), the Mamma Mia! films, and Paddington (2014) and its sequel. To younger audiences she is Molly Weasley in the Harry Potter film series. Television work includes Dinnerladies, Mo (2010), for which she won the BAFTA TV Award for Best Actress as Mo Mowlam, and National Treasure (2016).

Honours

Walters was appointed CBE in 1999 and DBE in 2017 for services to drama. In 2020 she disclosed that she had been treated for stage III bowel cancer, recovering to return to acting.
