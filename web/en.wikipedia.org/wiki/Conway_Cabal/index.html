Conway Cabal

The Conway Cabal was an alleged plot in late 1777 and early 1778 by a group of Continental Army officers and members of the Continental Congress to replace George Washington as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. It takes its name from Brigadier General Thomas Conway, whose critical letters about Washington became the affair's centerpiece.

Background

After the American defeats at Brandywine and Germantown and the loss of Philadelphia in autumn 1777, Washington's leadership came under criticism, while Horatio Gates's victory at Saratoga burnished his reputation. Conway, an Irish-born French officer, wrote to Gates disparaging Washington — a letter containing the line that "Heaven has been determined to save your Country; or a weak General and bad Counsellors would have ruined it" — and its contents reached Washington through intermediaries.

Course of events

Washington confronted Conway, who equivocated; suspicion spread that Gates, Congressman Samuel Adams, and members of the Board of War were conspiring against Washington. In January 1778 General John Cadwalader wounded Conway in a duel after Conway's maneuvering against Washington. Conway later apologized and resigned. Historians debate whether there was ever an organized "cabal" at all: the evidence suggests loose grumbling, factional correspondence and ambition among a few officers and congressmen rather than a coordinated conspiracy, and the episode mainly revealed — and strengthened — Washington's political position.
