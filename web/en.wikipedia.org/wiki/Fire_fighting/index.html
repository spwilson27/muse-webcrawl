Firefighting

Firefighting is a profession aimed at controlling and extinguishing fires. It involves personnel known as firefighters, who rescue persons, safeguard property, and protect the environment during fires and other emergencies. Firefighters also respond to medical emergencies, hazardous materials incidents, vehicle collisions and technical rescues, and work in fire prevention and public education.

Methods

Fire requires fuel, oxygen and heat — the fire triangle. Extinguishment works by removing one or more of these elements: water cools the fuel below its ignition temperature; foams and other agents smother the fire by excluding oxygen; and removing fuel — as in cutting a firebreak ahead of a wildfire — starves it. Structural firefighting generally follows an incident command system and proceeds through stages including size-up, rescue, exposure protection, confinement, extinguishment, ventilation and overhaul.

Equipment

Firefighters wear personal protective equipment including turnout coats and trousers, helmets, gloves, boots and self-contained breathing apparatus. They deploy fire apparatus such as engines (pumpers), aerial ladders and tenders, and use tools including hoses, nozzles, ladders, thermal imaging cameras and hydraulic rescue tools.

Organisation

Most firefighters belong to municipal fire departments, though volunteer departments protect many smaller communities, particularly in the United States. Industrial, airport, military and wildland firefighters specialise in particular environments. Training standards in North America are set largely by the National Fire Protection Association, and firefighters commonly work extended shifts, such as 24 hours on followed by 48 hours off.

Hazards

Firefighting is among the more dangerous occupations: hazards include burns, smoke inhalation, structural collapse, heat stress, vehicle accidents and long-term exposure to carcinogens in smoke and contaminated gear, as well as cardiovascular strain — heart attacks are a leading cause of on-duty firefighter deaths.

History

Organised firefighting dates to ancient Rome, whose vigiles patrolled the city. Modern departments grew out of 17th- and 18th-century insurance brigades and volunteer companies in cities such as London, Amsterdam, Boston and New York. Benjamin Franklin helped organise one of America's first volunteer fire companies in Philadelphia in 1736. Steam and later motorised pumpers, professional career departments, and modern building and fire codes transformed the service during the 19th and 20th centuries.
