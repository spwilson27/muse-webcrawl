Quin Abbey (Irish: Mainistir Chuinche) is a ruined Franciscan friary in Quin, County Clare, Ireland. It was built in the Gothic style in the early 15th century on the remains of an earlier Norman castle and is regarded as one of the finest and most complete Franciscan ruins in Ireland.

History

The site was first fortified around 1280 by the Anglo-Norman de Clare family, who built a large square castle with round towers at its corners. The castle was attacked and burned in 1318 after the battle of Dysert O'Dea.

Around 1433, Sioda Cam MacNamara founded a Franciscan friary on the site, incorporating the fire-blackened remains of the castle towers into the new building. His son Maccon MacNamara completed the work, building the cloister around 1450. The friary was suppressed during the Reformation in 1541, but friars returned intermittently, and a small community persisted — at times in secret — into the 19th century. The last friar, Father John Hogan, died in 1820.

Architecture

The abbey preserves much of its church, with a long nave and chancel separated by a slender central tower, and an unusually complete cloister garth with its covered walkway still roofed on all four sides. The cloister is considered the best-preserved feature of its kind in an Irish friary. The MacNamara family tomb, with carved effigies, is located in the chancel.

The abbey is a National Monument in state care and is accessible to visitors.
