Cleitus the Black (Greek: Κλεῖτος ὁ Μέλας; c. 375 – 328 BC) was a Macedonian officer in the army of Alexander the Great, famed for saving Alexander's life at the Battle of the Granicus — and for being murdered by Alexander in a drunken quarrel at Samarkand.

Career

Cleitus commanded the Royal Squadron of Companion cavalry. At the Granicus (334 BC), as the Persian Spithridates raised his sword behind Alexander, Cleitus severed the Persian's arm with a single blow. He later distinguished himself at Gaugamela and in the eastern campaigns, and Alexander appointed him satrap of Bactria and Sogdiana.

Death

At a banquet in Samarkand in 328 BC, wine loosened tongues: Cleitus defended Philip II against sycophants who belittled him before Alexander, and taunted Alexander with his adoption of Persian customs. In a rage Alexander seized a spear and killed him. The tradition (Arrian; Plutarch) reports days of inconsolable remorse.

Legacy

The killing marks, in the ancient tradition, the moral deterioration of Alexander's reign and the danger of unrestrained power. It remains a set-piece of Greek moral biography.
