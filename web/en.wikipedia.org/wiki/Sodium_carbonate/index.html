Sodium carbonate

Sodium carbonate (also known as washing soda, soda ash and soda crystals) is the inorganic compound with the formula Na2CO3 and its various hydrates. All forms are white, odourless, water-soluble salts that yield alkaline solutions in water.

Production

Historically it was extracted from the ashes of plants grown in sodium-rich soils ("barilla"). Modern production relies mainly on the Solvay process, in which sodium chloride and limestone are converted to sodium carbonate and calcium chloride, and on mining of the mineral trona, the principal source in the United States (notably from Green River, Wyoming). The earlier Leblanc process was the first industrial route.

Applications

The largest use of sodium carbonate is in glass manufacture, where soda ash lowers the melting point of silica. It is also used as a water softener in laundry (washing soda), as a food additive (E500) and acidity regulator, in the manufacture of detergents, paper and other chemicals, and as a relatively strong base in domestic and laboratory chemistry.

Occurrence

Sodium carbonate crystallises from water as the decahydrate (washing soda) and occurs naturally in soda lakes and as the minerals natron, trona and thermonatrite.
