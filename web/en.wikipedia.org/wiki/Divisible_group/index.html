Divisible group

In a divisible group, every element can be "divided" by any positive integer.

In mathematics, in the theory of groups, a divisible group is an abelian group in which every element can be divided by every positive integer. Formally, an abelian group G is divisible if, for every element g in G and every positive integer n, there exists an element h in G such that

n h = g.

Equivalently, multiplication by any positive integer is a surjective map on G. Important examples are the additive group of the rational numbers, the additive group of the real numbers, and the Prüfer groups.

Structure

The structure theorem for divisible groups states that every divisible abelian group is a direct sum of copies of the rational numbers and of Prüfer p-groups for various primes p. Divisible groups are exactly the injective objects in the category of abelian groups (that is, the injective Z-modules), a fact that makes them central in homological algebra: every abelian group can be embedded in a divisible group.

Properties

The image of a divisible group under any homomorphism is again divisible, as is any direct sum of divisible groups. A divisible group that is also torsion-free is simply a vector space over the rationals. No non-trivial finite group is divisible. See also abelian group and injective module.
