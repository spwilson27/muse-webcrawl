Sierpiński triangle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sierpiński triangle (also called the Sierpiński gasket or sieve) is a fractal attractive fixed set with the shape of an equilateral triangle subdivided recursively into smaller equilateral triangles. It is named after the Polish mathematician Wacław Sierpiński, who described it in 1915, although the pattern had appeared in art and decoration centuries earlier.

Construction

Start with an equilateral triangle. Subdivide it into four congruent equilateral triangles and remove the central one. Repeat the process indefinitely on the three remaining triangles. At each step the area is multiplied by 3/4, so in the limit the set has zero area, while its perimeter grows without bound. The figure is exactly self-similar: each corner triangle is a scaled copy of the whole.

Properties

The Sierpiński triangle has Hausdorff dimension log(3)/log(2) ≈ 1.585. It can also be generated by the "chaos game" — repeatedly moving halfway from a random starting point toward a randomly chosen vertex — and by cellular automaton Rule 90, whose rows reproduce the pattern of odd entries in Pascal's triangle modulo 2.
