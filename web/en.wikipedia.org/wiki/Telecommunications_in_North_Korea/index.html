Telecommunications in North Korea

Telecommunications in North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea) are among the most restricted in the world. Internet access is limited to a small number of government officials and institutions, while most citizens use a domestic intranet known as Kwangmyong. Mobile telephony is provided chiefly by Koryolink, a joint venture launched in 2008.

Telephone system

Fixed-line telephony is state-run and serves ministries, factories and some residences. Mobile subscriptions grew after Koryolink began service in Pyongyang in December 2008; by the mid-2010s several million subscriptions were reported, though foreign residents were segregated onto separate networks and ordinary citizens could not call abroad.

Internet and media

Global internet access is confined to a handful of elites, universities and foreign organizations. Most North Koreans access Kwangmyong, a walled-garden intranet carrying state-approved content. Television and radio are state-controlled, with broadcasts from the Korean Central Television and Korean Central Broadcasting Station.
