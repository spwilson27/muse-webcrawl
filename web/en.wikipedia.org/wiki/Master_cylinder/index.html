Master cylinder

Master cylinder
Used inHydraulic brakes and clutches
FluidBrake fluid (DOT 3/4/5.1)

In automotive engineering, the master cylinder is a control device that converts force (commonly from a driver's foot) into hydraulic pressure. It is the central component of hydraulic brake and clutch systems, pushing fluid through lines to slave cylinders at the wheels.

Operation

When the brake pedal is pressed, a pushrod drives pistons inside the master cylinder bore, pressurizing brake fluid. Modern vehicles use tandem master cylinders with two independent circuits, so failure of one circuit still leaves partial braking; a reservoir atop the cylinder supplies fluid and compensates for pad wear.

Failure symptoms

A worn master cylinder can cause a spongy or sinking pedal, uneven braking, or fluid leaks—conditions requiring prompt repair for safety.
