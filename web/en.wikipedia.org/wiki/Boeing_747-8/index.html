The Boeing 747-8 is the final and largest variant of the Boeing 747 wide-body airliner family, developed from the 747-400 with a lengthened fuselage, redesigned wings, and new General Electric GEnx engines. It was produced in two main versions: the 747-8F freighter and the 747-8 Intercontinental (747-8I) passenger airliner. At 76.3 metres (250 ft), it is the longest passenger aircraft ever built.

Development

Boeing launched the 747-8 in November 2005, applying technology from the 787 Dreamliner, including the GEnx-2B engine and a redesigned wing with raked wingtips. The freighter first flew on 8 February 2010 and entered service with Cargolux in October 2011; the Intercontinental first flew in March 2011 and entered service with Lufthansa in June 2012. The fuselage stretch added about 5.6 metres over the 747-400, allowing roughly 467 passengers in a typical three-class layout, though most operators chose the type for premium-heavy configurations.

Sales and production

The 747-8F proved the more successful variant, becoming a mainstay of global cargo fleets with carriers such as Cargolux, Cathay Pacific Cargo, UPS, and Atlas Air, valued for its nose-loading door and payload of about 137 tonnes. Passenger sales were limited to Lufthansa, Korean Air, and Air China amid the industry shift to large twinjets. Boeing announced the end of 747 production in 2020; the final aircraft, a 747-8F for Atlas Air, was delivered on 31 January 2023. Two 747-8s are being converted as the next Air Force One aircraft (designated VC-25B).

Specifications (747-8I)

Length 76.3 m; wingspan 68.4 m; maximum takeoff weight 447,700 kg; range about 13,600 km (7,340 nmi); typical cruise Mach 0.85, powered by four GEnx-2B67 engines.
