Zona Rosa, Mexico City

Zona Rosa (English: "Pink Zone") is a neighbourhood in Mexico City, within the Cuauhtémoc borough, roughly bounded by Paseo de la Reforma, Avenida Insurgentes, Avenida Chapultepec and the colonia Juárez. It is known for its shopping, nightlife, restaurants and hotels, and since the 1990s it has also been recognised as the centre of the city's gay community. Its name has been explained variously: that its buildings were painted pink, that it was a "red-light" district made respectable ("pink" rather than red), or, in the words of writer Carlos Monsiváis, that it was "too timid to be red and too presumptuous to be white".

History

The area developed as part of the elite colonia Juárez in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when wealthy families built European-style mansions there. By the 1950s and 1960s it had become a fashionable bohemian quarter of cafés, galleries, bookshops and restaurants frequented by intellectuals and artists. Commercialisation and the arrival of fast-food chains and offices from the 1970s onward changed its character, and the 1985 Mexico City earthquake damaged a number of buildings. Although there have been periods of decline and crime concerns, successive renovation projects on streets such as Génova have sought to revive its pedestrian and tourist appeal.

Today

Zona Rosa contains major hotels along Reforma, antique shops, the El Péndulo bookshop-café, and the city's "Koreatown" (Pequeño Seúl) in its southern streets, with many Korean restaurants and businesses. It is also the historic heart of Mexico City's gay nightlife, with bars and clubs concentrated around streets such as Amberes and Florencia; the annual pride march passes along Reforma at its edge, by the Angel of Independence monument. The neighbourhood is served by the Insurgentes station of the Mexico City Metro and by Metrobús services on Reforma and Insurgentes.
