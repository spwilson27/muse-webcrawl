Service-level agreement

A service-level agreement (SLA) is an agreement between a service provider and a customer, defining the level of service expected from the provider. SLAs are output-based in that their purpose is specifically to define what the customer will receive. SLAs do not define how the service is provided or delivered.

The concept has grown out of the practice of incorporating service-level commitments into contracts for services, and is now used widely in telecommunications, information technology outsourcing, cloud computing, and managed services.

Contents of an SLA

A typical SLA describes the services to be delivered, the performance metrics by which delivery is measured (such as availability, response time, and error rates), problem-reporting and escalation procedures, monitoring and reporting arrangements, and the remedies or penalties ("service credits") that apply if the provider fails to meet the agreed levels. It may also set out the customer's responsibilities, security and legal compliance requirements, and procedures for changing the agreement.

Service-level indicators and objectives

Measurable characteristics such as uptime percentage, latency, throughput, and support response times are called service-level indicators (SLIs); the target values committed to in the SLA are service-level objectives (SLOs). Cloud providers commonly express availability in "nines" — for example, 99.9% ("three nines") or 99.99% availability over a billing period.

Types

SLAs may be customer-based (covering all services used by one customer), service-based (the same terms for all customers of a service), or multilevel, combining corporate, customer, and service layers. Agreements between internal departments are sometimes called operational-level agreements (OLAs), while contracts with third-party suppliers underpinning an SLA are called underpinning contracts.
