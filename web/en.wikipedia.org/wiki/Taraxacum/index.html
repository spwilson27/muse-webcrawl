Taraxacum

Taraxacum is a large genus of flowering plants in the family Asteraceae, which consists of species commonly known as dandelions. The genus is native to Eurasia but has been introduced worldwide; two species, T. officinale and T. erythrospermum, are found as common weeds globally. The common dandelion is well known for its yellow flower heads that turn into round balls of silvery, windborne seeds.

Description

Species grow from taproots and produce a basal rosette of leaves and hollow, leafless stems bearing a single composite flower head of yellow ray florets. After flowering, the head matures into a spherical "clock" of achenes, each topped with a parachute-like pappus that carries the seed on the wind. All parts exude a milky latex when broken.

Taxonomy

The genus is taxonomically difficult because many species reproduce asexually by apomixis, producing numerous microspecies; specialists have described thousands of named forms, while others group them into a few dozen aggregate species.

Uses

Dandelions are edible: the leaves are used in salads, the roots have been roasted as a coffee substitute, and the flowers made into wine. The plants feature in traditional herbal medicine as a diuretic, and T. kok-saghyz (Russian dandelion) has been investigated as a source of natural rubber.
