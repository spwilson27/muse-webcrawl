John Nicholas Ringling (May 31, 1866 – December 2, 1936) was an American entrepreneur and art collector, the best known of the seven Ringling brothers who founded the Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus.

Circus career

The Ringling brothers began their circus in Baraboo, Wisconsin, in 1884. John became the driving force behind its expansion, acquiring the American Circus Corporation in 1929 and, with the earlier purchase and merger involving Barnum & Bailey, consolidating the largest travelling circus in the United States. By the end of his life he was the last surviving brother and sole head of the enterprise.

Sarasota and art collection

Ringling invested heavily in Sarasota, Florida, making it the circus's winter quarters. He built the Venetian Gothic mansion Cà d'Zan and founded the John and Mable Ringling Museum of Art, which he bequeathed to the state of Florida along with his collection of Baroque paintings, including works by Rubens.
