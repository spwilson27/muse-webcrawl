A tool is an object that can extend an individual's ability to modify features of the surrounding environment or help them accomplish a particular task. Broadly, tools include hand tools such as hammers, axes, saws, and wrenches; power tools driven by electricity or fuel; and machine tools used in manufacturing. The use of tools by humans dates back millions of years to the stone tools of early hominins, and tool use is also observed in other species, including primates, elephants, and certain birds.

History

The earliest stone tools, such as the Oldowan choppers of East Africa, date to about 2.6 million years ago; shaped hand axes of the Acheulean industry appeared roughly 1.7 million years ago. Through the Stone, Bronze, and Iron Ages, humans developed specialised implements for agriculture, woodworking, metalworking, and construction. The Industrial Revolution introduced mass-produced tools and machine tools capable of machining metal to precise tolerances, and the twentieth century added portable electric and pneumatic power tools.

Classification

Tools are commonly classed by function: cutting tools (knives, saws, drills), striking tools (hammers, mallets), gripping tools (pliers, wrenches), measuring tools (rules, calipers, levels), and fastening tools (screwdrivers, nail guns). They may also be divided into hand tools, powered by the user's muscles, and power tools, which draw on an external energy source. In a wider sense, the term extends to instruments of abstract work — a "tool" of analysis or computation — including software utilities.

Tool use in animals

Chimpanzees fish for termites with prepared sticks and crack nuts with stone hammers; sea otters use rocks to open shellfish; New Caledonian crows fashion hooks to extract insects. Such behaviour was once thought uniquely human, but it is now recognised as widespread, though human toolmaking — the manufacture of tools to make other tools, and the cumulative improvement of designs — remains distinctive in its scale and complexity.
