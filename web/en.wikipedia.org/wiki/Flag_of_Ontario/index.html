Flag of Ontario

Proportion: 1:2
Adopted: May 21, 1965
Design: A Red Ensign with the shield of the provincial coat of arms in the fly

The flag of Ontario is the provincial flag of Ontario, Canada. It is a defaced Red Ensign, with the Union Flag in the canton and the provincial coat of arms in the fly. The flag was adopted by the Legislature in 1965, taking effect on May 21 of that year, after the federal government adopted the Maple Leaf flag as Canada's national flag.

Design

The shield of the coat of arms of Ontario, granted in 1868 and augmented in 1909, consists of a red Cross of St George on a white (silver) chief, above three golden maple leaves on a green field. The green and gold are Ontario's official colours; the maple leaves represent Canada, and the cross recalls the province's English heritage and its status as the base of many Loyalists after the American Revolution.

History

Before 1965 Ontario used the Canadian Red Ensign as an unofficial provincial flag. When Parliament replaced it with the maple leaf design, Ontario premier John Robarts introduced the Flag Act to preserve the Red Ensign as the provincial flag, defaced with Ontario's arms. Manitoba adopted a similar ensign at the same time. The flag was raised for the first time over the Ontario Legislative Building at Queen's Park in Toronto in 1965.
