Dorking

Dorking is a market town in Surrey, England, about 21 miles (34 km) south of London. It lies in the valley of the Pipp Brook between the North Downs and the Greensand Ridge, and is well known for its proximity to Box Hill, a popular beauty spot and viewpoint over the town. The town lends its name to the Dorking chicken, a distinctive five-toed breed of poultry.

History

Dorking appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Derchinges. The town grew as a coaching stop on the London to Worthing road, and for centuries held a market, first recorded in the 13th century. During the 19th century Dorking became a fashionable residential town, and the arrival of the railway in 1849 and 1867 connected it to London. The surrounding countryside, including the Deepdene estate, attracted writers and artists; the novelist George Meredith lived at Box Hill.

Geography

The town sits on the Lower Greensand at the edge of the Mole Gap, where the River Mole cuts through the North Downs. Nearby landmarks include Box Hill, Leith Hill — the highest point in south-east England — and the vineyards of Denbies Wine Estate, one of the largest in the United Kingdom.

Transport

Dorking has three railway stations: Dorking, Dorking Deepdene and Dorking West, with services to London Victoria, London Waterloo, Reading and Gatwick Airport. The A24 and A25 roads pass through the town.

The Dorking chicken

The Dorking is one of the oldest British breeds of chicken, reputedly introduced by the Romans. It is distinguished by having five toes instead of the usual four, and was historically prized as a table bird. The breed appears in the town's coat of arms.

Notable people

Composer Ralph Vaughan Williams grew up at Leith Hill Place near Dorking and is commemorated in the town. Laurence Olivier was born in Dorking in 1907.
