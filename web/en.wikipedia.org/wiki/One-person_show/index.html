A one-person show, also called a solo show, is a theatrical performance featuring a single performer, who typically plays one or many characters and carries the entire production. Such shows may be written by the performer — frequently drawing on autobiographical material — or by a separate playwright.

History

The form has roots in the monologues of nineteenth-century lecturers and character sketch artists, and in vaudeville turns. Hal Holbrook's Mark Twain Tonight!, first performed in 1954 and brought to Broadway in 1966, became one of the most celebrated examples, with Holbrook impersonating Mark Twain delivering his own writings.

From the 1970s onward, the rise of performance art and alternative theatre spaces made the solo show a vehicle for personal and political testimony. Spalding Gray's autobiographical monologues, including Swimming to Cambodia (1985), and the works of Anna Deavere Smith, who performs verbatim interviews as multiple characters, expanded the form's possibilities.

Contemporary practice

One-person shows are a staple of fringe festivals such as the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, where their low cost makes them accessible to emerging artists. Phoebe Waller-Bridge's Fleabag began as a one-woman show at Edinburgh in 2013 before being adapted for television. John Leguizamo, Whoopi Goldberg, and Lily Tomlin (in Jane Wagner's The Search for Signs of Intelligent Life in the Universe) have all brought successful solo shows to Broadway.
