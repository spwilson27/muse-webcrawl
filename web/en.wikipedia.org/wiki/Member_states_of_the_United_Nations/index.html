Member states of the United Nations

The 193 member states of the United Nations

The member states of the United Nations are the 193 sovereign states that are members of the United Nations (UN) and have equal representation in the UN General Assembly. In addition, the UN General Assembly has granted observer status to two non-member states: the Holy See and Palestine. The UN is the world's largest intergovernmental organization.

Admission criteria

The criteria for admission of new member states to the UN are established in Chapter II, Article 4 of the UN Charter: membership is open to all peace-loving states which accept the obligations contained in the Charter and, in the judgment of the Organization, are able and willing to carry out these obligations. Admission is effected by a decision of the General Assembly upon the recommendation of the Security Council.

History

The UN was founded in 1945 with 51 original members, including the signatories of the UN Charter at the San Francisco Conference. Membership grew rapidly with decolonization in the 1950s and 1960s. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, its seat was taken by the Russian Federation, and the former Soviet republics joined as separate members. The most recent state to join is South Sudan, admitted in July 2011 following its independence from Sudan.

Observers and non-members

Besides its 193 member states, the UN has two permanent observer states in the General Assembly: the Holy See and the State of Palestine, the latter elevated from observer entity status in 2012. Some territories and partially recognized states, such as Kosovo, Taiwan, and Western Sahara, are not UN members, generally because of contested sovereignty or Security Council vetoes.

Representation issues

Questions of representation have occasionally arisen, most notably in 1971, when General Assembly Resolution 2758 transferred the "China" seat from the Republic of China (Taiwan) to the People's Republic of China. The seat of the Soviet Union passed to the Russian Federation in 1991 without a formal admission vote.
