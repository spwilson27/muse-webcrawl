Tetbury

Tetbury is a town and civil parish inside the Cotswold district in England. It lies on the site of an ancient hill fort, on the A433 road in Gloucestershire. The population of the parish was 6,453 at the 2021 census. The town is known for its medieval buildings and its many antique shops, and as the home of Highgrove House, the family residence of King Charles III.

History

Tetbury developed as a market town for the wool trade in the Cotswolds. The Market House, built in 1655 with a distinctive dolphin crest, stands at the centre of the town. St Mary's Church, a Georgian Gothic Revival building completed in the late 18th century, is noted for its tall spire and elegant interior. During the Civil War era and later, the town's inns served travellers on the road between Cirencester and Bristol.

Events and local customs

The town hosts the annual Woolsack Races each May bank holiday, in which competitors carry heavy sacks of wool up and down the steep Gumstool Hill — a tradition said to date from the 17th century. Nearby Westonbirt Arboretum, managed by Forestry England, holds a nationally important collection of trees and shrubs.

Transport

Tetbury railway station closed in 1964; the nearest stations are at Kemble and Stroud. The town is served by local bus routes and lies near the Fosse Way Roman road.
