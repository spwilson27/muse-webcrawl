The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) is an international human rights treaty of the United Nations intended to protect the rights and dignity of persons with disabilities. Adopted by the UN General Assembly on 13 December 2006 and opened for signature on 30 March 2007, it entered into force on 3 May 2008 after the twentieth ratification. It is the first comprehensive human rights convention of the twenty-first century, the fastest-negotiated human rights treaty in UN history, and among the most widely ratified, with around 190 parties including the European Union.

Philosophy

The Convention marks a shift from the medical model of disability — treating disability as an individual defect to be cured or pitied — to the social model, in which disability arises from the interaction between persons with impairments and attitudinal and environmental barriers. Its guiding slogan, drawn from the disability movement, is "Nothing about us without us": disabled people's organisations participated directly in the drafting through an Ad Hoc Committee process unprecedented for a UN treaty. Article 3 sets out general principles including respect for inherent dignity and individual autonomy, non-discrimination, full participation and inclusion, accessibility, and equality of opportunity.

Key rights

Among the Convention's fifty articles: Article 9 requires accessibility of the built environment, transport, information and communications; Article 12 affirms equal recognition before the law, including legal capacity; Article 19 guarantees the right to live independently and be included in the community; Article 21 covers freedom of expression and access to information, including sign languages; Article 24 mandates inclusive education; Article 25 covers health; Article 27 covers work and employment, including reasonable accommodation; and Articles 29 and 30 guarantee participation in political and public life and in cultural life, recreation and sport.

Monitoring

Implementation is monitored by the Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, a body of independent experts that examines state reports. An Optional Protocol, adopted alongside the Convention, allows individuals to bring complaints to the Committee and permits inquiry procedures into grave or systematic violations. States must also designate national focal points and independent monitoring frameworks, and consult disabled people's organisations in implementation.

Signatories and parties

On its opening day the Convention attracted 82 signatures, the highest number ever recorded for a UN convention on its first day. The United States signed in 2009 but has not ratified, the Senate having declined consent in 2012. The European Union concluded the Convention in 2010, the first time the EU became party to a core UN human rights treaty.
