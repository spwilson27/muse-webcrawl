The Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA) is an art museum on Wilshire Boulevard in the Miracle Mile district of Los Angeles, California. It is the largest art museum in the western United States, holding more than 150,000 works spanning the history of art from ancient times to the present, and attracting around a million visitors annually.

History

LACMA was established in 1961, splitting from the Los Angeles Museum of History, Science and Art, and opened in 1965 in a complex of three buildings designed by William Pereira in Hancock Park, adjacent to the La Brea Tar Pits. Major later additions included the Art of the Americas Building, the Pavilion for Japanese Art (1988) designed by Bruce Goff, and the Broad Contemporary Art Museum and Resnick Pavilion, both designed by Renzo Piano and opened in 2008 and 2010 respectively.

Collection and campus

The museum's holdings are particularly strong in American, Latin American, Asian and Islamic art, modern and contemporary art, photography, and film. Chris Burden's Urban Light (2008), an installation of 202 restored cast-iron street lamps at the museum's entrance, has become an iconic Los Angeles landmark, as has Michael Heizer's monumental Levitated Mass (2012).

Redevelopment

In the 2020s LACMA undertook a controversial redevelopment in which its older Wilshire Boulevard buildings were demolished and replaced by a single large gallery building, the David Geffen Galleries, designed by the Swiss architect Peter Zumthor and spanning across Wilshire Boulevard.
