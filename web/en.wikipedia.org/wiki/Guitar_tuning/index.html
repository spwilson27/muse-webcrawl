Guitar tuning

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guitar tunings are the assignment of pitches to the open strings of guitars. Standard tuning assigns pitches according to the convention of E–A–D–G–B–E, from the lowest (sixth) string to the highest (first). A wide range of alternative tunings, called scordatura in classical practice, are used across folk, blues, rock and metal.

Standard tuning is based on perfect fourths, with a single major third between the G and B strings — a compromise that balances simple chord shapes against ease of playing scales. Concert pitch is usually referenced to A440.

Alternative tunings

Dropped tunings lower the sixth string a whole tone (drop D: D–A–D–G–B–E), enabling one-finger power chords and used widely in rock and metal; drop C and lower variants follow the same pattern. Open tunings tune the strings to a chord — open G (D–G–D–G–B–D) and open D are favourites for slide guitar and Delta blues. DADGAD, with its suspended, modal character, is common in Celtic folk music. Regular tunings such as all-fourths and major-thirds tuning appeal to jazz and experimental players.

Extended-range instruments

Seven-string guitars typically add a low B; eight-strings add F♯ (or E), extending the instrument's range for modern metal. Classical ten-string and baritone guitars use their own conventions.
