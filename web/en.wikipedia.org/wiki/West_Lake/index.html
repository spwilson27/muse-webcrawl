West Lake (Chinese: Xi Hu) is a freshwater lake in Hangzhou, China, celebrated for more than a thousand years as one of the most beautiful scenic places in the country. Divided by the Su and Bai causeways, it is surrounded by hills, temples, pagodas, gardens and arched bridges.

History

The lake began as a lagoon of the Qiantang River and was progressively dredged and landscaped from the Tang dynasty onward. The poet-governor Bai Juyi built one causeway in the 9th century; another was built under Su Dongpo in the 11th century, and both remain landmarks bearing their names. Marco Polo described Hangzhou, and its lake, as the finest city in the world.

Ten Scenes

The celebrated "Ten Scenes of West Lake", named in the Southern Song dynasty, include "Spring Dawn at Su Causeway", "Lotus in the Breeze at Crooked Courtyard", "Autumn Moon over the Calm Lake", "Broken Bridge in Melting Snow" — site of the Legend of the White Snake — and "Leifeng Pagoda in Evening Glow". In 2011 the West Lake Cultural Landscape was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognising its profound influence on garden design across East Asia.
