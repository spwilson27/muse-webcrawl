The Amphiareion of Oropos (Greek: Ἀμφιαρεῖον), situated in the hills southeast of the port of Oropos in Attica, Greece, was a sanctuary dedicated to the hero Amphiaraus and functioned as a healing shrine and incubation oracle, comparable to the sanctuaries of Asclepius. According to myth, Amphiaraus, a seer and one of the Seven against Thebes, was swallowed alive by the earth as he fled the battle and emerged as a healing hero-god at a spring at Oropos.

Site and ritual

The sanctuary was established in the late 5th century BC and flourished in the 4th century BC and again under Roman patronage, including from Sulla. Petitioners purified themselves, made offerings and a sacrifice, and slept in the long incubation stoa (enkoimeterion) hoping for a dream in which the god prescribed a cure — a practice called incubation. The site includes the temple of Amphiaraus with a cult statue described by Pausanias, a sacred spring into which healed visitors threw coins, a monumental altar, a large stoa about 110 metres long, baths, a small theatre with well-preserved stone seats and proedria thrones, and a stadium where the Amphiaria games were held.

History

Control of Oropos and its sanctuary shifted between Athens and Boeotia, and the shrine's revenues were a recurring source of dispute. Excavations, begun by the Greek Archaeological Society in 1884, uncovered the principal buildings and many inscribed cure records and decrees.
