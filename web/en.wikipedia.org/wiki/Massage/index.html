Massage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Massage is the manipulation of the body's soft tissues — muscles, connective tissue, tendons, ligaments and skin — using the hands, fingers, elbows, knees or devices. It is practised for relaxation, rehabilitation and the relief of pain and stress. A person who practises massage professionally is a massage therapist. The word derives from the French massage, "friction of kneading", ultimately from Arabic massa, "to touch, feel or handle".

History

Massage is among the oldest healing arts: it appears in Chinese medical texts dating back some 5,000 years, in Ayurvedic practice in India, and in Egyptian, Greek and Roman medicine. Hippocrates wrote of the benefits of rubbing for stiff joints. In the 19th century the Swedish educator Per Henrik Ling developed the system that became known as Swedish massage, which remains the most common style in the West. During the twentieth century massage moved from mainstream physiotherapy into complementary and alternative medicine, and later back into sports medicine, hospice care and corporate wellness.

Techniques and styles

Swedish massage uses five basic strokes: effleurage (gliding), petrissage (kneading), tapotement (rhythmic tapping), friction and vibration. Other styles include deep tissue massage, sports massage, trigger point therapy, myofascial release, and traditional forms such as Thai massage, Japanese shiatsu, Chinese tui na and reflexology, which applies pressure to points on the feet and hands. Aromatherapy massage combines massage with scented plant oils; hot stone massage uses heated basalt stones.

Benefits and evidence

Research supports short-term benefits for anxiety, stress and some types of pain, including lower-back pain, and massage is widely used as supportive care in oncology and palliative settings. Claims of "detoxification" lack scientific support. Contraindications include deep-vein thrombosis, burns, open wounds, fractures and some skin conditions. Massage is often delivered on a padded table, with the client draped in towels or sheets, in spas, clinics, hospitals and workplaces.

Regulation

Many jurisdictions license massage therapists and prescribe minimum training hours. Because the term "massage parlour" has also been used as a euphemism for brothels, professional bodies emphasise the therapeutic and non-sexual nature of legitimate practice.
