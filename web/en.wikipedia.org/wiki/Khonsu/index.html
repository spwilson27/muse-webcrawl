Khonsu

Khonsu (Egyptian: ḫnsw, "traveller") is the ancient Egyptian god of the Moon and time. The son of Amun and Mut, he formed the Theban Triad worshipped at Karnak, where his principal temple stands. His name reflects the moon's journey across the night sky.

Iconography and mythology

Khonsu is usually depicted as a youthful mummiform figure wearing the sidelock of youth, a crescent and full lunar disk upon his head, and holding the crook and flail; he also appears as a falcon-headed man. As a lunar deity he governed time and the calendar, and was invoked as a healer — a famous legend tells of a statue of Khonsu travelling to a foreign land to cure an ailing princess. In earlier texts he had a more fearsome aspect as a god who could afflict as well as cure.

Worship

His cult flourished especially at Thebes during the New Kingdom, when the Temple of Khonsu was built within the Karnak precinct. During the Opet Festival the Theban Triad travelled in procession between Karnak and Luxor temples.
