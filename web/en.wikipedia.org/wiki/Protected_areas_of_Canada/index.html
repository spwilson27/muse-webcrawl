Protected areas of Canada

Total area: over 12% of Canada's land and freshwater
Agencies: Parks Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada, provincial agencies
First national park: Banff (1885)

Protected areas of Canada consist of approximately 12.1 percent of the nation's landmass and freshwater, which is protected as national parks, provincial parks, national wildlife areas, migratory bird sanctuaries, and other designations. The system is administered by federal, provincial, and territorial governments together with Indigenous partners.

History

Canada's protected-area system began with the establishment of Banff National Park in 1885, the world's third national park. Parks Canada, founded in 1911 as the Dominion Parks Branch, was the world's first national park service.

Categories

Federal protected areas include national parks, national marine conservation areas, national wildlife areas, and migratory bird sanctuaries. Provincial and territorial parks cover large additional areas, and Indigenous Protected and Conserved Areas (IPCAs) are increasingly recognized.

Targets

Canada has committed to conserving 25 percent of its lands and waters by 2025 and 30 percent by 2030, as part of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.
