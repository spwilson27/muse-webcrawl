Jim Sanborn

Herbert James Sanborn Jr. (born November 14, 1945) is an American sculptor best known for Kryptos, the encrypted sculpture installed in 1990 at the headquarters of the Central Intelligence Agency in Langley, Virginia, whose final passage of code has resisted solution for decades.

Career

Sanborn was born in Washington, D.C., studied archaeology and fine art, and received his MFA in sculpture from the Pratt Institute in 1981. He taught at Montgomery College in Maryland while developing large-scale works that combine natural forms, text and light. His sculptures often incorporate projections of texts through cut-metal screens, lodestones, and references to science and secret communication.

Kryptos and other works

Kryptos consists of a sweeping, S-curved copper screen pierced with some 1,800 characters forming four encrypted passages, together with other elements including a compass needle and a petrified tree. Working with retired CIA cryptographer Ed Scheidt, Sanborn devised the encryptions; three of the four passages were solved publicly by 1999, but the fourth, known as K4, remains one of the world's most famous unsolved codes, attracting sustained attention from cryptanalysts. Sanborn periodically releases clues to K4 and has discussed plans for the solution to pass to a custodian after his death. Other notable works include the Cyrillic Projector (1993), a cylinder bearing encrypted Russian text, and Indian Run Park and numerous commissions for universities, museums and government buildings.
