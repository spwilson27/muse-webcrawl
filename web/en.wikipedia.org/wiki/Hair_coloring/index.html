Hair coloring

Hair coloring, or hair dyeing, is the practice of changing the colour of hair. The main reasons are cosmetic — to cover grey hair, to change to a colour regarded as more fashionable, or to restore the original colour after bleaching or sun exposure.

History

Ancient peoples used plant dyes such as henna, indigo, walnut and chamomile, and mineral preparations. Modern synthetic hair dyes developed after the 19th-century discovery of para-phenylenediamine and the founding of companies such as L'Oréal in the early 20th century.

Types

Colouring is classified as permanent, demi-permanent, semi-permanent or temporary, depending on how long the colour lasts and whether chemicals penetrate the hair shaft. Techniques include full-head colour, highlights, lowlights, balayage and ombré. Bleaching lightens hair by oxidising melanin.

Safety

Some dye ingredients can cause allergic contact dermatitis, and patch testing is recommended. Epidemiological studies of long-term dye use and cancer risk have been extensively reviewed by regulators.
