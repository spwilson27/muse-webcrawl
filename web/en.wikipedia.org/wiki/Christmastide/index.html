Christmastide

Christmastide (also known as Christide) is a season of the liturgical year in most Christian churches. For the Catholic Church, the Lutheran Church, the Anglican Church, and the Methodist Church, Christmastide begins on Christmas Eve at sunset or Vespers, which is liturgically the beginning of Christmas Day, and ends on the Feast of the Baptism of the Lord.

In the traditional Western practice, the Christmas season comprises the Twelve Days of Christmas, running from 25 December to the eve of the Epiphany on 5 January (Twelfth Night), with the feast of the Epiphany following on 6 January. Historically, the season was sometimes extended to Candlemas on 2 February.

Observance

Within Christmastide fall several important feasts: Saint Stephen's Day (26 December), the feast of Saint John the Evangelist (27 December), the Holy Innocents (28 December), the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God (1 January), and the Epiphany. In many countries, Boxing Day (26 December) is a public holiday.

The season is preceded by Advent, a period of preparation and fasting in some traditions, and is marked by Christmas carols, feasting, gift-giving, and decorations such as Christmas trees and Nativity scenes, which traditionally are kept in place until the end of the season.
