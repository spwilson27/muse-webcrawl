Rebekah (Hebrew: רִבְקָה, Rivqa) appears in the Hebrew Bible as the wife of Isaac and the mother of Jacob and Esau. Rebekah first appears in Genesis 24, where Abraham sends his servant to find a wife for Isaac from among his kin in Mesopotamia rather than from the Canaanites. At a well in the city of Nahor, the servant finds Rebekah, the granddaughter of Abraham's brother Nahor, drawing water; her generous offer to water his camels is taken as the sign that she is the chosen bride.

In Genesis

Rebekah leaves her family and travels to Canaan, where she marries Isaac. After twenty years of barrenness she conceives twins, Esau and Jacob, after Isaac pleads with God on her behalf. During the pregnancy she receives an oracle: "Two nations are in your womb... and the older shall serve the younger."

Rebekah favours the younger twin, Jacob, while Isaac favours Esau. When Isaac is old and blind, Rebekah engineers the deception by which Jacob receives the blessing intended for Esau, dressing Jacob in Esau's clothes and covering his hands and neck with goatskins. Fearing Esau's anger, she arranges for Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran — the journey during which Jacob marries Rebekah's nieces Leah and Rachel.

Earlier in the narrative (Genesis 26), at Gerar, Isaac presents Rebekah as his sister to the Philistine king Abimelech, echoing similar episodes in Abraham's life. Rebekah is traditionally held to be buried in the Cave of Machpelah in Hebron alongside Isaac and Abraham and Sarah.

Interpretation

In Jewish tradition Rebekah is counted among the four matriarchs of Israel, and rabbinic commentary often portrays her decisiveness as prophetic insight derived from the oracle she received. In the New Testament, Paul cites her story in Romans 9 as an example of divine election.
