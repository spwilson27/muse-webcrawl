Roselle (plant)

Roselle (Hibiscus sabdariffa) is a species of flowering plant in the mallow family, Malvaceae, native to Africa and widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions. It is best known for its fleshy red calyces, which are used to make tart herbal drinks and jams around the world.

Description

Roselle is an annual or perennial shrub growing 2–2.5 m tall, with deeply lobed leaves and pale yellow flowers with a dark red centre. After flowering, the calyx enlarges, turning bright red, fleshy and juicy; these calyces, rich in citric and malic acids and anthocyanin pigments, are the principal commercial product. The plant also yields bast fibre from its stems, and its seeds are used for oil and animal feed.

Uses

Dried calyces are brewed into drinks known as karkadé in Egypt and Sudan, agua de jamaica in Mexico, sorrel in the Caribbean, bissap in West Africa, and rosella tea in Australia; they are also made into jams, jellies and food colourings. Leaves are eaten as a sour vegetable in parts of Africa and Asia. Traditional medicine uses roselle for hypertension, and clinical studies have found modest blood-pressure-lowering effects.
