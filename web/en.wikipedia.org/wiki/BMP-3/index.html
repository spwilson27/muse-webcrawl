BMP-3

BMP-3
Type: Infantry fighting vehicle
Origin: Soviet Union / Russia
In service: 1987–present
Crew: 3 + 7 passengers
Main armament: 100 mm 2A70 gun-launcher, 30 mm 2A72 cannon

The BMP-3 is a Soviet and Russian amphibious infantry fighting vehicle, successor to the BMP-2. Unusually heavily armed for its class, it combines a 100 mm rifled gun capable of firing conventional shells and anti-tank guided missiles with a coaxial 30 mm autocannon.

Design

The vehicle has a rear-mounted 500 hp diesel engine, aluminium alloy armour with steel reinforcement, and carries three crew and seven infantry. Two bow-mounted 7.62 mm machine guns are operated by seated infantry. It is fully amphibious, propelled in water by waterjets.

Service

Entering Soviet service in 1987, the BMP-3 was exported to countries including the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, South Korea, Cyprus, Indonesia and Venezuela. Russian and Ukrainian forces have used it extensively in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where its thin armour proved vulnerable to modern anti-tank weapons and drones.
