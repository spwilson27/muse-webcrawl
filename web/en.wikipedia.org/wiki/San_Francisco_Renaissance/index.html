San Francisco Renaissance
Periodc. 1945 – mid-1960s
LocationSan Francisco, California
Key figuresKenneth Rexroth, Gary Snyder, Michael McClure, Philip Whalen, Jack Spicer, Robert Duncan

The San Francisco Renaissance was a flowering of avant-garde poetry and literary activity centred on San Francisco from the mid-1940s into the 1960s. Anchored by the poet and critic Kenneth Rexroth, it combined anarchist politics, interest in Asian philosophy and religion, ecology, and experimental form, and it provided the West Coast seedbed from which the Beat Generation emerged.

Background

Rexroth's Libertarian Circle and the magazine The Ark gathered poets including Robert Duncan, Jack Spicer and Philip Lamantia. After the Second World War the scene absorbed influences from Zen Buddhism, jazz and surrealism, and found institutions in City Lights Bookstore (founded 1953 by Lawrence Ferlinghetti) and the Poetry Center at San Francisco State.

Six Gallery reading

On 7 October 1955, five poets — Allen Ginsberg, Gary Snyder, Michael McClure, Philip Whalen and Philip Lamantia — read at the Six Gallery before an audience that included Jack Kerouac. Ginsberg's first public performance of Howl made the evening a founding legend of the Beat movement; the obscenity trial of its publisher in 1957 brought the San Francisco scene national fame.

Legacy

The Renaissance's emphases — Snyder's ecological Buddhism, McClure's performance poetry, Spicer's "dictated" serial poems — influenced later American poetry, the counterculture of the 1960s, and environmental writing. The term has also been applied retrospectively to the city's broader post-war cultural boom.
