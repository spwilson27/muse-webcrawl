Kaliningrad

Kaliningrad (Russian: Калининград), known until 1946 as Königsberg, is the largest city and administrative centre of Kaliningrad Oblast, a Russian exclave on the Baltic Sea between Poland and Lithuania. Founded in 1255 by the Teutonic Knights, it was the capital of East Prussia and the coronation city of the Prussian kings, and is famed as the home of the philosopher Immanuel Kant and the Königsberg bridge problem.

History

The Teutonic Order founded a castle on the Pregel (Pregolya) River in 1255 on the site of an Old Prussian settlement called Twangste, naming it Königsberg ("King's Mountain") in honour of King Ottokar II of Bohemia. The city joined the Hanseatic League and grew into three towns — Altstadt, Löbenicht, and Kneiphof — formally united in 1724. Leonhard Euler used its seven bridges crossing the Pregel to found graph theory in 1736. Kant spent his entire life in the city and is buried at its cathedral.

Königsberg was devastated by British air raids in August 1944 and by the Red Army's siege in April 1945. Under the Potsdam Agreement northern East Prussia passed to the Soviet Union; the German population was expelled and replaced by settlers from across the USSR. In 1946 the city was renamed for the Soviet official Mikhail Kalinin.

Geography and exclave status

Kaliningrad Oblast is separated from the rest of Russia, bordered by Lithuania to the northeast and Poland to the south. The city is Russia's only ice-free Baltic port in year-round operation together with nearby Baltiysk, headquarters of the Baltic Fleet.

Landmarks

The Gothic Königsberg Cathedral on Kant Island (formerly Kneiphof) was restored in the 1990s and houses Kant's tomb and a museum. Other sights include the Brandenburg Gate and other surviving city gates, the amber museum (the region holds the world's largest amber deposits), the reconstructed Fishing Village quarter, and Kaliningrad Stadium, built for the 2018 FIFA World Cup.

Economy

Manufacturing, shipbuilding, fishing, and amber mining dominate the oblast economy; a special economic zone established in the 1990s offers customs privileges intended to offset the exclave's isolation.
