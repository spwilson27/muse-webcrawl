Rack and pinion

A rack and pinion is a type of linear actuator that comprises a circular gear (the pinion) engaging a linear gear (the rack). Together, they convert rotational motion into linear motion, or the reverse. Rotating the pinion causes the rack to be driven in a straight line. Conversely, moving the rack causes the pinion to rotate.

Rack and pinion systems are used in a variety of applications. In rack railways, a centrally mounted pinion drives the train up steep grades. In automobiles, rack-and-pinion steering converts the rotation of the steering wheel into the side-to-side movement of the front wheels; it is valued for its simplicity, directness, and compactness compared to recirculating-ball steering. Rack and pinion mechanisms are also found in machine tools, linear actuators, and drilling rigs, where a travelling rack raises and lowers the drill string.

Geometry

The rack is effectively a spur gear of infinite radius unrolled flat: its teeth have straight sides matching the involute profile of the mating pinion. The distance the rack travels per pinion revolution equals the pitch circumference of the pinion.
