Seneca Lake (New York)

Seneca Lake is the largest of the glacial Finger Lakes of the U.S. state of New York, and the deepest glacial lake entirely within the state. It is promoted as the lake trout capital of the world, and hosts the National Lake Trout Derby. Because of its depth and resulting stable cool temperatures, its shores support an important wine-growing region, and the lake is also known for the "Guns of the Seneca" — unexplained booming sounds reported from its surface, an example of the phenomenon known worldwide as skyquakes or mistpouffers.

Geography

The lake stretches roughly 38 miles from Geneva at its northern end to Watkins Glen at its southern end, with a maximum width of about 3 miles. At 618 feet deep, its bottom lies below sea level. Like the other Finger Lakes it occupies a valley deepened by Pleistocene glaciers. Seneca Lake drains northward through the Seneca River into Lake Ontario. The Cayuga–Seneca Canal links it to Cayuga Lake and the New York State Canal System.

Wine and recreation

The deep lake moderates the climate of the surrounding slopes, protecting grapevines from spring and autumn frosts. The Seneca Lake Wine Trail, with dozens of wineries on its eastern and western shores, is the largest of the Finger Lakes wine trails and is especially noted for Riesling. The lake is a major recreational fishery for lake trout, rainbow trout, landlocked salmon and smallmouth bass, and its southern end adjoins Watkins Glen State Park and the racing circuit at Watkins Glen International.

History

The lake takes its name from the Seneca people, one of the nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), whose towns along its shores were destroyed in the Sullivan Expedition of 1779 during the American Revolutionary War. In the twentieth century the U.S. Navy operated a sonar test facility on the lake from a platform near Dresden, taking advantage of its depth and quiet acoustics. Since the 1960s residents have reported the mysterious "Seneca guns" — loud booms of uncertain origin.
