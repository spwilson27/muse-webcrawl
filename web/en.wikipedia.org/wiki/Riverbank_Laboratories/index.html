Riverbank Laboratories

Founded: 1916
Location: Geneva, Illinois, United States
Founder: George Fabyan
Field: Acoustics

Riverbank Laboratories is a research institution in Geneva, Illinois, founded in 1916 by the wealthy textile magnate Colonel George Fabyan on his Riverbank estate. It became notable as the first dedicated acoustics laboratory in the United States and as an improbable center of early American codebreaking.

Riverbank estate

Fabyan assembled a large estate along the Fox River, which at its height included a Japanese garden, a windmill, a lighthouse, greenhouses, and a Roman-style swimming pool. Frank Lloyd Wright remodeled the estate's main house, and other structures were designed in eclectic styles. Fabyan, an enthusiast with wide-ranging scientific interests and little formal training, used his fortune to fund research in genetics, acoustics, and cryptology.

Acoustics

In 1916 Fabyan hired Wallace Clement Sabine, the Harvard physicist who had founded the modern science of architectural acoustics, to direct research. After Sabine's death in 1919, his cousin Paul E. Sabine took over the laboratory. Riverbank became a leading center for measuring sound absorption and testing building materials, work that continued through the twentieth century.

Cipher department

Riverbank's cipher department grew out of Fabyan's interest in the Baconian theory of Shakespeare authorship—the claim that Francis Bacon wrote the plays of Shakespeare and hid ciphers in them. Elizabeth Wells Gallup directed the effort, and in 1916 she hired a young geneticist, William F. Friedman, and later Elizebeth Smith, to assist. William Friedman and Elizebeth Smith met at Riverbank, married, and went on to become the most celebrated cryptanalysts in American history.

During the First World War, with no other American codebreaking capacity available, Riverbank's staff solved intercepted messages for the United States government, and Friedman trained Army officers in cryptanalysis in 1918. The department is regarded as the birthplace of organized American cryptology.
