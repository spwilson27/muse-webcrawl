Sariputra

Sariputra (Pali: Sāriputta; Sanskrit: Śāriputra) was one of the two chief male disciples of Gautama Buddha, together with Maudgalyayana (Moggallana). Renowned as foremost among the disciples in wisdom (prajñā), he is a central figure in Theravada Buddhism and is also venerated in Mahayana texts, notably as an interlocutor in the Heart Sutra and the Lotus Sutra.

Life

Born as Upatiṣya (Upatissa) near Rajagriha to a Brahmin family — his mother was named Sari (Rupasari), hence "son of Sari" — he wandered as an ascetic under the teacher Sanjaya Belatthiputta alongside his friend Maudgalyayana. Hearing a short verse on causality from the monk Assaji ("Of those things that arise from a cause, the Tathagata has stated the cause, and also their cessation"), he attained stream-entry and brought his friend to the Buddha, under whom both ordained and became arahants.

Role in the Sangha

Sariputra served as the Buddha's right-hand disciple and a principal systematiser of the teaching; much of the Abhidharma literature is traditionally associated with him. The Buddha praised him as a "general of the Dharma". He predeceased the Buddha, dying at his birth village of Nalaka after falling ill — an event recorded with moving detail in the commentaries. His relics, along with Maudgalyayana's, were enshrined in stupas and in the modern era were ceremonially returned to India and enshrined at the Mahabodhi Temple.

In Mahayana Buddhism

Sariputra features prominently in Mahayana sutras: he receives the teaching of the Heart Sutra, debates the goddess in the Vimalakirti Sutra, and in the Lotus Sutra receives a prediction of future Buddhahood.
