Ted Chiang (born 1967) is an American science fiction writer. His work has won four Hugo Awards, four Nebula Awards, and four Locus Awards, among many others, despite a relatively small body of short fiction. His novella "Story of Your Life" was adapted as the film Arrival (2016), directed by Denis Villeneuve.

Life and career

Chiang was born in Port Jefferson, New York, to parents who had emigrated from China by way of Taiwan. He studied computer science at Brown University and has worked as a technical writer in the software industry. He attended the Clarion Workshop in 1989. His first published story, "Tower of Babylon" (1990), won the Nebula Award for Best Novella.

Works

Chiang's fiction is noted for rigorous exploration of philosophical and scientific ideas, including free will, language and perception, time, and artificial intelligence. His first collection, Stories of Your Life and Others (2002), gathered eight stories. His second collection, Exhalation: Stories (2019), was named one of the top ten books of the year by The New York Times and includes "Exhalation" and "The Lifecycle of Software Objects". Other notable works include "Hell Is the Absence of God", "Story of Your Life", "Division by Zero", and "Understand".

Chiang has also written essays on artificial intelligence, including pieces for The New Yorker questioning analogies between large language models and human understanding.
