Sahul

Sahul, also called Sahul-land, Meganesia, Papualand or Greater Australia, was a palaeocontinent that encompassed the present-day landmasses of mainland Australia, Tasmania, New Guinea and the Aru Islands. During periods of low sea level in the Pleistocene, the shallow Sahul Shelf — including the Arafura Sea, the Gulf of Carpentaria and Torres Strait — was exposed as dry land, joining these regions into a single continent.

Geography

Sahul was separated from the neighbouring continent of Sunda (mainland Southeast Asia extended to Sumatra, Java and Borneo) by the island chain of Wallacea. Even at the lowest glacial sea levels, crossing from Sunda to Sahul required open-water voyages across straits tens of kilometres wide — a fact of central importance to the peopling of Australia.

Human settlement

Sahul was first settled by anatomically modern humans at least 50,000 years ago, and archaeological evidence such as the Madjedbebe rock shelter in Arnhem Land suggests dates as early as about 65,000 years ago. The settlers crossed Wallacea by sea from Sunda, making them among the earliest known seafarers. Their descendants became the Aboriginal Australians, Papuans and related peoples.

Breakup

Rising seas at the end of the last glacial period flooded the Sahul Shelf. Tasmania was separated from the mainland by the flooding of Bass Strait roughly 12,000 years ago, and New Guinea was cut off from Australia by the inundation of Torres Strait about 8,000 years ago, leaving the modern geography.
