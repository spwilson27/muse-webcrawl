Sliding puzzle

A sliding puzzle, sliding block puzzle, or sliding tile puzzle is a combination puzzle that challenges a player to slide pieces along certain routes on a board to establish a target configuration. The best-known example is the 15 puzzle, invented by Noyes Chapman and popularised in 1880, which caused a worldwide craze.

Unlike jigsaw puzzles, sliding puzzles have been studied extensively in mathematics and computer science: the parity of the permutation plus the row of the blank determines solvability, so exactly half of the arrangements of the 15 puzzle can be solved. Sliding puzzles are also used as test problems for search algorithms and planning in artificial intelligence.

Types

Variants include the 8 puzzle, Klotski, Sokoban-style block puzzles and sliding pieces with restricted moves such as Rush Hour.
