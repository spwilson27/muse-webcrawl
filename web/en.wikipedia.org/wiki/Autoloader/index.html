Autoloader

An autoloader or auto-loader is a mechanical aid or replacement for the personnel that load ordnance into a weapon system. Generally, the term is used to describe a mechanism which was designed to load a gun or other weapon system, replacing the need for one or more human loaders. Autoloaders are most commonly used in tank guns, naval artillery, and large-calibre self-propelled artillery.

Advantages

Autoloaders eliminate the need for a dedicated loader crew member, allowing the vehicle crew to be reduced in size (typically from four to three in a tank). Because the loading mechanism can be placed in the turret bustle or in a carousel beneath the turret, the overall turret profile can be smaller, presenting a reduced target. A mechanical loader can also sustain a consistent rate of fire regardless of crew fatigue or the motion of the vehicle, and can handle heavy rounds that would be difficult for a human to load at speed.

Disadvantages

Against these advantages, autoloaders introduce mechanical complexity and additional failure modes. Early Soviet designs, such as those fitted to the T-64 and T-72 tanks, store ammunition in a carrousel directly beneath the crew compartment, which can increase the risk of catastrophic ammunition detonation if the hull is penetrated. Western designs such as the French Leclerc and Japanese Type 90 use bustle-mounted autoloaders with blowout panels intended to vent an explosion away from the crew.

History

Autoloading mechanisms were experimented with during the Second World War, and entered widespread service with the Soviet T-64 in the 1960s. The concept has since been adopted in many modern main battle tanks and in naval gun systems, where automated handling is essential for high rates of fire.
