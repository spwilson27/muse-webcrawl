Objectives Resolution

The Objectives Resolution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly of Pakistan on 12 March 1949. Moved by the country's first prime minister, Liaquat Ali Khan, on 7 March 1949, it set out the guiding principles for Pakistan's future constitution and has served as the preamble — and since 1985 as a substantive article (Article 2-A) — of every Pakistani constitution.

Content

The resolution declares that sovereignty over the entire universe belongs to Almighty Allah alone, and that the authority exercised by the people of Pakistan within the limits prescribed by Him is a sacred trust. It promises that the principles of democracy, freedom, equality, tolerance, and social justice as enunciated by Islam shall be fully observed; that Muslims shall be enabled to order their lives in accordance with the Qur'an and Sunnah; and that adequate provision shall be made for minorities freely to profess and practise their religions and develop their cultures. It further affirms fundamental rights, an independent judiciary, and the federation of autonomous units.

Debate and legacy

Non-Muslim members of the assembly — particularly Hindu members from East Bengal such as Bhupendra Kumar Datta — criticized the resolution for blending religion and state and proposed amendments to separate them; all such amendments were voted down. Liaquat Ali Khan defended the document as a charter of rights that protected minorities.

The resolution has remained a touchstone of Pakistan's constitutional identity and a focus of continuing debate between advocates of an Islamic state and supporters of a secular interpretation of Jinnah's vision. Under the Eighth Amendment (1985), General Zia-ul-Haq's government made its text a substantive, justiciable part of the constitution as Article 2-A rather than a preamble alone.
