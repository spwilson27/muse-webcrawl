Fremont is a city in Alameda County, California, in the East Bay region of the San Francisco Bay Area. With a population of about 230,000 at the 2020 census, it is the fourth most populous city in the Bay Area. Fremont was incorporated in 1956 by merging five historic townships — Centerville, Niles, Irvington, Mission San José, and Warm Springs — and is named after the explorer and politician John C. Frémont. It is noted for its large and diverse Asian American population, its proximity to Silicon Valley, and landmarks such as Mission San José (founded 1797), Mission Peak, and the historic film district of Niles.

History

The area was home to Ohlone people for millennia before Spanish missionaries founded Mission San José in 1797, which became one of the most prosperous of the California missions. After secularisation in the 1830s, ranchos and then Gold Rush-era settlements grew up; the town of Niles became an early centre of filmmaking in the 1910s, where Charlie Chaplin made several films for Essanay Studios. The five villages amalgamated into the city of Fremont in 1956 to resist annexation and control suburban growth. Manufacturing arrived with a General Motors assembly plant in 1963 (later the NUMMI joint venture with Toyota, and since 2010 Tesla's Fremont Factory, the city's largest employer).

Geography and districts

Fremont stretches from the eastern shore of San Francisco Bay up into the Diablo Range foothills. Its five original districts retain distinct identities: Mission San José, with the mission and the Mission Peak Regional Preserve (whose summit is a popular hiking destination); Niles, with an antique-shop main street and silent-film museum; Centerville, the traditional downtown; Irvington; and Warm Springs, which has grown rapidly around a BART station opened in 2017 and technology employers. Lake Elizabeth in Central Park is the city's largest park lake.

Demographics and economy

Fremont is among the most ethnically diverse large cities in the United States; a majority of residents are Asian American, including large Indian, Chinese, and Filipino communities, and the city has one of the largest Afghan American populations in the country, centred in a district sometimes called "Little Kabul". The economy is anchored by advanced manufacturing and technology — Tesla, and numerous semiconductor, electronics, and biotechnology firms — and residents commute throughout Silicon Valley and the wider Bay Area via Interstates 680 and 880, BART, and ACE commuter rail.
