Kilimanjaro

Mount Kilimanjaro rising above the savanna with acacia trees

Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant volcano in Tanzania. It is the highest mountain in Africa and the highest single free-standing mountain above sea level in the world, rising to 5,895 metres at Uhuru Peak on the crater rim of its Kibo cone. Kilimanjaro has three volcanic cones: Kibo, Mawenzi and Shira.

Despite lying just south of the equator, Kilimanjaro is capped by glaciers and ice fields, which have shrunk dramatically since the early 20th century. The mountain is part of Kilimanjaro National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage site, and is a popular, non-technical climbing destination attracting tens of thousands of trekkers each year.

Climate zones

A trek up Kilimanjaro passes through five ecological zones: cultivated lower slopes, montane rainforest, heath and moorland, alpine desert, and the arctic summit zone. The first recorded ascent was made in 1889 by Hans Meyer and Ludwig Purtscheller.
