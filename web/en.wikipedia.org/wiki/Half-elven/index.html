Half-elven

In the works of J. R. R. Tolkien, the Half-elven (Sindarin: Peredhil, singular Peredhel) are the descendants of unions between Elves and Men. Born to mixed ancestry, the Half-elven were granted a unique privilege: to choose irrevocably whether to be counted among Elves, sharing their immortality, or among Men, accepting the Gift of Men — mortality.

Unions of Elves and Men

Tolkien records three great unions of the Eldar and the Edain: Beren and Lúthien, Tuor and Idril, and Aragorn and Arwen. From the marriage of Eärendil (son of Tuor and Idril) and Elwing (descendant of Beren and Lúthien) descend the principal Half-elven line.

The Choice

The Valar decreed that the Half-elven must choose to which kindred their fate would be bound. Eärendil and Elwing chose the Elves. Their twin sons chose differently: Elrond chose the Elves and became a great lord of the Third Age, while Elros chose Men, accepting a greatly extended but mortal lifespan of 500 years, and became the first King of Númenor. Elrond's children were likewise granted the choice; his daughter Arwen chose mortality to wed Aragorn, while her brothers Elladan and Elrohir's choice is left unstated.

Other Half-elven

The Princes of Dol Amroth were said to descend from the Elf Mithrellas, companion of Nimrodel, who wed the Númenórean Imrazôr. Dior, son of Beren and Lúthien, is sometimes called the first Half-elf, though the formal choice was granted only to Eärendil's line.
