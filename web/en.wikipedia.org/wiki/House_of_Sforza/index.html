House of Sforza

The House of Sforza was a ruling family of Renaissance Italy, ruling the Duchy of Milan from 1450 to 1535. Founded by the condottiere Muzio Attendolo, nicknamed "Sforza" ("the Forcer") for his strength, the family rose from peasant origins in Romagna to become one of the great dynasties of fifteenth-century Italy.

Rule of Milan

Muzio's son Francesco Sforza, a brilliant mercenary captain, married Bianca Maria Visconti, heiress of the last Visconti duke, and seized Milan in 1450 after the collapse of the Ambrosian Republic. His son Galeazzo Maria was assassinated in 1476; after the brief regency for Gian Galeazzo, power passed to Ludovico Sforza, "il Moro", a famous patron of Leonardo da Vinci and Bramante, who commissioned works such as The Last Supper.

Decline

Ludovico's invitation to Charles VIII of France to invade Italy in 1494 unleashed the Italian Wars. He was captured by the French in 1500 and died a prisoner at Loches. His sons Massimiliano and Francesco II were briefly restored with Swiss and Imperial support, but on Francesco II's death in 1535 Milan passed to Emperor Charles V and then to Spain. Cadet branches of the family, including the Sforza of Santa Fiora and the counts of Borgonovo, survived for centuries, and the name passed through marriage into families such as the Cesarini.
