Bequia (pronounced "BEK-way") is the largest island of the Grenadines chain administered by Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, lying about 15 kilometres south of the main island of Saint Vincent. Its name derives from an Arawak word often rendered as "island of the clouds". The island's main settlement, Port Elizabeth, sits on Admiralty Bay, one of the Caribbean's most popular natural anchorages for cruising yachts.

History and culture

Bequia has a long seafaring tradition of boatbuilding, sailing, and whaling. Under an International Whaling Commission aboriginal-subsistence quota, Bequian harpooners may take a small number of humpback whales each year (a quota of up to four annually in recent periods) using traditional hand-thrown harpoons from open sailing boats, a practice commemorated at the whaling museum at Petit Nevis. The island's Easter Regatta is a major regional sailing event.

Tourism

Tourism, yachting, and related services dominate the economy. Attractions include Princess Margaret Beach, Lower Bay, the Old Hegg Turtle Sanctuary, and model-boat shops in Port Elizabeth. The island is served by J. F. Mitchell Airport and by ferry from Kingstown, Saint Vincent.
