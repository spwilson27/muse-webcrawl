Psychological Science is a monthly peer-reviewed academic journal of the Association for Psychological Science (APS), published by SAGE Publishing. Founded in 1990, it is the flagship empirical journal of the APS and one of the most prominent journals in psychology, publishing cutting-edge research articles spanning the entire spectrum of the science of psychology.

Scope

The journal publishes short-format research reports on topics across cognitive, social, developmental, personality, clinical, health and biological psychology, as well as work at the intersections of psychology with neuroscience, economics and other fields. Submissions are expected to report research of broad interest and theoretical significance.

Editorial history and reforms

Founding editor John T. Cacioppo set the journal's emphasis on timely, broadly significant findings. During the 2010s, under editor Eric Eich and his successors, Psychological Science became a prominent venue in the methodological reform movement in psychology: it introduced badges for open data, open materials and preregistration, encouraged registered replication reports, and adopted statistical-checking procedures in response to the replication crisis, in which some well-known findings first published in high-profile journals failed to replicate.

Related publications

The APS also publishes Current Directions in Psychological Science, Perspectives on Psychological Science, Psychological Science in the Public Interest, Clinical Psychological Science and Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science.
