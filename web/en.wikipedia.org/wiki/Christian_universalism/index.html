Christian universalism

Christian universalism is the theological view that all people will ultimately be reconciled to God and saved, often associated with the doctrine of apocatastasis — the restoration of all things. Universalists typically interpret hell as corrective and temporary rather than eternal conscious torment.

History

Ideas of universal restoration appear in some early Church Fathers, notably Origen and Gregory of Nyssa. In the 18th and 19th centuries, Universalist churches formed in America (the Universalist Church of America, which merged in 1961 into Unitarian Universalism), led by figures such as John Murray and Hosea Ballou. Modern advocates include theologians who argue from God's love and the scope of Christ's atonement.

Beliefs

Christian universalists generally affirm the Trinity, the incarnation, and salvation through Christ, while holding that God's salvific will cannot ultimately fail.
