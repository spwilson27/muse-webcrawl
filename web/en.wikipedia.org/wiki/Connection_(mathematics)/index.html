Connection (mathematics)

Parallel transport around a closed loop: on a curved space a vector returns rotated — the measurable effect of a connection's curvature.

In geometry, the notion of a connection makes precise the idea of transporting data along curves or families of curves in a consistent ("parallel") way. Connections are used to define covariant derivatives: they specify how a vector, tensor, or other geometric object changes infinitesimally as it moves in a given direction. The failure of parallel transport around a closed loop to return an object to its original value is measured by curvature.

Affine connections

An affine connection on the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold M is a map ∇ that takes two vector fields X and Y to a third, ∇XY, linear in X, satisfying the Leibniz rule in Y. In coordinates it is encoded by the Christoffel symbols Γkij. Two combinations of ∇ are of fundamental importance: its torsion, measuring the failure of infinitesimal parallelograms to close, and its curvature tensor, measuring non-commutativity of covariant derivatives.

On a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold there is a unique torsion-free connection compatible with the metric — the Levi-Civita connection — fundamental to Riemannian geometry and to general relativity, where it describes how spacetime curvature guides freely falling bodies along geodesics.

Connections on bundles

The concept generalises: an Ehresmann connection on a fibre bundle specifies "horizontal" directions along which fibres can be transported; a connection on a principal bundle — described locally by a Lie-algebra-valued 1-form, the connection form — is the mathematical object that in physics corresponds to a gauge field. Its curvature is the field strength (in electromagnetism, the Faraday tensor; in particle physics, the Yang–Mills field). The Aharonov–Bohm effect shows that a connection's holonomy can be physically observable even where its curvature vanishes.

Related notions

Connections underlie the definitions of geodesic, parallel transport, holonomy groups, covariant exterior derivative, and characteristic classes (via Chern–Weil theory). In algebraic geometry, connections on vector bundles (including Grothendieck's notion and connections with regular singularities) play a parallel role.
