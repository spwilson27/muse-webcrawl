Continuing professional development

Continuing professional development (CPD) is the intentional maintenance and enhancement of the knowledge and skills needed to deliver a professional service. It describes the learning activities professionals engage in after initial qualification to develop and maintain their capabilities throughout their careers. In medicine the closely related term is continuing medical education (CME); in law, continuing legal education (CLE).

Purpose

CPD keeps professional practice current in the face of changing knowledge, technology, regulation and client expectations. Professional bodies and regulators often require members to complete and record a minimum amount of CPD — commonly a set number of hours per year — as a condition of continued registration or licensure, and revalidation schemes (such as those for UK doctors and nurses) incorporate CPD evidence.

Forms

  • Structured learning: courses, conferences, workshops, seminars, e-learning and further qualifications.
  • Work-based learning: projects, secondments, mentoring, coaching and reflective practice.
  • Self-directed learning: reading journals and guidance, research and writing.
  • Professional activity: committee work, teaching, examining and peer review.

Many schemes distinguish "verifiable" CPD, with documented learning outcomes and evidence of attendance, from general CPD. Reflective models — keeping a personal development plan and a portfolio of evidence — are encouraged by regulators in fields such as accountancy, engineering, teaching, social work and the health professions.

Criticism

Critics argue that hour-counting can turn CPD into a box-ticking exercise that rewards attendance rather than improved practice, and that costs in time and fees fall unevenly on practitioners. Supporters respond that well-designed, reflective CPD aligned with appraisals can change behaviour and outcomes.
