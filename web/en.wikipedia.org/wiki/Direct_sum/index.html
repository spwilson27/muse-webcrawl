Direct sum

The direct sum is an operation in abstract algebra that combines several algebraic structures of the same kind into a new, larger structure of that kind. It is written with the symbol ⊕. For vector spaces V and W, the direct sum V ⊕ W consists of ordered pairs (v, w) with componentwise operations; more generally, the direct sum of a family of modules, groups, or spaces is the set of tuples whose entries are zero (or the identity) in all but finitely many components.

A structure is said to be the (internal) direct sum of substructures if every element decomposes uniquely as a sum of elements of the substructures. For vector spaces this corresponds to a space splitting as V = U ⊕ W when U ∩ W = {0} and U + W = V, in which case dimensions add.

The direct sum is the coproduct in the categories of modules and abelian groups, in contrast to the direct product, which for infinite families allows arbitrary tuples. Direct sum decompositions are fundamental in representation theory, where Maschke's theorem guarantees complete reducibility of representations of finite groups into direct sums of irreducibles, and in linear algebra through eigenspace and generalized-eigenspace decompositions.
