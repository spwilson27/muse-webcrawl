Amaterasu, in full Amaterasu Ōmikami, is the goddess of the sun in Japanese mythology and one of the principal Kami of Shinto. She is the ancestress of the Japanese imperial line: her grandson Ninigi descended to earth bearing the three imperial regalia, and his great-grandson Jimmu became, by tradition, Japan's first emperor — a genealogy that grounded imperial authority for over a millennium.

Myths

The early chronicles Kojiki (712 CE) and Nihon Shoki (720 CE) tell how Amaterasu was born from the left eye of Izanagi as he purified himself after escaping the land of the dead, and was given rule of the High Plain of Heaven. Her most famous episode is her withdrawal: enraged and grieved by the violent rampages of her brother, the storm god Susanoo, she hid in the Heavenly Rock Cave, plunging the world into darkness. The other gods lured her out with a bawdy dance by the goddess Ame-no-Uzume, laughter, and a mirror hung on a tree — she emerged, and light returned. The mirror, Yata no Kagami, became one of the imperial regalia.

Worship

Her great shrine is the Ise Grand Shrine in Mie Prefecture, the holiest site in Shinto, whose wooden halls are ritually rebuilt every twenty years in the shikinen sengū ceremony (most recently in 2013). The imperial house maintains a special relationship with the shrine, whose chief priestess was traditionally an imperial princess. State Shinto of the Meiji era elevated Amaterasu as divine ancestor of the emperor, a role formally renounced in the imperial rescript of 1946 while her religious veneration continued.
