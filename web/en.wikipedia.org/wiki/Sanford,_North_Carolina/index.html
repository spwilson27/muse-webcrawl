Sanford, North Carolina

Sanford is a city in and the county seat of Lee County, North Carolina, United States. The population was about 30,000 at the 2020 census. Located in the state's central Piedmont, roughly 40 miles southwest of Raleigh, Sanford grew from a railroad junction and became noted for tobacco, textiles, and one of the largest brickmaking industries in the United States.

History

Sanford was founded in the 1870s where the Raleigh and Augusta Air Line Railroad met the Western Railroad. It was named after C.O. Sanford, a railroad civil engineer. The town incorporated in 1874 and became the seat of the newly formed Lee County in 1908. Rich deposits of clay and shale made Sanford a center of brick production — it was long among the leading brick-manufacturing cities in the nation, supplying much of the brick used in construction across the Southeast.

Geography

Sanford sits near the fall line between the Piedmont and the Coastal Plain and at the geographic center of the state, around the headwaters of the Deep River. The Deep River coal field near the city was one of the few coal-mining districts in North Carolina.

Economy and community

Modern Sanford's economy includes manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, and agriculture, with growth driven by its location near the Research Triangle. The city hosts the Lee County Fair and is known for the Temple Theatre, a restored 1920s vaudeville house that serves as a regional performing arts center. Central Carolina Community College has its main campus in Sanford.
