Coinage (linguistics)

In linguistics, coinage is the invention of a totally new word (a neologism) that subsequently enters common use. Coinages are a minor but vivid source of new vocabulary, often arising from brand names, literature, science or popular culture.

[ Image placeholder: pages of a dictionary open at newly coined words ]

Examples

Famous coinages include "robot" (introduced by Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R.), "quark" (Murray Gell-Mann, borrowing from Joyce's Finnegans Wake), "nerd" (Dr. Seuss, 1950), "cyberspace" (William Gibson) and "googol" (coined by the young nephew of mathematician Edward Kasner). Lewis Carroll's "chortle" and "galumph" exemplify literary coinage; some coinages, like "Kodak", were invented as trade names with no prior meaning.

Mechanisms of word formation

Coinage differs from other word-formation processes such as compounding, derivation, blending, clipping, acronym formation and borrowing, in that the new form is not built from existing morphemes of the language.
