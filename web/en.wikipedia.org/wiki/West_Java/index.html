West Java

West Java (Indonesian: Jawa Barat) is a province of Indonesia on the western part of the island of Java. Its capital and largest city is Bandung. With a population of around 48 million, it is the most populous province in Indonesia. The native population is predominantly Sundanese, and the Sundanese language is widely spoken alongside Indonesian.

Geography

West Java is bordered by Banten and Jakarta to the west, the Java Sea to the north, Central Java to the east, and the Indian Ocean to the south. The province is mountainous and volcanic, with peaks such as Mount Gede, Mount Pangrango and Mount Ciremai, fertile highlands, and tea plantations around Puncak and Bandung.

History

The region was home to the Tarumanagara and Sunda kingdoms and later the Sultanate of Banten and the Priangan regencies under Dutch colonial rule. The province of West Java was established after Indonesian independence; Banten was split off as a separate province in 2000.

Economy

West Java is a major manufacturing centre, with industrial estates around Bekasi, Karawang and Bogor producing electronics, textiles and automobiles, as well as a large agricultural sector producing rice, tea and vegetables.
