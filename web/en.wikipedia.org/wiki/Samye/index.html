Samye

Samye (Tibetan: བསམ་ཡས་, bsam yas), formally Samye Mighur Lhundrub Tsula Khang, is the first Buddhist monastery built in Tibet. Founded in the late 8th century (c. 779) by King Trisong Detsen with the Indian masters Shantarakshita and Padmasambhava, it stands on the north bank of the Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra) in present-day Shannan prefecture. Samye is revered as the site where Buddhism was formally established in Tibet and where the first Tibetan monks — the "seven elect" — were ordained.

History

King Trisong Detsen invited the abbot Shantarakshita from Nalanda to establish monastic Buddhism in Tibet; when obstacles arose, tradition holds that the tantric master Padmasambhava subdued the local spirits and completed the project. The monastery was laid out as a giant mandala representing the Buddhist universe, with the central three-storey temple — built in Tibetan, Chinese and Indian styles on successive floors — as Mount Meru, surrounded by temples for the continents and subcontinents, four coloured chortens and a circular enclosing wall. Around 792–794 Samye hosted the famous debate between the Indian gradualist Kamalasila and the Chinese Chan teacher Heshang Moheyan, after which Tibet officially adopted the Indian form of Buddhism. Translation teams at Samye produced the first systematic Tibetan translations of the Buddhist scriptures.

Samye suffered fires and was rebuilt several times; it was heavily damaged during the Cultural Revolution and has since been restored. It remains an important pilgrimage destination, reached historically by ferry across the Yarlung Tsangpo.

Architecture

The central temple (Ü-Tsé) preserves old murals and statues amid later reconstruction. The mandala plan, with temples symbolising the four continents (in the four cardinal directions) and their satellites, the sun and moon temples, and the four great stupas in white, red, black and green, makes Samye one of the most distinctive monastic plans in Asia.
