Emperor Gaozong of Song (12 June 1107 – 9 November 1187), personal name Zhao Gou, was the tenth emperor of the Song dynasty and the first emperor of the Southern Song period. He reigned from 1127 to 1162, when he abdicated in favour of his adopted son, the future Emperor Xiaozong, and lived on as a retired emperor for another 25 years.

Accession

Gaozong was the ninth son of Emperor Huizong. When the Jurchen Jin dynasty captured the Song capital Kaifeng in the Jingkang incident of 1127, Huizong and Gaozong's elder brother Qinzong were taken prisoner and carried north. Zhao Gou, who had been sent on a diplomatic mission, escaped capture and was proclaimed emperor in June 1127, re-establishing the Song court in the south. After years of flight — at one point pursued to sea off the Zhejiang coast — the dynasty stabilised with its capital at Lin'an (modern Hangzhou).

War and peace with Jin

The early Southern Song faced continued Jin invasions. Song generals, most famously Yue Fei, won significant victories and by 1140 were advancing toward Kaifeng. Gaozong, however, favoured peace: he feared that recovering the north might lead to the return of his captive brother Qinzong and threaten his own throne. Under Chancellor Qin Hui the court concluded the Treaty of Shaoxing (1141–42), which fixed the border at the Huai River, obliged the Song to pay annual tribute in silver and silk, and required the execution of Yue Fei on fabricated charges — an act for which Gaozong and Qin Hui have been condemned in Chinese historical memory.

In 1161 the Jin ruler Hailing Wang invaded again but was defeated at Caishi and assassinated; the following year Gaozong abdicated.

Assessment

Gaozong is credited with preserving the Song dynasty and overseeing the flourishing of the south — Lin'an became one of the world's largest and richest cities — but criticised for appeasing Jin and for the judicial murder of Yue Fei. He was also an accomplished calligrapher and patron of the arts.
