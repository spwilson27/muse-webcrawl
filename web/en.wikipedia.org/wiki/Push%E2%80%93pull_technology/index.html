Push–pull technology

Push–pull technology is a cropping method developed for integrated pest management, in which repellent "push" plants and attractive "pull" plants are used together in a field. The strategy was developed at the International Centre of Insect Physiology and Ecology (ICIPE) in Kenya by Zeyaur Khan and colleagues, in collaboration with Rothamsted Research in the United Kingdom.

How it works

Farmers plant a repellent intercrop such as Desmodium between rows of a cereal crop such as maize or sorghum. The Desmodium repels ("pushes") stemborer moths away from the crop and also suppresses the parasitic weed Striga. Around the field, an attractive trap crop such as Napier grass or Brachiaria is planted to "pull" the moths, where their larvae survive poorly.

Benefits

The system increases cereal yields, provides high-quality fodder for livestock from both Desmodium and the trap grass, fixes nitrogen in the soil, and reduces reliance on synthetic pesticides and fertilisers. It has been adopted by tens of thousands of smallholder farmers in East Africa.
