Los del Río

OriginDos Hermanas, Seville, Spain
GenresLatin pop, sevillanas, rumba
MembersAntonio Romero Monge; Rafael Ruiz Perdigones
Years active1962–present

Los del Río (Spanish for "Those from the River") is a Spanish musical duo consisting of Antonio Romero Monge and Rafael Ruiz Perdigones, formed in Dos Hermanas, Seville, in 1962. They are best known internationally for their 1993 song "Macarena", one of the best-selling singles of all time.

Career

For three decades the duo performed Andalusian folk styles — sevillanas and flamenco rumba — at festivals and on Spanish television, recording dozens of albums for a domestic audience. Their fortunes changed when "Macarena", a rumba-flamenco track recorded in 1993, was remixed by the Bayside Boys with English lyrics in 1995.

"Macarena"

The remix topped the Billboard Hot 100 for 14 weeks in 1996 and spent 60 weeks on the chart, while its accompanying line dance became a global craze, performed at weddings, sporting events and the 1996 Democratic National Convention. Although Los del Río released further albums, none matched the phenomenon; the duo remains the archetypal one-hit wonder in the English-speaking world, while retaining a long and respected career in Spain.
