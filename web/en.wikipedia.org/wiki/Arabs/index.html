Arabs (Arabic: عَرَب, ʿarab) are an ethnic and linguistic grouping native to West Asia and North Africa, united primarily by the Arabic language and shared cultural heritage. Arab identity is generally defined by language, culture, and descent rather than by religion: the majority of Arabs are Muslim, but there are significant Arab Christian communities and smaller groups of other faiths. The Arab world comprises the 22 member states of the Arab League, stretching from Mauritania and Morocco in the west to Oman and the Comoros in the east.

History

Arabs first appear in historical records in the 9th century BC in Assyrian inscriptions referring to nomadic peoples of the Syrian desert. Arab kingdoms such as Nabataea (with its capital at Petra), Palmyra, and the South Arabian kingdoms flourished in antiquity. In the 7th century AD, the rise of Islam in the Arabian Peninsula was followed by the early Muslim conquests, which spread Arabic language and culture across the Middle East and North Africa, a process of Arabisation that continued for centuries as local populations adopted Arabic.

Culture and identity

Arabic, a Semitic language, exists as Modern Standard Arabic for formal use alongside many regional spoken dialects. Arab culture encompasses major traditions in poetry, calligraphy, architecture, music, cuisine, and science; medieval Arab scholars preserved and extended Greek, Persian, and Indian learning in mathematics, astronomy, medicine, and philosophy. Modern Arab identity was reshaped by 19th- and 20th-century movements including the Nahda (cultural renaissance), Arab nationalism, and pan-Arabism, alongside the politics of the individual nation-states. Large Arab diasporas exist in the Americas and Europe. See also Arabic, Arab League, and Arab world.
