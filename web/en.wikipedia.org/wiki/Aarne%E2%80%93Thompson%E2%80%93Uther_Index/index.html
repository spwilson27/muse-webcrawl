The Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index (ATU Index) is a catalogue of folktale types used in folklore studies. It organises folk narratives from around the world into numbered "tale types" based on their shared plots and motifs, allowing scholars to compare stories across cultures and centuries.

History

The Finnish folklorist Antti Aarne published the first version of the index in 1910 as part of the historic–geographic ("Finnish") method of folklore research. The American folklorist Stith Thompson translated and greatly expanded Aarne's catalogue in editions of 1928 and 1961, producing the Aarne–Thompson (AT) system. In 2004, the German scholar Hans-Jörg Uther published a thoroughly revised and expanded edition, The Types of International Folktales, and the system has since been known as ATU.

Structure

Tale types are grouped into broad ranges, including animal tales (types 1–299), tales of magic (300–749), religious tales (750–849), realistic tales (850–999), tales of the stupid ogre (1000–1199), anecdotes and jokes (1200–1999), and formula tales (2000–2399). Well-known examples include type 510A ("Cinderella") and type 333 ("Little Red Riding Hood"). The system is used together with Thompson's Motif-Index of Folk-Literature, which catalogues the individual motifs from which tales are built.
