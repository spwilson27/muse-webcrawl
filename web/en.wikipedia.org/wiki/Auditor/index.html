Auditor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An auditor examining financial records

An auditor is a person or a firm appointed by a company or another organization to carry out an audit. To act as an auditor, a person should be certified by the regulatory authority of accounting and auditing or possess certain specified qualifications. Generally, to act as an external auditor of a company, a person should have a certificate of practice from the regulatory authority.

Types of auditors

External auditors

External auditors are independent firms engaged to express an opinion on whether an organization's financial statements are free of material misstatement and prepared in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework. In many jurisdictions, statutory audits of public companies must be performed by registered audit firms, and auditors operate under standards issued by bodies such as the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board or, in the United States, the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board.

Internal auditors

Internal auditors are employees of the organization they audit. They evaluate internal controls, risk management, and governance processes, and report to management and the audit committee of the board. The Institute of Internal Auditors issues global standards for the profession.

Government auditors

Government auditors, such as those of supreme audit institutions (for example the U.S. Government Accountability Office or the UK National Audit Office), audit public spending and report to legislatures on the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of government programs.

Duties and responsibilities

An auditor plans and performs procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence: inspecting documents, observing processes, confirming balances with third parties, recalculating amounts, and performing analytical procedures. The auditor must maintain professional skepticism, assess the risk of fraud, and communicate findings to those charged with governance. Auditors may be liable to clients and, in some circumstances, to third parties for negligent work, and high-profile audit failures have repeatedly led to reforms of auditing regulation.

Qualifications

Professional auditors typically hold an accounting qualification such as Certified Public Accountant (CPA) in the United States, Chartered Accountant in Commonwealth countries, or Certified Internal Auditor for internal audit practice, together with continuing professional education requirements.
