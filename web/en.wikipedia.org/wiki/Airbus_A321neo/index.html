The Airbus A321neo is a single-aisle airliner created by Airbus. The A321neo ("neo" meaning "new engine option") is the longest variant of the A320neo family of aircraft, which was developed from the A321ceo (current engine option). It typically seats 180 to 220 passengers in a two-class layout, with up to 244 in a high-density configuration. The aircraft is powered by either CFM International LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-JM geared turbofan engines, offering fuel burn per seat roughly 20% lower than the previous generation.

Development

Airbus launched the A321neo in December 2010 as part of the A320neo family. The prototype made its first flight on 9 February 2016 from Hamburg. Following certification, the first aircraft was delivered to Virgin America in April 2017 and entered service the following month. Two long-range derivatives followed: the A321LR (long range), which entered service in 2018 with a range of about 4,000 nautical miles, and the A321XLR (extra long range), with additional fuel capacity giving a range of about 4,700 nautical miles, which entered service in 2024.

Market position

The A321neo has become the best-selling member of the A320neo family, accounting for the majority of the family's orders, as airlines favoured its lower seat-mile costs and the ability to open thinner long-haul routes previously served only by wide-body aircraft. It competes with the Boeing 737 MAX, particularly the MAX 10, and has been widely adopted by low-cost carriers such as Wizz Air and IndiGo as well as network carriers including American Airlines and Delta Air Lines.

Specifications (A321neo)

Length 44.51 m (146 ft); wingspan 35.8 m (117 ft 5 in) with sharklets; typical range about 3,500 nmi (6,480 km); cruising speed Mach 0.78. Maximum takeoff weight is 93.5 tonnes for the standard variant.
