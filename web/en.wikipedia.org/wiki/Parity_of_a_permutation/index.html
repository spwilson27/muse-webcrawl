Parity of a permutation

In mathematics, the parity of a permutation classifies a permutation of a finite set as even or odd according to whether it can be expressed as a product of an even or an odd number of transpositions (swaps of two elements). This classification is well defined: although a permutation admits many expressions as products of transpositions, the parity of the number of transpositions is always the same.

Sign of a permutation

The sign or signature of a permutation σ is +1 if σ is even and −1 if σ is odd. Even permutations form the alternating group, a normal subgroup of the symmetric group containing exactly half of its elements.

Examples

The identity permutation is even. A single transposition is odd. A cycle of length k can be written as k − 1 transpositions, so it is even exactly when k is odd. Parity also governs the solvability of sliding puzzles such as the 15 puzzle: only configurations with the correct combined parity of permutation and blank position are reachable.
