Don Cossacks

The Don Cossacks (Russian: Донские казаки, Donskiye kazaki) are Cossacks who settled along the middle and lower Don River. Historically, they lived in the autonomous democratic republic of the Don Cossack Host in Imperial Russia, in what was then called the Don Cossack Oblast, from the late 16th century until 1918.

History

The Don Cossacks formed from freebooters, runaway serfs and adventurers on the steppe frontier, organising themselves under elected atamans. Famous leaders include Yermak Timofeyevich, conqueror of Siberia, and the rebels Stenka Razin, Kondraty Bulavin and Yemelyan Pugachev, who led great uprisings against the Russian state.

In return for military service the Don Host enjoyed substantial autonomy until it was progressively brought under Imperial control in the 18th century. Don Cossacks served in all Russia's wars, famously charging at the Battle of Balaclava era campaigns and in the Napoleonic Wars, when Ataman Matvei Platov's horsemen reached Paris.

20th century

During the Russian Civil War the Don region was a centre of White resistance under Atamans Kaledin and Krasnov; after the Bolshevik victory, the policy of decossackization brought mass repression, deportation and famine. The Don Cossack Host was abolished in 1920. Cossack units were later re-formed in the Red Army, and a Cossack cultural revival followed the fall of the Soviet Union. The Don Cossack Choir Serge Jaroff carried their song tradition worldwide.
