Czechoslovak Air Force

Active1918–1992
CountryCzechoslovakia
TypeAir force
EngagementsSecond World War, Cold War

The Czechoslovak Air Force (Czech: Československé letectvo) was the air force branch of the Czechoslovak Army, existing from the founding of Czechoslovakia in 1918 until the dissolution of the state at the end of 1992, whereupon its assets were divided between the Czech Air Force and the Slovak Air Force.

Interwar period

The air force was built up in the 1920s and 1930s around a strong domestic aircraft industry, including the firms Avia, Aero and Letov. Aircraft such as the Avia B-534 biplane fighter equipped the front-line squadrons. By the time of the Munich crisis in 1938, the Czechoslovak Air Force fielded several hundred modern aircraft, but it was never committed to combat against Germany.

Second World War

After the German occupation of Bohemia and Moravia in 1939, many Czechoslovak airmen escaped abroad and fought with the Allies. Czechoslovak-manned squadrons — Nos. 310, 311, 312 and 313 Squadrons — served in the Royal Air Force, and Czechoslovak pilots took part in the Battle of Britain; Josef František was among the highest-scoring Allied aces of that battle. Other airmen served with the Soviet forces on the Eastern Front.

Cold War

After 1948, under communist rule and within the Warsaw Pact, the air force was re-equipped with Soviet types, including the MiG-15, MiG-19, MiG-21 and later MiG-23 and MiG-29 fighters, as well as the domestically produced Aero L-29 Delfín and L-39 Albatros jet trainers, which were exported throughout the Eastern bloc.

Dissolution

With the Velvet Revolution of 1989 and the federation's breakup, the air force's aircraft and personnel were divided between the Czech Republic and Slovakia on 1 January 1993 in roughly a 2:1 ratio.
