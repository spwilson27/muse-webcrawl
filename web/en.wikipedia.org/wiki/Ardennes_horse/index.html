The Ardennes or Ardennais is one of the oldest breeds of draft horse, named for the Ardennes region of Belgium, Luxembourg, and France. One of the oldest documented draft breeds in Europe, it descends from heavy horses used since Roman times and is believed to have ancestry related to the prehistoric Solutré horse.

An Ardennes horse in harness

History

Horses from the Ardennes were used as war horses during the Crusades and by the armies of Napoleon Bonaparte; legend holds that the breed carried knights of Godfrey of Bouillon. In the 19th century, breeders crossed Ardennes horses with Brabant (Belgian draft) blood to produce a heavier animal for agriculture. Separate national stud books are maintained in Belgium, France, Luxembourg, and Sweden, where the Swedish Ardennes was developed as its own breed population.

Characteristics

The Ardennes is a compact, heavily muscled horse with a broad head, short thick neck, and strong, feathered legs. It is known for its docile temperament, hardiness in cold climates, and economical maintenance, thriving on less feed than many other draft breeds. Traditional uses included farm work, hauling timber in forested and mountainous terrain, and pulling artillery and wagons.

Modern use

Today the breed is used in forestry work where machinery access is limited, in competitive driving and pulling, in heritage and promotional hitches, and in some countries for horse meat production. Crosses with the Ardennes have influenced breeds such as the Baltic Ardennes and the Russian Heavy Draft.
