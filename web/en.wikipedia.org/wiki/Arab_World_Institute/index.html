The Arab World Institute (French: Institut du Monde Arabe, IMA) is an organisation founded in Paris in 1980 by France together with the twenty-two member states of the Arab League to research and disseminate information about the Arab world and its cultural and spiritual values. It also promotes cooperation and exchanges between France and the Arab nations, particularly in science and technology.

Building

The institute's building, designed by Jean Nouvel in collaboration with Architecture-Studio and inaugurated by President François Mitterrand in 1987, stands between the Seine and the Jussieu campus in the 5th arrondissement. It was one of Mitterrand's Grands Projets. The south facade is composed of 240 metal apertures (moucharabiehs) whose diaphragms were designed to open and close photoelectrically with the light, like the lens of a camera, modulating the interior light in a modern interpretation of the traditional Islamic mashrabiya screen. The mechanism became unreliable and the apertures were for many years fixed in place; a major renovation restored the building and its systems in the 2010s. The building received the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 1989.

Activities

The IMA houses a museum of Arab art and civilisation (refounded with a new museography in 2012), a library, an auditorium, a bookshop, and a rooftop restaurant with views over the Seine and Notre-Dame. It mounts major temporary exhibitions — on topics from Islamic art and the Hajj to contemporary Arab photography — and hosts concerts, film screenings, debates and Arabic language courses. Jack Lang, former French culture minister, became its president in 2013.
