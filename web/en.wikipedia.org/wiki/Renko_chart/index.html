Renko chart

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Renko bricks forming a price trend

A Renko chart (Japanese: 練行足, romanized: renkōashi; also written 連行足) is a type of financial chart of Japanese origin used in technical analysis that measures and plots price changes. A Renko chart consists of bricks (煉瓦, renga), which the chart creator claims help to filter the "noise" of minor price fluctuations and to clarify trends for traders.

Construction

Each brick is drawn at a 45-degree angle, up (usually white or green) or down (usually black or red), from the previous brick. A new brick is plotted only when the price moves by a predetermined amount — the box size — in one direction. Time is not represented uniformly: a single brick may take minutes or months to form, depending on price movement, unlike candlestick charts in which each candle represents a fixed time interval.

Use

Traders use Renko charts to identify support and resistance levels, breakouts and trend reversals, since the fixed box size suppresses small fluctuations that can generate false signals in time-based charts. A reversal brick in the opposite direction requires the price to retrace, typically by twice the box size from the last brick, before it is drawn.

History

Renko charting is one of several Japanese charting methods, alongside candlesticks, Kagi charts and three-line break charts, that were introduced to Western traders largely through Steve Nison's books on Japanese charting techniques in the 1990s.
