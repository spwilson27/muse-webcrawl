F. Hoffmann-La Roche AG, commonly known as Roche, is a Swiss multinational healthcare company that operates worldwide under two divisions: Pharmaceuticals and Diagnostics. Founded in Basel in 1896 by Fritz Hoffmann-La Roche, it is one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies and a leader in in-vitro diagnostics.

The Roche Tower in Basel, the company's headquarters.

History

Roche grew from a small Basel manufacturer of medicines into a global group, pioneering the industrial synthesis of vitamin C and producing landmark drugs in oncology, virology and neuroscience. Its products have included the benzodiazepines Valium and Librium, the influenza treatment Tamiflu, and monoclonal antibody cancer therapies developed through its Californian subsidiary Genentech, which Roche acquired fully in 2009.

Divisions

The Pharmaceuticals division develops medicines in oncology, immunology, infectious diseases, ophthalmology and neuroscience. The Diagnostics division, based in Rotkreuz, Switzerland, supplies laboratory and point-of-care testing systems and is the world market leader in in-vitro diagnostics. Roche also owns the American diagnostics company Ventana Medical Systems and a majority stake in Japan's Chugai Pharmaceutical.
