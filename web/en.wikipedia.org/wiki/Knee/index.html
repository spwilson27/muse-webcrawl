Anatomy of the right knee

In humans and other primates, the knee joins the thigh with the leg and consists of two joints: one between the femur and tibia (the tibiofemoral joint) and one between the femur and patella (the patellofemoral joint). It is the largest joint in the human body.

Structure

The knee is a modified hinge joint that permits flexion and extension with a small degree of medial and lateral rotation. The rounded condyles of the femur roll and glide on the relatively flat tibial plateau; two crescent-shaped fibrocartilages, the medial and lateral menisci, deepen the socket and distribute load. The joint is stabilised by the anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments inside the joint and the medial and lateral collateral ligaments outside it. The patella (kneecap), a sesamoid bone embedded in the quadriceps tendon, increases the leverage of the thigh muscles and shields the front of the joint. The whole joint is enclosed in a capsule lined with synovial membrane.

Clinical significance

The knee is vulnerable to both acute injury and degenerative disease. Common problems include tears of the anterior cruciate ligament and menisci — frequent in sport — osteoarthritis, bursitis and patellar tendinitis. Severe joint damage may be treated with total or partial knee replacement (arthroplasty), one of the most common major operations in modern medicine.
