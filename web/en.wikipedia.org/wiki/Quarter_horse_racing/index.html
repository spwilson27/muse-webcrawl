Quarter horse racing

Quarter horse racing is a form of horse racing in which American Quarter Horses compete in sprint races over short distances, traditionally a quarter of a mile (about 400 metres) or less. The sport is governed in the United States by the American Quarter Horse Association (AQHA).

History

Short match races were run in colonial Virginia and the Carolinas in the 17th and 18th centuries, giving the breed its name. Organized quarter horse racing grew in the American Southwest in the 20th century, with tracks such as Ruidoso Downs in New Mexico and Los Alamitos in California becoming leading venues.

Races

Distances range from 220 to 870 yards, most commonly 350–440 yards on a straight track, with finishes timed to the thousandth of a second; top speeds approach 55 mph (89 km/h). The richest race is the All American Futurity at Ruidoso Downs, held on Labor Day for two-year-olds, with a purse of around $3 million. Quarter Horses receive speed index ratings rather than the figure systems used in Thoroughbred racing.
