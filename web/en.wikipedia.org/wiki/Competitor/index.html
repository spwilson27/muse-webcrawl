Competitor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Competitors in a foot race

A competitor is a person, team, organization, or other entity that takes part in a competition, or that offers products or services rival to those of another in a market. The word derives from the Latin competere, "to strive together". In ordinary usage the term covers both participants in contests — such as athletes in a race or candidates in an election — and businesses competing for customers.

In sport and games

In sporting contexts, a competitor is an individual or team entered in an event, striving to win against other entrants under a common set of rules. Competitors may be ranked by past performance, and governing bodies typically regulate eligibility, conduct, and fair play. The term is also used in games, spelling bees, esports, and other structured contests.

In business and economics

In business, a competitor is a firm that supplies similar goods or services to the same customers. Economists study competitors within models of market structure: in perfect competition there are many competitors with no individual power over price, while in oligopoly a few large competitors dominate, and in monopoly there is effectively no competitor. Businesses analyse competitors' prices, products, and strategies — a practice known as competitor analysis or competitive intelligence — to position their own offerings. Competition law (antitrust) exists in many jurisdictions to prevent competitors from colluding or abusing market power.

In biology

In ecology, competitors are organisms or species that vie for the same limited resources, such as food, light, territory, or mates. Interspecific competition between species and intraspecific competition within a species are important drivers of natural selection and population dynamics.
