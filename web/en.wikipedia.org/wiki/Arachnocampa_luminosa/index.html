Arachnocampa luminosa is a species of fungus gnat endemic to New Zealand, famous for the bright blue-green bioluminescence of its larval stage — the animal known as the New Zealand glowworm. Colonies in caves such as Waitomo create a celebrated "living ceiling" of lights.

Life cycle and hunting

The larva builds a silk nest on a cave ceiling or sheltered bank and suspends sticky mucous threads. Its light, produced by modified Malpighian tubules, attracts flying insects, which become entangled in the threads and are hauled up and eaten. The larval stage lasts many months; the adult fly lives only a few days and does not feed.

Distribution and tourism

The species occurs in caves and damp, sheltered forest on both main islands. Glowworm grottos, above all the Waitomo Caves on the North Island, have drawn visitors since the late 19th century.
