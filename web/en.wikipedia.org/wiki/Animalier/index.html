Animalier

[Image: Bronze animalier sculpture of a lion by Antoine-Louis Barye]

An animalier is an artist, mainly from the 19th century, who specialises in (or is known for) realistic depictions of animals. The term is most often applied to sculptors, and to the small bronze animal sculptures — animalier bronzes — produced in large numbers in France.

History

The French school was led by Antoine-Louis Barye (1796–1875), whose dramatic studies of wild beasts at the Jardin des Plantes earned him the title "Michelangelo of the menagerie". Other notable animaliers include Pierre-Jules Mêne, Isidore Bonheur, Rosa Bonheur (better known as a painter), Christophe Fratin and Emmanuel Frémiet. Their works were cast in bronze by Parisian foundries and widely collected.

Characteristics

Animalier art emphasises anatomical accuracy, observed behaviour and lively surfaces rather than idealised or heraldic animals. The vogue faded in the early 20th century as modernism shifted taste, though animalier bronzes remain popular with collectors.
