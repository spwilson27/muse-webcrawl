Little Italy

A street scene in a Little Italy district

Little Italy is the name given to ethnic enclaves of Italian immigrants and their descendants in cities around the world, particularly in the United States, Canada, and Australia. These neighbourhoods formed during the great wave of Italian emigration between roughly 1880 and the 1920s, and were characterised by Italian-language churches, shops, social clubs, and restaurants.

Characteristics

Italian immigrants, many from southern Italy and Sicily, clustered in districts where they could find work, worship in Roman Catholic parishes staffed by Italian-speaking priests, and patronise businesses from their home regions. Festivals such as the Feast of San Gennaro in New York became signature events. After the Second World War, suburbanisation dispersed many residents, and numerous Little Italys shrank into restaurant and heritage districts, although some remain living Italian-American communities.

Notable Little Italys

  • New York City — centred on Mulberry Street in Manhattan; once home to tens of thousands, now a compact strip largely surrounded by Chinatown. Other Italian enclaves include Bensonhurst and Arthur Avenue in the Bronx.
  • Boston — the North End, Boston's oldest residential community, still known for its Italian restaurants and feasts.
  • Chicago — centred near Taylor Street and the University of Illinois Chicago campus.
  • San Diego — a revived neighbourhood near downtown, centred on India Street, with a large "Little Italy" sign.
  • San Francisco — North Beach, also associated with the Beat Generation.
  • Philadelphia — South Philadelphia, home of the Italian Market.
  • Baltimore, Cleveland, St. Louis ("The Hill"), Providence (Federal Hill), New Haven (Wooster Square) — long-established enclaves.
  • Toronto — Little Italy along College Street, and a second enclave on St. Clair Avenue (Corso Italia).
  • Montreal — centred on Saint-Laurent Boulevard and Jean-Talon Street, home of the Jean-Talon Market.
  • Sydney and Melbourne, Australia — Leichhardt in Sydney and Lygon Street, Carlton in Melbourne are known as Italian districts.

Culture

Little Italys exported Italian-American cuisine — dishes such as spaghetti and meatballs, chicken parmigiana, and New York-style pizza developed in these communities — and remain focal points for Columbus Day and religious feast celebrations.
