HMT Empire Windrush was a British troopship best known for bringing one of the first large groups of postwar West Indian immigrants to the United Kingdom. On 22 June 1948 she docked at Tilbury, Essex, carrying 1,027 passengers, among them several hundred Caribbean men — many of them RAF veterans of the Second World War — who became the symbol of the "Windrush generation" and of modern multicultural Britain.

History

The ship was launched in 1930 as the German passenger liner Monte Rosa for Hamburg Süd's South America service. During the war she served as a German troop transport and depot ship, and was taken by Britain as a war prize in 1945, renamed for the River Windrush in the Cotswolds, and converted to a troopship.

The 1948 voyage

An advertisement in Jamaican newspapers had offered cheap passage (£28 10s) to anyone wishing to work in Britain, then suffering a postwar labour shortage. The passengers who disembarked at Tilbury — photographed and filmed by newsreels — included the calypso musicians Lord Kitchener and Lord Beginner; Kitchener sang "London Is the Place for Me" on the dock. Most of the new arrivals were temporarily housed in the Clapham South deep-level air-raid shelter and found work through the nearby Brixton labour exchange, seeding London's Afro-Caribbean community.

Later service and loss

Empire Windrush continued trooping voyages, including bringing home troops from the Korean War. In March 1954, while returning from the Far East, a catastrophic fire broke out in her engine room in the Mediterranean; four crew members were killed. The passengers and remaining crew were rescued, but the burned-out ship sank on 30 March 1954 while under tow to Gibraltar, southeast of Algiers.

Legacy

The ship gave its name to the Windrush generation of Commonwealth migrants and, decades later, to the 2018 Windrush scandal, in which long-settled residents were wrongly threatened with deportation. Windrush Day is commemorated in Britain on 22 June, and one of the ship's anchors was recovered from the wreck in 2023 for public display.
