The University of Stirling is a public university in Stirling, Scotland, founded by royal charter in 1967. It was the first genuinely new university in Scotland in more than 400 years, created as part of the Robbins expansion of British higher education. Its campus, widely regarded as one of the most beautiful in the United Kingdom, is set around Airthrey Loch within the grounds of the historic Airthrey Castle estate.

History

The university opened in September 1967 with 164 students. It pioneered the semester system and modular degree structures in Scotland, and its founding principal, Tom Cottrell, emphasized interdisciplinary study. The campus includes the Macrobert Arts Centre and lies in view of the National Wallace Monument.

Academics and sport

Teaching and research are organized in faculties of Arts and Humanities; Health Sciences and Sport; Natural Sciences; Social Sciences; and the Stirling Management School. The university is Scotland's University for Sporting Excellence: its facilities host national swimming and tennis academies, and its Institute for Aquaculture is internationally recognized. The Institute for Social Marketing and the Dementia Services Development Centre are also notable research centers.

Rankings

Stirling consistently scores highly in UK student-satisfaction surveys and has been recognized for its campus environment and sports facilities.
