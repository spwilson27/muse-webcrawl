A cone is a three-dimensional geometric shape that tapers smoothly from a flat base (frequently, though not necessarily, circular) to a point called the apex or vertex. A cone is formed by the set of line segments connecting the apex to all points of the base curve. When the base is a circle and the apex lies on the axis perpendicular to the base through its centre, the shape is a right circular cone, the form most commonly meant by the word.

Geometry

For a right circular cone of base radius r and height h, the volume is V = (1/3)πr²h, exactly one third of the volume of the circumscribing cylinder. The slant height is ℓ = √(r² + h²), and the lateral surface area is πrℓ, so the total surface area is πr² + πrℓ. Cutting a cone with a plane produces the conic sections: circle, ellipse, parabola, and hyperbola.

General cones

More generally, a cone over any base shape is the union of rays from the apex through the base; its volume is one third of the product of the base area and the perpendicular height. An oblique cone has its apex displaced from the axis of the base. A truncated cone, cut by a plane parallel to the base, is a frustum.

Occurrences

Cones occur in nature and technology as traffic cones, ice-cream cones, volcanoes, conifer seed cones, loudspeaker cones, and the Mach cone of a supersonic object.
