Microsoft Teams is a team collaboration application developed by Microsoft as part of the Microsoft 365 family of products. It combines persistent chat, video and voice meetings, file storage and sharing through SharePoint and OneDrive, and integration with other Microsoft and third-party applications. Work in Teams is organised into "teams" and "channels". Teams was launched worldwide in March 2017 and replaced Skype for Business as Microsoft's principal communications platform; it competes principally with Slack, Zoom, and Google Meet.


The Microsoft Teams desktop interface, showing teams, channels, and chat.

History

Microsoft announced Teams at an event in New York on 2 November 2016 and released it generally on 14 March 2017. A free tier was introduced in 2018. Usage grew rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic as organisations adopted remote work: Microsoft reported 75 million daily active users in April 2020 and 145 million in April 2021, with monthly active users later reported at around 270–320 million. In 2023 Microsoft rolled out a rebuilt, faster "new Teams" client, and in 2024 it began offering Teams unbundled from Microsoft 365 in response to regulatory scrutiny in the European Union following a complaint by Slack. Features added over time include breakout rooms, live captions and transcription, webinars, and integration of Microsoft Copilot AI assistance.

Features

Core features include one-to-one and group chat, threaded channel conversations, audio and video meetings with screen sharing, PSTN calling through Teams Phone, file collaboration on Office documents, a wiki and notebook (OneNote), app integrations and bots, and administration, compliance, and data-loss-prevention tools for enterprise customers. Teams is also widely used in education through Teams for Education assignments and classes.
