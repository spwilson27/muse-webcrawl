Unification Church

The Unification Church (now the Family Federation for World Peace and Unification) is a new religious movement founded by Sun Myung Moon in Seoul, South Korea, in 1954.

The Unification Church is a new religious movement founded in 1954 by Sun Myung Moon in South Korea. Its official name was the Holy Spirit Association for the Unification of World Christianity until 1994, when Moon changed it to the Family Federation for World Peace and Unification. Members are sometimes called "Moonies," a term many regard as pejorative.

Beliefs and practices

Unification teaching is set out in the Divine Principle, which interprets the Bible through Moon's revelations. It teaches that Jesus' mission was cut short by his crucifixion and that Moon and his wife Hak Ja Han are the "True Parents" who complete that mission by restoring humanity through the Blessing — marriages arranged or approved by the church, famously celebrated in mass wedding ceremonies. The movement strongly emphasizes family values, anti-communism, and the eventual unification of Korea and of humanity.

History and controversies

The church expanded rapidly in the 1970s in the United States and elsewhere, attracting controversy over recruitment practices, fundraising, and political influence; Moon was convicted of tax fraud in the US in 1982. The movement built a large business and media empire, including the Washington Times. After Moon's death in 2012, leadership disputes split the movement into competing organizations. In Japan, scrutiny of the church's fundraising intensified after the 2022 assassination of former prime minister Shinzo Abe by a man who said the church had bankrupted his family; in 2025 a Tokyo court ordered the church's Japanese arm dissolved.
