The Sheriff of Nottingham is the chief antagonist in the legend of Robin Hood. As the local representative of royal authority in Nottinghamshire, he is typically portrayed as a greedy and oppressive official who taxes the poor, enforces the forest laws and hunts the outlaw Robin Hood through Sherwood Forest, repeatedly failing and often being duped, robbed or humiliated in the process.

In the early ballads

The Sheriff appears in the earliest surviving Robin Hood ballads, including Robin Hood and the Potter and A Gest of Robyn Hode (c. 1450), in which he is a formidable enemy who breaks his sworn word, besieges the outlaws and is eventually killed by Robin. In later versions, especially in children's literature and on screen, he became subordinate or parallel to Prince John as the villain of the piece.

Historical background

The office of High Sheriff was a real one: the sheriff was the king's officer responsible for law enforcement, tax collection and the county court in medieval Nottinghamshire (the post at times also covered Derbyshire). No single historical sheriff matches the legend, though several real sheriffs of the 12th and 13th centuries — such as William de Wendenal and Philip Marc, a notoriously unpopular sheriff under King John — have been proposed as partial models.

Portrayals

The Sheriff has been played by actors including Melville Cooper, Peter Cushing, Alan Rickman (in a celebrated turn in Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves, 1991), Roger Rees, Matthew Macfadyen and Ben Mendelsohn. Disney's animated Robin Hood (1973) cast him as a corpulent wolf. In modern retellings he is sometimes given names such as George or merged with Guy of Gisbourne as the chief villain.
