Exercise book


A typical school exercise book

An exercise book or composition book is a notebook used in schools by pupils and students for written work, homework and notes. It consists of leaves of paper, usually ruled, stitched or stapled into a light card cover and commonly bearing a space on the front for the owner's name, class and subject. Exercise books are among the most familiar artefacts of schooling in much of the world.

Formats and rulings

Exercise books are produced in standard sizes — A4 and A5 in most countries, with local equivalents elsewhere — and with rulings suited to subjects: lined pages with margins for general writing, narrow "feint" ruling, squared (quadrille) paper for mathematics and science, manuscript staves for music, and handwriting guidelines for young children. In France, the Séyès ruling of fine squares with vertical lines guides letter formation. Colour-coded covers often distinguish subjects, and school systems may specify standard types, such as the British practice of distinct books for each subject.

Related forms

The North American composition book is a bound notebook, often with a distinctive black-and-white marbled cover, used for journaling and schoolwork. A copy book historically contained printed handwriting exemplars to be imitated. Exercise books remain in use alongside digital devices, and in many countries they are supplied free by schools or governments as part of basic educational provision.
