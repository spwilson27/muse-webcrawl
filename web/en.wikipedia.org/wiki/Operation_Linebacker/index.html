Operation Linebacker

Operation Linebacker was the codename of a U.S. Seventh Air Force and U.S. Navy Task Force 77 aerial interdiction campaign conducted against North Vietnam from 9 May to 23 October 1972, during the Vietnam War. Its purpose was to halt or slow supplies reaching North Vietnamese forces invading South Vietnam in the Easter Offensive, and to compel Hanoi to return to negotiations.

Background

On 30 March 1972, North Vietnam launched the Nguyen Hue (Easter) Offensive, a large conventional invasion of South Vietnam. In response, President Richard Nixon ordered the resumption of bombing north of the Demilitarized Zone and the mining of Haiphong and other North Vietnamese harbours (Operation Pocket Money).

The campaign

Unlike the earlier Operation Rolling Thunder, Linebacker employed few political restrictions: bridges, rail yards, petroleum storage, power plants and airfields across North Vietnam were attacked, using new precision-guided munitions such as laser-guided bombs, which famously destroyed the Thanh Hóa Bridge after years of failed attempts. The campaign badly disrupted North Vietnamese logistics and helped South Vietnamese forces, supported by US airpower, to halt the offensive.

Aftermath

Linebacker was suspended in October 1972 as peace talks progressed. After negotiations stalled, the even more intensive Operation Linebacker II — the "Christmas bombings" — was flown in December 1972, preceding the Paris Peace Accords of January 1973.
