In music, 24 equal temperament, called 24-TET, 24-EDO, or 24-ET, is the musical scale obtained by dividing the octave into 24 equal steps, each of exactly 50 cents. It is the most widely used system of quarter tones: every interval of the standard twelve-tone equal temperament is present in 24-TET, with an additional note inserted exactly halfway between each adjacent pair of semitones.

Properties

Because 24 is a multiple of 12, all of 12-TET's intervals — including its perfect fifth of 700 cents and major third of 400 cents — are reproduced exactly. The new pitches add the neutral intervals familiar from many Middle Eastern and microtonal traditions: the neutral second (150 cents), neutral third (350 cents), and neutral seventh (1050 cents) among others. The neutral third approximates the just ratio 11/9 (347.4 cents) within about 3 cents, considerably better than 12-TET's minor and major thirds approximate their respective just counterparts.

History and use

Quarter-tone divisions were theorised as early as the 19th century, and composers including Julián Carrillo, Alois Hába, Ivan Wyschnegradsky, and Charles Ives wrote explicitly for 24-TET in the early 20th century, sometimes using specially built quarter-tone pianos. The tuning is a standard benchmark in Arabic music theory, where scales are often described in quarter-tone terms, and it is the most common temperament in contemporary microtonal composition because it extends rather than replaces the familiar chromatic system.

Notation

Quarter tones are conventionally written with reversed flat signs and crossed or half-sharp signs for quarter-sharps and quarter-flats. In the system adopted by many theorists, a half-sharp raises a note by 50 cents and a sesquisharp by 150 cents, allowing all 24 pitch classes to be spelled within key signatures based on the diatonic scale.
