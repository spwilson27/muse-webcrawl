Radiohead

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Radiohead performing live: Thom Yorke at the microphone, the band in low light, in the austere staging of their later tours]

Radiohead are an English rock band formed in Abingdon, Oxfordshire, in 1985, comprising Thom Yorke, brothers Jonny and Colin Greenwood, Ed O'Brien and Philip Selway. Hailed as one of the most influential bands of their era, they moved from the guitar rock of The Bends (1995) to the millennial landmarks OK Computer (1997) and Kid A (2000), fusing rock with electronic music, jazz and modern classical harmony.

Their third album OK Computer — anxious hymns to transport, technology and alienation — topped countless polls of the greatest albums; Kid A, recorded in its shadow of fame, discarded guitars for electronics and debuted at No. 1 in America. Later landmarks include In Rainbows (2007), released on a pay-what-you-want basis that rattled the industry's economics, and A Moon Shaped Pool (2016).

History in outline

Schoolfriends at Abingdon, the band (as On a Friday) signed to EMI and broke through with 'Creep' (1992) — a hit they long treated as an albatross. The Bends established the songwriting; the OK Computer tour's exhaustion, documented in the film Meeting People Is Easy (1998), pushed Yorke to refuse repetition on Kid A and Amnesiac (2001), made with Nigel Godrich, their permanent collaborator-producer.

Working methods and standing

Radiohead's long career models artistic autonomy inside fame: self-managed recording, surprise releases, environmental campaigning (carbon-accounted tours), and side projects (Yorke's solo work and the Smile with Jonny Greenwood and Tom Skinner) that feed the parent band. They entered the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019. Jonny Greenwood's film scores (There Will Be Blood, Phantom Thread) added a parallel canon.
