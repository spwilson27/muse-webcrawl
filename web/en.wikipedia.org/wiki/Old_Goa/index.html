Old Goa (Konkani: Ponnje; Portuguese: Velha Goa) is a historic city in North Goa district, India, on the banks of the Mandovi River. It was the capital of Portuguese India from the 16th to the 18th century, when epidemics drove the administration to relocate to Panjim. Its churches and convents were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

History

Originally a port of the Bijapur Sultanate, the city was captured by Afonso de Albuquerque in 1510. Old Goa grew into a great entrepôt of Asian trade — called "Golden Goa" and "the Rome of the East" — with a population rivaling Lisbon's at its height. It was also a centre of Christian missionary activity in Asia; Saint Francis Xavier preached there, and his incorrupt remains are preserved in the Basilica of Bom Jesus, attracting vast pilgrim gatherings during the decennial exposition.

Monuments

Surviving monuments include the Basilica of Bom Jesus (1605), the Sé Cathedral — one of the largest churches in Asia — the Church and Convent of St. Francis of Assisi, the Chapel of St. Catherine, and the ruins of the Church of St. Augustine, of whose great tower only a fragment remains standing. The viceregal arch and the Gate of Adil Shah's palace recall the city's layered past.
