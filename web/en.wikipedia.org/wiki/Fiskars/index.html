Fiskars Group (stylized as FISKARS) is a Finnish consumer goods company founded in 1649 in the village of Fiskars (in the present-day town of Raseborg), where it began as an ironworks. One of the oldest businesses in the Western world, Fiskars is best known for its orange-handled scissors, created in 1967, which have sold more than a billion pairs worldwide.

History

The company was founded in 1649 when Dutch merchant Peter Thorwöste received a charter to build a blast furnace and forge in Fiskars, Finland, then part of Sweden. The ironworks produced nails, wire, hoes, and wheels. In the 19th century the works was developed by Johan Jacob Julin into a center for fine metalwork, and a cutlery factory was founded to produce knives, forks, and scissors.

In 1967 Fiskars introduced the first plastic-handled scissors with injection-molded orange handles — the orange color reportedly resulted from leftover plastic used in a prototype. The scissors became an icon of Finnish design and are exhibited in museums including the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Brands and products

The Fiskars Group today manages a portfolio of international brands including Fiskars (scissors and garden tools), Gerber (knives and outdoor gear), Iittala, Arabia, Royal Copenhagen, Wedgwood, Waterford, and Rörstrand. Fiskars Group is listed on Nasdaq Helsinki.
