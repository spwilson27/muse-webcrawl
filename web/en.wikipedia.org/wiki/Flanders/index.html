Flanders

Flanders
Country: Belgium (Flemish Region and Flemish Community); historical county extended into modern France and the Netherlands
Capital (of the Flemish Region/Community): Brussels (shared capital institutions)
Largest cities: Antwerp, Ghent, Bruges, Leuven
Language: Dutch (Flemish)
Population (Flemish Region): about 6.8 million

Flanders (Dutch: Vlaanderen) is the Dutch-speaking northern portion of Belgium and one of the communities, regions and language areas of Belgium. The historic County of Flanders, a fief of the kingdom of France from the 9th century, covered a wider area than today, stretching into what is now northern France (French Flanders) and the southwestern Netherlands (Zeelandic Flanders). In modern usage, "Flanders" denotes the Flemish Region (provinces of West Flanders, East Flanders, Antwerp, Limburg and Flemish Brabant) together with the Flemish Community, which also serves Dutch-speakers in Brussels.

History

Medieval Flanders grew rich on the cloth trade: the cities of Bruges, Ghent and Ypres imported English wool and exported finished textiles throughout Europe, and Bruges became a leading port and financial centre. The counts of Flanders, nominally vassals of France, repeatedly clashed with the French crown; at the Battle of the Golden Spurs (1302) Flemish urban militias famously defeated French knights near Kortrijk. Flanders passed to the dukes of Burgundy (1384), then to the Habsburgs; the northern provinces joined the Dutch Republic while the south ("Spanish", then "Austrian", Netherlands) remained under Habsburg rule. It became part of Belgium in 1830. In the First World War the western corner of Flanders — "Flanders Fields" around Ypres — was devastated by trench warfare, commemorated in John McCrae's poem In Flanders Fields (1915) and by the remembrance poppy.

The Flemish Movement and modern Flanders

The 19th and 20th centuries saw the Flemish Movement campaign for the Dutch language against the dominance of French in Belgian public life, achieving official equality and, through successive state reforms (1970–1993), a federal Belgium in which Flanders governs its own cultural, educational, economic and other regional affairs. Economically, Flanders is Belgium's most populous and prosperous region: the port of Antwerp-Bruges is one of Europe's largest, and the region is a hub for chemicals, logistics, diamonds (Antwerp), and technology around Leuven and Ghent. Flemish nationalist parties (notably the N-VA and Vlaams Belang) advocate greater autonomy or independence, making the Flemish question central to Belgian politics.

Culture

Flanders is the homeland of the Flemish Primitives — Jan van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden, Hans Memling — and of Peter Paul Rubens and Anthony van Dyck in the Baroque era. The region is famed for cycling (the Tour of Flanders race), lace, beer, chocolate, and its medieval belfries and beguinages, several of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
