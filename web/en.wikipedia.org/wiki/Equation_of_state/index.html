Equation of state

In physics and chemistry, an equation of state is a thermodynamic equation relating state variables—such as pressure, volume, temperature and internal energy—that describe the state of matter under given physical conditions. Equations of state are essential for describing fluids, mixtures, stellar interiors and dense matter.

Ideal gas law

The simplest equation of state is the ideal gas law, PV = nRT, accurate for gases at low pressure. Generalizations followed: Boyle's and Charles's laws are special cases.

Real gas equations

The van der Waals equation (1873) adds parameters for intermolecular attraction and finite molecular volume. Later cubic equations—Redlich–Kwong, Soave, and Peng–Robinson (1976)—are widely used in chemical engineering. Virial equations expand the compressibility factor in powers of density.

Other applications

Equations of state describe neutron star matter, cosmological fluids (characterized by the ratio w = p/ρ), and solids under high pressure (e.g. Birch–Murnaghan). Cosmological dark energy corresponds to w ≈ −1.
