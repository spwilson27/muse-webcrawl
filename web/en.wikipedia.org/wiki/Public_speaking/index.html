Public speaking, also called oratory, is the act of performing a speech to a live audience. It is commonly understood as formal, face-to-face communication intended to inform, persuade, entertain, or commemorate. Public speaking has been a central skill in politics, law, religion, education, and business since antiquity, and is studied as a branch of rhetoric.

History

In classical Athens and Rome, oratory was a cornerstone of citizenship. Aristotle's Rhetoric analysed persuasion through ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), and logos (argument). Roman theorists such as Cicero and Quintilian codified the five canons of rhetoric: invention, arrangement, style, memory, and delivery. Great orators — Demosthenes, Cicero, and later figures such as Abraham Lincoln, Winston Churchill, and Martin Luther King Jr. — shaped history through their speeches.

Types and occasions

Speeches are commonly classified by purpose: informative (lectures, briefings), persuasive (debates, campaign addresses), ceremonial (weddings, eulogies, graduations), and entertaining (after-dinner speeches). Formats include keynotes, panel discussions, debates, sermons, courtroom argument, and impromptu speaking in competitions.

Technique

Effective delivery involves clear structure (opening, body, conclusion), vocal variety in pace, pitch, and volume, eye contact, gesture, and adaptation to the audience. Visual aids such as slides are widely used. Speakers prepare by researching the topic, organising the argument, rehearsing aloud, and managing time.

Anxiety and training

Fear of public speaking — glossophobia — is among the most common social anxieties. Training organisations include Toastmasters International, founded in 1924, school and university forensics programmes, and professional coaching. Repeated practice, exposure, and feedback are the standard remedies.

Modern developments

Recording and the internet have extended public speaking beyond the live hall: TED talks, podcasts, webinars, and video platforms allow speeches to reach global audiences, while teleprompters and presentation software have reshaped delivery styles.
