Silvopasture

Silvopasture (from Latin silva, "forest") is the practice of integrating trees and pasture for grazing livestock on the same land, a form of agroforestry. Trees, forage and livestock are intentionally combined and managed together so that each component benefits the others.

Benefits

Trees provide shade and shelter for livestock, improving animal welfare and sometimes productivity; they produce timber, fruit or nuts as an additional income stream; and they sequester carbon, build soil and support biodiversity. Livestock control undergrowth and fertilise the trees. Traditional examples include the dehesa of Spain and montado of Portugal, where cork and holm oaks are grazed by pigs and cattle.

Establishment

Silvopasture can be created by planting trees into pasture, thinning forest for forage, or grazing livestock in orchards. Successful systems require careful management of tree density, forage species and grazing rotation.
