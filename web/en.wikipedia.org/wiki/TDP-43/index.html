TDP-43 (transactive response DNA binding protein 43 kDa) is a protein encoded in humans by the TARDBP gene. It is a predominantly nuclear RNA- and DNA-binding protein involved in regulating transcription, alternative splicing, mRNA transport and stability, and microRNA processing.

Role in disease

TDP-43 is the major constituent of the ubiquitinated protein inclusions found in the brains and spinal cords of patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and the most common form of frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD-TDP). Landmark papers in 2006 identified TDP-43 as the disease protein in these conditions. In diseased neurons the protein is typically cleared from the nucleus and accumulates in cytoplasmic aggregates that are hyperphosphorylated, ubiquitinated and cleaved into C-terminal fragments; both loss of nuclear function (leading, for example, to cryptic exon splicing errors in targets such as stathmin-2) and toxic gain of function are thought to contribute to neurodegeneration. Mutations in TARDBP itself are a rare cause of familial ALS.

Wider significance

TDP-43 pathology has since been found in limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy (LATE), in many cases of Alzheimer's disease, in chronic traumatic encephalopathy and in other disorders—leading to the description of a spectrum of "TDP-43 proteinopathies". The protein's low-complexity, prion-like C-terminal domain mediates aggregation and is a focus of therapeutic research.
