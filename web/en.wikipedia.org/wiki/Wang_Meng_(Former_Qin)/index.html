Wang Meng (Former Qin)

Wang Meng (Chinese: 王猛; pinyin: Wáng Měng; 325 – August 375), courtesy name Jinglüe (景略), was a Chinese statesman and strategist who served as chancellor of the Former Qin dynasty under the Di emperor Fu Jian. A Han Chinese official serving a non-Han state, he is credited with transforming Former Qin into the dominant power of northern China.

Life and reforms

Born into poverty, Wang Meng was discovered living as a recluse near Mount Hua by Fu Jian before he seized the throne. As chancellor from 359 until his death, Wang carried out sweeping reforms: he curbed corruption and the privileges of aristocratic clans, enforced laws impartially, promoted agriculture and water conservancy, and selected officials by merit. Under his administration Former Qin destroyed Former Yan in 370 and unified northern China.

Death and legacy

On his deathbed in 375, Wang Meng warned Fu Jian against invading the Jin dynasty in the south and against trusting the Qiang and Xianbei generals within the state. Fu Jian disregarded the advice; his catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Fei River in 383 shattered Former Qin, a collapse often attributed to the ignoring of Wang Meng's counsel. Wang Meng is traditionally ranked among the greatest chancellors of Chinese history.
