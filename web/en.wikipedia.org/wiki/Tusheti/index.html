Tusheti (Georgian: თუშეთი) is a historic region in the high Caucasus of northeastern Georgia, on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus range bordering Chechnya and Dagestan. It is famed for its dramatic alpine scenery, medieval defensive stone towers, and the seasonal transhumant culture of the Tush people. Much of the region lies within Tusheti National Park, one of Europe's largest protected areas.

Geography

Tusheti is drained by the Pirikiti and Gometsari Alazani rivers and is accessible from the lowlands only via the Abano Pass (about 2,850 m), one of the highest drivable passes in Europe, open roughly from late June to early October. The principal villages — Omalo, Diklo, Dartlo, Shenako, and Kvavlo — sit in side valleys divided by high ridges.

History and culture

The Tush, Georgian-speaking highlanders with both Christian and older pagan traditions, historically lived in fortified villages where tall slate towers served as refuges during raids. In the mid-20th century many families were resettled to the Alvani villages in the Kakheti lowlands; today most residents winter in the lowlands and return to Tusheti for summer grazing, cheese-making, and festivals. Tushetian wool, cheese, and beer are regional specialities, and the annual Zezvaoba and other festivals draw visitors.

Conservation

Tusheti Protected Areas, established with the national park in 2003, safeguard bezoar ibex, chamois, lynx, brown bears, and lammergeiers. The region is a popular trekking destination, with routes between Omalo, Dartlo, and neighbouring Khevsureti.
