Kingdom of Portugal

Years: 1139 – 1910

Capital: Lisbon (from 1255)

Founder: Afonso I (Afonso Henriques)

Last king: Manuel II

The Kingdom of Portugal (Portuguese: Reino de Portugal) was the Portuguese monarchy from 1139, when Afonso Henriques proclaimed himself king after the Battle of Ourique, until the revolution of 5 October 1910 replaced it with the Portuguese Republic. From 1415 onward it built the first global maritime empire, and from 1815 to 1822 it formed part of the United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil and the Algarves.

Dynasties

The kingdom was ruled successively by the Burgundian (Afonsine) dynasty, the House of Aviz — whose accession in 1385 under John I, victor at Aljubarrota, inaugurated the age of exploration under Henry the Navigator — the Philippine (Habsburg) dynasty during the Iberian Union of 1580–1640, and the House of Braganza from the Restoration War of 1640 until 1910, latterly as a constitutional monarchy.

Empire and decline

Portuguese fleets pioneered the sea route to India (Vasco da Gama, 1498), reached Brazil (1500) and traded across Africa and Asia, financing monuments such as the Jerónimos Monastery. The loss of Brazil (1822), the liberal wars, and colonial and financial crises eroded the monarchy in the nineteenth century. The regicide of Charles I in 1908 and the republican revolution two years later ended 771 years of kingship; Manuel II died in exile in England in 1932.
