Weather Underground

Founded1969, Ann Arbor, Michigan
Dissolvedc. 1977
IdeologyMarxism, anti-imperialism, communism
Key figuresBernardine Dohrn, Bill Ayers, Mark Rudd, Jeff Jones

The Weather Underground was an American far-left Marxist militant organization first active in 1969, founded on the Ann Arbor campus of the University of Michigan. Originally called the Weathermen, the group emerged as a faction of Students for a Democratic Society (SDS) and was dedicated to the revolutionary overthrow of the United States government, which it regarded as an imperialist power, and to ending the Vietnam War. Its name came from the Bob Dylan lyric "You don't need a weatherman to know which way the wind blows," from the song "Subterranean Homesick Blues."

History

The Weathermen organized the "Days of Rage" protests in Chicago in October 1969. In March 1970 three members died in an accidental explosion in a Greenwich Village townhouse while constructing bombs, after which the group went fully underground, renamed itself the Weather Underground, and shifted to bombing government and corporate buildings with advance warnings intended to avoid casualties. Attacks included the U.S. Capitol (1971), the Pentagon (1972), and the State Department (1975).

Decline and legacy

The end of the Vietnam War and internal disputes weakened the organization; it took responsibility for its last bombing in 1976 and effectively dissolved by 1977. Illegal FBI surveillance and break-ins under COINTELPRO led prosecutors to drop many charges; most members resurfaced in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The group remains a reference point in debates over political violence and 1960s radicalism, and was the subject of the 2002 documentary The Weather Underground.
