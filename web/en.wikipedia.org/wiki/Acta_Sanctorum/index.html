The Acta Sanctorum, the Bollandists' monumental collection of saints' lives.

The Acta Sanctorum ("Acts of the Saints") is a monumental critical edition of the lives of Christian saints, arranged according to their feast days in the calendar, undertaken by the Bollandists, a scholarly association of Jesuits based in Belgium. It is a foundational work of hagiography studied with critical historical method.

History

The project was conceived by Heribert Rosweyde, who gathered manuscripts of saints' lives, and was taken up by Jean Bolland, who published the first volumes — covering the saints of January — at Antwerp in 1643. Bolland and his successors, including Godfrey Henschen and Daniel Papebroch, applied rigorous source criticism, printing the earliest texts with introductions and commentaries. The work was interrupted by the suppression of the Jesuits in the 18th century and later resumed in Brussels; by 1940 the series had progressed through the saints of 10 November in 68 folio volumes.

Method and legacy

The Bollandists' critical approach — comparing manuscripts, questioning legends and distinguishing authentic documents from later forgeries — pioneered modern historical scholarship and sometimes brought them into conflict with religious orders whose cherished traditions they questioned. The enterprise continues today through the Société des Bollandistes in Brussels.
