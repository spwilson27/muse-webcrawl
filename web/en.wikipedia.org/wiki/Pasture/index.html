Pasture

Placeholder image

Pasture (from the Latin pastus, past participle of pascere, "to feed") is land used for grazing. Pasture lands in the narrow sense are enclosed tracts of farmland, grazed by domesticated livestock, such as horses, cattle, sheep, or swine.

Management

The vegetation of tended pasture consists mainly of grasses, with an interspersion of legumes and other forbs. Pasture is typically grazed throughout the summer on land that was previously used for other purposes or left fallow. Management practices include rotational grazing, in which livestock are moved between paddocks to allow forage to recover.

Factors affecting pasture productivity include soil fertility, rainfall, temperature, and grazing intensity. Overgrazing can lead to erosion and degradation. Unenclosed grazing land is usually called rangeland. See also pastoral farming and meadow.
