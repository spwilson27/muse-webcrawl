The 2004 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXVIII Olympiad, were held from 13 to 29 August 2004 in Athens, Greece — the home of the ancient Olympic Games and host of the first modern Olympics in 1896. Under the motto "Welcome Home," 10,625 athletes from 201 nations competed in 301 events.

Highlights

American swimmer Michael Phelps won eight medals, six of them gold, in his breakout Games. The men's basketball tournament produced a famous upset as Argentina defeated the United States on its way to gold. The marathon was marred — and partly redeemed — when Brazilian leader Vanderlei Cordeiro de Lima was attacked by a spectator but continued to take bronze. The shot put was staged in the ancient stadium at Olympia, and women's wrestling debuted. The United States topped the medal table with 36 golds, followed by China (32) and Russia (27).

Costs and legacy

The Games cost an estimated €9–11 billion, far over budget, amid tight security following the September 11 attacks. Greek sprinters Kostas Kenteris and Katerina Thanou withdrew after missing a doping test on the eve of the Games, foreshadowing many doping cases. Many venues later fell into disuse, and the Games' costs were often cited in discussion of Greece's subsequent debt crisis.
