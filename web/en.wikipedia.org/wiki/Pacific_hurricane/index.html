Pacific hurricane

A Pacific hurricane is a tropical cyclone that develops in the northeastern Pacific Ocean, east of the International Date Line and north of the equator, or in the north central Pacific between the date line and 140°W. Tropical cyclones in these basins are called hurricanes, as in the Atlantic; cyclones of the same nature west of the date line are called typhoons.

Basins and forecasting responsibility

The eastern North Pacific basin extends from the west coast of the Americas to 140°W and is monitored by the US National Hurricane Center (NHC) in Miami. The central North Pacific, from 140°W to the date line, is the responsibility of the Central Pacific Hurricane Center in Honolulu. The eastern Pacific is the second most active tropical cyclone basin in the world, after the western North Pacific.

Climatology

The eastern Pacific season officially runs from 15 May to 30 November, and the central Pacific season from 1 June to 30 November. Most storms form off the coast of southern Mexico and move west-northwest out to sea, but some turn north or northeast into Mexico, and a few reach Hawaii or, rarely, the southwestern United States. Sea surface temperatures, the monsoon trough, and easterly waves travelling across Central America favour development, and El Niño years tend to be more active.

Notable storms

Hurricane Patricia (2015) became the strongest tropical cyclone ever reliably measured in the Western Hemisphere, with maximum sustained winds of 345 km/h (215 mph) and a minimum pressure of 872 mbar, before making landfall in Jalisco, Mexico. Hurricane Otis (2023) devastated Acapulco after explosively intensifying from a tropical storm to a Category 5 hurricane within a day. Other notable storms include Kenna (2002), Lane (2018), and Jova (2023).
