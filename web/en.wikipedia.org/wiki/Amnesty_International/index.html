Amnesty International

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Amnesty International is an international non-governmental organisation focused on human rights, founded in London in 1961 by the lawyer Peter Benenson, whose newspaper article "The Forgotten Prisoners" appealed for amnesty for prisoners of conscience. The organisation — winner of the Nobel Peace Prize in 1977 — states that it has millions of members and supporters worldwide and campaigns for compliance with the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and international human rights law.

Work

Amnesty's methods are research (fact-finding missions and reports), advocacy, litigation support and mass mobilisation (letter-writing Urgent Actions — its emblem is a candle in barbed wire, symbolising hope in darkness). Historically focused on political prisoners, torture, the death penalty and unfair trials, its mandate widened to economic, social and cultural rights, corporate accountability, arms trade and climate justice. It is independent of any government or ideology and funds itself mainly from individual donors, declining government money for its research work.

Criticism

Governments criticised in its reports routinely dispute its findings; scholars and former staff have debated its organisational culture, geographic emphases and expanding mandate. Its annual report on the state of the world's human rights is among the most cited NGO publications.
