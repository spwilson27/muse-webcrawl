Giro d'Italia

The Giro d'Italia (Italian for "Tour of Italy"; also known as the Giro) is an annual multiple-stage bicycle race primarily held in Italy, while also passing through, and sometimes starting in, other countries. It is one of cycling's prestigious three-week-long Grand Tours, alongside the Tour de France and the Vuelta a España. The race is organised by RCS Sport, a subsidiary of the publisher of La Gazzetta dello Sport, and is part of the UCI World Tour.

History

The first Giro was held in 1909, organised by La Gazzetta dello Sport to boost circulation of the newspaper. It started and finished in Milan, covered eight stages and some 2,448 km, and was won by Luigi Ganna. Because La Gazzetta is printed on pink paper, the race leader's jersey — introduced in 1931 — is the maglia rosa (pink jersey). The race has been interrupted only by the two World Wars. Alfredo Binda, Fausto Coppi and Eddy Merckx share the record of five overall victories.

Classifications

The overall winner is the rider with the lowest cumulative time, wearing the maglia rosa. Subsidiary competitions include the points classification (maglia ciclamino, cyclamen jersey), the mountains classification (maglia azzurra, blue jersey) and the best young rider (maglia bianca, white jersey). Routes characteristically feature the great Alpine and Dolomite passes — the Stelvio, Mortirolo and Passo Gavia among them — and the race traditionally finishes in Milan, though Rome and other cities have hosted the finale.

The race today

Held annually over 21 stages in May and early June, the Giro is famous for brutal mountain stages and capricious weather, including snow on the high passes. Its roll of honour includes Charly Gaul, Jacques Anquetil (the first French winner), Miguel Induráin, Marco Pantani — whose 1998 Giro-Tour double remains the last achieved — Alberto Contador and Vincenzo Nibali.
