Dr. Gideon Fell

Dr. Gideon Fell is a fictional character created by John Dickson Carr. He is the protagonist of 23 novels published between 1933 and 1967, as well as a few short stories. Carr was an American who lived most of his adult life in England, and Fell is an Englishman who lives in the London suburbs.

An enormous man with a florid face, twinkling eyes, and a bandit's moustache, Fell walks with the aid of two canes and wears a shovel hat and a flowing cape. He is a lexicographer by profession who describes himself as an amateur criminologist, and he specializes in seemingly impossible crimes — locked-room mysteries and murders that appear to have been committed by supernatural means.

The locked room lecture

In the novel The Three Coffins (1935, published in Britain as The Hollow Man), Fell delivers his famous "locked room lecture", a chapter-length taxonomy of the methods by which a murder can appear to have been committed in a sealed room. The chapter is widely considered the definitive analysis of the device and is often reprinted as an essay in its own right.

Character and model

Fell was widely understood to be modelled on G. K. Chesterton, the writer of the Father Brown stories, whom Carr admired. Fell's frequent collaborator in the later novels is Superintendent Hadley of Scotland Yard, who supplies the official investigation while Fell supplies the explanation.
