Swiss cheese (North America)

Swiss cheese is any variety of yellow, medium-hard cheese that resembles Emmental cheese, a yellow cheese with characteristic large holes, or "eyes". The term is generic in North America; in other countries, similar cheeses are sold under names such as Emmental or Jarlsberg. Swiss cheese is widely used in sandwiches, most famously the Reuben sandwich, and in fondue blends.

Swiss cheese was developed in the United States by Swiss immigrants who settled in areas such as Green County, Wisconsin — sometimes called "Little Switzerland" — and Holmes County, Ohio, which became a major centre of production. Because it was modelled on Emmental but made with different cultures and processes, American Swiss does not qualify for the protected Swiss designation in Switzerland.

The holes, or "eyes", in Swiss cheese are formed by bubbles of carbon dioxide produced during ripening.

Production

Swiss cheese is made from cow's milk using cultures of Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus helveticus, together with Propionibacterium freudenreichii. After the curd is cooked, pressed, and brined, the cheese is ripened in warm rooms. During this stage, the propionibacteria consume lactic acid and release carbon dioxide gas, which collects in pockets and forms the eyes. The bacteria also produce compounds — including propionic acid and acetic acid — that give Swiss cheese its characteristic nutty, slightly sweet flavour. Larger eyes generally result from longer warm-room ripening, while cheese ripened without prominent eyes is sold as "baby Swiss", which is milder and made from whole milk rather than partly skimmed milk.

Regulation

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration sets a standard of identity for "Swiss cheese" and for "Swiss cheese for manufacturing": the cheese must contain no more than 41% moisture and at least 43% milkfat, must be aged at least 60 days, and must possess the characteristic eyes and flavour developed through the action of bacteria. Swiss-type cheeses are graded in part by the size and distribution of their eyes.

Varieties

  • Baby Swiss — a smaller-eyed, milder cheese made with whole milk and shorter aging.
  • Lacey Swiss — a low-fat variety with a delicate, lacy pattern of small holes, developed in Ohio.
  • Jarlsberg — a Norwegian Swiss-type cheese with large eyes and a buttery flavour, often grouped with Swiss cheeses in North American retail.

Uses

Swiss cheese melts smoothly and is common in sandwiches (Reuben, French dip, Cuban sandwich), burgers, quiches, gratins, and fondue, where it is often blended with Gruyère. It is one of the most popular sliced cheeses in American delicatessens.
