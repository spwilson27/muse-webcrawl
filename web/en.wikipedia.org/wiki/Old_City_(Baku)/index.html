The Old City (Azerbaijani: İçərişəhər, "Inner City") is the historical core of Baku, the capital of Azerbaijan. It is the most ancient part of the city and is surrounded by well-preserved fortress walls. In 1977 the Old City was designated a historical and architectural reserve, and in December 2000 it became Azerbaijan's first location inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List, together with the Shirvanshahs' Palace and the Maiden Tower.

History

The Old City stands on a site inhabited since the Paleolithic period. It developed into a walled town during the medieval period, when Baku became an important port on the Caspian Sea and a centre of the Shirvanshah state. Most of the surviving walls and towers date to the 12th century, when the fortifications were strengthened after an earthquake prompted the Shirvanshahs to move their capital from Shamakhi to Baku.

The Palace of the Shirvanshahs, built mainly in the 15th century, forms the architectural heart of the quarter. The Maiden Tower, whose origins remain debated, may incorporate masonry from as early as the 7th–6th centuries BCE, with its main structure generally dated to the 12th century.

Layout and landmarks

The Old City covers about 22 hectares and retains a dense tangle of narrow lanes, mosques, caravanserais, bathhouses, and dwellings. Notable monuments include:

  • The Maiden Tower (Qız Qalası), Baku's best-known landmark.
  • The Palace of the Shirvanshahs, a complex of palace, mosque, mausoleum and baths.
  • The Muhammad Mosque (1078–1079), the oldest mosque in Baku, with its Siniggala minaret.
  • Caravanserais such as Multani and Bukhara, which served merchants on the Silk Road.
  • Historic bathhouses (hamams), including the Haji Gayib and Aga Mikayil baths.

The reserve is divided into traditional neighbourhoods (mahallas) such as Agshalvarlilar, Agha Zeynal, and Kichik Gala. Despite tourism and restoration, the Old City remains a living quarter with a resident population.

Conservation

Following its UNESCO inscription, concerns about damage from the 2000 Baku earthquake and insensitive development led to the site being placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2003; it was removed from the danger list in 2009 after improvements in management and preservation. The Icherisheher State Historical-Architectural Reserve Department administers the district.
