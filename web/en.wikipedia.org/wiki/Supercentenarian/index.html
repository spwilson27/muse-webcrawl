Supercentenarian

Supercentenarians have attained an age of 110 years or more.

A supercentenarian is a person who has reached the age of 110 years or more. This age is achieved by roughly one in a thousand centenarians. Supercentenarians typically live a life free of major age-related diseases until very close to the end of their lives.

Incidence

The Gerontology Research Group maintains one of the most widely cited lists of verified supercentenarians. Studies estimate that the maximum reported age at death has plateaued in the vicinity of 115–125 years, though this remains a subject of scientific debate. The best-documented case is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who lived to 122 years and 164 days, the longest verified human lifespan.

Research

Supercentenarians are studied by gerontologists seeking to understand the genetic and lifestyle factors associated with exceptional longevity and the compression of morbidity — the postponement of serious illness until shortly before death. Research includes whole-genome sequencing of supercentenarians and comparisons with younger control populations.

Validation

Because exaggerated age claims are common, ages of supercentenarians are validated against birth records, census data, marriage certificates, and other documents. Organizations such as the Gerontology Research Group and the International Database on Longevity perform this verification work.
