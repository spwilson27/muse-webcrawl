Academic fraud

Academic fraud, also called academic dishonesty or academic misconduct in its institutional sense, refers to deceptive practices in education and scholarship. Forms include plagiarism, cheating on examinations, falsification of credentials or research data, contract cheating through essay mills, and impersonation. Academic fraud is distinguished from honest error by its element of deliberate deception for credit or advantage.

Forms

Plagiarism is presenting another's words, ideas or work as one's own without attribution. Cheating covers unauthorised aids in examinations and collusion on individual work. Contract cheating, in which a student outsources assessed work to a third party, grew substantially with the internet; several jurisdictions, including England and Australia, have criminalised providing or advertising such services. Research fraud — fabrication, falsification or plagiarism in proposing, performing or reporting research — is treated as research misconduct and can lead to retraction of publications and loss of funding.

Causes and prevalence

Surveys of students consistently report high rates of self-admitted minor dishonesty, which researchers attribute to performance pressure, perceived peer norms, heavy workloads and low perceived risk of detection. Honor codes and assessment design — such as varied, staged or oral assessment — are associated with lower rates.

Detection and sanctions

Institutions use text-matching software, invigilation and authorship analysis to detect fraud. Sanctions range from mark penalties to expulsion and degree revocation. At the research level, prominent cases of data fabrication have led to mass retractions and reforms in peer review and data sharing.
