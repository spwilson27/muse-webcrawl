Mercury(II) sulfide

Mercury(II) sulfide
Formula: HgS
Molar mass: 232.66 g/mol
Appearance: red (α) or black (β) solid
Density: 8.10 g/cm³

Mercury(II) sulfide, also known as mercuric sulfide, is a chemical compound composed of mercury and sulfur with the formula HgS. It is virtually insoluble in water. It occurs naturally as the minerals cinnabar (the red α form) and metacinnabar (the black β form), and cinnabar is the principal ore of mercury.

Structure and forms

HgS crystallizes in two main forms. The red α form has a hexagonal structure in which mercury and sulfur form helical chains; it is the structure of the mineral cinnabar. The black β form (metacinnabar) has a zincblende (cubic) structure. When heated, black HgS converts to the red form, and synthetic mercury(II) sulfide was historically prepared by treating mercury salts with hydrogen sulfide, precipitating the black form which reddens on sublimation or prolonged heating.

Uses

The red form of HgS is the pigment vermilion, prized since antiquity for its brilliant scarlet color and used in painting, lacquerware, and cosmetics. Cinnabar has been mined since Neolithic times and was the chief historical source of elemental mercury, obtained by roasting the ore. Mercury(II) sulfide is a semiconductor with applications explored in photoconductive and infrared detection devices.

Safety

Although HgS is far less soluble and less bioavailable than many mercury compounds, it is still toxic, and mercury compounds are environmental hazards. Its use in pigments has largely been discontinued in many countries in favor of cadmium reds and modern synthetic pigments.
