Netscape

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Netscape Navigator interface with its big N logo and ship's wheel icon]

Netscape Communications Corporation was an American software company best known for Netscape Navigator, the dominant web browser of the mid-1990s, and for its spectacular 1995 initial public offering that ignited the dot-com boom.

Founded in April 1994 as Mosaic Communications by Marc Andreessen and Jim Clark (renamed Netscape after licensing disputes), it released Navigator that December. Its IPO on 9 August 1995 — shares priced at $28 opening at $71 — became a cultural symbol of the internet era.

Browser war and fall

Microsoft, seeing the web as an existential platform threat, licensed Mosaic code, released Internet Explorer, and bundled it free with Windows; by the late 1990s IE had overtaken Navigator — conduct central to the United States v. Microsoft antitrust case (1998–2001). Netscape open-sourced its code in 1998, founding the Mozilla project (ancestor of Firefox), and was acquired by AOL in 1999 (announced November 1998, ~$4.2 billion in stock).

The Navigator brand lingered until AOL ended support in 2008. SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) also originated at Netscape, securing e-commerce — a durable legacy beyond the browser itself.

Legacy

Netscape's arc — garage-scale startup to industry earthquake to acquisition in four years — is the archetypal dot-com saga; its people seeded Silicon Valley's next generation (including Andreessen's venture firm a16z, founded 2009).
