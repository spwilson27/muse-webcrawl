Health informatics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Health informatics is the interdisciplinary field concerned with the acquisition, storage, retrieval, exchange and use of information in health and medicine. It combines information science, computer science and health care to improve the quality, safety and efficiency of care — from electronic health records (EHRs) and clinical decision support systems to telemedicine, health data standards and population-health analytics. The term was coined in the 1960s in Europe (French informatique médicale) and the field is also called medical informatics, clinical informatics and biomedical informatics.

Scope and subfields

  • Clinical informatics — informatics applied to direct patient care; in the United States it became a board-certified medical subspecialty in 2013.
  • Nursing, dental, pharmacy and public health informatics — discipline-specific applications, the latter covering disease surveillance and health data at population level.
  • Bioinformatics and imaging informatics — computational management of genomic data and medical images (e.g., PACS archives and DICOM formats).
  • Consumer health informatics — patient portals, personal health records, wearables and health apps.

Standards and interoperability

Because health data must move between organisations and systems, the field depends on standards: HL7 (including the modern FHIR API standard) for messages and data exchange; terminologies such as SNOMED CT, ICD-10/11 and LOINC; and DICOM for imaging. Interoperability, privacy and security — governed by regimes such as HIPAA in the US and GDPR in Europe — are central concerns, as are usability and the unintended consequences of poorly designed systems, including clinician burnout.

Applications

Health informatics underlies computerised physician order entry, e-prescribing, clinical decision support (alerts, guidelines, risk scores), telehealth, remote monitoring, hospital information systems, and data-driven quality improvement. More recently, machine learning and "big data" analytics have been applied to prediction, medical imaging and literature mining, while raising new questions about bias and validation.

History

Early milestones include hospital computing in the 1960s, systems such as COSTAR at Massachusetts General Hospital and HELP at LDS Hospital, the founding of professional bodies such as AMIA and the IMIA, the spread of EHR adoption incentivised by the US HITECH Act (2009), and the mainstreaming of telehealth during the COVID-19 pandemic.
