Hypergeometric series

In mathematics, a hypergeometric series is a power series in which the ratio of successive coefficients is a rational function of the index. The series, if convergent, defines a hypergeometric function, which may then be defined over a wider domain by analytic continuation. A hypergeometric series is usually written pFq, where p and q count the numerator and denominator parameters.

The basic series

The (ordinary or Gaussian) hypergeometric series 2F1(a, b; c; z) is the sum over n of the terms (a)n(b)n / (c)n · zn/n!, where (x)n denotes the rising Pochhammer symbol. It generalises the elementary geometric series, to which the name refers. The corresponding function satisfies the hypergeometric differential equation and includes many familiar functions as special cases: for example (1−z)−a, log(1+z), the inverse trigonometric functions and complete elliptic integrals.

History and generalisations

The series was studied by John Wallis, Leonhard Euler and systematically by Carl Friedrich Gauss, whose 1812 dissertation treated convergence and numerous identities. Generalised hypergeometric series pFq, basic (q-) hypergeometric series, and multivariable analogues such as the Appell and Lauricella series extend the theory into wide areas of analysis, combinatorics and mathematical physics, including solutions of differential equations arising in quantum mechanics.
