Fashion show

A fashion show (French: défilé de mode) is an event in which a fashion designer or house presents a new collection of clothing and accessories, worn by models on a runway (catwalk) before an audience of buyers, press and invited guests.

History

Live presentations of garments on models developed in the Paris couture houses of the nineteenth century; Charles Frederick Worth is often credited with using live models rather than mannequins. In the early twentieth century, department stores in the United States staged regular fashion parades, and by the 1940s organised events such as New York's Press Week (the forerunner of New York Fashion Week) had formalised the seasonal show calendar.

Format

Shows are typically staged twice a year for the spring/summer and autumn/winter seasons, during the fashion weeks of Paris, Milan, New York and London. Models walk a runway in a set sequence of looks, accompanied by music and staging designed to express the collection's theme. Some designers stage elaborate theatrical spectacles; others use intimate salon presentations or digital formats, which expanded during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Types

  • Haute couture shows — one-of-a-kind garments from Paris couture houses.
  • Ready-to-wear (prêt-à-porter) shows — seasonal commercial collections.
  • Resort/cruise and pre-fall shows — inter-seasonal collections, often staged in destination locations.
  • Trade and bridal shows — aimed primarily at retail buyers.
