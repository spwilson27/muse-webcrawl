Working boat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A working boat is a watercraft designed and fitted for a practical occupation rather than for pleasure or combat: fishing, cargo carrying, towing, dredging, pilotage, passenger ferrying, harbour duties or servicing offshore installations. The term covers an enormous range, from open rowing and sailing fishing boats worked by one or two people to industrial factory trawlers, tugboats and offshore supply vessels — most of the world's fleet by number consists of small working boats, particularly artisanal fishing craft.

Types

Working boats are classified by function: fishing vessels (trawlers, seiners, longliners, crabbers), tugs and towboats, ferries, pilot boats, lifeboats, dredgers, barges, lighters, patrol and harbour craft, and specialised vessels for aquaculture, salvage and offshore energy.

Fishing boats

Fishing craft dominate the world's working fleets. The Food and Agriculture Organization estimates several million fishing vessels worldwide, the great majority small, undecked and motorised or sail-powered boats used in artisanal fisheries. Traditional designs — the Scottish fifie, the Cornish lugger, the Arab dhow, the Indonesian pinisi — were refined by local conditions; many survive in heritage fleets. Modern commercial vessels add hydraulics for hauling nets and lines, refrigeration, and electronic fish-finding.

Cargo and service vessels

Inland waterways once depended on working boats such as English narrowboats, Dutch barges and the Rhine fleet for bulk cargo, and canal carrying families lived aboard their boats. Tugs — compact vessels of disproportionate engine power — guide ships in harbour and tow barges at sea. Pilot boats deliver maritime pilots to incoming vessels, and harbour launches run crew, stores and officials.

Design characteristics

Working boats are typically built for robustness, stability under load, ease of maintenance and economical operation, with working decks kept clear for gear, winches and hatches. Deck layout follows the trade: a trawler's stern ramp and gantry, a tug's towing hook set low amidships, a ferry's vehicle deck and ramps. Materials run from traditional timber and riveted steel to aluminium and glass-reinforced plastic.
