The Arc is a 127.5-kilometre (79-mile) river in the department of Savoie in south-eastern France. It is a left tributary of the Isère, which it joins near Aiton. The Arc drains the Maurienne valley, one of the great transalpine valleys of the French Alps, running roughly east–west between the Graian Alps to the north and the Cottian Alps and Dauphiné Alps to the south.

Course

The river rises from glaciers below the Col de l'Iseran near the Italian border, at over 2,700 metres, and flows through Bonneval-sur-Arc, Bessans, Lanslebourg, Modane, Saint-Michel-de-Maurienne, and Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne before descending to its confluence with the Isère in the Combe de Savoie.

Human geography

The Maurienne corridor has been a strategic route between France and Italy since antiquity; today the Arc valley carries the A43 motorway and the Culoz–Modane railway toward the Fréjus tunnels, and the Mont d'Ambin Base Tunnel of the Turin–Lyon high-speed line is under construction from the valley. The river is heavily developed for hydroelectric power, with a chain of dams operated to supply Alpine industry — historically aluminium smelting and ferro-alloy plants concentrated around Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne.

Floods

The Arc is notorious for violent floods, notably in 1957, when a major flood devastated parts of the valley, and it remains subject to debris flows from its steep tributary torrents.
