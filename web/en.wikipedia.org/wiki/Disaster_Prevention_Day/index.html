Disaster Prevention Day

Disaster Prevention Day (防災の日, Bōsai no hi) is a day of remembrance and preparedness observed in Japan on 1 September each year. It commemorates the victims of the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake, which struck the Tokyo–Yokohama region on that date, and serves as an occasion for nationwide disaster-preparedness drills.

History

The Great Kantō earthquake of 1 September 1923, with a magnitude of about 7.9, devastated Tokyo and Yokohama; fires following the quake killed well over 100,000 people. Disaster Prevention Day was established by the Japanese government in 1960 (Shōwa 35), partly in response to the destructive Isewan Typhoon of September 1959, to raise public awareness of natural hazards such as earthquakes, typhoons, and tsunamis. A broader Disaster Prevention Week runs from 30 August to 5 September.

Observance

Each year on or around 1 September, the national government conducts large-scale exercises, often involving the Prime Minister and cabinet acting out an emergency-response scenario based on a hypothetical major earthquake. Prefectural and municipal governments, schools, businesses, and neighbourhood associations hold evacuation drills, fire-fighting practice, and emergency-ration and shelter exercises. Television and radio stations broadcast preparedness programming, and the day marks the start of heightened public messaging about household emergency kits and evacuation routes.
