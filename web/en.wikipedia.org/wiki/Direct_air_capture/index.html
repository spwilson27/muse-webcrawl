Direct air capture

Direct air capture (DAC)
Type: Carbon removal technology
Captures: CO₂ from ambient air
Notable plants: Orca and Mammoth, Iceland (Climeworks)
Related: Carbon capture and storage, Carbon dioxide removal

Direct air capture (DAC) is a technology that uses chemical processes to extract carbon dioxide directly from the ambient air. The captured CO₂ can be permanently stored underground or used in products.

How it works

Large fans draw air over sorbents — liquid solvents or solid filters — that bind CO₂. Heat or pressure changes then release a concentrated stream for storage, regenerating the sorbent. Because air is only about 0.04% CO₂, DAC is more energy-intensive than capture from flue gas.

Deployment

Climeworks opened the Orca plant in Iceland in 2021 and the larger Mammoth plant in 2024, storing CO₂ mineralized by Carbfix. Projects in the United States, supported by the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act and Department of Energy hubs, aim for million-tonne scale, and firms such as 1PointFive are building large facilities in Texas.

Costs and debate

Costs remain high — often estimated at several hundred dollars per tonne — and critics question energy use and scalability, while supporters see DAC as a durable, measurable complement to emissions cuts.
