Grimethorpe Colliery Band

The Grimethorpe Colliery Band is a brass band from Grimethorpe in South Yorkshire, England, founded in 1917. Regarded as one of the finest brass bands in the world, it achieved its greatest successes under conductor Elgar Howarth in the 1970s and became widely known through the 1996 film Brassed Off, which was loosely based on the band's story.

History

The band grew out of a wartime concert group attached to Grimethorpe Colliery and the local Cudworth Prize Band. Rising through the contesting sections, Grimethorpe won the British Open in 1967 and then enjoyed a golden era under Howarth, winning the National Championships in 1977, 1978 and back-to-back titles thereafter, the British Open again, and completing an unprecedented "grand slam." The band commissioned and premiered major new works and toured internationally, including performances at the Sydney Opera House and Carnegie Hall.

Brassed Off and after

In 1992 Grimethorpe Colliery closed, and the pit's destruction became the backdrop to Mark Herman's film Brassed Off (1996), starring Pete Postlethwaite and Ewan McGregor, in which the band played itself in the climactic Albert Hall scenes. The film brought the band global recognition. Grimethorpe continues to contest at the highest level, won the National Championships again in 2006 and 2022 under the name Grimethorpe Colliery Band, and remains a symbol of northern working-class musical culture.
