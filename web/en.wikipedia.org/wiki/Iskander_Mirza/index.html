Iskander Mirza

Iskander Ali Mirza
1st President of Pakistan
In office: 23 March 1956 – 27 October 1958
4th Governor-General of Pakistan: 1955–1956
Born: 13 November 1899, Murshidabad, British India
Died: 13 November 1969 (aged 70), London, England

Iskander Ali Mirza (13 November 1899 – 13 November 1969) was a Pakistani politician and military officer who served as the last Governor-General of Pakistan (1955–1956) and its first President, from 1956 until he was deposed in a coup d'état in 1958. A former officer of the British Indian Army and civil servant, he was a key figure in the politics of Pakistan's first decade.

Career

Mirza was educated at Elphinstone College, Bombay, and the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, serving in the British Indian Army before transferring to the Indian Political Service. After Partition he became Pakistan's first Defence Secretary, playing a role in the war over Kashmir, and later served as Governor of East Bengal and as Minister of the Interior.

Presidency and fall

Appointed Governor-General in 1955, Mirza became President when Pakistan became a republic under the 1956 constitution. His presidency was marked by political instability, with four prime ministers in two years, and his own frequent interventions, including the creation of the Republican Party. On 7 October 1958, fearing the incoming elections, Mirza abrogated the constitution and imposed martial law, appointing army chief Ayub Khan as Chief Martial Law Administrator. Within three weeks, on 27 October 1958, Ayub Khan forced Mirza to resign and exiled him to London, where he died in 1969. He was buried in Tehran, Iran.
