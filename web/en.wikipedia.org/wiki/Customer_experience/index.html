Customer experience

Customer experience (CX) is a customer's perception of, and response to, their interactions with a company or brand across the entire relationship, from initial awareness through purchase, use, support, and advocacy. It encompasses every touchpoint — advertising, websites, retail stores, products, customer service, and billing — and the cumulative impression those touchpoints create.

The discipline of customer experience management emerged as businesses recognised that competing on product features and price alone was insufficient, and that the quality of the overall journey increasingly determined loyalty and lifetime value.

Definition and scope

Customer experience is distinct from customer satisfaction, which measures how well a specific transaction or product met expectations. Experience is broader and more holistic: it is the sum of cognitive, emotional, sensory, and behavioural responses a customer has during all stages of consumption. Researchers commonly divide CX into dimensions such as ease, effectiveness, and emotion.

Customer journey

Organisations map the customer journey to understand the sequence of interactions a customer has with a brand. Journey mapping identifies moments of truth — interactions that disproportionately shape perception — and pain points where friction, delay, or inconsistency damages the experience. Journey maps are typically organised by persona and stage: awareness, consideration, purchase, onboarding, usage, support, and renewal or advocacy.

Measurement

Common CX metrics include the Net Promoter Score (NPS), which asks how likely a customer is to recommend the company; Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT); and Customer Effort Score (CES), which measures how easy an interaction was. These survey measures are increasingly combined with behavioural data such as repeat purchase rates, churn, and operational metrics like wait times and first-contact resolution.

Management

Customer experience management (CEM or CXM) is the practice of designing and reacting to customer interactions to meet or exceed expectations. It involves cross-functional coordination because experiences cut across departmental boundaries, investment in employee experience, and closed-loop processes in which feedback from individual customers triggers follow-up action. Digital transformation has made omnichannel consistency — a seamless experience across web, mobile, social, and physical channels — a central concern.

History

The concept gained prominence in the late 1990s and 2000s. B. Joseph Pine II and James H. Gilmore's 1998 work on the "experience economy" argued that experiences are a distinct economic offering. Consultancies and software vendors subsequently built practices and platforms around journey mapping, voice-of-the-customer programmes, and experience analytics.
