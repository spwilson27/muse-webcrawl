Canon EF lens mount

The Canon EF lens mount is the standard lens mount on the Canon EOS family of 35 mm and digital single-lens reflex cameras. Introduced in March 1987 with the Canon EOS 650, "EF" stands for "Electro-Focus": all communication between camera and lens takes place through electrical contacts, and focusing and aperture control are driven by motors inside the lens itself.

Design

The EF mount abandoned every mechanical linkage between body and lens. Autofocus is driven by motors built into each lens — over the years including ring-type and micro ultrasonic motors (USM), stepping motors (STM), and the Nano USM — while the aperture diaphragm is stopped down electrically. The mount's large 54 mm throat and short 44 mm flange distance were designed to accommodate wide-aperture lenses and future developments.

Variants

The EF-S mount, introduced in 2003 with the EOS 300D, serves Canon's APS-C DSLRs; EF-S lenses project a smaller image circle and protrude further into the mirror box, so they cannot be mounted on full-frame bodies. The EF-M mount (2012) served the EOS M mirrorless line and is incompatible with EF lenses except via adapter. EF lenses can be used on Canon's RF-mount mirrorless cameras with full functionality through Canon's mount adapters.

Lenses

Canon produced well over 100 EF lens models, and cumulative EF lens production passed 100 million units in 2014 and 150 million in 2021. Third-party makers including Sigma, Tamron, and Tokina also produced EF-mount lenses. Notable lenses include the EF 50mm f/1.8 "nifty fifty", the L-series professional zooms and telephotos, tilt-shift TS-E lenses, and the EF 1200mm f/5.6L, among the longest autofocus lenses ever sold.

With Canon's shift to the RF mount after 2018, development of new EF lenses wound down, ending a production run of more than three decades.
