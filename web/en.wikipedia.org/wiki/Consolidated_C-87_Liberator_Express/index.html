The C-87 was the transport version of the B-24 Liberator bomber, stripped of armament and fitted for cargo and passengers.

The Consolidated C-87 Liberator Express was a transport derivative of the B-24 Liberator heavy bomber built for the United States Army Air Forces during the Second World War. A total of 287 C-87s were produced alongside the B-24 at Consolidated's Fort Worth plant and used for long-range, high-altitude transport missions, particularly across the Atlantic and on the "Hump" airlift route from India to China.

Design

The C-87 deleted the bomber's turrets and bomb bay, installing a cargo floor, a hinged loading door in the nose, and seating or litters in the former bomb bay and rear fuselage. It could carry about 20–25 passengers in the basic freighter configuration or roughly 12,000 lb (5,400 kg) of cargo, with a range well over 3,000 miles. The C-87A was a dedicated VIP version with airline-style seating for about 16 passengers and sleeping berths; a small number served high-ranking officials. The US Navy designation was RY.

Service

C-87s flew for Air Transport Command and contract carriers on strategic routes worldwide. The type was unpopular with some crews: its fuel system and cargo loading gave it a reputation for poor handling when fully loaded and for icing difficulties on the Hump, and Winston Churchill's personal aircraft, a modified Liberator, was a related VIP conversion ("Commando"). After the war, surplus C-87s passed to airlines and cargo operators, including service in Latin America; Mexico's air force also used the type. None remain in military service, though several B-24s survive in the transport configuration in museums.
