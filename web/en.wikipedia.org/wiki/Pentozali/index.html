Pentozali

The pentozali (Greek: πεντοζάλι) is a traditional folk dance from the island of Crete, Greece. Its name derives from pente ("five") and zala ("steps"), referring to its characteristic five-step pattern, and is also popularly associated with the idea of the "fifth dash" of the brave.

The dance is performed in an open circle, with dancers holding each other by the shoulders or hands, led by the first dancer, who may improvise acrobatic figures and turns. It begins slowly and gradually accelerates to a fast, lively tempo, driven by the Cretan lyra and laouto. Legend connects the dance to the era of the Daskalogiannis revolt against Ottoman rule in 1770, when it is said to have been danced by Cretan fighters as a war dance.

Along with the syrtos and sousta, the pentozali is one of the signature dances performed at Cretan festivals (glentia) and weddings.
