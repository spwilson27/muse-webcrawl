Market (place)

A market, or marketplace, is a location where people regularly gather for the purchase and sale of provisions, livestock, and other goods. A market is a group of buyers and sellers of a particular good or service, but in its physical sense it refers to the place itself: a square, street, or hall where traders set out their wares on stalls or on the ground.

Markets are among the oldest forms of human exchange, arising wherever surpluses of food or craft goods made trade worthwhile. In many parts of the world a weekly market day remains a central social and economic event for a town and its surrounding villages.

History

Historians believe marketplaces emerged in ancient times wherever routes crossed or where temples, ports, and administrative centres drew crowds. The Greek agora and the Roman forum combined commerce with civic life. In medieval Europe, markets required a charter from a lord or monarch, and market crosses marked the spot where trade was licensed. Weekly markets and annual fairs structured the rural calendar for centuries.

Types

Markets vary widely: farmers' markets sell fresh produce directly from growers; flea markets trade second-hand goods; fish markets, livestock markets, and wholesale markets serve particular trades; and covered market halls house permanent stalls. Street markets remain common across Asia, Africa, Latin America, and southern Europe, where they supply fresh food at low prices and support small traders.

Regulation

Public markets are usually regulated by local authorities, which allocate pitches, set market days, and enforce weights, measures, and hygiene standards. Historically, officials such as the clerk of the market supervised fair dealing and punished fraud.
