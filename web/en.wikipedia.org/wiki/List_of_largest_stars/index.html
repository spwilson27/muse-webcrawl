List of largest stars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The following is a list of largest stars currently known, ordered by radius in units of the Sun's radius (R☉). The largest stars are red supergiants and red hypergiants, stars near the end of their lives that have expanded to enormous sizes. Their measured radii are uncertain: the surfaces of such stars are ill-defined, their sizes vary with wavelength, many are pulsating variables, and distance uncertainties translate directly into radius uncertainties. As a result, rankings change as measurements improve, and stars listed here with radii above roughly 1,500 R☉ should be treated as approximate.

Measurement caveats

Stellar radii for the largest stars are obtained by combining an angular diameter — measured by interferometry or lunar occultation — with a distance, usually from parallax. For red supergiants embedded in circumstellar dust, both quantities are problematic. Gaia parallaxes for such bright, extended, variable stars can be unreliable, and the "surface" of a red supergiant is a diffuse layer whose position depends on the wavelength observed. Pulsations can change a star's apparent size by tens of percent over months. Consequently, different reputable studies may rank the same stars in different orders, and this list reflects approximate consensus values rather than precise measurements.

Physical limits

There is a theoretical upper limit to the size of a star. Above radii of roughly 1,500–2,000 R☉, a cool supergiant would exceed the Hayashi limit for hydrostatic equilibrium, and radiation pressure and pulsation-driven mass loss are expected to strip the envelope. The largest observed stars cluster near this bound, which may explain why no star is unambiguously known to be much larger than about 2,000 R☉.
