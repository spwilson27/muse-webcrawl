Abelian category

In mathematics, an abelian category is a category in which morphisms and objects can be added and in which kernels and cokernels exist and have desirable properties. The motivating prototype is the category of abelian groups, and the theory was formalised by Alexander Grothendieck in his 1957 "Tôhoku paper" to axiomatise the settings of homological algebra.

Definition

A category is abelian if it is preadditive (morphisms form abelian groups and composition is bilinear), has a zero object, has all binary products and coproducts, has all kernels and cokernels, and every monomorphism is a kernel and every epimorphism is a cokernel. These axioms guarantee that notions such as exact sequences, images, and quotients behave as they do for modules.

Examples

Principal examples include the category of abelian groups, categories of modules over a ring, categories of vector spaces, and categories of sheaves of abelian groups on a topological space. The category of finitely generated abelian groups is abelian, whereas the category of all groups is not (it lacks additive structure on morphisms).

Properties and theorems

Abelian categories support diagram chasing: the snake lemma, the five lemma, and the nine lemma hold. The Freyd–Mitchell embedding theorem states that every small abelian category embeds fully, faithfully, and exactly into a category of modules over some ring, justifying element-style arguments. Abelian categories are the natural home of derived functors and derived categories in modern algebraic geometry and representation theory.
