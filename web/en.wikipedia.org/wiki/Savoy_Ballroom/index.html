The Savoy Ballroom was a large ballroom for music and public dancing on Lenox Avenue between 140th and 141st Streets in the Harlem neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City. Opened in 1926 by Moe Gale, it occupied the second floor of a block-long building and was famous as the home of the Lindy Hop and the heart of Harlem's swing era.

History

The Savoy was one of the first racially integrated public places in the United States, with a non-discrimination policy unusual for its time. Its sprung wooden dance floor drew thousands of dancers. Manager Charles Buchanan booked two house bands that alternated on twin bandstands, allowing continuous music; the celebrated "battles of the bands" pitted visiting orchestras against the house band. Chick Webb's orchestra was the Savoy's most famous house band, with the young Ella Fitzgerald as vocalist; their 1937 contest against the Benny Goodman Orchestra is legendary.

Lindy Hop

The Savoy's best dancers, including Frankie Manning and the troupe Whitey's Lindy Hoppers, developed and showcased the Lindy Hop there, introducing aerial steps ("air steps") in a reserved corner of the floor nicknamed the "Cat's Corner". The dance took its name from Charles Lindbergh's 1927 transatlantic flight ("Lindy hops the Atlantic").

Closure

The ballroom closed in 1958 and the building was demolished in 1959 for a housing project. A commemorative plaque now marks the site on Lenox Avenue.
