European emission standards

The European emission standards are vehicle emission standards for exhaust emissions of new vehicles sold in the European Union, European Economic Area, and the United Kingdom. The standards define acceptable limits for carbon monoxide (CO), hydrocarbons, nitrogen oxides (NOx), and particulate matter from cars, vans, trucks, and buses, and have been progressively tightened in stages known as Euro 1 through Euro 6 for light-duty vehicles and Euro I through Euro VI for heavy-duty vehicles.

History

The first standard, Euro 1, took effect in 1992 and effectively required catalytic converters on petrol cars. Successive stages in 1996, 2000, 2005, and 2009 (Euro 5) cut diesel NOx and particulate limits, forcing the adoption of diesel particulate filters. Euro 6, applying from 2014–2015, sharply reduced diesel NOx limits.

The 2015 Volkswagen emissions scandal revealed large gaps between laboratory results and real-world emissions, prompting the introduction of Real Driving Emissions (RDE) testing with portable equipment and the Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicles Test Procedure (WLTP). A Euro 7 regulation, adopted in 2024, extends regulation to brake and tyre wear particles and sets durability requirements for electric vehicle batteries.

CO2 standards

Separately from pollutant standards, EU law sets fleet-average CO2 targets for manufacturers, reaching a 100% reduction target for new cars and vans by 2035.
