Visual art

The visual arts are art forms such as painting, drawing, printmaking, sculpture, ceramics, photography, video, filmmaking, design, crafts and architecture. Many artistic disciplines — performing arts, conceptual art, and textile arts — also involve aspects of the visual arts as well as arts of other types. Also included within the visual arts are the applied arts, such as industrial design, graphic design, fashion design, interior design and decorative art.

History

Visual art is as old as humanity: Upper Paleolithic cave paintings, such as those at Chauvet and Lascaux (roughly 40,000–15,000 years old), and carved figurines such as the Venus of Hohle Fels, testify to an early impulse to make images and objects. Civilisations across Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, India, the Islamic world, Africa, Oceania and the pre-Columbian Americas each developed rich visual traditions in sculpture, painting, textiles, pottery and metalwork, often serving religious and political purposes.

Education and training

Historically, artists trained through apprenticeship and workshop practice; the Italian Renaissance saw the founding of art academies that elevated painting and sculpture from craft to liberal art. In the modern era, art education takes place in art schools and university programmes as well as in informal and community settings. Drawing is traditionally regarded as the foundational skill, and training typically includes life drawing, composition, colour theory and art history.

Media and forms

  • Painting — pigment applied to a surface; media include oil, acrylic, watercolour, fresco, ink and encaustic.
  • Drawing — marks made with pencil, charcoal, chalk or ink.
  • Printmaking — images transferred from a matrix: woodcut, engraving, etching, lithography, screen printing.
  • Sculpture — three-dimensional work in stone, metal, wood, clay and modern materials, by carving, modelling, casting or assemblage.
  • Photography and film — lens-based media from the 19th century onward.
  • Architecture — the art and technique of designing buildings and structures.
  • New media — video art, computer art, installation and digital forms from the late 20th century onward.
