God complex


The God complex describes a conviction of personal infallibility and omnipotence.

A God complex is an unshakable belief characterised by consistently inflated feelings of personal ability, privilege or infallibility. A person with a God complex may refuse to admit the possibility of error or failure, even in the face of complex or intractable problems, and regards their personal opinions as unquestionably correct. The term is a colloquial and journalistic one rather than a formal psychiatric diagnosis.

Description

The individual may demand to be treated as an authority beyond challenge, dismiss the views and expertise of others, and show little empathy for the consequences of their decisions. Such behaviour overlaps with traits discussed in clinical psychology under narcissistic personality disorder — grandiosity, a need for admiration, and lack of empathy — and with the concept of hubris in leadership research, where "hubris syndrome" has been proposed for the exaggerated pride and overconfidence sometimes acquired by people in positions of great power. The related concept of the messiah complex describes a belief that one is destined to save others.

Usage

The phrase has been used since the early twentieth century in discussions of psychoanalysis and has entered popular usage in medicine, politics and business, often to describe surgeons, executives or heads of government perceived as authoritarian or resistant to criticism. In psychiatry proper, grandiose delusions — fixed beliefs of god-like status or divine identity — are a recognised symptom of conditions such as schizophrenia and bipolar mania, and differ from the colloquial God complex, which refers to a personality style rather than a psychotic belief.
