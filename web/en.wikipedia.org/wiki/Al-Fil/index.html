Al-Fil

Al-Fil (Arabic: الفيل, "The Elephant") is the 105th chapter (surah) of the Quran. It is a short Meccan surah of five verses (ayat) that alludes to the divine protection of the Kaaba at Mecca against an invading army with elephants.

Content

The surah asks: "Have you not considered how your Lord dealt with the companions of the elephant? Did He not make their plot go astray? And He sent against them birds in flocks, striking them with stones of baked clay, and made them like eaten straw." The text presumes the audience's familiarity with the story rather than narrating it in detail.

Historical background

According to Islamic tradition, the allusion is to the expedition of Abraha, the Aksumite viceroy of Yemen, who marched on Mecca with a force that included war elephants around 570 CE — the "Year of the Elephant", traditionally the year of the Prophet Muhammad's birth. The army was destroyed before reaching the city, by disease or, in tradition, by flocks of birds (the ababil) dropping stones. Exegetes connected the "stones of baked clay" with an outbreak of smallpox or similar pestilence; the sanctuary's preservation was read as a sign of God's protection of the Kaaba.
