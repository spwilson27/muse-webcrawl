Centre Point

Centre Point
Location: 101–103 New Oxford Street, London
Height: 117 m (385 ft)
Floors: 34
Completed: 1966
Architect: R. Seifert and Partners

Centre Point is a Grade II listed concrete and glass office building in central London, England, at the junction of Oxford Street and Tottenham Court Road, above Tottenham Court Road station. It stands 117 metres (385 ft) high with 34 floors, and was designed by R. Seifert and Partners for the developer Harry Hyams, being built between 1963 and 1966 by Wimpey Construction.

Controversy

The building became notorious in the 1960s and 1970s as a symbol of property speculation: it stood largely empty for years after completion because Hyams preferred to let the whole building to a single tenant and to wait for rents to rise, while its capital value appreciated. During the housing crisis of the 1970s the empty tower was occupied by housing campaigners in 1974, and it gave rise to the expression "Centre Point" being used by the homeless charity founded in 1969, which took its name from the building and works with young homeless people in London.

Later use

The building was eventually let to the Confederation of British Industry, which had its headquarters there for many years, and it was given Grade II listed status in 1995. In the 2010s, under owners Almacantar, Centre Point was converted from offices to luxury residential apartments, with new retail and restaurant space at its base; the conversion formed part of the regeneration around the Elizabeth line station at Tottenham Court Road.

Architecture

Centre Point is notable for its prefabricated concrete lattice façade, assembled without scaffolding, an early example of the technique. Its sculptural grid of white concrete gives the slim tower its distinctive honeycomb appearance, one of the landmarks of 1960s London architecture.
