Permutation matrix

In mathematics, particularly in matrix theory, a permutation matrix is a square binary matrix that has exactly one entry of 1 in each row and each column with all other entries 0. Such a matrix, say P, represents a permutation of m elements and, when used to multiply another matrix, results in permuting the rows (when pre-multiplying) or columns (when post-multiplying) of the other matrix.

Properties

  • Every permutation matrix is an orthogonal matrix: its inverse equals its transpose, and the inverse corresponds to the inverse permutation.
  • The product of two permutation matrices is a permutation matrix; together with the identity matrix they form a group isomorphic to the symmetric group.
  • The determinant of a permutation matrix equals the sign of the corresponding permutation, either +1 or −1.
  • A matrix is a permutation matrix if and only if it is a doubly stochastic matrix with only integer entries; the permutation matrices are the extreme points of the set of doubly stochastic matrices (the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem).

Application

Multiplying a vector by a permutation matrix reorders its components. Permutation matrices appear in sorting networks, combinatorial optimisation (as in the assignment problem), and as the matrices used in partial pivoting during Gaussian elimination.
