In mathematics, differential forms provide a unified approach to define integrands over curves, surfaces, and higher-dimensional manifolds. The modern notion of differential forms was pioneered by Élie Cartan. It has many applications, especially in geometry, topology, and physics.

The concept of a differential form may be thought of as a generalisation of the functions, derivatives, and integrals familiar from calculus. A differential 0-form is a smooth function; a 1-form in coordinates can be written as a sum f1 dx1 + … + fn dxn; and higher forms are built from these using the antisymmetric wedge product.

Definition

Formally, a differential k-form on a smooth manifold is a smooth section of the k-th exterior power of the cotangent bundle. At each point, a k-form assigns an alternating multilinear map on k tangent vectors, varying smoothly from point to point. The set of all k-forms is denoted Ωk(M).

Operations

Exterior derivative

The exterior derivative d maps k-forms to (k+1)-forms and generalises the gradient, curl, and divergence of vector calculus. It satisfies d2 = 0, a fact equivalent to the equality of mixed partial derivatives and to the vanishing of the curl of a gradient and the divergence of a curl.

Integration and Stokes' theorem

A k-form can be integrated over a k-dimensional oriented submanifold. The generalized Stokes theorem states that the integral of dω over a manifold equals the integral of ω over its boundary, subsuming the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, the divergence theorem, and the classical Stokes theorem.

Applications

Differential forms give an coordinate-free formulation of Maxwell's equations in electromagnetism, underpin de Rham cohomology in algebraic topology, and appear throughout general relativity and gauge theory. The closed and exact forms distinguished by de Rham theory measure topological features of manifolds.
