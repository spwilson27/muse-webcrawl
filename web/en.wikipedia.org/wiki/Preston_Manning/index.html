Preston Manning

Ernest Preston Manning (born June 10, 1942) is a retired Canadian politician. He was the founder and only leader of the Reform Party of Canada, a Canadian federal political party that evolved into the Canadian Alliance, which in turn merged with the Progressive Conservative Party to form today's Conservative Party of Canada. Manning represented Calgary Southwest in the House of Commons from 1993 to 2002 and served as Leader of the Official Opposition from 1997 to 2000.

Early life

Manning was born in Edmonton, Alberta, the son of Ernest Manning, Alberta's Social Credit premier from 1943 to 1968. He studied physics at the University of Alberta and later worked as a management consultant and researcher in Calgary, co-authoring work with his father on political realignment.

Reform Party

Dissatisfied with western alienation and the policies of the Mulroney government, Manning founded the Reform Party in 1987 under the slogan "The West Wants In". The party broke through in the 1993 federal election, winning 52 seats, and in 1997 became the Official Opposition. Manning sought to broaden the party into a national alternative through the "United Alternative" process, which produced the Canadian Alliance in 2000; in the ensuing leadership race he lost to Stockwell Day.

Later life

Manning retired from Parliament in 2002 and founded the Manning Centre for Building Democracy, a conservative training and networking organisation. He was made a Companion of the Order of Canada in 2007.
