Gus Green Van Sant Jr. (born 24 July 1952) is an American filmmaker, photographer, painter and musician. He has earned acclaim as an independent filmmaker, with films ranging from art-house dramas to mainstream successes, and is regarded as one of the most prominent auteurs of New Queer Cinema.

Van Sant in 2015

Career

Van Sant first gained recognition with Drugstore Cowboy (1989) and My Own Private Idaho (1991), the latter starring River Phoenix and Keanu Reeves. He achieved mainstream success with Good Will Hunting (1997), which earned nine Academy Award nominations including Best Director, and Finding Forrester (2000). He won the Palme d'Or at Cannes for Elephant (2003), a minimalist depiction of a school shooting, and received further acclaim for Milk (2008), the biographical film about Harvey Milk that brought him a second Best Director nomination.

His other works include To Die For (1995), a shot-for-shot remake of Psycho (1998), Last Days (2005), Paranoid Park (2007), Promised Land (2012) and Don't Worry, He Won't Get Far on Foot (2018). Van Sant lives in Portland, Oregon, where many of his films are set.
