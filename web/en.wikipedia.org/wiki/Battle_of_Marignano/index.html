The Battle of Marignano was fought between France and the Old Swiss Confederacy on 13 and 14 September 1515 near the town today called Melegnano, 16 km southeast of Milan. The young King Francis I, in his first great battle, defeated the Swiss — then considered the finest infantry in Europe — winning the Duchy of Milan and prompting the veteran marshal Trivulzio, who had fought in eighteen battles, to call it "the battle of the giants" beside which all others were children's games. The battle ended the Swiss Confederacy's career as an expansionist great power in Italy.

Background

The Swiss, virtual masters of the Duchy of Milan under their protégé Massimiliano Sforza, dominated the Alpine passes. Francis I, newly crowned, led his army across the Alps by the unexpected Col de Larche, surprising the Swiss at Villafranca, then advanced on Milan. Attempts to buy the Swiss off split their cantons: some contingents took French gold and went home, but the war party, led by Cardinal Matthäus Schiner, carried the rest into battle.

The battle

On the afternoon of 13 September the Swiss columns attacked in their customary pike squares, aiming to overrun the French artillery — some seventy guns — before it could dominate. The fighting raged around the guns and the French landsknecht infantry until darkness (and a moonlit continuation) separated the armies amid heaps of dead. Francis spent the night in the saddle among his men. At dawn on the 14th the Swiss renewed the assault; the outcome hung in the balance until the Venetian light cavalry of Bartolomeo d'Alviano arrived with shouts of "San Marco!", threatening the Swiss rear. The Swiss then retreated in good order, leaving their dead — perhaps a third of their army — on the field.

Aftermath

Francis entered Milan; the 1516 "Perpetual Peace" of Fribourg between France and the Swiss endured until the French Revolution and channelled Swiss soldiers into French service as honoured mercenaries rather than enemies. Marignano is remembered as the last great battle of Swiss expansion and a landmark in the rise of artillery and combined arms.
