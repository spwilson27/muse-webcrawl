Ground plane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In electrical engineering, a ground plane is an electrically conductive surface, usually connected to electrical ground. The term has two different meanings in separate areas of electrical engineering.

  • In antenna theory, a ground plane is a conducting surface large in comparison to the wavelength, such as the Earth, which is connected to the transmitter's ground wire and serves as a reflecting surface for radio waves.
  • In printed circuit board (PCB) design, a ground plane is a large area of copper foil on the board which is connected to the power supply ground terminal and serves as a return path for current from many different components.

Radio antenna theory

In telecommunications, a ground plane is a flat or nearly flat horizontal conducting surface that serves as part of an antenna structure, to reflect radio waves from the other antenna elements. A monopole antenna, for example, uses a ground plane as the "missing half" of a dipole: the plane creates an electrical image of the single radiating element, so the combination radiates like a dipole in the space above the plane. For good reflection the plane should extend at least a quarter-wavelength around the base of the antenna; where a natural earth ground is a poor conductor, artificial ground planes or sets of radial wires are used, as in the classic ground-plane antenna with drooping radials.

Printed circuits

On a multilayer PCB, one entire layer is often devoted to a ground plane. A solid copper plane lowers the impedance of return paths, reduces noise and crosstalk, provides shielding against electromagnetic interference, and helps carry heat away from components. Variants include split planes for separating analogue and digital grounds and hatched (cross-hatched) planes used on flexible circuits.
