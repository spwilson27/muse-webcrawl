Supercompact cardinal

In set theory, a supercompact cardinal is a type of large cardinal. A cardinal number κ is λ-supercompact for a cardinal λ ≥ κ if there exists an elementary embedding j : V → M with critical point κ such that j(κ) > λ and M is closed under λ-sequences. The cardinal κ is supercompact if it is λ-supercompact for every λ.

Properties

Supercompact cardinals were introduced by Robert Solovay and William Reinhardt in the early 1970s. If κ is supercompact, then the set of cardinals below κ that are measurable, strongly compact, and otherwise large is large, and the existence of a supercompact cardinal implies the consistency of many other large cardinal notions. Under the same hypothesis, Vopěnka's principle is equivalent to the existence of supercompact cardinals of arbitrarily high strength.

Ultrafilter characterization

Equivalently, κ is λ-supercompact if and only if there is a fine, normal ultrafilter on the set of subsets of λ of cardinality less than κ, allowing supercompactness to be phrased in the language of measures rather than embeddings.
