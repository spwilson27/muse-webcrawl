Narratology is the study of narrative and narrative structure, and the ways that these affect human perception. It is a branch of literary theory that examines how narratives are constructed, how they convey meaning, and how readers and audiences make sense of stories. The term was popularised by the Bulgarian-French historian and philosopher Tzvetan Todorov in his 1969 work Grammaire du Décaméron.

History

The roots of narratology reach back to Aristotle's Poetics, which analysed tragedy in terms of plot (mythos), character, and catharsis. In the twentieth century, the Russian formalists, and in particular Vladimir Propp's Morphology of the Folktale (1928), sought a systematic grammar of narrative. Propp identified thirty-one narrative functions and seven character roles that he claimed underlay all Russian wonder tales. French structuralists such as Claude Lévi-Strauss, Algirdas Julien Greimas, and Roland Barthes extended this structural approach to myth and narrative in general.

Gérard Genette's Narrative Discourse (1972) became the most influential single work of classical narratology, introducing precise terminology for the relations between story, narrative, and narrating. Later "postclassical" narratologies broadened the field to include feminist, cognitive, rhetorical, and transmedial approaches.

Key concepts

  • Story and discourse — the distinction between the events narrated (story, or fabula) and the manner of their telling (discourse, or syuzhet).
  • Focalization — Genette's term for the perspective through which a narrative is perceived: zero, internal, or external focalization.
  • Narrator types — homodiegetic narrators participate in the story they tell; heterodiegetic narrators stand outside it.
  • Order, duration, and frequency — Genette's categories for temporal relations between story and narrative, including anachronies such as flashbacks (analepsis) and flashforwards (prolepsis).
  • Actants — Greimas's model of six narrative roles (subject, object, sender, receiver, helper, opponent).

Postclassical narratology

From the 1980s onward, narratologists such as Mieke Bal, Shlomith Rimmon-Kenan, and Monika Fludernik revised and expanded the classical structuralist framework. Cognitive narratology studies how readers construct mental models of storyworlds; transmedial narratology applies narrative analysis to film, comics, video games, and other media. David Herman has characterised narratology as part of a broader interdisciplinary "narrative turn" across the humanities and social sciences.
