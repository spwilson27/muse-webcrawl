Alexei Ivanovich Rykov (25 February 1881 – 15 March 1938) was a Russian revolutionary and Soviet politician who succeeded Vladimir Lenin as head of the Soviet government, serving as Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars from 1924 to 1930, before being destroyed in the Great Purge.

Revolutionary career

Born to a peasant family in Saratov, Rykov joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1898 and sided with the Bolsheviks after the 1903 split. Repeatedly arrested and exiled under the Tsar, he was an underground organiser in Moscow and St Petersburg. In 1917 he became People's Commissar for Internal Affairs in the first Soviet government but resigned within days over his support for a broad coalition socialist government, a position he later recanted. He went on to head the Supreme Council of the National Economy (Vesenkha) and served as a deputy chairman under Lenin.

Premier and the Right Opposition

On Lenin's death in January 1924 Rykov became premier of the USSR (and simultaneously of the Russian SFSR). A supporter of the New Economic Policy, he aligned with Nikolai Bukharin and Mikhail Tomsky — the "Right Opposition" — against first Trotsky and then, from 1928, against Stalin's turn to forced collectivisation and breakneck industrialisation. Defeated at the party plenums of 1929, Rykov was removed as premier in December 1930 and demoted to People's Commissar for Communications.

Arrest and execution

Expelled from the party in 1936 and arrested in February 1937, Rykov was a principal defendant, alongside Bukharin, at the Trial of the Twenty-One in March 1938 — the third and last of the great Moscow show trials. Charged with espionage, sabotage and plotting to murder Stalin, he confessed under duress and was shot on 15 March 1938. He was rehabilitated by the Soviet Supreme Court in 1988.
