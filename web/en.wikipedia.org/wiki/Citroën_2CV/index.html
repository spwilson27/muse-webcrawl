Citroën 2CV

Citroën 2CV

Manufacturer: Citroën
Production: 1948–1990
Designer: Flaminio Bertoni; André Lefèbvre; Paul Magès
Class: Economy car

The Citroën 2CV (French: deux chevaux, pronounced [dø ʃəvo], lit. 'two horses', meaning 'two taxable horsepower') is an economy car produced by French manufacturer Citroën from 1948 to 1990. Introduced at the 1948 Paris Salon de l'Automobile, it has an air-cooled front engine, front-wheel drive configuration, with a two-cylinder engine, a distinctive partly canvas roof, and an extremely soft long-travel suspension.

It was designed to motorise the large number of farmers in France who were still using horses and carts in the 1930s. The design brief required a car able to carry four people and 50 kg of farm goods at 50 km/h across a ploughed field — famously, carrying a basket of eggs without breaking them — while using little fuel. Between 1948 and 1990, more than 3.8 million 2CVs were produced, making it the world's first mass-produced front-wheel-drive car and one of the longest production runs of any single design.

History

The TPV (Toute Petite Voiture, 'very small car') project began in 1936 under Citroën's Pierre-Jules Boulanger. Prototypes were hidden during the Second World War, and the car finally launched after the war with a 375 cc flat-twin engine producing 9 hp. Engines grew over the decades to 602 cc and about 29 hp, but the basic design — interconnected suspension, inboard front brakes, and minimalist bodywork — remained.

Production in France ended in 1988; the last 2CVs were built in Mangualde, Portugal, ending on 27 July 1990. Derivatives included the Dyane, Ami, Méhari, and the twin-engined Sahara 4×4.

Engineering

The 2CV's suspension used leading and trailing arms interconnected front-to-rear on each side by coil springs in a horizontal canister, giving a soft ride over rough roads. The air-cooled flat-twin engine eliminated the need for a radiator and coolant. Body panels were thin and largely bolted on, making repairs simple and cheap.

Legacy

The 2CV became a cultural icon of France, appearing in films such as For Your Eyes Only, and remains popular with collectors; restored examples and enthusiast clubs are numerous worldwide.
