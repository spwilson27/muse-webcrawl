Negative equity

Negative equity
A home "underwater" on its mortgage
Also called"Underwater" mortgage; negative equity (UK usage)
DefinitionLiability exceeds the market value of the asset securing it
Common assetsResidential property, motor vehicles

Negative equity is a deficit of owner's equity, occurring when the value of an asset used to secure a loan is less than the outstanding balance on the loan. In the United States, properties with loans in negative equity are often called "underwater", and loans with negative equity are said to be "upside down". If the borrower defaults, the lender will not be able to recover its investment by selling the asset, and may lose money.

Causes

Negative equity typically arises when an asset falls in value after purchase — for example during a housing-market downturn — or when a loan is taken out for more than the asset's value, or when an asset depreciates faster than the loan is repaid, as is common with new cars. High loan-to-value lending during property booms left many borrowers exposed when prices fell in the late 1980s and early 1990s in the United Kingdom, and again on a much larger scale during the United States housing bubble and the financial crisis of 2007–08, when at the peak more than a quarter of mortgaged American homes were estimated to be underwater.

Effects

Owners in negative equity cannot sell the asset without paying the shortfall, which restricts moving house, refinancing and borrowing. Economists distinguish negative equity itself from default: most underwater borrowers continue to pay, but negative equity combined with an income shock such as unemployment is a strong predictor of foreclosure. In jurisdictions with non-recourse loans, deeply underwater borrowers may rationally "walk away" (strategic default), returning the asset to the lender.

Other uses

In accounting, the term also describes a company or individual whose total liabilities exceed total assets (a negative net worth or balance-sheet insolvency). For vehicles, guaranteed asset protection (GAP) insurance is sold to cover the negative-equity shortfall if the car is written off.
