House of Cardona

The House of Cardona was a prominent Catalan noble family that rose to become one of the most powerful lineages in the Crown of Aragon and, later, in the Kingdom of Spain. Taking their name from the town and castle of Cardona in Catalonia, the family held the titles of Viscounts, then Counts, and finally Dukes of Cardona, a dignity created in 1482 that ranked among the foremost of the Spanish nobility.

Origins

The Cardona lineage emerged in the 10th and 11th centuries as viscounts of Osona and Cardona, frontier lords in the Spanish March charged with defending the borderlands against Muslim incursions. The family's arms — thistles (Catalan: cards) on a red field — are a canting reference to their name and to the thistles abundant around their stronghold. The lords of Cardona also controlled the valuable salt mines of Cardona, a source of considerable wealth.

Rise to ducal rank

Through loyal service to the counts of Barcelona and the kings of Aragon, the family accumulated extensive lordships in Catalonia and Valencia. In the late 15th century, Joan Ramon Folc IV de Cardona was raised to the rank of Duke of Cardona, and the family became grandees of Spain. Members of the house served as viceroys, admirals and royal councillors; Pere de Cardona was Archbishop of Tarragona, while other branches settled in Sicily and Naples, where the Cardona held high office under the Spanish Habsburgs.

Later history

The male line of the Dukes of Cardona became extinct in the later 17th century, and the dukedom passed by marriage to the Fernández de Córdoba family, Dukes of Medinaceli, in whose line the title of Duke of Cardona continues to be borne today. Collateral branches of the house persisted in Catalonia and Italy for several centuries.
