Stirling (Scots: Stirlin; Scottish Gaelic: Sruighlea) is a city in central Scotland, 26 miles (42 km) northeast of Glasgow and 37 miles (59 km) northwest of Edinburgh. The market town, surrounded by rich farmland, grew up around the medieval Stirling Castle on its crag, and was created a royal burgh by King David I in 1130.

History

Strategically located at the lowest bridging point of the River Forth and the gateway to the Highlands, Stirling was long described as "the brooch which clasps the Highlands and the Lowlands together". Its position made it the scene of major battles in the Wars of Scottish Independence: the Battle of Stirling Bridge (1297), William Wallace's victory over the English, and the Battle of Bannockburn (1314), fought nearby, where Robert the Bruce defeated Edward II.

Stirling Castle was a favoured residence of the Stewart monarchs; Mary, Queen of Scots was crowned there in 1543, and James VI was baptised and raised there. The town was granted city status in 2002 as part of Queen Elizabeth II's Golden Jubilee.

Today

The city is home to the University of Stirling (founded 1967) and is a centre for government, retail, and tourism. The National Wallace Monument, completed in 1869, stands on the Abbey Craig overlooking the site of Wallace's victory.
