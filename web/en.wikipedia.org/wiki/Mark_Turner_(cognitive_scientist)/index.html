Mark Turner (born 1954) is an American cognitive scientist, linguist and author. He is Institute Professor and Professor of Cognitive Science at Case Western Reserve University. With Gilles Fauconnier, he is a founder of conceptual blending theory, a framework in cognitive semantics for how the mind combines concepts to create new meaning.

Work

Turner's research concerns the mental operations underlying human thought and language: metaphor, analogy, narrative, and conceptual integration. In The Literary Mind (1996) he argued that story and projection — the mind's habit of understanding one story in terms of another — are basic instruments of everyday thought rather than literary ornaments. In The Way We Think (2002), written with Fauconnier, he set out the theory of conceptual blending, in which "mental spaces" are selectively projected and integrated into a blended space with emergent structure.

Earlier books include Death is the Mother of Beauty (1987), Reading Minds (1991) on the cognitive approach to literature, and, with George Lakoff, More Than Cool Reason (1989), a study of metaphor in poetry. His later work, including The Origin of Ideas (2014), extends blending theory to creativity, mathematics, religion and the evolution of advanced cognition.

Career

Turner earned his bachelor's degree in mathematics and his master's and doctorate in English from the University of California, Berkeley. He taught at the University of Maryland, College Park, where he chaired the English department, and served as dean of the College of Arts and Sciences at Case Western Reserve University before becoming Institute Professor.
