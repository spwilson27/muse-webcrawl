Breakaway roping

[Image placeholder: breakaway roping competition]

Breakaway roping is a variation of calf roping in which a rider on horseback ropes a calf, but the rope is tied to the saddle horn with a string rather than being secured. When the calf hits the end of the rope, the string breaks, ending the run and stopping the clock. It is one of the most popular events for women in rodeo, and is also contested by youth and amateur competitors.

The event

As in calf roping, the calf is given a head start out of a chute, and the roper must catch it around the neck with a loop of rope thrown from horseback. The rope passes through a loop or is tied lightly to the saddle horn with a short piece of nylon or cotton string. A small flag or handkerchief is often attached to the end of the rope so judges can easily see the break. When the rope comes tight and the calf pulls away, the string snaps, the flag drops, and the time is recorded. The fastest clean run wins.

History and popularity

Breakaway roping grew out of ranch work and amateur rodeo in the American West. Because the event emphasizes horsemanship, speed, and accuracy rather than strength, it became a mainstay of high school, college, and women's professional rodeo. In 2020, breakaway roping was featured at the National Finals Rodeo in conjunction with the Women's Professional Rodeo Association (WPRA) championship, a milestone that significantly raised the event's profile. It is now one of the fastest-growing events in professional rodeo.

Equipment

Competitors use a short, light lariat, typically around 28 feet long, a trained roping horse known for quick starts and hard stops, and a saddle with a horn wrapped to hold the string. Calves used in competition are generally smaller than those used in tie-down roping.
