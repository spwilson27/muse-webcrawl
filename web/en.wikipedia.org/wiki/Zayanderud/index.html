Zayanderud (Zayandeh River)

Country: Iran

Source: Zagros Mountains (Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province)

Mouth: Gavkhouni wetland

Length: about 400 km

Major city: Isfahan

The Zayanderud or Zayandeh River (Persian: زاینده‌رود, "life-giving river") is the largest river of the Iranian Plateau's central basin and the lifeline of the city of Isfahan. It rises in the Zagros Mountains in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province, flows eastward for about 400 kilometres, passes through Isfahan, and terminates in the Gavkhouni salt marsh southeast of the city.

The river in Isfahan

In Isfahan the river is crossed by a celebrated series of bridges, most of them built in the Safavid era (17th century), including the Si-o-se-pol (Bridge of Thirty-Three Arches), the Khaju Bridge, the Shahrestan Bridge (the oldest), the Marnan Bridge, and the Joui Bridge. The bridges and the riverside promenades are central to the city's identity and social life.

Water management

The river's flow is regulated by the Zayandeh Rud Dam (Chadegan Dam), completed in 1971, and by tunnels diverting water from the Karun basin. Since the early 2000s, drought, upstream withdrawals, and inter-basin transfers to other provinces have caused the river to run dry through Isfahan for long periods, provoking public protests by farmers downstream. The drying of the riverbed and of the Gavkhouni wetland is one of Iran's most prominent environmental issues.
