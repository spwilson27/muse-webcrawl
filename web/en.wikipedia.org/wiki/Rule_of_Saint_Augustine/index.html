Rule of Saint Augustine

The Rule of Saint Augustine is a set of guidelines for living in religious community written by Augustine of Hippo around the end of the 4th and beginning of the 5th century. It is the oldest monastic rule in the Western Church that is still in use, predating the Rule of Saint Benedict by about a century.

Contents

The Rule, which survives in versions for men and for women, emphasises charity as the foundation of community life: members are to hold all things in common, to pray together at set hours, to practise moderation in food and dress, to care for the sick, and to resolve conflicts peacefully. Unlike the more detailed Benedictine Rule, the Augustinian Rule is brief and focuses on the spirit of communal life rather than on detailed regulation.

Orders following the Rule

The Rule was widely adopted in the Middle Ages by communities of canons regular and by religious orders including the Augustinians (Order of Saint Augustine), the Dominicans, the Premonstratensians, the Servites and the Brigittines, as well as many congregations of women.
