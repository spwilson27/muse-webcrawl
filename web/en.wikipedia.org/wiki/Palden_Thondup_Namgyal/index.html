Palden Thondup Namgyal

Palden Thondup Namgyal (23 May 1923 – 29 January 1982) was the 12th and last Chogyal (king) of the Kingdom of Sikkim, reigning from 1963 until the abolition of the monarchy and Sikkim's merger with India in 1975.

Early life

Born at Gangtok, the son of Chogyal Tashi Namgyal, he was educated at St Joseph's Convent in Kalimpong and at Bishop Cotton School in Shimla. He was identified in childhood by lamas as the reincarnation of an abbot of Phodong monastery. He served in the Sikkim administration and led negotiations with India after 1947.

Reign

Palden Thondup succeeded his father in 1963. In the same year he married Hope Cooke, an American, whose presence drew international attention to the tiny Himalayan kingdom. Under a 1950 treaty Sikkim was an Indian protectorate, with India controlling defence, external affairs and communications. Political agitation for democracy and closer integration with India grew through the 1960s and early 1970s, amid the Chogyal's wish to preserve Sikkim's separate identity.

Deposition and later life

Following unrest and clashes in 1973, India assumed a larger administrative role; a referendum in 1975 voted to abolish the monarchy and join India, and Sikkim became India's 22nd state. Palden Thondup never accepted the legitimacy of the merger. He died of cancer in New York in 1982.
