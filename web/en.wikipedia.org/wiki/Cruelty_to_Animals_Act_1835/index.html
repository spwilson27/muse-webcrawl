Cruelty to Animals Act 1835

The Cruelty to Animals Act 1835 (5 & 6 Will. 4 c. 59) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom intended to protect animals from cruelty. It extended and amended Martin's Act of 1822, which had protected cattle, and it outlawed blood sports including bear-baiting, bull-baiting and cockfighting in England and Wales.

Provisions

The Act prohibited keeping premises for baiting or fighting animals, extended protections to a wider range of domestic animals than the 1822 Act, and provided penalties for wanton cruelty. It built on the work of the Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (founded 1824, later the RSPCA), whose inspectors enforced the legislation.

Legacy

The 1835 Act was followed by further legislation, including the Cruelty to Animals Act 1849, and formed part of the nineteenth-century development of animal welfare law in Britain. Most of its provisions were eventually superseded by the Protection of Animals Act 1911.
