Binary classification

Binary classification is the task of classifying the elements of a set into two groups (called classes) on the basis of a classification rule. Typical binary classification problems include medical testing (does a patient have a certain disease or not), quality control in manufacturing, information retrieval (is a document relevant or not), and email spam filtering.

Evaluation

A binary classifier's predictions fall into four categories, summarised in a confusion matrix:

Predicted positivePredicted negative
Actually positiveTrue positive (TP)False negative (FN)
Actually negativeFalse positive (FP)True negative (TN)

From these, standard measures are derived: sensitivity (recall, true positive rate) = TP/(TP+FN); specificity = TN/(TN+FP); precision = TP/(TP+FP); and accuracy = (TP+TN)/total. The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, and the ROC curve plots the true positive rate against the false positive rate as a decision threshold varies, summarised by the area under the curve (AUC).

Methods

Common algorithms include logistic regression, support vector machines, naive Bayes classifiers, decision trees and random forests, perceptrons, and neural networks. Probabilistic classifiers output a score or probability that is compared with a threshold (often 0.5) to obtain a class label; choosing the threshold trades off false positives against false negatives.
