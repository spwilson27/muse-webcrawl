Whig Party (United States)

The Whig Party was a political party in the United States active from the 1830s to the 1850s, part of the Second Party System. It was formed in opposition to the policies of President Andrew Jackson and the Democratic Party, taking its name from the British Whigs who had opposed royal tyranny. Whigs supported the supremacy of Congress over the presidency, a strong national bank, protective tariffs and federally funded internal improvements — Henry Clay's "American System".

Rise and presidencies

The Whigs won the presidency in 1840 with William Henry Harrison, who died after a month and was succeeded by John Tyler, promptly expelled from the party after vetoing its banking bills. They won again in 1848 with Mexican War hero Zachary Taylor, who also died in office (1850), succeeded by Millard Fillmore. The party's giants in Congress were Henry Clay and Daniel Webster.

Collapse

The party fatally split over slavery's extension. The Compromise of 1850 and especially the Kansas–Nebraska Act (1854) — which Southern Whigs supported and Northern Whigs abhorred — destroyed it as a national organisation. Northern Whigs such as Abraham Lincoln migrated to the new Republican Party; Southern Whigs drifted to the American (Know Nothing) Party and later Constitutional Union Party. The Whigs held their last national convention in 1856.
