Women's marathon

The women's marathon is a long-distance running event over 42.195 kilometres (26 miles 385 yards) contested by female athletes. Although women ran the distance informally for decades, official recognition came only in the 1970s, and the event joined the Olympic programme in 1984.

Early history

Women were long excluded from marathon races on medical and social grounds that lacked evidence. Pioneers included Violet Piercy in London in 1926, Roberta "Bobbi" Gibb, who ran Boston unofficially in 1966, and Kathrine Switzer, who entered Boston officially in 1967 despite an official attempting to remove her during the race.

Official recognition

The Boston Marathon admitted women officially in 1972. An international women's marathon championship developed through the 1970s, and the International Olympic Committee added the women's marathon to the Los Angeles 1984 Games, where Joan Benoit won gold in 2:24:52.

World record progression

The world record fell rapidly as opportunities expanded. Grete Waitz broke 2:30 in 1978–79, Paula Radcliffe ran 2:15:25 in London in 2003, Brigid Kosgei ran 2:14:04 in Chicago in 2019, and Tigst Assefa ran 2:11:53 in Berlin in 2023. In 2024 Ruth Chepngetich became the first woman under 2:10, running 2:09:56 in Chicago.

Major races

Women's fields headline the World Marathon Majors — Tokyo, Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago and New York City — as well as the World Championships and Olympic marathons. Separate women's elite starts are now standard at major races.
