FIDE

FIDE (French: Fédération Internationale des Échecs) is the international federation governing the sport of chess, recognized by the International Olympic Committee as the world chess authority. Founded in Paris in 1924, it organizes the World Chess Championship and the Chess Olympiad, calculates the Elo ratings of players, and awards international titles such as Grandmaster.

Role and history

FIDE unified the rules of chess and, from 1948, took control of the World Championship after the death of Alexander Alekhine. Its history includes the Cold War-era dominance of the Soviet school, the 1993 split in which Garry Kasparov and Nigel Short broke away to form the Professional Chess Association, and reunification of the title in 2006. FIDE also governs rapid, blitz and correspondence chess, maintains the Laws of Chess, and trains and certifies arbiters and organizers.

Structure

National federations are FIDE's members; its president is elected by the General Assembly. Recent presidents include Florencio Campomanes, Kirsan Ilyumzhinov (1995–2018) and Arkady Dvorkovich. FIDE celebrated its centenary in 2024, the same year chess's record-breaking global events drew new attention to the federation.
