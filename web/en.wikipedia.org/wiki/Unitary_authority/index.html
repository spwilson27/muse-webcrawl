Unitary authority

A unitary authority is a type of local authority responsible for all local government functions within its area, or performing additional functions in an area which is divided into several councils. The term is most widely used in England, where unitary authorities replaced the two-tier system of county councils and district councils in a series of local government reorganisations beginning in the 1990s, and also in New Zealand, where the term describes territorial authorities that also perform the functions of a regional council.

England

In England, unitary authorities are single-tier councils created principally under the Local Government Act 1992 following a review by the Local Government Commission for England. The first wave of unitary authorities was established in 1995–1998, largely covering the larger towns and cities that had previously been county boroughs before 1974. A second wave followed in 2009, when several counties including Cornwall, Durham, Northumberland, Shropshire and Wiltshire became single unitary councils, and further reorganisations in 2019–2023 created new unitaries in areas such as Dorset, Buckinghamshire, Northamptonshire, Cumbria, North Yorkshire and Somerset.

Unitary authorities combine the functions of county councils (education, social services, transport, waste disposal) and district councils (housing, planning, waste collection, environmental health) in a single body. As of the mid-2020s there are 62 unitary authorities in England, covering roughly half the country's population. London boroughs and metropolitan boroughs are also single-tier authorities, though they are not usually called "unitary authorities" because their structure predates the 1990s reforms.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland

Scotland and Wales each have a single tier of principal local authorities: 32 council areas in Scotland and 22 principal areas in Wales, established in 1996. Although functionally unitary, they are rarely described by that term. Northern Ireland has 11 district councils with more limited powers, many services being delivered by central government departments.

New Zealand

In New Zealand, five of the 67 territorial authorities are unitary authorities — Auckland Council (created in 2010), Gisborne District Council, Nelson City Council, Tasman District Council and Marlborough District Council, plus the Chatham Islands Council with a bespoke structure. These bodies exercise both territorial and regional council functions.

Criticism and debate

Supporters argue unitary structures are simpler, more accountable and cheaper to run than two-tier government. Critics contend that large unitaries can be remote from local communities and that savings from reorganisation have often been modest. The debate has continued through successive rounds of English devolution and reorganisation proposals.
