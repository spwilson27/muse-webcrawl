Hydrogen vehicle

A hydrogen vehicle is a vehicle that uses hydrogen as fuel. Most hydrogen vehicles are electric vehicles powered by a fuel cell that converts hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water (fuel cell electric vehicles, FCEVs); a smaller number burn hydrogen directly in a modified internal combustion engine. Examples of production fuel-cell cars include the Toyota Mirai and Hyundai Nexo.

Technology

In a fuel-cell vehicle, hydrogen stored in high-pressure carbon-fibre tanks (typically at 700 bar) feeds a proton-exchange membrane fuel cell stack, whose electricity drives a traction motor; a small battery buffers power and captures regenerative braking. Refuelling takes a few minutes, comparable to petrol, and ranges of 500–650 km are typical. The only tailpipe emission is water vapour.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters cite fast refuelling, long range, and suitability for heavy transport. Critics point to low round-trip efficiency compared with battery-electric vehicles (green hydrogen production, compression, and reconversion lose much of the original energy), the scarcity of refuelling infrastructure, high vehicle and fuel costs, and the fact that most hydrogen today is produced from natural gas with significant carbon emissions.
