The League of Women Voters (LWV) is a nonpartisan American nonprofit political organization founded in 1920. Its ongoing mission is to expand informed participation in government, increase understanding of major public policy issues, and influence public policy through education and advocacy. Although the League states it is officially nonpartisan—neither supporting nor opposing candidates or parties—it has often supported liberal political positions on issues such as campaign finance reform, voting rights, and gun control.

History

The League was founded by Carrie Chapman Catt in 1920 during the convention of the National American Woman Suffrage Association, just months before the Nineteenth Amendment was ratified, granting women the right to vote. Maud Wood Park became its first president. The League's initial purpose was to help newly enfranchised women exercise their vote responsibly through education. In 1973 the League amended its bylaws to admit men as full members.

Activities

The League registers voters, publishes nonpartisan voter guides, and hosts candidate forums and debates. From 1976 to 1984 it sponsored the United States presidential debates before the Commission on Presidential Debates took over. Its website Vote411.org provides personalized ballot information. The League operates at local, state (49 state leagues plus the District of Columbia and the Virgin Islands), and national levels, with more than 700 local leagues.

Positions

The League reaches positions through a study-and-consensus process among its members. It supports measures including the Equal Rights Amendment, campaign finance reform, universal voter registration, and opposition to voter ID laws it regards as restrictive.
