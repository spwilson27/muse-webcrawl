The American Psychological Association (APA) is the main professional organisation of psychologists in the United States and the largest psychological association in the world, with more than 150,000 members including scientists, educators, clinicians, consultants and students. It has 54 divisions covering specialties from clinical and cognitive psychology to social and military psychology.

History and role

The APA was founded in July 1892 at Clark University by a small group of academics, with G. Stanley Hall as its first president. It accredits doctoral programs in clinical, counseling and school psychology, publishes hundreds of journals and books, maintains the PsycINFO database, and issues the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual's counterpart guidelines for practice — though the DSM itself is published by the separate American Psychiatric Association.

APA style

The association is widely known beyond psychology for APA Style, the citation and formatting system set out in the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association (7th edition, 2019), used across the social sciences.

Controversies

The APA has faced criticism over its positions at various times, notably an independent review (the 2015 Hoffman Report) which found that APA officials had colluded with the US Department of Defense to shape ethics guidelines permissive of psychologists' involvement in harsh interrogations; the association subsequently banned psychologists from national-security interrogations and issued apologies.
