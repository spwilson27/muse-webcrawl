Pedro Menéndez de Avilés

Pedro Menéndez de Avilés
Born: 15 February 1519, Avilés, Asturias, Spain
Died: 17 September 1574 (aged 55), Santander, Spain
Occupation: Naval officer, explorer, colonial administrator
Known for: Founding St. Augustine, Florida; first governor of Spanish Florida

Pedro Menéndez de Avilés, founder of St. Augustine.

Pedro Menéndez de Avilés (15 February 1519 – 17 September 1574) was a Spanish admiral, explorer, and conquistador. He founded St. Augustine in Florida in 1565, the oldest continuously inhabited European-established settlement in the continental United States, and served as the first governor of Spanish Florida.

Early life and naval career

Menéndez was born in Avilés, in Asturias, into a family of minor nobility with a maritime tradition. He went to sea as a youth and built a reputation fighting French and Barbary corsairs in the Atlantic and the Bay of Biscay. His abilities brought him to the attention of King Philip II, who employed him in commanding treasure fleets between Spain and the Indies.

Florida expedition

In 1565 Philip II appointed Menéndez adelantado of Florida, charging him with colonizing the territory and expelling the French Huguenots who had established Fort Caroline near the St. Johns River. Menéndez landed on the Florida coast in late August 1565 and on 8 September formally founded St. Augustine. He subsequently captured Fort Caroline and ordered the execution of its French garrison and of shipwrecked French soldiers under Jean Ribault near an inlet later named Matanzas ("slaughters"), actions that secured Spanish control of the coast but have drawn lasting historical controversy.

Later career and death

As governor, Menéndez established missions and garrisons along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts and explored the Chesapeake Bay region. Recalled to Spain, he was given command of an armada being prepared against England but died of fever at Santander in September 1574 before it sailed.

Legacy

Menéndez is commemorated as the founder of St. Augustine, and his remains were eventually transferred to Avilés. Historians debate his record, weighing his organizational achievement in establishing Spanish Florida against the violence of the Fort Caroline campaign.
