Suspended animation

Suspended animation is the temporary slowing or stopping of biological functions — including metabolism, breathing, and heartbeat — with the intention of later revival, without incurring death or serious injury. The term covers phenomena ranging from natural states such as hibernation and cryptobiosis to speculative medical and spaceflight technologies.

Natural suspended animation

Many organisms enter states of greatly reduced metabolism: bears and other mammals hibernate through winter; tardigrades, brine shrimp, and some nematodes undergo cryptobiosis, surviving desiccation, freezing, and vacuum for years before reviving on rehydration; wood frogs tolerate partial freezing of their bodies; and many seeds and spores remain viable for decades or centuries. Embryonic diapause in mammals such as kangaroos and some bats delays development until conditions improve.

Medical approaches

Therapeutic hypothermia is already standard care after cardiac arrest and for newborns with hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, cooling patients a few degrees to protect the brain. In trauma surgery, researchers have trialled "emergency preservation and resuscitation" (EPR): rapidly cooling patients with exsanguinating injuries to around 10 °C by replacing blood with cold saline, buying up to an hour or two for surgical repair. In 2019 surgeons at the University of Maryland reported placing a human patient in this state for the first time. Experiments inducing torpor-like states with hydrogen sulphide or adenosine agonists in rodents have shown reversible metabolic suppression, but safe, prolonged whole-body suspended animation in humans remains beyond current technology.

In spaceflight and fiction

Suspended animation — as "hibersleep", stasis, or cryosleep — is a staple of science fiction for interstellar travel, appearing in works from 2001: A Space Odyssey to Alien and Passengers. Space agencies have funded studies of torpor habitats for Mars missions, since a hibernating crew would need less food, water, and volume and would be shielded partly from radiation and psychological strain.
