Auguste de Montferrand

Auguste de Montferrand (23 January 1786 – 10 July 1858), born Henri Louis Auguste Ricard de Montferrand, was a French Neoclassical architect who worked principally in Russia. He is best known as the architect of Saint Isaac's Cathedral in Saint Petersburg, one of the largest churches in the world, and of the Alexander Column in Palace Square.

Life and career

Montferrand studied architecture in Paris and served in Napoleon's armies before moving to Russia in 1816, bringing a portfolio of designs that won the attention of Alexander I. In 1818 his design was chosen for the rebuilding of Saint Isaac's Cathedral, a project that occupied him for forty years. The cathedral's construction was a feat of engineering, involving monolithic granite columns quarried in Finland and a pioneering cast-iron dome structure.

Major works

Besides Saint Isaac's, Montferrand designed the Alexander Column (1830–1834), a single 600-tonne granite monolith erected in Palace Square to commemorate Russia's victory over Napoleon — still the tallest triumphal column of its kind. He also built mansions and interiors in Saint Petersburg and supervised works at Nizhny Novgorod. He died in 1858, weeks after the cathedral's consecration, and, denied burial in his own cathedral because he was a Catholic, was interred in Paris. He should not be confused with the Egyptologist Auguste Mariette.
