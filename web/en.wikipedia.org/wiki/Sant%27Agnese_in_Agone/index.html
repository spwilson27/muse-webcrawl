Sant'Agnese in Agone

Sant'Agnese in Agone is a 17th-century Baroque church in Rome, Italy, facing Piazza Navona. It stands on the spot where, according to tradition, Saint Agnes was martyred in the ancient Stadium of Domitian, whose outline the piazza preserves.

History

A church dedicated to Saint Agnes existed on the site from early medieval times. In 1652 Pope Innocent X commissioned a new church as part of his family's building programme around Palazzo Pamphilj. Girolamo Rainaldi and his son Carlo began the work; Francesco Borromini took over in 1653 and gave the church its distinctive concave façade, twin bell towers and dome. After Borromini's dismissal in 1657, Carlo Rainaldi completed the building, consecrated in 1672.

Architecture and art

The church has a Greek-cross plan with a richly decorated interior honouring Saint Agnes, including sculptures of her martyrdom and a crypt chapel containing the relic of her skull. Its façade forms one of the defining elements of Piazza Navona, in dialogue with Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers opposite.
