Byron Otto Thomas (born March 20, 1969), known professionally as Mannie Fresh, is an American record producer, DJ and rapper. He is best known for his work as the in-house producer of Cash Money Records from 1993 to 2005, where he produced all of the label's releases during that period and helped define the sound of New Orleans bounce-influenced hip hop.

Career

Thomas grew up in the 7th Ward of New Orleans, the son of a disc jockey. He began DJing at parties as a teenager and formed a hip hop duo, New York Incorporated, in the late 1980s. He also worked with rapper Gregory D before joining Cash Money Records, the label founded by brothers Bryan "Birdman" Williams and Ronald "Slim" Williams.

At Cash Money, Mannie Fresh produced hit albums and singles for the Hot Boys, Juvenile, Lil Wayne, B.G. and Big Tymers, the duo he formed with Birdman. His productions, built on drum machines, bright synthesizers and bounce rhythms, powered songs such as Juvenile's "Back That Azz Up" (1999) and Big Tymers' "Still Fly" (2002). As half of Big Tymers he released several albums, including How You Luv That (1998) and Hood Rich (2002).

After leaving Cash Money in 2005 amid a financial dispute, he released the solo album The Mind of Mannie Fresh (2004 had preceded his departure) and continued producing for artists including T.I., Rick Ross and Young Jeezy. He later collaborated with Mos Def (Yasiin Bey) in the duo OMFGOD and reunited periodically with his former Cash Money colleagues.
