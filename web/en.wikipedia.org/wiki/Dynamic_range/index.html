Dynamic range

Dynamic range
Abbreviation: DR
Expressed in: decibels, stops, bits

Dynamic range (abbreviated DR, DNR, or DYR) is the ratio between the largest and smallest values that a certain quantity can assume. It is often used in the context of signals, such as sound and light, and is measured as a ratio or as a base-10 (decibel) or base-2 (doublings, bits, or stops) logarithmic value of the difference between the largest and smallest signal values.

Applications

In audio, dynamic range describes the span between the quietest audible sound and the loudest undistorted level; human hearing spans roughly 120 dB, while a compact disc offers about 96 dB. In photography and imaging, dynamic range is the luminance ratio between the brightest and darkest recordable parts of a scene, measured in stops; exceeding it produces clipped highlights or crushed shadows, a limitation addressed by high-dynamic-range (HDR) imaging techniques.

Electronics measurements — of amplifiers, converters, and sensors — all specify dynamic range, since noise floors and saturation levels bound every real system. See dynamic range compression.
