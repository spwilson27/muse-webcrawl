GIMP

GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is a free and open-source raster graphics editor used for image manipulation (retouching) and image editing, free-form drawing, transcoding between different image file formats, and more specialised tasks. It is extensible by means of plugins and scripts, and is available for Linux, macOS, Windows, and other operating systems.

History

GIMP was originally developed in 1995 by Spencer Kimball and Peter Mattis as the "General Image Manipulation Program" at the University of California, Berkeley. After becoming part of the GNU Project, the name was retroactively interpreted as "GNU Image Manipulation Program". The toolkit GTK was created for GIMP and later used widely in the GNOME desktop.

Long anticipated, version 3.0 was released in March 2025, migrating to GTK 3 and adding non-destructive editing with layer effects. GIMP's mascot is Wilber, a cartoon coyote-like character.

Features

GIMP supports layers, masks, channels, paths, a wide selection of selection and painting tools, colour correction, cloning and healing, and batch processing. Its native file format is XCF. It is frequently compared to Adobe Photoshop as a free alternative, though its interface and workflow differ.
