East African campaign (World War II)

The East African campaign was fought in East Africa during the Second World War by Allies of World War II, mainly from the British Empire, against Italy and its colony of Italian East Africa, between June 1940 and November 1941.

East African campaign
Date: 10 June 1940 - 27 November 1941
Location: Italian East Africa (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somaliland)
Result: Allied victory

Course

Italy invaded British Somaliland in August 1940. From January 1941, forces under Alan Cunningham advanced from Kenya and William Platt from Sudan, capturing Mogadishu, Addis Ababa (April 1941) and restoring Emperor Haile Selassie. The Battle of Keren was a hard-fought key engagement in Eritrea.

Conclusion

The Duke of Aosta surrendered at Amba Alagi in May 1941; Italian guerrilla resistance continued until 1943, but the last regular forces surrendered at Gondar in November 1941, ending the threat to Red Sea shipping.
