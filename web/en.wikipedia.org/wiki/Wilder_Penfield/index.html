Wilder Penfield
Born: 26 January 1891, Spokane, Washington
Died: 5 April 1976, Montreal, Canada
Field: Neurosurgery

Wilder Graves Penfield (1891–1976) was an American-Canadian neurosurgeon who pioneered surgical techniques for treating epilepsy and mapped the functions of the human brain. He founded the Montreal Neurological Institute in 1934.

Work

Using electrical stimulation of the exposed cortex in awake patients undergoing surgery under local anaesthesia, Penfield systematically mapped sensory and motor areas, producing the famous "homunculus" diagrams of how body parts are represented in the brain. His "Montreal procedure" removed scarred brain tissue causing seizures while sparing eloquent cortex. Stimulation of the temporal lobes also elicited vivid experiential responses — memories, sounds, and scenes — that influenced understanding of memory.

Honours

Penfield was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society, appointed to the Order of Merit, and is commemorated in Canada as one of the country's greatest scientists.
