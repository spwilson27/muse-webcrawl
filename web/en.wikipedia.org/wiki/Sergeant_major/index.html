Sergeant major

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sergeant major is a senior non-commissioned rank or appointment in many militaries around the world. Historically, in the 16th and 17th centuries, the sergeant major (from Spanish sargento mayor) was a senior field officer — the third in command of a regiment or army staff officer responsible for drill and deployment — before the word "major" detached itself as a separate commissioned rank. The modern role is that of the senior enlisted adviser and disciplinarian.

By country

United States. In the U.S. Army, sergeant major (SGM) is the E-9 pay grade, with the related command sergeant major (CSM) serving as the senior enlisted adviser to a battalion or higher commander; at the top of the service stands the Sergeant Major of the Army. The U.S. Marine Corps has both sergeant major (an administrative/senior-leadership track) and master gunnery sergeant (a technical track) at E-9, headed by the Sergeant Major of the Marine Corps.

United Kingdom. In the British Army, "sergeant major" generally survives as an appointment — notably the regimental sergeant major (RSM), a Warrant Officer Class 1 who is the senior soldier of a regiment and adviser to its commanding officer — rather than as a substantive rank. Similar appointments exist across Commonwealth armed forces.

Duties

Sergeants major typically advise commanders on enlisted matters, oversee standards of drill, dress, and discipline, mentor junior NCOs, and serve as the institutional memory of their units.
