Solomon Islands campaign

The Solomon Islands campaign was a major campaign of the Pacific War of World War II, fought between Allied and Imperial Japanese forces from 1942 to 1945 in and around the Solomon Islands archipelago. The campaign began with Japanese landings and occupation of several areas in early 1942 and ended with the isolation and bypassing of remaining Japanese garrisons by the war's end.

Background

Japan occupied Tulagi in May 1942 and began building a seaplane base, then started an airfield on nearby Guadalcanal. The Allies feared the islands would be used to sever supply lines between the United States and Australia, and made Guadalcanal the target of their first major offensive of the Pacific War.

Guadalcanal

On 7 August 1942, US Marines landed on Guadalcanal and Tulagi, capturing the unfinished airfield — renamed Henderson Field. Japan repeatedly tried to retake the island, leading to bitter land battles (Edson's Ridge, the Tenaru, and the Matanikau actions), the night surface actions around "Ironbottom Sound", and the decisive Naval Battle of Guadalcanal in November 1942. Japan withdrew its surviving troops by February 1943.

Advance up the Slot

Through 1943 Allied forces advanced northwest up the island chain — the New Georgia campaign, landings at Rendova and Munda, and the Bougainville campaign beginning in November 1943. Air power from captured fields, the work of coastwatchers, and hard-fought naval battles such as Vella Gulf and Empress Augusta Bay steadily isolated the great Japanese base at Rabaul, which was neutralised by air attack and bypassed rather than assaulted. Australian forces conducted mopping-up operations on Bougainville and New Britain in 1944–45.

Significance

Together with the New Guinea campaign, the Solomons campaign marked the turning of the tide in the South Pacific: Japan lost irreplaceable ships, aircraft and aircrews, while the Allies developed the amphibious and logistical methods used for the rest of the Pacific advance.
