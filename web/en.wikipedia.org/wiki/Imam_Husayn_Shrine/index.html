The Imam Husayn Shrine (Arabic: مقام الإمام الحسين) is a mosque and burial site in the city of Karbala, Iraq. It stands on the site of the grave of Husayn ibn Ali, the third Imam of Shia Islam and grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, who was killed at the Battle of Karbala in 680 CE (61 AH). It is one of the holiest places for Shia Muslims, after Mecca, Medina, and Najaf.

History

A shrine was first erected over Husayn's grave shortly after his burial; it was repeatedly enlarged under the Buyids and later dynasties, and repeatedly destroyed, including by the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil in the 9th century and in the Wahhabi sack of Karbala in 1802. The present complex, with its gold-covered dome and gilded minarets, largely reflects reconstructions of the 19th and 20th centuries and continuing renovation.

Pilgrimage

The shrine is the destination of two of the largest annual gatherings in the world: Ashura, commemorating Husayn's martyrdom, and Arba'een, forty days later, when millions of pilgrims — many walking from Najaf — visit Karbala. The nearby shrine of Husayn's half-brother al-Abbas faces it across an open plaza.
