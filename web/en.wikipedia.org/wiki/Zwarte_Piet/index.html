Zwarte Piet

Zwarte Piet (Dutch for "Black Pete"; plural Zwarte Pieten) is the companion of Sinterklaas (Saint Nicholas) in the folklore of the Netherlands and, in related forms, Belgium. In the annual Sinterklaas festival, which culminates on the evening of 5 December (and 6 December in Belgium), Zwarte Piet traditionally helps Sinterklaas deliver presents and sweets — such as pepernoten and chocolate letters — to children, arriving by steamboat supposedly from Spain.

Origins and traditional portrayal

Helpers of Saint Nicholas exist in many European traditions (compare Knecht Ruprecht in Germany or Krampus in Alpine folklore). The modern Dutch character was codified in Jan Schenkman's popular 1850 book Sint Nikolaas en zijn knecht ("Saint Nicholas and his servant"), which introduced the servant in Moorish dress — colourful Renaissance-style clothing with a feathered cap, ruff collar and gold earring — as well as details such as the steamboat arrival. Tradition holds that Piet's black appearance results variously from being a Moor from Spain, or from soot acquired climbing down chimneys to deliver gifts. Traditionally, naughty children were threatened (usually in jest) with being carried off in Piet's sack to Spain, or given a lump of coal or a switch (roe).

Controversy and change

Since the late 20th century, and much more intensely after about 2011, the character has been the subject of public debate in the Netherlands and internationally. Critics argue that the traditional portrayal — an actor in blackface make-up with exaggerated red lips and curly wig — is a racist caricature and a relic of colonialism; defenders argue that Piet is a beloved, harmless children's figure whose blackness derives from chimney soot. Protests and counter-protests have accompanied the national arrival (intocht) of Sinterklaas, and the issue has reached Dutch courts and been noted by UN human rights bodies. In response, many cities, broadcasters and schools have modified the character — for example "soot Pieten" (roetveegpieten) with smudged faces, or multi-coloured "rainbow Pieten" — and since 2019 the national television arrival has used soot-smudged Pieten only. Polls have consistently shown a majority of the Dutch public favouring keeping the character in some form, with support shifting gradually toward the soot-based portrayal.
