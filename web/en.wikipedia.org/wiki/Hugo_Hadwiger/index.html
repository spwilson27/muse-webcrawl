Hugo Hadwiger


Swiss mathematician Hugo Hadwiger (1908–1981).

Hugo Hadwiger (23 December 1908 in Karlsruhe, Germany – 29 October 1981 in Bern, Switzerland) was a Swiss mathematician, known for his work in geometry, combinatorics, and cryptography.

Contributions

Hadwiger is remembered for the Hadwiger conjecture in graph theory, which states that if a graph has no minor isomorphic to the complete graph on t vertices, then its vertices can be colored with at most t−1 colors; the conjecture remains one of the deepest unsolved problems in graph theory. He also proved Hadwiger's theorem in integral geometry, characterizing the continuous rigid-motion-invariant valuations on convex bodies in Euclidean space, and the Hadwiger–Debrunner problem on covering systems. In 1943 he conjectured the Hadwiger conjecture on unit cubes covering a larger cube.

Career

Hadwiger studied at the University of Bern and the University of Hamburg, and spent most of his career as a professor at Bern. During the Second World War he contributed to improvements of the Swiss cipher machine derived from the Enigma.
