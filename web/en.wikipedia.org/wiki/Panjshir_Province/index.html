Panjshir Province

Panjshir (Dari: پنجشیر, "Five Lions") is one of the thirty-four provinces of Afghanistan, located in the northeastern part of the country. It consists almost entirely of the Panjshir Valley, carved by the Panjshir River beneath the Hindu Kush, and has a population of roughly 170,000, predominantly ethnic Tajiks. Its capital is Bazarak. Panjshir became a separate province in 2004, having previously been part of Parwan Province.

History

The valley's narrow entrance and defensible terrain made it famous in modern Afghan history. During the Soviet–Afghan War, mujahideen commander Ahmad Shah Massoud — the "Lion of Panjshir" — repelled repeated Soviet offensives into the valley. In the 1990s the valley served as the heartland of the Northern Alliance against the Taliban, and it was the last region to fall after the Taliban returned to power in August 2021, when resistance forces under Ahmad Massoud and Amrullah Saleh held out briefly before Taliban fighters entered the valley that September.

Geography and economy

The province is renowned for its emeralds, mined in the mountains above the valley, along with agriculture along the river terraces and a growing reputation as one of Afghanistan's most secure and scenic regions.
