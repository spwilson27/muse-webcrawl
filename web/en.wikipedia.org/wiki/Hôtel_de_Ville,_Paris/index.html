Hôtel de Ville, Paris

The Hôtel de Ville (French for "City Hall") is the city hall of Paris, France, standing on the Place de l'Hôtel-de-Ville—Esplanade de la Libération in the 4th arrondissement, on the north bank of the Seine. It has housed the municipality of Paris since 1357 and is the seat of the Mayor of Paris and the Paris City Council.

History

The first municipal building on the site was acquired in 1357 by Étienne Marcel, provost of the merchants. A Renaissance palace designed under François I was completed in 1628 and was the centre of several revolutionary episodes, including the proclamation of the French Second Republic in 1848 and of the Third Republic in 1870. The Paris Commune set the building ablaze in May 1871, destroying its interiors and archives.

The present building

Reconstruction between 1874 and 1882 by architects Théodore Ballu and Édouard Deperthes reproduced the Renaissance façade, including a central clock tower, while providing lavish reception rooms decorated by leading artists of the Third Republic. The square in front, long the site of executions, was made a pedestrian esplanade and renamed in 2013 for the Liberation of Paris.
