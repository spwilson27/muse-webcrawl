House of Romanov

The House of Romanov (Russian: Рома́новы, Romanovy) was the reigning imperial house of Russia from 1613 to 1917. They achieved prominence after the Zemsky Sobor of 1613 elected Michael Romanov, then aged sixteen, as tsar, ending the Time of Troubles. The dynasty ruled the Tsardom of Russia and the subsequent Russian Empire for just over three centuries, until Nicholas II abdicated during the February Revolution of 1917.

Origins

The Romanovs were an old boyar family whose fortunes rose when Ivan the Terrible married Anastasia Romanovna in 1547. Her brother Nikita was grandfather of Michael, the first Romanov tsar. Michael's father, Feodor Nikitich Romanov, had been forced into monastic life under the name Filaret and later became Patriarch of Moscow, sharing power with his son as co-ruler in all but name.

The dynasty

Early Romanov rulers consolidated the autocracy and codified serfdom in the Sobornoye Ulozheniye of 1649. Peter I (r. 1682–1725) transformed Russia into a European great power, founded Saint Petersburg in 1703 and proclaimed the Russian Empire in 1721. A succession crisis after his death produced an era of palace coups, ended by the long reign of Catherine II (1762–1796), under whom the empire expanded into Crimea, Poland and the Black Sea steppe. In the nineteenth century the empire defeated Napoleon in 1812, emancipated the serfs under Alexander II in 1861, and industrialised under Sergei Witte in the 1890s.

Fall

Defeat in the Russo-Japanese War triggered the Revolution of 1905, after which Nicholas II conceded the October Manifesto and a State Duma. The strains of the First World War brought military collapse and revolution: Nicholas abdicated in March 1917, and he and his family were executed by the Bolsheviks at Yekaterinburg on the night of 16–17 July 1918. Eighteen Romanovs in total were killed in 1918–1919; others escaped into exile aboard ships including HMS Marlborough, sent to rescue the Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna from Crimea.

After 1917

The remains of Nicholas II and his family were recovered from unmarked graves near Yekaterinburg, identified by DNA testing, and reburied in the Peter and Paul Fortress in 1998; the Russian Orthodox Church canonised them as passion bearers in 2000. The headship of the house in exile has been disputed between descendants of Grand Duke Kirill Vladimirovich — notably Grand Duchess Maria Vladimirovna — and other branches of the family organised in the Romanov Family Association. The tercentenary of the dynasty was lavishly celebrated in 1913, the last great imperial spectacle before the fall.
