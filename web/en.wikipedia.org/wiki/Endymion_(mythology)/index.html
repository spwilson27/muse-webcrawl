Endymion (mythology)

In Greek mythology, Endymion (Ancient Greek: Ἐνδυμίων) was variously a handsome Aeolian shepherd, hunter, or king who was said to rule and live at Olympia in Elis. He was also venerated as a "beautiful youth" sent to eternal sleep. Most famously, Endymion was loved by Selene, the goddess of the Moon, who visited him every night as he slept.

Myth

Accounts of Endymion's parentage vary: he was said to be a son of Aethlius or of Zeus, and founder-king of Elis. According to one tradition, Zeus granted Endymion any gift he wished, and he chose immortality and eternal youth in never-ending sleep. He was laid out in a cave on Mount Latmus in Caria, where Selene, who loved him, came down each night to gaze upon him. By Selene he was said to have had fifty daughters. Other versions attribute his sleep to punishment, or to his own desire to remain forever young and beautiful.

In literature and art

The myth of Endymion has been a popular subject in art and literature, symbolising eternal beauty and youthful slumber. The English Romantic poet John Keats made him the subject of his narrative poem Endymion (1818), which opens with the famous line "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever." The story was also a frequent subject for painters, including Girodet, whose The Sleep of Endymion (1791) became celebrated for its ethereal lighting.
