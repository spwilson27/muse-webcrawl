Contamination

Contamination is the presence of a constituent, impurity, or some other undesirable element that renders something unsuitable, unfit, or harmful for its intended use or environment. Contaminants may be physical, chemical, biological, or radiological. The concept is used in environmental science, food safety, medicine, laboratory practice, and linguistics, where it also has a technical meaning in textual and etymological studies.

TypesChemical, biological, radiological, particulate
SettingsEnvironment, food, water, laboratories, medicine
CountermeasureDecontamination, prevention, quality control

Environmental contamination

Environmental contamination occurs when pollutants — such as heavy metals, pesticides, petroleum products, or radioactive materials — enter soil, water, or air at levels that pose risks to ecosystems or human health. Remediation of contaminated land may involve excavation, bioremediation, or containment. Contamination is closely related to, but narrower than, pollution: contamination implies the presence of a substance where it should not be, regardless of demonstrated harm, whereas pollution typically denotes contamination that causes adverse effects.

Food and water

In food safety, contamination can be biological (bacteria such as Salmonella, viruses, parasites), chemical (cleaning agents, allergens), or physical (glass, metal fragments). Cross-contamination — the transfer of pathogens from one food or surface to another — is a major cause of foodborne illness. Drinking-water contamination similarly arises from microbial pathogens, nitrates, lead, and industrial chemicals.

Laboratory and medical contexts

In laboratories, contamination of samples, cultures, or reagents can invalidate experiments; techniques such as sterile technique and blank controls guard against it. In medicine, contamination of blood cultures or wounds complicates diagnosis and treatment.
