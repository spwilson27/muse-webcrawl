Climateprediction.net (CPDN) is a distributed computing project based at the University of Oxford that uses the idle processing capacity of volunteers' personal computers to run very large ensembles of climate models. Launched on 12 September 2003, it was the largest climate modelling experiment of its kind and one of the early projects built on the Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing (BOINC).

Method

Because a single climate model run can take weeks of computation, climateprediction.net distributes thousands of slightly different versions of a model — varying physical parameters or initial conditions — to participants worldwide. Comparing the ensemble's results lets researchers map the range of possible future climates and estimate probabilities, rather than relying on a single projection.

Experiments

Early results, published in Nature in 2005, suggested that warming by 2100 in response to doubled carbon dioxide could range from under 2 °C to over 11 °C across the ensemble, with later work narrowing the plausible range. Subsequent experiments have included the BBC Climate Change Experiment (2006), seasonal attribution studies of floods and heatwaves, and regional modelling for southern Africa and other areas through the Weather@Home project.
