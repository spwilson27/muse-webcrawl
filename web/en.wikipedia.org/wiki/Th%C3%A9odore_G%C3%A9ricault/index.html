Jean-Louis André Théodore Géricault (26 September 1791 – 26 January 1824) was a French painter and lithographer, one of the pioneers of the Romantic movement. Though he died young, his work had a lasting influence on French art.

Life and career

Born in Rouen to a wealthy family, Géricault trained under Carle Vernet and Pierre-Narcisse Guérin. His early painting The Charging Chasseur (1812) showed his vigorous style. After travel in Italy, where he studied Michelangelo and the Baroque masters, he embarked on his masterpiece.

The Raft of the Medusa

The Raft of the Medusa (1818–19), exhibited at the Paris Salon of 1819, depicts the survivors of the wreck of the French naval frigate Méduse, a contemporary scandal. Its dramatic composition, realistic detail and implicit criticism of the government made it an icon of French Romanticism. Géricault's late portraits of the insane and his lithographs of English life also remain celebrated. He died in Paris at 32 after riding accidents and illness.
