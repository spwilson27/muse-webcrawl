Stenotype

A stenotype, stenograph or steno machine is a specialized chorded keyboard or typewriter used by stenographers for shorthand use. In order to pass the Registered Professional Reporter test in the United States, a trained court reporter or closed captioner must write speeds of approximately 180, 200, and 225 words per minute (wpm) at very high accuracy in the categories of literary, jury charge, and testimonial, respectively.

How it works

The machine has only 22 keys plus a number bar. Instead of typing letters one at a time, the operator presses several keys simultaneously in a "chord" to represent whole syllables, words or phrases, following a phonetic theory. Output is produced either on paper tape in steno code or, with modern machines, translated in real time by computer software (CAT — computer-aided transcription) into ordinary text.

Uses

Stenotype machines are used for court reporting, live television captioning (including for deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences), and Communication Access Realtime Translation (CART) in classrooms and meetings. Practitioners regard skilled machine shorthand as faster and more accurate for verbatim capture than voice writing or typing.
