Treaty of Sèvres


Signed: 10 August 1920, Sèvres, France
Parties: Allied Powers and the Ottoman Empire
Status: Never ratified; superseded by the Treaty of Lausanne (1923)

The Treaty of Sèvres was the 1920 peace treaty between the Ottoman Empire and the Allied Powers of the First World War. It proposed partitioning Ottoman territory: the Arab provinces became League of Nations mandates, parts of Anatolia were assigned to Greek, Italian, French and Armenian control, and the Turkish heartland was drastically reduced.

Terms

The treaty internationalised the Straits, curtailed the Ottoman army and finances, recognised the Kingdom of Hejaz, and envisaged an independent Armenia and possible Kurdish autonomy. It was signed by the Sultan's government in Constantinople but never ratified.

Aftermath

Turkish nationalists under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk repudiated the treaty and fought the Turkish War of Independence. Their victory forced the Allies to negotiate anew, and the Treaty of Lausanne (1923) replaced Sèvres, recognising the Republic of Turkey within roughly its modern borders.
