Verein für Bewegungsspiele Stuttgart 1893 e. V., commonly known as VfB Stuttgart, is a German professional sports club based in Stuttgart, Baden-Württemberg. Its football team is one of Germany's most successful, having won the national championship five times — most recently the Bundesliga title of 2006–07 — and the DFB-Pokal several times, including the 2024–25 cup.

History

The club was founded in 1893 as a rugby club and adopted football soon after; the present name dates from the 1912 merger of Stuttgarter FV and Kronen-Club Cannstatt. Stuttgart won German championships in 1950 and 1952 before the Bundesliga era, then Bundesliga titles in 1984, 1992 and 2007. The 1992 title was won dramatically on the final day, and the 2007 triumph under coach Armin Veh featured a young side including Mario Gómez and Sami Khedira. VfB also reached the UEFA Cup final in 1989, losing to Napoli.

The club's youth academy is renowned in German football, producing internationals such as Khedira, Timo Werner, Joshua Kimmich (via RB Leipzig after formation at VfB) and Serge Gnabry. After relegations in 2016 and 2019, the club rebuilt; under coach Sebastian Hoeneß, VfB finished second in the Bundesliga in 2023–24 to qualify for the UEFA Champions League, and won the DFB-Pokal in 2025, ending a long trophy wait.

Stadium and supporters

VfB play at the MHPArena (formerly Neckarstadion, then Mercedes-Benz Arena) in the Neckarpark district of Bad Cannstatt, a venue that hosted matches at the 1974 and 2006 World Cups and Euro 2024. The club has one of the largest memberships of any German sports club and its distinctive white shirt with a red chest ring (Brustring) is iconic.
