Hour record

The hour record is the record for the longest distance cycled in one hour on a bicycle, from a stationary start, traditionally on a velodrome track. It is one of the most prestigious records in cycling, contested by many of the sport's greatest riders, including Fausto Coppi, Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Miguel Induráin, Tony Rominger, Chris Boardman, Jens Voigt, Bradley Wiggins, Victor Campenaerts and Filippo Ganna.

History

The first recorded hour record was set in 1876 by Frank Dodds, who rode about 26.5 km on a penny-farthing. Henri Desgrange, later founder of the Tour de France, set the first record recognized under modern rules in 1893. The record steadily progressed through the twentieth century; Eddy Merckx's 1972 mark of 49.431 km, set at altitude in Mexico City, stood for many years as a benchmark of "traditional" equipment.

Equipment controversies and rule changes

In the 1980s and 1990s, advances in aerodynamics — disc wheels, triathlon handlebars, and radical riding positions such as Graeme Obree's "egg" and Chris Boardman's "Superman" positions — drove the record beyond 56 km. In 2000 the UCI reset the record under the so-called "Athlete's Hour" rules restricting equipment to that similar to Merckx's, but in 2014 it reunified the record under modern track pursuit bike regulations, sparking a new wave of attempts.

Recent records

Under the unified rules, Jens Voigt set 51.110 km in 2014, followed by improvements from Matthias Brändle, Rohan Dennis, Alex Dowsett and Bradley Wiggins (54.526 km in 2015). Victor Campenaerts raised the mark to 55.089 km in 2019, and on 8 October 2022 Filippo Ganna set 56.792 km in Grenchen, Switzerland. The women's record has been held by riders including Molly Shaffer Van Houweling, Evelyn Stevens, Vittoria Bussi, Joss Lowden and Ellen van Dijk.
