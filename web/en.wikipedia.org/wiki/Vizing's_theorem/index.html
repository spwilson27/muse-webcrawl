Vizing's theorem

Vizing's theorem in graph theory states that the edges of any simple undirected graph can be colored with at most Δ+1 colors, where Δ is the maximum degree.

Statement

Proved by Vadim Vizing in 1964, the theorem divides graphs into Class 1 (needing Δ colors) and Class 2 (needing Δ+1). Deciding which class is NP-complete in general.

Proof idea

The proof uses augmenting fan sequences and Kempe chains to recolor edges and extend partial colorings.
