Ismaily Sporting Club (Arabic: النادي الإسماعيلي), commonly known as Ismaily, is an Egyptian football club based in Ismailia, on the Suez Canal. Founded in 1921, it is one of the historic clubs of Egyptian football and the first Egyptian club to win the African Cup of Champions Clubs, in 1969. Its yellow and blue kit and flowing style of play earned it the nickname "the Brazilians of Egypt".

History

Founded by Egyptian students in 1921 as Nahdat El Ismaily, the club was a symbol of Egyptian identity during the British presence in the Canal Zone. Ismaily won the Egyptian Premier League in 1967, 1991 and 2002, and the Egyptian Cup twice. Its 1969 African Cup of Champions Clubs triumph — defeating TP Englebert of Congo in the final — made it the first Egyptian and first Arab club to win Africa's premier club competition.

Stadium and identity

Ismaily plays at Ismailia Stadium, with a capacity of around 18,500. The club's supporters are known for their loyalty, and its academy has produced many players for the Egypt national team, including Mohamed Barakat and Mohamed Homos. Ismaily's rivalry with Al Ahly and Zamalek is among Egypt's fiercest.
