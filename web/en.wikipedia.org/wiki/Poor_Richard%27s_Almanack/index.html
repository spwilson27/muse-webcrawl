Poor Richard's Almanack

Title page of a 1739 edition of Poor Richard's Almanack.

Poor Richard's Almanack (sometimes Almanac) was a yearly almanac published by Benjamin Franklin; the publication appeared continually from 1732 to 1758. It sold exceptionally well for a pamphlet published in the Thirteen Colonies; print runs reached 10,000 per year, making it one of the most popular publications in colonial America.

Content

Franklin published the almanac under the pseudonym of "Richard Saunders", a fictional astrologer and author. The almanac contained the calendar, weather forecasts, poems, sayings and astronomical and astrological information typical of the period. It is especially remembered for the aphorisms and proverbs scattered throughout, many of which live on in American English:

  • "Early to bed and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise."
  • "A penny saved is a penny earned."
  • "God helps them that help themselves."

The Way to Wealth

In the 1758 edition, Franklin prefaced the almanac with a speech by "Father Abraham" quoting Poor Richard's maxims on industry and frugality. Reprinted separately as The Way to Wealth, it became one of the most widely reprinted texts of the era and helped fix Franklin's public image as the voice of practical American wisdom.

Significance

The almanac was Franklin's most profitable publishing venture and a major vehicle for spreading Enlightenment practicality and humour in the colonies. Napoleon Bonaparte later had it translated into Italian.
