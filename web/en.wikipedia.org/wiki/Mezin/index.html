Mezin

Placeholder image: Engraved mammoth-bone artefacts from Mezin

Mezin (Ukrainian: Мезин, also transliterated Mezine or Mizyn) is a village in Chernihiv Oblast, Ukraine, near the Desna River, famous for the Upper Paleolithic archaeological site in its vicinity. The Mezin site, dating to roughly 15,000 years ago and associated with the Epigravettian culture, is one of the most significant late Ice Age hunter settlements in Eastern Europe, celebrated for its mammoth-bone dwellings and remarkable engraved art.

The site

The site was discovered in 1908 when a wolf den exposed worked bones, and systematic excavations followed under Ukrainian archaeologists including Fedir Vovk. Excavations revealed the remains of several large dwellings constructed from mammoth bones, skulls, tusks, and hides stretched over frames — among the most famous examples of mammoth-bone architecture anywhere, comparable to the structures at Mezhirich.

Finds

Mezin yielded a rich assemblage: flint tools, bone needles and awls, amber and shell ornaments, and mammoth-ivory objects engraved with geometric meander and zigzag patterns, including bracelets and figurines. A celebrated set of painted and engraved mammoth bones from Mezin has been interpreted as percussion instruments — sometimes described as an Ice Age "orchestra" — making the site a candidate for some of the oldest known musical instruments. The finds are held principally by museums in Kyiv.

Setting

The modern village lies within the Mezynskyi National Nature Park, which protects the Desna floodplain forests and river landscapes near the site. The locality gives its name to the "Mezinian" in some schemes of East European Paleolithic chronology.
