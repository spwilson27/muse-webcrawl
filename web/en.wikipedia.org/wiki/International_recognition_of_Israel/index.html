International recognition of Israel

The international recognition of Israel concerns the diplomatic recognition of the State of Israel, proclaimed on 14 May 1948. Israel was admitted to the United Nations in 1949 and is recognized by the large majority of UN member states, though a number of states—mostly Arab and Muslim-majority countries—do not recognize it, and a few states that once recognized Israel later withdrew recognition.

History

Following the declaration of independence, the United States extended de facto recognition almost immediately, and the Soviet Union was the first to grant de jure recognition, followed by many other states. Israel's admission to the United Nations on 11 May 1949, by General Assembly Resolution 273, followed recognition by more than fifty states.

After the 1948 Arab–Israeli War most Arab states refused recognition, a position formalized in the Khartoum Resolution of 1967. Egypt broke with this position by signing a peace treaty with Israel in 1979, followed by Jordan in 1994. In 2020 the Abraham Accords led the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco and Sudan to normalize relations with Israel.

Current status

A group of UN member states, including Iran, Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Saudi Arabia, Yemen, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela and most members of the Arab League that have not signed peace agreements, do not recognize Israel. Some of these states do not accept Israeli passports, and some prohibit entry to holders of passports containing Israeli stamps or visas.
