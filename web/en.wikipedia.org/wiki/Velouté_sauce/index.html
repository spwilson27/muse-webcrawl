Velouté sauce

Velouté sauce (French pronunciation: [vəluːte]) is a savoury sauce made from a roux (butter and flour) and a light stock. It is one of the five "mother sauces" of French cuisine listed by Auguste Escoffier in the early 20th century, along with espagnole, tomato, béchamel and hollandaise. The name means "velvety" in French.

Velouté sauce
TypeSauce
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsRoux, light stock (veal, chicken or fish)

Preparation

A blond roux is cooked briefly without colouring, then hot light stock is whisked in and simmered until smooth and lightly thickened, coating the back of a spoon. Varieties are named for the stock: veal velouté, chicken velouté and fish velouté.

Derivative sauces

Many small sauces derive from velouté: sauce allemande (with egg yolk and cream liaison), suprême (chicken velouté with cream), Normande (fish velouté with mushrooms and cream), and white wine sauce. Such derivatives serve poultry, seafood, eggs and vegetables.
