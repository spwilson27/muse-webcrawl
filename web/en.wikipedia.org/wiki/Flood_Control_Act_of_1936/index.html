Flood Control Act of 1936

The Flood Control Act of 1936 is a United States federal law that authorised the construction of dams, levees, dikes, and other flood control measures on navigable waters and their tributaries, enacting a national policy that flood control was a proper activity of the federal government.

Background

Passed in response to devastating floods including those on the Mississippi and Ohio rivers, the act followed earlier flood control legislation of 1917 and 1928.

It declared that destructive floods constituted a menace to national welfare and that the federal government should improve navigable waters in the interest of flood control.

Provisions

The act authorised numerous projects to be carried out by the United States Army Corps of Engineers and established cost-sharing principles under which local interests contributed land and rights-of-way.

Subsequent flood control acts in 1937, 1938, 1941, and later years amended and expanded its authorisations.
