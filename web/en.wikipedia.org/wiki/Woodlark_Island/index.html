Woodlark Island

LocationSolomon Sea, Papua New Guinea
ProvinceMilne Bay Province
Area~739 km²

Woodlark Island, known to its inhabitants as Muyua (also spelled Muyuw), is a volcanic island in the Solomon Sea, part of Milne Bay Province in Papua New Guinea. It lies east of the mainland in the Woodlark Islands group and is the largest island of that archipelago.

Geography and people

The island is roughly 60 km long, with forested lowlands and raised coral limestone. Its people speak Muyuw, an Austronesian language, and participate in regional exchange networks related to the Kula ring of the Massim. The main settlement is Kulumadau.

History

Woodlark experienced a gold rush in the 1890s following discoveries of alluvial and reef gold; mining has recurred periodically since. During the Second World War, Allied forces occupied the island in mid-1943—Operation Chronicle—building airfields at Guasopa Bay as part of the advance toward Rabaul. A large modern gold mine has operated near Kulumadau in recent decades, and proposals for deep-sea and large-scale mining have drawn local and environmental debate.

Ecology

The island hosts endemic species including the Woodlark cuscus. Logging and mining pressures make its lowland rainforest of conservation concern.
