Sherlock (TV series)

Sherlock is a British crime television series based on Sir Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes detective stories. Created by Steven Moffat and Mark Gatiss, it stars Benedict Cumberbatch as Sherlock Holmes and Martin Freeman as Doctor John Watson. The series is set in present-day London and was produced by Hartswood Films for the BBC, airing from 2010 to 2017.

Overview

Thirteen episodes were produced, in four series of three feature-length episodes each, plus a special episode, "The Abominable Bride" (2016), set partly in Victorian London. Holmes is portrayed as a brilliant, eccentric "high-functioning sociopath" consulting detective who assists the Metropolitan Police, principally Detective Inspector Greg Lestrade, while Watson — a former army doctor invalided home from Afghanistan — documents their cases on his blog. Recurring characters include Holmes's brother Mycroft, landlady Mrs Hudson, forensic pathologist Molly Hooper, and the criminal mastermind Jim Moriarty, played by Andrew Scott.

Episodes

The first series (2010) comprised "A Study in Pink", "The Blind Banker" and "The Great Game". The second series (2012) adapted three of the best-known Holmes stories as "A Scandal in Belgravia", "The Hounds of Baskerville" and "The Reichenbach Fall". The third series (2014) included "The Empty Hearse", "The Sign of Three" and "His Last Vow". The fourth series (2017) comprised "The Six Thatchers", "The Lying Detective" and "The Final Problem".

Reception

Sherlock received widespread critical acclaim and numerous awards, including Primetime Emmy Awards and BAFTAs. Cumberbatch and Freeman both won Emmys for their performances. The series developed a large international fan following and significantly boosted the careers of its leads. Its stylistic devices — on-screen text messages and visualised deductions — were widely praised and imitated.
