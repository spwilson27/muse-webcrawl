Sodium is the chemical element with the symbol Na (from Latin natrium) and atomic number 11. It is a soft, silvery, highly reactive alkali metal that tarnishes instantly in air and reacts violently with water, so it is stored under oil.

Sodium compounds are everywhere: sodium chloride (common salt) is essential to diet and industry, and sodium ions are crucial to nerve impulses and fluid balance in the body. Metallic sodium is used in some lamps and as a coolant in fast nuclear reactors.

History

Sodium was first isolated in 1807 by Humphry Davy, who electrolysed molten caustic soda (sodium hydroxide) at the Royal Institution in London — one of the first elements obtained by electrolysis.

Chemistry

Sodium readily loses its single outer electron to form Na⁺ ions. Dropped in water it melts into a ball, skates across the surface on a cushion of hydrogen gas, and may ignite with its characteristic intense yellow-orange flame — the same colour used in sodium-vapour street lamps.
