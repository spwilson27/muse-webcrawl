Bradford is a city in West Yorkshire, England, lying in the foothills of the Pennines about 8.6 miles (13.8 km) west of Leeds. It grew explosively in the 19th century as a centre of the woollen textile industry and became known as the "wool capital of the world". The metropolitan district, which includes Keighley, Shipley and Ilkley, has a population of over half a million.

History

Bradford (the "broad ford" over Bradford Beck) was a manor of the de Lacy family and a market town by the Middle Ages. Industrialisation transformed it: worsted mills multiplied, the Wool Exchange opened in 1867, and German merchants settled in the "Little Germany" district. Titus Salt built the model village of Saltaire (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) nearby. The city was a cradle of the Independent Labour Party, founded at Bradford in 1893.

Post-industrial Bradford

Textile decline in the later 20th century brought economic hardship, partly offset by a large South Asian community — many originally recruited to the mills — which has made Bradford famous for its curry restaurants. The National Science and Media Museum, the Alhambra Theatre and Bradford City A.F.C. and Bradford Bulls are landmarks of civic life; the 1985 Valley Parade fire disaster, which killed 56 spectators, remains a profound civic memory. Bradford was designated UK City of Culture 2025.

Geography and governance

The city is governed by Bradford Metropolitan District Council. It sits in a bowl of hills at the junction of three valleys, with the Bradford Canal and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal linking it to the wider network.
