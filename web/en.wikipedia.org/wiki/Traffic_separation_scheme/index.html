Traffic separation scheme

A traffic separation scheme (TSS) is a maritime traffic-management routeing measure managed by the International Maritime Organization (IMO). A TSS separates opposing streams of vessel traffic by the establishment of traffic lanes: vessels travelling in one direction use one lane, and vessels travelling in the opposite direction use another, with the lanes divided by a separation zone or line. TSSs are used to regulate traffic in busy, confined waterways or around capes where traffic converges.

A traffic separation scheme: two one-way lanes divided by a separation zone.

Rules

The use of traffic separation schemes is governed by Rule 10 of the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGS). A vessel using a TSS must proceed in the appropriate traffic lane in the general direction of traffic flow, keep clear of the separation line or zone as far as practicable, and join or leave a lane at its termination or at a small angle. Vessels under 20 metres, sailing vessels, and fishing vessels must not impede the safe passage of power-driven vessels following a traffic lane. Crossing a lane is permitted when necessary but should be done as nearly as practicable at right angles to the traffic flow.

Notable schemes

The first internationally adopted TSS was established in the Dover Strait in 1967, the world's busiest shipping lane, following a series of collisions; it was also the first to be made mandatory and radar-surveilled. Other major schemes serve the Strait of Malacca and Singapore Strait, the approaches to Rotterdam and the German Bight, the Strait of Gibraltar, the Torres Strait, and the approaches to San Francisco and the English Channel. In total, more than 250 routeing measures of various kinds have been adopted by the IMO.

Enforcement

Compliance is monitored by coastal Vessel Traffic Services (VTS) using radar and AIS. The Dover Strait scheme, for example, is jointly monitored by the British and French coastguards (Channel Navigation Information Service), and vessels that contravene the scheme are regularly prosecuted by the flag or coastal state.
