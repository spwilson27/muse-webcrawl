William Randolph Hearst

William Randolph Hearst
Born29 April 1863, San Francisco
Died14 August 1951
OccupationPublisher, Hearst Communications founder
[Image: Hearst Castle at San Simeon, California]

William Randolph Hearst (1863–1951) was an American newspaper magnate who built the largest media chain of his era from the San Francisco Examiner and the New York Journal, pioneered yellow journalism, and amassed newspapers, magazines (Cosmopolitan, Harper's Bazaar), newsreels and radio. His life and castle at San Simeon partly inspired Orson Welles' Citizen Kane (1941).

Empire and politics

Hearst served in Congress, sought the presidency and New York offices, and used his papers to campaign on issues from war (1898) to prohibition of 'Reefer Madness'-era campaigns. His empire strained in the Depression but the company bearing his name remains a major private media group.
