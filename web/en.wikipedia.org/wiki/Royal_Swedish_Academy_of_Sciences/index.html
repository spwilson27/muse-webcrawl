The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (Swedish: Kungliga Vetenskapsakademien) is one of the Swedish Royal Academies, founded in 1739. It is an independent, non-governmental scientific organisation whose fellows include Carl Linnaeus among its founders.

Role

The Academy promotes science, publishes journals, runs research institutes (such as the Beijer Institute), and awards major prizes: the Nobel Prizes in Physics and Chemistry, the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, the Crafoord Prize, and the Rolf Schock Prizes.

Structure

It has about 470 Swedish and 175 foreign members divided into ten classes spanning mathematics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, geoscience, biology, medicine, engineering, and social sciences, based at Frescati in Stockholm.
