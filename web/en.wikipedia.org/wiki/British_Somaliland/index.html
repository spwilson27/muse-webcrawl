British Somaliland was a British protectorate in the Horn of Africa, on the southern coast of the Gulf of Aden. Established in 1884, it covered much of the territory of the present-day self-declared (but internationally unrecognised) Republic of Somaliland in north-western Somalia. It gained independence on 26 June 1960 as the State of Somaliland and united five days later with the former Italian Somaliland to form the Somali Republic.

Establishment

Britain's interest centred on the port of Berbera, which supplied the garrison at Aden with meat and provisions. In 1884–85, following the Egyptian withdrawal from the region, Britain concluded treaties with Somali clans and assumed protection over the coast. The protectorate was administered from India until 1898 and then by the Foreign and Colonial Offices; its capital was Berbera, moving to Hargeisa in 1941.

The Dervish war

From 1899 to 1920 the protectorate was convulsed by the resistance of the Dervish movement led by Mohammed Abdullah Hassan, whom the British called the "Mad Mullah". Four expeditions failed to defeat him, and the British repeatedly withdrew to the coast. The campaign ended in 1920 with an early use of air power by the RAF, and Hassan died of influenza later that year.

Later years and independence

In August 1940, during the Second World War, Italian forces from Ethiopia invaded and occupied British Somaliland — the only British territory conquered by Italy in the war; British forces retook it in March 1941. After the war, political parties developed and demands for union with the other Somali territories grew. British Somaliland became independent on 26 June 1960 and, on 1 July, united with the Trust Territory of Somaliland (the former Italian colony) to create the Somali Republic. In 1991, after the collapse of the Somali state, the north-west declared the restoration of the independence of Somaliland.
