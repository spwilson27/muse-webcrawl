Lex Alamannorum

The Lex Alamannorum is the law code of the Alamanni, a Germanic people of early medieval southwestern Germany, compiled under Frankish overlordship. It is one of the principal leges barbarorum — the written tribal law codes of the early Middle Ages — and survives in some fifty manuscripts.

Versions and dating

The code exists in two main recensions. The earlier Pactus legis Alamannorum, of the early 7th century (traditionally dated to about 613–623, in the reign of Chlothar II), survives only in fragments. The fuller Lex Alamannorum proper was compiled in the early 8th century, around 712–725, during the rule of Duke Lantfrid of Alamannia, though under Frankish suzerainty.

Contents

The code opens with matters concerning the Church, protecting church property and clergy, then treats the duke and his officials, and finally the free population. Much of it consists of tariffed compositions (wergild and fines) for injuries, theft, and homicide, scaled by the victim's rank — noble, free, half-free, or slave. It regulates inheritance, marriage, duelling, and offences against the duke's peace, offering detailed evidence for Alamannic society and its relations with the Frankish kingdom.

Significance

Together with the contemporary Lex Baiuvariorum for Bavaria, the Lex Alamannorum illustrates how the peripheral duchies of the Frankish world codified customary law in Latin while accommodating Roman and ecclesiastical influence.
