Belgrave Square

Belgrave Square is a grand garden square in Belgravia, in the City of Westminster, London. Laid out in the 1820s for the 2nd Earl Grosvenor, it is one of the largest and most architecturally distinguished squares in London, comprising four terraces of stucco-fronted townhouses around a private central garden. From the nineteenth century it was among the most fashionable addresses in the capital, and today many of its houses serve as embassies, diplomatic residences and institutional offices.

Architecture

The square was designed by George Basevi and built largely by Thomas Cubitt between 1825 and about 1835. Each side consists of a palace-like terrace of tall white stucco houses, mostly Grade I listed, with detached villas at three corners, including Seaford House and Lindsey House. The fourth corner is occupied by the Spanish Embassy's area near St Michael's Church, Chester Square side. Statues in and around the square commemorate figures including Prince Henry the Navigator and José de San Martín, reflecting the square's diplomatic character.

Residents and uses

Early residents included dukes and cabinet ministers; the square was home at various times to the Duke of Bedford, the Duke of Buccleuch and Lord Reith, first director-general of the BBC. During the twentieth century many houses passed to embassies — among them those of Spain, Portugal, Norway, Turkey, Bahrain and Germany at various times — and to organisations such as the Royal College of Defence Studies. The private gardens at the centre, about 8 acres, contain plane trees, tennis courts and statues, and are maintained for the square's occupants.
