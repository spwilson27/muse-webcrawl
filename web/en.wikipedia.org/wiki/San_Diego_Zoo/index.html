The San Diego Zoo is a zoo in Balboa Park, San Diego, California, housing more than 12,000 animals of more than 650 species and subspecies on 100 acres of Balboa Park leased from the city of San Diego. Its parent organization, San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, is a private nonprofit conservation organization and has one of the largest zoological membership associations in the world, with more than 250,000 member households.

History

The zoo was founded by Dr. Harry M. Wegeforth, who was inspired by the roar of a lion left over from the 1915 Panama–California Exposition. The Zoological Society of San Diego was established in 1916, and the zoo opened to the public in its present Balboa Park location in the early 1920s.

The San Diego Zoo pioneered cageless, moated exhibits designed to recreate natural habitats. Its Center for Reproduction of Endangered Species (now the Beckman Center for Conservation Research) became a leader in conservation science, famed for "frozen zoo" genetic banking and for breeding programs including the California condor and giant panda.

Exhibits

Major exhibits include Monkey Trails and Forest Tales, Elephant Odyssey, the Kopje, Tiger Trail, Lost Forest, Africa Rocks, and the Australian Outback. The zoo is renowned for its botanical collection—more than 700,000 plants—valued as highly as its animals, and for its Skyfari aerial tram.

From 1996 to 2019 the zoo hosted giant pandas under a research agreement with China, producing several cubs. Its sister facility is the San Diego Zoo Safari Park near Escondido.

Conservation

San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance works in more than 35 countries on conservation projects, including reintroduction programs for the California condor, mountain yellow-legged frog, and 'alalā (Hawaiian crow).
