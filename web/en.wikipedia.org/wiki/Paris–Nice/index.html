Paris–Nice is a professional cycling stage race held each March, traditionally starting near Paris and finishing in Nice on the French Riviera. Nicknamed the "Race to the Sun", it is one of the most prestigious week-long stage races in the sport and forms part of the UCI World Tour.

The race was created in 1933 by Albert Lejeune, director of the newspaper Le Petit Journal, to publicise his publications in both Paris and Nice. Interrupted by the Second World War, it resumed in 1946 and has passed through several organisers; it is now run by the Amaury Sport Organisation (ASO), which also organises the Tour de France.

Route

The route typically begins with flat or time-trial stages in the Paris region, crosses the Massif Central or the Rhône valley — where crosswinds often split the peloton — and ends with mountainous stages in the hills behind Nice, frequently including the Col de la Madone and the Col d'Èze, before finishing on the Promenade des Anglais. The contrast between gloomy northern weather and the sunny Mediterranean finish inspired the race's nickname.

Notable winners

Sean Kelly holds the record with seven consecutive victories (1982–1988). Other multiple winners include Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Raymond Poulidor, Miguel Indurain, Alberto Contador, Richie Porte and Primož Roglič. The race is traditionally an early-season test for contenders for the Tour de France.
