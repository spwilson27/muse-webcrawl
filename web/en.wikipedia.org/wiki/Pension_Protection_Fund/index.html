Pension Protection Fund


Formation: 6 April 2005
Type: Statutory fund / public corporation
Purpose: Compensation for members of failed defined benefit pension schemes
Headquarters: Croydon, London

The Pension Protection Fund (PPF) is a statutory pension protection scheme in the United Kingdom, established by the Pensions Act 2004 and launched on 6 April 2005. It pays compensation to members of eligible defined benefit (final salary) occupational pension schemes when the sponsoring employer becomes insolvent and the scheme lacks sufficient assets to pay benefits at PPF levels.

How it works

When a qualifying insolvency event occurs, the scheme enters an assessment period during which the PPF tests whether it can be rescued or whether it should transfer into the Fund. Members already over the scheme's normal pension age generally receive 100 per cent of their pension; others receive 90 per cent subject to a compensation cap, with limited indexation. The PPF is funded by the assets of schemes that transfer in, an annual levy on eligible schemes, and investment returns.

Scale

The PPF has taken in hundreds of failed schemes—including those of high-profile collapses such as BHS and Carillion—and pays compensation to well over a quarter of a million members, managing tens of billions of pounds of assets. It also administers the Financial Assistance Scheme on behalf of the government.

Governance

The Fund is run by a board accountable to Parliament through the Department for Work and Pensions, and works alongside The Pensions Regulator, which supervises scheme funding and employer obligations.
