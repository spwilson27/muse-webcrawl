Misuse of Drugs Act (Singapore)

Enacted1973
Administered byCentral Narcotics Bureau
JurisdictionSingapore

The Misuse of Drugs Act 1973 is a drug control law in Singapore that classifies substances as controlled drugs, sets out offences for trafficking, manufacture, import, export, possession and consumption, and prescribes penalties including long imprisonment, caning, and the death penalty for trafficking above specified quantities.

Overview

The Act classifies controlled drugs into three classes — Class A, Class B and Class C — listed in its First Schedule. It creates a legal presumption of trafficking when a person possesses more than certain threshold amounts of a drug, such as 15 grams of diamorphine (heroin), 30 grams of cocaine, 250 grams of methamphetamine, or 500 grams of cannabis. Trafficking above the capital thresholds carries the mandatory death penalty, subject to limited exceptions introduced by amendments in 2012 that give courts discretion where the offender acted only as a courier and either cooperated substantively with the Central Narcotics Bureau or suffered from a qualifying abnormality of mind.

Enforcement

The Act is enforced primarily by the Central Narcotics Bureau (CNB). It also provides for drug rehabilitation regimes, under which persons found consuming drugs may be detained in drug rehabilitation centres rather than prosecuted for a first or second offence.

Criticism

Singapore's drug laws, and the mandatory death penalty in particular, have drawn criticism from international human rights organisations, while the Singapore government maintains that the laws are a necessary deterrent that has kept drug abuse rates low.
