Tomoyuki Yamashita

Tomoyuki Yamashita (山下 奉文, 8 November 1885 – 23 February 1946) was a general of the Imperial Japanese Army during World War II. He is best known for conquering Malaya and Singapore in 1941–42, earning the nickname "Tiger of Malaya".

Yamashita's forces advanced down the Malay Peninsula on bicycles and captured Singapore, then considered an impregnable fortress, in February 1942, taking some 80,000 British, Australian, and Indian troops prisoner. He later commanded Japanese forces in the Philippines, where he surrendered in September 1945 after a prolonged campaign.

Trial and execution

Yamashita was tried by an American military commission in Manila for war crimes committed by troops under his command, including the Manila massacre. He was convicted under the doctrine of command responsibility — controversially, since the prosecution did not prove he ordered the atrocities — and hanged on 23 February 1946. The case established the "Yamashita standard" in the law of command responsibility.
