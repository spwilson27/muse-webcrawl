Garden of Eden

In Abrahamic religions, the Garden of Eden (Hebrew: גַּן־עֵדֶן, gan-ʿĒḏen) or Garden of God is the biblical earthly paradise created by God to be inhabited by his first human creations, Adam and Eve. In the Book of Genesis, the garden is the setting for the creation of humanity and for the Fall of man: after eating the forbidden fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, Adam and Eve are expelled from the garden.

Biblical narrative

Genesis 2 describes God planting a garden "in Eden, in the east", placing in it the man he had formed, and causing every tree pleasant to the sight and good for food to grow there, including the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. A river flows out of Eden to water the garden and divides into four headwaters: the Pishon, the Gihon, the Tigris (Hiddekel) and the Euphrates. Adam is put in the garden "to till it and keep it", and Eve is created as his companion.

In Genesis 3, a serpent tempts Eve to eat the forbidden fruit; she gives some to Adam, and their eyes are opened to their nakedness. God curses the serpent and the ground, and expels the couple from the garden, stationing cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life.

Location

The identification of the Euphrates and Tigris among the four rivers has led many to place Eden in Mesopotamia, in present-day Iraq, though the Pishon and Gihon have no agreed identifications. Proposals have included the head of the Persian Gulf, Armenia, and southern Mesopotamia near the marshes where the Tigris and Euphrates meet. Many scholars treat the garden as a mythological rather than a geographical location.

In other traditions

The Quran describes the garden (Jannah or the Garden of Eden, ʿAdn) where Adam and his wife dwelt before their fall to earth. Eden is also referenced in later biblical books, including Ezekiel and Isaiah, and the garden became a rich subject in Jewish and Christian art, literature and theology, often contrasted with the restored paradise of Revelation. In the theology of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the garden is located in Missouri, Adam-ondi-Ahman being the place Adam dwelt after expulsion.
