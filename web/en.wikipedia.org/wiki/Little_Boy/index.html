Little Boy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Little Boy
Type: Uranium gun-type bomb
Yield: about 15 kilotons
Detonated: Over Hiroshima, 6 August 1945

Little Boy was the codename for the uranium gun-type atomic bomb dropped on the Japanese city of Hiroshima on 6 August 1945 — the first nuclear weapon used in warfare.

The design was simple and considered so reliable it was never tested before combat use: a cordite charge fired one subcritical piece of uranium-235 down a gun barrel into another, forming a supercritical mass and a runaway chain reaction. The bomb was about 3 metres long and contained 64 kg of uranium, of which only a small fraction actually fissioned.

Carried by the B-29 Enola Gay, commanded by Colonel Paul Tibbets, Little Boy exploded some 580 metres above the city with a yield of about 15 kilotons. The explosion and firestorm destroyed most of Hiroshima and killed tens of thousands of people immediately, with many more dying later from injuries and radiation. Three days later the plutonium bomb Fat Man was dropped on Nagasaki.
