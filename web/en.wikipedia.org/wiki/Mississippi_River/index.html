Mississippi River

[Image: The Mississippi River at St. Louis with the Gateway Arch]
The Mississippi River at St. Louis with the Gateway Arch

The Mississippi River is the primary river of the second-largest drainage basin in the world. It is the second-longest river in North America at about 3,730 kilometres (some measurements, including the Missouri and Jefferson tributaries, make the system longer than the Nile). The river flows from Lake Itasca in northern Minnesota generally southward through the centre of the United States and into the Gulf of Mexico via the Mississippi Delta in Louisiana.

The Mississippi drains all or part of 32 US states and two Canadian provinces. Cities on its banks include Minneapolis, St. Louis, Memphis, Vicksburg, Baton Rouge and New Orleans. Its name derives from an Ojibwe word meaning 'great river'.

Economy

The Mississippi is one of the world's most important commercial waterways: barges move grain, coal, petroleum and other bulk goods between the agricultural Midwest and Gulf ports. A system of locks and dams, built largely in the 1930s, maintains navigation on the upper river.

Culture

The river is central to American literature and music, most famously in Mark Twain's Adventures of Huckleberry Finn and Life on the Mississippi, and in the history of steamboats, blues and jazz. The river's floods, including the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927, reshaped the region.
