The Nuyorican Poets Cafe is a non-profit organisation and performance venue in the Alphabet City neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, and a central institution of the Nuyorican Movement. It was founded in 1973 by Miguel Algarín, Miguel Piñero, Pedro Pietri and other Puerto Rican writers and artists, initially in Algarín's apartment and then in rented spaces, before settling in its present home at 236 East 3rd Street in 1980.

Activities

The Cafe hosts poetry open mics, theatre productions, Latin jazz, hip hop and film events, and is credited as a birthplace of the poetry slam: its Friday night slam, developed in the 1990s under figures such as Bob Holman, became internationally famous, and teams from the Cafe won national slam titles. Performers associated with the Cafe include Pedro Pietri, Tato Laviera, Sandra María Esteves, and later spoken-word artists who went on to broader fame; the venue also appeared in popular culture through the Def Poetry movement and the 1990s poetry scene.

Significance

The Nuyorican Poets Cafe gave an institutional home to a generation of Puerto Rican and Latino writers who were largely excluded from mainstream publishing, blending poetry with performance, bilingual wordplay and political activism. It remains an active cultural landmark, presenting emerging poets and artists and preserving the legacy of the Nuyorican Movement.
