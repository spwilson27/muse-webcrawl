PenTile matrix family

TypeSubpixel rendering / display matrix
DeveloperClairvoyante; later Samsung
LayoutsRGBG, RGBW, RGBY

The PenTile matrix family is a family of subpixel arrangements for electronic displays, developed by the company Clairvoyante and acquired by Samsung in 2008. Unlike a conventional RGB stripe display, in which every pixel contains one red, one green, and one blue subpixel, PenTile layouts use repeating groups with fewer subpixels per pixel, exploiting subpixel rendering techniques that rely on the human visual system's greater sensitivity to luminance and green light than to colour detail.

Layouts

The most common variant, PenTile RGBG, alternates red-green and blue-green pixel pairs, so that green subpixels occur at full resolution while red and blue are sampled at half resolution. This layout has been used extensively in Samsung AMOLED smartphone screens. PenTile RGBW adds a white (unfiltered) subpixel to raise brightness and reduce power consumption, and was used in some LCD and OLED products, including early Motorola and Lenovo phones and the display of some Samsung devices.

Advantages and criticism

Manufacturers cite lower manufacturing cost, higher achievable brightness for a given power budget, and longer panel life — benefits that were significant for early OLED production. Critics note that at a given nominal resolution a PenTile panel renders text and fine detail less sharply than an RGB stripe panel, since the effective chrominance resolution is lower; early implementations were sometimes criticised for visible colour fringing. As pixel densities rose well above 300 pixels per inch, the practical impact of these differences largely disappeared.
