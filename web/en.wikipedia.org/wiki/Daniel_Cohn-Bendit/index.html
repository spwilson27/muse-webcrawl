Daniel Cohn-Bendit

Daniel Marc Cohn-Bendit (born 4 April 1945) is a French-German politician. He was a student leader during the unrest of May 1968 in France and was also active in the German student movement. During the 1968 events he became known by the nickname "Dany le Rouge" ("Danny the Red"), partly because of his politics and partly because of his red hair.

Cohn-Bendit went on to serve as a Member of the European Parliament for the German and French Green parties between 1994 and 2014, co-chairing the Greens–European Free Alliance group. He later became a political commentator, documentary filmmaker and public intellectual, publishing numerous works on European integration, ecology and the legacy of 1968.

Early life

Cohn-Bendit was born in Montauban, in south-western France, to German-Jewish parents who had fled Nazi Germany in 1933. His father, Erich Cohn-Bendit, was a lawyer and friend of the philosopher Hannah Arendt. Daniel grew up between France and Germany, attending the Odenwaldschule in Germany, and later studied sociology at the University of Paris at Nanterre under Alain Touraine.

May 1968

At Nanterre, Cohn-Bendit helped found the Movement of 22 March, named after the date students occupied the administration building in protest. When the university was closed in early May 1968, the movement spread to the Sorbonne in Paris and helped ignite the general crisis of May 1968, which grew into a nationwide strike involving millions of workers. The French government expelled Cohn-Bendit from France in May 1968, branding him an "undesirable" foreign agitator; students responded with the slogan "Nous sommes tous des Juifs allemands" ("We are all German Jews"). He was not permitted to return to France until 1978.

Career in Germany

In Frankfurt am Main, Cohn-Bendit became involved in the extra-parliamentary left, co-founding the group Revolutionärer Kampf and working in a left-wing bookstore and publishing house. In the 1980s he joined the Green Party and served as deputy mayor of Frankfurt (1989–1994) under mayor Andreas von Schoeler, holding the office for multicultural affairs — the first such post in Germany.

European Parliament

Cohn-Bendit was elected to the European Parliament in 1994 for the German Greens, then in 1999 for the French Greens, alternating between the two countries' lists. From 2002 he co-chaired the Greens/EFA group alongside Monica Frassoni and later Rebecca Harms. A committed federalist, he co-authored with Guy Verhofstadt the 2012 manifesto For Europe! calling for a post-national, federal European Union. He did not stand in the 2014 European elections.

Later work

After leaving parliament, Cohn-Bendit worked as a television and radio commentator, hosted cultural programmes, and supported Emmanuel Macron during the 2017 French presidential campaign. He remains a prominent voice in debates on European democracy, migration and climate policy.
