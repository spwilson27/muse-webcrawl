Jaume Plensa

The Crown Fountain in Chicago, one of Jaume Plensa's best-known public works

Jaume Plensa (born 1955) is a Spanish sculptor and artist from Barcelona, internationally known for monumental public artworks that combine sculpture, light, text, and sound, often centered on the human figure and on letters drawn from many alphabets.

Life and work

Plensa studied at the Escola de la Llotja and the Sant Jordi School of Fine Arts in Barcelona. His early work in the 1980s used forged iron; from the 1990s he increasingly used glass, resin, light, and stainless steel, producing translucent seated and kneeling figures formed from interwoven letters and symbols.

Major works

His most famous work is the Crown Fountain (2004) in Chicago's Millennium Park, where two 15-meter glass-brick towers display video portraits of Chicagoans whose mouths spout water like modern gargoyles. Other prominent installations include Breathing (2008), a memorial at the BBC's Broadcasting House in London; Echo (2011) in Madison Square Park, New York; and Water's Soul (2021), a 24-meter white head facing the Hudson River at Jersey City.

Themes

Plensa's work explores language as a metaphor for human community, the body as a vessel of memory and spirituality, and silence and contemplation in public space. He has exhibited worldwide and received Spain's National Prize for Plastic Arts (2012) and the Velázquez Prize for Plastic Arts (2013).
