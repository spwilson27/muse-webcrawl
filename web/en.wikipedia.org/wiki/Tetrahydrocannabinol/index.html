Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the principal psychoactive constituent of the cannabis plant. It was first isolated and its structure determined in 1964 by Raphael Mechoulam and colleagues in Israel. Chemically, THC is a cannabinoid with the formula C₂₁H₃₀O₂; the term usually refers to the naturally occurring Δ⁹ isomer (dronabinol in pharmaceutical form), while Δ⁸-THC is a closely related, less potent isomer.

Pharmacology

THC acts mainly as a partial agonist at cannabinoid CB1 receptors — densely expressed in the brain — and CB2 receptors, mimicking the endogenous cannabinoids anandamide and 2-AG. Its effects include euphoria, altered perception of time, relaxation, increased appetite, and impairment of short-term memory, attention and coordination. Higher doses may cause anxiety or transient psychotic-like symptoms, and heavy use is associated with cannabis use disorder and, in vulnerable individuals, increased risk of psychosis.

When smoked, THC is absorbed rapidly; oral ingestion gives slower onset and longer duration. THC is metabolised in the liver, principally by CYP2C9, to the active 11-hydroxy-THC and then to inactive THC-COOH, which is excreted and serves as the urinary marker in drug testing. THC is highly lipophilic and accumulates in fatty tissue.

Biosynthesis

In the plant, THC is produced in glandular trichomes as its acidic precursor THCA (from cannabigerolic acid via THCA synthase) and is converted to neutral THC by decarboxylation on heating or with age.

Medical use

Dronabinol and the synthetic analogue nabilone are approved in several countries for chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and (for dronabinol) AIDS-related appetite loss. Evidence supports modest benefit in some chronic pain and spasticity indications, generally as adjunctive therapy.

Legal status

THC is listed in the UN Single Convention framework and is a Schedule I substance under US federal law, though many US states and several countries (including Canada and Uruguay) permit recreational or medical cannabis, and dronabinol has been rescheduled separately in some systems.
