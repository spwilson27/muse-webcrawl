Tarantism

Tarantism is a form of hysterical behaviour once popular in southern Europe, chiefly associated with the region of Apulia (Puglia) in Italy. Sufferers, known as tarantati, were believed to have been bitten by a wolf spider — the tarantula (Lycosa tarantula) — and to be curable only through frenzied dancing to particular music, a ritual known as the tarantella.

History

The phenomenon is documented from at least the 11th century and reached its height in the 15th to 17th centuries, when outbreaks of dancing mania swept parts of Italy, especially around the city of Taranto, from which the spider and the dance take their names. Victims would dance for hours or days, accompanied by musicians playing fast, repetitive rhythms on tambourines, violins and guitars.

Cultural interpretation

Modern scholars generally interpret tarantism as a culture-bound syndrome rather than a toxicological reaction to spider venom, which is in fact relatively harmless to humans. The ritual provided a socially sanctioned outlet for psychological distress, particularly among poor rural women. The tradition survives in folk festivals such as the Notte della Taranta in Salento, which celebrates pizzica music and dance.
