A financial ratio or accounting ratio is a relative magnitude of two selected numerical values taken from an enterprise's financial statements. Financial ratios are used by managers, investors, and creditors to evaluate a company's liquidity, profitability, efficiency, and solvency, and to compare performance across periods or against other firms.

Liquidity ratios

Liquidity ratios measure a firm's ability to meet its short-term obligations. The current ratio divides current assets by current liabilities, while the more conservative quick ratio excludes inventory from the numerator.

Profitability ratios

Profitability ratios include gross and net profit margins, return on assets (ROA), and return on equity (ROE). The DuPont analysis decomposes ROE into profit margin, asset turnover, and financial leverage components.

Leverage ratios

Solvency or leverage ratios, such as the debt-to-equity ratio and the interest coverage ratio, indicate the extent to which a business is financed by debt and its capacity to service that debt.

Market ratios

Market valuation ratios relate a company's share price to its earnings, dividends, or book value; the price–earnings (P/E) ratio is the most widely quoted example.

Limitations

Ratios are only meaningful in comparison—with a firm's own history, its competitors, or industry benchmarks—and can be distorted by differences in accounting policies, one-off items, and seasonal effects.
