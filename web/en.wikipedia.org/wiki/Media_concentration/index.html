Concentration of media ownership

Concentration of media ownership, also known as media consolidation or media convergence, is a process whereby progressively fewer individuals or organizations control increasing shares of the mass media. Contemporary research has demonstrated increasing levels of consolidation, with many media industries already highly concentrated and dominated by a very small number of firms, such as Comcast, Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, Paramount, and News Corp in the United States.

Causes and debates

Concentration arises from deregulation (for example the US Telecommunications Act of 1996, which relaxed radio ownership caps), economies of scale, cross-ownership, and vertical integration. Critics argue that consolidation reduces viewpoint diversity, local news coverage, and competition, and gives owners outsized political influence. Defenders respond that fragmentation of audiences across digital platforms makes markets more competitive than ownership counts suggest, and that scale is needed to fund content production.

Regulation

Many countries impose ownership caps, cross-media rules, or plurality tests in merger review. The rise of large technology platforms has redirected debate toward the "attention economy" and the gatekeeping power of search and social media companies.
