Spinal muscular atrophy

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is a genetic neuromuscular disorder characterised by the loss of motor neurons in the spinal cord and brainstem, leading to progressive muscle wasting (atrophy) and weakness. It is caused in the great majority of cases by mutations in the SMN1 gene on chromosome 5 and is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. SMA must be distinguished from spinal stenosis, a mechanical narrowing of the spinal canal with a completely different cause.

Classification

SMA is traditionally classified by the age of onset and highest motor milestone achieved: type 0 (prenatal), type 1 (infantile, historically Werdnig–Hoffmann disease; children never sit), type 2 (children sit but never walk independently), type 3 (Kugelberg–Welander disease; children walk), and type 4 (adult onset). Severity correlates largely with the copy number of the backup gene SMN2.

Treatment

Since 2016, disease-modifying therapies have transformed the prognosis: nusinersen (Spinraza), an antisense oligonucleotide that increases SMN protein from SMN2; onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma), a one-time gene replacement therapy approved in 2019; and the oral small molecule risdiplam (Evrysdi), approved in 2020. Newborn screening now allows treatment before symptoms appear.
