Shiva Sutras of Vasugupta

The Shiva Sutras of Vasugupta (Sanskrit: Śivasūtra) are a foundational scripture of Kashmir Shaivism, traditionally attributed to the sage Vasugupta, who lived around the late eighth or early ninth century CE in Kashmir. The text consists of 77 aphorisms (sutras) describing the nature of consciousness and the path to self-realization.

Origin

According to legend, the god Shiva appeared to Vasugupta in a dream and directed him to a large rock (Shankarpal) on the Mahadev mountain near Srinagar, on which the sutras were inscribed. Vasugupta is said to have taught them to his disciples, and the text was later commented upon by scholars such as Bhaskara and Kshemaraja.

Contents

The sutras are divided into three sections corresponding to three means (upāya) of realization: śāmbhavopāya, the divine or immediate means; śāktopāya, the means of energy, centered on thought and contemplation; and āṇavopāya, the individual means, involving breath, mantra, and meditation on the body. The opening sutra, caitanyam ātmā, declares that consciousness itself is the Self.

Influence

The Shiva Sutras stand at the head of the Trika tradition of Kashmir Shaivism and influenced later teachers including Abhinavagupta. Commentaries remain widely studied by practitioners and scholars of Shaiva philosophy.
