Staatskapelle Dresden

The Staatskapelle Dresden (Saxon State Orchestra, Dresden) is a German orchestra based in Dresden, founded in 1548 by Elector Maurice of Saxony. It is among the oldest orchestras in the world in continuous existence and serves as the orchestra of the Saxon State Opera at the Semperoper.

History

The ensemble's early masters included Heinrich Schütz, who directed Dresden's court music in the seventeenth century. Johann Adolph Hasse and later Carl Maria von Weber shaped its operatic tradition, and Richard Wagner — Kapellmeister in Dresden in the 1840s — conducted the premieres of Rienzi, The Flying Dutchman and Tannhäuser there. Under Ernst von Schuch, the orchestra premiered several operas of Richard Strauss, including Salome, Elektra and Der Rosenkavalier, beginning a Strauss tradition closely associated with the orchestra.

Principal conductors

Twentieth-century chief conductors included Fritz Busch, Karl Böhm, Franz Konwitschny, Kurt Sanderling and Herbert Blomstedt; later came Giuseppe Sinopoli, Bernard Haitink as principal conductor, Fabio Luisi, and Christian Thielemann (2012–2024). The orchestra performs around 260 opera and ballet performances a season at the Semperoper alongside symphony concerts, tours internationally, and has made an extensive catalogue of recordings.
