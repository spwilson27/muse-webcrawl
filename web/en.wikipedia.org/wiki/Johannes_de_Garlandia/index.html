Johannes de Garlandia

Johannes de Garlandia (fl. c. 1230–1275) was a French music theorist of the early Gothic period, associated with the University of Paris and the Notre-Dame school of polyphony. He should not be confused with the English grammarian and poet John of Garland of roughly the same era, with whom he was long conflated.

Works

Two treatises are attributed to him: De mensurabili musica (c. 1240) and Introductio musice. The De mensurabili musica is the earliest known treatise to discuss measured (mensural) music systematically, describing the six rhythmic modes and the notation of the Notre-Dame repertory of organum and conductus.

Significance

Garlandia's writings are the principal source for how 13th-century musicians understood rhythmic modes before the innovations of Franco of Cologne. His accounts of musica ficta and of the rules for dissonance treatment inform modern performances of medieval polyphony.
