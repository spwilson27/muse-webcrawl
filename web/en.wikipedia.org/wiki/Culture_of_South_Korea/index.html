Culture of South Korea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Placeholder image

The culture of South Korea is the shared cultural heritage of Korea as it has developed since the division of the Korean Peninsula in 1948. Rooted in Confucian, Buddhist and shamanic traditions and in the Korean language and Hangul script, it has combined rapid modernization with strong family and educational values. Since the late 1990s South Korean popular culture has spread worldwide in the phenomenon known as the Korean Wave, or Hallyu.

Traditions and society

Confucian values of filial piety, respect for elders and hierarchy — reflected in honorific speech levels — remain influential. Traditional arts include pansori narrative singing, talchum mask dance, celadon pottery, hanbok dress and a cuisine built around rice, kimchi, soups and shared side dishes (banchan). Major holidays are Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (harvest festival), when families honour ancestors through the charye rite.

The Korean Wave

K-pop groups such as BTS and Blackpink, television dramas (K-dramas), and films — Bong Joon-ho's Parasite (2019) won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and the series Squid Game (2021) became a global streaming phenomenon — made South Korean culture a leading soft-power export, driving interest in the Korean language, food, beauty products and tourism.

Technology and daily life

South Korea is among the world's most connected societies, with a famous PC bang (gaming café) and esports culture, ubiquitous high-speed internet, and a strong national emphasis on education and examination success.
