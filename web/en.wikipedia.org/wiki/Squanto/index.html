Squanto

Squanto (Tisquantum), c. 1585 – 1622.

Tisquantum (c. 1585 – November 30, 1622), more commonly known as Squanto, was a member of the Patuxet tribe of Wampanoag people best known for being an early liaison between the Native American population in Southern New England and the Mayflower Pilgrims who made their settlement at the site of Tisquantum's former summer village, now Plymouth, Massachusetts. The Patuxet tribe had lived in villages near the site for centuries before the arrival of the English.

Early life and captivity

In 1614, Squanto was captured by English explorer Thomas Hunt, who carried him and other Native Americans to Spain to be sold as slaves. Squanto escaped, eventually reaching London, where he lived for a time with merchant John Slany. He learned English and in 1619 joined an expedition back to New England. On returning home, he discovered that the Patuxet had been devastated by an epidemic — traditionally identified as smallpox or leptospirosis — that had swept the coast between 1616 and 1619, killing most of his people.

Role at Plymouth

In March 1621, Squanto was introduced to the Plymouth colonists by Samoset, an Abenaki sagamore. Acting as interpreter and guide, Squanto taught the Pilgrims how to cultivate maize, using fish as fertilizer, and how to fish and gather food from the land and sea. He helped broker the peace treaty between the colonists and the Wampanoag sachem Massasoit (Ousamequin), which lasted for decades. He served as an emissary and translator until his death.

Death

Squanto died in November 1622 at Chatham, Massachusetts, while acting as a guide on an expedition around Cape Cod. Governor William Bradford recorded that he was seized by a fever and bleeding at the nose, which Bradford took as a sign of imminent death. He was buried in an unmarked grave, most likely in Chatham.
