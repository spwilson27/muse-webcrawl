SANZAAR is a joint venture of the rugby union governing bodies of South Africa (SARU), New Zealand (NZR), Australia (Rugby Australia) and Argentina (UAR). The name is an acronym of South Africa, New Zealand and Australia Rugby, with the additional "A" reflecting Argentina's membership.

History

The body was founded in 1996 as SANZAR, at the dawn of professional rugby union, to administer the Super 12 club competition and the Tri Nations international series between the Springboks, All Blacks and Wallabies. Argentina's Pumas joined the international competition in 2012, which was renamed The Rugby Championship, and the organisation became SANZAAR.

SANZAAR administered the Super Rugby club competition until the disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, after which South African franchises moved to European competitions and the trans-Tasman nations established Super Rugby Pacific under their own governance. SANZAAR continues to run The Rugby Championship, the premier annual international tournament of the southern hemisphere.
