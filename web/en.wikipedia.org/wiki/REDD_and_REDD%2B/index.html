REDD and REDD+

REDD+
Full nameReducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation
FrameworkUNFCCC
Key agreementWarsaw Framework (2013); Paris Agreement (2015)

REDD (Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation) is a framework developed under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change to create financial value for the carbon stored in forests, offering incentives for developing countries to reduce emissions from forested lands. REDD+ extends the scope to include conservation, sustainable management of forests, and enhancement of forest carbon stocks.

History

The concept was first proposed at the 2005 Montreal climate conference, expanded by the 2007 Bali Action Plan, and given a full rulebook by the Warsaw Framework for REDD-plus in 2013. Article 5 of the 2015 Paris Agreement affirmed its role in climate mitigation.

Criticism

Debate surrounds measurement and verification of emission reductions, permanence, leakage, and the rights of Indigenous peoples and forest communities, as well as the integrity of forest carbon credits sold in voluntary markets.
