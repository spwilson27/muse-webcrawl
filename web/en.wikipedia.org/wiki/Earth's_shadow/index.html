Earth's shadow

Earth's shadow (or Earth shadow) is the shadow that Earth itself casts through its atmosphere and into outer space, toward the antisolar point. During twilight, the shadow is visible in the sky above the horizon opposite the Sun.

Earth's shadow
Phenomenon: Atmospheric optics
Visible: Twilight, opposite the Sun
Related: Belt of Venus, lunar eclipse

Appearance

As the Sun sets in the west, a dark blue-grey band rises in the east — Earth's shadow projected on the atmosphere — topped by a pink band called the Belt of Venus, caused by backscattered reddened sunlight.

Lunar eclipses

When the full Moon passes through Earth's shadow, a lunar eclipse occurs: the umbra totally darkens the Moon, which often appears red from sunlight refracted through Earth's atmosphere.
