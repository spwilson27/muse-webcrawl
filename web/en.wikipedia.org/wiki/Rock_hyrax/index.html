Rock hyrax

The rock hyrax (Procavia capensis), also called the dassie, Cape hyrax, rock rabbit, or (from some interpretations) coney, is a medium-sized terrestrial mammal native to Africa and the Middle East. Commonly referred to in South Africa as the dassie, it is one of the five living species of the order Hyracoidea, and the only one in the genus Procavia.

Description

Rock hyraxes are squat, heavily built animals weighing 4–5 kg (9–11 lb), with short ears and a short tail. Their feet have rubbery, moist pads that aid in climbing steep rock surfaces. Despite their rodent-like appearance, hyraxes are most closely related to elephants and manatees.

Behaviour

They live in colonies of up to 80 individuals, with a sentinel posted to watch for predators such as leopards, eagles, and pythons. Hyraxes are poor thermoregulators and spend much of the morning basking in the sun. Their complex vocalisations include songs by territorial males.

Distribution

The species occurs across sub-Saharan Africa in rocky habitats, and along the Arabian Peninsula to Lebanon, where it is mentioned in the Bible as the "coney".
