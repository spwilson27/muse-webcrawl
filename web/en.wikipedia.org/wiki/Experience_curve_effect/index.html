Experience curve effect

The experience curve effect is the observation that the cost of producing a unit of output tends to fall by a roughly constant percentage each time cumulative production doubles. The concept was first quantified in the 1930s in aircraft manufacturing and was formalised and popularised by the Boston Consulting Group in the 1960s under Bruce Henderson.

The effect is broader than the narrower learning curve: besides labour efficiency, it encompasses improved processes, product redesign, economies of scale, and technological progress. Experience curves have been used in business strategy — for example in pricing ahead of cost declines — and in forecasting cost reductions for technologies such as solar photovoltaic modules, where the related regularity is known as Swanson's law.
