Göttingen Seven

The Göttingen Seven (German: Göttinger Sieben) were seven professors at the University of Göttingen who protested in 1837 against the abolition of the liberal constitution of the Kingdom of Hanover by the new king, Ernest Augustus I, and were dismissed from their posts.

The protest

When Ernest Augustus acceded to the Hanoverian throne in 1837, he annulled the constitution of 1833. The historian Friedrich Christoph Dahlmann drafted a formal protest arguing that the professors' oath to the constitution remained binding. Six colleagues signed with him: the brothers Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm, the jurist Wilhelm Eduard Albrecht, the orientalist Heinrich Ewald, the physicist Wilhelm Eduard Weber, and the literary historian Georg Gottfried Gervinus.

Consequences

The king dismissed all seven from the university in December 1837, and three of them — including the Brothers Grimm — were ordered to leave the kingdom. The case became a celebrated cause throughout Germany: the seven were supported by public subscriptions, most found posts elsewhere, and several later sat in the Frankfurt Parliament of 1848. The affair remains a landmark in the history of academic freedom and constitutionalism in Germany.
