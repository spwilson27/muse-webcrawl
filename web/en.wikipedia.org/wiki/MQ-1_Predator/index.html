The General Atomics MQ-1 Predator is an American remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) built by General Atomics and used primarily by the United States Air Force and Central Intelligence Agency. Originally conceived in the early 1990s for aerial reconnaissance and forward observation roles, the Predator was later modified to carry and fire two AGM-114 Hellfire missiles, becoming one of the first armed unmanned combat aerial vehicles.

Development

The Predator system consists of four aircraft with sensors, a ground control station and a satellite link. The aircraft was deployed operationally to the Balkans in 1995 and subsequently to Afghanistan and Iraq, where its long endurance — over 24 hours for surveillance missions — and live video feed transformed battlefield reconnaissance.

Operations

Armed Predators have been used extensively for intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR) and for strikes in counter-terrorism campaigns. The type's use in strikes outside declared war zones has been the subject of legal and ethical debate. The US Air Force retired the MQ-1 in 2018, replacing it with the larger MQ-9 Reaper, while export variants and derivatives such as the MQ-1C Gray Eagle continue in service.

Specifications

The MQ-1 is 8.22 m (27 ft) long with a wingspan of 14.8 m (48.7 ft), is powered by a Rotax piston engine driving a pusher propeller, and has a maximum speed of about 217 km/h (135 mph) with a service ceiling around 7,600 m (25,000 ft).
