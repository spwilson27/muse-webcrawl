Time

Time is the continued sequence of existence and events that occurs in an apparently irreversible succession from the past, through the present, and into the future. It is a component quantity of various measurements used to sequence events, compare durations, and quantify rates of change.

Measurement

The SI unit of time is the second, defined since 1967 by the frequency of radiation from the caesium-133 atom. Historically, timekeeping relied on astronomy — the rotation of the Earth and the cycles of the Moon — giving rise to calendars. Modern civil time is coordinated through Coordinated Universal Time (UTC).

Philosophy

Philosophers debate whether time is fundamental or emergent, and whether only the present exists (presentism) or past and future are equally real (eternalism). Augustine, Newton, Leibniz, Kant and Heidegger all developed influential accounts.

Physics

In Newtonian mechanics time is absolute and flows uniformly. Einstein's relativity made time relative: elapsed time depends on the observer's motion and gravitational field, and time is woven with space into spacetime. The thermodynamic arrow of time is associated with increasing entropy. Whether time had a beginning — at the Big Bang — remains an open question in cosmology.

Perception

Humans perceive time through circadian rhythms and psychological processes; perceived duration varies with attention, age and emotion.
