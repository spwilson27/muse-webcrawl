Human tooth

A human tooth is a hard, calcified structure found in the jaws, used for biting and chewing food. Humans are diphyodont — they develop two sets of teeth: 20 deciduous (primary or "milk") teeth in childhood and up to 32 permanent teeth in adulthood.

Structure

The visible crown is covered by enamel, the hardest substance in the human body. Beneath it lies dentin, and at the centre the pulp contains nerves and blood vessels. The root is anchored in the jawbone by cementum and the periodontal ligament. Teeth are classified by shape and function: incisors cut, canines tear, premolars and molars crush and grind.

Health

Dental caries (tooth decay) and periodontal disease are among the most prevalent human conditions, driven by bacterial plaque and dietary sugars; fluoride, oral hygiene and dentistry are the main preventive measures. Dental anatomy is also used in forensic identification and anthropology.
