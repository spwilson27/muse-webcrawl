Haugbui

A haugbui (Old Norse haugbúi, "mound-dweller") is an undead creature in Norse mythology and Scandinavian folklore that inhabits a burial mound. Unlike the wandering draugr, which ranges widely from its grave, the haugbui is bound to its mound and rarely leaves it, typically acting only when the mound itself is disturbed or its treasure threatened.

Characteristics

In the sagas, a haugbui is usually the spirit or reanimated body of the person buried in the mound — often a king, chieftain or wealthy farmer buried with grave goods. Those who break into the mound to rob it must wrestle or contend with its guardian. Famous mound-breaking episodes in Old Norse literature include the hero Grettir's descent into the mound of Kár the Old and Hervör's summoning of her father Angantýr from his mound to demand his sword, Tyrfing.

Interpretation

Scholars have connected the haugbui with ancestor veneration: the dead chieftain remaining in his mound could be consulted or propitiated, and the mound served as a focus of local cult. The belief also provided a supernatural explanation for the sanctity of burial sites and a deterrent against grave robbery.
