Herbarium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A herbarium (plural: herbaria) is a collection of preserved plant specimens and associated data used for scientific study. Specimens are usually pressed and dried, mounted on sheets of paper, labelled with collection data (species name, locality, date, collector), and stored in cabinets in systematic order. Herbaria are essential resources for taxonomy, documenting plant diversity, distributions, and changes over time.

History

The practice of pressing plants between sheets of paper is credited to the Italian physician and botanist Luca Ghini in the 16th century. The oldest surviving herbaria date from this period. Carl Linnaeus's own herbarium, used in developing his classification system, is preserved at the Linnean Society of London.

Major collections

The largest herbaria include those of the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle in Paris, the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, and the New York Botanical Garden, each holding millions of specimens, including type specimens — the original material on which species descriptions are based. Many herbaria are now digitising their collections, making images and data available online for research in biodiversity, ecology, and climate change.

Uses

Herbarium specimens are used to identify plants, verify names, study morphology and genetics (including DNA from old specimens), track invasive species, and establish historical flowering times and distributions.
