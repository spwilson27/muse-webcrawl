Seven Countries Study

The Seven Countries Study was a pioneering epidemiological study of the relationship between diet, lifestyle, and cardiovascular disease, initiated in 1958 by the American physiologist Ancel Keys of the University of Minnesota. It was the first major study to systematically examine the association between diet and coronary heart disease across contrasting populations, and it strongly influenced the development of the diet–heart hypothesis and modern nutritional guidelines.

Background

Keys had become interested in international differences in heart disease after observing that American business executives suffered high rates of heart attacks while post-war European populations with food shortages had lower rates. Earlier ecological comparisons, such as his 1953 analysis relating fat calories to heart deaths in six countries, had been criticised for selective use of data. The Seven Countries Study was designed as a rigorous prospective investigation using standardised methods across culturally contrasting populations.

Design

The study enrolled 16 cohorts of men aged 40 to 59 in seven countries: the United States, Finland, the Netherlands, Italy, Yugoslavia (in what are now Croatia and Serbia), Greece, and Japan — a total of 12,763 men. Baseline examinations measured diet, blood pressure, serum cholesterol, smoking, physical activity, and anthropometry. Participants were followed for decades, with major analyses published at 5, 10, 15, and 25 years, and follow-up of some cohorts continuing for 50 years.

Findings

The study found striking differences in coronary mortality: rates were very high in Eastern Finland and the United States, intermediate in the Netherlands and Italy, and low in Mediterranean cohorts such as Crete and in Japan. Average serum cholesterol and the proportion of dietary energy from saturated fat strongly predicted population rates of coronary death. The Mediterranean cohorts, whose diets were rich in olive oil, grains, legumes, vegetables, and fish, helped popularise the concept of the Mediterranean diet.

Influence and criticism

The Seven Countries Study shaped dietary recommendations worldwide, encouraging reductions in saturated fat. Critics have questioned aspects of its design and interpretation, including the choice of countries, the confounding effects of sugar intake and other lifestyle variables, and the strength of causal inference from observational data. Defenders note that its core findings have been broadly corroborated by later cohort studies and trials. The full dataset and analyses are summarised in Keys's 1980 monograph Seven Countries: A Multivariate Analysis of Death and Coronary Heart Disease.
