Ludwig Guttmann

Sir Ludwig Guttmann
Born3 July 1899, Tost, Upper Silesia
Died18 March 1980, Aylesbury, England
Known forFounding the Paralympic Games

Sir Ludwig "Poppa" Guttmann (3 July 1899 – 18 March 1980) was a German-born British neurologist who founded the Paralympic Games. He revolutionized the treatment of people with spinal cord injuries, who before his work usually died within a short time of their injuries.

Life and career

Guttmann studied medicine and became one of Germany's leading neurosurgeons at Breslau, but as a Jew he was dismissed under Nazi rule; in 1933 he was limited to treating Jewish patients. He fled Germany in 1939 for England, settling in Oxford. In 1944 the British government asked him to open the National Spinal Injuries Centre at Stoke Mandeville Hospital in Aylesbury, where he introduced comprehensive rehabilitation — turning patients regularly to prevent pressure sores, managing bladder care, and using sport as physical and psychological therapy.

The Stoke Mandeville Games

On 29 July 1948, the day of the opening of the London Olympics, Guttmann organized an archery competition for 16 wheelchair athletes at Stoke Mandeville. The games grew annually, became international in 1952, and in 1960 were held in Rome in parallel with the Olympics — the event now recognized as the first Paralympic Games. Guttmann was knighted in 1966 and is remembered as the father of organized sport for people with disabilities.
