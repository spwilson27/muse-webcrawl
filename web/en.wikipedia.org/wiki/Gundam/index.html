Gundam (Japanese: ガンダム) is a Japanese military science fiction media franchise created by Yoshiyuki Tomino and Sunrise (now Bandai Namco Filmworks). The franchise began on 7 April 1979 with Mobile Suit Gundam, a television series that defined the "real robot" genre of mecha anime by depicting giant robots — mobile suits — as realistic weapons of war rather than superheroes.

The RX-78-2 Gundam from the original 1979 series

Overview

The original series is set in the Universal Century calendar, in which humanity has colonised space and the Earth Federation fights a war of independence against the Principality of Zeon. Its protagonist, Amuro Ray, pilots the prototype RX-78-2 Gundam. Although initial ratings were modest, the series' popularity grew through reruns and a compilation film trilogy, and above all through sales of model kits.

Numerous sequels, prequels and alternate-universe series followed, including Mobile Suit Zeta Gundam (1985), Mobile Suit Gundam Wing (1995), Mobile Suit Gundam SEED (2002), Mobile Suit Gundam 00 (2007), Mobile Suit Gundam: Iron-Blooded Orphans (2015) and Mobile Suit Gundam: The Witch from Mercury (2022).

Gunpla and cultural impact

Plastic model kits of the franchise's robots, known as Gunpla, have sold hundreds of millions of units and account for a large share of the Japanese character plastic-model market. Gundam is one of the highest-grossing media franchises of all time, and life-size statues of the Gundam have been erected in Tokyo and elsewhere in Japan.
