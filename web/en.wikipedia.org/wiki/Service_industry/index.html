Service industry

The service industry (also called the tertiary sector of the economy) consists of businesses and organisations that provide services — intangible goods and activities — rather than producing physical products. It is one of the three main sectors of the economy in the three-sector model, alongside the primary sector (extraction of raw materials) and the secondary sector (manufacturing).

Service industries include retail, wholesale, transport, distribution, food services, hospitality and tourism, financial services, insurance, banking, healthcare, education, entertainment, media, professional services such as law and accounting, information technology, telecommunications, real estate, and public administration.

Characteristics

Services differ from physical goods in several classic respects, often summarised as intangibility, inseparability, variability, and perishability. A service is generally consumed at the point of production: a haircut, a medical consultation, or a train journey cannot be stored in inventory for later sale. Production and consumption frequently occur simultaneously and involve the customer directly, which makes quality control and standardisation more difficult than in manufacturing.

Because services are intangible, customers often rely on reputation, branding, reviews, and physical cues — the appearance of premises or staff — to judge quality before purchase. Service businesses also tend to be labour-intensive, although automation and self-service technology have steadily reduced this in areas such as banking and retail.

Economic significance

In most developed economies the service sector accounts for the largest share of gross domestic product and employment — commonly 70–80 percent of GDP in advanced economies such as the United Kingdom, the United States, and Japan. The long-term shift of employment from agriculture to manufacturing and then to services is known as tertiarisation or deindustrialisation when it involves manufacturing decline.

Economists have debated whether the growth of services depresses productivity growth — a concern associated with Baumol's cost disease, the observation that productivity in many personal services (such as live performance or classroom teaching) grows more slowly than in manufacturing, causing their relative cost to rise over time.

Subsectors

Some classifications divide the tertiary sector further, identifying a quaternary sector concerned with information and knowledge-based services (research, information technology, consultancy) and a quinary sector covering high-level decision-making and care services such as healthcare and education leadership.
