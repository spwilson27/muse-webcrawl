Path of least resistance

Placeholder image

The path of least resistance is the physical or metaphorical pathway that provides the least resistance to forward motion by a given object or entity, among a set of alternative paths. The concept is often used to describe why an object or entity takes a given path.

In physics

In electrical circuits, current divides among parallel branches in inverse proportion to their resistance, so more current flows through the path of lower resistance — though it is a common misconception that current takes only the single easiest path. In fluid flow, liquids and gases preferentially move through channels offering least resistance, following pressure gradients.

Metaphorical use

Metaphorically, the phrase describes a person choosing the easiest available course of action, avoiding difficulty or confrontation. In geology and hydrology it explains phenomena such as river courses and the movement of water through soil. See also principle of least action and electrical resistance.
