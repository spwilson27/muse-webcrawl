Summit County is a county in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Ohio. Its county seat and largest city is Akron. The county takes its name from being the highest elevation along the Ohio and Erie Canal, whose summit level passed through Akron. As of the 2020 census its population was 540,428, making it the fourth-most populous county in Ohio. It forms the core of the Akron metropolitan area.

History

Summit County was created on 3 March 1840 from portions of Cuyahoga, Medina, Portage, and Stark counties. The canal era made Akron a boom town; the Pennsylvania and Ohio Canal soon followed. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries Akron became the "Rubber Capital of the World", home to Goodyear, Firestone, General Tire, and Goodrich. The county town of Hudson was an early centre of the Western Reserve and an abolitionist stronghold associated with John Brown, who was raised there.

Summit County adopted a charter government in 1979, replacing the three-commissioner system with an elected county executive and an eleven-member council — one of only two chartered counties in Ohio for many years (with Cuyahoga County, which adopted a charter in 2011).

Geography and communities

The county contains part of Cuyahoga Valley National Park along the Cuyahoga River in its northern reaches, the only national park in Ohio. Municipalities include Akron, Cuyahoga Falls, Stow, Hudson, Barberton, Green, Tallmadge, Twinsburg, Macedonia, and Fairlawn, together with villages such as Peninsula and Boston Heights within the valley. The Portage Lakes, a chain of reservoirs built for the canals, form a state park in the county's south.

Economy and transport

Although the tyre industry contracted sharply in the late twentieth century, Goodyear maintains its world headquarters in Akron, and the economy has diversified into polymers research (centred on the University of Akron), healthcare, and logistics. Interstates 76, 77, and 271, the Ohio Turnpike (I-80) in the county's north, and the Akron–Canton Airport (just across the Stark County line) serve the region.
