Landnámabók (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈlantˌnauːmaˌpouːk]; "The Book of Settlements") is a medieval Icelandic work describing in considerable detail the settlement (landnám) of Iceland by the Norse in the 9th and 10th centuries CE. It lists more than 400 primary settlers and some 3,000 people altogether, arranged by district, and records the boundaries of their claims and brief notes on their descendants and notable events.

The work survives in several redactions, including the Sturlubók (compiled by Sturla Þórðarson in the later thirteenth century), the Hauksbók of Haukr Erlendsson, and the fragmentary Melabók. Behind them lies a lost early-twelfth-century original, traditionally associated with Ari Þorgilsson, the author of Íslendingabók.

Contents

Landnámabók proceeds around the coast of Iceland quarter by quarter, naming each settler, the land he or she claimed — sometimes with the colourful ritual of marking boundaries with fire — and the settler's ancestry and posterity. It preserves origin traditions for many families that later appear in the family sagas, such as the settlement of Ingólfr Arnarson at Reykjavík, traditionally dated to 874.

Historical value

The book is a foundational source for Iceland's settlement era, for Norse naming practices and genealogy, and for the Celtic element among the settlers: a significant number of the named settlers and their households came from, or bore names from, the Norse-Gaelic world of Ireland and Scotland — a picture broadly consistent with modern genetic studies of the Icelandic population.
