Bolognese sauce

Ragù alla bolognese
Alternative namesRagù alla bolognese
TypeSauce (ragù)
Place of originItaly
RegionBologna, Emilia-Romagna
Main ingredientsGround beef and/or pork, soffritto (onion, celery, carrot), tomato, white wine, milk

Bolognese sauce, known in Italian as ragù alla bolognese or simply ragù, is a meat-based sauce in Italian cuisine, typical of the city of Bologna. It is customarily used to dress tagliatelle al ragù and lasagne alla bolognese.

The sauce is a slow-cooked mixture of ground meat (traditionally beef, often with pork or pancetta), a soffritto of onion, celery and carrot, tomato, dry white wine and milk or cream, simmered gently for several hours until rich and thickened. The earliest documented recipe for a meat ragù served with pasta dates to the late 18th century in Imola, near Bologna; Pellegrino Artusi published a recipe for "maccheroni alla bolognese" in 1891.

The registered recipe

In 1982 the Bologna delegation of the Accademia Italiana della Cucina deposited an official recipe for ragù alla bolognese with the city's Chamber of Commerce, specifying beef skirt or mince, pancetta, soffritto, tomato passata or paste, white wine, and whole milk, and excluding garlic, herbs (other than a bay leaf in some variants) and spices. Outside Italy, "spaghetti bolognese" — a preparation unknown in Bologna itself, where ragù is served with flat tagliatelle — became one of the most popular Italian-style dishes in Britain, Australia and elsewhere, often differing substantially from the traditional sauce.
