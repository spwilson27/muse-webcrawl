The Peony Pavilion

Written by: Tang Xianzu
Date: 1598
Form: Kunqu opera (chuanqi)

The Peony Pavilion (牡丹亭, Mǔdān Tíng) is a romantic tragicomedy in 55 scenes written by the Ming dynasty playwright Tang Xianzu in 1598. It is the most celebrated work of kunqu opera and one of the four great plays of the Ming dynasty, collectively known as the "Four Dreams of Linchuan."

Plot

Du Liniang, the beautiful sixteen-year-old daughter of a prefect, falls asleep in her family's garden and dreams of a young scholar, Liu Mengmei, with whom she falls deeply in love beside the peony pavilion. Pining away from lovesickness after the dream, she dies, asking that her portrait be buried beneath a plum tree. Years later Liu, traveling to the capital for the examinations, finds the portrait, summons her spirit, and — with her guidance — exhumes and resurrects her. After obstacles and official disbelief, the emperor pardons Liu and the couple are united.

Themes and reception

The play celebrates qing (love, passion) as a force capable of conquering death itself, mounting a subtle challenge to the strict Neo-Confucian rationalism (li) of its era. It caused a sensation among young readers, especially women; tradition holds that some died of longing after reading it. Tang Xianzu said he composed it upon hearing of a girl who died of love.

Modern productions

Full-length productions are rare because of the play's length (over 20 hours). Peter Sellars (1998) and Chen Shi-Zheng's 1999 Lincoln Center production brought complete versions to international attention.
