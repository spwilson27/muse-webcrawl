Ulmus americana

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Ulmaceae
Genus: Ulmus
Species: U. americana
Common names: American elm, white elm, water elm

Ulmus americana, generally known as the American elm or, less commonly, as the white elm or water elm, is a species of elm native to eastern North America, from Nova Scotia west to Alberta and Montana, and south to Florida and central Texas.

Description

The American elm is a large deciduous tree that can grow to 30 metres (98 ft) or more in height, with a trunk up to 1.2 metres in diameter. Its crown typically forms a wide-spreading, vase-shaped canopy of arching branches, a silhouette that made it the classic street tree of North American towns. The doubly serrate leaves are 7–20 cm long with the asymmetrical base typical of elms.

Dutch elm disease

The species was decimated by Dutch elm disease after the fungus arrived in North America around 1928. Tens of millions of trees died, and elm-lined avenues largely disappeared. The species survives widely in the wild, often succumbing once it reaches maturity, and resistant cultivars such as 'Princeton', 'Valley Forge' and 'New Harmony' have been developed for replanting.

Significance

The American elm is the state tree of Massachusetts and North Dakota. Famous specimens include the Liberty Tree of Boston, under which colonists protested British rule, and the Survivor Tree in Oklahoma City, which withstood the 1995 bombing.
