Germano Celant

Born: 11 September 1940, Genoa, Italy
Died: 29 April 2020, Milan, Italy
Known for: Arte Povera; curating; art criticism

Germano Celant (11 September 1940 – 29 April 2020) was an Italian art historian, critic, and curator, best known for coining the term Arte Povera ("poor art") in 1967 and for championing the generation of Italian artists — including Giovanni Anselmo, Alighiero Boetti, Pier Paolo Calzolari, Luciano Fabro, Jannis Kounellis, Mario Merz, Giulio Paolini, Giuseppe Penone, and Michelangelo Pistoletto — who worked with humble, everyday materials.

Arte Povera

In September 1967 Celant organised the exhibition Arte povera – Im spazio at the Galleria La Bertesca in Genoa and published the manifesto-like text "Arte Povera: Notes for a Guerrilla War" in the journal Flash Art. He framed the movement as a guerrilla stance against the established structures of art, culture, and commerce, favouring process, nature, and elemental matter over finished, marketable objects.

Career

Celant served as a senior curator at the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York, organised major exhibitions internationally, and from 2013 was artistic and scientific director of the Fondazione Prada in Milan, where he curated landmark exhibitions and research projects. He also directed exhibitions at the Fondazione Prada's Ca' Corner della Regina in Venice and served as artistic director of large-scale shows including the 1997 Venice Biennale's future-focused programming. His extensive writings include monographs and exhibition catalogues on artists ranging from the Arte Povera generation to figures in contemporary art and design.

Death

Celant died in Milan on 29 April 2020, at the age of 79, from complications of COVID-19 during the pandemic in Italy.
