Skin gambling

Skin gambling is the practice of using virtual items — "skins," cosmetic finishes for weapons and characters in video games — as virtual currency to wager on the outcome of professional matches or on games of chance run by third-party websites. The practice became prominent around Valve's game Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (CS:GO) after its 2013 "Arms Deal" update created a marketplace in which rare skins acquired real-money value on the Steam Community Market.

Third-party sites allowed players, many of them minors, to deposit skins and bet them on roulette, coin-flip, jackpot, and esports-betting games. A 2016 Bloomberg report estimated that skins worth some $2.3 billion were wagered in 2015. Scandals followed: YouTubers were found promoting sites they secretly owned, and class-action lawsuits accused Valve of facilitating unlicensed gambling.

Crackdown

In July 2016, after the Washington State Gambling Commission intervened, Valve issued cease-and-desist letters to gambling sites and stated that using the Steam API for gambling violated its terms. Many sites closed, though skin betting persisted in unregulated offshore markets and later spread to other games. Regulators in several countries have since treated skin gambling as a form of gambling subject to licensing and age restrictions, and researchers link it to problem-gambling behaviour in adolescents.
