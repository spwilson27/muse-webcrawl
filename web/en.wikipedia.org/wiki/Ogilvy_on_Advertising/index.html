Ogilvy on Advertising

Ogilvy on Advertising is a book by advertising executive David Ogilvy, published in 1983. Drawing on Ogilvy's decades of experience — including founding the agency Ogilvy & Mather and creating celebrated campaigns for Hathaway shirts, Dove, and Rolls-Royce — the book sets out his philosophy and practical rules for creating effective advertising. It is a companion in spirit to his earlier bestseller Confessions of an Advertising Man (1963).

Content

The book is richly illustrated with advertisements and organised into chapters addressing, among other topics: how to produce advertising that sells; advertising for business-to-business and direct-response markets; television commercials; print headlines and copy; typography and layout; research; and how to run an agency and win clients. Ogilvy emphasises respect for the consumer — "the consumer isn't a moron, she is your wife" — and argues that advertising should sell rather than merely entertain or win creative awards.

Key principles

Among Ogilvy's best-known prescriptions are that the headline carries most of the advertisement's selling power ("on the average, five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy"), that long copy can sell provided it is interesting, that brand image and consistency matter, and that agencies should ground creative work in research and knowledge of the product. His famous Rolls-Royce headline — "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock" — is frequently cited as a model of factual, product-centred copywriting.

Reception and influence

Ogilvy on Advertising has been translated into many languages and remains in print as a standard text in marketing education and agency training. Though written before the digital era, its insistence on testing, research, and respect for the consumer continues to be invoked by practitioners.
