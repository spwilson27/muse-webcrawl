Wigan

Wigan is a town in Greater Manchester, England, on the River Douglas. It forms part of the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan and lies about 10 miles (16 km) west of Manchester and 17 miles (27 km) east of Liverpool. Wigan is known for its industrial history as a centre of coal mining and cotton milling, and in popular culture for Wigan Pier, made famous by George Orwell's The Road to Wigan Pier (1937).

History

There was a Roman settlement, possibly Coccium, on the site, and Wigan is recorded in the Domesday Book era as part of the parish of Newton. The town received its borough charter in 1246. From the Middle Ages it was a centre for coal mining — the surrounding Lancashire Coalfield was among the richest in Britain — and later for cotton spinning and weaving, canals, and engineering. In the 19th century Wigan was notorious for the poverty of its working population; Orwell's book documented conditions in the 1930s.

Sport and culture

Wigan is strongly associated with rugby league: Wigan Warriors are one of the most successful clubs in the sport's history. The town's football club, Wigan Athletic, won the FA Cup in 2013, defeating Manchester City in the final, in the same season it was relegated from the Premier League. Wigan is also known for the Wigan Casino, a legendary venue of the Northern soul movement in the 1970s, and for its meat pies — the town's inhabitants are jokingly known as "pie-eaters".
