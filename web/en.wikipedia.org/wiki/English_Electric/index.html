English Electric

English Electric Company Ltd
Founded: 1918
Defunct: 1968 (merged into GEC)
Headquarters: London, England
Industry: Electrical engineering, aircraft, rail, computing

The English Electric Company Limited (EE) was a British industrial manufacturer formed in 1918 by the merger of several electrical engineering firms, including Dick, Kerr & Co. of Preston and the Phoenix Dynamo Manufacturing Company. Over the following half-century it became one of Britain's largest engineering concerns, producing electric motors, transformers, diesel engines, locomotives, trams, aircraft and some of the earliest British computers.

History

English Electric began by consolidating wartime production capacity. Through the interwar years it built electric traction equipment and acquired further businesses, including Siemens Brothers Dynamo Works and the Marconiphone radio interests (later sold to EMI). During the Second World War its factories produced aircraft — notably building over 3,000 Handley Page Hampden and Halifax bombers — and tanks and radar equipment.

Aircraft

After the war English Electric developed its own designs at Preston and Samlesbury. The Canberra (1949) was Britain's first jet bomber and a major export success; the Lightning (1954) was the RAF's only all-British Mach 2 fighter. In 1960 EE's aviation division was merged into the new British Aircraft Corporation (BAC), though English Electric retained a shareholding.

Rail and diesel engines

The company was a pioneer of diesel-electric traction, supplying locomotives at home (including classes for British Rail) and exporting worldwide. Its works at the Vulcan Foundry, Newton-le-Willows, built steam and diesel locomotives for decades.

Computing and merger

Through its LEO and ICT associations and its own KDF series, English Electric was an early British computer manufacturer. In 1968, after government pressure to consolidate the electrical industry, English Electric merged with the General Electric Company (GEC), ending its independent existence.
