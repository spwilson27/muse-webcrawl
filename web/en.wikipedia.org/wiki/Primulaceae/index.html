Primulaceae

Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Ericales
Family: Primulaceae
Type genus: Primula
Genera: about 58

Primulaceae, commonly known as the primrose family, is a family of herbaceous and woody flowering plants in the order Ericales, with about 58 genera and some 2,600 species. The family is characterised by flowers with the stamens opposite the corolla lobes and a free central placenta.

Taxonomy

In the APG III and APG IV classification systems, the family was greatly expanded to include the former families Myrsinaceae and Theophrastaceae, as well as the genus Maesa, which had previously been placed in its own family. Subfamilies include Primuloideae, Myrsinoideae, Theophrastoideae and Maesoideae.

Genera and species

Well-known members include the primroses (Primula), cyclamens (Cyclamen), loosestrifes (Lysimachia), scarlet pimpernel (Lysimachia arvensis, formerly Anagallis), and the tropical genus Ardisia. Many primulas and cyclamens are popular ornamental plants, while alpine members of the family are classic rock-garden subjects in temperate horticulture.

Distribution

The family is nearly cosmopolitan: Primuloideae are concentrated in temperate and arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere, especially the mountains of Asia, while the former Myrsinaceae are widespread in the tropics and subtropics.
