Sleepwalking

Sleepwalking, also known as somnambulism or noctambulism, is a phenomenon of combined sleep and wakefulness classified as a parasomnia. Sleepwalkers arise from slow-wave (deep) sleep, usually in the first third of the night, and perform activities that are normally performed during wakefulness — most simply walking about, but potentially including dressing, eating, cleaning, or even driving — while remaining in a state of low consciousness. The episode is typically not remembered.

Sleepwalking arises from deep NREM sleep, most often in childhood.

Characteristics

During an episode the sleepwalker's eyes are usually open with a glassy stare, and they may respond only poorly to others. Episodes last from a few seconds to half an hour or more. Contrary to popular belief, sleepwalkers are not acting out dreams, because episodes occur during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, when dreaming is minimal. Waking a sleepwalker is not dangerous, though the person may be confused or disoriented for a short time.

Causes and prevalence

Sleepwalking occurs in roughly 10–30% of children at some point and usually resolves by adolescence; in adults the prevalence is about 2–4%. There is a strong genetic component: the likelihood of sleepwalking is much higher if both parents were sleepwalkers. Triggers include sleep deprivation, fever, stress, alcohol, and certain medications such as sedative-hypnotics. It is associated with other disorders of arousal from deep sleep, including sleep terrors and confusional arousals.

Safety and treatment

Most episodes are harmless and need no treatment, but injuries can occur from falls or leaving the house. Management centres on safety measures (locking doors and windows, removing obstacles), good sleep hygiene, and avoiding triggers. In severe cases, scheduled awakenings or medications such as benzodiazepines may be used. Rare cases of violent or complex behaviour during sleepwalking have figured in legal defences, notably the 1987 Canadian case of Kenneth Parks, who was acquitted of murder after driving 23 kilometres and killing his mother-in-law while asleep.
