Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis

Born: 29 June 1893, Calcutta, British India

Died: 28 June 1972, Calcutta, India

Fields: Statistics, physics, economics

Known for: Mahalanobis distance; Indian Statistical Institute; Second Five-Year Plan

Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis OBE, FNA, FASc, FRS (29 June 1893 – 28 June 1972) was an Indian scientist and statistician. He is best remembered for the Mahalanobis distance, a statistical measure, and for founding the Indian Statistical Institute. He was a key figure in India's planning era and a pioneer of large-scale sample surveys.

Early life and education

Mahalanobis was born in Calcutta into a family associated with the Brahmo Samaj movement. He studied physics at Presidency College, Calcutta, graduating in 1912, and then at King's College, Cambridge. At Cambridge he encountered the journal Biometrika, which awakened his interest in statistics.

Contributions

In 1936 he introduced the Mahalanobis distance, a multivariate measure of the distance between a point and a distribution, based on his studies of anthropometric data. He championed the method of large sample surveys for estimating crop yields and socio-economic conditions, leading to the National Sample Survey of India. In 1931 he founded the Indian Statistical Institute in Calcutta, and in 1933 the journal Sankhyā.

Planning

A close adviser of Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, Mahalanobis drafted the strategy for India's Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961), built around the two-sector Mahalanobis model emphasising rapid growth of heavy capital-goods industries. His birthday, 29 June, is celebrated in India as National Statistics Day.
