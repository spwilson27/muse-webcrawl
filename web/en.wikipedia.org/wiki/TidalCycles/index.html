TidalCycles (often shortened to Tidal) is a free and open-source software environment for live coding musical patterns, created by Alex McLean and written in the Haskell programming language. It allows performers to generate and manipulate musical patterns in real time using a concise notation in which events are organized over repeating cycles, from which the software takes its name. TidalCycles is closely associated with the algorave movement, in which performers improvise electronic dance music by coding live in front of an audience.

Design

In TidalCycles, music is described as patterns of events over time. A "mini-notation" lets users write sequences such as "bd sn bd sn" for drum samples, and functions can transform patterns by reversing, speeding up, degrading, layering or combining them. Although patterns are cyclical, transformations can produce evolving, polyrhythmic results. TidalCycles itself does not generate sound; it sends Open Sound Control messages to a sound engine, most commonly SuperDirt, a sampler and synthesizer built on SuperCollider.

History and community

McLean began developing the pattern language around 2009, originally under the name Tidal. The software grew out of research into the perception of pattern and time in music, and became a central tool of the TOPLAP live coding community. TidalCycles is maintained as a community project and is used in teaching, research and performance worldwide.
