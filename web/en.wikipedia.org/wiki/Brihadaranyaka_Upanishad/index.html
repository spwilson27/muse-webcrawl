Brihadaranyaka Upanishad

[Image placeholder: Brihadaranyaka Upanishad]

The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (Sanskrit: Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad, "Great Forest Teaching") is one of the principal and oldest Upanishads, likely composed in the 7th–6th centuries BCE. It is embedded in the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa of the White Yajurveda and consists of three sections (kāṇḍas) of dialogues, hymns and doctrines.

Content

The text is celebrated for the teachings of the sage Yājñavalkya, including his dialogues with his wife Maitreyī and with King Janaka, the doctrine of neti neti ("not this, not this") for approaching the Self (Ātman), and the identity of Ātman with Brahman. It contains the famous prayer asato mā sad gamaya ("from the unreal lead me to the real … from death lead me to immortality") and is a foundational text of Vedānta philosophy, extensively commented on by Śaṅkara.
