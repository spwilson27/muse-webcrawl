Florenz Edward Ziegfeld Jr. (March 21, 1867 – July 22, 1932) was an American Broadway impresario, notable for his series of theatrical revues, the Ziegfeld Follies (1907–1931), inspired by the Folies Bergère of Paris. He also produced the musical Show Boat. He was known as the "glorifier of the American girl". Ziegfeld is a member of the American Theater Hall of Fame.

Early life

Ziegfeld was born in Chicago, Illinois, the son of Dr. Florenz Edward Ziegfeld, a German immigrant who founded the Chicago Musical College, and Rosalie de Hez, who was of Belgian and French descent. As a young man he assisted his father by booking theatrical acts for the college's nightclub, and he produced the entertainment for the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893, where he famously promoted the strongman Eugen Sandow.

Career

In 1896 Ziegfeld traveled to Europe to recruit performers and became the manager and promoter of the Polish-born French singer Anna Held, whom he brought to America and presented in a series of musical comedies. With financial backing from the gambler and theatrical syndicate figures, he launched the first Ziegfeld Follies in 1907 at the Jardin de Paris rooftop theatre in New York. The Follies combined beautiful chorus girls, lavish costumes and sets, comedy sketches, and songs by leading composers, and they became an annual institution of Broadway.

Ziegfeld's productions launched or advanced the careers of many stars, including Fanny Brice, Will Rogers, Eddie Cantor, W. C. Fields, Marilyn Miller, and Ed Wynn. In 1927 he produced Show Boat, with music by Jerome Kern and book and lyrics by Oscar Hammerstein II, one of the landmark works of American musical theatre. He built the Ziegfeld Theatre on Sixth Avenue in 1927.

Personal life and death

Ziegfeld lived for many years with Anna Held as his common-law wife. In 1914 he married actress Billie Burke, with whom he had a daughter, Patricia. The Wall Street Crash of 1929 destroyed his fortune, and although he staged several more productions he never recovered financially. He died of pleurisy in Hollywood on July 22, 1932.

Legacy

Ziegfeld's life was dramatized in the Academy Award-winning film The Great Ziegfeld (1936). His name endures in the Ziegfeld Theatre and in the enduring image of the lavish Broadway revue.
