Punic Wars

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Roman and Carthaginian war galleys in battle during the First Punic War]

The Punic Wars were three wars fought between Rome and Carthage from 264 to 146 BC — among the largest conflicts of the ancient world — which decided the mastery of the western Mediterranean. 'Punic' derives from the Latin Poenicus, 'Phoenician', Rome's name for the Carthaginians. Rome's ultimate victory destroyed Carthage, delivered Sicily, Sardinia, Iberia and North Africa into Roman hands, and set Rome on the path to empire.

First and Second Wars

The First Punic War (264–241 BC), fought mainly for Sicily and at sea, forced Rome to build a navy from a captured Carthaginian wreck; victory at the Aegates Islands made Sicily Rome's first province, and Rome soon seized Sardinia and Corsica too. Carthage answered by building an empire in Iberia under the Barcids. The Second Punic War (218–201 BC) — the Hannibalic War — saw Hannibal cross the Alps and win Cannae, yet Rome's refusal to surrender, its manpower reserves and Scipio's campaigns in Iberia and Africa ended with Hannibal's defeat at Zama and a harsh peace.

Third War and aftermath

Half a century later Carthage, though disarmed and compliant, had recovered enough commercially to alarm Roman hardliners led by Cato the Elder. Rome manufactured a pretext in 149 BC; Carthage resisted a siege for two years before Scipio Aemilianus stormed and systematically destroyed the city in 146 BC, enslaving its survivors. Its territory became the Roman province of Africa, and a century later Julius Caesar refounded Carthage as a Roman colony, which prospered for centuries.
