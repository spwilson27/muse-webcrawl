The Filmfare Awards South are annual awards honouring artistic and technical excellence in the South Indian film industries, namely Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and Kannada cinema. They are presented by Filmfare magazine, part of The Times Group, and are the southern counterpart of the Filmfare Awards for Hindi cinema.

History

The awards were first held in 1954 in Chennai (then Madras), initially covering Tamil and Telugu films released in 1953. Malayalam films were included from the 1960s and Kannada films soon afterwards, making the ceremony one of the oldest and most prominent award events dedicated to South Indian cinema. For many years the ceremony was held in Chennai; in later years it has rotated among cities such as Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Kochi.

Winners receive a statuette depicting a woman whose arms rise in a dance pose, commonly called the "Black Lady". A Lifetime Achievement Award for South Indian cinema was instituted to honour veterans of the four industries.

Categories

Each of the four industries has its own set of categories, which typically include:

  • Best Film
  • Best Director
  • Best Actor and Best Actress
  • Best Supporting Actor and Best Supporting Actress
  • Best Music Director
  • Best Lyricist
  • Best Male and Female Playback Singer
  • Best Cinematographer and Best Choreographer
  • Best Debut awards for newcomers

Records and notable winners

Actors such as Kamal Haasan, Chiranjeevi, Mammootty, Mohanlal and Rajinikanth are among the most honoured performers at the awards. Kamal Haasan holds the distinction of winning Filmfare Awards across multiple languages and categories, including for acting, writing and production. In recent editions, performers from the younger generation across the four industries have dominated the popular categories, while critics' choice awards recognise acclaimed performances in smaller films.
