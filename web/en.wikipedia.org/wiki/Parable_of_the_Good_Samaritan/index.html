Parable of the Good Samaritan

The Parable of the Good Samaritan is a parable told by Jesus in the Gospel of Luke (10:29–37). A traveller is beaten, robbed, and left half dead on the road from Jerusalem to Jericho. A priest and a Levite pass by, but a Samaritan — a member of a group despised by the audience — stops, treats the man's wounds, and pays for his care at an inn.

Interpretation

Jesus tells the story in answer to the question "Who is my neighbour?", concluding that the neighbour is the one who shows mercy. Because Samaritans and Jews were hostile to one another, the Samaritan's compassion crosses ethnic and religious boundaries.

Influence

The phrase "Good Samaritan" has entered common usage for anyone who helps a stranger in need, and "Good Samaritan laws" in many jurisdictions protect rescuers from liability.
