The Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined railway and motorway cable-stayed bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with the artificial island of Peberholm and the Drogden Tunnel on the Danish side, it forms the Øresund fixed link between Copenhagen and Malmö, and it is the longest combined road and rail bridge in Europe.

History

A fixed link across the Øresund was debated for most of the 20th century. Denmark and Sweden signed an agreement to build the link in 1991, and construction began in 1995. The bridge was completed in 1999 and formally opened by Queen Margrethe II of Denmark and King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden on 1 July 2000.

Structure

The bridge runs nearly 8 km from the Swedish coast to Peberholm, an artificial island built from dredged material, from which the 4 km Drogden Tunnel continues to the Danish island of Amager. The tunnel segment was chosen so as not to obstruct shipping or the flight paths of Copenhagen Airport. The bridge's two pylons rise 204 m, and its main span of 490 m was the world's longest cable-stayed main span for road and rail traffic.

Traffic

The link carries the E20 motorway on its upper deck and a double-track railway below. Millions of vehicles cross annually, and the Øresund trains connect Copenhagen Central Station with Malmö in about 35 minutes, enabling a large cross-border commuter market.

Border controls

Although both countries are in the Schengen Area, Sweden reintroduced identity checks on travellers from Denmark in 2015 during the European migrant crisis, and Denmark later introduced its own temporary controls; the checks were widely noted for slowing the region's celebrated integration.
