Al-Ikhlāṣ (Arabic: الإخلاص, "Sincerity"), also known as Al-Tawḥīd, is the 112th chapter (surah) of the Quran. In its four brief verses it offers the Quran's most concentrated declaration of God's absolute oneness (tawhid), and it is among the most frequently recited passages in Muslim prayer and devotion.

Text

The surah reads (in translation): "Say: He is Allah, the One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent." Its absolute, uncompromising monotheism has made it a touchstone of Islamic theology and a standard rebuttal of Trinitarian and polytheistic doctrines.

Significance

Traditions attributed to the Prophet Muhammad state that reciting Al-Ikhlas carries the reward of reciting one-third of the Quran, because it encapsulates the doctrine of divine unity. It is paired with Al-Falaq and An-Nas in daily supplications, and its text appears widely in Islamic calligraphy, architecture and amulets.
