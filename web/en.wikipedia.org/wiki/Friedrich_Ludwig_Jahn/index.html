Friedrich Ludwig Jahn

Friedrich Ludwig Jahn (11 August 1778 – 15 October 1852) was a German educator, nationalist writer and founder of the German gymnastics movement. Known in Germany as Turnvater Jahn ("Father Jahn of gymnastics"), he established the first open-air gymnasium, or Turnplatz, at Berlin's Hasenheide in 1811 and coined the word Turnen for physical exercise.

Early life

Jahn was born in Lanz in the Prignitz region of Brandenburg. He studied at gymnasiums in Salzwedel, Berlin and Greifswald before enrolling at the University of Greifswald, where he became involved in student fraternity life and developed his patriotic ideas.

The Turnen movement

After Prussia's defeat by Napoleon at Jena and Auerstedt in 1806, Jahn came to believe that the physical and moral strengthening of German youth was essential to national revival. His 1810 work Deutsches Volkstum (German Folkdom) set out his philosophy. In 1811 he opened the first Turnplatz, where young men trained together in gymnastics, patriotic songs and lectures, regardless of class.

Later life

The gymnastics movement grew rapidly but was suppressed after the Carlsbad Decrees of 1819; Jahn was arrested and spent several years in confinement and under police supervision. He was later rehabilitated and in 1848 was elected to the Frankfurt Parliament, but his nationalist views placed him increasingly on the margins. He died in 1852 in Freyburg.

Legacy

Jahn is remembered as the inventor of apparatus such as the horizontal bar, parallel bars, rings and the pommel horse, and as a foundational figure of German physical education, though scholars have also criticized the chauvinistic aspects of his ideology.
