Asbestosis

Asbestosis
SpecialtyPulmonology, occupational medicine
CauseProlonged inhalation of asbestos fibres
SymptomsProgressive breathlessness, dry cough, chest tightness, finger clubbing
ComplicationsRespiratory failure, cor pulmonale; increased risk of lung cancer and mesothelioma

Asbestosis is a chronic lung disease caused by the inhalation of asbestos fibres over a prolonged period. The fibres, deposited deep in the lungs, provoke inflammation and scarring (pulmonary fibrosis) that gradually stiffens the lungs, reduces their capacity and impairs gas exchange. It is one of the pneumoconioses — lung diseases caused by inhaled mineral dusts — and develops typically after heavy occupational exposure over ten years or more, with symptoms appearing decades after exposure began.

Signs and symptoms

The cardinal symptom is slowly progressive shortness of breath on exertion, often with a dry cough, chest tightness and, in advanced disease, finger clubbing. Examination may reveal fine crackles at the lung bases. Lung function tests show a restrictive pattern with reduced diffusing capacity. Chest imaging shows irregular opacities, mainly in the lower lobes, often with pleural thickening or calcified pleural plaques.

Cause and risk

Asbestosis was historically common among asbestos miners and millers, shipyard workers, insulation workers ("laggers"), demolition workers and others exposed to airborne fibres before strict controls were introduced in the 1970s and 1980s. All commercial types of asbestos can cause it, with risk increasing with cumulative dose. Smoking greatly compounds the separate risk of lung cancer in asbestos-exposed workers.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on a history of significant exposure, compatible imaging and lung function, and exclusion of other causes of fibrosis. There is no cure: treatment is supportive, with smoking cessation, vaccination, pulmonary rehabilitation and supplemental oxygen; antifibrotic drugs used in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis are sometimes considered. Prevention — eliminating exposure through bans and controlled removal — is the effective remedy, and asbestosis is compensable as an industrial disease in many countries.

Related conditions

Asbestos exposure also causes pleural plaques, diffuse pleural thickening, benign asbestos pleural effusions, lung cancer and mesothelioma — a cancer of the pleura and peritoneum that can follow much lighter exposure and is the subject of extensive litigation against asbestos manufacturers and employers.
