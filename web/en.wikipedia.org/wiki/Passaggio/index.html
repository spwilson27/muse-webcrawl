Passaggio (Italian: [pasˈsaddʒo], "passage") is a term in vocal pedagogy for the transition area between vocal registers — for example between chest voice and head voice — where a singer must adjust vocal production to move smoothly from one register to another without an audible break or change in quality. Managing the passaggio is a central technical challenge in classical singing, and is handled through adjustments of vowel, breath, and resonance (a technique historically associated with bel canto and with "covering" the tone).

Approximate locations of the passaggi across voice types.

Primo and secondo passaggio

Most singers have two transition zones. The primo passaggio is the lower transition, roughly between the chest/middle registers, and the secondo passaggio is the higher transition into full head voice. Their exact pitches vary with voice type: as a rough guide, the secondo passaggio lies around F♯4 for tenors, around E♭4–E4 for baritones and basses, and around F5 for sopranos and mezzo-sopranos (the primo passaggio roughly an octave lower in female voices). The zone is not a single note but a region of several semitones in which registration events occur.

Technique

Untrained voices often "break" or flip at the passaggio, or push the lower register too high, causing strain. Classical training teaches gradual adjustment: modifying vowels toward a more closed or covered quality (aggiustamento), maintaining steady breath support, and allowing the laryngeal mechanism to shift gradually from the thyroarytenoid-dominant production of chest voice to the cricothyroid-dominant production of head voice. The goal is an even scale — a seamless line from the bottom to the top of the range.
