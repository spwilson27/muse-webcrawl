Sign test

The sign test is a statistical method used to test for consistent differences between pairs of observations, such as the weight of subjects before and after treatment. It is a non-parametric test that makes very few assumptions about the distribution of the data.

Method

For each pair, the sign of the difference (+ or −) is recorded, ignoring pairs with no difference. Under the null hypothesis of no systematic difference, positive and negative signs are equally likely, so the number of positive signs follows a binomial distribution with p = 0.5, which provides the test.

Applications

The sign test can be used to test whether the median of a distribution equals a specified value, or to compare two paired samples. It is less powerful than the Wilcoxon signed-rank test but requires weaker assumptions, using only the direction of differences rather than their magnitude.
