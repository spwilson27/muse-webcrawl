Brown ale is a style of beer with a dark amber to brown colour, originating in England. The term is now associated mainly with strongly flavoured, malt-forward ales brewed with a proportion of darker malts. Modern brown ales fall into two broad families: the sweeter, lower-alcohol Southern English style and the drier, hoppier Northern English style exemplified by Newcastle Brown Ale, plus an American craft variant that is hoppier still.

History

Before the widespread use of pale malts in the 18th century, most English beer was brown because malt was kilned over wood or coal fires. London brown ales of the 1700s, brewed from 100% brown malt, were the ancestors of porter. As pale malt became cheaper, "brown ale" faded, and by the late 19th century the term was little used. Bottled brown ale was revived as a distinct commercial style in the early 20th century — Mann's Brown Ale (1902) is an early example — and Newcastle Brown Ale, launched in 1927 by Newcastle Breweries after three years of development by Colonel Jim Porter, became the defining strong brown ale, a blend historically of a strong dark ale and Newcastle Amber.

Substyles

Southern English brown ale

Sweet, caramel- and toffee-flavoured, with low hop bitterness and moderate alcohol (about 4.2–6.1%); a rare, mostly bottled style.

Northern English brown ale

Drier and nuttier, with a firmer hop character and alcohol around 4.6–5.0%. Newcastle Brown Ale, brewed since 2005 in Gateshead and later Tadcaster and (for the US market) under licence in the Netherlands and California, is the best-known example.

American brown ale

Developed by homebrewers and early craft breweries (Pete's Wicked Ale was an early commercial example), American browns use American hops for citrus and pine notes and may be stronger and hoppier than their English models.

Characteristics

Brown ales emphasize malt flavours — caramel, biscuit, chocolate, and nuts — with restrained fruitiness from ale yeast. They are generally medium-bodied and pair classically with roasted meats, mushrooms, and aged cheeses.
