Immelmann turn

The path of an Immelmann turn: a half loop followed by a half roll

The Immelmann turn, named after German World War I Eindecker fighter ace Leutnant Max Immelmann, is one of two different aircraft maneuvers that have been referred to as such. The modern aerobatic maneuver now called an Immelmann consists of a half loop followed by a half roll at the top to level flight in the opposite direction at a higher altitude.

The historical maneuver

The maneuver Immelmann actually performed in combat was more like a steep climbing chandelle: after diving on an opponent, the attacking aircraft climbed sharply, and at the top of the climb, just before stalling, a rudder turn reversed its direction, allowing the pilot to dive again. Few First World War aircraft had the power to perform the true half-loop version attributed to him.

The aerobatic maneuver

In the modern aerobatic version, the pilot pulls the aircraft up into the first half of a loop. At the top, inverted, the pilot rolls the aircraft upright, completing the maneuver flying in the opposite direction and having gained altitude while losing speed. In air combat, the Immelmann could be used to reposition behind or above an opponent, though the loss of airspeed at the top made the aircraft vulnerable.
