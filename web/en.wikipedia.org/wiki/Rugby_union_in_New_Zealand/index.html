Rugby union in New Zealand
Governing body: New Zealand Rugby
National teams: All Blacks (men), Black Ferns (women)
First played: 1870, Nelson
Major competitions: Super Rugby Pacific, NPC, Ranfurly Shield

Rugby union is the national sport of New Zealand, deeply embedded in the country's culture and identity. The men's national team, the All Blacks, is the most successful team in international rugby history, and the women's team, the Black Ferns, has dominated the Women's Rugby World Cup.

History

Rugby was introduced by Charles John Monro, who had played at Christ's College, Finchley in London; the first recorded match took place in Nelson in 1870. The New Zealand Rugby Football Union was founded in 1892. The 1905–06 "Originals" tour of Britain established New Zealand as a rugby power and popularised the haka — the Māori war dance performed before matches — as the team's trademark.

Structure

The game is administered by New Zealand Rugby through 26 provincial unions. The domestic National Provincial Championship (NPC) and the Heartland Championship sit below Super Rugby Pacific franchises such as the Crusaders, Blues and Hurricanes. The Ranfurly Shield, contested since 1904, remains one of the most prized trophies in domestic rugby. New Zealand hosted and won the Rugby World Cups of 1987 and 2011, and won again in South Africa in 2015 (held in England); the Black Ferns won a home World Cup in 2022.

Rugby's place in New Zealand life extends to schools, Māori and Pasifika communities and national symbolism, although the sport faces competition from rugby league, football and basketball for young players.
