Purusha


The cosmic Man (Purusha), from whose sacrifice the universe is created in the Rigveda.

Purusha (Sanskrit: पुरुष) is a concept in Indian philosophy meaning "man," "person," "self," or "cosmic being." Its meaning evolved across traditions: in the Rigveda it is the primeval cosmic being from whose body the universe is formed; in Samkhya philosophy it denotes pure consciousness, the passive witness contrasted with prakriti (nature/matter); and in the Upanishads it is associated with the inner self (atman).

The Purusha Sukta

The famous Purusha Sukta (Rigveda 10.90) describes the thousand-headed, thousand-eyed Purusha, who encompasses all that has been and will be. The gods sacrifice Purusha, and from his dismembered body arise the cosmos: the moon from his mind, the sun from his eye, the wind from his breath, and the four social classes (varnas) from his mouth, arms, thighs, and feet—the passage later used to explain the origin of the caste order.

In Samkhya and Yoga

In Samkhya, one of the oldest schools of Indian philosophy, reality consists of two eternal principles: purusha, pure, inactive consciousness, and prakriti, unconscious primordial matter that evolves into the world of mind and body. Liberation (kaivalya) comes when purusha realizes its complete distinctness from prakriti. The Yoga school adopts a similar framework, speaking of Ishvara as a special purusha untouched by afflictions.

Other usages

In the Upanishads and later Vedanta, purusha often signifies the innermost self dwelling in the heart. Ayurvedic texts such as the Charaka Samhita use the term for the person as a composite of body, senses, mind, and self.
