Battle of Uhud

The Battle of Uhud was fought on 19 March 625 AD (3 AH) at Mount Uhud, north of Medina in the Hejaz (present-day Saudi Arabia), between the early Muslim community led by the Prophet Muhammad and a Meccan force led by Abu Sufyan ibn Harb. The Meccans sought revenge for their defeat at Badr the previous year. The battle turned against the Muslims when their archers abandoned a key position, and it is remembered as a costly setback in early Islamic history.

The battle

Muhammad posted fifty archers on a hill (later called Jabal al-Rumah, the Archers' Hill) with strict orders to hold their position. The Muslim attack initially drove the Meccans back, but most of the archers left their post to join the plundering of the Meccan camp. Khalid ibn al-Walid's cavalry seized the undefended hill and attacked the Muslims from the rear. Muhammad was wounded, a rumour of his death spread, and the Muslim army broke. Among the dead was his uncle Hamza.

Aftermath

The Quraysh, having avenged Badr in their own estimation, withdrew without attacking Medina itself. In Islamic tradition, verses of the Quran (Surah Al-Imran) comment on the battle, attributing the reverse to disobedience and the desire for spoils; the martyrs of Uhud are venerated, and their cemetery at the foot of the mountain remains a site of visitation.
