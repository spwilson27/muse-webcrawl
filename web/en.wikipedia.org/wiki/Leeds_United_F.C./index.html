Leeds United Football Club

Leeds United Football Club is a professional football club based in Leeds, West Yorkshire, England. The club plays its home matches at Elland Road and has competed for most of its history in the top two tiers of English football. Its traditional kit is all white, and its nickname is "the Whites".

The club was formed in 1919 following the disbanding of Leeds City F.C. Under manager Don Revie, Leeds became one of the strongest teams in England and Europe during the 1960s and 1970s, winning two league titles, the FA Cup, the League Cup and two Inter-Cities Fairs Cups. Leeds won a third league title in 1991–92, the last season of the old First Division before the creation of the Premier League.

History

After election to the Football League in 1920, the club spent much of the interwar period moving between the First and Second Divisions. Revie's appointment in 1961 transformed the club: built around players such as Billy Bremner, Jack Charlton, Norman Hunter and Johnny Giles, Leeds won the First Division in 1968–69 and 1973–74 and reached the European Cup final in 1975, losing controversially to Bayern Munich.

Under Howard Wilkinson the club won the 1991–92 league championship. David O'Leary's young side reached the UEFA Champions League semi-finals in 2001, but financial collapse followed, leading to relegation from the Premier League in 2004 and from the Championship in 2007. After sixteen years outside the top flight, Marcelo Bielsa led Leeds back to the Premier League in 2020 as Championship winners.

Stadium

Elland Road has been the club's home since its foundation. The ground, which seats around 37,000, lies in the Beeston area of Leeds, close to the M621 motorway.

Rivalries

Leeds' fiercest historic rivalries are with Manchester United — rooted in the Wars of the Roses — and with Chelsea, dating from the 1960s and the 1970 FA Cup Final. Local rivals include Sheffield United, Sheffield Wednesday, Huddersfield Town and Bradford City.
