Santa Clarita, California

State: California
County: Los Angeles
Incorporated: December 15, 1987
Population: about 228,000 (2020)

Santa Clarita (Spanish for "Little St. Clare") is a city in northwestern Los Angeles County in the U.S. state of California. With a 2020 census population of 228,673, it is the third-most populous city in Los Angeles County, the 17th-most populous in California, and the 103rd-most populous in the United States. It is located about 30 miles (48 km) northwest of downtown Los Angeles, and occupies most of the Santa Clarita Valley.

History

The city was incorporated in 1987 by merging the unincorporated communities of Canyon Country, Newhall, Saugus, and Valencia. The valley has a rich history, including the first documented discovery of gold in California at Placerita Canyon in 1842, six years before the famous Gold Rush discovery at Sutter's Mill.

Geography and communities

Santa Clarita comprises the neighbourhoods of Canyon Country, Newhall, Saugus, Valencia, Stevenson Ranch, and others. The Santa Clara River runs through the valley. Valencia is a master-planned community known for its paseo network of pedestrian bridges.

Economy and recreation

The city is home to the Six Flags Magic Mountain amusement park and Six Flags Hurricane Harbor. Major employers include Princess Cruises (headquartered in Valencia), the Henry Mayo Newhall Hospital, and the College of the Canyons. California Institute of the Arts (CalArts) is located in Valencia. The area has frequently served as a filming location and is a popular suburban community often ranked among the safest cities of its size in the United States.
