The Tunisia campaign (also known as the Battle of Tunisia) was a series of battles that took place in Tunisia during the North African campaign of the Second World War, between Axis forces (Germany and Italy) and Allied forces (the British, Americans, and Free French). It began in November 1942, after the Allied landings of Operation Torch in Morocco and Algeria, and ended on 13 May 1943 with the surrender of the Axis forces in the Cape Bon peninsula.

Course of the campaign

Axis forces under Erwin Rommel and Hans-Jürgen von Arnim were squeezed in a pocket between the British Eighth Army advancing from Libya under Bernard Montgomery and the Allied forces advancing from Algeria. Early battles went badly for the inexperienced Americans, notably the defeat at the Battle of Kasserine Pass in February 1943. Under new leadership — including George S. Patton at the US II Corps — the Allies recovered, broke the Mareth Line in March, and in April–May 1943 advanced on Tunis and Bizerte in Operation Vulcan and Strike.

Outcome

More than 230,000 German and Italian troops surrendered, a haul of prisoners comparable to Stalingrad, leading some German wits to call the collapse "Tunisgrad". The victory cleared North Africa, secured the Mediterranean sea lanes, and set the stage for the Allied invasion of Sicily in July 1943.
