T-shirt

A T-shirt (also spelled tee shirt, or tee) is a style of fabric shirt named after the T shape of its body and sleeves. It normally has short sleeves and a round neckline (a crew neck), and is made of light, stretchable cotton or cotton-blend jersey. Once worn only as an undershirt, the T-shirt became one of the most common items of casual clothing worldwide in the second half of the 20th century.

History

The T-shirt evolved from one-piece union suits split into separate tops and bottoms in the late 19th century. The US Navy issued crew-neck cotton undershirts around the time of the Spanish–American War, and by the 1920s "T-shirt" had entered the dictionary. After the Second World War, veterans wearing T-shirts as casual dress, and film stars such as Marlon Brando in A Streetcar Named Desire (1951) and James Dean in Rebel Without a Cause (1955), popularised the garment as outerwear.

Decoration and culture

Screen printing, commercialised from the 1950s and 1960s, turned the T-shirt into a medium for advertising, political slogans, band merchandise and souvenir graphics. Printed and tie-dyed T-shirts became emblems of youth culture, and the garment remains a staple of both fast fashion and designer collections, as well as a subject of criticism over the environmental and labour costs of mass-produced clothing.
