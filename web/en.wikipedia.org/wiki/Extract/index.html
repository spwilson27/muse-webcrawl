Extract

Image placeholder: Commercial flavouring extracts

An extract (essence) is a substance made by extracting a part of a raw material, often by using a solvent such as ethanol, oil or water. Extracts may be sold as tinctures, absolutes or powders. The aromatic principles of many spices, nuts, herbs, fruits, etc., and some flowers are marketed as extracts, among the best known being almond, cinnamon, cloves, ginger, lemon, nutmeg, orange, peppermint, pistachio, rose, spearmint, vanilla, violet and wintergreen.

Extraction techniques

Most natural extracts are obtained by macerating or percolating the raw material in a solvent, followed by concentration of the resulting solution. Vanilla extract, for example, is made by macerating vanilla pods in alcohol and water; United States standards require it to contain at least 35% alcohol and a specified quantity of vanilla beans per volume. Other techniques include expression (cold pressing, used for citrus oils), absorption in fat (enfleurage), and distillation, which yields essential oils and hydrosols.

Uses

Extracts are widely used as flavourings in cooking and baking, in perfumery and cosmetics, and in herbal medicine, where plant extracts are ingredients in tinctures and supplements. Synthetic or imitation extracts reproduce characteristic flavours with artificial flavour compounds.
