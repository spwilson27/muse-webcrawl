The Krk Bridge (Croatian: Krčki most) is a 1,430 m (4,692 ft) long reinforced concrete arch bridge connecting the Croatian island of Krk to the mainland. It carries the D102 state road across the Krk Channel of the Adriatic Sea, spanning the islet of Sveti Marko in two arches. More than a million vehicles cross the bridge each year.

Design and construction

Completed in 1980, the bridge consists of two concrete arches: the larger, between the mainland and Sveti Marko, spans 390 m (1,280 ft) — at the time of completion the longest concrete arch span in the world, surpassing the Gladesville Bridge in Sydney by 85 m. The shorter arch, between Sveti Marko and Krk, spans 244 m (801 ft). The arches were built by cantilevering from both shores using temporary cable stays, a method pioneered on this scale by the Yugoslav construction firm Mostogradnja, with the bridge designed by Ilija Stojadinović in cooperation with Vukan Njagulj and Bojan Možina.

History

The bridge opened on 19 July 1980 and was originally named the Tito Bridge (Titov most) in honour of Yugoslav president Josip Broz Tito, who had died two months earlier. It was renamed after Croatian independence. A toll was charged from opening until 15 June 2020, when tolls were abolished except for heavier vehicle categories.

Conditions

The bridge is famous for the severe bora winds that sweep the channel; it is periodically closed to high-sided vehicles and sometimes to all traffic when gusts become dangerous, and special wind barriers and monitoring stations have been installed. The exposed marine environment also requires ongoing maintenance of the concrete arches against salt corrosion.
