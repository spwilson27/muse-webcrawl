Wayeb

The Wayeb (also written Wayeb', Uayeb, or Wayeb in Yucatec; from way, "sleep" or "transformation") is a five-day period at the end of the Haabʼ, the 365-day solar calendar of the ancient Maya. While the Haabʼ consists of 18 months of 20 days each (360 days), the Wayeb added five "extra" days to complete the solar year, reaching 365 days.

Beliefs and observances

The Wayeb was widely regarded as a dangerous and unlucky time, when the portals between the mortal realm and the underworld dissolved, allowing malevolent deities and ill fortune to enter. According to sources such as Diego de Landa's Relación de las cosas de Yucatán, the Maya made offerings, set up effigies at the entrances of towns, abstained from work and washing, and avoided leaving home unnecessarily. At the close of the Wayeb, New Year ceremonies renewed idols, household goods, and temple fires.

Modern observance

Wayeb observances continue among modern Maya communities and in contemporary Maya calendrical practice, where the days are used for reflection, divination, and preparation for the new year.
