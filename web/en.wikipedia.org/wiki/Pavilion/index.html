Pavilion

Pavilion
TypeLightweight garden or park structure
Related formsGazebo, bandstand, folly, exhibition pavilion

A pavilion is a type of building or structure, often a lightweight, free-standing shelter in a garden, park, or other open area. The word derives from the Old French pavillon, from the Latin papilio meaning "butterfly" or "tent" — a pavilion was originally a large, showy tent used in medieval warfare and tournaments.

Garden pavilions

In architecture, a pavilion may be a subsidiary building in a symmetrical classical composition, positioned at the ends or corners of a main block, or a detached pleasure house in a landscaped garden. Garden pavilions range from simple open-sided shelters offering shade and a place to sit, to elaborate structures such as Chinese garden pavilions (ting), Islamic garden pavilions, and the ornamental follies of English landscape gardens.

Exhibition pavilions

Since the Great Exhibition of 1851, national and thematic pavilions have been a defining feature of world's fairs and expositions, showcasing architecture, industry, and culture; the Barcelona Pavilion (1929) by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe became a landmark of modernist architecture. Temporary contemporary pavilions, such as the annual Serpentine Gallery Pavilion in London, continue the tradition of experimental small-scale architecture.

Other uses

The term is also applied to sports pavilions (especially in cricket, where it denotes the building housing dressing rooms and seating beside the ground), hospital wards housed in separate blocks on a campus, and large halls used for concerts, dances, and seaside entertainment, such as the Brighton Pavilion — properly the Royal Pavilion, a former royal residence built in the Indo-Saracenic style.
