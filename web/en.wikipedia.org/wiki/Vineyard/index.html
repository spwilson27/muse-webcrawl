Vineyard

A vineyard is a plantation of grape-bearing vines, grown mainly for winemaking, but also for raisins, table grapes, and non-alcoholic grape juice. The science, practice, and study of vineyard production is known as viticulture. Vineyards are often characterized by their terroir — the combination of soil, climate, topography, and local tradition that shapes the character of the wine produced.

Establishment and management

Vineyards are typically planted with vines grafted onto disease-resistant rootstock, a practice made necessary by the phylloxera epidemic that devastated European vineyards in the nineteenth century. Vines are trained onto trellises or posts in rows spaced to suit mechanization, sunlight exposure, and air circulation. Annual work includes winter pruning, canopy management, pest and mildew control, irrigation in dry climates, and the harvest, called the vintage, in late summer or autumn. Vine density varies widely: some prestigious Old World vineyards plant 10,000 vines per hectare or more, while mechanized New World plantings may use far fewer.

History

Grape cultivation dates back thousands of years to the Near East and the Caucasus, spreading through the Mediterranean world with Greek and Roman trade. Monasteries preserved and advanced viticulture in medieval Europe, and European colonists carried vines to the Americas, South Africa, Australia, and New Zealand. Today major vineyard regions include Bordeaux, Burgundy, Tuscany, Rioja, the Mosel, Napa Valley, Mendoza, and the Barossa Valley.
