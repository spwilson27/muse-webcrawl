Sitting Bull College is a public tribal land-grant college in Fort Yates, North Dakota, on the Standing Rock Sioux Reservation. Chartered by the Standing Rock Sioux Tribe in 1973, it is named for the Hunkpapa Lakota leader Sitting Bull and serves students from Standing Rock and surrounding communities.

History

The college began as Standing Rock Community College and was renamed in 1996 to honour Sitting Bull. It is accredited by the Higher Learning Commission and is a member of the American Indian Higher Education Consortium of tribal colleges and universities.

Academics

Sitting Bull College offers associate and bachelor's degrees and a master's degree in areas including environmental science, business administration, education, nursing and Lakota studies. Its building programme has produced a modern campus in Fort Yates with satellite sites in McLaughlin and Mobridge, South Dakota.

Mission

As a tribal college, it aims to provide higher education grounded in Lakota and Dakota culture and values, supporting the self-determination and economic development of the Standing Rock nation.
