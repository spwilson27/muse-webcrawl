Overview

The Chicago Seven were seven defendants charged with conspiracy and inciting to riot in connection with anti-Vietnam War protests at the 1968 Democratic National Convention in Chicago. The defendants were Abbie Hoffman, Jerry Rubin, David Dellinger, Tom Hayden, Rennie Davis, John Froines and Lee Weiner; an eighth defendant, Black Panther leader Bobby Seale, was severed from the case.

Trial

The trial before Judge Julius Hoffman, beginning in September 1969, became a theatrical confrontation between the antiwar movement and the legal establishment. Seale was famously bound and gagged in the courtroom before his case was severed. In February 1970 the jury acquitted all defendants of conspiracy; five — Hoffman, Rubin, Dellinger, Hayden and Davis — were convicted of individual charges of crossing state lines with intent to incite a riot, while Froines and Weiner were acquitted entirely.

Outcome

Judge Hoffman also issued numerous contempt citations against the defendants and their lawyers, William Kunstler and Leonard Weinglass. In 1972 the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit reversed the riot convictions, citing judicial and prosecutorial misconduct, and most contempt convictions were overturned or not retried. The case remains a landmark of American political trial history and has been dramatised in the 2020 film The Trial of the Chicago 7.
