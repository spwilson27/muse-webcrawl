Fenian Brotherhood

The Fenian Brotherhood was an Irish republican organisation founded in the United States in 1858 by John O'Mahony and Michael Doheny, exiles of the failed Young Irelander Rebellion of 1848. It was the American counterpart of the Irish Republican Brotherhood (IRB), founded the same year in Dublin by James Stephens. Its members were collectively known as the Fenians, a name O'Mahony took from the Fianna, the warrior bands of Irish mythology.

Aims and organisation

The Brotherhood sought to end British rule in Ireland by force, raising money and men among the Irish-American diaspora. The American Civil War (1861–1865) gave thousands of Irish immigrants military training, and many veterans joined the movement, which organised conventions, issued bonds of a future "Irish Republic", and split into rival factions in 1865.

Fenian raids on Canada

One faction concluded that striking British North America would advance the cause. In 1866 Fenian forces under John O'Neill crossed the Niagara River, won the Battle of Ridgeway against Canadian militia on 2 June, but withdrew as US authorities intervened. Further raids followed in 1870 (Eccles Hill) and 1871 (an unauthorised incursion toward Manitoba). The raids helped spur Canadian Confederation in 1867.

Decline and legacy

In Ireland the Fenian Rising of 1867 failed, but the movement seeded later republicanism; the dynamite campaign of the 1880s was waged by offshoots, and the Brotherhood's American successor, Clan na Gael (founded 1867), funded Irish republican activity into the 20th century, including support behind the Easter Rising of 1916. "Fenian" survives in Northern Ireland as a sectarian term for Irish nationalists/Catholics.
