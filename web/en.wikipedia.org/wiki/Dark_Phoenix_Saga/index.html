"The Dark Phoenix Saga" is an extended X-Men storyline in the fictional Marvel Comics universe, focusing on Jean Grey and the Phoenix Force. It was written by Chris Claremont and illustrated by John Byrne, and published in Uncanny X-Men #129–138 (1980). It is one of the most well-known and heavily referenced stories in American superhero comics, and is widely regarded as a classic of the medium.

Plot

Jean Grey, having bonded with the cosmic Phoenix Force, is manipulated by the Hellfire Club's illusionist Mastermind, who seeks to induct her into the Club as its Black Queen. His mental tampering, aided by the White Queen Emma Frost, shatters Phoenix's restraints and unleashes her dark side. Freed of restraint, Dark Phoenix travels to the stars and consumes the sun of the D'Bari system, killing billions. The X-Men battle her, but it is Jean's resurfacing personality that allows her to seize control long enough to sacrifice herself on the Moon, before the Shi'ar Imperial Guard, rather than risk becoming a threat to the universe.

Legacy

The saga's tragic ending — driven by editor-in-chief Jim Shooter's insistence that Jean could not simply be forgiven for genocide — set a new standard for stakes in superhero comics. Jean Grey's death stood for several years before she was retconned back to life. The story has been adapted in the animated X-Men series and, loosely, in the films X-Men: The Last Stand (2006) and Dark Phoenix (2019).
