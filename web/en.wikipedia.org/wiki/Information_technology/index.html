Information technology

Information technology (IT) is a set of related fields within engineering that involve computer systems, software, programming languages, data and information processing, and storage. It forms part of the broader field of information and communications technology (ICT). An information technology system generally consists of an information system, a communications system, or a computer system, including all hardware, software, and peripheral equipment.

History

The term first appeared in a 1958 Harvard Business Review article by Harold J. Leavitt and Thomas L. Whisler. The field grew out of earlier data-processing departments as stored-program computers spread through business in the second half of the 20th century, and expanded dramatically with the rise of personal computing, the Internet, and mobile devices.

Scope

IT encompasses computer hardware and software, networking, databases, cloud computing, cybersecurity, technical support, and systems administration. Organizations rely on IT for communication, record keeping, commerce, and decision-making.

Careers

Common occupations include systems administrators, network engineers, software developers, database administrators, security analysts, and help-desk technicians. Certifications and degrees in computer science and information systems are frequent entry paths.

Ethical issues

The field raises questions of privacy, data protection, surveillance, intellectual property, and equitable access — sometimes called the digital divide.
