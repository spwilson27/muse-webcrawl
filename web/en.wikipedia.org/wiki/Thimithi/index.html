Thimithi

Thimithi
Type: Hindu fire-walking festival
Honours: Goddess Draupadi (Mariamman)
Observed: Tamil Nadu; diaspora communities incl. Singapore, Malaysia, Fiji, South Africa

Thimithi (Tamil: திமிதி) is a fire-walking ceremony and Hindu festival originating in Tamil Nadu, India, celebrated in honour of the goddess Draupadi, regarded by devotees as an incarnation of the goddess Mariamman. During the festival, devotees walk across a pit of hot embers or coals as an act of devotion and fulfilment of vows.

Rituals

The festival is usually held in the Tamil month of Aipasi (October–November), beginning about a week before Deepavali. Ceremonies retell episodes from the Mahabharata, culminating in the fire-walk, which commemorates Draupadi walking through fire to prove her purity after the war. A priest or lead devotee carrying a sacred water pot (karakattam) typically crosses first, followed by rows of devotees.

Outside India

Thimithi is a major event in places with large Tamil communities. In Singapore it is held at the Sri Mariamman Temple in Chinatown, the country's oldest Hindu temple, drawing thousands of participants and spectators; similar celebrations take place in Malaysia, Mauritius, Fiji, Réunion, and South Africa.
