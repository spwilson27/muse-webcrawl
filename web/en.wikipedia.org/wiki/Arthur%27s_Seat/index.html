Arthur's Seat

Elevation251 m (823 ft)
LocationEdinburgh, Scotland
GeologyExtinct volcano (Carboniferous)
Parent rangeHolyrood Park

Arthur's Seat (Scottish Gaelic: Suidhe Artair) is the main peak of the group of hills in Holyrood Park in Edinburgh, Scotland. It rises 251 metres (823 ft) above the city and provides excellent panoramic views. The hill is the eroded remnant of an extinct volcano that was active around 335 million years ago during the Carboniferous period.

Geology

Arthur's Seat is part of a volcanic complex that includes Salisbury Crags and the Lion's Head and Lion's Haunch vents. Glacial erosion during the ice ages sculpted the hill, producing classic features such as the crag-and-tail formation shared with Edinburgh Castle Rock. The hill was an important site in the history of geology: James Hutton studied its exposures in the 18th century.

Name

The name's origin is uncertain. It is popularly associated with King Arthur, though some scholars connect it to a Gaelic phrase meaning "Archer's Seat" or to a legendary battle location. There is no evidence of a direct connection with the historical Arthur.

History and recreation

A hill fort crowns the summit and there are traces of Iron Age settlement and cultivation terraces on the slopes. In 1836, schoolboys discovered miniature coffins hidden in a cave on the hill, which remain an unsolved curiosity. Today Arthur's Seat is a popular walking destination, easily climbed from several paths including Dunsapie Loch and the Radical Road.
