Arusha National Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Mount Meru seen from Arusha National Park

Arusha National Park covers Mount Meru, a prominent volcano with an elevation of 4,566 metres (14,980 ft), in the Arusha Region of north eastern Tanzania. The park is small but varied, with spectacular landscapes in three distinct areas. In the west, the Meru Crater funnels the Jekukumia River; the peak of Mount Meru lies on its rim. Ngurdoto Crater in the south-east is grassland. The shallow alkaline Momella Lakes in the north-east have varying algal colours and are known for their wading birds.

Wildlife

Wildlife in the park includes giraffe, Cape buffalo, zebra, warthog, the black-and-white colobus monkey, the blue monkey, flamingo, elephant, lion and leopard, although lions are uncommon. Mount Meru's slopes are covered in montane forest giving way to alpine desert near the summit.

History and access

The park was established in 1960 as Ngurdoto Crater National Park and was renamed in 1967 when Mount Meru was included. It lies a few kilometres northeast of the city of Arusha, making it a popular day-trip safari destination; it is one of the few Tanzanian national parks where walking safaris and canoeing on the Momella Lakes are offered.
