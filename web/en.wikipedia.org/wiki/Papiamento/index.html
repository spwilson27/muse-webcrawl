Papiamento

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Papiamento (English: /ˌpɑːpiəˈmɛntoʊ/) or Papiamentu (in Curaçao and Bonaire) is a Portuguese-based creole language spoken in the Dutch Caribbean. It is the most widely spoken language on the Caribbean islands of Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao (the ABC islands), with roughly 350,000 speakers. The language, spelled Papiamento in Aruba and Papiamentu elsewhere, has official status in Aruba and Curaçao alongside Dutch.

History

Papiamento developed on Curaçao in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Its origins are debated: scholars generally agree that its core derives from an Afro-Portuguese pidgin or creole brought by enslaved Africans and Portuguese-speaking (including Sephardic Jewish) traders, subsequently influenced by Spanish, Dutch, and West African languages, as well as Arawak and English. The oldest surviving written Papiamento documents date from the late 18th century.

Characteristics

The vocabulary is largely Iberian (Portuguese and Spanish), with a significant Dutch component and smaller contributions from African languages, Arawak, English, and French. Papiamento is a tonal language, using pitch accent as well as stress to distinguish words. Verbs are uninflected; tense, mood, and aspect are marked by preverbal particles such as ta (present/progressive), a (perfective), and lo (future).

Orthography

Two spelling systems are in official use: the etymological orthography of Curaçao and Bonaire (Papiamentu) and the phonetic orthography of Aruba (Papiamento). Papiamento-language media include newspapers such as Extra and Bon Dia Aruba, radio, and television broadcasting.

Status

Papiamento was declared an official language of Aruba in 2003 and of Curaçao in 2007. It is widely used in parliament, education, and media on the islands, alongside Dutch and, in practice, English and Spanish.
