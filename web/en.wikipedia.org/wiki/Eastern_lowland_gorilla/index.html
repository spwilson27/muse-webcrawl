The eastern lowland gorilla (Gorilla beringei graueri), also known as Grauer's gorilla, is a subspecies of eastern gorilla endemic to the mountainous forests of eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. Significant populations live in Kahuzi-Biega and Maiko National Parks and their adjacent forests, the Tayna Gorilla Reserve, and the Itombwe Massif.

Description

It is the largest of the four gorilla subspecies. Adult males can weigh over 200 kg in the wild and stand up to about 1.7 m tall. The coat is jet black, and mature males develop a saddle of silver-grey hair on the back. Compared with the mountain gorilla, Grauer's gorilla has a shorter, narrower face and shorter hair.

Behaviour and diet

Eastern lowland gorillas live in stable family groups led by a dominant silverback, typically numbering from a few individuals to more than 30. They are primarily herbivorous, feeding on leaves, stems, bark, fruit and occasionally ants and termites. Groups travel modest daily distances and build new nests of vegetation each night, on the ground or in trees.

Conservation

The subspecies has suffered a catastrophic decline driven by poaching for bushmeat, habitat loss, and the insecurity associated with armed conflict and illegal mining for coltan and other minerals in eastern Congo. A 2016 assessment estimated a decline of roughly 77 percent in one generation, to fewer than 4,000 individuals, and the IUCN lists Grauer's gorilla as Critically Endangered.
