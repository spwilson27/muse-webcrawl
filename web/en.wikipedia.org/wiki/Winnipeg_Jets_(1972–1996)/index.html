Winnipeg Jets (1972–1996)

The Winnipeg Jets were a professional ice hockey team based in Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada. They began play in the World Hockey Association (WHA) in 1972, moving to the National Hockey League (NHL) in 1979 following the NHL–WHA merger. The team relocated to Phoenix, Arizona in 1996, becoming the Phoenix Coyotes (now the Arizona Coyotes). The Jets name was revived in 2011 when the Atlanta Thrashers relocated to Winnipeg.

WHA years

The original Jets were among the most successful WHA franchises, winning the Avco World Trophy in 1976, 1978 and 1979. The club made headlines by signing Bobby Hull away from the NHL's Chicago Black Hawks in 1972, and built a strong roster featuring Swedish stars such as Anders Hedberg and Ulf Nilsson, pioneering the recruitment of European talent in North American professional hockey.

NHL years

In the NHL the Jets were a competitive but rarely contending team, hampered by playing in the strong Smythe Division alongside the Edmonton Oilers and Calgary Flames dynasties. Notable players included Dale Hawerchuk, Thomas Steen, Teemu Selänne — who set the NHL rookie record with 76 goals in 1992–93 — and Keith Tkachuk.

Relocation

Rising player salaries, a weak Canadian dollar and the small Winnipeg market led to the team's sale and relocation to Phoenix in 1996. The departure was mourned by fans who had staged a "Save the Jets" campaign. Fifteen years later, NHL hockey returned to Winnipeg.
