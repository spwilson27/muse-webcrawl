Wakhan Corridor

Country: Afghanistan (Badakhshan Province)

Length: About 350 km (220 mi)

Width: Roughly 13–65 km (8–40 mi)

Borders: Tajikistan (north), Pakistan (south), China (east)

The Wakhan Corridor (Pashto: واخان دهلېز; Dari: دالان واخان) is a narrow strip of territory in Badakhshan Province in north-eastern Afghanistan. Extending eastward from the main body of the country, the corridor separates Tajikistan to the north from Pakistan's Gilgit-Baltistan to the south and gives Afghanistan a short border — about 92 km — with China at the Wakhjir Pass. It lies between the Pamir Mountains to the north and the Karakoram range to the south, following the valleys of the Panj and Wakhan rivers.

History

The corridor is an artefact of the 19th-century "Great Game" between the British and Russian empires. Agreements in 1873 and the Pamir Boundary Commission of 1895–96 assigned the strip to Afghanistan as a buffer so that British India and Russian Central Asia would not share a border; the Durand Line agreement of 1893 fixed its southern boundary. Historically, the Wakhan valley had been a minor branch route of the Silk Road and was traversed by travellers including Xuanzang and Marco Polo.

Geography and people

The corridor is high, remote and sparsely populated, with settlements at elevations between roughly 2,600 and 4,900 metres. Its inhabitants include Wakhi farmers in the lower valleys and Kyrgyz pastoralists on the high Pamir pastures. The district capital is Qazideh. The Little Pamir at its eastern end provides traditional grazing grounds. China has kept its side of the Wakhjir Pass closed, though road-building to the pass has periodically raised speculation about a future trade route; the corridor also attracted attention for a proposed Wakhan national park and conservation of snow leopards and Marco Polo sheep.
