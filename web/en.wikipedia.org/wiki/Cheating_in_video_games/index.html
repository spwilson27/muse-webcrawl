Cheating in video games

A wallhack overlay reveals opponents through solid surfaces

Cheating in video games is the use of methods outside a game's intended rules to gain an unfair advantage or alter the experience. In single-player games, cheating has historically been tolerated or even built in through cheat codes and secret modes. In multiplayer games, cheating is broadly condemned because it harms other players, and developers actively detect and punish it.

Types of cheats

  • Aimbots — software that automatically aims a player's weapon, often snapping to opponents' heads.
  • Wallhacks — modifications that render opponents (or items) visible through walls, sometimes as part of an "ESP" (extra-sensory perception) overlay showing health and distance.
  • Trainers and memory editors — tools that alter values in the game's memory, such as health or currency.
  • Bots and macros — automation of gameplay, including farming and grinding; in online games this is usually prohibited.
  • Exploits — abuse of bugs or unintended mechanics; whether an exploit counts as cheating is often debated by communities.
  • Boosting and account sharing — having a more skilled player raise an account's rank, or purchasing wins.

Anti-cheat measures

Publishers deploy anti-cheat systems such as Valve Anti-Cheat (VAC), Easy Anti-Cheat, BattlEye, PunkBuster and Riot Vanguard. Methods include signature scanning, behavioural analysis, server-side validation, hardware bans and, controversially, kernel-level drivers that monitor the system at a deep level. Developers periodically ban large numbers of accounts in "ban waves". Some companies have sued cheat makers; in cases such as MDY Industries v. Blizzard Entertainment, litigation centred on bots that automated World of Warcraft play.

Cheating in esports

Cheating scandals have periodically shaken professional play. In 2014 the Counter-Strike player Hovik "KQLY" Tovmassian received a VAC ban days before a major tournament, leading to his team's disqualification and a lifetime ban, amid a wider wave of professional bans. LAN events now routinely inspect players' equipment, and coaches and analysts have also been sanctioned for abusing spectator bugs.

History

Deliberate cheats are as old as commercial video games: developers embedded codes for testing that later became features, most famously the Konami Code (1986). Devices such as Game Genie and Action Replay let console players modify games, a practice Nintendo challenged unsuccessfully in court in the early 1990s. The rise of persistent online games from the late 1990s turned cheating into an arms race and a commercial industry, with subscription cheat software sold to thousands of users.
