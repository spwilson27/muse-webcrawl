Billboard charts

The Billboard charts tabulate the relative weekly popularity of songs and albums in the United States and, increasingly, worldwide. Published by Billboard magazine, they are the music industry's standard measure of commercial success. The two flagship charts are the Billboard Hot 100, ranking singles by sales, radio airplay and streaming, and the Billboard 200, ranking albums by equivalent album units.

The Hot 100 has ranked America's top songs since 1958.

History and methodology

Billboard printed its first chart of sheet-music best sellers in 1913 and its first record chart, the "Chart Line" of most-played songs, in 1936. The album chart began in 1945 and became the Billboard 200 in 1992. Methodology has repeatedly modernised: Nielsen SoundScan point-of-sale data was adopted in 1991, ending reliance on retailer reports; digital downloads were incorporated in the 2000s; on-demand and later programmatic streams, and YouTube views, joined the Hot 100 formula in 2012–2013. Since 2022 global charts (the Billboard Global 200) have tracked worldwide activity.

Chart categories

Beyond the flagships, Billboard publishes genre charts for country, R&B/hip-hop, Latin, rock, dance/electronic, Christian, gospel and jazz, as well as component charts for airplay, sales and streaming, and international charts produced with affiliates.

Criticism

Chart rules are periodically tightened to counter manipulation, including bans on merchandise-and-ticket bundles counting toward album units (2020) and changes to recurrent rules, bulk-buying and radio promotion practices.
