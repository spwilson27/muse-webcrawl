Delegation (law)

In contract and administrative law, delegation is the assignment of a duty or authority to another party. A delegator transfers performance of a contractual obligation to a delegate, but generally remains liable unless a novation releases them.

Contract law

Duties involving personal skill, trust or services are non-delegable; routine duties usually are. Anti-delegation clauses may bar transfer. Delegation differs from assignment, which transfers rights (benefits) rather than duties.

Administrative law

Legislatures delegate rulemaking power to agencies; the US nondelegation doctrine limits this, requiring an "intelligible principle" to guide the agency, though it has rarely invalidated statutes since the 1930s.
