Konstantin Melnikov

The Melnikov House (1929), Moscow — the architect's own home and masterpiece

Konstantin Stepanovich Melnikov (Russian: Константин Степанович Мельников; 3 August 1890 – 28 November 1974) was a Russian and Soviet architect and painter, a leading figure of the avant-garde and of Constructivist architecture. In a career compressed into little more than a decade of building (1923–1933), he produced some of the most original structures of the twentieth century, most famously his own cylindrical house in Moscow.

Career

Melnikov rose to prominence with the Makhorka tobacco pavilion (1923) and a wooden sarcophagus displayed at Lenin's funeral period, then won international attention with the USSR Pavilion at the 1925 Paris Exposition of Decorative Arts — a light, diagonally-cut construction of glass and timber. Back in Moscow he built a series of workers' clubs (Rusakov, Burevestnik, Kauchuk, Frunze and others), parking garages with experimental spiral and sawtooth layouts, and department stores. His six surviving clubs are celebrated for their bold sculptural forms and transformable theatre halls.

The Melnikov House

Between 1927 and 1929 Melnikov built his family home and studio in Krivoarbatsky Lane: two intersecting white cylinders, the rear one sliced by a full-height glazed screen and honeycombed with some sixty hexagonal windows. Granted a unique exemption to build a private house in Soviet Moscow, he designed it as a manifesto of his ideas on light, space, and economy of material. He lived there until his death, refusing commissions under Stalin's socialist realism; from 1937 he was effectively barred from building and worked as a teacher and painter.

Legacy

Long neglected, Melnikov's buildings were championed internationally from the 1960s, though many remain in precarious condition. The Melnikov House, preserved by his son Viktor, also an artist, became a state museum in 2014 after a long inheritance dispute, securing the survival of the most famous private house of the Soviet avant-garde.
