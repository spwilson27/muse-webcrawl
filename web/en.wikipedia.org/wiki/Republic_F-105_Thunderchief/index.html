The Republic F-105 Thunderchief was an American supersonic fighter-bomber used by the United States Air Force. The largest single-seat, single-engine combat aircraft in history, it was designed in the 1950s as a nuclear strike aircraft but became famous as the main strike aircraft of the early Vietnam War, flying the majority of bombing missions over North Vietnam.

Design

The F-105 was powered by a single Pratt & Whitney J75 turbojet, used distinctive forward-swept wing-root intakes, and carried an internal bomb bay plus external stores. Nicknamed the "Thud" by its crews, it could exceed Mach 2 and carried a heavier bomb load than Second World War heavy bombers. Two-seat Wild Weasel variants pioneered suppression of enemy air defences, hunting North Vietnamese surface-to-air missile sites.

Operational history

Thunderchiefs flew more than 20,000 sorties over Southeast Asia, but suffered heavy losses — 334 were lost in combat — and the type was withdrawn from front-line service by 1970, serving in reserve units until 1984.
