Sopwith Aviation Company

Industry: Aircraft manufacturing

Founded: 1912, Kingston upon Thames, England

Founder: Thomas Sopwith

Defunct: 1920

Successor: Hawker Aircraft

The Sopwith Aviation Company was a British aircraft manufacturer founded in 1912 by Thomas Sopwith, a pioneer aviator and yachtsman. Based at Kingston upon Thames, with a flying ground at Brooklands, the company became one of the most important suppliers of military aircraft during the First World War.

History

Thomas Sopwith had taught himself to fly in 1910 and won the Baron de Forest prize for the longest flight from Britain to the Continent. He established a flying school at Brooklands before forming the Sopwith Aviation Company in 1912. An early success was the Sopwith Tabloid, a small fast biplane that won the 1914 Schneider Trophy as a floatplane and became a scout aircraft at the start of the war.

During the First World War, Sopwith produced more than 18,000 aircraft. Famous types included the Sopwith Pup, the Sopwith Triplane, the two-seat 1½ Strutter, and above all the Sopwith Camel, credited with shooting down more enemy aircraft (1,294) than any other Allied type. Many Sopwith designs were built under licence by other manufacturers.

Post-war and closure

After the Armistice, the company attempted to adapt designs for the civil market, but the glut of surplus military aircraft and heavy Excess Profits Duty liabilities made trading impossible. Sopwith Aviation went into voluntary liquidation in 1920. Thomas Sopwith, with his chief test pilot Harry Hawker and colleagues, immediately formed a new company, H. G. Hawker Engineering, which became Hawker Aircraft and later part of Hawker Siddeley — the lineage that produced the Hurricane and the Harrier.
