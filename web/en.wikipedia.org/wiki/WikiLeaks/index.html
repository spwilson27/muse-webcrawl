WikiLeaks

Type: Non-profit media organisation

Founded: 2006

Founder: Julian Assange

WikiLeaks is a non-profit media organisation and publisher founded in 2006 by Australian editor Julian Assange. It specialises in the analysis and publication of large datasets of censored or otherwise restricted official materials involving war, spying, and corruption. The organisation states that it has published more than ten million documents over its history.

WikiLeaks came to international attention in 2010 when it published a series of leaks provided by U.S. Army intelligence analyst Chelsea Manning. These included the "Collateral Murder" video of a 2007 Baghdad airstrike, the Iraq War logs, the Afghan War diary, and a large cache of U.S. diplomatic cables commonly known as "Cablegate".

History

The website was established in late 2006 and launched its first document in December of that year. Early publications included a decision by the Somali Islamic Courts Union leadership and documents relating to offshore banking in the Cayman Islands. By 2008 it had won an Economist magazine New Media Award.

Notable releases

Beyond the 2010 releases, WikiLeaks published the Guantanamo Bay files (2011), emails from the Syrian government (2012), draft chapters of the Trans-Pacific Partnership, and in 2016 internal emails from the Democratic National Committee and Hillary Clinton campaign chairman John Podesta. In 2017 it began releasing "Vault 7", documents describing CIA hacking capabilities.

Reception and controversies

Supporters credit WikiLeaks with exposing government and corporate wrongdoing and advancing press freedom; it has received several journalism awards. Critics, including U.S. government officials, have argued that some releases endangered lives and national security. Assange was arrested in London in 2019 after Ecuador revoked his asylum in its embassy, where he had lived since 2012, and in June 2024 he accepted a plea deal in the United States and was released.
