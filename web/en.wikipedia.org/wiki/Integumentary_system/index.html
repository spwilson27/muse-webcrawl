Integumentary system

The integumentary system is the set of organs forming the outermost layer of an animal's body. It comprises the skin and its appendages — hair, scales, feathers, hooves and nails — and acts as a physical barrier between the external environment and the inside of the body, protecting it and maintaining homeostasis.

Structure

Human skin consists of two main layers. The epidermis, a stratified squamous epithelium derived from ectoderm, provides a waterproof barrier and creates skin tone through melanocytes. Beneath it, the dermis, of mesodermal origin, contains connective tissue, blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, sweat glands and sebaceous glands. Below the dermis lies the hypodermis, or subcutaneous fat. Appendages such as hair and nails are formed from keratin, and glands secrete sweat and sebum onto the surface.

Functions

  • Protection against mechanical injury, pathogens, ultraviolet radiation and water loss.
  • Thermoregulation through sweating, insulation and changes in surface blood flow.
  • Sensation via receptors for touch, pressure, pain, vibration and temperature.
  • Metabolism, including synthesis of vitamin D in the skin.
  • Excretion of salts and wastes in sweat, and storage of water, fat, glucose and vitamin D.

Diseases of the system include dermatitis, psoriasis, skin cancers and burns; the medical specialty concerned with it is dermatology.
