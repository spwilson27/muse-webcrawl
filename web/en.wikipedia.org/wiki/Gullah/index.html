Gullah

The Gullah are an African American ethnic group who predominantly live in the Lowcountry region of the U.S. states of South Carolina, Georgia, Florida and North Carolina, including both the coastal plain and the Sea Islands. Their culture and language developed among enslaved Africans brought to work on rice, indigo and cotton plantations, and preserved strong West and Central African influences due to the relative isolation of the island communities.

The Gullah language, also called Geechee or Sea Island Creole English, is an English-based creole language with substantial African vocabulary and grammatical features. Gullah culture is known for its oral storytelling traditions, arts such as sweetgrass basket weaving, spirituals and ring shouts, and a cuisine built on rice, seafood, okra and other West African-influenced dishes.

In 2006 the U.S. Congress established the Gullah/Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor to help preserve the culture, and a complete translation of the New Testament into Gullah was published in 2005 after decades of work.
