Real-time locating system

TypePositioning technology
TechnologiesRFID, Wi-Fi, UWB, Bluetooth, infrared, ultrasound
UsesAsset tracking, hospitals, logistics, manufacturing

Real-time locating systems (RTLS) are used to automatically identify and track the location of objects or people in real time, usually within a building or other contained area.

How it works

Wireless RTLS tags are attached to objects or worn by people, and fixed reference points receive signals from the tags to compute positions, using methods such as trilateration, time of flight, angle of arrival, or signal-strength fingerprinting. Ultra-wideband (UWB) systems can achieve accuracies of tens of centimeters indoors.

Applications

Hospitals track medical devices and staff, warehouses and factories track assets and work in progress, and correctional facilities and mines track personnel for safety. RTLS is sometimes described as an indoor analogue of GPS, which performs poorly inside buildings.

Standards

The ISO/IEC 24730 series standardizes RTLS interfaces. Accuracy, update rate, battery life, and infrastructure cost trade off against one another in system design.
