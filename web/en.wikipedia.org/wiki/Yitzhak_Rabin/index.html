Yitzhak Rabin

Born: 1 March 1922, Jerusalem, Mandatory Palestine
Died: 4 November 1995 (aged 73), Tel Aviv, Israel
Office: Prime Minister of Israel (1974–1977; 1992–1995)
Awards: Nobel Peace Prize (1994)

Yitzhak Rabin (Hebrew: יצחק רבין; 1 March 1922 – 4 November 1995) was an Israeli statesman and general who served twice as prime minister of Israel, from 1974 to 1977 and again from 1992 until his assassination in 1995. A career soldier who became chief of staff of the Israel Defense Forces during the Six-Day War, Rabin later pursued a negotiated peace with the Palestinians, signing the Oslo Accords and receiving the Nobel Peace Prize.

Military career

Rabin joined the Palmach in 1941 and fought in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, commanding the Harel Brigade on the Jerusalem front. He rose steadily through the IDF, and as chief of staff from 1964 to 1968 he prepared the air strike and ground campaign that won the Six-Day War of June 1967. Afterwards he served as ambassador to the United States, deepening the strategic alliance between the two countries.

First premiership and return

Rabin became prime minister in 1974, succeeding Golda Meir, and negotiated the Sinai Interim Agreement with Egypt before resigning in 1977 over a financial scandal involving a US bank account held by his wife. As defence minister in the 1980s he oversaw Israel's partial withdrawal from Lebanon and the early response to the First Intifada, during which his "force, might and beatings" remark became notorious.

Oslo peace process

Winning the 1992 election on a security-and-peace platform, Rabin recognised the Palestine Liberation Organization and signed the Oslo I Accord in 1993, shaking hands with Yasser Arafat on the White House lawn. The Oslo II Agreement followed in 1995, as did the Israel–Jordan peace treaty. In 1994 Rabin shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Shimon Peres and Arafat. The agreements provoked fierce domestic opposition, including mass rallies at which Rabin was depicted in an SS uniform and denounced as a traitor.

Assassination

On 4 November 1995, minutes after addressing a mass peace rally in Tel Aviv — where he had sung the "Song for Peace" — Rabin was shot by Yigal Amir, a far-right Israeli law student opposed to the accords. He died in surgery at Ichilov Hospital. Rabin's assassination is regarded as a watershed in Israeli history, and the square where he was killed was renamed Rabin Square in his memory.
