Gasoline (North American English) or petrol (Commonwealth English) is a petrochemical product characterized as a transparent, yellowish, and flammable liquid, used primarily as a fuel for spark-ignited internal combustion engines. It consists mostly of organic compounds obtained by the fractional distillation of petroleum, typically C4–C12 hydrocarbons, enhanced with a variety of additives.

Properties and grades

The key performance measure is the octane rating, which expresses resistance to engine knocking. Regular grades are typically 87 AKI in North America (91–92 RON) and premium grades 91–93 AKI (95–98 RON). Historically, tetraethyl lead was added to raise octane; leaded gasoline was phased out for road vehicles worldwide by 2021 because of lead's toxicity. Modern blends include detergents, oxygenates such as ethanol (commonly E10, 10% ethanol), and dyes for tax purposes.

Production and use

Refineries produce gasoline by distillation, catalytic cracking, reforming and alkylation, blending streams to meet seasonal volatility specifications. Gasoline remains the principal transport fuel for light vehicles — global consumption is around 25 million barrels per day — though electric vehicles are eroding demand in many markets. Combustion of a litre of gasoline releases about 2.3 kg of carbon dioxide, making it a significant contributor to climate change.
