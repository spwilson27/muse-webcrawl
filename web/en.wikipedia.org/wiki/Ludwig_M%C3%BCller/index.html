Ludwig Müller (1883–1945) was a German theologian and Nazi who served as Reich Bishop of the German Evangelical Church from 1933. A leading figure in the pro-Nazi "German Christians" movement, he attempted to unify Germany's Protestant churches under Nazi control.

Career

Müller was ordained a Lutheran pastor, served as a naval chaplain in the First World War, and afterwards became a Wehrmacht chaplain in East Prussia, where he met and admired Adolf Hitler. He joined the Nazi Party and the German Christians, and with Hitler's backing was installed as Reich Bishop in September 1933 over the objections of much of the church.

Church struggle

Müller's attempts to impose the "Aryan paragraph" on the church and subordinate it to the regime provoked the Kirchenkampf: pastors formed the Confessing Church, issuing the Barmen Declaration of 1934 rejecting Nazi theological claims. Müller's authority steadily eroded, and from 1935 real administration passed to a state Ministry of Church Affairs. Marginalized and ineffective, he died in Berlin in July 1945, probably by suicide, shortly after the war's end.
