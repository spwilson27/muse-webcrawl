Rodrigues' rotation formula

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the theory of three-dimensional rotation, Rodrigues' rotation formula, named after Olinde Rodrigues, is an efficient algorithm for rotating a vector in space, given an axis and angle of rotation. By extension, this can be used to transform all three basis vectors to compute a rotation matrix in SO(3), the group of all rotations, from an axis-angle representation.

Definition

If v is a vector in three-dimensional space and k is a unit vector describing an axis of rotation about which v rotates by an angle θ according to the right-hand rule, the Rodrigues formula for the rotated vector vrot is:

vrot = v cos θ + (k × v) sin θ + k (k · v) (1 − cos θ).

The formula decomposes v into components parallel and perpendicular to the axis; the parallel component is unchanged, while the perpendicular component rotates in the plane orthogonal to the axis. In matrix form, the rotation matrix can be written as R = I + (sin θ) K + (1 − cos θ) K², where K is the cross-product matrix of k.

History and applications

Rodrigues published the formula in 1840. It is widely used in computer graphics, rigid-body dynamics, robotics, and aerospace for composing rotations from axis-angle data, and it underlies the exponential map from the Lie algebra so(3) to the rotation group SO(3).
