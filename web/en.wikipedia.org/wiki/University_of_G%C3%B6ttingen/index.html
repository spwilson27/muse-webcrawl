University of Göttingen


Founded: 1734 (opened 1737)
Location: Göttingen, Lower Saxony, Germany
Founder: George II of Great Britain, Elector of Hanover
Notable alumni: Gauss, Riemann, Hilbert, the Göttingen Seven context

The University of Göttingen, officially the Georg August University of Göttingen, is a public research university in the city of Göttingen, Germany. Founded in the Enlightenment era, it became one of Europe's leading universities, especially renowned in mathematics and the natural sciences.

History

George II founded the university in 1734 and it opened in 1737, quickly attracting scholars across Europe. In 1837 the protest of the Göttingen Seven against royal abrogation of the constitution made it famous for academic freedom. Around 1900, mathematicians David Hilbert and Felix Klein made Göttingen the world centre of mathematics, a position destroyed by the Nazi-era purge of Jewish and dissenting scholars in 1933.

Legacy

More than forty Nobel laureates have studied or taught at Göttingen, including Max Born, Werner Heisenberg and Otto Hahn. Its library, the Göttingen State and University Library, is one of Germany's largest.
