International standard

An international standard is a technical standard developed by one or more international standards organizations. International standards are available for consideration and use worldwide. The most prominent such organization is the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), alongside the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).

Purpose

International standards facilitate world trade by providing common specifications that ensure products, processes and services are fit for their purpose, safe, interoperable and of consistent quality. They allow manufacturers to build to a single specification for global markets and give purchasers and regulators a common basis for conformity assessment.

Development

International standards are typically developed through consensus processes in technical committees drawing on experts delegated by national standards bodies. Drafts proceed through stages of proposal, preparation, committee review, enquiry and approval before publication. The resulting documents are usually voluntary, though they may be referenced in legislation or regulation and thereby acquire legal effect.

Types

International standards include terminology and measurement standards, product and test method standards, and management system standards such as the ISO 9000 family for quality management and ISO 14000 for environmental management.
