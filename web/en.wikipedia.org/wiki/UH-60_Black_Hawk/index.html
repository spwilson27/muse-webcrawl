Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk

The Sikorsky UH-60 Black Hawk is a four-blade, twin-engine, medium-lift utility military helicopter manufactured by Sikorsky Aircraft. Sikorsky submitted the design for the United States Army's Utility Tactical Transport Aircraft System (UTTAS) competition in 1972. Named after the Native American war leader Black Hawk, the UH-60A entered service with the Army in 1979 and remains a workhorse of U.S. and allied forces.

Development

The Army's UTTAS programme sought a replacement for the Bell UH-1 Iroquois capable of carrying a full infantry squad, surviving battle damage, and being transportable in a C-130 Hercules. Sikorsky's YUH-60A won the competition in 1976. The design features ballistic-tolerant fuel tanks, a crashworthy structure, and folding rotor blades.

Variants and use

The Black Hawk family grew to include the improved UH-60L and UH-60M and many derivatives: the HH-60 Pave Hawk for combat rescue, the MH-60 special operations variants, the SH-60 Seahawk for naval use, and the VH-60 used for presidential transport. Black Hawks have served in conflicts from Grenada and Panama to Iraq and Afghanistan, and have been exported widely. The type is famous in popular culture from the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu, depicted in the film Black Hawk Down.
