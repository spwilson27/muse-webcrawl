INS Vikrant is an aircraft carrier in service with the Indian Navy. She is the first carrier to be designed and built in India, by Cochin Shipyard in Kochi, and the lead ship of her class. Commissioned on 2 September 2022, she made India one of a small group of nations able to design and construct an aircraft carrier indigenously. Her name honours the earlier INS Vikrant (the former HMS Hercules), which played a prominent role in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971; vikrānta means "courageous" in Sanskrit.

Design

Vikrant is about 262 metres long and displaces roughly 45,000 tonnes. She uses a STOBAR (short take-off but arrested recovery) configuration with a 14-degree ski-jump and three arrester wires. Propulsion is by four General Electric LM2500+ gas turbines driving two shafts, giving a top speed of about 28 knots and an endurance of some 7,500 nautical miles. She can operate around 30 aircraft, including MiG-29K fighters and Kamov Ka-31, MH-60R and Dhruv helicopters, and carries a crew of about 1,600. Defensive armament includes Barak 8 surface-to-air missiles and AK-630 close-in weapon systems.

Construction and trials

The keel was laid in February 2009 and the ship was floated out in 2011 and formally launched in August 2013. After delays including gearbox and equipment supply problems, basin trials were completed in late 2020 and sea trials began in August 2021. The ship was delivered in July 2022 and commissioned by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 2 September 2022. India has since contracted for Rafale M fighters to operate from her, and a developmental indigenous twin-engine deck-based fighter (TEDBF) is planned.
