Sibhudu Cave

Location: KwaDukuza, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa
Type: Rock shelter
Period: Middle Stone Age (c. 77,000–38,000 BP)
Excavations: 1998–present

Sibhudu Cave (also spelled Sibudu) is a rock shelter and major Middle Stone Age archaeological site in a sandstone cliff above the uThongathi (Tongati) River, about 40 kilometres north of Durban in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. Excavated systematically since 1998 under the direction of Lyn Wadley of the University of the Witwatersrand, and later Nicholas Conard and colleagues from the University of Tübingen, Sibhudu has produced some of the earliest evidence anywhere for technologies once thought to mark much later "modern" behaviour.

Key finds

The site's deeply stratified deposits span roughly 77,000 to 38,000 years ago, including the Still Bay and Howiesons Poort industries. Discoveries include some of the earliest known bone arrow points (about 61,000 years old), evidence for snares and traps, a bow-and-arrow hunting technology inferred from tiny backed stone segments, and stone points bearing residues of compound adhesives made with red ochre — among the earliest examples of multi-component hafting glue. Charred bedding layers of sedges and aromatic leaves, dated to about 77,000 years ago, represent the earliest known plant bedding; the leaves, from trees such as Cryptocarya woodii, contain natural insecticides, suggesting the occupants selected them deliberately. Needles, shell beads, worked ochre, and milkweed-pod containers of insect-repellent are also reported.

Significance

Sibhudu is central to debates about the origins of behavioural modernity in Africa. Its sequence shows that sophisticated hunting technology, symbolic material culture, and complex pyrotechnology appeared in southern Africa tens of thousands of years before their counterparts in Ice Age Europe, and that these innovations waxed and waned with environmental conditions rather than following a single inevitable progression. In 2024, Sibhudu was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing "The Emergence of Modern Human Behaviour: The Pleistocene Occupation Sites of South Africa", together with Diepkloof Rock Shelter and the Pinnacle Point complex.
