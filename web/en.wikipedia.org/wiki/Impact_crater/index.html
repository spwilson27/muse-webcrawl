Impact crater

An impact crater is a depression in the surface of a planet, moon or other solid body formed by the hypervelocity impact of a smaller body such as a meteorite, asteroid or comet.

Formation

Impacts occur in three stages: contact and compression, excavation, and modification. The projectile vaporizes and melts target rock, excavating a transient cavity that collapses into a simple bowl-shaped crater (for small impacts) or a complex crater with central peak and terraced walls (for larger ones).

Identification

Diagnostic evidence includes shatter cones, shocked quartz with planar deformation features, impact melt, breccia, and geochemical anomalies such as high iridium. On Earth erosion and plate tectonics erase most craters; about 200 are confirmed.

Examples

Well-known examples include Barringer Crater in Arizona, Chicxulub in Mexico (linked to the end-Cretaceous extinction), Vredefort in South Africa (the largest verified), and Sudbury in Canada. The Moon, Mercury and Mars preserve dense crater records.
