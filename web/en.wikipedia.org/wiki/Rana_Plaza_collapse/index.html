The Rana Plaza collapse occurred on 24 April 2013, when the eight-storey Rana Plaza commercial building in Savar, near Dhaka, Bangladesh, collapsed during working hours. The building housed several garment factories employing about 5,000 workers, along with shops and a bank.

Background

The building had been built for commercial use, with upper floors added without proper permits, and large industrial generators on the roof caused vibrations. The day before the collapse, cracks appeared in the walls and the building was evacuated, but factory owners ordered workers back inside the next morning.

Collapse and rescue

The building pancaked at about 8:45 a.m., trapping thousands. A 19-day rescue operation recovered 1,134 dead and rescued about 2,500 injured survivors, including Reshma Begum, who was pulled from the rubble 17 days after the collapse.

Aftermath and reforms

The disaster — the deadliest accidental structural failure in modern history and the deadliest garment-factory incident — prompted international outrage, the creation of the Accord on Fire and Building Safety in Bangladesh, compensation funds for victims, and prosecutions of the building's owner, Sohel Rana, and others.
