Chinese space program

The Chinese space program is the spaceflight programme of the People's Republic of China, administered principally by the China National Space Administration (CNSA) and the People's Liberation Army. It is one of the most active national space programmes in the world.

History

China began rocket development in the 1950s under scientists such as Qian Xuesen. The Dong Fang Hong I satellite, launched in 1970, made China the fifth country to place a satellite in orbit independently. Crewed flight began in 2003 when Yang Liwei flew aboard Shenzhou 5.

Major programmes

The programme includes the Tiangong space station, the Chang'e lunar exploration missions — including the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon in 2019 and lunar sample return in 2020 — and the Tianwen-1 Mars mission, which landed the Zhurong rover in 2021. Launch is conducted mainly with the Long March family of rockets from sites including Jiuquan, Xichang, Taiyuan and Wenchang.

Future plans

China plans a crewed lunar landing before 2030 and, with Russia, an International Lunar Research Station.
