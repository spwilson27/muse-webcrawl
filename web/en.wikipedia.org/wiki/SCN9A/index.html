SCN9A is a gene encoding the alpha subunit of the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.7. The channel is expressed predominantly in peripheral sensory neurons, including nociceptors (pain-sensing neurons) in the dorsal root ganglia, and it plays an essential role in the generation and conduction of action potentials in the pain pathway.

Clinical significance

Mutations in SCN9A cause several distinctive pain disorders. Gain-of-function mutations can cause inherited erythromelalgia, a condition of burning pain and redness in the extremities, and paroxysmal extreme pain disorder. Loss-of-function mutations causing a complete absence of functional Nav1.7 channels result in congenital insensitivity to pain, in which affected individuals are unable to perceive physical pain (often accompanied by loss of the sense of smell, anosmia) while other sensory functions remain largely intact.

Because Nav1.7 is largely restricted to the peripheral nervous system and is essential for pain signalling, it has been a major target for the development of new analgesic drugs intended to treat pain without the side effects of opioids.
