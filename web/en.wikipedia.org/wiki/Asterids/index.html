Asterids

Kingdom:Plantae

Clade:Angiosperms

Clade:Asterids

The asterids are members of the asterid clade, a large clade (monophyletic group) of flowering plants. Well known plants in this clade include the common daisy, forget-me-nots, nightshades (including potatoes, eggplants, tomatoes), the common sunflower, petunias, yacon, morning glory, lettuce, sweet potato and coffee. Members of the Asterids are generally characterized by fused petals, a situation that is thought to have evolved from primitive taxa that also had fused petals.

Many members of the clade are grown as food crops, ornamentals or medicinal plants, making the asterids one of the most economically important groups of flowering plants.

Taxonomy

The name "asterids" (plural, not necessarily capitalized) is the informal name for the clade. In the APG IV system, the asterids comprise two major subclades: the lamiids and the campanulids, together with several smaller orders branching earlier. The clade corresponds well to the older Cronquist classification's subclass Asteridae, but is more broadly defined.

Subdivisions

The asterids include orders such as the Lamiales, Asterales, Solanales, Gentianales, Apiales, Ericales and Cornales. The largest families include the Asteraceae (the daisy family), the Lamiaceae (the mint family) and the Solanaceae (the nightshade family).

Characteristics

Most asterids have flowers with fused (sympetalous) corollas, and many produce iridoid compounds. Ovules are typically unitegmic and tenuinucellate. The clade contains well over 80,000 species, roughly a third of all flowering plants.
