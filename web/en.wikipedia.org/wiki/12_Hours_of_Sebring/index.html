The 12 Hours of Sebring is an annual motorsport endurance race for sports cars held at Sebring International Raceway, on the site of the former Hendricks Army Airfield World War II air base in Sebring, Florida, US. The event is the second round of the IMSA SportsCar Championship and is regarded as one of the classic endurance races in the world, alongside the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the 24 Hours of Daytona; together they are sometimes called the "Triple Crown" of endurance racing.

History

The first 12-hour race was held on 15 March 1952, won by Harry Gray and Larry Kulok in a Frazer Nash. The circuit uses sections of the old airfield's concrete runways, giving it a famously bumpy surface that severely tests car durability — a characteristic summed up in the saying "respect the bumps." From 1973 the race was sanctioned by IMSA, and it later formed part of the American Le Mans Series and, briefly in 2012, the FIA World Endurance Championship season opener.

The race traditionally takes place in mid-March, the day before or after the Formula One season begins in some years, and serves as a warm-up for teams preparing for Le Mans. Overall winners have included Stirling Moss, Juan Manuel Fangio, Mario Andretti, Jacky Ickx, and Tom Kristensen, who holds the record with six overall victories.

Recent years

Since 2014 the race has been part of the unified IMSA SportsCar Championship (originally the United SportsCar Championship). In 2019, a separate 1000 Miles of Sebring WEC race was held on the same weekend. The 2020 edition was postponed to November because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
