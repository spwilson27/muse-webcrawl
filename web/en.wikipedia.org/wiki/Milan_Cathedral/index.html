Milan Cathedral

Milan Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Milano) is the cathedral church of Milan, Lombardy, Italy, dedicated to the Nativity of Saint Mary. It is the seat of the Archbishop of Milan and one of the largest churches in the world by interior volume.

History

Construction began in 1386 under Archbishop Antonio da Saluzzo and Gian Galeazzo Visconti, on the site of earlier basilicas. Built largely of pink-white Candoglia marble in an elaborate Italian Gothic style, the cathedral took nearly six centuries to complete; the facade was finished under Napoleon in the early 19th century and final details were completed in the 20th century.

Architecture

The roof is decorated with 135 spires and some 3,400 statues, crowned by the gilded Madonnina atop the main spire, a symbol of the city. Inside, the vast nave holds notable monuments, a large stained-glass programme, and the relic of a nail said to be from the True Cross. Visitors can walk on the roof terraces among the pinnacles.
