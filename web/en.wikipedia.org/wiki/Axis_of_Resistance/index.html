Axis of Resistance

The Axis of Resistance (Persian: محور مقاومت; Arabic: محور المقاومة) is a loose political and military coalition in the Middle East led by Iran and composed of state and non-state actors opposed to the influence of the United States, Israel, and their regional allies. The term was first used by the Libyan newspaper Al-Zahf Al-Akhdar in 2002 in reaction to U.S. President George W. Bush's "axis of evil" speech.

Members

The core members of the axis are generally considered to be Iran, Syria under the Assad government, the Lebanese movement Hezbollah, and various Iraqi Shia militias grouped in the Popular Mobilization Forces. The Palestinian groups Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad and the Yemeni Houthi movement (Ansar Allah) are also commonly included. Iran supports these partners through the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' Quds Force, providing funding, weapons, and training.

Ideology and strategy

The axis presents itself as a front of "resistance" (muqawama) against Israel and Western presence in the region. Its members share strategic goals but retain considerable autonomy, and the network is often analysed as a system of deterrence by proxy, allowing Iran to project power and threaten adversaries on multiple fronts while limiting direct confrontation.

Recent conflicts

Following the outbreak of the Gaza war in October 2023, members of the axis opened supporting fronts against Israel and U.S. forces, including Hezbollah rocket fire from Lebanon, Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping, and militia strikes on American bases in Iraq and Syria, leading to direct exchanges between Iran and Israel in 2024.
