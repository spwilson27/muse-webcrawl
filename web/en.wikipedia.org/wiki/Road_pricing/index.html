Road pricing

TypesTolls, congestion charges, cordon charges, distance-based fees
PurposeTraffic management, revenue, emissions reduction

Road pricing (also called road user charging) is the practice of charging motorists directly for the use of particular roads or road networks. It includes toll roads, congestion pricing schemes in city centres, and distance- or time-based charges. Road pricing is used to manage traffic demand, raise revenue for transport infrastructure, and reduce pollution and congestion externalities.

Types

  • Tolls — fixed charges for using a specific road, bridge or tunnel.
  • Congestion charges — fees for driving within a congested area at peak times, such as the London congestion charge (introduced 2003) and Stockholm congestion tax (2007).
  • Electronic road pricing — Singapore's ERP system, introduced in 1998, charges varying rates by gantry, time and vehicle type.
  • Distance-based charging — heavy-goods vehicle charges in Germany (LKW-Maut) levy per-kilometre fees on motorways.

Economics

The economic rationale, developed by Arthur Pigou and later William Vickrey, is that each additional vehicle imposes delay costs on others; a charge equal to the marginal external cost of congestion induces an efficient level of traffic. Critics argue that schemes can be regressive and that revenues must be visibly reinvested in transport to gain public acceptance.
