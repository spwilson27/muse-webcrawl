Art film

An art film, art movie, or arthouse film is typically an independent film aimed at a niche market rather than a mass market audience. It is intended to be a serious, artistic work, often experimental and not focused on mass appeal.

Characteristics

Art films often explore dreamlike imagery, psychological interiority, ambiguous narratives, and social realism, favouring director-driven expression (auteur theory) over formulaic plot. They are screened at film festivals and arthouse cinemas.

History

Precursors include 1920s avant-garde and German Expressionism. Post-war Italian neorealism, the French New Wave, and directors like Ingmar Bergman, Federico Fellini, Michelangelo Antonioni, Akira Kurosawa and Satyajit Ray defined the form. Later movements include New Hollywood auteurs, Slow cinema, and contemporary festival cinema.

Debate

Critics debate the art/entertainment divide, arguing many mainstream films have artistic merit and art films can be commercial successes.
