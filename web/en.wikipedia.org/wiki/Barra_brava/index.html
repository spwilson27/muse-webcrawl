Barra brava

A barra brava is an organized supporters' group of a football club in Latin America, particularly in Argentina, where the phenomenon originated. Comparable to European ultras and, in their violent fringe, to hooligan firms, barras bravas lead the chanting, drumming, flag displays, and tifo choreography in the popular terraces of stadiums, but have also been associated with violence, extortion, and links to club directors, politicians, and organized crime.

Characteristics

Barra members congregate in the cheap standing sections (the popular), play drums and trumpets, unfurl enormous banners, and organize travel to away matches. Groups have a hierarchical structure under a leader (jefe), funded through club contributions, ticket resales, parking and merchandising concessions, and payments connected with player transfers. Famous examples include La 12 of Boca Juniors and Los Borrachos del Tablón of River Plate.

Violence and social role

Since the 1950s, hundreds of deaths have been attributed to football-related violence in Argentina, much of it involving rival barras or factions within the same barra fighting for control of illicit income. Despite periodic government campaigns and bans on away supporters, the barras remain powerful social institutions in many working-class neighborhoods, providing identity, belonging, and sometimes welfare to their members.
