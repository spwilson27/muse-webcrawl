Excello Records was an American record label based in Nashville, Tennessee, founded in 1953 by Ernie Young as a subsidiary of his Nashboro Records operation and record shop, Ernie's Record Mart. Nashboro concentrated on gospel music, while Excello handled secular material — principally rhythm and blues, blues, and later the Louisiana style that came to be called swamp blues and swamp pop.

Artists and sound

Much of Excello's best-known material was recorded far from Nashville, at J. D. "Jay" Miller's studio in Crowley, Louisiana. Miller's echo-laden, laid-back productions defined the swamp blues sound in records by Lightnin' Slim, Slim Harpo, Lazy Lester, Lonesome Sundown and Silas Hogan. Slim Harpo gave the label its biggest successes: "I'm a King Bee" (1957), "Rainin' in My Heart" (1961) and the 1966 hit "Baby Scratch My Back", which topped the R&B chart and crossed into the pop Top 20. Other notable Excello artists included the Kelly Brothers, Jerry Washington, Charles Sheffield, Tabby Thomas and blues singer Arthur Gunter, whose "Baby Let's Play House" (1954) was later covered by Elvis Presley.

Later years

Young sold the Nashboro/Excello operation in the early 1970s, and the catalog passed through several hands; Excello was revived in the 1990s as part of the AVI group, which issued well-regarded reissue compilations of the swamp blues masters. The label's recordings have been widely sampled and covered — the Rolling Stones recorded Slim Harpo's "Shake Your Hips" and "I'm a King Bee", cementing Excello's reputation as one of the essential independent blues labels of the post-war era.
