Newton's law of universal gravitation

Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every particle attracts every other particle in the universe with a force that is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres. It was published by Isaac Newton in the Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica in 1687.

Formula

The magnitude of the gravitational force F between two masses m1 and m2 separated by distance r is:

F = G · (m1m2 / r2)

where G is the gravitational constant, approximately 6.674 × 10−11 m3 kg−1 s−2. Henry Cavendish performed the first laboratory measurement of G in 1798.

Scope and limits

Newton's law accurately describes the motion of planets, tides, and trajectories of spacecraft, and unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics for the first time. It was superseded as a fundamental theory by Einstein's general relativity (1915), which accounts for anomalies such as the precession of Mercury's orbit, but Newtonian gravitation remains an excellent approximation for most practical purposes.
