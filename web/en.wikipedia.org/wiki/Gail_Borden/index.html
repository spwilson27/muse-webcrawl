Gail Borden Jr. (November 9, 1801 – January 11, 1874) was an American inventor and businessman who developed a commercial process for condensed milk and founded the company that became the Borden Company.

Early life and Texas years

Born in New York state, Borden moved west and settled in Texas in 1829, where he worked as a surveyor, helped lay out the city of Galveston, and published the newspaper Telegraph and Texas Register during the Texas Revolution.

Condensed milk

After experiments with preserving foods — including an ill-fated "meat biscuit" — Borden patented a vacuum-pan process in 1856 for condensing milk with sugar, producing a shelf-stable product. His New York Condensed Milk Company, later renamed Borden, supplied the Union Army during the American Civil War and grew into a major dairy business. The community of Borden, Texas, where he died, is named after him, as is Borden County.
