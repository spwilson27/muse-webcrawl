Kingdom of Hawaii

Years: 1795 – 1893

Capital: Honolulu (from 1845)

Founder: Kamehameha I

Last monarch: Queen Liliʻuokalani

The Kingdom of Hawaii (Hawaiian: Aupuni Mōʻī o Hawaiʻi) was a sovereign state in the Hawaiian Islands from 1795 to 1893. It was established by Kamehameha I, who united most of the archipelago by conquest, and it was recognised internationally for much of the nineteenth century, maintaining treaties and legations with the United States, Britain, France and other powers.

History

Kamehameha I completed the unification of the islands by 1810 with the submission of Kauaʻi. His successors governed as the Kamehameha dynasty until 1872; the kingdom adopted constitutions in 1840, 1852 and 1864, and the Great Māhele of 1848 transformed land tenure. After the elected kings Lunalilo and Kalākaua, the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution" stripped the monarchy of much of its authority under pressure from foreign business interests.

Overthrow

In January 1893, a committee of American and European businessmen, backed by the United States minister and naval forces, overthrew Queen Liliʻuokalani. A Republic of Hawaii was proclaimed in 1894, and the islands were annexed by the United States in 1898 during the Spanish–American War. In 1993 the US Congress passed the Apology Resolution, acknowledging the illegality of the overthrow and the role of American agents.
