Equine art

Equine art is art depicting the horse, one of the oldest and most persistent subjects in human art. From Palaeolithic cave paintings to the equestrian monuments of antiquity, the horse portraits of the English sporting tradition and the photography that resolved the question of equine locomotion, the horse has served as a symbol of power, speed, nobility and freedom.

History

Horses appear among the earliest works of art: the caves of Lascaux and Chauvet contain celebrated painted horses dating back more than 17,000 years. In the ancient world, horses featured in Assyrian reliefs, Greek sculpture and the terracotta cavalry of the Qin dynasty. The equestrian statue became a standard format for imperial and military portraiture from Marcus Aurelius through the Renaissance monuments of Donatello and Leonardo's studies for colossal horse sculptures.

Sporting and modern art

In 18th-century England, George Stubbs elevated horse painting through anatomical study, producing works such as Whistlejacket (c. 1762). In France, Théodore Géricault and Edgar Degas painted racehorses, while in America Frederic Remington and Charles Marion Russell defined the imagery of the frontier horse. Eadweard Muybridge's 1878 photographic sequence The Horse in Motion, commissioned by Leland Stanford, proved that all four hooves leave the ground in a gallop, changing how artists depicted movement.
