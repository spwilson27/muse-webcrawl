Thanatosis

Thanatosis, also known as tonic immobility, apparent death or colloquially playing dead, is a behaviour in which an animal adopts a posture resembling death — lying limp or rigid, often with mouth open and tongue lolling — in response to a threat. The state is involuntary and can last from a few seconds to several hours. The term derives from Thanatos, the Greek personification of death.

Function

Thanatosis is primarily a defensive strategy. Many predators are stimulated to attack by movement and will lose interest in prey that appears dead; others avoid carrion because of the risk of disease. Experiments with Japanese quail and domestic chickens show that birds which remain immobile longer survive encounters with predators more often. The behaviour also occurs in reproductive contexts — male nursery web spiders that feign death while offering a nuptial gift are more likely to mate — and in predatory contexts, where some cichlid fish simulate death to lure scavenging prey within striking range.

Examples

  • Virginia opossum — the classic example; the animal collapses, drools, and emits a putrid odour from its anal glands ("playing possum").
  • Hognose snakes — roll onto their backs with mouths agape; if turned upright, they flip over again.
  • Many beetles, moths and spiders — drop from vegetation and remain motionless when disturbed.
  • Chickens and ducks — can be induced into tonic immobility by restraint, a phenomenon once used by ethologists to measure fearfulness.
  • Sharks and rays — enter tonic immobility when inverted, a response used by researchers and, apparently, by orcas when hunting.
  • Humans — involuntary freezing and collapse responses to extreme threat are sometimes compared with thanatosis in clinical literature on trauma.

Mechanism

Tonic immobility is mediated largely by brainstem and limbic circuits rather than by conscious decision, and is accompanied by changes in heart rate, respiration and responsiveness to pain. It is distinguished from ordinary feigning or resting by its fixed duration and by the fact that the animal remains alert to its surroundings, ready to escape when the threat passes.
