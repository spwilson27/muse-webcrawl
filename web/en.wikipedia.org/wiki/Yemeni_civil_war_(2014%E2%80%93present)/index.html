The Yemeni civil war (Arabic: الحرب الأهلية اليمنية) is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014. It is principally fought between the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah) and its Supreme Political Council, which controls the capital Sanaa and much of northern Yemen, and the internationally recognised government of Yemen, backed by a Saudi-led coalition. The conflict has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Background

The war's roots lie in the failed political transition following the 2011 Yemeni uprising, which forced long-time president Ali Abdullah Saleh to hand power to his deputy, Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. Hadi's government struggled with corruption, unemployment, food insecurity and separatist sentiment in the south. The Houthis, a Zaydi Shia movement from northern Yemen that had fought a series of wars against Saleh's government in the 2000s, capitalised on popular discontent and seized Sanaa in September 2014.

Course of the war

In early 2015 the Houthis dissolved parliament and advanced on Aden, prompting Hadi to flee abroad. In March 2015, Saudi Arabia launched Operation Decisive Storm, leading a coalition of Arab states in airstrikes and a ground intervention aimed at restoring Hadi's government; the coalition received logistical and intelligence support from the United States, the United Kingdom and France. The Houthis allied temporarily with former president Saleh until they killed him in December 2017 after he broke with them.

A separate front opened in the south, where the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council seized Aden from government forces. UN-brokered efforts produced the 2018 Stockholm Agreement on Hodeidah and a nationwide truce in 2022 that lapsed but was followed by a significant reduction in fighting. From late 2023, the Houthis launched attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea in response to the Gaza war, drawing US- and UK-led strikes on Yemen.

Humanitarian impact

The war has killed hundreds of thousands of people directly and indirectly, displaced millions, and left much of the population dependent on humanitarian aid. Yemen experienced a major cholera outbreak, widespread food insecurity, and the collapse of public services and salaries.
