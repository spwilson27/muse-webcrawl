Transport for London (TfL) is the local government body responsible for most of the transport network in London, United Kingdom. Created by the Greater London Authority Act 1999, it came into being on 3 July 2000, succeeding and unifying functions previously spread across several bodies. TfL implements the transport strategy of the Mayor of London, who chairs its board.

TfL's services are identified by mode-specific versions of the famous roundel.

Responsibilities

TfL runs or oversees the London Underground (the Tube), London Overground, the Elizabeth line, the Docklands Light Railway, London Trams, London's bus network (operated under contract by private companies to TfL specifications), river services, the Emirates Air Line cable car, cycling infrastructure and the Santander Cycles hire scheme. It also manages the 580 km network of main roads (the "red routes"), traffic signals, the Congestion Charge, the Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ), taxi and private-hire licensing, and the Oyster and contactless payment systems used across the network.

History

TfL's lineage runs through the London Passenger Transport Board (1933), London Transport Executive and London Regional Transport. The Underground transferred from national control to TfL in 2003. Major projects delivered under TfL include the Oyster card (2003), the Overground network (from 2007), the congestion-charge expansion, and the Elizabeth line (Crossrail), which fully opened in 2022. TfL's funding combines fares—the largest share—with government grants, business rates and road charges; the COVID-19 pandemic's collapse in ridership forced a series of emergency government funding settlements from 2020 onward, shaping ongoing debates about its financial model.
