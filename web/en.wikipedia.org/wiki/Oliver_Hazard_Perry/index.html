Oliver Hazard Perry

Oliver Hazard Perry (August 23, 1785 – August 23, 1819) was an American naval commander, born in South Kingstown, Rhode Island. The elder brother of Commodore Matthew C. Perry, he achieved his most famous victory at the Battle of Lake Erie during the War of 1812, after which he sent the celebrated report: "We have met the enemy and they are ours."

Early life and career

Perry was the son of Christopher Raymond Perry, a naval officer of the American Revolutionary War. He entered the United States Navy as a midshipman in 1799 and served in the Quasi-War with France, the First Barbary War — where he took part in the campaign against Tripoli — and in the years before the War of 1812, commanding the schooner Revenge, which was wrecked off Rhode Island in 1811; a court of inquiry cleared him of fault.

War of 1812

At the outbreak of the War of 1812, Perry requested sea duty and was ordered to Lake Erie, where at Presque Isle (Erie, Pennsylvania) he supervised the construction of a squadron of warships, including the brigs Lawrence and Niagara. On September 10, 1813, his squadron engaged the British squadron under Commander Robert Heriot Barclay. Perry's flagship Lawrence was battered into a wreck, whereupon Perry rowed to Niagara carrying his battle flag inscribed "DONT GIVE UP THE SHIP" (the dying words of Captain James Lawrence) and sailed back into action, forcing the British to surrender the entire squadron. It was the first time in history that a complete British naval squadron surrendered. The victory secured American control of Lake Erie and enabled William Henry Harrison's recapture of Detroit and victory at the Battle of the Thames.

Later life and death

Perry subsequently served in the Mediterranean against Algiers and commanded a diplomatic mission to Venezuela. In 1819, while returning from that mission, he contracted yellow fever and died on his 34th birthday aboard ship off Trinidad. He is buried in Newport, Rhode Island. Numerous ships, counties, towns, and the Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial in Ohio commemorate him.
