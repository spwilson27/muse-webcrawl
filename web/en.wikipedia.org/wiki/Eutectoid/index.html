Eutectoid

A eutectoid is a three-phase reaction in which, upon cooling, a solid phase transforms isothermally into two different solid phases. It is the solid-state analogue of the eutectic reaction, in which a liquid solidifies into two solids. The composition and temperature at which the transformation occurs are called the eutectoid point.

Iron-carbon system

The most industrially important eutectoid occurs in the iron–carbon system. At 727 °C and 0.77 weight percent carbon, the face-centred cubic phase austenite decomposes on cooling into ferrite and cementite (Fe3C), forming the lamellar aggregate known as pearlite. Steels with exactly the eutectoid composition are called eutectoid steels; those with less carbon are hypoeutectoid and those with more are hypereutectoid.

The cooling rate controls the fineness of the pearlite and hence the steel's hardness and strength, a principle exploited in heat treatment. If austenite is quenched rapidly enough, the eutectoid reaction is bypassed entirely and martensite forms instead.
