Chuppah

A wedding ceremony beneath a chuppah

A chuppah (Hebrew: חוּפָּה, plural chuppot) is a canopy under which a Jewish couple stands during their wedding ceremony. It consists of a cloth or sheet, sometimes a tallit, stretched or supported over four poles, or held up by attendants. The chuppah symbolises the home that the couple will build together.

Symbolism and customs

The open sides of the chuppah are said to recall the tent of Abraham and Sarah, open on all sides to welcome guests. Its covering represents the presence of God over the covenant of marriage. In many ceremonies the groom wears a kittel beneath the chuppah, and the ceremony concludes with the breaking of a glass.

The word chuppah appears in the Hebrew Bible, as in Joel 2:16, "let the bridegroom go forth from his chamber, and the bride out of her closet (chuppah)". In modern usage, "chuppah" can also refer to the wedding ceremony itself.

Variations

Chuppot range from a simple tallit held on poles by friends and family to elaborate floral structures. Outdoor chuppah ceremonies are traditional in many communities, symbolising that the couple's union is blessed under the heavens.
