Fundamental matrix (computer vision)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Epipolar geometry: a point x in one image constrains its match x′ to lie on the epipolar line Fx in the other image.

In computer vision, the fundamental matrix F is a 3×3 matrix of rank 2 which relates corresponding points in stereo images. In epipolar geometry, with homogeneous image coordinates x and x′ of a point in image 1 and image 2 respectively, Fx describes a line (an epipolar line) on which the corresponding point x′ on the other image must lie. That means, for all pairs of corresponding points:

x′T F x = 0

Being of rank two and determined only up to scale, the fundamental matrix can be estimated given at least seven point correspondences, though in practice the linear eight-point algorithm and its normalised variant are used, usually within a RANSAC framework to reject outliers.

Derivation and relation to the essential matrix

The fundamental matrix can be expressed in terms of the camera projection matrices P and P′ of the two views. When the cameras are calibrated, the corresponding object is the essential matrix E = K′T F K, where K and K′ are the intrinsic calibration matrices; the essential matrix encodes the relative rotation and translation between the cameras directly. The fundamental matrix generalises this to uncalibrated cameras.

Uses

  • Guiding the search for correspondences: matches need only be sought along epipolar lines, not the whole image.
  • Structure from motion and 3D reconstruction pipelines, as an initial estimate of two-view geometry.
  • Image rectification, stereo matching and camera calibration.
  • Video analysis tasks such as motion segmentation and background modelling.

Computation

The standard approach solves x′TFx = 0 as a linear system in the entries of F (eight-point algorithm), enforces rank 2 by singular value decomposition (zeroing the smallest singular value), and refines with nonlinear minimisation of the Sampson or reprojection error. The seven-point algorithm, which enforces the rank constraint via a cubic equation in det F = 0, yields up to three solutions and is popular inside RANSAC loops.
