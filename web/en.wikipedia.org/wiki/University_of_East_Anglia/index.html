University of East Anglia

The University of East Anglia (UEA) is a public research university in Norwich, England. Established in 1963 on a 320-acre campus west of the city centre, it is one of the "plate glass universities" founded in the 1960s, and its motto is "Do Different."

Campus

The campus is noted for its Brutalist architecture by Denys Lasdun, including the stepped ziggurat residence blocks and the Teaching Wall, several of which are listed buildings. The Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts, designed by Norman Foster and opened in 1978, houses the university's art collection. The campus parkland includes a broad lake on the River Yare.

Academics

UEA is internationally known for its MA in Creative Writing, founded in 1970 by Malcolm Bradbury and Angus Wilson — the first of its kind in the UK — whose graduates include Nobel laureate Kazuo Ishiguro and Booker Prize winners Ian McEwan and Anne Enright. Its Climatic Research Unit is a major centre for climate science, and the university is a partner in the Norwich Research Park.
