The Bielski partisans were a Jewish partisan group — sometimes called the Bielski otriad (detachment) — that operated in the forests of western Belarus during the Second World War. Led by the Bielski brothers, Tuvia, Alexander, Asael, and Aron, the group is renowned for rescuing Jews from Nazi persecution: by the time of the area's liberation in the summer of 1944, the forest community numbered about 1,236 people, the majority of them non-combatants — women, children, and the elderly — making it the largest Jewish rescue effort of the war.

Background

The Bielskis were a family of millers and farmers near Nowogródek (Navahrudak). After Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, the brothers' parents and other relatives were murdered in the ghetto massacres of December 1941. The surviving brothers fled to the forest, where they combined armed resistance with a rescue mission, sending guides back into the ghettos to lead escapees to their camp.

The forest community

The Bielski camp functioned as a self-contained village — sometimes called "forest Jerusalem" — with a bakery, workshops (shoemakers, tailors, carpenters, blacksmiths), a tannery, a school, a synagogue, and a small medical facility. Its fighters carried out sabotage against rail lines and German communications and sometimes coordinated with Soviet partisans, though relations with Soviet command were complicated by antisemitism and by Soviet insistence that the camp accept only able-bodied fighters. The group's guiding principle, attributed to Tuvia Bielski, was that saving one Jewish life mattered more than killing Germans.

Aftermath and legacy

Asael Bielski was killed in action in 1945 after being conscripted into the Red Army. Tuvia and Zus emigrated to Israel and later to the United States. The story of the partisans was publicised in Nechama Tec's book Defiance (1993) and the 2008 film of the same name, starring Daniel Craig as Tuvia Bielski.
