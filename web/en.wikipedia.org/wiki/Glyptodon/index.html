Glyptodon

Glyptodon is a genus of glyptodont, an extinct group of large, herbivorous armadillos that lived from the Pliocene, around 3.2 million years ago, to the early Holocene, around 11,000 years ago, in South and North America. It was roughly the same size and weight as a Volkswagen Beetle, about 3.3 metres (11 ft) long and 1.5 tonnes.

With its rounded, bony shell and squat limbs, it superficially resembled a tortoise, and also a much earlier group of armoured dinosaurs, the ankylosaurs — an example of convergent evolution. The carapace was made of over 1,000 bony scutes (osteoderms) fused into a rigid dome, and unlike modern armadillos it could not roll into a ball.

Glyptodon is named for its grooved, "carved" teeth (Greek glyptos, carved, and odon, tooth). Charles Darwin collected Glyptodon remains in Uruguay during the Beagle voyage, and Richard Owen later described the material. The genus went extinct as part of the end-Pleistocene megafaunal extinctions, possibly hastened by human hunting.
