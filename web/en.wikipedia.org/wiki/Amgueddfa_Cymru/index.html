Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales is a Welsh Government sponsored body that comprises seven museums in Wales. It was formerly known as the National Museums and Galleries of Wales and the National Museum of Wales (Welsh: Amgueddfa Genedlaethol Cymru). Entry to all seven sites is free, following the Welsh Government's policy of free admission for national museums, introduced in 2001.

History

A royal charter of 1907 established the National Museum of Wales. Its principal building, the National Museum in Cathays Park, Cardiff, opened to the public in 1927. Collections grew to encompass archaeology, art, geology, botany, zoology, and industrial and social history of Wales.

Museums in the group

  • National Museum Cardiff — art (including a notable collection of Impressionist paintings), natural history, and geology.
  • St Fagans National Museum of History — an open-air museum of Welsh life near Cardiff, with re-erected historic buildings.
  • Big Pit National Coal Museum, Blaenavon — a former working coal mine.
  • National Wool Museum, Dre-fach Felindre.
  • National Slate Museum, Llanberis.
  • National Roman Legion Museum, Caerleon.
  • National Waterfront Museum, Swansea — industry and innovation in Wales.

Collections

The organisation cares for millions of objects, including the art collection at Cardiff with works by Monet, Renoir, Cézanne, and Van Gogh, and important Welsh archaeology such as the Mold gold cape.
