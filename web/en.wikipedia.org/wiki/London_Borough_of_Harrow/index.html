The London Borough of Harrow is a London borough in north-west London. It borders Hertfordshire to the north and the London boroughs of Hillingdon to the west, Ealing to the south, Brent to the south-east and Barnet to the east. The borough was created on 1 April 1965 under the London Government Act 1963, corresponding to the former Municipal Borough of Harrow in Middlesex, which had been a single district of Greater London — unique among the new boroughs in exactly matching its predecessor's boundaries.

Geography and districts

Harrow is largely suburban in character. Its principal districts include Harrow on the Hill — the historic hilltop settlement around Harrow School — Harrow town centre, Wealdstone, Pinner, Stanmore, Edgware (partly), Kenton, Ruislip (partly), Northwood (partly) and Hatch End. Harrow on the Hill reaches over 100 metres above sea level and is one of the highest points in London. The River Pinn runs through the borough.

History

Harrow was recorded in Domesday Book and was long a rural parish of Middlesex. Harrow School, founded in 1572, drew development to the hill. The arrival of the Metropolitan railway in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries turned the area into classic "Metroland" suburbia, and the population grew rapidly between the wars. Harrow became a municipal borough in 1934 and an urban district had preceded it; in 1965 it passed from the abolished Middlesex county council to the new Greater London Council area.

Demographics

Harrow is one of the most ethnically diverse boroughs in the United Kingdom. At the 2011 census around 42% of residents were White, with a large Asian or Asian British population — predominantly of Indian heritage — making up a similar share; Harrow also has significant Black and other communities and a wide range of faiths, including a notable Jain population centred on the Jain temple in Potters Bar's wider network and the Shri Swaminarayan temple tradition in nearby Neasden.

Governance

Harrow Council is elected every four years and administers services from the Civic Centre in Wealdstone. The borough is represented in the London Assembly as part of the Brent and Harrow constituency and in Parliament by the constituencies of Harrow East, Harrow West and (partly) Ruislip, Northwood and Pinner.

Education

The borough is home to Harrow School, one of Britain's most famous public schools, whose old boys include Winston Churchill and Jawaharlal Nehru, as well as the University of Westminster's Harrow campus and North London Collegiate School (in Edgware/Stanmore area).
