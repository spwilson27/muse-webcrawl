New Mexican cuisine

New Mexican cuisine is the cuisine of the Southwestern US state of New Mexico and the Northern Mexican state of Chihuahua, reflecting the region's history of Pueblo Native American, Spanish colonial, Mexican, and American influences. Its defining ingredient is the New Mexico chile, used in red and green sauces; ordering both is known in New Mexico as "Christmas". The cuisine is distinct from Tex-Mex and from the cuisines of neighboring Arizona and Colorado, although it shares elements with them.

History

The cuisine developed over centuries in the upper Rio Grande valley. Pueblo peoples cultivated corn, beans, and squash — the "three sisters" — long before Spanish colonists arrived in 1598, bringing wheat, rice, livestock, and chile peppers from Mexico. Successive Spanish, Mexican, and American periods layered techniques and ingredients, while relative geographic isolation preserved older forms, such as blue corn and horno-baked breads, alongside newer additions.

Chile

The New Mexico chile is a cultivar group of Capsicum annuum developed in the region, famously associated with the Hatch Valley. Chile is served as a sauce made from dried red pods or from roasted green pods, ranging from mild to extra hot, and accompanies enchiladas, burritos, huevos rancheros, and many other dishes. In 1999 New Mexico adopted an official state question, "Red or Green?", referring to the choice of chile sauce.

Characteristic dishes and ingredients

Signature dishes include enchiladas (often stacked flat with a fried egg on top), burritos and breakfast burritos, posole (hominy stew), carne adovada (pork marinated in red chile), green chile stew, tamales, sopaipillas (fried dough served with honey), and biscochitos, anise-flavoured cookies designated the state cookie. Staple ingredients include blue corn, piñon (pine nuts), beans, and flour and corn tortillas.
