The tree of life (Hebrew: עץ החיים, Etz ha-Chayim) in Kabbalah is a diagrammatic representation of the process by which the universe came into being and of the structure of divine emanation. It consists of ten spheres, the sefirot, connected by twenty-two paths, corresponding to the ten numbers and twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet described in the Sefer Yetzirah.

The sefirot

From the top, the sefirot are: Keter (Crown), Chokhmah (Wisdom), Binah (Understanding), Chesed (Kindness), Gevurah (Severity), Tiferet (Beauty), Netzach (Eternity/Victory), Hod (Splendour), Yesod (Foundation) and Malkuth (Kingdom), the last representing the material world. A hidden, quasi-sefirah, Da'at (Knowledge), sometimes appears between Keter and the lower triads. The ten are arranged in three pillars — Mercy (right), Severity (left) and the balancing Middle Pillar — and in descending "worlds" from the infinite Ein Sof to creation.

Interpretation and use

Kabbalists use the tree as a map of God's attributes, of the soul, and of the path of spiritual ascent: the divine light descends through the sefirot in a "lightning flash" order, while the mystic retraces it upward. The diagram was adopted extensively by Western esoteric traditions, including the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, which mapped the paths to the letters of the Hebrew alphabet, the tarot trumps and astrological symbols.
