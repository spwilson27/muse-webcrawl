Albert Leornes Greene (born April 13, 1946), better known as Al Green, is an American singer, songwriter, pastor and record producer best known for recording a series of soul hit singles in the early 1970s, including "Take Me to the River", "Tired of Being Alone", "I'm Still in Love with You", "Love and Happiness", and his signature song, "Let's Stay Together".

Career

Born in Forrest City, Arkansas, Green began performing gospel music as a child. After an early hit with "Back Up Train" (1967), he signed with Hi Records in Memphis, where producer Willie Mitchell shaped his distinctive smooth, falsetto-laced sound. Between 1971 and 1974 Green's albums — including Let's Stay Together (1972) and I'm Still in Love with You (1972) — made him one of the era's defining soul artists.

Following a personal crisis in the mid-1970s, Green became an ordained pastor and turned to gospel music, founding the Full Gospel Tabernacle church in Memphis in 1976. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995, and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2002; he was a Kennedy Center Honors recipient in 2014.
