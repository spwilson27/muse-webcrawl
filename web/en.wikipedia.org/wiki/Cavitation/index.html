Cavitation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


[Image: cavitation bubble cloud forming on a model ship propeller in a test tunnel]
FieldFluid mechanics
CauseLocal pressure dropping to vapour pressure
EffectsErosion, noise, vibration, loss of efficiency

Cavitation is the formation of vapour-filled cavities (bubbles) in a liquid when it is subjected to rapid changes of pressure. When the local pressure in a liquid falls to its vapour pressure, the liquid vaporises into small bubbles; when these bubbles are carried into regions of higher pressure they collapse violently, producing shock waves, noise and intense local forces capable of eroding solid surfaces.

Mechanism

Cavitation is characterised by the cavitation number, which relates the pressure difference driving collapse to the dynamic pressure of the flow. Bubbles grow at nucleation sites such as microscopic gas pockets, impurities or surface roughness. The implosive collapse of a bubble near a solid wall is asymmetric and generates a high-speed microjet directed at the wall, along with shock waves; repeated collapses pit and fatigue metals, a form of damage known as cavitation erosion.

Occurrence

Cavitation is a major concern on ship propellers, rudders, hydrofoils and in pumps, turbines, fuel injectors and control valves, where it causes vibration, noise and loss of performance, and over time destroys components. It also occurs in nature: snapping shrimp and mantis shrimp generate cavitation bubbles with their claws, and rapid flow around aquatic plants and in xylem vessels can produce cavitation, which interrupts water transport in plants. In medicine and industry, controlled cavitation is exploited in ultrasonic cleaning, lithotripsy and cavitation-based water treatment.

Control

Designers reduce unwanted cavitation by reshaping blades and passages to avoid low-pressure zones, increasing the ambient pressure (for example by submerging pumps deeper), and using erosion-resistant materials or coatings. In some applications, such as supercavitating torpedoes, cavitation is deliberately induced so that the object travels inside a large gas cavity with greatly reduced drag.
