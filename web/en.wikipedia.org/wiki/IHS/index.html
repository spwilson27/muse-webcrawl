IHS

IHS is a Christogram—a monogram of Jesus Christ—that has been used since antiquity in the art, liturgy, and heraldry of Western Christianity. It derives from the first three letters of the Greek spelling of Jesus, ΙΗΣΟΥΣ (Iēsous): iota (Ι), eta (Η), and sigma (Σ, written in its lunate form as C or Latinised as S).

Interpretations

In the Latin West the letters were later reinterpreted as abbreviations of Latin phrases, including Iesus Hominum Salvator ("Jesus, Saviour of Men"), In Hoc Signo ("in this sign [you shall conquer]", echoing Constantine's vision), and In Hac Salus. Medieval devotion, promoted by figures such as Bernardine of Siena in the 15th century, popularised the emblem surrounded by rays of the sun.

Use

The trigram is the emblem of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits), whose founder Ignatius of Loyola adopted it; it appears in the Jesuit seal encircled by rays with a cross above and the three nails of the Crucifixion below. It is also common on altars, vestments, tombstones, and communion wafers. The variant IHC reflects the medieval transliteration of the lunate sigma. Humorous folk back-formations such as "In His Service" are later inventions.
