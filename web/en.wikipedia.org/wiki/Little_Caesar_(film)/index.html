Little Caesar is a 1931 American pre-Code crime film directed by Mervyn LeRoy and produced by First National Pictures. It stars Edward G. Robinson in his breakthrough role as the small-time hoodlum Caesar Enrico "Rico" Bandello, whose ruthless rise and fall in the Chicago underworld defined the gangster film genre. The film is based on the 1929 novel of the same name by W. R. Burnett.

Plot

Rico and his friend Joe Massara leave their small town for the big city. While Joe dreams of becoming a dancer, Rico joins Sam Vettori's gang and rapidly climbs the ranks through intimidation and violence, eventually taking over the city's North Side organization. His arrogance and refusal to flee after killing a police official bring about his downfall; hunted and alone, Rico is shot by Sergeant Flaherty and dies beneath a billboard advertising Joe's dance act, uttering the famous line: "Mother of mercy, is this the end of Rico?"

Reception and legacy

The film was a major box-office success and is credited, alongside The Public Enemy (1931) and Scarface (1932), with establishing the template of the American gangster film. It was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2000 as culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant, and appears on AFI's lists of top gangster films and villains.
