The Chicago Board Options Exchange (CBOE), now known as the Cboe Options Exchange, is an American options exchange and the largest options exchange in the United States by volume. Based in Chicago, Illinois, it was founded in 1973 by the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT) and was the first exchange to list standardized, exchange-traded stock options. Its parent company, Cboe Global Markets, also operates stock exchanges, the Bats exchanges, and markets in Europe and Asia-Pacific.

History

The CBOE began trading on April 26, 1973, with call options on 16 underlying stocks. It was spun off from the CBOT, whose members wanted a separate venue dedicated to the new instrument of listed options. Put options were introduced in 1977. In 1983 the exchange launched index options on the S&P 100 (ticker OEX), followed in 1993 by options on the S&P 500 (SPX), which became among the most heavily traded index products in the world.

In 1993 CBOE created the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), a measure of expected market volatility derived from S&P 500 index option prices, often called the market's "fear gauge." VIX futures began trading on the CBOE Futures Exchange in 2004, and VIX options followed in 2006.

The exchange's holding company held an initial public offering in June 2010. In 2017 CBOE Holdings acquired Bats Global Markets and rebranded itself as Cboe Global Markets, dropping the "Chicago Board Options Exchange" name from its corporate identity while the options exchange continues as the Cboe Options Exchange.

Products

Cboe lists options on thousands of equities and exchange-traded funds, index options (including SPX, VIX, and Russell 2000 products), and interest-rate and volatility derivatives. Exclusive licences for S&P Dow Jones Indices products are a central part of its business.
