The Oder–Neisse line (German: Oder-Neiße-Grenze; Polish: Granica na Odrze i Nysie Łużyckiej) is the modern border between Germany and Poland. Drawn mainly along the Oder and Lusatian Neisse rivers, the line runs from the Baltic Sea near Świnoujście and Szczecin south to the Czech border near Zittau.

History

The border was proposed during the Second World War as part of the westward shift of Poland, compensating Poland for territory annexed by the Soviet Union in the east. The Allied leaders confirmed the line at the Potsdam Conference in August 1945, placing the German territories east of it under Polish (and, in the north, Soviet) administration pending a final peace settlement. Millions of Germans fled or were expelled from the territories east of the line.

Recognition

East Germany recognised the border in the Treaty of Zgorzelec (1950). West Germany, after initial reluctance, accepted it in the Treaty of Warsaw (1970) as part of Willy Brandt's Ostpolitik. Following German reunification, the united Germany definitively confirmed the border in the German–Polish Border Treaty of 14 November 1990, a condition of the Two Plus Four Treaty. Divided cities along the line include Frankfurt (Oder)/Słubice and Görlitz/Zgorzelec.
