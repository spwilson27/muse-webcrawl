Holstentor

LocationLübeck, Germany
Built1464–1478
StyleBrick Gothic

The Holstentor ("Holsten Gate") is a city gate marking the western boundary of the old centre of Lübeck, Germany. Built in Brick Gothic style between 1464 and 1478, it is the city's most famous landmark and a symbol of Lübeck's power as a Hanseatic trading centre.

Architecture

The gate consists of two round towers joined by a central block with a stepped gable, bearing the inscription Concordia domi foris pax — "Harmony within, peace without". Its walls are up to 3.5 metres thick on the field side.

History

It was one of four gates in Lübeck's fortifications and the only one besides the Burgtor to survive. Threatened with demolition in the 19th century, it was preserved by a single-vote margin in the city senate in 1863. It now houses a museum of city history and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage old town.
