Milestones (book)

Milestones (Arabic: معالم في الطريق, Ma'alim fi al-Tariq, published in English as Milestones, sometimes translated Signposts on the Road) is a 1964 book by Sayyid Qutb, a leading member of the Egyptian Muslim Brotherhood. One of the most influential Islamist texts of the 20th century, it called for the overthrow of what Qutb termed jahiliyya — the state of ignorance he attributed to contemporary Muslim and non-Muslim societies alike — and helped shape the ideology of later militant movements.

Content

Qutb argued that the modern world — including societies calling themselves Muslim — had lapsed into jahiliyya, because sovereignty (hakimiyya) belonged to God alone and any human legislated order usurped it. Muslims, he said, must form a dedicated vanguard (tali'a) that would withdraw spiritually from jahili society, follow the method of the first Qur'anic generation, and work — ultimately through jihad in its full sense — to establish a genuinely Islamic order. He drew a sharp line between Islam and all other systems, rejecting nationalism, capitalism, and communism alike.

Publication and consequences

The book was published in 1964 while Qutb was still imprisoned under Gamal Abdel Nasser; after his brief release he was rearrested in 1965, and extracts from Milestones were used as evidence in the show trial that condemned him. Qutb was hanged in August 1966, becoming a martyr figure for his followers.

Influence and criticism

Milestones profoundly influenced Islamist thought, including Ayman al-Zawahiri, who called Qutb's message the spark of the modern jihadist movement. Critics within and outside Islamist circles argue that Qutb misread classical doctrine, that his labeling of Muslim societies as jahili opened the door to takfir (declaring Muslims apostates), and that the Muslim Brotherhood itself later repudiated aspects of the book's approach in favor of gradualist politics.
