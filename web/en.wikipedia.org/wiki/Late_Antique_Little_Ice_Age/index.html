Late Antique Little Ice Age

A period of cooling from about 536 to 660 CE.
Periodc. 536 – 660 CE
CauseLarge volcanic eruptions
Coined2016

The Late Antique Little Ice Age (LALIA) was a period of long-term cooling in the Northern Hemisphere lasting roughly from 536 to 660 CE. The term was introduced in a 2016 paper in Nature Geoscience based on tree-ring reconstructions, which identified it as one of the coldest periods of the last two millennia.

Causes

The cooling began with a series of major volcanic eruptions — in 536, 540 and 547 — which injected sulfate aerosols into the stratosphere and veiled the Sun. The eruption of 536 produced the famous "mystery cloud" or dust veil described by contemporary writers such as Procopius, who reported that the sun shone without brightness for a year. The cooling was likely sustained by feedbacks involving sea ice and ocean circulation.

Effects

The onset of the LALIA coincided with crop failures and famines across Europe and Asia, the Plague of Justinian (from 541), political upheavals in Eurasia, and migrations that some historians link to these stresses. Researchers have proposed connections to the fall of the Sasanian Empire, the collapse of South Arabian kingdoms, and Slavic migrations into Eastern Europe, though the scale of these effects remains debated.
