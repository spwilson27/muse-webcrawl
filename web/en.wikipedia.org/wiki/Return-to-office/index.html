Return-to-office

A return-to-office (RTO) policy is a requirement by an employer that employees who had been working remotely return to working at a physical office location, either full-time or for a specified number of days per week. Return-to-office mandates became widespread in the years following the COVID-19 pandemic, during which remote work had become common for office workers.

Background

The pandemic of 2020 forced a rapid shift to remote work worldwide. As restrictions eased, employers debated how much remote work to retain. Many adopted hybrid models requiring two or three days per week in the office, while others — including major technology firms and, in 2025, the United States federal government — moved toward full-time attendance requirements. Amazon announced a five-day-per-week requirement effective January 2025, and a January 2025 executive order directed U.S. federal employees to return to in-person work.

Debate

Employers commonly cite collaboration, mentoring, innovation, and organizational culture as reasons for RTO policies. Critics argue the mandates reduce flexibility, lengthen commutes, harm morale, and can function as a form of indirect layoff ("quiet firing") by prompting resignations. Research on the productivity effects of remote and hybrid work has produced mixed results, and employee surveys have consistently shown strong preferences for retaining some remote work. Some employees have engaged in "coffee badging" — briefly appearing at the office to be recorded as present before leaving — in response to badge-tracking enforcement.
