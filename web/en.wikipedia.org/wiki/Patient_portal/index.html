Patient portal

A patient portal is a secure online website or application that gives patients convenient 24-hour access to their personal health information from anywhere with an internet connection. Patient portals are typically offered by hospitals, clinics, and physician practices, and are connected to the provider's electronic health record (EHR) system.

A typical portal dashboard: test results, messages, appointments, and billing in one place.

Features

Common features of patient portals include:

  • Viewing laboratory and imaging test results
  • Secure messaging with the care team
  • Requesting prescription refills
  • Scheduling and managing appointments
  • Viewing discharge summaries, immunization records, and medication lists
  • Paying bills and updating insurance or demographic information
  • Completing intake forms before visits

History and adoption

Early portals appeared in the late 1990s and 2000s at integrated health systems such as the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (My HealtheVet) and Kaiser Permanente. Adoption accelerated in the United States after the HITECH Act of 2009 and the Meaningful Use program required providers to give patients electronic access to their health information. In the 21st Century Cures Act and its information-blocking rules, regulators further required that patients be able to access their electronic health information, including through third-party apps using FHIR-based APIs.

Benefits and limitations

Studies associate portal use with improved patient engagement, better chronic disease management, and greater satisfaction, and portals reduce administrative phone traffic. Limitations include disparities in access and digital literacy — portal use is lower among older, poorer, and non-English-speaking patients — as well as concerns about the anxiety caused by releasing abnormal test results to patients before a clinician has reviewed them.
