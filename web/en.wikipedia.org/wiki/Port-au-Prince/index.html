Overview

Port-au-Prince is the capital and most populous city of Haiti, located on the Gulf of Gonâve at the head of a natural harbour. Founded by the French in 1749, it became the capital of the colony of Saint-Domingue and, after independence in 1804, of the Haitian state. Its name — 'Port of the Prince' — is usually traced to a French ship, Le Prince, that anchored in the bay.

The metropolitan area, including Pétion-Ville, Delmas and Carrefour, holds roughly a third of Haiti's population and concentrates its government, port, university and commerce.

Recent history

On 12 January 2010 a magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck near the city, devastating Port-au-Prince: the National Palace, cathedral and many ministries collapsed, and the death toll across Haiti is estimated in the hundreds of thousands — figures remain disputed. Reconstruction has been slow, and in the 2020s the city has suffered a severe security crisis as armed gangs took control of large areas, displacing many residents; the specific situation changes rapidly and figures given here are deliberately general.
