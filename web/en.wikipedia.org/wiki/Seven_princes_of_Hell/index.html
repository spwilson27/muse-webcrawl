Seven princes of Hell

The seven princes of Hell are a classification of demons in medieval and early modern demonology, in which seven powerful demons are each associated with one of the seven deadly sins. The best-known version is that of the German bishop and theologian Peter Binsfeld, published in his Tractatus de confessionibus maleficorum et sagarum (1589).

Binsfeld's classification

  • Lucifer — pride
  • Mammon — greed
  • Asmodeus — lust
  • Leviathan — envy
  • Beelzebub — gluttony
  • Satan — wrath
  • Belphegor — sloth

Other schemes

Other occult writers proposed different orderings. The Lantern of Light, an anonymous Lollard tract of about 1410, was among the first to pair demons with sins. In the classification attributed to Alphonso de Spina and later repeated in the grimoire tradition, the seven were arranged by dominion rather than by sin. Johannes Wier's Pseudomonarchia Daemonum (1577) and the Ars Goetia of the Lesser Key of Solomon describe much larger infernal hierarchies of kings, princes, dukes and presidents of Hell.

The princes remain popular figures in modern fiction, games and television, though their pairings with the sins vary between works.
