Gebirgsjäger

Gebirgsjäger (German pronunciation: [ɡəˈbɪʁksˌjɛːɡɐ]) are the light infantry part of the alpine or mountain troops (Gebirgstruppe) of Germany, Austria and Switzerland. The word Jäger (lit. "hunter") is a characteristic term used for light infantry in German-speaking military contexts.

The mountain troops of Germany trace their origins to the Imperial German Army's Alpenkorps of the First World War, and the tradition continued in the Wehrmacht's mountain divisions during the Second World War and in the modern Bundeswehr.

History

Origins

Austria and Imperial Germany both raised mountain formations during the First World War. The German Alpenkorps, formed in 1915, fought on the Italian front, in Romania, and in the Balkans, and its soldiers adopted the edelweiss badge of their Austrian allies as a corps insignia — a tradition still maintained today.

Second World War

The Wehrmacht fielded several Gebirgs divisions, which fought in Norway, Finland, the Caucasus, the Balkans, and Italy. Mountain troops also figured in occupation duties, and some units were implicated in war crimes against civilians and prisoners, notably in Greece and Italy.

Modern forces

In the Bundeswehr, the mountain troops are concentrated in the 23rd Gebirgsjäger Brigade, headquartered in Bad Reichenhall in Bavaria. Austrian Gebirgsjäger form part of the Austrian Armed Forces, and Swiss mountain troops serve in the Swiss Army. Their insignia include the edelweiss and the mountain cap (Bergmütze).

Distinctive features

Gebirgsjäger are trained for operations in mountainous and extreme terrain, including skiing, mountaineering, and high-altitude survival. They traditionally use pack animals, particularly mules and Haflinger horses, for transport in terrain inaccessible to vehicles.
