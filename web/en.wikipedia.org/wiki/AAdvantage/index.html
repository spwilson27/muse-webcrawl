AAdvantage is the frequent-flyer program of American Airlines. Launched on 1 May 1981, it is widely credited as the first frequent-flyer program of the modern airline industry and grew into one of the largest travel loyalty programs in the world, with well over 100 million members.

History

American Airlines developed AAdvantage from its list of most frequent customers, using its Sabre reservation system to track flown mileage. Members earned one mile for each mile flown and could redeem miles for free tickets and upgrades. The concept was rapidly copied across the industry. Over the decades the program added partner airlines — American is a founding member of the Oneworld alliance — as well as hotel, car rental and credit-card partners, notably co-branded cards issued by Citi and Barclays.

Structure

Members earn redeemable miles from flights and partner activity; award flights use dynamic pricing. Elite status tiers — Gold, Platinum, Platinum Pro and Executive Platinum — are earned through Loyalty Points, a single metric introduced in 2022 that combines flying and partner spending, including credit-card purchases. Benefits include priority boarding, complimentary upgrades, free checked bags and lounge access at the higher tiers.

Economic significance

During the COVID-19 pandemic, American pledged the AAdvantage program as collateral for loans; appraisals valued the program in the range of tens of billions of dollars — at times exceeding the market capitalisation of the airline itself — illustrating the centrality of loyalty programs to airline finances.
