In number theory, a semiperfect number or pseudoperfect number is a natural number n that is equal to the sum of all or some of its proper divisors. A semiperfect number that is equal to the sum of all of its proper divisors is a perfect number.

The first few semiperfect numbers are 6, 10, 12, 18, 20, 24, 28, 30, 36, 40, … For example, 12 is semiperfect because 12 = 2 + 4 + 6, using a proper subset of its divisors {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}; 20 is semiperfect because 20 = 1 + 4 + 5 + 10.

Properties

Every multiple of a semiperfect number is semiperfect, and every abundant number that is not semiperfect is called a weird number. The smallest odd semiperfect number is 945. Semiperfect numbers are never deficient. Every perfect number is semiperfect, and every practical number is semiperfect. It is unknown whether any odd weird numbers exist, and determining whether a number is semiperfect is related to the subset-sum problem.

Primitive semiperfect numbers

A semiperfect number that is not divisible by any smaller semiperfect number is called primitive. The first few primitive semiperfect numbers are 6, 20, 28, 88, 104, 272, 304, 350, … Every semiperfect number is a multiple of a primitive semiperfect number.
