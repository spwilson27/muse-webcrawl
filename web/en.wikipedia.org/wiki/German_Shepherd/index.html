The German Shepherd, also known in Britain as the Alsatian, is a German breed of working dog of medium to large size. It was developed by Max von Stephanitz in the 1890s from herding dogs of rural Germany and is traditionally used for herding sheep. Because of its strength, intelligence, trainability, and obedience, it is employed worldwide in police, military, search-and-rescue, and assistance roles, and it consistently ranks among the most popular dog breeds.

History

Captain Max von Stephanitz, a German cavalry officer, set out in the 1890s to standardize a superior herding dog. In 1899 he bought a dog named Horand von Grafrath at a dog show, founded the Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde (Society for German Shepherd Dogs), and made Horand the first registered German Shepherd and foundation of the breed. During the First World War the breed served as messenger, rescue, and guard dogs; anti-German sentiment led British and American kennel clubs to rename it (Alsatian Wolf Dog in Britain), though the original name was later restored.

Characteristics

German Shepherds are moderately active, athletic dogs, longer than they are tall, with erect ears, a bushy tail, and a double coat that sheds heavily. The breed standard calls for a confident, self-assured temperament; German Shepherds are known for loyalty, courage, and an eagerness to work and learn, but require socialization, exercise, and mental stimulation. Famous individuals include the film stars Rin Tin Tin and Strongheart, and Buddy, the first guide dog for the blind in the United States.

Health

Common health concerns include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy, gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat), and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency. Modern breeding has been criticized for exaggerated sloping backs in show lines, which some associate with gait and spinal problems; working lines are generally bred with straighter backs and emphasis on temperament and health screening.

Uses

Beyond herding, German Shepherds serve as police K-9s (tracking, narcotics and explosives detection), military working dogs, search-and-rescue dogs, and service dogs for people with disabilities, as well as popular family companions and competitors in sports such as Schutzhund/IPO, agility, and obedience.
