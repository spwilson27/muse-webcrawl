Piper Eressea Kerman (born 28 September 1969) is an American memoirist and criminal-justice advocate. Her 2010 memoir Orange Is the New Black: My Year in a Women's Prison recounts the thirteen months she served in federal prison for drug-related offences committed a decade earlier, and was adapted into the acclaimed Netflix series Orange Is the New Black (2013–2019).

Background and conviction

A graduate of Smith College, Kerman in 1993 became involved with an international drug smuggling ring through her partner, carrying a suitcase of laundered money. Indicted in 1998, she pleaded guilty to money laundering and, after a long delay, reported to FCI Danbury, a minimum-security federal prison in Connecticut, in 2004.

Memoir and adaptation

The memoir contrasts Kerman's privileged background with the community she found among fellow inmates, and criticizes the effects of the war on drugs and mandatory sentencing. The Netflix adaptation, created by Jenji Kohan, took the fictionalized "Piper Chapman" as an entry point into an ensemble drama.

Advocacy

Since her release, Kerman has lectured and served on the board of the Women's Prison Association, advocating for sentencing reform and the rights of incarcerated women.
