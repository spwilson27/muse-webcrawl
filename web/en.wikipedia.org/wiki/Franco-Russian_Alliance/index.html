Franco-Russian Alliance

The Franco-Russian Alliance (French: Alliance franco-russe), also called the Dual Alliance, was a military alliance formed by France and the Russian Empire between 1891 and 1894, remaining in force until 1917. It ended France's diplomatic isolation after the Franco-Prussian War and countered the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy.

Formation

After Bismarck's dismissal and Germany's refusal to renew the Reinsurance Treaty in 1890, France and Russia drew together through financial loans, a political agreement in 1891 and a secret military convention in 1892, ratified in 1894: each pledged to mobilise if the other was attacked by a Triple Alliance power.

Consequences

The alliance anchored the alliance system that divided Europe before the First World War. Extended by the Entente Cordiale (1904) and the Anglo-Russian Convention (1907) into the Triple Entente, it shaped the mobilisation dynamics of July 1914. French capital financed Russian railways and industry, including strategic lines toward the western frontier.
