Lehendakari

The Lehendakari (Basque: lehendakari, "first executive"; Spanish: lendakari or presidente del Gobierno Vasco) is the head of government of the Basque Autonomous Community in northern Spain. The Lehendakari leads the Basque Government, is elected by the Basque Parliament, and is formally appointed by the King of Spain.

History

The office originated during the Spanish Civil War: after the Basque Statute of Autonomy was approved in October 1936, José Antonio Aguirre of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV) became the first Lehendakari, swearing his oath beneath the Tree of Gernika. Following the Nationalist victory, Aguirre headed a government-in-exile until his death in 1960.

Modern office

After the 1979 Statute of Autonomy, the office was restored; Carlos Garaikoetxea became the first modern Lehendakari in 1980. The official residence is the Ajuria Enea palace in Vitoria-Gasteiz. Most holders of the office have come from the PNV, with Patxi López (2009–2012) the first from the Socialist Party. Iñigo Urkullu served from 2012 to 2024, when he was succeeded by Imanol Pradales.
