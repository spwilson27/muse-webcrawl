Participatory art

Participatory art is an approach to making art in which the audience is engaged directly in the creative process, becoming co-authors or active participants rather than passive spectators. It overlaps with terms such as relational aesthetics, socially engaged art, community art and interactive art.

Participatory art

Precursors: Dada, Happenings, Fluxus
Theorists: Nicolas Bourriaud, Claire Bishop

History

Antecedents include Dada cabaret, Futurist evenings, Allan Kaprow's Happenings (1950s–60s), Fluxus event scores and Joseph Beuys's notion of "social sculpture". In the 1990s Nicolas Bourriaud's Relational Aesthetics (1998) theorised art based on human interaction, citing artists such as Rirkrit Tiravanija, who cooked meals for gallery visitors.

Debates

Critic Claire Bishop, in "Antagonism and Relational Aesthetics" (2004) and Artificial Hells (2012), questioned whether participation is inherently democratic or emancipatory, sparking wide debate about ethics, authorship and evaluation in socially engaged practice.
