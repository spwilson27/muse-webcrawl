Somerdale Factory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Somerdale Factory was a chocolate factory in Keynsham, near Bristol, England. Built by the Quaker chocolate firm J. S. Fry & Sons in the 1920s and 1930s, it became one of Britain's best-known model factories, surrounded by a garden suburb for its workforce. The factory later passed to Cadbury and closed in 2011, after which the site was redeveloped as the Somerdale housing neighbourhood, renamed Chocolate Quarter.

History

Fry's merged with Cadbury in 1919 but continued to expand its Bristol operations. Seeking a greenfield site with rail and river access, the company built the factory at Somerdale, beside the River Avon at Keynsham, opening in stages from 1923. Like Bournville in Birmingham, the development embodied the welfare ideals of its Quaker owners: the grounds included sports fields, a clubhouse, gardens, and workers' housing. At its peak the factory employed several thousand people producing Fry's and Cadbury brands, including the Crunchie bar, Double Decker, and Fry's Chocolate Cream.

Closure and redevelopment

In 2007 Cadbury announced the factory would close, with production transferred to Poland. Kraft Foods, which acquired Cadbury in 2010, confirmed the closure despite suggestions during the takeover that Somerdale might be saved; the last production took place in early 2011. The announcement was controversial and was examined by a parliamentary select committee. The site was subsequently redeveloped by St Monica Trust and Taylor Wimpey into a retirement village and housing known as The Chocolate Quarter, retaining several original factory blocks and the Fry Club.
