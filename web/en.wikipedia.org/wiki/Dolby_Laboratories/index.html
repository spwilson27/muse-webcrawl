Dolby Laboratories

Dolby Laboratories, Inc. is an American technology corporation specializing in audio noise reduction, audio encoding/compression, spatial audio, and HDR imaging. Founded in London in 1965 by American engineer Ray Dolby, the company relocated to California in 1976. Dolby licenses its technologies to consumer electronics manufacturers and develops them for cinemas and professional studios.

History

Ray Dolby founded the company in London in 1965 after completing his doctorate at Cambridge and working in India. His Dolby A-type noise reduction system (1965) reduced tape hiss in professional recording studios; the simplified Dolby B-type (1968) brought the technology to consumer cassette decks, greatly improving the cassette format's sound quality, followed by Dolby C (1980) and Dolby S (1989).

Film sound

Dolby Stereo (1975) encoded four channels of matrixed surround sound onto standard 35 mm optical soundtracks, debuting widely with films of the late 1970s. Dolby Digital (AC-3), introduced with Batman Returns (1992), provided discrete 5.1-channel digital sound and became the standard audio format on DVDs and much broadcast television. Later cinema formats include Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby Surround 7.1, and Dolby Atmos (2012), an object-based spatial audio system.

Imaging

Dolby Vision, launched in 2014, is a proprietary high-dynamic-range (HDR) imaging format supporting dynamic metadata, widely adopted by television and streaming platforms. Dolby Cinema is a premium cinema concept combining Dolby Vision projection and Dolby Atmos sound.

Other technologies

Dolby also develops voice and conferencing technologies (Dolby Voice), the AC-4 broadcast audio codec, and the E-AC-3 (Dolby Digital Plus) codec used by streaming services. Most of the company's revenue derives from licensing to manufacturers of televisions, soundbars, mobile devices, PCs, game consoles, and automobiles.
