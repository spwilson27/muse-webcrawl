Burgh hall

A typical Scottish burgh hall

A burgh hall is a municipal building in a Scottish burgh, built to house the town council's offices, the burgh court, and a public hall for meetings, concerts, dances and other community events. Comparable to the town hall in England and Wales, burgh halls are a distinctive feature of Scottish towns, particularly those erected during the civic building boom of the 19th century.

History

From the medieval period, Scottish burghs conducted business in tolbooths, which combined council chamber, court and jail. As municipal functions grew in the 19th century — and after the Burgh Police (Scotland) Acts empowered town councils to levy rates for public buildings — many burghs replaced or supplemented their tolbooths with purpose-built halls. Styles were often Scots Baronial or Italianate, and many halls included a clock tower as a symbol of civic pride.

Use today

Local government reorganisation in 1975 abolished the burghs, and most halls passed to district or unitary councils. Many remain in use as community halls, arts venues, libraries or register offices, and a number are listed buildings. Well-known examples include the burgh halls of Pollokshields in Glasgow, Maryhill Burgh Halls (restored as a community venue), and halls in towns such as Linlithgow, Peebles and Dumbarton.
