The Bank of Canada (French: Banque du Canada) is Canada's central bank. Established under the Bank of Canada Act of 1934, it began operations on 11 March 1935 and was nationalised in 1938; its shares are held by the Minister of Finance on behalf of the Crown. Its head office is in Ottawa, at the Bank of Canada Building on Wellington Street.

Mandate and functions

The Bank's mandate is to "promote the economic and financial welfare of Canada". Its core functions are monetary policy, the financial system, currency (it is the sole issuer of Canadian bank notes), and funds management for the Government of Canada. Since 1991, monetary policy has operated under an inflation-control agreement with the federal government, targeting 2 per cent annual CPI inflation within a 1–3 per cent band, renewed every five years. The Bank sets the target for the overnight interest rate, with eight fixed announcement dates a year, and publishes a quarterly Monetary Policy Report.

Governance

The Bank is led by a governor and Governing Council, with an independent board of directors. Notable governors include Graham Towers (1934–1954), James Coyne (whose conflict with the Diefenbaker government led to the "Coyne Affair"), John Crow, Gordon Thiessen, David Dodge, Mark Carney (later Governor of the Bank of England), Stephen Poloz, and Tiff Macklem, who became governor in 2020 and led the Bank's pandemic response and subsequent tightening cycle.

Museum

The Bank of Canada Museum, originally the Currency Museum, displays the National Currency Collection and exhibits on the Canadian economy.
