First Boer War

The First Boer War (Afrikaans: Eerste Vryheidsoorlog, literally "First Freedom War"), also known as the First Anglo-Boer War, the Transvaal War or the Transvaal Rebellion, was a war fought from 16 December 1880 until 23 March 1881 between the United Kingdom and Boers of the Transvaal (as the South African Republic was known then). The war resulted in a Boer victory and the eventual independence, in the form of self-government, of the South African Republic.

Background

The British had annexed the Transvaal in 1877, claiming insolvency and threats from the Pedi and Zulu kingdoms. Boer resentment grew under British administration, and after the British victory in the Anglo-Zulu War removed the perceived Zulu threat, the Boers demanded restoration of their republic. When peaceful petitions failed, the Boers proclaimed independence on 16 December 1880 at Heidelberg, raising the vierkleur flag.

Course of the war

Boer commandos besieged British garrisons across the Transvaal and won a series of engagements against British columns using superior marksmanship and knowledge of the terrain: Bronkhorstspruit (20 December 1880), Laing's Nek (28 January 1881), Schuinshoogte (8 February), and decisively Majuba Hill (27 February 1881), where General Sir George Pomeroy Colley was killed. British casualties were heavy relative to the small forces engaged.

Outcome

The government of William Gladstone, unwilling to escalate, negotiated peace. The Pretoria Convention of 1881 restored Transvaal self-government under nominal British suzerainty, replaced in 1884 by the London Convention. Tensions over gold discoveries and foreign (uitlander) rights later led to the Second Boer War (1899–1902).
