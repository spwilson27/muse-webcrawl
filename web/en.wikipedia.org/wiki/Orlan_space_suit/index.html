Orlan space suit

The Orlan space suit (Russian: Орлан, meaning "sea eagle") is a series of semi-rigid one-piece space suits designed and built by NPP Zvezda for spacewalks (extravehicular activity) in Earth orbit. First used on the Salyut 6 space station in 1977, Orlan suits have served aboard Salyut, Mir and the International Space Station. Unlike the American EMU, an Orlan suit is entered through a hatch in its rigid rear torso, allowing a cosmonaut to don it quickly and largely unaided.

Design

The suit consists of a rigid aluminium torso shell with flexible fabric arms and legs. The backpack, hinged as the rear door, contains the primary life-support system: oxygen, cooling water, electrical power and communications. The suit operates at a pressure of about 0.4 atmospheres of pure oxygen. Because of its one-size-adjustable construction, Orlan suits are station equipment shared between crew members, adjusted by sizing straps and interchangeable limb segments, rather than being personalised to an individual.

Versions

Major variants include the original Orlan-D (1977), the longer-duration Orlan-DM used on Mir, the Orlan-M (1997), which added greater sizing range and an improved visor, and the Orlan-MK with a computerised control and display unit. The current Orlan-MKS, introduced on the ISS in 2017, features an automatic thermal control system, a synthetic rubberised hermetic shell with a longer service life, and colour displays. Orlan suits have accumulated hundreds of hours of EVA without a serious failure.
