Jim Corbett National Park

Jim Corbett National Park is a national park in the Nainital district of Uttarakhand, India. Established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, it was the first national park in India and the first to come under Project Tiger, in 1973.

History

The park was created during British rule and was renamed first Ramganga National Park and then, in the 1950s, in honour of Jim Corbett, the hunter-turned-conservationist and author of Man-Eaters of Kumaon, who had helped delineate its boundaries.

Geography and wildlife

Situated in the foothills of the Himalayas along the Ramganga River, the park covers hills, riverine belts, marshy depressions, and grasslands. It is renowned for its Bengal tiger population and also protects Asian elephants, leopards, sloth bears, gharials, and over 600 species of birds. Tourism is permitted only in designated zones such as Dhikala and Bijrani.
