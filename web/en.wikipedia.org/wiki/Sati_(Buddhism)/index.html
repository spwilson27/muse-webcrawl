Sati (Pali: sati; Sanskrit: smṛti) is a concept in Buddhism usually translated as mindfulness. The word literally means "memory" or "recollection", and in the Buddhist context it denotes the faculty of maintaining awareness of the present moment — bearing in mind the body, feelings, mind, and phenomena as they arise and pass away.

In early Buddhism

Sati is one of the Seven Factors of Awakening, one of the five spiritual faculties (indriya), and a central element of the Noble Eightfold Path, where right mindfulness (sammā-sati) is defined chiefly through the four foundations of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhāna): mindfulness of the body (kāya), of feelings (vedanā), of mind (citta), and of mental objects or phenomena (dhamma). The Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta and the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta are the classic discourses on its cultivation.

Meaning

Scholars note that sati is not merely bare attention: because the term connotes remembering, it includes keeping the teachings and the purpose of practice in mind. In the early texts it functions as the faculty that guards the sense doors and prevents wholesome states from being lost, and it works together with clear comprehension (sampajañña).

Modern usage

In the 20th and 21st centuries, sati — commonly rendered "mindfulness" — became the basis of secular meditation programmes such as Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR), developed by Jon Kabat-Zinn, and of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, even as scholars debate how far the modern usage departs from the canonical meaning.
