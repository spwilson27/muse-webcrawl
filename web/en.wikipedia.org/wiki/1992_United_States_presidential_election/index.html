The 1992 United States presidential election was the 52nd quadrennial presidential election, held on November 3, 1992. Democratic governor Bill Clinton of Arkansas defeated incumbent Republican president George H. W. Bush. Independent businessman Ross Perot won 18.9 percent of the popular vote, the strongest showing by a candidate outside the two major parties since 1912, but received no electoral votes.

Campaign

Bush entered the race with high approval after the Gulf War, but a recession, a broken “read my lips” tax pledge and a perception of distance from ordinary economic hardship weakened him. Clinton focused relentlessly on the economy; his campaign's internal slogan “It's the economy, stupid” entered political folklore. Perot campaigned against budget deficits and free-trade agreements with charts on television, withdrew in July, then re-entered in October and appeared in the debates.

Results

Clinton won 43 percent of the popular vote, Bush 37.5 percent and Perot 18.9 percent. In the Electoral College Clinton won 370 votes to Bush's 168. The election ended twelve years of Republican presidencies and began a decade shaped by deficit reduction debates, welfare and crime legislation, and conflict between President Clinton and a Republican Congress after 1994.
