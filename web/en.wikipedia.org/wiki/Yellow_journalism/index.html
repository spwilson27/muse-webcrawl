Yellow journalism

[Image: An 1890s New York newspaper front page with enormous headlines]

Yellow journalism is sensationalised, scandal-driven reporting with bold headlines, crusades and stunts, named for the 1890s New York circulation war between Joseph Pulitzer's World and William Randolph Hearst's Journal — and the 'Yellow Kid' comic over which they fought. The era's exaggerations are popularly (if overstatedly) blamed for pushing the US toward the Spanish–American War of 1898.

Legacy

Yellow journalism pioneered techniques still visible — banner headlines, human-interest crusades, self-promotion — and provoked the professional reaction: the 20th-century objectivity norm and journalism ethics codes.
