Dionysios Solomos

Dionysios Solomos (Greek: Διονύσιος Σολωμός; 8 April 1798 – 9 February 1857) was a Greek poet from Zakynthos, regarded as the national poet of Greece. He is best known for the Hymn to Liberty (1823), whose first stanzas, set to music by Nikolaos Mantzaros, became the Greek national anthem, and for championing the demotic (spoken) Greek language as the medium of modern poetry.

Life

Solomos was born into a wealthy aristocratic family on Zakynthos and educated in Italy, where he took a law degree at Pavia and began writing Italian verse. Returning to his island in 1818, he turned deliberately to Greek, studying the folk songs and Cretan Renaissance literature to forge a poetic language from living speech — a revolutionary step at a time when learned Greeks, following Korais, favoured archaic forms.

Works

The Hymn to Liberty, 158 stanzas written in May 1823 as the War of Independence raged, made him famous across Europe. His mature work is fragmentary and visionary: Lambros, The Woman of Zakynthos, The Cretan and the unfinished Free Besieged, inspired by the siege of Missolonghi, combine folk metres, demotic diction and Dante-like spiritual striving. From 1828 he lived on Corfu, the centre of the Heptanesian School of poetry. Though he published little in his lifetime and left major poems in draft, his example established demotic Greek as the language of the nation's literature.
