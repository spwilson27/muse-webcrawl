Pope Martin I (Latin: Martinus I; died 16 September 655) was the bishop of Rome from July 649 until his death. He is the last pope recognised as a martyr by the Catholic Church, and he is venerated as a saint in both the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches. His pontificate was consumed by the Monothelite controversy, in which he defied the Byzantine emperor Constans II and suffered arrest, imprisonment, and exile.

Early life and election

Martin was born near Todi in Umbria. He served as papal apocrisiarius (ambassador) to Constantinople under Pope Theodore I, gaining first-hand experience of imperial court politics and of the theological disputes dividing the empire. On Theodore's death in May 649, Martin was elected pope and was consecrated in July of that year without waiting for the customary confirmation from the emperor or his exarch in Ravenna — an omission that gravely offended the imperial authorities.

The Lateran Council of 649

In October 649 Martin convened a synod at the Lateran basilica in Rome, attended by more than a hundred bishops. The council condemned Monothelitism — the doctrine that Christ had only one will — together with the imperial documents that promoted it: the Ecthesis of Heraclius and the Typos of Constans II, which forbade all discussion of the question. It also condemned the Monothelite patriarchs of Constantinople. The council's acts, influenced by the theology of Maximus the Confessor, affirmed that Christ possessed two wills, human and divine, in accordance with his two natures.

Arrest and trial

Emperor Constans II ordered Martin's arrest. A first attempt by the exarch Olympius failed when Olympius instead revolted. In June 653 the new exarch, Theodore Calliopas, seized Martin in the Lateran basilica — the pope, ill and infirm, was carried from the altar where he had taken refuge — and conveyed him by ship to Constantinople. After months of imprisonment under harsh conditions, Martin was tried before the imperial senate on charges of treason, including alleged complicity with Olympius and hostility to the emperor. He was publicly humiliated, stripped, and paraded through the city, and sentenced to death; the sentence was commuted to exile through the intercession of the dying Patriarch Paul II.

Exile and death

Martin was exiled to Cherson in the Crimea, then suffering famine. His letters from exile describe his suffering, his sense of abandonment by Rome, and his refusal to compromise on doctrine. Meanwhile, the Romans, under imperial pressure, elected Eugene I as pope in August 654 while Martin still lived; Martin is reported to have acquiesced in the election rather than cause schism. He died at Cherson on 16 September 655. His relics were later brought to Rome. Martin's stand was vindicated posthumously when the Third Council of Constantinople (680–681) condemned Monothelitism and honoured his memory.
