Saint Catherine (Arabic: سانت كاترين) is a town in the South Sinai Governorate of Egypt, lying in the mountainous heart of the Sinai Peninsula at about 1,600 metres above sea level. It is Egypt's highest town and one of its coldest settlements, occasionally seeing snow in winter. The town grew around the famous Saint Catherine's Monastery at the foot of Mount Sinai.

Geography and climate

Saint Catherine sits between the Sinai mountain ranges, close to Mount Catherine (2,629 m), Egypt's highest peak, and Mount Sinai (2,285 m), traditionally identified as the biblical mountain where Moses received the Ten Commandments. The arid high-desert climate brings hot summer days, cold nights year-round, and rare winter frosts and snowfalls.

Population and economy

The inhabitants are mostly members of the Jebeliya Bedouin tribe, whose ancestors, according to tradition, were brought from the Balkans and Egypt in the sixth century to serve the monastery, and who still work as its guides, gardeners and camel handlers. The local economy depends on religious tourism and trekking: visitors climb Mount Sinai for sunrise, visit the monastery, and hike the surrounding wadis and gardens. The Saint Catherine Protectorate, established in 1988, covers much of the surrounding area and protects its endemic plants and wildlife, including the Sinai baton blue butterfly.
