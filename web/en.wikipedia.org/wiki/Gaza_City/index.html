Gaza City (Arabic: غزة, Ġazza) is a city in the Gaza Strip, Palestine, and the largest city in the State of Palestine by population before the mass displacement of the Gaza war. Located on the eastern Mediterranean coast about 76 kilometres south-west of Jerusalem, it has been continuously inhabited for millennia and is one of the oldest cities in the world.

History

Gaza was a major Canaanite and Philistine city — the southernmost of the five Philistine cities (the Pentapolis) — and later a flourishing Egyptian, Persian, Hellenistic and Roman trading hub on the Via Maris and the incense route from southern Arabia. It passed through Byzantine, early Islamic, Crusader, Ayyubid, Mamluk and Ottoman rule, each leaving landmarks such as the Great Omari Mosque, originally a Philistine temple and later a church, and the Qasr al-Basha palace.

During the First World War, British forces captured Gaza in 1917 after three battles. The city was part of Mandatory Palestine, then under Egyptian administration from 1948, Israeli occupation from 1967, and Palestinian Authority rule after the 1994 Gaza–Jericho Agreement. Hamas took control of the Gaza Strip in 2007. From October 2023, Gaza City was a principal theatre of the Gaza war, during which most of its population was displaced and large parts of the city were destroyed.

Geography and districts

The historic core is the Old City, divided into quarters including al-Daraj, al-Tuffah, Zaytoun and Shuja'iyya. Modern districts include Rimal, Tel al-Hawa and Sheikh Radwan, together with the Beach (Shati) refugee camp. The city's port, university institutions (including the Islamic University of Gaza and Al-Azhar University – Gaza) and al-Shifa Hospital made it the administrative and economic centre of the Strip.
