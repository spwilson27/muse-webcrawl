Cloud Gate

Cloud Gate is a public art sculpture by Indian-born British artist Anish Kapoor, located at the center of AT&T Plaza in Millennium Park, Chicago, Illinois. Constructed between 2004 and 2006, the sculpture is nicknamed "the Bean" because of its shape, which reflects and distorts the city's skyline. Made of stainless steel, it is one of the world's largest permanent outdoor art installations.

Design and construction

Kapoor's design was inspired by liquid mercury. The sculpture is composed of 168 stainless steel plates welded together; its highly polished exterior has no visible seams. Underneath is a 12-foot-high (3.7 m) arch, the "omphalos" (Greek for navel), a concave chamber whose multiply-reflected surfaces warp visitors' images. The plates were fabricated by Performance Structures, Inc. of Oakland, California, shipped to Chicago, and assembled over a steel frame. Polishing the exterior to its mirror finish required extensive hand-finishing.

History

Kapoor's proposal won a design competition for the park's centerpiece sculpture. The work was first assembled for the park's opening in July 2004 in unfinished form, then covered by a tent while polishing was completed; it was formally dedicated on May 15, 2006. Initial cost estimates of $9 million rose to about $23 million, funded by private donations.

Reception

Cloud Gate has become one of Chicago's most popular tourist attractions and an icon of the city, drawing visitors who photograph its reflections and pass beneath its arch. Kapoor has objected to the "Bean" nickname but acknowledged its popular appeal. In 2023–2024 the plaza was renovated and the sculpture temporarily fenced off, reopening to the public in June 2024.
