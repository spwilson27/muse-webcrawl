Braemar (Scottish Gaelic: Bràigh Mhàrr) is a village in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, around 58 miles (93 km) west of Aberdeen in the Highlands. It is the largest settlement in the upper Deeside area, sitting at an elevation of about 339 metres (1,112 feet) on the Clunie Water near its confluence with the River Dee, within the Cairngorms National Park. Braemar is among the coldest inhabited places in the United Kingdom and has twice recorded the country's record low temperature of −27.2 °C (−17.0 °F), on 11 February 1895 and 10 January 1982.

Braemar Gathering

The village is best known for the Braemar Gathering, a Highland games held on the first Saturday in September since the early 19th century in its modern form, traditionally attended by members of the Royal Family, whose Scottish residence, Balmoral Castle, lies about nine miles to the east. Events include caber tossing, hammer throwing, tug of war, Highland dancing, and piping competitions.

History and landmarks

Braemar Castle, a 17th-century tower house built by the Earl of Mar in 1628 and later held by the Farquharsons of Invercauld, stands just outside the village. The ruins of the 13th-century Kindrochit Castle lie within the village itself. The area was a centre of the Jacobite rising of 1715, which was proclaimed at Braemar by the Earl of Mar. Today the village serves tourism, hillwalking, and skiing at nearby Glenshee.
