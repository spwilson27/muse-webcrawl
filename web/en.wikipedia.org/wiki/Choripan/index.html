Choripan

Choripán
Type: sandwich
Place of origin: Argentina / Southern Cone
Main ingredients: chorizo, crusty bread, chimichurri

Choripán is a type of sandwich popular in Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, Paraguay, and other parts of Latin America, consisting of a grilled chorizo sausage served on crusty bread. The name is a portmanteau of chorizo and pan (Spanish for bread).

Preparation

The chorizo — usually a coarse pork sausage, sometimes a pork-and-beef mixture — is grilled over charcoal, often butterflied by splitting it lengthwise (mariposa style), and served in a baguette-style roll. It is traditionally dressed with chimichurri, and sometimes with salsa criolla (a chopped onion, tomato, and pepper relish).

Culture

Choripán is a staple of the asado (barbecue), where it is eaten as a starter while the main cuts cook, and is ubiquitous street food sold outside football stadiums and at public gatherings. In Argentina it is closely associated with working-class culture and political rallies alike.

Variations

Regional versions include the Chilean choripán served with pebre, and completos-style toppings such as mayonnaise and avocado in some countries.
