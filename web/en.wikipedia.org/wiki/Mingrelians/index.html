Mingrelians

The Mingrelians (Mingrelian: მარგალეფი, Margalepi; Georgian: მეგრელები, Megrelebi) are an ethnic subgroup of the Georgians native to Samegrelo (Mingrelia), a historic region in western Georgia. They speak Mingrelian, a Kartvelian language related to Georgian, Laz, and Svan, and are predominantly Orthodox Christians.

History

Mingrelia corresponds roughly to the ancient kingdom of Colchis, famous in Greek myth as the land of the Golden Fleece. From the 15th century the region was ruled by the Dadiani princes as the Principality of Mingrelia, a semi-independent state under nominal Ottoman or Georgian suzerainty until it was absorbed into the Russian Empire in the 19th century. In the 1920s and 1930s Soviet censuses counted Mingrelians separately, but they were later classified simply as Georgians. The region's capital and largest city is Zugdidi.

Culture

Mingrelian cuisine is noted for its spiciness, featuring adjika (a fiery chili paste), sulguni cheese, khachapuri in the local style, and elarji, a cornmeal dish stretched with cheese. The Mingrelian language is unwritten in general use — Georgian serves as the literary language — and UNESCO classifies Mingrelian as endangered, though it remains widely spoken at home. Many prominent Georgian political figures of the late 20th century, including Zviad Gamsakhurdia and figures around Lavrenty Beria, were of Mingrelian origin.
