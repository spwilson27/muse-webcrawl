Brioni Agreement

Signed: 7 July 1991

Location: Brijuni (Brioni) Islands, Croatia

Parties: Slovenia, Croatia, and the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia

Effect: Ended the Ten-Day War; three-month moratorium on independence

The Brioni Agreement (also called the Brioni Declaration) is an agreement signed on 7 July 1991 on the Brijuni Islands off the coast of Croatia by representatives of Slovenia, Croatia, and Yugoslavia, under the political sponsorship of the European Community. It ended the Ten-Day War between Slovenia and the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) and established a three-month moratorium on the implementation of the Slovenian and Croatian declarations of independence.

Background

Slovenia and Croatia declared independence from Yugoslavia on 25 June 1991. The JNA moved to secure Slovenia's border crossings, sparking the Ten-Day War, in which Slovenian territorial defence forces blockaded barracks and defeated federal units in several skirmishes. Fighting cost several dozen lives. The European Community, alarmed by the first armed conflict in Europe since 1945, sent a troika of foreign ministers to mediate.

Terms

The agreement provided for an immediate ceasefire and the withdrawal of JNA units to their barracks, the lifting of Slovenian blockades, the return of control of Slovenia's borders to Slovenian police (although customs revenue questions remained disputed), and a three-month suspension of the implementation of independence, during which negotiations on Yugoslavia's future would take place under EC chairmanship. An EC monitoring mission (the European Community Monitor Mission) was deployed to oversee the ceasefire.

Consequences

The moratorium expired on 7–8 October 1991, whereupon Slovenia and Croatia proceeded with full independence; the JNA completed its withdrawal from Slovenia by late October. Slovenia thus escaped the wars that soon engulfed Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina. The agreement is widely regarded as having consolidated Slovenia's path to sovereignty, though it failed to resolve the wider Yugoslav crisis, and fighting in Croatia escalated into the Croatian War of Independence.
