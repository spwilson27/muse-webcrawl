Future Farmers of America

The National FFA Organization, originally called the Future Farmers of America, is an American youth organization that promotes and supports agricultural education. Founded in 1928 in Kansas City, Missouri, FFA today has membership in the hundreds of thousands across all 50 states, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, making it one of the largest youth organizations in the United States.

History

FFA was founded by a group of young farmers at the Virginia Polytechnic Institute and organized nationally in 1928. It merged with the New Farmers of America, the organization for African American students, in 1965, and women were admitted to full membership in 1969. The name was changed to the National FFA Organization in 1988 to reflect the growing diversity of agriculture beyond farming alone.

Activities and symbols

FFA members wear the official dress of a blue corduroy jacket — an iconic symbol adopted in 1933 — along with black slacks or skirt, white shirt, and tie or scarf. The organization runs career and leadership development events, supervised agricultural experience programs, and an annual national convention. Its motto is "Learning to Do, Doing to Learn, Earning to Live, Living to Serve."
