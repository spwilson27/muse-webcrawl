Socorro Island (Spanish: Isla Socorro) is a volcanic island in the Pacific Ocean, the largest of Mexico's Revillagigedo Islands, lying about 600 kilometres south-west of Cabo San Lucas at the southern tip of Baja California. The island is the summit of a massive submarine shield volcano, Mount Evermann, which rises to 1,050 metres above sea level. Socorro has no permanent civilian population; a Mexican naval station on the southern coast houses roughly 250 personnel and their facilities.

Geology

Socorro is a peralkaline shield volcano built mainly of trachyte and basalt lavas. Its most recent eruption occurred in 1993, when a submarine flank vent produced floating pumice and discoloured water offshore. The island's surface is cut by lava flows, cinder cones and a summit caldera complex, and fumaroles testify to continuing volcanic activity.

Wildlife

Isolated for millions of years, Socorro has high endemism. It is the only home of the Socorro mockingbird, one of the world's rarest birds, and historically of the Socorro dove, which became extinct in the wild after introduced cats and sheep devastated the island's ecology; a captive population survives and reintroduction has been planned. Other endemics include the Socorro parakeet, Townsend's shearwater (which breeds in burrows on the island), endemic lizards, land crabs and many plants. The surrounding waters are famous among divers for giant oceanic manta rays, humpback whales, dolphins and several shark species, including hammerheads and whale sharks.

Conservation

The Revillagigedo Archipelago was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2016, and in 2017 Mexico created the Revillagigedo National Park, one of the largest fully protected marine reserves in North America, banning fishing around the islands. Feral sheep, introduced in the nineteenth century, were eradicated by 2010, and feral cat control continues to allow native species to recover. Visitation is restricted, principally to liveaboard dive boats and authorised researchers.
