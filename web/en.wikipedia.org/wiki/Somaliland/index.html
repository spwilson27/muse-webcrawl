Somaliland

Somaliland, officially the Republic of Somaliland, is an internationally unrecognised state in the Horn of Africa, regarded internationally as part of Somalia. It lies in northwestern Somalia, bordering Djibouti to the northwest, Ethiopia to the south and west, and the Gulf of Aden to the north. Its capital and largest city is Hargeisa, and its population is estimated at around 5.7 million.

History

The territory corresponds roughly to the former British Somaliland protectorate, which became independent as the State of Somaliland on 26 June 1960 before voluntarily uniting with the Trust Territory of Somaliland (former Italian Somaliland) five days later to form the Somali Republic. Following the collapse of Siad Barre's regime and the civil war — including the destruction of Hargeisa and the mass killing of Isaaq civilians in 1988 — the Somali National Movement declared the restoration of Somaliland's independence on 18 May 1991, a position confirmed by referendum in 2001.

Politics and recognition

Somaliland has its own government, constitution, currency (the Somaliland shilling), armed forces, and a hybrid political system combining an elected presidency and parliament with a council of clan elders (the Guurti). It has held several competitive elections with peaceful transfers of power. No UN member state formally recognises it, though several maintain informal relations, and it operates representative offices abroad. In January 2024 a memorandum of understanding with Ethiopia, reportedly involving port access at Berbera in exchange for possible recognition, provoked a diplomatic dispute with Somalia.

Economy

The economy rests mainly on livestock exports to the Arabian Peninsula through the port of Berbera — expanded by DP World — remittances from the diaspora, and telecommunications. Eastern regions such as Sool and Sanaag are contested with Puntland and the SSC-Khatumo administration, and fighting around Las Anod in 2023 displaced much of that city's population.
