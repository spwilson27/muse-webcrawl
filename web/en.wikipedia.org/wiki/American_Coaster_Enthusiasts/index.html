American Coaster Enthusiasts (ACE) is a nonprofit organisation dedicated to the appreciation, enjoyment and preservation of roller coasters. Founded in 1978, it is one of the largest amusement ride enthusiast groups in the world, with members in many countries and regional chapters throughout North America and beyond.

History

ACE traces its origins to a roller coaster marathon event held at Kings Dominion in Virginia in 1977, organised in connection with the film Rollercoaster. Attendees Roy Brashears, Paul Greenwald and Becky Reese founded the club the following year, and it grew steadily as coaster enthusiasm spread during the "coaster wars" of the 1980s and 1990s.

Activities

The organisation holds national and regional events at amusement parks, often including exclusive ride time (ERT) sessions before or after park hours. It publishes a bimonthly magazine, ACE News, and the quarterly journal RollerCoaster! ACE also works to preserve historic coasters: it has designated certain rides as "ACE Roller Coaster Landmarks" and operated a preservation fund that helped save threatened wooden coasters such as the Phoenix at Knoebels and Leap-The-Dips, the world's oldest operating roller coaster.

Coaster preservation

ACE's preservation efforts focus on classic wooden coasters that might otherwise be demolished. Its landmark plaque programme recognises rides of historic or design significance, and members frequently volunteer expertise and labour to parks maintaining traditional wooden rides.
