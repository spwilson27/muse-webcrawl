The South African Border War, also known as the Namibian War of Independence in Namibia and sometimes as the "Bush War" in South Africa, was a largely asymmetric conflict fought between 1966 and 1990 in South West Africa (now Namibia) and southern Angola. It pitted South Africa and its allied forces against the South West Africa People's Organisation (SWAPO) and its armed wing, the People's Liberation Army of Namibia (PLAN), later becoming intertwined with the Angolan Civil War.

Background

South Africa had administered South West Africa under a League of Nations mandate after seizing the German colony in 1915, and refused to place it under United Nations trusteeship after 1945. The UN revoked the mandate in 1966, but South Africa refused to withdraw and extended its apartheid laws to the territory. SWAPO, founded in 1960, launched its armed struggle with the clash at Omugulugwombashe on 26 August 1966, conventionally taken as the war's start.

Course of the war

PLAN guerrillas infiltrated northern Namibia from bases in Zambia and, after Angolan independence in 1975, from Angola. South Africa responded with counter-insurgency operations and major cross-border raids and incursions into Angola — including Operation Savannah (1975–76), Reindeer (1978, including the controversial Cassinga raid), Protea (1981), and Askari (1983). From the mid-1980s the war escalated into large conventional battles between South African forces and Angolan FAPLA brigades supported by Cuban troops and Soviet advisers, culminating around Cuito Cuanavale in 1987–88, one of the largest battles in Africa since the Second World War.

Settlement

Battlefield stalemate and diplomatic pressure produced the 1988 Tripartite (New York) Accords: Cuba withdrew its troops from Angola, South Africa implemented UN Security Council Resolution 435, and free elections in 1989 brought SWAPO to power. Namibia became independent on 21 March 1990.
