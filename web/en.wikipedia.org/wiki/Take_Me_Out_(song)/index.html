"Take Me Out" is a song by Scottish indie rock band Franz Ferdinand. It was released as the second single from their eponymous debut studio album in January 2004 and became the band's breakthrough hit, reaching number three on the UK Singles Chart and number eight on the US Billboard Modern Rock Tracks chart.

Composition

The song is famous for its abrupt change of tempo: it begins with brisk, uptempo guitar interplay before slowing, about a minute in, into a slower, heavier groove built around a pounding riff and disco-influenced rhythm. Frontman Alex Kapranos wrote the lyrics as a metaphor of two snipers aiming at each other — "take me out" as a plea to be taken out, whether on a date or with a bullet — the title playing on both meanings. The distinctive guitar parts were worked out when Kapranos and Nick McCarthy lived in Glasgow and wrote songs together in each other's flats.

Reception and legacy

"Take Me Out" topped numerous year-end critics' lists in 2004, won the Ivor Novello Award for Best Contemporary Song, and was nominated for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group at the Grammy Awards. Its video, directed by Jonas Odell, used Dadaist and constructivist-style cut-out animation inspired by the 1920s avant-garde. The song is frequently ranked among the best songs of the 2000s and became a defining anthem of the post-punk revival; it has been widely licensed in films, television and video games.
