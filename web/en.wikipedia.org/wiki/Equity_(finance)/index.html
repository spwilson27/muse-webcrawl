Equity (finance)

In finance, equity is an ownership interest in property that may be offset by debts or other liabilities. Equity is measured for accounting purposes by subtracting liabilities from the value of the assets owned. For example, if someone owns a car worth $24,000 and owes $10,000 on the loan used to buy the car, the difference of $14,000 is equity. Equity can apply to a single asset, like a car or house, or to an entire business. A business that needs to start up or expand its operations can sell its equity in order to raise cash that does not have to be repaid on a set schedule.

Equity in a company

When starting a business, its owners fund the business. Over time they may take on new co-owners (investors) through selling more shares or raise funds without adding new owners by borrowing from a bank or issuing bonds. The value of equity in a company is the difference between assets and liabilities; on the balance sheet this appears as shareholders' equity, comprising share capital and retained earnings. In the event of liquidation, equity holders are paid only after all debts are settled — equity is therefore the residual claim on a firm's assets.

Types of equity

  • Common stock / ordinary shares — the standard ownership units of a corporation, usually carrying voting rights and dividends at the board's discretion.
  • Preferred stock — shares with priority for dividends and liquidation proceeds, often without voting rights.
  • Private equity — ownership in companies not listed on a public exchange, typically held through funds that buy, restructure and sell firms.
  • Home equity — the market value of a homeowner's interest in their property.
  • Brand equity — a marketing usage: the added value a recognised brand name brings to a product.

Valuation

The market value of equity (market capitalisation for a listed company) is the share price multiplied by shares outstanding, and can differ greatly from book equity. Investors analyse equity through ratios such as return on equity (ROE), price-to-earnings, and price-to-book. In the capital structure, equity is the riskiest and most junior claim, and therefore generally demands the highest expected return.
