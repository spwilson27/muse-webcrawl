National symbols of France

The national symbols of France are the emblems that represent the French Republic and its values, many of them products of the French Revolution. Article 2 of the Constitution of the Fifth Republic names the tricolour flag, the anthem La Marseillaise, and the motto Liberté, égalité, fraternité as symbols of the Republic.

The tricolour

The blue-white-red flag, adopted in its modern form in 1794, combines the colours of Paris (blue and red) with the white of the Bourbon monarchy. It is one of the most influential national flags, imitated by many other countries.

Marianne

Marianne is the female personification of the Republic, representing liberty and reason. Her bust, wearing the Phrygian cap of freed slaves, appears in every town hall in France, and her likeness has been modelled by famous French women and features on stamps and coins.

La Marseillaise

Composed by Rouget de Lisle in 1792 as a war song for the Army of the Rhine, and adopted as the anthem of the Republic, La Marseillaise takes its name from volunteer soldiers from Marseille who sang it as they marched on Paris.

Other symbols

  • The Gallic rooster — an emblem dating from Roman wordplay (gallus meaning both a Gaul and a cockerel), used especially in sport
  • The motto Liberté, égalité, fraternité, inscribed on public buildings
  • Bastille Day, 14 July, the national day
  • The fasces and the seal of the Republic, used on official documents
  • The Phrygian cap and the tricolour cockade
