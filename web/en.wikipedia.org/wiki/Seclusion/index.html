Seclusion

Seclusion is the act of secluding, that is, shutting or keeping away from society, or the state of being shut off or private. Seclusion may be voluntary, as in religious retreat or solitary contemplation, or involuntary, as in certain medical, legal or custodial practices.

Religious seclusion

Many religious traditions value periods of withdrawal: monastic orders include hermits and enclosed orders; retreatants withdraw temporarily for prayer and reflection; practices such as the purdah and various rites of passage involve prescribed seclusion. In some cultures, mourning and postpartum periods are traditionally spent in seclusion.

In medicine and care

In psychiatric and acute care, seclusion is the involuntary confinement of a patient alone in a room from which they cannot freely exit, used as a last-resort safety intervention. Its use is regulated and widely debated, with policy generally favouring de-escalation and the least restrictive alternative. Isolation for infection control is a related but distinct practice.

In law

Seclusion may also arise in custodial settings, such as solitary confinement in prisons, which is subject to legal limits in many jurisdictions. In privacy law, "intrusion upon seclusion" is a recognised form of the tort of invasion of privacy.
