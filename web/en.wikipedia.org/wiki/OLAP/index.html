OLAP

Online analytical processing, or OLAP, is an approach to answering multi-dimensional analytical queries swiftly in business intelligence and data analysis. OLAP systems are optimised for complex aggregations and reporting over large historical datasets, in contrast to OLTP systems, which handle day-to-day transactions.

The OLAP cube

The central metaphor of OLAP is the data cube: numeric facts (measures, such as sales revenue) are categorised by dimensions (such as product, region, and time period). Analysts interact with cubes through operations such as slicing and dicing (selecting subsets), drill-down and roll-up (moving between levels of detail), and pivoting (reorienting the axes). The term OLAP was coined in a 1993 paper by database pioneer Edgar F. Codd.

Types

OLAP implementations are commonly grouped into MOLAP (multidimensional storage in proprietary cube arrays), ROLAP (queries translated to SQL against a relational star schema), and HOLAP (a hybrid of the two). OLAP engines are a standard component of data warehousing platforms and BI tools.
