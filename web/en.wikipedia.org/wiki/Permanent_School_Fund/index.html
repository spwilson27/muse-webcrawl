Permanent School Fund

Permanent School Fund
Established: 1854 (Texas)
Administered by: Texas State Board of Education; General Land Office
Purpose: Funding public primary and secondary education in Texas

The Permanent School Fund (PSF) is a sovereign wealth fund established by the State of Texas to support public primary and secondary education. Created by the Texas legislature in 1854 with an initial appropriation of $2 million, it is one of the largest educational endowments in the United States.

History

The fund originated in the Republic of Texas era, when public lands were set aside for education. The 1854 act of the Fifth Texas Legislature appropriated $2 million from the settlement of Texas's boundary claims with the United States and dedicated extensive public lands to the fund. The Texas Constitution of 1876 further protected the fund, declaring that the lands and proceeds granted for public schools would constitute a perpetual school fund.

Assets and management

The fund's assets include financial investments managed through the Texas Education Agency and the State Board of Education, and surface and mineral interests in millions of acres of state lands managed by the Texas General Land Office. Revenue from oil and gas royalties, grazing leases, and land sales flows into the fund, and earnings are distributed to public schools through the Available School Fund on a per-student basis.

Distributions

Distributions from the PSF support the Foundation School Program, helping equalize funding among Texas school districts. The fund also guarantees bonds issued by local school districts, allowing them to borrow at lower interest rates.
