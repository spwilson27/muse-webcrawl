Lázaro Cárdenas del Río (21 May 1895 – 19 October 1970) was a Mexican army officer and statesman who served as President of Mexico from 1934 to 1940. A leading figure of the post-revolutionary consolidation, he is remembered for sweeping agrarian reform, the expropriation of the foreign-owned oil industry in 1938, and support for organised labour and for Spanish Republican refugees — reforms that define the left-nationalist tradition of cardenismo.

Presidency

Cárdenas rose through the revolutionary army and served as governor of his native Michoacán, where he built schools and redistributed land. Handpicked by the "Jefe Máximo" Plutarco Elías Calles as the PNR's candidate, he unexpectedly broke with Calles in 1935–36, exiling him and establishing presidential authority. His Six-Year Plan government redistributed some 18 million hectares to communal ejidos — more land than all his predecessors combined — including the collectivised cotton lands of La Laguna and the henequen zone of Yucatán.

On 18 March 1938, after a labour dispute in which foreign oil companies defied a Supreme Court ruling, Cárdenas decreed the expropriation of the oil industry, creating the state company Pemex amid a British and American boycott that Mexico weathered; the date remains a national civic holiday. His government also nationalised the railways, expanded rural education through "socialist education" and rural normal schools, organised labour and peasantry into the restructured ruling party (PRM), granted asylum to Leon Trotsky in 1937 and to some 20,000–40,000 Spanish Civil War refugees after 1939. He chose the moderate Manuel Ávila Camacho as his successor in 1940, served as Secretary of National Defense during the Second World War, and remained an elder statesman of the Mexican left until his death in 1970. The port city and municipality of Lázaro Cárdenas, Michoacán (home of Mexico's largest steelworks region and Pacific port), and Mexico City's Eje Central avenue bear his name.
