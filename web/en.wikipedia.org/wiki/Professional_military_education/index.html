Professional military education

Professional military education (PME) is the professional education and training of military personnel, particularly officers, designed to develop the knowledge, judgment, and leadership skills required at successive levels of responsibility. PME systems typically comprise a ladder of institutions: service academies and officer candidate schools for initial commissioning; company-grade and field-grade courses; staff colleges; and senior war colleges for flag and general officers.

Structure

In the United States, PME is organized under the framework of the Goldwater–Nichols Act and the Officer Professional Military Education Policy (OPMEP), with joint professional military education (JPME) phases delivered by institutions such as the Command and General Staff College, the Naval War College, the Air War College, and the National War University. In the United Kingdom, comparable institutions include the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst, the Joint Services Command and Staff College, and the Royal College of Defence Studies.

Curriculum

Curricula combine military history, strategy and operational art, joint and combined operations, leadership and ethics, national security policy, and increasingly topics such as cyber operations, space, and information warfare. Methods include seminars, war gaming, staff rides, and case studies of historical campaigns.

Purpose

PME aims to produce officers capable of critical and creative thinking in complex, ambiguous environments, to foster a shared professional ethos, and to prepare leaders for joint and multinational command. It is distinguished from skills training by its emphasis on education — developing judgment rather than proficiency in specific tasks.
