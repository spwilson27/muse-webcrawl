Le Pen, founder of the National Front and five-time presidential candidate.

Jean-Marie Le Pen (June 20, 1928 – January 7, 2025) was a French politician and the founder of the National Front (Front national), the far-right party he led from its creation in 1972 until 2011. A central and controversial figure in French politics for four decades, he ran for the presidency five times and shocked France in the 2002 presidential election by reaching the second round, where he lost heavily to Jacques Chirac.

Early life and early career

Born in Brittany, the son of a fisherman who died in 1942, Le Pen studied law in Paris and became involved in student politics. He volunteered for the French Foreign Legion's paratroop regiment and served in Indochina and during the Suez crisis and in Algeria. In 1956 he was elected to the National Assembly as its youngest deputy, on the Poujadist list. After losing his seat, he founded a company selling gramophone records of political speeches and military music.

The National Front

In 1972 Le Pen united several nationalist currents to found the National Front, whose electoral breakthrough came in the 1980s, including the 1983 municipal elections in Dreux and the 1984 European elections, in which the party won about 11 percent. Le Pen campaigned on opposition to immigration, law and order, economic protectionism, and French national identity. His presidential scores grew from 0.7 percent in 1974 to 14.4 percent in 1988 and 15 percent in 1995, before the 2002 result, in which he edged out the Socialist Lionel Jospin for a place in the runoff.

Controversies and legal issues

Le Pen was repeatedly convicted in French courts for statements about the Holocaust — he notoriously described the gas chambers as a "detail" of the history of the Second World War — and for remarks targeting immigrants and Muslims. His provocations kept him at the center of political controversy and led mainstream parties to maintain a "republican front" against his movement. In 2011 his daughter Marine Le Pen succeeded him as party leader, and in 2015, after renewed disputes over his statements, he was expelled from the party he had founded; the party was renamed National Rally in 2018.

Death and legacy

Le Pen died on January 7, 2025, at the age of 96. His career reshaped the French political landscape, moving the far right from the margins to a durable electoral force, and his party — under his daughter — reached the presidential runoff again in 2017 and 2022.
