Ernakulam district is one of the fourteen districts of the Indian state of Kerala, situated in the central part of the state. It takes its name from the Ernakulam area of the city of Kochi. The district headquarters are at Kakkanad. Ernakulam is the most industrialised and urbanised district in Kerala and hosts the state's principal port and commercial hub.

Geography

The district stretches from the Arabian Sea coast in the west to the foothills of the Western Ghats in the east. It is crossed by the Periyar and Muvattupuzha rivers and contains extensive backwaters, paddy fields and midland laterite hills. The climate is tropical monsoon, with heavy rainfall from June to September.

History

The region was historically part of the Kingdom of Cochin and the Kingdom of Travancore. Ernakulam district was formed on 1 April 1958. Kochi's natural harbour made it a centre of the spice trade for centuries, attracting Portuguese, Dutch and British merchants.

Economy

The district contributes a large share of Kerala's industrial output. Major installations include the Kochi Port, Cochin Shipyard, the Kochi refinery of BPCL, FACT fertiliser plants and the Infopark and SmartCity IT campuses at Kakkanad. Tourism, fisheries and remittances are also significant.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census, Ernakulam district had a population of 3,282,388, with a literacy rate among the highest in India. Malayalam is the predominant language. Hindus, Christians and Muslims form the principal religious communities.

Administration

The district is divided into seven taluks: Ernakulam, Kanayannur, Kochi, North Paravur, Aluva, Kunnathunad and Muvattupuzha. The Kochi Municipal Corporation and several municipalities govern its urban areas.
