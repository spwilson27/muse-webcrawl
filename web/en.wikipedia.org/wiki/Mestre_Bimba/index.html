Manuel dos Reis Machado (23 November 1899 – 5 February 1974), commonly called Mestre Bimba, was a Brazilian capoeira master (Portuguese: mestre) and the founder of the Capoeira Regional style.

Biography

Born in Salvador, Bahia, the son of a champion of batuque (an African-Brazilian wrestling style), Bimba began practising capoeira at age 12 with Mestre Bentinho. Capoeira was then persecuted and associated with marginality; Bimba worked as a carpenter, coal miner, and stevedore while developing his teaching.

Capoeira Regional

In 1932 Bimba founded the first recognised capoeira school, the Centro de Cultura Física e Capoeira Regional, teaching a systematised method he called Luta Regional Baiana — incorporating movements from batuque and other martial arts, a graded sequence of teaching, and strict discipline. In 1937 his academy received official recognition, and in 1953 he performed for President Getúlio Vargas, who declared capoeira "the only truly national sport". His work was instrumental in the legalisation and popularisation of capoeira.

Later life

Feeling undervalued in Bahia, Bimba moved to Goiânia in 1973 and died there in 1974. His academy continues under his descendants and students, and he is commemorated as one of the two great patriarchs of modern capoeira, alongside Mestre Pastinha of Capoeira Angola.
