Biathlon

The biathlon is a winter sport that combines cross-country skiing and rifle shooting. It is rooted in the military patrol training of Scandinavian armies, where soldiers travelled on skis while carrying rifles. The sport is governed internationally by the International Biathlon Union (IBU).

Governing bodyInternational Biathlon Union
First played18th century, Scandinavia (military)
Olympic debut1960 (Squaw Valley)
EquipmentSkis, poles, .22 calibre rifle

Format

Competitors ski laps of a course, stopping at a shooting range to fire at five targets from a distance of 50 metres, alternating between prone and standing positions. Missed targets incur penalties: a 150-metre penalty loop in most formats, or a one-minute time addition in the individual race. Athletes carry their rifles on their backs while skiing.

Events

Standard formats include the individual (20 km men, 15 km women), sprint, pursuit, mass start, and relay, as well as mixed relays. The pursuit start order is determined by sprint results, and the mass start pits the top thirty competitors head-to-head from a simultaneous start.

History

A forerunner event, military patrol, appeared at the 1924 Winter Olympics. The modern biathlon was standardized in the 1950s, became an Olympic sport in 1960, and added women's events in 1992. Ole Einar Bjørndalen of Norway is the most decorated biathlete in Olympic history.
