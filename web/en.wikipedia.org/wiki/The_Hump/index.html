The Hump

The Hump was the name given by Allied pilots in the Second World War to the eastern end of the Himalayan Mountains over which they flew military transport aircraft from India to China, to resupply the Chinese war effort of Chiang Kai-shek and units of the United States Army Air Forces based in China.

The Hump airlift
PeriodApril 1942 – November 1945
RouteAssam, India – Kunming, China
Cargo deliveredAbout 650,000 tons
OperatorsUSAAF Air Transport Command, CNAC

Background

After Japanese forces closed the Burma Road in 1942, the airlift over the Himalayas became China's only supply line. Aircraft — first C-47s, then C-46 Commandos, C-54s and C-87s — flew from airfields in Assam over 15,000-foot ridges and through violent weather, with primitive navigation aids and the threat of Japanese fighters.

Costs and results

The operation was exceptionally dangerous: hundreds of aircraft and more than a thousand airmen were lost, and wreckage along the route gave it the nickname "the Aluminum Trail". At its 1945 peak the airlift delivered over 71,000 tons in a single month. It demonstrated that large-scale sustained air transport was feasible and foreshadowed the Berlin Airlift.
