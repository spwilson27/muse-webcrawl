Convair, previously the Consolidated Vultee Aircraft Corporation, was an American aircraft-manufacturing company that later expanded into rockets and spacecraft. It was formed in 1943 by the merger of Consolidated Aircraft and Vultee Aircraft, and in 1953 became a division of General Dynamics. Based in San Diego, California, Convair produced some of the most distinctive aircraft of the early Cold War.

Aircraft

Convair's piston airliners, the Convair 240/340/440 series, were widely used by airlines after the Second World War, and the Convair 880 and 990 Coronado were early (though commercially unsuccessful) jet airliners. For the U.S. Air Force, Convair built the B-36 Peacemaker, the largest piston-engined bomber ever put into production; the B-58 Hustler, the first operational supersonic bomber; the F-102 Delta Dagger and F-106 Delta Dart delta-wing interceptors; and the world's first seaplane fighter and other experimental types. Its San Diego division also developed the Atlas intercontinental ballistic missile, which evolved into the Atlas family of space launch vehicles that launched the first Americans into orbit.

Later years

After the 1960s, Convair shifted toward subcontracting, missile and space work, and airframe structures for other manufacturers, including fuselage sections for McDonnell Douglas airliners. General Dynamics sold or closed the Convair operations in the 1990s as the post–Cold War defence industry consolidated; the aircraft division's remnants were wound down by 1996, ending the Convair name as an aircraft manufacturer.
