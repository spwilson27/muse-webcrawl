Toby David Ord (born 1979) is an Australian philosopher at the University of Oxford whose work on existential risk and global priorities has shaped the effective altruism movement he helped to found. With William MacAskill he co-founded Giving What We Can in 2009, pledging (as its members do) a substantial share of his own income to effective charities, and he has advised governments and international bodies on catastrophic risk. His book The Precipice (2020) is the standard popular statement of the case that humanity's survival through this century cannot be taken for granted.

The Precipice

Ord argues that humanity has entered a uniquely dangerous period: since 1945 our power to destroy ourselves has outrun our wisdom in wielding it. He surveys the risks — nuclear war, engineered pandemics, unaligned artificial intelligence, extreme climate change, and natural risks such as asteroid impacts and supervolcanoes (which he finds comparatively small) — and offers his personal, explicitly judgement-based estimates: roughly a one-in-six chance of existential catastrophe within the next hundred years, with the largest shares assigned to AI and biotechnology. The book blends the analysis with a moral claim — that safeguarding humanity's vast potential future is among the defining tasks of our generation — that became foundational for Longtermism.

Other work

Ord's academic work spans ethics and decision theory and the philosophy of global priorities; with MacAskill and Aron Vallinder he co-authored work on moral uncertainty, and he contributed early analyses of the ethics of AI and of information hazards. His estimates and framings are debated — critics question whether such probabilities can be responsibly assigned — but his practice of stating them plainly, as judgements to be argued with, has set a norm in the field.
