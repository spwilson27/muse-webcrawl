Ibrox Stadium is a football stadium on the south side of the River Clyde in the Ibrox area of Glasgow, Scotland. It is the home ground of Rangers Football Club, who have played there since it opened in 1899. With a capacity of 50,817, it is one of the largest football stadiums in Scotland.

History

Rangers moved to Ibrox after their previous ground at Kinning Park became too small. The stadium's red-brick Main Stand, designed by the celebrated football architect Archibald Leitch and opened in 1929, is a listed building and one of the most recognisable facades in British football.

Disasters and redevelopment

Ibrox has been the scene of two major disasters: in 1902, 25 people died when a wooden terrace collapsed during a Scotland–England match, and on 2 January 1971, 66 supporters died in a crush on Stairway 13 after an Old Firm match. The 1971 disaster prompted a complete redevelopment of the stadium into one of Britain's first modern all-seater grounds, completed in the early 1980s; a memorial and statue of John Greig now commemorate the victims.
