Aidan of Lindisfarne (Irish: Áedán; c. 590 – 31 August 651) was an Irish monk and missionary, founder and first bishop of Lindisfarne, credited with converting the Anglo-Saxons of Northumbria to Christianity. He is known as the "Apostle of Northumbria" and is venerated as a saint in several traditions.

Mission to Northumbria

Aidan was a monk of the monastery of Iona in Scotland. When King Oswald of Northumbria, who had converted to Christianity in exile among the Irish, asked Iona to send a bishop, Aidan was chosen after a first candidate returned reporting the English too stubborn. Aidan arrived in 635 and made Lindisfarne — a tidal island near Oswald's fortress at Bamburgh — his see and base. Because his English was initially poor, Oswald himself interpreted his sermons.

Ministry

The Venerable Bede, the principal source for Aidan's life, praises his gentleness, itinerant preaching on foot, ransoming of slaves, and training of English disciples such as Hild, Cedd, and Chad. From Lindisfarne his monks founded daughter houses including Melrose and Hartlepool, and Lindisfarne became a centre of learning and art that later produced the Lindisfarne Gospels.

Death and legacy

Aidan died in 651 at Bamburgh, reportedly leaning against a church buttress, shortly after the death of his patron Oswald's successor Oswine. His feast day is 31 August, and he remains the patron of firefighters in some traditions and a key figure in the revival of interest in Celtic Christianity.
