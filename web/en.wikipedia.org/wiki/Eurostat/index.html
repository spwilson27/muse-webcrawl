Eurostat is a Directorate-General of the European Commission located in the Kirchberg quarter of Luxembourg City, Luxembourg. Its main responsibilities are to provide statistical information to the institutions of the European Union (EU) and to promote the harmonisation of statistical methods across its member states and candidates for accession, as well as EFTA countries.

History

Eurostat's origins go back to 1953, when a statistical division was set up for the European Coal and Steel Community. It became the Statistical Office of the European Communities in 1958 following the Treaties of Rome, and adopted the name Eurostat in 1959. Over time its role expanded with the growth of Community policies requiring comparable data, and it is now the statistical office of the European Union, working within the European Statistical System together with the national statistical institutes of the member states.

Work

Eurostat does not generally collect data itself; instead, the national statistical authorities of the member states collect and verify data in their own countries and send them to Eurostat, which consolidates and harmonises them to ensure comparability. Eurostat publishes statistics on the economy and finance, population and social conditions, industry, trade and services, agriculture and fisheries, international trade, transport, environment and energy, and science and technology. Its data underpin important EU processes, including the excessive deficit procedure based on government debt and deficit figures, regional funding allocations, and the monitoring of the Europe 2020 and European Green Deal objectives.

Access to data

Most Eurostat statistics are available free of charge through its online database and data browser, and are widely used by journalists, researchers, businesses, and policymakers. Eurostat also releases scheduled news releases on indicators such as GDP, inflation (the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices), and unemployment.
