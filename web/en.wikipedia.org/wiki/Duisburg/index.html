Duisburg is a city in the western German state of North Rhine-Westphalia, situated at the confluence of the Rhine and Ruhr rivers in the Ruhr metropolitan region. With roughly half a million inhabitants it is one of the largest cities in the region, and its river harbour is the largest inland port in the world.

History

The site was settled in Roman times and grew as a trading place on the Hellweg route. Duisburg received town privileges in the 12th century and joined the Hanseatic League. The cartographer Gerardus Mercator lived and worked in Duisburg, and its university, founded in 1655, operated until 1818; a modern successor, the University of Duisburg-Essen, was formed in 2003. Industrialisation made the city a centre of coal, steel and inland shipping.

Economy and port

The Port of Duisburg (duisport) handles well over 100 million tonnes of goods a year across its several harbour basins, linking Rhine barges with rail and road networks and, since the 2010s, with freight trains on the China–Europe railway corridor. Steel production, once dominant, has contracted, and the city has redeveloped former industrial land such as the Innenhafen (inner harbour) with offices, museums and housing.

Culture

Landmarks include the Landschaftspark Duisburg-Nord, a landscaped park built around a disused ironworks, the Wilhelm Lehmbruck Museum of sculpture, and the Tiger and Turtle – Magic Mountain roller-coaster-like sculpture. In 2010 a crowd crush at the Love Parade festival in Duisburg killed 21 people, one of Germany's worst disasters of its kind.
