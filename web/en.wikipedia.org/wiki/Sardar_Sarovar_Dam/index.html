Sardar Sarovar Dam

The Sardar Sarovar Dam is a large concrete gravity dam on the Narmada River near Kevadia in the Narmada district of Gujarat, India. Standing 163 metres tall from its deepest foundation, it is the centrepiece of the Sardar Sarovar Project, providing irrigation water, drinking water and hydroelectric power to Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Rajasthan. Its installed generating capacity is 1,450 MW.

History

Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru laid the foundation stone in 1961, but interstate disputes over water sharing delayed the project until the Narmada Water Disputes Tribunal award of 1979. Construction proceeded from the late 1980s amid intense controversy. The dam's height was raised in stages, reaching its full 138.68-metre crest level in 2017, when it was dedicated to the nation.

Controversy

The project displaced large numbers of people, predominantly Adivasi communities in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat, and became the focus of the Narmada Bachao Andolan ("Save the Narmada") movement led by Medha Patkar and others, which campaigned on resettlement, environmental and human-rights grounds. The independent Morse Commission review for the World Bank (1992) was highly critical, and the Bank withdrew in 1993. India's Supreme Court allowed construction to continue, subject to rehabilitation conditions, in a landmark 2000 judgment.

Nearby attractions

Adjacent to the dam at Kevadia stands the Statue of Unity, the world's tallest statue, depicting Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, for whom the dam is also named.
