Engineer

Engineers at work

An engineer is a professional who applies scientific, mathematical and practical knowledge to design, build, analyse and maintain structures, machines, devices, systems, materials and processes. The word derives from the Latin ingenium ("cleverness") and ingeniare ("to contrive, devise"); historically it referred to builders of military engines and works, and in many countries the term still overlaps with the operator of an engine — for example, a locomotive engineer or a ship's engineer.

Branches

Engineering is conventionally divided into major branches, each with many sub-disciplines:

  • Civil engineering — infrastructure: buildings, bridges, roads, dams, water systems
  • Mechanical engineering — machines, engines, thermal and mechanical systems
  • Electrical and electronic engineering — power, circuits, communications, computing hardware
  • Chemical engineering — industrial chemical processes and materials production
  • Software engineering — systematic design and development of software systems
  • Others include aerospace, biomedical, industrial, environmental, mining, nuclear and marine engineering

Education and licensing

Engineers typically hold a bachelor's degree in engineering or a related field, often from a programme accredited by a body such as ABET in the United States or under the Washington Accord internationally. In many jurisdictions, engineers offering services directly to the public in safety-critical fields must be licensed — for example as a Professional Engineer (PE) in the US and Canada, or as a Chartered Engineer (CEng) in the UK. Professional ethics codes oblige engineers to hold paramount the safety, health and welfare of the public.

Practice

The engineer's work centres on solving problems under constraints — cost, safety, regulation, materials and time — using analysis, modelling, simulation and testing. Famous engineers include Isambard Kingdom Brunel, Gustave Eiffel, Nikola Tesla, the Wright brothers and Fazlur Rahman Khan. Engineering achievements range from aqueducts and cathedrals to steam engines, electrical grids, aircraft, computers and spacecraft.
