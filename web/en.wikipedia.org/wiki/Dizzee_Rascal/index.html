Dizzee Rascal

Dizzee Rascal (born Dylan Kwabena Mills, 18 September 1984) is an English rapper and songwriter. A pioneer of the grime genre, he rose to prominence with his debut album Boy in da Corner (2003), which won the Mercury Prize. His later crossover hits, including "Dance Wiv Me", "Bonkers", "Holiday", and "Dirtee Disco", all reached number one on the UK Singles Chart.

Early life

Mills was raised by his Ghanaian mother in Bow, East London, after his Nigerian father died when he was young. Expelled from several schools, he began making music on school computers and performing on pirate radio stations, becoming a member of the Roll Deep crew.

Career

Boy in da Corner, released when he was 18, was critically acclaimed for its stark production and vivid depiction of inner-city life, and is widely credited with defining grime. Subsequent albums include Showtime (2004), Maths + English (2007), and Tongue n' Cheek (2009), the last spawning four UK number-one singles. He founded his own label, Dirtee Stank, performed at the London 2012 Olympic opening ceremony, and was appointed MBE in 2020 for services to music.

Personal life

In 2022 Mills was convicted of assaulting his former fiancée; his appeal was dismissed in 2023.
