The Sukhoi Su-24 (NATO reporting name: Fencer) is a supersonic, all-weather attack aircraft developed in the Soviet Union. The first Soviet aircraft to carry an integrated digital navigation/attack system, the Su-24 features a variable-sweep wing, two engines, and a side-by-side seating arrangement for its crew of two. Designed for low-level penetration and precision strike, it was broadly the Soviet counterpart to the American F-111.

Development

The Su-24 programme began in the early 1960s in response to the poor low-level performance and payload/range of earlier Soviet fighter-bombers. Sukhoi's design went through fixed-wing and lift-jet STOL configurations before settling on a variable-geometry design influenced by the F-111 and the TSR-2. The T-6 prototype flew in 1967 with fixed wings; the variable-sweep T6-2I first flew on 17 January 1970. The aircraft entered service in 1974.

Design

The Su-24 is powered by two Lyulka AL-21F turbojets and can reach Mach 1.6 at altitude. Its PNS-24 integrated navigation and weapons system, combining the Orion-A attack radar and Relyef terrain-following radar, allowed automatic flight at very low altitude in all weather. The crew of pilot and navigator/weapons officer sit side by side under individual canopies and are provided with K-36D ejection seats. The aircraft can carry up to 8,000 kg of ordnance on nine hardpoints, including laser- and TV-guided bombs, anti-radiation missiles, and a built-in six-barrel 23 mm GSh-6-23 cannon.

Variants and service

Major variants include the Su-24M improved strike model, the Su-24MR reconnaissance version, and the Su-24MP electronic warfare aircraft. Some 1,400 were built at Novosibirsk between 1967 and 1993, and the type was exported to Algeria, Iran, Libya, Syria, and others. The Su-24 has seen extensive combat: in the Soviet–Afghan War, the Iran–Iraq War (Iraqi aircraft), the Russian intervention in Syria, and the Russo-Ukrainian War, where both Russia and Ukraine have operated the type. Russia has been progressively replacing it with the Su-34, while upgraded Su-24M2 and Ukrainian aircraft remain in service.
