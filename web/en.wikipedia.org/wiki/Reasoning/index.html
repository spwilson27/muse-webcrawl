Reasoning

Reasoning is the mental process of drawing conclusions from premises or evidence. It is the systematic use of logic to move from given information to new beliefs or decisions, and it is studied by philosophers, psychologists, logicians, and computer scientists. Reasoning is closely associated with thinking, cognition, and rationality, and contrasts with intuition, perception, and emotion-driven response.

Reasoning moves from premises to conclusions by way of inference.

Types of reasoning

Deductive reasoning

Deductive reasoning derives conclusions that are logically guaranteed by the premises: if the premises are true and the form of argument is valid, the conclusion cannot be false. Syllogisms and mathematical proofs are examples. Deductive inference is truth-preserving but does not expand the information contained in the premises.

Inductive reasoning

Inductive reasoning generalises from observed cases to broader conclusions that are probable but not certain, such as inferring that all swans are white from many white swans. Most empirical science rests on induction, and the problem of induction — how such inference can be justified — is a classic issue raised by David Hume.

Abductive reasoning

Abduction, or inference to the best explanation, forms a hypothesis that would explain the available evidence. It is characteristic of scientific theorising and medical diagnosis.

Other forms

Analogical reasoning transfers conclusions between similar cases; causal reasoning identifies causes and effects; and practical reasoning concerns what one ought to do rather than what is true. Psychologists distinguish fast, intuitive "System 1" processes from slow, deliberate "System 2" reasoning, following Daniel Kahneman's dual-process framework.

Fallacies and biases

Reasoning can go astray through formal fallacies (errors in logical form), informal fallacies (such as ad hominem attacks or straw men), and systematic cognitive biases like confirmation bias and base-rate neglect, documented extensively in the heuristics-and-biases research programme.

Machine reasoning

In artificial intelligence, automated reasoning systems — theorem provers, logic programming languages such as Prolog, and expert systems — implement formal inference, while recent large language models have renewed debate about what counts as genuine reasoning in machines.
