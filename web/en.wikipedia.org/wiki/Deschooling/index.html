Deschooling is a term with two related meanings. In educational philosophy, it refers to the critique of institutional schooling — most famously in Ivan Illich's Deschooling Society (1971) — and the proposal to replace compulsory schools with decentralised, self-directed "learning webs". In the homeschooling community, "deschooling" also names the adjustment period after a child leaves school, during which they shed school-based habits and rediscover intrinsic motivation to learn.

Illich's critique

Illich argued that schools monopolise education, confuse the process of learning with its institutional certification, and teach a "hidden curriculum" of dependence, consumption and obedience. He proposed learning networks: reference services to educational objects, skill exchanges, peer-matching, and access to experienced practitioners, allowing anyone to learn or teach outside a school framework.

Deschooling as transition

Among homeschoolers, deschooling denotes a decompression phase — often suggested as roughly one month per year spent in school — in which a child (and family) unwinds classroom routines before beginning home education. It is a precursor stage frequently recommended before unschooling, the philosophy, associated with John Holt, in which children's learning is driven by their own interests.
