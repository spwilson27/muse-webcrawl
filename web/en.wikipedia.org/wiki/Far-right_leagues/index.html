Far-right leagues

The far-right leagues (French: ligues d'extrême droite) were a group of nationalist, anti-parliamentary movements active in France during the Third Republic, particularly in the interwar period. They included the Croix-de-Feu, the Jeunesses Patriotes, the Solidarité Française, the Francistes, and Action Française.

Origins and ideology

The leagues grew out of earlier nationalist organizations formed during the Dreyfus Affair era, such as the Ligue des Patriotes. They shared anti-communism, nationalism, hostility to parliamentary politics, and, in varying degrees, monarchism or authoritarianism, though they differed in organization and in their relationship to fascism — a subject of continuing historiographical debate.

The riots of 6 February 1934

On 6 February 1934, league demonstrations in Paris escalated into riots outside the Chamber of Deputies that left at least 15 people dead and brought down the government of Édouard Daladier. The crisis spurred the formation of the Popular Front and the dissolution of several leagues by decree in 1936; the Croix-de-Feu reorganized as the Parti Social Français.
