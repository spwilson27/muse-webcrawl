James Naismith

Born: November 6, 1861, Almonte, Ontario, Canada

Died: November 28, 1939 (aged 78), Lawrence, Kansas, U.S.

Occupation: Educator, physical training instructor

Known for: Inventing basketball

James Naismith (November 6, 1861 – November 28, 1939) was a Canadian-American physical educator, physician, and sports coach, best known as the inventor of basketball. He founded the University of Kansas basketball program and served as the sport's first coach.

Life and education

Naismith was born in Almonte, Ontario, to Scottish immigrant parents. He attended McGill University in Montreal, where he excelled in sports and graduated in 1887. After studying theology and teaching physical education, he was hired by the International YMCA Training School in Springfield, Massachusetts, in 1890.

Invention of basketball

In December 1891, tasked by Luther Gulick with devising an indoor team sport for a restless class, Naismith wrote thirteen basic rules for a game he called "basketball," using two peach baskets as goals and a soccer ball. The first game was played on December 21, 1891. His original rules were published in January 1892, and the game spread rapidly through YMCA networks worldwide.

Legacy

In 1898, Naismith became the first basketball coach at the University of Kansas, where he coached the legendary Phog Allen. He also helped introduce basketball to the 1904 Olympics as a demonstration event. He was inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame, which is named the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in his honor.
