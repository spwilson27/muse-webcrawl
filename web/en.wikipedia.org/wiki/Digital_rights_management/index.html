Digital rights management

Digital rights management (DRM) is a set of access-control technologies used by publishers, copyright holders and hardware manufacturers to control the use of digital content and devices after sale. DRM technologies attempt to control the use, modification and distribution of copyrighted works, as well as systems within devices that enforce these policies.

Technologies

Common schemes include licence servers and product activation for software, encryption and account binding for e-books and downloads, and streaming protections such as Widevine, FairPlay and PlayReady. Music downloads largely abandoned DRM in the late 2000s, while streaming video, games and e-books retain it.

Controversy

Critics argue DRM restricts legitimate uses such as backup, format-shifting, interoperability, preservation and accessibility, and can render purchased content unusable when servers shut down. Anti-circumvention laws such as the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act make bypassing DRM largely illegal even for lawful purposes, with limited exemptions. Supporters credit DRM with enabling rental and subscription business models.
