Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary

The Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary, also known as the Wild Ass Wildlife Sanctuary, is located in the Little Rann of Kutch in the Indian state of Gujarat. Established in 1972 under the Wildlife Protection Act, it covers about 4,954 km² and is one of the largest wildlife sanctuaries in India. It protects the last remaining population of the Indian wild ass (Equus hemionus khur), locally called the khur or ghudkhur.

Landscape and habitat

The sanctuary occupies a vast seasonal salt marsh and desert plain that floods during the monsoon and dries into cracked saline flats for the rest of the year. Raised islands of higher ground, called bets, support grasses and shrubs such as Prosopis and remain refuges for animals during flooding. The area is also noted for salt production by traditional Agariya salt workers.

Wildlife

The wild ass population, once reduced to a few hundred animals, recovered to several thousand (over 6,000 in the 2020 census) thanks to protection. Other mammals include nilgai, blackbuck, Indian wolf, striped hyena, desert fox, jungle cat and chinkara. The sanctuary is an important birding area: flamingos breed in the "Flamingo City" of the Great Rann region nearby, and the Little Rann hosts houbara bustards, cranes, pelicans, and large numbers of migratory waterfowl in winter.

Threats and conservation

Threats include expansion of salt works, grazing pressure, vehicle traffic, conversion of habitat and disease transmission from domestic livestock. The wild ass is listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN, and the sanctuary forms part of the proposed Kutch Biosphere Reserve.
