Non-disclosure agreement

A non-disclosure agreement (NDA) is a legal contract between parties that outlines confidential material, knowledge or information that the parties wish to share with one another for certain purposes but restrict from wider disclosure.

Types

NDAs may be unilateral (one party discloses), bilateral or mutual (both parties disclose), or multilateral. Common provisions define confidential information, exclusions (such as information already public), obligations of the receiving party, the term of confidentiality, and permitted disclosures to employees, advisers or regulators.

Uses

NDAs are used in business negotiations, employment, mergers and acquisitions, and protection of trade secrets. Settlement agreements frequently contain confidentiality clauses. In some jurisdictions, laws limit NDAs that would conceal harassment, discrimination or whistleblowing, and employees generally retain rights to report illegal conduct.

Enforcement

Remedies for breach can include injunctions and damages; enforceability depends on reasonableness, consideration and public-policy limits in the governing jurisdiction.
