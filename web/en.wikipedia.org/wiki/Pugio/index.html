Pugio

The pugio was a dagger used by Roman soldiers as a sidearm and utility weapon. It was worn on the belt opposite the gladius and served for close combat, everyday tasks and as a status symbol; decorated examples with inlaid sheaths are known from military sites across the empire.

Design

The blade was typically 18–28 cm long and 4–5 cm wide, leaf-shaped or waisted, with a prominent midrib. Hilts were often of layered horn, wood or ivory over an iron tang, with a flattened spherical pommel expansion. Sheaths were of iron or bronze, sometimes tinned or decorated.

Use

Probably adopted from Iberian peoples during the Punic Wars era, the pugio remained standard legionary equipment into the 3rd century CE. It is also famous historically: daggers of this type were among the weapons used in the assassination of Julius Caesar in 44 BCE.
