Paul Mark Oakenfold (born 30 August 1963) is an English record producer, remixer and DJ, one of the pioneers who brought acid house and trance from clubs to a global mainstream audience. A key figure in the Balearic beat story of Ibiza, he founded the Perfecto label, produced and remixed artists from U2 to Madonna, and was for years ranked among the world's highest-earning DJs.

Career

Oakenfold began visiting New York clubs such as the Paradise Garage as a teenager, absorbing disco, soul and early hip hop, and worked as an A&R man and promoter in London. A now-legendary holiday in Ibiza in 1987 with friends including Danny Rampling and Nicky Holloway exposed him to the eclectic Balearic sound of DJ Alfredo; back in London his club nights — Spectrum and the Land of Oz at Heaven — helped ignite Britain's acid house explosion of the "Second Summer of Love" in 1988.

With Steve Osborne he remixed and produced for the Happy Mondays (including the album Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches, 1990) and The Cure. As Perfecto became a leading trance imprint, Oakenfold's Goa mixes for Mixmag and his residency at Liverpool superclub Cream made him the archetypal superstar DJ; his 1999 mix album Tranceport and Global Underground: New York were genre landmarks. He was voted the world's No. 1 DJ in DJ Mag's poll in 1998 and 1999, and Guinness World Records listed him as the most successful DJ in the world.

Film, pop and later work

Oakenfold scored and contributed music to films including Swordfish (2001), for which he composed the soundtrack, and remixed themes for the James Bond series, The Bourne Identity and Speed Racer. His solo album Bunkka (2002) featured collaborations with Nelly Furtado, Ice Cube and Shifty Shellshock ("Starry Eyed Surprise"). He produced Madonna's "Celebration" (2009) and toured with her, and holds residencies that have included Las Vegas's Rain nightclub, where he helped launch the modern American DJ-residency era. In 2017 he performed a widely publicised DJ set at Everest Base Camp.
