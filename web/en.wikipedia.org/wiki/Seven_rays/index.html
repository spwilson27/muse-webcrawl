Seven rays

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The seven rays is an occult concept that has appeared in several religions and esoteric philosophies in both the Western culture and in India since at least the sixth century BCE. In Western culture it can be seen in early Western mystery traditions, such as Gnosticism, and in texts such as the Kabbalah, and later in Theosophy and the teachings of Alice Bailey, where it became a central doctrine.

The Theosophical concept

In Theosophical teaching, the seven rays are seven streams of cosmic energy or divine emanations that underlie all manifestation. Each ray is associated with a colour, a quality, a planetary influence and a presiding Master or Chohan. Helena Blavatsky referred to the rays in The Secret Doctrine, and Charles W. Leadbeater and Alice Bailey elaborated the system extensively.

The rays in Alice Bailey's writings

Alice Bailey, in works such as A Treatise on the Seven Rays (1936–1960), described the rays as follows:

  • First Ray: Will or Power
  • Second Ray: Love-Wisdom
  • Third Ray: Active Intelligence
  • Fourth Ray: Harmony through Conflict
  • Fifth Ray: Concrete Knowledge or Science
  • Sixth Ray: Devotion and Idealism
  • Seventh Ray: Ceremonial Order or Magic

According to Bailey, every human being belongs to one of the rays at the level of the soul, the personality, the mind and the emotions, and the interplay of these ray energies shapes individual temperament and development. Nations and civilisations were also said to have ray qualities.

Later influence

The seven rays teaching was taken up by various New Age and Ascended Master movements, including the "I AM" Activity and the Church Universal and Triumphant, in which the rays are associated with the seven archangels, the seven Elohim and coloured flames used in visualisation and invocation. The psychologist and writer Joshua David Stone and others further popularised ray typology in the late twentieth century.
