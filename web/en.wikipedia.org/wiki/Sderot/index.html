Sderot

Sderot is a city in the Southern District of Israel, in the western Negev desert, about a kilometre from the Gaza Strip. Founded in 1951 as a transit camp (ma'abara) for Jewish immigrants, chiefly from Kurdistan and Iran and later large numbers from Morocco, it became a development town and was granted city status in 1996. Its population is around 30,000, including many immigrants from the former Soviet Union and Ethiopia.

Rocket attacks

From 2001, Sderot was repeatedly targeted by Qassam rockets fired from the Gaza Strip, which caused deaths, injuries and widespread psychological trauma, particularly among children; a warning system nicknamed "Color Red" gives residents roughly 15 seconds to reach shelter. The city was attacked during the Hamas-led assault of 7 October 2023, in which its police station was seized and destroyed, and most residents were evacuated for months.

Culture

Despite its difficulties, Sderot is noted for a disproportionately rich music scene — bands such as Teapacks and Knesiyat Hasekhel emerged from the city — and hosts Sapir Academic College and the Sderot Cinematheque.
