The Gbe languages are a cluster of about twenty related languages spoken in West Africa along the coast of the Gulf of Guinea, from eastern Ghana through Togo and Benin into southwestern Nigeria. They form a dialect continuum within the Kwa branch of the Niger–Congo family and are spoken by roughly 20 million people.

Languages

The major Gbe languages include Ewe (about 7 million speakers, Ghana and Togo), Fon (Benin), Gen, Aja, Gungbe and the Phla and Pherá varieties. Fon and Ewe are widely used in education, media and religion in Benin, Togo and Ghana.

Features

Gbe languages are tonal and largely isolating (analytic), with subject–verb–object word order, serial verb constructions and extensive use of body-part nouns as spatial relators. Mutual intelligibility varies across the continuum, with neighbouring varieties generally intelligible to one another.

History

The Gbe-speaking peoples were historically associated with kingdoms including Whydah and Dahomey. Through the Atlantic slave trade, Gbe languages contributed to Caribbean creoles such as Haitian Creole, and Gbe ritual vocabulary survives in Afro-Brazilian and Afro-Cuban religions as the "Jeje" tradition, as well as in Vodun (Voodoo) practice in Benin and Togo.
