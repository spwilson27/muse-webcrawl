Mount Ibuki

Elevation: 1,377 m (4,518 ft)
Location: Shiga and Gifu prefectures, Japan
Range: Ibuki Mountains
Listing: 100 Famous Japanese Mountains

Mount Ibuki (伊吹山, Ibuki-yama) is a mountain in Japan, standing 1,377 metres (4,518 ft) on the border between Shiga and Gifu prefectures, northeast of Lake Biwa. It is the highest peak in Shiga Prefecture and one of the 100 Famous Japanese Mountains. The mountain's distinctive isolated cone, visible from the plains of Kansai and Tōkai, has been an object of worship and a landmark since ancient times, appearing in the earliest Japanese chronicles, the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, where the hero Yamato Takeru is said to have been stricken by the mountain's god while crossing it.

Geography and climate

Mount Ibuki is largely limestone, extensively quarried on its flanks. Its position at the western end of the narrow corridor between the mountains of central Honshu and the Sea of Japan makes it famous for winter weather: cold Siberian air funnels past the peak, and on 14 February 1927 the summit recorded 11.82 metres of snow accumulation, a world record for a staffed observation site. The mountain's weather station also long provided forecasts important to the region. The summit area supports alpine plants at an unusually low altitude because of the heavy snow and wind.

Flora

The upper slopes are noted for fields of wildflowers in summer, including endemic and rare species (the "Ibuki" herbs, such as Ibuki-jakōsō thyme), which historically supplied medicinal herbs gathered on the mountain. Limestone-loving plant communities make the peak a designated natural monument area.

Access

The Ibuki Driveway toll road (opened 1965) climbs to a parking area near the summit, from which short trails lead to the top; hiking routes also ascend from the base, and the mountain was once served by a ski area on its northern slopes (closed in 2010). Ibuki is a popular day trip from Nagoya, Kyoto, and Osaka.
