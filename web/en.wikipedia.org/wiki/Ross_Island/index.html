Ross Island

Ross Island is a volcanic island in the Ross Sea, Antarctica, formed by four volcanoes, including the active Mount Erebus (3,794 m), Earth's southernmost active volcano, and the dormant Mount Terror. It was discovered in 1841 by Sir James Clark Ross, for whom it is named.

Exploration history

Because its coast is accessible by ship late in the season, Ross Island became the base for the heroic age of Antarctic exploration. Historic huts survive at Cape Evans (Scott's Terra Nova expedition), Hut Point (Discovery expedition) and Cape Royds (Shackleton's Nimrod expedition). Scott and Shackleton both launched their South Pole attempts from the island, and the cross on Observation Hill commemorates the five men of Scott's polar party who died in 1912.

Research stations

Today the island hosts McMurdo Station (United States), the largest station in Antarctica, and New Zealand's Scott Base, both at its southern tip. Ross Island lies within the Ross Dependency claimed by New Zealand and is protected under the Antarctic Treaty System.
