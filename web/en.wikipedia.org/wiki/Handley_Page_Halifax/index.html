The Handley Page Halifax is a British four-engined heavy bomber of the Second World War, designed and built by Handley Page. It was one of the three principal heavy bombers of the Royal Air Force, alongside the Avro Lancaster and the Short Stirling, and flew more than 75,000 sorties with RAF Bomber Command.

Development

The Halifax originated in Air Ministry specification P.13/36 for a twin-engined bomber powered by Rolls-Royce Vulture engines. When the Vulture proved unreliable, the design was reworked with four Rolls-Royce Merlin engines, first flying on 25 October 1939. It entered service with No. 35 Squadron in November 1940 and flew its first operational raid against Hamburg in March 1941.

Operational history

Early marks suffered from underpowered engines and handling problems, but the definitive Mk III, powered by Bristol Hercules radial engines and fitted with a larger wing and modified tail, was a capable and popular aircraft. Halifaxes bombed targets across occupied Europe, served with Coastal Command against U-boats, towed gliders, dropped paratroops and supplies to resistance movements with special duties squadrons, and were used for electronic countermeasures.

The type was flown by squadrons of several Commonwealth and Allied air forces, including Canadian, Australian, New Zealand, Polish and Free French units. After the war, Halifaxes were used as transports and freighters; the civilian Halton derivative flew in the Berlin Airlift. The last RAF aircraft was retired in the early 1950s. A total of 6,176 Halifaxes were built.
