Onion dome

An onion dome is a dome whose shape resembles an onion: it bulges out beyond the diameter of its drum and tapers to a point at the top. Such domes are most famously associated with Russian architecture — notably the colourful domes of Saint Basil's Cathedral in Moscow — and are widespread in Eastern Orthodox church architecture in Russia, Ukraine and elsewhere.

Origins

The origins of the onion dome are debated. Some scholars trace the form to Mongol or Islamic influence transmitted through the Golden Horde, others to native wooden architecture, in which bulbous cupolas could be built of shingles. Russian domes were helmet-shaped before the Mongol period; the distinctly onion profile appears by the 16th century and became standard in the 17th. A practical benefit often cited is that the shape sheds snow.

Symbolism

In Russian tradition the domes are read symbolically: the pointed flame-like form is likened to a burning candle, and groupings carry meaning — five domes for Christ and the four Evangelists, three for the Trinity. Domes are frequently gilded or painted (the patterns of St Basil's date largely to the 17th century).

Spread

Onion domes also crown Mughal and later Indian buildings (a related bulbous profile appears on the Taj Mahal), Central European baroque churches and synagogues, and many Russian Revival buildings of the 19th century.
