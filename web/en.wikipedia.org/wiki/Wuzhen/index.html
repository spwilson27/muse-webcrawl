Wuzhen

Province: Zhejiang
Administered by: Tongxiang, Jiaxing
Known for: Canal town; World Internet Conference

Wuzhen (simplified Chinese: 乌镇; traditional Chinese: 烏鎮) is a historic water town in Tongxiang, northern Zhejiang province, China, within the triangle formed by Hangzhou, Suzhou and Shanghai. It is one of the best known of the ancient canal towns of the Jiangnan region south of the Yangtze River.

History

The town's history stretches back more than a thousand years — the settlement of Wudun dates to the Tang dynasty, and the name Wuzhen emerged in the Song dynasty. The town prospered on silk, rice and its position on the canal network linking to the Grand Canal. Wuzhen is the birthplace of the modern Chinese writer Mao Dun (1896–1981), whose former residence is preserved as a museum.

Scenic areas

Development for tourism has concentrated on two zones: Dongzha (East Gate), with museums devoted to folk customs, indigo dyeing, footbinding and shadow puppetry; and Xizha (West Gate), a larger resort area of restored Ming- and Qing-dynasty streets, canals, bridges and waterside inns, especially celebrated for its night views.

Modern events

Since 2014 Wuzhen has hosted the annual World Internet Conference, China’s flagship summit on internet policy, and it is also the site of the Wuzhen Theatre Festival, co-founded in 2013 by Meng Jinghui, Huang Lei and Stan Lai.
