Lumer–Phillips theorem

Lumer–Phillips theorem
FieldFunctional analysis
Proved1961 (Günter Lumer, Ralph Phillips)
SubjectGenerators of strongly continuous semigroups

The Lumer–Phillips theorem is a result in functional analysis that characterises the generators of strongly continuous contraction semigroups (C0 semigroups) on Banach spaces. It complements the Hille–Yosida theorem with conditions that are often easier to verify in applications to partial differential equations.

Statement

Let A be a densely defined linear operator on a Banach space X. Then A generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup if and only if A is dissipative — for every x in the domain of A there exists a functional in the duality set with Re⟨Ax, x*⟩ ≤ 0 — and the range of λI − A is all of X for some (equivalently, every) λ > 0.

Significance

The theorem converts the abstract problem of solving evolution equations u′(t) = Au(t) into checking dissipativity, often a simple integration-by-parts estimate, plus a solvability (range) condition for an associated elliptic problem. It is fundamental in the modern theory of heat, wave, and Schrödinger equations, transport equations, and control theory.
