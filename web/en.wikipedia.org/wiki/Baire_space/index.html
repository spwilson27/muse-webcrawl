Baire space

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Diagram of a tree of finite sequences representing the Baire space of infinite sequences of natural numbers]

In mathematics, the term Baire space is used in two related senses. In topology, a Baire space is a topological space in which the intersection of any countable collection of open dense sets is dense — the conclusion of the Baire category theorem. In descriptive set theory, the Baire space is the specific space of all infinite sequences of natural numbers, usually denoted ωω, with the product topology.

Baire spaces in topology

The Baire category theorem states that every complete metric space is a Baire space, as is every locally compact Hausdorff space. Sets that are countable unions of nowhere dense sets are called meagre (of the first category); a Baire space is precisely one in which no non-empty open set is meagre. The theorem has major applications in functional analysis, including the uniform boundedness principle and the open mapping theorem. The spaces are named after the French mathematician René-Louis Baire.

The Baire space in descriptive set theory

The set of infinite sequences of natural numbers with basic open sets determined by finite initial segments is homeomorphic to the irrational numbers with their usual topology. It is a zero-dimensional, Polish, nowhere locally compact space, and serves as a universal space for Polish spaces in descriptive set theory; every uncountable Polish space contains a copy of it as a Borel subset.
