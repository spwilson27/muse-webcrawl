Andrés Pico

Andrés Pico (18 November 1810 – 14 February 1876) was a Californio military commander, rancher, and politician. He was the younger brother of Pío Pico, the last Mexican governor of Alta California.

Mexican–American War

During the Mexican–American War, Pico led Californio lancers to victory over United States forces at the Battle of San Pasqual in December 1846, one of the bloodiest engagements of the conquest of California. In January 1847 he signed the Treaty of Cahuenga with John C. Frémont, which ended hostilities in Alta California on terms favourable to the Californios.

Later life

Under American rule, Pico served in the California State Assembly and Senate. He authored the Pico Act of 1859, which proposed dividing California to create a southern Territory of Colorado — a measure approved by voters and the legislature but never acted upon by Congress. His former estate in the San Fernando Valley is preserved at the Andrés Pico Adobe, one of the oldest residences in Los Angeles.
