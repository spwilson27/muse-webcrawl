Cross tuning

Cross tuning or cross key is a practice in folk fiddling, especially in American old-time and bluegrass music, in which the fiddle (violin) is tuned differently from its standard GDAE tuning — a form of scordatura. The strings are retuned so that open strings form the chord of the key in which a tune is played, producing resonant drones and double stops that reinforce the melody.

Common tunings

  • AEAE ("A cross" or "cross A") — the G and D strings are raised a whole step, giving open strings that sound an A major chord; used for tunes in A major.
  • GDGD — the A and E strings lowered, for tunes in G.
  • DDAD — associated with tunes such as "Bonaparte's Retreat", popularised by William Stepp's 1937 recording.
  • AEAC♯ — sometimes called "Calico tuning" or "Black Mountain Rag" tuning.

Effect and use

Cross tuning lets adjacent open strings ring sympathetically with the fingered melody, filling out the sound of a solo fiddler playing for dances — historically the context in which the practice developed in the Appalachian Mountains and the American South. Because retuning changes string tension and intonation, fiddlers often keep favourite tunings for particular tune families, and some cross-tuned pieces cannot be played convincingly in standard tuning at all. The practice parallels open tunings on banjo and guitar in the same traditions.
