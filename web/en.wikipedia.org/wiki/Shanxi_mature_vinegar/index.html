Shanxi mature vinegar

Shanxi mature vinegar (Chinese: 山西老陈醋; pinyin: Shānxī lǎochéncù) is a famous black vinegar produced in Shanxi Province, China, most notably in Qingxu County near Taiyuan. It is counted among the four great vinegars of China, alongside Zhenjiang aromatic vinegar, Sichuan Baoning vinegar and Fujian Yongchun vinegar.

Production

The vinegar is brewed primarily from sorghum, with peas, barley and bran used in the fermentation starter (daqu). Traditional production follows a sequence of steaming, fermenting, smoking the fermented grains (pei), leaching and long aging. The smoking step gives the vinegar its deep black colour and characteristic roasted aroma. Quality vinegar is aged for at least a year, and premium grades for three to five years or more, growing thicker, darker and mellower with time; evaporation during aging concentrates the flavour, so that a batch may lose a substantial share of its volume.

Characteristics and use

Shanxi mature vinegar is noted for its balance of sourness, fragrance, sweetness and richness, and it does not spoil easily in storage. It is a defining seasoning of Shanxi's wheat-based cuisine — served with dumplings, noodles and cold dishes — and is used throughout northern China in dipping sauces, dressings and braises.
