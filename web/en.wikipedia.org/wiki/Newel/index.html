Newel

A newel, also called a central pole or support column, is the central pillar of a spiral, helical or winder staircase. More broadly, the term refers to a newel post: the upright post that supports or terminates a handrail at the end of a flight of stairs.

Structural role

In a spiral staircase the newel is the vertical column around which the treads radiate; it carries much of the stair's load and gives the structure its rigidity. In masonry spiral stairs of medieval castles and towers, the newel was built as stacked stone drums forming an integral part of each step.

Newel posts

At the foot and top of a straight flight, a newel post anchors the balustrade and provides the handrail's main structural support. Newel posts are often made of hardwood and historically were finished with a decorative newel cap — a ball, acorn, or other turned finial. In grand houses the newel might be oversized and elaborately carved. A hollow newel or "newel drop" may serve as a finishing piece hanging below a landing.

Terminology

The word is from the Middle English nowel, from Old French noiel ("kernel, core"), from Latin nucalis ("nut-like").
