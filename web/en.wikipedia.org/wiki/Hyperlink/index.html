Hyperlink

In computing, a hyperlink (or simply a link) is a digital reference that a user can follow or be guided by, connecting one part of a document or resource to another. Hyperlinks are the defining feature of hypertext and are most familiar as the links of the World Wide Web.

History

The concept descends from Vannevar Bush's Memex proposal (1945) and the work of Douglas Engelbart and Ted Nelson, who coined the term "hypertext" in the 1960s and developed the Xanadu project. Tim Berners-Lee implemented hyperlinks at the heart of the Web from 1989–1991: a link in an HTML document is created with the anchor element and its href attribute, which holds a URL.

Behaviour and appearance

Hyperlinks are typically displayed in a contrasting colour, often blue and underlined, and change appearance when hovered over or visited. Activating a link — by clicking, tapping or keyboard navigation — causes the browser to retrieve and display the target resource. Links may point to whole pages, to fragments within a page, or to files and other media.

Types and issues

Distinctions include internal versus external links, deep links to content below a home page, and embedded links in images. Problems associated with hyperlinks include link rot, where targets disappear over time, and legal disputes over practices such as framing, inline linking and deep linking to copyrighted material.
