Rhythm

Rhythm is the pattern of events in time, especially the arrangement of sounds, silences, accents and durations in music and speech. It creates expectation through repetition and contrast and is one of the fundamental elements by which listeners organize musical experience.

Elements

Beat is a recurring pulse. Tempo is the speed of that pulse. Meter groups beats into measures with stronger and weaker positions, such as two, three or four beats per bar. Individual notes may align with the beat or depart from it through syncopation, while subdivisions create patterns such as triplets and dotted rhythms.

Perception

Humans tend to group similar or proximate sounds and infer a beat even when not every pulse is sounded. Rhythm perception engages motor areas of the brain, which helps explain tapping, dancing and marching in time. Infants respond to rhythmic patterns before they understand melody or harmony.

Cultural variation

Musical traditions organize time in diverse ways. West African drumming may layer interlocking cycles; Indian classical music uses tala systems with long, structured cycles; Balkan dances include additive meters such as 7/8 grouped as 2+2+3. Western notation emphasizes bar lines, but not all rhythm is metric.

Beyond music

Poetry uses rhythm through meter, stress and phrasing. The term also describes recurring patterns in architecture, visual design, daily life and biology, including circadian rhythms. In these extended senses, rhythm means patterned recurrence rather than sound alone.

Components and related concepts

Rhythm interacts with melody, harmony, timbre and dynamics but is not identical to any of them. A melody can be recognized when its pitch contour changes if its rhythmic profile is retained, while the same pitches in different rhythms can produce entirely different music.
