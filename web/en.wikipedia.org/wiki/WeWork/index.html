WeWork

WeWork Inc. is an American provider of coworking spaces, including shared offices, private offices, and meeting rooms, founded in 2010 in New York City by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey. It leases large floors of office buildings, subdivides them, and rents them to freelancers, startups, and enterprises on flexible terms.

Growth and IPO failure

Backed heavily by SoftBank, WeWork grew to hundreds of locations worldwide and reached a private valuation of about $47 billion in early 2019. Its planned initial public offering that year collapsed amid scrutiny of its losses, governance, and Neumann's conduct; Neumann stepped down as CEO and the valuation fell sharply. A later public listing was achieved in 2021 through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company.

Bankruptcy and restructuring

WeWork filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in November 2023, citing the downturn in office demand following the COVID-19 pandemic, and emerged from bankruptcy in 2024 with a reduced footprint and new ownership.
