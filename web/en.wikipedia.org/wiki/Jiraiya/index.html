Jiraiya

Jiraiya (自来也) is a fictional character and folk hero of Japanese literature, best known from the tale Jiraiya Gōketsu Monogatari ("The Tale of the Gallant Jiraiya"), serialised between 1839 and 1868. In the story, Jiraiya is a noble outlaw and ninja (in earlier versions a chivalrous robber, or gizoku) who masters toad magic (gama no jutsu) learned from a giant toad sage, and who can summon and ride an enormous toad.

Legend

Jiraiya, heir of a fallen clan of Kyushu, is taught toad magic by the hermit Senso Dojin. He marries Tsunade, a maiden who wields slug magic, and together they oppose their former comrade Orochimaru, who commands serpent magic. In the best-known episodes, Orochimaru poisons the couple, who are saved by a loyal follower; the tale was left unfinished at its final instalment. The trio's toad–slug–snake triangle, derived from a Chinese tale and from a game of mutual strengths (toad defeats slug, slug defeats snake, snake defeats toad), became a staple of Japanese popular culture.

Origins and influence

The name and story draw on the Chinese outlaw figure Ji Leiye ("Jiraiya" in Japanese pronunciation) of a Song-dynasty tale retold in the 19th-century novel Kaikan Kyōki Rōjin Hōshi... The character was popularised by kōdan storytelling, kabuki plays (from 1852), and later novels. In modern media, Jiraiya, Tsunade and Orochimaru appear together as the "Legendary Sannin" in the manga and anime Naruto, and the character has been adapted in film, video games (such as the Ninja Gaiden and Persona series' references) and tokusatsu.
