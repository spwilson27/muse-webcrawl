Marquette Building (Chicago)

Location140 S. Dearborn Street, Chicago, Illinois
Completed1895
ArchitectsHolabird & Roche
StyleChicago School
Height16 storeys
DesignationChicago Landmark (1975); National Historic Landmark (1977)

The Marquette Building is a steel-frame high-rise in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, built in 1895 by the architectural firm Holabird & Roche. It is considered one of the classic examples of the Chicago School of architecture, with its exposed steel frame, large "Chicago windows" (a wide fixed pane flanked by two narrow sashes) and restrained terra-cotta ornament.

History

The building was named for Jacques Marquette, the seventeenth-century French Jesuit missionary and explorer who, with Louis Jolliet, explored the Mississippi River and the Illinois country. Its entrance lobby, atrium and elevator halls feature Tiffany mosaics and bronze relief panels depicting Marquette's expedition.

The Marquette Building served for many years as a hub for Chicago's legal community. It was designated a Chicago Landmark in 1975 and a National Historic Landmark in 1977, and after a period of decline it was restored in the 2000s. The building is frequently cited alongside the Monadnock and Reliance buildings as a model of early skyscraper design.
