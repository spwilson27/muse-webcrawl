Languages of the Philippines

There are some 180 living languages native to the Philippines, almost all belonging to the Philippine branch of the Austronesian language family. The two official languages of the country are Filipino and English. Filipino is the national language, based principally on Tagalog, the language of the capital region, and serves as the standard taught in schools alongside English.

Major languages

According to the Ethnologue and census-based estimates, the most widely spoken native languages include:

  • Tagalog / Filipino — the largest by number of second-language speakers and, as Filipino, the national language.
  • Cebuano — spoken widely in the Visayas and northern Mindanao; historically the language with the most native speakers.
  • Ilocano — dominant in northern Luzon and a lingua franca of the region.
  • Hiligaynon (Ilonggo) — western Visayas.
  • Waray — eastern Visayas.
  • Bikol languages — the Bicol Region of southeastern Luzon.
  • Kapampangan and Pangasinan — central Luzon.
  • Maranao, Maguindanao and Tausug — spoken by Moro groups in Mindanao.

Language policy

The 1987 Constitution designates Filipino and English as official languages and names Spanish and Arabic as languages to be promoted on a voluntary basis. Since 2012 the Department of Education has implemented mother-tongue-based multilingual education, using regional languages as the medium of instruction in the early grades. The Komisyon sa Wikang Filipino is the government body charged with the development of Filipino and the preservation of the country's other languages.

Other languages

Chavacano, a Spanish-based creole spoken mainly in Zamboanga, is the only Spanish-based creole in Asia. Philippine Sign Language is recognized by law. Many smaller indigenous languages, including those of the Negrito groups (such as Aeta and Agta languages), are endangered. Chinese varieties, especially Hokkien, are spoken in the Filipino-Chinese community.
