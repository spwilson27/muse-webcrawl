Handsome Lake (in Seneca, Sganyodaiyo; c. 1735 – 10 August 1815) was a Seneca religious leader of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy). Half-brother of the chief Cornplanter, he became a prophet in 1799 and founded the movement known as the Longhouse Religion, whose teachings are codified in the Gaiwiio, the "Good Message".

Visions and teaching

After years of alcoholism and illness in the devastated aftermath of the American Revolutionary War, Handsome Lake reported a series of visions beginning in June 1799 in which messengers of the Creator instructed him to preach a code combining traditional Haudenosaunee practices and ceremonies with selected values adopted from settlers — including temperance, opposition to land sales and witchcraft accusations, support for education and farming by men, and the preservation of communal land and the ceremonial cycle of thanksgiving feasts.

Legacy

His movement is widely credited with helping arrest the social disintegration of the Seneca and other Haudenosaunee communities in the early 19th century. The Code of Handsome Lake is still recited in longhouses today, and the Longhouse Religion remains a living tradition among the Haudenosaunee in New York and Ontario.
