Sears, Roebuck and Co., commonly known as Sears, is an American chain of department stores founded in 1892 by Richard Warren Sears and Alvah Curtis Roebuck, and reincorporated in 1906 by Sears and Julius Rosenwald. It began as a mail-order catalogue company in Chicago, Illinois, selling watches and jewellery, and grew into the largest retailer in the United States. Its famous general catalogue — the "Big Book" — brought consumer goods to rural America, and the company later sold nearly everything, including houses (the Sears Modern Homes programme), automobiles (the Allstate), and insurance and financial services.

History

Mail-order origins

Richard Sears founded the R.W. Sears Watch Company in Minneapolis in 1886 after buying a shipment of watches a local jeweller had refused. He moved to Chicago, hired watchmaker Alvah Roebuck, and in 1893 the firm became Sears, Roebuck and Co. Its catalogue expanded from watches to a vast range of goods, printed on cheap paper and aimed at farmers who distrusted high-markup country stores. By 1895 the catalogue ran to 532 pages.

Growth and department stores

Julius Rosenwald, who bought into the firm in 1895, rationalised fulfilment with mass-production principles at the huge Chicago mail-order plant. Sears opened its first retail store in 1925 and expanded rapidly after the Second World War, anchoring suburban shopping malls across America. Brands such as Kenmore appliances, Craftsman tools and the DieHard battery became household names. In 1973 the company completed the Sears Tower in Chicago (now Willis Tower), then the world's tallest building.

Decline and bankruptcy

From the 1980s Sears lost ground to discounters such as Walmart and category specialists, and discontinued its general catalogue in 1993. In 2005 it merged with Kmart under Eddie Lampert's Sears Holdings. Years of store closures and asset sales followed, and Sears Holdings filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in October 2018. In 2019 the Sears brand and remaining stores were sold to Transformco; only a small number of stores remain in operation.

Legacy

Sears has been described as the Amazon of its era: its catalogue democratised consumption, helped standardise American consumer credit, and during the Jim Crow era gave Black customers in the South a way to shop free of discriminatory treatment. Its mail-order houses remain cherished architectural artefacts across the United States.
