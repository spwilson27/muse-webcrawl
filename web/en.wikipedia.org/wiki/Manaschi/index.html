Manaschi

A manaschi (Kyrgyz: манасчы) is a traditional Kyrgyz storyteller who performs the Epic of Manas, the great oral epic of the Kyrgyz people. Skilled manaschis memorise and improvise hundreds of thousands of lines of verse, reciting them in a distinctive rhythmic chant accompanied by expressive gestures.

The epic

The Epic of Manas tells of the hero Manas and his descendants, their battles and their efforts to unite the Kyrgyz tribes. Together with its sequels concerning Manas's son Semetey and grandson Seitek, the trilogy in its fullest recorded versions exceeds 500,000 lines — among the longest epic poems in the world, far surpassing the Odyssey and the Mahabharata in length.

Training and performance

Traditionally, a manaschi receives his calling through a dream in which Manas or his companions appear and command him to recite. Apprentices learn by listening to masters and gradually mastering episodes, formulaic passages and the techniques of improvisation, expanding or compressing episodes according to the audience and occasion. Great manaschis such as Sagymbai Orozbakov and Sayakbai Karalaev had their versions recorded and transcribed in the Soviet period.

Cultural significance

The manaschi tradition is central to Kyrgyz national identity. In 2013 the Kyrgyz epic trilogy was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Competitions, festivals and state honours continue to support the art of the manaschis in modern Kyrgyzstan.
