Magnus liber

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Magnus liber organi ("Great Book of Organum") is a compilation of organa — early polyphonic settings of Gregorian chant — associated with the Notre Dame school of Paris in the late 12th and early 13th centuries. According to the English music theorist known as Anonymous IV, the book was compiled by the composer Léonin (Leoninus), described as the "best composer of organum", and later revised and expanded by Pérotin (Perotinus), who shortened many pieces and composed substitute sections (clausulae) and works for three and four voices.

Contents

The collection contains two-voice settings of the solo portions of responsorial chants for the major feasts of the church year, for both the Mass and the Divine Office. Its styles range from organum purum, in which the chant notes are sustained beneath florid melismatic upper voices, to discant passages in modal rhythm where both voices move in measured patterns. Pérotin's great three- and four-voice organa, such as Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes, represent the summit of the style.

Manuscripts

No autograph survives; the repertory is preserved in later manuscript sources, principally the Florence manuscript (Pluteus 29.1) and the two Wolfenbüttel manuscripts (W1 and W2). The Magnus liber is regarded as a landmark in Western music history, both for its scale and because its notation helped drive the development of rhythmic notation.
