The Florida pompano (Trachinotus carolinus) is a species of marine fish in the jack family, Carangidae. It is a popular game fish and food fish along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of the United States, prized for its firm, mild flesh. It is the most commercially valuable of the pompano species.

Description

The Florida pompano has a deep, laterally compressed body with a blunt, rounded head and a deeply forked tail. Its back is silvery green-blue and its flanks are silver, often with golden or yellow tints on the fins and lower body. It lacks the vertical bars of the similar permit. Adults commonly reach 43 cm (17 in) and about 1.4 kg (3 lb), though specimens may approach 64 cm and 3.6 kg.

Distribution and habitat

The species ranges in the western Atlantic from Massachusetts and Bermuda south along the U.S. coast and throughout the Gulf of Mexico to Brazil, though it is uncommon north of the Chesapeake Bay. Adults inhabit coastal waters, surf zones, and inlets over sandy bottoms, while juveniles concentrate in sandy surf along beaches.

Biology

Florida pompano feed on mollusks, crustaceans, and other benthic invertebrates, using their blunt teeth to crush shells. Spawning occurs offshore in spring and summer. The fish are fast-growing and can reach marketable size within a year.

Fisheries

The species supports commercial and recreational fisheries, primarily in Florida. It is caught by hook and line, gill nets, and haul seines. Because of its high market price, the Florida pompano has been a target of marine aquaculture research and limited commercial farming.
