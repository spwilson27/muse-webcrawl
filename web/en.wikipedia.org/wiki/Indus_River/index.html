Indus River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Indus river gorge in the mountains of northern Pakistan, a turquoise river between barren rocky slopes]
The Indus in northern Pakistan.

The Indus is a transboundary river of Asia, about 3,180 km (1,980 mi) long. Rising on the Tibetan Plateau in western Tibet, it flows northwest through Ladakh, then south through Pakistan — the country through which most of its course runs — to empty into the Arabian Sea near Karachi. The Indus and its tributaries water one of the largest irrigation systems in the world and gave their name to India and to the ancient Indus Valley Civilisation.

Course

From its source near Lake Mansarovar, the Indus crosses the Himalaya and Karakoram in deep gorges, then emerges onto the plains of Punjab, where it is joined by the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej. Below their confluence it flows through Sindh to a large delta on the Arabian Sea.

History and waters

The Bronze Age Indus Valley Civilisation (c. 3300–1300 BC), with cities such as Mohenjo-daro and Harappa, flourished along the river. Today the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) between India and Pakistan governs sharing of the system's waters, which are vital to Pakistan's agriculture.
