Quotient ring

Quotient ring
Field: Abstract algebra (ring theory)
Construction: Ring modulo a two-sided ideal
Notation: R / I

In ring theory, a branch of abstract algebra, a quotient ring, also known as a factor ring, difference ring, or residue class ring, is a construction quite similar to the quotient group in group theory and to the quotient space in linear algebra. Starting from a ring R and a two-sided ideal I in R, one forms a new ring, the quotient ring R / I, whose elements are the cosets of I in R.

Construction

The elements of R / I are the cosets a + I = {a + x : x ∈ I}. Addition and multiplication are defined by (a + I) + (b + I) = (a + b) + I and (a + I)(b + I) = ab + I; these are well-defined precisely because I is a two-sided ideal. The natural map R → R / I is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel I, and the first isomorphism theorem states that the image of any homomorphism is isomorphic to a quotient of the domain.

Examples

  • The ring of integers modulo n, Z/nZ, is the quotient of Z by the ideal of multiples of n. It is a field exactly when n is prime.
  • The polynomial ring quotient R[x] / (x² + 1) over the reals is isomorphic to the complex numbers.
  • More generally, if I is a maximal ideal, then R / I is a field; if I is a prime ideal, then R / I is an integral domain.
