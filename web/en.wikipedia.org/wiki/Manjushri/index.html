Manjushri

Manjushri (Sanskrit: मञ्जुश्री, Mañjuśrī) is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism associated with wisdom (prajñā). He is one of the most important figures in the Mahayana pantheon and is regarded as the embodiment of the wisdom of all the Buddhas. In Tibetan Buddhism he is also revered in tantric forms, and in Chinese Buddhism he is one of the Four Great Bodhisattvas, with his pilgrimage centre at Mount Wutai in Shanxi province.

Iconography

Manjushri is typically depicted as a beautiful young prince, symbolising that wisdom is ever fresh. In his right hand he brandishes a flaming sword that cuts through ignorance and duality; in his left hand, held at the heart, he holds the stem of a lotus supporting a volume of the Prajñāpāramitā (Perfection of Wisdom) sutra. He is sometimes shown riding a lion.

In the sutras

Manjushri appears as an interlocutor in many Mahayana sutras, including the Vimalakīrti Sutra, where he debates the lay bodhisattva Vimalakīrti, and the Lotus Sutra. The Avataṃsaka Sutra associates him with Mount Qingliang (Wutai). A sutra attributed to his early cult, the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa, is among the earliest tantric Buddhist texts.

Worship

Manjushri is worshipped across China, Japan (as Monju), Tibet, Nepal and Mongolia. His mantra is oṃ arapacana dhīḥ; the syllables A RA PA CA NA are associated with the insights of the Prajñāpāramitā. In Nepal he is credited in legend with draining the Kathmandu Valley lake by cutting a gorge with his sword.
