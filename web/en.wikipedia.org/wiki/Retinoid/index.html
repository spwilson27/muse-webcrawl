Retinoid

The retinoids are a class of chemical compounds that are vitamers of vitamin A or are chemically related to it. Retinoids are used in medicine where they regulate skin cell growth. They bind to retinoic acid receptors and retinoid X receptors, altering gene expression involved in cell proliferation and differentiation.

Types and generations

Retinoids are classified into generations. First-generation retinoids include retinol, retinal, tretinoin (retinoic acid), isotretinoin, and alitretinoin. Second-generation retinoids include etretinate and its metabolite acitretin. Third-generation retinoids include adapalene, bexarotene, and tazarotene. A fourth-generation agent, trifarotene, is selective for the RAR-γ receptor predominantly found in skin.

Uses

Retinoids are used in the treatment of acne, psoriasis, photoaging, and other skin disorders, and some are used in oncology — tretinoin for acute promyelocytic leukemia and bexarotene for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. Cosmetic products widely use retinyl palmitate and retinol as anti-aging ingredients.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include skin irritation, dryness, and photosensitivity. Systemic retinoids are potent teratogens: isotretinoin use during pregnancy can cause severe birth defects, so it is dispensed under strict pregnancy-prevention programs such as iPLEDGE in the United States. Excessive intake can cause hypervitaminosis A.
