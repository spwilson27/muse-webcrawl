WhatsApp Messenger is a freeware, cross-platform instant-messaging and voice-over-IP service owned by Meta Platforms. Founded in 2009 by Jan Koum and Brian Acton, both former Yahoo! employees, it was acquired by Facebook in 2014 for roughly US$19 billion, as widely reported — one of the largest acquisitions of its kind at the time.

The app lets users send text and voice messages, make voice and video calls, and share images, documents and locations, with end-to-end encryption by default using the Signal protocol. It became the dominant messaging app in much of Europe, Latin America, Africa and South Asia, with billions of users.

History

WhatsApp began as a status-updating app before pivoting to messaging. It grew by being simple, ad-free and cheap compared with SMS, charging a small annual fee in some countries before going fully free in 2016. Business accounts, Communities, Channels and payments in selected countries were added later.

Criticism

WhatsApp has been criticised over misinformation spreading in large groups, a 2021 privacy-policy update controversy that drove some users to rivals such as Signal and Telegram, and tensions with governments over encryption and traceability.
