Grammatical person

In linguistics, grammatical person is the grammatical distinction between deictic references to participant roles in an event: the speaker (first person), the addressee (second person), and others (third person). Person is typically marked on pronouns and, in many languages, through verb agreement.

The three persons

The first person refers to the speaker or a group including the speaker (I, we); the second person refers to the addressee (you); and the third person refers to anyone or anything else (he, she, it, they). Some languages distinguish inclusive and exclusive first-person plural pronouns, depending on whether the addressee is included in "we". A few languages also have a fourth person, used for indefinite or obviative reference.

Agreement

In English, person agreement appears mainly in the present tense of verbs, where the third-person singular takes the suffix -s. Languages such as Spanish, Arabic, and many others have much richer person inflection, allowing the subject pronoun to be omitted. Person interacts with grammatical number and, in some languages, with politeness distinctions, as in the French tu/vous contrast.
