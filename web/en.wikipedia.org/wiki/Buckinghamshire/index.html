Buckinghamshire (abbreviated Bucks) is a ceremonial county in South East England. It is bordered by Greater London to the south-east, Berkshire to the south, Oxfordshire to the west, Northamptonshire to the north, Bedfordshire to the north-east and Hertfordshire to the east. The county town is Aylesbury, and its largest settlement is the new city of Milton Keynes.

Geography

The county is divided by the Chiltern Hills, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty of chalk escarpment and beech woods running from south-west to north-east. North of the hills lies the Vale of Aylesbury, and the River Thames forms much of the southern boundary. The southern part of the county lies within the London commuter belt, reflected in affluent towns such as Beaconsfield, Amersham and Marlow.

History and landmarks

The county takes its name from Buckingham, "the homestead of Bucca's people". Landmarks include Stowe House and its landscape gardens, Waddesdon Manor, Bletchley Park (the wartime codebreaking centre), Chequers (the Prime Minister's country residence) and the Roald Dahl Museum in Great Missenden, where the author lived. Since 2020 the ceremonial county has been administered by Buckinghamshire Council in the south and centre and the separate unitary authority of Milton Keynes City Council in the north-east.
