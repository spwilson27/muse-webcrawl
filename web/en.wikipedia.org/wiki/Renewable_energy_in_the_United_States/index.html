Renewable energy in the United States

Renewable energy in the United States accounted for about 13% of domestically produced energy used for electricity, heating and transport in recent years, and roughly a quarter of U.S. electricity generation by the early 2020s. According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, renewable sources — hydroelectric, wind, solar, geothermal and biomass — have grown rapidly since the 2000s, driven mainly by wind and solar photovoltaic expansion.

Electricity generation

Wind is the largest renewable source of electricity, led by Texas, Iowa, Oklahoma and Kansas. Hydroelectric power, long the dominant renewable, is concentrated in the Pacific Northwest and Tennessee Valley. Utility-scale and rooftop solar have grown fastest in percentage terms, led by California, Texas and Florida. Geothermal generation is centred in California and Nevada.

Policy

Federal support has included the Production Tax Credit for wind and the Investment Tax Credit for solar, together with state renewable portfolio standards. The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 substantially expanded long-term clean-energy incentives. Biofuels, chiefly corn ethanol and renewable diesel, dominate renewable energy use in transportation.
