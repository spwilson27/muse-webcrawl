MuZero

MuZero is a reinforcement learning algorithm developed by DeepMind that achieves superhuman performance in board games and video games without being given the rules of the game. Announced in 2019 and published in Nature in 2020 ("Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model"), MuZero generalises the approach of AlphaZero by learning a model of the environment's dynamics that is useful for planning, rather than relying on a perfect simulator.

Algorithm

MuZero learns three functions: a representation function that encodes the current observation into a hidden state, a dynamics function that predicts the next hidden state and immediate reward after an action, and a prediction function that estimates the policy and value from a hidden state. Because the hidden states need not correspond to actual game positions, the model is trained to predict only the quantities needed for planning: rewards, values and policies. As in AlphaZero, planning is performed with Monte Carlo tree search guided by the learned policy and value.

Results

Trained from scratch with no game rules beyond the legal actions (and, for Atari, the pixels and score), MuZero matched AlphaZero's performance in Go, chess and shogi and set a new state of the art on a benchmark of 57 Atari games. Its ability to learn a model in environments without a known simulator made it a landmark in model-based reinforcement learning, and later work by DeepMind applied related methods to problems such as video compression and data-centre cooling challenges; a 2022 successor, EfficientZero, improved its sample efficiency.
