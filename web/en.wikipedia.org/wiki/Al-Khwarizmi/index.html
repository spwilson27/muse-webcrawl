Al-Khwarizmi

Bornc. 780
Diedc. 850
EraIslamic Golden Age
Known forAlgebra; Hindu–Arabic numerals; the term 'algorithm'
[Image: a medieval manuscript page of Arabic mathematics, with geometric diagrams illustrating the completion of the square]

Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (c. 780 – c. 850) was a Persian mathematician, astronomer and geographer who worked at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad during the Islamic Golden Age. His writings introduced Hindu–Arabic numerals and systematic algebra to the Islamic world and, through Latin translations, to Europe. The word 'algorithm' derives from the Latinised form of his name, Algoritmi, and 'algebra' from al-jabr in the title of his most famous book.

Little is known of his life; his name suggests origins in Khwarizm, a region south of the Aral Sea in present-day Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan.

Works

Around 820 he wrote Al-Kitāb al-mukhtaṣar fī ḥisāb al-jabr wa-l-muqābala ('The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing'), the foundational text of algebra, which solved linear and quadratic equations by systematic, geometric methods and applied them to inheritance, trade and land measurement.

His book on arithmetic explained the Indian decimal place-value system and its numerals; in Latin translation ('Algoritmi de numero Indorum') it helped spread the numerals that became the standard Western digits. He also produced astronomical tables (zij), works on the astrolabe and sundials, and a geography, Kitāb Ṣūrat al-Arḍ, revising Ptolemy's coordinates.

Legacy

Al-Khwārizmī's systematic approach to equations made algebra an independent discipline, distinct from geometry and arithmetic. His name, in the form 'algorithm', came to mean any step-by-step computational procedure — a term now central to Computer science.

A crater on the far side of the Moon is named Al-Khwarizmi in his honour.
