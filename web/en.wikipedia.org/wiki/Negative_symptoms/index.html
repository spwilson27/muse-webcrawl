Negative symptoms

Negative symptoms are a core feature of schizophrenia and other psychotic disorders, referring to functions that are diminished or absent relative to normal behaviour. They are contrasted with positive symptoms such as hallucinations and delusions, which represent an excess or distortion of normal functions.

Domains

Negative symptoms are conventionally grouped into five domains: blunted affect (reduced expression of emotion), alogia (poverty of speech), avolition (diminished motivation and goal-directed activity), anhedonia (reduced capacity for pleasure), and asociality (reduced social interaction and interest in relationships). They are further divided into expressive deficits and deficits of motivation and pleasure.

Primary and secondary

Primary negative symptoms are intrinsic to schizophrenia itself, whereas secondary negative symptoms arise from other causes such as depression, medication side effects, social deprivation, or positive symptoms themselves. Persistent primary negative symptoms are sometimes termed the deficit syndrome.

Treatment

Negative symptoms respond poorly to most antipsychotic medications and are strong predictors of poor functional outcome. Psychosocial interventions, cognitive remediation, and exercise have modest supportive evidence; cariprazine has shown some benefit in trials.
