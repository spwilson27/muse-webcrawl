The national parks of Canada are protected natural spaces throughout the country administered by Parks Canada, a government agency. The system represents the country's major natural regions and protects representative ecosystems, wildlife and landscapes.

History

Canada's first national park began with the reservation of hot springs at Banff in 1885, formalised as Rocky Mountains Park in 1887 — making Canada's park service, founded in 1911, the world's first national parks service. The National Parks Act of 1930 dedicated the parks to the people of Canada "to leave them unimpaired for future generations".

System

The system now includes more than forty national parks and national park reserves, from Pacific Rim on the west coast to Torngat Mountains in Labrador, and from Ivvavik in the Arctic to Point Pelee, the southernmost tip of mainland Canada. Separate designations cover national urban parks, such as Rouge National Urban Park in Toronto, and national marine conservation areas.

Ecological integrity

Since amendments in 2001, maintaining or restoring ecological integrity is the first priority of park management. Parks Canada works increasingly with Indigenous partners, including through Indigenous Guardians programmes and co-managed sites.
