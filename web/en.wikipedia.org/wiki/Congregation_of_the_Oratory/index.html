Congregation of the Oratory

The Congregation of the Oratory (Latin: Congregatio Oratorii Sancti Philippi Nerii) is a Roman Catholic society of apostolic life of priests and lay brothers founded by Saint Philip Neri in Rome in 1575 and approved by Pope Gregory XIII the same year.

History

Philip Neri gathered a community at the church of San Girolamo della Carità, where informal prayer meetings — the "Oratory" — combined preaching, music, and charitable work. The congregation settled at Santa Maria in Vallicella, the Chiesa Nuova, in 1575. Unlike religious orders, Oratorians take no perpetual vows; each house is autonomous, and members are bound by charity rather than by rule of an order.

Spread and notable members

The Oratory spread through Italy and later to France, where Cardinal Pierre de Bérulle founded a distinct French Oratory in 1611. In England, John Henry Newman founded the first English Oratory at Birmingham in 1848. The composer Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina was closely associated with the early Roman Oratory, and the musical form of the oratorio takes its name from the Oratory's prayer hall.

Character

Oratorian spirituality emphasizes prayer, preaching, the sacraments, and pastoral work in urban parishes, with a characteristic emphasis on joy inherited from Philip Neri himself.
