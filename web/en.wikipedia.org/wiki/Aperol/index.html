Aperol is an Italian bitter apéritif made from gentian, rhubarb and cinchona, among other ingredients. It has a bright orange hue and a lightly bitter, citrus-and-herb flavour. Its name derives from apéro, the French slang for apéritif.

History

Aperol was launched in 1919 in Padua by brothers Luigi and Silvio Barbieri, who had inherited their father's liqueur company. It grew steadily in Italy after the Second World War and was acquired by the Campari Group in 2003, after which international sales expanded dramatically.

Aperol Spritz

The drink is best known as the base of the Aperol Spritz, a wine cocktail popularised in north-east Italy and promoted globally from the 2000s: typically three parts prosecco, two parts Aperol and a splash of soda water, served over ice with a slice of orange. The cocktail's success made Aperol one of the world's fastest-growing spirit brands and a staple of summer drinking culture.
