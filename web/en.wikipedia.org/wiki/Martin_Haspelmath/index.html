Martin Haspelmath (born 1963) is a German linguist specialising in linguistic typology, morphology and language change. He worked for many years at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig and has been an influential figure in the development of large cross-linguistic databases and in debates about the foundations of grammatical theory.

Work

Haspelmath's early research concerned the Lezgian language of the Caucasus, on which he published a comprehensive grammar (1993), and the typology of indefinite pronouns (1997), a landmark study mapping the functions of indefinite pronoun series across languages. He has written extensively on grammaticalization, word-class universals, ditransitive constructions, and morphological theory, arguing for functional, usage-based explanations of language structure rather than innate formal universals.

He was a co-editor of the World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS, 2005), a large database of structural properties of the world's languages, and a co-editor of Glottolog, a comprehensive catalogue of the world's languages and language families. He also co-edited the journal and open-access publishing initiatives in linguistics, advocating open science in the field.
