Entrée

An entrée (from French, meaning "entrance") is a dish served before the main course of a meal, or in North American English, the main course itself. In modern French cuisine and in much of the English-speaking world outside North America, an entrée is the first course of a formal meal, preceding the plat principal.

Usage

In the classical French menu, the entrée was a substantial hot dish served after the hors d'oeuvre and soup. In the United States and anglophone Canada, the term shifted during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries to denote the principal dish of a meal, usually meat, fish, or a vegetarian centrepiece accompanied by vegetables and starches.
