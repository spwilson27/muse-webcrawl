Minshuku

A minshuku (Japanese: 民宿) is a family-run guesthouse in Japan, roughly equivalent to a bed and breakfast or small pension in Western countries. Minshuku are typically located in resort areas, fishing villages, mountain districts, and onsen towns, and are usually considerably cheaper than ryokan, the traditional Japanese inns.

Features

Minshuku are often converted family homes or purpose-built annexes in which the proprietors live on the premises. Rooms are typically Japanese-style, with tatami floors and futon bedding laid out at night, though some offer Western-style beds. Bathrooms are usually shared, sometimes with a communal bath. Rates normally include two meals — dinner and breakfast — featuring home cooking and local specialities; in coastal areas this often means fresh seafood, while in mountain areas it may feature river fish and wild vegetables.

Regulation and context

Minshuku are licensed under Japan's Inn and Hotel Business Law and are generally small in scale, with a limited number of guest rooms. They became widespread in the post-war decades as domestic tourism grew, offering affordable lodging near ski resorts, beaches, and hot springs. Compared with ryokan, minshuku tend to be simpler and less formal, with fewer amenities and less elaborate service, but a homelier atmosphere. The corresponding term for simple lodgings at temples and shrines is shukubō.
