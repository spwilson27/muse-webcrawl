Peberholm (Danish for "Pepper Islet") is an artificial island in the Danish part of the Øresund, created as part of the Øresund fixed link between Denmark and Sweden. Its name was chosen as a companion to the natural island immediately to the north, Saltholm ("Salt Islet").

Construction and purpose

The island was built between 1995 and 1999 largely from material dredged from the seabed for the link's construction. Peberholm is about 4 km long and covers roughly 130 hectares. It serves as the crossover point where the Øresund Bridge, arriving from Sweden, meets the Drogden Tunnel, which continues under the sound to Amager near Copenhagen; the island carries about 4 km of motorway and railway between the two.

A tunnel rather than a bridge was chosen for the western section to avoid obstructing air traffic to Copenhagen Airport and to allow unimpeded shipping, making an intermediate artificial island necessary. The island's edges were designed with environmental considerations, and it has since been colonized naturally by plants, insects, birds, and amphibians, which scientists monitor as an unplanned experiment in rewilding; there is no public access.
