Saudeleur dynasty

The Saudeleur dynasty was the first organised ruling dynasty of Pohnpei, in what is now the Federated States of Micronesia, reigning from roughly the 12th or 13th century until about 1628. The Saudeleurs unified the island's clan territories under a single theocratic ruler and governed from Nan Madol, a remarkable city built of stacked basalt logs on nearly one hundred artificial islets in a lagoon off Temwen Island.

Saudeleur dynasty
Capital: Nan Madol, Pohnpei
Period: c. 1180–1628 (approx.)
Religion: Pohnpeian religion centred on the eel god Luhk
Succeeded by: Nahnmwarki system

Founding legend

According to Pohnpeian oral tradition, twin sorcerer brothers, Olisihpa and Olosohpa, arrived by canoe from a distant land (Katau) seeking a place to build an altar to worship their god. On the reef at Temwen they began raising the stone city. When Olisihpa died, Olosohpa became the first Saudeleur — the title meaning "Lord of Deleur", Deleur being an old name for Pohnpei.

Rule and decline

The Saudeleur ruled as priest-king from Nan Madol, demanding tribute of food — famously first fruits and turtles reserved for the ruler — delivered by canoe from across the island. Later Saudeleurs were remembered as increasingly tyrannical; the last, Saudemwohl, offended the thunder god Nahn Sapwe, who sent the warrior-hero Isokelekel to invade. Around 1628 Isokelekel defeated the last Saudeleur at Nan Madol and founded the more decentralised Nahnmwarki system of chiefly titles that endures in Pohnpei today. Nan Madol was gradually abandoned and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
