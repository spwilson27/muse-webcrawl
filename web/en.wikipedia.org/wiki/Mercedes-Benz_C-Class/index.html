The Mercedes-Benz C-Class is a series of compact executive cars produced by Mercedes-Benz Group AG. Introduced in 1993 as the replacement for the 190 (W201) range, the C-Class was the smallest model in the marque's line-up until the W168 A-Class arrived in 1997. It is built at plants in Germany, South Africa, the United States, China, and elsewhere.

Generations

W202 (1993–2000) — the first model officially named C-Class, offered as a saloon and estate. W203 (2000–2007) — added a SportCoupé hatchback. W204 (2007–2014) — a strong seller, with saloon, estate, and coupé bodies. W205 (2014–2021) — aluminium-hybrid construction, plus a cabriolet for the first time. W206 (2021–present) — all models use four-cylinder engines with mild-hybrid or plug-in-hybrid assistance, and styling and technology drawn from the S-Class.

AMG models

High-performance versions have been produced by Mercedes-AMG, from the C 36 AMG and C 43 AMG of the W202 era through the V8-powered C 63 AMG to the current C 63 S E Performance, which pairs a turbocharged four-cylinder with a hybrid system.

Sales

The C-Class has repeatedly been Mercedes-Benz's best-selling model line, with the W204 and W205 generations each selling in the millions worldwide, particularly in Germany, the United States, and China.
