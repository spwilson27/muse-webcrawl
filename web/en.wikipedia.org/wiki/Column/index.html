Column

A column or pillar in architecture and structural engineering is a structural element that transmits, through compression, the weight of the structure above to other structural elements below. In other words, a column is a compression member. Columns are frequently used to support beams or arches on which the upper parts of walls or ceilings rest. A column might also be a decorative element not needed for structural purposes; many columns are "engaged" — attached to and projecting from a wall.

Structure and parts

A classical column consists of a capital at the top, which spreads the load and provides a transition to the entablature; a shaft, often slightly tapered and gently curved (entasis) for optical refinement; and a base at the bottom (absent in the Greek Doric order). Fluting — vertical concave grooves — is a common treatment of the shaft.

The classical orders

  • Doric — the sturdiest and plainest Greek order, with a simple cushion capital and no base.
  • Ionic — slimmer, with a scrolled volute capital and a moulded base.
  • Corinthian — the most ornate, its capital carved with acanthus leaves.
  • Tuscan and Composite — Roman additions to the Greek canon.

Engineering

Long, slender columns fail not by crushing but by buckling, a phenomenon analysed by Euler, whose formula gives the critical load in terms of the column's length, stiffness, and end conditions. Column behaviour depends on slenderness ratio; modern columns are built of reinforced concrete, structural steel, or timber, and may be designed as part of moment frames or braced systems. A row of columns is a colonnade; a freestanding commemorative column (e.g. Nelson's Column, Trajan's Column) serves a monumental function.

History

Stone columns appear in ancient Egyptian architecture (e.g. the hypostyle hall at Karnak) and in Persian palaces such as Persepolis. Greek architects refined them into the orders that Roman builders disseminated, and Renaissance theorists from Alberti to Palladio codified rules revived in neoclassical architecture worldwide.
