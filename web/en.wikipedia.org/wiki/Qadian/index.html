Qadian

Qadian (Punjabi: ਕਾਦੀਆਂ) is a town and municipal council in Gurdaspur district in the Indian state of Punjab. It is famous as the birthplace of Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, the founder of the Ahmadiyya Muslim movement.

Religious significance

Mirza Ghulam Ahmad founded the Ahmadiyya movement in Qadian in 1889. Landmarks include the Aqsa Mosque, the White Minaret (Minaratul Masih), and the founder's grave in the Bahishti Maqbara cemetery. Each year in late December, Ahmadi Muslims gather in Qadian for the Jalsa Salana, an annual convention first held in 1891.

History

The town is said to have been founded in the 16th century by Mirza Hadi Beg, a descendant of Timur and ancestor of Ghulam Ahmad, who named it Islam Pur Qazi Majhi, later shortened to Qadi Majhi and then Qadian. After the Partition of India in 1947, the Ahmadiyya community's headquarters moved to Rabwah in Pakistan, but Qadian remains its spiritual centre in India.

Demographics

At the 2011 census Qadian had a population of roughly 23,000, with a mix of Sikh, Hindu and Muslim residents.
