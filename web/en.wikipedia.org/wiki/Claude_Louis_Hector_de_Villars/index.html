Claude Louis Hector de Villars, Prince of Martigues, Marquis and Duke of Villars (8 May 1653 – 17 June 1734) was a French military commander and the last great general of Louis XIV. He is regarded as one of the most brilliant commanders in French military history, famous for rescuing France from near-defeat in the War of the Spanish Succession with his victory at the Battle of Denain in 1712. In 1733 he received the rare rank of Marshal General of France, previously held only by Turenne.

Early career

Villars was born at Moulins into a noble family. He entered the army young and served in the Franco-Dutch War under Turenne and Condé. He gained a reputation for daring cavalry leadership and also served on diplomatic missions to Vienna, where he met Prince Eugene of Savoy, his future opponent and friend. During the Nine Years' War he served in Germany and Italy.

War of the Spanish Succession

Villars won victories at Friedlingen (1702) and Höchstädt (1703) in Germany, earning his marshal's baton. In 1705 he campaigned successfully along the Moselle. After suppressing the Camisard revolt in the Cévennes, he returned to Flanders and in 1709 fought the Duke of Marlborough at Malplaquet — the bloodiest battle of the war — where, though forced to retreat, he inflicted such heavy casualties that he claimed "one more such victory and the enemy will have no troops left." Wounded in the battle, he returned in 1712 to win the decisive victory of Denain, relieving the pressure on France and enabling the favorable Peace of Utrecht.

Later life

Villars served in the Regency after Louis XIV's death, was elected to the Académie française, and became head of the war council. At the age of eighty he took command in Italy in the War of the Polish Succession and won the campaign of 1733 before dying at Turin in June 1734. His memoirs remain a classic of military literature.
