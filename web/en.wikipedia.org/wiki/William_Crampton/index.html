William Crampton

Born1930, Blackpool, Lancashire, England
DiedJune 2021 (aged 91)
OccupationPhysiotherapist, historian of flags
Known forFounding the Flag Institute; designing the flag of Northumbria

William George Crampton (1930 – June 2021) was a British vexillologist — a scholar of flags — who played a central role in establishing the study of flags in the United Kingdom. He founded the Flag Institute in 1971, the largest national flag organisation in the world, and served as its president for many years.

Career

Crampton worked professionally as a physiotherapist while pursuing his study of flags. He became a leading figure in international vexillology, presiding over the Fédération internationale des associations vexillologiques (FIAV) for a decade, from 1989 to 1999. He wrote and edited numerous publications on flag history and design, including works for the Flag Institute's journal Flagmaster, and advised public bodies on the correct design, registration and flying of flags.

Flag designs and civic heraldry

Crampton helped to promote the registration of local and county flags in Britain. He designed the registered flag of Northumbria, based on the historic banner associated with St Oswald, and encouraged the revival of historic county banners across England. His work contributed to the modern British custom of county flags flown from public buildings.
