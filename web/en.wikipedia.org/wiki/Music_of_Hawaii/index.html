Music of Hawaii

The music of Hawaii includes an array of traditional and popular styles, ranging from native Hawaiian folk music to modern rock and hip hop. Hawaii's musical contributions to the music of the United States outsize its small population: it gave the world the slack-key guitar, the steel guitar, and the modern invention of the ukulele.

A performer playing slack-key guitar, a style developed in Hawaii.

Native Hawaiian music

Traditional Hawaiian music centres on chant (mele) and dance (hula). Mele were used to record genealogy, mythology, and history, accompanied by percussion instruments such as the ipu (gourd drum), pahu (sharkskin drum), and 'uli'uli (feathered gourd rattle). Sacred hula kahiko preserves ancient forms, while hula 'auana is the modern, melodic style accompanied by stringed instruments.

Guitar styles

Hawaiians developed two distinctive guitar techniques. Slack-key (kī hō'alu) involves loosening ("slacking") strings to create open tunings, allowing a self-contained style with bass, rhythm, and melody played together; masters include Gabby Pahinui, Sonny Chillingworth, and Keola Beamer. The steel guitar, invented by Joseph Kekuku in the 1890s, is played horizontally with a metal bar and became a defining sound of country music as well as Hawaiian music.

Ukulele

The ukulele derives from the Portuguese machete and rajão brought by Madeiran immigrants in 1879. It became a symbol of Hawaiian music during the tourism boom of the early 20th century and enjoyed worldwide popularity; modern virtuosos such as Jake Shimabukuro have expanded its repertoire.

Popular music

In the early 20th century, "Hapa haole" songs — Hawaiian-themed songs in English — were popular on the mainland. King Bennie Nawahi, Sol Ho'opi'i, and later Don Ho ("Tiny Bubbles") became stars. The Hawaiian Renaissance of the 1970s revived traditional music through groups such as the Sons of Hawai'i and the Makaha Sons. More recent artists include Israel Kamakawiwo'ole, whose medley "Somewhere Over the Rainbow/What a Wonderful World" became internationally famous, and Bruno Mars, born and raised in Honolulu.
