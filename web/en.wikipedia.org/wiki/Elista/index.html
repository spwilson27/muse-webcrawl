Elista (Russian: Элиста́) is the capital city of the Republic of Kalmykia, Russia. Situated on the Caspian Depression steppe in the south of European Russia, it is the principal city of the Kalmyks, the only region in Europe where Buddhism is the predominant religion. The 2021 census recorded a population of roughly 103,000.

History

Elista was founded in 1865 as a small settlement. It took its name from the Kalmyk word for the sandy slope above the Elista river valley. In 1930 it received town status and became the capital of the Kalmyk Autonomous Oblast. In 1943 the entire Kalmyk population was deported to Siberia by the Soviet government, and the town was renamed Stepnoy; it regained its former status and name in 1957 when the Kalmyks were permitted to return.

Culture and sights

The city has been strongly shaped by its former president Kirsan Ilyumzhinov, a chess enthusiast who built Chess City, a complex that hosted the 1998 Chess Olympiad and several FIDE world championship matches. Chess is a compulsory subject in Kalmyk schools. The Golden Abode of the Buddha Shakyamuni, opened in 2005, is one of the largest Buddhist temples in Europe. Numerous statues and monuments celebrate the Kalmyk epic hero Dzhangar.

Climate

Elista has a humid continental climate with hot, dry summers and cold winters, characteristic of the Eurasian steppe. Temperatures can exceed 40 °C in summer and fall well below freezing in winter, with strong winds common year-round.
