Pituitary gland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pituitary gland
Location: Base of the brain, in the sella turcica
Size: About that of a pea (~0.5 g)
Controls: Growth, thyroid, adrenals, gonads, lactation

The pituitary gland is a pea-sized endocrine gland at the base of the brain, suspended from the hypothalamus by a stalk and seated in a bony saddle of the sphenoid bone. Though tiny, it is called the "master gland" because its hormones direct most other glands of the endocrine system.

Lobes and hormones

The anterior pituitary synthesises and releases growth hormone, prolactin, ACTH (driving the adrenals), TSH (driving the thyroid), and the gonadotropins LH and FSH — each under the command of releasing hormones from the hypothalamus and the restraint of negative feedback. Dopamine from the hypothalamus tonically suppresses prolactin. The posterior pituitary stores and releases two hypothalamic hormones: oxytocin (childbirth, lactation, bonding) and vasopressin (water balance).

Pituitary tumours are usually benign but consequential — excess growth hormone causes gigantism or acromegaly; prolactinomas are the commonest functioning tumours — and their position beside the optic chiasm makes visual-field loss a classic presenting sign.
