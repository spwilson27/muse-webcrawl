The Parliament of Wallonia (French: Parlement de Wallonie) is the legislative body of Wallonia, the predominantly French-speaking southern region of Belgium. It has 75 members, elected for five-year terms by proportional representation in multi-member constituencies across the region's five provinces. It sits in the Hospice Saint-Gilles building in Namur, the regional capital, on the banks of the Meuse.

The Hospice Saint-Gilles in Namur, seat of the Parliament of Wallonia.

History

Belgium's federalisation created the Walloon Region through the state reforms of 1970, 1980, and 1988–93. A Walloon Regional Council was created in 1980, initially composed of Walloon members of the national parliament; from the regional elections of 21 May 1995 its members have been directly elected, and it took the name Parliament of Wallonia. (An informal Walloon assembly had met as early as 1912, under Jules Destrée, symbolically asserting Walloon identity.)

Powers

As the legislature of a federated region, the Parliament legislates by decree (décret) in the region's competences, which include economic policy, employment, transport and mobility, agriculture, environment, energy, housing, land-use planning, and local government supervision. It elects and oversees the Government of Wallonia, headed by a Minister-President. The Parliament also includes a small number of members from the German-speaking Community area; questions specific to the French Community (education and culture) are handled by the separate Parliament of the French Community, whose membership overlaps with Wallonia's.
