Sai (weapon)

The sai (Japanese: 釵) is a traditional Okinawan melee weapon. It consists of a long, pointed metal prong with two shorter upturned prongs (yoku) projecting from the handle, forming a three-pronged, trident-like implement. Despite its appearance it is typically blunt, and it is used for striking, blocking, trapping and disarming rather than for cutting.

History

The sai is believed to have developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom, possibly adapted from agricultural tools or from similar weapons found elsewhere in Asia, such as the Chinese jian-like hairpin weapons and the Indonesian chabang. In Okinawa it became associated with local law-enforcement officials, who are said to have carried sai to restrain suspects and control crowds. With the spread of karate in the twentieth century, sai training became a standard part of Okinawan kobudō and was exported worldwide.

Technique

Sai are normally used in pairs, one in each hand, with a third sometimes tucked into the belt as a reserve or throwing weapon. The practitioner grips the handle so that the main prong extends along the forearm for defensive blocks, or flips the weapon outward — a movement called nukisashi — to strike or thrust with the point. The side prongs can trap an opponent's blade or limb, enabling locks, throws and disarms. Forms (kata) for the sai preserve these combinations in solo practice.

In popular culture

The sai is familiar to Western audiences as the signature weapon of Raphael of the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles and of Elektra in Marvel Comics, depictions that popularised (and somewhat distorted) its use as a pair of hand-held daggers.
