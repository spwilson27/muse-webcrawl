Peng Dehuai

Peng Dehuai (simplified Chinese: 彭德怀; traditional Chinese: 彭德懷; pinyin: Péng Déhuái)
Born: 24 October 1898, Xiangtan, Hunan, Qing Empire
Died: 29 November 1974 (aged 76), Beijing, China
Rank: Marshal of the People's Republic of China

Peng Dehuai, Marshal of the People's Republic of China

Peng Dehuai (24 October 1898 – 29 November 1974) was a Chinese military leader and politician, one of the Ten Marshals of the People's Liberation Army (PLA). He is best known internationally as the commander of the Chinese People's Volunteer Army during the Korean War, and domestically for his outspoken criticism of Mao Zedong's Great Leap Forward at the 1959 Lushan Conference, which led to his political downfall.

Early life and military career

Peng was born into a poor peasant family in Xiangtan County, Hunan. After a brief period as a soldier in a Hunan warlord army, he joined the Kuomintang's National Revolutionary Army and participated in the Northern Expedition. In 1928 he joined the Chinese Communist Party and led the Pingjiang Uprising, establishing a Red Army unit in Hunan. Peng became one of the most capable field commanders of the Red Army during the Chinese Civil War and the Second Sino-Japanese War, commanding forces in the Hundred Regiments Offensive of 1940.

People's Republic of China

After the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, Peng was appointed commander of the People's Volunteer Army in October 1950, leading Chinese forces in the Korean War against United Nations forces. In 1954 he became Minister of National Defense and undertook a programme of professionalising and modernising the PLA along Soviet lines. In 1955 he was awarded the rank of Marshal.

Lushan Conference and downfall

At the Lushan Conference in July 1959, Peng wrote a private letter to Mao criticising the excesses of the Great Leap Forward, including unrealistic production targets and the hardships imposed on the peasantry. Mao interpreted the letter as a personal attack and orchestrated Peng's dismissal; Peng was replaced as defense minister by Lin Biao and placed under house arrest. During the Cultural Revolution he was publicly humiliated, beaten by Red Guards, and subjected to repeated struggle sessions. He died in 1974, reportedly of tuberculosis, without medical care and in obscurity. Peng was posthumously rehabilitated in 1978.
