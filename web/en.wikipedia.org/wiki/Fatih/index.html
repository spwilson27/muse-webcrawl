Fatih (Turkish pronunciation: [ˈfaːtih]) is a municipality and district of Istanbul Province, Turkey. It occupies the peninsula that formed the historic core of Constantinople, bounded by the Golden Horn to the north, the Bosphorus to the east, and the Sea of Marmara to the south, and enclosed on the west by the Theodosian Walls.

Name and history

The district is named after the Ottoman sultan Mehmed the Conqueror (Turkish: Fatih Sultan Mehmet), who conquered Constantinople in 1453. The Fatih Mosque complex, built on his orders on the site of the Byzantine Church of the Holy Apostles, gave its name to the surrounding quarter.

Landmarks

Fatih contains many of Istanbul's most famous monuments, including the Hagia Sophia, Topkapı Palace, the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque), the Grand Bazaar, the Süleymaniye Mosque, the Chora Church, and the Valens Aqueduct. Much of the district is within the Historic Areas of Istanbul UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Demographics

Once the centre of Ottoman Istanbul, Fatih's population declined in the late 20th century as wealthier residents moved to newer districts, though it remains a densely populated and commercially important area, with a large university and hospital presence.
