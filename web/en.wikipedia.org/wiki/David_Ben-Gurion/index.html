David Ben-Gurion

David Ben-Gurion (born David Grün; 16 October 1886 – 1 December 1973) was the primary national founder of the State of Israel and its first prime minister. A leader of the Zionist movement and the Jewish community (Yishuv) in Mandatory Palestine, he proclaimed Israel's independence on 14 May 1948.

David Ben-Gurion
Born: 16 October 1886, Płońsk, Congress Poland
Died: 1 December 1973, Ramat Gan, Israel
Office: Prime Minister of Israel (1948–1953, 1955–1963)
Party: Mapai
Known for: Israeli Declaration of Independence

Early life

Born in Płońsk, then in Russian-ruled Poland, he immigrated to Ottoman Palestine in 1906, worked as a farm labourer, and was expelled during the First World War. He helped organise the Jewish Legion and, after returning, led the Histadrut trade union federation (from 1921) and the Jewish Agency (from 1935).

Founding of Israel

Ben-Gurion accepted the 1947 UN Partition Plan, proclaimed independence in 1948, and led Israel through the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, unifying militias into the Israel Defense Forces. As prime minister and defence minister he oversaw mass immigration, austerity, state-building, German reparations, and the 1956 Suez Crisis campaign alongside Britain and France.

Later life

He retired in 1953 to Kibbutz Sde Boker in the Negev—symbolising his vision of developing the desert—returned to office in 1955, and finally retired in 1963. He later split from Mapai to form Rafi. He died in 1973; Ben Gurion Airport and Ben-Gurion University bear his name.
