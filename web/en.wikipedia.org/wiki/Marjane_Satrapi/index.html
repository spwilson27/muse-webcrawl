Marjane Satrapi (born 22 November 1969) is an Iranian-born French graphic novelist, cartoonist, illustrator, film director and children's book author, best known for the autobiographical graphic novel Persepolis and its animated film adaptation.

Early life

Satrapi was born in Rasht and grew up in Tehran in a politically engaged, middle-class family descended from Nasser al-Din Shah, the Qajar ruler of Persia. Her childhood coincided with the 1979 Islamic Revolution and the Iran–Iraq War, experiences that form the core of her memoir work. At fourteen, fearing for her safety, her parents sent her to live in Vienna, Austria, where she attended the Lycée Français; after illness, heartbreak and a period of homelessness she returned to Iran, later leaving again for France to study decorative arts in Strasbourg.

Career

In Paris Satrapi was introduced by cartoonist David B. to the artists of the independent publisher L'Association. Her four-volume Persepolis (2000–2003) recounted her youth in Iran and exile in Europe, in a stark black-and-white style influenced by expressionist woodcuts. It won international acclaim and was translated into numerous languages. Her later books include Embroideries and Chicken with Plums (2004).

Satrapi co-directed the animated film Persepolis (2007) with Vincent Paronnaud; it shared the Jury Prize at Cannes and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. She has since directed live-action films including Chicken with Plums (2011), The Voices (2014) and Radioactive (2019), a biopic of Marie Curie.
