2000 Baia Mare cyanide spill

The 2000 Baia Mare cyanide spill was a leak of cyanide-contaminated water near Baia Mare, Romania, on 30 January 2000. It has been called the worst environmental disaster in Europe since Chernobyl.

Event

Following heavy rain and melting snow, a dam at the Aurul SA gold-mining tailings pond burst, releasing about 100,000 cubic metres of cyanide-laden water into the Lăpuș, Someș, Tisza and ultimately the Danube river systems. Mass fish kills were reported in Romania, Hungary and Yugoslavia, and drinking water supplies were affected.

Aftermath

The disaster led to tighter European regulations on mining waste, culminating in the EU Mining Waste Directive of 2006.
