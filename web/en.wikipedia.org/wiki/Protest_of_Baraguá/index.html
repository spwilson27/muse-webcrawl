Protest of Baraguá

Date: 15 March 1878
Place: Baraguá, Oriente Province, Cuba
Leader: Antonio Maceo
Context: End of the Ten Years' War

The Protest of Baraguá (Spanish: Protesta de Baraguá) was a historic act of defiance by Cuban independence forces during the Ten Years' War against Spain. On 15 March 1878, General Antonio Maceo met the Spanish captain-general Arsenio Martínez Campos at Mangos de Baraguá and rejected the Pact of Zanjón, the peace agreement signed a month earlier that ended the war without granting Cuban independence or full abolition of slavery.

The meeting

Maceo declared that the pact did not fulfil the aims for which the war had been fought and announced that hostilities would resume on 23 March. His stance rested on the twin goals of independence and the abolition of slavery, neither of which Zanjón secured.

Outcome and legacy

Unable to sustain the fight against a reinforced Spanish army, Maceo left Cuba in May 1878 and resistance collapsed, but the protest became a founding symbol of uncompromising Cuban patriotism, celebrated in Cuban historiography and invoked during later independence struggles.
