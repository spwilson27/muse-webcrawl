Viburnum Trend

The Viburnum Trend is a geological structure in the U.S. state of Missouri, part of the Southeast Missouri Lead District, one of the world's great lead-mining regions. The trend is a narrow, roughly north–south belt of lead–zinc mineralisation in Cambrian sedimentary rocks on the flank of the St. Francois Mountains, discovered in 1960.

Mine headframe in the Viburnum Trend, Missouri's "New Lead Belt".

Geology

The ore deposits of the Viburnum Trend are Mississippi Valley-type deposits hosted mainly in the Bonneterre Formation, a Cambrian dolomite. Mineralisation is localised along a buried Precambrian ridge, where lead (galena), zinc (sphalerite), and smaller amounts of copper, silver, and cobalt were deposited from brines. The trend extends for roughly 45 miles (70 km) through Iron, Reynolds, Shannon, and Washington counties.

Mining history

Exploration drilling by the St. Joseph Lead Company (St. Joe) discovered the first orebody near Viburnum in 1960 — the town itself was founded by the company to house miners. Because the older Old Lead Belt around Flat River was becoming depleted, the Viburnum Trend was developed as the "New Lead Belt", with a series of deep underground mines and mills operated principally by St. Joe and later by the Doe Run Company. At its height the district made Missouri the leading lead-producing state in the United States and supplied a large share of the world's lead.
