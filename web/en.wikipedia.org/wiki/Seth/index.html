Seth

Seth (Egyptian: Swtḫ, also rendered Set, Sutekh, or Setesh) is a god of deserts, storms, disorder, violence and foreigners in ancient Egyptian religion. He is one of the five children of Geb and Nut and a member of the Ennead of Heliopolis.

Iconography

Seth is usually depicted as a man with the head of the enigmatic "Set animal" or sha: a creature with a curved snout, tall squared ears and a forked tail, which may be a composite or stylised animal not corresponding to any known species. He carries the was-sceptre, combining its forked base with his own imagery.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, Seth murders and dismembers his brother Osiris, usurping the throne before being opposed by Osiris's son Horus. The long struggle between Horus and Seth, recounted in texts such as The Contendings of Horus and Seth, ends with Horus's triumph and the restoration of legitimate kingship, though Seth retains a positive role: he stands at the prow of Ra's solar barque, spearing the serpent Apophis to protect the sun god on his nightly journey.

History of the cult

Seth was an important god in early Egypt, patron of Upper Egyptian Ombos and of the Hyksos capital Avaris, where he was identified with the Canaanite storm god Baal. He was honoured by several Ramesside pharaohs — the names Seti and Setnakhte derive from his — but in the first millennium BC he was increasingly demonised because of his association with foreign invaders and the murder of Osiris, and his images were defaced or reinterpreted in some temples.
