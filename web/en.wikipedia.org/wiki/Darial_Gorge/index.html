Darial Gorge

The Darial Gorge (Georgian: დარიალის ხეობა, Darielis kheoba; Russian: Дарьяльское ущелье) is a river gorge in the Greater Caucasus on the border between Russia and Georgia. Cut by the Terek River east of Mount Kazbek, it stretches for about 13 kilometres (8 miles) and has steep granite walls rising as much as 1,800 metres (5,900 ft).

History

The gorge is one of only two crossings of the Greater Caucasus range — the other being the Derbent passage on the Caspian — and has been fortified since antiquity. Known in classical sources as the Caucasian Gates or Gates of Alexander, and in Arabic as Bab al-Lan ("Gate of the Alans"), it guarded the route between the North Caucasus steppe and Transcaucasia. A fortress at its entrance, associated in legend with Queen Tamar, controlled passage for centuries.

Modern times

The Georgian Military Road runs through the gorge, linking Vladikavkaz in Russia with Tbilisi in Georgia; the border crossing at Dariali/Larsi is the only legal land crossing between the two countries. The gorge inspired Russian Romantic writers, notably Pushkin and Lermontov, who set his poem The Demon among the peaks above it.
