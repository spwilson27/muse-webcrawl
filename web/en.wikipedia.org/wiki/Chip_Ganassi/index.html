Chip Ganassi

Ganassi at Indianapolis Motor Speedway

Floyd "Chip" Ganassi Jr. (born May 24, 1958) is an American businessman and motorsports team owner, and a former racing driver. He is the owner of Chip Ganassi Racing, one of the most successful teams in American open-wheel racing, with multiple Indianapolis 500 victories and IndyCar Series championships, as well as wins in NASCAR and sports car racing, including the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 24 Hours of Le Mans (in class).

Racing career

Born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, Ganassi competed in CART Indy car racing during the 1980s, making five starts in the Indianapolis 500 with a best finish of eighth in 1983. His driving career was curtailed by a serious crash at Michigan International Speedway in 1984 that left him with severe injuries and burns. He earned a degree in finance from Duquesne University and worked in his family's businesses before moving into team ownership.

Team ownership

Ganassi founded his team in 1990 in partnership with Pat Patrick, then took sole ownership the following year. Chip Ganassi Racing won four consecutive CART championships from 1996 to 1999 with Jimmy Vasser, Alex Zanardi (twice), and Juan Pablo Montoya, and later dominated the IndyCar Series with Scott Dixon, Dario Franchitti, and Álex Palou. His teams have won the Indianapolis 500 multiple times, the Daytona 500, the Brickyard 400, and numerous sports car titles. Ganassi is the only team owner to win the "big three" of American racing marquee events and major endurance races across disciplines in a single stretch, and he was inducted into the Motorsports Hall of Fame of America.
