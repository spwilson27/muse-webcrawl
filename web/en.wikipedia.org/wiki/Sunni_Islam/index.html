Sunni Islam is the largest branch of Islam, followed by roughly nine in ten of the world's Muslims. Sunnis hold that the Muslim community rightly chose Abu Bakr as the Prophet Muhammad's successor (caliph) by consensus, and they ground law and doctrine in the Quran and the Prophet's sunnah as preserved in hadith collections. Four legal schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) coexist as equally valid interpretations.

Diversity

'Sunni' covers enormous diversity — from Sufi orders to scripturalist Salafi movements — united by the six articles of faith and, traditionally, deference to scholarly consensus and the caliphate's historical legitimacy. The Sunni–Shia split began as a succession dispute in 632 and hardened over centuries of theology and politics, though on most daily practice the branches closely resemble each other.
