Konnakol

Konnakol
Tamilகொன்னக்கோல்
TraditionCarnatic music (South India)
RelatedSolkattu, mridangam, tala

Konnakol (Tamil: கொன்னக்கோல்) is the art of performing percussion syllables vocally in South Indian Carnatic music. It is the spoken form of solkattu, the system of mnemonic syllables (such as tha, ki, ta, thom) that represent the strokes and sounds of drums, principally the mridangam.

Practice

Konnakol performers recite rhythmic patterns of great complexity at high speed while keeping the tala (metre) with hand gestures, counting out cycles and subdivisions. It serves both as a performance art in its own right and as the main pedagogical and compositional tool by which percussionists learn, notate, and communicate rhythms. Master performers can improvise long compositions involving changing metres, cross-rhythms, and intricate mathematical patterns.

Influence

Konnakol has attracted international attention through performers such as V. Selvaganesh and through collaborations with Western jazz and fusion musicians; John McLaughlin's bands Shakti and later groups studied and performed Carnatic rhythm using konnakol. Its syllable-counting method has influenced drummers and composers far beyond South India.
