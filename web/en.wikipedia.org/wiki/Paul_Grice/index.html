Paul Grice

Herbert Paul Grice (13 March 1913 – 28 August 1988) was a British philosopher of language, best known for his theory of conversational implicature and the cooperative principle, which became foundational to modern pragmatics.

Life

Grice was educated at Corpus Christi College, Oxford, and taught at Oxford for most of his career, where he was associated with ordinary language philosophy and the play group discussions with J. L. Austin and others. In 1967 he moved to the University of California, Berkeley, where he taught until shortly before his death. Much of his work was published late or posthumously in Studies in the Way of Words (1989).

Ideas

In his 1967 William James lectures, "Logic and Conversation", Grice proposed that conversation is governed by a cooperative principle and four maxims — quantity, quality, relation and manner. Speakers who appear to flout a maxim lead hearers to infer an implicature: a meaning conveyed beyond what is literally said. His earlier paper "Meaning" (1957) analysed meaning in terms of speakers' intentions to produce effects in an audience by means of recognition of that intention, distinguishing natural from non-natural meaning.
