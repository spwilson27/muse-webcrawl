Substituted methylenedioxyphenethylamine

The substituted methylenedioxyphenethylamines (MDxx) are a chemical class of psychoactive compounds derived from phenethylamine by the addition of a methylenedioxy group (–O–CH2–O–) attached to the aromatic ring, forming a benzodioxole ring system, together with various other substitutions. The best-known member is MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine), commonly called ecstasy.

Chemistry

The parent structure combines the phenethylamine backbone with a 3,4-methylenedioxy substitution on the phenyl ring. Further variation occurs at the nitrogen (e.g., N-methyl in MDMA, N-ethyl in MDEA), at the alpha carbon of the side chain (amphetamine-type homologues such as MDMA versus the phenethylamine-type compounds such as MDPEA), and by additional ring substituents. Shorthand names follow the pattern MDxx, where the variable letters encode the substituents.

Pharmacology

Most compounds in this class act on monoamine neurotransmitter systems. MDMA and its close analogues are primarily serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine releasers and reuptake inhibitors, producing stimulant and entactogenic effects. Other members differ: some are predominantly stimulants, some have psychedelic activity mediated by serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, and heavier alpha-substitutions or extended chains generally reduce potency. Several members were first described in the work of Alexander Shulgin and are documented in his book PiHKAL.

Examples

  • MDMA — 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methylamphetamine
  • MDA — 3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine
  • MDEA — 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-ethylamphetamine
  • MBDB — N-methyl-1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-2-butanamine
  • MDPEA — 3,4-methylenedioxyphenethylamine itself, largely inactive orally

Legal status

Many substituted methylenedioxyphenethylamines are controlled substances in numerous jurisdictions, and analogue legislation in some countries extends control to related structures not explicitly listed.
