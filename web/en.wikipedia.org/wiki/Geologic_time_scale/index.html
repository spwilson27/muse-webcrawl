Geologic time scale

The geologic time scale divides Earth's 4.54-billion-year history into eons, eras, periods, epochs and ages, based on rock strata and major events such as mass extinctions. It is the calendar of Palaeontology and geology.

Structure

The Phanerozoic eon (‘visible life’, 541 million years ago to present) splits into the Palaeozoic, Mesozoic and Cenozoic eras; its periods include the Cambrian, when animal phyla appear (see Burgess Shale), the Jurassic and Cretaceous of dinosaur fame, and the current Quaternary.

Earlier eons (Hadean, Archaean, Proterozoic) cover Earth's formation, the origin of life (Abiogenesis), and the rise of oxygen and complex cells.

Dating

Boundaries are defined by global marker sections (GSSPs, ‘golden spikes’) and dated radiometrically. Whether the Anthropocene is a formal epoch remains under debate.
