Hong Kong Sevens

The Hong Kong Sevens is a rugby sevens tournament held annually in Hong Kong and considered the premier event of the World Rugby Sevens Series. Founded in 1976, it is one of the oldest and most famous rugby sevens tournaments in the world, traditionally held over a weekend in late March or early April.

History

The tournament began in 1976 at the Hong Kong Football Club with twelve teams. It later moved to the Government Stadium and, in 1994, to the rebuilt Hong Kong Stadium in So Kon Po, with a capacity of 40,000. From 2025, the tournament moved to the new Kai Tak Sports Park.

Format

Since joining the World Series in 1999–2000, the event has featured more teams (28 for many years) than other series legs, with pool winners contesting the Cup, and lesser trophies — Plate, Bowl and Shield — for other finishers. Fiji and New Zealand have been the most successful teams historically, with Fiji famed for its passionate travelling support.

Atmosphere

The Hong Kong Sevens is famous for its carnival atmosphere, elaborate fancy dress — especially in the South Stand — and for attracting overseas visitors as the centrepiece of "Rugby Week" in the city.
