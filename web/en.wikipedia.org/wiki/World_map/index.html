Overview

A world map is a map of most or all of Earth's surface on a single sheet — the most demanding task in cartography, because a sphere cannot be flattened without the distortion every map projection must somehow distribute. The Babylonian Imago Mundi of about the sixth century BC is the oldest known attempt; Greek maps culminated in Ptolemy's world picture, and the modern outline of the continents was complete by the eighteenth century, once the Pacific coasts and Australia's shores were charted and Antarctica sighted.

Even so familiar an object hides choices: most world maps centre the Atlantic so the Americas and Europe–Africa face each other across the middle, while Pacific-centred maps, common in East Asia and Oceania, centre the world's largest ocean instead. Which continent sits in the middle is convention, not geography.
