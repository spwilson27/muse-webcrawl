World Community Grid

The World Community Grid (WCG) is a public computing grid network that uses the idle computing time of volunteers' computers to perform calculations for scientific research projects. It was launched on November 16, 2004, by IBM in collaboration with the United Nations Foundation and other partners. The grid runs on the BOINC platform, and since 2021 its operations have been run by the Krembil Research Institute at the University Health Network in Toronto, Canada, after IBM donated the platform.

History

IBM launched the World Community Grid in 2004 as a philanthropic initiative, initially using the United Devices grid platform before migrating to BOINC in 2007, which allowed the grid to run on Linux and macOS in addition to Windows. Volunteers download a small program that downloads data units from project servers, computes results during idle time, and returns them. By its twentieth anniversary the grid had attracted millions of registered devices and the equivalent of millions of years of computing time.

In September 2021 IBM announced it would donate the World Community Grid to the Krembil Research Institute, part of Toronto's University Health Network, which relaunched the grid in 2022 after a migration of the platform's infrastructure.

Projects

Research projects hosted on the grid have included work on cancer markers (Mapping Cancer Markers), tuberculosis drug discovery, AIDS research (FightAIDS@Home), clean energy, clean water, Ebola virus drug discovery, Zika virus research during the 2016 outbreak, and COVID-19 protein research through the OpenPandemics project run with Scripps Research. The Africa Rainfall Project, run with Delft University of Technology and the Trans-African Hydro-Meteorological Observatory, used weather-model calculations to simulate rainfall in sub-Saharan Africa.

Projects are vetted by an advisory board and must produce results that are placed in the public domain.
