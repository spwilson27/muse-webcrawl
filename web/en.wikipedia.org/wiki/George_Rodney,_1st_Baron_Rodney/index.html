George Rodney, 1st Baron Rodney

George Brydges Rodney, 1st Baron Rodney KB (bap. 13 February 1718 – 24 May 1792) was a British naval officer and admiral.

Born: Walton-on-Thames, Surrey
Died: Hanover Square, London
Service: Royal Navy
Known for: Victory at the Battle of the Saintes (1782)

George Brydges Rodney, 1st Baron Rodney was a British naval officer of the eighteenth century, celebrated for his victory over the French fleet at the Battle of the Saintes in 1782, which restored British naval supremacy in the Caribbean during the American Revolutionary War.

Early career

Rodney entered the Royal Navy at fourteen and rose through patronage and ability, becoming a post-captain in 1742. He served in the Western Approaches, in Newfoundland, and in the Seven Years' War, taking part in the capture of Louisbourg (1758) and the conquest of Martinique (1762). Promoted rear-admiral in 1759, his bombardment of Le Havre destroyed invasion barges assembled against England.

American War

After a period dogged by debts from electioneering and gambling, which forced him to live in Paris, Rodney was appointed commander-in-chief in the Leeward Islands in 1779. En route he won the "Moonlight Battle" of January 1780 against a Spanish squadron off Cape St Vincent and relieved Gibraltar. In the Caribbean he captured the Dutch island of St Eustatius in 1781, seizing its vast stores — a prize that brought him lawsuits and controversy.

Battle of the Saintes

On 12 April 1782, Rodney's fleet decisively defeated the Comte de Grasse off the Îles des Saintes between Dominica and Guadeloupe, capturing five French ships of the line including the flagship Ville de Paris. His fleet famously broke the French line — whether by Rodney's order or by chance remains debated. The victory frustrated Franco-Spanish plans to invade Jamaica and strengthened Britain's hand at the peace negotiations. Rodney was raised to the peerage as Baron Rodney and awarded a pension of £2,000 a year.

Death

Rodney died in London in 1792. The Rodney class of battleships and several Royal Navy ships (HMS Rodney) commemorate his name.
