Alvars

The Alvars (Tamil: ஆழ்வார், Āḻvār, "those immersed [in God]") were Tamil poet-saints of South India who espoused bhakti (devotion) to Vishnu and his avatars, especially Krishna. Traditionally twelve in number, they lived between roughly the 6th and 9th centuries CE and are regarded as the earliest exemplars of the Vaishnava bhakti movement. Their Tamil hymns — some 4,000 verses — were collected by Nathamuni in the 10th century as the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, which is often described as the "Tamil Veda" and is still recited in Sri Vaishnava temples.

The twelve Alvars

The twelve Alvars are Poigai Alvar, Bhoothath Alvar, Pey Alvar, Thirumalisai Alvar, Nammalvar, Madhurakavi Alvar, Kulasekara Alvar, Periyalvar, Andal, Thondaradippodi Alvar, Thiruppan Alvar, and Thirumangai Alvar. Nammalvar of Thirukkurugur is considered the foremost; his Thiruvaymoli is the most revered portion of the canon. Andal, the only woman among them, is venerated as a goddess in her own right and is the author of the Thiruppavai, sung during the month of Margazhi.

Legacy

Singing the Alvars' hymns became central to temple worship and to the philosophy of Sri Vaishnavism, systematised later by Ramanuja. Alongside the Shaivite Nayanars, the Alvars helped transform Tamil devotional religion and spread bhakti across the subcontinent.
