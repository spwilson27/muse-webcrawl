William Ronald Reid (January 12, 1920 – March 13, 1998) was a Canadian artist of Haida heritage whose work — jewellery, sculpture and carving — was instrumental in reviving and renewing Haida art and Northwest Coast Indigenous art more broadly.

Early life

Reid was born in Victoria, British Columbia, to an American father of Scottish-German descent and a Haida mother. He worked as a radio announcer for the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation in Toronto and Vancouver, and it was while preparing a documentary on Haida Gwaii that he reconnected with his mother's people and their artistic traditions. He apprenticed briefly in jewellery-making and studied surviving Haida works, particularly those of his great-granduncle Charles Edenshaw.

Career

Reid produced celebrated works in gold, silver, argillite and wood, and later in monumental bronze, including the model poles at the University of British Columbia's Museum of Anthropology and the canoe Lootaas. His best-known sculpture, The Spirit of Haida Gwaii (1991), depicts a canoe filled with figures from Haida mythology — Raven, Bear Mother, Frog and others — and appeared on the Canadian twenty-dollar bill from 2004 to 2012. The original bronze casts stand at the Canadian Museum of History and the Canadian Embassy in Washington, D.C.

Legacy

Reid was made an Officer of the Order of Canada and received honorary degrees from several universities. The Bill Reid Gallery of Northwest Coast Art opened in Vancouver in 2008. He died in 1998 after a long struggle with Parkinson's disease.
