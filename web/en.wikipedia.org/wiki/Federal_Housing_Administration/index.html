Federal Housing Administration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Federal Housing Administration (FHA) is a United States government agency founded by President Franklin Delano Roosevelt, established in 1934 as part of the New Deal. It insures mortgages made by private lenders for single-family and multifamily homes, and is the largest insurer of mortgages in the world, having insured tens of millions of properties since its creation. The FHA is a wholly owned government corporation overseen by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

History

During the Great Depression, the housing market collapsed: loans were short-term, required large down payments of up to 50 percent, and often ended in balloon payments borrowers could not refinance. The FHA introduced the modern long-term, fully amortizing mortgage with a low down payment, standardizing appraisal and underwriting practices. This made homeownership accessible to a much wider share of Americans and underpinned postwar suburbanization. Critics note that early FHA underwriting manuals endorsed racial segregation and redlining, refusing insurance in Black and integrated neighborhoods — policies that contributed to the racial wealth gap; the agency stopped such practices after mid-century court decisions and formally turned to promoting fair housing under HUD after 1965.

Loan programs

FHA-insured loans permit down payments as low as 3.5 percent for borrowers with qualifying credit scores. Borrowers pay an upfront mortgage insurance premium and an annual premium, which protect the lender against default. The agency's flagship program is under Section 203(b) of the National Housing Act; other programs cover condominium loans, rehabilitation loans under Section 203(k), reverse mortgages (HECMs) for seniors, and loans in rural and underserved areas subject to county-by-county loan limits.
