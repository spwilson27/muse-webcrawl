QR algorithm

In numerical linear algebra, the QR algorithm is an eigenvalue algorithm: a procedure to calculate the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a matrix. It is based on repeated QR decomposition, developed in the late 1950s by John G. F. Francis and Vera Kublanovskaya.

Algorithm

Given a matrix A, compute its QR decomposition A = QR, then form A' = RQ. Repeating this iteration drives A toward upper triangular (Schur) form, whose diagonal entries are the eigenvalues. Practical implementations first reduce the matrix to Hessenberg form and use implicit shifts for rapid convergence.

Significance

The QR algorithm is regarded as one of the most important algorithms of the 20th century and is the basis of eigenvalue routines in LAPACK and most numerical software.
