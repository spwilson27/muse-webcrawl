Western Macedonia (Greek: Δυτική Μακεδονία, Dytikí Makedonía) is one of the thirteen administrative regions of Greece, consisting of the western part of Greek Macedonia in the country's northwest. It is the only entirely landlocked region of Greece, bordering Albania, North Macedonia, and the Greek regions of Central Macedonia, Thessaly, and Epirus. Its capital is Kozani.

Geography

The region is mountainous, dominated by the Pindus range in the west and Mounts Vermio, Askio, Vitsi, and Grammos. It contains the Prespa Lakes, shared with Albania and North Macedonia, and the lakes of Kastoria and Vegoritida. The Aliakmon, Greece's longest river, flows through the region. Its regional units are Florina, Grevena, Kastoria, and Kozani.

Economy

Western Macedonia was historically the centre of Greek lignite mining and electricity generation, with large power stations around Ptolemaida and Kozani supplying much of the country's electricity. Greek decarbonisation policy has scheduled the phase-out of lignite, making the region the focus of a major "just transition" programme. Other important activities include the fur trade of Kastoria, saffron (krokos) production around Kozani, agriculture, and winter tourism at the ski resorts of Vasilitsa and Vigla.
