Grout

Grout is a dense fluid that hardens over time, used to fill gaps or used as reinforcement in existing structures. Grout is generally a mixture of water, cement, and sand, and is employed in pressure grouting, embedding rebar in masonry walls, connecting sections of pre-cast concrete, filling voids, and sealing joints such as those between tiles. Common uses for grout in the household include filling in tiles of shower floors and kitchen tiles. It is often color tinted when it has to be kept visible and sometimes includes fine gravel when being used to fill large spaces (such as the cores of concrete blocks). Unlike other structural pastes such as plaster or joint compound, correctly mixed and applied grout forms a water resistant barrier.

Varieties

  • Cementitious grout – the traditional type, based on Portland cement. Sanded grout contains silica sand for wider joints; unsanded grout is used for narrow joints and delicate tile.
  • Epoxy grout – made from epoxy resins and a filler powder; highly resistant to stains, chemicals and water, and stronger than cement grout.
  • Furan grout – based on furan resins, used in industrial settings requiring extreme chemical and heat resistance.
  • Structural grout – high-strength, non-shrink formulations used under machinery base plates, precast elements and in post-tensioning ducts.

Application

Cementitious grout is mixed with water to a paste, applied to tile joints with a rubber float worked diagonally across the surface, and the excess wiped away with a damp sponge before the material cures. After curing, cement grout is porous and is commonly protected with a penetrating sealer to resist staining and moisture.

Grout in construction engineering

In civil engineering, grouting refers to the injection of pumpable materials into soil or rock to change its physical characteristics, for example to reduce permeability beneath dams, stabilise foundations, or fill voids in tunnelling. Pressure grouting with cement or chemical grouts is a standard ground-improvement technique.
