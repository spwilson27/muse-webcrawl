Commonwealth of Nations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Commonwealth of Nations, simply referred to as the Commonwealth, is an association of 56 member states, mostly former territories of the British Empire, working together on shared values of democracy, human rights and the rule of law as set out in the Commonwealth Charter (2012). Founded in its modern form by the London Declaration of 1949 — which allowed India to remain as a republic, recognising the monarch as Head of the Commonwealth rather than as sovereign — it spans every continent and about 2.7 billion people.

Structure and activities

Fifteen members are Commonwealth realms sharing the same monarch as head of state; five have their own monarchs; the rest are republics. The Commonwealth has no binding treaties or collective security role; its work — through the Secretariat in London (Marlborough House), biennial heads-of-government meetings (CHOGM), ministerial networks, election observation, small-states advocacy and the Commonwealth Games — is soft, developmental and symbolic. Members have been suspended for democratic breaches (Nigeria 1995, Pakistan, Fiji, Zimbabwe — which withdrew in 2003), and recent admissions of states never under British rule (Mozambique 1995, Rwanda 2009, Gabon and Togo 2022) reflect its reinvention as a values-and-network club — even as debates on colonial legacies, reparations and the association's purpose animate its summits.
