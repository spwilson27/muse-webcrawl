Quang Tri province


The old citadel of Quang Tri

Quảng Trị is a province in the North Central Coast region of Vietnam, north of the former imperial capital of Huế. Its capital is Đông Hà. The province covers about 4,747 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 630,000 in recent censuses. It borders Quang Binh province to the north, Thua Thien Hue to the south, Laos to the west and the South China Sea to the east.

Vietnam War

Quang Tri lay just south of the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) along the Ben Hai River, which divided North and South Vietnam from 1954 to 1976, and was among the most heavily fought-over provinces of the Vietnam War. Sites include the former American combat bases at Khe Sanh and Con Thien, the "McNamara Line", and the Vinh Moc tunnels. During the 1972 Easter Offensive, North Vietnamese forces captured Quang Tri city; South Vietnamese forces retook it after an 81-day battle that destroyed the city and its ancient citadel almost entirely.

Legacy

The province remains heavily contaminated with unexploded ordnance, and clearance organisations have worked there for decades. Memorial sites — the Hien Luong Bridge, the Quang Tri Citadel and the Truong Son Martyrs' Cemetery — draw large numbers of Vietnamese visitors.
