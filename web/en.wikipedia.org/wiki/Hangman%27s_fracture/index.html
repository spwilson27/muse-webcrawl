Hangman's fracture

Hangman's fracture is the colloquial name for a traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis, or C2 vertebra: a fracture of the pars interarticularis of C2 on both sides, which can allow the body of C2 to displace forward on C3.

Despite its name, the injury is now most often caused by motor vehicle collisions and falls involving hyperextension of the neck, rather than by judicial hanging, in which the mechanism and outcome differed.

Mechanism and classification

The typical mechanism is forced hyperextension with axial loading. The Levine and Edwards classification grades the fractures by displacement and angulation, guiding treatment. Neurological injury is relatively uncommon because the fracture can decompress the spinal canal.

Treatment

Stable fractures are commonly treated with external immobilisation in a rigid cervical collar or halo vest. Unstable injuries may require surgical fusion of C2 to C3.
