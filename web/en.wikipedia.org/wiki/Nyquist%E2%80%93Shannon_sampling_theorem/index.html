Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem

The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem is a fundamental result in information theory and signal processing. It states that a band-limited continuous signal can be perfectly reconstructed from its samples if the sampling rate is more than twice the highest frequency present in the signal. That minimum rate, twice the bandwidth, is called the Nyquist rate.

History

The theorem builds on work by Harry Nyquist in the 1920s and was given a complete formulation by Claude Shannon in his 1949 paper "Communication in the Presence of Noise." Related results were developed independently by E. T. Whittaker, J. M. Whittaker, and Vladimir Kotelnikov.

Aliasing

If a signal is sampled below the Nyquist rate, higher frequencies are folded into lower ones — a distortion known as aliasing. Practical systems therefore apply an anti-aliasing filter before sampling to remove frequencies above half the sampling rate.

Applications

The theorem underpins all digital audio and communications: compact discs sample at 44.1 kHz to capture the audible range up to about 20 kHz, and digital telephony samples speech at 8 kHz. Reconstruction uses interpolation with sinc functions, approximated in practice by digital-to-analog converters.
