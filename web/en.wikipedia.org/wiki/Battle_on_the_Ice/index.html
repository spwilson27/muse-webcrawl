Battle on the Ice

The Battle on the Ice, also known as the Battle of Lake Peipus, was fought on 5 April 1242 on the frozen surface of Lake Peipus between the forces of the Novgorod Republic, led by Alexander Nevsky, and a crusading army of the Livonian Order (a branch of the Teutonic Knights) and their allies. Nevsky's victory halted eastward crusader expansion and made him a enduring Russian national hero.

Background

As part of the Northern Crusades, the Livonian Order seized Pskov and advanced into Novgorod's territory while Rus' lands reeled from the Mongol invasion. Novgorod recalled the exiled Alexander Nevsky, who had beaten the Swedes on the Neva in 1240; he retook Pskov and raided into Livonian territory before withdrawing onto the lake ice.

Battle

The crusaders charged in their wedge formation ("boar's head") and drove into the Novgorodian centre, but were flanked and surrounded after prolonged fighting on the ice. The Livonian Rhymed Chronicle admits some 20 knights killed and 6 captured — modest numbers, though Russian chronicles claim far more; later tradition added the dramatic detail of knights drowning as the ice broke, which the earliest sources do not record.

Aftermath

The Order abandoned its gains and signed a peace with Novgorod. The battle was mythologised in Sergei Eisenstein's 1938 film Alexander Nevsky, with Prokofiev's score, as a symbol of resistance to German invasion.
