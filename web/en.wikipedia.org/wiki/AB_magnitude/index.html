AB magnitude

The AB magnitude system is a system of astronomical magnitudes defined by reference to a flat spectral flux density, rather than to the spectrum of a particular star such as Vega, which is used in the Vega magnitude system. It was proposed by J. B. Oke in the 1970s and 1980s and is now widely used in survey astronomy, including the Sloan Digital Sky Survey.

Definition

An object with an AB magnitude of zero has a spectral flux density of 3,631 janskys at every frequency. The AB magnitude is defined by mAB = −2.5 log10(fν / 3631 Jy). Because the reference spectrum is flat in frequency, AB magnitudes are directly related to physical flux densities without colour-dependent corrections to a standard star.

Comparison with Vega magnitudes

In the Vega system, Vega itself has magnitude close to zero in all bands by definition, while in the AB system Vega's magnitudes vary by band, roughly +0.02 in V but offset by up to about a magnitude in the ultraviolet and infrared. Use

The AB system is the standard for modern digital sky surveys and for magnitude systems tied to spectrophotometric standards. See also Apparent magnitude and Vega.
