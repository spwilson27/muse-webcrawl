The AEC Routemaster is a front-engine double-decker bus designed by London Transport and built by the Associated Equipment Company (AEC) and Park Royal Vehicles. Introduced in 1954 and entering service in 1956, it became the standard London bus for half a century and one of the city's most recognisable symbols, notable for its hop-on, hop-off open platform at the rear, separated driver's half-cab, and lightweight aluminium construction.

Design

The Routemaster was designed specifically for London operating conditions, using aircraft-style construction techniques to save weight and fuel. It featured a front-mounted AEC diesel engine, power steering, independent front suspension, and an integral body-chassis structure at a time when most buses used separate chassis. The open platform allowed passengers to board and alight quickly — including in slow-moving traffic — while a conductor collected fares, letting the driver concentrate on driving.

Service and withdrawal

A total of 2,876 Routemasters were built, most for London Transport. The type gradually gave way to one-person-operated buses and, later, accessibility requirements that the open platform could not meet. The last regular Routemaster-operated route in London ended on 9 December 2005, although refurbished examples continued on heritage routes for years afterwards, and hundreds survive in preservation and commercial use worldwide. Its spiritual successor, the New Routemaster introduced in 2012, revived the two-staircase layout and a rear platform (later operated closed).
