Proto-Malayo-Polynesian (PMP) is the reconstructed ancestor of all the Malayo-Polynesian languages, which is the largest branch of the Austronesian language family by number of speakers and geographical extent, stretching from Madagascar in the west to Easter Island (Rapa Nui) in the east, and from Hawaiʻi and Taiwan in the north to New Zealand in the south.

Reconstruction

PMP is reconstructed from the comparative study of languages such as Malay, Javanese, Sundanese, Cebuano, Tagalog, Malagasy and the Oceanic languages, building on earlier work on Proto-Austronesian (PAn) associated with scholars such as Otto Dempwolff, Isidore Dyen and Robert Blust. PMP is distinguished from PAn by innovations including the merger of PAn *t and *C, of *n and *ñ, and of *s and *h in many positions, as well as a set of shared lexical innovations for the maritime, rice-growing culture of its speakers.

Phonology

The PMP consonant inventory included *p, *t, *k, *q, *b, *d, *j, *g, *m, *n, *ñ, *ŋ, *s, *h, *l, *r, *R, *w and *y, with a simple four-vowel system *i, *u, *ə and *a. Word structure was typically disyllabic at the root level, with penultimate stress.

Grammar and vocabulary

PMP retained the Austronesian voice (focus) system of verb morphology inherited from PAn, with actor, patient, locative and circumstantial voices, and clitic pronouns. Reconstructed vocabulary includes terms for outrigger canoes and sails, fishing gear, domesticated animals (pig, dog, chicken), and cultivated plants such as taro, yam, banana, sugarcane, coconut and rice — evidence of the seafaring agricultural society that carried the language family across Island Southeast Asia and into the Pacific.

Subgrouping

The internal classification of Malayo-Polynesian remains debated. A widely used scheme distinguishes a Western Malayo-Polynesian grouping (a geographical rather than strictly genealogical grouping) from Central–Eastern Malayo-Polynesian, within which Central Malayo-Polynesian and Eastern Malayo-Polynesian (South Halmahera–West New Guinea and Oceanic) are recognized.
