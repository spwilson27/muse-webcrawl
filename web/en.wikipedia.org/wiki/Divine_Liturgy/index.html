Divine Liturgy

The Divine Liturgy (Greek: Θεία Λειτουργία) is the principal eucharistic service of the Byzantine Rite, celebrated in the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Greek Catholic (Byzantine Catholic) churches, and other Eastern churches. It centres on the consecration of bread and wine and the communion of the faithful, framed by hymns, litanies, scripture readings and processions.

Forms

Three forms are in common use. The Liturgy of St John Chrysostom is celebrated on most Sundays and weekdays of the year. The Liturgy of St Basil, with longer and more theologically expansive prayers, is appointed for the Sundays of Great Lent, Holy Week and certain feasts. The Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, celebrated on Lenten weekdays, is a communion service using gifts consecrated the previous Sunday, since the full consecratory liturgy is considered too joyful for penitential days. The ancient Liturgy of St James is celebrated in some places on his feast day.

Structure

The service unfolds in three broad parts: the Prothesis (preparation of the gifts at a side table); the Liturgy of the Catechumens (or of the Word), with the Little Entrance, readings from the Epistle and Gospel, and homily; and the Liturgy of the Faithful, with the Great Entrance bearing the gifts to the altar, the Creed, the Anaphora (eucharistic prayer) including the epiclesis invoking the Holy Spirit, the Lord's Prayer, communion, and dismissal. The pentecostal hymn "We have seen the true light" and the distribution of antidoron close the service.

Music and languages

The liturgy is almost entirely sung or chanted, in Greek, Church Slavonic, Arabic, Romanian, English and many other languages, in traditions ranging from Byzantine chant to Russian choral settings by composers such as Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff (whose Liturgy of St John Chrysostom and All-Night Vigil are landmarks of sacred music).
