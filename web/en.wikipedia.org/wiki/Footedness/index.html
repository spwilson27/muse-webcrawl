Footedness

Footedness is the natural preference of one's left or right foot for various purposes. While purposes vary, footedness is most commonly defined by the foot a person prefers for kicking a ball. Roughly 80 to 90 percent of people are right-footed, a proportion broadly similar to (though somewhat lower than) right-handedness.

Types

Individual applications vary in their precision and importance, but feet are generally specialised: one foot, typically the dominant one, is used for manipulation tasks such as kicking and tapping, while the other is specialised for support and stability, such as standing on one leg or balancing. People who are equally skilled with both feet are described as two-footed; in sports such as association football this ambidexterity is highly valued.

Studies

Large studies of footedness find consistent sex differences, with men somewhat more likely than women to be left-footed. Footedness also correlates with handedness, but imperfectly: a notable minority of right-handers are left-footed and vice versa. Like handedness, foot preference emerges early in development and may reflect asymmetries in the nervous system.

In sport

In football, a player's footedness influences tactics, positioning and set-piece roles. In surfing, skateboarding and snowboarding, stance preference is described as "regular" (left foot forward) or "goofy" (right foot forward), which is conceptually related though not identical to kicking preference.
