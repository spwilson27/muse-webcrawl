Te Araroa (Māori for "The Long Pathway") is New Zealand's long-distance tramping route, stretching about 3,000 kilometres from Cape Reinga at the northern tip of the North Island to Bluff on the southern coast of the South Island. It is composed of a mixture of older tracks and walkways, new sections, and linking sections along roads and across private land.

History

The idea of a national trail was proposed as early as 1975, when a Federated Mountain Clubs initiative suggested a route the length of the country. Journalist Graeme Wilson revived the concept in the 1990s and founded the Te Araroa Trust in 1994 to build it. After years of negotiation with landowners, the Department of Conservation and local councils, the trail was officially opened on 3 December 2011 by the Governor-General, Sir Jerry Mateparae.

Route

The North Island section passes beaches of Northland, Auckland's volcanic cones, the Waikato River and the Tararua Range before reaching Wellington. The South Island section follows the Richmond Range, the Southern Alps via high passes such as Waiau Pass, the Canterbury plains and the Longwoods to the coast at Bluff. Thru-walkers typically take four to five months, walking in the warmer months, and often paddle part of the Whanganui River as part of the route.

Usage

Te Araroa attracts hundreds of thru-hikers each season plus far larger numbers of day and section walkers. The Trust maintains maps and trail notes, and a network of "trail angels" offers accommodation and support along the way.
