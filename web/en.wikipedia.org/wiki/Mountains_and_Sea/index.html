Mountains and Sea

Mountains and Sea

Artist: Helen Frankenthaler

Year: 1952

Medium: Oil and charcoal on canvas

Dimensions: 218.9 cm × 297.8 cm (86 3/8 in × 117 1/4 in)

Location: National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. (on loan from the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation)

Mountains and Sea is a 1952 painting by the American artist Helen Frankenthaler (1928–2011). Painted when Frankenthaler was 23 years old, it is considered a landmark of postwar American art and the founding work of her "soak-stain" technique, in which paint thinned with turpentine is poured onto unprimed canvas so that it soaks into the fabric.

Background

Frankenthaler painted Mountains and Sea in October 1952, shortly after a summer trip to Nova Scotia, Canada, whose coastal landscape of rocks, water and sky informed the composition. Working with the canvas spread on her studio floor, she poured oil paint diluted with turpentine directly onto unprimed cotton duck, letting the colours bleed into the weave, and drew into the composition with charcoal. The result was a luminous, watercolour-like field of blues, greens and pinks with areas of bare canvas showing through.

Influence

The painting had an immediate impact on other artists. After the critic Clement Greenberg brought Morris Louis and Kenneth Noland to Frankenthaler's studio in 1953, both artists adopted and developed staining methods, Louis in his Veils and Noland in his targets and stripes. Mountains and Sea thus became a bridge between Abstract Expressionism and the Color Field painting of the late 1950s and 1960s.
