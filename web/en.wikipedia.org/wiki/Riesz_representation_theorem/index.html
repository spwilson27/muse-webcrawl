Riesz representation theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Riesz representation theorem, also called the Riesz–Fréchet representation theorem, is a fundamental result in functional analysis. In its best-known form it states that every continuous linear functional on a Hilbert space can be written uniquely as an inner product with a fixed vector of that space. Consequently, a Hilbert space is isometrically isomorphic to its own dual space — a property that underlies the bra–ket notation of quantum mechanics.

FieldFunctional analysis
Named afterFrigyes Riesz, Maurice Fréchet
Proved1907 (Riesz and Fréchet, independently)
SettingHilbert spaces

Statement

Let H be a Hilbert space over the real or complex numbers, and let φ : H → 𝕜 be a continuous linear functional. Then there exists a unique vector y in H such that φ(x) = ⟨x, y⟩ for every x in H, and moreover ‖φ‖ = ‖y‖. The map y ↦ ⟨·, y⟩ is therefore a conjugate-linear isometric isomorphism from H onto its dual H*.

Related results

Several other theorems share the name. The Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem identifies continuous linear functionals on spaces of continuous functions with regular Borel measures, providing the foundation for the modern construction of Lebesgue measure and integration. Representation theorems also exist for the duals of Lp spaces. Together these results exemplify a central theme of functional analysis: abstract functionals can be concretely represented by vectors, densities, or measures.
