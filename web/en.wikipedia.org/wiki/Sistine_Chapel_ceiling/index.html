Sistine Chapel ceiling

The Sistine Chapel ceiling (La volta della Cappella Sistina), painted in fresco by Michelangelo between 1508 and 1512, is a cornerstone work of High Renaissance art in the Apostolic Palace, Vatican City.

Scheme

Commissioned by Pope Julius II after the chapel's earlier starry ceiling, Michelangelo's scheme covers some 500 square metres with nine central scenes from Genesis – from the Separation of Light from Darkness to the Drunkenness of Noah – including the iconic Creation of Adam, where God's and Adam's outstretched fingers nearly touch. These are framed by prophets, sibyls, ignudi (nude youths), ancestors of Christ and corner pendentives showing Haman, Judith and Holofernes, and the brazen serpent.

Creation and restoration

Michelangelo, who considered himself primarily a sculptor, painted standing on a specially designed scaffold, contrary to the myth of lying on his back. A major restoration (1980–1994) removed centuries of grime and candle soot, revealing vivid colours and sparking debate about whether it also removed Michelangelo's own shading.
