The Chatham Main Line is a railway line in Kent, England, running from London Victoria through the Medway towns and Faversham to Dover and Ramsgate. Built by the London, Chatham and Dover Railway (LC&DR) in the late 1850s and early 1860s, it provides one of the two principal rail routes between London and east Kent, the other being the South Eastern Main Line.

History

The East Kent Railway opened from Strood to Faversham in 1858 and was renamed the London, Chatham and Dover Railway in 1859. The line was extended westward to Bromley and on toward Victoria, which opened in 1862, and eastward from Faversham to Canterbury and Dover by 1861. At Herne Hill the route divides: the main line continues through Bromley South, while a loop via Catford serves inner south London. At Faversham the line splits into branches to Dover Priory via Canterbury East and to Ramsgate via Margate.

Route and services

Trains serve the Medway towns of Rochester, Chatham and Gillingham, then Sittingbourne and Faversham. High-speed services using High Speed 1 join the line for parts of their journeys to the Kent coast. The line is electrified throughout with the 750 V DC third-rail system and carries intensive suburban and regional passenger traffic, with freight largely routed elsewhere.

Features

Notable engineering features include the Medway Viaduct near Rochester (superseded for road traffic by later bridges) and the crossing of the North Kent marshes. The line passes close to Rochester Castle and Canterbury Cathedral, both visible from trains.
