LGBT slang

LGBT slang, LGBT speak, queer slang, or gay slang is a set of slang lexicon used predominantly among LGBT people. It has been used in various languages since the early 20th century as a means by which members of the LGBT community identify themselves and speak in code with brevity and speed to others.

Drag culture has contributed many terms to mainstream slang

Origins

Because of the persecution faced by LGBT people for much of history, coded languages served a protective function. In Britain, Polari — a cant drawing on Italian, Romani, Yiddish, and thieves' slang — was used by gay men in the early-to-mid 20th century. In the United States, much modern gay slang originated in African-American and Latino queer communities, particularly the Harlem and New York ball culture of the 1980s, whose lexicon of "reading", "shade", "werk", and "fierce" was documented in the film Paris Is Burning (1990) and later popularized globally by drag television franchises.

Examples

Common terms include bear, otter, and twink (body-type descriptors for gay men), butch and femme (presentations among lesbians), chosen family, coming out, drag (performance in exaggerated gender presentation), kiki (a gathering or gossip session), read (to insult wittily), shade (subtle insult), slay (to excel), tea (gossip), and yas (exuberant affirmation). Words such as queer itself were reclaimed from slurs.

Mainstreaming

Many terms have crossed into mainstream English, sometimes with shifted meanings, a process scholars note both spreads queer culture and dilutes the terms' origins in Black and Latinx communities. Similar coded vocabularies exist in other languages, such as bahasa gay in Indonesia, pajubá in Brazil, and the Swardspeak of the Philippines.
