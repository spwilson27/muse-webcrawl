Jerónimos Monastery

Jerónimos Monastery
Location: Belém, Lisbon, Portugal
Founded: 1501
Style: Manueline (Portuguese Late Gothic)
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1983

The Jerónimos Monastery (Portuguese: Mosteiro dos Jerónimos) is a former monastery of the Order of Saint Jerome near the Tagus river in the parish of Belém, in the Portuguese capital of Lisbon. It is one of the most prominent examples of the Manueline style of architecture, the Portuguese late Gothic style, and was classified as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, together with the nearby Tower of Belém.

History

The monastery was commissioned by King Manuel I around 1501, shortly after Vasco da Gama's return from his pioneering voyage to India, and was funded in part by a tax on goods imported from Africa and the Orient. Construction began under the architect Diogo de Boitaca and continued through the sixteenth century, with contributions from architects including João de Castilho and Diogo de Torralva, producing the vast cloister, chapter house, refectory, and the church of Santa Maria.

The monastery stands on the site of a hermitage where Vasco da Gama and his crew are said to have prayed before departing for India in 1497. The tombs of Vasco da Gama and of the national poet Luís de Camões are located in the church, as are those of several Portuguese kings.

After the dissolution of the religious orders in Portugal in 1834, the building was used for various purposes, including as a school and orphanage (the Casa Pia). In the twentieth century it came to house the Navy Museum and the National Archaeology Museum. The monastery was also the site of the signing of the Treaty of Lisbon in 2007.

Architecture

The monastery's two-storey cloister is celebrated for its richly carved Manueline ornamentation, incorporating maritime motifs such as ropes, armillary spheres, and exotic plants and animals recalling the Portuguese voyages of discovery. The south portal, by João de Castilho, is a profusion of sculpted figures and decoration. The church interior features slender, palm-like columns supporting a wide ribbed vault.
