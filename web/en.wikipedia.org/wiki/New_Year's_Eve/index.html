New Year's Eve

New Year's Eve, also known as Old Year's Day, is the last day of the year, 31 December in the Gregorian calendar. In many countries it is celebrated with evening social gatherings at which people dance, eat, drink and watch fireworks to mark the coming of the new year.

Celebrations by region

In the United States, the most famous celebration is the ball drop in Times Square, New York City, watched by millions since 1907. London marks midnight with fireworks over the River Thames and the chimes of Big Ben. Sydney's harbour fireworks are among the earliest major displays because of its time zone. In Spain, revellers eat twelve grapes at the strokes of midnight; in Denmark people leap from chairs; in Scotland, Hogmanay traditions include first-footing — the first guest of the new year bringing symbolic gifts of coal, bread and whisky.

Music and customs

In English-speaking countries the Scots song "Auld Lang Syne", from a poem by Robert Burns, is traditionally sung at midnight. Many people make New Year's resolutions for the following day, New Year's Day. Religious observances include Watch Night services in many Christian traditions.

Related observances include Saint Sylvester's Day, celebrated on the same date in Catholic tradition, and Ōmisoka in Japan, when temple bells are rung 108 times.
