The Icacinaceae are a family of flowering plants, consisting of trees, shrubs, and lianas found in the tropics, principally in Africa, Asia, and the Americas. The family takes its name from the genus Icacina.

Taxonomy

Traditionally the family was broadly circumscribed, with around 55 genera and several hundred species, but phylogenetic studies showed this assemblage to be polyphyletic. In the APG III system (2009) the family could not be placed in an order and several genera were left uncertain; the APG IV system (2016) narrowed Icacinaceae to roughly 24 genera and placed it in its own order, Icacinales, alongside the small family Oncothecaceae. Genera formerly included have been moved to families such as Cardiopteridaceae, Stemonuraceae, Metteniusaceae, and Pennantiaceae.

Description and uses

Members are typically woody plants with alternate, simple leaves and small, often five-parted flowers; the fruit is usually a drupe. Some species have local uses: Icacina oliviformis (false yam) produces edible tubers and seeds used as a famine food in West and Central Africa, and several genera are used in traditional medicine.
