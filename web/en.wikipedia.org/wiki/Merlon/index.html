Merlon

A merlon is the solid upright part of a battlement (a crenellated parapet) in medieval architecture. The gaps between merlons are called crenels or embrasures, and the alternating pattern of solid merlons and open crenels is known as crenellation. Merlons protected defenders on a wall walk from arrows and other missiles while the crenels allowed them to shoot or observe.

Forms

Merlons vary in shape by period and region. They may be rectangular, or have curved, pointed, or trefoil tops. In Italy, the distinction between rectangular "Guelph" merlons and swallowtail ("swallow-tailed" or dovetail) "Ghibelline" merlons became a famous symbol of the rival factions of the Holy Roman Empire era: square merlons were associated with the Guelphs (supporters of the Pope) and swallowtail merlons with the Ghibellines (supporters of the Emperor)—though the association is not consistent everywhere.

History

Crenellated defenses date to antiquity, appearing in Assyrian reliefs and Roman fortifications. Merlons were a standard feature of medieval castles, city walls, and fortified churches, and persisted into early gunpowder fortifications in modified form. In later centuries, merlons became largely decorative, appearing in Gothic Revival and castellated-style buildings.
