Naja

Naja
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Reptilia
Order: Squamata
Family: Elapidae
Genus: Naja Laurenti, 1768

Naja is a genus of venomous elapid snakes commonly known as cobras. The genus contains several of the most medically significant snakes in Africa and Asia, including the Indian cobra (Naja naja), the Egyptian cobra (Naja haje), the monocled cobra (Naja kaouthia), and the Chinese cobra (Naja atra). The name derives from the Sanskrit word nāga, meaning snake.

Description

Species of Naja range from about 1 metre (3.3 ft) to over 3 metres (the forest cobra, Naja melanoleuca) in length. Most are capable of rearing up and spreading a hood when threatened, formed by elongated ribs in the neck region. Their fangs are short and fixed at the front of the upper jaw.

Venom

Naja venom is predominantly neurotoxic, containing postsynaptic neurotoxins that block nerve signals to muscles, potentially causing paralysis and respiratory failure. Some species also contain cytotoxins that cause local tissue damage. Several "spitting cobra" species can eject venom from their fangs toward the eyes of a perceived threat.

Distribution

The genus is distributed across Africa and southern Asia, from the Sahara and Arabian Peninsula to India, Southeast Asia, Indonesia, and southern China. Species occupy habitats ranging from desert and savanna to tropical forest and rice paddies.

Species

Roughly 38 species are currently recognized, divided among the subgenera Naja, Uraeus, Boulengerina, and Afronaja. Taxonomy of the group has been substantially revised by molecular studies in the 21st century.
