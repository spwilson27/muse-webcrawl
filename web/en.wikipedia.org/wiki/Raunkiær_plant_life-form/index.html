Raunkiær plant life-form

The Raunkiær system is a classification of plants into life forms based on the position of the plant's growth point (bud) during seasons with adverse conditions, such as winter cold or summer drought. It was devised by the Danish botanist Christen C. Raunkiær and set out in his works of 1904–1907 and in the 1934 book The Life Forms of Plants and Statistical Plant Geography.

The main Raunkiær life forms, classified by the position of the perennating buds

Classes

  • Phanerophytes – woody plants whose surviving buds stand well above the ground on persistent stems: trees and shrubs. Subdivided by height into megaphanerophytes, mesophanerophytes, microphanerophytes, and nanophanerophytes.
  • Chamaephytes – plants whose buds are borne on persistent shoots close to the ground, no more than about 25 cm above the soil surface: cushion plants and low shrubs.
  • Hemicryptophytes – plants whose buds sit at the soil surface, often in a basal rosette.
  • Cryptophytes – plants whose buds lie below the surface: geophytes with bulbs, rhizomes or tubers, as well as helophytes (marsh plants) and hydrophytes (aquatic plants) budding under water.
  • Therophytes – annual plants that survive the unfavourable season only as seeds.

Biological spectra

Raunkiær proposed describing a flora by its biological spectrum: the percentage of its species falling into each life-form class. Comparing a flora's spectrum with his "normal spectrum" for the whole world (phanerophytes 46%, chamaephytes 9%, hemicryptophytes 26%, cryptophytes 6%, therophytes 13%) allows the climate of a region to be characterised — deserts, for example, are rich in therophytes, while cold temperate regions are dominated by hemicryptophytes.

Use and criticism

The system remains widely used in plant ecology and biogeography because bud position is a simple, observable trait closely tied to climate. Later workers have subdivided Raunkiær's classes further and combined them with leaf-size and other functional traits.
