Paediatric endocrinology

Paediatric endocrinology (American spelling: pediatric endocrinology) is a subspecialty of paediatrics dealing with disorders of the endocrine glands in infants, children and adolescents, including variations of physical growth and sexual development in childhood.

Scope

Conditions commonly managed include type 1 diabetes mellitus — the largest part of most practices — growth disorders such as growth hormone deficiency and short stature, disorders of puberty (precocious and delayed puberty), thyroid disorders including congenital hypothyroidism, adrenal disorders such as congenital adrenal hyperplasia, hypoglycaemia, pituitary disorders, bone and mineral disorders such as rickets, and disorders of sex development.

Training and practice

Paediatric endocrinologists train first in paediatrics and then in a fellowship in the subspecialty. Care is usually delivered in children's hospitals in multidisciplinary teams with specialist nurses, dietitians, psychologists and diabetes educators, since most endocrine conditions in childhood require long-term follow-up through growth and adolescence into adult services.
