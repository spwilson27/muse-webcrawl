To Kill a Mockingbird (film)

To Kill a Mockingbird is a 1962 American drama film directed by Robert Mulligan, adapted by Horton Foote from Harper Lee's 1960 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel. It stars Gregory Peck as lawyer Atticus Finch.

Plot

In the Depression-era Alabama town of Maycomb, widowed lawyer Atticus Finch raises his children Scout and Jem. When Atticus defends Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused of raping a white woman, the children confront racial injustice, while also unravelling the mystery of their reclusive neighbour Boo Radley, who ultimately saves them.

Reception and legacy

The film won three Academy Awards, including Best Actor for Peck, whose Atticus was named the greatest hero in American cinema by the American Film Institute. It is preserved in the National Film Registry and remains a staple of American cultural education.
