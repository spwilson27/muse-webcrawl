Lambic

Lambic is a type of beer brewed in the Pajottenland region of Belgium southwest of Brussels and in Brussels itself. Unlike most beers, which are fermented with carefully cultivated strains of yeast, lambic is produced by spontaneous fermentation: the hot wort is cooled overnight in a shallow open vessel called a coolship (koelschip), exposing it to wild yeasts and bacteria — including Brettanomyces — native to the Zenne valley, after which it ferments and matures in wooden barrels for one to three years or more.

Production

Lambic wort traditionally contains at least 30% unmalted wheat alongside malted barley, and is hopped with aged (suranné) hops valued for their preservative effect rather than bitterness or aroma. Brewing takes place only in the cooler months, traditionally between October and May, so that the wort cools safely overnight. The resulting beer is still or lightly carbonated, dry, sour and complex, with flavours attributed to the mixed microbial culture.

Styles

  • Gueuze — a blend of young (one-year) and old (two- to three-year) lambics, refermented in the bottle and sparkling.
  • Kriek — lambic refermented with sour cherries; framboise uses raspberries, and other fruits are also used.
  • Faro — a lower-alcohol blend sweetened with sugar or caramel.
  • Straight (unblended) lambic — served still, from a single barrel.

Protection and producers

The terms Oude ("old") Gueuze and Oude Kriek are protected as traditional specialities guaranteed (TSG) in European Union law, requiring spontaneous fermentation and traditional methods. Historic producers and blenders include Cantillon, Boon, Lindemans, Timmermans, 3 Fonteinen and Girardin.
