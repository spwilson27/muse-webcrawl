Cataloguing

Cataloguing (in American English, cataloging) is the process of creating metadata representing information resources — books, serials, recordings, maps, datasets, and other materials — and organising that metadata into catalogues so that users can discover, identify, select, and obtain the resources. It is a core activity of libraries, archives, and museums.

Practice

Descriptive cataloguing records the physical and bibliographic features of an item, while subject cataloguing assigns classification numbers and subject headings. Major standards and tools include the Anglo-American Cataloguing Rules (AACR2) and its successor RDA (Resource Description and Access), the MARC formats for machine-readable records, the Dewey Decimal and Library of Congress classifications, and authority control of names and subjects. The FRBR model and its successor, the IFLA Library Reference Model, underpin modern rules by modelling works, expressions, manifestations, and items.

Shared cataloguing

Cooperative databases such as OCLC's WorldCat allow libraries to copy records created by others ("copy cataloguing"), greatly reducing duplication of effort. Card catalogues have been replaced almost everywhere by online public access catalogues (OPACs) and discovery systems, and cataloguing practice now extends to digital objects, linked data, and the BIBFRAME vocabulary.
