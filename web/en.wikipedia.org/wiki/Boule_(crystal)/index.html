Boule (crystal)

A boule is a single-crystal ingot produced by synthetic means. The term is most commonly applied to the cylindrical ingots of silicon, gallium arsenide, sapphire, ruby and other crystalline materials grown for the semiconductor, optics and jewellery industries, from which thin wafers are sliced.

Growth methods

In the Czochralski method, a seed crystal is dipped into a crucible of molten material and slowly withdrawn while rotating; the melt solidifies on the seed, producing a large cylindrical boule, typically 200 or 300 mm in diameter for silicon and weighing hundreds of kilograms. In the older Verneuil (flame fusion) process, fine powder falls through an oxy-hydrogen flame and accumulates as a pear-shaped boule, the traditional form for synthetic ruby and sapphire; the Bridgman and float-zone techniques produce boules of other shapes and higher purity.

Processing

After growth, the boule is ground to a precise diameter, given flats or notches to mark crystallographic orientation, and sliced with diamond or wire saws into wafers. A substantial fraction of the material is lost in kerf and grinding. Silicon boules are the raw starting material of nearly all integrated circuits, and sapphire boules are cut into watch crystals, optical windows and substrates for gallium nitride LEDs.
