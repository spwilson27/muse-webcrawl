ASCOD

The ASCOD (Austrian Spanish Cooperation Development) is a family of modern armoured fighting vehicles developed jointly by the Austrian company Steyr-Daimler-Puch Spezialfahrzeug and the Spanish company Santa Bárbara Sistemas (both now part of General Dynamics European Land Systems). The family includes an infantry fighting vehicle (IFV) variant and a light tank variant, and is in service with the armies of Spain, Austria and, in a British derivative form, the United Kingdom.

Development

The ASCOD project began in the 1990s as a cooperative venture intended to replace ageing M113 armoured personnel carriers in Spanish and Austrian service. The design combines a steel hull, modular add-on armour and a torsion bar suspension. Spain fields the vehicle as the Pizarro, named after the conquistador Francisco Pizarro, while Austria fields it as the Ulan.

Design

The IFV variant has a crew of three (commander, gunner and driver) and carries up to eight dismounted infantry. Armament typically consists of a 30 mm Mauser MK 30-2 cannon in a two-man turret, a coaxial machine gun and smoke grenade launchers. The hull protects against 14.5 mm armour-piercing rounds over the frontal arc and small-arms fire all round, with appliqué armour packages available for higher threat levels.

Variants and operators

  • Pizarro — Spanish IFV, produced in two phases with a command post variant.
  • Ulan — Austrian IFV with Austrian-specific electronics and armament fits.
  • Ajax — a heavily redesigned British family of tracked reconnaissance vehicles based on the ASCOD 2 platform, ordered for the British Army.
  • ASCOD 2 — an evolved version with increased payload, volume and protection, offered in IFV, light tank (with 105 mm or 120 mm gun) and support configurations.
