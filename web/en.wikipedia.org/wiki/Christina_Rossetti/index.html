Christina Rossetti

Christina Georgina Rossetti
Born: 5 December 1830, London
Died: 29 December 1894, London
Occupation: Poet
Notable works: 'Goblin Market', 'Remember', 'In the Bleak Midwinter'

Christina Georgina Rossetti (1830–1894) was an English poet who wrote romantic, devotional and children's poems. She is best remembered for Goblin Market and the Christmas carol 'In the Bleak Midwinter', and is regarded as one of the foremost Victorian women poets.

Life

Born into an artistic Italian émigré family, she was the sister of artist-poet Dante Gabriel Rossetti, a founder of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, for whom she modelled. Deeply Anglican and devout, she twice declined marriage on religious grounds and lived a largely reclusive life devoted to writing and faith.

Works

Goblin Market and Other Poems (1862) established her reputation; its title poem, a fairy-tale narrative about two sisters and tempting goblin fruit merchants, has been read as a fable of temptation, sacrifice and sisterhood. Her sonnets, devotional verse, and children's collection Sing-Song (1872) followed. The sonnet 'Remember' and the poem 'Uphill' remain widely anthologised.
