Univers

Univers is a sans-serif typeface designed by Adrian Frutiger and released by Deberny & Peignot in 1957. Classified as a neo-grotesque typeface, it was one of the first typefaces to be launched as a large, coordinated family with a systematic numbering scheme for weights and widths rather than individual names.

Design

Frutiger's numbering system assigns two-digit numbers to each variant: the first digit indicates weight (3 for the lightest through 8 for the heaviest) and the second indicates width and posture (odd numbers for roman, even for oblique; lower numbers wider). Thus Univers 55 is the regular text weight, 65 is bold, 45 is light, and 57 is regular condensed.

Use

Univers was widely adopted for corporate identity and signage in the 1960s and 1970s. It was used by the 1972 Munich Olympics, in the logo and livery of Swiss International Air Lines, and (in modified form) on British road signs' predecessors and in the branding of organisations such as Deutsche Bank and General Electric. Frutiger later revised the family as Univers Next.
