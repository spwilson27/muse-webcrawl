Colt (horse)

A colt in a paddock

A colt is a male horse, usually below the age of four years. The term "colt" only describes young male horses and is not to be confused with foal, which is a horse of either sex less than one year of age. Similarly, a yearling is a horse of either sex between the ages of one and two. A young female horse is called a filly, and a mare once she is an adult animal. In horse racing, particularly for Thoroughbreds in the United Kingdom, a colt is defined as an uncastrated male less than five years of age.

Usage

The term is derived from Old English colt, of uncertain origin, possibly related to words for a young animal or a lump. An adult male horse, if left intact, is called a stallion; if castrated, a gelding. Historically, the word "colt" was also used for the young of other large animals, including camels and donkeys, and in early translations of the Bible.

In racing and sport

Many classic horse races are restricted to colts and fillies of a specified age, such as the Epsom Derby and the Kentucky Derby, which are open to three-year-old Thoroughbred colts and fillies. The British Classic races include colts-only events such as the 2000 Guineas.
