Mitosis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mitosis is the process of cell division in eukaryotic cells in which one cell divides into two genetically identical daughter cells, each with the same number of chromosomes as the parent. It is how multicellular organisms grow, repair tissues and (in many species) reproduce asexually.

Phases

Before mitosis, in interphase, the cell copies its DNA. Mitosis itself proceeds through prophase (chromosomes condense; the spindle forms), metaphase (chromosomes align at the cell's equator), anaphase (sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles) and telophase (two new nuclei form), followed by cytokinesis, the splitting of the cytoplasm. Checkpoint mechanisms police the process; failures can produce cells with abnormal chromosome numbers and contribute to cancer.

Mitosis contrasts with meiosis, the reduction division that produces gametes with half the chromosome number. The whole cycle is powered and regulated through the cell's energy and signalling systems, including ATP (see ATP) and the metabolic network described under metabolism.
