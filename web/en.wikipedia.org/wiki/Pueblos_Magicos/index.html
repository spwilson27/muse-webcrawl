Pueblos Mágicos ("Magical Towns") is a programme of the Mexican federal Secretariat of Tourism (SECTUR) that designates towns and localities whose cultural, historical, or natural heritage merits promotion for tourism. Towns receive the designation — and access to federal and state funding for infrastructure, restoration, and promotion — if they meet criteria concerning heritage assets, visitor services, and local governance. The first town designated, in 2001, was Huasca de Ocampo in Hidalgo.

Programme

Candidate localities must generally have at least 5,000–20,000 inhabitants, lie within two hours' drive of a major population centre, and possess heritage elements such as historic architecture, festivals, gastronomy, or distinctive landscapes. Designated towns form a committee with local authorities and business owners to manage the brand. Towns that fail to meet maintenance and investment requirements can have the designation withdrawn — as happened to Mexcaltitán (2009) and, briefly, Papantla and Tepoztlán.

Effect

Studies credit the programme with substantially increasing tourism and investment in many towns, while critics argue it concentrates visitors, commodifies heritage, and can displace residents through gentrification. Towns include Taxco (silver), Tequila (agave and the drink's birthplace), Dolores Hidalgo (cradle of independence), and Bacalar (the "Lagoon of Seven Colours").
