Monstera

Monstera is a genus of about 50 species of flowering plants in the arum family, Araceae, native to tropical regions of the Americas. The genus is named from the Latin word for "monstrous" or "abnormal", in reference to the unusual leaves with natural holes and splits (fenestrations) borne by members of the genus.

Description

Monsteras are evergreen hemiepiphytes: they germinate on the forest floor and climb trees toward the light, clinging with aerial roots. Juvenile leaves are entire and heart-shaped; as the plant matures and climbs, leaves become larger and develop the characteristic pinnatifid splits and elliptical fenestrations. The inflorescence is a spadix surrounded by a cream-white spathe, followed (in Monstera deliciosa) by a cone-like fruit that ripens to an edible, pineapple-and-banana-flavoured pulp — though unripe fruit and other parts contain irritating oxalate crystals.

Cultivation

Monstera deliciosa, the Swiss cheese plant, is one of the world's most popular houseplants, valued for its dramatic foliage and tolerance of indoor conditions. Variegated cultivars such as 'Albo Variegata' and 'Thai Constellation' are highly prized. Plants prefer bright indirect light, support for climbing, and well-drained soil.
