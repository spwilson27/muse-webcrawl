Ethical banking

An ethical bank, also known as a social, alternative, civic, or sustainable bank, is a bank concerned with the social and environmental impacts of its investments and loans. The ethical banking movement includes ethical investment, impact investment, socially responsible investment, corporate social responsibility, and is also related to such movements as fair trade, social enterprise, and microfinance.

Practices

Ethical banks typically apply screening to their lending: negative screening excludes activities such as weapons manufacture, tobacco, fossil-fuel extraction or exploitative labour practices, while positive screening directs finance to renewable energy, social housing, organic farming, education and community enterprises. Many publish full lists of the organisations they finance, and some are certified B Corporations or members of the Global Alliance for Banking on Values, founded in 2009.

Examples

Long-established ethical banks include Triodos Bank in the Netherlands (founded 1980), the Co-operative Bank in the United Kingdom, GLS Bank in Germany, and Banca Etica in Italy. In Islamic finance, religious prohibitions on interest and on investment in alcohol, gambling and pork-related industries impose a related—but distinct—system of ethical screening.

Criticism

Critics question whether ethical screening materially changes corporate behaviour, and whether smaller ethical banks can compete on price and convenience with large conventional institutions. The sector nonetheless grew significantly after the 2008 financial crisis, as depositors sought alternatives to mainstream banks.
