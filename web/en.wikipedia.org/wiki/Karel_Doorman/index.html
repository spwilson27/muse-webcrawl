Karel Willem Frederik Marie Doorman (23 April 1889 – 28 February 1942) was a Dutch naval officer who commanded the Combined Striking Force of the American-British-Dutch-Australian Command (ABDACOM) during the early months of the Pacific War. He was killed in action at the Battle of the Java Sea, going down with his flagship, the light cruiser HNLMS De Ruyter.

Early career

Doorman was born in Utrecht and entered the Royal Naval College at Den Helder in 1906. He served in the Dutch East Indies and later became one of the pioneers of Dutch naval aviation, helping to establish the Netherlands Naval Aviation Service and commanding the naval air base at Morokrembangan near Surabaya. In the 1930s he commanded several warships in the East Indies, including the cruiser HNLMS Sumatra.

Second World War

After the Japanese invasion of the Dutch East Indies in early 1942, Doorman was appointed commander of the Combined Striking Force, a scratch Allied squadron of Dutch, American, British and Australian cruisers and destroyers. In February 1942 his force fought a series of actions, including the Battle of Badung Strait.

On 27 February 1942, Doorman led his fleet to intercept a Japanese invasion convoy bound for Java. In the ensuing Battle of the Java Sea, the Allied force was outmatched by the Imperial Japanese Navy. The cruisers De Ruyter and Java were torpedoed and sunk during the night action; Doorman, who refused to abandon his flagship, was lost with most of the crew. He is remembered for the signal "Attack the enemy" and is often quoted as saying "Ik val aan, volg mij" ("I am attacking, follow me"), though the exact wording is disputed.

Legacy

Several ships of the Royal Netherlands Navy have been named in his honour, including two aircraft carriers (ex-British Colossus-class) and the Karel Doorman-class frigates. In 2024 the joint support ship HNLMS Karel Doorman is the largest vessel in the Dutch navy.
