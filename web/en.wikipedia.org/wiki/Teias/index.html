Teias (also spelled Teia or Theia; died 553 AD) was the last Ostrogothic king of Italy. He was raised to the throne after the death of King Totila at the Battle of Taginae in 552, during Justinian's Gothic War to reconquer Italy for the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire. His brief, desperate reign ended Gothic resistance in Italy.

Reign

Teias, described by the historian Procopius as a distinguished officer, was chosen king by the Gothic remnant at Pavia after Totila died of wounds received at Taginae. He sought an alliance with the Franks and gathered the remaining Gothic treasuries and forces in Campania, but the Byzantine general Narses cornered his army near Mount Vesuvius. After a stand-off of two months along the Sarno river, the Goths were forced onto Mons Lactarius ("Milk Mountain").

Death

At the Battle of Mons Lactarius (October 552 or March 553), Teias fought a famous last stand: Procopius recounts that the king stood in the front rank, sheltering behind his shield, killing many attackers, and that when his shield grew heavy with spears he called for another — being struck and killed in the moment of exchange. The Goths fought on for a day after his death before surrendering on terms, allowed to leave Italy. Some Gothic garrisons in the north held out longer, but Teias's death effectively ended the Ostrogothic kingdom.
