Odds ratio

From a 2 × 2 table with cells a, b, c, d, the odds ratio is (a/c) / (b/d) = ad/bc.

An odds ratio (OR) is a statistic that quantifies the strength of the association between two events, A and B. The odds ratio is defined as the ratio of the odds of event A occurring in the presence of B to the odds of A occurring in the absence of B — or equivalently, by symmetry, the ratio of the odds of B in the presence and absence of A. An OR of 1 indicates no association; values above 1 indicate a positive association, and values below 1 a negative one.

Definition

For a population divided into exposed and unexposed groups, with outcome probabilities p1 (exposed) and p0 (unexposed), the odds ratio is [p1/(1−p1)] / [p0/(1−p0)]. In a case–control study arranged as a 2 × 2 table, it reduces to the cross-product ratio ad/bc.

Use in medical research

Odds ratios are the standard effect measure in case–control studies, where the relative risk cannot be estimated directly, and they are the natural output of logistic regression. When the outcome is rare, the odds ratio approximates the relative risk; when the outcome is common, it exaggerates it, a frequent source of misinterpretation.

Statistical inference

The logarithm of the odds ratio is approximately normally distributed, which allows confidence intervals and significance tests to be computed from the standard error of ln(OR), equal to the square root of (1/a + 1/b + 1/c + 1/d) for a 2 × 2 table.
