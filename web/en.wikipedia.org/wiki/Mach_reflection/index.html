Mach reflection

Mach reflection is a shock wave phenomenon in supersonic flow in which a shock wave reflects off a boundary in a three-shock configuration. Unlike regular reflection, where the incident shock simply bounces off the surface, Mach reflection produces a nearly normal shock, called the Mach stem, standing perpendicular to the boundary near the reflection point.

Description

When a shock wave strikes a solid wedge at a shallow angle, the flow behind the reflected shock may be unable to turn parallel to the surface. The result is a triple point where the incident shock, reflected shock and Mach stem meet. Behind the Mach stem the flow is subsonic, while the flow passing through the incident and reflected shocks remains supersonic, and the two streams are separated by a slip line.

History and naming

The phenomenon is named after the Austrian physicist Ernst Mach, who described related effects in the nineteenth century. Mach reflection is important in the design of supersonic inlets, in blast-wave physics, and in understanding explosions and shock interactions in aerodynamics.

Types

Depending on the flow conditions, Mach reflection may be direct, stationary or inverted, and transitions between regular and Mach reflection occur at critical wedge angles described by the von Neumann criterion.
