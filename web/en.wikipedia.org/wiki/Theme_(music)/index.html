Theme (music)

In music, a theme is a recognisable melody, melodic fragment, or characteristic figure upon which part or all of a composition is based. A theme is usually a self-contained musical idea—often a phrase or period—that serves as the primary material for development within a work. Themes are frequently contrasted with one another (as with the first and second subjects of sonata form) and may be transformed through variation, fragmentation, sequence, and reharmonization as the piece proceeds.

Role in musical forms

In sonata form, an exposition presents (usually) two contrasting themes or theme groups, which are then developed and recapitulated. In a theme and variations, a single theme is stated and then repeated in altered forms. In fugues, the principal theme is called the subject. Cyclic works, from Beethoven and Berlioz onward, bring themes back across movements, and Wagner's use of the leitmotif made short thematic cells carry dramatic associations throughout an opera. In film and television music, a "theme" commonly denotes the main title music or a recurring character theme.

Theme, motif, and subject

Analysts distinguish the theme from the shorter motif—a brief cell that may generate thematic material—and from the tune or melody in general. Schoenberg's idea of "developing variation" treats whole themes as evolving out of a basic motif (Grundgestalt), as in the music of Brahms. In jazz and popular music, the term "theme" often refers to the head: the main composed melody of a standard, stated before and after improvisations.
