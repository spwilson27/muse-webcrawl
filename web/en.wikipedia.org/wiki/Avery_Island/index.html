Avery Island

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Live oaks draped in Spanish moss along a bayou road on Avery Island]
Live oaks draped in Spanish moss along a bayou road on Avery Island

Avery Island is a salt dome in Iberia Parish, Louisiana, rising some 50 metres above the surrounding marshes of the Gulf Coast — a geological island in a sea of wetlands. It is the home of the McIlhenny Company and the original (and principal) production site of Tabasco sauce since 1868, as well as one of America's oldest rock-salt mines and a celebrated wildlife sanctuary, the Jungle Gardens and Bird City.

Geology and salt

Avery Island is the surface expression of a vast evaporite dome pushed up from Jurassic salt beds; Native Americans boiled brine there, and commercial mining began during the Civil War — the island was attacked in 1863 precisely for its salt. The mine (operated today by Cargill) works chambers hundreds of metres down; the island's elevation made it a refuge and stronghold in the low-lying bayous.

Tabasco and the McIlhennys

The Avery and McIlhenny families developed the island into a company domain: pepper fields, the sauce factory, a village for workers, and, from the 1890s, Edward Avery McIlhenny's Jungle Gardens — 170 acres of semi-tropical plantings through which alligators, deer and migratory birds move — and his Bird City rookery, which rescued the snowy egret from the plume trade's near-extermination. The island remains family-held, its factory tours a Louisiana institution.
