Dowd Report

The Dowd Report is the colloquial name for the 1989 investigative report prepared by attorney John M. Dowd for Major League Baseball Commissioner A. Bartlett Giamatti concerning Pete Rose's gambling on baseball. The 225-page report, delivered in June 1989, concluded that Rose had bet on baseball games, including games of the Cincinnati Reds, the team he managed.

Investigation

In February 1989, following reports about Rose's gambling, Commissioner Peter Ueberroth engaged Dowd as special counsel. The investigation compiled testimony from bookmakers, runners and associates, including Rose's former friend Paul Janszen, together with betting records and telephone records. Rose denied betting on baseball during the investigation, though he admitted to betting on other sports.

Findings and aftermath

The report documented bets on 52 Reds games in 1987 and concluded Rose had bet on baseball in 1985, 1986 and 1987. On August 24, 1989, Rose accepted a settlement under which he was placed permanently on baseball's ineligible list — effectively a lifetime ban — while formally making no finding on the question of his betting. Rose publicly admitted for the first time that he had bet on baseball, including on the Reds, in his 2004 autobiography. The Dowd Report remains central to the continuing debate over Rose's eligibility for the Baseball Hall of Fame.
