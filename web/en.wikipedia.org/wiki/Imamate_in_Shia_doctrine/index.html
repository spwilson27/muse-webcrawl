Imamate in Shia doctrine

Imamate (إمامة)
Central to: Twelver, Ismaili, Zaydi Shia Islam
First Imam: Ali ibn Abi Talib
Doctrine: divinely appointed leadership

The Imamate in Shia Islam is the doctrine that the Muslim community requires divinely appointed, infallible leaders — Imams — descended from the Prophet Muhammad through his daughter Fatima and his cousin and son-in-law Ali ibn Abi Talib. It is the defining belief distinguishing Shia from Sunni Islam, for whom the caliphate was an elective/political office.

Twelver doctrine

Twelvers hold there are twelve Imams, beginning with Ali and ending with Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Mahdi, who is believed to be in occultation since 874 and will return as the Mahdi. Imams possess 'isma (infallibility), divinely granted knowledge ('ilm), and were each designated (nass) by their predecessor. The hadith of Ghadir Khumm and the 'hadith of the two weighty things' are cited as proofs. During the occultation, jurists exercise limited deputyship — a basis developed in the modern era into wilayat al-faqih, guardianship of the jurist, the governing doctrine of the Islamic Republic of Iran.

Other branches

Ismailis maintain a living hereditary Imam — the Aga Khan for Nizaris — and an esoteric hermeneutics (ta'wil). Zaydis accept as Imam any qualified descendant of Hasan or Husayn who rises to claim the role, without doctrines of infallibility or occultation. See Twelve Imams and Mahdi.
