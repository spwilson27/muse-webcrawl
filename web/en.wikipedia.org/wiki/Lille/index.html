Lille (French pronunciation: [lil]; Dutch: Rijsel) is a city in northern France, the capital of the Hauts-de-France region and the prefecture of the Nord department. Located on the Deûle River near the Belgian border, it anchors one of France's largest metropolitan areas, together with Roubaix, Tourcoing and Villeneuve-d'Ascq.

The name derives from L'Isle ("the island"), recalling its origins in marshland along the river. Lille grew into a wealthy cloth town in medieval Flanders, passing between the counts of Flanders, the dukes of Burgundy, the Habsburgs and Spain before Louis XIV took it in 1667 after the siege commemorated by Vauban's great star-shaped Citadel.

History

A settlement existed by the 11th century. Lille's cloth trade made it one of the great cities of medieval Flanders, though it was repeatedly besieged, notably in 1708 during the War of the Spanish Succession and in 1792, when its resistance to Austrian bombardment is commemorated by the Column of the Goddess in the Grand-Place. In the 19th century it industrialised heavily on textiles, coal and engineering; Charles de Gaulle was born there in 1890. The city was occupied by Germany in both world wars.

Geography

Lille lies in the plain of Flanders about 15 km from the Belgian border, at the meeting point of several canals. Its climate is oceanic, with cool winters and mild summers.

Economy

After the decline of textiles and mining, Lille reinvented itself as a service, university and logistics hub, helped by its position between Paris, London and Brussels. The Euralille district, built around the TGV station in the 1990s, symbolised this reinvention. It is a major student city, home to the University of Lille.

Culture

The Old Town (Vieux-Lille) preserves Flemish baroque merchant houses; the Palais des Beaux-Arts is one of France's largest art museums. Lille was European Capital of Culture in 2004. Each September the Braderie de Lille fills the streets with what is reputed to be Europe's largest flea market, where eating moules-frites is a ritual.

Transport

Lille-Europe and Lille-Flandres stations put the city about one hour from Paris, 35 minutes from Brussels and 80 minutes from London by high-speed train. The city opened the world's first fully automated metro (VAL) in 1983.
