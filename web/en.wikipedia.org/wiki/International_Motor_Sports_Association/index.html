The International Motor Sports Association (IMSA) is a North American sports car racing sanctioning body based in Daytona Beach, Florida. It was founded in 1969 by John Bishop, a former executive director of the SCCA, and Bill France Sr., the founder of NASCAR. IMSA is owned by NASCAR and sanctions the IMSA SportsCar Championship, among several other series.

The Rolex 24 at Daytona opens the IMSA SportsCar Championship season.

History

IMSA began by sanctioning Formula Vee and Formula Ford support events, launching its GT Championship in 1971. Through the 1970s and 1980s the Camel GT series became famous for production-based GT cars and the spectacular GTP prototypes of the 1980s. Financial troubles and rival series fragmented American sports car racing in the 1990s; the Grand-Am Rolex Sports Car Series and the American Le Mans Series competed until their 2012 merger announcement reunified the sport under IMSA from 2014, creating the United SportsCar Championship, now the IMSA SportsCar Championship (branded with WeatherTech as title sponsor).

Series sanctioned

  • IMSA SportsCar Championship — featuring GTP (LMDh hybrid prototypes), LMP2, GTD Pro and GTD classes, with marquee events at Daytona, Sebring, Watkins Glen, and Road Atlanta (Petit Le Mans).
  • Michelin Pilot Challenge — production-based GS and TCR touring cars.
  • VP Racing SportsCar Challenge, Mazda MX-5 Cup, and single-make series for Ferrari, Lamborghini, and Porsche Carrera Cup North America.
  • IMSA Prototype Challenge and historic racing events.

Convergence

Since 2023, IMSA's GTP class uses LMDh cars eligible for the FIA World Endurance Championship and the 24 Hours of Le Mans, a convergence agreement with the ACO and FIA intended to unify global prototype racing.
