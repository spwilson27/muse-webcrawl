Omamori

Omamori (お守り) are Japanese amulets commonly sold at Shinto shrines and Buddhist temples, dedicated to particular kami or Buddhist figures and said to provide various forms of luck and protection. The word mamori (守り) means "protection", with the honorific prefix o-. An omamori typically consists of a small brocade pouch containing a prayer or religious inscription; the pouch is not meant to be opened, as doing so is traditionally said to negate its protective benefit.

Uses and types

Omamori are made for specific purposes, including:

  • kōtsū-anzen — traffic safety, for drivers and travellers
  • gōkaku-kigan — success in examinations, for students
  • anzan — safe childbirth
  • kanai-anzen — safety of the household
  • shōbai-hanjō — prosperity in business
  • en-musubi — luck in love and relationships
  • byōki-heyu — recovery from illness

Amulets are often attached to bags, carried in pockets, hung in cars or placed in homes. Generic amulets, local designs and character-themed versions are sold at shrines and temples throughout Japan, and similar amulets exist across East and Southeast Asia.

Customs

Omamori are traditionally obtained for a donation at a shrine or temple, and are often given as gifts, for instance to students before examinations. It is customary to return an old omamori to the shrine or temple where it was obtained after about a year, where it is ritually burned in ceremonies such as the New Year fires, so that the protective spirit is respectfully released and thanks given.
