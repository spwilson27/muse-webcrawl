UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists

The UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists are maintained by UNESCO under the 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage. Their purpose is to ensure better protection of important intangible cultural heritages — living traditions including oral traditions, performing arts, social practices, rituals, festive events, knowledge concerning nature and the universe, and traditional craftsmanship — and to raise awareness of their significance.

The lists

The Convention establishes two principal lists. The Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, first inscribed in 2008, recognises elements that help demonstrate the diversity of cultural heritage; it now contains more than 700 elements from over 140 countries. The List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding covers elements whose viability is at risk despite community efforts. A separate register highlights programmes and projects that best reflect the Convention's principles of safeguarding.

Background and notable elements

Before the 2003 Convention, UNESCO proclaimed 90 "Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity" between 2001 and 2005; these were incorporated into the Representative List in 2008. Well-known inscriptions include the Mediterranean diet, tango, flamenco, the Traditional Mexican cuisine, yoga, falconry (a multinational file), reggae music, and the art of the Neapolitan pizzaiuolo. Inscription requires states to demonstrate community participation and present safeguarding measures, emphasising that the lists celebrate living practice rather than freezing traditions.
