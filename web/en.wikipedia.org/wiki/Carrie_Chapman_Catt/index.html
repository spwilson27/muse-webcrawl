Carrie Chapman Catt (born Carrie Clinton Lane; 9 January 1859 – 9 March 1947) was an American women's suffrage leader who campaigned for the Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution, which gave US women the right to vote in 1920. She served as president of the National American Woman Suffrage Association (NAWSA) and founded the League of Women Voters.

Early life

Raised in Iowa, Catt graduated from Iowa State Agricultural College — the only woman in her class — and worked as a teacher and school superintendent, one of the first women in the United States to hold such a post. After the death of her first husband, Leo Chapman, and her marriage to engineer George Catt, whose support enabled her activism, she devoted herself to the suffrage movement.

Suffrage leadership

Catt succeeded Susan B. Anthony as president of NAWSA in 1900, serving until 1904 and again from 1915. Her "Winning Plan" of 1916 coordinated state campaigns with a push for a federal amendment, a strategy credited with securing passage of the Nineteenth Amendment in 1919 and ratification in 1920. She also founded the International Woman Suffrage Alliance in 1902, uniting suffragists across dozens of countries. In 1920 she founded the League of Women Voters to educate newly enfranchised women.

Later life

In the 1920s and 1930s Catt was a prominent peace activist, founding the National Committee on the Cause and Cure of War. She publicly protested Nazi persecution of Jews in 1933. She died in 1947 and is buried in Woodlawn Cemetery in the Bronx.
