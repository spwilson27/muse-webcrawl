Higashiyama-ku, Kyoto

Higashiyama (東山区, Higashiyama-ku) is one of the eleven wards of the city of Kyoto, in Kyoto Prefecture, Japan. Its name means "eastern mountain district", referring to the Higashiyama mountains along its eastern edge. The ward stretches from the Kamo River eastward to the hills and contains much of Kyoto's best-preserved historic streetscape.

Sights

Higashiyama is home to the famous geisha district of Gion, with its traditional wooden machiya teahouses along Hanamikoji street, and to major temples and shrines including Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka Shrine, Kodai-ji, Kennin-ji and the Kyoto National Museum. The preserved lanes of Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka, lined with shops and cafes in traditional buildings, are among the most visited streets in Japan.

Despite its fame, the ward has the smallest population of Kyoto's wards — roughly 38,000 residents — partly because much of its area is taken up by temples, historic preservation districts and parkland.
