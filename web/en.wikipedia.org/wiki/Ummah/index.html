Ummah

Ummah (Arabic: أُمَّة, romanised ʾumma, meaning "community") is an Arabic word used in Islam to denote the worldwide community of Muslims, bound together by their faith across national, ethnic and cultural lines. The concept is central to Islamic political and social thought.

In the Quran and early Islam

The word ummah occurs over sixty times in the Quran, applied not only to the Muslim community but also to earlier religious communities, to groups of people generally, and occasionally to a period of time or an exemplary individual. After the Hijra (622 CE), Muhammad organised the inhabitants of Medina — Muslims, Jews and pagans — under the document known as the Constitution of Medina, which some scholars describe as founding an ummah that initially transcended religion before the term narrowed to the Muslim community alone.

Later usage

Throughout Islamic history the ideal of a single ummah coexisted with political fragmentation into rival caliphates, sultanates and nation-states. The concept underlies ideas of Muslim solidarity and institutions such as the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, while in Arabic it is also used in a secular sense for "nation", as in al-Umam al-Muttaḥida, the United Nations.
