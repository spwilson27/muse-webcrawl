Wilhelm II

Wilhelm II (Friedrich Wilhelm Viktor Albert; 27 January 1859 – 4 June 1941) was the last German Emperor (Kaiser) and King of Prussia, reigning from 15 June 1888 until his abdication on 9 November 1918 in the wake of Germany's defeat in the First World War. He was the eldest grandchild of Queen Victoria and the son of Frederick III and Victoria, Princess Royal.

Reign

Wilhelm came to the throne in the "Year of the Three Emperors", after the deaths of his grandfather Wilhelm I and his father Frederick III in 1888. In 1890 he dismissed Chancellor Otto von Bismarck and embarked on a more personal, assertive rule. His foreign policy — including Weltpolitik, a challenge to British naval supremacy through a large battle fleet, and episodes such as the Kruger telegram and the Moroccan crises — strained Germany's relations with Britain, France and Russia.

First World War and abdication

During the July Crisis of 1914 Wilhelm supported Austria-Hungary's confrontation with Serbia, and Germany declared war on Russia and France. As the war turned against Germany, real power passed to the military high command of Hindenburg and Ludendorff. In November 1918, amid revolution at home, Chancellor Max von Baden announced Wilhelm's abdication on 9 November; Wilhelm fled to the Netherlands, where he formalised the abdication later that month. The Dutch government refused Allied demands to extradite him for trial.

Exile and death

Wilhelm lived in exile at Huis Doorn in the Netherlands, writing his memoirs and following German politics with increasing bitterness. He died there on 4 June 1941 and was buried at Doorn, during the German occupation of the Netherlands.
