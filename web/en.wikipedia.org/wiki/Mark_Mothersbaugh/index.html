Mark Allen Mothersbaugh (born 18 May 1950) is an American musician, composer and artist. He achieved fame as the co-founder, lead singer and keyboardist of the new wave band Devo, known for songs such as "Whip It" (1980) and for its satirical concept of "de-evolution". Since the late 1980s he has also built a prolific career as a composer for film, television and video games.

Devo

Mothersbaugh formed Devo in Akron, Ohio in the early 1970s with Gerald Casale and others, influenced by the Kent State shootings of 1970, which he witnessed as a student. The band's angular music, matching stage costumes — including the red "energy dome" hats — and subversive humour made it a distinctive part of the new wave era. Devo's albums include Q: Are We Not Men? A: We Are Devo! (1978) and Freedom of Choice (1980).

Scoring career

Through his production company Mutato Muzika, Mothersbaugh has scored numerous television series, including Pee-wee's Playhouse and the animated series Rugrats, and many films, among them Wes Anderson's Bottle Rocket, Rushmore, The Royal Tenenbaums and The Life Aquatic with Steve Zissou; The Lego Movie (2014); and Marvel's Thor: Ragnarok (2017). He has also composed music for video games, including the Crash Bandicoot and Jak and Daxter series, and is a visual artist whose work has been exhibited internationally. See also Devo.
