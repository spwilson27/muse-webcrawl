Heidelberg School

The Heidelberg School was an Australian art movement of the late 19th century, named after the village of Heidelberg near Melbourne where its artists camped and painted. Closely associated with Australian impressionism, its leading figures — Tom Roberts, Frederick McCubbin, Arthur Streeton and Charles Conder — painted the Australian landscape en plein air, capturing the brilliant light, blue distances and eucalyptus bush that earlier colonial art had rendered in European tones.

Streeton's Golden Summer, Eaglemont (1889) epitomises the Heidelberg style.

Origins

In 1885 Tom Roberts returned from Europe advocating outdoor painting in the manner of the French impressionists and the Barbizon School. From 1888 Roberts, McCubbin, Streeton and others established camps at Box Hill and then at Eaglemont, near Heidelberg, where they painted directly from nature, often on small cedar cigar-box panels. In August 1889 they held the famous 9 by 5 Impression Exhibition in Melbourne — named for the dimensions (in inches) of many of the works — which announced the new movement to the public; it was seen as both a provocation and a landmark.

Themes and style

Heidelberg paintings celebrated a distinctive national landscape and way of life: sunlit sheep stations in Streeton's Shearing the Rams (1890), the pioneering bush life in McCubbin's triptych The Pioneer (1904), and heroic pastoral labour. Yet the work appeared just as Australia's population became predominantly urban; the bush imagery became a founding national mythology in the lead-up to Federation in 1901. Roberts' The Big Picture (1903), depicting the opening of the first federal Parliament, crowned the school's national role.

Legacy

The Heidelberg School is generally regarded as the first distinctively Australian movement in Western art, and its works anchor the collections of the National Gallery of Victoria and the National Gallery of Australia. Though later modernists reacted against its nationalism, Roberts, McCubbin, Streeton and Conder remain among Australia's most celebrated painters.
