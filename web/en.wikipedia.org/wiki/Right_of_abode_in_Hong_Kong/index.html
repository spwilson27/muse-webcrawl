Right of abode in Hong Kong

The right of abode in Hong Kong is the status of a Hong Kong permanent resident under Article 24 of the Basic Law and the Immigration Ordinance. Permanent residents enjoy the rights to land, to enter and remain without restriction, and not to be deported from the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

Eligible categories

Categories include: Chinese citizens born in Hong Kong; Chinese citizens ordinarily resident in Hong Kong for a continuous period of at least seven years; persons of Chinese nationality born outside Hong Kong to such residents; non-Chinese citizens who entered with valid travel documents, ordinarily resided for seven continuous years and taken Hong Kong as their place of permanent residence; and children born in Hong Kong to permanent residents under the pre-2001 rules.

Litigation

The scope of the right was the subject of landmark litigation after the 1997 handover, including the Ng Ka Ling case (1999), in which the Court of Final Appeal's interpretation was referred to the NPC Standing Committee, and the later Vallejos case (2013) concerning foreign domestic helpers, who are excluded from "ordinary residence". Non-Chinese permanent residents lose the status (retaining a right to land) after 36 months' continuous absence.
