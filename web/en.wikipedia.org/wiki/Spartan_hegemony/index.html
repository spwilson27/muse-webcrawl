Spartan hegemony

The Spartan hegemony period of Greek history covers the years 404 to 371 BC, when Sparta was the dominant military land-power of the Greek world following its victory over Athens in the Peloponnesian War. Under commanders such as Lysander and King Agesilaus II, Sparta imposed oligarchic governments and garrisons on former Athenian allies, intervened throughout Greece and western Asia Minor, and briefly stood as the arbiter of the Greek states.

Establishment

After Athens surrendered in 404 BC, Sparta dismantled the Athenian empire, installed the Thirty Tyrants in Athens, and set up decarchies (boards of ten) loyal to Lysander in many cities. Sparta's attempt to support Cyrus the Younger against Artaxerxes II of Persia (401 BC) led to a decade of campaigning in Asia Minor under Agesilaus. The resulting Corinthian War (395–386 BC) pitted Sparta against Thebes, Athens, Corinth, and Argos; it ended with the King's Peace (Peace of Antalcidas) of 387/386 BC, dictated with Persian backing, which guaranteed the autonomy of the Greek cities while confirming Persian control of the Greeks of Asia — terms Sparta then enforced as prostates (champion) of the peace, using it as a pretext to break up rival leagues and seize the Theban Cadmea in 382 BC.

Collapse

Spartan high-handedness provoked the formation of the Boeotian League under Thebes. After Athens and Thebes defeated Sparta at sea and land in the 370s, the Theban general Epaminondas crushed the Spartan army at the Battle of Leuctra in 371 BC, shattering the myth of Spartan invincibility and ending the hegemony. The Theban invasion of Laconia and the liberation of Messenia that followed permanently crippled Spartan power, and the Theban hegemony succeeded Sparta's.
