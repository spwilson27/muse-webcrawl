Keigo

Placeholder image: formal Japanese business interaction.

Keigo (敬語, literally "respectful language") is the system of honorific speech registers in the Japanese language. It encodes the relative social status of the speaker, the listener, and the person being spoken about, and is an essential part of polite communication in business and formal settings in Japan.

Categories

Japanese government guidelines divide keigo into five categories:

  • Sonkeigo (尊敬語) — respectful language that elevates the listener or the person referred to.
  • Kenjōgo (謙譲語) — humble language that lowers the speaker or the speaker's in-group.
  • Teichōgo (丁重語) — courteous language used to speak politely without elevating or humbling a specific person.
  • Teineigo (丁寧語) — polite language, including the desu/masu forms.
  • Bikago (美化語) — beautification, such as adding the prefix o- or go- to nouns.

Usage

Keigo employs special verb forms (for example irassharu for "to be/go/come"), suppletive nouns, and honorific prefixes. Mastery is expected of employees in customer-facing roles, and mistakes can cause social embarrassment; language training in keigo is a standard part of new-employee induction in Japan.

Historical development

The honorific system developed from court language of the Heian period and became increasingly codified during the Edo and Meiji periods. Post-war language reforms simplified some aspects, but keigo remains a complex and socially significant feature of Japanese.
