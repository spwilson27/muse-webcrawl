Dragon Boat Festival

Also calledDuanwu Festival; Tuen Ng; Double Fifth
Date5th day of the 5th month of the Chinese lunisolar calendar
ObservancesDragon boat racing; eating zongzi; hanging mugwort and calamus

The Dragon Boat Festival (Chinese: 端午節, Duānwǔ jié) is a traditional Chinese holiday occurring on the fifth day of the fifth month of the Chinese lunisolar calendar, corresponding to late May or June in the Gregorian calendar. It is a public holiday in mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau, and related festivals are observed in Korea (Dano), Japan (Tango no sekku) and Vietnam (Tết Đoan Ngọ).

Origins

The best-known origin story commemorates Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BC), a poet and minister of the state of Chu who drowned himself in the Miluo River in protest against corruption after his king allied with the rising state of Qin. According to legend, local people raced out in boats to save him or recover his body, and dropped balls of sticky rice into the water so that fish would eat them instead of the poet's body — the origin of both dragon boat racing and zongzi. Other traditions link the festival to the filial daughter Cao E, to the statesman Wu Zixu, or to older midsummer rites for warding off disease and evil spirits at the season when pestilence peaked.

Customs

Dragon boat racing, in long narrow boats decorated with dragon heads and tails and paddled to the beat of a drum, is the festival's signature activity and is now an international competitive sport. Foods include zongzi — glutinous rice dumplings wrapped in bamboo or reed leaves — and, in some regions, realgar wine. People hang mugwort and calamus on doors, wear perfumed sachets and five-colour threads, and children may have a character written on their foreheads to ward off evil. In 2009 the festival was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, the first Chinese festival so honoured.
