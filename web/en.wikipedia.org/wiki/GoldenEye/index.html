GoldenEye

GoldenEye is a 1995 spy film and the seventeenth entry in the James Bond series produced by Eon Productions. It is the first film to star Pierce Brosnan as James Bond, and the first Bond film made after the dissolution of the Soviet Union, with a plot centred on the theft of a satellite-based electromagnetic-pulse weapon, the "GoldenEye". Directed by Martin Campbell, it revitalised the franchise after a six-year hiatus and grossed over US$350 million worldwide.

Plot

In 1986 Bond and fellow agent Alec Trevelyan (006) infiltrate a Soviet chemical weapons facility; Trevelyan is apparently executed by Colonel Ourumov. Nine years later, Bond investigates the theft of a Tiger helicopter and the destruction of the Severnaya satellite control station by the GoldenEye weapon — two satellites capable of firing electromagnetic pulses. He discovers that the Janus crime syndicate is led by the still-living Trevelyan, a Lienz Cossack descendant seeking revenge on Britain, who plans to use GoldenEye to rob the Bank of England and erase its records. With programmer Natalya Simonova, Bond destroys Janus's Cuban satellite base and kills Trevelyan.

Production and reception

The film introduced Judi Dench as M — the first woman in the role — and features famous set pieces including the opening bungee jump from the Verzasca (Contra) Dam in Switzerland and a tank chase through St Petersburg. The title derives from Ian Fleming's Jamaican estate, Goldeneye, where he wrote the Bond novels. The accompanying Nintendo 64 game, GoldenEye 007 (1997), became a landmark first-person shooter and one of the best-selling games for the console. Critics welcomed the film as a successful modernisation of the series, and it restored the Bond franchise commercially after the disappointing returns of Licence to Kill (1989).
