Zest (ingredient)

Zest is a food ingredient prepared by scraping or peeling the outer, coloured skin of unwaxed citrus fruit such as lemon, orange, lime and citron. Lemon zest is the most common form. The zest contains the fruit's aromatic essential oils, concentrated in oil glands in the flavedo, and is used to add intense citrus flavour to dishes without the acidity or liquid of the juice. The white pith (albedo) beneath is bitter and is generally avoided when zesting.

Preparation

Zest is taken with a dedicated zester, which cuts thin shreds; with a microplane grater, which produces a fine, fluffy zest suited to baking; with a vegetable peeler, for wide strips used in cocktails and infusions (a lemon "twist"); or with a paring knife or channel knife. For baking, the zest is often rubbed into sugar or creamed with butter to release its oils. Candied zest, blanched and simmered in syrup, is used in fruitcakes and confectionery, and dried zest appears in spice blends.

Culinary uses

Lemon zest flavours cakes, biscuits, puddings and lemon curd; savoury dishes such as gremolata (zest, parsley and garlic, served with osso buco); risottos and pasta; and drinks from lemonade to cocktails, where an expressed twist of peel perfumes the surface of the drink. Zest from other citrus fruits — orange, lime, yuzu and grapefruit — is used similarly in their respective cuisines.

Chemistry

The characteristic aroma of lemon zest comes largely from limonene, which makes up the great majority of lemon peel oil, together with citral and smaller amounts of other terpenes. Cold-pressed lemon oil, extracted commercially from the peel, is used in food flavouring, perfumery and cleaning products.
