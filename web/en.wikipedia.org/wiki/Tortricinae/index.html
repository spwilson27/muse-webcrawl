The Tortricinae are the nominate subfamily of the moth family Tortricidae. It is one of the three generally recognised subfamilies of the family, alongside the Chlidanotinae and Olethreutinae, and contains a large share of the classic "leafroller" moths whose caterpillars shelter in rolled or folded leaves.

Characteristics

Members of the Tortricinae share structural features of the wing venation and genitalia used by lepidopterists to define the group. The larvae typically feed externally on foliage, tying leaves together with silk, though some feed in buds, shoots or flowers. Many species are polyphagous, feeding on a wide range of trees, shrubs and herbaceous plants.

Tribes and notable genera

The subfamily is divided into tribes including the Tortricini, Archipini, Cochylini, Cnephasiini, Sparganothidini and others. Economically and ecologically notable genera include:

  • Choristoneura – the spruce budworms, among the most destructive forest insects in North America, whose outbreaks defoliate vast areas of spruce and fir.
  • Archips – fruit-tree leafrollers.
  • Pandemis – leafrollers of orchards and gardens.
  • Acleris – a large genus of mostly northern species.
  • Cochylis and relatives (Cochylini, sometimes treated as a separate subfamily) – often flower- and seed-feeders.

Ecology

Tortricine larvae are important herbivores in temperate forests and orchards and form a major food source for insectivorous birds and parasitoid wasps. Outbreak species such as the spruce budworm undergo cyclical population explosions that reshape forest composition.
