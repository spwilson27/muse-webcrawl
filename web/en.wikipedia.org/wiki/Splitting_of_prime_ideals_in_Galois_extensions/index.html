Splitting of prime ideals in Galois extensions

The splitting of prime ideals in Galois extensions is a central topic in algebraic number theory. Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group G, and let 𝔭 be a prime ideal of the ring of integers of K. In the ring of integers of L, the ideal 𝔭 factors as a product of prime ideals 𝔓 lying above it. Because G acts transitively on these primes, they all have the same ramification index e and the same residue degree f, and the factorisation has the uniform shape 𝔭 = (𝔓1···𝔓g)e, with efg = [L : K].

Decomposition and inertia groups

The stabiliser of a chosen prime 𝔓 in G is its decomposition group D𝔓, of order ef. Inside it sits the inertia group I𝔓, of order e, consisting of automorphisms acting trivially on the residue field at 𝔓. The quotient D𝔓/I𝔓 is isomorphic to the Galois group of the residue field extension, which is cyclic, generated by the Frobenius automorphism. For an unramified prime (e = 1), the decomposition group itself is cyclic and is generated by the Frobenius element at 𝔭; its conjugacy class in G, the Frobenius class, determines how 𝔭 splits — 𝔭 splits completely if and only if the Frobenius element is the identity.

Consequences

This connection between prime splitting and group theory underlies the Chebotarev density theorem, which says primes are distributed among conjugacy classes of G in proportion to class size, and it culminates in class field theory's description of splitting in abelian extensions by congruence conditions. Examples computed by hand — such as how rational primes split in quadratic fields according to the Legendre symbol, or in cyclotomic fields according to their residue class modulo the conductor — are the classical special cases of the theory.
