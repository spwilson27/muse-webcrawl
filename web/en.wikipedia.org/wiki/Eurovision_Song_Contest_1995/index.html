Eurovision Song Contest 1995

The Eurovision Song Contest 1995 was the 40th edition of the Eurovision Song Contest. It took place in Dublin, Ireland, following the country's victory at the 1994 contest with the song "Rock 'n' Roll Kids" performed by Paul Harrington and Charlie McGettigan. The contest was held at the Point Theatre on Saturday 13 May 1995 and was hosted by Canadian-Irish television presenter Mary Kennedy.

Eurovision Song Contest 1995

Dates: Final – 13 May 1995

Host venue: Point Theatre, Dublin, Ireland

Presenter: Mary Kennedy

Host broadcaster: RTÉ

Winner: Norway – "Nocturne" performed by Secret Garden

Participating countries: 23

Background

Ireland hosted the contest for the sixth time, and for the second consecutive year, after an unprecedented run of three straight Irish victories in 1992, 1993 and 1994. RTÉ reportedly considered declining to host for financial reasons, but ultimately staged the event at Dublin's Point Theatre, which had also hosted the 1994 contest.

Participating countries

Twenty-three countries took part. Belgium, Denmark, Israel, Slovenia and Turkey returned after being relegated following the 1993 contest, while Estonia, Finland, Lithuania, Netherlands, Romania, Slovakia and Switzerland were relegated and did not participate. No new countries debuted in 1995.

Results

The winner was Norway with the song "Nocturne", performed by the Irish-Norwegian duo Secret Garden. The song is notable for containing only 24 words of lyrics, with the bulk of the performance consisting of an instrumental violin interlude played by Fionnuala Sherry. It was Norway's second victory in the contest. Spain finished second with Anabel Conde's "Vuelve conmigo", and Sweden's Jan Johansen placed third with "Se på mej". Ireland, the host nation, finished 14th with Eddie Friel's "Dreamin'".

Voting

Each participating country awarded 12, 10, 8 and 7 down to 1 points to its ten favourite songs, determined by national juries. Norway took an early lead in the voting and held it throughout the evening.

Notable entries

The Greek entry, Elina Konstantopoulou's "Pia prosefhi", and the German entry, Stone & Stone's "Verliebt in Dich", which finished last with a single point, were among the talking points of the evening. The contest also saw the interval act featuring Irish performers including Clannad and Brian Kennedy, performing the specially composed piece "The Voice of the Continent"-style celebration of forty years of the contest.
