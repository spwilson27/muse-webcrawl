Adolf Hurwitz (26 March 1859 – 18 November 1919) was a German mathematician who worked in algebra, analysis, geometry and number theory. He spent the latter half of his career at the ETH Zurich, where his lecture notes influenced generations of students.

Born: 26 March 1859, Hildesheim, Kingdom of Hanover
Died: 18 November 1919, Zurich, Switzerland
Institutions: University of Königsberg; ETH Zurich

Life

Hurwitz studied at the universities of Munich, Berlin and Leipzig, receiving his doctorate under Felix Klein in 1881 with a dissertation on modular functions. After habilitation at Göttingen he became a professor at Königsberg in 1884, where he befriended David Hilbert and Hermann Minkowski. In 1892 he moved to the Eidgenössisches Polytechnikum (now ETH Zurich), where he remained until his death in 1919.

Work

Hurwitz is remembered for the Hurwitz zeta function, a generalisation of the Riemann zeta function; the Hurwitz stability criterion in control theory (developed independently by Edward Routh); Hurwitz's theorem on the finiteness of automorphism groups of Riemann surfaces, bounding them by 84(g − 1) for genus g ≥ 2; and his theorem that the only normed division algebras over the reals are the reals, the complex numbers, the quaternions and the octonions. He also studied continued fractions, Diophantine approximation (Hurwitz's theorem on irrational numbers) and Fourier analysis.
