Berserker hypothesis

The berserker hypothesis, also known as the deadly probes scenario, is the idea that self-replicating interstellar probes — sometimes called von Neumann probes — destroy intelligent life that they encounter, and that this explains the Fermi paradox: the apparent absence of extraterrestrial civilisations despite the vast number of stars and planets.

Concept

Under the hypothesis, a civilisation (or machine intelligence) creates autonomous, self-replicating spacecraft programmed to seek out and exterminate emerging intelligent life, perhaps to eliminate future competition or as a pre-emptive defence. Because such probes could replicate exponentially using raw materials found in target systems, a relatively small initial fleet could, over geological timescales, sterilise much of a galaxy.

The name derives from the Berserker series of science fiction stories by Fred Saberhagen, in which robotic war machines left over from an ancient conflict roam space destroying all life. Related ideas appear in discussions of "deadly probes" by astrophysicist Richard Gott and others, and the scenario is one of several proposed resolutions of the Fermi paradox, alongside the Great Filter and zoo hypotheses.

Implications

If berserker probes exist, deliberately broadcasting Earth's location through active SETI transmissions (METI) could be dangerous, an argument raised by critics of messaging extraterrestrial intelligence. No evidence for such probes has been found within the Solar System, where they might be expected to lurk if the hypothesis were true.
