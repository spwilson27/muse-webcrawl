Orange Free State
Oranje-Vrijstaat (Dutch)
Capital: Bloemfontein
Established: 23 February 1854
Dissolved: 31 May 1902
Currency: Orange Free State pound

The Orange Free State (Dutch: Oranje-Vrijstaat) was an independent Boer republic in southern Africa during the second half of the 19th century, which ceased to exist after it was defeated and surrendered to the British Empire at the end of the Second Boer War in 1902. A British colony of the same name existed between 1902 and 1910, after which it became the Free State Province of the Union of South Africa.

History

The territory was settled by Voortrekkers who had left the Cape Colony during the Great Trek. After the Battle of Boomplaats in 1848, the British proclaimed the Orange River Sovereignty, but withdrew under the Bloemfontein Convention of 1854, recognising the republic's independence. Its capital, Bloemfontein, grew around a fort established in 1846.

The republic maintained generally good relations with the South African Republic (Transvaal). Diamonds discovered near the Vaal River in the late 1860s led to border disputes with the British and the Griqua, and in 1871 Britain annexed the diamond fields at Kimberley.

Second Boer War

Under President Martinus Theunis Steyn, the Orange Free State allied with the Transvaal in 1899. British forces occupied Bloemfontein in March 1900 and annexed the territory as the Orange River Colony. Guerrilla resistance continued under generals such as Christiaan de Wet until the Treaty of Vereeniging in May 1902 ended the war.

Geography and people

The republic lay between the Orange and Vaal rivers, a land of open grassland (veld) suited to stock farming. Its population consisted largely of Dutch-speaking Afrikaner farmers, alongside Basotho and other African peoples whose labour underpinned the farming economy.
