Public holidays in the United States

Public holidays in the United States include eleven federal holidays established by Congress in 5 U.S.C. § 6103, on which federal offices close and federal employees receive paid leave. Federal holidays do not bind state governments or private employers, though most states observe the same list. The Uniform Monday Holiday Act of 1968 moved several holidays to Mondays to create long weekends.

Inauguration Day is an additional holiday for federal workers in the Washington, DC area every four years. States add their own observances, such as Patriots' Day in Massachusetts and Maine, Cesar Chavez Day in California, and state Confederate memorial days in parts of the South. Holidays falling on a weekend are observed by federal employees on the adjacent Friday or Monday.
