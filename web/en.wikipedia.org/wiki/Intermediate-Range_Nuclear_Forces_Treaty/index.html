Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty

Signed8 December 1987, Washington, D.C.
Effective1 June 1988
Expiry2 August 2019 (United States withdrawal)
SignatoriesRonald Reagan (United States); Mikhail Gorbachev (Soviet Union)

The Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty (INF Treaty) was an arms control treaty between the United States and the Soviet Union (and later Russia) signed in Washington, D.C. on 8 December 1987 by President Ronald Reagan and Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev. The treaty banned all land-based ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and missile launchers with ranges of 500 to 5,500 kilometres (310 to 3,420 miles), whether armed with nuclear or conventional warheads. Sea-launched and air-launched systems were not covered.

Background

The treaty arose from the "Euromissile crisis" of the late 1970s and 1980s. The Soviet deployment of SS-20 intermediate-range missiles prompted NATO's 1979 Double-Track Decision to deploy American Pershing II ballistic missiles and ground-launched cruise missiles in Western Europe while pursuing negotiations — a deployment that triggered mass protests across Europe. Reagan's "zero option" proposal matured, after the 1986 Reykjavík Summit, into an agreement to eliminate the entire class of weapons worldwide.

Provisions and implementation

By the treaty's deadline of 1 June 1991, the two sides had destroyed a total of 2,692 missiles (846 American and 1,846 Soviet), along with their launchers and support equipment. The treaty established an unprecedented regime of on-site inspections, which continued for thirteen years. It was the first arms control agreement to eliminate an entire category of nuclear weapons, and is credited with helping to end the Cold War.

Collapse

From 2014 the United States accused Russia of violating the treaty by developing and deploying the 9M729 (SSC-8) ground-launched cruise missile with a prohibited range; Russia denied the charge and made counter-allegations about American systems. The United States suspended its obligations on 2 February 2019 and formally withdrew on 2 August 2019, ending the treaty. Both countries subsequently developed previously banned missile classes, and the episode is often cited as a marker of the erosion of the Cold War arms control architecture.
