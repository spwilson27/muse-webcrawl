David Benjamin Kaplan (born 17 September 1933) is an American philosopher, a professor at the University of California, Los Angeles. His work in the philosophy of language and philosophical logic, especially his theory of indexicals and demonstratives, is among the most influential in analytic philosophy.

Work

Kaplan distinguished between the character of an expression — the meaning that determines its content in a context — and its content in that context. For the indexical "I", the character is a rule taking any context to the speaker of that context, while the content is the person referred to. These ideas were developed in his celebrated 1977 manuscript "Demonstratives", circulated for years before publication in 1989, which reshaped the semantics of indexical expressions and direct reference. Kaplan also made foundational contributions to quantified modal logic, free logic and the theory of direct reference for proper names, and is known for his celebrated paradox concerning propositions (Kaplan's paradox).
