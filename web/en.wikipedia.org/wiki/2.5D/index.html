2.5D

2.5D (also written two-and-a-half-dimensional) describes visual presentations and techniques that simulate three-dimensional depth while being built from, or constrained to, two-dimensional elements. The term is most common in video games, animation, graphic design, and machining.

Video games

In gaming, 2.5D commonly refers to games that use 3D graphics but restrict movement or gameplay to a two-dimensional plane, such as side-scrolling platformers rendered with polygonal models. It also describes 2D games that fake depth through isometric or oblique projection, parallax scrolling, and pre-rendered 3D imagery, as in many 1990s titles. Classic examples include Doom, whose engine rendered a fundamentally 2.5D map, and isometric role-playing games such as Diablo.

Other uses

In film and animation the term is used for layered 2D elements arranged in depth, sometimes called parallax or multiplane effects. In manufacturing, "2.5D" machining refers to cutting parts whose features vary in height but lack undercuts, such as engraved plates.
