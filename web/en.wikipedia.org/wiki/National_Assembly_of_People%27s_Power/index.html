National Assembly of People's Power

Type: Unicameral legislature
Country: Cuba
Seats: 470
Meeting place: Palacio de las Convenciones, Havana

The National Assembly of People's Power (Spanish: Asamblea Nacional del Poder Popular) is the supreme organ of state power in Cuba and the country's unicameral legislature under its constitution.

Composition and powers

Deputies are elected for five-year terms from municipal constituencies. The Assembly elects the President of the Republic, the Council of State and the Council of Ministers, enacts legislation, and amends the constitution. It normally meets in short sessions twice a year, with the Council of State exercising many of its functions between sessions.

History

The Assembly was established under the 1976 constitution and first convened that year, replacing earlier revolutionary governing structures. Constitutional reforms in 2019 reduced its size from 605 to 470 deputies and restored the office of Prime Minister. Candidates are nominated through mass organisations and municipal assemblies, and elections are held without competing political parties.
