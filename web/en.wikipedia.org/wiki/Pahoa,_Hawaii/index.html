Pāhoa is a census-designated place and community in the Puna District of Hawaiʻi County, on the island of Hawaiʻi (the "Big Island"), Hawaiʻi, United States. It serves as the commercial hub of lower Puna and is known for its preserved early-twentieth-century wooden storefronts along Pāhoa Village Road, giving the town an old-west character unusual in Hawaii.

History

Pāhoa grew with the sugar and timber industries of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, serving plantations and ʻōhiʻa logging operations; from 1910 it was a station on the Hawaii Consolidated Railway. After the decline of sugar, the town became a centre for the surrounding agricultural and countercultural communities of Puna.

Volcanic hazards

Pāhoa lies within Lava Flow Hazard Zone 2 on the flank of Kīlauea volcano. In 2014 a lava flow from the Puʻu ʻŌʻō vent advanced on the town, destroying one house and threatening the main road and police and fire stations before stalling. During the 2018 lower Puna eruption, lava flows destroyed hundreds of homes in nearby Leilani Estates and Kapoho, and Pāhoa served as an evacuation and relief centre, although the town itself was spared.

Climate and surroundings

The area has a tropical rainforest climate with abundant rainfall. Nearby attractions include Lava Tree State Park, the warm springs at Pohoiki, and the black-sand beaches formed by the 2018 eruption.
