Gabriel Fauré

Gabriel Urbain Fauré
Born: 12 May 1845, Pamiers, France
Died: 4 November 1924, Paris, France
Occupation: Composer, organist, teacher
Notable works: Requiem, Pavane, Pelléas et Mélisande

Gabriel Urbain Fauré (12 May 1845 – 4 November 1924) was a French composer, organist, pianist and teacher. He was one of the foremost French composers of his generation, and his musical style influenced many 20th-century composers. Among his best-known works are his Pavane, Requiem, Sicilienne, nocturnes for piano, and the songs "Après un rêve" and "Clair de lune".

Career

Fauré was trained at the École Niedermeyer in Paris, where Saint-Saëns was among his teachers and became a lifelong friend. He spent decades as a church organist, culminating at La Madeleine in Paris, while composing steadily. In 1896 he became professor of composition at the Paris Conservatoire, where his pupils included Ravel and Nadia Boulanger; he served as the Conservatoire's director from 1905 to 1920, modernising the institution.

Style and late works

His early music is lyrical and harmonically warm; his late style, written as deafness overtook him in his final two decades, is more austere and introspective. His Requiem (1888–1890), unusually gentle in character, remains one of the most beloved settings of the mass for the dead.
