Single Euro Payments Area

TypePayment-integration initiative of the European Union
Members41 countries and territories (EU member states, EEA countries, and others)
CurrencyEuro
Key instrumentsSEPA Credit Transfer, SEPA Direct Debit, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer

The Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) is a payment-integration initiative of the European Union for simplification of bank transfers denominated in euros. Under SEPA, customers can make cashless euro payments to any account located anywhere in the area using a single bank account and a single set of payment instruments, so that cross-border euro payments are as easy as domestic ones.

Scope

SEPA covers all European Union member states, including those that have not adopted the euro, together with Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, and several microstates and territories such as Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, and Vatican City. All SEPA payments are made in euros, even where the account itself is held in another currency.

Payment schemes

The European Payments Council maintains the main SEPA schemes: the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) scheme, launched in 2008; the SEPA Direct Debit (SDD) Core and Business-to-Business schemes, launched in 2009; and the SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) scheme, launched in 2017, which settles payments within seconds around the clock. SEPA transactions are identified by IBAN account numbers, and scheme messages are based on the ISO 20022 standard.

Implementation

In the euro area, migration to SEPA instruments was made mandatory by EU Regulation No 260/2012, with domestic legacy credit transfers and direct debits phased out by 2014. The project grew out of the Lisbon Agenda goal of making the euro area a single domestic market for payments, reducing costs and fragmentation between national payment systems.
