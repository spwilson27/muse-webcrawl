John Charles Turner (7 September 1947 – 23 July 2011) was a British social psychologist whose work on groups and social identity, developed with Henri Tajfel, transformed the study of intergroup relations. He is best known as the principal developer of self-categorization theory.

Career

Turner studied psychology at the University of Bristol, where he completed his PhD under Henri Tajfel. He held posts at Bristol and the University of Oxford before moving to Australia in 1982, first to Macquarie University and then to the Australian National University in Canberra, where he spent most of his career.

Social identity approach

With Tajfel, Turner developed social identity theory, arguing that people's sense of who they are derives in part from the groups to which they belong, and that identification with a group can reshape perception and behaviour. In Rediscovering the Social Group (1987), Turner and colleagues presented self-categorization theory, which explains how people categorize themselves and others at different levels of abstraction — as unique individuals, group members, or human beings — and how group membership becomes psychologically salient ("depersonalization").

Influence

Together dubbed the "social identity approach", these theories have been applied to leadership, organisational behaviour, crowd psychology, health and political mobilisation. His later work, including Social Influence (1991) and collaborative research on power and leadership, argued that influence flows from shared identity rather than merely from personal attributes.
