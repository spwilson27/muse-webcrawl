La Grande Illusion

La Grande Illusion (French for "The Grand Illusion") is a 1937 French war film directed by Jean Renoir, who co-wrote the screenplay with Charles Spaak. Widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made, it examines class, nationality, and the obsolescence of the old European aristocratic order during the First World War.

DirectorJean Renoir
StarringJean Gabin, Pierre Fresnay, Erich von Stroheim, Dita Parlo, Marcel Dalio
Release8 June 1937
Runtime114 minutes
LanguageFrench, German, English

Plot

During the First World War, French aviators Captain de Boëldieu (Pierre Fresnay), an aristocrat, and Lieutenant Maréchal (Jean Gabin), a working-class mechanic, are shot down and taken prisoner by the German ace Captain von Rauffenstein (Erich von Stroheim). Through a series of prisoner-of-war camps they form bonds that cross national lines but are divided by class: Boëldieu shares more in common with his aristocratic captor than with his own compatriots. The film culminates in an escape attempt in which Boëldieu sacrifices himself so Maréchal and the Jewish officer Rosenthal (Marcel Dalio) can reach Switzerland.

Reception and legacy

The film was banned in Nazi Germany and Italy, and won acclaim at the Venice Film Festival. Orson Welles called it one of the films he would take to a desert island. It was the first foreign-language film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Critics consistently place it among the finest films of all time, and its humanist, anti-war message remains influential.

Title

The title derives from Norman Angell's 1910 book The Great Illusion, which argued that war between economically interdependent nations was futile; Renoir extended the "illusion" to the beliefs that war could be noble and that class and racial boundaries divided people more than nationality united them.
