Cumbric

Cumbric was a variety of the Common Brittonic language spoken during the Early Middle Ages in the Hen Ogledd ("Old North") — what is now the counties of Cumbria and northern England and the southern Scottish Lowlands. It was closely related to Old Welsh and more distantly to Cornish and Breton.

Evidence

No continuous Cumbric texts survive. The language is known from place names (such as Carlisle, Penrith, and Glasgow), personal names in charters and saints' lives, a handful of legal terms preserved in the Leges inter Brettos et Scottos, and the traditional shepherds' counting scores of northern England — yan, tan, tethera — which resemble Welsh numerals.

History

Cumbric was the language of the Brittonic kingdoms of Rheged and Strathclyde. After the annexation of Strathclyde by Scotland and the Norman settlement of the north, it declined under pressure from Gaelic, Old English, and Old Norse, becoming extinct probably by the 12th century.

Revival attempts

Since the late 20th century enthusiasts have attempted to reconstruct and revive Cumbric based on Welsh and place-name evidence, though such reconstructions are necessarily speculative.
