Calciopoli (Italian pronunciation: [kalˈtʃɔːpoli]) is the name given to the 2006 Italian football scandal, involving Italy's top professional leagues, Serie A and Serie B. The scandal was uncovered in May 2006 by Italian police, implicating league champions Juventus and other major teams including AC Milan, Fiorentina, Lazio and Reggina in a network of relationships between team managers and referee organisations to influence the appointment of referees.

Background and revelations

The scandal emerged from police wiretaps conducted during a doping investigation at Juventus. Transcripts published by newspapers in May 2006 appeared to show Juventus general manager Luciano Moggi and others exerting influence over referee designations. Moggi resigned, the board of the Italian Football Federation (FIGC) resigned, and a special commissioner was appointed.

Sanctions

Following an accelerated sporting trial in the summer of 2006, Juventus were relegated to Serie B for the first time in their history, stripped of their 2004–05 and 2005–06 Serie A titles (the latter reassigned to Inter Milan), and given a points penalty. Milan, Fiorentina, Lazio and Reggina received points deductions but remained in Serie A. Several executives, most prominently Moggi and Antonio Giraudo, received lengthy bans from football.

Legal aftermath

Criminal proceedings in Naples continued for years; convictions in the first instance were largely overturned or time-barred on appeal, with the Italian Supreme Court ruling in 2015 that the statute of limitations had expired. Sporting and civil litigation over compensation continued, and debate persists in Italy over the evidence and the severity of the sporting sanctions.
