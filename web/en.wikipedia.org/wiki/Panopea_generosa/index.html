Panopea generosa

Pacific geoduck
Scientific namePanopea generosa
Common namePacific geoduck ("gooey-duck")
RangePacific coast, Alaska to Baja California
LifespanUp to ~168 years

Panopea generosa, the Pacific geoduck, is a species of very large saltwater clam in the family Hiatellidae, native to the Pacific coastal waters of western Canada and the northwestern United States. The name "geoduck" derives from a Lushootseed (Nisqually) word meaning "dig deep".

Description

The geoduck is one of the largest burrowing clams in the world, with a shell up to about 20 cm and a long fused siphon ("neck") that can extend a metre or more; adults burrow up to a metre into the sediment and cannot retract the siphon into the shell. It is also among the longest-lived animals known: individuals commonly exceed 100 years, and the oldest recorded was 168 years old, making it valuable for sclerochronology and climate studies.

Fishery

Geoducks support a lucrative fishery in Washington and British Columbia, harvested individually by divers using water jets, and are farmed in intertidal plots. Most of the catch is exported to China and Japan, where the siphon meat is prized in sashimi and hot pot; prices can exceed US$100 per kilogram retail in Asia.
