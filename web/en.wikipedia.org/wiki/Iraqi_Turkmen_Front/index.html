The Iraqi Turkmen Front (Turkish: Irak Türkmen Cephesi; Arabic: الجبهة التركمانية العراقية, al-Jabha al-Turkmāniya al-ʿIrāqiya), abbreviated ITF, is a political movement representing the Iraqi Turkmen people. It was founded on April 24, 1995, as a coalition of several Turkmen parties and organisations, and has historically received support from Turkey.

History and politics

The ITF advocates for the political and cultural rights of the Turkmen, Iraq's third-largest ethnic group, who are concentrated in northern Iraq in a region the movement calls Turkmeneli, including the contested, oil-rich city of Kirkuk. The front opposes Kurdish incorporation of Kirkuk into the Kurdistan Region and supports a special status for the city within a unified Iraq.

After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, the ITF participated in successive elections and held seats in the Council of Representatives and provincial councils. Prominent leaders have included Sadettin Ergeç, Arshad al-Salihi, and Hassan Turan. The movement's constituency has also been affected by the rise of the Islamic State, against which Turkmen communities — Sunni and Shia alike — suffered attacks, including in Tal Afar and Tuz Khurmatu.
