Macanese pataca

The pataca (Chinese: 澳門元; Portuguese: pataca de Macau; sign: MOP$; code: MOP) is the currency of Macau, a special administrative region of China. It has been pegged to the Hong Kong dollar at a rate of HK = MOP 1.03 since 1977, and through it indirectly to the US dollar.

History

The pataca was introduced in 1894 as a unit of account, succeeding earlier use of the Mexican peso and other foreign silver coins whose name in Portuguese gave the currency its title. Banknotes were first issued in 1906 by the Banco Nacional Ultramarino.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 10, 20 and 50 avos and 1, 2, 5 and 10 patacas; banknotes run from 10 to 1,000 patacas and are issued by two commercial banks under the supervision of the Monetary Authority of Macao.
