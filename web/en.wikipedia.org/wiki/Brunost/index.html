Brunost (Norwegian for "brown cheese") is a common Norwegian name for mysost, a family of whey cheeses made by boiling down the whey left over from milk production, often with added milk or cream, until the lactose caramelises and the mixture thickens into a brown, firm, sweet cheese.

Production and varieties

The best-known variety is Gudbrandsdalsost, made from cow's and goat's milk whey, while geitost is made purely from goat's whey. The cheese is traditionally sliced very thinly with a cheese slicer (a Norwegian invention of 1925) and eaten on bread, crispbread or waffles. Brunost is not a true cheese in the rennet-curd sense, since it is made from whey rather than curds.

History and incidents

Whey cheese has been made in Scandinavia for centuries; the modern commercial brunost is credited to Anne Hov of the Gudbrandsdalen valley in the 1860s, who added cream to the whey. In January 2013 a lorry carrying 27 tonnes of brunost caught fire in the Bratli tunnel in northern Norway, and the fat- and sugar-rich cheese burned fiercely for five days, closing the tunnel for weeks.
