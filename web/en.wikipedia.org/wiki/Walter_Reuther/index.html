Walter Philip Reuther (September 1, 1907 – May 9, 1970) was an American leader of organized labor and civil rights activist who built the United Auto Workers (UAW) into one of the most powerful unions in the United States. As the UAW's president from 1946 until his death, he led a series of landmark strikes and bargained industry-defining contracts with the major automobile manufacturers.


Walter Reuther, president of the UAW

Early life and union rise

Reuther was born in Wheeling, West Virginia, to German immigrant parents; his father was a socialist brewery worker and union activist. Walter left school to work in the steel mills, moved to Detroit in 1927 and became a skilled tool-and-die maker at Ford. Fired for union activity, he travelled with his brother Victor through Europe and worked for nearly two years in the Soviet Union at the Gorky automobile plant before returning in 1935. During the 1936–37 Flint sit-down strike era he rose in the young UAW, and he survived beatings by Ford security men in the 1937 "Battle of the Overpass" and two assassination attempts.

UAW presidency

Elected UAW president in 1946, Reuther purged Communist influence from the union and won pattern contracts that included cost-of-living adjustments, pensions and health benefits — the 1950 "Treaty of Detroit" with General Motors became a model for postwar labour relations. He also served as president of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO) and helped engineer its 1955 merger with the AFL, though he later led the UAW out of the AFL-CIO in 1968.

Civil rights and death

Reuther was a leading supporter of the civil rights movement, speaking at the 1963 March on Washington and marching in Selma, and the UAW funded and organised support for Martin Luther King Jr.'s campaigns. He died with his wife May in a plane crash near Pellston, Michigan, in May 1970. In 1995 he was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
