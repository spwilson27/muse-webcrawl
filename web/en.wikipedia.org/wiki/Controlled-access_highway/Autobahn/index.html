Autobahn

The Autobahn (German: Bundesautobahn, literally "federal motorway", plural Autobahnen) is the federal controlled-access highway system in Germany. It is officially termed the Bundesautobahn and is known worldwide for stretches of motorway with no federally mandated general speed limit for some classes of vehicles.

Network

The German autobahn network totals approximately 13,200 kilometres (8,200 miles), one of the densest and longest controlled-access systems in the world. Autobahnen are numbered with the prefix "A" (for example A 7, the longest national motorway route at 962 km), carry at least two lanes per direction separated by a central median, and are accessible only at grade-separated interchanges. Service areas (Raststätten) are spaced at intervals along the network.

Speed limits

About half of the network has no general speed limit, though an advisory speed (Richtgeschwindigkeit) of 130 km/h (81 mph) applies, and permanent or variable limits are imposed near cities, at interchanges, in construction zones, and in poor weather. Vehicles exceeding the advisory speed may bear partial liability after a crash. Trucks are generally limited to 80 km/h. Proposals for a general limit recur in German politics, usually on environmental and safety grounds.

History

The first autobahn-like road was the AVUS in Berlin (opened 1921), and the Cologne–Bonn road (now A 555) opened in 1932 under mayor Konrad Adenauer. The Nazi regime expanded the network as the Reichsautobahnen, partly as a public works program, building several thousand kilometres before the Second World War. Postwar West and East Germany extended their networks, which were unified after 1990.
