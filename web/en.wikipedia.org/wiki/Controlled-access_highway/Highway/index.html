Highway

A highway is any public or private road or other public way on land. It includes major roads, but also minor and rural routes. Highways, in the modern sense, are designed for high-speed vehicular traffic, often with multiple lanes, medians, and regulated access, and they form the principal arteries of national and regional road networks. The term is used differently by jurisdiction: in North America it may denote any major numbered route, while elsewhere "motorway", "freeway", or "expressway" describe the highest class of controlled-access highways.

Types

Road hierarchies range from local streets to collectors, arterials, and freeways. A controlled-access highway (freeway or motorway) prohibits direct property access and at-grade intersections, providing full separation of crossing traffic by interchanges. Divided highways use a median to separate opposing flows, reducing head-on collisions. Some highways are tolled to finance construction and maintenance.

History

Important precedents include Roman roads, the turnpikes of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and early twentieth-century parkways. Germany's autobahnen and Italy's autostradas pioneered purpose-built high-speed roads in the 1920s–30s; the United States' Interstate Highway System, authorized in 1956, became the largest such network, and China's expressway network has since surpassed it in total length.

Effects

Highways reduce journey times and transport costs, support trade and suburban development, and are major national infrastructure investments. Countervailing concerns include traffic noise and air pollution, habitat fragmentation, land consumption, induced demand — in which new capacity generates additional traffic — and the division of communities through which highways pass.
