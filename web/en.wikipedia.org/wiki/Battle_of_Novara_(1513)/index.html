Battle of Novara (1513)

The Battle of Novara, also known as the Battle of Ariotta, was fought on 6 June 1513 near Novara in northern Italy during the War of the League of Cambrai. A French army under Louis II de la Trémoille, besieging Novara, was surprised at dawn and routed by a relieving army of Swiss mercenaries fighting for the Sforza Duke of Milan. It was the last great victory of Swiss infantry in the Italian Wars.

Background

After the French recaptured Milan early in 1513, Massimiliano Sforza took refuge in Novara, defended by a Swiss garrison. Trémoille besieged the town while a fresh Swiss army, raised by the cantons and unpaid, marched to its relief.

The battle

Arriving after a forced march, the Swiss attacked the French camp at dawn without waiting for their artillery. Their pike columns overran the French guns and Landsknecht infantry in savage fighting; French cavalry charges could not save the infantry, and the French army collapsed, abandoning its artillery and baggage.

Aftermath

France was driven from Milan again, and Sforza was restored. Novara confirmed the Swiss reputation as the finest infantry in Europe, but their ascendancy was nearing its end: two years later they were defeated at Marignano (1515), where French artillery and cavalry prevailed.
