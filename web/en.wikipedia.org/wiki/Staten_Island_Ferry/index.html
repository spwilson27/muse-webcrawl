Staten Island Ferry

The Staten Island Ferry is a passenger ferry route operated by the New York City Department of Transportation, running between Whitehall Terminal in Manhattan and St. George Terminal on Staten Island. The 5.2-mile crossing of Upper New York Bay takes about 25 minutes and offers celebrated views of the Statue of Liberty, Ellis Island, and the Manhattan skyline. Since 1997 the service has been free, and it carries roughly 25 million passengers a year, making it one of the busiest ferry systems in the United States.

History

Ferry service between Staten Island and Manhattan dates to the early 18th century; Cornelius Vanderbilt began his career operating such a route as a teenager. Steam ferryboats were introduced in the 19th century under companies including the Staten Island Railway. The city took over the service in 1905, a year after the disastrous sinking of the excursion steamer General Slocum highlighted safety concerns in the harbor. The fare was famously five cents for most of the 20th century; it rose to 50 cents by 1990 before being abolished in 1997.

Service and vessels

The ferry runs 24 hours a day, every day of the year, with departures every 15–20 minutes at peak times and every 30 minutes overnight — over 100 boat trips daily. The fleet's bright orange boats, including the Barberi, Molinari, and newer Ollis classes, are icons of the city. The St. George Terminal connects with the Staten Island Railway; Whitehall Terminal adjoins the South Ferry subway station. The free ride has made the ferry a beloved attraction for tourists as well as a vital commuter link — Staten Island's only direct public-transport connection to Manhattan.
