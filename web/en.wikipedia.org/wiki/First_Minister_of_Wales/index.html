First Minister of Wales

The First Minister of Wales (Welsh: Prif Weinidog Cymru) is the leader of the Welsh Government and keeper of the Welsh Seal. The First Minister chairs the Welsh Cabinet, is responsible for devolved policy, and represents Wales in relations with the UK government and abroad.

Formation1999 (as First Secretary); title from 2000s, statutory 2006
AppointerThe Monarch, on nomination by the Senedd
ResidenceTŷ Hywel, Cardiff (office)

Role and appointment

After each Senedd election, Members of the Senedd nominate a First Minister, whom the monarch appoints; the First Minister then appoints cabinet ministers with the monarch's approval. The office was created by the Government of Wales Act 1998 as "First Secretary" following the 1997 devolution referendum.

Office holders

Alun Michael (1999–2000) was succeeded by Rhodri Morgan (2000–2009), then Carwyn Jones (2009–2018), Mark Drakeford (2018–2024), Vaughan Gething (2024) — the first Black leader of a European nation — and Eluned Morgan (from August 2024), the first woman to hold the post. All have been Welsh Labour leaders.

Powers

Devolved responsibilities include health, education, transport, agriculture and some taxation (Welsh rates of income tax, land transaction tax), while defence, foreign affairs and most welfare remain reserved to Westminster.
