Salento (Salentino: Salentu) is the peninsular "heel" of Italy, the southeasternmost part of the Apulia region, bounded by the Gulf of Taranto to the southwest, the Strait of Otranto, and the Adriatic and Ionian seas. The ancient Greeks knew it as Messapia, land of the Messapii; its culture mingles Italian with a surviving Griko (Greek) linguistic heritage in the Grecìa Salentina towns.

Geography and towns

The low limestone peninsula is covered in ancient olive groves — many trees centuries old, though devastated in recent years by the Xylella fastidiosa bacterium — dry-stone walls, and fortified farmhouses called masserie. The baroque city of Lecce, "the Florence of the South", famed for its golden Lecce stone, is the chief city; other centres include the white city of Ostuni, the ports of Otranto (Italy'e easternmost town), Gallipoli and Brindisi, and Santa Maria di Leuca at the tip, where the Adriatic and Ionian are said to meet.

Culture

Salento is famous for the pizzica, the frenetic folk dance linked to the tarantella and the ritual healing of the taranta spider's "bite", celebrated at the Notte della Taranta festival in Melpignano; for its wines (Primitivo di Manduria, Negroamaro, Salice Salentino); and for cuisine based on olive oil, durum wheat, broad beans and seafood. In recent decades it has become one of Italy's most fashionable summer destinations, the "Maldives of Salento" beaches drawing visitors to both coasts.
