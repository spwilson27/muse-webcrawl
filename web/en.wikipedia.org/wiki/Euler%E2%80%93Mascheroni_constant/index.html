The Euler–Mascheroni constant (also called Euler's constant) is a mathematical constant, usually denoted by the lowercase Greek letter gamma (γ), defined as the limiting difference between the harmonic series and the natural logarithm. Its value is approximately 0.57721.

Definition

The constant arises as the limit of the difference between the nth partial sum of the harmonic series and the natural logarithm of n as n tends to infinity. It also appears as an integral involving the floor function and the exponential integral.

Properties

It is not known whether γ is rational or irrational. The constant appears throughout analysis and number theory, including in the study of the gamma function, the Riemann zeta function, and the distribution of prime numbers via Mertens' theorems.

History

Leonhard Euler introduced the constant in 1734; Lorenzo Mascheroni later computed it to 32 decimal places (with some errors) in 1790. Modern computations have evaluated γ to hundreds of billions of digits.
