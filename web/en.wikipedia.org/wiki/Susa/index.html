Susa

Location: Khuzestan Province, Iran

Region: Mesopotamia / Elam

Type: Settlement

Founded: c. 4200 BC

UNESCO World Heritage Site: Inscribed 2015

Susa was an ancient city in the lower Zagros Mountains about 250 km (160 mi) east of the Tigris, in modern Khuzestan province, Iran. One of the most important cities of the Ancient Near East, Susa served as the capital of Elam and later as a winter capital and administrative centre of the Achaemenid Empire under Darius I and his successors. It remained inhabited through the Parthian, Sasanian, and early Islamic periods before being largely abandoned.

History

Settlement at Susa began around 4200 BC, and the city was a major centre of the Elamite civilisation, rival to the kingdoms of Mesopotamia. The Code of Hammurabi stele was found at Susa, having been carried there as war booty by the Elamite king Shutruk-Nakhunte in the 12th century BC, along with other famous monuments such as the Victory Stele of Naram-Sin. Cyrus the Great took the city in the 6th century BC, and Darius I built an enormous palace complex there, making Susa one of the capitals of the Persian Empire. Alexander the Great captured the city in 331 BC and held the mass Susa weddings there in 324 BC.

Archaeology

The site comprises several mounds, including the Acropolis, the Apadana, and the Royal City. French archaeological missions excavated Susa from the late 19th century onward, recovering the Darius palace inscriptions, glazed-brick reliefs, and the Hammurabi stele now in the Louvre. Susa was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015.
