Vladimir (Russian: Владимир) is a city and the administrative center of Vladimir Oblast, Russia, located on the Klyazma River, 200 kilometers (120 mi) east of Moscow. It is served by a railway and the M7 motorway. Population: 349,951 (2021 census).

Vladimir is one of the medieval capitals of Russia, and two of its cathedrals are among the White Monuments of Vladimir and Suzdal, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city was the capital of the Grand Duchy of Vladimir-Suzdal, one of the most powerful Rus' principalities, before the Mongol invasion of Rus' shifted the balance of power.

History

The founding date of Vladimir is disputed. One tradition attributes it to Vladimir Monomakh in 1108; another, drawing on chronicle entries, holds that the city was founded in 990 by Vladimir the Great, after whom it is named. Archaeological evidence suggests settlement on the site by the late 10th century.

The city rose to prominence under Yuri Dolgorukiy and especially under his son Andrey Bogolyubsky, who moved the capital from Suzdal to Vladimir in 1157 and embarked on an ambitious building program. Under Andrey and his brother Vsevolod III, Vladimir became the political and cultural center of northeastern Rus'. The Mongol invasion of 1238 devastated the city — it was stormed and burned in February of that year — and it never fully recovered its primacy, gradually yielding to Moscow.

Main sights

The Golden Gate (1164), a fortified tower-gate with a chapel, is the sole surviving entrance of the medieval city wall and one of the symbols of Vladimir. The Dormition (Assumption) Cathedral (1158–1160, rebuilt 1185–1189) was the model for later cathedrals in Moscow and once housed the icon of Our Lady of Vladimir; it contains frescoes by Andrei Rublev and Daniil Cherny (1408). The Cathedral of Saint Demetrius (1194–1197) is famed for its white-stone carvings covering its exterior walls.

Geography and transport

Vladimir lies on the left bank of the Klyazma River at an elevation of about 150 meters. The climate is humid continental, with cold winters and warm summers. The city is a stop on the Trans-Siberian Railway route between Moscow and Nizhny Novgorod and a gateway for tourists visiting the Golden Ring of ancient Russian towns.

Economy

Modern Vladimir is a center for food processing, chemical production, electrical engineering, and precision instruments. Tourism associated with the Golden Ring and the UNESCO-listed monuments is a significant contributor to the local economy.
