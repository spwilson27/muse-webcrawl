Baton Rouge, Louisiana

Baton Rouge is the capital city of the U.S. state of Louisiana, located on the eastern bank of the Mississippi River. With about 227,000 residents in the city and roughly 870,000 in its metropolitan area, it is Louisiana's second-largest city after New Orleans and a major industrial, petrochemical, and university centre.

History

French explorer d'Iberville in 1699 recorded a red pole (bâton rouge) marking Native American hunting boundaries, giving the city its name. The site passed among French, British, and Spanish rule; the 1810 West Florida Rebellion briefly made it part of an independent republic before U.S. annexation. Baton Rouge became state capital in 1849, was occupied by Union forces in the Civil War, and regained the capital in 1882. The 1930s Louisiana State Capitol, championed by Huey Long — who was assassinated in its corridor in 1935 — remains the tallest U.S. state capitol.

Economy and education

The Port of Greater Baton Rouge is one of the largest U.S. ports by tonnage, serving petrochemical plants along the river's "Chemical Corridor". Louisiana State University (LSU) and Southern University anchor higher education. The city absorbed many evacuees after Hurricane Katrina in 2005, temporarily becoming Louisiana's largest city.
