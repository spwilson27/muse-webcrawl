Panchet Dam

LocationDhanbad district, Jharkhand, India
RiverDamodar River
OperatorDamodar Valley Corporation
Opened1959
Installed capacity80 MW (2 × 40 MW)

The Panchet Dam is one of the four multi-purpose dams built as part of the first phase of the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC), India's first multi-purpose river valley project, modelled on the Tennessee Valley Authority. It was opened in 1959 and is the last of the four dams (after Tilaiya, Konar and Maithon) constructed across the Damodar River system. Located in Dhanbad district of Jharkhand, near the border with West Bengal, the dam lies at the point where the Damodar emerges from the Chota Nagpur plateau country onto the plains.

Structure and reservoir

The dam is an earthen embankment, about 6.8 km long — among the longer earth dams in India — with a concrete spillway section. Behind it, the Panchet reservoir spreads across the valleys of the Damodar and its tributaries, providing flood moderation for the lower Damodar valley in West Bengal, irrigation supplies, and water storage for the industrial belt around Dhanbad and Asansol.

Power generation

The Panchet hydel power station has an installed capacity of 80 MW from two units of 40 MW each, and it contributes to the DVC grid serving the industrial region of Jharkhand and West Bengal. The dam and reservoir also support fisheries and are a local scenic attraction, particularly at the nearby Panchet hill.

Role in the DVC system

Together with Maithon, Tilaiya and Konar dams, Panchet was designed to tame the Damodar — once called the "River of Sorrow" for its devastating floods — by moderating flood peaks, supplying irrigation water to hundreds of thousands of hectares in West Bengal, and generating hydroelectric power for the coal and steel belt of eastern India.
