Convergence (eye)

Convergence
TypeVergence eye movement
MusclesMedial rectus muscles

Convergence is the simultaneous inward movement of both eyes toward each other, usually in an effort to maintain single binocular vision when viewing an object. It is one of the vergence movements of the eyes.

Both eyes rotate inward to fixate a near target.

Mechanism

Convergence is driven mainly by the medial rectus muscles, innervated by the oculomotor nerve (CN III). Its strength is measured in prism dioptres; the near point of convergence in young adults is about 5–10 cm from the eyes.

Near triad

When shifting gaze to a near object, three responses occur together: convergence, accommodation of the lens, and miosis (pupillary constriction). The ratio of accommodative convergence to accommodation (AC/A ratio) is clinically important.

Disorders

Convergence insufficiency causes eyestrain, headaches, and double vision during reading, and is treated with vision therapy exercises. Convergence excess and divergence insufficiency are related vergence anomalies.
