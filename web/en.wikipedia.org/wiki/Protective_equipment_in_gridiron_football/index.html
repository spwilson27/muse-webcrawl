Protective equipment in gridiron football

Key items: Helmet, shoulder pads, hip/thigh/knee pads, mouthguard
Helmet standard: NOCSAE certification
Mandatory in: NFL, NCAA, high school football

Protective equipment in gridiron football refers to the pads, helmets, and other gear worn by players of American and Canadian football to reduce the risk of injury in a high-contact sport. Modern equipment developed over the twentieth century as the game grew more violent and organizers sought to protect players while preserving its physical character.

The helmet

The football helmet, with a hard polycarbonate shell, internal padding, and a metal or polymer facemask, is the most recognizable item. Leather helmets became common in the 1920s–30s, plastic shells in the 1940s, and modern helmets incorporate energy-absorbing liners and, in some leagues, position-specific designs tested against laboratory impact standards.

Pads and other gear

Shoulder pads protect the chest, shoulders, and upper back; hip, tailbone, thigh, and knee pads slide into pockets in the uniform pants. Mouthguards are mandatory in most leagues, and many players add gloves, rib protectors, neck rolls, and visors.

Safety debate

Despite improvements, equipment cannot eliminate concussions or chronic traumatic encephalopathy risk, prompting rule changes, tackling-technique programs, and soft-shell practice caps such as Guardian Caps.
