Lazarus Group

The Lazarus Group (also known by aliases including Hidden Cobra, Guardians of Peace, APT38, and ZINC) is a cybercrime and cyber-espionage group widely attributed by governments and security researchers to the government of North Korea, specifically to the Reconnaissance General Bureau. Active since at least 2009, it is blamed for some of the most notorious state-linked hacking operations ever recorded, combining espionage, destructive attacks, and large-scale financial theft used to fund the regime.

Major attributed operations

  • Operation Troy (2009–2013) — espionage campaigns against South Korean and US targets.
  • DarkSeoul (2013) — destructive wiper attacks on South Korean banks and broadcasters.
  • Sony Pictures hack (2014) — as the self-styled "Guardians of Peace", the group leaked Sony Pictures data and issued threats over the film The Interview; the United States formally attributed the attack to North Korea and sanctioned it in response.
  • Bangladesh Bank heist (2016) — fraudulent SWIFT messages attempted to steal nearly US$1 billion from Bangladesh Bank's account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York; about US$81 million was successfully laundered through Philippine casinos.
  • WannaCry (2017) — ransomware worm that infected hundreds of thousands of computers in around 150 countries, disrupting hospitals, railways, and factories; the US, UK, and others attributed it to Lazarus/North Korea.
  • Cryptocurrency theft — extensive campaigns against exchanges and DeFi platforms, including the 2022 Ronin Network theft of about US$620 million attributed by the FBI to Lazarus actors, and the February 2025 theft of roughly US$1.5 billion from the Bybit exchange, attributed to North Korean hackers.

Structure and investigations

Researchers often treat "Lazarus" as an umbrella for several North Korean clusters, including the financially focused APT38 (Bluenoroff) and the group tracked as Andariel. In 2018 the United States charged North Korean programmer Park Jin-hyok with involvement in the Sony hack, WannaCry, and the Bangladesh Bank theft. UN experts have estimated that North Korea's cyber operations have generated billions of dollars for its weapons programmes.
