Hyung

Hyeong, pumsae, or teul (often romanised hyung) is a sequence of movements — a form or pattern — performed in Korean martial arts such as taekwondo and tang soo do. It is a systematic, prearranged sequence of offensive and defensive techniques, analogous to the Japanese kata. The Korean word 형 (型) means "form" or "pattern".

Purpose and practice

Forms are practised to develop technique, balance, breathing, power, and focus in the absence of a partner. Each hyung is associated with a belt level, with complexity and difficulty increasing through the grades: colour-belt students typically learn the Taegeuk series (in Kukkiwon/WTF taekwondo) or the older Palgwe and Pyong Ahn series (in tang soo do and traditional schools), while dan-grade practitioners study black-belt forms such as Koryo, Keumgang, and Taebaek, whose names and symbolism draw on Korean history and philosophy. In the ITF style codified by Choi Hong-hi, the 24 patterns (teul) represent the 24 hours of a day.

Competition

Poomsae is a recognised competitive discipline in its own right, judged on accuracy, power, rhythm, and expression; it has been contested at the World Taekwondo Poomsae Championships since 2006 and freestyle poomsae adds acrobatic and musical elements.
