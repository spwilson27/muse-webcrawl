Alfonso XIII (17 May 1886 – 28 February 1941), also known as El Africano ("the African") for his interest in Spain's colonial ventures in Africa, was King of Spain from his birth until the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic in 1931. He was a monarch from birth, as his father Alfonso XII had died the previous year; his mother Maria Christina acted as regent until he came of age in 1902.

Reign

Alfonso's reign was marked by political instability, the loss of Spain's remaining empire after the Spanish–American War of 1898 (just before his majority), and the brutal colonial war in Morocco, where Spanish forces suffered the catastrophic defeat of Annual in 1921. In 1906 he married Princess Victoria Eugenie of Battenberg, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria; the couple survived an anarchist assassination attempt on their wedding day.

In 1923 the king acquiesced in the military coup of General Miguel Primo de Rivera, whose dictatorship he supported until 1930. The association with the dictatorship fatally undermined the monarchy, and after municipal elections in April 1931 were interpreted as a plebiscite against him, Alfonso left Spain as the Second Republic was proclaimed, without formally abdicating.

Exile and death

Alfonso lived in exile in France and Italy. During the Spanish Civil War he sympathised with the Nationalist cause but was not restored by Franco. In January 1941 he formally renounced his rights in favour of his son Infante Juan, Count of Barcelona — the father of King Juan Carlos I. He died in Rome on 28 February 1941. His remains were transferred to El Escorial in 1980, after the restoration of the monarchy.
