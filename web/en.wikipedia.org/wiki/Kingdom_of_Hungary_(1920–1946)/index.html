The Kingdom of Hungary (Hungarian: Magyar Királyság) between 1920 and 1946 was a de facto kingdom without a monarch, ruled by Regent Miklós Horthy. Restored after the collapse of the Hungarian Soviet Republic and the Romanian occupation, it was defined by the trauma of the Treaty of Trianon (1920), which stripped Hungary of about two-thirds of its territory and population, and by the revisionist drive to recover those lands.

The Horthy regency

Admiral Miklós Horthy was elected regent on 1 March 1920 by the National Assembly. Two attempts by the exiled king Charles IV to reclaim the throne in 1921 failed under Allied and Little Entente pressure, and the Habsburgs were formally dethroned. Under Prime Minister István Bethlen the 1920s brought conservative consolidation, limited land reform, currency stabilisation with the pengő, and League of Nations membership; politics remained dominated by the gentry and large landowners, with an early anti-Jewish law (the numerus clausus of 1920).

Revisionism and the Second World War

Seeking revision of Trianon, Hungary aligned itself with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy. The Vienna Awards of 1938 and 1940 returned southern Slovakia, Carpathian Ruthenia, and northern Transylvania. Hungary joined the invasion of Yugoslavia (1941) and the Axis invasion of the USSR (Operation Barbarossa), and declared war alongside the Axis. The Hungarian Second Army was destroyed on the Don in early 1943. Jewish anti-discrimination laws escalated into persecution; after Germany occupied Hungary in March 1944, some 440,000 Hungarian Jews were deported, most to Auschwitz, in one of the fastest phases of the Holocaust. Horthy's attempt to leave the war in October 1944 was thwarted by a German-backed Arrow Cross coup; Budapest fell to the Red Army in February 1945 after a devastating siege.

End of the kingdom

Postwar elections in November 1945 were won by the Independent Smallholders' Party. On 1 February 1946 the National Assembly proclaimed the Second Hungarian Republic, ending the kingdom, before Hungary became a communist people's republic in 1949.
