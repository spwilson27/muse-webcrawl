San Francisco Symphony


Founded: 1911
Location: San Francisco, California, United States
Concert hall: Louise M. Davies Symphony Hall
Music director: Esa-Pekka Salonen (since 2020)

The San Francisco Symphony, founded in 1911, is an American orchestra based in San Francisco, California. Since 1980 the orchestra has performed at the Louise M. Davies Symphony Hall in the city's Hayes Valley/Civic Center area. Finnish conductor Esa-Pekka Salonen has served as music director since 2020, succeeding Michael Tilson Thomas, whose 25-year tenure (1995–2020) was among the longest and most celebrated in the orchestra's history.

History

The orchestra's first concert took place in December 1911 under Henry Hadley. Subsequent music directors included Alfred Hertz, Issay Dobrowen, Pierre Monteux (1935–1952), Enrique Jordá, Josef Krips, Seiji Ozawa, Edo de Waart, and Herbert Blomstedt. Under Monteux the orchestra began regular broadcasts and recordings.

Michael Tilson Thomas greatly expanded the orchestra's international reputation, emphasizing American music, Mahler, and innovative programming such as the American Mavericks festival. The San Francisco Symphony Chorus, founded in 1973, and the Youth Orchestra are affiliated ensembles. The orchestra has won numerous Grammy Awards, including for its Mahler recording cycle released on its own SFS Media label, and won an Academy Award-related association through the documentary series Keeping Score. It performs more than 200 concerts annually, including free community concerts, and tours internationally.
