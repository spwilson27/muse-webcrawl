Leonardo da Vinci, Lady with an Ermine

The Lady with an Ermine is a portrait painting in oils on walnut panel by the Italian Renaissance artist Leonardo da Vinci, dated to about 1489–1491. The sitter is Cecilia Gallerani, the mistress of Leonardo's patron Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan, and the animal she holds is an ermine. It is one of only four portraits of women attributed to Leonardo, and it is now held by the Czartoryski Museum in Kraków, Poland.

Subject and symbolism

Cecilia Gallerani was a young woman of the Milanese court, celebrated for her learning and poetry. The ermine carries layered meanings: it was an emblem of Ludovico Sforza, who had been awarded the Order of the Ermine and was nicknamed "the White Ermine"; the animal also punned on Cecilia's surname, since the Greek for ermine, galê, resembles "Gallerani"; and the ermine traditionally symbolised purity, as legend held it would rather die than soil its white coat.

History

The painting was acquired around 1798 by Prince Adam Jerzy Czartoryski in Italy for his mother Izabela's collection at Puławy. It survived the upheavals of the nineteenth century, was seized by the Nazis during the Second World War for Hans Frank's collection, and was recovered in 1945 and returned to Kraków. In 2016 the Czartoryski collection, including the painting, was sold to the Polish state, and the work is displayed at the Princes Czartoryski Museum.
