Scottish Travellers

Scottish Travellers, or the working Scottish Romani and Traveller community, are a traditionally nomadic people of Scottish origin, distinct from both Scottish Highland Travellers (who are Indigenous to Scotland) and Irish Travellers, though the communities have intermarried and their histories overlap. They speak Scots, Scottish Cant and other varieties, and have historically worked as tinsmiths, hawkers, horse dealers, and seasonal agricultural labourers.

History

Travelling communities have been recorded in Scotland since at least the 12th century. The so-called "tinkler" (tinsmith) communities of the Highlands and Lowlands maintained a semi-nomadic lifestyle well into the 20th century, camping in bow-tents and later in caravans. Notable families include the Williamsons, McPhees, Townsleys and Whytes. Duncan Williamson of Fife became celebrated as a storyteller, preserving a vast oral tradition.

Culture

Scottish Travellers are renowned for storytelling, ballad singing — including the "big ballads" or Child ballads preserved by singers such as Jeannie Robertson and Lizzie Higgins — and traditional crafts such as horn-working, basketry and tin-smithing. Their oral culture has been a major source for folklorists and for the Scottish folk revival.

Modern situation

Today many Scottish Gypsy/Travellers live in houses or on council sites, though roadside encampments persist. The community is recognised as an ethnic group under equality legislation and continues to face discrimination and disputes over unauthorised encampments.
