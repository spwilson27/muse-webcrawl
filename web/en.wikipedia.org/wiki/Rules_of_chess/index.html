Rules of chess

Governing bodyFIDE (International Chess Federation)
CodifiedFIDE Laws of Chess, periodically revised
PlayersTwo
Pieces16 per side: king, queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights, 8 pawns

The rules of chess govern the play of the board game chess. The standard rules are codified by FIDE, the international chess federation, in its Laws of Chess. Two players, White and Black, move alternately on an 8×8 board; White moves first.

Movement

Each piece type moves differently: the king one square in any direction; the queen any distance along ranks, files and diagonals; rooks along ranks and files; bishops along diagonals; knights in an L-shape, jumping over pieces; and pawns forward one square (two from their starting square), capturing diagonally, with the special capture en passant and promotion on reaching the far rank. Castling is a special king-and-rook move permitted once per player under strict conditions.

Object and endings

The object is to checkmate the opposing king — to attack it so it cannot escape. Games also end by resignation, loss on time, stalemate (a draw), agreement, the fifty-move rule, threefold repetition, or insufficient material. A player whose king is in check must resolve the attack. Touch-move applies in competitive play, and games are commonly recorded in algebraic notation.
