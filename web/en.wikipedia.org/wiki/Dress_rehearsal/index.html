Dress rehearsal


A final dress rehearsal in full costume.

A dress rehearsal is a full-scale rehearsal of a theatrical or musical production in which the cast wears complete costumes and make-up and performs with all technical elements — scenery, lighting, sound, props and orchestra — exactly as in a public performance. It is typically the last rehearsal before the first preview or opening night.

Purpose

The dress rehearsal integrates elements that have previously been rehearsed separately: actors in costume (often the first time they must manage quick changes, bulky garments or period footwear), the technical crew executing lighting and sound cues, and stage management coordinating the whole. Directors and designers use it to identify problems — costume malfunctions, sight-line issues, timing of scene changes — while there is still time to correct them.

Types

Productions may hold a technical dress rehearsal, concentrating on cues and changes with permission to stop and restart, followed by a final dress, run without interruption as a true performance. In opera and dance, a seated "Italian" rehearsal or a piano dress may precede the full version. Schools and amateur companies often invite a small audience of friends and family to the dress rehearsal.

Figurative use

The term is also used figuratively for any full practice run of a planned event — a ceremony, a military operation, or a product launch — carried out under conditions as close as possible to the real occasion.
