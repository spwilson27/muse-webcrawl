Leon Trotsky (born Lev Davidovich Bronstein; 7 November 1879 – 21 August 1940) was a Russian revolutionary, Marxist theorist, and Soviet politician. As chairman of the Petrograd Soviet he helped organize the October Revolution of 1917; as People's Commissar for Military and Naval Affairs he founded the Red Army and led it to victory in the Russian Civil War. Expelled from power by Joseph Stalin, he became the leader of the anti-Stalinist Left Opposition and was ultimately assassinated in exile in Mexico.

Early revolutionary career

Born to a prosperous Jewish farming family in Ukraine, Bronstein became a Marxist as a teenager, was exiled to Siberia, and escaped using a forged passport bearing the name "Trotsky." He participated in the Revolution of 1905 as leader of the St. Petersburg Soviet and developed, with Alexander Parvus, the theory of permanent revolution. After years of exile journalism in Europe and New York, he returned to Russia in 1917 and joined the Bolsheviks.

Revolution and civil war

Trotsky negotiated the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk as foreign commissar, then built the Red Army from almost nothing — recruiting former tsarist officers, imposing iron discipline, and directing operations from his famous armored train. Under his command the Red Army defeated the White forces and foreign interventionists by 1921, while also enforcing the Red Terror against the regime's enemies; he defended those methods in print against critics such as Kautsky and Rosa Luxemburg.

Struggle with Stalin and exile

After Lenin's death in 1924, Trotsky lost the succession struggle to Stalin, whose doctrine of "socialism in one country" he opposed in the name of world revolution. He was expelled from the Communist Party in 1927, exiled from the USSR in 1929, and stripped of citizenship. From exile he wrote The History of the Russian Revolution and The Revolution Betrayed, and in 1938 founded the Fourth International. On 20 August 1940, NKVD agent Ramón Mercader attacked him with an ice axe at his home in Coyoacán, Mexico City; Trotsky died the following day.

Legacy

Trotskyism remains an influential current of Marxism, and Trotsky's writings on revolution, fascism, and bureaucratic degeneration continue to be widely read. Within the Soviet Union his name was erased from official history until the era of glasnost.
