Babinski sign

The Babinski sign (also called the Babinski reflex or plantar reflex) is a neurological sign elicited by firmly stroking the sole of the foot. It is named after the French neurologist Joseph Babinski, who described it in 1896. The response of the toes helps clinicians distinguish lesions of the upper motor neurons from other causes of weakness.

Method and interpretation

Using a blunt instrument, the examiner strokes the lateral side of the sole from the heel toward the toes. In a healthy adult, the toes flex downward (flexor plantar response). In the abnormal, or "positive", Babinski sign, the big toe extends upward (dorsiflexes) and the other toes fan out. This response indicates dysfunction of the corticospinal tract, as seen in conditions such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury and motor neurone disease.

In infants

The extensor plantar response is normal in infants under roughly 12–24 months of age, because the corticospinal tracts are not yet fully myelinated. Its persistence beyond that age, or its reappearance in adults, is pathological. Absent or equivocal responses also occur, and related variants (such as the Chaddock and Oppenheim signs) stroke the shin or lateral foot instead.
