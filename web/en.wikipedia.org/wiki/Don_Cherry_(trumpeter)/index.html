Donald Eugene Cherry (November 18, 1936 – October 19, 1995) was an American jazz trumpeter, bandleader, and multi-instrumentalist. He came to prominence in the late 1950s as the trumpeter in saxophonist Ornette Coleman's groundbreaking free jazz quartet, and went on to become a pioneer of world fusion music, incorporating musical traditions from Africa, India, and the Middle East into jazz.

Cherry was known for playing a small pocket trumpet and for his interest in musical cultures from around the world.

Career

Cherry was raised in Los Angeles, where he played with Dexter Gordon and other local musicians before joining Ornette Coleman's quartet in 1958. The group, with Charlie Haden and Billy Higgins, recorded the landmark albums The Shape of Jazz to Come (1959) and Free Jazz (1960), which helped define the free jazz movement. Cherry's singing, vocalised trumpet tone became one of the most recognisable sounds of the era.

After leaving Coleman, Cherry led his own groups, recording albums such as Complete Communion (1966) and Symphony for Improvisers (1966) for Blue Note. In the late 1960s and 1970s he lived in Europe, studied Indian and African music, and formed the trio Codona with Collin Walcott and Naná Vasconcelos, as well as the group Old and New Dreams with former Coleman associates. His album Brown Rice (1975) is frequently cited as a foundation of world fusion.

Personal life and legacy

Cherry was the stepfather of singer Neneh Cherry and father of singer Eagle-Eye Cherry and musician Christian Cherry. He died of liver failure in Málaga, Spain, in 1995. He was inducted into the Oklahoma Jazz Hall of Fame in 2011.
