Dardanelles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dardanelles
Location: Northwestern Turkey
Connects: Aegean Sea and Sea of Marmara
Length: about 61 km

The Dardanelles is a narrow natural strait in northwestern Turkey connecting the Aegean Sea with the Sea of Marmara. Together with the Sea of Marmara and the Bosphorus, it forms the Turkish Straits, the only sea route between the Mediterranean and the Black Sea.

In antiquity the strait was known as the Hellespont — in myth, the water where Helle fell from the golden ram, and which Leander swam nightly to reach Hero. Xerxes crossed it on a bridge of boats in 480 BC during his invasion of Greece, and Alexander the Great crossed in the opposite direction in 334 BC. The ancient city of Troy stood near its Aegean entrance.

In the First World War the strait was the scene of the Gallipoli campaign (1915–1916), when Allied forces tried and failed to force the strait and knock the Ottoman Empire out of the war. Since 1936 passage through the straits has been governed by the Montreux Convention. The 1915 Çanakkale Bridge, opened in 2022, now spans the strait — the longest suspension bridge in the world by main span.
