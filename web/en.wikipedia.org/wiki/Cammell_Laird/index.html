Cammell Laird is a British shipbuilding and ship repair company based at Birkenhead on the River Mersey. Founded by the Laird family in 1828, it became one of the most famous names in British shipbuilding, building many notable warships and liners.

History

William Laird established an ironworks at Birkenhead in 1824 and began shipbuilding in 1828; the yard pioneered iron-hulled ships, including the John Randolph (1834) and the gunboat Nemesis (1839). During the American Civil War the yard controversially built the Confederate raider CSS Alabama. In 1903 Laird Brothers merged with the Sheffield steel firm Charles Cammell & Co. to form Cammell Laird.

Notable ships built at Birkenhead include the battleship HMS Rodney, the carrier HMS Ark Royal (1937) and the second Mauretania (1938). After nationalisation in 1977 the yard's fortunes declined; new shipbuilding ended in 1993 with the submarine HMS Unicorn. The site was revived from 2001 under new owners for ship repair and conversion, winning major Royal Navy support contracts, and the historic name returned with work including the polar research ship RRS Sir David Attenborough, launched in 2018.
