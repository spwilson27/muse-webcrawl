The National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) is a background check system in the United States created by the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993 and launched by the FBI on 30 November 1998. Licensed firearms dealers use the system to determine whether a prospective buyer is eligible to purchase firearms.

How it works

Before selling a firearm, a Federal Firearms Licensee (FFL) has the buyer complete ATF Form 4473 and then contacts NICS — directly or through a state point of contact — with the buyer's identifying information. The system searches three databases: the National Crime Information Center, the Interstate Identification Index, and the NICS Index of persons prohibited from possessing firearms. Most checks resolve within minutes with a "proceed" or "deny" response; a "delayed" response gives the FBI up to three business days to complete research, after which, if no determination has been made, the dealer may legally proceed with the sale under federal law (some states impose longer waits).

Prohibited persons

Federal law bars firearm possession by convicted felons, fugitives, persons convicted of misdemeanor domestic violence, those subject to qualifying restraining orders, persons adjudicated mentally ill or committed to a mental institution, unlawful users of controlled substances, and several other categories. Critics have long noted gaps where state and local records are incompletely reported to the federal databases; the Fix NICS Act of 2018 sought to improve reporting.

Volume and appeals

NICS processes tens of millions of checks per year, with annual volumes exceeding 30 million in the early 2020s. Buyers who believe they were wrongly denied may appeal through the FBI or, where applicable, the state point of contact, and may request a Voluntary Appeal File so that verified identity information can prevent future mistaken denials.
