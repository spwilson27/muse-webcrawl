Richard Hell (born Richard Lester Meyers; October 2, 1949) is an American singer, songwriter, bass guitarist and writer. A central figure in the early New York punk scene, he was a founding member of the Neon Boys, Television and the Heartbreakers before fronting Richard Hell and the Voidoids, whose 1977 album Blank Generation — and its title track — became anthems of punk rock.

Career

Hell moved to New York City in the late 1960s to become a poet, publishing and working in bookshops. With school friend Tom Verlaine he formed the Neon Boys in 1972, which evolved into Television, a house band at CBGB. After leaving Television in 1975 he co-founded the Heartbreakers with Johnny Thunders and Jerry Nolan, then formed the Voidoids with guitarists Robert Quine and Ivan Julian. Hell's torn-shirt, spiked-hair look and nihilistic, literary lyricism strongly influenced British punk — Malcolm McLaren, who had managed the New York Dolls and seen Hell perform, carried the aesthetic back to London for the Sex Pistols.

Writing

From the mid-1980s Hell increasingly concentrated on writing, publishing the novels Go Now (1996) and Godlike (2005), poetry and essays, the anthology of his art writing Hot and Cold, and the memoir I Dreamed I Was a Very Clean Tramp (2013). He also acted in independent films, including Smithereens and a brief appearance in Desperately Seeking Susan.
