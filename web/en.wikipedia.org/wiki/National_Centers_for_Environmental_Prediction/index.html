National Centers for Environmental Prediction

The National Centers for Environmental Prediction (NCEP) is a unit of the United States National Weather Service (NWS), part of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). NCEP delivers national and global weather, water, climate and ocean forecasts and analyses. Since 2012 its headquarters have been at the NOAA Center for Weather and Climate Prediction in College Park, Maryland.

Organisation

NCEP comprises nine specialised service centres. The best known to the public are the Weather Prediction Center (WPC), which issues national precipitation, winter weather and medium-range forecasts; the Storm Prediction Center (SPC) in Norman, Oklahoma, which issues severe thunderstorm and tornado watches and convective outlooks; the National Hurricane Center (NHC) in Miami; the Ocean Prediction Center; the Climate Prediction Center, responsible for seasonal outlooks including El Niño guidance; the Environmental Modeling Center (EMC), which develops and runs the operational forecast models; the Space Weather Prediction Center in Boulder, Colorado; the Aviation Weather Center in Kansas City; and NCEP Central Operations, which runs the supercomputers and disseminates products.

Models and operations

NCEP operates the principal American global and regional forecast models, including the Global Forecast System (GFS), long informally known in weather communities as the "American model" in comparison with the ECMWF model; the North American Mesoscale Model (NAM); the High Resolution Rapid Refresh (HRRR); and ensemble systems such as the GEFS. Its analyses and reanalyses, including the NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis, are foundational data sets for atmospheric science worldwide. NCEP forecasts underpin the warnings and local forecasts issued by the NWS's 122 Weather Forecast Offices and are used by aviation, shipping, agriculture and emergency management.

History

NCEP was created in 1995 by reorganising existing NWS units, including the former National Meteorological Center, whose origins lie in the Weather Bureau's analysis centres of the 1940s and the Joint Numerical Weather Prediction Unit of 1954, a pioneer of computer forecasting. The move to College Park brought most centres together adjacent to the University of Maryland.
