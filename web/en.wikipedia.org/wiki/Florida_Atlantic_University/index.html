Florida Atlantic University

Florida Atlantic University (FAU) is a public research university in Boca Raton, Florida, with additional campuses in Davie, Dania Beach, Jupiter, Fort Pierce, and Fort Lauderdale. Founded in 1961 and opened in 1964, it serves more than 30,000 students and is part of the State University System of Florida. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" by the Carnegie Classification.

History

FAU was established on the site of the former Boca Raton Army Air Field, a World War II airbase. It was the first university in the United States to open as an upper-division and graduate institution only, admitting juniors, seniors, and graduate students; it became a full four-year university in 1984. President Lyndon B. Johnson spoke at the dedication ceremony in October 1964.

Academics

The university offers more than 180 degree programs through ten colleges, including the Charles E. Schmidt College of Medicine (founded 2010), the Harriet L. Wilkes Honors College in Jupiter, and the Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institute in Fort Pierce, acquired in 2007. Its athletic teams, the Owls, compete in the American Athletic Conference; the football team plays at FAU Stadium, known for its proximity to the Atlantic Ocean.
