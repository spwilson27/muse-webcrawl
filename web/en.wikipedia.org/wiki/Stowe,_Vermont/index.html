Stowe is a town in Lamoille County, Vermont, United States. The population was 5,223 at the 2020 census. The town is widely regarded as the "Ski Capital of the East" and is a year-round resort destination situated at the foot of Mount Mansfield, Vermont's highest peak. Stowe Mountain Resort operates ski areas on Mount Mansfield and Spruce Peak.

History

Stowe was chartered by Royal Governor Benning Wentworth on June 8, 1763, during the era of the New Hampshire Grants, and named after Stowe in Buckinghamshire, England. Settlement began in 1794, and the town was organized in 1797. Early industries included sawmills, gristmills, tanneries, and farming. Tourism began in the mid-19th century, when visitors came for the mountain scenery; the large Summit House hotel on Mount Mansfield opened in 1858. Downhill skiing was developed in the 1930s, in part by the Civilian Conservation Corps, which cut the first trails on Mount Mansfield.

Geography

According to the United States Census Bureau, the town has a total area of 72.8 square miles (188.5 km²), one of the largest towns in Vermont by area. The West Branch of the Little River flows through the town centre. Vermont Route 100 runs north–south through Stowe, and Route 108 (the Mountain Road) climbs through Smugglers' Notch to Jeffersonville. The village is known for its white-steepled Stowe Community Church and covered bridges, including the Gold Brook Covered Bridge, locally called "Emily's Bridge".

Tourism and culture

Beyond skiing, Stowe supports mountain biking, hiking, and fall foliage tourism. The Trapp Family Lodge, founded by the von Trapp family of The Sound of Music fame, is located in Stowe. The Stowe Recreation Path, a 5.3-mile paved greenway, runs from the village into the mountains. Events include the Stowe Winter Carnival and an annual hot-air balloon festival.
