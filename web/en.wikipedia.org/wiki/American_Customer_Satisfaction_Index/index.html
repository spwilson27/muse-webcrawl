American Customer Satisfaction Index

The American Customer Satisfaction Index (ACSI) is an economic indicator that measures household consumer satisfaction with goods and services in the United States. Founded in 1994 and developed from work associated with the University of Michigan and Claes Fornell, it reports scores on a 0–100 scale.

Methodology

ACSI uses survey interviews and a structural model linking expectations, perceived quality and perceived value to satisfaction, complaints and loyalty. Results are published for companies, industries, sectors and the national economy. Because the model is consistent across categories, scores can be compared between a bank, an airline and a manufacturer, though the underlying experiences differ greatly.

Use

Companies use ACSI benchmarks to track service performance and investors have studied connections between satisfaction, customer retention and financial outcomes. Critics note that surveys capture perceptions at a moment in time and that high satisfaction does not always map neatly onto market share or profitability. Similar national indices have been created in other countries.
