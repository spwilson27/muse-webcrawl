Escape from Sobibor is a 1987 British television film directed by Jack Gold and written by Reginald Rose, based on Richard Rashke's 1982 non-fiction book of the same name. It dramatises the prisoner uprising and mass escape from the Nazi extermination camp at Sobibor in German-occupied Poland on 14 October 1943 — the most successful revolt by Jewish prisoners at a Nazi death camp during the Second World War.

Plot

The film follows Jewish prisoners selected for slave labour at Sobibor, including Leon Feldhendler (Alan Arkin), and Soviet Jewish prisoners of war led by Alexander "Sasha" Pechersky (Rutger Hauer). Together they plan and execute a revolt: SS officers are lured to workshops and killed quietly, weapons are seized, and some 300 prisoners break out of the camp. Many were killed by mines, gunfire and the subsequent manhunt, but around 50 escapees survived the war. The camp was closed and dismantled by the Nazis shortly after the revolt.

Reception

The film was widely praised on its broadcast by CBS and won the Golden Globe Award for Best Miniseries or Television Film; Rutger Hauer won the Golden Globe for Best Supporting Actor. It remains a prominent screen depiction of resistance during the Holocaust.
