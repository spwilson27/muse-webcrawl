ESCON (Enterprise Systems Connection) is a data connection created by IBM, and is commonly used to connect their mainframe computers to peripheral devices such as disk storage, tape drives and printers. ESCON is an optical fiber, half-duplex, serial interface, announced by IBM in 1990 with the System/390 family as a replacement for the older copper-based parallel (bus and tag) channels.

Characteristics

ESCON operates at a raw signalling rate of 200 Mbit/s, giving an effective data rate of about 17 MB/s per channel. Using optical fiber, it extended the distance between a mainframe and its control units from tens of metres (with copper bus-and-tag cabling) to up to 3 km point to point, and as far as 43 km with the use of ESCON directors (channel switches) and repeaters. ESCON also supported dynamic reconnection: a channel could be switched between different control units, enabling much more flexible, switch-managed configurations than parallel channels allowed.

History

ESCON was introduced in September 1990 together with the IBM ES/9000 and System/390 processors. It became the standard mainframe channel architecture of the 1990s before being superseded by the faster, open-standard-based FICON (Fibre Connection), introduced in 1998, which runs over Fibre Channel physical layers at much higher speeds. ESCON support persisted on IBM Z systems for many years for the benefit of legacy installations.
