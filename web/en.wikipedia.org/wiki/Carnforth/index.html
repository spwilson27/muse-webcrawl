Carnforth is a small market town and civil parish in the City of Lancaster district of Lancashire, England, situated at the northeast end of Morecambe Bay and near the boundary with Cumbria. It grew from a village into a railway town in the nineteenth century and is known throughout Britain for its association with the classic 1945 film Brief Encounter.

History

The name Carnforth derives from Old English, likely "ford of the cranes" or from a ford over the River Keer. In the 19th century the town developed around ironworks and, above all, the railway: Carnforth station opened in 1846 and became an important junction on the West Coast Main Line, with a large locomotive depot. Much of the town's Victorian fabric dates from this railway expansion.

Brief Encounter

Carnforth station achieved lasting fame as the filming location for David Lean's Brief Encounter (1945), adapted from Noël Coward's play and starring Celia Johnson and Trevor Howard. The station's clock, refreshment room, and subway became iconic images of wartime romance. After years of decline, the station was restored and reopened in 2003 as the Carnforth Station Heritage Centre, with a visitor centre devoted to the film and the station's history, and the refreshment room recreated in the style of the film.

Transport and surroundings

Today Carnforth station is served by trains on the West Coast Main Line (stopping services) and the lines to Leeds and Barrow-in-Furness. The town lies close to the Lancaster Canal, Leighton Moss nature reserve (an RSPB reserve famous for bitterns and bearded tits), and the Arnside and Silverdale Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.
