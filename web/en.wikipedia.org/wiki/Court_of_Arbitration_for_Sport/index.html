The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS; French: Tribunal arbitral du sport, TAS) is an international quasi-judicial body established in 1984 to settle disputes related to sport through arbitration. Headquartered in Lausanne, Switzerland, with additional courts in Sydney and New York, it is often called the "supreme court of sport."

The CAS headquarters in Lausanne.

History and structure

The CAS was created by the International Olympic Committee under president Juan Antonio Samaranch, with judge Kéba Mbaye a leading figure. Concerns about its dependence on the IOC led, after the Swiss Federal Tribunal's 1993 Gundel ruling, to reforms creating the International Council of Arbitration for Sport (ICAS) in 1994 to oversee its administration and finances independently.

Jurisdiction and procedure

The CAS hears commercial disputes (sponsorship, transfers, broadcasting), appeals against decisions of sports federations — most prominently anti-doping cases referred from WADA and federations — and eligibility disputes. Panels of one or three arbitrators, chosen from a list of several hundred, issue binding awards; appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal is possible only on narrow procedural grounds. Since 1996, ad hoc divisions have operated at each Olympic Games, resolving urgent cases within 24 hours.

Notable cases

High-profile CAS cases include the reduction of Maria Sharapova's doping ban (2016), the upholding of the ban on double-amputee sprinter Oscar Pistorius's initial exclusion and later eligibility, the Caster Semenya testosterone-rule case (2019), and the four-year ban on Russia's RUSADA (2020, reduced from a WADA-imposed sanction on appeal).
