Cope rearrangement

The Cope rearrangement is an extensively studied organic reaction involving the [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement of 1,5-dienes. It was developed by Arthur C. Cope and Elizabeth M. Hardy and first reported in 1940. The reaction proceeds through a chair-like transition state, in which the carbon skeleton is reorganized while the number and type of bonds remain the same.

Mechanism

The rearrangement is a pericyclic reaction in which the σ-bond between C3 and C4 breaks as new π-bonds form, and a new σ-bond joins the terminal carbons. The process is thermally allowed and usually reversible, so product distributions reflect the relative stability of the diene isomers, with more substituted double bonds favoured.

Variants

The oxy-Cope rearrangement involves a hydroxyl group on the 3-position; deprotonation gives an anionic oxy-Cope that is accelerated by many orders of magnitude and yields, after work-up, a δ,ε-unsaturated carbonyl compound. The aza-Cope and Claisen rearrangement are heteroatom relatives, the Claisen being the oxygen analogue that rearranges allyl vinyl ethers.

Applications

The Cope rearrangement is used in synthesis to construct medium-ring systems and to explain fluxional molecules such as bullvalene, which continually rearranges through degenerate Cope shifts.
