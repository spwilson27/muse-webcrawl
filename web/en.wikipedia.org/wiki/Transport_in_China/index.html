Transport in China

China has built the world's largest high-speed rail network since 2008.

Transport in China has experienced major growth and expansion in recent decades. Though China's transport system comprises a vast network, transport bottlenecks continue to limit growth. Since 1949, and especially since the economic reforms of 1978, the central government has invested heavily in railways, expressways, airports, ports and urban transit.

Railways

Rail transport remains a principal mode of travel. By the early 2020s China had over 150,000 km of railway, including more than 40,000 km of high-speed rail — about two-thirds of the world total — operated under the Fuxing and Hexie brands at speeds up to 350 km/h. The Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway, opened in 2011, is among the most heavily used in the world.

Roads and aviation

The National Trunk Highway System and its successor expressway network, begun in 1988, now exceed 180,000 km, the largest on Earth. China is also the world's largest automobile market. Civil aviation has likewise expanded explosively, with hundreds of airports including Beijing Capital, Shanghai Pudong and the Beijing Daxing airport opened in 2019.

Waterways and ports

China has some 110,000 km of navigable inland waterways, dominated by the Yangtze and Pearl rivers and the historic Grand Canal. Its container ports — Shanghai, the world's busiest, plus Shenzhen, Ningbo-Zhoushan, Guangzhou and Qingdao — handle a large share of global trade.

References: National Bureau of Statistics of China, annual statistical communiqués; Ministry of Transport reports.
