The Kantō region (関東地方, Kantō-chihō) is a geographical area of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. In a common definition, the region includes the Greater Tokyo Area and encompasses seven prefectures: Chiba, Gunma, Ibaraki, Kanagawa, Saitama, Tochigi, and Tokyo.

Geography

Most of Kantō is covered by the Kantō Plain, Japan's largest lowland, surrounded by mountains on three sides. The region's coastline runs along the Pacific Ocean and Tokyo Bay. Major cities include Tokyo, Yokohama, Kawasaki, Saitama, Chiba, and Sagamihara.

History

The name Kantō means "east of the barrier (checkpoint)" and reflects the region's historical position beyond the mountain passes guarding the route from the old capitals of Nara and Kyoto. During the Kamakura period, the shogunate was established at Kamakura, making Kantō a political centre. In the Edo period, Tokugawa Ieyasu made Edo (modern Tokyo) the seat of the shogunate, and the region developed into Japan's political and economic heartland.

Economy

Kantō is Japan's most industrialised and urbanised region, producing a large share of the national GDP. It hosts the Keihin and Keiyō industrial belts, the ports of Tokyo, Yokohama, and Chiba, and the headquarters of many of Japan's largest companies.
