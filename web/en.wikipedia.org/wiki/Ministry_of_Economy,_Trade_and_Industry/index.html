The Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (Keizai Sangyō-shō; METI) is a ministry of the Government of Japan responsible for economic, industrial and trade policy. It was created in the 2001 central government reform from the former Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI), whose guidance had been central to Japan's postwar economic growth.

Responsibilities

METI oversees industrial policy, international trade negotiations and export controls, energy policy (through the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy), small and medium enterprise support, and the Japan Patent Office. It also supervises bodies concerned with nuclear safety regulation policy and the compensation and decommissioning arrangements following the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster.

History

MITI, founded in 1949, became famous for its role in the "Japanese miracle", using administrative guidance, industrial targeting and trade policy to promote export industries. The 2001 reorganisation broadened the ministry's remit to economic policy generally and gave it its present name and structure.
