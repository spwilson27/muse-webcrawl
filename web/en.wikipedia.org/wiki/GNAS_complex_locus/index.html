GNAS complex locus

GNAS complex locus is a gene locus in humans on chromosome 20 (20q13.32) that undergoes genomic imprinting. It gives rise to multiple gene products, including Gs alpha subunit (Gαs), by using multiple promoters, alternative splicing and first exons.

Its main product, the stimulatory G-protein alpha subunit, couples many hormone and neurotransmitter receptors to adenylyl cyclase, activating cAMP-dependent pathways. Other transcripts from the locus include XLαs, NESP55, and the antisense transcript GNAS-AS1.

Clinical significance

Inactivating mutations in GNAS cause pseudohypoparathyroidism type Ia and Albright hereditary osteodystrophy, characterised by short stature, round facies, subcutaneous ossifications, and resistance to parathyroid hormone and other hormones. Because of imprinting, the phenotype depends on whether the mutation is inherited from the mother or father: maternal inheritance causes hormone resistance (PHP Ia), while paternal inheritance causes pseudopseudohypoparathyroidism (PPHP) with the skeletal features but normal hormone response.

Activating (gain-of-function) somatic mutations in GNAS cause McCune–Albright syndrome, with fibrous dysplasia of bone, café-au-lait skin spots, and endocrine hyperfunction, and are found in some pituitary and other tumours.
