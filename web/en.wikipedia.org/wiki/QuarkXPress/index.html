QuarkXPress is a desktop publishing software program for creating and editing complex page layouts in a WYSIWYG environment, developed by Quark Software Inc. It runs on macOS and Windows. First released in 1987 for the Macintosh, QuarkXPress was one of the defining applications of the desktop publishing revolution and at its peak in the 1990s held a dominant share of the professional page-layout market, used for magazines, newspapers, catalogues, and books.

History

Quark was founded in Denver in 1981 by Tim Gill and Mark Pope. QuarkXPress's precise typographic control — accurate kerning, tracking, and hyphenation — and its support for PostScript output made it the tool of choice for design studios and publishing houses. The XTensions architecture allowed third-party plug-ins to extend the program. Version 4 (1997) added Bézier tools and long-document features.

In 1999 Adobe released InDesign, and competitive pricing, bundled suite sales, and Quark's slow transition to Mac OS X eroded XPress's market share sharply during the 2000s. Quark responded with support for digital publishing: exporting layouts as ebooks, HTML5 publications, and mobile apps. The software continues to be developed, with annual releases adding features such as IDML import, flex layouts, and improved PDF output.

Features

  • Precise typography and style-sheet-driven layout
  • Master pages, automatic page numbering, and long-document tools
  • Colour management and professional PDF/X output for print
  • Digital publishing to HTML5, ePub, and app formats
