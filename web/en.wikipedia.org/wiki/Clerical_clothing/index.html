Clerical clothing

Clerical clothing is non-liturgical clothing worn exclusively by clergy and is distinct from vestments in that it is not reserved specifically for services. Practices vary between denominations: some clergy wear special clothing at all times, others only in public or ministerial settings, and some wear no distinctive dress at all.

Elements

The most widely recognized item is the clerical collar, a white band worn at the neck, originating in 19th-century Protestant practice and later adopted by Catholic and Orthodox clergy as well. Other common items include the black clerical suit, the cassock, and, for Roman Catholic priests, the fascia (sash) and zucchetto. Colours may indicate rank: black for priests, purple for bishops, scarlet for cardinals, and white for the pope.

By tradition

Eastern Orthodox clergy commonly wear a black ryasa (outer cassock) and kamilavka. Anglican and Lutheran clergy often wear the clerical collar with a suit or cassock. In many Reformed and evangelical churches, ministers dress like laypeople, reflecting a theology that minimizes distinction between clergy and laity.
