The Technical University of Munich (German: Technische Universität München, TUM) is a public research university in Munich, Germany. Founded in 1868, it specialises in engineering, technology, medicine and applied and natural sciences. TUM is a member of the TU9 association of German technical universities and has repeatedly been designated a "University of Excellence" under the German Universities Excellence Initiative.

History

TUM was founded by King Ludwig II of Bavaria in 1868 as a polytechnic school. It gained university status as a Technische Hochschule and in 1970 adopted its current name. The university has been associated with numerous Nobel laureates and inventors, including Rudolf Mössbauer and Robert Huber.

Campuses

The university operates major sites in central Munich, in Garching (home to a large research campus including the FRM II research reactor), in Weihenstephan (life sciences, at Freising) and in Straubing (biotechnology and sustainability). TUM also maintains a campus in Singapore, TUM Asia, founded in 2002.

Academics

TUM is organised into schools covering computation and engineering, natural sciences, life sciences, medicine, management, and social sciences and technology. It is regularly ranked among the top technical universities in Europe and is noted for entrepreneurship, producing many spin-off companies.
