The Spanish protectorate in Morocco was established on 27 November 1912 by a treaty between France and Spain that partitioned Morocco, which had become a French protectorate under the Treaty of Fez earlier that year. The Spanish zone comprised a northern strip along the Mediterranean and Atlantic coasts — including the Rif mountains and the cities of Tetouan, Larache, Ksar el-Kebir, and Chefchaouen — together with a southern enclave, the Tarfaya Strip (Cape Juby), bordering the Spanish Sahara. The port of Tangier was given special international status.

History

Spain's involvement in Morocco long predated the protectorate: the presidios of Ceuta and Melilla had been held since the 15th and 17th centuries, and Spain fought a war against Morocco in 1859–1860, winning the Battle of Tetouan. The 1912 agreement confirmed Spain's zone of influence recognized at the Algeciras Conference of 1906.

The Rif War

Spain's attempt to subdue the interior of the northern zone met fierce resistance from the tribes of the Rif, led by Abd el-Krim, who proclaimed the Republic of the Rif in 1921. That year Rifian forces destroyed a Spanish army at the Battle of Annual (the "Disaster of Annual"), killing more than 10,000 Spanish soldiers — one of the worst defeats of a European colonial power in Africa. Spain recovered through a joint Franco-Spanish campaign, including the Alhucemas landing of 1925, and Abd el-Krim surrendered in 1926. Spanish forces notoriously used chemical weapons, including mustard gas dropped from aircraft, against Rifian villages.

Administration

The protectorate was administered by a High Commissioner resident in Tetouan, while formal sovereignty nominally remained with the Sultan, represented in the Spanish zone by a Khalifa (viceroy). The Army of Africa, including the Spanish Legion and Moroccan Regulares, was recruited and stationed there; it was from Morocco that Francisco Franco and other officers launched the military uprising of July 1936 that began the Spanish Civil War.

End of the protectorate

When France ended its protectorate and recognized Moroccan independence in March 1956, Spain followed: the northern zone was retroceded to Morocco on 7 April 1956. The southern Tarfaya Strip was handed over in 1958 after the Ifni War. Spain retained the plazas de soberanía (Ceuta, Melilla, and smaller territories) and, for a time, Ifni (retroceded 1969) and the Spanish Sahara (abandoned 1975–1976).
