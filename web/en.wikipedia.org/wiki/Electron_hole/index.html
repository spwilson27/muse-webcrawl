Electron hole

An electron hole (often simply called a hole) is one of two types of charge carriers that are responsible for electrical currents in semiconductor materials. A hole is the absence of an electron at a position where one could exist in an atomic lattice. The concept is used in solid-state physics to describe the movement of valence electrons: when an electron moves into a vacant position, the vacancy appears to move in the opposite direction, and it can be treated mathematically as a positively charged particle.

Physical concept

In a semiconductor crystal, electrons occupy energy bands. The valence band is nearly full of electrons, while the conduction band is nearly empty. When an electron is excited—thermally or by absorbing a photon—it leaves the valence band and enters the conduction band, leaving behind a vacancy. A nearby valence electron can then move into that vacancy, creating a new vacancy behind it. The net effect is that the vacancy, the hole, propagates through the lattice.

Although holes are not fundamental particles, they behave in many respects like particles with positive charge and positive effective mass. In an electric field, holes drift in the direction of the field, opposite to the drift of electrons, and they contribute to the electrical current just as electrons do.

Holes in semiconductor types

In doped semiconductors, the balance of carriers can be controlled. In n-type material, electrons are the majority carriers and holes the minority carriers. In p-type material—created by doping with acceptor impurities such as boron in silicon—holes are the majority carriers. The junction between p-type and n-type material, the p–n junction, is the basis of diodes, transistors, and most semiconductor devices.

Hall effect

The existence of positive charge carriers was demonstrated experimentally through the Hall effect: in some materials the Hall voltage has the sign expected for positive charges rather than electrons. This was historically one of the strongest pieces of evidence that holes act as genuine positive carriers.
