The South American Football Confederation (Spanish: Confederación Sudamericana de Fútbol; Portuguese: Confederação Sul-Americana de Futebol), known by its acronym CONMEBOL, is the continental governing body of football in South America and one of FIFA's six continental confederations. Founded in 1916, it is the oldest continental football confederation in the world.

History

CONMEBOL was founded in Buenos Aires on 9 July 1916, at the initiative of the Uruguayan Héctor Rivadavia Gómez, during the first South American Championship — the tournament that became the Copa América. Its founding members were Argentina, Brazil, Chile and Uruguay. The confederation's headquarters are now in Luque, near Asunción, Paraguay.

Members

The confederation has ten member associations: Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Paraguay, Peru, Uruguay and Venezuela. Guyana, Suriname and French Guiana, although geographically in South America, are members of CONCACAF instead.

Competitions

CONMEBOL organises the Copa América, the oldest continental national-team tournament, and the South American World Cup qualifiers. At club level it runs the Copa Libertadores (founded 1960, comparable to Europe's Champions League), the Copa Sudamericana, the Recopa Sudamericana, and women's, youth, futsal and beach soccer competitions, including the Copa Libertadores Femenina.

World Cup record

South American nations have won ten FIFA World Cups: Brazil (5), Argentina (3) and Uruguay (2).
