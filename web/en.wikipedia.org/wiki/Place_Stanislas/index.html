The Place Stanislas is a large pedestrian square in the French city of Nancy, in the Lorraine region of north-eastern France. Built between 1752 and 1756 under the direction of architect Emmanuel Héré, it is often considered one of the most beautiful royal squares in Europe and a masterful example of 18th-century urban planning.

History

The square was commissioned by Stanisław Leszczyński, former King of Poland and father-in-law of Louis XV, who had become Duke of Lorraine. It was created to link the medieval old town of Nancy with the newer Charles III town to the south, and originally carried a statue of Louis XV at its center; after the French Revolution that statue was replaced, eventually, by one of Stanislas himself, erected in 1831.

Architecture

The square measures 106 by 124 metres and is surrounded by elegant classical pavilions housing, among others, the Hôtel de Ville (town hall), the Opéra national de Lorraine, the Grand Hôtel, and the Musée des Beaux-Arts. Its corners are adorned with ornate gilded wrought-iron gates and railings by the locksmith Jean Lamour, and with the Neptune and Amphitrite fountains. Together with the adjoining Place de la Carrière and Place d'Alliance, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983. In 2005, after a major restoration, the square became fully pedestrian.
