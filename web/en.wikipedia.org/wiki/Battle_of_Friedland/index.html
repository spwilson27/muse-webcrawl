Battle of Friedland

The Battle of Friedland was a major engagement fought on 14 June 1807 between French forces under Napoleon and a Russian army under Count Levin August von Bennigsen, near Friedland in East Prussia (now Pravdinsk, Russia). In a decisive afternoon battle, French artillery and infantry destroyed the Russian army in a bend of the Alle river, ending the War of the Fourth Coalition and bringing Alexander I of Russia to the peace settlement of the Treaty of Tilsit.

Course

After the drawn winter slaughter of Eylau (February 1807) and the campaign's resumption, Bennigsen advanced on Königsberg and crossed the Alle at Friedland, attacking Lannes's screening corps on the morning of 14 June. Lannes bought time until Napoleon arrived at noon with the main army. In the late afternoon Ney's corps smashed the Russian left — Senarmont's artillery advancing to point-blank range in a famous demonstration — and drove the Russians into the river bend, where thousands were killed or drowned trying to escape across fords and burning bridges.

Consequences

The Russian army ceased to exist as a fighting force in the theatre. Alexander, his German ally prostrate after Battle of Jena–Auerstedt, chose negotiation; the two emperors met at Tilsit within a fortnight. Friedland — on the anniversary of Marengo — completed Napoleon's domination of continental Europe.
