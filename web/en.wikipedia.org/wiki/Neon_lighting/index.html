Neon lighting

Neon lighting consists of brightly glowing, electrified glass tubes or bulbs filled with rarefied neon or other gases. Neon lights are a type of cold cathode gas-discharge light. A neon tube is a sealed glass tube containing metal electrodes at each end and a small amount of gas at low pressure. A high voltage applied to the electrodes causes the gas to glow.

History

Neon was discovered in 1898 by William Ramsay and Morris Travers. The French engineer Georges Muse demonstrated the first neon lamp in something like its modern form at the Paris Motor Show in 1910, and neon signs became iconic in advertising, particularly in the United States from the 1920s through the 1960s — epitomized by the Las Vegas Strip and Times Square.

Colours

Neon gas itself glows a characteristic reddish orange. Other colours are obtained using different gases (argon with mercury vapour for blue), fluorescent phosphor coatings inside the tube, or tinted glass.

Applications

Beyond signage, small neon indicator lamps were widely used in electronics. Since the early 2000s, LED lighting has displaced neon in most new signage for cost and energy reasons, though traditional glass neon remains valued in art and heritage restoration.
