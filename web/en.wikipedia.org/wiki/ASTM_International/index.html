ASTM International, formerly known as the American Society for Testing and Materials, is an international standards organization that develops and publishes voluntary consensus technical standards for a wide range of materials, products, systems, and services. More than 12,500 ASTM standards are used around the world in fields from metals, petroleum and construction to medical devices, consumer products, and additive manufacturing.

History

The organization traces its origin to 1898, when chemist Charles Benjamin Dudley and a group of engineers and scientists formed the American Section of the International Association for Testing Materials to address frequent rail breaks in the growing railroad industry by standardizing steel quality. It became the American Society for Testing Materials in 1902, added "and" in 1961, and adopted the name ASTM International in 2001 to reflect its global membership.

Process and structure

ASTM standards are written by over 140 technical committees composed of producers, users, consumers, government, and academia, with balanced voting designed to ensure consensus: negative votes must be addressed before publication. Standards are designated by a letter–number code (for example ASTM E18 for Rockwell hardness testing, ASTM A36 for structural steel) and reviewed at least every five years. ASTM is one of the largest standards-developing organizations in the world and an ANSI-accredited developer.
