Irrational rotation

Field: Dynamical systems

Map: Tθ : x ↦ x + θ (mod 1)

Key property: Minimal and uniquely ergodic when θ is irrational

In the mathematical theory of dynamical systems, an irrational rotation is a map of the circle to itself that rotates every point by a fixed angle whose ratio to a full turn is an irrational number. Formally, for θ irrational, the map Tθ(x) = x + θ mod 1 on the circle group ℝ/ℤ.

Properties

Unlike a rational rotation, whose orbits are all finite and periodic, an irrational rotation has no periodic points: every orbit is infinite. Moreover, each orbit is dense in the circle, so the map is minimal. The irrational rotation is uniquely ergodic, with Lebesgue measure as its only invariant Borel probability measure, a fact closely related to Weyl's equidistribution theorem.

Significance

Irrational rotations are among the simplest examples of ergodic transformations and serve as building blocks in the classification of dynamical systems. Their spectral and rigidity properties connect to Diophantine approximation: how well θ can be approximated by rationals governs properties such as whether the rotation is smoothly conjugate to nearby systems, a theme central to KAM theory and the work of Herman and Yoccoz.
