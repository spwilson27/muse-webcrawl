Sympathetic string

Sympathetic strings or resonance strings are auxiliary strings found on many Indian musical instruments, as well as some Western Baroque instruments and a variety of folk instruments. They are typically not played directly by the performer; instead they vibrate in sympathy with the notes played on the main strings, through the phenomenon of sympathetic resonance, enriching the tone with a shimmering, reverberant quality.

Instruments

The sitar typically has 11 to 13 sympathetic strings (taraf) beneath its main playing strings, tuned to the notes of the raga being performed. The sarod and sarangi also use sympathetic strings, as do the Norwegian Hardanger fiddle, the Swedish nyckelharpa, the viola d'amore, and the baryton, an instrument for which Haydn wrote many works. The Mohan veena and other modern slide guitars adapted for Indian music also include them.

Tuning

Sympathetic strings are usually tuned to the scale of the piece being played, so that when a matching pitch is sounded on a main string, the corresponding sympathetic string resonates. On the sitar they run through small holes in the main bridge and are tuned with pegs along the neck or body.
