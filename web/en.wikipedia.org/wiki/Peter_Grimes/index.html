Peter Grimes

ComposerBenjamin Britten
LibrettistMontagu Slater
PremiereJune 7, 1945, Sadler's Wells Theatre, London

Peter Grimes, Op. 33, is an opera in three acts by Benjamin Britten, with a libretto by Montagu Slater based on the poem The Borough (1810) by George Crabbe. It premiered on June 7, 1945, at Sadler's Wells Theatre in London, with Peter Pears in the title role, and its success established Britten as the foremost British opera composer of his generation.

Synopsis

The opera is set in a fictionalised Suffolk fishing village, "the Borough", around 1830. Peter Grimes, a fisherman and outsider, is cleared by an inquest after his apprentice dies at sea, but the townspeople remain suspicious. Grimes dreams of wealth and of marrying the schoolmistress Ellen Orford, and takes a new apprentice, John, from the workhouse. Despite Ellen's kindness, Grimes strikes the boy, and when John later falls to his death from the cliff hut, Grimes is hounded by a hostile crowd. Advised by Captain Balstrode, he sails out to sea and sinks his boat, as the Borough returns to its daily routine.

Music

The orchestral "Sea Interludes" — Dawn, Sunday Morning, Moonlight and the Passacaglia — are frequently performed separately and depict the sea as a shifting presence mirroring Grimes's torment. Britten's score blends lyrical vocal lines with the folk-like choruses of the Borough, whose collective cruelty is a central theme: the opera is read as a study of an outsider destroyed by a small community's intolerance.
