Brine shrimp
Genus: Artemia
Class: Branchiopoda
Habitat: inland salt lakes and salterns

Brine shrimp are small aquatic crustaceans of the genus Artemia, found in inland saltwater lakes and artificial salterns around the world. They tolerate extreme salinity and are widely used as live food in aquaculture and the aquarium trade; their dormant cysts are sold as "Sea-Monkeys".

Biology

Adults reach about 8–10 mm. They swim upside down, beating leaf-like appendages that also serve for feeding and respiration. Females can reproduce sexually or by parthenogenesis, producing either live young or desiccation-resistant cysts that may remain viable for years.

Uses

Newly hatched nauplii are a staple first food for larval fish and crustaceans. Major harvest sites include the Great Salt Lake in Utah and salt lakes in Kazakhstan and China.
