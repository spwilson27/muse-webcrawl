The Battle of Toulouse was fought on 9 June 721 between the forces of Odo the Great, Duke of Aquitaine, and an Umayyad Muslim army from al-Andalus commanded by its governor, Al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani, who was besieging Toulouse. Odo's surprise attack routed the besiegers and Al-Samh was mortally wounded — the first serious check to Umayyad expansion north of the Pyrenees, a decade before the more famous Battle of Tours.

Background

After conquering Visigothic Spain, Umayyad forces crossed the Pyrenees, seized Narbonne in 719 and advanced into Aquitaine. In 721 Al-Samh laid siege to Toulouse, the chief city of Odo's duchy. Odo left to gather reinforcements, possibly including Frankish troops, and returned undetected.

The battle

On 9 June 721 Odo attacked the besieging army from an unexpected direction, catching it between his relief force and the city's garrison. The Umayyad army was encircled and largely destroyed; Al-Samh escaped with a remnant but died of his wounds shortly afterwards. Chroniclers' casualty figures are certainly inflated, but the defeat was severe enough to halt Umayyad operations in the region for several years.

Aftermath

The victory confirmed Odo's prestige and Aquitaine's independence. Umayyad raiding resumed in the 720s, culminating in the campaign defeated by Charles Martel at Tours in 732.
