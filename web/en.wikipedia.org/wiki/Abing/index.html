Abing (Chinese: 阿炳; pinyin: Ābǐng; 1893–1950), born Hua Yanjun (華彥鈞), was a blind Chinese folk musician from Wuxi, Jiangsu, celebrated as a master of the erhu and pipa. His composition Erquan Yingyue ("The Moon Reflected on the Second Spring") is among the most famous pieces in Chinese music.

Life

Abing was raised in a Daoist temple where his father was a priest and musician, and he became accomplished in Daoist ritual music as well as folk styles. Eye disease progressively blinded him in adulthood, and after the temple's decline he earned his living as an itinerant street performer in Wuxi, often wearing dark glasses and playing along the city streets and bridges. Poverty and illness marked his later years.

Recordings and legacy

In the summer of 1950, months before his death, the musicologists Yang Yinliu and Cao Anhe recorded Abing performing six pieces — three erhu solos, including Erquan Yingyue, Ting Song ("Listening to the Pines") and Han Chun Feng Qu, and three pipa pieces. These recordings preserved his art from oblivion, and his grave in Wuxi, marked by a celebrated statue of him playing the erhu, has become a site of commemoration.
