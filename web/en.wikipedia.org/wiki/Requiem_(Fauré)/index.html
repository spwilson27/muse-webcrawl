The Requiem in D minor, Op. 48, by Gabriel Fauré is a setting of the Latin Requiem Mass for soprano and baritone soloists, mixed choir, orchestra, and organ. Composed between 1887 and 1890, it is Fauré's best-known large work and one of the most beloved requiems in the repertoire, noted for its calm, consoling character rather than dramatic depictions of judgement.

History

Fauré, organist and choirmaster at the Madeleine church in Paris, wrote the Requiem without a specific commission; the deaths of his father (1885) and mother (1887) are often linked to the work. The first version was performed at the Madeleine in January 1888. Fauré later expanded the instrumentation and added the Offertory and the Libera me; a full-orchestra version was published in 1901.

Movements

The work has seven movements: Introit and Kyrie; Offertory; Sanctus; Pie Jesu (for soprano solo, its most famous movement); Agnus Dei; Libera me; and In Paradisum. Fauré omitted the Dies irae sequence almost entirely, describing death as "a happy deliverance, an aspiration towards happiness above".
