Iñupiaq language

Iñupiaq (also spelled Inupiaq) is an Inuit language, or dialect group, spoken by the Iñupiat people of northern and northwestern Alaska. It belongs to the Inuit branch of the Eskaleut language family and is closely related to Canadian Inuktitut and Greenlandic, with which it forms a dialect continuum across the Arctic. The number of fluent speakers is estimated in the low thousands, and the language is considered endangered.

Dialects

Alaskan Iñupiaq falls into two main groups: Seward Peninsula Iñupiaq (including the Bering Strait and Qawiaraq dialects) and North Slope Iñupiaq (including the Malimiutun and North Slope dialects). Differences include sound correspondences and vocabulary, and mutual intelligibility varies.

Writing system

Iñupiaq is written in a Latin-based alphabet developed in the mid-20th century, using letters such as ġ, ł and ñ. Earlier writing used systems devised by missionaries. The language is polysynthetic, building complex words from roots and suffixes, and has a dual number in addition to singular and plural.

Revitalization

Immersion schools, university courses at the University of Alaska Fairbanks, and projects such as the Iñupiaq language programme on the North Slope aim to reverse language shift to English. Iñupiaq is one of Alaska's official languages under a 2014 state law.
