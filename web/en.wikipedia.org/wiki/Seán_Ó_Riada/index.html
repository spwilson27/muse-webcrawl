Seán Ó Riada

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Seán Ó Riada (born John Reidy; 1 August 1931 – 3 October 1971) was an Irish composer and arranger who is widely regarded as the single most influential figure in the revival of Irish traditional music in the twentieth century. Through his work with the ensemble Ceoltóirí Chualann and his broadcasts and writings, he brought traditional music into the concert hall and reshaped how it was arranged and understood.

Life and career

Ó Riada was born in Cork and educated at Farranferris College and University College Cork, where he studied music under Aloys Fleischmann. After a period as assistant music director at Radio Éireann and music director of the Abbey Theatre, he achieved national fame with his score for the 1959 documentary film Mise Éire ("I am Ireland"), whose music, drawn from Irish traditional airs, became an iconic sound of modern Ireland.

Ceoltóirí Chualann

In 1960 Ó Riada founded Ceoltóirí Chualann, an ensemble of traditional musicians — including Paddy Moloney, Seán Keane and Peadar Mercier — that performed traditional tunes with arranged parts, dynamics and instrumentation influenced by classical practice. The group recorded influential albums such as Ó Riada sa Gaiety (1969) and provided a template for later bands including The Chieftains, which grew directly out of it.

Later work and death

Ó Riada moved with his family to Cúil Aodha in the West Cork Gaeltacht, where he founded the choir Cór Chúil Aodha and composed liturgical music in Irish, including the Ó Riada Mass. He lectured at University College Cork from 1963. His health declined through alcohol-related illness, and he died in London in 1971, aged forty. He is buried in St Gobnait's cemetery, Baile Bhuirne.
