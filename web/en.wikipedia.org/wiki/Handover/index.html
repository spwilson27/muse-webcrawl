Handover

In cellular telecommunications, the concept of handover (known as handoff in North American English) is the process of transferring an ongoing call or data session from one channel connected to the core network to another — most commonly when a mobile device moves from the coverage area of one cell site to another, or when the current channel becomes degraded.

Types

Handovers are classified as hard (break-before-make: the old connection is released before the new one is established, as in GSM) or soft (make-before-break: the device connects to the new cell before dropping the old, as in CDMA systems). They may be horizontal (between cells of the same network technology) or vertical (between technologies, e.g., from cellular to Wi-Fi).

Purpose

Successful handover maintains service continuity and quality as users move, and allows load balancing between cells. Failed handovers result in dropped calls.
