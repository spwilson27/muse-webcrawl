LVM3

The LVM3 (Launch Vehicle Mark-3), previously referred to as the GSLV Mk III, is a three-stage medium-lift launch vehicle developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Primarily designed to launch communication satellites into geostationary orbits, it is the heaviest and most powerful launch vehicle in India's fleet. It launched the Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3 lunar missions and is human-rated for the Gaganyaan crewed programme.

Design

The LVM3 comprises two S200 solid strap-on boosters, an L110 liquid core stage powered by two Vikas engines, and a C25 cryogenic upper stage powered by the CE-20 engine. With a liftoff mass of about 640 tonnes, it can place roughly 8 tonnes into geostationary transfer orbit and about 8–10 tonnes into low Earth orbit.

History

Development began in the early 2000s. An experimental suborbital flight (LVM3-X/CARE) in December 2014 tested the vehicle and a crew module re-entry experiment. The first orbital flight, GSLV Mk III-D1 in June 2017, carried the GSAT-19 satellite. The vehicle launched Chandrayaan-3, which achieved the first soft landing near the lunar south polar region in August 2023. In 2022–2023, two LVM3 flights deployed 72 OneWeb broadband satellites in total. A human-rated variant, HLVM3, is being prepared for India's first crewed spaceflight programme, Gaganyaan.
