James Oglethorpe

Born: 22 December 1696, London
Died: 30 June 1785
Known for: Founding the Province of Georgia

James Edward Oglethorpe (22 December 1696 – 30 June 1785) was a British soldier, Member of Parliament, and philanthropist who founded the Province of Georgia in British North America. As the driving force behind the Georgia trustees, he designed the colony as a haven for the "worthy poor" and a buffer between British South Carolina and Spanish Florida.

Early life and politics

Oglethorpe was educated at Eton and Oxford and served briefly in European military campaigns. Elected to Parliament in 1722, he gained prominence chairing a committee investigating conditions in debtors' prisons after a friend died in the Fleet Prison. His campaign for prison reform fed directly into his colonial scheme.

Georgia

In 1732 George II granted a charter to the Trustees for the Establishment of the Colony of Georgia, with Oglethorpe as its leading spirit. He sailed with the first colonists in 1732–33, founded Savannah according to the celebrated grid plan known as the Oglethorpe Plan, and governed the colony in practice for a decade. Georgia under the trustees initially prohibited slavery and large landholdings. Oglethorpe negotiated with the Yamacraw chief Tomochichi, defended the colony against Spain in the War of Jenkins' Ear — notably at the Battle of Bloody Marsh (1742) — and returned to England in 1743.

Later life and legacy

Oglethorpe remained an MP until 1754, opposed the Jacobite rising of 1745 in arms, and in later life moved in London literary society. He died in 1785 aged 88. Savannah's squares, the city of Oglethorpe in Georgia, and Oglethorpe University in Atlanta commemorate his name.
