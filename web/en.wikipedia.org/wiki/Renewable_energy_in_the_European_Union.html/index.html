Renewable energy in the European Union

Renewable energy in the European Union has grown substantially since the early 2000s under EU directives setting binding targets for member states. Renewables supplied roughly a quarter of the EU's gross final energy consumption in the early 2020s, with higher shares in electricity generation, led by wind, hydro, solar and biomass.

The Renewable Energy Directive established a target of at least 42.5% renewable energy by 2030, part of the European Green Deal goal of climate neutrality by 2050. Sweden, Finland, Latvia and Denmark have among the highest renewable shares, driven by hydropower, biomass and wind.
