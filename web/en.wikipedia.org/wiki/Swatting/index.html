Swatting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Swatting is a criminal harassment tactic of deceiving an emergency service (via a false report of a serious law enforcement emergency, such as a bomb threat, murder, or hostage situation) into sending a police response — usually a SWAT team — to another person's address. The term derives from SWAT (Special Weapons and Tactics), a specialised type of law enforcement unit in the United States.

Overview

Perpetrators typically conceal their identity through caller ID spoofing and internet telephony, and target victims including celebrities, politicians, journalists, and online streamers, sometimes broadcasting the resulting raid live. Swatting is dangerous: armed officers arrive expecting a violent emergency, and occupants may be injured or killed. In a widely reported 2017 case in Wichita, Kansas, an innocent man, Andrew Finch, was shot and killed by police responding to a hoax call arising from a dispute between online gamers; the caller was later sentenced to 20 years in prison.

Legal response

Swatting is prosecuted under laws against false emergency reports, computer fraud, and interstate threats, and several US states have enacted specific anti-swatting statutes with enhanced penalties, especially where injury or death results. The FBI has tracked the phenomenon since the 2000s and maintains a database of incidents. Law enforcement agencies have adopted verification protocols and registries for at-risk addresses to reduce the danger.

In popular culture

Swatting has been associated with online gaming culture and harassment campaigns, and has been depicted in television dramas and documentaries about internet crime.
