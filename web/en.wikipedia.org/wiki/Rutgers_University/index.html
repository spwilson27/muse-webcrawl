Rutgers University (formally Rutgers, The State University of New Jersey) is a public land-grant research university consisting of three regional campuses in New Brunswick, Newark, and Camden, New Jersey. It is the state's flagship public university and the eighth-oldest institution of higher education in the United States.

History

Chartered in 1766 as Queen's College by Dutch Reformed clergy, the college was renamed Rutgers College in 1825 after philanthropist Colonel Henry Rutgers. It became the land-grant college of New Jersey under the Morrill Act of 1862 and was designated the state university in 1945 and 1956. In 1869 Rutgers and Princeton played the first intercollegiate American football game, which Rutgers won 6–4. The University of Newark merged into Rutgers in 1946, and the College of South Jersey in Camden followed in 1950. In 2013 most of the University of Medicine and Dentistry of New Jersey was integrated into Rutgers, greatly expanding its health sciences presence.

Academics and athletics

Rutgers is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity". Its New Brunswick campus is a member of the Big Ten Conference, and its teams compete as the Scarlet Knights. Notable alumni include Nobel laureates, the actor James Gandolfini, and Paul Robeson, the distinguished singer, actor, and activist who graduated in 1919.
