Theodoric the Great (454–526) was king of the Ostrogoths, ruler of the independent Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy, and regent of the Visigothic Kingdom. Raised as a hostage at the Byzantine court in Constantinople, he became a Roman consul and patrician before leading his people into Italy.

Rule in Italy

Commissioned by the emperor Zeno, Theodoric invaded Italy in 489 and defeated Odoacer, whom he killed in 493 after a negotiated settlement. Ruling from Ravenna, he governed Romans and Goths under separate laws, maintained Roman administration and the Senate, promoted religious tolerance between his Arian Goths and Chalcedonian Romans, and undertook major building works, including his palace and the church now called La Rotonda. His mausoleum in Ravenna, with its great monolithic dome, still stands.

Final years and legacy

His later reign was marred by the execution of the philosopher Boethius (524) and Pope John I's imprisonment. He died in 526; his kingdom collapsed in the Gothic War a generation later. In Germanic legend he is remembered as Dietrich von Bern.
