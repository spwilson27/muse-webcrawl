The National Air and Space Museum (NASM) of the Smithsonian Institution holds the world's largest collection of historic aircraft and spacecraft. It was established in 1946 as the National Air Museum, and its main building on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., opened in 1976. In 2003 the museum opened a companion facility, the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center near Dulles International Airport in Virginia.

Collection

Landmark artifacts include the original 1903 Wright Flyer, Charles Lindbergh's Spirit of St. Louis, the Bell X-1 that first broke the sound barrier, the Apollo 11 command module Columbia, a lunar rock sample visitors may touch, the Space Shuttle Discovery (at the Udvar-Hazy Center), and an SR-71 Blackbird. The museum holds more than 68,000 objects in total, along with archives on aviation and space history.

History and renovation

The Smithsonian's aeronautical collection began with kites acquired in the 19th century and grew with donations from the Wright brothers and the U.S. military. The Mall building, designed by architect Gyo Obata, opened for the United States Bicentennial on 1 July 1976 and long ranked among the most-visited museums in the world. A multi-year revitalisation begun in 2018 reconfigured the galleries, replaced the building's stone facade, and reopened in phases from 2022 onward.

Research

The museum's Center for Earth and Planetary Studies conducts planetary science research, including work on the geology of Mars, the Moon, and other bodies, and participates in NASA missions.
