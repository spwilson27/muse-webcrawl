Nashik

Nashik
State: Maharashtra, India
Population: approx. 1.5 million (city, 2011)
Known for: Kumbh Mela, vineyards, wine industry

Nashik is a city in the northern region of the Indian state of Maharashtra, situated on the banks of the Godavari River about 165 kilometres northeast of Mumbai. It is one of the Hindu pilgrimage sites of the Kumbh Mela, held here every twelve years on the river's banks at Ram Kund, and is associated with episodes of the Ramayana: the Panchavati area is traditionally where Rama, Sita and Lakshmana spent part of their exile, and where Lakshmana is said to have cut off the nose (nāsikā) of Surpanakha, giving the city its name.

Economy

Nashik is a major centre of agriculture, especially onions and table grapes, and has become India's leading wine-producing region, home to Sula Vineyards and dozens of other wineries in the surrounding valley. Industry includes the Hindustan Aeronautics aircraft plant at Ojhar, the India Security Press and Currency Note Press, and a growing cluster of engineering and pharmaceutical firms.

Sites

Notable sites include the Trimbakeshwar Shiva Temple (one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, near the Godavari's source), the Pandavleni Buddhist caves, Sita Gufa, and the Kalaram Temple in Panchavati built in black stone in the 18th century.
