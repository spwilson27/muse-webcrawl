The T-54 and T-55 are Soviet main battle tanks introduced after the Second World War. Together with their derivatives—including the Chinese Type 59—they form the most-produced tank family in history, with estimates of 86,000 to 100,000 vehicles. The T-54/55 became the standard tank of the Warsaw Pact and was exported to dozens of countries, seeing combat in conflicts from the Middle East and Africa to the Balkans, and upgraded versions remain in service today.

Development

Developed at Nizhny Tagil as a successor to the T-44, the T-54 entered production in 1946–47 with a low, well-armoured hull, hemispherical turret and 100 mm D-10 gun. Early models lacked NBC protection and gun stabilisation. The T-55 (1958) added a full NBC protection system, a more powerful V-55 engine, increased ammunition stowage and other refinements, without major external changes.

Service

The tanks first drew attention during the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, and fought extensively in the Arab–Israeli wars of 1967 and 1973, the Indo-Pakistani wars, the Iran–Iraq War, the Ogaden War, and the Yugoslav Wars. Numerous upgrade programmes (T-55M, Israeli Tiran conversions, Slovenian M-55S, Ukrainian variants) have kept the type viable with new fire-control systems, thermal sights and explosive reactive armour. Russia and Ukraine have both employed stored T-54/55-series vehicles during the war that began in 2022.

Variants

Variants include command tanks, the ZSU-57-2 self-propelled anti-aircraft gun, engineer vehicles and armoured recovery vehicles, as well as licensed production in Poland, Czechoslovakia and China.
