Debutante

[Image: Debutantes in white gowns being presented at a debutante ball]

A debutante, also spelled débutante (from French débutante, "beginner"), is a young woman of aristocratic or upper-class background who has reached maturity and is introduced to society at a formal "debut" presentation, traditionally a debutante ball. Historically, the debut marked a young woman's eligibility for marriage to suitable bachelors of similar standing, and her formal presentation—often at court—was a central ritual of the social Season.

History

In Britain, debutantes were presented to the monarch at court, a practice formalised in the 18th and 19th centuries and accompanied by strict dress codes, including white gowns and ostrich feathers. The last presentations at Buckingham Palace took place in 1958, after which the custom was abolished by Queen Elizabeth II. In the United States, debutante balls flourished in cities such as New York, Charleston, and New Orleans, and cotillion traditions became especially prominent in the South.

Modern debutante balls

Today debutante balls survive as social and charitable events rather than marriage markets. The International Debutante Ball in New York presents young women from many countries, often the daughters of prominent families. In Paris, le Bal des Débutantes brings together around twenty young women from notable families worldwide, dressed by haute couture houses, and raises money for charity. Similar traditions exist in African-American society, where debutante balls sponsored by organisations such as The Links celebrate scholarship and community service, and in Filipino culture, where the "debut" celebrates a young woman's 18th birthday.
