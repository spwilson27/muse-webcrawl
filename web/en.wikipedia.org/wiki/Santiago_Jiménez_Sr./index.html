Santiago Jiménez Sr. (1913 – 18 December 1984), known as Don Santiago or El Flaco, was an American accordionist from San Antonio, Texas, regarded as one of the founding fathers of conjunto music. He was the father of Leonardo "Flaco" Jiménez and Santiago Jiménez Jr., both celebrated accordionists in their own right.

Life and music

Jiménez was born in San Antonio to a musical family; his father Patricio Jiménez had played accordion at dances in South Texas. Santiago took up the instrument young, mastering the older German-style two-row diatonic button accordion that remained his trademark long after three-row accordions became fashionable. He first recorded in 1936 for Decca Records, and his early hit "Dicesa" (also known as "La Piedrera") helped establish his reputation.

Through the 1940s–1960s he recorded prolifically for regional labels such as Corona and Discos RyN, playing polkas, redovas, waltzes, huapangos and canciones with bajo sexto accompaniment. Unlike the jazz- and orquesta-influenced stylists, Jiménez Sr. preserved an archaic, dance-hall-rooted style: spare, staccato, and strongly melodic, closely tied to the working-class dance culture of San Antonio's west side.

Legacy

With Narciso Martínez, Jiménez Sr. is one of the two great patriarchs of the accordion in conjunto; his clean two-row technique and traditional repertoire became the standard against which later players were judged. His sons carried the music to international audiences — Flaco Jiménez through collaborations with rock, country and roots musicians worldwide, and Santiago Jr. as the self-conscious standard-bearer of his father's traditional style.

Jiménez Sr. died in San Antonio in 1984. He was inducted into the Conjunto Music Hall of Fame, and his recordings have been repeatedly reissued by Arhoolie and Smithsonian Folkways as essential documents of Mexican-American music.
