Pellet stove

TypeHeating appliance
FuelWood or biomass pellets
FeedMotorised auger
EfficiencyTypically 70–90%

A pellet stove is a stove that burns compressed wood or biomass pellets to create a source of heat for residential and sometimes industrial spaces. Pellet stoves are a type of pellet fuel appliance.

Operation

Pellets are loaded into a storage hopper and fed into the burn chamber by a motor-driven auger at a controlled rate. Electric fans supply combustion air and circulate warmed air into the room, so pellet stoves require electricity but offer thermostatic control and long unattended burn times between refills. Ash accumulates in a pan that needs periodic emptying.

Advantages and disadvantages

Pellet fuel is made from sawdust and wood waste, is dense and dry, and burns more cleanly and efficiently than cordwood, with typical efficiencies of 70–90 percent and low particulate emissions compared with conventional wood stoves. Disadvantages include the cost of the appliance, dependence on electricity, fan noise, and the need for dry pellet storage.
