Wide receiver

A wide receiver (WR), also called a wideout, is an offensive position in American football and Canadian football. Wide receivers are among the fastest and most agile players on the field; their primary role is to run pass routes, get open, and catch forward passes from the quarterback. They line up split wide near the sidelines, on or near the line of scrimmage.

A wide receiver catching a pass

Roles and types

Offences typically deploy two to five receivers. Common designations include the split end (X receiver), who lines up on the line of scrimmage, often facing press coverage; the flanker (Z receiver), set slightly off the line; and the slot receiver, who lines up between the split end and the offensive line and often works the middle of the field. Receivers must also block on running plays and screen passes.

Skills and records

The position rewards speed, route-running precision, hands, body control, and the ability to gain yards after the catch. Jerry Rice, who played chiefly for the San Francisco 49ers, holds the NFL career records for receptions, receiving yards, and receiving touchdowns and is widely regarded as the greatest wide receiver in history. Other celebrated receivers include Randy Moss, Larry Fitzgerald, and Calvin Johnson.
