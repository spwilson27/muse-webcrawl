La La Land is a 2016 American musical romantic drama film written and directed by Damien Chazelle. It stars Ryan Gosling and Emma Stone as Sebastian Wilder, a struggling jazz pianist, and Mia Dolan, an aspiring actress, who meet and fall in love in Los Angeles while pursuing their dreams. The title refers both to the city — "La La Land" being a nickname for Los Angeles — and to the idiom of being out of touch with reality.

Plot and production

Mia, a barista on a studio lot, and Sebastian, a purist who dreams of opening his own jazz club, meet amid Los Angeles traffic in the opening number "Another Day of Sun", filmed on a freeway ramp in a single take. Their romance blossoms through set pieces evoking classic Hollywood musicals — a tap duet at Griffith Park ("A Lovely Night"), a planetarium waltz at the Griffith Observatory — but ambition pulls them apart: Sebastian tours with a commercially successful band led by Keith (John Legend), while Mia stages a one-woman play and then wins a major audition with her song "Audition (The Fools Who Dream)". An epilogue presents an extended musical fantasy of the life they might have had together, before a bittersweet final exchange of glances in Sebastian's club. Songs by Justin Hurwitz, with lyrics by Benj Pasek and Justin Paul, include "City of Stars" and "Mia & Sebastian's Theme".

Reception and awards

Premiering at Venice in August 2016, the film was a critical and commercial success, grossing $472 million worldwide. It received a record-tying 14 Academy Award nominations and won six Oscars, including Best Director (Chazelle, the youngest winner in the category at 32), Best Actress (Stone), Best Original Score and Best Original Song ("City of Stars"). At the 89th Academy Awards the film was mistakenly announced as Best Picture — the envelope error — before the award was corrected to Moonlight, an incident that became one of the most famous moments in Oscar history. It also won a record seven Golden Globes from seven nominations and five BAFTAs.
