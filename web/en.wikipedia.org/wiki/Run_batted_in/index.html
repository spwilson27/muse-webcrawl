Run batted in

A run batted in or runs batted in (RBI) is a statistic in baseball and softball that credits a batter for making a play that allows a run to be scored (except in certain situations such as when an error is made on the play).

Rules

RBIs are awarded when a run scores as a result of a hit, a sacrifice bunt or fly, an infield out or fielder's choice, a bases-loaded walk or hit by pitch, or (with exceptions) an error or interference call. A batter is not credited with an RBI when a run scores on a ground-ball double play, or on a wild pitch or passed ball. RBIs have been an official Major League Baseball statistic since 1920.

Records

The career RBI record in Major League Baseball is held by Hank Aaron with 2,297. The single-season record is 191, set by Hack Wilson in 1930. The statistic has been criticised by sabermetricians because it depends heavily on the opportunities a batter receives from teammates reaching base, but it remains a popular measure of offensive production.
