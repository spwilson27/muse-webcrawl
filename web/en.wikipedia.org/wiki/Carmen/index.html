Carmen is an opera in four acts by the French composer Georges Bizet, with a libretto by Henri Meilhac and Ludovic Halévy, based on the novella of the same name by Prosper Mérimée. It was first performed at the Opéra-Comique in Paris on 3 March 1875. Initially a critical failure that scandalised audiences with its realism and tragic ending, it went on to become one of the most popular and frequently performed operas in the world.

Synopsis

Set in and around Seville, Spain, the opera tells of Don José, a naïve soldier who is seduced by the fiery gypsy Carmen, a cigarette-factory worker. José abandons his childhood sweetheart Micaëla and his military duties to join Carmen's smuggler companions. When Carmen tires of him and turns to the toreador Escamillo, José's jealousy drives him to murder her outside the bullring while the crowd inside acclaims Escamillo's victory.

Music

The score contains some of opera's best-known numbers: Carmen's "Habanera" (L'amour est un oiseau rebelle), the "Seguidilla," Escamillo's "Toreador Song," Don José's "Flower Song," and the fate motif that threads through the work. Bizet's writing combines Spanish colour, brilliant orchestration, and memorable melody.

Reception

Bizet died suddenly three months after the premiere, never knowing the work's success. The Vienna production later in 1875 began its international triumph; Brahms, Wagner, and Tchaikovsky admired it, and Nietzsche praised it as an antidote to Wagnerian heaviness. Carmen is a cornerstone of the operatic repertoire and has been adapted into ballets, musicals (such as Carmen Jones), and numerous films.
