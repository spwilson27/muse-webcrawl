BFI Southbank is a four-screen cinema complex on the South Bank of the River Thames in London, operated by the British Film Institute (BFI). It began as the National Film Theatre, which was first established in 1952, using the Telekinema built for the 1951 Festival of Britain, and moved in 1957 to its present home, built into the arches beneath Waterloo Bridge.

Programming

The venue is the United Kingdom's leading repertory and arthouse cinema, screening retrospectives, directors' seasons, restored classics, and previews alongside new releases. It is the principal home of the BFI London Film Festival, founded in 1957, and of BFI Flare, the London LGBTQIA+ film festival. The building also contains the BFI Reuben Library, a mediatheque offering free access to archive film and television, bars, and a bookshop.

Redevelopment

Following a major refurbishment, the building reopened in 2007 with a new entrance on Theatre Avenue and was renamed BFI Southbank, retiring the National Film Theatre name. The largest auditorium, NFT1, seats over 400 and is regularly used for on-stage interviews and festival galas.
