American Notes

American Notes for General Circulation is a travelogue by Charles Dickens detailing his trip to North America from January to June 1842. Published in October 1842, it describes his journey through the United States and Canada in frequently critical terms, examining American institutions, manners, and the practice of slavery.

Background

Dickens, already famous at thirty, sailed to Boston aboard the steamship Britannia and was fêted across the young republic. He visited Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Washington, D.C., Richmond, St. Louis, Cincinnati, Niagara Falls, and Montreal, touring prisons, factories, asylums, and schools. He was initially lionised but grew weary of invasions of his privacy and of the American press, and his campaigning for international copyright, which the United States did not recognise for foreign authors, angered American newspapers.

Content and reception

The book combines vivid comic set pieces with sharp criticism of American spitting tobacco, sensational journalism, boosterism, and, most powerfully, slavery, which Dickens condemned in a chapter assembled partly from abolitionist sources. American reviewers largely panned the book, while British critics were mixed. Dickens returned to American subjects in his novel Martin Chuzzlewit (1843–44), which satirised the United States even more harshly, though he returned in 1867–68 for a triumphant reading tour and softened his views in a later postscript to American Notes.
