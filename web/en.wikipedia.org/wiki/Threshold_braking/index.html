Threshold braking

ContextVehicle dynamics, motorsport driving technique
Related conceptsAnti-lock braking system (ABS), cadence braking, trail braking, slip ratio

Threshold braking or limit braking is a driving technique in which the driver applies the brakes to the maximum pressure possible without locking the wheels or, in vehicles equipped with an anti-lock braking system (ABS), just short of the point at which ABS intervenes. Braking is most effective when the tires operate at their peak friction, which occurs at a modest slip ratio—slightly below the slip at which the wheel locks and begins to slide.

Principle

A tire generates peak longitudinal (braking) force when it is slipping slightly relative to the road surface—typically in the region of 10–20% slip on dry pavement. Brake pressure beyond this "threshold" locks the wheel; a locked, sliding tire generates less braking force and no directional (steering) control, lengthening stopping distances and making steering ineffective. Threshold braking keeps the tire at the top of its friction curve, minimizing stopping distance while preserving the ability to steer around obstacles.

Technique

The driver applies firm, progressive pedal pressure and modulates it in response to feedback through the pedal and steering—a shudder or squeal indicates impending lockup, at which point pressure is eased slightly. The threshold varies with surface grip (rain, gravel, and ice lower it considerably) and changes as weight transfers forward under braking and as speed decreases. Skilled drivers vary pressure per surface and adjust continuously.

Comparison with ABS and cadence braking

ABS performs rapid modulation of brake pressure automatically, simulating threshold braking many times per second and preserving steering control for average drivers, generally outperforming manual threshold braking on dry and wet paved surfaces. On loose surfaces such as gravel or deep snow, a locked wheel can build a wedge of material that shortens stopping distance, so ABS (and true threshold braking) may stop less quickly there. Cadence braking—manually pumping the pedal—is an older, less effective predecessor technique for non-ABS vehicles. Threshold braking remains a core skill in racing, where ABS is often prohibited and drivers exploit the threshold together with trail braking into corners.
