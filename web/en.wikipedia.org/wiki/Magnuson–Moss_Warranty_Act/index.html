Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act

The Magnuson–Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 2301–2312) is a United States federal law enacted in 1975 governing warranties on consumer products. Sponsored by Senator Warren G. Magnuson and Representative John E. Moss, it was designed to make warranties easier for consumers to understand and enforce, and to give the Federal Trade Commission authority to police deceptive warranty practices.

Provisions

The Act does not require any product to be sold with a warranty, but if a written warranty is offered it must be designated as either "full" or "limited," meet federal minimum standards if called full, and be available for the consumer to read before purchase. It also protects consumers' rights under implied warranties, prohibits "tie-in" provisions that void a warranty merely because the owner used independent parts or service, and encourages informal dispute resolution.

Enforcement

Consumers who are unable to resolve a warranty dispute may sue in state or federal court; a successful consumer can recover court costs and attorney's fees from the warrantor. The Act works alongside state "lemon laws," which provide additional remedies for defective vehicles.
