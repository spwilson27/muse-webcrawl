Oligomenorrhea

Oligomenorrhea is infrequent menstrual bleeding, conventionally defined as menstrual cycles lasting longer than 35 days, or fewer than nine periods in a year. It is common in adolescence and during the approach to menopause, but persistent oligomenorrhea in adults may indicate an underlying endocrine disorder.

Causes

Common causes include polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), thyroid dysfunction, hyperprolactinemia, excessive exercise or low energy availability (as in the female athlete triad), eating disorders, and certain medications. Physiological oligomenorrhea also occurs during breastfeeding and perimenopause.

Evaluation and treatment

Evaluation typically includes pregnancy testing, hormone assays (such as TSH, prolactin, and androgens), and pelvic imaging where indicated. Treatment addresses the underlying cause; hormonal contraception is often used to regularize cycles, and managing chronic anovulation is important because unopposed estrogen exposure increases the risk of endometrial hyperplasia.
