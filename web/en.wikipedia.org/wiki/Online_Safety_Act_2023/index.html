Online Safety Act 2023

JurisdictionUnited Kingdom
Royal assent26 October 2023
RegulatorOfcom
OriginOnline Harms White Paper (2019); draft Online Safety Bill (2021)

The Online Safety Act 2023 (c. 50) is an act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom to regulate online content. Designed to protect children and adults online, it imposes duties of care on social media companies, search engines and other user-to-user services, enforced by the regulator Ofcom, which can levy fines of up to £18 million or 10% of global turnover, and in some cases block services or hold managers criminally liable.

Provisions

In-scope services must assess the risk of illegal content and of content harmful to children appearing on their platforms, and take proportionate measures to remove illegal material — including terrorism and child sexual abuse material — and to prevent children from encountering pornography and content promoting self-harm, bullying or dangerous behaviour. Very large platforms face additional duties, including user-verification and content-empowerment tools for adults. The Act created new offences, including cyberflashing and sending communications intended to cause non-trivial harm, and requires age-assurance measures for pornography sites.

Controversy

The Act, developed from the 2019 Online Harms White Paper through years of contested drafting, was criticised by digital-rights groups and technology firms over its earlier "legal but harmful" duties (largely removed before passage), its powers to require scanning of encrypted messages, and its potential effects on privacy, free expression, and smaller websites and community forums, some of which closed rather than bear compliance costs. Supporters, including bereaved families and children's charities, hailed it as a landmark in holding platforms to account. Ofcom began phased enforcement, with child-safety duties coming into force during 2025.
