Atahualpa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The ransom room (Cuarto del Rescate) at Cajamarca, with the line marking the fill height]

Atahualpa (c. 1502 – 26 July 1533) was the last effective Sapa Inca of the Inca Empire, whose capture by Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca (16 November 1532) and execution the following year opened the Spanish conquest of Peru. Victor in the Inca civil war against his half-brother Huáscar — a war fought as smallpox (probably preceding the Spaniards) killed their father Huayna Capac and destabilised the succession — Atahualpa met Pizarro's 168 men with a retinue of thousands, was seized in an ambush in Cajamarca's plaza, and from captivity offered the famous ransom: a room filled once with gold and twice with silver. The ransom was paid — some six tonnes of gold — and Atahualpa was nevertheless garrotted after a show trial, baptised at the end with the name Juan de Atahualpa (some accounts say Juan Santos).

Civil war and Cajamarca

Atahualpa, based at Quito with his father's veteran northern army and its great generals (Quizquiz, Chalcuchima, Rumiñawi), defeated Huáscar's Cuzco forces in a brutal war (1529–1532) culminating at Quipaipán; Huáscar was captured and his lineage massacred. Days after the victory, news came of the strangers on the coast. At Cajamarca Atahualpa entered the plaza in procession with thousands of attendants (mostly unarmed, the sources say, or lightly armed — a matter still argued); after the Dominican Valverde's requerimiento and the throwing down of his book (breviary or Bible), Pizarro's hidden men attacked with cannon, cavalry and steel, killing thousands and capturing the Inca untouched.

Ransom and execution

The ransom room at Cajamarca (about 6.7 × 5.2 metres, filled to a line 2.75 metres high) accumulated the empire's masterworks in gold and silver, melted into ingots for division among the conquerors — an artistic annihilation as much as a payment. Fearing rescue, and pressed by rumours of an approaching army (and by his prisoner Huáscar's murder at Atahualpa's order), Pizarro's council condemned Atahualpa for treason, idolatry and fratricide; he was executed by garrote on 26 July 1533 (some sources give 29 August) after baptism spared him burning. His death shattered Inca command at a stroke; resistance continued under Manco Inca and the neo-Inca state of Vilcabamba until 1572.
