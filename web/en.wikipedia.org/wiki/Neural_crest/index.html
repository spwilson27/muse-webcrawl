Neural crest

The neural crest is a collection of cells that arises from the embryonic ectoderm at the border of the neural plate during neurulation in vertebrates. Neural crest cells undergo an epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, delaminate from the closing neural tube, and migrate extensively throughout the embryo, giving rise to an exceptionally wide range of cell types.

Derivatives

Neural crest derivatives include most of the peripheral nervous system (sensory and autonomic neurons and Schwann cells), melanocytes, much of the craniofacial skeleton and connective tissue, smooth muscle, the adrenal medulla's chromaffin cells and parts of the heart's outflow tract. Because of this range, the neural crest is sometimes called the "fourth germ layer."

Clinical significance

Disorders of neural crest development, termed neurocristopathies, include Hirschsprung disease, Waardenburg syndrome, DiGeorge syndrome and neuroblastoma.
