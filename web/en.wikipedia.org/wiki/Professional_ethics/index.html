Professional ethics

Professional ethics encompass the personal and corporate standards of behavior expected of professionals. The term describes the moral principles and codes of conduct that govern practice within a particular profession, such as medicine, law, engineering, accounting, journalism, and teaching. Professional ethics are usually codified by professional bodies in formal documents — codes of ethics or codes of conduct — that members are expected to follow, with sanctions ranging from reprimand to expulsion for violations.

Components

Most professional codes share common elements: honesty and integrity in dealings with clients and the public; competence and diligence in the performance of professional duties; confidentiality regarding information obtained in a professional capacity; avoidance or disclosure of conflicts of interest; and responsibility to the public interest, which may override obligations to individual clients or employers.

Examples by profession

In medicine, professional ethics derive in part from the Hippocratic Oath and principles such as beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, and justice. In law, codes of professional responsibility govern duties to clients, courts, and opposing parties. Engineers' codes typically emphasize holding paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public. Journalistic ethics address accuracy, fairness, and the protection of sources.

Enforcement

Professional ethics are enforced by licensing boards and professional associations, which may investigate complaints and discipline members. In regulated professions, ethical breaches can result in loss of licensure. Ethics education is a required component of training in many professions, and continuing ethics education is commonly required for license renewal.
