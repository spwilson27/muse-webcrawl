Hendiadys

Hendiadys (from Greek hen dia dyoin, "one through two") is a figure of speech in which two words joined by a conjunction are used to express a single complex idea, where one word modifies the other, rather than to denote two separate things. For example, "nice and warm" can mean "nicely warm", and "sound and fury" conveys an idea of noisy fury rather than two unrelated nouns.

Usage

The device is common in Latin poetry, especially Virgil, and in the Hebrew Bible, where pairs such as "tohu wa-bohu" ("formless and void") occur. Shakespeare used it extensively — scholars have counted dozens of examples in Hamlet alone, such as "the perfume and suppliance of a minute".

Hendiadys differs from a simple pair of synonyms because the two terms together produce a meaning neither carries alone; the first element typically functions semantically as a modifier. A related figure, hendiatris, uses three words.
