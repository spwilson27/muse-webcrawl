Russian science fiction and fantasy

Russian science fiction and fantasy have been part of Russian literature since the 18th century. Russian science fiction developed in the Soviet period into one of the most popular genres in the USSR, and Russian writers such as Yevgeny Zamyatin, Alexander Belyaev, Ivan Yefremov and the brothers Arkady and Boris Strugatsky gained international recognition.

History

Early Russian speculative works included utopian and satirical novels of the 18th and 19th centuries. Zamyatin's dystopian novel We (1921) influenced later Western dystopias. In the Soviet Union, Alexander Belyaev ("the Russian Jules Verne") popularised scientific adventure, and Ivan Yefremov's Andromeda: A Space-Age Tale (1957) opened the "Golden Age" of Soviet SF. The Strugatsky brothers brought philosophical and social themes to the genre in works such as Hard to Be a God and Roadside Picnic.

Fantasy and modern era

Fantasy emerged later, drawing on Slavic folklore; after 1991 it became a dominant commercial genre in Russia, alongside science fiction, with authors such as Sergey Lukyanenko (the Night Watch series) achieving bestseller status and film adaptations.
