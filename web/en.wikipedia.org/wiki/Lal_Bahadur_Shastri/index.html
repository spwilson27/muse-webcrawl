Lal Bahadur Shastri (2 October 1904 – 11 January 1966) was an Indian politician who served as the second Prime Minister of India from 1964 until his death in 1966. A senior leader of the Indian National Congress and a close associate of Jawaharlal Nehru, he was noted for his modesty, integrity, and administrative ability.

Early life and career

Born in Mughalsarai in what is now Uttar Pradesh, Shastri joined the independence movement as a young man, was repeatedly imprisoned by the British, and earned the title "Shastri" as a scholar of the Kashi Vidyapith. After independence he held key portfolios including Railways — resigning in 1956 after a major rail accident, accepting moral responsibility — and Home Affairs, and was widely credited as an effective consensus-builder within the Congress party.

Premiership

Succeeding Nehru in June 1964, Shastri confronted food shortages and, in 1965, war with Pakistan. His calm leadership during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 and his slogan "Jai Jawan Jai Kisan" ("Hail the soldier, hail the farmer") made him immensely popular. He promoted the White Revolution in milk production and laid the groundwork for the Green Revolution, backing figures such as M. S. Swaminathan and Verghese Kurien. On 10 January 1966 he signed the Tashkent Declaration with Pakistan's President Ayub Khan, restoring peace; he died in Tashkent the following night, officially of a heart attack — a death that has remained the subject of enduring speculation in India. He was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna.
