Francisco Rabaneda Cuervo (18 February 1934 – 3 February 2023), more commonly known under the nom de plume Paco Rabanne, was a Spanish fashion designer. In the 1960s, he was known as an enfant terrible of fashion and became famous for his metallic, space-age designs made from unconventional materials such as metal, plastic, and paper.

Early life

Rabanne was born in the Basque town of Pasajes, Gipuzkoa. His mother was a chief seamstress at Cristóbal Balenciaga's first couture house in Spain, and his father, a Republican colonel, was executed by Francoist troops during the Spanish Civil War. The family fled to France in 1939. Rabanne studied architecture at the École Nationale des Beaux-Arts in Paris while making sketches and accessories for fashion houses.

Career

He began his career creating jewellery for Givenchy, Dior, and Balenciaga. In 1966 he presented his first collection, "Twelve Unwearable Dresses in Contemporary Materials", featuring dresses assembled from metal discs and plastic linked with pliers and a blowtorch. Coco Chanel reportedly called him "the metal worker". His futuristic designs appeared in films such as Barbarella (1968), for which he designed costumes.

His fashion house was acquired by the Spanish group Puig, which expanded the brand's fragrance business; perfumes such as Paco Rabanne Pour Homme (1973), Calandre, XS, and later 1 Million (2008) and Lady Million became international bestsellers. Rabanne retired from fashion in 1999. The house, since 2023 known simply as Rabanne, continues under Puig with creative direction by Julien Dossena from 2013.
