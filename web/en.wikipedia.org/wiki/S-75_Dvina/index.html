The S-75 Dvina (Russian: С-75 «Двина»; NATO reporting name SA-2 Guideline) is a Soviet-designed, high-altitude air defence system built around a command-guided surface-to-air missile. First deployed in 1957, it became the most widely used air defence missile system in history and the first SAM to shoot down an aircraft in combat conditions over an adversary's territory.

Design

The system centres on the V-750 missile, a two-stage weapon with a solid-fuel booster and a liquid-fuel sustainer, carrying a large fragmentation warhead. A typical battery comprised six single-rail launchers arranged around a Fan Song engagement radar and a Spoon Rest early-warning radar, all coordinated from a command van. Effective engagement altitude reached roughly 25 km with a range of up to about 45 km in later versions.

Combat history

The S-75 gained worldwide fame on 1 May 1960, when a battery near Sverdlovsk shot down Francis Gary Powers' U-2 spyplane. In the Vietnam War, North Vietnamese S-75s (with the related S-75M Volkhov) forced US aircraft to fly lower and spurred the development of Wild Weasel suppression tactics and electronic countermeasures. The system was exported throughout the Warsaw Pact and to China, Egypt, Syria, India, and many others, and saw action in the Six-Day War, Yom Kippur War, Iran–Iraq War, and Gulf War. China's unlicensed copy is the HQ-2. Though obsolete against modern threats, upgraded S-75s remain in service in a number of countries, and Yemen's Houthi forces have used adapted missiles in air-to-air roles.
