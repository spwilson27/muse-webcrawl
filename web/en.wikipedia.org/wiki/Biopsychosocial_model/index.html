Biopsychosocial model
Proposed byGeorge L. Engel (1977)
DomainsBiological, psychological, social
FieldMedicine, psychiatry, health psychology

The biopsychosocial model is a framework for understanding health and illness that holds that biological, psychological, and social factors all interact to influence a person's condition, in contrast to a narrowly biomedical model that focuses on physical processes alone.

Origins

The model was proposed by psychiatrist George L. Engel in a 1977 article in Science, drawing on general systems theory. Engel argued that to understand and respond adequately to patients' suffering, clinicians must attend simultaneously to the biological, psychological, and social dimensions of illness.

Application

The model is influential in psychiatry, chronic pain management, rehabilitation, and health psychology, where treatment plans combine medication or surgery with psychotherapy, behavior change, and attention to family, culture, and socioeconomic circumstances. Critics have argued that in practice the model can be applied in an eclectic or superficial way, and that it provides little guidance on how the three domains should be weighed against one another.
