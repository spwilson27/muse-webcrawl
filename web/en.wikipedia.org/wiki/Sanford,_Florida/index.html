Sanford, Florida

Sanford
County seat of Seminole County, Florida
Population: approx. 61,000 (2020 census)
Nickname: "Historic Waterfront Gateway City"

Sanford is a city in and the county seat of Seminole County, Florida, United States. Located on the southern shore of Lake Monroe at the head of navigation of the St. Johns River, Sanford is part of the Orlando metropolitan area and is known for its preserved historic downtown and waterfront.

History

The area was inhabited by the Timucua people before European contact. In the 1870s, General Henry Shelton Sanford purchased a large tract of land west of Mellonville and founded the city that bears his name, promoting it as a gateway to South Florida and experimenting with citrus cultivation at his Belair grove. Sanford became an important steamboat and railroad port and a centre of celery farming, earning it the nickname "Celery City". Commercial development shifted after the Great Freeze of 1894–95 damaged citrus, but celery and citrus packing sustained the economy into the twentieth century.

Modern era

Sanford is home to Orlando Sanford International Airport and the Central Florida Zoo and Botanical Gardens. The city drew national attention in 2012 following the killing of Trayvon Martin by George Zimmerman in nearby gated community Twin Lakes, an event that sparked nationwide protests. In recent decades Sanford has revitalised its historic commercial district along First Street with shops, restaurants, craft breweries, and a riverwalk along Lake Monroe.
