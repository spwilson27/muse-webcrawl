Stable theory

Stable theory is a central branch of model theory, a field of mathematical logic, concerned with the classification of first-order theories according to their structural complexity. A complete first-order theory is called stable if it does not have the order property: that is, there is no formula and no sequence of tuples in a model of the theory such that the formula orders those tuples in the manner of a linear order.

The theory originates in the work of Saharon Shelah, beginning with his 1969 doctoral thesis and developed extensively in his 1978 monograph Classification Theory and the Number of Non-Isomorphic Models. Shelah showed that stable theories admit a rich structure theory built around the notion of forking independence, a generalisation of linear independence in vector spaces and algebraic independence in fields.

Definitions

A formula φ(x, y) has the order property in a theory T if there are elements ai, bi in a model of T such that φ(ai, bj) holds exactly when i < j. The theory T is stable if no formula has the order property in T. Equivalently, T is stable if and only if for every model M of T and every infinite cardinal κ, the number of complete types over a subset of M of size κ is at most κ for sufficiently large κ.

Forking

In a stable theory, a type is said to fork over a set if it implies a disjunction of formulas each of which divides over that set, where a formula divides if it belongs to an inconsistent family of conjugates. Non-forking extensions behave like independent extensions and satisfy symmetry, transitivity, and local character, giving stable theories a well-behaved dimension theory.

Examples

  • The theory of algebraically closed fields of fixed characteristic is stable (indeed, totally transcendental / ω-stable).
  • The theory of dense linear orders without endpoints is unstable, as is the theory of the random graph.
  • Modules over a ring are stable, though not necessarily ω-stable.

Classification

Stable theories form one side of Shelah's "main gap" programme: classifiable theories admit a structure theory, while unstable or otherwise wild theories have the maximal number of non-isomorphic models in every uncountable cardinality. Generalisations of stability include NIP theories, simple theories, and NSOP theories, which extend ideas from stable theory to wider classes.
