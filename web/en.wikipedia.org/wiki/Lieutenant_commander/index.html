Lieutenant commander is a commissioned officer rank in many navies, ranking above lieutenant and below commander, and equivalent to a major in armies. In the United States Navy and Coast Guard the pay grade is O-4 and the insignia is a gold oak leaf with two full stripes and one half stripe on the sleeve.

History

The rank emerged in the 19th century as senior lieutenants took command of smaller vessels. The Royal Navy formally created the rank in 1914 by promoting senior lieutenants with eight years' seniority; before that, such officers were styled "lieutenant and commander". The United States Navy adopted the rank in the 19th century.

Role

Lieutenant commanders serve as executive officers or department heads on larger ships, commanding officers of small vessels such as patrol ships and minesweepers, and as specialists ashore. In naval aviation they may lead squadrons as executive officer.

Address

A lieutenant commander is customarily addressed as "commander" in verbal communication in the United States Navy, though formally the full rank is used.
