IS–LM model

The IS–LM model (investment–saving / liquidity preference–money supply), also known as the Hicks–Hansen model, is a Keynesian macroeconomic model showing how the market for goods interacts with the money market to determine equilibrium national income and interest rates in the short run. It was developed by John Hicks in 1937 as a formalisation of Keynes's General Theory (1936) and later popularised by Alvin Hansen.

The curves

The IS curve (investment equals saving) slopes downward: lower interest rates stimulate investment and thus income. The LM curve (liquidity preference equals money supply) slopes upward: higher income raises money demand, requiring higher interest rates to restore money-market equilibrium given a fixed money supply. Their intersection represents simultaneous equilibrium in both markets.

Policy analysis

Fiscal expansion shifts IS rightward (raising income and rates), while monetary expansion shifts LM rightward (raising income, lowering rates). Special cases include the "liquidity trap" (flat LM, monetary policy ineffective) and the "classical case" (vertical LM, fiscal policy fully crowded out). The model long anchored undergraduate macroeconomics and the neoclassical synthesis, though it has been criticised for lacking microfoundations and dynamic expectations, and is often extended as IS–LM–BP (Mundell–Fleming) for open economies.
