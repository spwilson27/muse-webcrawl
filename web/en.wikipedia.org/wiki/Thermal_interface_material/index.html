Thermal interface material
Use: Heat transfer between surfaces
Forms: Pastes, pads, phase-change materials, metal alloys

A thermal interface material (often abbreviated TIM) is any material inserted between two surfaces (commonly between a heat-generating device and a heat sink) in order to enhance the thermal coupling between them. Even apparently smooth surfaces make contact only at microscopic high points, leaving insulating air gaps; a thermally conductive interface material fills those gaps and greatly improves heat conduction.

Types

Common types include thermal grease or paste (silicone or non-silicone based, filled with thermally conductive particles such as aluminium oxide or silver), thermal pads, phase-change materials that soften at operating temperature, thermal adhesives, and metallic interface materials such as indium foil or liquid metal alloys based on gallium.

Applications

Thermal interface materials are widely used in electronics cooling — for example between a CPU or power transistor and its heat sink — as well as in LED lighting, automotive electronics, and battery packs for electric vehicles.

Properties

Performance is characterised by bulk thermal conductivity, the thickness of the bond line, and contact resistance. Lower-viscosity materials generally form thinner bond lines and achieve lower thermal impedance, but must resist pumping-out and drying over the product's lifetime.
