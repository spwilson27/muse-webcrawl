Dreadzone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dreadzone is a British electronic music group formed in London in 1993 by Greg Roberts and Tim Bran, both formerly of Big Audio Dynamite's rhythm and live circle. Fusing dub, reggae, techno, folk and breakbeat, the band became a fixture of the 1990s UK festival circuit and is best known for the top-20 single "Little Britain" (1996).

History

Roberts and Bran emerged from the orbit of Mick Jones's Big Audio Dynamite, in which Roberts had drummed. Dreadzone's debut album 360° (1993) was followed by Second Light (1995), released on Virgin Records, which blended deep dub basslines with samples from film and folk sources. John Peel was an early and vocal champion, playing the band heavily on BBC Radio 1, and Dreadzone supported groups including Oasis at their peak.

"Little Britain", built around a sample from the 1968 film If...., reached the UK top 20 in 1996. Subsequent albums included Biological Radio (1997), Sound (2001), Once Upon a Time (2005), Eye on the Horizon (2010), Escapades (2013), Dread Times (2017) and Nine (2024). The band's live shows, long built around MC Spee and singer Earl Sixteen, made it a perennial draw at festivals such as Glastonbury.

Style

Dreadzone's sound is characterised by cavernous dub production, rolling breakbeats and melodic folk instrumentation — a combination sometimes labelled "dub-dance". The group's work sits in the lineage running from punk-reggae crossover through Big Audio Dynamite to the 1990s UK dub scene, and members of that circle also appeared in projects such as Carbon/Silicon.
