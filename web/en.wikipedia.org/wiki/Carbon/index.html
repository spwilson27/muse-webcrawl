Carbon is a chemical element with symbol C and atomic number 6 — the backbone of all known life, because its atoms form stable bonds with many elements, including each other, making vast chains and rings possible (organic chemistry). It is nonmetallic and tetravalent.

Carbon's allotropes include diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerenes and nanotubes. It is the fourth most abundant element in the universe by mass and about 18.5% of the human body. Isotopes: stable carbon-12 (the standard for atomic mass) and carbon-13, and radioactive carbon-14 used in dating. Burning fossil fuels (ancient carbon) drives modern climate change via carbon dioxide. Charcoal, soot and graphite have been used since prehistory; the element's name comes from Latin carbo (coal).
