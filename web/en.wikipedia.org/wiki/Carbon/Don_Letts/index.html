Don Letts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Donovan "Don" Letts (born 10 January 1956) is a British film director, DJ and musician. A central figure in the original London punk scene, he is credited with introducing a generation of punks to reggae and dub, and he later co-founded the post-punk group Big Audio Dynamite with Mick Jones. His documentary work includes the landmark film The Punk Rock Movie (1978).

Early life and the Roxy

Letts was born in Brixton, London, to Jamaican parents. In the mid-1970s he managed the clothing shop Acme Attractions on the King's Road, a hang-out for the emerging punk scene. In 1977 he became the resident DJ at the Roxy club in Covent Garden; because there were almost no punk records to play, he filled his sets with dub reggae — heavy, bass-led records by artists such as King Tubby and U-Roy. The cross-pollination between punk and reggae that followed shaped bands including the Clash, whose song-writing drew heavily on reggae rhythms and themes.

Filmmaking

Letts shot much of the Roxy era on Super-8 film; the resulting footage became The Punk Rock Movie (1978), one of the few contemporary documents of the scene. He went on to direct hundreds of music videos, including many for the Clash ("London Calling", "Rock the Casbah"), and documentaries such as Westway to the World (2000), the Grammy-winning history of the Clash, and films on Bob Marley, Gil Scott-Heron and Sun Ra. His 2023 documentary Rebel Dread told his own story; his autobiography There and Black Again appeared in 2021.

Music and broadcasting

With Mick Jones, Letts co-founded Big Audio Dynamite in 1984, contributing film samples, effects and vocals; the group scored hits with "E=MC²" and "Rush". He has also worked with Jones's later project Carbon/Silicon and presented the long-running BBC Radio 6 Music show Culture Clash Radio. Letts remains a prominent DJ and commentator on Black British music culture.
