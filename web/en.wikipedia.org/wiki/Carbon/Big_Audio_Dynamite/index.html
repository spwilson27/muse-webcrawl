Big Audio Dynamite

Big Audio Dynamite (later known as Big Audio Dynamite II and Big Audio, and often abbreviated BAD) is an English band formed in London in 1984 by Mick Jones, the former guitarist and co-lead vocalist of the Clash, with filmmaker and musician Don Letts. The group pioneered a fusion of punk rock with dance music, hip hop sampling, reggae and dub, funk, and film dialogue samples, anticipating later sample-based pop and big beat. Their best-known songs include "E=MC²", "Medicine Show", "The Bottom Line", "Rush" (a US alternative number one), and "The Globe".

History

After being fired from the Clash in 1983, Mick Jones briefly worked with General Public before forming Big Audio Dynamite with Don Letts (who had directed the Clash's videos and introduced Jones to reggae and film culture), bassist Leo "E-Zee-Kill" Williams, drummer Greg Roberts, and keyboardist Dan Donovan. Their debut album, This Is Big Audio Dynamite (1985), produced hits "E=MC²" — a tribute to film director Nicolas Roeg, packed with dialogue samples — and "Medicine Show".

No. 10, Upping St. (1986) was partly co-written and co-produced with Jones's former Clash partner Joe Strummer. Tighten Up Vol. 88 (1988) yielded "Just Play Music!", and Megatop Phoenix (1989) closed the first line-up's run. Jones then rebuilt the group as Big Audio Dynamite II, whose album The Globe (1991) produced the US Modern Rock chart-topper "Rush" — built around a sample of the Who's "Baba O'Riley" keyboard riff and featuring the hit title track. As Big Audio, the band released Higher Power (1994) and recorded Entering a New Ride (1997), which was shelved and distributed online. The original line-up reunited for a well-received tour in 2011, including Coachella, but did not record a new album.

Style and influence

BAD's collage approach — breakbeats, dub basslines, spaghetti-western and British film samples, rap interludes, and punk guitars — was unusual in 1985 and influenced later acts in alternative dance and big beat. The spin-off project Dreadzone, formed by Roberts and Williams, carried the sound into 1990s UK dance music.
