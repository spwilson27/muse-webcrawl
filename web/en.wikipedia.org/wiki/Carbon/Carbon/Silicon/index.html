Carbon/Silicon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Carbon/Silicon was a British rock band formed in London in 2002 by Mick Jones, former guitarist of the Clash, and Tony James, former bassist of Generation X and of Sigue Sigue Sputnik. The band became known for its embrace of the Internet and file sharing, releasing much of its early material as free downloads from its own website at a time when record labels were fiercely opposed to the practice.

History

Jones and James had been friends since the 1970s London punk scene. After years of occasional collaboration they began writing and recording together in 2002, initially operating as a loose studio project. Rather than seeking a record deal, Carbon/Silicon posted songs online for free, encouraging fans to share them, burn them to CDs and distribute them at gigs — an approach summarised in the duo's slogan of "no borders, no boundaries".

The band's early download-only releases were collected on discs such as A.T.O.M. (Assault Tactical Operation Manual) and Western Front. Its first conventionally released studio album, The Last Post, appeared in 2007, followed by The Carbon Bubble in 2009. Live, the group expanded to a full band; bassist Dominic Greensmith and drummer Danny The Red were among those who played in the touring line-up.

Style and themes

Carbon/Silicon's music blended rock, dub, samples and electronic loops with Jones's melodic guitar work. Lyrically the songs dealt with technology, globalisation, war and the music industry itself, often with a wry, slogan-like directness inherited from punk. Tracks such as "The News", "Soylent Green" and "Really the Blues" became staples of the band's sets.
