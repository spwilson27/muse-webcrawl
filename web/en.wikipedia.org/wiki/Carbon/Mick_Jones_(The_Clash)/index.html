Mick Jones (The Clash)

Michael Geoffrey Jones (born 26 June 1955) is an English musician, singer, and songwriter, best known as the co-founder, lead guitarist, co-lead vocalist, and principal co-songwriter of the punk rock band the Clash, in partnership with Joe Strummer. After leaving the Clash in 1983, he founded Big Audio Dynamite, blending punk with dance, dub, and hip hop, and later formed Carbon/Silicon with Tony James. Jones has also worked as a producer for the Libertines and played live with Gorillaz. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003 as a member of the Clash.

Early life and the Clash

Jones was born in Wandsworth, London, and raised largely by his grandmother in Brixton and Stockwell after his parents separated. He attended Strand School and art school, formed the proto-punk band London SS in 1975, and in 1976, with Paul Simonon and manager Bernie Rhodes, recruited Joe Strummer from the 101ers to form the Clash. Jones wrote most of the band's music and many of its melodies, sang lead on songs including "Train in Vain", "Should I Stay or Should I Go" (co-written), "Lost in the Supermarket", and "The Card Cheat", and steered the band's absorption of reggae, dub, funk, and hip hop on albums such as London Calling (1979) and Sandinista! (1980). Growing tensions over his unreliability and musical direction led Strummer and Simonon to dismiss him in September 1983.

Big Audio Dynamite and after

In 1984 Jones formed Big Audio Dynamite with Don Letts, pioneering sample-driven dance-rock on albums from This Is Big Audio Dynamite (1985) to The Globe (1991), which produced the US alternative hit "Rush". A near-fatal bout of pneumonia in 1988 briefly sidelined him. In the 2000s he formed Carbon/Silicon with childhood friend Tony James (ex-Generation X), notable for releasing music freely online, and produced the Libertines' first two albums, helping define the 2000s British indie revival. He toured as guitarist with Damon Albarn's Gorillaz in 2010 alongside Simonon. His extensive archive of pop-culture memorabilia, the "Mick Jones Library", has been exhibited in London.
