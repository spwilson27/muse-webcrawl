Tony James (musician)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tony James (born 12 April 1953) is an English musician, best known as the bassist and co-founder of Generation X and as the driving force behind the futuristic pop group Sigue Sigue Sputnik. In 2002 he co-founded Carbon/Silicon with Mick Jones of the Clash.

Generation X

James first played in the early punk band London SS, a short-lived group whose members went on to form the Clash, the Damned and Generation X. In 1976 he and singer Billy Idol formed Generation X (named after a 1960s sociology book James's mother kept), one of the first punk bands to court a pop audience. Singles such as "Ready Steady Go", "Valley of the Dolls" and the anthem "King Rocker" established the band, though chart success remained modest and it split in 1981.

Sigue Sigue Sputnik

James then conceived Sigue Sigue Sputnik as "the fifth generation of rock 'n' roll": a deliberately cartoonish, media-saturated group dressed like characters from A Clockwork Orange and Mad Max, who sold advertising space between tracks on their debut album Flaunt It (1986). The Giorgio Moroder-produced single "Love Missile F1-11" reached number 3 in the UK. The hype-driven experiment became a famous case study in pop marketing.

Later work

James played briefly with the Sisters of Mercy and in the early 1990s pursued writing and production. In 2002 he reunited with Mick Jones — a friend from the 1970s scene around bands managed in the orbit of the Clash — to form Carbon/Silicon, which released its music freely online. He has also performed with revived line-ups of Generation X material under the name Generation Sex, with Steve Jones and Paul Cook of the Sex Pistols.
