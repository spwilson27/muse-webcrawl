The Clash were an English rock band formed in London in 1976. As a key part of the original wave of British punk rock, they broadened punk's musical palette — incorporating reggae, dub, funk, ska and rockabilly — and its political ambitions, earning the nickname "The Only Band That Matters". The classic line-up was Joe Strummer (vocals, rhythm guitar), Mick Jones (lead guitar, vocals), Paul Simonon (bass) and Topper Headon (drums).

History

Signed to CBS, the band's self-titled debut (1977) and Give 'Em Enough Rope (1978) established them in Britain. The double album London Calling (1979) is widely ranked among the greatest albums of all time; the triple album Sandinista! (1980) followed. Combat Rock (1982), featuring "Rock the Casbah" and "Should I Stay or Should I Go", brought major American success, but internal tensions led to Headon's dismissal in 1982 and Jones's in 1983. A reconfigured band released the poorly received Cut the Crap (1985) before Strummer disbanded the group in 1986.

Legacy

The Clash were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2003, months after Strummer's death in December 2002. Their fusion of political conviction and musical eclecticism influenced generations of punk, alternative and activist musicians.
