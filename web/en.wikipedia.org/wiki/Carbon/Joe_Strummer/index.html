Joe Strummer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

John Graham Mellor (21 August 1952 – 22 December 2002), known professionally as Joe Strummer, was a British musician. He was the co-founder, lyricist, rhythm guitarist and co-lead vocalist of the punk rock band the Clash, formed in 1976. After the Clash disbanded in 1986, Strummer worked with the Pogues, acted in films, and in 1999 formed Joe Strummer and the Mescaleros, with whom he recorded three albums before his sudden death from a congenital heart defect in 2002.

Early life

Mellor was born in Ankara, Turkey, where his father was a British foreign-service clerk; his mother was a nurse from Scotland. The family moved frequently before he was sent to boarding school in England. After leaving school he played in the pub-rock band the 101ers, adopting the name Joe Strummer — a self-deprecating nod to his rudimentary guitar style — before being recruited in 1976 by Mick Jones and Paul Simonon to front the Clash.

The Clash

With Jones writing much of the music and Strummer most of the lyrics, the Clash became the most politically charged and musically expansive of the British punk bands. Albums such as The Clash (1977), London Calling (1979) and Combat Rock (1982) folded reggae, dub, rockabilly, funk and hip-hop into the band's sound; London Calling is routinely ranked among the greatest albums ever made. Strummer's lyrics tackled unemployment, racism, war and Americanisation. The band split in 1986 after the departures of drummer Topper Headon and Jones.

Later work and death

Strummer recorded the soundtrack to Sid and Nancy, acted for Alex Cox and Jim Jarmusch (Mystery Train, 1989), toured as singer with the Pogues, and in 1999 formed the Mescaleros, whose albums Rock Art and the X-Ray Style (1999), Global a Go-Go (2001) and the posthumous Streetcore (2003) won him renewed acclaim. He died at home in Broomfield, Somerset, on 22 December 2002. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Clash in 2003.
