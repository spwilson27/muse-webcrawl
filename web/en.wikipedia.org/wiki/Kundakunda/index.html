Kundakunda

Kundakunda was a Digambara Jain monk and philosopher, traditionally placed in the 2nd century CE or later (modern scholars propose dates between the 2nd and 8th centuries). Revered as one of the most important Jain authors, he wrote foundational Prakrit works on Jain metaphysics and spirituality, including the Samayasāra, Niyamasāra, Pravacanasāra and Pancastikayasāra.


A statue of Kundakunda, a central figure of the Digambara tradition.

Life and lineage

According to tradition, Kundakunda — also known by the names Padmanandin, Vakragrīva, Elācārya and Gṛddhapiccha — belonged to the Mulasangha and came from Konakundala in southern India. Digambara tradition honours him as the reviver of the Jain doctrine in its pure form, and several monastic lineages (such as the Kundakunda anvaya) trace themselves to him. His biography is largely legendary.

Philosophy

Kundakunda's thought centres on the distinction between the two standpoints (naya): the ultimate, transcendental standpoint (niścaya-naya), from which the soul is pure consciousness untouched by karma, and the conventional standpoint (vyavahāra-naya), which describes the soul's bondage and the path of conduct. His Samayasāra ("Essence of the Self") develops this radically interior view of liberation and remains one of the most commented-upon texts in Jainism. He also systematised the doctrine of the six eternal substances (dravya) and the five extensive substances (astikāya).

Works

  • Samayasāra — on the nature of the self
  • Pravacanasāra — on the Jain doctrine and conduct
  • Niyamasāra — on the essential path to liberation
  • Pancastikayasāra — on the five astikāyas
  • Aṣṭapāhuḍa — eight treatises
