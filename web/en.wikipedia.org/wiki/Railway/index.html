Rail transport

Rail transport (also known as train transport) is a means of transport using wheeled vehicles running on tracks, which usually consist of two parallel steel rails. Rail transport is one of the two primary means of land transport, next to road transport. It is used for about 8% of passenger and freight transport globally, thanks to its energy efficiency and potentially high speed.

Trains consist of locomotives or self-propelled multiple units hauling passenger coaches or freight wagons. Rolling stock runs on fixed track — rails fastened to sleepers over ballast — and is guided by flanged wheels, giving low rolling resistance and allowing heavy loads and long trains. Railways are used for urban transit (metros, trams, commuter rail), intercity passenger travel including high-speed rail, and bulk freight such as coal, ore, containers, and grain.

History

Railed wagonways existed in mines from the 16th century; steam locomotion began in the early 19th century in Britain, and public steam railways spread rapidly after the opening of the Stockton and Darlington Railway in 1825 and the Liverpool and Manchester Railway in 1830. Railways drove industrialization and standardized time (railway time). In the 20th century, diesel and electric traction replaced steam, and high-speed trains — beginning with Japan's Shinkansen in 1964 — revived passenger rail on dense corridors.
