Religion in the United States

Religion in the United States is characterised by a diversity of religious beliefs and practices, within a legal framework of religious freedom guaranteed by the First Amendment to the Constitution, which prohibits an established church and protects free exercise. Historically, Christianity — above all Protestantism — has been the majority faith, shaped by successive waves of immigration and revival movements such as the Great Awakenings.

Demographics

In recent decades surveys by organisations such as Pew Research Center have found Christians comprising roughly 60–65 percent of adults, with the religiously unaffiliated ("nones") the fastest-growing group at around 28–30 percent. Jewish, Muslim, Hindu, Buddhist and other communities each make up small but significant minorities. Religious practice and affiliation vary strongly by region, with higher attendance in the South and Midwest and lower in the Northeast and West.
