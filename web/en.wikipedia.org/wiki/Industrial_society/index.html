Industrial society

An industrial society is a society characterised by the use of industrial technology and powered machinery to support a large population, typically with a highly developed division of labour. Such societies arose from the Industrial Revolution, first in Britain and then across Europe, North America and much of the rest of the world, succeeding agrarian societies in which most people worked in agriculture.

Characteristics

Industrial societies concentrate production in factories, employ wage labour on a large scale, and draw populations into cities through urbanisation. They rely on fossil fuels and, later, electricity as energy sources; they support mass education, mass consumption and complex bureaucracies. Sociologists from Saint-Simon and Durkheim onward analysed the shift, contrasting the mechanical solidarity of pre-industrial communities with the organic solidarity of interdependent industrial ones.

Transitions

In the later twentieth century many industrial societies were described as becoming post-industrial societies, in which services, information and knowledge work displace manufacturing as the dominant sector, although manufacturing remains central to the world economy as a whole.
