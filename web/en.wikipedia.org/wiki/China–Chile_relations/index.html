China–Chile relations

Relations established: 15 December 1970

Key milestone: Free trade agreement signed 2005 (in force 2006)

China–Chile relations are the bilateral relations between the People's Republic of China and the Republic of Chile. Chile was the first South American country to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China, on 15 December 1970, shortly after the election of President Salvador Allende. Relations continued, unusually, after the 1973 Chilean coup d'état, making Chile one of the few countries whose ties with Beijing survived changes of regime on both sides.

Trade

China is Chile's largest trading partner. In 2005 the two countries signed a free trade agreement — the first between China and an individual Latin American country — which entered into force in 2006 and was later expanded to cover services and investment. Chilean exports to China are dominated by copper, along with lithium, wine, fruit (especially cherries) and salmon, while Chile imports Chinese manufactured goods, electronics and machinery.

Political relations

The relationship was upgraded to a comprehensive strategic partnership in 2016 during a state visit. Chile was also the first Latin American country to support China's accession to the World Trade Organization and to recognise China as a market economy. Chile participates in the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum and joined China's Belt and Road Initiative through a memorandum signed in 2018.
