The Strait of Magellan (Spanish: Estrecho de Magallanes) is a navigable sea route in southern Chile separating mainland South America to the north and Tierra del Fuego to the south. It is the most important natural passage between the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, lying mostly within Chilean territorial waters.

History

Ferdinand Magellan's expedition first transited the strait in November 1520, taking some 38 days to pass its winding channels; Magellan named it the Strait of All Saints. The route was prized but feared for its violent williwaw winds, strong currents, and narrow passages. After the opening of the Panama Canal in 1914, use of the strait declined sharply, though it remains an important route for vessels too large for the canal and for Chilean and Argentine regional trade.

Geography

About 570 km long and varying from about 2 to 32 km wide, the strait connects the Atlantic at its eastern mouth near Punta Dungeness with the Pacific at Cape Pilar. The Chilean port of Punta Arenas, founded in 1848, lies on its northern shore and remains the principal settlement.
