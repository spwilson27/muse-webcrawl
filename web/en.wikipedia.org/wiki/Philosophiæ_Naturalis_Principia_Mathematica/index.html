Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica

Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica (Latin for "Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy"), often referred to simply as the Principia, is a book by Isaac Newton published on 5 July 1687. In it Newton formulated the laws of motion and of universal gravitation that dominated scientists' view of the physical universe for the next three centuries.

The Principia consists of three books. Book 1 treats motion of bodies free of resistance, Book 2 motion in resisting media, and Book 3 the system of the world, applying gravitation to the planets, the Moon, comets and tides. Newton derived Kepler's laws of planetary motion from his theory and introduced a rigorous mathematical treatment of mechanics, though expressed largely in geometrical form.
