Pine nut

Pine nuts, also called piñón (Spanish), pinoli (Italian), or pignoli, are the edible seeds of pines (family Pinaceae, genus Pinus). About twenty species of pine produce seeds large enough to be worth harvesting for human food; many more are used locally. Pine nuts have been eaten in Europe and Asia since the Paleolithic period and are an essential ingredient of Italian pesto.

Species and harvest

The most widely traded species include the stone pine (Pinus pinea) of the Mediterranean, Korean pine (Pinus koraiensis) of northeast Asia, chilgoza pine (Pinus gerardiana) of the western Himalaya, and the pinyon pines of the American Southwest and Mexico. Seeds are extracted from cones that are dried to release the hard-shelled kernels — a labour-intensive process that keeps prices high. Native American peoples such as the Paiute and Shoshone traditionally relied on pinyon nuts as a staple food.

Culinary use and nutrition

Pine nuts are rich in fats (largely unsaturated), protein, vitamin E, vitamin K, and minerals such as magnesium and zinc. They are eaten raw or toasted in salads, sauces, baked goods, and confectionery across Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and East Asian cuisines. Eating certain species, particularly Pinus armandii, has been linked to "pine mouth", a temporary bitter metallic taste disturbance (dysgeusia) lasting days to weeks.
