Augmented Lagrangian method

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The augmented Lagrangian method is a certain class of algorithms for solving constrained optimization problems. They have similarities to penalty methods in that they replace a constrained optimization problem by a series of unconstrained problems and add a penalty term to the objective; the difference is that the augmented Lagrangian method adds yet another term, designed to mimic a Lagrange multiplier. The augmented Lagrangian is related to, but not identical with, the method of Lagrange multipliers.

Viewed differently, the unconstrained objective is the Lagrangian of the constrained problem, with an additional penalty term (the augmentation).

Formulation

For the problem of minimizing f(x) subject to equality constraints ci(x) = 0, the augmented Lagrangian is:

LA(x, λ, μ) = f(x) + Σ λi ci(x) + (μ/2) Σ ci(x)2

The method proceeds by approximately minimizing LA over x, then updating the multiplier estimates λ by λi ← λi + μ ci(x), and increasing the penalty parameter μ as needed.

History

The method was originally known as the method of multipliers and was introduced independently by Magnus Hestenes and Michael J. D. Powell in 1969. R. Tyrrell Rockafellar extended the approach to inequality constraints and connected it with proximal point algorithms and monotone operator theory.

Advantages

Unlike pure penalty methods, the augmented Lagrangian method can converge to an accurate solution without driving the penalty parameter to infinity, which avoids ill-conditioning. The multiplier estimates converge to the true Lagrange multipliers, giving useful sensitivity information. A closely related modern variant is the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), widely used in machine learning and signal processing, which splits the minimization step across blocks of variables.
