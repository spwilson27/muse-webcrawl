Treaty of Oliva

Signed3 May 1660
LocationOliva (Oliwa) Abbey, near Gdańsk
PartiesSweden, Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Austria, Brandenburg-Prussia

The Treaty of Oliva (Peace of Oliva) was one of the peace treaties ending the Second Northern War (1655–1660). It was signed on 3 May 1660 at the monastery of Oliva (Oliwa), near Gdańsk (Danzig), in Royal Prussia.

Terms

By the treaty:

  • John II Casimir of Poland renounced his claims to the Swedish throne, which the Polish Vasas had pursued since Sigismund III's deposition in 1599;
  • Poland formally ceded Swedish Livonia, which Sweden had held since the 1620s, together with Estonia and the island of Ösel (Saaremaa);
  • The Hohenzollern Elector of Brandenburg, Frederick William, was confirmed in full sovereignty over the Duchy of Prussia, ending its status as a Polish fief (first granted by the Treaty of Wehlau in 1657);
  • Stettin and Swedish Pomerania remained with Sweden.

Significance

The Treaty of Oliva, together with the Treaty of Copenhagen concluded the same year between Sweden and Denmark–Norway, marked the high point of the Swedish Empire in the Baltic. For Brandenburg-Prussia, confirmation of sovereignty over East Prussia was a crucial step toward the proclamation of the Kingdom in Prussia in 1701 and the eventual rise of Prussia as a great power.
