William John Clifton Haley (July 6, 1925 – February 9, 1981) was an American rock and roll musician. He is credited by many with first popularizing this form of music in the early 1950s with his band Bill Haley & His Comets and their million-selling hits such as "Rock Around the Clock", "See You Later, Alligator", "Shake, Rattle and Roll", "Rocket 88", "Skinny Minnie", and "Razzle Dazzle".

Career

Haley began his career as a country music performer, yodeler and radio music director, leading the Four Aces of Western Swing and then the Saddlemen. In 1952 the group renamed itself Bill Haley & His Comets and adopted a style fusing rhythm and blues with country boogie.

"Rock Around the Clock", recorded in April 1954, became an enormous international hit after it was featured in the 1955 film Blackboard Jungle, spending eight weeks at number one on the Billboard chart and becoming one of the best-selling singles of all time. Haley became the first major American rock and roll star to tour Britain, in 1957, and remained hugely popular in Europe and Latin America even as his U.S. chart fortunes faded in the early 1960s.

Later life and legacy

Haley struggled with alcoholism in his later years and settled in Texas. He died in 1981. He was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987. "Rock Around the Clock" was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and selected for preservation in the U.S. National Recording Registry.
