Bessie Smith

[Image placeholder: Bessie Smith portrait by Carl Van Vechten]

Bessie Smith (April 15, 1894 – September 26, 1937) was an African-American blues singer widely renowned during the Jazz Age. Nicknamed the "Empress of the Blues", she was one of the most popular female blues singers of the 1930s. Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989, she is often regarded as one of the greatest singers of her era and was a major influence on fellow blues singers, as well as jazz vocalists.

Born in Chattanooga, Tennessee, Smith began street singing at a young age and later performed in traveling shows, where she was mentored by Ma Rainey. She signed with Columbia Records in 1923; her first record, "Downhearted Blues", was a major hit. Her recordings with musicians such as Louis Armstrong, including "St. Louis Blues", are considered classics.

Death and legacy

Smith died from injuries sustained in a car crash in Mississippi in 1937. Her powerful contralto voice and hits such as "Empty Bed Blues" and "Nobody Knows You When You're Down and Out" shaped the classic blues style. She was posthumously inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and her life inspired the play and HBO film Bessie.
