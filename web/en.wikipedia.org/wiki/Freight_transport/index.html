Freight transport

Intermodal containers at a port terminal.

Freight transport, also referred to as shipping, is the physical process of transporting commodities and merchandise goods and cargo. Freight is carried by many modes, including ship, aircraft, train, and truck, and is often combined in intermodal transport using standardised containers that can be transferred between modes without unloading.

Modes

Maritime shipping carries the great majority of world trade by volume — roughly 80–90 per cent — in container ships, bulk carriers, and tankers. Air freight accounts for a small share by weight but a large share by value, suited to perishable and high-value goods. Rail freight is efficient for bulk and container traffic over land, while road freight provides flexible door-to-door delivery, especially over shorter distances. Pipelines transport liquids and gases.

Organisation

Shipments are arranged by carriers, freight forwarders, and third-party logistics providers. Key documents include the bill of lading, and international sales terms are standardised by Incoterms. Freight is commonly measured in tonne-kilometres, and container traffic in twenty-foot equivalent units (TEU).

Environmental impact

Freight transport is a significant source of greenhouse gas emissions, with shipping, aviation, and trucking all subject to decarbonisation efforts through efficiency, alternative fuels, and regulation.
