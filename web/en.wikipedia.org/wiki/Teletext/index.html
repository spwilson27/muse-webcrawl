Teletext

Teletext, or broadcast teletext, is a standard for displaying text and simple graphics on television sets equipped with a decoder. Developed in the United Kingdom in the early 1970s, it transmits information in the vertical blanking interval of the analogue television signal. Users select pages by entering three-digit numbers on their remote control; pages cycle continuously in a carousel, offering news, weather, sports results, television listings, financial data, and subtitles for the hard of hearing.

History

The BBC launched Ceefax in 1974, generally considered the first teletext service, while the British commercial broadcasters' system, ORACLE, followed on ITV. A unified specification, the World System Teletext, standardised the technology internationally, and services proliferated across Europe — including Germany's Videotext, France's Antiope (later replaced by the European standard), Spain's Teletexto, and Sweden's Text-TV. In North America, services such as Electra and the NAPLPS-based systems saw limited adoption. At its peak in the 1990s, teletext reached tens of millions of users weekly in Britain alone, and Ceefax became a cultural institution.

Technology

Teletext data is transmitted in spare lines of the vertical blanking interval at a rate that allows a magazine of roughly a hundred pages to repeat every few tens of seconds. The display format is a grid of 40 columns by 24 rows of characters, with a palette of eight colours and blocky mosaic graphics formed from character cells. Later enhancements (levels 2.5 and 3) added additional colours, redefinable characters, and improved graphics, though most services remained at the basic level. Subtitling for the deaf and hard of hearing was carried on designated pages — page 888 in the UK convention.

Decline and legacy

The rise of the World Wide Web, 24-hour news channels, and digital television eroded teletext's role. Analogue switch-off ended most services: ORACLE closed in 1992, replaced by Teletext Ltd. on analogue terrestrial television, which itself ended in 2009; Ceefax ceased with the UK digital switchover in 2012. Some services survive on digital platforms or as internet streams — SVT's Text-TV and ARD/ZDF services in Germany continue to attract loyal audiences — and the medium's distinctive aesthetic has found a nostalgic afterlife in art and design. Teletext is widely regarded as an important precursor of online information services.
