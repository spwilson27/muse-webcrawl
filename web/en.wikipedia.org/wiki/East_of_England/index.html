The East of England is one of the nine official regions of England at the first level of ITL for statistical purposes. It was created in 1994 and comprises the ceremonial counties of Bedfordshire, Cambridgeshire, Essex, Hertfordshire, Norfolk and Suffolk. The region covers 19,116 km2 and has a population of over six million.

Geography

The region is largely low-lying, including part of the Fens, some of the lowest land in the United Kingdom, much of it reclaimed from the sea. The Norfolk Broads, the Suffolk Coast and Heaths, and the Chiltern Hills in the south-west are notable landscapes. Major rivers include the Great Ouse, the Nene, the Stour and the Thames, which forms part of the southern boundary.

Economy

The East of England has major strengths in research and technology centred on Cambridge (the "Silicon Fen"), biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and agriculture. The ports of Felixstowe — the United Kingdom's largest container port — Harwich and Tilbury make it a logistics hub, and London Stansted and Luton airports serve the south of the region. Many residents commute into London.

Governance

The region was previously served by the Government Office for the East of England and the East of England Regional Assembly, both abolished by 2011. Strategic coordination is now undertaken by local authorities and combined authorities, including the Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Combined Authority, established in 2017 with an elected mayor.
