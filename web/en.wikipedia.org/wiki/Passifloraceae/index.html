Passifloraceae

Passifloraceae
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Malpighiales
Genera: about 27
Species: over 900
Best-known genus: Passiflora (passion flowers)

The Passifloraceae are a family of flowering plants, known as the passion flower family, containing more than 900 species in about 27 genera. Members occur mainly in tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Australia, and include herbs, shrubs, vines, and trees.

Description

Most members of the family are climbing plants with tendrils. The best-known genus, Passiflora, has distinctive flowers with a prominent corona of filaments arranged in rings, a raised central column (the androgynophore) bearing stamens and the ovary, and often showy petals. The fruits of several species are edible, most famously the passion fruit (Passiflora edulis). Leaves are usually alternate and simple or lobed, often with extrafloral nectaries.

Classification

In the APG IV system, Passifloraceae is placed in the order Malpighiales and in an expanded circumscription includes the former families Malesherbiaceae and Turneraceae. The genus Passiflora alone accounts for more than 500 species. Passion flowers are also ecologically notable as host plants for the caterpillars of heliconiine butterflies, with which they have an extensively studied coevolutionary relationship involving cyanogenic defensive compounds.
