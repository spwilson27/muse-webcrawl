Text corpus

In linguistics, a corpus (plural corpora) is a large, structured collection of texts used for empirical analysis of language. Modern corpora are stored electronically and are often annotated with part-of-speech tags, lemmas and syntactic parses. Corpus linguistics studies frequency, collocation and usage patterns in such collections rather than relying on intuition alone.

Notable corpora

The Brown Corpus (1961) was the first major computerised corpus, containing one million words of American English. Later landmarks include the British National Corpus (100 million words), the Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA), and web-scale corpora of billions of words.

Applications

Corpora underpin lexicography (dictionary examples drawn from real usage), language teaching materials, translation studies, forensic linguistics, and the training and evaluation of natural language processing systems, including large language models trained on massive web corpora.
