Mach–Zehnder interferometer

The Mach–Zehnder interferometer is a device used to determine the relative phase shift variations between two collimated beams derived from splitting a single light source. It was developed from proposals by Ludwig Zehnder (1891) and Ludwig Mach (1892) and is widely used in physics, engineering and telecommunications.

Design

A beam of light is split by a beam splitter into two paths, which pass through separate arms — one often containing a sample — and are then recombined at a second beam splitter. Differences in optical path length produce interference fringes whose pattern reveals phase differences as small as a fraction of a wavelength.

Applications

Mach–Zehnder interferometers are used to measure refractive index changes, pressure, temperature and flow in wind tunnels, and in integrated-photonics form as electro-optic modulators in fibre-optic communications. The device also features in famous thought experiments about quantum superposition and entanglement.
