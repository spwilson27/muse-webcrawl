Thameslink is a 24-hour, 115-station main-line route in the British railway system, running from Bedford, Luton, St Albans City, Peterborough and Cambridge in the north to Brighton, Sutton, Orpington and Rainham in the south, via central London. It is operated by Govia Thameslink Railway as part of the Thameslink, Southern and Great Northern franchise.

A Class 700 train in central London

History

The route was created in 1988 when British Rail reopened the Snow Hill tunnel under central London, closed to passengers since 1916, allowing trains to run directly from the Midland Main Line to the Brighton Main Line. Principal central stations are St Pancras International, Farringdon, City Thameslink, Blackfriars and London Bridge.

Thameslink Programme

The £6–7 billion Thameslink Programme, largely completed with the December 2019 timetable, rebuilt London Bridge station, introduced new Siemens Class 700 trains, and added automatic train operation through the central core, enabling up to 24 trains per hour between Blackfriars and St Pancras. New through services connected Cambridge and Peterborough to Horsham, Brighton and Maidstone.
