The 2002 Winter Olympics, officially the XIX Olympic Winter Games and commonly known as Salt Lake 2002, were an international winter multi-sport event held from 8 to 24 February 2002 in and around Salt Lake City, Utah, United States. A record 2,399 athletes from 78 nations participated, and the Games returned to the United States for the first time since 1980.

Background and bid scandal

Salt Lake City was awarded the Games in 1995, but in 1998–99 revelations of bribes paid to IOC members by bid officials triggered the largest corruption scandal in Olympic history, leading to the expulsion of ten IOC members and sweeping reforms of the bidding process. Under new leadership (Mitt Romney took over the organising committee in 1999), the Games were delivered successfully and reported a financial surplus. Security was greatly heightened as the Games took place five months after the September 11 attacks; the opening ceremony featured the flag recovered from the World Trade Center.

Highlights

Skeleton returned to the programme after 54 years, and women's bobsleigh debuted. Norway topped the medal table, led by biathlete Ole Einar Bjørndalen's four gold medals. Croatia's Janica Kostelić won three golds and a silver in alpine skiing, and Australia's Steven Bradbury won his country's first Winter gold in the most famous short-track race ever, after all his rivals crashed on the final corner. A judging scandal in pairs figure skating — in which a French judge admitted being pressured to favour the Russian pair — resulted in duplicate gold medals being awarded to Canada's Jamie Salé and David Pelletier. Canada won both ice hockey golds, the men's team ending a 50-year drought.
