Edwin Smith Papyrus

The Edwin Smith Papyrus is an ancient Egyptian medical text, the oldest known surgical treatise, dating to about 1600 BC, likely copied from a much older source (c. 3000–2500 BC, sometimes attributed to Imhotep's era).

Edwin Smith Papyrus
Date: c. 1600 BC
Length: ~4.7 m scroll, 48 cases
Location: New York Academy of Medicine
Language: Hieratic

Content

Written in hieratic, it presents 48 cases of injuries — head, neck, spine, chest — organized head-to-toe, each with examination, diagnosis, prognosis ("an ailment I will treat / contend with / not treat"), and treatment. Remarkably rational and empirical, it contains early descriptions of the brain, sutures, and immobilization.

Provenance

American Egyptologist Edwin Smith bought it in Luxor in 1862; James Henry Breasted published the translation in 1930. The verso contains incantations and recipes.
