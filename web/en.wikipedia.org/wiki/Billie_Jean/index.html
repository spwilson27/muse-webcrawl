"Billie Jean" is a song by the American singer Michael Jackson, released by Epic Records on January 3, 1983, as the second single from his sixth studio album, Thriller (1982). It was written and composed by Jackson and produced by Jackson and Quincy Jones. The lyrics describe a woman, Billie Jean, who claims that the narrator is the father of her newborn son, which he denies. Jackson said the lyrics were influenced by groupies who made similar claims about his older brothers when he toured with them as part of the Jacksons.

Composition and recording

The song blends post-disco, R&B, funk, and dance-pop, driven by a distinctive bassline played by Louis Johnson and a lean drum pattern by Ndugu Chancler. Jackson recorded the lead vocal in a single take after extensive preparation, and the arrangement features string and horn parts arranged by Jerry Hey. Producer Quincy Jones initially disliked both the title and the long introduction, fearing listeners would confuse it with tennis player Billie Jean King; Jackson insisted on keeping both.

Commercial performance

"Billie Jean" topped the Billboard Hot 100 for seven weeks and reached number one in many countries, including the United Kingdom. It became one of the best-selling singles of all time, with worldwide sales estimated at over 10 million copies, and helped propel Thriller to become the best-selling album in history.

Music video and performances

The music video, directed by Steve Barron, featured Jackson dancing through city streets as pavement tiles light up beneath his feet. It was one of the first videos by a Black artist to receive heavy rotation on MTV, breaking the channel's racial barrier. On the television special Motown 25: Yesterday, Today, Forever (March 25, 1983), Jackson performed the song wearing a single rhinestone glove and debuted his signature moonwalk dance move, an iconic moment in popular culture.

Legacy

"Billie Jean" won two Grammy Awards and is frequently ranked among the greatest songs of all time; Rolling Stone placed it at number 44 on its 2021 list of the 500 greatest songs. The recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
