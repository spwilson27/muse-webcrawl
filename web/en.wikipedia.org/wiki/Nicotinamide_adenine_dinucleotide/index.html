Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide
FormulaC21H27N7O14P2
Molar mass663.43 g/mol
Other namesNAD, NAD+, DPN

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) is a coenzyme central to metabolism, found in all living cells. It consists of two nucleotides joined through their phosphate groups: one containing an adenine nucleobase and the other nicotinamide. In metabolism, NAD is involved in redox reactions, carrying electrons from one reaction to another.

Forms

NAD exists in two forms: an oxidised form, NAD+, and a reduced form, NADH. NAD+ accepts electrons from other molecules and becomes reduced; NADH then donates those electrons, acting as a reducing agent in processes such as glycolysis, the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, where reduction of NAD+ ultimately drives ATP synthesis.

Phosphorylated form

The related compound NADP (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate) differs by an additional phosphate group; its reduced form NADPH supplies reducing power for anabolic reactions, such as fatty-acid synthesis, and for photosynthesis.

Other functions

Beyond redox chemistry, NAD+ is consumed by enzymes including sirtuins and poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases, linking cellular energy status to DNA repair, gene expression and ageing research. Cells synthesise NAD from dietary precursors such as nicotinic acid and nicotinamide (forms of vitamin B3) and from the amino acid tryptophan.
