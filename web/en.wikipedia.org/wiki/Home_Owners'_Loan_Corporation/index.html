Home Owners' Loan Corporation

The Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) was a United States government-sponsored corporation created by the Home Owners' Loan Act of 1933 as part of President Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal. It refinanced home mortgages that were in default or at risk of foreclosure, converting them into long-term, low-interest, self-amortising loans, and is credited with helping stabilise the housing market during the Great Depression. The HOLC is also widely discussed for its neighbourhood grading maps, which scholars associate with the practice known as redlining.

Operations

Between 1933 and 1936 the HOLC issued roughly one million loans totalling about $3 billion — refinancing about a fifth of the mortgaged homes in urban America. Borrowers received 15-year (later up to 25-year) loans at around 5 per cent interest, replacing short-term, interest-only mortgages that lenders had refused to renew. The corporation foreclosed on about a fifth of its loans but ultimately wound up its lending in 1936 and returned a small surplus to the Treasury when it was liquidated in 1951–54. Its standardised appraisal methods strongly influenced modern mortgage practice.

Residential security maps and redlining

From 1935 the HOLC's City Survey Program appraised neighbourhoods in more than 200 cities, grading them A ("best") through D ("hazardous") and colouring maps accordingly, with D areas outlined in red. The appraisals explicitly recorded racial and ethnic composition, and neighbourhoods with Black, immigrant or working-class residents were routinely downgraded. Historians debate how directly HOLC's maps caused later private and federal redlining — the maps were made after most HOLC lending and were not widely distributed — but the HOLC's appraisal system institutionalised race as a criterion of credit risk, and its maps have become emblematic of structural discrimination in American housing. Digitised "Mapping Inequality" projects have made the maps a major resource for studying segregation and its lasting effects on wealth and health.
