Royal Canal

The Royal Canal (Irish: An Chanáil Ríoga) is a canal in Ireland, running 146 kilometres from the River Shannon at Cloondara and Tarmonbarry in County Longford, eastward through the Midlands to Dublin, where it enters the River Liffey at Spencer Dock. Together with the Grand Canal, which reaches the Shannon further south, it forms one of Ireland's two great cross-country navigation routes linking Dublin with the Shannon.

History

Construction began in 1790, driven partly by rivalry with the Grand Canal to the south. The Royal Canal Company struggled financially amid soaring costs — the canal ultimately cost about £1.4 million — and the company collapsed into bankruptcy in 1794 before the state stepped in; the canal reached the Shannon in 1817. Branch lines served towns such as Longford and Kilcock's harbour served local trade, while a short branch to Broadstone in Dublin became an important passenger terminus.

In its heyday the canal carried cargoes of turf, grain, coal, and building materials, and passenger "fly boats" that, drawn by teams of horses changed at stages, could make the Dublin–Mullingar journey in a matter of hours. The poet and engineer Thomas Meas described the enterprise, and the canal famously inspired Brendan Behan's song "The Auld Triangle", associated with the canal near Mountjoy Prison.

Decline and restoration

The arrival of the Midland Great Western Railway, which bought the canal in 1845 and built its line alongside it, undercut canal traffic; steam and then diesel road transport finished the job. The canal was officially closed to navigation in 1961, and parts were derelict, filled, or built over. From the 1970s the Royal Canal Amenity Group campaigned for restoration. The canal was taken into state care under the Office of Public Works and later Waterways Ireland, and after decades of work the main line was fully reopened to navigation in 2010. The Longford branch reopened in 2023.

Route and features

The canal passes through or near Maynooth, Leixlip, Kilcock, Enfield, Mullingar, and Longford. It has 46 locks, numerous stone bridges and aqueducts — notably the Ryewater Aqueduct carrying it over the Rye Water at Leixlip — and a summit level near Mullingar. In Dublin it runs through the north inner city, passing Croke Park and Mountjoy Prison before descending through locks to the Liffey. The towpath forms the Royal Canal Greenway and waymarked walking and cycling routes through much of its length, popular for recreation, angling, and commuting.
