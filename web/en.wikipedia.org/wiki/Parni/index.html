Parni

The Parni (Latin: Parni; Greek: Πάρνοι, Parnoi) were an eastern Iranian people who lived in the valley of the Ochus River, southeast of the Caspian Sea, in what is now Turkmenistan and northeastern Iran. They were one of the three tribes of the Dahae confederation and spoke an Eastern Iranian language.

The Parni are best known as the tribe from which Arsaces I founded the Arsacid dynasty. In about 247 BC, Arsaces led the Parni south into Parthia, then a satrapy of the Seleucid Empire in revolt under Andragoras, whom he defeated and killed. The Parni then settled in Parthia, adopted the local Parthian language and culture, and gave their name — in its Parthian form — to the empire they built, which endured until AD 224.

History

Before their invasion, the Parni were nomadic pastoralists of the steppe, skilled as mounted archers. Their migration southward formed part of a wider movement of Iranian peoples on the northeastern frontier of the Hellenistic world. Once established in Parthia, the Arsacids gradually expanded westward at Seleucid expense, culminating under Mithridates I (171–132 BC) in the conquest of Media and Mesopotamia.
