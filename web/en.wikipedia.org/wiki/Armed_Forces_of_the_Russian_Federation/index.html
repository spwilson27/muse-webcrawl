The Armed Forces of the Russian Federation (Russian: Вооружённые Силы Российской Федерации) are the military forces of Russia, established on 7 May 1992 following the dissolution of the Soviet Union. The President of Russia is the Supreme Commander-in-Chief. The armed forces are among the largest in the world and possess the largest stockpile of nuclear weapons.

Structure

The Ministry of Defence is the administrative body, while operational control runs through the General Staff. The forces are divided into three services: the Russian Ground Forces, the Russian Aerospace Forces (formed in 2015 by merging the Air Force with the Aerospace Defence Forces), and the Russian Navy. Two independent arms of service are the Strategic Rocket Forces and the Russian Airborne Forces. The country is divided into military districts that function as joint strategic commands.

Personnel and conscription

Russia maintains conscription for men aged 18–27 (with terms and eligibility revised over time), supplemented by a growing share of contract professional soldiers. Authorized strength expanded significantly in the 2020s, and the forces underwent restructuring and expansion amid the Russo-Ukrainian War that began with the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

History

The armed forces inherited the bulk of the Soviet Armed Forces stationed on Russian territory. They fought in the two Chechen wars, the Russo-Georgian War of 2008, intervened in Syria from 2015, and annexed Crimea in 2014 before the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Reforms under Defence Minister Anatoliy Serdyukov after 2008 sought to professionalize and modernize the force, with large rearmament programs for nuclear and conventional systems.
