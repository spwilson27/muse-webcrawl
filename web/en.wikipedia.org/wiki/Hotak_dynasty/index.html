The Hotak dynasty was an Afghan monarchy of the Ghilji Pashtuns, founded by Mirwais Hotak after he led a successful revolution of the Ghilji in Kandahar in April 1709 against their Safavid Persian overlords. At its height, the dynasty briefly ruled much of present-day Afghanistan, Iran, and parts of Pakistan, having overthrown the once-powerful Safavid Empire.

Rise

Mirwais Hotak, chief of the Hotak clan, killed the Safavid governor of Kandahar, Gurgin Khan, in 1709 and established an independent Pashtun kingdom. After Mirwais's death in 1715, his brother Abdul Aziz succeeded him briefly before being killed by Mirwais's son Mahmud Hotak, who took power in 1717.

Conquest of Persia

Mahmud invaded Safavid Persia, defeating its forces at the Battle of Gulnabad (March 1722) and besieging the capital, Isfahan, until famine forced Shah Sultan Husayn to surrender and abdicate in October 1722. Mahmud was crowned Shah of Persia, but his reign was marked by paranoia and massacre; he was deposed and killed in 1725 and succeeded by his cousin Ashraf Hotak.

Decline and fall

Ashraf defeated an Ottoman invasion in 1726 and negotiated peace, but he could not withstand the resurgent Safavid claimant Tahmasp II and his general Nader Qoli Beg (later Nader Shah). Ashraf was defeated at Damghan and Mehmandust in 1729 and fled toward Kandahar, where he was killed. The last Hotak stronghold in Kandahar held out under Hussain Hotak until Nader captured it in 1738, ending the dynasty; many Ghilji were deported, and Kandahar was rebuilt as Naderabad.

Legacy

The Hotaks are remembered in Afghan national historiography as founders of the first independent Afghan state, and Mirwais Hotak is honoured with the title Neeka ("grandfather") of the nation.
