A chemical compound is a substance made of two or more different chemical elements combined in a fixed ratio, held together by chemical bonds. Water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂) and sodium chloride (NaCl) are compounds; a compound's properties are usually very different from those of its elements.

Compounds are described by chemical formulas showing which atoms are present and in what proportion. They can be separated into their elements only by chemical reactions, unlike mixtures, whose components keep their own properties and can be separated physically.

Types of compound

Compounds are grouped by bonding and structure: molecular compounds of discrete molecules (most organic substances), ionic compounds of ions in a lattice (salts), network solids with bonds extending throughout (diamond, quartz), and intermetallic compounds between metals. Organic chemistry studies carbon compounds; inorganic chemistry the rest.

Naming

Systematic names follow IUPAC nomenclature, which encodes composition and structure — for example, 'sodium chloride' for NaCl and 'ethanoic acid' for CH₃COOH — alongside older common names such as vinegar or caustic soda.
