The Bank of Scotland (Scottish Gaelic: Banca na h-Alba) is a commercial and clearing bank based in Edinburgh, Scotland. Founded by an Act of the Parliament of Scotland in 1695, it is the fifth-oldest bank in the United Kingdom and the only commercial institution created by the old Scottish Parliament that still exists.

History

Unlike the Bank of England, founded the previous year, the Bank of Scotland was created to lend to commerce and government without a prohibition on lending to the state, and it pioneered several banking practices, including printing banknotes on its own credit. It is traditionally credited with issuing the first banknotes in Europe printed in colour and with the invention of the overdraft (1728, to merchant William Hogg). For a century it competed fiercely with the Royal Bank of Scotland (founded 1727) in the episodes known as the "bank wars".

Modern era

In 2001 the Bank of Scotland merged with the Halifax Building Society to form HBOS. During the 2008 financial crisis HBOS was rescued through a takeover by Lloyds TSB, forming Lloyds Banking Group, with a large government bailout. The Bank of Scotland brand continues for Scottish retail and business banking, and the bank remains one of the three Scottish banks authorised to issue its own sterling banknotes, backed by deposits at the Bank of England.
