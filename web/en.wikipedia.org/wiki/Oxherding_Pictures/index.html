Oxherding Pictures

The Oxherding Pictures, most famously the Ten Bulls or Ten Oxherding Pictures, are a series of short poems and accompanying drawings used in Zen Buddhism to describe the stages of a practitioner's progress toward enlightenment.

The stages

In the best-known version, attributed to the 12th-century Chinese Zen master Kuoan Shiyuan, the ten stages are: searching for the bull; discovering the footprints; perceiving the bull; catching the bull; taming the bull; riding the bull home; the bull transcended; both bull and self transcended (represented by an empty circle); reaching the source; and finally returning to the world with gift-bestowing hands. The ox symbolises the true self or Buddha-nature, and the herdsman the practitioner seeking it.

Influence

Earlier versions had five, six or eight pictures. Kuoan's ten-stage version, transmitted to Japan, became the standard, and the images — widely reproduced in ink paintings and woodblock prints — remain among the most famous teaching devices in Zen, illustrating that awakening ends not in withdrawal but in compassionate return to ordinary life.
