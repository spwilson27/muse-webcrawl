Jack Benny

Jack Benny

Born: Benjamin Kubelsky, February 14, 1894, Chicago, Illinois

Died: December 26, 1974, Los Angeles, California

Occupation: Comedian, actor, violinist

Spouse: Mary Livingstone (m. 1927)

Signature persona: Perpetually 39, miserly, vain, poor violinist

Jack Benny (born Benjamin Kubelsky; February 14, 1894 – December 26, 1974) was an American comedian, actor, and violinist who was one of the leading entertainers of the twentieth century. With a career spanning vaudeville, radio, film, and television, Benny perfected a comic persona — stingy, vain, perpetually 39 years old, and a hopeless violinist — that audiences embraced for more than three decades.

Early life and vaudeville

Benny was born in Chicago and raised in Waukegan, Illinois, where his Lithuanian-Jewish immigrant parents ran a saloon. He learned the violin as a boy and left school to perform in vaudeville, first in music acts and then, after serving in the Navy during World War I, increasingly as a comedian. In 1927 he married Sadie Marks, who under the name Mary Livingstone became his on-air partner for life.

Radio

The Jack Benny Program premiered on radio in 1932 and ran until 1955, becoming one of the most popular shows in the medium's golden age. Its ensemble — Mary Livingstone, bandleader Phil Harris, singer Dennis Day, announcer Don Wilson, and Eddie "Rochester" Anderson as Benny's valet — made the program a model of character-based situation comedy. Its most famous gag ran in 1948: a mugger demands "Your money or your life!" and Benny, after a long, agonized pause, replies, "I'm thinking it over!"

Television and film

Benny moved to television in 1950 and continued his program until 1965, remaining a fixture of specials afterward. His best-known film is Ernst Lubitsch's To Be or Not to Be (1942), in which he played a ham actor in occupied Warsaw opposite Carole Lombard. Though he played a terrible violinist, Benny was a competent musician who performed at charity concerts with major orchestras, raising millions for their support.

Death and legacy

Benny died of pancreatic cancer in 1974. His timing-based, self-deprecating style influenced generations of comedians, and his radio and television programs are preserved in the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry and broadcasting halls of fame.
