Orthostatic intolerance

FieldNeurology, cardiology (autonomic medicine)
SymptomsLightheadedness, palpitations, fatigue, brain fog and syncope on standing
FormsOrthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS), neurally mediated syncope

Orthostatic intolerance is a category of symptoms that develop when assuming and maintaining an upright posture and that are relieved by recumbency. It reflects dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system's control of blood pressure and heart rate — a form of dysautonomia. When a healthy person stands, several hundred millilitres of blood pool in the legs and splanchnic circulation; baroreflexes normally compensate within seconds. In orthostatic intolerance this compensation fails or is excessive, producing symptoms such as lightheadedness, dizziness, palpitations, tremulousness, fatigue, headache, nausea, exercise intolerance and cognitive difficulty ("brain fog"), and in some cases fainting (syncope).

Types

Orthostatic hypotension is defined as a sustained fall in blood pressure within three minutes of standing (at least 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic) and may be neurogenic, as in multiple system atrophy, Parkinson's disease, diabetes and pure autonomic failure, or non-neurogenic (dehydration, drugs, anaemia). Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS) is characterised by an excessive rise in heart rate on standing (≥30 beats per minute in adults, ≥40 in adolescents) without orthostatic hypotension, accompanied by chronic orthostatic symptoms; it often follows viral illness and is frequently associated with hypermobility disorders and chronic fatigue syndromes. Neurally mediated (vasovagal) syncope is typically provoked by prolonged standing and involves a paradoxical reflex causing bradycardia and vasodilatation.

Diagnosis and management

Evaluation includes measurement of blood pressure and heart rate supine and after standing (the active stand test) and, where needed, head-up tilt table testing. Management is largely non-pharmacological: increasing fluid and salt intake, compression garments, physical counter-manoeuvres, gradual reconditioning exercise, and avoiding triggers such as heat and prolonged standing. Drugs used in selected patients include fludrocortisone, midodrine, droxidopa, pyridostigmine, and low-dose beta-blockers or ivabradine for POTS.
