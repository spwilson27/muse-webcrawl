Héloïse
Born: c. 1100–1101
Died: 16 May 1164
Occupation: Nun, abbess, scholar, writer
Known for: Correspondence with Peter Abelard

Héloïse (c. 1100/1101 – 16 May 1164) was a French nun, philosopher, writer, scholar, and abbess, renowned for her learning in Latin, Greek, and Hebrew and for her celebrated love affair and correspondence with Peter Abelard.

Life

Educated at the convent of Argenteuil, Héloïse was tutored in Paris by Abelard; their secret marriage and its discovery led to Abelard's castration by her uncle Fulbert's men. Both entered religious life; Héloïse became abbess of the Paraclete convent.

Letters

Their surviving letters — among the most famous in medieval literature — debate love, marriage, faith, and monastic life, with Héloïse's writings admired for their intellectual force and emotional honesty. She also posed theological questions in the Problemata Heloissae.

Legacy

Héloïse and Abelard are buried together at Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris, and their story has inspired centuries of literature and art.
