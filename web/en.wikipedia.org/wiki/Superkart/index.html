Superkart

Superkart is a form of kart racing conducted on full-size road racing circuits using highly developed racing karts with engine capacities up to 250 cc. Unlike conventional sprint karts, superkarts have full aerodynamic bodywork, long-circuit gearing, and large fuel tanks, and race on car circuits such as Laguna Seca, Silverstone, and the Isle of Man-style street courses in Australia and New Zealand.

Performance

The premier Division 1 / 250 International class uses twin-cylinder two-stroke engines producing around 90–100 horsepower in a vehicle weighing, with driver, roughly 200 kg. Top speeds exceed 240 km/h (150 mph), and lap times on many circuits rival or beat those of much more expensive racing cars; at Laguna Seca, superkarts have lapped faster than many production-based racers. Acceleration, braking and cornering forces are extreme for the driver's exposed, reclined position.

Classes and racing

Superkart categories include 250 International (twin), 250 National (single-cylinder), 125 Gearbox and, historically, 250 four-stroke and 80 cc classes. Formally sanctioned world and European championships have been run under CIK-FIA auspices, and the discipline is especially popular in the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand, where national series draw strong grids at modest cost relative to car racing.
