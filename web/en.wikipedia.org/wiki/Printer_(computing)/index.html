Printer (computing)

A printer is a peripheral device which makes a durable representation of graphics or text, usually on paper, from data stored in a computer. While most output is human-readable, bar code printers are an example of an expanded use for printers. The different types of printers include 3D printers, inkjet printers, laser printers, and thermal printers, among others.

History

The first computer printer designed was a mechanically driven apparatus for Charles Babbage's difference engine in the 19th century; however, his mechanical printer design was not built until 2000. The first patented printing mechanism for applying a marking medium to a recording medium or electric printer was the telegraph receiver invented by Charles Wheatstone in 1858. The first commercial printers used typewriter-like mechanisms: daisy wheel and line printers dominated business computing in the 1960s and 1970s, followed by dot matrix printers that could produce graphics as well as text. The Xerox PARC laser printer of the 1970s led to the desktop-publishing revolution after the launch of the Apple LaserWriter in 1985, while Hewlett-Packard's DeskJet (1988) brought inkjet printing to home users.

Types of printers

  • Laser printers use a laser beam and electrostatic charges to transfer powdered toner to paper, then fuse it with heat; they are fast and economical for text.
  • Inkjet printers propel droplets of liquid ink onto paper; they excel at colour and photo printing.
  • Thermal printers heat coated paper or transfer wax, and are ubiquitous in receipts, labels and point-of-sale systems.
  • Dot matrix printers strike an inked ribbon with pins; still used for multipart forms because they can print through carbon copies.
  • 3D printers build objects layer by layer from digital models, using plastics, resins or metals.
  • Large-format printers produce posters, banners and technical drawings on wide media.

Printing technology

Print quality is measured in dots per inch (DPI) and speed in pages per minute (PPM). Modern printers are described by page description languages such as PostScript and PCL, or receive rasterised output rendered by the host computer. Colour printing generally uses the CMYK colour model — cyan, magenta, yellow and black (key). Printers connect via USB, Ethernet or Wi-Fi, and many support driverless printing standards such as AirPrint and IPP Everywhere.

Attributes and costs

The purchase economics of printers are often described by the "razor and blades" model: machines may be sold cheaply while replacement ink and toner cartridges carry high margins. Total cost of ownership, duty cycle, duplex (two-sided) printing, and features such as scanning and copying in multifunction devices all affect buying decisions.
