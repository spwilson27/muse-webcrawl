Math rock bands emphasize rhythmic complexity and interlocking guitar lines.

Math rock is a style of alternative and indie rock with roots in bands such as King Crimson and Rush. It is characterized by complex, atypical rhythmic structures (including irregular stopping and starting), counterpoint, odd time signatures such as 5/4, 7/8, or 11/8, and angular, dissonant melodies. The style emerged in the late 1980s and 1990s in the American indie underground, with important scenes in Chicago, Pittsburgh, San Diego, and Japan.

Characteristics

Whereas most rock music is in 4/4 time, math rock frequently uses asymmetrical meters and shifting groupings that make the music feel as though it lurches or resets unexpectedly. Guitar parts often interlock in hocket-like patterns, and extended techniques such as two-handed tapping are common. Vocals, when present, are usually subordinate to the instrumental interplay. The genre is closely related to post-rock, though math rock tends to be denser, more rhythmically aggressive, and more song-oriented.

History

Pioneers include Slint, whose album Spiderland (1991) helped define the emerging sound; Don Caballero, led by drummer Damon Che; Shellac; Chavez; and Polvo. The term "math rock" was initially used half-derisively by musicians and journalists, playing on the music's cerebral, calculated precision. In Japan, bands such as Ruins and later Toe, Lite, and Tricot developed parallel scenes. A revival in the 2010s — including American Football's influence on "midwest emo," TTNG, Covet, and Polyphia's virtuosic offshoot — brought the style renewed attention.
