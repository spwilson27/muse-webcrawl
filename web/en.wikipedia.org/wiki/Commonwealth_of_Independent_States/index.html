Commonwealth of Independent States

Founded: 8 December 1991

Headquarters: Minsk, Belarus

Members: Nine full members (as of mid-2020s)

The Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS) is a regional organisation formed by former Soviet republics following the dissolution of the Soviet Union. It coordinates cooperation in trade, security and cross-border issues but is far looser than the USSR it succeeded.

Formation

Russia, Ukraine and Belarus created the CIS in the Belovezha Accords of 8 December 1991; eight more republics joined by the Alma-Ata Protocols later that month. The Baltic states never joined.

Changes in membership

Georgia withdrew after the 2008 Russo-Georgian War. Ukraine progressively disengaged after 2014 and especially after Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022; Moldova also reduced participation. Several members pursue deeper integration through the Eurasian Economic Union.

Activities

The CIS sponsors summits of heads of state and government, a free-trade area (from 2011), election observation missions and military cooperation, though its practical influence varies widely among members.
