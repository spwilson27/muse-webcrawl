2060 Chiron is a small Solar System body in the outer Solar System, orbiting the Sun between Saturn and Uranus. Discovered in 1977 by Charles Kowal, it was the first-identified member of a new class of objects now known as centaurs, bodies orbiting between the asteroid belt and the Kuiper belt. It is named after the centaur Chiron in Greek mythology.

Dual classification

Chiron displays behaviour typical of both an asteroid and a comet. In 1988 it brightened unexpectedly, behaviour consistent with a cometary outburst, and in 1989 a coma of gas and dust was observed around it. As a result, Chiron holds both a minor planet designation (2060) and a periodic comet designation (95P/Chiron), one of only a handful of bodies cross-listed in both catalogues.

Rings

Observations of a stellar occultation in 2011, analysed in 2015, suggested that Chiron is surrounded by a system of two narrow rings, similar to the rings discovered around the centaur 10199 Chariklo. The putative rings lie at radii of about 324 and 340 kilometres and are only a few kilometres wide. Their origin may lie in debris from a collision or in material ejected during cometary activity.

Physical characteristics

Chiron's nucleus has been measured at roughly 200 kilometres in diameter, making it one of the largest known centaurs. Its surface spectra show water ice, and its rotation period is about 5.9 hours. Because centaur orbits are chaotic and unstable on million-year timescales, Chiron is thought to have originated in the Kuiper belt and will eventually be perturbed into a shorter-period orbit.
