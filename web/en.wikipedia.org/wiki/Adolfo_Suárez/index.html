Adolfo Suárez González (25 September 1932 – 23 March 2014) was a Spanish lawyer and politician. Suárez was Spain's first democratically elected prime minister since the Second Spanish Republic and a key figure in the country's transition to democracy following the death of dictator Francisco Franco.

Early career

Born in Cebreros, Ávila, Suárez studied law and rose within the Francoist administration, serving as civil governor of Segovia and as director-general of the state broadcaster RTVE from 1969 to 1973. In 1975 he became secretary-general of the Movimiento Nacional, the regime's sole political movement — a position that paradoxically equipped him to dismantle it.

Prime minister

King Juan Carlos I appointed Suárez prime minister in July 1976. Within months he pushed the Political Reform Act through the Francoist Cortes, effectively voting the old regime out of existence, and secured its approval in a referendum. He legalized political parties, including, controversially, the Communist Party in April 1977, granted amnesties, and called Spain's first free elections in 41 years in June 1977, which his centrist UCD coalition won.

His government oversaw the negotiation of the Moncloa Pacts to stabilize the economy and the drafting of the democratic Constitution of 1978, as well as the first statutes of autonomy for Catalonia and the Basque Country.

Resignation and later life

Worn down by party infighting, terrorism, and military unrest, Suárez resigned in January 1981; during the investiture of his successor, Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo, Civil Guards under Antonio Tejero stormed the Congress in the failed 23-F coup attempt. Suárez later founded the Democratic and Social Centre (CDS). He retired from politics in 1991 and was created Duke of Suárez by the King in 1981. He died in Madrid in 2014 after a long illness with Alzheimer's disease. Madrid's airport was renamed Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas Airport in his honour.
