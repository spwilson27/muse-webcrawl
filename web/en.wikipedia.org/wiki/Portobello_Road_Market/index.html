Portobello Road Market

Portobello Road Market is a street market in Portobello Road, Notting Hill, in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea, London. It is one of the world's largest antiques markets, with over 1,000 dealers, and attracts large crowds, especially on Saturdays, its main trading day.

History

Portobello Road takes its name from Portobello Farm, itself named after the British capture of Portobelo, Panama, in 1739. In the 19th century the road was a country lane lined with shops serving the growing suburbs; its market began with rag-and-bone men and gypsy horse dealers, and after the Second World War it became a centre for antiques dealers, many displaced from the Caledonian Market. By the 1960s the market was a well-known fixture of bohemian London.

The market today

The market runs for about two miles in several sections: antiques dominate the southern end near Notting Hill Gate, followed by food stalls selling fruit, vegetables, bread and street food, then fashion — including vintage clothing — under and beyond the Westway flyover, and general second-hand goods toward Golborne Road. A smaller market operates on other days of the week, with Friday as the second busiest day.

In popular culture

The market and road appear in the film Notting Hill (1999) and in the Disney film Bedknobs and Broomsticks (1971), which features the song "Portobello Road".
