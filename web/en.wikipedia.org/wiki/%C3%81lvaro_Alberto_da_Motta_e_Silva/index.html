Álvaro Alberto da Motta e Silva

Álvaro Alberto da Motta e Silva (22 April 1889 – 31 January 1976) was a Brazilian naval officer, physicist, and university professor who played a central role in the development of nuclear science and policy in Brazil. He served as president of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences and was a key figure in debates over Brazil's atomic energy program in the mid-twentieth century.

Early life and career

Álvaro Alberto was born in Rio de Janeiro in 1889. He entered the Brazilian Naval School and pursued a career in the navy, combining military service with scientific study. He developed an early interest in explosives and chemistry, areas in which he later taught and conducted research.

Scientific work

As a professor, Álvaro Alberto taught at naval and engineering institutions in Rio de Janeiro. He became one of the most visible advocates of building autonomous scientific and technological capacity in Brazil, arguing that the country should develop its own expertise in nuclear energy rather than depend entirely on foreign powers.

Nuclear policy

In the 1940s and 1950s, Álvaro Alberto represented Brazil in international discussions on atomic energy, including at the United Nations. He defended the principle that countries holding uranium and thorium reserves should receive compensation and technology transfer in exchange for access to those resources, a position associated with the so-called "specific compensations" thesis. He also participated in the creation of Brazil's National Research Council (CNPq) in 1951 and served as its president.

Legacy

Álvaro Alberto is remembered as a pioneer of Brazilian science policy. The Brazilian Navy named a nuclear-powered submarine project in his honor, and the Álvaro Alberto Medal is awarded for contributions to science and technology in Brazil.
