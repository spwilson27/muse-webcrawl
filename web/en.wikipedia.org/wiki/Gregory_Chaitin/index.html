Gregory Chaitin

Gregory John Chaitin (born 15 November 1947) is an Argentine-American mathematician and computer scientist. Beginning in the late 1960s, Chaitin made contributions to algorithmic information theory and metamathematics, in particular a computer-theoretic result equivalent to Gödel's incompleteness theorem. He is considered to be one of the founders of algorithmic (or Kolmogorov) complexity, together with Andrey Kolmogorov and Ray Solomonoff.

Work

Chaitin defined what is now known as Chaitin's constant, or Omega (Ω), a real number representing the probability that a randomly chosen program will halt. Ω has the remarkable property of being definable and computable in an approximate sense, yet its binary digits are algorithmically random: no formal system can determine more than finitely many of them, a result dramatised in Chaitin's incompleteness theorem, which shows that there is a constant L such that no consistent formal system can prove that any particular program is more than L bits long.

He worked for many years at IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center, where he also contributed to computer architecture, including work on register allocation by graph colouring ("Chaitin's algorithm") used in compilers. In later years he has been based in Brazil, associated with the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro, and has written popular works arguing for a "digital philosophy" of mathematics and nature, including The Unknowable, Meta Math! and Proving Darwin, in which he develops ideas about the evolution of biological complexity in algorithmic terms.
