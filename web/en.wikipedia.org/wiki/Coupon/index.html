Coupon

A ticket or document exchangeable for a discount or rebate.

In marketing, a coupon is a ticket or document that can be redeemed for a financial discount or rebate when purchasing a product. Customarily, coupons are issued by manufacturers of consumer packaged goods or by retailers, to be used as part of a sales promotion. They are often distributed through mail, magazines, newspapers, store flyers, email, mobile apps, and websites.

History

One of the earliest coupons is credited to Coca-Cola: in 1887–88 the company distributed handwritten tickets for a free glass of Coca-Cola, a promotion repeated in 1892 by pharmacist Asa Candler, which helped spread the drink nationally. Cereal maker C. W. Post offered a one-cent-off coupon for Grape-Nuts in 1895. The practice expanded greatly in the 20th century; by the 1960s, coupon clearinghouses processed redemptions for retailers and manufacturers, and "coupon clipping" became a common household habit. The word derives from the French couper, "to cut".

Types

Coupons include manufacturers' coupons (redeemed at any retailer and reimbursed by the maker), store coupons, instant-rebate coupons attached to packaging, catalogue and online promo codes, and digital coupons loaded to loyalty cards. Common forms offer a fixed amount off, a percentage discount, free shipping, or "buy one, get one" deals. In finance, "coupon" also denotes the interest payment on a bond—a usage surviving from bearer bonds with detachable interest tickets.

Economics

Coupons enable price discrimination: price-sensitive customers invest time to find and redeem them while others pay full price. Fraud and counterfeit coupons are persistent problems, mitigated by barcodes, unique codes, and redemption limits. Extreme couponing—combining coupons with sales to minimise cost—became a popular hobby and media subject in the 2010s.
