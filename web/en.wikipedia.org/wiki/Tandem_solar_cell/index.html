Tandem solar cell

A tandem solar cell (also called a multi-junction solar cell) is a photovoltaic device that stacks two or more sub-cells with different band gaps on top of one another. The top cell absorbs high-energy (short-wavelength) photons while transmitting lower-energy light to the cells beneath, allowing the device to harvest a much broader portion of the solar spectrum than a single-junction cell.

Efficiency

Single-junction cells are limited by the Shockley–Queisser limit (about 33% for the optimal band gap), largely because photons with energy far above the band gap waste their excess energy as heat. Tandems circumvent this: multi-junction concentrator cells have exceeded 47% efficiency in the laboratory, and perovskite-on-silicon tandems have surpassed 33–34%, exceeding the best single-junction silicon cells.

Technologies

III–V semiconductor stacks (such as GaInP/GaAs/Ge) power most spacecraft and satellites because of their efficiency and radiation resistance, though at high cost. Thin-film and perovskite/silicon tandems aim to bring multi-junction performance to terrestrial mass production, with challenges in current matching, tunnel junctions, stability and manufacturability.
