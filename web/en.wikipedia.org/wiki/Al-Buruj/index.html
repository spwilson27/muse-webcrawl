Al-Burūj (Arabic: البروج, "The Constellations" or "The Mansions of the Stars") is the 85th chapter (surah) of the Qur'an, containing 22 verses (ayat). A Meccan surah, it takes its name from the word al-burūj in its opening verse, in which God swears by "the sky full of constellations".

Contents

The surah opens with oaths by the sky with its constellations, the promised Day, and the witness and the witnessed. Its central passage recounts the story of the "People of the Ditch" (aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd): believers who were burned in trenches of fire by persecutors — traditionally identified with the martyrdom of Christians at Najran in Himyar (Yemen) in the 6th century, though other identifications have been proposed. The surah declares that those who persecute believing men and women and do not repent face the punishment of Hell and the burning fire, while the believers who do righteous deeds receive gardens beneath which rivers flow.

The latter verses affirm God's irresistible power — "Indeed, it is He who originates and repeats; and He is the Forgiving, the Affectionate, Lord of the Throne of Glory, Doer of whatever He wills" — and recount the fate of the hosts of Pharaoh and Thamud as warnings. The surah closes by affirming that the Qur'an is "a glorious Qur'an, [inscribed] in the Preserved Tablet" (Lawḥ Maḥfūẓ).

Themes

Exegesis commonly draws from the surah a lesson of patience under persecution: the believers of the ditch lost their lives but kept their faith, and divine justice is deferred to the Day of Judgement. Its opening oath relates to a broader Quranic motif of swearing by cosmic phenomena as signs of God's power.
