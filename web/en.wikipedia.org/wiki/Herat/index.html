Herat (Pashto/Dari: هرات) is the third-largest city in Afghanistan, capital of Herat Province in the fertile Hari River valley in the west of the country, near the borders with Iran and Turkmenistan.

History

An ancient oasis on the Silk Road — Aria/Alexandria Ariana under Alexander the Great — Herat flourished under the Kart dynasty and especially the Timurids in the 15th century, when under Shah Rukh and Queen Gawhar Shad it became a centre of Persian art, architecture and learning, famed for miniature painting. It was devastated by Mongols, contested by Safavids and Uzbeks, and incorporated into Afghanistan in the 19th century, enduring siege in the First Anglo-Afghan War and heavy fighting in the Soviet–Afghan War and after.

Landmarks

The Great Mosque (Friday Mosque), the Citadel of Alexander, the Musalla Minarets and the tomb of Gawhar Shad mark its Timurid heritage.

Economy and culture

Herat trades in saffron, carpets, dried fruit and marble, and its population is predominantly Persian-speaking Tajik.
