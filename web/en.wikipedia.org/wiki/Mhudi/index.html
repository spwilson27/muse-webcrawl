Mhudi is a historical novel by the South African writer, linguist and political activist Sol Plaatje (1876–1932). Written in 1919–1920 but not published until 1930, it is widely regarded as the first novel in English by a black South African.

Plot

Set in the 1830s against the backdrop of the upheavals of the Mfecane and the arrival of the Voortrekkers in the interior of southern Africa, the novel follows Mhudi, a Barolong woman separated from her people after her town is destroyed by the armies of Mzilikazi, king of the Matabele (Ndebele). She is rescued by Ra-Thaga, a Barolong hunter, whom she later marries. The plot intertwines their story with the alliance formed between the Barolong chiefs, the Boer trekkers led by figures such as Phil Jay, and the conflict with Mzilikazi, culminating in the Matabele defeat.

Significance

Plaatje, a founding member of the organisation that became the African National Congress, used the novel to challenge colonial versions of South African history, presenting African societies and African perspectives — including a strong female protagonist — at the centre of the narrative. The manuscript was completed a decade before publication because no publisher would take it; it was eventually issued by the Lovedale Press in 1930. Mhudi has since been republished many times and is a set text in South African literary studies.
