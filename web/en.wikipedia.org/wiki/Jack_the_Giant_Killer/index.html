Jack the Giant Killer

GenreFairy tale
CountryEngland (Cornwall)
First printedEarly 18th century (chapbook)

Jack the Giant Killer is an English fairy tale and legend about a young adult who slays a number of bad giants during the reign of King Arthur. The tale is characterised by violence, gore and blood-letting; yet Jack is a hero celebrated in Cornish folklore and in chapbooks printed throughout the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Synopsis

Jack, a farmer's son living near Land's End during Arthur's reign, traps and kills the giant Cormoran, who terrorises St Michael's Mount, and is rewarded with a sword and belt inscribed with his new title. He goes on to outwit further giants using a magic sword, shoes of swiftness, a cap of knowledge and a cloak of invisibility, frees captives, and eventually marries a duke's daughter and receives an estate from Arthur.

Origins and adaptations

The story combines motifs from Arthurian legend and Norse and classical giant-slaying tales. It has been adapted many times, including the 1962 film Jack the Giant Killer and, loosely, the 2013 film Jack the Giant Slayer. It is distinct from, but often shelved beside, "Jack and the Beanstalk", with which it shares the hero's name and giant adversaries.
