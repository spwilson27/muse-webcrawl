Adama Barrow (born 15 February 1965) is a Gambian politician and real estate developer who has served as the third president of the Gambia since 2017. His election victory over the authoritarian ruler Yahya Jammeh ended 22 years of Jammeh's rule.

Early life and career

Barrow was born in Mankamang Kunda near Basse. He attended Crab Island Secondary School in Banjul, worked for the energy company Alhagie Musa Njie & Sons, and lived in London in the early 2000s — where he worked as a security guard, famously at an Argos store — while studying for a real estate qualification. Returning to the Gambia, he founded the Majum Real Estate company and became treasurer of the opposition United Democratic Party (UDP).

2016 election and crisis

Chosen as the candidate of a seven-party opposition coalition, Barrow won the December 2016 presidential election with about 43% of the vote. Jammeh initially conceded, then reversed himself, declaring a state of emergency. Barrow was sworn in at the Gambian embassy in Dakar, Senegal, on 19 January 2017, and an ECOWAS military intervention compelled Jammeh to go into exile.

Presidency

Barrow's government freed political prisoners, established the Truth, Reconciliation and Reparations Commission (TRRC) to investigate Jammeh-era abuses, and rejoined the Commonwealth and the International Criminal Court. Having initially pledged to serve a three-year transitional term, he instead completed the full five years and won re-election in December 2021 with about 53% of the vote, at the head of his new National People's Party.
