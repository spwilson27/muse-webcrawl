Celtic League (rugby union)

The Celtic League brought together professional teams from Ireland, Scotland and Wales.

The Celtic League was an annual rugby union competition involving professional teams from Ireland, Scotland and Wales, held between 2001 and 2011. It was the predecessor of the competitions now known as the United Rugby Championship, having previously been branded the Magners League (2006–2011) before expanding to include Italian teams as the Pro12 in 2011.

History

The league was launched for the 2001–02 season with fifteen teams: the four Irish provinces, two Scottish districts, and nine Welsh clubs, initially playing in two pools followed by knockout rounds. Leinster won the first title, beating Munster in the final at Lansdowne Road. In 2003 Welsh rugby restructured into five regional teams (later four), and the competition settled into a conventional home-and-away league format. A play-off system was introduced in 2009–10, won by the Ospreys.

Champions

Munster and Leinster each won three Celtic League titles, the Ospreys won three (2005, 2007, 2010), and Ulster won once (2006). The league provided the core season structure for the Celtic nations' professional game and a qualification route to the European Heineken Cup.

Legacy

With the admission of two Italian teams in 2010–11 and the renaming to Pro12 the following season, the Celtic League identity ended, but its format and member unions formed the nucleus of the later Pro14 and United Rugby Championship, which subsequently added South African sides.
