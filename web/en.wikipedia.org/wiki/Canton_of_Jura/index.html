Canton of Jura

The canton of Jura (French: canton du Jura) is the newest of the 26 cantons of Switzerland, having joined the confederation on 1 January 1979. It is located in the northwestern part of the country, in the Jura Mountains along the border with France. Its capital is Delémont.

History

The territory belonged for centuries to the Prince-Bishopric of Basel and was assigned to the canton of Bern at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. The French-speaking, predominantly Catholic Jurassians came to resent rule by German-speaking, Protestant Bern, and a separatist movement grew after 1947. A series of plebiscites in the 1970s led to the northern districts voting to form a new canton, approved by Swiss voters in a national referendum in 1978. The southern districts remained with Bern, an arrangement periodically revisited; the town of Moutier voted in 2021 to transfer to Jura.

Geography and economy

The canton comprises three districts — Delémont, Porrentruy (the Ajoie) and Franches-Montagnes — with a population of about 73,000, making it one of Switzerland's least populous cantons. The economy is based on watchmaking and precision engineering, agriculture and the breeding of the Franches-Montagnes horse, Switzerland's only indigenous horse breed.
