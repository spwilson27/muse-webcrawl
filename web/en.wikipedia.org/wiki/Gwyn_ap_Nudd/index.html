Gwyn ap Nudd

Gwyn ap Nudd (pronounced [ɡwɪn ap ˈnɨːð], older form Gwyn ap Nudd) is a Welsh mythological figure, the king of the Tylwyth Teg (the "Fair Folk" or fairies) and ruler of the Otherworld realm of Annwn, and associated in Welsh tradition with the Wild Hunt. His name means "Gwyn, son of Nudd"; gwyn means "white, fair, blessed", and his father Nudd is cognate with the Celtic god Nodens.

Mythology

In the tale of Culhwch and Olwen (c. 1100), one of the earliest Arthurian prose tales, Gwyn is said to have been given dominion over the demons of Annwn lest they destroy the world, and he becomes the rival of Gwythyr ap Greidol for the maiden Creiddylad: the two must fight for her every May Day until Judgement Day, a myth often read as a seasonal conflict between summer and winter. Arthur imposes the arrangement. Gwyn is also one of the hunters who pursue the great boar Twrch Trwyth.

In later Welsh folklore, Gwyn rides at the head of the Cŵn Annwn, the spectral white hounds with red ears of the Otherworld, gathering the souls of the dead — the Welsh form of the Wild Hunt. He was particularly associated with Glastonbury Tor in Somerset, said to be an entrance to Annwn, and with the mountains of Wales, including Cadair Idris. A poem in the Black Book of Carmarthen, a dialogue between Gwyn and the hero Gwyddno Garanhir, lists the battles and the dead Gwyn has witnessed, presenting him as a psychopomp who has collected the souls of Welsh heroes.
