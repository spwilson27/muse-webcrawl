Wild Bunch

The Wild Bunch, also known as the Doolin–Dalton Gang, was a gang of outlaws based in Indian Territory (Oklahoma) in the 1890s, robbing banks, stores and trains and harbouring fugitives. Its best-known later association is with Butch Cassidy's separate Wyoming gang, also called the Wild Bunch.

The Doolin gang

Bill Doolin, a former Dalton Gang member, formed the gang after the Daltons' disastrous 1892 Coffeyville raid. Members included Bill Dalton, "Bitter Creek" Newcomb, Charlie Pierce, Dick Broadwell and others. Deputy marshals — the "Three Guardsmen" Heck Thomas, Bill Tilghman and Chris Madsen — hunted them; most members were killed or captured by 1896, and Doolin was shot by Heck Thomas.

Butch Cassidy's Wild Bunch

Cassidy's informal gang, operating from Hole-in-the-Wall, Wyoming, included the Sundance Kid and Elzy Lay and committed train and bank robberies around 1896–1901 before Cassidy and Sundance fled to South America.

Legacy

The name was immortalised by Sam Peckinpah's 1969 film The Wild Bunch, about an ageing outlaw gang in 1913.
