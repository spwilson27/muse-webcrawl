Fatherland Front (Austria)

The Fatherland Front (German: Vaterländische Front, VF) was the ruling political organisation of the Federal State of Austria, which pursued an ideology frequently characterised as Austrofascism. It was founded on 20 May 1933 by Chancellor Engelbert Dollfuss, who modelled it in part on Italian Fascism.

History

Dollfuss established the Front as a broad movement uniting the Christian Social Party, the paramilitary Heimwehr, and other groups supportive of his authoritarian, corporatist programme, after suspending parliament in March 1933. Its symbol was the crutch cross (Kruckenkreuz). Following the February 1934 Austrian Civil War, the Social Democratic Party was banned, and the May 1934 constitution made the Front the sole legal political organisation; all other parties were dissolved and absorbed.

After Dollfuss was assassinated by Nazi conspirators in July 1934, Kurt Schuschnigg succeeded him as Chancellor and leader of the Front. The organisation claimed some 3 million members at its height, though membership was effectively compulsory for civil servants and others. Schuschnigg dissolved the Front in 1938 following the Anschluss, Austria's annexation by Nazi Germany, and its leadership was persecuted by the Gestapo.

Ideology

The Front promoted Austrian nationalism (as distinct from German identity), Catholic corporatism based on the encyclical Quadragesimo anno, and authoritarian rule, while opposing both Marxist socialism and National Socialism. Historians debate whether its regime, sometimes called the Ständestaat (corporative state), should be classed as fascist.
