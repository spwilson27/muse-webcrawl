Gâteau

Gâteau is the French word for cake. In French usage it denotes baked sweet goods generally, while in English usage — particularly British English — "gateau" has come to refer to a rich, elaborate, often layered cake, typically filled and decorated with cream, fruit, or chocolate, and associated with pâtisserie rather than plain home baking.

Meaning and usage

In France, gâteau covers everything from a simple yogurt cake (gâteau au yaourt) to wedding confections, and savory preparations also exist, such as gâteau de foie. In Britain the word gained currency in the mid-20th century for cream-filled sponge cakes sold for special occasions. The Black Forest gâteau (Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte) — chocolate sponge layered with whipped cream, cherries, and kirsch — became the archetypal example in British popular culture, later acquiring a somewhat retro image.

Types

Well-known French gâteaux include the gâteau Saint-Honoré, the gâteau opéra (almond sponge, coffee buttercream, and chocolate ganache, created in Paris in 1955), the gâteau basque with cherry or pastry-cream filling, the Breton gâteau breton butter cake, and the galette des rois served at Epiphany. Modern pâtisserie gâteaux are often built as entremets with mousse layers, glazes, and precise decoration.
