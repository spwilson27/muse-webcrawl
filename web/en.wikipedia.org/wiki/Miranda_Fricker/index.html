Miranda Fricker

Miranda Fricker (born 12 March 1966) is a British philosopher best known for her work in epistemology and ethics, particularly for introducing the concept of epistemic injustice in her book Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing (2007). She is a Distinguished Professor at the Graduate Center of the City University of New York and an Honorary Professor at the University of Sheffield, and a Fellow of the British Academy.

Career

Fricker studied at the University of Oxford, where she completed her doctorate. She taught at Birkbeck, University of London, and then at the University of Sheffield, where she was Professor of Philosophy and head of department, before moving to the CUNY Graduate Center in New York. She has served professional roles including as president of the Aristotelian Society.

Epistemic injustice

In Epistemic Injustice (2007) Fricker identifies two forms of wrong done to a person specifically in their capacity as a knower. Testimonial injustice occurs when prejudice — for example, about a speaker's race, gender, or accent — causes a hearer to give less credibility to the speaker's words than they deserve. Hermeneutical injustice occurs when a gap in the shared interpretive resources of a society (for instance, before the concept "sexual harassment" existed) unfairly disadvantages someone in making sense of their own experience. The book has been highly influential across philosophy, law, medicine, education, and the social sciences, spawning a large research literature on epistemic injustice and related virtues such as testimonial justice.
