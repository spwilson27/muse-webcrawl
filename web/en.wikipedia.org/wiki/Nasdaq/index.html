The Nasdaq Stock Market is an American stock exchange based in New York City, the second-largest in the world by market capitalisation and the home of many of the largest technology companies (Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Nvidia and others at various times). Founded in 1971 as the National Association of Securities Dealers Automated Quotations system, it was the world's first electronic stock market — a dealer quotation network with no physical trading floor, the model that eventually displaced floor trading almost everywhere.

Indices and character

Its benchmark, the Nasdaq Composite, tracks several thousand listed companies and is heavily weighted toward technology and growth stocks; the Nasdaq-100 covers the largest non-financial listings. The Composite's peak in March 2000 and subsequent 78% collapse epitomise the dot-com bubble and crash, after which it took fifteen years to regain its high. The exchange, operated by Nasdaq, Inc., is known for the giant MarketSite tower's screens in Times Square and for attracting younger, faster-growing companies that once would have chosen the New York Stock Exchange; its listings and the stock market's tech leadership have made 'the Nasdaq' shorthand for growth shares.
