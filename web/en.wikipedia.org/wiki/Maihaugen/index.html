Maihaugen (De Sandvigske Samlinger på Maihaugen) is an open-air museum in Lillehammer, Norway. It is one of the largest outdoor museums in Norway, with more than 200 historic buildings dating from the 13th century onwards. Maihaugen is part of the Lillehammer Museum foundation, which also includes the Norwegian Olympic Museum and Bjerkebæk, the home of Nobel laureate Sigrid Undset.

History

The museum was founded by dentist Anders Sandvig (1862–1950), who began collecting old buildings and objects from the Gudbrandsdalen valley in the 1880s. His collection was first established at his home in Lillehammer; when it outgrew the site it was moved to Maihaugen. The museum opened at its present location on 2 July 1904, when King Oscar II placed it under royal protection. Sandvig served as the museum's first director.

Collections

Maihaugen is divided into several sections, including the Rural Section, which displays farm buildings from Gudbrandsdalen, the Town Section, which recreates the Lillehammer town centre as it appeared in the early 20th century, and the Residential Section, showing Norwegian homes from each decade of the 20th century. Notable buildings include the Garmo Stave Church, dating from around 1200, which was dismantled and moved to the museum from Lom. The museum's indoor collections hold some 45,000 objects relating to folk art, crafts, hunting and fishing.
