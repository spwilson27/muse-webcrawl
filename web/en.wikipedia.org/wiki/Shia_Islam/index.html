Shia Islam is the second-largest branch of Islam, distinguished by the belief that leadership of the community after Muhammad belonged by divine designation to his cousin and son-in-law Ali and to Imams descended from him. The martyrdom of Ali's son Husayn at Karbala in 680 — commemorated each year at Ashura with mourning processions — forged Shia identity around righteous suffering and resistance to tyranny.

Branches and today

The largest group, Twelvers, await the return of the twelfth Imam, the Mahdi, in occultation since 874; Ismailis follow a living Imam (the Aga Khan); Zaydis survive mainly in Yemen. Twelver clerical hierarchy (marja' al-taqlid) and Iran's post-1979 doctrine of guardianship of the jurist (velayat-e faqih) give Shia Islam distinctive political forms; centres of learning at Najaf and Qom anchor its scholarship.
