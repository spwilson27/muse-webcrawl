H&E stain

The H&E stain (haematoxylin and eosin stain) is one of the principal tissue stains used in histology and the most widely used stain in medical diagnosis. It is the standard method for the examination of tissue sections by pathologists, for example in the diagnosis of cancer from biopsy material.

Method and appearance

In the H&E procedure, tissue sections are stained first with haematoxylin, a basic dye that colours acidic (basophilic) structures — above all the nucleic acids of the cell nucleus — in blue and purple tones, and then with eosin, an acidic dye that colours basic (acidophilic) structures — including most cytoplasmic proteins and extracellular matrix such as collagen — in varying shades of pink and red. The resulting contrast allows the microscopist to distinguish cell nuclei from cytoplasm and connective tissue and to assess tissue architecture.

The combination was introduced in the 1870s, building on haematoxylin staining methods developed earlier in the decade, and remains fundamental to histopathology despite the growth of immunohistochemistry and molecular diagnostics.
