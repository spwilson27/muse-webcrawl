Solubility

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Solubility
Field: Chemistry

Solubility is the maximum amount of a solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature to form a saturated solution, usually quoted in grams per 100 mL. It varies enormously: sodium chloride dissolves about 36 g per 100 mL of water; silver chloride barely 0.0002 g.

Solubility generally rises with temperature for solids (the basis of recrystallisation) and falls for gases, while gas solubility rises with pressure (Henry's law) — why fizzy drinks bubble when opened. 'Like dissolves like' predicts miscibility from polarity.

Precipitation

When a solution's ion product exceeds a sparingly soluble salt's solubility product (Ksp), precipitate forms — the chemistry behind kidney stones, boiler scale, gravimetric analysis and qualitative-analysis group separations.

In biology and environment

Oxygen's low solubility limits aquatic life and makes warm water hold less oxygen; drug solubility governs absorption and is a major hurdle in pharmaceutical design.
