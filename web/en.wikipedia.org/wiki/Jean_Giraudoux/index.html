Jean Giraudoux

Hippolyte Jean Giraudoux (29 October 1882 – 31 January 1944) was a French novelist, essayist, diplomat and playwright, considered the most important French dramatist of the interwar period. His works, noted for elegant, poetic fantasy and stylised dialogue, include the plays Amphitryon 38, The Madwoman of Chaillot and Ondine, and the novel Suzanne and the Pacific.

Jean Giraudoux
Born: 29 October 1882, Bellac, France
Died: 31 January 1944, Paris
Occupation: Playwright, novelist, diplomat
Notable works: Amphitryon 38, Électre, La guerre de Troie n'aura pas lieu

Life

Born in Bellac, Haute-Vienne, Giraudoux studied at the École Normale Supérieure, travelled in Germany, and entered the foreign ministry in 1910. He was wounded and decorated in the First World War, an experience behind writings such as Adorable Clio. As a diplomat he served in postings and as head of the French information service at the start of the Second World War.

Plays

From Siegfried (1928) onward, usually in collaboration with actor-director Louis Jouvet, Giraudoux reworked classical and biblical myths for contemporary anxieties: Amphitryon 38 (1929), Judith (1931), La guerre de Troie n'aura pas lieu ("The Trojan War Will Not Take Place," 1935) on the coming war, Électre (1937) and Ondine (1939), from Fouqué's water-sprite tale, later famous in Audrey Hepburn's Broadway performance.

Legacy

The posthumously produced La folle de Chaillot (The Madwoman of Chaillot, 1945) became an international success. Giraudoux's lyrical, witty theatre of ideas influenced postwar French drama and English-language adaptations by Christopher Fry and Maurice Valency. His home town Bellac hosts a festival in his honour.
