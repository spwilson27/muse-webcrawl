Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer is a commune in the Bouches-du-Rhône department in southern France, on the Mediterranean coast in the heart of the Camargue. It is the capital of the Camargue and a major centre of pilgrimage, sacred both to Christians for its association with the Three Marys and to Romani people for the veneration of Saint Sarah.

Traditions

According to Provençal tradition, Mary Magdalene, Mary Salome and Mary Jacobe (Mary of Clopas) landed here by boat from the Holy Land after the Crucifixion, accompanied by their servant Sarah. The town's fortified Romanesque church, a landmark visible across the flat marshlands, houses the relics of the two Marys and the statue of Saint Sarah in its crypt.

Each May (24–25) the town hosts the great Romani pilgrimage (Pèlerinage des Gitans), when tens of thousands of Roma, Sinti and travellers gather: the statue of Sarah is carried into the sea in procession, as is the statue of the Marys on a boat. A second pilgrimage is held in October.

Geography and culture

The town sits amid the lagoons and salt marshes of the Rhône delta, country of white Camargue horses, black bulls and flamingos. Vincent van Gogh painted views of the town and its fishing boats during his stay in Provence in 1888.
