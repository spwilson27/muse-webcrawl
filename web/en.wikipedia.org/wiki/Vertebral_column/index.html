Vertebral column

The vertebral column, also known as the spinal column, spine, or backbone, is the core part of the axial skeleton in vertebrate animals. It is a flexible column made of a series of bones called vertebrae, separated by intervertebral discs. The vertebral column encloses and protects the spinal cord within the spinal canal and serves as the main support for the trunk, allowing posture and movement.

Structure

In adult humans the column normally consists of 33 vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 fused sacral vertebrae forming the sacrum, and 4 small fused coccygeal vertebrae forming the coccyx (tailbone). Every vertebra except the first two cervical (atlas and axis) has a drum-shaped body, a vertebral arch enclosing the vertebral foramen, and several processes for muscle attachment and articulation.

Between adjacent vertebral bodies lie intervertebral discs of fibrocartilage, each with a tough outer anulus fibrosus and a gel-like nucleus pulposus; these act as shock absorbers and permit limited movement at each joint, summing to considerable flexibility overall.

Curvatures

Viewed from the side, the adult human spine has four curvatures: the cervical and lumbar curves are lordotic (convex forward), while the thoracic and sacral curves are kyphotic (convex backward). These curves increase the spine's strength and resilience. Abnormal curvatures include scoliosis (lateral deviation), hyperkyphosis, and hyperlordosis.

Function

The vertebral column supports the head and trunk, transfers body weight to the pelvis and legs, protects the spinal cord and nerve roots, and provides attachment for ribs and the muscles of the back. Spinal nerves emerge through intervertebral foramina at each level, numbering 31 pairs in humans.

Clinical significance

Common disorders include herniated ("slipped") discs, spinal stenosis, vertebral compression fractures associated with osteoporosis, spondylolisthesis, and degenerative disc disease. Back pain attributed to spinal structures is among the most common causes of disability worldwide.
