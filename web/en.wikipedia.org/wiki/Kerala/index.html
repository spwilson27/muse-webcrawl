Kerala is a state on the Malabar Coast of southwestern India. Formed on 1 November 1956 under the States Reorganisation Act by combining Malayalam-speaking regions, it is bordered by Karnataka to the north, Tamil Nadu to the east, and the Arabian Sea to the west. It is known for its tropical landscapes, network of backwaters, high human development indicators, and a history of spice trade stretching back millennia.

Geography

Kerala is a narrow coastal strip wedged between the Western Ghats mountain range and the sea. Its geography descends from the high ranges — including Anamudi, South India's highest peak at 2,695 m — through midland hills and rubber and spice plantations to a coastal plain laced with 44 rivers, lagoons, and the famous Kerala backwaters, a chain of brackish lakes and canals centered on Vembanad Lake. The climate is tropical monsoon, with heavy rainfall from the southwest and northeast monsoons.

History

The region, ancient Chera country, traded spices, teak, and ivory with the Mediterranean world and the Middle East from antiquity; the port of Muziris is described in classical sources. Vasco da Gama's landing near Kozhikode in 1498 opened the European colonial era, followed successively by Portuguese, Dutch, and British dominance and the princely states of Travancore and Cochin. After Indian independence the state became a stronghold of democratic communist government: in 1957 Kerala elected one of the world's first democratically chosen communist ministries, under E. M. S. Namboodiripad, which pursued landmark land and education reforms.

Society and economy

Kerala leads India in literacy (about 96%), life expectancy, and sex ratio, with near-universal healthcare access — achievements often called the "Kerala model" despite historically modest income levels. Large-scale emigration to the Gulf states makes remittances a pillar of the economy, alongside tourism ("God's Own Country"), spices, rubber, fisheries, and information technology centered in Kochi and Thiruvananthapuram. Cultural signatures include the classical dance-drama Kathakali, the martial art Kalaripayattu, temple festivals such as Thrissur Pooram, snake-boat races, and the harvest festival Onam.
