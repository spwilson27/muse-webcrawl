The Bishop of Chelmsford is the ordinary of the Church of England Diocese of Chelmsford in the Province of Canterbury. The diocese covers the county of Essex and the east London boroughs of Barking and Dagenham, Havering, Newham, Redbridge and Waltham Forest, making it one of the largest and most populous Church of England dioceses.

History

The Diocese of Chelmsford was created in 1914, carved principally from the ancient Diocese of London along with territory of the short-lived Diocese of St Albans arrangements for Essex; Henry Watts was a suffragan bishop for the area before the first diocesan, John Watts Ditchfield, was appointed in 1914. The parish church of St Mary the Virgin, Chelmsford, became Chelmsford Cathedral. The bishop is assisted by area (suffragan) bishops of Barking, Bradwell and Colchester. The bishop's residence is Bishopscourt, Margaretting, near Chelmsford.

Notable holders of the see include John Robinson (1968–1973), an influential theologian and author of Honest to God, and Stephen Cottrell (2010–2020), who left Chelmsford to become Archbishop of York. Guli Francis-Dehqani, previously Bishop of Loughborough, became Bishop of Chelmsford in 2021 — among the first bishops of Iranian heritage in the Church of England.
