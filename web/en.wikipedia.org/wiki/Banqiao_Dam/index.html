The Banqiao Dam (Chinese: 板桥水库大坝) is a dam on the Ru River near Zhumadian in Henan Province, China. Its catastrophic failure during Typhoon Nina in August 1975, which triggered the collapse of dozens of other dams in a cascade, caused one of the deadliest floods in history and is often ranked among the worst technological disasters ever. The Chinese government kept details of the disaster largely secret for decades; it was declassified in 2005.

Construction

The dam was built in 1951–52 with Soviet advice during the "Harness the Huai River" campaign, promoted with the slogan of resisting a once-in-a-thousand-years flood. Hydrologist Chen Xing warned that its spillway capacity and the wider plan of building too many reservoirs were unsafe; his criticisms were suppressed and he was sidelined. Cracks and damage appearing soon after construction were repaired under Soviet guidance, earning the dam the propaganda nickname the "iron dam".

The 1975 disaster

In early August 1975, Typhoon Nina stalled over Henan and produced extraordinary rainfall — in places over 1,000 mm in days, with records of 1,060 mm in 24 hours, far beyond the design standard. Communications failed, evacuation orders were delayed or never sent, and requests to open the sluices came too late. Shortly after midnight on 8 August the Banqiao Dam breached, releasing a wall of water reported as some 10 metres high and kilometres wide that swept through the plain and caused 61 other dams, including the large Shimantan Dam, to fail in sequence. Whole towns were destroyed; roughly 11 million people were affected and over a million hectares flooded. Official estimates later put immediate drowning deaths at about 26,000, with total deaths including subsequent epidemics and famine commonly estimated around 171,000 or higher.

Aftermath

The dam was rebuilt between 1986 and 1993 to higher standards. The disaster prompted lasting reassessments of Chinese dam safety and hydrological design standards, though it was little reported at the time because of the political climate and the Cultural Revolution.
