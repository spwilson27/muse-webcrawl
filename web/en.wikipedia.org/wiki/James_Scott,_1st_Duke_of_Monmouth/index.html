James Scott, 1st Duke of Monmouth, 1st Duke of Buccleuch (9 April 1649 – 15 July 1685) was an English nobleman and soldier, the eldest illegitimate son of Charles II of England and his mistress Lucy Walter. A popular Protestant figure, he was advanced by Whig politicians during the Exclusion Crisis as an alternative heir to his Catholic uncle, James, Duke of York. In 1685 he led the Monmouth Rebellion against James II, was defeated at the Battle of Sedgemoor, and was executed for treason.

Early life

Born in Rotterdam during his father's exile, James was brought to England after the Restoration and taken into the king's care. In 1663 he was created Duke of Monmouth and married Anne Scott, Countess of Buccleuch, taking the surname Scott. Handsome and personable, he became a favourite at court and won military distinction commanding English forces in the Third Anglo-Dutch War and alongside the French in Flanders; in 1679 he defeated the Scottish Covenanters at the Battle of Bothwell Bridge.

Exclusion Crisis and exile

As fears of a Catholic succession grew, the Whigs promoted Monmouth — claiming, without evidence, that Charles had secretly married Lucy Walter — as the Protestant heir. Charles repeatedly denied the marriage and, after the discovery of the Rye House Plot in 1683, Monmouth fled to the Dutch Republic. Charles died in February 1685 and the Duke of York succeeded as James II.

Rebellion and execution

Encouraged by exiles, Monmouth landed at Lyme Regis on 11 June 1685 with a small force and was proclaimed king at Taunton, drawing support from West Country artisans and Nonconformists. His rising was coordinated with a rebellion in Scotland under the Earl of Argyll, which also failed. On the night of 5–6 July his army was routed at the Battle of Sedgemoor, the last pitched battle on English soil. Monmouth was captured hiding in a ditch in Hampshire, taken to London, and beheaded on Tower Hill on 15 July 1685; the executioner, Jack Ketch, notoriously required several blows. The reprisals that followed, conducted by Judge Jeffreys, became known as the Bloody Assizes.
