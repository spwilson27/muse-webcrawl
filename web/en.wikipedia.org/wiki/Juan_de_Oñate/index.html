Juan de Oñate

Born1550, Pánuco, Zacatecas, New Spain
Died3 June 1626, Guadalcanal, Spain
OccupationConquistador, explorer, colonial governor

Juan de Oñate y Salazar (1550–1626) was a Spanish conquistador and explorer from New Spain who established the colony of Santa Fe de Nuevo México in what is now the southwestern United States. He is a controversial figure, remembered both as the founder of New Mexico and for his brutal punishment of the Acoma Pueblo people.

Expedition to New Mexico

In 1595 Oñate received a contract from King Philip II to colonize the upper Rio Grande valley. His expedition crossed the Rio Grande at El Paso in 1598, where he claimed the territory for Spain, and established the first Spanish capital near present-day Ohkay Owingeh.

Acoma massacre

After Acoma warriors killed a Spanish party, Oñate ordered a reprisal in 1599 in which hundreds of Acoma people were killed and survivors were sentenced to servitude and mutilation. The severity of the punishment later brought him to trial.

Trial and later life

Oñate was recalled to Mexico City, tried for cruelty and other charges, convicted, fined and banished from New Mexico. He returned to Spain, where he died in 1626. His reputation remains deeply contested in New Mexico, where statues of him have been the subject of protests.
