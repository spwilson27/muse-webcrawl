Pegasus (constellation)

Pegasus is a constellation in the northern sky, named after the winged horse Pegasus of Greek mythology. It was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. It is the seventh-largest constellation, covering 1,121 square degrees.

Notable features

The constellation's most recognisable asterism is the Great Square of Pegasus, formed by the stars Markab (Alpha Pegasi), Scheat (Beta Pegasi), Algenib (Gamma Pegasi), and Alpheratz (Alpha Andromedae), which is shared with neighbouring Andromeda. The brightest star in Pegasus is Epsilon Pegasi, called Enif, an orange supergiant near the horse's muzzle.

Pegasus contains 51 Pegasi, the first Sun-like star found to host an exoplanet (51 Pegasi b, discovered in 1995 by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz), and the spiral galaxy NGC 7331, sometimes considered a twin of the Milky Way. The compact galaxy group Stephan's Quintet lies within its borders.

Mythology

In Greek myth, Pegasus was the winged horse born from the blood of Medusa when she was beheaded by Perseus. The hero Bellerophon rode Pegasus in defeating the Chimera, but when Bellerophon attempted to fly to Mount Olympus, Zeus caused him to fall, and Pegasus was placed among the stars.
