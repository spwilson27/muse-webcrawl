Textile industry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The textile industry is primarily concerned with the design, production and distribution of textiles: yarn, cloth and clothing. Raw materials may be natural fibres (cotton, wool, silk, flax) or synthetic (polyester, nylon, acrylic) produced by the chemical industry. It is one of the oldest and most geographically widespread of all industries, and its mechanisation in 18th-century Britain was the leading sector of the first Industrial Revolution.

Processes

Production typically proceeds through fibre preparation, spinning (converting fibres into yarn), weaving or knitting into fabric, and finishing processes such as dyeing, printing, bleaching and coating, before garment manufacture ("cut, make, trim"). The value chain extends to distribution and retail, and increasingly to textile recycling.

History

Hand production of textiles was a major household and guild activity for millennia. In Britain, a cascade of inventions — the flying shuttle (1733), spinning jenny (1764), water frame, spinning mule and power loom — concentrated cotton manufacture in steam-powered mills in Lancashire and elsewhere, a "factory system" later replicated in New England, continental Europe and Japan. Synthetic fibres arrived with rayon in the late 19th century and nylon (1935) and polyester in the 20th century.

Modern industry

Since the late 20th century, labour-intensive garment production has shifted strongly to lower-wage countries: China is the world's largest textile and clothing exporter, followed by producers including Bangladesh, Vietnam, India, Turkey and Pakistan. The abolition of the Multi Fibre Arrangement quotas in 2005 accelerated this shift. The industry is a pillar of export earnings and female employment in many developing economies, but has been criticised for low wages and unsafe conditions (highlighted by the 2013 Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh, which killed over 1,100 garment workers) and for environmental impacts: water use, dye pollution, microfibre release and the waste generated by "fast fashion". Technical textiles — for medical, automotive, geotechnical and protective uses — are a growing high-value segment.
