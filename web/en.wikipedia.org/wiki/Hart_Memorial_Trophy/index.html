Hart Memorial Trophy

The Hart Memorial Trophy, originally known as the Hart Trophy, is an annual award given to the player judged to be the most valuable to his team in the National Hockey League (NHL). It is voted on by members of the Professional Hockey Writers' Association at the end of the regular season.

History

The trophy was donated in 1923 by Dr. David Hart, father of Cecil Hart, the longtime coach and manager of the Montreal Canadiens. The original trophy was retired to the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1960, and the NHL began presenting the current Hart Memorial Trophy in its place. The first winner was Frank Nighbor of the Ottawa Senators in the 1923–24 season.

Notable winners

Wayne Gretzky won the award a record nine times, including eight consecutively with the Edmonton Oilers. Gordie Howe won it six times, and Eddie Shore, a defenceman, won it four times — the most by a player at that position. Goaltenders have rarely won; José Théodore, Carey Price and Dominik Hašek are among the few to do so.

Voting

Each voter selects five candidates on a 10-7-5-3-1 points basis. The three finalists are announced before the winner is revealed at the NHL Awards ceremony.
