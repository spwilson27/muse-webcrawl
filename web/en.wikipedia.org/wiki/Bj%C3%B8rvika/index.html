Bjørvika

CityOslo, Norway
DistrictSentrum
Known forOslo Opera House; Barcode Project; MUNCH museum

Bjørvika is a bay and neighbourhood in the Sentrum district of Oslo, Norway, at the innermost part of the Oslofjord, east of the Akershus Fortress. Historically the site of Oslo's harbour, it has been transformed since the 2000s into a new cultural and commercial waterfront district.

History

Bjørvika was the original harbour of medieval Oslo and later part of the industrial port, dominated by container terminals, motorways, and railway lines. The Fjord City (Fjordbyen) redevelopment programme moved the port and road traffic — the E18 was placed in the Bjørvika Tunnel under the bay — freeing the waterfront for public use.

Landmarks

The district is home to the Oslo Opera House (opened 2008), whose sloping marble roof the public can walk on; the Barcode Project, a row of narrow high-rise buildings; the new MUNCH museum (opened 2021) devoted to Edvard Munch; and the Deichman Bjørvika main public library (opened 2020). Sørenga, to the east, adds housing and a seawater swimming area.
