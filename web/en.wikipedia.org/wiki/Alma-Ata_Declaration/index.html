Alma-Ata Declaration

The Declaration of Alma-Ata was adopted at the International Conference on Primary Health Care held in Alma-Ata (now Almaty), Kazakh SSR, USSR, on 12 September 1978, jointly sponsored by the World Health Organization and UNICEF. It is a landmark statement in public health.

Content

The declaration defined health as "a state of complete physical, mental and social wellbeing, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity", reaffirmed it as a fundamental human right, and identified primary health care as the key to achieving the goal of "Health for All by the year 2000". It called on governments to make primary care universally accessible, affordable and community-based, addressing nutrition, safe water, sanitation, maternal and child health, immunisation and essential drugs.

Legacy

The declaration shaped global health policy for decades, inspiring community health worker programmes worldwide. Forty years later, the 2018 Declaration of Astana renewed its commitments in support of universal health coverage.
