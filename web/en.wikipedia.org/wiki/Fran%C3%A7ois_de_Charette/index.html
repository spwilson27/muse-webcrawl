François Athanase de Charette de la Contrie (2 May 1763 – 29 March 1796) was a French Royalist soldier and politician, and one of the principal leaders of the War in the Vendée, the counter-revolutionary uprising in western France.

Career

A former naval officer who had fought in the American Revolutionary War, Charette took command of peasant insurgents in the Marais breton in 1793. His elusive guerrilla tactics made him the most persistent of the Vendéan generals, earning him the nickname "King of the Vendée".

Death

After intermittent truces and renewed fighting in support of the Quiberon expedition, Charette was hunted down, captured near Saint-Sulpice-le-Verdon, and shot at Nantes on 29 March 1796, effectively ending the revolt.
