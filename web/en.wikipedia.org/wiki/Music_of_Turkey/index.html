Music of Turkey

The music of Turkey comprises Ottoman classical music, rich folk traditions, and vibrant popular genres, shaped by the country's position between Europe and Asia and its Turkic, Persian, Arab, Balkan, and Byzantine inheritances.

Classical music

Turkish classical music (Türk sanat müziği) developed at the Ottoman court, built on the makam modal system and rhythmic cycles called usul. Composers such as Itri (Buhurizade Mustafa Efendi) in the 17th century and Hammamizade İsmail Dede Efendi in the 19th are canonical. The Mevlevi Sufi order's ceremonial ayin music accompanies the whirling dervishes, with the ney flute and kudüm drums prominent.

Folk music

Turkish folk music (Türk halk müziği) varies by region, with instruments led by the bağlama (saz), kaval flute, kabak kemane, davul and zurna, and tulum bagpipe in the Black Sea region. The âşık (minstrel) tradition produced figures such as Karacaoğlan and Âşık Veysel. Folk dances include the zeybek of the Aegean, halay of the east, and horon of the Black Sea coast.

Popular music

In the republican era, arabesk music—exemplified by Orhan Gencebay and Müslüm Gürses—blended Arabic influences with Turkish song. Turkish pop developed from the 1960s with stars like Ajda Pekkan, Sezen Aksu, and Tarkan, whose 1997 hit "Şımarık" brought global attention. Anadolu rock fused rock with folk (Barış Manço, Cem Karaca), and Turkey has active rap, electronic, and indie scenes.
