Lough Neagh

Lough Neagh

Location: Northern Ireland

Surface area: 392 km² (151 sq mi)

Mean depth: 8.9 m (29 ft)

Primary outflow: River Bann

Lough Neagh (from Irish Loch nEachach, meaning "Eachaidh's lake") is a freshwater lake in Northern Ireland and is the largest lake on the island of Ireland and in the British Isles, with a surface area of 392 square kilometres (151 square miles). It is also one of the largest lakes in Western Europe. It supplies 40.7% of Northern Ireland's drinking water.

Geography

Five of the six counties of Northern Ireland have shores on the Lough: Antrim, Down, Armagh, Tyrone and Londonderry. Towns near the shore include Antrim, Toomebridge, Crumlin, Randalstown and Magherafelt. The River Bann flows out of the lake at its northern end to the Atlantic Ocean. Several islands lie in the lough, including Coney Island and Ram's Island with its round tower.

Ecology

The lough is famous for its eel fishery, once the largest wild eel fishery in Europe, and for the Lough Neagh fly. It is an important site for wildfowl. In recent years the lake has suffered severe environmental problems from nutrient pollution, with toxic blue-green algal (cyanobacterial) blooms becoming a major crisis in 2023–2024, prompting public debate and government action plans.

Mythology

In Irish mythology, the lake was said to have formed when the hero Fionn mac Cumhaill scooped up a clod of earth to throw at a Scottish rival; the clod became the Isle of Man and the hole filled with water to become Lough Neagh. It is named for Eachaidh, an ancient god or horse deity.
