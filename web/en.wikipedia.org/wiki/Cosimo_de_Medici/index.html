Cosimo di Giovanni de' Medici (27 September 1389 – 1 August 1464), known as Cosimo the Elder (Cosimo il Vecchio), was an Italian banker and politician who established the Medici family as the effective rulers of Florence during much of the Italian Renaissance. Although he never held formal office as ruler, his wealth — built on the Medici Bank founded by his father Giovanni di Bicci — and his network of clients let him dominate the Florentine Republic for thirty years.

Rise to power

Cosimo inherited the bank in 1429 and expanded its branches across Europe, including the branch serving the papal court. His rivalry with the Albizzi family culminated in his arrest and exile to Venice in 1433; recalled after a year when the Albizzi lost power, he returned in 1434 and engineered a regime that preserved republican forms while ensuring Medici loyalists controlled the councils.

Rule and diplomacy

Cosimo supported Francesco Sforza's seizure of Milan and brokered the Peace of Lodi (1454), stabilising Italy among Florence, Milan, Venice and Naples for a generation. He reorganised Florence's tax system, favoured his family's clients, and governed through a small circle of trusted associates, avoiding overt tyranny.

Patronage

Cosimo was one of the great patrons of the early Renaissance. He commissioned Michelozzo to build the Palazzo Medici and the rebuilt convent of San Marco, funded Brunelleschi's completion of Florence Cathedral's dome and the church of San Lorenzo, and supported Donatello, Fra Angelico and Fra Filippo Lippi. He paid for the first public library in Renaissance Europe at San Marco and sponsored Marsilio Ficino's translations of Plato, laying the groundwork for the Florentine Platonic Academy. After his death Florence honoured him with the title Pater Patriae ("Father of the Fatherland").
