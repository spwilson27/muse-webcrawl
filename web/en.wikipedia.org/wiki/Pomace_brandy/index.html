Pomace brandy

Pomace brandy is a liquor distilled from pomace — the solid remains (skins, pulp, seeds, and stems) left after grapes are pressed for wine. Traditionally a rough, farmhouse spirit, pomace brandy is produced in most wine-growing countries and today ranges from harsh, unaged spirits to refined, aromatic, and sometimes barrel-aged products. It is typically bottled at 40–60% alcohol by volume and drunk as a digestif.

Production

After pressing, the pomace retains fermentable sugars and aromatic compounds. It may be fermented further with water and yeast, or distilled directly if it already contains wine or partially fermented juice. Distillation is traditionally performed in copper pot stills (alembics), often in small batches by winegrowers using their own marc. Because the pomace is poor in sugar relative to wine, yields are low. The spirit may be bottled unaged and clear, rested in stainless steel, or aged in wood, which imparts colour and softer flavours.

Regional varieties

  • Grappa (Italy) — protected designation; made from the pomace of Italian-grown grapes, with single-varietal and aged expressions.
  • Marc (France) — e.g. Marc de Bourgogne and Marc de Champagne, frequently oak-aged.
  • Orujo (Spain) — especially from Galicia, sometimes infused with herbs or coffee.
  • Bagaceira (Portugal), Trester (Germany), Törkölypálinka (Hungary), Zivania (Cyprus), Tsipouro and Tsikoudia (Greece), Rakia (Balkans), Singani (Bolivia), and Pisco (Peru and Chile — though pisco is distilled from wine rather than fermented pomace and is classified separately in some definitions).

History

Pomace distillation arose as a way for peasant winemakers to extract value from a waste product, and production was often taxed or restricted by states that reserved distillation rights. In Italy, grappa was for centuries the drink of the poor of the Alpine and northern regions before being elevated in the 20th century by producers such as Nonino, who introduced single-varietal grappas in elegant bottles. European Union regulations now define "grape marc spirit" or "grape pomace spirit" as a protected category of spirit drink.
