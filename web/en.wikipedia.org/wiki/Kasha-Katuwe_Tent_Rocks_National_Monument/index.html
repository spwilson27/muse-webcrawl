Kasha-Katuwe Tent Rocks National Monument is a United States national monument in northern New Mexico, on the Pajarito Plateau southwest of Santa Fe. It is known for its cone-shaped "tent rock" formations, produced by erosion of volcanic ash and tuff deposits from eruptions of the Jemez volcanic field some 6–7 million years ago. The name Kasha-Katuwe means "white cliffs" in the Keresan language of the Pueblo de Cochiti.

Geology

The tent rocks are hoodoos of pumice and tuff capped by harder boulders that shield the softer material beneath from erosion, producing steep cones up to about 90 feet (27 m) tall. Layers of volcanic debris from pyroclastic flows vary in hardness and colour, giving the formations banded patterns.

Trails

Visitors can walk the Canyon Trail through a narrow slot canyon up to a mesa-top overlook of the Rio Grande valley and the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, and the gentler Cave Loop Trail past a small cave dwelling. The monument is administered by the Bureau of Land Management in cooperation with Cochiti Pueblo, on whose land access roads lie.
