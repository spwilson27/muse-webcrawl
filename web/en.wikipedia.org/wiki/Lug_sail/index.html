Lug sail

A lug sail, or lugsail, is a fore-and-aft, four-sided sail that is suspended from a yard (the "lug yard") which crosses the mast at an angle. Unlike a gaff sail, whose throat is at the mast, the lug yard extends forward of the mast, so the sail sets partly ahead of it. The lug rig was widely used in traditional working boats around the coasts of Britain and France and remains popular in small sailing dinghies.

Types

  • Standing lug: the tack of the sail is fixed near the foot of the mast and the yard is not lowered when tacking. The yard rests against the mast on one tack, giving a "good" and a "bad" tack.
  • Balance lug: the yard is permanently attached and crosses the mast low down, with part of the sail forward of the mast acting as a counterbalance; the boom extends aft. Used in boats such as the Chinese junk (in fully battened form) and many modern dinghies.
  • Dipping lug: common in the Scottish and Cornish fishing fleets; the yard is lowered and carried around to the leeward side of the mast at each tack, so the sail always sets clear of the mast. This gives excellent performance but demands more work from the crew.

Characteristics

The lug rig needs only a short, unstayed mast, is simple and cheap to make, and the sail can be struck quickly by dropping the yard — advantages that made it a favourite for open fishing boats such as the Cornish lugger and the cobles of the North Sea coast. Modern designers have revived the balance lug for small cruising dinghies because of its ease of handling and reefing.
