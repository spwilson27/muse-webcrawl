Automatic direction finder

An automatic direction finder (ADF) is an aircraft radio-navigation instrument that automatically and continuously displays the relative bearing from the aircraft to a suitable radio station. Aircraft ADFs use non-directional beacons (NDBs) operating in the low-frequency and medium-frequency bands, and can also be tuned to commercial AM broadcast stations.

Operation

The ADF receiver uses a combination of a loop antenna and a sense antenna to determine the direction from which a signal arrives. A needle on the instrument's display points toward the station relative to the aircraft's nose, allowing the pilot to home in on the beacon or to use bearings from two or more stations to fix a position. Early direction finders required the operator to rotate a loop antenna manually and listen for a signal null; the automatic direction finder automated this process.

Use in navigation

ADF navigation was once widespread in general aviation and commercial operations, particularly for non-precision approaches. The system is subject to errors including night effect, coastal refraction, terrain effect and interference from thunderstorms, which attract the needle toward lightning discharges. With the spread of GPS and the decommissioning of many NDBs, ADF use has declined, though the equipment remains fitted to many aircraft.
