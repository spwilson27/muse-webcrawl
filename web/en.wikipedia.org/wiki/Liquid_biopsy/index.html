Liquid biopsy

A liquid biopsy is the sampling and analysis of non-solid biological tissue, primarily blood, to detect and monitor disease — above all cancer. It is a non-invasive alternative or complement to surgical tissue biopsy, and can be repeated over time to follow a disease's course.

Analytes

Liquid biopsies analyse circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) shed by tumours into the bloodstream, circulating tumour cells (CTCs), extracellular vesicles such as exosomes, and other molecules including cell-free RNA and proteins. Tests range from single-mutation assays (for example, detecting EGFR mutations in lung cancer to guide targeted therapy) to multi-cancer early detection tests that screen methylation patterns in cell-free DNA.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages include minimal invasiveness, speed, and the ability to capture genetic heterogeneity across metastatic sites and to detect minimal residual disease or emerging drug resistance. Limitations include low sensitivity in early-stage disease, where ctDNA is scarce, and false positives from clonal haematopoiesis. Regulatory-approved tests include companion diagnostics for therapy selection and blood-based colorectal cancer screening assays.
