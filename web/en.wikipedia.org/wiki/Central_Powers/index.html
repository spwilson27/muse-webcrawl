Central Powers

The Central Powers, also known as the Central Empires, were one of the two main coalitions in the First World War (1914–1918), centred on the German Empire and Austria-Hungary, whose alliance — the "central" states of Europe between the Allied powers of Russia to the east and France and Britain to the west — gave the bloc its name. They were joined by the Ottoman Empire (October–November 1914) and the Kingdom of Bulgaria (October 1915). The Central Powers fought the Allied Powers and were defeated in November 1918.

Members

The core alliance rested on the Dual Alliance of 1879 between Germany and Austria-Hungary, extended (on paper) by Italy in the Triple Alliance of 1882, though Italy remained neutral in 1914 and joined the Allies in 1915. Smaller co-belligerents and client states at various times included the Senussi of Libya, the Sultanate of Darfur, and the short-lived Caucasian and Polish puppet governments of 1918.

War and defeat

Blockaded by the Royal Navy and outmatched in manpower and industry, the Central Powers won major victories in the east (forcing Russia out of the war at Brest-Litovsk in March 1918) but failed to break the Western Front. The collapse of Bulgaria (September 1918), the Ottoman armistice of Mudros (30 October), Austria-Hungary's armistice of Villa Giusti (3 November) and finally the German armistice of 11 November 1918 ended the war; all four empires of the bloc were dissolved or dismembered in the peace settlements that followed.
