Victor Babeș

Born28 July 1854, Vienna, Austrian Empire
Died19 October 1926, Bucharest, Romania
FieldsBacteriology, pathology, microbiology
Known forBabesiosis; early bacteriology textbook; rabies treatment research

Victor Babeș (28 July 1854 – 19 October 1926) was a Romanian physician, bacteriologist, academician, and professor. One of the founders of modern microbiology, Babeș is the author of one of the first treatises on bacteriology in the world — Les bactéries et leur rôle dans l'anatomie et l'histologie pathologiques des maladies infectieuses (1885), written together with French scientist André Victor Cornil.

Born in Vienna to a Romanian family from the Banat, Babeș studied medicine in Budapest and Vienna, then worked in Budapest before accepting a professorship at the University of Bucharest in 1887. In Romania he founded an institute of pathology and bacteriology that bears his name to this day.

Work

In 1888 Babeș discovered a group of intracellular parasites in the red blood cells of cattle suffering from a form of haemoglobinuria, organisms of the genus later named Babesia in his honor; the disease they cause is babesiosis. He also identified the small clustered inclusions in rabies-infected nerve cells known as Negri bodies independently (they are also called Babeș–Negri bodies in Romanian literature), worked on the bacteriology of tuberculosis, leprosy, diphtheria, and cholera, and introduced innovative methods in rabies vaccination in Romania. He described metachromatic granules in bacteria (Babeș–Ernst granules) and made early observations relating to what are now recognized as aspects of the immune response.

The Victor Babeș University of Medicine and Pharmacy in Timișoara is named after him, as is the national institute of pathology in Bucharest. Babeș is regarded, alongside figures such as Pasteur and Koch, as a pioneer of bacteriology, and he remains one of the most celebrated scientists in Romanian history.
