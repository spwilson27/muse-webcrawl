Forgery

Forgery is the making of a false document, signature, work of art, or other object with the intent that it be accepted as genuine, usually to deceive and to gain financially or otherwise. As a legal offence, forgery generally requires both the false making and an intent to defraud.

Types

Common forms include forging signatures and identity documents, counterfeiting currency (sometimes treated as a distinct offence), falsifying wills, contracts, and historical documents, and producing fake artworks or antiquities. Famous cases range from the Donation of Constantine, a forged imperial decree exposed by Lorenzo Valla in the fifteenth century, to the twentieth-century art forger Han van Meegeren, whose fake Vermeers deceived leading experts, and the "Hitler Diaries" hoax of 1983.

Detection

Forensic document examination analyzes handwriting, ink, and paper; art authentication uses provenance research, connoisseurship, and technical imaging and materials analysis. The increasing sophistication of digital reproduction has made forgery both easier to attempt and, through digital forensics, easier to trace.

Legal treatment

Forgery is criminalized in virtually all jurisdictions; penalties vary with the nature of the document and the loss caused. In England and Wales the main statute is the Forgery and Counterfeiting Act 1981; in the United States forgery is punished under both state laws and federal statutes covering specific instruments.
