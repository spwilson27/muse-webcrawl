The Party of European Socialists (PES) is a social-democratic European political party that brings together socialist, social-democratic, and labour parties from across the European Union and beyond. Its member parties sit together in the European Parliament as the Progressive Alliance of Socialists and Democrats (S&D), historically the second-largest group in that chamber.

History

The PES was founded in 1992, succeeding the Confederation of Socialist Parties of the European Community, which had coordinated national parties since 1974, ahead of the first direct elections to the European Parliament. The Maastricht Treaty era formalised the role of European parties, and the PES adopted its present structure as a federation of full member parties from EU states plus associate and observer parties from neighbouring countries.

Organisation and programme

The party is led by a president and a secretary general, with a congress of member parties adopting a common manifesto before each European election and nominating a lead candidate (Spitzenkandidat) for President of the European Commission. Its programme emphasises a social market economy, workers' rights, gender equality, public services, climate action under a "Social Green Deal" framing, and defence of the rule of law within the Union. Associated organisations include PES Women, the Young European Socialists (YES), and the Rainbow Rose network for LGBTI rights.

Member parties

Full members include, among others, the German SPD, the Spanish PSOE, the French Socialist Party, Italy's Democratic Party, Portugal's Socialist Party, the Romanian PSD, and the Scandinavian social democratic parties. The British Labour Party was a member until Brexit. In government, PES parties have at various times led coalitions in many member states, and the party coordinates its heads of government and ministers in the European Council and Council formations before EU summits.
