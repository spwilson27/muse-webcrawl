United States European Command

EUCOM
Founded: 1 August 1952
Headquarters: Patch Barracks, Stuttgart, Germany
Role: Geographic combatant command

The United States European Command (EUCOM) is one of the eleven U.S. Armed Forces combatant commands, headquartered at Patch Barracks in Stuttgart, Germany. Its area of focus covers 51 countries and territories across Europe, Russia, and parts of the Caucasus and the Levant.

History

EUCOM was established on 1 August 1952 to provide unified command of U.S. forces in Europe during the Cold War. Its commander traditionally also serves as NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR), reflecting the command's close integration with the Atlantic alliance.

Mission

The command deters conflict, supports NATO, builds partner capacity, and, with the U.S. Africa Command (which was split off in 2007), coordinates American military activity across the region. Components include U.S. Army Europe and Africa, U.S. Naval Forces Europe-Africa, U.S. Air Forces in Europe – Air Forces Africa, and U.S. Marine Corps Forces Europe and Africa.

Operations

EUCOM supported operations in the Balkans during the 1990s, including NATO interventions in Bosnia and Kosovo, and has coordinated support for allies following Russia's invasions of Ukraine in 2014 and 2022.
