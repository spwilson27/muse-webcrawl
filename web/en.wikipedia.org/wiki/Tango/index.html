Tango

Tango is a partner dance and musical genre that originated in the working-class port neighbourhoods of Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Montevideo, Uruguay, in the late 19th century. It blends European immigrant music, African rhythms brought by enslaved and free Black communities, and the local milonga and habanera traditions of the Río de la Plata.

History

Tango first emerged in the brothels and courtyards of the port districts, was carried to Paris in the 1910s, and from there conquered Europe and North America, returning home newly respectable. Its golden age (roughly 1935–1955) saw great orchestras led by Juan D'Arienzo, Carlos Di Sarli, Aníbal Troilo and Osvaldo Pugliese, and the songs of Carlos Gardel became emblematic of the genre. Astor Piazzolla later transformed tango music into the concert form known as nuevo tango.

The dance and music

Tango is danced in an embrace, improvised around a walking step with figures such as the ocho, giro and volcada, at social dances called milongas governed by codes of etiquette (códigos). The classic tango orchestra features bandoneóns — the genre's signature instrument — violins, piano and double bass, in 4/4 time.

Recognition

In 2009 UNESCO inscribed tango on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, jointly for Argentina and Uruguay.
