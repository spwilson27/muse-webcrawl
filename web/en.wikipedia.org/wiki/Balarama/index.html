Balarama

Balarama with his characteristic plough (hala)

Balarama (Sanskrit: बलराम, Balrāma) is a Hindu god and the elder brother of Krishna. He is particularly significant in the Jagannath tradition, and in some Vaishnava traditions he is listed among the ten principal avatars of Vishnu, while others regard him as an incarnation of Shesha, the serpent on whom Vishnu rests. He is also known as Baladeva, Balabhadra, Haladhara ("plough-bearer") and Halayudha.

Mythology

According to the Mahabharata, Bhagavata Purana and other texts, Balarama was the seventh child of Vasudeva and Devaki. To protect him from the tyrant Kamsa, he was transferred from Devaki's womb to that of Rohini, Vasudeva's other wife, and raised in Gokula alongside Krishna by Nanda and Yashoda. His characteristic weapons are the plough (hala) and the pestle (musala), reflecting his association with agriculture, fertility and farmers. Tales describe his great physical strength, his fondness for wine, his marriage to Revati, and his reluctant neutrality in the Kurukshetra War — he refused to fight for either side and went on pilgrimage instead. He is said to have taught mace-fighting to both Duryodhana and Bhima.

Worship

Balarama is worshipped together with Krishna and Subhadra in the Jagannath Temple at Puri, and in many Krishna temples across India. He is also revered in Jainism as one of the nine Balabhadras, and appears in Buddhist texts. Festivals such as Balarama Jayanti mark his birth.
