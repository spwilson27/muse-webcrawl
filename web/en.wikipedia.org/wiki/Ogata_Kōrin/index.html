Ogata Kōrin

Born: 1658, Kyoto, Japan

Died: June 2, 1716, Kyoto

Nationality: Japanese

Known for: Painting, lacquerware, textile design

Movement: Rinpa school

Ogata Kōrin (Japanese: 尾形 光琳; 1658 – June 2, 1716) was a Japanese painter, lacquerer, and designer of the Rinpa school. He is one of the most celebrated artists of the Edo period, renowned for bold, highly stylized compositions on gold-leaf screens and for designs that influenced Japanese decorative art for centuries, as well as European Art Nouveau through Japonisme.

Life

Kōrin was born in Kyoto to a wealthy merchant family, the Kariganeya, which supplied fine textiles to the imperial court and aristocracy. He studied painting in the Kanō school tradition and absorbed the legacy of Hon'ami Kōetsu and Tawaraya Sōtatsu, the founders of the Rinpa style. After squandering much of his inheritance, he turned seriously to art in his forties, receiving commissions from the imperial court and great feudal lords; in 1701 he was granted the honorary title Hokkyō. His younger brother Ogata Kenzan became a celebrated potter, and the two collaborated on decorated ceramics.

Works

Kōrin's best-known paintings include the pair of screens Red and White Plum Blossoms (c. 1710–1716, MOA Museum of Art), in which a stylized stream flows between two flowering plum trees rendered in tarashikomi (puddled ink) technique, and Irises at Yatsuhashi (Eight Bridges) (Metropolitan Museum of Art), adapted from The Tales of Ise. His Wind God and Thunder God screens copy and reinterpret Sōtatsu's famous composition. Kōrin also designed lacquerware, including writing boxes, and patterns for kimono, some in collaboration with Kenzan.

Legacy

The very name "Rinpa" derives from the final syllable of Kōrin's name (rin) plus "school" (pa). His flattening of natural forms into pattern, his use of negative space, and his daring asymmetry profoundly shaped Japanese design, and reproductions of his work circulated widely in the West in the late nineteenth century.
