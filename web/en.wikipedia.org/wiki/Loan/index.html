Loan

In finance, a loan is the lending of money by one or more individuals, organisations or other entities to other individuals or organisations. The recipient (the borrower) incurs a debt and is usually required to pay interest on that debt until it is repaid, as well as to repay the principal amount borrowed. The document evidencing the debt, such as a promissory note, normally specifies the principal, the interest rate, and the date of repayment.

Types of loans

  • Secured loan — backed by collateral (e.g. a mortgage secured on property, or an auto loan); the lender may seize the asset on default.
  • Unsecured loan — granted on the borrower's creditworthiness alone (e.g. credit cards, personal loans, overdrafts); interest rates are higher.
  • Demand loan — short-term and repayable on the lender's demand.
  • Term loan — repaid in regular instalments over a fixed period; an amortising loan pays principal and interest together.
  • Subsidised and concessional loans — made on below-market terms, e.g. student loans or development lending.
  • Syndicated loan — provided by a group of lenders to a large borrower.
  • Payday loan — small, short-term, high-cost unsecured lending.

Interest and repayment

Interest compensates the lender for the time value of money, inflation and the risk of default, and may be charged at a fixed or variable (floating) rate. Repayment schedules may be interest-only with a final "balloon" payment of principal, or amortising, in which each payment covers interest and part of the principal so the balance falls to zero at term. Default — failure to meet the legal obligations of the loan — can result in foreclosure, repossession, legal action and damage to the borrower's credit rating.

Regulation

Lending is regulated to protect consumers: many jurisdictions require standardised disclosure of the cost of credit (such as the annual percentage rate, APR), cap interest rates under usury laws, and license lenders. Loans are a core mechanism by which banks create money: when a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit in the borrower's account.
