The Bibliotheca Alexandrina (Latin for "Library of Alexandria") is a major library and cultural centre on the shore of the Mediterranean Sea in Alexandria, Egypt. It is a commemoration of the ancient Library of Alexandria, which was lost in antiquity, as well as an attempt to rekindle something of the brilliance that the original centre of study and erudition represented.

History

The idea of reviving the old library dates to 1974, championed by Mostafa El-Abbadi of the University of Alexandria. The project was adopted by UNESCO, which ran an international architectural competition won by the Norwegian firm Snøhetta in 1989. Construction began in 1995 and the library was inaugurated on 16 October 2002 in the presence of heads of state. The main building is a vast cylinder tilted toward the sea, its granite Aswan wall carved with characters from 120 human writing systems.

Collections and facilities

The main reading room spans seven cascading levels and 14 levels in total, with space for millions of volumes. The complex includes specialised libraries (maps, manuscripts, children's, young people's, and the Taha Hussein library for the visually impaired), four museums, a planetarium, conference centres, and a copy of the Internet Archive. It hosts the only mirror site of the Internet Archive outside the United States, storing snapshots of web pages since 1996.

The ancient library

The ancient Great Library of Alexandria, part of the Mouseion research institution founded under the Ptolemies in the 3rd century BCE, was the most famous library of the classical world. It declined over centuries through fires, purges and neglect; the new library stands near its presumed location in the royal quarter.
