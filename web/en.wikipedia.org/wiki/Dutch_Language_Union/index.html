The Dutch Language Union (Dutch: Nederlandse Taalunie) is an international intergovernmental organisation that governs matters of common policy for the Dutch language. It was established by treaty between the Netherlands and Belgium on 9 September 1980, with Suriname joining as an associate member in 2004.

Functions

The Union coordinates policy on the standard Dutch language across its member states. Its best-known responsibility is the official spelling of Dutch: it publishes the Woordenlijst Nederlandse Taal ("Word List of the Dutch Language"), popularly called the Groene Boekje ("Green Booklet"), whose spelling rules are legally binding for government and education in the Netherlands and Flanders. Major spelling reforms were issued in 1995 and 2005.

Beyond spelling, the Union supports the teaching of Dutch abroad, advises on terminology, funds research — including the Institute for Dutch Lexicology's dictionary projects — and promotes Dutch language and literature internationally. It also awards the Prijs der Nederlandse Letteren, the most prestigious literary prize in the Dutch language area, on behalf of the member states.

Structure

The organisation comprises a Committee of Ministers from the member governments, a parliamentary advisory body (the Interparliamentary Commission), a Council for the Dutch Language and Literature of experts, and a general secretariat based in The Hague.
