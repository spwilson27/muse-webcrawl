Chernobyl disaster

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Chernobyl New Safe Confinement arch over Reactor 4, and the abandoned ferris wheel in Pripyat]

The Chernobyl disaster was a catastrophic nuclear accident on 26 April 1986 at the No. 4 reactor of the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant near Pripyat in northern Ukraine (then part of the Soviet Union). It is the worst nuclear power disaster in history by cost and casualties, and the only one rated level 7 (maximum) on the International Nuclear Event Scale along with Fukushima in 2011.

During a flawed safety test, a power surge and steam explosions destroyed the reactor, and its graphite moderator burned for days, releasing large quantities of radioactive material across Ukraine, Belarus, Russia and Europe. Two plant workers died in the explosion and 28 firefighters and staff died of acute radiation sickness in the following weeks; Pripyat's ~49,000 residents were evacuated the next day.

Causes and aftermath

Investigators blamed reactor design flaws (the RBMK's positive void coefficient and graphite-tipped control rods) and operator actions under a secretive safety culture. A concrete 'sarcophagus' was built over the ruins in 1986, replaced by the New Safe Confinement in 2016-17. The surrounding Exclusion Zone remains restricted, though wildlife has flourished there.

Long-term effects

Thyroid cancers rose among those exposed as children; broader health and land impacts continue to be studied by WHO and UNSCEAR.
