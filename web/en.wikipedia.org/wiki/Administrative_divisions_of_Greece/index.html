The administrative divisions of Greece were fundamentally restructured by the Kallikratis Programme, which took effect on 1 January 2011. Under the current system, Greece is divided into decentralized administrations (state organs), self-governing regions, regional units and municipalities.

Structure

At the state level, seven decentralized administrations (Attica; Thessaly–Central Greece; Epirus–Western Macedonia; Macedonia–Thrace; Peloponnese–Western Greece–Ionian; Aegean; and Crete) are headed by secretaries-general appointed by the national government and supervise legality of local government acts.

The first tier of elected local government consists of thirteen regions (peripheries), each headed by an elected regional governor and council:

Below the regions are 74 regional units, largely successor bodies to the former prefectures without separate elected councils, and 332 municipalities led by elected mayors. The 2019 Kleisthenis I reform adjusted municipal organisation and electoral rules. Mount Athos is an autonomous monastic community under its own status, guaranteed by the Greek constitution.

History

Earlier systems, from the prefectures (nomoi) established in the 19th century to the 1987 creation of the regions and the 1997 Kapodistrias amalgamation of municipalities, were progressively replaced as Greece decentralised, a process accelerated by the 2010 debt crisis-era reforms under the Kallikratis plan.
