Suzuki method

A Suzuki group class

The Suzuki method is an approach to music education developed by Japanese violinist Shinichi Suzuki (1898–1998) in the mid-20th century. Suzuki observed that children learn their native language by listening and imitation long before they read, and proposed that music should be taught the same way — the "mother-tongue approach". His Talent Education movement, founded in Matsumoto, Japan, spread worldwide after the 1960s. (The method is unrelated to the Suzuki reaction in chemistry.)

Principles

Children begin as young as three or four, listening daily to recordings of the repertoire they will play, learning by ear before reading notation. Parents attend lessons and act as home teachers, and group classes supplement individual instruction. Suzuki believed talent is developed, not inborn — "every child can" — and that music study should build character as well as skill ("first character, then ability"). The standard violin repertoire progresses from variations on "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" through increasingly difficult pieces in ten graded volumes.

Spread and criticism

The method has been adapted for viola, cello, piano, flute, guitar, and other instruments, with Suzuki associations certifying teachers in the Americas, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. Critics have argued that delaying reading can leave students weaker sight-readers, and that the fixed repertoire and parental demands suit some families poorly; supporters reply that early fluency and confidence by ear mirror how language is acquired.
