The International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees, Moving Picture Technicians, Artists and Allied Crafts of the United States, Its Territories and Canada, known simply as the International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE), is a North American labour union representing technicians, artisans and craftspeople in the entertainment industry, including live theatre, motion picture and television production, broadcast and trade shows.

History

IATSE was founded in 1893, when representatives of stagehands' locals from several American cities met in New York. It expanded into projection and then film production work in the early twentieth century, growing through the studio system era, often in rivalry with other Hollywood unions. In the post-war decades the union organised television and, later, new media production.

Structure and membership

The union is composed of hundreds of semi-autonomous local unions organised by craft and geography — cinematographers, editors, grips, electricians, costumers, make-up artists, set designers and many others — coordinated by an international leadership headquartered in New York. Its distinctive bug, or logo, appears in the credits of most North American film and television productions.

Labour actions

In 2021 IATSE members voted overwhelmingly to authorise a strike over working hours, rest periods and streaming residuals; a last-minute agreement with the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers averted what would have been the union's first nationwide strike. The 2021 and subsequent contract rounds made the union a prominent voice in debates over working conditions in the streaming era.
