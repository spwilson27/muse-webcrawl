Health Level 7

Health Level Seven (HL7) is a set of international standards for the transfer of clinical and administrative data between software applications used by healthcare providers. The standards are produced by Health Level Seven International, a non-profit standards development organisation founded in 1987, and are adopted by hospitals and health systems worldwide.

The name refers to layer 7 — the application layer — of the OSI model of networking, reflecting the standards' concern with the format and content of data exchanged rather than with how it is physically transported.

HL7 version 2

The HL7 version 2.x messaging standard, first published in the late 1980s, became the most widely implemented healthcare interoperability standard in the world. Messages are composed of segments (such as MSH for the header, PID for patient identification, and OBX for observations) separated by carriage returns, with fields delimited by the pipe character (|). Common message types include ADT messages (admit, discharge, transfer), ORM (orders) and ORU (results). Version 2's flexibility — many fields are optional and sites may add local "Z-segments" — made it adaptable but also led to widely varying implementations, jokingly summarised as "once you've seen one HL7 interface, you've seen one HL7 interface."

Version 3 and CDA

Version 3, built on a formal Reference Information Model (RIM) and XML encoding, aimed at greater semantic rigour but saw limited adoption for messaging. Its Clinical Document Architecture (CDA) specification, however, became widely used for clinical documents such as discharge summaries, and underlies the Consolidated CDA (C-CDA) templates mandated in the United States.

FHIR

HL7's modern standard is FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), first released in draft in 2014. FHIR models healthcare data as modular "resources" (Patient, Observation, MedicationRequest, and so on) exchanged over web APIs using REST, JSON and XML, borrowing practices from the wider web industry. FHIR has become the basis of regulatory interoperability requirements in the United States and many other countries, and of patient-access apps on smartphones.

Organisation

Health Level Seven International is an ANSI-accredited standards development organisation with member organisations in more than 50 countries and national affiliate chapters. Its standards are developed by volunteer work groups through a balloting process.
