Studio Babelsberg, located in the Babelsberg district of Potsdam, Germany, is the oldest large-scale film studio in the world and one of Europe's largest, with a history of film production dating to 1912. It has hosted the production of numerous landmark German and international films.

History

Production began in 1911–12 when the Deutsche Bioscope company built a glass-house studio on the site; early productions included Urban Gad's The Girl from Acker Street era silents and Asta Nielsen films. The studio became the home of UFA (Universum Film AG) in the Weimar era, producing classics such as The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920, shot nearby), Fritz Lang's Metropolis (1927)—for which the huge "Neubabelsberg" stages were built—The Blue Angel (1930) with Marlene Dietrich, and works by F. W. Murnau and G. W. Pabst. Under Nazi control UFA's output included propaganda films. After the Second World War the site became the state studio of East Germany under DEFA (1946), producing that country's feature films until reunification.

Modern era

Privatised in the 1990s, the studio was redeveloped as "Medienstadt Babelsberg". International productions shot there in the 2000s and 2010s include The Pianist, Inglourious Basterds, The Grand Budapest Hotel, Bridge of Spies, The Hunger Games sequels, and the series Homeland and Dark. The adjacent Filmpark Babelsberg is a theme park, and the neighbouring Film University Babelsberg carries on the site's training tradition.
