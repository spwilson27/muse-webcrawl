Weapons-grade

Weapons-grade nuclear material is any fissionable nuclear material that is pure enough to make a nuclear weapon or has properties that make it particularly suitable for nuclear weapons use. Plutonium and uranium in grades normally used in nuclear weapons are the most common examples.

Definitions

Uranium is described as weapons-grade when it has been enriched to about 90% or more uranium-235 (highly enriched uranium, HEU), compared with 3–5% for low-enriched reactor fuel and 0.7% in natural uranium. Plutonium is weapons-grade when it consists of at least 93% plutonium-239, with low fractions of plutonium-240, which has a high spontaneous fission rate that can cause predetonation. Reactor-grade plutonium, produced in commercial power reactors, contains substantially more Pu-240 and other isotopes.

Production and control

Producing weapons-grade material requires enrichment or reprocessing infrastructure, which is the central concern of nuclear non-proliferation policy and the inspections regime of the International Atomic Energy Agency. The United States and Russia have down-blended large quantities of weapons-grade HEU to reactor fuel under agreements such as the 1993 Megatons to Megawatts program.
