Claddagh ring

The Claddagh ring (Irish: fáinne Chladaigh) is a traditional Irish ring in which a heart represents love, the crown stands for loyalty, and two clasped hands symbolise friendship. It is often worn as a token of Irish identity or heritage, or as a wedding or friendship ring. The design takes its name from the Claddagh, a fishing village on the western edge of Galway city.

OriginCladdagh, Galway, Ireland
SymbolismHeart = love; crown = loyalty; hands = friendship
MaterialsGold, silver

History and legend

The design dates from the 17th century. Legend attaches it to Richard Joyce, a Galway goldsmith's apprentice who was captured by Algerian corsairs, learned goldsmithing in captivity, and on his release returned to Galway and created the ring — the earliest surviving examples bear his maker's mark. The ring's motto is often given as "love, loyalty, and friendship" (grá, dílseacht, cairdeas).

How it is worn

Tradition interprets the wearer's relationship status from how the ring is worn: on the right hand with the heart pointing outward indicates the wearer is single and may be looking for love; on the right hand with the point inward indicates a relationship; on the left hand pointing outward indicates engagement; and on the left hand pointing inward indicates marriage. Claddagh rings have been presented to royalty and celebrities and are popular gifts marking Irish heritage worldwide.
