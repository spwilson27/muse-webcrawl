Robert Bolt

Born: August 15, 1924, Sale, Cheshire, England
Died: February 20, 1995, Petersfield, Hampshire
Occupation: Playwright, screenwriter
Known for: A Man for All Seasons; Lawrence of Arabia; Doctor Zhivago

Robert Oxton Bolt (August 15, 1924 – February 20, 1995) was an English playwright and screenwriter, best known for the play and film A Man for All Seasons and for his screenplays for David Lean's epics Lawrence of Arabia (1962) and Doctor Zhivago (1965), for which he won two Academy Awards.

Career

Bolt was educated at Manchester Grammar School and the University of Manchester, served in the Royal Air Force and the Army during the Second World War, and worked as a schoolteacher while writing radio plays for the BBC. His stage breakthrough came with Flowering Cherry (1957) and then A Man for All Seasons (1960), a drama about Sir Thomas More's refusal to endorse Henry VIII's divorce, first staged as a radio play and then in the West End and on Broadway. Its 1966 film version, directed by Fred Zinnemann from Bolt's own script, won the Academy Award for Best Picture and Best Adapted Screenplay.

For David Lean he wrote Lawrence of Arabia (with Michael Wilson, uncredited at the time), Doctor Zhivago, and Ryan's Daughter (1970). Later scripts included The Mission (1986), which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes, and The Bounty (1984). His other plays include Vivat! Vivat Regina! (1970), about Mary, Queen of Scots and Elizabeth I.

Personal life and politics

Bolt was a committed socialist and supporter of the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament; in 1961 he was briefly imprisoned, along with other members of the Committee of 100, for refusing to be bound over to keep the peace. He was married four times, including to actress Sarah Miles. A stroke in 1979 left him partially paralyzed and with impaired speech, but he continued writing. He died in 1995.
