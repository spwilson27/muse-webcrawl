Executive (government)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The executive is the branch of government exercising authority in, and holding responsibility for, the governance of a state. The executive carries out and enforces laws, conducts foreign relations and commands the armed forces. In political systems based on the separation of powers, authority is distributed among the executive, the legislature and the judiciary to prevent any one branch from exercising the core functions of another.

The person or persons who head the executive are the head of state, the head of government, or both, depending on the system. In presidential systems the president is both head of state and head of government; in parliamentary systems the roles are divided between a ceremonial head of state and a prime minister who leads the government; semi-presidential systems combine a powerful president with a prime minister accountable to the legislature.

Powers and functions

Executive powers typically include implementing legislation, appointing officials and judges, issuing regulations and executive orders, conducting diplomacy and negotiating treaties, granting pardons, and serving as commander-in-chief of the armed forces. The day-to-day work is performed by ministries and agencies staffed by a professional civil service, whose senior officials advise ministers and administer policy.

Accountability

Executives are checked by legislatures through budgetary control, questioning, and votes of no confidence or impeachment; by courts through judicial review of executive action; and by electors at elections. The precise balance differs: a fixed-term president can ordinarily be removed only by impeachment, whereas a prime minister holds office only while retaining the confidence of the legislature.
