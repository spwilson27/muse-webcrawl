Auguste Rodin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Thinker: a muscular bronze man seated chin-on-fist in intense contemplation, green patina in a garden]

François Auguste René Rodin (12 November 1840 – 17 November 1917) was a French sculptor generally considered the founder of modern sculpture. He was schooled traditionally and took a craftsmanlike approach to his work, yet the naturalism and expressive surface of his figures, and his refusal to idealise, drew controversy from the academy.

Rodin's great project was The Gates of Hell (1880–1917, after Dante), from which came his most famous independent works: The Thinker (1880), The Kiss (1882) and The Prodigal Son. Public monuments — The Burghers of Calais (1889) and the rejected, revolutionary Balzac (1898) — redefined commemorative sculpture. His studio practice with assistants (including Camille Claudel, his lover and a major sculptor herself) and the Musée Rodin in Paris preserve an oeuvre of some 7,000 sculptures and 7,000 drawings by common estimates.
