Lasse Virén

Lasse Virén

Born: July 22, 1949, Myrskylä, Finland
Occupation: Long-distance runner, politician
Olympic medals: 4 gold (1972, 1976)
Events: 5,000 m, 10,000 m, marathon

Lasse Artturi Virén (born July 22, 1949) is a Finnish former long-distance runner and politician. He won gold medals in both the 5,000 metres and 10,000 metres at the 1972 Munich Olympics and repeated the double at the 1976 Montreal Olympics — the only runner to win the 5,000/10,000 m double at two consecutive Olympic Games. He was the last of the celebrated "Flying Finns".

Career

Virén emerged from relative obscurity before Munich, training with coach Rolf Haikkola and peaking specifically for the Olympics. In the 1972 10,000 m final he fell after tangling with another runner around the halfway mark, got up, and still won in a world record of 27:38.35 — one of the most famous feats in Olympic history. He then won the 5,000 m over Mohamed Gammoudi and Ian Stewart. At Montreal in 1976 he again won both events, then attempted a third in the marathon — the first such bid since Emil Zátopek's 1952 triple — finishing fifth less than a day after the 5,000 m final. At the 1980 Moscow Olympics, hampered by injuries and declining form, he placed fifth in the 10,000 m and did not finish the marathon.

Doping speculation

Virén's pattern of modest results between Olympics and spectacular peaks at the Games prompted persistent speculation about blood doping (autologous transfusion), which was legal at the time and never proven in his case. He consistently denied it, attributing his peaks to training periodisation, altitude camps, and, as he famously joked, reindeer milk.

Later life

After retiring in 1980, Virén worked in business and entered politics, serving as a Member of the Finnish Parliament (Eduskunta) for the National Coalition Party from 1999 to 2007 and again from 2010 to 2011. He remains one of Finland's most revered sporting figures.
