Federal Bureau of Prisons

The Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) is a United States federal law enforcement agency within the Department of Justice responsible for the care, custody, and control of incarcerated individuals in the federal prison system.

Federal Bureau of Prisons

Formed: May 14, 1930
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Employees: ~38,000
Parent agency: U.S. Department of Justice
Motto: Correctional Excellence. Respect. Integrity.

History

The BOP was established in 1930 under President Herbert Hoover, with Sanford Bates as its first director, to consolidate and professionalize the management of federal prisons which until then had been overseen piecemeal. The system grew with federal criminal law expansion, especially drug offenses in the 1980s-90s.

Facilities and security levels

The Bureau operates institutions at five security levels: minimum (federal prison camps), low, medium, high (United States penitentiaries), and administrative (including ADX Florence, the supermax holding the most dangerous inmates, medical centers, and detention centers). It also manages community corrections via Residential Reentry Centers.

Population and programs

The federal inmate population peaked above 219,000 in 2013 and has since declined to roughly 155,000-160,000. Programs include drug abuse treatment (RDAP), education, vocational training, and UNICOR prison industries. The First Step Act of 2018 expanded earned-time credits.
