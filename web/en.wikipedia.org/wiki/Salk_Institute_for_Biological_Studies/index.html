Salk Institute for Biological Studies

Type: Independent nonprofit research institute
Founded: 1960
Founder: Jonas Salk
Location: La Jolla, San Diego, California
Architect: Louis Kahn

The Salk Institute for Biological Studies is an independent, nonprofit scientific research institute located in La Jolla, San Diego, California. It was founded in 1960 by Jonas Salk, the developer of the polio vaccine. The institute is widely regarded as one of the world's leading organizations for basic research in the life sciences.

History

Jonas Salk sought to create an institution where biologists, chemists, and other scientists could work together in a setting designed to foster creativity and collaboration. The city of San Diego gifted the land in the Torrey Pines area, and construction began in 1962. Salk worked closely with architect Louis Kahn, whose design for the campus is considered a masterpiece of modern architecture.

Architecture

The campus consists of two six-story laboratory blocks separated by a stark travertine plaza. A thin channel of water—the "River of Life"—runs down the center of the courtyard toward the Pacific Ocean, aligned with the sunset on the equinoxes. Kahn left the plaza deliberately empty, following the advice of Mexican architect Luis Barragán, who suggested that a plaza of stone, rather than a garden, would create a facade to the sky. The laboratory floors are open and flexible, with interstitial service floors above each level—an arrangement Salk insisted upon so that the labs could adapt to changing scientific needs. The buildings make extensive use of poured-in-place concrete with teak window walls.

The campus was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1991 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Research

Scientific research at the institute is organized into areas including neuroscience, genetics, plant biology, cancer biology, computational biology, and aging. Faculty members regularly rank among the most frequently cited scientists in the world, and the institute has been associated with several Nobel laureates, including Renato Dulbecco, Robert Holley, Roger Guillemin, and Elizabeth Blackburn.
