Planet Labs

Planet Labs PBC is an American Earth-imaging company based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 2010 by former NASA scientists, it operates the largest fleet of Earth observation satellites in history — several hundred small "Dove" CubeSats that photograph the entire landmass of the Earth every day at roughly 3-metre resolution, together with higher-resolution SkySat satellites for tasked imagery.

History

The company was founded in 2010 as Cosmogia Inc. by Will Marshall, Robbie Schingler and Chris Boshuizen, who had pioneered "PhoneSat" experiments putting smartphone hardware into orbit at NASA Ames. Its first Dove satellites launched in 2013. In February 2017, 88 Doves launched on a single Indian PSLV rocket set a world record and completed Planet's "Mission One" — a constellation able to scan the whole Earth daily. Planet acquired Google's Terra Bella division and its SkySat satellites in 2017, and BlackBridge (RapidEye) in 2015. The company went public via a SPAC merger in December 2021.

Products and uses

Daily PlanetScope imagery is sold by subscription to customers in agriculture, forestry, mapping, finance, insurance, journalism and government, and is widely used for monitoring deforestation, crop health, disaster response and conflict zones; news organisations frequently publish Planet imagery of wars, fires and floods. Higher-resolution SkySat imagery (about 0.5–0.7 m) can be tasked to revisit sites many times a day. The newer Pelican constellation is intended to succeed SkySat with still higher revisit and resolution.

Approach

Planet exemplifies the "NewSpace" model: inexpensive, mass-produced satellites built from consumer electronics, launched frequently and replaced every few years, trading per-satellite capability for fleet size, revisit frequency and low cost.
