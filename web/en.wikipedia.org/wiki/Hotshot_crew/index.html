Hotshot crew

An Interagency Hotshot Crew (IHC), or hotshot crew, is a 20-person wildland firefighting crew in the United States, trained and equipped for the most demanding assignments on wildfires. Hotshot crews are hosted by the U.S. Forest Service, Bureau of Land Management, National Park Service and other agencies.

Role

Hotshots are typically assigned to the hottest, most complex portions of a fire — hence the name — constructing fireline with chainsaws and hand tools, conducting burn-out operations, and hiking into remote terrain. They are a national resource, dispatched across the country during fire season.

History and standards

The first hotshot crews were organised in Southern California in the late 1940s. Crews must meet strict standards for physical fitness, training, experience and availability; a high proportion of each crew returns season after season, giving the crews an elite reputation.

Notable crews

The Granite Mountain Hotshots of Prescott, Arizona, gained national attention after 19 of their members were killed in the Yarnell Hill Fire on 30 June 2013, one of the deadliest incidents in U.S. wildland firefighting history.
