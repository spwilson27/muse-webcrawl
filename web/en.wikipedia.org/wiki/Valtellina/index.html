Valtellina (Italian: Valtellina; German: Veltlin) is a valley in northern Italy, in the province of Sondrio in Lombardy, running east–west along the Adda river from its headwaters near the Stelvio Pass to Lake Como. It is bounded by the Bernina range to the north and the Bergamo Alps to the south.

History

Because it controls passes between Italy and the Inn valley, the Valtellina was strategically contested for centuries. From 1512 to 1797 it was a subject territory of the Three Leagues (the Grisons, in present-day Switzerland), a period that included the religious violence of the Sacro Macello of 1620 and the struggles of the Thirty Years' War, when Spanish and French armies fought over the valley's passes. The Treaty of Campo-Formio (1797) attached it to the Cisalpine Republic, and it thereafter followed Lombardy into the Kingdom of Italy.

Geography and economy

The broad, glacial U-shaped valley is known for its steep, sun-facing terraced vineyards — supported by some 2,500 km of dry-stone walls, recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage — producing Nebbiolo wines under the Valtellina Superiore DOCG. The valley is also noted for bresaola, Bitto cheese, apples, hydroelectric power from the Adda basin, and winter tourism: resorts include Bormio, Livigno and Aprica, and Bormio's Stelvio piste has hosted Alpine Ski World Cup races. In July 1987 catastrophic floods and a massive landslide at Val Pola devastated parts of the valley, killing dozens.
