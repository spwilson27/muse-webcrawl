In physics and materials science, the Curie temperature (TC), or Curie point, is the temperature above which certain materials lose their permanent magnetic properties, which can (in most cases) be replaced by induced magnetism. The Curie temperature is named after Pierre Curie, who showed that magnetism is lost at a critical temperature.

The force of magnetism is determined by the magnetic moment, a dipole moment within an atom which originates from the angular momentum and spin of electrons. Materials have different structures of intrinsic magnetic moments that depend on temperature; the Curie temperature is the critical point at which a material's intrinsic magnetic moments change direction.

Definition

Permanent magnetism is caused by the alignment of magnetic moments, and induced magnetism is created when disordered magnetic moments are forced to align in an applied magnetic field. For example, the ordered magnetic moments (ferromagnetic, Figure 1) change and become disordered (paramagnetic, Figure 2) at the Curie temperature. Higher temperatures make magnets weaker, as spontaneous magnetism only occurs below the Curie temperature. Magnetic susceptibility above the Curie temperature can be calculated from the Curie–Weiss law, which is derived from Curie's law.

In analogy to ferromagnetic and paramagnetic materials, the Curie temperature can also be used to describe the phase transition between ferroelectricity and paraelectricity. In this case, it is also known as the Curie point.

Curie temperatures of common materials

Iron has a Curie temperature of 1,043 K (770 °C); nickel 627 K (354 °C); cobalt 1,388 K (1,115 °C); and gadolinium has a Curie point near room temperature, about 292 K (19 °C). Magnetite (Fe3O4) has a Curie temperature of about 858 K (585 °C).

Applications

The Curie temperature is used in thermomagnetic switches and temperature-sensitive magnetic devices, in the study of paleomagnetism and geology (where heating above the Curie point erases magnetic signatures in rocks), and in the design of magnetic storage media and electric generators, where operating temperatures must remain well below TC.
