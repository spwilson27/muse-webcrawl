Forgetting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Forgetting or disremembering is the apparent loss or modification of information already encoded and stored in an individual's short or long-term memory. It is a spontaneous or gradual process in which old memories are unable to be recalled from memory storage. Forgetting is an essential feature of a healthy memory system, allowing the brain to discard irrelevant information and reduce interference.

The forgetting curve

In 1885, Hermann Ebbinghaus published his pioneering experimental work on memory, in which he memorised lists of nonsense syllables and measured how much he retained over time. He found that retention declines exponentially: a large proportion of what is learned is lost within the first hour, after which the rate of forgetting slows. This pattern, the forgetting curve, has been replicated many times and underlies techniques such as spaced repetition, which schedules review at expanding intervals to counteract decay.

Explanations

Decay theory holds that memory traces fade over time if they are not accessed. Interference theory attributes forgetting to competition between memories: proactive interference occurs when old learning disrupts new recall, while retroactive interference occurs when new learning disrupts old recall. Retrieval failure occurs when information is stored but cannot be accessed because appropriate cues are missing, as in the tip-of-the-tongue phenomenon. Motivated forgetting, including Freudian repression and deliberate suppression, proposes that people may push distressing memories out of awareness. Finally, forgetting may result from encoding failure, where information was never properly stored in the first place.

Forgetting in pathology

Abnormal forgetting is a feature of amnesia, dementia and Alzheimer's disease, in which the ability to form or retain memories is severely impaired. By contrast, some individuals with hyperthymesia remember autobiographical events in extraordinary detail and experience abnormally little forgetting.
