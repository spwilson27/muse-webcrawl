Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania

LocationVilnius, Lithuania
Built15th–17th centuries; demolished 1801; rebuilt 2002–2018
StyleGothic, Renaissance, early Baroque
FunctionNational museum (National Museum – Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania)

The Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania (Lithuanian: Lietuvos Didžiosios Kunigaikštystės valdovų rūmai) is a palace in Vilnius, Lithuania, originally built in the 15th century for the rulers of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the future kings of Poland. The palace was located in the Lower Castle of Vilnius, at the foot of Gediminas Hill, and evolved through Gothic, Renaissance and early Baroque phases. Demolished in 1801 by the authorities of the Russian Empire, it was reconstructed in the early 21st century and now houses a national museum.

History

A fortified residence existed on the site from the early medieval period. The stone palace took shape under Grand Duke Alexander Jagiellon at the end of the 15th century and was greatly expanded in Renaissance style by Sigismund I the Old and his son Sigismund II Augustus in the 16th century, when Vilnius was one of the two principal royal residences of the Polish–Lithuanian state. Sigismund III Vasa rebuilt parts of it in early Baroque form in the 17th century.

The palace was devastated during the mid-17th-century wars with Muscovy and, after the Third Partition of the Commonwealth in 1795, the tsarist administration ordered its demolition, completed in 1801. For two centuries only foundations and cellars survived beneath later buildings.

Reconstruction

Archaeological excavations from the late 1980s uncovered extensive remains and tens of thousands of artefacts. In 2000 the Lithuanian parliament resolved to rebuild the palace as a symbol of statehood. Reconstruction proceeded from 2002, using historical plans, images and excavated fragments; parts of the museum opened in 2013 for the Lithuanian Presidency of the Council of the EU, and the full complex was inaugurated in 2018, during the centenary of the restored Lithuanian state.

Museum

The National Museum – Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania presents the history of the Grand Duchy, the palace's architectural development, restored historical interiors, and the rulers' treasury and everyday life. The authentic archaeological remains preserved in the basement form one of its principal exhibits.
