Operation Cartwheel

Operation Cartwheel (1943–1944) was a major military strategy for the Allies in the Pacific Theater of World War II. Cartwheel was a twin-axis offensive aimed at neutralising the major Japanese base at Rabaul, on New Britain in the Territory of New Guinea. The operation was directed by the Supreme Allied Commander in the South West Pacific Area (SWPA), General Douglas MacArthur, whose forces advanced along the northeast coast of New Guinea while Admiral William Halsey's forces in the South Pacific Area advanced through the Solomon Islands toward Bougainville.

Strategy

Rather than capture Rabaul by direct assault, the Allies planned to seize surrounding positions, build airfields, and isolate the base, cutting it off from resupply so that its large garrison — eventually some 100,000 Japanese troops — would "wither on the vine." The operation comprised a series of smaller operations including landings at Lae, Finschhafen, Arawe, Cape Gloucester, the Admiralty Islands, Hollandia and Aitape.

Outcome

By early 1944, Rabaul was effectively neutralised, its air and naval power destroyed and its garrison isolated for the remainder of the war. Cartwheel's success cleared the way for MacArthur's advance westward along New Guinea toward the Philippines, while the Hollandia landings bypassed large Japanese forces in eastern New Guinea.
