State Council of China

Formed: 27 September 1954
Headquarters: Zhongnanhai, Beijing
Head: Premier of China (Li Qiang since 2023)
Parent: National People's Congress and its Standing Committee
Composition: Premier, vice premiers, state councillors, ministers, auditor-general, secretary-general

The State Council of the People's Republic of China (Chinese: 国务院), also known as the Central People's Government, is the chief administrative authority and executive organ of the People's Republic of China. Under the Constitution, it is the executive body of the National People's Congress (NPC), the highest organ of state power, and is responsible and accountable to the NPC and its Standing Committee. It is headed by the Premier, who is nominated by the President and confirmed by the NPC.

Functions

The State Council exercises administrative leadership over China's ministries, commissions, the People's Bank of China and the National Audit Office, and supervises provincial-level governments. Its constitutional powers include adopting administrative measures and issuing regulations and decisions; submitting proposals to the NPC; drawing up and implementing the national economic and social development plan and the state budget; directing economic affairs, foreign affairs administration, education, science, culture, public health, public security and civil affairs; and altering or annulling inappropriate decisions of subordinate organs. It may declare a state of emergency in parts of provinces, subject to constitutional limits.

Organisation

The plenary State Council consists of the Premier, several vice premiers (four in recent terms), state councillors, the ministers of ministries and directors of commissions, the governor of the central bank, the auditor-general and the secretary-general. In practice most business is conducted by the Executive Meeting — the Premier, vice premiers, state councillors and secretary-general — which meets weekly, while the full body meets roughly every six months. Ministries and commissions number about two dozen after successive reorganisations (in 2018, 21 ministries and commissions plus other agencies were listed under the Council following a major restructuring).

Relationship with the Party

As with all PRC state organs, the State Council operates under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party; its leading members form a Party group within the Council, and the Premier is invariably a member of the Politburo Standing Committee of the CCP. The current Premier, Li Qiang, took office in March 2023, succeeding Li Keqiang.
