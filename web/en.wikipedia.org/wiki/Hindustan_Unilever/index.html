Hindustan Unilever

Hindustan Unilever Limited (HUL) is an Indian consumer goods company headquartered in Mumbai. It is a subsidiary of British company Unilever, which holds about 62% of its shares. Formed in 1933 as Lever Brothers India, it took its present name in 2007. Its products include foods, beverages, cleaning agents and personal care products, reaching nine out of ten Indian households.

History

Lever began exporting Sunlight soap to India in 1888. Subsidiaries for soap, vanaspati and personal products merged in 1956 as Hindustan Lever, with 10% Indian shareholding — a pioneer listed company. It expanded through acquisitions including Brooke Bond Lipton, Ponds, Lakmé (sold later), Kissan and Modern Foods, and in 2020 absorbed GlaxoSmithKline Consumer Healthcare India, bringing Horlicks and Boost.

Brands and operations

Portfolio spans home care (Surf Excel, Rin, Vim, Domex), beauty and personal care (Dove, Lux, Lifebuoy, Fair & Lovely — renamed Glow & Lovely in 2020, Sunsilk, Clinic Plus), and foods and refreshment (Brooke Bond Red Label, Taj Mahal tea, Lipton, Bru coffee, Kissan, Knorr, Kwality Wall's ice cream). Its distribution covers millions of retail outlets, supplemented by the Shikhar retailer app and Project Shakti, employing rural women entrepreneurs.

Sustainability and criticism

HUL promotes water conservation and plastic reduction under Unilever's Sustainable Living Plan. It has faced criticism over mercury contamination at its former Kodaikanal thermometer factory, skin-lightening marketing, and palm-oil sourcing.
