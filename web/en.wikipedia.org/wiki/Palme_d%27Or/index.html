The Palme d'Or (French for "Golden Palm") is the highest prize awarded at the Cannes Film Festival. It is presented to the director of the best film in the official competition section and is widely considered one of the most prestigious awards in cinema.

History

From 1946 the festival's top honor was the Grand Prix. The Palme d'Or was introduced in 1955, its palm motif taken from the coat of arms of Cannes; it was suspended between 1964 and 1974 when the Grand Prix briefly returned, and reinstated permanently in 1975. The physical trophy, a golden palm branch on a crystal cushion, is crafted by Chopard.

Notable winners

Winners include La Dolce Vita, The Leopard, Taxi Driver, Apocalypse Now, Sex, Lies, and Videotape, The Piano, Pulp Fiction, and Parasite, the first non-English-language film to also win the Academy Award for Best Picture. A small number of directors — including Alf Sjöberg, Francis Ford Coppola, Ken Loach, Michael Haneke, and the Dardenne brothers — have won the prize twice.
