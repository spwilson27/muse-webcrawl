The Detroit River is a 45-kilometre-long (28 mi) river in the Great Lakes system, flowing south from Lake St. Clair to Lake Erie and forming part of the border between Canada (Ontario) and the United States (Michigan). Its name comes from the French Rivière du Détroit ("River of the Strait"). The cities of Detroit, Michigan and Windsor, Ontario face each other across its upper reaches.

Geography and navigation

The river contains numerous islands, including Grosse Ile, Fighting Island and Belle Isle. It is a key link in the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence shipping system, carrying one of the world's largest volumes of commercial navigation tonnage. Crossings include the Ambassador Bridge (1929), the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel (1930), and the Gordie Howe International Bridge, under construction in the 2020s.

History and ecology

The river served as a strategic frontier in the War of 1812 and, in the 19th century, a crossing point on the Underground Railroad for enslaved people escaping to Canada. Industrial pollution once damaged the river severely, but clean-up efforts have restored fisheries and wildlife; it was designated an American Heritage River and a Canadian Heritage River, and the Detroit River International Wildlife Refuge protects wetlands along its lower course.
