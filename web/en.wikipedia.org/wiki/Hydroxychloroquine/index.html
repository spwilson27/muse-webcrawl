Hydroxychloroquine

Hydroxychloroquine, sold under the brand name Plaquenil among others, is a medication used to prevent and treat certain types of malaria and to treat autoimmune conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and some porphyrias. It is a less toxic derivative of chloroquine, differing by a hydroxyl group, and is taken by mouth. Hydroxychloroquine is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Hydroxychloroquine is used for chemoprophylaxis and treatment of malaria in regions where the parasite remains chloroquine-sensitive. As a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD), it is a first-line therapy for systemic lupus erythematosus, where it reduces flares and improves survival, and is used — often in combination with methotrexate — in rheumatoid arthritis. Its effects in autoimmune disease are thought to arise from interference with lysosomal acidification and toll-like receptor signaling in immune cells.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include nausea, stomach cramps, headache, and dizziness. The most serious long-term risk is retinopathy, an initially asymptomatic but potentially irreversible damage to the retina that occurs with prolonged high-dose use; guidelines recommend regular ophthalmological screening and dosing below 5 mg/kg of real body weight. Other rare effects include cardiomyopathy, hypoglycemia, QT prolongation, and skin reactions. Overdose is dangerous and can be fatal.

COVID-19

Early in the COVID-19 pandemic, hydroxychloroquine was widely promoted as a treatment on the basis of in vitro activity and small observational studies. Large randomized controlled trials — including the RECOVERY and WHO Solidarity trials — found no benefit in hospitalized patients, and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration revoked its emergency use authorization in June 2020. Subsequent trials of post-exposure prophylaxis likewise showed no meaningful benefit.
