Immigration to Australia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image placeholder: migrant ship arriving in Sydney]

Immigration to Australia has shaped the country's population since British colonisation began in 1788; Australia today has one of the highest proportions of foreign-born residents of any large country — around 30 per cent of the population was born overseas. Settler immigration succeeded the much older peopling of the continent by Aboriginal Australians tens of thousands of years earlier.

History

British settlement began with convict transportation (1788–1868), supplemented by free settlers and by gold-rush migration in the 1850s, which drew large numbers of Chinese miners and provoked restrictive colonial laws. At federation in 1901 the Immigration Restriction Act inaugurated the White Australia policy, which barred most non-European immigration for over half a century.

After the Second World War, immigration minister Arthur Calwell launched the "populate or perish" programme, bringing millions of British and European migrants — including displaced persons and assisted-passage "Ten Pound Poms". The White Australia policy was dismantled between 1966 and 1973, after which large-scale Asian immigration, including Vietnamese refugees in the 1970s, transformed the country's demography under an official policy of multiculturalism.

Contemporary programme

Modern migration is managed through a points-based skilled stream, family reunion and a humanitarian programme. The largest birthplace groups include England, India, China and New Zealand. Boat arrivals of asylum seekers and offshore detention policies have been recurrent political controversies since 2001.
