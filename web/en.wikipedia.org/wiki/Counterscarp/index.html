Counterscarp

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A counterscarp is a feature of fortification: the outer side of a ditch or moat surrounding a fortress, castle, or other defensive work — the side facing away from the defenders. Its counterpart, the wall of the ditch nearest the fortress, is the scarp.

Design

In bastion fortifications of the early modern period, the counterscarp was frequently revetted in masonry and topped by a covered way and glacis from which defenders could fire over the ditch while remaining under cover. A counterscarp gallery — a tunnel running within the counterscarp — allowed defenders to fire along the ditch at attackers who had descended into it, and to listen for enemy mining.
