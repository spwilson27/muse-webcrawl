Areca catechu is a species of palm in the family Arecaceae, grown for its commercially important seed crop, the areca nut (often called betel nut). It is native to the Philippines and widely cultivated across tropical Asia, the Pacific, and parts of East Africa.

Description

It is a slender, single-stemmed palm reaching 20–30 m, with a crown of pinnate fronds. The fruit is an egg-shaped drupe that turns orange or scarlet when ripe; the seed inside is the areca nut.

Use

The nut is chewed throughout much of Asia and Oceania for its stimulant alkaloid arecoline, typically wrapped in betel leaf (Piper betle) with slaked lime — the "betel quid". Chronic use is associated with oral cancers and other health harms, and the IARC classifies areca nut as a Group 1 carcinogen.
