Bulleen is a suburb of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia, 13 km north-east of Melbourne's central business district, located within the City of Manningham local government area. The suburb's name is believed to derive from the Bolin Bolin billabong on the Yarra River, from a Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung word meaning "lyrebird" or relating to the lagoons of the area.

Geography and history

Bulleen is bordered by the Yarra River to the north and west, across which lies the Heide Museum of Modern Art, set in parkland that was once the home of art patrons John and Sunday Reed and a centre of the Australian modernist Heide Circle. The river flats of Bulleen — including the Bolin Bolin billabong and the Banyule flats — were important meeting and ceremonial grounds for the Wurundjeri people and remain significant areas of native parkland.

European settlement began with farming along the river flats in the 1840s, and the area later supplied vegetables to Melbourne. Suburban housing development came mainly in the 1950s–1970s. Bulleen has no railway station; it is served by buses and by the Eastern Freeway along its southern fringe.

Amenities

Local facilities include the Bulleen Plaza shopping centre, several schools, sporting grounds used by local football and cricket clubs, and extensive walking and cycling trails along the Yarra, which form part of Melbourne's Main Yarra Trail. The North East Link road project, a major freeway tunnel under construction through Melbourne's north-east, has a significant works area in Bulleen, which has been the subject of local controversy over the loss of sporting grounds and trees.
