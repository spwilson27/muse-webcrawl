Hard power

In politics, hard power is the use of military and economic means to influence the behaviour or interests of other political actors. This form of political power is often aggressive (coercion), and is most immediately effective when imposed by one political actor on another that is less powerful in military or economic terms. Hard power contrasts with soft power, which comes from diplomacy, culture and history.

History of the term

The distinction between hard and soft power was developed by Joseph Nye, who introduced the concept of soft power in his 1990 book Bound to Lead and elaborated the contrast in later works. Hard power resources include military forces, economic sanctions, and inducements such as foreign aid and bribes — described by Nye as the powers of "command" based on coercion and payment.

Examples

The invasion and occupation of another country, the imposition of trade embargoes and economic sanctions, and threats of military intervention are classic exercises of hard power. Although effective in producing immediate compliance, hard power can be costly and may generate resentment; analysts therefore often advocate combining it with soft power into a strategy of "smart power".
