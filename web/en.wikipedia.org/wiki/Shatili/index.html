Shatili

Shatili (Georgian: შატილი) is a historic highland village in Georgia, in the historical province of Khevsureti on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus, near the border with Chechnya. It lies in the deep Arghuni gorge at about 1,400 metres above sea level and is now part of the Dusheti Municipality of the Mtskheta-Mtianeti region.

Architecture

The village is a remarkable example of medieval fortified settlement: some fifty to sixty flat-roofed stone houses and defensive towers are packed together into a single terraced fortress, their walls of local shale and mortar rising directly from the rocky slope. Dwellings and towers interconnect so that the whole village could be defended as one stronghold guarding the passes between Georgia and the North Caucasus. Nearby stand the Anatori vaults, communal ossuaries associated with a plague legend of the 18th century.

History and present day

Most of the population was resettled by the Soviet authorities in the 1950s, and the village was nearly abandoned; a handful of families have since returned, and the settlement is inhabited year-round by only a few households. Shatili is on Georgia's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status, together with the other tower villages of Khevsureti, and is a popular destination for trekkers travelling between Khevsureti and Tusheti.
