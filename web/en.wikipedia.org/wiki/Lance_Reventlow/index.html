Lance Graf von Haugwitz-Hardenberg-Reventlow (February 24, 1936 – July 24, 1972) was a British-born American entrepreneur, racing driver and heir to the Woolworth fortune as the only child of Barbara Hutton. Reventlow is best remembered for founding Scarab, the first American team to build and race its own Formula One cars.

Life and career

Born in London to Barbara Hutton and her second husband, Count Kurt von Haugwitz-Hardenberg-Reventlow, Lance was raised largely in the United States. He began racing sports cars in California in the mid-1950s, competing in Coopers, Maseratis and Porsches, and befriended fellow racers including James Dean and Bruce Kessler.

In 1957 he established Reventlow Automobiles Inc. in Venice, California, employing craftsmen such as Troutman and Barnes, Chuck Daigh and Phil Remington to build the front-engined Scarab sports cars, which dominated American road racing in 1958 powered by Chevrolet V8 engines. Reventlow then attempted the ambitious step of building an American Formula One car for 1960. The front-engined Scarab F1 arrived just as the rear-engined revolution made it obsolete, and the campaign yielded a single championship point, scored by Daigh in a later private entry. Reventlow withdrew from racing in the early 1960s.

Death

Reventlow later pursued business interests in Colorado, including real estate and a ski venture. He was killed on July 24, 1972, when the Cessna 206 in which he was a passenger crashed in the mountains near Aspen, Colorado, during a thunderstorm while he was surveying potential ski terrain.
