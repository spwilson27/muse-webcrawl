Glendalough

Glendalough (Irish: Gleann Dá Loch, "glen of the two lakes") is a glacial valley in County Wicklow, Ireland, renowned for its early medieval monastic settlement founded in the sixth century by St Kevin. For six centuries the monastery was one of the most important religious and scholarly centres of Ireland, and its evocative ruins, set between two dark lakes beneath steep wooded slopes, make it one of the country's most visited heritage sites.

The monastic city

According to tradition, Kevin, a descendant of one of the ruling families of Leinster, came to Glendalough seeking solitude, living first in a cave above the Upper Lake known as St Kevin's Bed. Disciples gathered around him, and the monastery grew to include churches, a cathedral, monks' cells, and workshops. The settlement was plundered repeatedly by Vikings and Irish raiders yet repeatedly rebuilt, remaining a place of pilgrimage long after its decline began in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries.

Principal remains

The best-known structure is the slender Round Tower, about 30 metres high, built of mica schist with a granite doorway nearly 3.5 metres above the ground. Other notable remains include the roofless Cathedral, the tiny St Kevin's Church ("St Kevin's Kitchen") with its miniature round tower, the Priest's House, St Mary's Church, and a high cross known as St Kevin's Cross carved from a single granite boulder. The monastic site lies within the Wicklow Mountains National Park, and waymarked trails of varying lengths — the popular "Spinc and Miners' Road" walk among them — explore the valley and its lakes.
