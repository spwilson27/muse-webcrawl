The Meteosat series of satellites are geostationary meteorological satellites operated by EUMETSAT under the Meteosat Transition Programme (MTP) and the Meteosat Second Generation (MSG) programme. Positioned over the Atlantic Ocean at the prime meridian, they provide continuous weather imagery of Europe, Africa and parts of the Atlantic and Indian Oceans.

First generation

The first Meteosat satellite, Meteosat-1, was launched on 23 November 1977 by a Delta rocket from Cape Canaveral. The first-generation satellites were spin-stabilised cylinders carrying the Meteosat Visible and Infrared Imager (MVIRI), which produced images in visible, infrared and water-vapour channels every 30 minutes. Seven first-generation satellites were launched, the last, Meteosat-7, in 1997.

Second generation (MSG)

Meteosat Second Generation satellites are larger spin-stabilised spacecraft carrying the Spinning Enhanced Visible and Infrared Imager (SEVIRI), which images the Earth every 15 minutes in 12 spectral channels, and the Geostationary Earth Radiation Budget (GERB) instrument. Four MSG satellites were launched between 2002 and 2015: MSG-1 (Meteosat-8), MSG-2 (Meteosat-9), MSG-3 (Meteosat-10) and MSG-4 (Meteosat-11).

Third generation (MTG)

Meteosat Third Generation is a system of six three-axis-stabilised satellites in two platforms: four imager satellites (MTG-I) carrying the Flexible Combined Imager and a lightning imager, and two sounder satellites (MTG-S) carrying an infrared sounder and the Copernicus Sentinel-4 ultraviolet/visible/near-infrared instrument. The first, MTG-I1 (Meteosat-12), was launched on 13 December 2022 aboard an Ariane 5.

Applications

Meteosat data support operational meteorology, nowcasting of severe weather, climate monitoring, volcanic ash and dust storm detection, and ocean and land surface monitoring. The satellites form part of a global geostationary ring alongside the American GOES, Japanese Himawari and other systems.
