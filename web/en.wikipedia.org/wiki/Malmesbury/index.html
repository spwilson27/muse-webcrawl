Malmesbury is a market town and civil parish in north-west Wiltshire, England, situated on a hilltop above the River Avon near the southern edge of the Cotswolds. It claims to be the oldest continuously inhabited town in England, and is best known for its partly ruined abbey.

History

A monastery was founded at Malmesbury in the 7th century; the scholar Aldhelm became its first abbot. The present Malmesbury Abbey, built in the 12th century, was one of the largest churches in England before the Dissolution of the Monasteries, after which part of it survived as the parish church. The abbey was the home of the historian William of Malmesbury and of the monk Eilmer, who around the year 1005 is reported to have attempted flight with artificial wings from the abbey tower, gliding some distance before crashing and breaking both legs.

Malmesbury was a fortified burh under Alfred the Great and later a centre of the wool trade. In modern times the town is associated with technology company Dyson, whose headquarters campus lies just outside the town.

Culture

The town holds an annual carnival in August and a festival commemorating Eilmer. Its residents are traditionally nicknamed "Jackdaws", after the birds nesting in the abbey tower.
