Live streaming

Live streaming is the real-time delivery of media over the internet as it is recorded, and by extension the interactive online broadcasts produced by streamers on platforms such as Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Live, TikTok LIVE and Kick. Unlike pre-recorded video, live streams typically include real-time audience interaction through chat, donations and subscriptions.

History

Streaming media emerged in the mid-1990s with services such as RealNetworks' RealAudio and early live event webcasts; the first live video streams of concerts and baseball games followed in 1995. Webcam communities such as JenniCam (1996) and Justin.tv (2007) pioneered lifecasting. Justin.tv's gaming section was spun off as Twitch in 2011 and acquired by Amazon in 2014 for $970 million. Periscope and Meerkat brought mobile live streaming to prominence in 2015, after which live video became a standard feature of major social platforms.

Applications

  • Video games and esports — the largest category on Twitch; streamers broadcast gameplay with live commentary.
  • Livestream shopping (live commerce) — enormously popular in China through Taobao Live and Douyin, blending entertainment with e-commerce.
  • News and events — press conferences, protests, sports and space launches streamed in real time.
  • Education and conferences — lectures, webinars and virtual events, greatly expanded during the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • Social and IRL streaming — "just chatting", music, art and in-real-life broadcasts.

Technology

Broadcasters encode video with software such as OBS Studio and send it to the platform using protocols such as RTMP; viewers receive adaptive-bitrate streams, increasingly delivered with low-latency variants of HLS or WebRTC. Monetisation includes subscriptions, advertising, channel points, tips and brand sponsorships.
