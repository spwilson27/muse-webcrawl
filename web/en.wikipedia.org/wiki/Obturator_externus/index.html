The obturator externus muscle (or obturator externus) is a flat, triangular muscle which covers the outer surface of the anterior wall of the pelvis. It is one of the six short external rotators of the hip, lying deep in the medial compartment of the thigh.

The muscle arises from the margin of bone around the obturator foramen and from the external surface of the obturator membrane. Its fibres converge into a tendon that passes beneath the neck of the femur to insert into the trochanteric fossa of the femur. It is supplied by the posterior branch of the obturator nerve (L3–L4), unlike its counterpart the obturator internus, which is supplied from the sacral plexus.

Function

Like the other short external rotators, the obturator externus laterally (externally) rotates the extended thigh and abducts the flexed thigh, and helps stabilise the head of the femur in the acetabulum.
