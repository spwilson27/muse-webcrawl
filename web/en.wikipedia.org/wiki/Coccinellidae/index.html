Coccinellidae is a widespread family of small beetles, commonly known as ladybugs in North America and ladybirds in the United Kingdom. The family contains more than 6,000 described species worldwide. Most are brightly coloured — typically red, orange or yellow with black spots — a warning (aposematic) coloration advertising their unpleasant taste.

Biology

Ladybirds are oval, dome-shaped beetles from under 1 mm to about 18 mm long, with short clubbed antennae and a large pronotum. The great majority are predators of aphids, scale insects and other plant pests, in both adult and larval stages, which makes them valued agents of biological pest control. A minority feed on plants or fungi; a few, such as the Mexican bean beetle, are agricultural pests. When threatened, ladybirds secrete bitter alkaloid fluid from their leg joints ("reflex bleeding").

In culture and horticulture

Ladybirds are beloved in folklore as bringers of luck and good harvests, and the name is traditionally linked to the Virgin Mary ("Our Lady"). Gardeners buy them for release against aphids, though introduced species such as the harlequin ladybird have become invasive and displace native species.
