Quake (video game)

Quake is a first-person shooter video game developed by id Software and published by GT Interactive, released in 1996. Building on the success of Doom, it was the first game in the medium to feature fully three-dimensional polygonal graphics, real-time lighting and a true 3D environment in which players could look up and down freely. Its dark fantasy and Lovecraftian setting, industrial soundtrack by Trent Reznor of Nine Inch Nails, and pioneering online multiplayer made it one of the most influential games ever made.

Quake's fully 3D engine allowed true vertical aiming and polygonal enemies.

Gameplay

The player, a nameless Ranger, travels through slipgates into other dimensions to stop an enemy code-named "Quake". The single-player campaign spans four episodes of gothic castles, dungeons and military bases, fighting monsters such as the chainsaw-wielding Ogre, the grenade-lobbing Zombie and the lightning-hurling Shambler. At the end, the player confronts Shub-Niggurath, the "All-Mother" of the monsters.

Technology and legacy

The Quake engine, principally programmed by John Carmack with Michael Abrash, introduced precomputed lightmaps, BSP-based visibility and polygonal models, and its code was later released under the GNU General Public License. Quake's internet-playable multiplayer (via QuakeWorld) and its modding community — which produced Team Fortress and helped popularise machinima and speedrunning — reshaped PC gaming; techniques such as rocket jumping became staples of the genre. Sequels followed, beginning with Quake II (1997), and a remastered edition was released in 2021.
