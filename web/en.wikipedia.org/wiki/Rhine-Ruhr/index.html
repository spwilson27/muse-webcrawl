The Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region (Metropolregion Rhein-Ruhr) is a polycentric metropolitan region in the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia, stretching along the Rhine River and the Ruhr valley. With roughly 10 to 11 million inhabitants, it is among the largest metropolitan regions in Europe and the largest in Germany. It combines the historic industrial Ruhr area—Dortmund, Essen, Duisburg, Bochum, Oberhausen—with the Rhineland cities of Düsseldorf (the state capital), Cologne, Bonn, Wuppertal, Mönchengladbach, Krefeld and Leverkusen.

Geography and structure

Unlike monocentric capitals such as Berlin or Paris, Rhine-Ruhr has no single dominant core: its cities grew together into a continuous urban belt where municipal boundaries are often imperceptible. The region covers some 7,000 km². The east–west axis follows the Ruhr and Hellweg corridor; the north–south axis follows the Rhine from Bonn through Cologne and Düsseldorf to Duisburg, site of the world's largest inland port.

History

The Ruhr's coal and steel industries powered the region's nineteenth- and twentieth-century growth; the Rhineland cities added trade, administration, chemicals and media. The structural decline of coal and steel from the 1960s forced a long transition toward services, logistics, education and culture—symbolised by the conversion of collieries such as Zollverein in Essen (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) into museums and design centres. The region was branded "RUHR.2010" when Essen and the Ruhr served as European Capital of Culture.

Transport

Rhine-Ruhr is served by the Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Ruhr (VRR), one of Europe's largest integrated transport associations, coordinating S-Bahn, regional trains, Stadtbahn light rail, trams and buses across most of the region (Cologne and Bonn belong to the neighbouring Verkehrsverbund Rhein-Sieg). Dense autobahn networks and two major airports—Düsseldorf and Cologne Bonn—link the region internationally.

Economy and culture

The region hosts corporate headquarters in energy, logistics, telecommunications, insurance and retail, several major universities (including Bochum, Duisburg-Essen, Dortmund, Düsseldorf and Cologne), and a dense landscape of theatres, museums and football clubs (Borussia Dortmund, FC Schalke 04, 1. FC Köln, Fortuna Düsseldorf and others).
