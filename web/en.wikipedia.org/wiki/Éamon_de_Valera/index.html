Éamon de Valera (first registered as Edward de Valera; 14 October 1882 – 29 August 1975) was an Irish statesman and political leader who dominated twentieth-century Ireland. He served several terms as head of government and head of state, and had a leading role in introducing the 1937 Constitution of Ireland. American-born, he was a commandant in the Easter Rising of 1916, president of Sinn Féin, and founder of the Fianna Fáil party.

Early life and revolution

Born in New York to an Irish mother and a Spanish father who died when he was young, de Valera was sent to live with relatives in County Limerick. He became a teacher of mathematics and an activist in the Gaelic League and the Irish Volunteers. As commandant at Boland's Mill during the Easter Rising, he was the last commander to surrender; his American birth is widely believed to have saved him from execution.

De Valera opposed the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 and led the anti-Treaty side in the Irish Civil War. In 1926 he broke with abstentionist Sinn Féin and founded Fianna Fáil, entering the Dáil in 1927.

Head of government

As President of the Executive Council and then Taoiseach (1932–1948), de Valera dismantled the remaining constitutional links with Britain, promulgated the 1937 Constitution — which declared Ireland a sovereign, independent, democratic state — and kept Ireland neutral during the Second World War ("the Emergency"). His governments pursued economic protectionism and promoted Irish language and culture; the resulting vision of a Catholic, rural, self-sufficient Ireland was later criticized for fostering emigration and stagnation.

Presidency and legacy

Elected President of Ireland in 1959 and re-elected in 1966, de Valera served two seven-year terms, retiring in 1973 at the age of 90. He died in 1975 and was buried in Glasnevin Cemetery after a state funeral. Assessments of his legacy remain divided between admiration for his nation-building and criticism of his conservative social vision and economic record, but his influence on modern Ireland is unmatched among its political figures.
