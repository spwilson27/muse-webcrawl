The Pyramid of Khafre is the second-tallest and second-largest of the three Ancient Egyptian Pyramids of Giza and the tomb of the Fourth-Dynasty pharaoh Khafre (Chefren), who reigned c. 2558–2532 BC. Because it is built on higher ground and at a slightly steeper angle than the Pyramid of Khufu, it appears to be the tallest of the Giza pyramids—a deliberate optical effect, it is often said, of Khafre's ambition.

Structure

The pyramid originally stood 143.5 metres (471 ft) tall with a base of about 215 metres. Unusually, it retains a cap of smooth Tura limestone casing near its apex, the only Giza pyramid to do so, giving a glimpse of how all three pyramids once gleamed white. The core was built of horizontally laid local limestone blocks; the lower courses were cased in pink granite.

Two entrances on the north face lead to the burial chamber, cut into the bedrock, which contains a polished granite sarcophagus sunk into the floor. Giovanni Battista Belzoni, who forced entry in 1818, found the chamber empty, its lid already removed by robbers in antiquity.

The complex

Khafre's mortuary complex is the best preserved of the Giza group. It includes a valley temple of massive granite blocks—where the famous diorite statue of Khafre was found in a well—a causeway, and a mortuary temple. The Great Sphinx of Giza lies within Khafre's complex and is widely believed to have been carved during his reign, its face possibly bearing his features.
