The Yoshinogari site (吉野ヶ里遺跡, Yoshinogari iseki) is a large archaeological complex of the Yayoi period located on the Yoshinogari hills in Saga Prefecture, Kyushu, Japan. It contains the remains of one of the largest moated settlements (kangō shūraku) yet discovered in Japan, occupied for roughly six centuries, and is a leading candidate in debates over the location of Yamatai, the polity ruled by Queen Himiko described in Chinese chronicles.

Discovery and excavation

Development of an industrial park on the site in 1986 led to salvage excavations by Saga Prefecture, which uncovered extensive Yayoi remains. Public interest was intense — newspapers speculated that this was Himiko's capital — and in 1989 the government halted the development plans and preserved the site, which was designated a Special Historic Site in 1991. The Yoshinogari Historical Park opened in 2001.

Features

The settlement was ringed by V-shaped defensive moats and palisades, with watchtowers at the enclosures. Excavations have documented more than 3,000 burials, including jar burials (kamekan), pit dwellings, raised-floor storehouses, and large ceremonial buildings, among them a unique three-storey structure often interpreted as a shrine. Finds include bronze mirrors, weapons and bells (dōtaku), iron tools, glass beads, silk and hemp textiles, and a decorated bronze sword — evidence of continental trade links and a stratified society. A distinctive burial mound in the northern enclosure, containing grave goods suggestive of a chiefly lineage, anchors reconstructions of Yayoi political organisation.

The Yamatai question

The Wei Zhi (Records of Wei) describes the country of Yamatai, ruled by the shaman-queen Himiko in the 3rd century AD. Yoshinogari's scale, fortifications and chronology make it one of two leading candidates — the other being the Kinai region around Nara — though the identification remains unproven and debated among archaeologists.
