Turret

A turret is a small tower that projects vertically from the wall of a building, especially a medieval castle or fortification. Originally defensive structures providing flanking fire and a vantage point, turrets later became largely decorative features of houses and civic buildings.

In fortification

Medieval turrets rose from wall corners, corbelled out from the masonry, and were often capped with conical roofs. They allowed defenders to observe and shoot along the face of the curtain wall. Larger projecting towers are generally distinguished as towers or bastions; a small overhanging turret on a corner is sometimes called a bartizan.

Later uses

In Victorian and Queen Anne architecture, turrets became popular ornamental features on houses, often containing a staircase or a bay of windows. The word is also applied by extension to rotating weapon mounts — gun turrets on warships, tanks, and aircraft — which share the form of a small, turnable tower, though these are treated as a separate technical topic.
