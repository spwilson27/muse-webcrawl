Isaaq genocide

The Isaaq genocide (Somali: Xasuuqii beesha Isaaq), also known as the Hargeisa Holocaust, was the systematic, state-sponsored genocide of Isaaq civilians between 1987 and 1989 by the Somali Republic under the dictatorship of Siad Barre. Estimates of the number killed range from 50,000 to 100,000 or more.

Background

The campaign targeted the Isaaq clan-family, the dominant population of north-western Somalia, in response to the insurgency of the Somali National Movement (SNM). A 1987 government document, known as the "Letter of Death", recommended harsh measures against the Isaaq population of the north.

Events

In 1988 government forces, including artillery and aircraft, virtually destroyed the cities of Hargeisa and Burao; around 90 per cent of Hargeisa was destroyed and tens of thousands fled across the border to Ethiopia. Mass graves have since been documented around the cities, and investigators from Human Rights Watch and the United Nations have described the campaign as genocide.

Legacy

The atrocities remain central to the case of Somaliland, the former British protectorate region, for international recognition of its 1991 declaration of independence. Memorials, including the Hargeisa war memorial, commemorate the victims.
