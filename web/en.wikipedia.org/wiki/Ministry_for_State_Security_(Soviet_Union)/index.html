Ministry for State Security (Soviet Union)

The Ministry of State Security (Russian: Министерство государственной безопасности, Ministerstvo gosudarstvennoy bezopasnosti), abbreviated MGB, was the Soviet secret police and intelligence ministry from 1946 to 1953. It was formed from the People's Commissariat for State Security (NKGB) when the Soviet people's commissariats were renamed ministries in March 1946.

History

The MGB inherited the functions of the wartime NKGB: foreign intelligence, counterintelligence, internal security, surveillance of the population and the armed forces (including the SMERSH military counterintelligence directorate absorbed after the war), border troops, and the guarding of the Communist Party and government leadership.

Under Viktor Abakumov the MGB was implicated in late-Stalinist repressions, including the Leningrad Affair (1949–50), in which leading officials of the Leningrad party organisation were executed. Abakumov was arrested in 1951 amid the Mingrelian Affair and the developing Doctors' plot; he was replaced by Semyon Ignatyev.

Abolition

Immediately after Stalin's death in March 1953, Lavrentiy Beria merged the MGB into the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MVD), which he headed. After Beria's arrest and execution later that year, state security functions were separated again in March 1954 into the newly created Committee for State Security, the KGB.
