N-gram

N-gram
FieldComputational linguistics, natural language processing
Used inLanguage modelling, text classification, information retrieval

An n-gram is a contiguous sequence of n items taken from a given sample of text or speech. The items can be phonemes, syllables, letters, words, or base pairs, depending on the application. When n is 1, the sequence is called a unigram; for 2, a bigram; for 3, a trigram; and "four-gram", "five-gram", and so on for larger values.

N-gram models are widely used in statistical language modelling: the probability of a word given the preceding context is estimated from the frequency of n-grams counted over a large corpus. They underpin applications ranging from speech recognition and machine translation to spelling correction, authorship attribution, and text prediction.

Examples

For the word "probability", the character trigrams are: pro, rob, oba, bab, abi, bil, ili, lit, ity. For the sentence "the cat sat", the word bigrams are "the cat" and "cat sat".

nNameExample (word-level)
1Unigram"the", "cat", "sat"
2Bigram"the cat", "cat sat"
3Trigram"the cat sat"

N-gram models

An n-gram model predicts the next item in a sequence using only the previous n − 1 items, an application of the Markov assumption. Because most possible n-grams never occur in training data, smoothing techniques such as Laplace smoothing and Kneser–Ney smoothing are used to assign non-zero probabilities to unseen sequences. Google's web-scale n-gram collections, released in 2006, popularised their use in search and language technology.

Applications

  • Speech recognition and optical character recognition error correction
  • Plagiarism detection and text categorisation
  • Cryptanalysis, including solving substitution ciphers
  • Genomics, where DNA sequences are analysed as strings over a four-letter alphabet
  • Approximate string matching and information retrieval
