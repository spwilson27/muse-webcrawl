The Revolutionary Front for an Independent East Timor (Portuguese: Frente Revolucionária de Timor-Leste Independente, abbreviated Fretilin) is a political party in Timor-Leste (East Timor). It was founded in May 1974 as the Timorese Social Democratic Association and renamed later that year. Fretilin led the resistance to Indonesian occupation and won the country's first free elections.

History

Following Portugal's Carnation Revolution, Fretilin proclaimed East Timor's independence on 28 November 1975. Days later Indonesia invaded, beginning a 24-year occupation during which Fretilin's armed wing, FALINTIL, waged a guerrilla war and a large portion of the population died from violence, famine and disease. After the 1999 referendum, in which Timorese voted overwhelmingly for independence, Fretilin won 55 of 88 seats in the 2001 Constituent Assembly elections and led the first government of the restored republic, under Prime Minister Mari Alkatiri.

Recent politics

Political violence in 2006 forced Alkatiri's resignation, and Fretilin has since alternated between government and opposition, often in rivalry with Xanana Gusmão's CNRT. Fretilin figures Francisco Guterres and José Ramos-Horta have served as President, and the party remains one of the two dominant forces in Timorese politics, advocating social-democratic policies and close ties with Portuguese-speaking countries.
