Presbyter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Presbyter is an honorific title for Christian clergy in the Catholic, Orthodox, and some other traditions. The word derives from the Greek presbyteros (πρεσβύτερος), meaning "elder", as used in the New Testament for the leaders of local Christian communities. Through Latin presbyter it is also the etymological source of the English word priest.

In the early Church

In the New Testament, presbyters and overseers (episkopoi) appear as the appointed leaders of congregations, charged with teaching, pastoral care, and oversight. By the early 2nd century, writings such as those of Ignatius of Antioch describe a threefold pattern of bishop, presbyters, and deacons, in which the presbyters formed a council around the bishop.

Later usage

In Catholic and Orthodox usage, a presbyter is a priest ordained to celebrate the Eucharist, preach, and administer most sacraments, subordinate to a bishop. In churches of the Reformed tradition, presbyter is retained as the title of elders who, together with ministers, govern congregations — the origin of the name Presbyterian and of the governing body called a presbytery.
