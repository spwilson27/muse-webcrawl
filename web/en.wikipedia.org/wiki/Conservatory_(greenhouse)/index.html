Conservatory (greenhouse)

A domestic conservatory: a glazed room attached to a dwelling, used for plants and as a sunroom.

A conservatory is a building or room having glass or other transparent roofing and walls, used as a greenhouse, sunroom or garden room, usually attached to a conventional building such as a house. The term originally denoted a place for conserving (overwintering) tender plants, and it retains this horticultural association in botanical gardens, where large public conservatories display plant collections under glass.

History

Predecessors include Roman glazed growing structures and the orangeries of the 17th and 18th centuries, built on great estates to shelter citrus trees through northern winters. Advances in iron and glass manufacture in the 19th century, the repeal of the British glass tax (1845), and Joseph Paxton's designs — culminating in the Crystal Palace of 1851 and the Palm House at Kew (1844–48, by Decimus Burton and Richard Turner) — made large curvilinear glasshouses feasible. The Victorian fashion for ferns and exotic plants, aided by the sealed Wardian case for transporting specimens, spread smaller conservatories to middle-class homes, where they served as fernery, garden room and status symbol.

Types and construction

Domestic conservatories are commonly described by plan and roof form: Victorian (faceted, steeply pitched), Edwardian (rectangular), lean-to, gable-front and lantern-roof styles. Modern construction uses aluminium or uPVC framing, dwarf brick walls, and double or polycarbonate glazing with solar-control coatings; temperature regulation through roof vents and shading is essential, since glazed rooms overheat in summer and lose heat in winter. Botanical conservatories — such as those at Kew, the New York Botanical Garden and the Garfield Park Conservatory in Chicago — are larger engineered structures with zoned climates (tropical, temperate, arid) for living collections.

Distinction

A conservatory differs from a standalone greenhouse in being attached to and integrated with a dwelling or institution as living or display space, and from an orangery in its predominantly glazed roof and lighter structure.
