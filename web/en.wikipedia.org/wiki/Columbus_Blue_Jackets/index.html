Columbus Blue Jackets

Founded: 2000
Arena: Nationwide Arena
City: Columbus, Ohio
Conference: Eastern
Division: Metropolitan
Colors: Union blue, goal red, capital silver

The Columbus Blue Jackets (often simply referred to as the Jackets) are a professional ice hockey team based in Columbus, Ohio. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Metropolitan Division in the Eastern Conference, and began play as an expansion team in 2000.

The Blue Jackets play their home games at Nationwide Arena in downtown Columbus. The name "Blue Jackets" celebrates Ohio's rich Civil War history and the Union Army uniforms manufactured in Columbus.

History

Columbus was awarded an NHL expansion franchise in 1997, and the team debuted in the 2000–01 season. The franchise struggled through its early years and did not qualify for the Stanley Cup playoffs until 2009, when it was swept in the first round by the Detroit Red Wings.

The team's most memorable moment came in the 2019 playoffs, when the Blue Jackets swept the Presidents' Trophy-winning Tampa Bay Lightning in the first round — the first playoff series victory in franchise history, and one of the greatest upsets in NHL history.

Traditions

A replica Civil War-era cannon in Nationwide Arena is fired when the Blue Jackets score a goal or win a game. The team's mascot is Stinger, a green anthropomorphic insect in a Blue Jackets uniform.

Notable players

Notable players in franchise history include Rick Nash, the team's first true star and all-time scoring leader of the franchise's early era; Sergei Bobrovsky, two-time Vezina Trophy winner with Columbus; Nick Foligno, a long-serving captain; and Johnny Gaudreau.
