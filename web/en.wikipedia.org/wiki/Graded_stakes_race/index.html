Graded stakes race

Grade I races such as the Kentucky Derby are the highest class of Thoroughbred contests.

A graded stakes race is a Thoroughbred horse race whose prestige and quality have been formally classified by an official grading committee. In North America races are designated Grade I (the highest), Grade II or Grade III, with Listed stakes ranking just below the graded level. The equivalent races in most other racing jurisdictions are called Group races (Group 1, 2 and 3) under the international Pattern system.

North American grading

The American Graded Stakes Committee of the Thoroughbred Owners and Breeders Association (TOBA) has assigned grades since 1973. To be eligible for grading, a race must have been run for at least two years under substantially the same conditions, carry a minimum purse (the threshold varies by grade and has been raised over time), be open to eligible horses without restrictive entry fees beyond nomination and starting fees, and be run in compliance with medication and testing rules. The committee reviews race quality annually using statistical measures of the fields' previous and subsequent performance, and may upgrade or downgrade races.

Canada maintains its own grading committee under The Jockey Club of Canada. Famous Grade I events include the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, Belmont Stakes, Breeders' Cup races, the Travers Stakes and the Arlington Million.

Group races and the Pattern

In Europe the Pattern was introduced in 1971, classifying races as Group 1, 2 or 3 (with Listed races below). The European Pattern Committee supervises the system, which now extends — through the International Federation of Horseracing Authorities' catalogue standards — to Japan (where races formerly graded "JpnI" have been progressively recognised internationally), Australia, South Africa, South America and the Middle East. Iconic Group 1 races include the Prix de l'Arc de Triomphe, the Derby at Epsom, the King George VI and Queen Elizabeth Stakes and the Dubai World Cup.

Importance

Graded/Group status is central to the bloodstock economy: wins and placings in top-level races ("black type" in sale catalogues, printed in bold) enormously increase a horse's value at stud or as a broodmare, and stallion fees are largely set by the graded success of their progeny. Because grading depends on the quality of recent fields, promoters compete through prize money to attract top horses and preserve or improve their races' grades.
