Stanisław Herman Lem (12 September 1921 – 27 March 2006) was a Polish writer of science fiction and essays on philosophy, technology and literary theory. His works have been translated into over 50 languages and have sold more than 45 million copies, making him one of the most widely read science-fiction writers in the world.

Life

Lem was born in Lwów (now Lviv, Ukraine) to a Jewish family. He studied medicine during the Second World War and settled in Kraków after the war, when Lwów was annexed by the Soviet Union. Under the communist regime he published in relative freedom by working in the science fiction genre.

Works

His best-known novel, Solaris (1961), about an alien ocean planet that defies human comprehension, was filmed by Andrei Tarkovsky (1972) and Steven Soderbergh (2002). Other major works include Fiasco, The Cyberiad, His Master's Voice, and the non-fiction treatise Summa Technologiae (1964), which anticipated virtual reality, biotechnology and artificial intelligence.

Themes

Lem's writing explores the limits of human communication and understanding, the nature of intelligence, and the unintended consequences of technology, often with satirical humour.
