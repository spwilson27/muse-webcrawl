The BMW 7 Series is a full-size luxury sedan manufactured by the German automaker BMW since 1977. It is the successor to the BMW E3 "New Six" sedans and is currently in its seventh generation. The 7 Series is BMW's flagship car, traditionally introducing technologies and design themes later adopted across the range, and competes principally with the Mercedes-Benz S-Class and Audi A8.

Generations

  • E23 (1977–1986) — the original 7 Series, with six-cylinder engines and, later, a turbocharged model.
  • E32 (1986–1994) — introduced the first post-war German production V12 engine in the 750i, plus features such as xenon headlights and traction control.
  • E38 (1994–2001) — the first European production car available with satellite navigation; offered in diesel, V8, and V12 forms and armoured "Protection" versions.
  • E65 (2001–2008) — controversial "flame surfacing" styling under Chris Bangle and the debut of the iDrive control system and a hydrogen-powered variant, the Hydrogen 7.
  • F01 (2008–2015) — introduced an eight-speed automatic, xDrive all-wheel drive on the flagship, and a hybrid (ActiveHybrid 7).
  • G11/G12 (2015–2022) — "Carbon Core" lightweight construction, gesture control, and a plug-in hybrid 740e/745e.
  • G70 (2022–present) — offered with petrol, diesel, plug-in hybrid, and the fully electric i7, with an optional 31-inch rear "Theatre Screen".

Variants and production

Long-wheelbase versions, badged with an "L", dominate sales in many markets, and high-performance variants have included the Alpina B7. Most 7 Series cars have been built at BMW's Dingolfing plant in Germany. The model is also a popular basis for chauffeur-driven, government, and hotel fleets.
