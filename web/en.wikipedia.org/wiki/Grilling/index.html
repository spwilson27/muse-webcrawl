Grilling

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Meat and vegetables grilling over charcoal]

Grilling is a form of cooking that involves dry heat applied to the surface of food from above, below or both, usually over charcoal, gas or electric elements via a gridiron or grill pan. It is among humanity's oldest cooking methods — direct fire on meat long predates pottery — and remains central from American barbecue culture (where "grilling" is distinguished from low-and-slow barbecuing) to Korean barbecue, Argentine asado, Japanese robata and yakitori, and the Mediterranean grill.

High heat sears surfaces (Maillard browning), renders fat and imparts smoke flavour from dripping juices. Marinades, rubs and bastes season the food; resting after grilling redistributes juices.

1. Health notes

Health notes

Charring meat at high temperatures forms heterocyclic amines and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, compounds linked with cancer risk in animal studies; marinating, avoiding flare-ups and not over-charring reduce them.
