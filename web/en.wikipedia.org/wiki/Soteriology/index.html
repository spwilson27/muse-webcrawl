Soteriology

Soteriology (from Greek sōtēria, "salvation", from sōtēr, "saviour, preserver") is the study of religious doctrines of salvation. Salvation theory occupies a place of special significance in many religions. In the academic field of religious studies, soteriology is understood by scholars as representing a key theme in a number of different religions and is often studied in a comparative context; that is, scholars may compare how different religions and traditions articulate the problem of the human condition and its remedy.

In Christianity

Christian soteriology centres on the person and work of Jesus Christ: his incarnation, crucifixion and resurrection are held to deliver humanity from sin and death. Different traditions emphasize different aspects — theories of the atonement include the ransom theory, Christus Victor, satisfaction (associated with Anselm of Canterbury), penal substitution, moral influence and governmental theories. Debates over grace, faith, justification and human free will distinguished Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Protestant traditions; Protestant Reformers taught justification by faith alone (sola fide), while Catholic theology stresses the cooperation of grace and works within a sacramental framework.

In other traditions

In Judaism, salvation is closely tied to the covenant, repentance (teshuvah), observance of the commandments and hopes for a messianic redemption. In Islam, salvation depends on faith in God, repentance and righteous deeds, with ultimate judgment resting on God's mercy. In Hinduism, the central soteriological goal is moksha — liberation from the cycle of rebirth (samsara) — pursued through paths of knowledge, devotion and action. Buddhism seeks release from suffering and rebirth through awakening (bodhi) and nirvana, following the Noble Eightfold Path and the insight that there is no permanent self. Jainism similarly aims at liberating the soul from karmic bondage, and Sikhism teaches liberation through devotion to God and selfless service.

Comparative themes

Scholars compare soteriologies by asking what humans need saving from (sin, ignorance, suffering, death), what the saved state consists in, and by what means it is attained — divine grace, ritual, knowledge, ethical action or meditative discipline.
