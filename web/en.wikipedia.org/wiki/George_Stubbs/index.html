George Stubbs

George Stubbs ARA (25 August 1724 – 10 July 1806) was an English painter, best known for his paintings of horses.

Born: Liverpool, England
Died: London
Notable works: Whistlejacket (c. 1762); The Anatomy of the Horse (1766)

George Stubbs was an English painter, best known for his masterful paintings of horses and other animals, grounded in a scientific study of anatomy that set him apart from earlier sporting artists.

Early life and anatomy

Stubbs was born in Liverpool, the son of a currier and leather merchant. Largely self-taught as an artist, he worked as a portrait painter in northern England and studied anatomy at York, illustrating a textbook on midwifery (1751), before visiting Rome in 1754. On returning to England he settled at Horkstow in Lincolnshire, where for some eighteen months he dissected horses in a barn, suspending carcasses from the ceiling to draw each layer of muscle. The result was The Anatomy of the Horse (1766), a landmark work of scientific illustration that he engraved himself.

Painting career

The anatomical studies brought Stubbs commissions from aristocratic patrons of the turf. For the Marquess of Rockingham he painted Whistlejacket (c. 1762), the famous near-life-size portrait of a rearing racehorse against a plain ground, now in the National Gallery, London. Stubbs painted racehorses, mares and foals, grooms and stable scenes, as well as more dramatic subjects — including a celebrated series on the theme of a lion attacking a horse — and exotic animals such as zebras, kangaroos, and a cheetah.

Later work and legacy

Stubbs experimented with painting in enamel on Wedgwood ceramic plaques, became an associate of the Royal Academy in 1780, and spent his last years on a comparative anatomy of man, tiger, and fowl. He died in London in 1806. Long valued chiefly by the racing world, he is now regarded as one of the greatest British artists of the eighteenth century.
