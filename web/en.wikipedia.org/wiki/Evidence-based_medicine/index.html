Evidence-based medicine

Evidence-based medicine (EBM) is "the conscientious, explicit and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about the care of individual patients". It integrates clinical expertise, the best available research evidence, and patient values and preferences.

History and methods

EBM emerged in the early 1990s around David Sackett, Gordon Guyatt and the McMaster University group, building on Archie Cochrane's call for systematic summaries of randomised trials. Evidence is graded: systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials rank highest, followed by individual RCTs, cohort, case-control and case studies, and expert opinion. Tools such as GRADE help rate quality and strength of recommendations. Organisations such as Cochrane produce systematic reviews.

Criticism

Critics note publication bias, industry funding effects, over-reliance on average effects that may not fit individual patients, and limited evidence for many real-world decisions. Supporters reply that EBM explicitly includes clinical judgement and patient values.
