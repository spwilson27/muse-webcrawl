Rutuli

The Rutuli or Rutulians were an ancient Italic people of Latium in central Italy, closely related to the Latins. Their chief town was Ardea, south of Rome, which tradition held to have been founded by Danaë. In Roman mythology and in Virgil's epic poem the Aeneid, the Rutuli under their king Turnus were the principal opponents of Aeneas and the Trojan settlers in Italy.

History and origins

Ancient sources differ on the origins of the Rutuli: some traditions linked them with the Ligurians, others with the Umbrians or with Greek colonists. Modern scholarship treats them as an Italic people of Latium, possibly with a distinct dialect closely related to Latin. Historically attested into the early Roman Republic, Ardea appears among the Latin cities and was involved in the wars of early Rome; by the 4th century BCE the Rutuli had been absorbed into the Roman state and lost their separate identity, though Ardea retained municipal status and was refounded as a Roman colony in 442 BCE.

In the Aeneid

In books 7–12 of Virgil's Aeneid, Turnus, king of the Rutuli, is betrothed to Lavinia, daughter of King Latinus of the Latins. When Latinus, guided by omens and oracles, promises Lavinia instead to the newly arrived Aeneas, Juno stirs war through the fury Allecto. Turnus leads the Italic coalition against the Trojans and their allies. The war culminates in a single combat between Aeneas and Turnus; after Turnus is wounded and pleads for mercy, Aeneas, catching sight of the sword-belt of the slain Pallas on Turnus's shoulder, kills him in a rage—the abrupt and much-debated ending of the poem.

Language

Almost nothing survives of any distinctive Rutulian language. A handful of inscriptions from the territory of Ardea, including the debated "Rutulian" fragment from the Satricum area and the Lapis Satricanus (which is attributed to the Volscians or Latins), have been claimed for the Rutuli, but the evidence is too scant to define a separate language; most scholars consider their speech a form of Latino-Faliscan close to Latin.
