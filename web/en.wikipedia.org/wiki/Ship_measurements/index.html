Ship measurements

Ship measurements consist of a multitude of terms and definitions specifically related to ships and measuring or defining their characteristics. Because ships vary greatly in form and function, several distinct quantities — some measuring volume, others weight, and others linear dimensions — are used simultaneously, and confusion between them (notably between the several kinds of "tonnage" and "displacement") is common.

Linear dimensions

Length overall (LOA) is the maximum length of the hull between the extreme ends; length between perpendiculars (LBP) is measured between the forward and after perpendiculars at the load waterline, and length at the waterline (LWL) is the length of the hull where it meets the water. Beam is the maximum width of the hull; depth is the vertical distance from keel to the main deck; draft (or draught) is the vertical distance from the waterline to the keel, and hence varies with loading; freeboard is the distance from the waterline to the deck. Air draft is the height above the waterline of the highest point of the vessel, relevant to passing under bridges.

Tonnage and displacement

Displacement is a weight: the mass of water displaced by the floating hull, equal to the vessel's own total weight. Light displacement excludes cargo, fuel, and crew; loaded displacement includes them. Deadweight tonnage (DWT) is the difference between loaded and light displacement — the carrying capacity for cargo, fuel, stores, and crew. By contrast, gross tonnage (GT) and net tonnage (NT), governed by the International Convention on Tonnage Measurement of Ships (1969), are dimensionless logarithmic measures of the vessel's enclosed volume and earning capacity respectively, used for registration, port dues, and manning rules. Older measures include gross register tonnage (GRT), in which one "ton" equalled 100 cubic feet of enclosed space, and specialised canal tonnages for the Panama and Suez canals.
