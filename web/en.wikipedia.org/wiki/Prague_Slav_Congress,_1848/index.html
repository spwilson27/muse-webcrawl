Prague Slav Congress, 1848

The Prague Slav Congress of 1848 was a gathering of Slavic delegates from across the Austrian Empire and beyond, held in Prague from 2 to 12 June 1848 during the Revolutions of 1848. It was the first major political manifestation of pan-Slavism and sought equal rights for the Slavic peoples within a federalised Austrian monarchy.

Background

The Congress was convened partly in response to the German Frankfurt Parliament, whose programme seemed to threaten Slav interests in Bohemia. Czech historian František Palacký, who had famously declined an invitation to Frankfurt with the argument that a strong multinational Austria was necessary, presided over the proceedings.

Proceedings

Some 340 delegates attended, organised in three sections: Czecho-Slovak, Polish-Ruthenian (Ukrainian) and South Slav. The Russian anarchist Mikhail Bakunin also participated. The Congress drafted a Manifesto to the Nations of Europe demanding equality of nationalities and an end to oppression, and proposed transforming Austria into a federation of equal peoples.

Suppression

On 12 June, Pentecost demonstrations in Prague escalated into an uprising against the Austrian garrison. The military commander, Prince Alfred of Windischgrätz, bombarded the city, and the Congress dispersed amid the fighting. The Prague uprising was crushed within days, marking an early defeat for the revolutions of 1848 in the Habsburg lands.
