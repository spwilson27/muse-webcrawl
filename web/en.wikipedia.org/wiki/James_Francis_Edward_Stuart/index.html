James Francis Edward Stuart (10 June 1688 – 1 January 1766), nicknamed the Old Pretender by Whigs, was the son of the deposed King James II and VII of England, Scotland and Ireland and his second wife, Mary of Modena. As the Jacobite claimant to the thrones of England, Scotland and Ireland from 1701, he styled himself James III and VIII. His birth to a Catholic royal couple displaced the Protestant succession and helped precipitate the Glorious Revolution of 1688.

Early life

James was born at St James's Palace amid wild rumours — promoted by his parents' enemies — that the baby was an impostor smuggled into the bedchamber in a warming pan. His birth to the Catholic king by his Catholic queen meant the prospect of a Catholic dynasty, displacing James II's Protestant daughters Mary and Anne; within months William of Orange invaded, James II fled to France, and the infant prince was carried into exile. He was raised at the Château de Saint-Germain-en-Laye under the protection of Louis XIV of France.

Claim and the Fifteen

On his father's death in 1701, Louis XIV recognised James as king of England, Scotland and Ireland, provoking outrage in Britain. The Act of Settlement 1701 settled the succession on the Protestant House of Hanover. James attempted to land in Scotland in 1708 but was frustrated by the Royal Navy. In 1715, following the accession of George I, the Jacobite rising known as "the Fifteen" broke out under the Earl of Mar; James landed at Peterhead in December 1715, but the rising collapsed after the inconclusive Battle of Sheriffmuir and the surrender at Preston, and he returned to France within weeks — earning his melancholy reputation among supporters.

Later life

James married Maria Clementina Sobieska, a granddaughter of John III Sobieski of Poland, in 1719. They settled in Rome at the Palazzo Muti, granted by the Pope, where their sons Charles Edward ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") and Henry Benedict were born. James gave Charles his blessing for the rising of 1745 but took no part in it himself. He lived the rest of his life in Rome, maintaining a small exiled court, and died on 1 January 1766. He is buried in St Peter's Basilica, where a monument to the Stuart claimants stands.

Legacy

After James's death the Jacobite claim passed to Charles and then to Henry, Cardinal York, with whose death in 1807 the direct Stuart line ended. The Holy See never again recognised the claim after 1766, effectively conceding the Hanoverian succession.
