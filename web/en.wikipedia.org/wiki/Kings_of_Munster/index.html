Kings of Munster

The Rock of Cashel was the ceremonial seat of the kings of Munster.

The kings of Munster ruled the southern Irish province of Munster from late antiquity until the 12th century. For much of the early medieval period the kingship was dominated by the Eóganachta, a confederation of dynasties claiming descent from Eógan Mór, seated at Cashel. In the 10th century power shifted to the Dál gCais of Thomond, from whom came Brian Bóruma (Brian Boru), who seized the kingship of Munster in 978 and went on to become High King of Ireland.

History

Early kings include Cathal mac Finguine (died 742) and Feidlimid mac Cremthanin (820–847), a cleric-king who raided monasteries across Ireland. The Viking wars disrupted succession in the 9th and 10th centuries. After Brian Boru's death at Clontarf in 1014, his descendants, the Ua Briain (O'Brien) dynasty, held Munster, but the province was divided in 1118 into the kingdoms of Thomond and Desmond, the latter dominated by the MacCarthy family. The Anglo-Norman invasion after 1169 ended the independent kingship.
