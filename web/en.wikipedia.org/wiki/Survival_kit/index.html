Survival kit

A survival kit is a package of basic tools and supplies prepared as an aid to survival in an emergency. Civilian and military aircraft, lifeboats, and spacecraft are equipped with survival kits. They may also be carried by hikers, campers, and emergency-preparedness enthusiasts, and can range from a small tin of essentials to a full "bug-out bag" intended to sustain a person for 72 hours or longer.

Contents

Typical contents address the core survival priorities: shelter, water, fire, food, first aid, and signalling. Common items include a knife or multitool, waterproof matches or a firestarter, cordage, a compass and map, water purification tablets or a filter, emergency food rations, a first aid kit, a whistle and signal mirror, a torch, and an emergency blanket or bivouac sack. Military aircrew kits additionally may include a survival rifle, fishing tackle, and snare wire.

Types

Kits are often classified by context: aviation and maritime kits mandated by regulation; personal kits carried in vehicles or on the person; and household emergency kits recommended by agencies such as the Red Cross and FEMA, generally intended to cover at least 72 hours without outside assistance.
