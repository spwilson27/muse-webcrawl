Christopher Codrington

Born: 1668, Barbados
Died: April 7, 1710, Barbados
Occupation: Colonial governor, soldier, plantation owner, scholar

Christopher Codrington III (1668 – 7 April 1710) was a Barbadian-born colonial governor, soldier, plantation owner, and scholar. He served as governor of the Leeward Islands and was a major benefactor of education in the Caribbean and at Oxford.

Life

Codrington inherited large sugar plantations and enslaved-workforce estates in Barbados and Antigua from his father and grandfather. Educated at Christ Church, Oxford, and elected a fellow of All Souls College, he served as a soldier in Flanders before being appointed captain-general and governor-in-chief of the Leeward Islands in 1699. His administration and military campaigns against the French during the War of the Spanish Succession were controversial, and complaints about his conduct led to his removal in 1704.

Legacy

By his will, Codrington left his Barbados plantations to the Society for the Propagation of the Gospel to endow a college in Barbados — which became Codrington College — along with his library and a bequest of £10,000 to All Souls College, Oxford, funding the Codrington Library. The town of Codrington, Barbuda, bears the family name. In the 21st century, institutions have re-examined his legacy as a slave owner.
