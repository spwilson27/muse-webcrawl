Dartmoor pony

The Dartmoor pony is a breed of pony that lives on Dartmoor in Devon, England, where herds have grazed the open moor for centuries. Hardy and sure-footed, the breed is one of Britain's native mountain and moorland ponies and is recognised for its gentle temperament, making it a popular children's riding pony.

History

Ponies have lived on Dartmoor since prehistoric times; a hoofprint preserved in peat has been dated to around 3,500 years ago. In the Middle Ages they were used as pack animals for the tin-mining industry, and in the 19th and early 20th centuries many were sent to work in coal mines as pit ponies. The Dartmoor Pony Society was founded in 1923 to maintain the stud book and breed standard. Numbers of semi-feral ponies on the moor have declined sharply from tens of thousands in the early 20th century to roughly a thousand or fewer, as markets for moorland-bred foals contracted, and conservation bodies now promote their use in conservation grazing and as riding ponies.

Characteristics

The breed standard calls for a pony not exceeding 12.2 hands, with a small head, strong body, and thick winter coat suited to the exposed moor. Dartmoor ponies are known for stamina, kind temperament, and a smooth action that makes them successful show and riding ponies.
