The Procter & Gamble Company (P&G) is an American multinational consumer goods corporation headquartered in Cincinnati, Ohio, founded in 1837 by William Procter and James Gamble. It specializes in a wide range of personal health/consumer health, personal care and hygiene products; these products are organized into several segments including beauty; grooming; health care; fabric and home care; and baby, feminine, and family care.

P&G's major brands include Tide, Ariel, Pampers, Gillette, Oral-B, Head & Shoulders, Pantene, Olay, Crest, Dawn, Febreze, Bounty, Charmin, and Always. Before the sale of Pringles to Kellogg's for $2.7 billion in 2012, its product range also included food, snacks, and beverages.

History

Candlemaker William Procter and soapmaker James Gamble, immigrants from England and Ireland respectively, settled in Cincinnati and married sisters. Their father-in-law persuaded them to become business partners, and on October 31, 1837 they signed a partnership agreement. The company grew supplying soap and candles to the Union Army during the American Civil War. In 1879, James Norris Gamble developed Ivory soap, a cheap white soap that floated in water; it became one of the company's signature products.

In the 20th century P&G became a pioneer of brand management, market research, and mass-media advertising, sponsoring radio and television serial dramas — the origin of the term "soap opera". Crisco shortening (1911), Tide detergent (1946), Pampers disposable diapers (1961), and Crest toothpaste followed. Acquisitions included Richardson-Vicks (1985), Tambrands (1997), and Gillette (2005) for $57 billion.

Operations

P&G sells products in about 180 countries and employs around 107,000 people. Annual net sales are on the order of $80 billion. The company is a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average. See also Consumer goods and Gillette.
