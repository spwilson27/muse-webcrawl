The Apprentice (American TV series)

The Apprentice is an American reality television program that judges the business skills of a group of contestants. It ran in various formats across fifteen seasons, on NBC, from January 8, 2004, to February 16, 2017.

Format

In the original format, contestants competing for a job as head of one of Donald Trump's companies were divided into two "corporations" and completed weekly business tasks, such as selling lemonade, renting out apartments, or running advertising campaigns. At the end of each episode, the project manager of the losing team and one or two selected teammates were brought into a boardroom meeting, after which Trump would eliminate one contestant by saying, "You're fired." The last remaining contestant was hired with a starting contract worth $250,000 and a one-year term.

The show was set in New York City, with Trump Tower serving as the production's headquarters, and its opening credits featured The O'Jays song "For the Love of Money".

The Celebrity Apprentice

Beginning with the eighth season in 2008, the series shifted to The Celebrity Apprentice format, in which well-known public figures competed for money for charities of their choice. Winners of the celebrity editions included Piers Morgan, Joan Rivers, Bret Michaels, John Rich, Arsenio Hall, Trace Adkins, Leeza Gibbons, and Matt Iseman.

Reception and legacy

The first season was a major ratings success for NBC and helped revive the reality competition genre on network television. It spawned numerous international adaptations, as well as a spin-off hosted by Martha Stewart. The fifteenth season, The New Celebrity Apprentice, was hosted by Arnold Schwarzenegger and was the final season produced.
