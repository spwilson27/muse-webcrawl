San Siro, officially the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza, is a football stadium in the San Siro district of Milan, Italy. It is the home of the city's two major clubs, AC Milan and Inter Milan, and is one of the most famous football venues in the world. The stadium is named after Giuseppe Meazza, the two-time World Cup winner who played for both Milan clubs, while "San Siro" is the name of the surrounding district.

History

Construction began in 1925 under AC Milan president Piero Pirelli, and the stadium opened in 1926 with a Milan derby. It was expanded in stages — a second tier in the 1950s and a third ring supported by eleven cylindrical towers, added for the 1990 FIFA World Cup, when it hosted the opening match. The stadium hosted matches at the 1934 and 1990 World Cups and the 1980 European Championship, as well as four European Cup/Champions League finals (1965, 1970, 2001, and 2016).

Future

The two clubs and the city have debated replacing San Siro with a new stadium for many years. Plans advanced in the 2020s for the clubs to acquire the site and build a new arena nearby, with the existing stadium's long-term fate — preservation of parts of the structure versus demolition — remaining a subject of public and legal dispute.

Other uses

Beyond football, San Siro has hosted major concerts by artists such as Bruce Springsteen and Madonna, rugby internationals, and boxing. It was also selected as a venue for the opening ceremony of the 2026 Winter Olympics.
