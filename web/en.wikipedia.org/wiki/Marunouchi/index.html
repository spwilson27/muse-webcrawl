Marunouchi

Marunouchi (丸の内) is a commercial district of Tokyo located in Chiyoda between Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace. The name, meaning "inside the circle", derives from its location within the palace's outer moat. It is one of Japan's premier business districts, home to the headquarters of many of the country's largest banks, trading companies, and the Mitsubishi group.

History

In the Edo period the area inside the outer moat was occupied by the mansions of daimyo. After the Meiji Restoration the land became an army parade ground. In 1890 the government sold it to Iwasaki Yanosuke of Mitsubishi for 1.5 million yen; the district was mockingly called "Mitsubishi Meadow" (Mitsubishi-ga-hara). Mitsubishi developed it as Japan's first modern office district, modelled on London — its red-brick first building, the Mitsubishi Ichigokan, opened in 1894, earning the area the nickname "Icchō Rondon" ("one block of London").

Modern district

Tokyo Station's red-brick Marunouchi building opened in 1914 and was restored to its original domed form in 2012. The district continues to be redeveloped by Mitsubishi Estate with towers such as the Marunouchi Building and Shin-Marunouchi Building, Marunouchi Oazo, and the brick square by the rebuilt Mitsubishi Ichigokan Museum. Together with neighbouring Ōtemachi and Yūrakuchō, it forms the core of Tokyo's central business district.
