Macaron

A macaron is a sweet meringue-based confection made with egg white, icing sugar, granulated sugar, almond meal and food colouring. It consists of two almond meringue shells sandwiched together with a filling such as ganache, buttercream or jam. Macarons are characterised by a smooth, domed top, a ruffled base known as the "feet" (pied), and a moist, chewy interior.

History

Macarons are commonly traced to Italy, where almond meringue biscuits were produced in Venetian monasteries from the Renaissance. They are said to have arrived in France in 1533 with the chefs of Catherine de' Medici. The filled sandwich form was popularised in Paris in the early twentieth century, notably by the pâtisserie Ladurée, whose employee Pierre Desfontaines is credited with the idea of joining two shells with ganache.

Preparation

Making macarons requires aged egg whites whipped to stiff peaks, folded carefully with almond flour and icing sugar in a process called macaronage. The batter is piped into rounds, rested until a skin forms, and baked so the shells rise and develop their characteristic feet. Fillings range from chocolate ganache and salted caramel to fruit curds and matcha cream.

Variations

The macaron should not be confused with the macaroon, a coconut-based confection. Regional French variants include the macarons of Nancy, Amiens and Montmorency, which differ in texture and recipe from the Parisian style.
