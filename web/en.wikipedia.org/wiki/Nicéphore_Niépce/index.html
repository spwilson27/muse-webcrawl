Nicéphore Niépce

Born7 March 1765, Chalon-sur-Saône, France
Died5 July 1833, Saint-Loup-de-Varennes, France
Known forEarliest surviving photograph; Pyréolophore engine

Joseph Nicéphore Niépce (7 March 1765 – 5 July 1833) was a French inventor and one of the inventors of photography. He developed heliography, a technique he used to create the oldest surviving product of a photographic process: a print made from a photoengraved printing plate in 1825, and the earliest known surviving photograph from nature, View from the Window at Le Gras (1826 or 1827).

Early life and the Pyréolophore

Niépce was born into a wealthy family in Chalon-sur-Saône. With his brother Claude he invented the Pyréolophore, one of the world's first internal combustion engines, powered by lycopodium powder, which propelled a boat on the river Saône in 1807.

Heliography

Seeking a way to copy images onto lithographic stone, Niépce experimented with light-sensitive materials and settled on bitumen of Judea dissolved in lavender oil, coated on pewter plates. Exposure to light hardened the bitumen; the unhardened remainder could be washed away. Around 1826 he placed such a plate in a camera obscura at his estate of Le Gras and exposed it for many hours, producing the famous view of rooftops and a barn. In 1829 he entered a partnership with Louis Daguerre, who after Niépce's death developed the daguerreotype process.

Legacy

The Niépce Heliograph at the Harry Ransom Center in Austin, Texas, preserves the Le Gras plate. A crater on the Moon is named in his honour.
