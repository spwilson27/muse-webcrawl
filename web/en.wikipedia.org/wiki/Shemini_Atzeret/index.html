Shemini Atzeret (Hebrew: שְׁמִינִי עֲצֶרֶת, "Eighth [Day] of Assembly") is a Jewish holiday observed on the 22nd day of the Hebrew month of Tishrei, immediately following the seven-day festival of Sukkot. Although closely associated with Sukkot, rabbinic tradition treats it as a distinct festival in its own right — a final, intimate day of celebration after the public festivities, often compared in midrash to a king who invites his son to remain for one more small meal after the guests of a great banquet have departed.

Observance

In Israel, Shemini Atzeret is combined with Simchat Torah in a single day; in the Diaspora, Simchat Torah is observed on the following day, the 23rd of Tishrei. The holiday is a yom tov on which work is prohibited. Its distinctive liturgical feature is Tefillat Geshem, the prayer for rain, after which the blessing for rain (mashiv ha-ruach u-morid ha-geshem) is inserted into the Amidah throughout the winter season in Israel. The memorial service Yizkor is recited in many communities.

Customs

Opinions differ on whether one still eats in the sukkah on Shemini Atzeret: the blessing for dwelling in the sukkah is no longer said, but many Diaspora Jews continue to take meals there. The book of Ecclesiastes (Kohelet), read on Sukkot, is associated with this part of the festival season in Ashkenazi custom.
