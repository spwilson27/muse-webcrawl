Orgelbüchlein

A page from Bach's autograph of the Orgelbüchlein.

The Orgelbüchlein ("Little Organ Book"), BWV 599–644, is a set of 46 chorale preludes for organ — one of them in two versions — by Johann Sebastian Bach. All but three were composed during Bach's tenure as organist at the ducal court in Weimar, roughly between 1708 and 1717.

Purpose

On the title page Bach explained that the collection was intended for a beginning organist as instruction in developing a chorale in many diverse ways, and acquiring facility in the study of the pedal — since in the chorales contained in it the pedal is treated entirely obbligato. The preludes are arranged according to the Lutheran liturgical year, from Advent through Pentecost, followed by settings grouped by theme such as catechism chorales.

Style

Most of the preludes are short, presenting the chorale melody in one voice while the other voices weave a motivic accompaniment derived from a figure associated with the chorale's text. Despite their brevity the pieces are regarded as masterpieces of the organ repertoire, combining compositional rigour with expressive intensity. The collection was left incomplete: Bach's plan, outlined in the autograph, listed 164 chorale titles.
