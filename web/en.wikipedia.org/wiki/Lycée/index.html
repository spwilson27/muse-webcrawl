Lycée

A lycée (French pronunciation: [lise]) is a secondary school in France, and in countries with education systems modelled on the French system, providing the final three years of secondary education for students generally aged 15 to 18. The lycée corresponds broadly to the last years of high school in other systems.

History

The lycées were created by Napoleon Bonaparte in 1802, replacing earlier royal and revolutionary schools with state-run institutions intended to form an educated administrative elite. Historically boys-only and highly selective, they were opened to girls in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and democratised over the course of the twentieth century.

Types and curriculum

French lycées fall mainly into three categories: the lycée général (general/academic), the lycée technologique (technological), and the lycée professionnel (vocational). The three grades are known as seconde, première and terminale. Study culminates in the baccalauréat examination, which qualifies students for university entrance. Elite Parisian lycées such as Louis-le-Grand and Henri-IV are particularly prestigious feeders to the grandes écoles. French lycées abroad, administered through the AEFE network, provide French-medium education worldwide.
