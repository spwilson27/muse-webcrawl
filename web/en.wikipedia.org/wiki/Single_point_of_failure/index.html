Single point of failure

Single point of failure (SPOF)
Field: Systems engineering, reliability
Mitigation: Redundancy, failover, load balancing
Related: Fault tolerance, high availability

A single point of failure (SPOF) is a part of a system that, if it fails, will stop the entire system from working. SPOFs are undesirable in any system with a goal of high availability or reliability, be it a business practice, software application, electrical grid, or other industrial system.

Overview

Systems are made robust by adding redundancy at the component level: duplicated hardware, multiple network paths, or replicated data. If a component has no backup, it becomes a potential single point of failure. Identifying SPOFs typically involves mapping dependencies and performing failure-mode analysis, asking of each element: "if this stops, what else stops?"

Examples

A single server hosting a website without a failover machine; one power supply or one data centre; a lone network link between sites; or, in an organisation, a "bus factor" of one — a single person with critical knowledge. Famous outages have repeatedly traced back to SPOFs such as a lone configuration file, one DNS provider, or a single undersea cable landing point.

Mitigation

Common remedies include clustering and load balancing, automatic failover, redundant power and network feeds, geographic distribution, and chaos-engineering exercises that deliberately fail components to expose hidden SPOFs. Total elimination is often uneconomical, so engineering focuses on the SPOFs whose failure would be most damaging.
