Ground bass

Ground bass (also called basso ostinato, Italian for "obstinate bass") is a short theme or harmonic pattern in the bass that is constantly repeated while the melody and harmony above it change. It was a central compositional technique of Baroque music, underlying forms such as the passacaglia and chaconne.

History and examples

Ground bass patterns circulated widely in the 16th and 17th centuries. Famous patterns include the romanesca, the passamezzo, and La Folia. Henry Purcell's aria "When I am laid in earth" (Dido's Lament) from Dido and Aeneas is built over a descending chromatic ground. Johann Pachelbel's Canon in D major is built on a two-bar ground bass of eight notes.

The technique survives in later music: the passacaglia finale of Brahms's Fourth Symphony, Britten's works, and, in popular music, looping bass lines in blues, rock, and hip hop can be seen as descendants of the ostinato bass.
