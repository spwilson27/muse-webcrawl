Kinase

A kinase is an enzyme that catalyses the transfer of phosphate groups from high-energy, phosphate-donating molecules to specific substrates. This process is known as phosphorylation, where the high-energy ATP molecule donates a phosphate group to the substrate molecule. The result is a phosphorylated substrate and ADP. Conversely, an enzyme that removes phosphate groups from substrates is known as a phosphatase.

Types

Protein kinases, which phosphorylate proteins on serine, threonine or tyrosine residues, constitute one of the largest and most functionally diverse gene families. The human genome contains about 518 protein kinase genes, roughly 2% of all human genes. Other kinases act on lipids (lipid kinases such as phosphoinositide 3-kinase), carbohydrates, amino acids and nucleotides.

Role in signalling

Phosphorylation by kinases is a central mechanism of cell signalling and regulation. Cascades of kinases, such as the MAPK/ERK pathway, relay signals from the cell surface to the nucleus, controlling cell division, differentiation, metabolism and apoptosis. Because aberrant kinase activity is a major cause of cancer, kinase inhibitors such as imatinib have become an important class of targeted drugs.

Regulation

Kinases are themselves tightly regulated by phosphorylation, by binding of regulatory subunits and second messengers such as cyclic AMP, and by subcellular localisation. Protein kinase A, activated by cyclic AMP, was among the first kinases to be characterised in detail.
