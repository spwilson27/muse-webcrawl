H.264, officially the ITU-T Recommendation H.264 and identically MPEG-4 Part 10, Advanced Video Coding (AVC), is a video compression standard based on block-oriented, motion-compensated prediction. Finalised in 2003 by the Joint Video Team of ITU-T VCEG and ISO/IEC MPEG, it became one of the most widely used video formats in the world — the standard codec for Blu-ray Discs, most web streaming, broadcast television and video recording devices.

Overview

H.264 achieves roughly twice the compression efficiency of its predecessor MPEG-2 at comparable quality, allowing the same picture quality at about half the bit rate. Its tools include variable block-size motion compensation down to 4×4 pixels, multiple reference frames, spatial prediction within frames, a 4×4 integer transform, and the entropy coding methods CAVLC and the more efficient CABAC. The standard defines profiles (Baseline, Main, High and others) and levels that constrain resolution, frame rate and bit rate for different applications.

Adoption

The format underpins YouTube and other streaming services, HDTV broadcasting, video conferencing, and the AVC files recorded by most cameras and phones. Its successor, H.265/HEVC, offers further gains but saw slower adoption because of licensing complexity. H.264 has been progressively superseded in some web uses by royalty-free codecs such as VP9 and AV1.

Licensing

Patents essential to H.264 were licensed through the MPEG LA patent pool. The last US patents expired in the mid-2020s, with the standard becoming effectively patent-free in most jurisdictions around 2027–2030 depending on the patent.
