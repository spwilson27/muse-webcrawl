Camposanto Monumentale

The Camposanto Monumentale ("Monumental Cemetery"), also known as the Campo Santo, is a historical monumental cemetery on the northern edge of the Cathedral Square (Piazza dei Miracoli) in Pisa, Tuscany, Italy. It is considered one of the most beautiful cemeteries in the world and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage ensemble that also includes the Pisa Cathedral, the Baptistery and the Leaning Tower of Pisa.

History

According to legend, the cemetery was built around a shipload of sacred soil brought back from Golgotha during the Crusades by Ubaldo Lanfranchi, Archbishop of Pisa, in the 12th century. Construction of the present cloister building began in 1278 under the architect Giovanni di Simone, who also worked on the Leaning Tower. Work was interrupted after the Pisan defeat at the Battle of Meloria in 1284 and the cemetery was not completed until 1464.

The building consists of a long rectangular cloister of white marble with round arches supported by slender columns. Inside, the walls were covered with extensive fresco cycles, most famously the Triumph of Death, attributed to Buonamico Buffalmacco, and the Last Judgment and Hell by the same circle of artists. These frescoes, painted in the 1330s and 1340s, are masterpieces of Italian Gothic painting and were an important influence on later art.

Damage and restoration

On 27 July 1944, during the Second World War, a fragment of an artillery shell set fire to the wooden roof beams of the Camposanto. The lead of the roof melted and ran down over the frescoes, causing catastrophic damage. A long restoration campaign followed; the frescoes were detached from the walls, revealing the underlying preparatory drawings (sinopie), which are now displayed in the nearby Museo delle Sinopie. Restoration of the frescoes themselves has continued for decades.

Contents

The Camposanto contains a notable collection of Roman sarcophagi, reused in the Middle Ages for the burial of prominent Pisans, as well as funerary monuments from later centuries. The mathematician Leonardo Fibonacci is among those commemorated there. Chains from the medieval port of Pisa, once taken as trophies by Genoa after the Battle of Meloria, were returned in the 19th century and hang in the cemetery.
