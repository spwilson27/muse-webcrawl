Wright Amendment

Enacted1979 (as part of the International Air Transportation Competition Act)
Named forU.S. Representative Jim Wright of Texas
Affected airportDallas Love Field (DAL)
RepealedEffective 13 October 2014

The Wright Amendment of 1979 was a United States federal law that governed air traffic at Dallas Love Field, an airport in Dallas, Texas. It restricted flights from Love Field to destinations within Texas and four neighboring states — Louisiana, Arkansas, Oklahoma, and New Mexico — with the aim of protecting the new Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport (DFW) from competition.

Background

When DFW opened in 1974, airlines at Love Field agreed to move, but Southwest Airlines, which began service in 1971 and had not signed the agreement, stayed and won court rulings allowing it to operate intrastate flights. House Majority Leader Jim Wright of Fort Worth pushed through the amendment in 1979, codifying limits on Love Field service as deregulation opened interstate routes.

Modifications

The amendment was loosened over time. The Shelby Amendment of 1997 added Kansas, Mississippi, and Alabama to the permitted states and allowed through-ticketing in limited form; Missouri was added in 2005. Airlines exploited workarounds such as selling two separate tickets for connecting itineraries.

Repeal

After years of lobbying — including Southwest's "Free Love Field" campaign — a compromise announced in 2006 led to the Wright Amendment Reform Act, which allowed through-ticketing immediately and fully repealed the geographic restrictions on 13 October 2014, in exchange for capping Love Field at 20 gates and ending most international and competing-airport provisions. Nonstop service from Love Field expanded rapidly after repeal.
