Michael Collins (Irish leader)

Born: 16 October 1890, Woodfield, near Clonakilty, County Cork
Died: 22 August 1922 (aged 31), Béal na Bláth, County Cork
Offices: Minister for Finance; Chairman of the Provisional Government (1922); Commander-in-Chief of the National Army

Michael Collins (Irish: Mícheál Ó Coileáin; 16 October 1890 – 22 August 1922) was an Irish revolutionary, soldier and politician who was a leading figure in the struggle for Irish independence in the early twentieth century. He has been called the man who won Ireland's war of independence.

Early life and the Easter Rising

Collins was born at Woodfield near Clonakilty, County Cork, and emigrated to London in 1906, working as a clerk. He joined the Irish Republican Brotherhood and returned to Ireland to fight in the Easter Rising of 1916, serving at the General Post Office in Dublin. Interned afterwards at Frongoch in Wales, he emerged as a natural organiser of the republican movement.

War of Independence

Elected to Westminster in 1918, he sat instead in the revolutionary Dáil Éireann as Minister for Finance, raising the Dáil loan that funded the underground government. As the IRA's Director of Intelligence during the Irish War of Independence (1919–21), Collins built a formidable spy network inside Dublin Castle and created "the Squad", a dedicated assassination unit that targeted British intelligence officers, notably on Bloody Sunday, 21 November 1920. The British put a price on his head, but he moved around Dublin openly and was never captured.

Treaty and Civil War

In 1921 Collins was, reluctantly, a plenipotentiary for the Anglo-Irish Treaty, which partitioned Ireland and created the Irish Free State as a dominion. He regarded the Treaty as "the freedom to achieve freedom" — a stepping stone — famously remarking that in signing it he had signed his own death warrant. When the Dáil split over the Treaty, Collins became Chairman of the Provisional Government and Commander-in-Chief of the National Army in the ensuing Civil War against anti-Treaty forces. On 22 August 1922 he was shot dead in an ambush at Béal na Bláth in his native West Cork; the identity of the man who fired the fatal shot remains uncertain. He was 31.

Legacy

Collins remains one of the most admired figures in modern Irish history, commemorated annually at Béal na Bláth. His life was dramatised in Neil Jordan's 1996 film Michael Collins, starring Liam Neeson.
