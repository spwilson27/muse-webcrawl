Rolex Learning Center


Location: EPFL campus, Lausanne, Switzerland
Architects: SANAA (Kazuyo Sejima, Ryue Nishizawa)
Opened: 22 February 2010
Floor area: 20,000 m²

The Rolex Learning Center is a building on the campus of the École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) in Lausanne, Switzerland. Designed by the Japanese architectural firm SANAA, winners of the 2004 international competition, it opened on 22 February 2010 and was inaugurated on 27 May 2010. Sponsored in part by Rolex, the building serves as the social and intellectual heart of the campus, housing a library, study spaces, restaurants, cafés, and offices in a single open space of 20,000 square metres.

Architecture

The building is a single rectangular volume whose floor and roof undulate in gentle parallel waves, rising and dipping to create hills, valleys, and plateaus within one continuous interior. The concrete shell is perforated by fourteen patios of different shapes and sizes that bring daylight into the plan and create outdoor rooms. Because the floor is a sloping landscape rather than a flat slab on columns, the building is supported by arches spanning up to 80 metres, with the structure pre-stressed and built using custom formwork.

There are almost no internal walls: functions are separated by topography and by "bubbles" — glazed meeting rooms and study pods placed on the slopes. Visitors enter beneath the raised edges of the shell, which lifts off the ground at its corners. The design was praised for its radical openness and became a landmark of early twenty-first century academic architecture; SANAA's principals received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2010.

Use

The center contains the main EPFL library with some 500,000 volumes, individual and group work spaces for students, a multipurpose hall (the Forum Rolex), a bookshop, a bank, restaurants, and the offices of several campus services. It is open to the public and to students nearly around the clock during term.
