Arrian

Lucius Flavius Arrianus (Greek: Ἀρριανός; c. 86/89 – c. after 146/160 AD), known simply as Arrian, was a Greek historian, public servant, military commander and philosopher of the Roman period. His Anabasis of Alexander is regarded as the best surviving source on the campaigns of Alexander the Great, and his Discourses of Epictetus preserve much of the teaching of his Stoic master.

Life

Arrian was born into a prosperous Greek family in Nicomedia, the capital of the Roman province of Bithynia. He studied philosophy under the Stoic Epictetus at Nicopolis and recorded his teacher's lectures. A Roman citizen, Arrian entered imperial service, became consul suffectus (c. 129/130), and was appointed governor of Cappadocia by Hadrian. In that post he repelled an invasion of the Alani and wrote a plan of battle against them. In later life he settled in Athens, where he held the archonship and continued writing.

Works

The Anabasis of Alexander, modelled on Xenophon's Anabasis, narrates Alexander's conquests in seven books, drawing chiefly on the eyewitness accounts of Ptolemy and Aristobulus, whom Arrian considered the most reliable sources. The Indica, written in the Ionic dialect, describes India and the voyage of Nearchus. Other works include the Discourses and Enchiridion of Epictetus, a history of the Diadochi (lost), the Periplus of the Euxine Sea, a treatise on hunting (Cynegeticus), and the Ektaxis against the Alans.

Reputation

Arrian styled himself a second Xenophon, sharing that author's combination of military experience and literary ambition. Modern historians value the Anabasis for its critical use of sources, while classicists study the Discourses as the fullest record of Epictetus's Stoicism.
