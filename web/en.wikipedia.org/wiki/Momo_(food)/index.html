Momo (food)

Momo is a type of steamed filled dumpling in Tibetan and Nepali cuisine, also popular across the Himalayan regions of South Asia, including Bhutan and the Indian states of Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Ladakh, and Assam. Momos are traditionally filled with minced meat — yak or buffalo in Tibet, and buff, chicken, pork, or goat in Nepal — seasoned with garlic, ginger, onion, and spices; vegetarian versions filled with cabbage, paneer, or mixed vegetables are also widespread.

The dumplings are wrapped in a plain wheat-flour dough and steamed, though they may also be pan-fried (kothey) or served in a spicy soup (jhol momo, served in a sesame-and-tomato-based broth). They are typically accompanied by a fiery dipping sauce, often made from tomatoes, sesame seeds, and chilies. Momo is thought to have spread from Tibet to the Kathmandu Valley through Newar merchants, and it has become one of the most popular fast foods in Nepal, sold everywhere from street stalls to restaurants.
