Mess

A mess is an area where military personnel socialise, eat and (in some cases) live. The term is also used for the group of people who belong to a particular mess, and for the communal meal itself. Aboard ship, similar spaces are called mess decks or mess rooms.

Types of mess

Naval and military tradition divides messes by rank. Commissioned officers belong to the officers' mess — aboard a Royal Navy or Commonwealth warship, the wardroom — while senior non-commissioned officers have their own sergeants' or chief petty officers' mess, and junior ranks eat in their own messes. This separation is held to preserve discipline while allowing relaxation off duty.

Mess dress and customs

Formal mess dinners follow established customs: loyal toasts, passing the port, and speeches. Officers wear mess dress — a formal military uniform akin to black tie — on such occasions. Historic regimental silver and trophies typically decorate the mess.

Etymology

The word derives from the Old French mes, "a portion of food", from the Latin missus, "something put (on the table)". The sense of a group eating together dates from the late Middle Ages; the unrelated sense of "a state of disorder" developed later.
