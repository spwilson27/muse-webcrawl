Super Bowl Most Valuable Player Award

The Super Bowl Most Valuable Player Award, officially the Pete Rozelle Trophy, is presented annually to the most valuable player of the Super Bowl, the championship game of the National Football League (NFL). The winner is chosen by a panel of sportswriters and broadcasters together with fan voting conducted during the game. The award has been given since the first Super Bowl in 1967, when it was won by Green Bay Packers quarterback Bart Starr, who won it again the following year.

Sport: American football
First awarded: 1967 (Super Bowl I)
Most wins: Tom Brady (5)

History and records

Quarterbacks have won the award more often than any other position. Tom Brady holds the record with five MVP awards (Super Bowls XXXVI, XXXVIII, XLIX, LI and LV), followed by Joe Montana with three. Only one player from a losing team has won: Dallas Cowboys linebacker Chuck Howley in Super Bowl V. The only co-MVPs were Cowboys defensive linemen Harvey Martin and Randy White in Super Bowl XII, and Dallas players have won the award a record number of times overall. Running backs, wide receivers, and defensive players — including safeties and linebackers such as Von Miller in Super Bowl 50 — have also won.

Trophy

The trophy is named after former NFL commissioner Pete Rozelle and was first awarded under that name at Super Bowl XXV. Winners traditionally receive the trophy at a ceremony after the game and have often appeared in the "I'm going to Disney World!" advertising campaign.
