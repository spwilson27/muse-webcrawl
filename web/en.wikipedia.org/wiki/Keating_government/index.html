Keating government

Keating government
Period: 20 December 1991 – 11 March 1996
Prime Minister: Paul Keating
Party: Australian Labor Party
Preceded by: Hawke government
Succeeded by: Howard government

The Keating government was the federal executive government of Australia led by Prime Minister Paul Keating of the Australian Labor Party from December 1991, when Keating defeated Bob Hawke in a leadership ballot, until March 1996, when the Labor Party lost the federal election to John Howard's Coalition.

Policies

Keating's agenda emphasised engagement with Asia, republicanism, reconciliation with Indigenous Australians, and cultural renewal. In response to the High Court's Mabo decision, his government passed the Native Title Act 1993, and Keating's Redfern Park speech of December 1992 acknowledged the dispossession of Aboriginal Australians. In economic policy the government continued the Hawke-era program of market reform, introducing compulsory superannuation (the Superannuation Guarantee), enterprise bargaining, the privatisation of Qantas and the remaining Commonwealth Bank, and a controversial "One Nation" program of 1992.

1993 and 1996 elections

Against expectations, Keating won the 1993 election — the "sweetest victory", as he called it — defeating John Hewson's Coalition and its proposed goods and services tax. But after thirteen years of Labor rule, and with Keating increasingly polarising, the government was heavily defeated at the 1996 election.
