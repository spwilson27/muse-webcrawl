Daishō


Image placeholder: Daishō

The daishō (Japanese: 大小, literally "big–small") is the traditional pair of Japanese swords worn by the samurai class: the long katana and the shorter wakizashi (or tantō). Together they symbolised the samurai's status, honour, and role as a warrior.

History and use

The practice of wearing two swords developed during the Muromachi and Azuchi–Momoyama periods and was formalised under the Tokugawa shogunate, when only samurai were permitted to wear the daishō. The katana was the primary fighting weapon, while the wakizashi served as a backup, for close quarters, and for ritual suicide (seppuku). When entering a house, the katana was usually left at the entrance while the wakizashi was retained.
