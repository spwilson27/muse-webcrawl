Chartreuse Mountains

The Chartreuse Mountains (French: Massif de la Chartreuse) are a mountain range in south-eastern France, in the departments of Isère and Savoie, forming part of the French Prealps. They lie north of Grenoble and south of Chambéry, and take their name from the Grande Chartreuse, the head monastery of the Carthusian order, founded by Saint Bruno in 1084 in a remote valley of the range.

Geography

The massif is a limestone range of folded plateaus, cliffs, gorges and caves, bounded by the Isère valley to the south and west. Its highest summit is Chamechaude (2,082 m), followed by the Dent de Crolles (2,062 m), famous for its extensive cave systems, and the Grand Som (2,026 m), which overlooks the Grande Chartreuse monastery.

Protected area and economy

Much of the range is protected within the Chartreuse Regional Natural Park (Parc naturel régional de Chartreuse), established in 1995. The region is known for forestry, pastoral farming, winter sports at stations such as Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse, and the green and yellow liqueurs made by the Carthusian monks, distilled since the eighteenth century at Voiron from a secret recipe of 130 herbs and plants.
