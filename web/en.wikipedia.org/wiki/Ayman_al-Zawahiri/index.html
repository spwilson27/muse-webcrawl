Ayman Mohammed Rabie al-Zawahiri (19 June 1951 – 31 July 2022) was an Egyptian-born pan-Islamist militant and physician who led the militant Islamist organisation al-Qaeda from June 2011 until his death. He was a senior member of Islamist organisations that carried out attacks in Asia, Africa and the West, and he served as Osama bin Laden's right-hand man for many years.

Al-Zawahiri, bin Laden's deputy, succeeded him as al-Qaeda's leader in 2011.

Biography

Born into a prosperous Cairo family, al-Zawahiri trained as a surgeon and was radicalised in his youth. He founded Egyptian Islamic Jihad and was imprisoned in Egypt after the 1981 assassination of President Anwar Sadat, in which he denied involvement. In the 1980s he travelled to Afghanistan during the Soviet–Afghan War. Egyptian Islamic Jihad merged with al-Qaeda in 1998, and al-Zawahiri was indicted in the United States for his role in the 1998 embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania.

Al-Zawahiri became al-Qaeda's chief ideologue and was widely regarded as one of the planners of the September 11 attacks. After bin Laden was killed in 2011, he led the organisation through a period in which affiliates, and later the rival Islamic State, overshadowed its central leadership. On 31 July 2022, al-Zawahiri was killed by a United States drone strike — reported to have used Hellfire R9X missiles — while on a balcony of a house in Kabul's Sherpur district. His death was announced by President Joe Biden.
