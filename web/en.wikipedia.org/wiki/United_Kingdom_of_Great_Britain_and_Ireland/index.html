The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland was the sovereign state that existed between 1801 and 1922 (continuing under the same name until 1927). It was formed by the Acts of Union 1800, which merged the Kingdom of Great Britain with the Kingdom of Ireland, uniting the whole of the British Isles under one parliament at Westminster.

History

The union followed the Irish Rebellion of 1798 and was accompanied by Catholic emancipation promises that were delayed until 1829. Nineteenth-century Ireland was marked by the Great Famine of 1845–1852, which reduced the island's population through death and emigration, and by growing nationalist movements demanding Home Rule, defeated in 1886 and 1893 but passed (and suspended) in 1914. At the same time, the United Kingdom became the world's foremost industrial, naval and colonial power, and by 1921 its empire covered about a quarter of the Earth's land surface.

Dissolution

After the Easter Rising of 1916 and the Irish War of Independence (1919–1921), the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921 led to the establishment of the Irish Free State as a dominion on 6 December 1922. Northern Ireland exercised its right to opt out and remain in the union. The Royal and Parliamentary Titles Act 1927 changed the state's name to the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, which persists today.
