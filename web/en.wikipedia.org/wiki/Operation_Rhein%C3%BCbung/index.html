Operation Rheinübung ("Exercise Rhine") was the sortie of the German battleship Bismarck and heavy cruiser Prinz Eugen into the Atlantic Ocean in May 1941 to attack Allied merchant shipping. It ended with the destruction of Bismarck after one of the most famous naval pursuits of the Second World War.

Course of the operation

Under Admiral Günther Lütjens, the two ships sailed from Norway and were intercepted in the Denmark Strait on 24 May by HMS Hood and HMS Prince of Wales. Hood blew up and sank with the loss of all but three of her crew; Prince of Wales was damaged. Bismarck, herself hit and leaking fuel, headed for Brest while Prinz Eugen detached and escaped.

Sinking of Bismarck

Briefly losing contact, the Royal Navy relocated Bismarck through an Enigma-related sighting and a Catalina flying boat. Torpedo attacks by Fairey Swordfish aircraft from HMS Ark Royal jammed her rudder, leaving her circling. On the morning of 27 May, British battleships King George V and Rodney battered Bismarck, which sank with the loss of more than 2,000 men; only 114 survived. The loss effectively ended German surface raiding by capital ships.
