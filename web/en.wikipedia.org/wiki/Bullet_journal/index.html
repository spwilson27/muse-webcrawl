Bullet journal
Type: Personal organization method
Created by: Ryder Carroll (2013)
Medium: Paper notebook

A bullet journal is a method of personal organization in a single notebook, developed by the digital product designer Ryder Carroll and publicly launched in 2013. Often shortened to "BuJo," the system combines a to-do list, diary, planner, and sketchbook using a notation of short, bulleted entries — a practice Carroll calls "rapid logging."

The system

Core components include an index, a future log for long-term planning, monthly and daily logs, and "collections" — themed pages for projects, habits, or lists. Signifiers mark entries: a dot for a task, a circle for an event, and a dash for a note, with tasks migrated, scheduled, or struck through as their status changes. Carroll presented the method in a 2013 Kickstarter campaign and later in the 2018 book The Bullet Journal Method.

Community and adaptations

An extensive online community shares elaborately decorated spreads, blending the organizational system with calligraphy and illustration, though Carroll's original method emphasizes a minimal, functional approach. Adaptations exist for work, study, mental-health tracking, and chronic illness management.
