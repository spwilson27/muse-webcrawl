Aripiprazole, sold under the brand name Abilify among others, is an atypical antipsychotic medication. It is used primarily in the treatment of schizophrenia and bipolar disorder; other uses include as an add-on treatment in major depressive disorder, tic disorders, and irritability associated with autism spectrum disorder. It is available orally and as long-acting injections.

Aripiprazole tablets marketed as Abilify

Pharmacology

Unlike most antipsychotics, which block dopamine D2 receptors, aripiprazole acts as a partial agonist at D2 and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors and as an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. This profile is sometimes described as that of a "dopamine stabilizer." It has a long half-life (about 75 hours), and its active metabolite, dehydro-aripiprazole, is similarly long-lived.

Medical uses

Aripiprazole was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2002 for schizophrenia and subsequently for bipolar mania and other indications. Long-acting injectable forms (Abilify Maintena and Aristada) are used for maintenance treatment. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, constipation, headache, dizziness, anxiety, insomnia, and akathisia (restlessness). Compared with other antipsychotics, aripiprazole tends to cause less weight gain and metabolic disturbance, though these can occur. Rare but serious effects include neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, and impulse-control problems such as pathological gambling, about which the FDA issued a warning in 2016. In elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis, antipsychotics carry an increased risk of death.
