Doom is a 1993 first-person shooter game developed and published by id Software for MS-DOS. It is the first installment in the Doom franchise. The player assumes the role of a space marine, later unofficially referred to as Doomguy, fighting through hordes of undead humans and demons on Mars and in Hell.

Doom was the third major independent release by id after Commander Keen (1990–1991) and Wolfenstein 3D (1992). Development began in November 1992, with programmers John Carmack and John Romero, game designer Tom Hall, and artist Adrian Carmack (unrelated to John Carmack). The game's engine, written chiefly by John Carmack, used binary space partitioning to render textured 3D environments far faster than contemporary rivals, though levels are fundamentally two-dimensional in layout.

Gameplay

Players navigate maze-like levels, fighting demons with weapons including a chainsaw, pistols, shotguns, the chaingun, rocket launcher, plasma gun, and the powerful BFG 9000 — while gathering ammunition, health, armor, and keycards. The game was organized into three nine-level episodes; the first, Knee-Deep in the Dead, was distributed free as shareware. Multiplayer modes over local networks — cooperative play and competitive deathmatch, a term Doom popularised — were landmark features.

Reception and legacy

Doom was an unprecedented success, with an estimated 10,000–20,000 copies sold in its first year and millions of shareware players; it was credited with popularising the first-person shooter genre, networked multiplayer gaming, and user-created modifications through its WAD file format. Its graphic violence drew controversy and congressional attention. It spawned the sequel Doom II (1994), an expanded Ultimate Doom (1995), and a long-running franchise including a 2016 reboot. id released the engine source code in 1997 under the GNU GPL in 1999, enabling countless source ports — leading to the running joke of porting Doom to arbitrary devices. In 2019 it was inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame.
