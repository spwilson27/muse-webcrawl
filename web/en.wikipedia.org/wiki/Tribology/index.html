Tribology

Tribology is the science and engineering of interacting surfaces in relative motion. It encompasses the study of friction, wear, and lubrication, and draws on physics, chemistry, materials science, and mechanical engineering. The term, from the Greek tribos ("rubbing"), was popularized by the 1966 Jost Report in the United Kingdom, which estimated large economic savings from better tribological practice.

Topics

Classical tribology covers contact mechanics (including Hertzian contact), surface roughness and asperity contact, hydrodynamic and elastohydrodynamic lubrication, boundary lubrication and lubricant additives, and wear mechanisms such as adhesive, abrasive, and fatigue wear — often summarized by relations like Amontons' laws of friction and the Archard wear equation. The Stribeck curve relates friction to lubrication regime.

Applications

Tribology is central to bearings, gears, engines, and manufacturing, and extends to biotribology (artificial joints, skin, hair) and nanotribology at atomic scales. Reducing friction and wear saves energy and materials; a significant fraction of the world's energy consumption is spent overcoming friction.
