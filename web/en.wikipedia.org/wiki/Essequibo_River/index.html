Essequibo River

Essequibo River

Country: Guyana

Length: about 1,014 km (630 mi)

Source: Acarai Mountains

Mouth: Atlantic Ocean

Basin size: about 156,000 km²

The Essequibo River (Spanish: Río Esequibo) is the largest river in Guyana and the largest river between the Orinoco and the Amazon. Rising in the Acarai Mountains near the Brazilian border, it flows northward for approximately 1,014 kilometres (630 mi) through forest and savanna to the Atlantic Ocean, forming a wide estuary some 20 kilometres across at its mouth.

Course

The river's source lies in the Acarai Mountains near latitude 1° north. From there it flows generally north through the Rupununi savannas region, receiving tributaries including the Rupununi, Potaro, Mazaruni, and Cuyuni rivers. On the Potaro tributary lies Kaieteur Falls, one of the world's most powerful single-drop waterfalls. The lower river is dotted with large islands, including Leguan, Wakenaam, and Hog Island in the estuary.

History

The Essequibo was first sighted by Europeans on the voyages of Alonso de Ojeda in 1499. The Dutch established the colony of Essequibo in 1616, with Fort Kyk-Over-Al at the junction of the Cuyuni and Mazaruni rivers; sugar and coffee plantations were worked by enslaved Africans along the lower river. The colony passed to Britain in the early nineteenth century and became part of British Guiana.

Territorial dispute

The territory west of the river, known as Guayana Esequiba, is claimed by Venezuela, which asserts a historical title predating an 1899 arbitral award that fixed the boundary largely along the river's western watershed. Guyana administers the region, which comprises roughly two-thirds of its national territory. The dispute intensified after major offshore oil discoveries in the 2010s, and the case has been brought before the International Court of Justice.
