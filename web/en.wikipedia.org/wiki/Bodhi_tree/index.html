Bodhi Tree

Bodhi Tree
Species: Ficus religiosa
Location: Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India
Coordinates: 24.696°N 84.991°E

The Bodhi Tree ("tree of awakening"), also called the Bo tree, is a large sacred fig tree (Ficus religiosa) located in Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India. Siddhartha Gautama, the spiritual teacher who became known as the Buddha, is said to have attained enlightenment (bodhi) while meditating underneath it.

History

According to Buddhist texts, the Buddha meditated without moving from his seat for seven weeks (49 days) under the tree following his enlightenment. A shrine, the Animisalocana Cetiya, was later erected on the spot. The present tree is believed to be a direct descendant of the original, grown from a sapling of the Sri Maha Bodhi tree in Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka, itself grown from a cutting taken by Sanghamitta, daughter of Emperor Ashoka, in the 3rd century BCE.

The site is now part of the Mahabodhi Temple complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the four holy sites related to the life of the Buddha.

Religious significance

The Bodhi Tree is an important pilgrimage destination. Its heart-shaped leaves are a Buddhist symbol. Descendants planted elsewhere include trees in Chennai, Hawaii, and at many Buddhist temples worldwide. See also Buddha, Bodh Gaya, Enlightenment in Buddhism, and Ficus religiosa.

Celebrations

Bodhi Day, observed on 8 December in Japan and on Vesak in many traditions, commemorates the Buddha's enlightenment under the tree.
