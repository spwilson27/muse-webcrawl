IBM PC DOS, commonly shortened to PC DOS, is a discontinued operating system for the IBM Personal Computer and compatibles. It was sold by IBM from the 1980s to the 2000s. PC DOS was developed jointly by IBM and Microsoft: Microsoft retained the rights to sell its own version, MS-DOS, to other computer manufacturers.

History

PC DOS originated with 86-DOS, written by Tim Paterson at Seattle Computer Products and purchased by Microsoft. The first version, PC DOS 1.0, shipped with the original IBM PC in August 1981 and supported only single-sided 160 KB floppy disks. Version 2.0 (1983) added support for hard disks and hierarchical directories. Version 3.0 (1984) supported the IBM PC/AT and larger disks. Version 4.0 (1988) added a graphical shell but was criticised for bugs. PC DOS 5.0 (1991) and 6.x followed the development of MS-DOS, but after version 6.1 IBM and Microsoft parted ways and IBM continued PC DOS independently, releasing PC DOS 7 and PC DOS 2000, which included Year 2000 fixes.

Differences from MS-DOS

For most versions, PC DOS and MS-DOS were nearly identical apart from branding, the names of some utilities, and certain bundled tools such as IBM's REXX interpreter and text editor (E) in later PC DOS releases.
