Peace of Antalcidas

The Peace of Antalcidas, also known as the King's Peace, was a peace treaty guaranteed by the Persian king Artaxerxes II that ended the Corinthian War in 387/386 BC. It takes its common name from Antalcidas, the Spartan diplomat who negotiated it at the Persian court at Susa. The treaty was the first "common peace" (koinē eirēnē) in Greek history — a peace imposed simultaneously on all the Greek states on set terms, rather than negotiated bilaterally.

Terms

The rescript of Artaxerxes declared that the Greek cities of Asia Minor, together with the islands of Clazomenae and Cyprus, belonged to Persia; that all other Greek cities, large and small, were to be autonomous, except Lemnos, Imbros and Scyros, which remained Athenian possessions; and that the king would make war on any party that refused the terms. Sparta, as Persia's ally and the treaty's enforcer (prostates), gained a recognised pretext to intervene throughout Greece.

Consequences

The autonomy clause was used by Sparta to break up rival federations, including the Boeotian League and the Chalcidian League of Olynthus, while Sparta's own Peloponnesian League was exempted. Sparta used the peace to punish Mantinea, occupy the Theban Cadmea in 382 BC, and campaign against Olynthus. The peace was renewed several times, most notably in 375 and 371 BC, but its autonomy principle proved unenforceable and Greek warfare resumed. The treaty marked the high point of Persian influence in Greek affairs and of Sparta's post-Peloponnesian War hegemony. See also Corinthian War.
