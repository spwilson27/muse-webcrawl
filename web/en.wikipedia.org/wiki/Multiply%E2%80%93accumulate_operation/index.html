Multiply–accumulate operation

A multiply–accumulate operation (MAC) computes the product of two numbers and adds it to an accumulator: a ← a + (b × c). It is the fundamental operation in digital signal processing, where it underlies filters, convolutions, Fourier transforms, and matrix multiplication.

Hardware implementation

Dedicated MAC units in digital signal processors perform a multiply-accumulate in a single cycle, keeping the accumulator in a wide register to preserve precision. In modern processors, the related fused multiply-add (FMA) computes a + (b × c) with a single rounding, improving both speed and accuracy; FMA units are standard in CPUs, GPUs, and machine-learning accelerators.

Applications

MAC operations dominate the computational cost of FIR filters, dot products, and the matrix multiplications used in neural networks. Processor performance for AI workloads is often quoted in MACs (or equivalent operations) per second, and arrays of MAC units — such as systolic arrays — form the core of many inference chips.
