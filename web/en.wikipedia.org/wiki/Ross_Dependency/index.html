Ross Dependency

The Ross Dependency is a region of Antarctica defined by a sector originating at the South Pole, lying between longitudes 160°E and 150°W, which is claimed by New Zealand. The claim, made in 1923, is recognised by only a few countries and is held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty System; it includes the Ross Ice Shelf, the Balleny Islands, Scott Island, and part of Victoria Land.

History

The territory is named after Sir James Clark Ross, who explored the region in 1841, claiming Victoria Land for Britain, discovering the Ross Sea, Ross Island, and the great ice barrier now known as the Ross Ice Shelf. The region became the staging point for the "heroic age" expeditions of Robert Falcon Scott and Ernest Shackleton, whose huts survive at Cape Evans, Cape Royds, and Hut Point.

By an Order in Council of 30 July 1923, the British government placed the territory under the administration of the Governor-General of New Zealand, and it has been administered by New Zealand ever since. The Governor of the Ross Dependency is ex officio the Governor-General of New Zealand.

Administration and law

Under the Antarctic Treaty (signed 1959, in force 1961), territorial claims are neither recognised nor disputed, and the continent is reserved for peaceful scientific activity. New Zealand administers the Dependency through Antarctica New Zealand, and the laws of New Zealand apply to acts in the territory in specified ways. The Dependency has its own postage stamps, issued since 1957, which are primarily of philatelic interest.

Research stations

New Zealand's Scott Base, established in 1957 on Ross Island, is the Dependency's principal station, supporting scientific research year-round. Nearby on Ross Island stands the much larger United States McMurdo Station, the biggest community in Antarctica. Other stations in the Dependency include Italy's Zucchelli Station in Terra Nova Bay and South Korea's Jang Bogo Station. Research focuses on climate, glaciology, marine biology, atmospheric science, and the ecosystems of the Ross Sea.

In 2016 the Ross Sea region marine protected area — at the time the world's largest — was agreed under the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources, covering over 1.5 million square kilometres largely within or adjacent to the Dependency's seas.

Geography

The Dependency includes the western part of the Ross Ice Shelf, the largest ice shelf in Antarctica; the Transantarctic Mountains; parts of the polar plateau; Marie Byrd Land lies outside it to the east. Its land area is often estimated around 450,000 square kilometres, excluding the ice shelf. There is no permanent population, only the rotating staff of research stations.
