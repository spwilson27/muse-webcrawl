Wong Kar-wai

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Wong Kar-wai behind his signature dark glasses on a Hong Kong location, cigarette in hand, framing a shot with his fingers]

Wong Kar-wai (born 17 July 1958) is a Hong Kong film director, screenwriter and producer, the poet of time and romantic longing in modern cinema. His films — shot largely with cinematographer Christopher Doyle in saturated colour and step-printed drift — follow lovelorn policemen, killers, expatriates and drifters through Hong Kong's neon and the far places of the Chinese diaspora.

His major works are As Tears Go By (1988), Days of Being Wild (1990), Chungking Express (1994), Fallen Angels (1995), Happy Together (1997, Best Director at Cannes), In the Mood for Love (2000) and 2046 (2004), and the martial-arts biopic The Grandmaster (2013).

Method

Born in Shanghai and emigrated to Hong Kong at five, Wong began as a television writer. His productions are famously unstructured — scripts evolving on set, actors kept for years (his stock company of Tony Leung, Maggie Cheung, Leslie Cheung, Faye Wong), release dates missed by epochs (2046 arrived just in time for its title's joke). The result is cinema of mood unmatched since the silent era: clocks, rain, corridors and unspoken desire.

In the Mood for Love

His most perfect film pairs two neighbours in 1962 Hong Kong whose spouses are having an affair with each other; they rehearse confrontations, eat noodles on the stairs, and do not — the film's ache — become lovers themselves. It placed fifth in the 2022 Sight and Sound critics' poll of the greatest films, the highest-ranked film of its century in that poll, and its cheongsams and corridors are now visual common property.
