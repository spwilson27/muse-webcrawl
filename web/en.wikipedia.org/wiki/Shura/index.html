Shura

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A majlis al-shura, or consultative council.

Shura (Arabic: شُورَىٰ, shūrā) is an Arabic word for "consultation", referring to a principle and institution in Islamic political thought by which rulers and communities deliberate collectively on matters of common concern. The Quran commends consultation — one surah is titled Ash-Shura ("The Consultation"), and verse 42:38 praises those "whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves" — and the Prophet Muhammad is described as consulting his companions. Shura is frequently invoked in modern debates about the compatibility of Islam and democracy.

Historical practice

In early Islamic history, shura was famously applied in the selection of the third caliph, Uthman, in 644 CE, when the dying caliph Umar appointed a council of six to choose his successor. Under later dynasties, consultation became less institutionalized, and the classical jurists generally treated shura as a moral obligation on rulers rather than a body with binding authority, differing over whether its advice was advisory or binding.

Modern institutions

The term survives in the names of consultative assemblies in several Muslim-majority states, including the Shura Council of Egypt (until its replacement by a Senate), Saudi Arabia's Majlis al-Shura (a 150-member appointed advisory council), Oman's Council of Oman, Qatar's Shura Council, and Pakistan's Parliament (styled Majlis-e-Shoora). The Taliban's leadership council in Afghanistan is also called a shura, as was its governing body in Quetta.

Shura and democracy

Reformist thinkers from the 19th century onward, including Rashid Rida and later Islamist and liberal Muslim writers, have argued that shura provides an indigenous basis for representative government, accountability, and even elections. Critics respond that classical shura was consultative rather than legislative, lacked procedures of popular sovereignty, and bound no ruler. The concept remains central to contemporary Islamic political theory.
