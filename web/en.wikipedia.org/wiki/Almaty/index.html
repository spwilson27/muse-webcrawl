Almaty

Almaty is the largest city in Kazakhstan, with about 2.2 million residents, located in the foothills of the Trans-Ili Alatau mountains in the southeast of the country, near the Kyrgyzstan border. It was Kazakhstan's capital from 1929 to 1997, when the capital moved to Astana, and remains its financial, cultural and scientific centre.

Almaty ('father of apples') stands in the region where the domestic apple originated, in the wild apple forests of the surrounding mountains. Founded as the Russian fort of Verny in 1854, it preserves landmarks such as the wooden Ascension Cathedral (1907, built without nails), the Green Bazaar and Kok Tobe hill. The Medeu skating rink and Shymbulak ski resort lie in the mountains above; Almaty bid unsuccessfully for the 2022 Winter Olympics, losing to Beijing by four votes.
