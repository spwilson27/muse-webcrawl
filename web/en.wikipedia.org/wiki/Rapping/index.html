Rapping

Rapping (also MCing or emceeing) is an artistic form of vocal delivery and performance in which rhyming lyrics are chanted, usually in syncopated 4/4 beats. A primary ingredient of hip hop music, rapping combines speech, prose, poetry and singing, with emphasis on flow, rhythm and wordplay.

Rapping
Stylistic origins: Griots, dub, Jamaican toasting, spoken word
Cultural origins: 1970s, The Bronx, New York
Elements: Flow, delivery, lyrics, breath control
Subgenres: Boom bap, trap, grime, conscious, gangsta

History

Roots include West African griot traditions, African-American oral forms (the dozens, signifying), Jamaican toasting over dub, and artists like Gil Scott-Heron and The Last Poets. In the Bronx in the 1970s, DJs such as Kool Herc and MCs such as Coke La Rock turned rapping into hip hop's vocal core; "Rapper's Delight" (1979) brought it mainstream.

Technique

Flow is the interplay of rhyme schemes, cadence and rhythm; techniques include multisyllabic and internal rhymes, off-beat phrasing, and battle/freestyle improvisation. Content ranges from party chants to storytelling and social commentary.

Global spread

Rapping is now practised in virtually every country and language, from French and Korean rap to UK grime and drill.
