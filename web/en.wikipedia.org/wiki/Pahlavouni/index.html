Pahlavouni

The Pahlavouni were an Armenian noble family prominent during the Bagratid kingdom and the medieval period. The family claimed descent from the ancient Kamsarakan house and, through it, from the Arsacid (Arshakuni) kings of Armenia, a lineage reflected in their name, derived from "Pahlav", the Parthian designation.

History

The Pahlavouni rose to prominence in the 10th and 11th centuries, holding lands around Amberd and Bjni. Their most famous member was Vahram Pahlavouni, commander of the Armenian forces who died fighting the Byzantines at Ani in 1045. His son, Grigor Magistros, was a celebrated scholar, translator, and statesman. Another branch of the family produced Catholicos Grigor II the Martyrophile and Catholicos Nerses IV the Gracious, one of Armenia's most revered churchmen and poets.

Legacy

Family members founded and endowed monasteries such as Kecharis, and the Pahlavouni remained influential under the Zakarid princes in the 12th and 13th centuries.
