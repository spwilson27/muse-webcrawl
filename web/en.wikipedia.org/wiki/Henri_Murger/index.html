Henri Murger

Louis-Henri Murger (27 March 1822 – 28 January 1861), also spelt Henry Murger, was a French novelist and poet best known as the author of Scènes de la vie de bohème (Scenes of Bohemian Life), the work that defined the romantic image of the impoverished Parisian artist.

Life

Murger was born in Paris, the son of a tailor and concierge of German origin. After a brief period in a lawyer's office and in the service of a Russian prince, he lived the precarious "bohemian" life he would describe: sharing garrets in the Latin Quarter, writing for small journals, and enduring chronic poverty and ill health.

Scènes de la vie de bohème

His sketches of bohemian life were first published serially in the journal Le Corsaire from 1845, then adapted with Théodore Barrière into a successful play in 1849, and issued in book form in 1851. The stories follow the painter Marcel, the poet Rodolphe, the musician Schaunard, and the philosopher Colline, and the grisette Mimi. They became the source for Puccini's opera La bohème (1896) and Leoncavallo's opera of the same name (1897), and later for the musical Rent.

Murger died in Paris at the age of 38; his later years were marked by modest success through journalism and a secretaryship, but his health had been broken by poverty.
