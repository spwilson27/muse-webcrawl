Dalit

Dalit (from Sanskrit dalita, "broken/scattered") is a name for people belonging to the lowest stratum castes in the traditional caste system of India. Dalits were historically excluded from the four-fold varna system and treated as "untouchables", confined to occupations regarded as ritually polluting. According to the 2011 census, Dalits — officially designated Scheduled Castes — number over 200 million, about 16.6% of India's population, with significant communities also in Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh.

History

The social exclusion of communities later called Dalits developed over centuries within the caste hierarchy. Under British rule, the colonial administration enumerated these groups as "Depressed Classes"; the Government of India Act 1935 introduced the term "Scheduled Castes". Mahatma Gandhi popularised the name Harijan ("people of God"), though many Dalits later rejected it as patronising. The term "Dalit" was revived and popularised in the 1970s by the Dalit Panthers movement, modelled on the Black Panthers, and by Dalit literature.

Leaders and reform movements

The foremost Dalit leader was B. R. Ambedkar (1891–1956), a jurist and economist who chaired the drafting committee of the Indian Constitution and campaigned against untouchability; in 1956 he converted to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of followers, founding the Dalit Buddhist movement. Other major figures include Jyotirao Phule, Periyar E. V. Ramasamy and Kanshi Ram, founder of the Bahujan Samaj Party. The Constitution of India abolished untouchability (Article 17) and instituted reservations (affirmative action) in education, legislatures and public employment for Scheduled Castes.

Present day

Despite legal protections, including the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989, Dalits continue to face discrimination, violence and economic disadvantage in parts of South Asia, and caste-based discrimination has also been reported in the South Asian diaspora. Dalit movements remain a major force in Indian politics and culture. See also caste system in India, varna and Devi.
