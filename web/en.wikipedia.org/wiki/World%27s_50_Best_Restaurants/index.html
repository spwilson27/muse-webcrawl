The World's 50 Best Restaurants

The World's 50 Best Restaurants is an annual list and awards ceremony ranking restaurants worldwide, published since 2002 by the British media company William Reed, originally through Restaurant magazine. The ranking is compiled from the votes of a panel of more than a thousand chefs, restaurateurs, writers and gourmets organised into regional academies.

History and notable winners

The list quickly became a prestigious counterweight to the Michelin Guide. El Bulli in Spain topped it a record five times under Ferran Adrià; other multiple winners include Noma in Copenhagen under René Redzepi, which took first place five times, and The Fat Duck, The French Laundry, Osteria Francescana, El Celler de Can Roca, Eleven Madison Park and Mirazur. Since 2019, past number-one restaurants have been moved to a separate "Best of the Best" category and are no longer eligible for the main list.

Methodology and criticism

Panellists vote for restaurants they have visited in the preceding period; there is no fixed judging checklist. The list has been criticised for favouring expensive tasting-menu formats, for the potential for lobbying, and for under-representing some regions, while supporters credit it with reshaping global fine dining and culinary tourism. Regional spin-offs include Asia's 50 Best, Latin America's 50 Best and the World's 50 Best Bars.
