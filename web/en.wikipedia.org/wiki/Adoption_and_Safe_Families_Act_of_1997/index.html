The Adoption and Safe Families Act (ASFA, Public Law 105-89) is a United States federal law enacted on November 19, 1997, that significantly amended the child welfare provisions of the Social Security Act. It was adopted in response to concerns that many children were languishing in foster care for long periods while efforts to reunify them with their parents dragged on.

Provisions

ASFA declared that a child's health and safety are the paramount concern in placement decisions. It required states to file a petition to terminate parental rights when a child has been in foster care for 15 of the most recent 22 months, subject to exceptions, and shortened the time frame for permanency hearings. The law also offered financial incentives to states that increased the number of adoptions from foster care and required criminal record checks for prospective foster and adoptive parents.

Impact

Adoptions from foster care rose substantially after ASFA's passage, and the law remains a central framework of U.S. child welfare policy, though critics debate its timelines' effects on families and on reunification efforts.
