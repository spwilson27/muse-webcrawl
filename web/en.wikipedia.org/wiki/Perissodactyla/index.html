Perissodactyla

Perissodactyla (odd-toed ungulates)
Families: Equidae, Tapiridae, Rhinocerotidae
Extant species: about 17

Perissodactyla is an order of ungulates — hoofed mammals — whose members bear most of their weight on the third toe of each foot; they are commonly called the odd-toed ungulates. The three living families are the horses (Equidae), the tapirs (Tapiridae) and the rhinoceroses (Rhinocerotidae).

Characteristics

Perissodactyls have a mesaxonic foot in which the axis of symmetry passes through the enlarged middle toe. Horses have a single functional toe, rhinos have three toes, and tapirs have four toes on the front feet and three on the hind feet. All are herbivores with simple stomachs and hindgut fermentation, aided by an enlarged caecum.

Evolution

The order appeared in the late Palaeocene and flourished through the Eocene and Oligocene, including now-extinct groups such as the brontotheres and chalicotheres, before declining as climates changed and even-toed ungulates diversified. Hyracotherium, once considered the earliest horse, is a well-known early member of the group.
