The University of Vienna (German: Universität Wien) is a public research university in Vienna, Austria. Founded in 1365 by Duke Rudolf IV, it is the oldest university in the German-speaking world and one of the largest universities in Europe, with around 90,000 students and some 15 faculties and numerous centres.

History

Modelled on the University of Paris, Vienna grew into a leading centre of medieval learning, theology, law and medicine. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries it stood at the heart of Viennese intellectual life: Sigmund Freud lectured in medicine, the Vienna Circle shaped philosophy of science, and the Austrian School of economics took form among its scholars. After the Anschluss of 1938, Jewish academics and students were expelled en masse, a catastrophe from which whole disciplines took decades to recover.

Campus and organisation

The main building, designed by Heinrich Ferstel in Italian Renaissance style, stands on the Ringstraße and was opened in 1884; its arcaded courtyard carries busts and memorials to famous scholars. The university is spread over more than 60 locations in Vienna. It offers a wide range of degree programmes, predominantly in German, and is a major research force in the humanities, social sciences, law, life sciences and computer science.
