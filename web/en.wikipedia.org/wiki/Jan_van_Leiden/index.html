Jan van Leiden

Jan van Leiden (born Jan Beukelszoon; 2 February 1509 – 22 January 1536) was a Dutch Anabaptist leader who became self-proclaimed king of the city of Münster, Westphalia, during the Münster Rebellion. His theocratic regime collapsed in 1535; he was arrested and executed in January 1536.

Early life

Jan Beukelszoon was a tailor's apprentice and innkeeper from Leiden who embraced Anabaptism and moved to Münster in 1533, joining Jan Matthys in proclaiming the city the "New Jerusalem".

King of Münster

After Matthys's death, van Leiden declared himself king in September 1534. He enforced communal ownership, introduced polygamy, and ruled the besieged city with theatrical pomp. In June 1535 the city fell to the forces of the prince-bishop Franz von Waldeck.

Death and memory

He was tortured and executed on 22 January 1536; his body was displayed in an iron cage hung from St. Lambert's Church, where the cages still hang. The episode became a scandal that hardened opposition to Anabaptism and is the subject of Meyerbeer's opera Le prophète.
