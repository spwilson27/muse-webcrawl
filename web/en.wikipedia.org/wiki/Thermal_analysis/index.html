Thermal analysis

[Hero image placeholder: a differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) curve.]

Thermal analysis is a branch of materials science where the properties of materials are studied as they change with temperature. Several methods are commonly used — these are distinguished from one another by the property being measured.

Methods

  • Differential thermal analysis (DTA): the temperature difference between a sample and an inert reference is recorded as both are heated.
  • Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC): heat flow into or out of a sample is measured; used to determine enthalpies of transitions such as melting and the glass transition.
  • Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA): mass change is measured as a function of temperature, revealing decomposition, oxidation and solvent loss.
  • Thermomechanical analysis (TMA) and dynamic mechanical analysis (DMA): dimensional change and mechanical response under temperature programmes.
  • Evolved gas analysis: gases released during heating are identified, often by coupling TGA to mass spectrometry or FTIR.

Applications

Thermal analysis is used in pharmaceuticals (polymorph screening, purity), polymers (glass transition, crystallinity, curing), metallurgy (phase diagrams), and in the study of cements, foods and energetic materials.
