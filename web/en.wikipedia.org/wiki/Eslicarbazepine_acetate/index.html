Eslicarbazepine acetate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eslicarbazepine acetate

Brand names: Aptiom, Zebinix
Class: Anticonvulsant; voltage-gated sodium channel blocker
Uses: Focal (partial-onset) seizures in epilepsy
Route: Oral, once daily
Prodrug of: Eslicarbazepine (S-licarbazepine)

Eslicarbazepine acetate, sold under the brand names Aptiom and Zebinix, is an anticonvulsant medication used in the treatment of epilepsy, specifically for focal (partial-onset) seizures, as monotherapy or adjunctive therapy in adults and children above a certain age. It was developed by the Portuguese pharmaceutical company Bial.

Pharmacology

Eslicarbazepine acetate is a prodrug that is rapidly and extensively converted to eslicarbazepine (also called S-licarbazepine), its major active metabolite. Eslicarbazepine is the active metabolite shared with the older drug oxcarbazepine, but unlike oxcarbazepine it is produced almost entirely in the S configuration, giving more predictable pharmacokinetics and allowing once-daily dosing. Like carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine, it stabilises the inactivated state of voltage-gated sodium channels, reducing repetitive neuronal firing; it may also modulate T-type calcium channels.

History and approvals

The drug was first approved in the European Union in 2009 (as Zebinix) for adjunctive therapy of partial-onset seizures, and in the United States in 2013 (as Aptiom). Approvals were later extended to monotherapy and to paediatric patients. It was designed as a third-generation member of the dibenzazepine family, following carbamazepine (first generation) and oxcarbazepine (second).

Adverse effects

Common side effects include dizziness, somnolence, headache, diplopia, nausea, vomiting, and ataxia. Like other drugs in its family it can cause hyponatraemia (low blood sodium) and, rarely, serious skin reactions such as Stevens–Johnson syndrome, which are associated with the HLA-B*1502 allele in patients of Han Chinese and other Asian ancestries; screening is recommended where relevant. It can reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives through hepatic enzyme effects.
