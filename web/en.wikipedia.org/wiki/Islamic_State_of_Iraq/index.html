Islamic State of Iraq

The Islamic State of Iraq (ISI; Arabic: دولة العراق الإسلامية, Dawlat al-ʿIrāq al-Islāmiyyah) was a Salafi jihadist militant organisation and self-proclaimed state in Iraq, proclaimed on 15 October 2006. It emerged from al-Qaeda in Iraq (AQI) and allied groups under the Mujahideen Shura Council. In April 2013 its leader, Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi, announced its expansion into Syria and merger into the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), the precursor of the group later known simply as the Islamic State.

ISI operated mainly in Iraq's Sunni-majority provinces.

History

After the 2003 US-led invasion of Iraq, Abu Musab al-Zarqawi's group pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda, becoming al-Qaeda in Iraq. Following Zarqawi's death in June 2006, the leadership passed to Abu Ayyub al-Masri, and in October 2006 the Islamic State of Iraq was declared with Abu Omar al-Baghdadi as its emir. ISI carried out widespread attacks against coalition forces, Iraqi security forces and civilians, particularly Shia Muslims.

The group was severely weakened by the 2007 US troop surge and the Sunni "Awakening" movement. Abu Omar al-Baghdadi and al-Masri were killed in April 2010, and Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi became leader. Exploiting the Syrian civil war and Iraqi sectarian tensions, the organisation rebuilt its strength, leading to its 2013 transformation into ISIL and its dramatic territorial expansion in 2014.
