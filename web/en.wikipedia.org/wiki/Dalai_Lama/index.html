The Dalai Lama is the foremost spiritual leader of the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism and was traditionally the head of state of Tibet. Holders of the title are believed by Tibetan Buddhists to be manifestations of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, reincarnated in a continuing lineage.

The 14th Dalai Lama

Tenzin Gyatso, recognised as the 14th Dalai Lama as a small child, fled Tibet for India in 1959 after the failed uprising against Chinese rule, and has lived in exile at Dharamshala in northern India (the article links to his homeland's capital). He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989 for his non-violent campaign for Tibetan autonomy, and in 2011 he devolved his political authority to an elected exile leadership.
