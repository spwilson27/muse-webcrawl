Stringed instrument tunings

Standard tunings of common stringed instruments

This article gives the tunings — the pitches to which the open strings are tuned — for a wide range of stringed instruments. Tunings are conventionally listed from the lowest-pitched string to the highest, or, where a player holds the instrument, in the order most natural to describe.

Bowed strings

InstrumentTuning
ViolinG3–D4–A4–E5 (fifths)
ViolaC3–G3–D4–A4 (fifths)
CelloC2–G2–D3–A3 (fifths)
Double bassE1–A1–D2–G2 (fourths)

The violin family is tuned in perfect fifths, except for the double bass, which is tuned in fourths to facilitate fingering in the lower register. Orchestral bass players sometimes use a five-string instrument or a C extension reaching C1.

Guitars

The standard tuning of the six-string guitar is E2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4, mostly in fourths with one major third. Common alternatives include drop D (D2–A2–D3–G3–B3–E4), open G, and DADGAD. The five-string bass guitar adds a low B (B0) or a high C to the four-string bass tuning E1–A1–D2–G2.

Other instruments

The mandolin shares the violin's tuning (G–D–A–E) with doubled courses. The banjo commonly uses open G tuning (gDGBd). The ukulele is usually tuned g'–c'–e'–a' ("reentrant" tuning, with the first string higher than expected). Historical instruments such as the lute and vihuela used a variety of tunings, often in fourths with a major third in the middle, resembling the guitar's pattern.
