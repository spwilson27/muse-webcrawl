Jean-Baptiste Kléber

Jean-Baptiste Kléber (9 March 1753 – 14 June 1800) was a French general of the Revolutionary Wars. Trained as an architect, he rose to prominence suppressing the Revolt in the Vendée and later accompanied Napoleon Bonaparte on the Egyptian campaign.

Born9 March 1753, Strasbourg
Died14 June 1800, Cairo
AllegianceFrance

Career

Kléber distinguished himself in the Rhine campaigns of the 1790s. In 1798 he sailed with Napoleon to Egypt, was wounded at Alexandria, and won the Battle of Mount Tabor in 1799. When Napoleon returned to France in August 1799, he left Kléber in command of the Army of the East.

Assassination

After defeating the Ottomans at Heliopolis in March 1800, Kléber was assassinated in Cairo on 14 June 1800 by Suleiman al-Halabi, a Syrian student. His name is inscribed on the Arc de Triomphe.
