List of current and historical women's universities and colleges in the United States

The following is a list of current and historical women's colleges in the United States. Women's colleges were founded from the early nineteenth century onward to provide higher education to women at a time when most colleges admitted only men. At their mid-twentieth-century peak there were more than 200 women's colleges in the United States; today roughly 30 remain as women's institutions at the undergraduate level.

Historical notes

The Seven Sisters colleges — Barnard, Bryn Mawr, Mount Holyoke, Radcliffe, Smith, Vassar, and Wellesley — were founded as counterparts to the Ivy League. Radcliffe merged into Harvard University, and Vassar became coeducational in 1969. Many other women's colleges closed or became coeducational following the wave of coeducation at formerly men's colleges in the late 1960s and 1970s.
