Hutsul pony

The Hutsul pony, also known as the Hutsul or Hucul, is a hardy breed of small horse or pony native to the Carpathian Mountains, in the region inhabited by the Hutsul people of Ukraine and Romania. It is a sure-footed mountain breed traditionally used for riding, pack work and light draught work in difficult terrain.

Characteristics

Hutsul ponies are small and compact, generally standing about 125–147 cm (12.1–14.2 hands) at the withers. They have a strong build, a short neck, sturdy legs and very hard hooves. Common colours include bay, black, chestnut and dun, often with primitive markings such as a dorsal stripe. The breed is noted for its endurance, calm temperament and ability to thrive on sparse mountain forage.

History

The breed developed over centuries in the high valleys of the Eastern Carpathians. In the nineteenth century a state stud was established at Lucina in Bukovina (now in Romania) to organise breeding, and several stallion lines were recorded. Numbers fell sharply during the two world wars, and conservation programmes in Poland, Romania, Ukraine, Slovakia and other countries have since maintained the breed. The Hutsul is recognised as a valuable genetic resource and is used today for trekking, driving and conservation grazing.
