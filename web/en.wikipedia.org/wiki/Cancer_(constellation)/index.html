Cancer (constellation)

Cancer is one of the twelve constellations of the zodiac and is located in the Northern celestial hemisphere. Its old astronomical symbol is ♋︎. Its name is Latin for crab, and it is commonly represented as one. Cancer is a medium-size constellation with an area of 506 square degrees; it is the faintest of the zodiacal constellations, its brightest star Beta Cancri having an apparent magnitude of about 3.5.

Features

The constellation's most famous deep-sky object is the Beehive Cluster (Praesepe, Messier 44), an open cluster known since antiquity, lying at the centre of the constellation and visible to the naked eye as a fuzzy patch under dark skies. Another Messier object, the open cluster M67, lies near the western border. The stars at the heart of Cancer form an inverted Y shape: Iota, Alpha (Acubens, "the claw"), and the Delta–Gamma pair called Asellus Borealis and Asellus Australis, the northern and southern donkeys, which flank the Beehive as if feeding from a manger (Praesepe).

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Cancer is the crab Karkinos, sent by Hera to bite Heracles during his fight with the Hydra. Though crushed, the crab was rewarded by being placed among the stars. In astrology, the Sun passes through Cancer from about 21 June to 22 July; the Tropic of Cancer is named for the constellation because, when the naming was fixed in antiquity, the Sun reached its northernmost point in Cancer at the June solstice — a position now shifted by precession into Taurus and Gemini.

Astronomy

Because of precession, the Sun now appears in Cancer from late July to early August. The constellation borders Gemini to the west, Leo to the east, Lynx to the north, and Hydra to the south. Several of its stars host known exoplanets, most famously 55 Cancri, a binary system with five confirmed planets, including one of the first exoplanets discovered around a Sun-like star in the main sequence.
