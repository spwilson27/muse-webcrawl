Fortriu

Fortriu was the most powerful Pictish kingdom in early medieval Scotland, attested from the 4th to the 9th century. Its rulers — the kings of Fortriu — often held overlordship over the other Pictish provinces, and the kingdom formed the core from which the later kingdom of Alba emerged.

Location debate

Fortriu was traditionally placed around Strathearn and Menteith in central Scotland, based on later sources. In a widely influential reassessment (2006), historian Alex Woolf argued it lay instead in the north, centred on Moray and Ross around the Moray Firth — consistent with the concentrations of Pictish power implied by Bede, the distribution of major strongholds, and the location of the great fortress at Burghead. The name derives from the Verturiones (or Uerturiones), a Pictish people named by Roman writers.

History

Under Óengus I (Onuist son of Uurguist), king from 732 to 761, Fortriu dominated Britain north of the Forth, subjugating Dál Riata and raiding Northumbria and Strathclyde. Later kings faced Viking pressure; after the catastrophic defeat of 839, in which King Eóganan and much of the Pictish elite were killed by Scandinavians, the line of succession passed to rulers such as Kenneth MacAlpin, in whom Pictish and Gaelic kingship merged.
