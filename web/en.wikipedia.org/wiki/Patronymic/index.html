Patronymic

A patronymic, or patronym, is a component of a personal name based on the given name of one's father, grandfather (avonymic), or an earlier male ancestor. A component of a name based on the name of one's mother or a female ancestor is a matronymic. Patronymics were and remain an essential element of naming systems in many cultures, including Slavic, Scandinavian, Icelandic, Arabic, and Ethiopian traditions.

Forms

In Russian and other East Slavic naming customs, the patronymic is a standard middle element formed with the suffixes -ovich/-evich for sons and -ovna/-evna for daughters: for example, Ivan Petrovich means "Ivan, son of Pyotr." Ukrainian and Bulgarian follow similar patterns, and South Slavic surnames such as Serbian Petrović originated as patronymics. In Iceland, where family surnames are generally not used, children receive a true patronymic (or matronymic) as their second name — Jón Einarsson is "Jón, son of Einar" — and directories are ordered by first name. Arabic names use the elements ibn (son of) and bint (daughter of), and many surnames elsewhere, such as Johnson, MacDonald, Fitzgerald, and Di Giovanni, fossilize older patronymic formations.

Historical use

Patronymics predate hereditary surnames in most societies and often coexisted with them; as populations grew and administration required fixed family names, many patronymics became permanent surnames. In Wales, the ap/ab ("son of") system gave rise to surnames like Pritchard (from ap Richard) and Powell (from ap Hywel). Some naming laws, notably in Iceland, preserve the live patronymic system to this day.
