Karahi

A karahi (also kadai, kadahi) is a thick, circular, deep cooking vessel used throughout South Asia, similar in shape to a wok but with a flatter base and steep sides. Traditionally made of cast iron — though also produced in copper, stainless steel and aluminium — it is used for deep frying and for cooking the stewed dishes that take its name. In Pakistani and North Indian cuisine, "karahi" dishes such as chicken karahi and mutton karahi are cooked and served in the vessel itself.

The vessel

The karahi's heavy construction retains heat for frying samosas, pakoras and pooris, and its two loop handles allow it to be carried to the table. A smaller cousin used for serving curries is sometimes called a handi, while large commercial karahis are standard equipment in dhabas and street-food stalls.

Karahi dishes

Chicken karahi, originating in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa region and popularised in Peshawar and Lahore, is made by stir-frying chicken on high heat with tomatoes, green chillies, ginger, garlic and black pepper in ghee or oil, with no onion-based gravy in the classic version. The dish is garnished with julienned ginger and coriander and eaten with naan. Variants include white karahi (with yoghurt and cream) and the tomato-heavy Lahori style.
