Southwark (pronounced /ˈsʌðərk/) is a district of Central London situated on the south bank of the River Thames, forming the north-western part of the London Borough of Southwark. It was at the southern end of the earliest London Bridge and has been a settlement since Roman times, when it served as the bridgehead suburb of Londinium.

History

The name derives from Old English Suðweorc, "southern defensive work". In the Middle Ages Southwark lay largely outside the City of London's jurisdiction, in the liberties of the Bishop of Winchester and others, and became notorious for entertainments banned across the river: bear-baiting, brothels ("stews") and theatres, including the Globe, the Rose and the Hope. Chaucer's pilgrims set out from the Tabard Inn on Borough High Street, and Dickens set much of his early life and work here. The area was also a centre of prisons — the Marshalsea, the Clink and King's Bench.

Modern Southwark

Severe wartime bombing and the decline of the riverside wharves were followed by dramatic regeneration from the 1990s: Bankside gained Tate Modern (2000), the Millennium Bridge and the rebuilt Shakespeare's Globe (1997); Borough Market revived as a food destination; and the Shard, completed in 2012 at 310 metres, became Western Europe's tallest building on completion. London Bridge station and the surrounding "London Bridge Quarter" anchor a major office and hospital district (Guy's Hospital).
