Œdipe (opera)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Œdipe, Op. 23, is an opera in four acts by the Romanian composer George Enescu, to a French libretto by Edmond Fleg, based on the Greek tragedies of Sophocles. Composed over more than two decades and completed in 1931, it premiered at the Paris Opéra on 13 March 1936. It is Enescu's only opera and is widely considered his masterpiece.

Synopsis

Uniquely among Oedipus settings, the opera spans the hero's entire life, from his birth and the exposure on Mount Cithaeron, through his unknowing murder of his father Laius and marriage to Jocasta, to his self-blinding and his death at Colonus. Enescu portrays Oedipus not as a victim of fate but as a man who struggles against destiny; the final scene presents his death as a kind of redemption.

Music

The score combines late-Romantic opulence with Romanian folk inflections and quarter-tone colors. It is notable for its continuous symphonic fabric, extensive use of leitmotifs, and the prominent role of the chorus. Long neglected outside Romania, Œdipe has been increasingly revived internationally, including major productions at the Royal Opera House, London, in 2016.
