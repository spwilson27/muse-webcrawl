In continuum mechanics, the Froude number (Fr) is a dimensionless number defined as the ratio of the flow inertia to the external field (mainly gravity), named after the British engineer and naval architect William Froude. It is usually written as Fr = v / √(gL), where v is a characteristic velocity, g is gravitational acceleration and L is a characteristic length.

Applications

In open-channel hydraulics, the Froude number compares the flow velocity with the speed of surface waves. Flow with Fr < 1 is subcritical (tranquil), Fr = 1 is critical, and Fr > 1 is supercritical (rapid), a distinction that governs phenomena such as the hydraulic jump. In naval architecture, the Froude number based on hull length is used to compare wave-making resistance between geometrically similar ships and their scale models — the basis of Froude's law of similitude used in towing-tank testing. Variants of the number also appear in the study of walking and running gaits, sediment transport and stratified flows.
