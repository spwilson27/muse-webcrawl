Vale of Leven
Country: Scotland
Region: West Dunbartonshire
River: River Leven (from Loch Lomond)

The Vale of Leven is an urban area and valley in West Dunbartonshire, Scotland, lying in the basin of the River Leven, which flows south from Loch Lomond to join the River Clyde at Dumbarton. The "Vale" comprises a chain of settlements including Alexandria, the largest town, and the villages of Balloch, Bonhill, Jamestown and Renton.

History

The valley was one of the cradles of the Scottish industrial revolution in textiles. From the late 18th century, water power from the Leven and the area's pure water attracted bleachfields, dye-works and printworks specialising in Turkey red dyeing, and by the 19th century the Vale was a world centre of calico printing and dyeing, with firms such as the United Turkey Red Company employing thousands. The industry collapsed in the mid-20th century under foreign competition, leaving significant unemployment.

Today

Balloch, at the southern tip of Loch Lomond, is a gateway to the Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park and hosts the Loch Lomond Shores visitor centre. Alexandria's surviving landmarks include the Smollett Fountain; Renton is notable as the birthplace of novelist Tobias Smollett and home to Vale of Leven F.C., twice winners of the Scottish Cup in the 1870s. The Vale of Leven Hospital opened in 1951 as one of Scotland's first NHS district general hospitals.
