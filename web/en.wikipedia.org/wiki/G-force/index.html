g-force

The g-force or gravitational force equivalent is a measure of the force of acceleration that causes a perception of weight. In straight and level flight, lift (L) equals weight (W). In a banked turn of 60°, lift equals double the weight (L = 2W), and the pilot experiences 2 g. The SI unit of g-force is the metre per second squared (m/s2).

One g is equal to the nominal acceleration due to gravity on Earth: about 9.80665 m/s2 (32.174 ft/s2).

Effects on humans

Human tolerance of g-force depends on magnitude, duration, direction, and body posture. Sustained positive g (eyeballs down) drives blood away from the head and can cause tunnel vision ("grey-out"), blackout, and ultimately g-induced loss of consciousness (G-LOC), which is a major hazard for fighter pilots. Pilots use anti-g suits and trained straining manoeuvres to raise tolerance.

Negative g (eyeballs up or out) drives blood toward the head and is tolerated much less well. Short-duration high g (as in crashes) can be survived if distributed, as demonstrated by racing drivers and by Colonel John Stapp's rocket-sled experiments.

Applications

g-force is discussed in aviation, astronautics (astronauts experience about 3 g at launch), roller coaster design, automotive crash safety, and consumer electronics (accelerometers rated in g).
