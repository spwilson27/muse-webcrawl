Śāriputra (Sanskrit: शारिपुत्र; Pali: Sāriputta; born Upatissa) was one of the two chief male disciples of Gautama Buddha, together with Maudgalyāyana (Moggallāna). He is praised in the canon as the disciple foremost in wisdom (prajñā), second only to the Buddha himself, and as "general of the Dharma" (dhammasenāpati).

Life

Born into a Brahmin family near Rājagṛha, Upatissa was a close friend of Kolita (Maudgalyāyana) from childhood; the two wandered together as ascetics under the teacher Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta. Hearing a short verse on causation from the monk Aśvajit — "Of those things that arise from a cause, the Tathāgata has stated the cause, and also their cessation" — Śāriputra attained stream entry and brought Maudgalyāyana to the Buddha, where both were ordained.

Role and legacy

Śāriputra is credited with systematising the Buddha's teaching and is traditionally associated with the Abhidharma literature. In Mahayana sutras such as the Heart Sutra and the Lotus Sutra he appears as an interlocutor of the Buddha. He died at his birthplace shortly before the Buddha's parinirvāṇa; his relics were enshrined in stupas at Jetavana and later at Sanchi, and were famously recovered in the 19th century and eventually returned to India, where they are kept at the Chethiyagiri Vihara in Sanchi.
