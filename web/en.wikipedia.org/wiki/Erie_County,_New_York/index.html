Erie County is a county along the shore of Lake Erie in the western portion of the U.S. state of New York. As of the 2020 census, the population was about 955,000. Its county seat is Buffalo, which also serves as the commercial and cultural centre of the Buffalo–Niagara Falls metropolitan area.

Geography

The county borders Lake Erie to the west and the Niagara River to the northwest, across which lies the Canadian province of Ontario. It encompasses three cities (Buffalo, Lackawanna and Tonawanda), 25 towns and 16 villages, as well as parts of several Native American reservations, including those of the Seneca Nation.

History

Erie County was created in 1821 from part of Niagara County and named after the Erie tribe of Native Americans and the lake that bears their name. The opening of the Erie Canal in 1825, with its western terminus at Buffalo, drove the county's rapid growth as a transshipment and industrial centre. The county is governed by an elected county executive and an 11-member legislature.
