2022 KwaZulu-Natal floods

Disaster details
Date8 – 21 April 2022
LocationKwaZulu-Natal, South Africa
FatalitiesAt least 448
DamageEstimated at over R17 billion

In April 2022, days of heavy rain across KwaZulu-Natal in southeastern South Africa led to devastating floods and landslides, centred on the Durban (eThekwini) metropolitan area. Some areas received more than 300 mm of rain in 24 hours — among the heaviest downpours recorded in the province in decades — in an event intensified by a cut-off low pressure system over the warm Indian Ocean.

Impact

At least 448 people were killed, making it the deadliest natural disaster in South Africa's recent history. Thousands of homes, many of them informal dwellings built on unstable hillsides, were destroyed or swept away, and more than 40,000 people were displaced. Roads, bridges, water and electricity infrastructure were badly damaged, the Port of Durban suspended operations, and industrial sites including a major Toyota plant were inundated.

Response

President Cyril Ramaphosa declared a national state of disaster, visited affected communities, and deployed the military to assist with rescue and recovery. Questions were raised about land-use planning, settlement on flood-prone land, and the role of climate change in intensifying extreme rainfall on South Africa's east coast.

Aftermath

Rebuilding of housing and infrastructure continued long afterwards, and further flooding struck the region in subsequent years, renewing debate over disaster preparedness and resilient settlement planning.
