Stephen Harper

Stephen Joseph Harper (born April 30, 1959) is a Canadian politician and economist who served as the 22nd prime minister of Canada from 2006 to 2015. He was the first prime minister to lead the modern Conservative Party of Canada, which he co-founded and led from 2004 to 2015. Harper governed through two minority parliaments (2006–2011) before winning a majority government in 2011, and his tenure was marked by tax reductions, a firm response to the 2008 financial crisis, an assertive foreign policy, and controversy over centralised control of government messaging.

Early life and education

Harper was born and raised in Toronto, the eldest of three sons of Margaret and Joseph Harper, an accountant with Imperial Oil. He worked in the petroleum industry in Edmonton after dropping out of the University of Toronto, then completed bachelor's and master's degrees in economics at the University of Calgary, where he became associated with a group of market-oriented academics sometimes called the "Calgary School". In the 1980s he worked as a policy adviser, notably for Progressive Conservative MP Jim Hawkes.

Rise in politics

Disillusioned with the Progressive Conservatives, Harper helped draft the platform of the new Reform Party in 1987 and was elected to Parliament for Calgary West in 1993. He left politics in 1997 to head the National Citizens Coalition, a conservative advocacy group. In 2002 he won the leadership of the Canadian Alliance, the Reform Party's successor, becoming leader of the Official Opposition. In 2003 he and Peter MacKay merged the Alliance with the Progressive Conservative Party to form the Conservative Party of Canada, with Harper elected its first leader in 2004.

Prime Minister (2006–2015)

The Conservatives won a minority government in the January 2006 election. Harper's government cut the Goods and Services Tax from 7% to 5%, introduced income splitting for families with children (later replaced by pension income splitting and targeted benefits), passed the Federal Accountability Act, and recognised the Québécois as a nation within Canada. It apologised in 2008 for the residential schools system. Harper steered Canada through the 2008–09 recession with stimulus spending; Canada's banks avoided failures and its recovery was comparatively strong, though the budget returned to deficit after years of surpluses.

After prorogation controversies and a 2011 non-confidence vote, the Conservatives won a majority. The second phase of his premiership emphasised trade agreements (including with the European Union and South Korea), Arctic sovereignty, support for Israel, combat operations against the Islamic State, and resource development. His government was criticised for muzzling scientists, the Senate expenses scandal, and his combative style. In the 2015 election the Liberals under Justin Trudeau defeated the Conservatives, and Harper resigned as party leader, later leaving Parliament in 2016.

Post-premiership

Harper founded Harper & Associates Consulting, chairs the International Democrat Union (elected 2018), and serves on corporate boards including the Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce. He published Right Here, Right Now (2018), arguing for a pragmatic conservatism responsive to working-class concerns. He remains an influential figure in Canadian conservatism and an adviser to subsequent Conservative leaders.
