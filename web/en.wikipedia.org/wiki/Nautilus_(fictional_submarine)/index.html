Nautilus is the fictional submarine commanded by Captain Nemo in Jules Verne's novels Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870) and The Mysterious Island (1874). Named after Robert Fulton's real submarine Nautilus (1800), Verne's vessel was far ahead of the technology of its day: a fully electric, double-hulled submarine capable of long voyages beneath all the world's oceans, serving as Nemo's home, laboratory, and instrument of vengeance against imperial powers.

Description in the novels

Verne describes the Nautilus as a long, spindle-shaped vessel about 70 metres long and 8 metres in beam, displacing roughly 1,500 tonnes when submerged. Its double hull is held together by T-shaped crossbeams, its plates riveted with extraordinary precision. Propulsion is provided by a powerful electric plant using sodium extracted from seawater, driving a four-bladed propeller that gives a top speed of about 50 knots. The vessel carries a library of 12,000 volumes, a museum of art and natural specimens, a great saloon with an organ, and a small launch. It rams surface ships with a spur at its bow, and its crew uses electrically charged defences and air reservoirs for diving excursions in armoured suits.

Construction and fate

As revealed in The Mysterious Island, Nemo — born Prince Dakkar of India — had the vessel's parts manufactured secretly in workshops around the world and assembled on a deserted Pacific island. After Nemo's death, the Nautilus becomes his tomb, sunk beneath Lincoln Island when the island is destroyed by a volcanic eruption.

Legacy

The Nautilus is the most famous fictional submarine in literature and a landmark of early science fiction. It inspired real vessels: the United States Navy's USS Nautilus (SSN-571), launched in 1954 as the world's first nuclear-powered submarine, was named in its honour, as was a later NASA module. Disney's 1954 film adaptation gave the vessel an iconic Victorian design by Harper Goff that has shaped popular depictions ever since.
