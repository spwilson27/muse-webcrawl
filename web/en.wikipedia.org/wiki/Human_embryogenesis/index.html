Human embryogenesis

Human embryogenesis is the process of development of a human embryo during the first eight weeks following fertilisation, after which it is termed a fetus until birth. It encompasses cell division, differentiation and the formation of the body's basic structures and organ systems.

Stages

Fertilisation produces a zygote, which undergoes cleavage divisions to form a morula and then a blastocyst that implants in the uterine wall around day 6–7. Gastrulation in the third week establishes the three germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm — from which all tissues derive. Neurulation follows, forming the neural tube that becomes the brain and spinal cord.

Organogenesis

During weeks 4–8 the major organ systems take shape: the heart begins beating, limb buds appear, and the face and sensory organs develop. This period is the most sensitive to teratogens, which can cause congenital malformations. Development is commonly described using Carnegie stages, a standardised system based on structural criteria.
