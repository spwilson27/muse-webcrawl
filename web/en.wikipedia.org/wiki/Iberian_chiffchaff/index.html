Iberian chiffchaff

The Iberian chiffchaff (Phylloscopus ibericus) is a species of leaf warbler endemic to Portugal, Spain and North Africa, in southwestern Europe and Maghreb. It is a small insectivorous passerine, very similar in appearance to the common chiffchaff, from which it was split as a separate species on the basis of its distinctive song, slight morphological differences and genetic divergence.

Description

The Iberian chiffchaff averages about 10–11 cm in length. Compared with the common chiffchaff it has brighter green upperparts, a whiter belly with a yellow tinge on the breast, a longer bill, and a somewhat longer wing. The song is the most reliable distinguishing feature: a distinctive sequence mixing chiffchaff-like notes with a descending rattle, quite unlike the familiar "chiff-chaff" of its close relative.

Distribution and habitat

It breeds in the Iberian Peninsula and far northwestern Africa, favouring open woodland, riverside trees and humid valleys. A long-distance migrant, it winters in West Africa. Where its range meets that of the common chiffchaff in the Pyrenees and northeast Spain, the two hybridise only rarely, supporting their treatment as separate species.

Taxonomy

Formerly treated as a subspecies of the common chiffchaff (Phylloscopus collybita ibericus), it was recognised as a full species in the late 1990s following studies of song, morphology and mitochondrial DNA.
