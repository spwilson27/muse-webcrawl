Geology of Pakistan

The Karakoram range, part of the active collision zone of the Himalaya.

The geology of Pakistan encompasses the varied landscapes that make up one of the most dynamic areas of the Earth in terms of plate tectonics. Pakistan sits astride the northwestern margin of the Indian Plate, which is colliding with the Eurasian Plate—an ongoing collision that has raised the Himalaya, Karakoram, and Hindu Kush ranges and continues to cause destructive earthquakes.

Tectonic framework

Three plates influence the region: the Indian Plate moving northward, the Eurasian Plate, and the Arabian Plate, which subducts beneath the Makran coast of Balochistan along the Makran trench. The Chaman Fault system forms the western transform boundary through Balochistan and accommodates strike-slip motion between the Indian and Eurasian plates. The northern mountain ranges preserve rocks from the ancient Tethys Ocean, including ophiolites marking the Indus-Yarlung suture in the Kohistan island arc terrane, sandwiched between the Indian and Karakoram plates.

Regions

Eastern Pakistan comprises the low-lying Indus Plain, built from river sediments shed from the mountains, overlying sedimentary basins rich in natural gas (such as the Sui field) and oil. The Salt Range exposes one of the world's famous fossiliferous Cambrian-to-Eocene sequences and large rock-salt deposits at Khewra. The Thar Desert in the southeast rests on the edge of the ancient Indian Shield, whose Precambrian basement crops out around Nagarparkar.

Seismicity and resources

Major earthquakes include the 1935 Quetta earthquake, the 1945 Makran earthquake and tsunami, and the 2005 Kashmir earthquake, which killed more than 80,000 people. Mineral resources include coal at Thar (among the world's largest lignite deposits), copper and gold at Reko Diq and Saindak in the Chagai volcanic belt, chromite, gypsum, and limestone.
