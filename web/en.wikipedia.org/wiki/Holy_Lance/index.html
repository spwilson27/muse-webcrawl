Holy Lance

The Holy Lance, also called the Lance of Longinus, the Spear of Destiny, or the Holy Spear, is the lance said to have pierced the side of Jesus as he hung on the cross during his crucifixion. According to the Gospel of John (19:31–37), a Roman soldier thrust a spear into the side of the crucified Jesus to confirm his death, and blood and water flowed from the wound. Later tradition named the soldier Longinus, who was subsequently venerated as a saint.

Relics

Several artifacts have been claimed over the centuries to be the Holy Lance, or parts of it. The most famous is the lance preserved among the Imperial Regalia in the Imperial Treasury at the Hofburg Palace in Vienna — a winged lancehead of Carolingian type, worked in iron and later embellished with gold and silver sleeves and an inscribed nail said to be one of the Holy Nails; it formed part of the regalia of the Holy Roman Emperors and was carried before armies as a talisman of victory. Another celebrated lance is preserved at Echmiadzin Cathedral in Armenia (the Geghard spear, long kept at the monastery of Geghard), and other fragments have been claimed at the Vatican and elsewhere. A lance discovered at Antioch during the First Crusade in 1098 — "found" by the visionary Peter Bartholomew — inspired the Crusaders at a critical moment, though its authenticity was contested even at the time.

In legend and culture

The lance features in Grail literature, including Wolfram von Eschenbach's Parzival, and in Wagner's operas Parsifal and Lohengrin. In modern popular culture, under the name "Spear of Destiny," it has been the object of occult legends — including unfounded stories linking it to Adolf Hitler — and appears in numerous films, novels, and video games.
