The Roland TR-909 Rhythm Composer is a drum machine introduced by the Roland Corporation in 1983. It was the first Roland drum machine to use samples for some sounds (crash, ride and hi-hat cymbals) alongside analog synthesis for the bass drum, snare, toms, rimshot and handclap, and the first MIDI-equipped drum machine.

History

Designed by Tadao Kikumoto, the engineer behind the TR-808, the 909 retailed for about US$1,195. It sold poorly — around 10,000 units — because its drum sounds were judged too hybrid and because cheaper digital machines were appearing. Production ended in 1985.

Legacy

Cheap second-hand units were adopted in Chicago house and Detroit techno in the late 1980s; the 909's punchy kick, crisp hi-hats and shuffle function became defining sounds of house, techno, acid and rave music. Artists such as Frankie Knuckles, Jeff Mills and Daft Punk made it a cornerstone of electronic dance music. Along with the 808, it is one of the most influential drum machines of all time.
