Gopuram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The monumental gopuram of a Dravidian temple, covered with painted stucco figures.

A gopuram (also spelled gopura) is a monumental entrance tower, usually ornate, at the entrance of a Hindu temple, particularly in South India. It is a prominent feature of the Dravidian style of temple architecture, in which the gopuram often becomes the tallest and most visible structure of the temple complex, dwarfing the inner sanctum tower (vimana).

Architecture

Gopurams are typically rectangular in plan and taper as they rise, sometimes to heights exceeding 60 metres. Their surfaces are covered with stucco or stone sculptures of gods, goddesses, guardian figures, and scenes from Hindu mythology, which are periodically repainted in bright colours. A gopuram is crowned by a barrel-vaulted roof (shala) and finials called kalasam.

The gateway passage passes through the centre of the tower's base, aligned with the temple's axis. Large temple complexes may have several concentric enclosure walls (prakaram), each pierced by gopurams of increasing size toward the outside, as at Srirangam, whose great white gopuram rises about 72 metres.

History

Early gopurams of the Pallava and early Chola periods were modest gateways. Under the later Chola, Pandya, Vijayanagara, and Nayak rulers, gateway towers grew ever larger and more elaborately sculpted, becoming statements of royal devotion and power. The Rayagopuram of the Ekambareswarar Temple and the towers of Madurai's Meenakshi Temple are celebrated examples.
