Isobar

An isobar is a line on a map connecting points of equal atmospheric pressure, usually reduced to sea level, at a given time. Isobars are drawn on surface weather weather maps and are a fundamental tool of synoptic meteorology and weather forecasting. The word derives from the Greek isos (equal) and baros (weight).

Isobars around high and low pressure systems.

Interpretation

The spacing of isobars indicates the pressure gradient: closely spaced isobars mark a steep gradient and strong winds, while widely spaced isobars indicate light winds. Because of the Coriolis effect, winds blow roughly parallel to isobars (counterclockwise around lows in the Northern Hemisphere). Curved patterns of isobars reveal low-pressure systems (cyclones), high-pressure systems (anticyclones), troughs and ridges.

The term "isobar" is also used in nuclear physics for nuclides with the same mass number, and in thermodynamics for processes at constant pressure (isobaric processes).
