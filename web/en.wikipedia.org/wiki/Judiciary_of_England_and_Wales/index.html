Judiciary of England and Wales

HeadLady Chief Justice
Apex courtSupreme Court of the United Kingdom (UK-wide)
Senior courtsCourt of Appeal, High Court, Crown Court

The judiciary of England and Wales comprises the judges and magistrates who sit in the courts of England and Wales, interpreting and applying the law of England and Wales. It is independent of the executive and legislature, a position strengthened by the Constitutional Reform Act 2005.

Structure

At the top for most England and Wales matters is the Court of Appeal (Civil and Criminal Divisions), below which sit the High Court — King's Bench, Chancery, and Family Divisions — and the Crown Court for serious criminal cases. County Courts and Family Court handle most civil and family work, while magistrates' courts deal with summary criminal offences. A system of tribunals covers immigration, tax, employment, and other specialist areas.

The Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, established in 2009 replacing the Appellate Committee of the House of Lords, is the final court of appeal for the whole UK.

Judges

Senior judges are appointed through the Judicial Appointments Commission on merit. The Lady Chief Justice is head of the judiciary of England and Wales. Judges enjoy security of tenure, protected by the Act of Settlement 1701, removable only on an address by both Houses of Parliament.

Reform

The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 removed the Lord Chancellor's judicial role, created the Supreme Court, and placed a statutory duty on ministers to uphold judicial independence.
