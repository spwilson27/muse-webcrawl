Standard Chinese (simplified Chinese: 现代标准汉语; traditional Chinese: 現代標準漢語; pinyin: Xiàndài Biāozhǔn Hànyǔ; literally "modern standard Han language") is a modern standard form of Mandarin Chinese that was first codified during the Republican Era (1912–1949). It is designated as the official language of mainland China, where it is called Putonghua (普通话; 普通話; Pǔtōnghuà; "common speech"), in Taiwan it is called Guoyu (國語; Guóyǔ; "national language"), and in Singapore it is called Huayu (华语; 華語; Huáyǔ; "Chinese language").

Standard Chinese is based on the Beijing dialect of Mandarin, with its grammar derived from a body of modern written vernacular literature and its vocabulary drawn from modern spoken Mandarin. It is one of the most widely spoken languages in the world by number of native speakers.

History

During the Ming and Qing dynasties, a Mandarin-based koiné known as Guanhua ("officials' speech") was used at the imperial court and in administration. In the early years of the Republic of China, intellectuals of the New Culture Movement campaigned for the replacement of Literary Chinese with a written vernacular (baihua) based on northern speech. The Commission on the Unification of Pronunciation, convened in 1913, settled on a standard largely following the Beijing dialect.

After the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, the government promoted Putonghua nationwide through the education system and media. The definition was formalised in 1955–56: Standard Chinese takes the Beijing dialect as its standard pronunciation, the northern dialects as its base dialect, and exemplary modern vernacular literature as its grammatical norm.

Phonology

The standard language has four lexical tones plus a neutral tone, a syllable inventory built from initials and finals, and features the retroflex consonants of the Beijing dialect. The romanisation system used to transcribe it is Hanyu Pinyin, adopted in mainland China in 1958 and now the international standard. Erhua—the addition of a rhotic suffix—is a feature of the Beijing dialect but is used sparingly in the standard language outside Beijing.

Grammar

Like other Sinitic varieties, Standard Chinese is largely analytic, with a basic subject–verb–object word order. It uses measure words (classifiers) with numerals and demonstratives, aspect markers such as le, guo and zhe rather than verb tense inflection, and topic-prominent constructions including the ba construction.

Vocabulary

Most words in Standard Chinese consist of one or two syllables, corresponding to one or two characters. Polysyllabic compounds expanded greatly during the 20th century as new scientific, political and technical terms were coined or borrowed, often through Japanese coinages using Chinese characters.

Names

Putonghua is the name used in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau. Guoyu remains the common name in Taiwan. In Singapore and Malaysia the standard is called Huayu. The phrase "Standard Chinese" is the most common English-language designation.
