Muslim Brotherhood

The Society of the Muslim Brothers (Arabic: جماعة الإخوان المسلمين, Jamāʿat al-Ikhwān al-Muslimīn), better known as the Muslim Brotherhood (الإخوان المسلمون, al-Ikhwān al-Muslimūn), is a transnational Sunni Islamist organization founded in Egypt by Islamic scholar and schoolteacher Hassan al-Banna in 1928.

History

Founding

Hassan al-Banna founded the Brotherhood in March 1928 in the Suez Canal city of Ismailia, initially as a religious and social welfare society preaching Islam, teaching the illiterate, and setting up hospitals and commercial enterprises. Within two decades it had become a major political force in Egypt, advocating the application of Islamic law (sharia) and opposing British colonial influence.

Mid-20th century

The Brotherhood was implicated in political violence in the late 1940s; after the assassination of Prime Minister Mahmoud El Nokrashy Pasha in 1948, al-Banna himself was assassinated in 1949, likely by government agents. Under Gamal Abdel Nasser, the movement was banned and thousands of members imprisoned after an alleged 1954 assassination attempt on Nasser. Prison radicalized thinkers such as Sayyid Qutb, whose writings influenced later Islamist movements.

Later decades

Under Anwar Sadat the Brotherhood was tolerated as a counterweight to leftists, though never fully legalized. It built a wide network of charities, professional syndicates, and businesses. In the first elections after the 2011 Egyptian revolution, its Freedom and Justice Party won a plurality in parliament, and its candidate Mohamed Morsi became president in 2012. Morsi was removed by the military in July 2013 after mass protests; the Brotherhood was then banned again and declared a terrorist organization by the Egyptian government, and thousands of members were imprisoned or fled.

Beliefs

The Brotherhood's credo, as formulated by al-Banna, holds that "Allah is our objective; the Prophet is our leader; the Qur'an is our law; Jihad is our way; dying in the way of Allah is our highest hope." The movement advocates the gradual Islamization of society from below through preaching (da'wa), education, and social services, alongside political participation where permitted.

International branches

The Brotherhood inspired or founded branches and affiliated movements in many countries, including Jordan, Syria, Kuwait, Palestine (where its branch evolved into Hamas), and elsewhere. Several governments — including Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Russia — designate it a terrorist organization; many Western governments do not, though debate over designation has recurred in the United States and Europe.
