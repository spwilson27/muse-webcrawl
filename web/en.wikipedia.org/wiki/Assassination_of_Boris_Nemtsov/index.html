Assassination of Boris Nemtsov
Date27 February 2015
LocationBolshoy Moskvoretsky Bridge, Moscow, Russia
TargetBoris Nemtsov, Russian opposition politician
PerpetratorsGunman Zaur Dadayev and accomplices (convicted 2017); organisers disputed

The assassination of Boris Nemtsov took place on the night of 27 February 2015 in Moscow, when the Russian opposition politician Boris Nemtsov was shot dead on the Bolshoy Moskvoretsky Bridge, within sight of the Kremlin walls. Nemtsov, a former deputy prime minister under Boris Yeltsin, had become one of the most prominent critics of President Vladimir Putin.

The murder

Nemtsov was walking home across the bridge with his companion, the Ukrainian model Anna Duritskaya, shortly before midnight, when an assailant fired several shots into his back from a passing car. He died at the scene. The murder came two days before a planned opposition march in Moscow, which was converted into a memorial march attended by tens of thousands.

Investigation and trial

Five men from Chechnya and Ingushetia were arrested. In June 2017 a jury convicted them: the gunman, Zaur Dadayev, a former officer in a Chechen interior-ministry battalion, received 20 years in prison. Nemtsov's family and supporters have criticised the investigation for failing to identify who ordered the killing, and the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe and the OSCE have issued reports raising concerns about the inquiry. The bridge has become the site of an unofficial, repeatedly vandalised and repeatedly restored memorial, known as Nemtsov Most.

Context

At the time of his death Nemtsov was preparing a report on Russian military involvement in eastern Ukraine. He had earlier co-authored a series of reports alleging corruption in the Putin government.
