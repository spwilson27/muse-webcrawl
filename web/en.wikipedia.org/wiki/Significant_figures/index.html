Significant figures

Significant figures (also known as significant digits, precision or resolution) of a number in positional notation are digits in the number that are reliable and necessary to indicate the quantity of something. They express the precision of a measurement: a result should be reported with only as many digits as the measurement justifies.

Identifying significant figures

  • All nonzero digits are significant: 123.45 has five significant figures.
  • Zeros between nonzero digits are significant: 1002 has four.
  • Leading zeros are not significant: 0.0045 has two.
  • Trailing zeros in a decimal number are significant: 1.200 has four.
  • Trailing zeros in a whole number without a decimal point are ambiguous and usually not counted unless specified.

Arithmetic rules

For multiplication and division, the result should have as many significant figures as the factor with the fewest. For addition and subtraction, the result should be rounded to the position of the least precise term. Rounding is normally done by the round-half-up or round-half-to-even convention.

Relation to accuracy and precision

Significant figures encode the precision of a value but not its accuracy. In science and engineering they are a simple form of rounding based on uncertainty, though explicit uncertainty statements (such as ± values) are preferred in careful work. Exact numbers, such as counts and defined constants, have unlimited significant figures.
