The Victorian Football League (VFL) is a state-level Australian rules football league based in the state of Victoria. The name has been used by two competitions: the elite Victorian league founded in 1897, which in 1990 was renamed the Australian Football League (AFL), and the present-day semi-professional state league, which adopted the VFL name in 1996 after previously being the Victorian Football Association (VFA).

A VFL match at a suburban Melbourne ground.

History

The original VFL was formed in 1897 when eight clubs — Carlton, Collingwood, Essendon, Fitzroy, Geelong, Melbourne, St Kilda, and South Melbourne — broke away from the VFA. It grew into the dominant competition in the sport, expanding interstate in the 1980s and becoming the national AFL in 1990.

The modern VFL descends from the VFA, founded in 1877. After the AFL's reserves competition was wound up, the VFA merged with it for the 2000 season under the VFL name, combining standalone suburban clubs (such as Port Melbourne, Williamstown, and Coburg) with the reserves teams of AFL clubs. In 2021 the league expanded to incorporate teams from New South Wales and Queensland, becoming a de facto second-tier competition across eastern Australia.
