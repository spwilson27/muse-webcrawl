Button accordion

Placeholder image: a chromatic button accordion.

A button accordion is a type of accordion on which the melody side keyboard consists of buttons rather than piano-style keys. The term usually refers to the chromatic button accordion, widely used in European classical, folk and popular music, though diatonic button accordions (such as the melodeon) also exist.

Layout

The chromatic button accordion has three or more rows of buttons arranged in a chromatic pattern, giving it a much wider range in a smaller space than a piano accordion of comparable size. Two main systems exist: the C system and the B system, differing in the arrangement of the repeated rows. The left hand operates a bass button board, commonly the Stradella system of 120 bass buttons providing bass notes and preset chords. Most instruments are unisonoric — each button gives the same note whether the bellows are pushed or drawn — in contrast to bisonoric diatonic instruments.

Use

Button accordions are central to French musette, Russian and Balkan folk music, Finnish and Italian traditions, and modern classical accordion repertoire; the related bayan is the Russian form of the instrument. Players value the compact fingering patterns and the ability to span large intervals.
