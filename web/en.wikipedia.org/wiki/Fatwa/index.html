Fatwa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fatwa is a non-binding legal opinion on a point of Islamic law given by a qualified jurist (a mufti) in response to a question, based on the jurisprudence of the relevant school. Fatwas range from everyday guidance (prayer, fasting, finance) to opinions of public consequence. Because any qualified scholar may issue one, and questioners may seek opinions from multiple muftis (“fatwa shopping”), fatwas vary in authority with the issuer's standing; states have sought to regulate issuance (official muftis and fatwa councils in many Muslim-majority countries).

The word entered Western headlines with Ayatollah Khomeini's 1989 fatwa calling for the death of the novelist Salman Rushdie for The Satanic Verses — an opinion that many other jurists disputed, and whose popular image (fatwa as death sentence) specialists note is atypical of the genre. Historically, fatwa collections are among the richest sources for the social history of Muslim societies, recording real questions put to jurists across centuries.
