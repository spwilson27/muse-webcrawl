Treaty of Åbo

The Treaty of Åbo (Swedish: Fredsfördraget i Åbo), also known as the Treaty of Turku, was signed between the Russian Empire and Sweden in Åbo (Turku) on 18 August 1743. It ended the Russo-Swedish War of 1741–1743, also called the Hats' War, and was a significant diplomatic outcome for Russia, which gained territory in eastern Finland.

Background

The war began when Sweden's ruling Hats party sought to recover territory lost to Russia in the Treaty of Nystad (1721). The Swedish offensive failed badly, and Russian forces occupied much of Finland. Under the terms proposed by Empress Elizabeth of Russia, Sweden was compelled to accept a peace that ceded further Finnish land to Russia.

Terms

Under the treaty, Sweden ceded to Russia the towns of Lappeenranta and Hamina together with the strip of Finland east of the Kymijoki River, including the fortress of Olavinlinna. The new border meant that Russia gained control of southeastern Finland, pushing its frontier considerably westward.

Consequences

The treaty also carried a political condition: at Russia's insistence, Adolf Frederick of Holstein-Gottorp, a relative of Empress Elizabeth, was chosen as crown prince and heir to the Swedish throne. Finland would remain a borderland between the two empires until the Finnish War of 1808–1809, when Russia annexed the whole of Finland.
