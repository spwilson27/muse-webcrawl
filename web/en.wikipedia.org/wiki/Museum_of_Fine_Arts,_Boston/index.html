Museum of Fine Arts, Boston

The Museum of Fine Arts (often abbreviated as MFA Boston or MFA) in Boston, Massachusetts, is one of the largest art museums in the United States, with a collection of more than 450,000 works spanning ancient Egyptian art to contemporary art. Founded in 1870, it moved to its present neoclassical building on Huntington Avenue in the Fenway neighborhood in 1909.

Collection

The museum is especially strong in Egyptian artifacts (gathered partly through its sponsorship of excavations with Harvard University), Japanese art — one of the finest holdings outside Japan — Chinese painting, and American art, including works by John Singleton Copley, Winslow Homer, and John Singer Sargent. Its Impressionist and Post-Impressionist holdings include one of the largest collections of paintings by Claude Monet outside Paris, as well as works by Renoir, Degas, Gauguin, and Van Gogh.

Building and expansions

The original building was designed by Guy Lowell. Later additions include the West Wing by I. M. Pei (1981) and the Art of the Americas Wing, designed by Foster + Partners, which opened in 2010 and greatly expanded display space for the museum's American collections.

Education

The museum is affiliated with the School of the Museum of Fine Arts at Tufts University and operates the Nagoya/Boston Museum of Fine Arts partnership that operated in Japan from 1999 to 2018.
