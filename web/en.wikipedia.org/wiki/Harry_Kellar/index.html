Harry Kellar

Harry Kellar (July 11, 1849 – March 10, 1922), born Heinrich Keller, was an American stage magician who presented large travelling magic shows, and the predecessor of Harry Houdini. He was a successor to Robert Heller and Isaiah Hughes, with whom he had apprenticed.

Career

Born in Erie, Pennsylvania, to German immigrant parents, Kellar ran away as a boy and worked for the Fakir of Ava (Isaiah Hughes), learning stage magic. He toured the world with his own company through the 1870s and 1880s, performing in Australia, India, Egypt, and South America, and in 1884 launched "Kellar's Wonders" in the United States.

Kellar's large-scale illusions included the levitation of Princess Karnac (an effect he bought after seeing it performed in India, and later refined with John Nevil Maskelyne), the Vanishing Lamp, the Nested Boxes, and "The Blue Room". His elaborate posters, produced by the Strobridge Lithographing Company, are now classics of magic advertising. In 1908 he retired and sold his show to Howard Thurston, whom he designated his successor; Houdini, who had hoped for the mantle, remained a close friend and visited Kellar annually.

Death and legacy

Kellar died in Los Angeles in 1922. He is considered a founding figure of the grand American touring magic show and helped establish the modern image of the magician with moustache, evening dress, and impressive stage machinery.
