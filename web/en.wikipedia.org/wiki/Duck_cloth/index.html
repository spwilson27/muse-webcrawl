Duck cloth

Duck cloth (cotton duck)
Material: Plain-woven cotton (historically linen)
Uses: Sails, tents, workwear, bags, upholstery

Duck cloth, also called cotton duck, is a heavy, plain-woven fabric traditionally made from cotton. The word "duck" derives from the Dutch doek, meaning cloth or canvas. Duck cloth is closely related to canvas, though canvas today generally denotes a somewhat lighter or differently finished material.

Classification

Cotton duck is graded by weight using a numbered system: the lower the "duck number", the heavier the fabric. Numbered duck ranges from No. 1 (heaviest, about 24 oz per square yard) to No. 12, while lighter single-fill ducks are sold simply by ounce weight. The weave uses two yarns together in the warp and a single heavier yarn in the weft, producing a dense, durable, smooth-surfaced fabric.

Uses

Historically duck was the standard material for ships' sails, tents and wagon covers. Modern uses include work clothing and chore coats, tote bags and sneakers, hammocks, slipcovers and upholstery, sandbags, artist's canvas (usually primed with gesso), and industrial belting and filtration. Its tight weave resists wind and water better than most cotton fabrics, and it takes dye and printing well.
