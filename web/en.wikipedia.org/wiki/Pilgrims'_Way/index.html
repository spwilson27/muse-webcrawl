Pilgrims' Way

The Pilgrims' Way on the North Downs escarpment

The Pilgrims' Way is an ancient trackway in southern England running from Winchester in Hampshire to Canterbury in Kent, a distance of about 192 kilometres (119 miles). It is traditionally associated with pilgrims travelling to the shrine of St Thomas Becket at Canterbury Cathedral, following his murder in 1170 and canonisation in 1173.

Route

The way follows the chalk escarpment of the North Downs, keeping largely to the higher, drier ground along the southern slopes. From Winchester it passes through Alton, Farnham, Guildford, Dorking, Reigate, and along the downs through Kent via Wrotham, Hollingbourne and Charing to Canterbury. Much of the route coincides with the modern North Downs Way National Trail.

Antiquity

Although named for the medieval pilgrims, the track itself is far older: a prehistoric route along the downs has been used since the Stone Age, taking advantage of the dry chalk ridgeway above the wet clay valleys. In places the way is a sunken holloway, and it passes near prehistoric monuments and yew trees traditionally linked with pilgrim rest stops.

In literature

The route was popularised in the Victorian and Edwardian periods; Hilaire Belloc retraced and celebrated it in The Four Men (1911), and Julia Cartwright wrote an early guide to the way. Geoffrey Chaucer's pilgrims in The Canterbury Tales travelled from Southwark in London, joining the route's eastern end rather than following the Winchester road for its whole length.
