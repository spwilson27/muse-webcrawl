Trade unions in South Africa

Trade unions in South Africa have a history dating back to the 1880s and played a central role in the struggle against apartheid and in the country's post-1994 labour relations system. South African unions are organised principally into several competing federations.

History

Early craft unions served mainly white workers, while organisations such as the Industrial and Commercial Workers' Union (ICU), founded in 1919, mobilised black workers on a mass scale. Under apartheid, African unions were long excluded from official recognition; the strikes of 1973 and the Wiehahn reforms of 1979 opened the way to legal black unionism. The Congress of South African Trade Unions (COSATU), founded in 1985, became a key ally of the African National Congress in the liberation struggle.

Federations today

The major federations include COSATU, the Federation of Unions of South Africa (FEDUSA), the National Council of Trade Unions (NACTU), and the South African Federation of Trade Unions (SAFTU), formed in 2017 after splits within COSATU. Prominent individual unions include the National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) and the Association of Mineworkers and Construction Union (AMCU), whose rivalry was a factor in the 2012 Marikana events.
