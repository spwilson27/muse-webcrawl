Hot air balloon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Balloons over Cappadocia.

A hot air balloon is a lighter-than-air aircraft consisting of a large fabric envelope filled with heated air and a basket (gondola) carrying passengers and a burner. The Montgolfier brothers launched the first free flight with humans aboard in Paris in November 1783. Balloons cannot be steered directly — pilots climb or descend to find winds going the desired way — and today ballooning is chiefly a leisure and festival activity, most famously at Albuquerque and Cappadocia.

Principle

Heating air inside the envelope makes it less dense than the surrounding air, generating lift of roughly a quarter-kilogram per cubic metre for typical temperature differences. A propane burner heats the air; a parachute valve at the top vents it to descend. Envelopes are usually ripstop nylon.

History

Joseph-Michel and Jacques-Étienne Montgolfier demonstrated their balloon publicly in 1783; the first passengers were a sheep, a duck and a rooster, and on 21 November 1783 Pilâtre de Rozier and the Marquis d\'Arlandes made the first untethered human flight. Gas balloons followed within weeks (Jacques Charles), and ballooning later served military observation and record attempts, including the first non-stop round-the-world flight by Breitling Orbiter 3 in 1999.

Modern ballooning

Sport balloons carry 2–20 passengers in larger ride balloons. Festivals draw hundreds of balloons; competitions test precision navigation to targets. Weather limits are strict — calm dawn or dusk conditions are preferred because thermals and gusts make midday flight unsafe.
