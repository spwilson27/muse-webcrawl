The special wards of Tokyo (特別区, tokubetsu-ku) are 23 municipalities that together make up the core of the Metropolis of Tokyo, Japan — the area formerly constituting the City of Tokyo before it was abolished in 1943. Each special ward is a local government unit operating much like a city, with its own elected mayor and assembly, though the Tokyo Metropolitan Government retains authority over certain services such as water, sewerage, and fire fighting that are normally municipal functions.

The 23 wards

The wards are: Adachi, Arakawa, Bunkyō, Chiyoda, Chūō, Edogawa, Itabashi, Katsushika, Kita, Kōtō, Meguro, Minato, Nakano, Nerima, Ōta, Setagaya, Shibuya, Shinagawa, Shinjuku, Suginami, Sumida, Taitō, and Toshima. Chiyoda contains the Imperial Palace and the national government district; Shinjuku hosts the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building; Shibuya, Minato, and Chūō are major commercial and financial centres.

History

The wards originated when the old City of Tokyo's wards (expanded to 35 in 1932) were reorganized. In 1943 the city and prefecture were merged into the Tokyo Metropolis and the wards came under direct metropolitan control. Under the Local Autonomy Law of 1947 the area was reorganized into 23 special wards. Their autonomy was gradually increased by reforms in 1975 (direct election of ward mayors restored) and 2000, which made them "basic local public entities" comparable to cities.

Governance

Each ward elects a mayor (kuchō) and a ward assembly. Because key services are metropolitan, the system differs from other Japanese cities: a fiscal adjustment system redistributes tax revenues among the wards to equalize services. Together the 23 wards form the most densely populated part of the Greater Tokyo Area, the world's largest metropolitan economy.
