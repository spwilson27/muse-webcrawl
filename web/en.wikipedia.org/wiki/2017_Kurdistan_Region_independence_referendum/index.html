The 2017 Kurdistan Region independence referendum was held on 25 September 2017 in the autonomous Kurdistan Region of Iraq and in Kurdish-held disputed territories, including the city of Kirkuk. Voters were asked whether they wanted the region to become an independent state. With turnout of about 72 percent, 92.73 percent voted in favour. The referendum was organised by the Kurdistan Regional Government under President Masoud Barzani and was held despite strong opposition from the Iraqi federal government in Baghdad, neighbouring Turkey and Iran, and most of the international community, including the United States.

Background

Iraqi Kurds have long sought an independent state, and the Kurdistan Region gained constitutional autonomy after 2003. During the war against the Islamic State from 2014, Kurdish Peshmerga forces took control of disputed areas, including oil-rich Kirkuk, after the Iraqi army withdrew. Barzani announced the referendum in June 2017, arguing that the partnership with Baghdad had failed.

Aftermath

Baghdad declared the vote unconstitutional — a position upheld by Iraq's Federal Supreme Court — and responded with an international flight ban on Kurdish airports, border pressure with Turkey and Iran, and, in mid-October 2017, a military operation in which Iraqi forces and allied militias retook Kirkuk and most disputed territories with little resistance. The Kurdistan Regional Government offered to "freeze" the referendum's results and enter dialogue, and Barzani stepped down as president on 1 November 2017. Independence was not achieved, and the referendum is generally regarded as having set back the region's position within Iraq.
