Foreign Military Sales

Foreign Military Sales (FMS) is a program of the United States government through which the U.S. sells defense articles, services, and training to foreign governments and international organizations. It is authorized principally by the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 and the Arms Export Control Act of 1976, and is managed by the Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA) within the Department of Defense.

How it works

Under FMS, the U.S. government itself acts as the seller: the purchasing country signs a government-to-government agreement (a Letter of Offer and Acceptance) and the Department of Defense procures the equipment from American contractors on its behalf, typically at the same price and under the same contract terms as U.S. forces. This is distinct from Direct Commercial Sales, in which a foreign buyer contracts directly with a U.S. company under a State Department export license.

Scale and oversight

Major sales above statutory thresholds must be notified to Congress, which may block them by joint resolution. FMS sales regularly total tens of billions of dollars per year — exceeding 00 billion in fiscal year 2024 amid demand from allies following Russia's invasion of Ukraine — and cover everything from F-35 fighter aircraft to munitions, spare parts, and training packages.
