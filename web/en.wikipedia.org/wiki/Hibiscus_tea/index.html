Hibiscus tea


Hibiscus tea (karkade)

Hibiscus tea is an herbal tea made as an infusion or decoction from the calyces (sepals) of the roselle flower (Hibiscus sabdariffa). It is consumed both hot and cold, has a tart, cranberry-like flavour, and is a deep red colour. The beverage is known by many names around the world, including karkade in Arabic-speaking countries, agua de Jamaica in Mexico and Central America, sorrel in the Caribbean, bissap in West Africa, and zobo in Nigeria.

Consumption and culture

In Egypt and Sudan, karkade is traditionally served at weddings and is sold by street vendors both hot and chilled. In Mexico, agua fresca de Jamaica is a staple in taquerías. In Senegal, bissap is considered the national drink. Caribbean sorrel drink is strongly associated with Christmas and is often flavoured with ginger and spices. The tea is rich in vitamin C and anthocyanins and contains minerals, though it is naturally caffeine-free.

Health effects

Clinical studies and meta-analyses have found that regular consumption of hibiscus tea can modestly reduce systolic and diastolic blood pressure in people with hypertension. Because of this effect, consumption is sometimes cautioned for people taking antihypertensive medication, and it has traditionally been advised against during pregnancy.
