Nancy Hartsock

Nancy C. M. Hartsock (1943/1944 – 19 March 2015) was an American feminist political theorist and philosopher, best known for originating the concept of the feminist standpoint, a foundational idea in feminist epistemology.

Life and career

Hartsock earned her doctorate from the University of Chicago in 1972 and taught for many years at the University of Washington, where she helped build women's studies and taught political science until her retirement. Her first book, Money, Sex, and Power (1983), reworked Marxist categories to analyse male dominance.

Feminist standpoint theory

In her widely cited essay "The Feminist Standpoint: Developing the Ground for a Specifically Feminist Historical Materialism" (1983), Hartsock argued, by analogy with Marx's account of the proletariat, that women's lives — shaped by the sexual division of labour, bodily experience and care work — provide a standpoint from which the social order can be understood more completely and less partially than from the dominant male perspective. A standpoint, she stressed, is not automatic but achieved through political struggle and analysis.

Standpoint theory became one of the most debated frameworks in feminist theory, developed by scholars such as Sandra Harding and Dorothy Smith and criticised for essentialism by postmodern and intersectional theorists. Hartsock's later work, including The Feminist Standpoint Revisited (1998), addressed Foucault, power and globalisation.
