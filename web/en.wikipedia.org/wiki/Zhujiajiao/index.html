朱家角镇
Country: China
Municipality: Shanghai
District: Qingpu

Zhujiajiao (Chinese: 朱家角; pinyin: Zhūjiājiǎo) is an ancient water town in Qingpu District, Shanghai, China. Established over 1,700 years ago, it is sometimes called the "Venice of Shanghai" for its network of canals, stone bridges, and traditional architecture dating to the Ming and Qing dynasties.

History

The town grew as a trading centre for rice, cloth, and oil, served by its position on waterways linking Shanghai with Suzhou and the interior. Many of its surviving buildings, gardens, and bridges date from the Ming (1368–1644) and Qing (1644–1912) periods, when merchant families built large residences along the canals.

Features

Zhujiajiao is noted for its 36 stone bridges, the most famous being the Fangsheng Bridge, a five-arch bridge built in 1571 and the largest stone arch bridge in Shanghai. Other attractions include the Kezhi Garden, with its distinctive artificial hill and pavilion, the Yuanjin Buddhist Temple, and narrow lanes lined with shops selling local snacks such as zongzi and braised pork.

Transport

The town is served by Shanghai Metro Line 17, which opened in 2017 with a Zhujiajiao station, as well as bus services from central Shanghai. Its proximity to Dianshan Lake makes it a popular day-trip destination.
