Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (born Eliezer Yitzhak Perlman, 1858–1922) was a Lithuanian-Jewish lexicographer and newspaper editor who was the driving spirit behind the revival of the Hebrew language as a modern spoken tongue in Ottoman and Mandate Palestine.

Life and work

Inspired by Jewish national revival, Ben-Yehuda concluded that Hebrew — used for prayer and scholarship but not daily speech for centuries — must be spoken in homes and schools. Emigrating to Jerusalem in 1881, he raised his son Ben-Zion as the first native speaker of modern Hebrew, founded Hebrew newspapers (HaZvi), co-founded the Hebrew Language Committee (later the Academy of the Hebrew Language), and coined thousands of words for modern concepts.

Dictionary

His life's work, the monumental Complete Dictionary of Ancient and Modern Hebrew (17 volumes), was completed by his widow Hemda and son after his death in 1922 — the year Hebrew became an official language of Mandate Palestine.
