Kaluga (fish)

Scientific nameHuso dauricus (syn. Acipenser dauricus)
FamilyAcipenseridae (sturgeons)
RangeAmur River basin, Sea of Okhotsk, Sea of Japan
StatusCritically Endangered (IUCN)

The kaluga (Huso dauricus) is a large predatory sturgeon found in the Amur River basin of Russia and China and in adjacent coastal waters. It is one of the largest freshwater fishes in the world: historical specimens exceeded 5 metres in length and a tonne in weight, though such sizes are now rare.

Biology

Kaluga are anadromous or semi-anadromous, moving between the Amur estuary and the river's middle and lower reaches to spawn. Unlike most sturgeons they are strongly piscivorous as adults, feeding on salmon, carp and other fish; juveniles eat invertebrates. They mature late, females often not spawning until well into their second decade, and spawn only at intervals of several years.

Conservation

Heavy fishing for meat and the valuable roe sold as caviar, together with pollution and habitat change, caused a steep decline in the twentieth century. The species is listed as Critically Endangered and is included in CITES Appendix II; Russia and China operate restocking and protected-area programmes in the Amur basin.
