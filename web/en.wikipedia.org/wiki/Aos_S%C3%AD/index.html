Aos Sí

The aos sí (Irish pronunciation roughly "ees shee"; singular aes sídhe) are a supernatural race in Irish and Scottish mythology — the people of the sídhe, the hollow hills or fairy mounds. In Irish tradition they are often understood as descendants of the Tuatha Dé Danann, the god-like invaders of Ireland who withdrew underground after their defeat by the Milesians.

Beliefs

The aos sí were believed to live beneath mounds, in the western sea, or in an invisible parallel world. They could bring blessing or harm: folk practice sought to appease them with offerings of milk and food, avoided disturbing their forts and paths (the "fairy rings" and lone thorn trees), and used euphemisms such as "the Good People" or "the Fair Folk" to avoid attracting their attention. The bean sídhe (banshee) is one of their most famous figures.

In literature

Figures of the aos sí appear throughout medieval Irish literature and were revived in the Irish Literary Revival by writers such as W. B. Yeats and Lady Gregory, who collected folklore about them. They remain a staple of modern fantasy.
