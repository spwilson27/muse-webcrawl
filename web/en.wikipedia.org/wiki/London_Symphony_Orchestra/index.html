The London Symphony Orchestra (LSO) is a British symphony orchestra based in London. Founded in 1904 as a self-governing body of players who had resigned from the Queen's Hall Orchestra in a dispute over the use of deputy players, it is the oldest of London's symphony orchestras. The LSO was the first British orchestra to play abroad, performing in Paris in 1906, and the first to tour the United States, in 1912 — a trip for which it was booked on the RMS Titanic but changed sailing at the last minute.

History and work

The orchestra has been resident at the Barbican Centre since 1982. It is one of the world's most recorded orchestras, with an extensive discography and a large body of film soundtrack work, including John Williams's scores for the Star Wars films, and collaborations across many genres. Its education and community programme, LSO Discovery, was established in 1990, and its own record label, LSO Live, was launched in 1999.

Principal conductors have included André Previn, Claudio Abbado, Michael Tilson Thomas, Sir Colin Davis (the orchestra's longest-serving principal conductor), Valery Gergiev, and Sir Simon Rattle, who became music director in 2017.
