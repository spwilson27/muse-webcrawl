Peripheral

A peripheral device, or simply peripheral, is an external device that provides input to or output from a computer. In the early decades of computing, the CPU and main memory were considered the core of the system and everything else — storage, terminals, printers — was peripheral. While the term has largely fallen out of formal use as functions have been integrated onto motherboards and system-on-chip designs, it remains common in everyday usage for accessories such as keyboards, mice, monitors, printers, scanners and external drives.

Categories

Peripherals are commonly classified by the direction of data flow:

  • Input devices send data to the computer: keyboards, mice, trackballs, scanners, webcams, microphones, joysticks and graphics tablets.
  • Output devices receive data from the computer: monitors, printers, speakers, headphones and projectors.
  • Input/output devices do both: external hard drives, USB flash drives, network adapters, modems and touchscreens.

Connection

Historically, peripherals connected through a variety of dedicated ports — serial (RS-232), parallel, PS/2, SCSI and FireWire among them. Modern peripherals predominantly connect via USB, Thunderbolt, HDMI and DisplayPort, or wirelessly via Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. Operating systems communicate with peripherals through device drivers, and standardised device classes allow generic drivers to support many products without vendor software.
