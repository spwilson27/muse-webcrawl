Lattice-based cryptography

Lattice-based cryptography is the construction of cryptographic primitives based on the computational hardness of lattice problems, such as the shortest vector problem. It is a leading approach to post-quantum cryptography, because no efficient quantum algorithms are known for these problems.

Hard problemsSVP, LWE, SIS
Key constructionsKyber, Dilithium, NTRU
FieldPost-quantum cryptography

Foundations

A lattice is a regular grid of points generated by integer combinations of basis vectors. Miklós Ajtai's 1996 work showed that solving certain average-case lattice problems is as hard as worst-case instances, a prized property. Regev's Learning With Errors (LWE) problem (2005) underpins many schemes and won the Gödel Prize.

Standardisation

In 2024 NIST finalised post-quantum standards including ML-KEM (based on CRYSTALS-Kyber) for key encapsulation and ML-DSA (based on CRYSTALS-Dilithium) for signatures, both lattice-based, alongside the hash-based SLH-DSA. Lattice schemes offer relatively small keys and fast operations, though signature sizes can be larger than classical ones.
