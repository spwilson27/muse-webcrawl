Bonegilla is a bounded rural locality near Wodonga in north-eastern Victoria, Australia, on the southern shore of Lake Hume. It is best known as the site of the Bonegilla Migrant Reception and Training Centre, Australia's largest and longest-operating migrant reception centre of the post-war immigration programme.


Block 19, now a museum

Migrant centre

The site began as an army camp during the Second World War. In December 1947 it received its first migrants — displaced persons from the Baltic states arriving under Arthur Calwell's immigration scheme. Over the following 24 years, more than 300,000 migrants from some 50 nationalities passed through Bonegilla, making it the first Australian home of roughly one in twenty modern Australians. New arrivals were housed in unlined corrugated-iron and timber huts, given English lessons and training, and allocated jobs around Australia under two-year work contracts.

Conditions were basic, and the camp saw protests, most notably the riots of 1952 and 1961 by Italian and German migrants frustrated by unemployment and delays in job allocation. The centre closed in 1971 as reception policy moved to urban hostels.

Heritage

The one surviving block, Block 19, is heritage-listed and operates as a museum and national monument, telling the stories of post-war migration to Australia. Bonegilla has become a symbolic starting point for many migrant family histories, and reunions of former residents are regularly held there.
