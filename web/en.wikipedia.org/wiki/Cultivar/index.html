Cultivar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cultivar
DefinitionA plant variety produced by selective breeding in cultivation
Term coinedLiberty Hyde Bailey, 1923
Naming governed byInternational Code of Nomenclature for Cultivated Plants (ICNCP)
NotationSingle quotes, e.g. Malus domestica 'Granny Smith'

A cultivar (short for cultivated variety) is a kind of cultivated plant that people have selected for desired traits and which retains those traits when propagated. Methods used to propagate cultivars include division, root and stem cuttings, offsets, grafting, tissue culture, or carefully controlled seed production.

Etymology

The word cultivar was coined by the American horticulturist Liberty Hyde Bailey in 1923, blending cultivated and variety. Bailey distinguished cultivars from botanical varieties, which occur naturally.

Nomenclature

Cultivar names are regulated by the International Code of Nomenclature for Cultivated Plants. Since 1959, new cultivar epithets must be in a modern language rather than Latin, and they are written in single quotation marks after the scientific name — for example, Malus domestica 'Granny Smith' or Rosa 'Peace'. The epithet is not italicized.

Legal protection

Commercially valuable cultivars may be protected by plant breeders' rights or plant patents, giving breeders control over propagation and sale for a period of years. Trademarked selling names are distinct from cultivar epithets, though the two are often confused in commerce.

Importance

Most food crops are grown as cultivars selected for yield, flavor, disease resistance, or climate tolerance. Famous examples include the Cavendish banana, the Hass avocado, and thousands of named apple, grape, and rose cultivars maintained by orchards and gardens worldwide.
