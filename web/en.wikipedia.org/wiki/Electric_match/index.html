Electric match

An electric match is a device used in pyrotechnics and model rocketry to ignite a combustible material electrically. When an electric current is passed through it, a small bridge wire heats rapidly and ignites a bead of pyrotechnic composition, producing a burst of flame capable of igniting black powder, flash powder or a propellant charge.

Construction

The typical electric match consists of two lead wires joined by a thin, high-resistance bridge wire — often made of nichrome — which is coated with a small bead of heat-sensitive pyrotechnic material, protected by a lacquer coating and sometimes by a plastic shroud. Firing currents are commonly around one ampere; commercial units specify both a no-fire current and an all-fire current for safety and reliability.

Uses

Electric matches are widely used to fire fireworks displays remotely and precisely (via electronic firing systems), to ignite model rocket motors, in theatrical effects, and in some airbag and blasting applications, where similar devices are classed as igniters or detonators and are subject to regulation. Because they contain a small quantity of energetic material and can be ignited by static discharge or stray currents, they must be handled with their leads shunted until use.
