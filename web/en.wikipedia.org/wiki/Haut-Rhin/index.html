Haut-Rhin

Haut-Rhin (68) Country: France
Region: Grand Est
Prefecture: Colmar
Largest city: Mulhouse
Area: 3,525 km²
Population: ~765,000

Haut-Rhin (French pronunciation: [oʁɛ̃]; Alsatian: Owerelsàss, German: Oberelsass) is a department in the Grand Est region of north-eastern France, bordering Germany to the east and Switzerland to the south. Named after the river Rhine, it is the southern of the two historical Alsatian departments, paired with Bas-Rhin ("Lower Rhine") to the north — the names refer to their position along the river's course, not altitude.

The Alsace wine route runs through the vineyards of Haut-Rhin.

Geography and history

The department stretches from the Rhine plain in the east to the Vosges mountains in the west, whose highest point, the Grand Ballon (1,424 m), lies within it. The prefecture is Colmar, while Mulhouse is the largest city and an historic industrial centre for textiles, chemicals, and automobiles. Created in 1790 during the French Revolution, Haut-Rhin was truncated in 1871 when Germany annexed Alsace and kept the Belfort area French; the department's area was further adjusted after the First World War. Since 2021, Haut-Rhin and Bas-Rhin have shared many functions through the European Collectivity of Alsace, while retaining their departmental status.

Economy and culture

The department is known for Alsace wines produced along the Route des Vins, for tourism in picturesque villages such as Eguisheim, Riquewihr, and Kaysersberg, and for cross-border commuting to Basel and Freiburg. The Alsatian dialect, a form of Alemannic German, is still spoken by a minority of residents.
