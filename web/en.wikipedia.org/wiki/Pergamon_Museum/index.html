Overview

The Pergamon Museum (Pergamonmuseum) is a listed building on Berlin's Museum Island, part of the Berlin State Museums. Opened in 1930, it was built to house monumental reconstructed architecture excavated by German archaeologists in the Ottoman Empire and beyond: the Pergamon Altar from Pergamon (Turkey), the Market Gate of Miletus, and the Ishtar Gate and Processional Way of Babylon (Iraq). It is Germany's most-visited art museum in many years.

Collections and controversies

The museum holds the Antikensammlung, the Museum of the Ancient Near East (Vorderasiatisches Museum) and the Museum of Islamic Art, whose treasures include the Mshatta Façade from Jordan and the Aleppo Room. Like other great universal museums, it faces provenance and restitution debates — Turkey has sought the Pergamon Altar's return (the altar hall has been closed for renovation for years, with the museum undergoing a long phased refurbishment).
