Champ (folklore)

Champ (also spelled "Champy") is the name given to a lake monster said to live in Lake Champlain, a large freshwater lake on the border of Vermont and New York in the United States and Quebec in Canada. Reports of a large, serpent- or plesiosaur-like creature in the lake date from the 19th century, and Champ has become a beloved regional mascot and a fixture of local folklore and tourism, comparable to Scotland's Loch Ness Monster.

History of sightings

The name of the lake's namesake, French explorer Samuel de Champlain, is often tied to a 1609 journal entry describing a large fish the Indigenous inhabitants called chaousarou — now generally identified as a gar — rather than a monster. Documented monster reports accumulated in the 19th century: in 1873 the New York Times reported an "enormous serpent" seen by a railroad crew near Dresden, New York, and showman P. T. Barnum offered a reward for the creature's hide. Sightings rose again in the 1970s and 1980s.

The most famous evidence is a 1977 photograph by Sandra Mansi, showing a dark shape with a curved neck-like protrusion rising from the water near St. Albans, Vermont. The photograph has resisted definitive debunking, though sceptics suggest a floating log or other mundane objects; Mansi never revealed the exact location and the original negative was lost.

Explanations

Proposed natural explanations include misidentified lake sturgeon, schools of fish, otters swimming in line, floating logs, and the standing waves (seiches) and wakes that can produce humps on the lake's surface. Cryptozoologists have suggested a surviving plesiosaur or a primitive whale-like creature, but the lake — formed at the end of the last ice age roughly 10,000 years ago — could not sustain a breeding population of large unknown animals, and scientific consensus regards Champ as folklore.

Cultural impact

Champ appears on signs, merchandise, and festival imagery around the lake; the Vermont Lake Monsters minor-league baseball team took its name and mascot from the legend, and Port Henry, New York, holds an annual Champ Day. In the 1980s both Vermont and New York passed resolutions recognising Champ and discouraging its harm — an early, tongue-in-cheek form of "protection" for a cryptid.
