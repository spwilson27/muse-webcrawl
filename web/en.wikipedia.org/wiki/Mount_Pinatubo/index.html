Mount Pinatubo is an active stratovolcano in the Zambales Mountains on the island of Luzon in the Philippines, at the junction of the provinces of Zambales, Tarlac, and Pampanga. Its eruption on 15 June 1991 was the second-largest terrestrial eruption of the 20th century (Volcanic Explosivity Index 6), and it occurred after roughly 500 years of dormancy, coinciding with the passage of Typhoon Yunya.

The 1991 eruption

Precursory activity began in April 1991, and scientists from PHIVOLCS and the U.S. Geological Survey successfully forecast the climactic eruption, enabling the evacuation of tens of thousands of people, including the personnel of Clark Air Base, and saving many lives. The climactic blast ejected more than 10 cubic kilometres of material, sent ash columns over 40 kilometres into the stratosphere, and formed a summit caldera 2.5 kilometres wide. Coinciding with the typhoon, wet ash accumulation collapsed roofs across the region; the eruption and subsequent lahars killed roughly 800–900 people and displaced some 200,000. The eruption injected about 20 million tonnes of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, which formed a global haze that cooled the Earth's surface by approximately 0.5 °C over the following two years and temporarily increased ozone depletion.

Aftermath

For years afterward, monsoon rains remobilised the ash deposits as destructive lahars. The caldera filled to form Lake Pinatubo, a turquoise crater lake that has since become a trekking destination. The eruption accelerated the closure of U.S. bases at Clark and Subic Bay, and the indigenous Aeta people, many of whom lived on the mountain's slopes, suffered lasting displacement.
