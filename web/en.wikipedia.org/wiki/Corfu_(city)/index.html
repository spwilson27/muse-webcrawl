Corfu (Greek: Κέρκυρα, Kérkyra) is a city and a former municipality on the island of Corfu in the Ionian Islands of Greece. It is the capital of the Corfu regional unit and the Ionian Islands region, and the island's principal port and commercial centre. Its Old Town, shaped by nearly four centuries of Venetian rule and later French and British administrations, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007.

The Old Fortress (Palaio Frourio) guards the entrance to Corfu's harbour.

History

Ancient Korkyra, a colony of Corinth founded around 734 BC, lay south of the present city near Palaiopolis; its naval power and quarrel with Corinth helped trigger the Peloponnesian War. The modern city grew between two fortresses — the Byzantine-Venetian Old Fortress and the Venetian New Fortress — as Venice held Corfu from 1386 to 1797, successfully resisting Ottoman sieges, most famously in 1716. France (1797–99, 1807–14) and then Britain (1815–1864), as capital of the United States of the Ionian Islands, left further marks before union with Greece in 1864.

Sights

The Old Town's landmarks include the Spianada, one of Greece's largest squares; the French-built Liston arcade, modelled on the Rue de Rivoli; the Old and New Fortresses; the Palace of St. Michael and St. George, housing the Museum of Asian Art; and the church of Saint Spyridon, the island's patron saint, whose relics are carried in procession four times a year. The Ionian University is based in the city, and the Achilleion palace lies in nearby Gastouri.

Transport

Corfu International Airport ("Ioannis Kapodistrias") lies just south of the city, and ferries connect the port with Igoumenitsa on the mainland, Italy, and Albania.
