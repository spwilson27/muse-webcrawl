Gestapo

Full name: Geheime Staatspolizei
Formed: 1933
Abolished: 1945
Chiefs: Rudolf Diels, Reinhard Heydrich, Heinrich Müller

The Gestapo (short for Geheime Staatspolizei, "Secret State Police") was the official secret police of Nazi Germany and German-occupied Europe, notorious for its brutal suppression of opposition without judicial oversight.

History

Founded in Prussia in 1933 by Hermann Göring from the Prussian political police, control passed to Heinrich Himmler, who extended the model across the Reich. Under Reinhard Heydrich and then Heinrich Müller, the Gestapo was folded into the RSHA in 1939 as Amt IV. It could impose "protective custody" — imprisonment in concentration camps without trial — and relied heavily on denunciations from the public.

Role in Nazi terror

The Gestapo persecuted Jews, political opponents, resisters, clergy, homosexuals and others, deported victims to camps, and in occupied Europe fought resistance movements with torture and reprisals. Despite its fearsome image, it was numerically small and depended on collaboration and informers. At Nuremberg it was declared a criminal organisation. See SS and Nazi Germany.
