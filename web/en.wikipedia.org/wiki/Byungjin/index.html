Byungjin

Byungjin couples nuclear-armed military strength with economic construction.

Byungjin (Korean: 병진; Hanja: 竝進; literally "parallel development" or "simultaneous advancement") is a policy of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) of simultaneously developing the national economy and the country's nuclear weapons programme. The term was revived by leader Kim Jong Un in March 2013 and was adopted as the official strategic line of the Workers' Party of Korea, though its origins lie in a slogan first advanced by Kim Il Sung in the 1960s.

History

Kim Il Sung first articulated a policy of building the economy and the armed forces in parallel at the Fifth Plenary Meeting of the Fourth Central Committee of the Workers' Party in December 1962, in response to the perceived threat from the United States and the strain of the Sino-Soviet split. The idea was later overshadowed by the Songun (military-first) politics emphasised under Kim Jong Il.

Kim Jong Un's byungjin line

At the March 31, 2013 plenary meeting of the Workers' Party Central Committee, Kim Jong Un proclaimed a "new strategic line of simultaneously pushing forward economic construction and the building of nuclear armed forces." The policy held that a nuclear deterrent would allow North Korea to reduce spending on conventional forces while shielding economic development, and it was written into law by the Supreme People's Assembly, which adopted an ordinance on consolidating the position of a nuclear weapons state. Byungjin legitimised the accelerated missile and nuclear tests of 2013–2017, including thermonuclear and intercontinental ballistic missile tests in 2017.

Suspension and aftermath

On April 20, 2018, ahead of summits with South Korean President Moon Jae-in and U.S. President Donald Trump, Kim Jong Un declared the byungjin line "victoriously concluded," announcing that North Korea would suspend nuclear and ICBM tests and concentrate all efforts on socialist economic construction. Analysts debate whether the policy was genuinely completed or merely rebranded; North Korea resumed and expanded its weapons programmes after the collapse of diplomacy in 2019, and in 2022–2023 enshrined its nuclear status in law while abandoning the test moratorium.
