Leo III the Isaurian

Leo III the Isaurian (Greek: Λέων ὁ Ἴσαυρος; c. 685 – 18 June 741), also known as Leo the Syrian, was Byzantine emperor from 717 until his death in 741. He founded the Isaurian dynasty, repelled the great Arab siege of Constantinople in 717–718, and initiated the Byzantine iconoclasm controversy by prohibiting the veneration of religious images.

Rise and defence of Constantinople

Leo was born in Germanikeia in northern Syria (modern Kahramanmaraş) — hence "the Syrian"; the label "Isaurian" derives from his family's later relocation and became attached to his dynasty. A successful general (strategos) of the Anatolic theme, he negotiated his way to the throne in 717, deposing Theodosios III. Months later the Umayyad Caliphate launched an enormous land and sea assault on Constantinople under Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik. Using Greek fire, the city's walls, and a harsh winter, Leo starved the besiegers into withdrawal in August 718 — a victory often ranked among the decisive defensive stands in European history. His later victory at Akroinon (740) further marked the turning point against Arab raids in Anatolia.

Reforms and iconoclasm

Leo issued the Ecloga, a streamlined law code in Greek, reformed taxation and administration, and resettled populations to secure the frontiers. In 726 he began moving against the veneration of icons — reportedly prompted in part by a volcanic eruption he read as a sign of divine anger — and in 730 deposed Patriarch Germanos I and formally banned icon veneration. The policy split the empire, alienated the papacy and Italy, and launched a controversy that would convulse Byzantium for more than a century. Leo died of dropsy in 741 and was succeeded by his son Constantine V.
