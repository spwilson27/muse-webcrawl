Bernard Grenfell

Bernard Pyne Grenfell FBA (16 December 1869 – 18 May 1926) was an English scientist and Egyptologist, professor of papyrology at the University of Oxford. With his friend and colleague Arthur Surridge Hunt, he excavated the Egyptian city of Oxyrhynchus and recovered hundreds of thousands of ancient papyri, transforming the study of the ancient world.

Career

Grenfell was educated at The Queen's College, Oxford, where he became a fellow. Working for the Egypt Exploration Fund (later Society), he and Hunt began excavating at Oxyrhynchus, an ancient rubbish-heap site about 160 km south of Cairo, in the winter of 1896–97. The ancient town's dumps yielded an unparalleled trove of papyrus documents from the Ptolemaic and Roman periods.

Among the Oxyrhynchus Papyri the pair identified were lost works of Greek literature — poems of Sappho, Pindar and Bacchylides, plays of Menander and Sophocles — alongside private letters, official documents and early Christian texts, including fragments of the Gospels and the sayings collection known as the Logia of Jesus. Grenfell and Hunt founded and edited the publication series The Oxyrhynchus Papyri, whose volumes continue to appear.

Grenfell became Oxford's first professor of papyrology in 1908. Ill health, including a breakdown in 1906 and worsening mental illness, curtailed his later career; Hunt carried on the partnership's work until his own death in 1934.
