Fishing trawler

Fishing trawler
TypeCommercial fishing vessel
GearTrawl nets (bottom or midwater)
ClassesStern trawler, side trawler, freezer trawler, factory trawler

A fishing trawler is a commercial fishing vessel designed to operate trawls — large nets towed through the water, either along the seabed (bottom trawling) or at mid-depth (pelagic or midwater trawling). Trawlers are among the most important vessels of the world's fishing fleets, landing large quantities of groundfish such as cod, haddock and hake, as well as shrimp and pelagic species.

Design

Trawlers range from small open boats to large factory ships over 100 metres long. The net is spread horizontally by a pair of steel "otter boards" (trawl doors) that plane apart as the vessel moves, and is hauled aboard with powerful winches over the stern (stern trawlers) or the side (older side trawlers). Modern trawlers carry sonar and net-sounders to locate fish and monitor the gear.

Types

  • Wetfish trawlers keep the catch fresh on ice for short trips.
  • Freezer trawlers freeze the catch at sea, allowing voyages of weeks.
  • Factory trawlers process, fillet and freeze fish on board and are effectively floating factories.
  • Beam trawlers tow nets held open by a rigid beam, mainly for flatfish and shrimp.
  • Pair trawlers tow a single large net between two vessels.

History

Sailing trawlers developed in the nineteenth century at ports such as Brixham in England, whose smacks pioneered trawling in the North Sea. Steam and then diesel power, combined with the stern ramp and onboard freezing after the Second World War, transformed trawling into an industrial-scale fishery. Bottom trawling has been criticised for habitat damage and bycatch, leading to gear restrictions and marine protected areas in many jurisdictions.
