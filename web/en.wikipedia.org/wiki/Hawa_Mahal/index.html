Hawa Mahal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The pink, honeycombed five-storey facade of the Hawa Mahal in Jaipur
The pink, honeycombed five-storey facade of the Hawa Mahal in Jaipur

The Hawa Mahal ('Palace of the Winds') is a palace in Jaipur, India, built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh. Its distinctive five-storey facade of pink and red sandstone is a screen of 953 small windows (jharokhas) decorated with intricate latticework, designed so that royal women, observing strict purdah, could watch street festivals unseen — and so that the Venturi effect breeze cools the chambers behind, giving the palace its name. It is Jaipur's most famous landmark.

Architecture

Designed by Lal Chand Ustad in the form of Krishna's crown, the Hawa Mahal is properly the rear facade of the City Palace complex, only one room deep at the top. Its pyramidal profile, domed canopies and filigreed screens epitomise Rajput architecture under Mughal influence; a ramp (rather than stairs) connects its floors, built for palanquins and wheeled chairs.

Today

Maintained by the Rajasthan archaeology department, the palace houses a small museum and is viewed best in early morning, when sunlight strikes the facade across the bazaar street it was built to overlook.
