Albert-László Barabási (born 30 March 1967) is a Hungarian-American physicist and a leading figure in the modern science of networks. He is a professor at Northeastern University in Boston, where he directs the Center for Complex Network Research, and is also affiliated with Harvard Medical School.

Career and research

Barabási was born in Cârța, in the Hungarian-speaking Szekler region of Romania, and studied physics and engineering in Bucharest and Budapest before earning his PhD at Boston University in 1994. While at the University of Notre Dame, he and Réka Albert published their influential 1999 paper introducing the Barabási–Albert model, which showed that many real-world networks — including the World Wide Web, social networks and biological networks — grow by preferential attachment and develop scale-free degree distributions with highly connected hubs.

Books and other work

His popular books include Linked: The New Science of Networks (2002), Bursts (2010), on patterns in human behaviour, and The Formula (2018), on the science of success. He has also worked on network medicine, applying network methods to human disease. His surname is sometimes rendered without diacritics as "Barabasi," and his given name is hyphenated as Albert-Laszlo in some listings.
