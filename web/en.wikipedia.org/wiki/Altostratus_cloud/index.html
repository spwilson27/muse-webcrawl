Altostratus cloud

Altostratus is a middle-altitude cloud genus composed primarily of water droplets and ice crystals. It is a mid-level cloud belonging to the stratiform clouds, characterised by a generally uniform sheet or layer of grey or bluish cloud that covers part or all of the sky.

Characteristics

Altostratus forms at heights of roughly 2,000 to 7,000 metres (about 6,500–23,000 ft) in temperate regions. The cloud sheet is thick enough to obscure the sun, which may appear as a dim disc through thinner parts, as if seen through ground glass; unlike cirrostratus, altostratus produces no halo phenomenon. Shadows on the ground are weak or absent beneath thicker altostratus. Precipitation may fall from altostratus as virga, or as light continuous rain or snow that reaches the ground, in which case the cloud may merge into nimbostratus.

Formation and weather significance

Altostratus commonly forms by the gentle lifting of a large, stable air mass over a wide area, often ahead of a warm front or occluded front of a mid-latitude cyclone. In a typical frontal sequence, cirrus and cirrostratus give way to thickening altostratus, which heralds the approach of more persistent precipitation. Altostratus can also form from the thickening of cirrostratus or the spreading of cumulonimbus mid-sections.

Classification

The genus has species including altostratus translucidus, opacus, duplicatus, undulatus, and radiatus, and varieties distinguished by transparency and wave structure. In the WMO International Cloud Atlas its abbreviation is As.
