Adolph Alexander Weinman (December 11, 1870 – August 8, 1952) was a German-born American sculptor and architectural sculptor. He is best known to the general public as the designer of two celebrated United States coins: the Mercury dime (1916–1945) and the Walking Liberty half dollar (1916–1947).

Life and career

Weinman was born in Durmersheim, near Karlsruhe, Germany, and emigrated to the United States as a boy. He studied at Cooper Union and the Art Students League of New York and worked in the studios of sculptors including Augustus Saint-Gaudens, whose influence marked his classically styled work. He became a sought-after architectural sculptor, contributing sculpture to buildings such as the Pennsylvania Railroad Station in New York, the Municipal Building in Manhattan, and numerous memorials and monuments.

Coin designs

In 1916 the United States Mint adopted Weinman's designs for the dime and half dollar as part of a redesign of American coinage. The dime's obverse, a portrait of Liberty wearing a winged cap, was popularly mistaken for the Roman god Mercury, giving the coin its enduring nickname. The Walking Liberty half dollar's obverse — Liberty striding toward the dawn — was later reused for the American Silver Eagle bullion coin first issued in 1986.
