Alma Har'el (born 1976) is an Israeli-American music video and film director. She is best known for her documentaries Bombay Beach (2011) and Love True (2016) and her narrative feature debut, Honey Boy (2019), and is the founder of the advocacy organisation Free the Bid, later relaunched as Free the Work, which campaigns for the hiring of women and underrepresented directors in advertising and film.

Career

Born in Tel Aviv, Har'el began her career directing music videos and live visuals in Israel and Europe, including work with the band Balkan Beat Box, before moving to the United States. Her first feature documentary, Bombay Beach — a dreamlike portrait of residents of a decaying resort community on California's Salton Sea, with choreographed dance sequences and music by Beirut and Bob Dylan — won the top documentary prize at the 2011 Tribeca Film Festival. Love True (2016) blended documentary and fiction to explore young people's experiences of love in Alaska, New York, and Hawaii.

Honey Boy and later work

Har'el's narrative debut Honey Boy, written by Shia LaBeouf from his own childhood as a child actor with an abusive father, premiered at the 2019 Sundance Film Festival, where it won a Special Jury Award for vision and craft; Har'el also became the first woman to win the Directors Guild of America Award for Outstanding First-Time Feature Film. She later directed the Apple TV+ limited series Lady in the Lake (2024), starring Natalie Portman. In advertising she has directed campaigns for major brands and was the first woman to win the DGA Award for Outstanding Commercial Direction.

Advocacy

Frustrated by the low representation of women directing commercials, Har'el founded Free the Bid in 2016, asking agencies and brands to include at least one woman in every director bidding process; the initiative expanded in 2019 as Free the Work, a curated database of women and underrepresented creators.
