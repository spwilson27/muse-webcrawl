Treaty of Nerchinsk

Treaty of Nerchinsk

Signed7 September 1689 (27 August O.S.)
LocationNerchinsk, Siberia
PartiesQing Empire; Tsardom of Russia
Language of recordLatin
InterpretersJean-François Gerbillon; Tomás Pereira (Jesuits)

The Treaty of Nerchinsk of 1689 was the first treaty concluded between China — then ruled by the Qing dynasty under the Kangxi Emperor — and a European power, the Tsardom of Russia. It ended decades of border conflict over the Amur River valley, fixed the Russo-Chinese frontier along the Argun River and the Stanovoy Mountains, and opened regular trade between the two empires.

Background

From the 1640s Russian Cossack explorers and traders pushed east to the Amur, clashing with the Qing, for whom the region bordered the Manchu homeland. In 1685 and 1686 Qing forces besieged the Russian fort of Albazin, and both sides — Russia overextended, the Kangxi Emperor anxious to turn against the Dzungar Mongols — chose negotiation. Plenipotentiaries Songgotu for the Qing and Fyodor Golovin for Russia met at the Russian post of Nerchinsk in the summer of 1689, each side backed by thousands of troops.

Terms

Because the negotiators shared no language, two Jesuit missionaries at the Qing court, Jean-François Gerbillon and Tomás Pereira, interpreted, and the authoritative text was drawn up in Latin. Russia abandoned Albazin and all claims south of the Stanovoy range; the border ran along the Argun and Gorbitsa rivers. In return, Russian caravans gained the right to trade in Beijing, fugitives were to be extradited, and the territory between the Uda River and the mountains was left undelimited. The settlement held for nearly 170 years, until Russia exploited Qing weakness in the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860).

Significance

Negotiated between equals in the Westphalian diplomatic manner, Nerchinsk is remembered as the moment Qing China first engaged European-style treaty diplomacy — a precedent later Chinese governments viewed with pride, in contrast to the "unequal treaties" of the nineteenth century.
