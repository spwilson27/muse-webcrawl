Dutch Sandwich

The Double Irish with a Dutch sandwich: royalties pass through a Netherlands company to avoid Irish withholding tax.

The Dutch Sandwich is a tax-avoidance structure used by multinational companies as part of the Double Irish arrangement. It inserts a company in the Netherlands between two Irish companies so that royalty income can move from the first Irish company to a second, offshore-managed Irish company without incurring Irish withholding tax.

How it works

In the classic structure, an Irish operating company pays royalties for intellectual property to a Dutch subsidiary; because Ireland levies no withholding tax on royalty payments to EU companies, the money passes to the Netherlands untaxed. The Dutch company then pays the royalties on to a second Irish company that is tax-resident in a zero-tax jurisdiction such as Bermuda. Dutch law historically imposed no withholding tax on outbound royalties, so the Dutch layer—the "sandwich" filling—lets the payment exit the structure without the Irish withholding tax that would have applied on a direct payment to the offshore company.

Closure of the loophole

Under international pressure, Ireland announced in 2014 that the Double Irish would be phased out, closing to new entrants in 2015 and ending entirely in 2020. The Netherlands likewise introduced a conditional withholding tax on royalties paid to low-tax jurisdictions from 2021, and the OECD's BEPS programme and the global minimum tax agreement have further reduced the value of such structures. Large technology companies that once used the arrangement have since restructured their intellectual-property holdings.
