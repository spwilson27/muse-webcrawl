Virtual Boy

Virtual Boy
Manufacturer: Nintendo
Type: Video game console
Released: 1995 (Japan, North America)
Discontinued: 1996
Units sold: ~770,000

The Virtual Boy is a 32-bit tabletop portable video game console developed and manufactured by Nintendo. Released in 1995, it was marketed as the first console capable of displaying stereoscopic "3D" graphics. The player looks into a neoprene eyepiece on the front of the console, which displays monochromatic red-and-black visuals using a pair of LED arrays and oscillating mirrors.

History

The Virtual Boy was designed by Gunpei Yokoi, creator of the Game Boy and the Metroid series, together with Nintendo's Research & Development 1 division. Nintendo had acquired the underlying display technology from the Massachusetts-based company Reflection Technology. The console was released in Japan on 21 July 1995 and in North America on 21 August 1995, at a price of US$179.95.

Hardware

The system uses a NEC V810 32-bit RISC processor running at 20 MHz. Its display renders a 384×224 image for each eye in four shades of red against black. Because it is not worn like a headset, the console stands on a folding bipod on a tabletop.

Reception and legacy

The Virtual Boy was a commercial failure, selling only about 770,000 units. Reviewers criticized its monochromatic graphics, awkward ergonomics, high price, and a small library of just 22 games, including Mario's Tennis, Teleroboxer, Wario Land, and Red Alarm. Some users reported headaches and eyestrain. Nintendo discontinued it in 1996, less than a year after launch, making it the company's shortest-lived console. Despite its failure, it is remembered as an early, ambitious attempt at consumer virtual reality.
