The 1888–89 Football League was the first season of the Football League, the first professional association football league competition in the world. It ran from 8 September 1888 until 20 April 1889 and was contested by twelve clubs from the Midlands and the North of England. Preston North End won the championship without losing a single match, completing the season unbeaten and also winning the FA Cup to become the first team to achieve the league and cup "Double". Their unbeaten side became known as "The Invincibles".

History

The Football League was founded on 17 April 1888 at the Anderton's Hotel in London by William McGregor, a director of Aston Villa. McGregor had written to leading professional clubs proposing a regular, organised fixture list to replace the haphazard friendly matches that were frequently cancelled when clubs preferred cup ties. The twelve founding members were Accrington, Aston Villa, Blackburn Rovers, Bolton Wanderers, Burnley, Derby County, Everton, Notts County, Preston North End, Stoke, West Bromwich Albion and Wolverhampton Wanderers.

Under the original rules, teams earned two points for a win and one point for a draw. There was no automatic relegation; instead, the bottom four clubs at the end of the season had to apply for re-election along with clubs wishing to join the League.

Season summary

Preston North End dominated the season from start to finish, winning 18 and drawing 4 of their 22 matches and scoring 74 goals. They were confirmed as champions on 5 January 1889, with several games still to play. Aston Villa finished second, eleven points behind, and Wolverhampton Wanderers third. At the bottom, Stoke finished last for the second consecutive position reckoning, but all four bottom clubs — Stoke, Burnley, Derby County and Notts County — were successfully re-elected.

John Goodall of Preston was the league's top scorer with 21 goals. The season also saw the first recorded league hat-tricks and, on 8 September 1888, five matches played on the opening day, including Aston Villa's 1–1 draw with Wolverhampton Wanderers, in which Gershom Cox of Villa was credited by many with the first-ever league goal — later research suggested it may have been an own goal, and that Fred Dewhurst of Preston may in fact have scored first.

Legacy

The success of the first league season established the round-robin league format as the foundation of English football. Preston's unbeaten achievement was not matched in the English top flight until Arsenal repeated it in the 2003–04 Premier League season. The twelve founder members are commemorated with plaques at their grounds and a monument at the site of the Anderton's Hotel.
