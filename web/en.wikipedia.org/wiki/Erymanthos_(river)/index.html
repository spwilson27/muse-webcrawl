The Erymanthos (Greek: Ερύμανθος) is a river in the western Peloponnese of Greece. It rises on the southern slopes of Mount Erymanthos in Arcadia and flows south and southwest through a deep, forested gorge, forming part of the boundary between Arcadia and Elis, before joining the river Alfeios (Alpheus).

Course and landscape

The upper valley is mountainous and largely unspoiled, with plane trees, firs and chestnut forest along the banks. The river passes near villages such as Tripotama, where it is joined by tributaries, and is popular for rafting and kayaking in spring. A road through the gorge connects the region with Patras and Olympia.

Mythology

The mountain and river share their name with the Erymanthian Boar of Greek mythology, a gigantic boar that ravaged the area around Psophis. Capturing it alive was the fourth of the Labours of Heracles: the hero chased the beast through deep snow, netted it, and carried it back to Eurystheus at Mycenae.
