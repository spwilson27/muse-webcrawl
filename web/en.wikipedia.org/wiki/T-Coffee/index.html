T-Coffee

T-Coffee (Tree-based Consistency Objective Function for Alignment Evaluation) is a multiple sequence alignment program for biological sequences (DNA, RNA and proteins). It is notable for combining the results of multiple pairwise alignment methods and for evaluating alignment quality using a consistency-based objective function.

Method

T-Coffee first builds a library of pairwise alignments of all the input sequences, generated by methods such as ClustalW and Lalign, and extends the library by triplet-based consistency processing: aligned residue pairs are re-scored according to how consistently they appear across intermediate sequences. A progressive alignment is then constructed to maximise agreement with this extended library. This typically yields alignments a few percent more accurate than standard progressive methods, at a cost in speed.

Variants and usage

The T-Coffee package has grown into a family of tools, including M-Coffee (which combines the outputs of many aligners), R-Coffee for RNA sequences with secondary structure, PSI-Coffee for distantly related proteins using profile information, and 3D-Coffee/Expresso, which incorporate structural information. T-Coffee can also be used with the CORE index, which scores the reliability of each aligned position. It is widely used in comparative genomics and phylogenetics, especially where alignment accuracy matters more than speed.
