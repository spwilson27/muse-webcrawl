Anak Krakatau (Indonesian for "Child of Krakatoa") is an island volcano in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra in the Indonesian province of Lampung. It emerged from the sea in 1927 within the caldera left by the catastrophic 1883 eruption of Krakatoa, which destroyed the original volcanic island and killed more than 36,000 people. Anak Krakatau grew through repeated eruptions for nearly a century before a major flank collapse in December 2018 drastically reduced its height and generated a deadly tsunami.

History

Submarine eruptions at the site were first observed in 1927, and the new island broke the surface permanently in 1930. Thereafter the cone grew steadily, averaging several metres per year during active periods, through Strombolian eruptions that built up layers of ash and lava. By 2018 it stood about 338 metres above sea level. The volcano has been a natural laboratory for the study of island colonisation by plants and animals on new volcanic land.

2018 eruption and tsunami

On 22 December 2018, an eruption triggered the collapse of the volcano's southwestern flank — roughly two-thirds of the cone's volume slid into the sea. The displacement generated a tsunami that struck the coasts of Banten and Lampung without warning, killing at least 437 people and injuring thousands. The volcano's height fell to about 157 metres. The event reshaped scientific understanding of volcano-generated tsunamis and prompted improvements in Indonesia's monitoring and warning systems.

Subsequent activity

Anak Krakatau has continued to erupt intermittently since 2018, gradually rebuilding its cone. Activity in 2020, 2022, and 2023 produced ash plumes, lava flows, and small explosions, closely monitored by Indonesia's Center for Volcanology and Geological Hazard Mitigation (PVMBG). The island remains uninhabited and access is restricted.
