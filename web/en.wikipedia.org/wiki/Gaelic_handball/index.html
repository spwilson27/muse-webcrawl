Gaelic handball

Gaelic handball (Irish: liathróid láimhe) is a sport played in Ireland in which players hit a ball with a hand or fist against a wall in such a way that the opponent cannot return it. It is one of the four Gaelic games organised by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA), and is closely related to American handball, Basque pelota, and Welsh handball.

Handball is played in enclosed courts called alleys.

History

Handball-like games have been played in Ireland for centuries; the Tailteann Games are said to have included a form of the game, and references appear in the Statutes of Galway of 1527. The GAA codified the sport from 1884, and handball alleys became fixtures of Irish towns. Irish emigrants carried the game to the United States, where it fed into American handball.

Codes

  • 60x30 — played in a large four-wall court (60 by 30 feet) with a softer ball, traditionally the main summer code
  • 40x20 — played in the smaller international-size court with a smaller, harder ball; the code used for world championships
  • One-wall — played against a single wall, popular internationally
  • Hardball — the traditional game played with a very hard ball in the 60x30 court

Matches are usually played to 21 points (aces). Ireland competes at the World Handball Championships organised under the World Handball Council.
