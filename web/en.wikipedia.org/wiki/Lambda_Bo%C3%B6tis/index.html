Lambda Boötis

Lambda Boötis (λ Boötis, λ Boo) is a star in the northern constellation of Boötes. It is the prototype of the Lambda Boötis stars, a class of chemically peculiar stars with anomalously low abundances of iron-peak elements but near-solar abundances of lighter elements such as carbon, nitrogen and oxygen.

Characteristics

Lambda Boötis is a white A-type star located about 99 light-years from the Sun. It has an apparent visual magnitude of about 4.18, making it visible to the naked eye under good conditions. Its peculiar surface chemistry is thought to result from accretion of metal-depleted gas from a circumstellar disk, combined with diffusion processes in its atmosphere.

Lambda Boötis stars

The class named after this star comprises Population I, late B to early F type stars showing the characteristic pattern of underabundance of heavy elements while volatile elements remain near solar. Explaining their origin has been an ongoing problem in stellar astrophysics, involving accretion, diffusion, and mass loss.
