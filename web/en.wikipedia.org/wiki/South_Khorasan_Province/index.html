South Khorasan Province

South Khorasan Province
Persian: استان خراسان جنوبی
Capital: Birjand
Area: approx. 151,193 km2
Population: about 770,000 (2016 census)

South Khorasan Province (Persian: استان خراسان جنوبی, Ostān-e Khorāsān-e Jonūbī) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the east of the country along the border with Afghanistan. Its capital is the city of Birjand. The province was created in 2004 when the former Khorasan Province — then the largest in Iran — was divided into three provinces: Razavi Khorasan, North Khorasan, and South Khorasan.

Geography

South Khorasan is Iran's third-largest province by area but one of its most sparsely populated. Much of it is arid: the Dasht-e Lut desert touches its northwestern margins and the Dasht-e Kavir lies to the northwest, while mountain ranges run through the centre and east. The climate is dry, with agriculture dependent on qanats (underground aqueducts) and wells. Counties include Birjand, Qaen, Tabas, Ferdows, Nehbandan, Sarayan, Boshruyeh, Sarbisheh, Darmian, Khusf, and Zirkuh.

Economy

The province is one of the world's leading producers of saffron — the Qaen area is particularly renowned — and also produces barberry (zereshk), of which it is a dominant world source, as well as jujube and cotton. Mining, including coal around Tabas, and cross-border trade with Afghanistan are also important.

History and sights

The region formed the medieval district of Quhistan, known for its castles of the Nizari Ismailis (Assassins). Notable sites include the Akhund Khorasani and Birjand citadel, the historic village fabric of Ferdows, and the desert city of Tabas.
