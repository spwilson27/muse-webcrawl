Grand Mufti of Jerusalem

The Grand Mufti of Jerusalem is the Sunni Muslim cleric in charge of Jerusalem's Islamic holy places, including the Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock on the Haram al-Sharif/Temple Mount. The office in its modern form was created by the British Mandate authorities in 1921, elevating the traditional position of Mufti of Jerusalem into the most senior Islamic religious post in Palestine, with authority over Muslim courts and religious endowments (waqfs).

History

Under Ottoman rule, the Mufti of Jerusalem was one of several city muftis, subordinate in rank to the Şeyhülislam in Istanbul. In 1921 the British High Commissioner, Herbert Samuel, appointed Amin al-Husseini as Grand Mufti and made him president of the new Supreme Muslim Council, combining religious office with de facto political leadership of the Palestinian Arab community. Al-Husseini became a leading figure of Palestinian nationalism, was stripped of office and exiled by the British after the 1936–39 Arab revolt, and spent the Second World War in Axis Europe broadcasting propaganda for Nazi Germany — a record that has kept him a deeply controversial figure.

After 1948 the position fell under Jordanian authority when Jordan annexed the West Bank; King Abdullah appointed successors, notably Sheikh Saad al-Din al-Alami. Israel occupied East Jerusalem in 1967 but left the administration of the Haram al-Sharif to the Jordanian-backed waqf. Following the Oslo Accords, the Palestinian Authority asserted its own claim to the appointment: Yasser Arafat appointed Sheikh Ekrima Sa'id Sabri in 1994, while Jordan recognised its own appointees — a rivalry that has made the office a focus of competing Palestinian and Jordanian claims over Jerusalem. Sabri was removed by Mahmoud Abbas in 2006 and succeeded by Muhammad Ahmad Hussein, who has served as Grand Mufti under the Palestinian Authority, while the Jordanian-appointed Council and the waqf administration continue to manage the holy sites day to day.

Role

The Grand Mufti issues fatwas, oversees sharia courts in Jerusalem, supervises imams and Islamic preaching, and speaks for the Islamic establishment on matters affecting the Al-Aqsa Mosque compound, where questions of access and control are among the most sensitive in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
