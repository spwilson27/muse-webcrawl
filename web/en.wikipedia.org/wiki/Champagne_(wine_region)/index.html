The Champagne wine region in northeastern France is the legally defined area, centred around the cities of Reims and Épernay, entitled to produce the sparkling wine known as Champagne. Its boundaries and production rules are set by the Champagne appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC).

Sub-regions

The region is traditionally divided into sub-areas including the Montagne de Reims, the Vallée de la Marne, the Côte des Blancs, the Côte de Sézanne and the Côte des Bar in the Aube. Chalky soils and a cool continental climate give the wines their character.

Production

Champagne is made by the traditional method, with a secondary fermentation in the bottle. The principal grapes are Chardonnay, Pinot noir and Meunier. The historic hillsides, houses and cellars of Champagne were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015.
