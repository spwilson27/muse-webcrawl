Runway

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: An airport runway stretching toward the horizon with centreline lights]
Looking down the runway.

A runway is a defined rectangular area on a land aerodrome prepared for the landing and takeoff of aircraft. Runways are numbered by magnetic heading (e.g. 27L = 270°, left of a parallel pair), surfaced in asphalt or concrete, marked with threshold, centreline and touchdown-zone markings, and lit for night and low-visibility operations. The longest public runway is at Qamdo Bamda Airport in Tibet (5,500 m, built long because of thin air at 4,334 m altitude); major hubs use 3,500–4,000 m runways.

Dimensions and strength

Length depends on aircraft performance, altitude and temperature (\'hot and high\' penalises both): a fully laden 747 needs about 3,300 m at sea level. Pavement strength is rated by PCN; grooving and rubber removal maintain friction, and runway status lights and FOD detection guard safety.

Operations

Airports sequence traffic via ATC with wake-turbulence separations; declared distances (TORA, TODA, ASDA, LDA) define usable lengths. Runway excursions and incursions are leading accident categories, countered by EMAS arrestor beds, perimeter systems and runway-safety teams.

Lighting and aids

Edge, centreline and touchdown-zone lighting, approach light systems, PAPI glide-slope indicators and instrument landing systems (ILS) enable operations down to near-zero visibility for equipped aircraft and trained crews (CAT III).
