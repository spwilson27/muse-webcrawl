The Northman is a 2022 American epic historical drama film directed by Robert Eggers, who co-wrote the screenplay with Icelandic poet and novelist Sjón. Based on the medieval Scandinavian legend of Amleth — the same source material that inspired Shakespeare's Hamlet — the film stars Alexander Skarsgård as a Viking prince who sets out to avenge the murder of his father. The ensemble cast includes Nicole Kidman, Claes Bang, Anya Taylor-Joy, Ethan Hawke, Björk and Willem Dafoe.

Plot

In AD 895, King Aurvandill War-Raven returns to his kingdom on the island of Hrafnsey and initiates his young son Amleth into kingship. Shortly afterward, Aurvandill is ambushed and beheaded by his half-brother Fjölnir, who seizes the throne and takes Amleth's mother, Queen Gudrún, as his wife. Amleth escapes by boat, swearing vengeance: "I will avenge you, Father. I will save you, Mother. I will kill you, Fjölnir." Years later the grown Amleth, now a berserker raiding with a band of Vikings in the lands of the Rus, learns that Fjölnir has lost his throne and lives as a sheep farmer in Iceland. Amleth enslaves himself aboard a ship bound for Fjölnir's farm, aided by the Slavic sorceress Olga, and infiltrates the household as a thrall, seizing a magical sword and methodically destroying his uncle's family before a final duel on the slopes of the volcano Hekla, where both men die as Gudrún's revelation — that she was complicit in the old king's murder — shatters Amleth's understanding of his quest.

Production

Eggers, known for The Witch (2015) and The Lighthouse (2019), pursued extreme historical authenticity, working with historians and archaeologists on costumes, weapons, rituals and sets, and filming largely in Northern Ireland and Iceland. The production, his largest, was marked by long takes staged with elaborate choreography and by difficult location shooting. Focus Features released the film in the United States in April 2022.

Reception

The Northman received strong critical praise for its direction, cinematography, performances and immersive historical detail, though its box office returns — about $69.6 million worldwide against a production budget reported between $70 and $90 million — fell short of its cost in theatres; it performed more successfully on home video and streaming. It has since been frequently cited among the most notable Viking films and among the major auteur-driven studio films of its decade.
