Sir Michael Morpurgo (born 5 October 1943) is an English author, poet and playwright, best known for his children's novels, many of which explore war, animals and rural life. He has written more than 150 books and served as the third British Children's Laureate, from 2003 to 2005.

Life and work

Morpurgo worked as a primary school teacher before becoming a full-time writer. With his wife Clare he founded the charity Farms for City Children in 1976, which gives inner-city children experience of working farms in Devon, Wales and Gloucestershire.

Major works

War Horse (1982), the story of a farm horse sold to the army in the First World War, became a phenomenon after its adaptation for the stage by the National Theatre (2007), using life-size puppet horses, and Steven Spielberg's film version (2011). Other acclaimed works include Private Peaceful (2003), about a young soldier shot for cowardice; Kensuke's Kingdom (1999), which won the Children's Book Award; The Butterfly Lion (1996); Why the Whales Came (1985); and Shadow (2010). His books frequently give voice to animals and to children caught up in historical events.
