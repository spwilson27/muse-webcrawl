Spanda

Spanda (Sanskrit: स्पन्द, "vibration", "pulse", "throb") is a central concept of Kashmir Shaivism denoting the primordial, creative vibration or pulsation of universal consciousness (Shiva) through which the universe manifests. It is the subject of the Spanda-kārikā ("Verses on Vibration"), a foundational text of the tradition attributed to the sage Vasugupta (9th century CE) or his disciple Kallaṭa.

Doctrine

In the Spanda school, reality is one undivided consciousness whose intrinsic dynamism — its freedom (svātantrya) and self-awareness — expresses itself as a subtle oscillation between rest and activity, contraction and expansion. Unlike a material vibration, spanda is said to be a "movement in that which is motionless": consciousness does not change its nature, yet from it arise subject, object and all experience.

Commentators such as Kṣemarāja, in his Spanda-nirṇaya, elaborated the doctrine and linked it to the moment-to-moment way perception opens and closes upon its objects. Recognition (pratyabhijñā) of one's own spanda nature is held to be liberating: the yogin who perceives the pulsation underlying even distraction and desire realises identity with Shiva.

Legacy

The Spanda tradition merged into the broader Trika and Pratyabhijñā schools of Kashmir Shaivism, and its imagery of cosmic pulsation influenced later Śākta and tantric practice. Modern teachers of Kashmir Shaivism, and writers such as Jaideva Singh — whose English translation of the Spanda-kārikā (1980) is standard — brought the concept to a global audience; the term is also used loosely in contemporary yoga discourse for the universal pulse of contraction and expansion.
