Barry William Boehm (16 May 1935 – 20 August 2022) was an American software engineer, distinguished professor of computer science, industrial and systems engineering at the University of Southern California. He was known for his many contributions to software engineering, including the spiral model of software development, the COCOMO cost estimation model, and the economics of software engineering.

Career

Boehm received his B.A. in mathematics from Harvard University in 1957 and his Ph.D. from UCLA in 1964. He worked at the RAND Corporation, then at TRW, where he rose to chief scientist of the defense systems group, before joining USC in 1992, where he founded the Center for Software Engineering (later the Center for Systems and Software Engineering).

Contributions

In his 1981 book Software Engineering Economics, Boehm introduced COCOMO, the Constructive Cost Model, which estimates the effort and cost of software projects from their size; a revised COCOMO II followed in 2000. His 1986 paper introduced the spiral model, an iterative, risk-driven process that combined prototyping with the discipline of the waterfall model. He also developed the Wideband Delphi estimation technique, helped popularise the observation that the cost of fixing defects rises exponentially the later they are found, and co-originated (with Richard Turner) work on balancing agility and discipline in software methods.

Honours

Boehm was a fellow of the ACM and IEEE and a member of the National Academy of Engineering. He received the IEEE Simon Ramo Medal and other major awards recognising his influence on software practice in industry and government.
