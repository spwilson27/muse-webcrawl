George S. Kaufman

George Simon Kaufman (November 16, 1889 – June 2, 1961) was an American playwright, theatre director, and drama critic.

Born: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania
Died: New York City
Notable works: You Can't Take It with You; The Man Who Came to Dinner; Of Thee I Sing

George S. Kaufman was an American playwright and director, one of the most successful writers for the Broadway stage between the wars. Nearly all of his hits were collaborations — with Marc Connelly, Edna Ferber, Moss Hart, Morrie Ryskind, and others — and he won two Pulitzer Prizes for Drama.

Career

Born in Pittsburgh, Kaufman began as a newspaper humour columnist and became drama editor of The New York Times (1917–1930). A member of the Algonquin Round Table, he was famed for his wit. His first Broadway success was Dulcy (1921, with Marc Connelly). With Connelly he also wrote Merton of the Movies and Beggar on Horseback; with Edna Ferber, The Royal Family, Dinner at Eight, and Stage Door.

Major works

Kaufman won the Pulitzer Prize for the Gershwin musical satire Of Thee I Sing (1931, with Morrie Ryskind) and again for You Can't Take It with You (1936, with Moss Hart), the beloved comedy of the eccentric Sycamore family. With Hart he also wrote Merrily We Roll Along (1934) and The Man Who Came to Dinner (1939), whose monstrous guest Sheridan Whiteside was modelled on their friend Alexander Woollcott. Kaufman directed many of his own plays and the original production of Guys and Dolls (1950), winning a Tony Award.

Later life

Kaufman wrote the screenplay for the Marx Brothers' A Night at the Opera among other film work, and appeared as a panellist on the early television programme This Is Show Business. He died in New York City in 1961. His reputation as the "play doctor" and master of the well-made comedy endures, and his plays remain staples of American repertory.
