Noordoostpolder (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈnoːrtoːstˌpɔldər]; English: "North-East Polder") is a polder and municipality in the province of Flevoland in the central Netherlands. It was created as part of the Zuiderzee Works, the great Dutch land-reclamation project that closed off and partly drained the Zuiderzee. The polder was drained in 1942, during the Second World War, and is mostly used for agriculture.

History

Work on the Noordoostpolder began in the late 1930s under the direction of the Zuiderzee Works service. The ring dike was closed in 1940 and pumping stations — Leemans, Wortman and Colijn — drained the land by 1942. During the war the polder acquired the nickname "Dutch Underground District" (Nederlands Onderduikers Paradijs), because labourers could hide from forced labour in Germany there. Settlers were carefully selected in the post-war years, and farms were allocated under a strict government plan.

Geography

The municipal seat is Emmeloord, laid out at the polder's centre with ten surrounding villages arranged in a star pattern: Bant, Creil, Ens, Espel, Kraggenburg, Luttelgeest, Marknesse, Nagele, Rutten and Tollebeek. The former island of Schokland, now landlocked within the polder, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Nagele was designed by leading modernist architects of the Nieuwe Bouwen movement.

Economy

Agriculture dominates the polder economy, particularly potatoes, onions, sugar beet and bulbs. The Netherlands Aerospace Centre's German-Dutch Wind Tunnels and the National Aerospace Laboratory facilities are located near Marknesse.
