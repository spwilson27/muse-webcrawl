The Schmalkaldic War (German: Schmalkaldischer Krieg) was the short period of violence from 1546 to 1547 between the forces of Emperor Charles V, commanded by the Duke of Alba and Duke Maurice of Saxony, and the Lutheran Schmalkaldic League within the domains of the Holy Roman Empire.

Background

The Schmalkaldic League was a defensive alliance of Lutheran princes and cities formed in 1531 at Schmalkalden to resist imperial enforcement of the Edict of Worms and the Catholic restoration. Truces at Nuremberg (1532) and Frankfurt (1539) deferred conflict while Charles V was preoccupied with France and the Ottomans. After the Peace of Crépy (1544) and the opening of the Council of Trent, Charles prepared to settle the religious question by force, securing papal troops and funding, and winning over the Lutheran Maurice of Saxony with the promise of the Saxon electoral dignity.

Course of the war

In 1546 the League's armies under Sebastian Schertlin occupied Füssen and threatened the Brenner route, but the League's divided command squandered the initiative. Imperial forces cleared southern Germany; the League's army disintegrated over the winter. Maurice invaded electoral Saxony, prompting John Frederick I to hurry north. On 24 April 1547, at the Battle of Mühlberg on the Elbe, Charles's forces surprised and routed the Saxon army; John Frederick was captured. Philip of Hesse, lured by negotiated promises, surrendered at Halle and was also imprisoned.

Aftermath

The Capitulation of Wittenberg (19 May 1547) transferred the Saxon electorship to Maurice and reduced John Frederick's lands. Both League leaders remained imperial prisoners for years. Charles imposed the Augsburg Interim (1548), a provisional religious settlement restoring many Catholic practices in Protestant territories, which provoked widespread resistance — including the defiance of Magdeburg — and contributed to the princes' revolt of 1552, led ironically by Maurice, which forced the Peace of Passau and ultimately the Peace of Augsburg (1555), granting legal recognition to Lutheranism within the Empire.
