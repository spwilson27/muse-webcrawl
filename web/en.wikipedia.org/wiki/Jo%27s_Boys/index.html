Jo's Boys, and How They Turned Out is a novel by American author Louisa May Alcott, published in 1886. It is the final sequel in the series that began with Little Women (1868), following Little Men (1871), and concludes the story of Josephine "Jo" March, now Jo Bhaer, and the boys she and her husband raised at Plumfield school.

Plot

Set ten years after Little Men, the novel follows the grown Plumfield boys — among them Emil, Franz, Dan, Nat, Demi and Tommy — as they set out into the world, at sea, in medicine, music, farming and business. Dan, the former wild boy, struggles with temptation and ultimately sacrifices himself protecting others in the West; Nat pursues music in Germany; and Jo, now a famous author, reflects on her school's influence. The book also threads in themes Alcott cared about, including women's education and temperance.

Reception

Though less celebrated than Little Women, Jo's Boys has remained in print as the closing volume of the March family story and is noted for its darker, more realistic treatment of its characters' adult struggles.
