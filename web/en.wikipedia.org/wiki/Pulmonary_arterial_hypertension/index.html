Pulmonary arterial hypertension

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a rare, progressive disease characterized by abnormally high blood pressure in the arteries of the lungs, caused by narrowing and remodelling of the small pulmonary arteries. It is classified as Group 1 of the clinical classification of pulmonary hypertension. The increased resistance forces the right ventricle of the heart to work harder, eventually leading to right heart failure.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms are nonspecific and include breathlessness (initially on exertion), fatigue, chest pain, palpitations, light-headedness or syncope, and peripheral edema. Because of this, diagnosis is often delayed. On examination there may be a loud pulmonary component of the second heart sound, a right ventricular heave, and signs of right heart failure.

Causes

PAH may be idiopathic, heritable — most often due to mutations in the BMPR2 gene — drug- or toxin-induced (classically by appetite suppressants such as aminorex and fenfluramine derivatives), or associated with conditions such as connective tissue diseases (especially systemic sclerosis), congenital heart disease, portal hypertension, HIV infection, and schistosomiasis.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires right heart catheterization showing a mean pulmonary arterial pressure above 20 mmHg with a pulmonary arterial wedge pressure of 15 mmHg or less and elevated pulmonary vascular resistance, after exclusion of other causes of pulmonary hypertension. Echocardiography is the usual screening test.

Treatment

Therapy targets three pathways: the prostacyclin pathway (epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost, selexipag), the nitric oxide pathway (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors such as sildenafil and tadalafil, and the guanylate cyclase stimulator riociguat), and the endothelin pathway (bosentan, ambrisentan, macitentan). The activin-signalling inhibitor sotatercept was approved in 2024. Supportive care includes diuretics, oxygen, and anticoagulation in selected patients; lung transplantation remains an option for advanced disease.
