Queen Emma Bridge

The Queen Emma Bridge (Dutch: Koningin Emmabrug) is a pontoon bridge across St. Anna Bay in Willemstad, Curaçao. It connects the Punda and Otrobanda quarters of the capital city. The bridge is hinged and opens regularly to enable the passage of oceangoing ships. On the opposite end from the hinge is a small shelter where an operator controls two diesel engines turning propellers. The propellers are mounted perpendicular to the length of the bridge and allow it to swing parallel to the shore.

The bridge was built in 1888 and was originally a toll bridge. It was named after Emma of Waldeck and Pyrmont, Queen consort of the Netherlands. When the bridge swings open, two ferries spring into service to bring pedestrians across the water free of charge. Since 1974, motor vehicles have been prohibited from using the bridge, and crossing is free for pedestrians.

History

The bridge was constructed by Leonard Burlington Smith, an American businessman and United States consul on the island. Initially, those wearing shoes were charged a toll of two cents, while the poor, who crossed barefoot, were allowed to pass free. The bridge has been renovated several times, including a major restoration in the early 2000s. It is one of the most famous landmarks of Curaçao and a symbol of Willemstad.
