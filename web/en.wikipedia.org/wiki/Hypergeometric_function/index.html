Hypergeometric function

In mathematics, the term hypergeometric function sometimes refers to a confluent hypergeometric function. Historically, the term "hypergeometric function" was first used by John Wallis in his 1655 book Arithmetica Infinitorum. The modern theory was developed in the 19th century, notably by Bernhard Riemann and Ernst Kummer.

The Gaussian or ordinary hypergeometric function 2F1(a, b; c; z) is a special function represented by the hypergeometric series, which includes many other special functions as specific or limiting cases. It is a solution of a second-order linear ordinary differential equation (the hypergeometric differential equation). Every second-order linear ODE with three regular singular points can be transformed into this equation.

Definition

The hypergeometric series is defined for |z| < 1 by the power series

2F1(a, b; c; z) = Σn=0∞ (a)n (b)n / (c)n · zn / n!

where (q)n is the rising Pochhammer symbol, defined by (q)0 = 1 and (q)n = q(q+1)…(q+n−1) for n > 0. The series is a polynomial in z if either a or b is a non-positive integer; otherwise it generally converges for |z| < 1 and has a singularity at z = 1.

Special cases

Many elementary and special functions are special cases of the hypergeometric function. For example, the geometric series is 2F1(1, b; b; z) = 1/(1−z); the logarithm is −ln(1−z) = z 2F1(1, 1; 2; z); and the complete elliptic integrals can be expressed in terms of 2F1. The confluent hypergeometric function (Kummer's function) 1F1 arises as a limit and covers the exponential, Bessel, and error functions.

Hypergeometric differential equation

The hypergeometric function is a particular solution of Euler's hypergeometric differential equation:

z(1−z) w″ + [c − (a+b+1)z] w′ − ab w = 0

which has three regular singular points: 0, 1, and ∞. Riemann's approach characterises solutions by their exponent differences at these points (the Riemann P-symbol), from which the 24 Kummer solutions and the connection formulas between them follow.

Integral representations

Euler gave an integral representation: for Re(c) > Re(b) > 0,

2F1(a, b; c; z) = Γ(c) / (Γ(b)Γ(c−b)) ∫01 tb−1 (1−t)c−b−1 (1−zt)−a dt

which provides analytic continuation beyond the unit disc. Barnes integral representations express the function in terms of contour integrals of gamma functions.
