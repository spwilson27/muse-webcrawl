Epoché

Epoché (ἐποχή, epokhē, "cessation, suspension") is an ancient Greek term which, in Hellenistic philosophy, describes the suspension of judgement about the real nature of things. It is the hallmark of the Pyrrhonist and Academic sceptical schools, in which the sceptic, finding opposing arguments equally persuasive, refrains from assenting to any dogmatic claim, thereby attaining tranquillity (ataraxia).

Pyrrhonism

In Pyrrhonism, as reported by Sextus Empiricus, epoché is produced by setting appearances and judgements in opposition, so that, owing to the equipollence (isostheneia) of the matters in question, the sceptic neither affirms nor denies how things are in themselves, while continuing to live according to appearances.

Husserl

Edmund Husserl revived the term in phenomenology for the bracketing of the "natural attitude": the suspension of belief in the existence of the external world so that consciousness and its intentional objects can be studied in themselves. This phenomenological epoché is the first step of the phenomenological reduction.
