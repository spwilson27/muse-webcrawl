Personal prelature

A personal prelature is an institution of the Catholic Church comprising clergy and laity who undertake specific pastoral activities under the authority of a prelate appointed by the Pope. Unlike a diocese, which is defined by territory, a personal prelature is defined by the persons who belong to it, wherever they may live.

Legal basis

The institution was created by the Second Vatican Council's decree Presbyterorum Ordinis (1965) and regulated by the 1983 Code of Canon Law (canons 294–297). Its statutes are issued by the Holy See; the prelate governs with powers comparable to those of a diocesan bishop over the prelature's works, while lay members remain also under their local diocesan bishops.

Opus Dei

To date the only personal prelature erected is Opus Dei, established as such by Pope John Paul II in 1982 through the apostolic constitution Ut sit. Its prelate was traditionally consecrated a bishop; following the motu proprio Ad charisma tuendum (2022), the prelate is no longer ordained a bishop and the prelature reports to the Dicastery for the Clergy. In 2023 the motu proprio further specified that the prelate may not use episcopal insignia.
