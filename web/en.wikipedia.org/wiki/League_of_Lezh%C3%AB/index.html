League of Lezhë

The League of Lezhë (Albanian: Lidhja e Lezhës) was a military and diplomatic alliance of Albanian aristocrats formed in the city of Lezhë in northern Albania on 2 March 1444. Initiated and organised under Prince Skanderbeg, it united the Albanian princes in resistance to the Ottoman Empire and is often regarded as the first unified Albanian state formation of the medieval period.

Formation

Skanderbeg, who had deserted the Ottoman army at the Battle of Niš in 1443 and seized Krujë, convened the princes of the Kastrioti, Dukagjini, Arianiti, Muzaka, Thopia, and other families at Lezhë, in Venetian-held territory. The assembly created a league with a common treasury, an army under Skanderbeg as commander-in-chief, and a council in which each member kept sovereignty over his own domain.

War with the Ottomans

League forces won a series of victories, beginning at Torvioll in June 1444, and resisted major Ottoman campaigns, including the sieges of Krujë, for over two decades. Internal rivalries — notably with Lekë Dukagjini — weakened the alliance, and after Skanderbeg's death in January 1468 the League dissolved, though Albanian resistance continued until the fall of Krujë in 1478 and Shkodra in 1479.

Legacy

The League is commemorated in Albania as a founding moment of national unity; the date of its meeting is celebrated, and the former Latin cathedral of St Nicholas in Lezhë, where the assembly met and where Skanderbeg was buried, is a national memorial.
