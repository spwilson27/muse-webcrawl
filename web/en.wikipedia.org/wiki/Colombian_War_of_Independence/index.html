Colombian War of Independence

The Colombian War of Independence was a series of conflicts fought between 1810 and 1825 by the inhabitants of the Viceroyalty of New Granada (present-day Colombia, Panama, Venezuela, and Ecuador) to achieve independence from the Spanish Empire. It formed part of the wider Spanish American wars of independence and resulted in the creation of the Republic of Colombia (Gran Colombia).

The Battle of Boyacá (1819) secured the liberation of New Granada.

Background

Discontent with Spanish colonial rule had grown during the 18th century, fuelled by Bourbon reforms, heavy taxation, and the exclusion of American-born Creoles from high office. The Comunero Rebellion of 1781 was an early expression of this unrest. Napoleon's invasion of Spain in 1808 and the deposition of Ferdinand VII created the political opening: on 20 July 1810, a junta was proclaimed in Santa Fe de Bogotá, an event commemorated as Colombia's Independence Day.

Patria Boba and reconquest

The first republican period, known as the Patria Boba ("Foolish Fatherland"), was marked by conflict between centralists led by Antonio Nariño and federalists of the United Provinces of New Granada. Divided, the patriots were defeated when Spain, after Ferdinand VII's restoration, sent a large expedition under Pablo Morillo in 1815. The ensuing "Reconquista" reimposed royal rule with considerable brutality.

Liberation campaign

Simón Bolívar, having proclaimed the Third Republic of Venezuela, joined forces with Francisco de Paula Santander. In 1819 Bolívar's army crossed the frozen Andes at the Páramo de Pisba in a daring campaign, defeating the royalists at the Battle of Vargas Swamp (25 July) and decisively at the Battle of Boyacá (7 August 1819), which liberated Bogotá and effectively ended Spanish rule in New Granada.

Gran Colombia and aftermath

The Congress of Angostura (1819) proclaimed the Republic of Colombia, uniting New Granada and Venezuela; the Constitution of Cúcuta (1821) organised the new state, with Bolívar as president and Santander as vice-president. The victory at Carabobo (1821) secured Venezuela, and Sucre's triumph at Pichincha (1822) liberated Quito. Panama joined voluntarily in 1821. The war's final phase in Peru culminated at Ayacucho (1824). Gran Colombia dissolved in 1830–31 into Colombia (New Granada), Venezuela, and Ecuador.
