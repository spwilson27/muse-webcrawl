Leadscrew

A leadscrew (or lead screw), also known as a power screw or translation screw, is a screw used as a linkage in a machine to translate rotational motion into linear motion. Because of the large area of sliding contact between the screw and nut threads, leadscrews have high friction losses but are simple, compact, and often self-locking.

Applications

Leadscrews drive the carriages of lathes, the tables of milling machines, linear actuators, vises and presses, and the focusing and positioning mechanisms of instruments and 3D printers. Thread forms used include Acme, trapezoidal, square, and buttress threads, each balancing strength, efficiency, and ease of manufacture.

Properties

A leadscrew's mechanical advantage allows a small torque to produce a large axial force. The same friction that reduces efficiency makes most leadscrews self-locking: the load cannot back-drive the screw. Where high efficiency and low wear are required, a ball screw is used instead, replacing sliding contact with rolling balls.
