Mnesikles

Mnesikles (Greek: Μνησικλῆς) was an ancient Athenian architect active in the middle of the 5th century BC, during the age of Pericles. He is best known as the architect of the Propylaea, the monumental gateway to the Acropolis of Athens, built between 437 and 432 BC.

The Propylaea

The Propylaea was part of Pericles' great building programme on the Acropolis, alongside the Parthenon (by Iktinos and Kallikrates), the Erechtheion and the Temple of Athena Nike. Mnesikles had to solve a difficult problem: the gateway crosses a steep slope and had to link, within a constrained and irregular site, a picture gallery (the Pinakotheke) to the north, a shrine of Athena Nike to the south, and the processional way of the Panathenaea. His design combines a Doric exterior with Ionic columns inside the central hall, whose marble coffered ceiling was famous in antiquity. Because of the site's asymmetry, the south wing was left truncated, probably out of respect for the sanctuary of Athena Nike. Work was abandoned unfinished in 432 BC as Athens approached the Peloponnesian War.

Other attributions

Ancient sources name Mnesikles for little else; later writers have speculatively attributed other works to him, including parts of the Eleusinian Telesterion, but the Propylaea is his one secure work. It has been praised since antiquity as a masterpiece of classical Greek architecture, ingeniously reconciling symmetry with an awkward site.
