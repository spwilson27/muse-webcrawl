Seminole Wars

Seminole Wars
Date: 1816–1858
Location: Florida, United States
Result: Most Seminoles removed to Indian Territory; a remnant remained in Florida
Principal leaders: Osceola, Micanopy, Billy Bowlegs; Andrew Jackson, Zachary Taylor

The Seminole Wars (also known as the Florida Wars) were a series of three military conflicts between the United States and the Seminole people of Florida, fought between about 1816 and 1858. They are often considered together as the longest and most costly of the American Indian Wars. The central issue was the U.S. government's determination to remove the Seminoles — who included Creek refugees and escaped enslaved people — from Florida to Indian Territory west of the Mississippi.

First Seminole War (1816–1819)

Conflict began with raids across the Spanish Florida border and the destruction of the "Negro Fort" on the Apalachicola River in 1816. In 1818 General Andrew Jackson invaded Florida, seizing St. Marks and Pensacola. The Adams–Onís Treaty (1819) transferred Florida to the United States, removing Spain from the struggle.

Second Seminole War (1835–1842)

The Indian Removal Act and the disputed Treaty of Payne's Landing (1832) set the stage for the longest conflict. It opened with the Dade Battle and the killing of Indian agent Wiley Thompson in December 1835. Under leaders such as Osceola and Micanopy, the Seminoles waged guerrilla warfare from the swamps. Osceola was captured under a flag of truce in 1837 and died at Fort Moultrie. By 1842 most Seminoles had been killed or deported, though no peace treaty was signed.

Third Seminole War (1855–1858)

The final war centred on the band of Billy Bowlegs (Holata Micco) in the Big Cypress. After years of skirmishing, Bowlegs accepted payment and emigrated in 1858. A few hundred Seminoles never surrendered, retreating deep into the Everglades; their descendants form today's Seminole Tribe of Florida and Miccosukee Tribe, who proudly call themselves the "Unconquered People".
