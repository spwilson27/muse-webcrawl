Maui (Hawaiian mythology)

Māui is a culture hero and trickster in Hawaiian mythology, part of a wider Polynesian cycle of tales told across the Pacific, from New Zealand to Tahiti. In Hawaiian tradition he is a demigod — a kupua — born to the goddess Hina, whose feats explained the features of the natural world and gave gifts to humankind.

The feats of Māui

Fishing up the islands. Using a magical fishhook, Manaiakalani, baited with the ear of his mother Hina (or, in some versions, the alae bird), Māui hauled the Hawaiian islands up from the ocean floor while fishing with his brothers, after warning them not to look back.

Snaring the sun. To win longer days for his mother's kapa-cloth making, Māui climbed Haleakalā ("house of the sun") on the island of Maui — named after him — and lassoed the sun's rays with ropes of coconut fibre, forcing the sun to travel more slowly across the sky in summer.

Stealing fire. Māui won the secret of fire from the mud hens (ʻalae), who had guarded it; when the bird resisted, the struggle left the red mark on its forehead that the bird still bears, and Māui revealed to humans that fire could be made by rubbing sticks together.

Lifting the sky. In another tale Māui pushed the sky higher so that people could walk upright, and he is credited with raising the heavens with the help of a tall kahuna.

Death

Māui died attempting to win immortality for mankind by entering the body of the goddess Hine-nui-te-pō (in Māori telling) — in Hawaiian tradition his death is connected with the goddess of death at Mokuhia — and being crushed when a bird's laughter betrayed him. The island of Maui and the demigod remain enduring figures in Hawaiian chant, hula, and place names.
