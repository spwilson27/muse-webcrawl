The 1994 Winter Olympics, officially the XVII Olympic Winter Games, were held in Lillehammer, Norway, from 12 to 27 February 1994. They were the first Winter Games to be held in a different year from the Summer Olympics, following the International Olympic Committee's decision to stagger the summer and winter editions, and the first since 1964 in which no boycotts marred the event.

The Lysgårdsbakken ski jump hosted the opening ceremony at Lillehammer.

Highlights

Hosts Norway won 26 medals, including ten golds. Speed skater Johann Olav Koss won three gold medals, each in world record time, and donated his prize money to Olympic Aid for Sarajevo — the besieged former Winter Olympic host city. Norway's Bjørn Dæhlie added two golds in cross-country skiing. In ice hockey, Sweden defeated Canada in the first shootout in Olympic final history to win gold.

The Games attracted enormous public attention in the United States because of the figure skating rivalry between Tonya Harding and Nancy Kerrigan, following the attack on Kerrigan arranged by associates of Harding's ex-husband before the US championships. In the event, Ukraine's Oksana Baiul won the ladies' gold medal ahead of Kerrigan. American speed skater Bonnie Blair won two golds, and Dan Jansen finally won the 1,000 metres in world record time in his last Olympic race.

Medal table

Russia topped the medal table with 11 gold medals and 23 medals overall, ahead of Norway (10 gold, 26 total) and Germany (9 gold, 24 total). The Games were praised for their intimate atmosphere, environmental programme and enthusiastic crowds, and are often cited among the most successful Winter Olympics ever staged.
