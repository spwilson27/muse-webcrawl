Spaced repetition

Spaced repetition is an evidence-based learning technique that incorporates increasing intervals of time between subsequent reviews of previously learned material to exploit the psychological spacing effect.

Spaced repetition
Based onSpacing effect; forgetting curve (Ebbinghaus)
ToolsFlashcards, Anki, SuperMemo

Principles

Memory decays roughly exponentially (Hermann Ebbinghaus, 1885). Reviewing just before forgetting strengthens retention, so intervals can grow from hours to days to months. Active recall — testing rather than re-reading — is central.

Algorithms and software

Piotr Woźniak's SuperMemo algorithms (SM-2 and later) schedule items by user-graded recall. The Leitner system sorts flashcards into boxes by success. Anki, a free program, popularised spaced repetition for language and medical study.

Research

Spacing is one of the most robust findings in learning science, effective for vocabulary, facts and concepts, though less directly for complex skills.
