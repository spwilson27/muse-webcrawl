ASCAP

The American Society of Composers, Authors, and Publishers (ASCAP) is an American not-for-profit performance-rights organisation (PRO). It protects its members' musical copyrights by monitoring public performances of their music, whether via broadcast or live performance, and compensating them accordingly. Founded in 1914, ASCAP is one of the two major performance-rights organisations in the United States, alongside BMI.

History

ASCAP was founded in New York City in 1914 by composer Victor Herbert, music publisher George Maxwell, lawyer Nathan Burkan and a group of composers and publishers seeking to enforce the public-performance right granted by the Copyright Act of 1909. A landmark 1917 Supreme Court decision, Herbert v. Shanley Co., affirmed that restaurants and other venues owed royalties for music performed for profit. The organisation's consent decree with the US Department of Justice, first entered in 1941, has governed much of its licensing practice ever since.

Licensing and royalties

ASCAP licenses the public performance of its members' works to broadcasters, streaming services, venues, bars, restaurants and other users, typically through blanket licences, and distributes the fees (minus operating costs) as royalties to members based on surveys and censuses of performances. It collects well over a billion dollars in revenue in recent years, distributing the great majority to its members.
