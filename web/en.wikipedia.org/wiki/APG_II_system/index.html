APG II system

The APG II system is a system of flowering plant classification published in 2003 by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG) as the second revision of their modern, largely molecular-based classification of the angiosperms. Its full title is "An update of the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group classification for the orders and families of flowering plants: APG II", published in the Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society.

Background

The first APG system (1998) departed radically from traditional systems such as Cronquist's by grouping families into orders and informal clades based on DNA sequence evidence rather than morphology alone. APG II refined that framework in light of new phylogenetic studies, recognizing 45 orders (an increase from 40) and 457 families.

Principal features

APG II retained the major clades of the original system — including the magnoliids, monocots, and eudicots, with the core eudicots divided into rosids and asterids. Among its changes, it clarified the position of several previously unplaced families, expanded some orders, and offered "bracketed" families: optional alternative circumscriptions in which certain families could be either merged into a larger family or kept separate, a pragmatic concession to users who preferred narrower family concepts. Notable examples included the treatment of Asparagaceae, Agavaceae, and related families in Asparagales.

Succession

APG II was superseded by the APG III system in 2009, which abandoned the bracketed-family device, and in turn by APG IV in 2016. The APG approach nonetheless became the standard reference for flowering plant classification in floras, herbaria, and databases.
