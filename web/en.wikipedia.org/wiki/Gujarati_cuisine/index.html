Gujarati cuisine

Gujarati cuisine is the cuisine of the state of Gujarat in western India. It is predominantly vegetarian, reflecting the strong influence of Jainism and Hinduism in the region, and is famous for its balance of sweet, salty and spicy flavours — a touch of sweetness, often from jaggery or sugar, is characteristic of many dishes.

The thali

The best-known presentation is the Gujarati thali, a platter of many small preparations served together: rotli (thin wheat flatbread), shaak (seasoned vegetable dishes), dal or kadhi, rice, farsan (savoury snacks such as dhokla, khandvi and patra), sweets, pickles, and chaas (spiced buttermilk).

Regional varieties

The cuisine varies across North Gujarat, Kathiawad (Saurashtra), Kutch and South Gujarat (Surat), which differ in their use of oils, chillies, sweetness and gram-flour dishes. Staples include bajra (pearl millet) rotla in Kathiawad and the elaborate festive sweets of Surat.

Snacks and sweets

Gujarat is celebrated for its snack foods (farsan and nasta): dhokla, thepla, khakhra, fafda, and handvo are eaten across India and worldwide, as are sweets such as mohanthal, basundi and shrikhand.
