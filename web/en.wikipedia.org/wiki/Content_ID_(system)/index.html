Content ID (system)

Content ID is a digital fingerprinting system developed by Google for YouTube, launched in 2007 as "Video Identification". It automatically detects uploaded videos that match copyrighted audio or video supplied by rights holders, who can then monetize, block or track the matching uploads.

Operation

Rights holders submit reference files; YouTube generates acoustic and visual fingerprints stored in a database of tens of millions of files. Every upload is scanned against them. On a match, the rights holder's chosen policy applies per country: monetize (ads are shown and revenue shared), block, or track. Google has reported paying billions of dollars to the music industry from claimed videos.

Policies and disputes

Uploaders receive a Content ID claim (distinct from a DMCA copyright strike) and can dispute it; the claimant has 30 days to respond. Claims do not usually penalize the channel's standing.

Criticism

The system has been criticized for false positives (public-domain and white-noise claims), for shifting the burden of proof onto uploaders, and for enabling "copyright trolling" of fair-use content such as reviews and parodies.
