Special Field Orders No. 15

Special Field Orders, No. 15 were military orders issued during the American Civil War by Union General William Tecumseh Sherman, on 16 January 1865. They provided for the confiscation of land along the Atlantic coasts of South Carolina, Georgia, and Florida and its redistribution to newly freed Black families, in plots of no more than 40 acres (16 ha) each. The orders are the origin of the phrase "forty acres and a mule".

Background

Sherman issued the orders after his March to the Sea, following a meeting in Savannah on 12 January 1865 with Secretary of War Edwin M. Stanton and twenty Black community leaders, mostly Baptist and Methodist ministers, who identified land ownership as essential to freedom. The orders reserved the Sea Islands from Charleston to Jacksonville, and abandoned rice fields and lands up to 30 miles inland from the sea, exclusively for settlement by Black families, with military protection of their possession. Some 40,000 freedpeople settled on roughly 400,000 acres under the scheme, and some were later lent army mules — hence the enduring phrase.

Reversal

After President Abraham Lincoln's assassination, President Andrew Johnson overturned Sherman's orders in the fall of 1865, returning the land to its pre-war white owners and evicting most of the Black settlers. The episode became a lasting symbol of the federal government's failure to deliver land reform during Reconstruction, and "forty acres and a mule" entered American political discourse as shorthand for unfulfilled promises and for reparations for slavery.
