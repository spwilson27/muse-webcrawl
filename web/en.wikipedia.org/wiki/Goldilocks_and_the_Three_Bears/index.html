Goldilocks and the Three Bears

"Goldilocks and the Three Bears" is a 19th-century British fairy tale of which three versions exist. The original version of the tale tells of a young woman named Goldilocks who enters the forest home of three bachelor bears while they are away. She tastes their porridge, sits in their chairs, and finally lies down in one of their beds, where she falls asleep. When the bears return, they discover her; she wakes, is frightened, and runs away.

History

The tale was first recorded in narrative form by the British writer and poet laureate Robert Southey and published anonymously in 1837 in his collection The Doctor. In Southey's version the intruder is not a young girl but an elderly, foul-mouthed woman. The character's name also evolved: she was variously "Silverhair", "Silverlocks", and "Golden Hair" before becoming "Goldilocks" in the early 20th century, and the three bachelor bears gradually became a father, mother, and baby bear.

Interpretations and legacy

The story has been read as a fable about respecting others' property and privacy, and about finding what is "just right". That phrase gives its name to the Goldilocks principle — applied in astronomy to habitable zones, in economics to balanced growth, and elsewhere, for conditions that are neither too extreme nor too moderate, but in between.
