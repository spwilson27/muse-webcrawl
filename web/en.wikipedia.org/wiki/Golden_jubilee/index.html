Golden jubilee

A golden jubilee marks a fiftieth anniversary. It is applied in various cultures and institutions, and is most widely known as the fiftieth anniversary of a monarch's accession to the throne.

Monarchies

In the United Kingdom and Commonwealth realms, golden jubilees of monarchs have included those of George III in 1809 — the first to be celebrated in any significant way — and of Queen Victoria in 1887. Queen Elizabeth II celebrated her Golden Jubilee in 2002, marked by tours of the United Kingdom and Commonwealth, a pop concert at Buckingham Palace, and the opening of the Golden Jubilee Bridges in London. Other monarchs to mark fifty years include Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria, Emperor Hirohito of Japan, and Queen Margrethe II of Denmark in 2022.

Other uses

The term is also used for the fiftieth anniversary of a wedding (the golden wedding anniversary), of a priest's ordination, of a company's founding, and for fifty-year milestones generally. In the sequence of anniversary materials, fifty years corresponds to gold, following silver (25), ruby (40), and preceding diamond (60/75) and platinum (70).
