State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness

The State of Texas Assessments of Academic Readiness, commonly known as STAAR, is the standardized test program used in Texas public primary and secondary schools to assess students' attainment of the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS) curriculum standards. Administered by the Texas Education Agency, STAAR replaced the Texas Assessment of Knowledge and Skills (TAKS) beginning in the 2011–2012 school year.

Program structure

Students are tested in grades 3–8 in reading and mathematics, with additional tests in writing, science, and social studies at specified grades. At the high school level, end-of-course assessments are given for Algebra I, English I and II, Biology, and U.S. History. Performance is reported in categories such as "Did Not Meet," "Approaches," "Meets," and "Masters" grade level. The tests were designed to be more rigorous than TAKS, with a greater emphasis on critical thinking and readiness for the next grade or course, and for college and careers.

Controversy and changes

STAAR has been persistently controversial. Parents and educators have criticized the volume of testing, its influence on instruction, and its use in school accountability ratings. Scoring errors, computer glitches during the transition to online testing, and questions over the reading level of test passages have prompted legislative scrutiny. In 2015 the legislature removed the requirement that end-of-course scores count toward students' final grades, and later reforms restructured the tests to include more writing and fewer multiple-choice items. The alignment between STAAR and the state's A–F school accountability system remains a subject of political debate.
