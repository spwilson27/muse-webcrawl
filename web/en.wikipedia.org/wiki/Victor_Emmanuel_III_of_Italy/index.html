Victor Emmanuel III (Italian: Vittorio Emanuele III; 1869–1947) was King of Italy from 1900 until his abdication in 1946, Emperor of Ethiopia (1936–41) and King of the Albanians (1939–43). His nearly 46-year reign spanned the Belle Époque liberal era, both world wars and the Fascist dictatorship of Benito Mussolini.

Early reign and First World War

He succeeded his father Umberto I, assassinated in 1900. A shy, diminutive man devoted to numismatics, he delegated government to liberal prime ministers such as Giovanni Giolitti. He authorised Italy's entry into the First World War on the Allied side in 1915 and remained near the front, earning the nickname Re soldato ("Soldier King").

Fascism and empire

Faced with Mussolini's March on Rome in October 1922, the king refused to sign a state of siege and instead appointed Mussolini prime minister — a decision that enabled the Fascist dictatorship and for which he remains widely criticised. He later accepted the imperial crown of Ethiopia after the 1935–36 invasion, signed the racial laws of 1938, and brought Italy into the Second World War in 1940.

Downfall

After Allied landings in Sicily, the king had Mussolini dismissed and arrested in July 1943 and concluded an armistice, fleeing Rome for Brindisi — leaving the army without orders. To salvage the monarchy he transferred powers to his son Umberto in 1944 and abdicated in May 1946, but the referendum of 2 June 1946 abolished the monarchy. He died in exile in Alexandria, Egypt; his remains were returned to Italy in 2017.
