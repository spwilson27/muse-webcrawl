Almadén is a town in the province of Ciudad Real, Castile-La Mancha, Spain. It is famous for its mercury (quicksilver) mines, among the largest and oldest in the world, worked since Roman times for the production of cinnabar and mercury.

Mining history

The Almadén deposit is the largest known concentration of mercury ore. Mercury from Almadén was crucial to the silver-refining amalgamation process in Spain's American colonies. In 2012 the mines, together with Idrija in Slovenia, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Heritage of Mercury". Mining ceased in the early 21st century, and the site is now a mining park and museum.

Name

The name derives from the Arabic al-maʿdin, "the mine".
