Alan John "A. J." Hackett (born May 1958) is a New Zealand entrepreneur who popularised the extreme sport of bungy jumping and co-founded the adventure tourism company AJ Hackett Bungy. Inspired by the land-diving ritual of Vanuatu and experiments by the Oxford University Dangerous Sports Club, Hackett developed a super-stretch latex cord system with scientific help and tested it with jumps in New Zealand and France.

Eiffel Tower jump and commercialisation

In June 1987 Hackett made an illegal early-morning bungy jump from the Eiffel Tower in Paris, an audacious stunt that drew worldwide publicity. In November 1988 he and partner Henry van Asch opened the world's first commercial bungy site at the Kawarau Gorge Suspension Bridge near Queenstown, New Zealand, where jumpers leap 43 metres toward the Kawarau River.

Business

AJ Hackett Bungy grew into one of New Zealand's best-known tourism brands, operating jump sites and adventure attractions—including bridge climbs and giant swings—in New Zealand, Australia, and other countries, with sites as high as the 233-metre Macau Tower. Hackett was appointed an Officer of the New Zealand Order of Merit for services to tourism.
