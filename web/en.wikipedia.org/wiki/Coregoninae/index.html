The Coregoninae, commonly known as the freshwater whitefishes, are a subfamily of the salmon family Salmonidae. The subfamily contains three genera: Coregonus (whitefishes, ciscoes and inconnu relatives), Prosopium (round whitefishes), and Stenodus (inconnu / nelma). Members are distributed across cold lakes and rivers of North America, Europe and northern Asia.

Characteristics

Coregonines are silvery, moderately deep-bodied fishes with small mouths relative to other salmonids, numerous fine gill rakers adapted for feeding on plankton and benthic invertebrates, and an adipose fin. Most spawn in autumn or winter over gravel shoals. The taxonomy of Coregonus is notoriously difficult: postglacial lakes in Europe and North America host radiations of closely related lake whitefish forms whose species status is debated, and many local populations have been described as separate species.

Fisheries and conservation

Whitefishes support important commercial and subsistence fisheries, notably in the North American Great Lakes and in Siberian rivers. Several forms have declined or become extinct through overfishing, eutrophication (which de-oxygenates the deep cold water they require) and competition or predation from introduced species such as the sea lamprey and alewife.
