Ball (dance party)

A ball is a formal dance party characterised by a banquet followed by social dance that includes ballroom dancing. Balls were a feature of aristocratic and upper-class social life from the 17th to the early 20th century, and survive today in forms such as debutante balls, charity balls, university balls and the Vienna opera ball season.

History

The word derives from the Latin ballare, "to dance." Formal balls developed at European courts, reaching an elaborate peak in the 18th and 19th centuries, when the evening followed a strict order: a reception, a seated supper, and a programme of dances — minuets, quadrilles, and later the waltz and polka — recorded on printed dance cards. Strict rules of etiquette governed introductions, gloves, dress (white tie for men, ball gowns for women) and chaperonage for unmarried women.

Types of ball

  • Debutante ball — presents young women to society;
  • Masquerade ball — guests attend in costume and masks;
  • Charity ball — fundraising gala common today;
  • Military ball — formal celebration by armed services;
  • Prom — the North American school dance, descended from the ball tradition;
  • Vienna balls — some 450 balls held each carnival season in Vienna, Austria, several inscribed on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage.

In culture

Balls are a stock setting in literature and film — from the Netherfield ball in Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice and the ball scenes of Tolstoy's War and Peace to the fairy-tale ball of Cinderella, where the heroine must leave before midnight.
