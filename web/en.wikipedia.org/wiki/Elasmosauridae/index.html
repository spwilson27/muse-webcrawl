Elasmosauridae

Elasmosauridae
Fossil range: Jurassic – Late Cretaceous
Order: Plesiosauria
Notable genera: Elasmosaurus, Styxosaurus, Albertonectes

The Elasmosauridae are a family of plesiosaurs, marine reptiles that lived from the Jurassic until the end of the Cretaceous Period, about 66 million years ago. They are distinguished by the longest necks of any plesiosaurs — in some species the neck alone accounted for more than half the animal's total length — combined with a relatively small head armed with slender, interlocking teeth suited to catching fish and squid.

Anatomy

Elasmosaurids possessed streamlined bodies with four paddle-like flippers, short tails, and necks containing as many as 76 cervical vertebrae in Albertonectes, the highest number known in any vertebrate. Despite popular depictions, the neck was not especially flexible and could not be raised out of the water in a swan-like posture. Gastroliths (swallowed stones) are frequently found with skeletons and may have aided digestion or buoyancy control.

Discovery and genera

The type genus, Elasmosaurus, was described by Edward Drinker Cope in 1868 from Kansas; Cope famously reconstructed the skull on the wrong end of the skeleton, an error that became an episode in the Bone Wars with Othniel Charles Marsh. Other well-known genera include Styxosaurus, Thalassomedon, Hydrotherosaurus, Terminonatator, and the Southern Hemisphere Aristonectes and Mauisaurus. The family became extinct in the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event.
