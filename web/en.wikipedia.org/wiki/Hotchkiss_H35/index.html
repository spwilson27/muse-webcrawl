Hotchkiss H35

TypeLight tank
Place of originFrance
In service1936–1952
Mass11 tonnes
Main armament37 mm SA 18 (later SA 38)

The Hotchkiss H35 was a French cavalry tank developed in the mid-1930s and used in the Second World War. About 1,200 were built in the H35, H38 and H39 variants; the later models received a more powerful engine and the H39 a longer, more effective 37 mm gun.

Design

The H35 had a cast hull and turret with armour up to 40 mm, a crew of two, and a 37 mm SA 18 cannon with a coaxial machine gun. Its 78 hp engine gave a top speed of only about 28 km/h, a limitation addressed in the improved H38 and H39.

Service

H35s fought in the Battle of France in 1940 with cavalry and infantry units. After the armistice, captured examples were used by Germany as the Panzerkampfwagen 35H, including conversions into self-propelled guns and artillery tractors. A handful served with French forces after the war, and some were used by Israel in 1948.
