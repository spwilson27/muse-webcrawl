The Peace of Münster was a treaty signed on 30 January 1648 in Münster, Westphalia, between the Dutch Republic and Spain. It formally ended the Eighty Years' War (1568–1648) and recognised the independence of the Dutch Republic from the Spanish Habsburg monarchy.

The negotiations formed part of the wider Congress of Westphalia, which later produced the treaties collectively known as the Peace of Westphalia in October 1648. The Dutch delegation, led by Adriaen Pauw, pressed for full sovereignty, freedom of trade, and retention of overseas conquests made by the Dutch East India and West India Companies.

Terms

Spain recognised the United Provinces as a free and sovereign state. The treaty confirmed Dutch possession of territories captured in Brazil, Africa, and Asia, closed the river Scheldt to trade (to the detriment of Antwerp), and allowed each party to retain the lands it held at the time of signing. Religious practice in occupied territories was also regulated.

Significance

The Peace of Münster is regarded as a founding moment of the Dutch Golden Age state and a landmark in the emergence of the modern system of sovereign states. The ceremonial swearing of the oath on 15 May 1648 was famously depicted by the painter Gerard ter Borch.
