Saturated calomel electrode

The saturated calomel electrode (SCE) is a reference electrode based on the reaction between elemental mercury and mercury(I) chloride (calomel). It has been widely replaced in many applications by silver/silver chloride electrodes, but the calomel electrode has a reputation of being more robust, especially at high temperatures.

Structure and chemistry

The electrode consists of a platinum wire inserted into a mixture of liquid mercury and mercury(I) chloride paste (Hg2Cl2), in contact with a saturated solution of potassium chloride. The half reaction is:

Hg2Cl2(s) + 2e− ⇌ 2Hg(l) + 2Cl−

The potential depends on the chloride concentration, which is fixed by saturation with KCl. The SCE potential is +0.241 V versus the standard hydrogen electrode at 25 °C. Variants use unsaturated KCl solutions (e.g. 0.1 M or 1 M), giving slightly different potentials.

Applications

Calomel electrodes are used in electrochemistry for measuring electrode potentials, in pH meters and voltammetry as stable references. Because of the toxicity of mercury, their use has declined in favour of silver chloride electrodes.
