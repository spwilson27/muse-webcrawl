The Sixaxis (trademarked SIXAXIS) is a wireless gamepad produced by Sony for the PlayStation 3 video game console. It was introduced alongside the PlayStation 3 in 2006 and remained the console's official controller until it was succeeded by the DualShock 3 in 2007–2008.

Design and features

The Sixaxis retains the familiar shape and button layout of the earlier DualShock controllers but connects wirelessly via Bluetooth and charges over USB. Its name refers to its built-in motion-sensing capability, which detects movement along six axes of freedom: three translational and three rotational. Unlike its predecessors, the Sixaxis omitted vibration (rumble) feedback, a decision Sony attributed to interference with the motion sensors; the omission was widely speculated to relate to a patent dispute with Immersion Corporation, which was later settled.

The controller includes two analogue sticks, a directional pad, four action buttons, shoulder buttons and analogue triggers (L2 and R2), a central PlayStation button, and four LEDs indicating the controller number. An internal lithium-ion battery provides power.

DualShock 3

Following the settlement of the patent dispute, Sony introduced the DualShock 3, which restored vibration feedback while retaining the Sixaxis motion-sensing technology; the DualShock 3 is branded with both names. The original Sixaxis was gradually discontinued.
