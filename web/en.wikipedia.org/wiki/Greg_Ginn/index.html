Greg Ginn

Gregory Regis Ginn (born 8 June 1954) is an American musician and songwriter, best known as the guitarist, primary songwriter and sole continuous member of the hardcore punk band Black Flag, which he founded in 1976 in Hermosa Beach, California. He also founded SST Records, the independent label that became a cornerstone of American underground music in the 1980s. Ginn's abrasive, dissonant guitar style — drawing on punk, free jazz and heavy metal — helped define hardcore punk.

Black Flag

Ginn formed the band as Panic in 1976 with singer Keith Morris, renaming it Black Flag in 1978. Through relentless touring — the band's DIY "Get in the Van" ethos was chronicled by later singer Henry Rollins — Black Flag built a national underground network. Albums such as Damaged (1981) and the sludgy, experimental My War (1984) expanded hardcore's vocabulary, though Ginn's increasingly jazz- and metal-influenced direction and his control of the band contributed to its breakup in 1986. Ginn revived Black Flag in 2013 with new personnel, a move contested by former members touring as FLAG, and again in the 2020s.

SST Records

Ginn founded SST in 1978 (growing out of his mail-order electronics business, Solid State Tuners) to release Black Flag's records when no label would. SST went on to release landmark albums by Minutemen, Hüsker Dü, Sonic Youth, Bad Brains, Meat Puppets, Dinosaur Jr. and Soundgarden, operating with minimal budgets and an idiosyncratic aesthetic. The label's later years were marked by disputes with former artists over royalties and reissues.

Other work and style

Outside Black Flag, Ginn has recorded prolifically with projects including Gone, October Faction, Mojack and the Taylor Texas Corrugators, favouring improvisational instrumentals; he is also known for playing the theremin live. His guitar sound — typically a clear acrylic Dan Armstrong guitar through solid-state amplifiers at high volume — is characterised by jagged, atonal solos and dense, detuned riffs influenced as much by the Grateful Dead and free jazz as by the Ramones. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.
