Emirates of the United Arab Emirates

The United Arab Emirates consists of seven emirates (Arabic: إمارات), which are the constituent political units of the federation. Each emirate is governed by an emir (ruler) from a hereditary ruling family, and together the rulers form the Federal Supreme Council, the highest constitutional authority in the country.

The seven emirates

The seven emirates are Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, and Fujairah. Six of them formed the federation on 2 December 1971; Ras Al Khaimah joined on 10 February 1972. Abu Dhabi is by far the largest emirate, covering about 87 percent of the country's land area, while Ajman is the smallest. Abu Dhabi city is the federal capital, and Dubai is the largest city and commercial hub.

Government

Each emirate retains considerable autonomy, particularly over natural resources: oil and gas revenues belong to the individual emirates rather than the federal government. The Federal Supreme Council, comprising the seven rulers, elects the President and Vice President of the UAE from among its members; by convention the ruler of Abu Dhabi serves as President and the ruler of Dubai as Vice President and Prime Minister.

History

Before federation, the emirates were known as the Trucial States, having signed maritime truces and protectorate agreements with Britain in the 19th and early 20th centuries. When Britain announced its withdrawal from east of Suez, the rulers negotiated a union, and the United Arab Emirates was established after British protection ended in 1971.
