Doge's Palace

LocationPiazza San Marco, Venice, Italy
StyleVenetian Gothic
Construction1340 onwards

The Doge's Palace (Italian: Palazzo Ducale) is a palace built in Venetian Gothic style, and one of the main landmarks of the city of Venice in northern Italy. The palace was the residence of the Doge of Venice, the supreme authority of the former Republic of Venice, and housed the republic's government offices, law courts and prisons.

History

The present palace was constructed principally from 1340, when work began on the wing facing the lagoon, with later expansions through the 15th and 16th centuries. The ornate pink-and-white patterned façade, with its open loggias of pointed arches, is a masterpiece of Venetian Gothic architecture. Interiors were decorated by artists including Tintoretto and Veronese; the Great Council Chamber contains Tintoretto's Il Paradiso, among the largest oil paintings in the world. The Bridge of Sighs connects the palace to the New Prison.

Museum

Since 1923 the palace has been a public museum, administered as part of the Fondazione Musei Civici di Venezia.
