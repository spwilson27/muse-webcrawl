Vendace

Vendace (Coregonus albula)
FamilySalmonidae
GenusCoregonus
HabitatDeep, cold lakes of northern Europe
SizeTypically 15–25 cm
ConservationLeast Concern globally; endangered in the UK

The vendace (Coregonus albula) is a small freshwater whitefish of the salmon family, found in deep, cold lakes across northern Europe from Scandinavia and the Baltic basin to Russia. It is a slim, silvery fish with a protruding lower jaw, feeding almost entirely on zooplankton, and forms large shoals that move between deep water by day and surface waters at night.

Distribution and ecology

Vendace are a glacial relict species, colonizing northern lakes as the ice sheets retreated. In the British Isles they survive naturally only in a handful of lakes — in England at Derwentwater and Bassenthwaite Lake in Cumbria, and formerly in Lochmaben in Scotland, where they became extinct. They require cold, well-oxygenated, nutrient-poor water, and are highly sensitive to eutrophication and warming.

Conservation

In Britain the vendace is one of the rarest freshwater fish and is legally protected. Populations in Bassenthwaite declined to near-extinction, with the fish declared extinct there in the 2010s, while the Derwentwater population persists but remains vulnerable to pollution, introduced species (notably ruffe, which eat vendace eggs), and climate change. Conservation refuges have been established by translocating fish to suitable lakes in Scotland and Cumbria.

Fisheries

In Finland and around the Baltic, vendace (muikku) are an important food fish, caught commercially and celebrated in dishes such as fried vendace and Kalakukko. Vendace roe from the Gulf of Bothnia, Kalix löjrom, is a prized delicacy with protected status in the European Union.
