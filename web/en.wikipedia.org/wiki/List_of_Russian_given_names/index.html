List of Russian given names

Russian names
Language: Russian
Structure: Given name + patronymic + surname
Sources: Slavic, Greek, Latin, Hebrew via Orthodoxy

Russian given names are provided at birth or selected during a name change. The bulk of Russian names are of Greek origin (through Byzantine Christianity), with many others from Latin, Hebrew and native Slavic roots. Traditionally, a child's name was often chosen from the Orthodox Church's calendar of saints (svyattsy) according to the feast day near the birth or baptism.

Forms and diminutives

Russian names exist in full forms and in a rich system of diminutive and hypocoristic forms used by family and friends: for example Aleksandr → Sasha, Sanya; Ekaterina → Katya; Mikhail → Misha; Vladimir → Volodya, Vova. A person's full formal address combines the given name with a patronymic derived from the father's name (e.g. Ivan Petrovich, Anna Sergeyevna).

Common names

Widely used masculine names include Aleksandr, Aleksei, Andrei, Anton, Boris, Dmitri, Fyodor, Grigori, Igor, Ivan, Konstantin, Leonid, Maxim, Mikhail, Nikolai, Oleg, Pavel, Pyotr, Sergei, Stanislav, Vasili, Viktor, Vladimir and Yuri. Common feminine names include Alexandra, Anastasia, Anna, Darya, Ekaterina, Elena, Elizaveta, Galina, Irina, Ksenia, Larisa, Lyudmila, Maria, Marina, Natalia, Nina, Olga, Sofia, Svetlana, Tatiana, Vera, Yekaterina and Yulia. After the 1917 Revolution, newly coined names such as Vladlen and Ninel appeared, while many pre-Christian Slavic names (e.g. Svyatoslav, Yaroslav, Lyubov) remained or returned to use.
