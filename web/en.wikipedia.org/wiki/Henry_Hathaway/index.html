Henry Hathaway (born Marquis Henry Leopold de Fiennes; March 13, 1898 – February 11, 1985) was an American film director and producer. He is best known as a director of Westerns, especially starring Randolph Scott and John Wayne. He gave Gary Cooper his first major leading role and worked at 20th Century Fox, where he directed a series of acclaimed semidocumentary-style films noir and thrillers. His film True Grit (1969) won John Wayne the Academy Award for Best Actor.

Early life

Hathaway was born in Sacramento, California, the son of an American actor and stage manager, Rhody Hathaway, and a Hungarian-born Belgian aristocrat, the Marquise Lillie de Fiennes, who acted under the name Jean Hathaway. He grew up in show business and began working in films as a child, first as a child actor and later in the property department and as an assistant director in silent films.

Career

Hathaway began directing in 1932 with a series of low-budget Westerns based on Zane Grey stories, several starring Randolph Scott. His breakthrough came with The Lives of a Bengal Lancer (1935), starring Gary Cooper, which was a large commercial success and earned him his only Academy Award nomination for Best Director.

At 20th Century Fox under Darryl F. Zanuck, Hathaway directed a series of influential semidocumentary thrillers, including The House on 92nd Street (1945), 13 Rue Madeleine (1947), Kiss of Death (1947), and Call Northside 777 (1948), notable for their location shooting and realistic style. Other notable films of this period include the film noir Niagara (1953), starring Marilyn Monroe.

In his later career Hathaway became closely associated with John Wayne, directing him in films including Legend of the Lost (1957), North to Alaska (1960), The Sons of Katie Elder (1965), and True Grit (1969). He also directed the Cinerama epic How the West Was Won (1962), co-directed with John Ford and George Marshall. His final film was Hangup (1974).

Style and reputation

Hathaway was known as a tough, no-nonsense craftsman with a strong visual sense shaped by extensive location shooting. Although rarely treated as an auteur by critics during his lifetime, his Westerns and thrillers have been reassessed for their professionalism, landscape photography, and hard-edged storytelling.

Death

Hathaway died of a heart attack on February 11, 1985, in Los Angeles, aged 86. He has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
