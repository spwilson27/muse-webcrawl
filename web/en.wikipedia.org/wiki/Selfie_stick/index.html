Selfie stick

A selfie stick is an extendable monopod used to take selfie photographs by positioning a smartphone or camera beyond the normal reach of the arm. Typically telescoping from about 20 to 120 centimetres (8 to 47 inches), it has a handle at one end and an adjustable clamp at the other, and the shutter is operated either by a button in the handle connected by cable or Bluetooth, or by the camera's self-timer.

History

Precursors include a telescoping extender patented in Japan in the 1980s by Hiroshi Ueda and Yujiro Mima, marketed as part of a compact camera kit, and a 1995 book of "useless inventions" that pictured a similar device. Canadian inventor Wayne Fromm patented his Quik Pod in 2005. The selfie stick surged in popularity in the early 2010s as smartphone front cameras improved, first in South Korea and then worldwide; Time magazine named it one of the 25 best inventions of 2014.

Restrictions

Because of safety concerns and obstruction of other visitors' views, selfie sticks have been banned at many museums, galleries, stadiums, theme parks, and concerts. Walt Disney Parks banned them in 2015, and they are also prohibited at sites such as the Palace of Versailles, the Colosseum, and many major museums. In South Korea, regulators moved to control the sale of unregistered Bluetooth-equipped sticks as radio devices.

Criticism

The device has attracted mockery as a symbol of narcissism — a satirical name in South Korea likened it to a symbol of selfishness — and critics have raised concerns about privacy and accidents caused by inattentive users. Defenders note that the sticks allow group photographs and wider framing without handing a phone to a stranger.
