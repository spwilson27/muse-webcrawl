Subnetwork

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Diagram of an IP address split into network prefix and host part by a subnet mask]

A subnetwork, or subnet, is a logical subdivision of an IP network. The practice of dividing a network into two or more networks is called subnetting.

An IP address consists of a network prefix and a host identifier; the subnet mask (written as dotted decimal, e.g. 255.255.255.0, or as a CIDR suffix, /24) marks where one ends and the other begins. All devices in a subnet can reach each other directly; traffic for other subnets goes through a router. Subnetting lets an organisation carve its address space into departmental networks, limits broadcast traffic, and improves security and performance.

Subnetting was introduced in 1985 (RFC 950) within the original classful addressing system; classless inter-domain routing (CIDR, 1993) then allowed prefixes of any length, making allocation far more efficient and slowing IPv4 address exhaustion. In IPv6, subnets are conventionally /64 — a single subnet holds more addresses than the entire IPv4 Internet.
