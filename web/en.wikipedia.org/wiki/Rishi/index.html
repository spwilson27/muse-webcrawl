Rishi

In Indian religions, a rishi (Sanskrit: ऋषि, ṛṣi) is an accomplished and enlightened sage or seer. In Hinduism, the rishis are the inspired poets and visionaries to whom the hymns of the Vedas were revealed.

The Sapta Rishis

The seven great rishis, the Saptarishi, are especially revered and are identified with the seven stars of the Big Dipper (Ursa Major) asterism. Lists vary by text and era, commonly including figures such as Vashishtha, Vishvamitra, Bharadvaja, Gautama, Atri, Kashyapa, and Jamadagni (or Agastya).

Role in tradition

Rishis are credited with composing Vedic hymns, transmitting sacred knowledge, and founding lineages (gotras). Later usage extended the honorific to ascetics and teachers in Buddhism and Jainism as well — the Buddha and the Jain tirthankara Rishabhanatha among them.
