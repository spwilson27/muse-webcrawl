Margaret Tudor (28 November 1489 – 18 October 1541) was Queen of Scotland from 1503 until 1513 as the wife of King James IV. She was the eldest daughter of King Henry VII of England and Elizabeth of York, and the elder sister of Henry VIII. Through her granddaughter Mary, Queen of Scots, and her great-grandson James VI and I, Margaret is the ancestress of the later Stuart monarchs who united the crowns of England and Scotland.

Marriage to James IV

Margaret's marriage formed part of the Treaty of Perpetual Peace between England and Scotland, signed in 1502. She travelled north in the summer of 1503 and married James IV at Holyrood Abbey on 8 August 1503. The union was celebrated in verse by the Scottish poet William Dunbar in "The Thistle and the Rose". The couple had several children, of whom only the future James V survived infancy.

Regency and widowhood

James IV was killed at the Battle of Flodden in 1513, leaving Margaret as regent for her infant son James V under the terms of his will, provided she remained unmarried. Her position was undermined when she married Archibald Douglas, 6th Earl of Angus, in 1514, and the regency passed to John Stewart, Duke of Albany. Margaret's daughter by Angus, Margaret Douglas, became the mother of Henry Stuart, Lord Darnley, and thus grandmother of James VI and I.

Later life

Margaret's relations with her brother Henry VIII and with the Scottish nobility remained turbulent. She obtained an annulment of her marriage to Angus in 1527 and married Henry Stewart, later Lord Methven. She frequently sought to arrange meetings between Henry VIII and James V, hoping to secure lasting peace between the two kingdoms. She died at Methven Castle in October 1541.
