Dormition of the Mother of God

Dormition of the Theotokos

The Dormition of the Mother of God (Greek: Κοίμησις Θεοτόκου, Koímēsis Theotókou, "the falling asleep of the God-bearer") is a Great Feast of the Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and Eastern Catholic Churches, celebrating the death of Mary, the mother of Jesus, and her bodily resurrection before being taken up into heaven. It is celebrated on August 15 (for those following the Julian calendar, August 28). The Roman Catholic Church celebrates the corresponding doctrine as the Assumption of Mary on the same date.

Tradition

According to Orthodox tradition, Mary died a natural death like any human, and her soul was received by Christ. Her body, however, was resurrected on the third day after her death, at which time she was taken up into heaven bodily, in anticipation of the general resurrection. The apostles, miraculously gathered, were present at her passing; the Apostle Thomas, arriving late, found her tomb empty. The feast is preceded by a two-week Dormition Fast (August 1–14) and is often called the "Summer Pascha."

History

The feast originated in Jerusalem in the 5th–6th centuries, associated with the Tomb of Mary in the Kidron Valley, and spread through the Byzantine Empire; it was fixed on August 15 under Emperor Maurice around 600. Apocryphal "Transitus Mariae" accounts supplied the narrative details.

Differences with the Assumption

The Orthodox Dormition emphasises Mary's real death and subsequent resurrection by her Son; the Catholic dogma of the Assumption (defined in 1950 in Munificentissimus Deus) teaches that Mary was assumed body and soul into heaven at the end of her earthly life, without formally defining whether she died first.
