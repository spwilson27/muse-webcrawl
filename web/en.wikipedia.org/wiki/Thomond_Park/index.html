Thomond Park is a stadium in Limerick in the Irish province of Munster. It is the home ground of Munster Rugby in the United Rugby Championship and European competitions, and its atmospheric terraces and famously partisan crowd have made it one of the most renowned venues in club rugby. Thomond is the ancient name for North Munster.

History

The ground opened in 1934 and was gradually developed as the home of Limerick rugby, shared by the clubs Shannon and UL Bohemians, whose clubhouses stand behind the terraces. Thomond Park's legend was sealed on 31 October 1978, when Munster defeated the touring New Zealand All Blacks 12–0 — the first Irish side ever to beat the All Blacks — before a capacity crowd. In the professional era Munster's long unbeaten home record in the European Cup (from 1995 to 2007) added to the stadium's mystique; the ground also hosted a pool match of the 1999 Rugby World Cup and Irish international fixtures.

Redevelopment

Between 2007 and 2008 the stadium was comprehensively redeveloped: new east and west grandstands were built, linked by the distinctive arc-shaped roof truss and floodlighting, raising capacity to about 25,600 while keeping the traditional terraces at both ends. The redeveloped ground reopened in November 2008 with a match between Munster and New Zealand. The stadium includes the Munster Rugby museum and is adorned with the silhouettes of marching supporters, a motif drawn from the Munster anthem "Stand Up and Fight".

Other uses

Thomond Park has hosted soccer internationals — the Republic of Ireland played friendly matches there in 2009 while the Aviva Stadium was under construction — as well as concerts and community events. The adjacent "Fanzone" and the Limerick FC's brief tenancy underline its wider sporting role in the city.

Reputation

The stadium is celebrated for the near-total silence afforded to opposition place-kickers, a Limerick tradition, and for the intense noise generated by the sixteen-thousand-strong terracing. Opposition players and coaches have frequently rated it among the most intimidating grounds in European rugby.
