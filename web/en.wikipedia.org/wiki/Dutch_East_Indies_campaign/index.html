The Dutch East Indies campaign of 1941–1942 was the conquest of the Netherlands East Indies (present-day Indonesia) by forces of the Empire of Japan in the early months of the Pacific War. Japan sought the colony's oil fields and raw materials after the Allied oil embargo of 1941; the campaign ended with the surrender of Dutch forces on Java on 9 March 1942 and the start of a three-and-a-half-year occupation.

Course of the campaign

Japanese landings began in December 1941 in northern Borneo and spread through the outer islands—Tarakan, Balikpapan, Ambon, and Timor—securing airfields step by step. The Allied ABDA Command under General Archibald Wavell attempted a joint defence, but its naval forces were destroyed piecemeal, most decisively in the Battle of the Java Sea on 27 February 1942, where Rear Admiral Karel Doorman's strike force was sunk. Japanese troops landed on Java on 1 March; the Dutch commander Hein ter Poorten signed the surrender at Kalijati on 9 March, and organised resistance ended, though Australian and American forces continued guerrilla operations on Timor.

Occupation and aftermath

The Japanese occupation dismantled the colonial administration, interned Dutch civilians, exploited the population through forced labour (romusha) and encouraged Indonesian nationalism for its own ends. The defeat fatally weakened Dutch colonial authority; two days after Japan's surrender in August 1945, Sukarno and Hatta proclaimed Indonesian independence, leading to the Indonesian National Revolution against returning Dutch forces.
