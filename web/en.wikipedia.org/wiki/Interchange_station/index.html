Interchange station

An interchange station is a railway station that enables passengers to transfer between different lines or services, often operated as a single station complex or as two or more linked stations. In North America the equivalent term is transfer station; on the London Underground such stations are marked with a white circle on the Tube map and are commonly called interchanges.

Design

Interchanges range from simple cross-platform arrangements — where two lines share an island platform so passengers walk a few metres between trains — to vast multi-level complexes joining metro, suburban rail, intercity rail and bus services. Convenience depends heavily on walking distance, vertical circulation, wayfinding and whether transfers stay within the fare-paid area ("in-system") or require leaving and re-entering ("out-of-system"). Famous examples include Shinjuku in Tokyo, among the world's busiest stations; Gare du Nord and Châtelet–Les Halles in Paris; Grand Central and Penn Station in New York; and King's Cross St Pancras in London.

Importance

The value of a transit network grows with the journeys its interchanges make possible, so planners treat walking time and reliability at transfer points as central design constraints. Integrated ticketing and timed transfers reduce the penalty further, while station development around major interchanges often anchors commercial districts.
