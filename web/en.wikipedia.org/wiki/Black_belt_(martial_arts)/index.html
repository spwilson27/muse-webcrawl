Black belt (martial arts)

TermBlack belt
OriginJapanese martial arts (judo), late 19th–early 20th century
Rank gradesDan grades, typically 1st–10th dan
Below black beltKyū grades (coloured belts)

A black belt is a rank worn by experienced practitioners in many martial arts, denoting achievement of the dan (段) grades. Coloured belt ranking was introduced by Kanō Jigorō, founder of judo, who awarded the first black belts (1st dan) to students in the 1880s; the familiar white-to-black progression of kyū grades was developed in the early twentieth century and spread worldwide through judo, karate, taekwondo, aikido and other arts.

Meaning and grades

Contrary to a common popular belief, a black belt — particularly at 1st dan (shodan, literally "beginning grade") — signifies a solid mastery of fundamentals rather than ultimate expertise. Dan grades typically ascend from 1st to 10th dan; the highest grades are often honorary. Some arts use embroidered stripes on the belt for each dan, while others, such as judo, allow red-and-white panelled belts for 6th–8th dan and solid red belts for 9th and 10th dan.

Requirements

Requirements vary greatly by art and school, but commonly include a minimum training period (often three to five years to first dan), examinations in technique, forms (kata), sparring, theory and teaching ability, and a minimum age. Brazilian jiu-jitsu, which uses a black belt with red bars, generally requires a substantially longer path — often a decade or more.

Popular culture

The black belt has become a metaphor in general English for high expertise ("a black belt in negotiation"). The martial arts magazine Black Belt, founded in 1961, is named for the rank. Critics of commercial schools sometimes use the term "McDojo" for establishments perceived to award belts too readily.
