Overview

The Battle of Algiers was a campaign fought during the Algerian War (1954–62) between the French Army and the Algerian National Liberation Front (FLN) for control of Algiers. After a wave of FLN bombings of European civilians in 1956 and reprisals, the government gave General Jacques Massu's paratroopers police powers in January 1957; using systematic cordons, informers and torture, they dismantled the FLN's Zone Autonome d'Alger by autumn 1957, capturing or killing its leaders (Larbi Ben M'hidi died in custody; Yacef Saadi was captured).

Significance

The battle was a French military success but a political catastrophe: revelations of systematic torture corroded support for Algérie française in France and internationally, and the FLN ultimately won independence in 1962. Gillo Pontecorvo's 1966 film The Battle of Algiers, shot in the Casbah with Saadi playing himself, became the classic depiction of urban insurgency and counter-insurgency, screened by militaries and movements alike — including, controversially, at the Pentagon in 2003.
