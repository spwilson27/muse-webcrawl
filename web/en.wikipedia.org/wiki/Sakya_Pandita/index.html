Sakya Pandita Kunga Gyeltsen (1182–1251) was a Tibetan spiritual leader and Buddhist scholar, the fourth of the Five Venerable Supreme Masters of the Sakya school and one of the most influential figures in Tibetan intellectual history. Renowned for his mastery of Sanskrit learning and Indian Buddhist philosophy, he bore the title Pandita—"scholar" in the Indian sense—rarely granted to Tibetans. He is counted among the "Three Emanations of Mañjuśrī" of Tibet.

Life

Born into the Khön family at Sakya, he studied under his uncle Drakpa Gyeltsen and mastered the five sciences, including Sanskrit grammar, logic, medicine and the arts, as well as tantric practice. He became abbot of Sakya Monastery and a celebrated teacher and debater; tradition holds that he defeated the Hindu scholar Harinanda in debate. In 1244 the Mongol prince Köden, grandson of Genghis Khan, summoned him to the Mongol court. Sakya Pandita travelled to Liangzhou with his young nephews Phagpa and Chana Dorje, arriving in 1247, where he negotiated the submission of Tibet to Mongol rule and wrote a famous letter urging Tibetan leaders to accept Mongol authority peacefully. He died at Liangzhou in 1251.

Works

His writings shaped Tibetan Buddhism for centuries. The Treasury of Logic on Valid Cognition (Tshad ma rigs gter) systematised the epistemology of Dharmakīrti and diverged from earlier Tibetan interpretations. The Discernment of the Three Vows critically examined the compatibility of śrāvaka, bodhisattva and tantric vows. His Elegant Sayings (Legs bshad), a collection of aphorisms on conduct, became one of the most popular works in Tibetan and Mongolian literature.
