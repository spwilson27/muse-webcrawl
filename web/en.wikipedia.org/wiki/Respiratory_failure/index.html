Respiratory failure

Respiratory failure is a condition in which the respiratory system fails in one or both of its gas-exchange functions: oxygenation of the blood and elimination of carbon dioxide. It is conventionally defined by arterial blood gas thresholds — a partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) below 60 mmHg (8.0 kPa) — and is classified as type 1 (hypoxaemic, with normal or low carbon dioxide) or type 2 (hypercapnic, with PaCO2 above 50 mmHg). It may be acute, chronic, or acute-on-chronic.

Causes

Type 1 failure results from diseases of the lung parenchyma or vasculature that impair oxygenation: pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, acute respiratory distress syndrome, pulmonary embolism and fibrotic lung disease. Type 2 failure reflects inadequate alveolar ventilation — the "pump" fails — as in severe COPD, drug overdose depressing respiratory drive, neuromuscular diseases such as Guillain–Barré syndrome, and severe asthma.

Management

Treatment begins with airway, breathing and circulation support and diagnosis of the underlying cause. Supplemental oxygen corrects hypoxaemia; non-invasive ventilation (CPAP or BiPAP) can avert intubation in COPD exacerbations and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, while severe failure requires endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation in intensive care. In refractory cases ECMO may provide gas exchange outside the body. Chronic hypercapnic failure may be managed with long-term home ventilation.
