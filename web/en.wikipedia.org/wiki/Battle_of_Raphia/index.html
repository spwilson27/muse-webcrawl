The Battle of Raphia, fought on 22 June 217 BC near modern Rafah, was a major battle of the Fourth Syrian War between Ptolemy IV of Egypt and Antiochus III of the Seleucid Empire. It is notable as one of the largest battles of the Hellenistic age and for the clash of African and Indian war elephants.

The armies

Polybius gives the Ptolemaic army about 70,000 infantry, 5,000 cavalry and 73 elephants, and the Seleucid army about 62,000 infantry, 6,000 cavalry and 102 elephants. Ptolemy had, controversially, armed some 20,000 native Egyptians in the phalanx.

The battle

On the Ptolemaic left the smaller African elephants gave way before the Indian elephants of Antiochus, who then routed the opposing cavalry but pursued too far. Meanwhile the Ptolemaic phalanx, led by Ptolemy in person, crushed the Seleucid centre and decided the day.

Aftermath

Egypt retained Coele-Syria for the moment. The arming of native Egyptians is often linked by historians to subsequent Egyptian revolts against Ptolemaic rule.
