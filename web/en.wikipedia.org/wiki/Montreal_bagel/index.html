Montreal bagel

The Montreal-style bagel or Montreal bagel (sometimes referred to as a beigel; Yiddish: beygl; French: bagel de Montréal) is a distinctive variety of handmade and wood-fired bagel originating in Montreal, Quebec, Canada.

In contrast to the New York-style bagel, which also contains sourdough, the Montreal bagel is smaller, thinner, sweeter and denser, with a larger hole, and is always baked in a wood-fired oven. It contains malt, egg and no salt, and is boiled in honey-sweetened water before being baked.

In some Montreal establishments, bagels are still produced by hand and baked on site in wood-fired ovens, often in view of the customers. There are two predominant variants: black-seed (poppy seed) or white-seed (sesame seed).

Montreal bagels, like the similarly shaped New York bagels, were brought to North America by Jewish immigrants from Poland and other Eastern European countries; the differences in texture and taste reflect the style of the particular area in Poland in which the immigrant bakers learned their trade. Minor variations in recipe and baking methods developed in different bagel shops across North America.

The primary ingredient is sometimes maple syrup or honey, with Montreal style claiming that the water used for boiling contains honey and that this gives the bagels their distinctive sweetness. Notable Montreal bagel bakeries include St-Viateur Bagel, founded in 1957, and Fairmount Bagel, founded in 1919; both are located in the Mile End neighbourhood.

In 2019, the first Montreal-style bagels were launched into space by astronaut Greg Chamitoff, who carried three dozen with him aboard the Space Shuttle. The bagels became a recurring point of civic pride and of friendly rivalry with New York.
