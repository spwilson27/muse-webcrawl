The Soap Box Derby is a youth racing programme in the United States in which children and teenagers build and race small, motorless cars powered only by gravity down a sloped course. Competitors construct their cars — originally from wooden soap crates and discarded materials, giving the event its name — and race them in pairs in elimination heats.

History

The Derby began in 1933 in Dayton, Ohio, after newspaper photographer Myron Scott watched boys racing homemade cars and organised a race with 19 entries. The event drew enormous crowds and was moved in 1935 to Akron, Ohio, home of the rubber industry, where a permanent track, Derby Downs, was built in Tallmadge Avenue. The All-American Soap Box Derby finals have been held in Akron every year since, except during the Second World War.

Competition

Local races are held in hundreds of communities across the United States and in several other countries; local champions advance to the world championship finals in Akron. Modern competition is divided into divisions by age and car type, principally the Stock, Super Stock and Masters divisions, with cars increasingly built from standardised kits to keep the contest focused on driving skill and craftsmanship. Girls were first admitted to competition in the early 1970s.

The 1973 scandal

The Derby's most notorious episode came in 1973, when the winner, fourteen-year-old Jimmy Gronen of Boulder, Colorado, was found to have used an electromagnet concealed in the nose of his car, activated by a switch, which pulled the car forward against the metal starting plate. Gronen was stripped of his title, and the scandal — engineered with the help of his uncle and guardian, former hot-rodder Robert Lange — led to tighter inspection rules and a temporary decline in the event's prestige and sponsorship.

Legacy

The phrase "soapbox derby" has entered the language generically for homemade gravity racing. The event remains a fixture of American childhood engineering culture, promoting hands-on building skills, sportsmanship and family involvement.
