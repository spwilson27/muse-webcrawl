Conservatoire de Paris

The Conservatoire de Paris, officially the Conservatoire national supérieur de musique et de danse de Paris (CNSMDP), is a French college of music and dance founded in 1795. One of the world's leading conservatories, it stands in the Parc de la Villette in the 19th arrondissement of Paris, having moved there in 1990.

History

The Conservatoire was created during the French Revolution by merging the École Royale de Chant and the music school of the Garde Nationale under Bernard Sarrette. Early directors included Luigi Cherubini, Gabriel Fauré, and later Marcel Dupré. Its system of competitive examinations, the concours, and the Prix de Rome made it a model for music schools worldwide.

Alumni

Distinguished former students include Hector Berlioz, Claude Debussy, Maurice Ravel, Nadia Boulanger, Olivier Messiaen, and Pierre Boulez, as well as instrumentalists such as cellist Maurice Gendron and flautist Jean-Pierre Rampal. The institution is the senior of the two French national conservatories, the other being the conservatory in Lyon, founded in 1982.
