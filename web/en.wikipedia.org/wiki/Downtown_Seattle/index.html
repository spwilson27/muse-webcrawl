Downtown Seattle

Downtown is the central business district of Seattle, Washington, in the United States. It is relatively compact compared with other city centres on the West Coast, owing to its geographical situation: it is hemmed in on the north and east by hills, on the west by Elliott Bay, and on the south by reclaimed land that was once tidal flats. Downtown contains Seattle's tallest buildings, including the Columbia Center, the tallest building in Washington state at 284 metres (932 ft).

Features

Landmarks include the Pike Place Market, founded in 1907; the Seattle Central Library, an 11-storey glass-and-steel building designed by Rem Koolhaas that opened in 2004; the Seattle Art Museum; and the historic Smith Tower of 1914, once the tallest building west of the Mississippi River. The downtown retail core around Pine Street and the waterfront piers along Alaskan Way are major visitor destinations, and the area is served by the Link light rail through the Downtown Seattle Transit Tunnel.
