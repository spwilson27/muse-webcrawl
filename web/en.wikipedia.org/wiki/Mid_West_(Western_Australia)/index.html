Mid West (Western Australia)

Mid West region
State: Western Australia
Largest centre: Geraldton
Area: approx. 472,000 km²
Population: approx. 70,000
Coastline near Geraldton, the Mid West's main city

The Mid West is one of nine regions of Western Australia, located on the west coast about 370 kilometres north of Perth. It stretches from the Indian Ocean coast inland to the edge of the Western Desert, covering roughly 472,000 square kilometres — about one-sixth of the state's land area. The regional centre is the port city of Geraldton.

Economy

The region's economy rests on mining (iron ore, gold, mineral sands, and historically lead from Geraldton's hinterland), agriculture in the higher-rainfall coastal strip (wheat and other grains, lupins and livestock), fishing — the western rock lobster fishery centred on Geraldton is among Australia's most valuable — and tourism, including the wildflower season, the Abrolhos Islands and the stromatolites and gorges of Kalbarri National Park. The Mid West is also a major renewable energy zone with large wind farms, and hosts part of the Murchison Radio-astronomy Observatory associated with the Square Kilometre Array project.

History and communities

The region is the traditional land of Aboriginal peoples including the Yamatji peoples. European settlement began in the 1850s around Geraldton (founded 1850). Inland gold rushes created towns such as Cue and Mount Magnet, many of which have declined to ghost towns. Local government areas in the region include the City of Greater Geraldton and the shires of Irwin, Carnamah, Coorow, Mingenew, Morawa, Perenjori, Three Springs, Northampton, Chapman Valley, Meekatharra, Mount Magnet, Murchison, Sandstone, Yalgoo and Cue.
