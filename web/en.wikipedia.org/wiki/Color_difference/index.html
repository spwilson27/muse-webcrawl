Color difference

In colorimetry, the color difference or color distance is the separation between two colours, formally the distance between two points in a color space. A consistent way of measuring differences allows colours to be specified and compared numerically, which is essential for quality control in printing, textiles, paints and displays, where a tolerance for an acceptable difference must be defined.

Delta E

Colour differences are commonly expressed as Delta E (ΔE). In the CIELAB colour space, the original formula, CIE76, is simply the Euclidean distance between the two points. Because CIELAB proved not to be perceptually uniform as intended, refined formulas were developed: CMC l:c, CIE94, and most comprehensively CIEDE2000, which adds corrections for lightness, chroma and hue as well as an interactive term, giving results that correlate much better with visual assessments.

Perceptual interpretation

A difference of about 1 ΔE unit is often taken as roughly one just-noticeable difference, though the threshold varies with colour region and viewing conditions. In industrial practice, acceptable tolerances are set in contracts and standards: small values indicate a close match, while larger differences are progressively more obvious to an untrained observer.

Applications

Colour-difference metrics are embedded in colour management systems, spectrophotometer software and gamut-mapping algorithms, and are used to evaluate how accurately devices reproduce target colours.
