Edict of Gulhane

Date3 November 1839
Issued bySultan Abdulmejid I, Ottoman Empire
LocationGülhane Park, beside the Topkapı Palace, Istanbul
SignificanceOpening act of the Tanzimat reforms

The Edict of Gulhane (also spelled Edict of Gülhane; Turkish: Gülhane Hatt-ı Şerifi, "the Sacred Rescript of the Rosehouse") was a landmark proclamation issued by the Ottoman Sultan Abdulmejid I on 3 November 1839, at the start of his reign. Read aloud by Grand Vizier Mustafa Reşid Pasha in the Rose Garden of the Topkapı Palace, it launched the Tanzimat period of administrative and legal reform in the Ottoman Empire.

Contents

The edict guaranteed the security of life, honour, and property of all the sultan's subjects, Muslim and non-Muslim alike. It promised regular and fair assessment and collection of taxes, abolition of tax farming, orderly conscription with fixed terms of military service, and public trials in accordance with law, with no punishment without due process. It also vowed that new laws embodying these principles would be framed and that officials would be bound by them.

Significance

Although partly motivated by the need to win European — especially British — support against Muhammad Ali of Egypt, the edict is generally taken as the opening charter of the Tanzimat. It marked a shift toward a state bound by law and toward legal equality of subjects, principles extended by the Reform Edict of 1856 and subsequent reforms in taxation, education, and administration.
