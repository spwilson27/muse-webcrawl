Gold panning

Gold panning, or simply panning, is a form of placer mining and traditional mining that extracts gold from a placer deposit using a pan. The process is among the simplest ways of extracting gold, and is popular with geology enthusiasts and recreational prospectors, especially because of its low cost and relative simplicity.

Process

The pan is filled with gravel and sediment from a stream bed and submerged in water. The prospector shakes and swirls the pan so that the dense gold particles settle to the bottom while lighter material is washed over the edge. Through repeated dipping and gentle agitation, the remaining concentrate — black sand, garnets, and any gold present — is reduced to a small residue in which flakes and nuggets of gold can be picked out.

History

Panning dates back to ancient times, including Roman mining, and became emblematic of the great gold rushes of the 19th century, such as the California Gold Rush of 1849, where the "forty-niners" used pans before graduating to rockers and sluice boxes. Today panning remains a common first step in prospecting new ground, and competitive gold panning is an organised sport with world championships governed by the World Goldpanning Association.
