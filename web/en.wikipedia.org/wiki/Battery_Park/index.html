Battery Park

Battery Park, officially The Battery, is a 25-acre (10 ha) public park located at the southern tip of Manhattan in New York City, facing New York Harbor. The park takes its name from the artillery batteries that were positioned there in the city's early years to protect the settlement behind them. At its centre stands Castle Clinton (originally Castle Garden), a circular sandstone fort built between 1808 and 1811 that later served as an immigrant landing depot before Ellis Island. The park is bounded by Battery Place to the north and west, State Street to the east, and the harbor on the south.

History

The area was part of the Dutch settlement of New Amsterdam and then British New York, where defensive guns — a "battery" — were mounted from the 17th century onward. Much of the park's land is landfill added during the 19th century. Castle Garden processed some eight million immigrants between 1855 and 1890. In the 20th century the park was reshaped by Robert Moses-era projects, including the Brooklyn–Battery Tunnel and the elevated West Side Highway (later removed). The adjacent neighbourhood of Battery Park City was built on landfill from the excavation of the World Trade Center in the 1970s.

Features

Today the park contains Castle Clinton National Monument, the departure point for ferries to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island; the SeaGlass Carousel, opened in 2015; the East Coast Memorial commemorating American servicemen who died in the Atlantic during World War II; the Sphere, the Fritz Koenig sculpture damaged at the World Trade Center on 9/11 and reinstalled here; and extensive gardens maintained with the Battery Conservancy. It is also home to the Staten Island Ferry's Whitehall Terminal nearby.
