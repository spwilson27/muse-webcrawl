Metallica (album)

Metallica (commonly known as The Black Album) is the fifth studio album by American heavy metal band Metallica, released on August 12, 1991, through Elektra Records. Produced by Bob Rock with James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich, it marked a shift toward slower tempos, simpler arrangements and a more radio-friendly sound than the band's thrash metal origins.

Reception and legacy

The album debuted at number one in ten countries and spawned hit singles including "Enter Sandman", "The Unforgiven", "Nothing Else Matters", "Wherever I May Roam" and "Sad but True". It is Metallica's best-selling album and one of the best-selling albums worldwide, with sales exceeding 30 million copies; in the United States it has been certified 16× platinum. Its dark cover, with the band's logo and a coiled rattlesnake faintly visible, gave it its popular nickname.
