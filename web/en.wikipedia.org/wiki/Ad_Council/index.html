The Advertising Council, commonly known as the Ad Council, is an American non-profit organisation that distributes public service announcements (PSAs) on behalf of government agencies and non-profit sponsors. Advertising agencies create the campaigns voluntarily and pro bono, and media outlets donate airtime and space to run them.

History

The council was founded in 1941 and renamed the War Advertising Council in 1942 to mobilise the advertising industry for the Second World War effort, promoting war bonds, security of war information ("Loose lips sink ships"), and home-front conservation. After the war it reverted to the Advertising Council name and turned to peacetime social issues.

Famous campaigns

  • Smokey Bear (1944–present) — wildfire prevention for the U.S. Forest Service; "Only YOU can prevent forest fires." One of the longest-running PSA campaigns in American history.
  • United Negro College Fund — "A mind is a terrible thing to waste" (1972).
  • McGruff the Crime Dog — "Take a bite out of crime" (1980).
  • "Friends don't let friends drive drunk" — drunk-driving prevention for the Department of Transportation.
  • The Crying Indian (1971) — Keep America Beautiful's anti-pollution campaign featuring Iron Eyes Cody.
  • Love Has No Labels (2015) — diversity and inclusion campaign.
  • COVID-19 vaccine education campaigns (2021).

Operation

The Ad Council selects issues of national significance, matches sponsors with volunteer agencies from its member firms, and distributes finished spots to thousands of media outlets. Donated media for its campaigns has been valued at well over a billion dollars per year.
