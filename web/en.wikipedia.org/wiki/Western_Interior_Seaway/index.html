Western Interior Seaway

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

TypeInland sea (epicontinental sea)
LocationCentral North America
PeriodMiddle Jurassic to early Cenozoic; maximum extent in Cretaceous
DimensionsAbout 3,000 km (1,900 mi) long by 1,000 km (620 mi) wide

The Western Interior Seaway (also known as the Niobrara Sea) was a vast inland sea that split the continent of North America into two landmasses, Laramidia to the west and Appalachia to the east. It extended from the Arctic Ocean south to the Gulf of Mexico, at its greatest extent covering much of present-day Canada and the central United States.

Geology

The seaway resulted from high global sea levels during the greenhouse climate of the Cretaceous Period, combined with subsidence of the foreland basin east of the rising Rocky Mountains. The sediment deposited in its waters formed thick chalk and shale strata, including the Pierre Shale and Niobrara Formation, which preserve rich fossil assemblages of ammonites, mosasaurs, plesiosaurs, giant fish such as Xiphactinus, and flightless birds such as Hesperornis.

The seaway shrank as sea levels fell and the Laramide orogeny uplifted the western part of North America, finally closing in the early Cenozoic.
