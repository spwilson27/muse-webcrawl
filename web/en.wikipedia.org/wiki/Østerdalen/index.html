Østerdalen (Norwegian: [ˈœ̂stəˌɖɑːɳ], "The Eastern Valley") is a valley and traditional district in Innlandet county in Eastern Norway. It stretches some 250 km northward from Elverum along the valley of the Glomma, Norway's longest river, toward Røros. It is one of the large valleys of eastern Norway, lying between Gudbrandsdalen to the west and the Swedish border to the east.

Geography and economy

Østerdalen is Norway's most heavily forested district: vast spruce and pine forests clothe the valley slopes, and forestry, sawmilling, and wood processing have long been the backbone of its economy. The valley floor carries agriculture in the lower, wider southern reaches, while the northern municipalities — Tolga, Tynset, Alvdal, Folldal — include older mountain-farming country around the headwaters of the Glomma and the Folla. Winter tourism centres on Trysil, home to Trysilfjellet, Norway's largest ski resort.

Transport and culture

The valley is served by the Røros Railway (Rørosbanen), opened in stages between 1862 and 1877, and by National Road 3, the principal freight route between Oslo and Trondheim. The district has a strong tradition of folk music and a distinctive dialect group (Østerdalsmål). The Norwegian Forest Museum (Norsk Skogmuseum) is located in Elverum, the regional centre, which is also famous for the Elverum Authorization of April 1940, when the Norwegian government, fleeing the German invasion, was granted full powers there.
