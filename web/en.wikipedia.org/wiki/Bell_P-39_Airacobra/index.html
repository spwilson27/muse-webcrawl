The Bell P-39 Airacobra was an American fighter aircraft produced by Bell Aircraft for the United States Army Air Forces during World War II. It was one of the principal American fighters in service when the United States entered the war. The P-39 was notable for its unusual layout: the engine was mounted amidships, behind the pilot, driving the propeller through a long shaft, which allowed a large 37 mm cannon to fire through the propeller hub; it also pioneered the tricycle undercarriage among American fighters and featured car-style doors instead of a sliding canopy.

Design and development

Designed by Robert Woods and Harland Poyer around a 1935 specification, the XP-39 first flew in April 1938 and achieved impressive speeds in testing with a turbo-supercharged Allison V-1710 engine. However, the production aircraft lost the turbo-supercharger — partly because the drag-reducing work of NACA and Bell and production priorities led to a single-stage supercharger instead — leaving the P-39 with poor performance above about 17,000 feet. This deficiency limited its usefulness in the high-altitude air war over Europe but mattered far less on the Eastern Front.

Operational history

The RAF briefly used the type as the Airacobra I but rejected it after limited operations in 1941. The USAAF employed P-39s in the Pacific (including at Guadalcanal), the Aleutians, North Africa and Italy, mainly at low altitude and increasingly in the ground-attack role.

The type's greatest success came with the Soviet Union, which received roughly half of all P-39s built — about 4,700 aircraft, with further deliveries of the improved P-63 Kingcobra — under Lend-Lease. Soviet pilots valued its heavy armament, ruggedness and good low- and medium-altitude performance in the predominantly low-altitude Eastern Front fighting. Several of the highest-scoring Soviet aces, including Alexander Pokryshkin, Grigory Rechkalov and Pavel Kutakhov, flew the Airacobra, which Soviet pilots nicknamed the "Kobra" or, for its door arrangement, endearingly referred to via Pokryshkin's call sign.

Specifications (P-39Q)

  • Crew: 1
  • Powerplant: 1 × Allison V-1710-85 liquid-cooled V-12, 1,200 hp
  • Maximum speed: ~376 mph (605 km/h) at 15,000 ft
  • Range: ~525 mi (845 km)
  • Service ceiling: ~35,000 ft
  • Armament: 1 × 37 mm M4 cannon firing through the propeller hub (30 rounds); 2 × .50 cal synchronised machine guns; 4 × .30 cal wing guns (later variants: 2 × .50 cal underwing pods)
