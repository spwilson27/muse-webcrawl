The Kamakura period (鎌倉時代, 1185–1333) is a period of Japanese history marking the rule of the Kamakura shogunate, established by Minamoto no Yoritomo after his victory in the Genpei War. It was the first period in which Japan was governed by the warrior class (samurai) through a military government, the bakufu, while the emperor remained in Kyoto.

Establishment

Yoritomo received the title sei-i taishōgun in 1192. After his death, power passed to the Hōjō clan as regents (shikken) for puppet shōguns. The shogunate defeated the retired Emperor Go-Toba in the Jōkyū War (1221), tightening its control over the court and land steward appointments.

Mongol invasions and decline

The shogunate repelled Mongol invasions in 1274 and 1281, aided by storms remembered as kamikaze ("divine wind"), but the cost — with no new lands to reward defenders — strained loyalty. Growing discontent enabled Emperor Go-Daigo's Kenmu Restoration to overthrow the shogunate in 1333, opening the Muromachi period.

Culture and religion

The era saw the flourishing of new Japanese Buddhist schools — Pure Land (Hōnen, Shinran), Nichiren, and Zen (Rinzai brought by Eisai, Sōtō by Dōgen) — alongside war tales such as The Tale of the Heike, the Great Buddha of Kamakura, and a realist sculpture tradition.
