Big Four (banks)

The term "Big Four" is used in several countries to denote the four largest banks that dominate the national banking market. Membership varies by country, and the expression carries connotations of market concentration and systemic importance — such banks are often regarded as "too big to fail".

In many countries four banks dominate deposits and lending.

By country

In Australia, the Big Four are the Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ and National Australia Bank, protected historically by the "four pillars policy" forbidding mergers among them. In the United Kingdom, the traditional Big Four clearing banks are Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds Banking Group and NatWest Group. In China, the Big Four state-owned commercial banks are the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Agricultural Bank of China and Bank of China — among the largest banks in the world by assets. South Africa's Big Four are Standard Bank, FirstRand, Absa and Nedbank, while Canada more often speaks of its "Big Five".

Significance

Concentration among four banks can simplify regulation and promote stability, but critics argue it reduces competition, raises fees and concentrates risk. The Big Four in each country are typically designated systemically important and subject to additional capital requirements.
