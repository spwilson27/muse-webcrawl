Indian wild ass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian wild ass (Equus hemionus khur), also called the khur, ghudkhur or Indian onager, is a subspecies of the onager native to South Asia. It is listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN. Its last refuge is the Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary in the Little Rann of Kutch in Gujarat, India, where it lives on the saline desert flats and grasslands of the Rann.

Description

The Indian wild ass is sandy to reddish-brown in colour, with a dark brown dorsal stripe running from the mane along the back to the tail, bordered by a pale band. It stands about 110–127 cm at the shoulder and weighs between 200 and 290 kg. It is among the fastest of Indian mammals, capable of speeds up to about 70 km/h over short distances, and is well adapted to arid conditions, surviving on coarse grasses, leaves of shrubs, and the pods of Prosopis trees.

Distribution and population

The khur was once distributed across western India, Pakistan, Afghanistan and into Iran, but hunting and habitat loss reduced it to the Rann of Kutch. From a low of fewer than 400 animals in the 1960s, the population has recovered under protection; recent censuses have estimated more than 6,000 individuals, and the animals have increasingly dispersed beyond the sanctuary into surrounding districts of Gujarat.

Threats

Threats include habitat conversion, salt panning and agricultural expansion around the Rann, competition with livestock, road and canal construction, and disease spillover from domestic animals. The species is protected under Schedule I of India's Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
