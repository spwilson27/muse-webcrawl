The River Tay (Scottish Gaelic: Tatha) is the longest river in Scotland and the seventh-longest in the United Kingdom, flowing 193 km (120 miles) from the slopes of Ben Lui in the southern Highlands eastward through Loch Tay, past the towns of Aberfeldy, Dunkeld, and Perth, to the Firth of Tay and the North Sea at Dundee. By volume of discharge — around 170 cubic metres per second — it is the largest river in Britain, draining a catchment of some 6,500 km².

Course

The river rises on Ben Lui and flows north-east as the Fillan and Dochart before entering Loch Tay at Killin, emerging at Kenmore. Below Aberfeldy it passes beneath Wade's Bridge and through the wooded gorge at Dunkeld. At Perth the river becomes tidal; it then widens into the Firth of Tay, crossed by the Tay road and rail bridges at Dundee — the present rail bridge replaced the one destroyed in the Tay Bridge disaster of 1879.

Salmon and wildlife

The Tay is one of Scotland's most celebrated salmon rivers, historically producing the largest rod-caught salmon in Britain — a fish of 64 lb (29 kg) taken by Georgina Ballantine in 1922, still the British record. The river is designated a Special Area of Conservation for Atlantic salmon, otters, and lampreys, and beavers — reintroduced to the catchment controversially from escapes — now breed along its banks.

History

The Tay has long been a strategic artery of Scotland: the Romans built the fortress of Inchtuthil near its banks, and Perth, commanding the lowest bridging point and head of navigation, was for centuries one of Scotland's principal royal burghs. Severe floods, notably in 1993, have repeatedly inundated Perth and the Carse of Gowrie.
