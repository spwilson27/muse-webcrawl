HMT Empire Windrush, originally MV Monte Rosa, was a passenger liner and troopship launched in Hamburg in 1930. She is best known for her voyage from Jamaica to London in June 1948 carrying several hundred Caribbean migrants to Britain, an arrival that came to symbolise the beginning of modern multicultural Britain and gave its name to the "Windrush generation".

German service

Monte Rosa was built for the Hamburg Süd line's South America service and later used for Strength Through Joy (KdF) cruises. During the Second World War she served as a troop transport and accommodation ship, participated in the invasion of Norway, and in 1944 helped evacuate refugees from the Baltic; among her passengers were survivors of the Cap Arcona disaster. She was taken as a prize of war at Kiel in May 1945.

The 1948 voyage

Renamed Empire Windrush for the River Windrush in England, the ship, managed by the New Zealand Shipping Company as a troopship, docked at the Port of Tilbury on 22 June 1948 carrying 1,027 passengers listed on her manifest. Of these, several hundred were Caribbean — chiefly Jamaican — men, many of them ex-servicemen, responding to post-war labour shortages under the British Nationality Act 1948, which confirmed their right to settle as British subjects. Also aboard were passengers from elsewhere, including Polish women displaced from Siberia and Africa. The arrival was filmed by Pathé News; among the arrivals interviewed was the Trinidadian calypsonian Lord Kitchener, who sang "London Is the Place for Me" on the gangway.

Loss

On 28 March 1954, while returning from Japan with troops and military families, Empire Windrush suffered an explosion and fire in her engine room off the coast of Algeria. Four engine-room crew were killed; all other 1,541 people aboard were rescued. Attempts to tow her to Gibraltar failed and she sank on 30 March 1954.

Legacy

"Windrush" became shorthand for post-war Caribbean migration to Britain; 22 June is marked as Windrush Day. The name also attaches to the 2018 Windrush scandal, in which members of that generation were wrongly denied rights and deported, leading to a government apology and compensation scheme.
