Playing card

French-suited playing cards

A playing card is a piece of specially prepared card stock or thin cardboard, typically palm-sized, marked with distinguishing motifs and used as one of a set in playing card games. A complete set is called a deck or pack; the side bearing the value is the face and the identical patterned side is the back.

History

Playing cards were invented in China, probably in the ninth century, and spread through the Islamic world — Mamluk Egypt produced elaborately decorated decks — reaching Europe by the late fourteenth century. European regional patterns developed: Latin suits (cups, coins, swords, batons) in Italy and Spain, German suits (hearts, bells, acorns, leaves), and the French suits (trèfles, carreaux, cœurs, piques) which, in simplified form, became the international standard: clubs, diamonds, hearts and spades. The standard 52-card French-suited deck, with its court cards and later the joker (added in the United States in the nineteenth century), underlies games from poker and bridge to patience (solitaire).

Manufacture and use

Modern cards are made of laminated card stock or plastic with a smooth or linen finish for shuffling and dealing. Beyond games, playing cards are used in cartomancy and fortune-telling, in magic tricks and cardistry, and as collectible art. The largest manufacturers include the United States Playing Card Company, maker of the Bicycle brand.
