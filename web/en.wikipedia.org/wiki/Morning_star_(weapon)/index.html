Morning star (weapon)

A morning star (German: Morgenstern) is any of several forms of medieval club-like weapon consisting of a shaft with an attached ball adorned with one or more spikes. Each spike is usually well-rounded, unlike the shepherd's axe. It was used by both infantry and cavalry; the horseman's weapon had a shorter shaft.

The name is a calque of the German Morgenstern, referring to the appearance of the spiked head, resembling a star. Morning stars are similar to maces but distinguished by their prominent spikes, and should be distinguished from flails, in which the head is attached to the haft by a chain.

Use

The morning star was used in northern Europe, notably by German and Flemish foot soldiers, from the 14th to the 17th century. The spikes allowed the weapon to penetrate or damage armour and mail better than a plain club, and it was effective and cheap to produce. A notable Flemish variant, the goedendag, combined a thick club with a steel spike. Morning stars appear widely in illuminated manuscripts and surviving examples fill armoury collections.

In popular culture

The morning star remains a popular weapon in fantasy literature and role-playing games, where it is frequently (though inaccurately) depicted as a spiked ball on a chain — properly a flail.
