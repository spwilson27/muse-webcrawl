The Secretariat of Tourism (Spanish: Secretaría de Turismo, SECTUR) is the government department in charge of the nation's tourism promotion and policies in Mexico. It is one of the secretariats of state that make up the legal cabinet of the President of Mexico, and its head, the Secretary of Tourism, is appointed by the president.

History

Federal tourism promotion in Mexico began with commissions and departments attached to other ministries in the late 1920s and the creation of the National Tourism Council. The sector was elevated to cabinet rank in 1974, when the Secretariat of Tourism was established as a separate secretariat of state, reflecting the growing economic importance of tourism — Mexico is consistently one of the world's most visited countries, and tourism contributes a significant share of national GDP and employment.

Functions

SECTUR formulates and conducts national tourism policy, promotes Mexico as a destination in domestic and international markets, coordinates with state and municipal governments and the private sector, regulates and classifies tourism service providers, and supports the development of tourism infrastructure and destinations. Subordinate and affiliated bodies have historically included the National Fund for Tourism Development (FONATUR), which developed integrated resort centres such as Cancún, Los Cabos, Ixtapa, Loreto and Huatulco, and the Mexico Tourism Board, formerly responsible for international promotion.

The secretariat also administers programmes such as Pueblos Mágicos ("Magical Towns"), which designates towns recognised for their cultural, historical or natural appeal in order to diversify tourism beyond the major beach resorts.
