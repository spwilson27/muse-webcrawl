Vladimir Tenev
Born: 13 February 1987, Bulgaria
Occupation: Co-founder and CEO of Robinhood

Vladimir "Vlad" Tenev (born 13 February 1987) is a Bulgarian-American entrepreneur, billionaire and co-founder and chief executive officer of Robinhood, a financial technology company best known for its commission-free stock trading app.

Early life and education

Tenev was born in Bulgaria and immigrated to the United States with his parents, both economists, when he was a child. He grew up in Washington, D.C. He studied mathematics at Stanford University, where he met Baiju Bhatt, his future business partner. He began a PhD in mathematics at UCLA but left to pursue business ventures with Bhatt.

Career

Tenev and Bhatt first worked in high-frequency trading, founding firms that built trading software for financial institutions. In 2013 they founded Robinhood in Palo Alto, California, aiming to democratize access to the stock market through a mobile app offering commission-free trades. The company grew rapidly, went public on the Nasdaq in July 2021, and made Tenev a billionaire.

GameStop short squeeze

In January 2021, during the GameStop short squeeze, Robinhood restricted purchases of GameStop and other heavily traded "meme stocks", drawing intense public and political scrutiny. Tenev testified before the U.S. House Financial Services Committee in February 2021, explaining that the restrictions resulted from collateral demands by clearinghouses. Robinhood later paid regulatory settlements related to the episode and other matters.
