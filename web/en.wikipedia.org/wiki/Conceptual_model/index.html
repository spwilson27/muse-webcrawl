Conceptual model

A conceptual model is a representation of a system, made of concepts, which helps people know, understand, or simulate a subject the model represents. Conceptual models are used across science, engineering, information systems and design to make explicit the entities, relationships and processes that matter in a domain, while deliberately omitting implementation detail.

A conceptual model diagram showing entities and their relationships.

In information systems

In database and software design, a conceptual model (or conceptual schema) describes the semantics of a domain at a high level of abstraction, independent of any particular technology. The entity–relationship model, introduced by Peter Chen in 1976, is the best-known technique: it represents entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them, forming the basis from which logical and physical database designs are derived. Conceptual modelling in this sense precedes and constrains later design stages.

In science and other fields

Scientists use conceptual models to summarise how a system is understood to work before formalising it mathematically or computationally. In human–computer interaction, a conceptual model is the user's mental model of how a system behaves, which designers try to make accurate through interface design. In economics and the social sciences, conceptual models identify variables and hypothesised relationships that guide empirical research.
