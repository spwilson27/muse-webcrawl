Fermat's little theorem

FieldNumber theory
Named afterPierre de Fermat
First stated1640

Fermat's little theorem states that if p is a prime number, then for any integer a, the number ap − a is an integer multiple of p. In modular arithmetic notation:

ap ≡ a (mod p)

A frequent special case is that if p is prime and a is not divisible by p, then ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p). Fermat first stated the theorem in a letter to Bernard Frénicle de Bessy dated 18 October 1640, without proof; the first published proof was given by Leonhard Euler in 1736, and a proof had also been found earlier by Leibniz in an unpublished manuscript.

Applications

The theorem underlies the Fermat primality test and is a special case of Euler's theorem. It also plays a role in the theory behind the RSA cryptosystem and in proofs concerning cyclic groups and finite fields.

Generalizations

The theorem generalizes to Euler's theorem for arbitrary moduli, to Carmichael's theorem, and, in abstract algebra, to the statement that every element of a finite field of order p satisfies xp = x.
