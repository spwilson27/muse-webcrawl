Back brace

A back brace is a device designed to limit the motion of the spine in cases of bone fracture or in post-operative fusions, as well as a preventative measure against progression of certain conditions such as scoliosis. Back braces are also commonly used in occupational settings to support the lower back during lifting, though the evidence for their preventative benefit is mixed.

Types

Common back braces include rigid braces, such as the Boston brace and Milwaukee brace used in the treatment of scoliosis, and flexible corsets and belts. A thoracolumbosacral orthosis (TLSO) is a rigid brace that covers the back from the sacrum to the thoracic region and is used after spinal surgery or for vertebral fractures. The Jewett brace is a hyperextension orthosis used for anterior compression fractures of the thoracic and lumbar spine.

Use in scoliosis

Bracing is a standard non-surgical treatment for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis with curves of moderate severity. The goal is not to correct the curve but to prevent it from progressing while the patient is still growing. The 2013 Bracing in Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis Trial (BrAIST) showed that bracing significantly decreased the progression of high-risk curves to the threshold for surgery, with greater benefit the longer the brace was worn each day.
