Cadastre

A cadastre (or cadaster) is a comprehensive register of the real property of a country, recording the extent, value, ownership, and tenure of land parcels. Cadastral records typically combine surveyed maps showing parcel boundaries with registers describing legal rights and, historically, tax assessments.

History

Cadastral surveys date to ancient Egypt and Rome. In Europe, systematic cadastres were created for taxation: the Habsburg and Napoleonic cadastres mapped parcels across large empires in the 18th and 19th centuries, and Napoleon's cadastre became a model for many countries. England's Domesday Book of 1086 is an early, though not graphical, cadastral record.

Modern cadastres

Modern land administration distinguishes the fiscal cadastre (supporting valuation and taxation), the legal cadastre (supporting conveyancing and registration of title), and the multipurpose cadastre, which integrates parcel data into geographic information systems used for planning, utilities, and environmental management. Many developing countries are building or formalizing cadastres to secure land tenure, a theme emphasized in the work of Hernando de Soto on property rights and capital formation.
