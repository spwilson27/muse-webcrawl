Canoe slalom

Canoe slalom (previously known as whitewater slalom) is a competitive sport in which athletes race individually in a kayak or canoe through a course of hanging gates on river rapids or an artificial whitewater course, in the fastest time possible.

Rules

Courses are typically 200–300 metres long with 18–25 gates: green-and-white downstream gates and red-and-white upstream gates, which must be negotiated in sequence. Touching a gate adds a 2-second penalty, and missing a gate or passing it incorrectly adds 50 seconds. Each competitor usually takes two runs, with the better (or combined) result counting depending on the format.

Events

Olympic events include men's and women's kayak single (K1) and canoe single (C1); kayak cross (formerly extreme slalom), a head-to-head race, debuted at the Paris 2024 Olympics. World Championships are organised by the International Canoe Federation (ICF).

History

The sport began in Switzerland in the 1930s, modelled on ski slalom. It was introduced to the Olympics at Munich in 1972, dropped, and returned permanently at Barcelona in 1992.
