SHOUTcast is a service for streaming media over the Internet, originally developed by Nullsoft (the makers of Winamp), launched in 1999. Its DNAS (Distributed Network Audio Server) software allowed anyone with a computer and an Internet connection to broadcast audio, typically MP3, to listeners using media players, making SHOUTcast a foundational technology of early internet radio.

How it works

A broadcaster uses a source plug-in (historically the SHOUTcast DSP for Winamp) to send an audio stream to a SHOUTcast server, which then distributes it to connected listeners over HTTP using a protocol closely related to Icecast's. Stations could be listed in the central SHOUTcast directory (the "Yellow Pages"), searchable by genre, bitrate, and listener count; at its peak the directory listed tens of thousands of stations.

History

Nullsoft's acquisition by AOL in 1999 brought SHOUTcast and Winamp under the same corporate roof. SHOUTcast changed hands in 2014, when AOL sold it (with Winamp) to the Belgian online-radio company Radionomy; it later passed to Targetspot/Azerion following Radionomy's difficulties and the shutdown of its free hosting service in 2019. The competing open-source Icecast system, protocol-compatible in many respects, became the preferred tool for many independent broadcasters, but SHOUTcast's directory long remained one of the largest catalogues of internet radio stations.
