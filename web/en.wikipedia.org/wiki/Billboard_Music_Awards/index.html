The Billboard Music Awards are honors given out annually by Billboard, a publication covering the music business and a music popularity chart. The Billboard Music Awards show has been held annually since 1990, with the exception of the years 2007 through 2010. The event was formerly staged in December, but since returning in 2011, it has been held in May.

Unlike other awards, such as the Grammy Awards, which determine nominations as a result of votes cast by members of the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences, the Billboard Music Awards finalists are based on United States year-end chart performance according to Nielsen data for sales, number of downloads and total airplay.

History

The first Billboard Music Awards ceremony was held in 1990 in Santa Monica, California. The awards were staged annually in December until 2006. After a hiatus, the show returned in 2011 under new production, moving to Las Vegas and to a May slot. Since 2018 the ceremony has been broadcast by NBC. In 2023, the show transitioned to a primarily digital format with performances and acceptance speeches presented online from various locations.

Categories

The general categories include Top Artist, Top New Artist, Top Male Artist, Top Female Artist, Top Duo/Group, Top Billboard 200 Artist, Top Hot 100 Artist, Top Streaming Artist, Top Song Sales Artist, Top Radio Artist and Top Touring Artist, alongside genre categories for pop, rock, country, R&B, rap, Latin, Christian, gospel, dance/electronic and other fields.

Most wins

Drake is the most awarded artist in the history of the Billboard Music Awards, with more than 40 wins. Taylor Swift is the most awarded female artist, and has surpassed Whitney Houston's long-standing record. Other major winners include Justin Bieber, Adele, Beyoncé and BTS.
