Date Masamune

Date Masamune (伊達 政宗, 5 September 1567 – 27 June 1636) was a Japanese daimyō during the Azuchi–Momoyama period through the early Edo period. Heir to a long line of powerful rulers in the Tōhoku region, he founded the city of Sendai and is remembered as the "One-Eyed Dragon of Ōshū" (dokuganryū), having lost the sight in one eye to smallpox as a child. His distinctive helmet bore a huge golden crescent moon.

Life

Masamune succeeded his father Date Terumune in 1584 and rapidly expanded Date territory in northern Honshu, at times coming into conflict with Toyotomi Hideyoshi, whom he submitted to in 1590 after the Siege of Odawara, and later serving Tokugawa Ieyasu at Sekigahara and the Osaka campaigns. As lord of Sendai Domain he developed the region's economy, irrigation and castle town. In 1613 he sponsored the Keichō embassy, sending his retainer Hasekura Tsunenaga on a diplomatic mission to Mexico and Europe, where Hasekura met the Pope in Rome.

Legacy

Masamune remains the emblematic figure of Sendai; his mausoleum is the Zuihōden, and his equestrian statue overlooks the ruins of Sendai Castle. He is a frequent character in Japanese popular culture, often depicted with an eyepatch.
