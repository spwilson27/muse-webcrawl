Amalienborg

Amalienborg is the official residence of the Danish monarch in Copenhagen. It consists of four identical rococo palace façades arranged around an octagonal courtyard, with an equestrian statue of King Frederick V at its centre.

History

The palaces were built in the 1750s for four noble families as part of the Frederiksstaden district. After Christiansborg Palace burned in 1794, the royal family moved into Amalienborg and gradually acquired the buildings. The complex remains closely associated with the Danish monarchy and the daily changing of the Royal Life Guards.

Architecture and museum

Designed principally by Nicolai Eigtved, the palaces are considered masterpieces of Danish rococo architecture. One palace houses the Amalienborg Museum, presenting royal interiors and the history of the Glücksburg dynasty. The axial view from the courtyard leads toward the Marble Church and, across the harbour, the Copenhagen Opera House.
