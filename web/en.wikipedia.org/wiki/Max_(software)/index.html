Max, also known as Max/MSP/Jitter, is a visual programming language for music and multimedia developed and maintained by the San Francisco-based company Cycling '74. It originated at IRCAM in Paris in the mid-1980s, where Miller Puckette developed the original version, initially called the Patcher, to control musical performances interactively.

Overview

In Max, users build programs ("patches") by placing objects — small boxes representing operations such as oscillators, filters, arithmetic, or user-interface elements — on a canvas and connecting them with patch cords along which messages and signals flow. The MSP extensions add real-time digital audio signal processing, and Jitter adds video and matrix processing. Max for Live, released in 2009 in partnership with Ableton, embeds Max inside the Ableton Live digital audio workstation, allowing users to build custom instruments and effects.

History

Miller Puckette developed Max at IRCAM beginning in 1985; David Zicarelli founded Cycling '74 in 1997 to commercialise it after acquiring the rights from Opcode Systems. Successive versions added the Gen environment for low-level code generation, RNBO for exporting patches to web and hardware targets, and extensive integration with JavaScript and Node.js. Max has been widely used in electronic music performance, sound design, interactive installation art and academic research.
