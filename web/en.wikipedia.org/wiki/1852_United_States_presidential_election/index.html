1852 United States presidential election

The 1852 United States presidential election was the 17th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 2, 1852. Democrat Franklin Pierce defeated Whig nominee Winfield Scott in a landslide. The election is notable as the last in which the Whig Party fielded a presidential nominee, and the party collapsed soon afterwards under the strain of the slavery question.

Background

Both major parties sought to put the slavery controversy behind them by endorsing the Compromise of 1850. At the Democratic convention in Baltimore, delegates deadlocked among leading candidates before turning on the 49th ballot to Franklin Pierce of New Hampshire, a former senator and Mexican–American War general who had not been a prominent contender. The Whigs nominated General Winfield Scott, hero of the Mexican war, but their platform's endorsement of the Compromise — including the Fugitive Slave Act — alienated Northern Whigs without winning over the South.

Campaign and results

Pierce campaigned as a unifying figure acceptable to both sections. The deaths during the campaign of Whig statesmen Henry Clay and Daniel Webster symbolised the end of an era. Pierce won 27 of 31 states and 254 of 296 electoral votes. Scott carried only Massachusetts, Vermont, Kentucky and Tennessee. Free Soil candidate John P. Hale, running on an anti-slavery platform, won just under five percent of the popular vote.

Aftermath

The Whig Party disintegrated after its defeat, and the sectional crisis deepened during Pierce's presidency, particularly after the Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854. Opposition to that act gave rise to the new Republican Party, which would win the presidency in 1860.
