The New General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars (abbreviated NGC) is an astronomical catalogue of deep-sky objects compiled by John Louis Emil Dreyer in 1888. It contains 7,840 objects, known as NGC objects, including galaxies, star clusters, and emission and reflection nebulae. It remains one of the most comprehensive general catalogues of its kind and is still widely used by amateur and professional astronomers.

History

Dreyer compiled the NGC while working as director of the Armagh Observatory in Ireland, building on the General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars published by John Herschel in 1864, which itself consolidated the observations of William Herschel and other astronomers. Dreyer later published two supplements, the Index Catalogues (IC) of 1895 and 1908, adding more than 5,000 further objects discovered largely by photography.
