Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of anything (for example, atoms, ions, molecules, energy) generally from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration. Diffusion is driven by a gradient in Gibbs free energy or chemical potential.

Mechanism

Particles move randomly by thermal (Brownian) motion; where there are more particles, more move out than in, producing a net flux down the concentration gradient until equilibrium. Fick's laws quantify this: the first law states that flux is proportional to the negative gradient of concentration, and the second law predicts how concentration changes over time.

Examples

Diffusion spreads a drop of dye through water, carries oxygen from the lungs into the blood, flavours tea from a teabag, and dopes semiconductors. In solids it is much slower but underlies creep, carburising of steel and geological mixing.

Related processes

Osmosis is diffusion of solvent (usually water) across a semipermeable membrane. Effusion, thermal diffusion and facilitated diffusion in cell membranes are related transport phenomena.
