Wildfowling

Wildfowling is the hunting of wild ducks, geese, and other waterfowl and wading birds, traditionally carried out on coastal marshes, estuaries, and wetlands, often at dawn or dusk when birds flight between feeding and roosting areas. In North America the activity is usually called waterfowl hunting.

A wildfowler waiting on the foreshore at first light.

Practice

Wildfowlers typically use shotguns, decoys, and calls to draw birds within range, and may hunt from blinds (hides), boats, or by waiting on the foreshore below the high-water mark. Historically, large punt guns were used for commercial wildfowling, but these are now heavily restricted. Modern wildfowling is regulated by open seasons, bag limits, and protected species lists; in many countries lead shot is banned over wetlands because of its toxicity to waterbirds, with steel or other non-toxic shot required instead.

Conservation

Wildfowling organisations have played a significant role in wetland conservation, acquiring and managing marshes that benefit quarry and non-quarry species alike. In the United Kingdom the sport is represented by the British Association for Shooting and Conservation (BASC), and in North America by organisations such as Ducks Unlimited. Hunting of migratory birds is governed internationally by agreements such as the African-Eurasian Migratory Waterbird Agreement and, in North America, the Migratory Bird Treaty Act.
