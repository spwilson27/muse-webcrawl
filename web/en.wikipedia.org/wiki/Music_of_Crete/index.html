Music of Crete

The music of Crete is a form of traditional Greek folk music called κρητική μουσική (kritiki mousiki). It is centred on the Cretan lyra, a three-stringed bowed instrument, usually accompanied by the laouto (Cretan lute), and is closely tied to dance, festivals and improvised rhyming couplets known as mantinades.

Instruments and forms

The lyra and laouto form the core duo of Cretan music; other instruments include the mandolin, the askomandoura (bagpipe) and the daouli drum. Signature dances include the pentozali, syrtos, sousta and maleviziotis. Two especially famous musical forms are the rizitika — slow, stately songs of the foothills of the White Mountains, often sung a cappella around a table — and the mantinada, improvised rhyming verse sung at celebrations.

Musicians

Cretan music was popularised in the 20th century by performers such as Nikos Xylouris, whose voice became emblematic of the island, as well as lyra players Kostas Moundakis, Athanasios Skordalos and Leonidas Klados. Contemporary performers continue to blend the tradition with rock and world-music influences.
