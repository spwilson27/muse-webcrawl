Viking art

Viking art, also known as Norse art, is the art of the Scandinavian Vikings and their settlements from the late 8th to the early 12th century. It is dominated by ornament — interlacing, gripping beasts and ribbon animals — applied to wood carving, metalwork, runestones, ship decoration and textiles.

Styles

Scholars divide Viking art into a sequence of styles named after find sites: Oseberg (c. 800–850), Borre, Jellinge, Mammen, Ringerike and Urnes (to c. 1125). Early styles favour tightly coiled "gripping beasts"; later Ringerike and Urnes work develops slender, elegant tendrils influenced by European Romanesque.

Media

Surviving masterpieces include the Oseberg ship burial carvings, the Jelling stones, and stave church portals such as Urnes. Much perishable art — painted wood, textiles, carvings on ordinary objects — is lost.
