Baltasar Garzón


Baltasar Garzón.

Baltasar Garzón Real (born 26 October 1955) is a Spanish lawyer and former judge. As an examining magistrate at Spain's central criminal court, the Audiencia Nacional, he gained international prominence for applying the principle of universal jurisdiction to crimes against humanity — most famously by ordering the arrest of the former Chilean dictator Augusto Pinochet in London in 1998.

Career

Born in Torres, Jaén, Garzón studied law at the University of Seville and became a judge in 1981. Appointed to the Audiencia Nacional in 1988, he investigated drug trafficking, money laundering, and the Basque separatist group ETA and its political wing Batasuna, which he suspended. In 1993–94 he briefly left the bench to serve as a Socialist deputy and government delegate for the national anti-drugs plan, resigning over the GAL scandal.

His investigation of the GAL — state-sponsored death squads that had killed ETA members and bystanders in the 1980s — led to the conviction of senior officials of Felipe González's government, including former interior minister José Barrionuevo.

The Pinochet case

In October 1998, acting on complaints about the torture and disappearance of Spanish citizens in Chile and Argentina, Garzón issued an international arrest warrant for Augusto Pinochet, who was detained in London. The British House of Lords ruled that Pinochet did not enjoy immunity for torture, a landmark in international human rights law, though the British government ultimately allowed him to return to Chile on health grounds in 2000. Garzón also indicted Argentine junta members and pursued cases concerning Guantánamo detainees and other alleged crimes.

Franco-era investigation and disbarment

In 2008 Garzón opened an investigation into the disappearance of over 100,000 victims of Francoist repression during and after the Spanish Civil War, asserting jurisdiction over crimes against humanity despite a 1977 amnesty law. The move provoked fierce controversy; he was prosecuted for knowingly exceeding his authority (prevaricación). In February 2012 the Spanish Supreme Court convicted him in a separate case concerning illegal wiretaps in the Gürtel corruption investigation and barred him from the judiciary for eleven years, ending his judicial career. Human rights organisations criticised the prosecution as retaliatory.

Later activities

Garzón subsequently practised law, advised international bodies, and worked on human rights cases. He served for a period as coordinator of the legal defence of WikiLeaks founder Julian Assange. In 2019 the European Court of Human Rights declined to find that his trial had violated his rights.
