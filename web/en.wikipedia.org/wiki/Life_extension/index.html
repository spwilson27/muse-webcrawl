Life extension is the concept of extending the human lifespan, either modestly through improvements in medicine or dramatically by increasing the maximum lifespan beyond its generally settled biological limit of around 125 years. Several researchers in the area, along with "life extensionists", "immortalists" or "longevists" (those who wish to achieve longer lives themselves), postulate that future breakthroughs in tissue rejuvenation, stem cells, regenerative medicine, molecular repair, gene therapy, pharmaceuticals and organ replacement (such as with artificial organs or xenotransplantations) will eventually enable humans to have indefinite lifespans through complete rejuvenation to a healthy youthful condition (agerasia).

History

The desire to extend life is ancient, appearing in myths such as the Epic of Gilgamesh and in the alchemical search for the elixir of life. In the modern era, scientific interest grew with the work of biogerontologists such as Leonard Hayflick, who demonstrated in 1961 that human cells divide only a limited number of times in culture — the Hayflick limit — linked to telomere shortening. The free radical theory of aging, proposed by Denham Harman in the 1950s, suggested that accumulated oxidative damage drives senescence.

In 1993, Cynthia Kenyon showed that mutations in the daf-2 gene could double the lifespan of the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, demonstrating that aging is genetically regulated and potentially modifiable.

Strategies

Caloric restriction

Caloric restriction without malnutrition extends lifespan in many model organisms, from yeast to rodents. Whether it extends maximum human lifespan remains unproven, though the CALERIE trial showed improvements in biomarkers of aging in humans.

Senolytics

Senolytics are drugs that selectively clear senescent cells, which accumulate with age and secrete inflammatory factors. Compounds such as dasatinib plus quercetin, and fisetin, have shown promise in animal studies and early human trials.

Rapamycin and mTOR inhibition

Rapamycin, an inhibitor of the mTOR pathway, extends lifespan in mice even when started late in life, one of the most robust pharmacological results in the field.

Partial reprogramming

Expression of the Yamanaka factors (Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc) can reset epigenetic markers of aging in cells; cyclic partial reprogramming has rejuvenated tissues in mice without causing teratomas, an approach pursued by companies such as Altos Labs.

Ethics and society

The ethics of life extension are debated. Bioethicists such as Leon Kass have argued that radical life extension could undermine the meaning of life and exacerbate overpopulation and inequality, while proponents such as Aubrey de Grey and Nick Bostrom argue that aging causes immense suffering and that extending healthy life is a moral imperative. The sale of purported anti-aging products is a multibillion-dollar industry with minimal regulation.
