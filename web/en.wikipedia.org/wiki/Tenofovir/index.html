Tenofovir

ClassNucleotide reverse-transcriptase inhibitor (NtRTI)
ProdrugsTenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF); tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)
UsesHIV treatment and prevention (PrEP); chronic hepatitis B
Trade namesViread (TDF); Vemlidy (TAF); in combinations such as Truvada, Descovy, Biktarvy

Tenofovir is an antiretroviral medication used to treat HIV infection and chronic hepatitis B, and to prevent HIV infection in pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP). It belongs to the class of nucleotide analogue reverse-transcriptase inhibitors: because tenofovir already carries a phosphonate group mimicking a monophosphate, it requires only two phosphorylation steps inside the cell to reach its active form, tenofovir diphosphate, which terminates the growing viral DNA chain.

Forms

Tenofovir itself is poorly absorbed orally, so it is administered as a prodrug. Tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), marketed as Viread and in the fixed-dose combinations Truvada (with emtricitabine) and Atripla, was approved by the US FDA for HIV in 2001 and for hepatitis B in 2008. The later prodrug tenofovir alafenamide (TAF, Vemlidy, and in Descovy and Biktarvy) achieves higher intracellular concentrations at much lower plasma levels, reducing effects on the kidneys and bone.

Medical uses

In HIV treatment, tenofovir forms the backbone of many first-line regimens, combined with emtricitabine and an integrase inhibitor or other agents. As PrEP, daily or on-demand TDF/emtricitabine greatly reduces the risk of acquiring HIV when taken as prescribed, and was first approved for this use in 2012. In chronic hepatitis B, tenofovir is a preferred first-line therapy because of its potency and high barrier to resistance.

Adverse effects

TDF can cause kidney injury, including proximal tubular dysfunction (Fanconi syndrome) in rare cases, and decreases in bone mineral density; TAF was developed to mitigate both. Tenofovir's chemistry descends from the acyclic nucleoside phosphonates developed by Antonín Holý in Prague, from which the related drugs adefovir and cidofovir also derive. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
