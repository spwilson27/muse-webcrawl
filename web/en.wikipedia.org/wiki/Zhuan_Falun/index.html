Zhuan Falun

Zhuan Falun (Chinese: 轉法輪; pinyin: Zhuǎn Fǎlún, literally "Turning the Law Wheel") is the principal book of Falun Gong (Falun Dafa), a Chinese spiritual practice introduced by Li Hongzhi in 1992. Published in 1995, the book is a compilation of Li's lectures given in China between 1992 and 1994, transcribed and edited by his followers.

Contents

The book consists of nine lectures. It presents the core principles of Falun Gong — Truthfulness, Compassion and Forbearance (Zhen, Shan, Ren) — and teaches that practitioners cultivate both the mind (xinxing) and the body through five qigong exercises. It describes the falun ("law wheel") that Li says he installs in practitioners' abdomens, discusses karma and its role in illness, the opening of the celestial eye, and a cosmology involving multiple dimensions and beings. The text also contains teachings on moral conduct and criticism of contemporary qigong and martial-arts practices.

Publication and reception

Zhuan Falun was published in January 1995 by the China Broadcasting and Television Publishing House and briefly became a bestseller in Beijing. After the Chinese government banned Falun Gong in 1999, the book was prohibited in mainland China and copies were confiscated and destroyed; it remains freely published and distributed abroad and online, and has been translated into dozens of languages. Practitioners regard reading the book repeatedly as essential to cultivation, while scholars of religion analyse it as the scriptural foundation of a new religious movement, and critics — including the Chinese government — denounce aspects of its teachings.
