Unaccredited institutions of higher education

Unaccredited institutions of higher education are colleges, universities, and seminaries that have not been accredited by a recognized accrediting body. Such institutions may lack accreditation because they have not sought it, because they have failed to meet standards, because they operate in jurisdictions where accreditation is not required, or — in the case of diploma mills — because they sell credentials without providing substantive education.

Legal status

The legality of operating an unaccredited school varies widely. In many countries higher education is licensed by the state, and operating without authorization is unlawful. In the United States, authority over education rests largely with the states, which differ in how strictly they regulate degree-granting institutions; some states have historically served as havens for diploma mills, while others, such as Oregon and Texas, publish lists of institutions whose degrees they regard as unlawful to use.

Consequences for students

Degrees from unaccredited institutions may not be recognized by employers, licensing boards, or other universities, and credits earned generally do not transfer to accredited institutions. Students at unaccredited schools are typically ineligible for government financial aid. However, not all unaccredited institutions are fraudulent: some small religious institutions, experimental colleges, or schools in the process of seeking accreditation operate legitimately without it.

Accreditation mills

Some unaccredited institutions bolster their appearance of legitimacy by claiming accreditation from "accreditation mills" — bodies that grant accreditation for a fee without substantive review. Recognized accreditors in the United States are listed by the U.S. Department of Education and the Council for Higher Education Accreditation.
