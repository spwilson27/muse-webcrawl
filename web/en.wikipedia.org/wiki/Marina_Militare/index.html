The Marina Militare (Italian Navy) is the navy of the Italian Republic and one of the four branches of the Italian Armed Forces. It was formed in 1946 from what remained of the Regia Marina (Royal Navy) after the Second World War.

History

The Italian Navy traces its origins to the Regia Marina, created in 1861 following the unification of Italy. It fought in both world wars and, after 1946, became a founding naval force of NATO, operating extensively in the Mediterranean Sea.

Fleet

The fleet includes the aircraft carriers Cavour and Trieste, destroyers of the Horizon and FREMM programmes, submarines, and numerous patrol and amphibious vessels. As of the 2020s it operates around 180 commissioned ships and craft, plus naval aviation.

Organisation

The Chief of the Naval Staff commands the service from Rome. Major commands include the Fleet Command (CINCNAV) at Santa Rosa and naval stations at Taranto, La Spezia, Augusta and Brindisi.
