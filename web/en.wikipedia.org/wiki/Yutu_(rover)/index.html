Yutu (rover)

Yutu (玉兔)
Mission: Chang'e 3 lunar exploration
Operator: China National Space Administration
Landing: 14 December 2013, Mare Imbrium
Distance travelled: 114.8 metres

Yutu (Chinese: 玉兔; pinyin: Yùtù, lit. "Jade Rabbit") was a robotic lunar rover that formed part of the Chinese Chang'e 3 mission to the Moon. It was launched at 17:30 UTC on 1 December 2013 and reached the lunar surface on 14 December 2013, making China the third country to soft-land on the Moon and operate a rover there. The rover's name was chosen in an online poll, referencing the Jade Rabbit of Chinese mythology that accompanies the Moon goddess Chang'e.

Mission

Yutu was a six-wheeled rover of about 140 kilograms equipped with panoramic cameras, a lunar penetrating radar, and infrared and X-ray spectrometers to study lunar soil composition and structure. It landed in Mare Imbrium and travelled about 114.8 metres. After surviving its first lunar night, the rover suffered a mechanical control abnormality in January 2014 — attributed to the harsh lunar environment — and became immobile, though it continued to return data intermittently for nearly three years, far beyond its planned three-month design life. Contact was lost in mid-2016, giving it the longest operational life of any lunar rover at the time.

A successor, Yutu-2, landed with Chang'e 4 on the far side of the Moon in January 2019.
