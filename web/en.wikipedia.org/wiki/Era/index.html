Era

An era is a span of time defined for the purposes of chronology or historiography, as in a regnal era in the history of a given monarchy, a calendar era, or — in geology — a geological era of the Earth's past. Eras are typically marked off by distinctive characteristics or by significant events, and give their name to the period they bound.

Calendar eras

Calendar eras count years from a chosen epoch. The most widely used is the Christian era (AD/CE), reckoned from the traditional year of Jesus's birth. Others include the Byzantine era, the era of the Seleucids, the Spanish era, the Hindu Kali Yuga era, and the regnal eras of Japan, in which years are numbered within the reign (nengō) of each emperor — Reiwa, Heisei, Shōwa and so on. The Holocene calendar proposes adding 10,000 to CE years to make all recorded history positive.

Geological eras

In the geologic time scale, an era is the second-largest division of time, larger than a period and smaller than an eon. The Phanerozoic eon is divided into the Paleozoic, Mesozoic and Cenozoic eras — the "old life", "middle life" and "new life" — each defined by distinctive fossil assemblages.

Historical eras

Historians divide the past into eras such as the Victorian era, the Edwardian era, the Elizabethan era, the Meiji era in Japan, or informal modern labels like the "digital era" and the "Cold War era". Such divisions are conventional and often contested, since social and technological change does not break neatly at single dates.
