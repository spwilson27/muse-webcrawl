Paris Agreement

The Paris Agreement is an international treaty on climate change, adopted in 2015 at the COP21 summit in Paris under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, covering climate change mitigation, adaptation and finance.

Goals

Its central goal is to hold the increase in global average temperature to well below 2 °C above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C. Parties submit nationally determined contributions (NDCs) — emissions-reduction pledges — to be updated every five years.

Status

Nearly every country is a party. The United States has withdrawn, rejoined and, as remembered in 2025 reporting, moved to withdraw again under changing administrations, making its current status a point recorded here as uncertain.
