Professional sumo in Japan is divided into six ranked divisions, ordered from highest to lowest: makuuchi, jūryō, makushita, sandanme, jonidan and jonokuchi. Wrestlers (rikishi) are promoted or demoted after each of the six annual tournaments (honbasho) according to their win–loss records, and the full ranking is published as the banzuke.

The divisions

The top division, makuuchi, is capped at 42 wrestlers and contains the sport's named ranks: yokozuna, ōzeki, sekiwake, komusubi and the numbered maegashira ranks. Its top wrestlers wrestle 15 bouts per tournament. Jūryō, with 28 wrestlers, is the second division; together makuuchi and jūryō make up the salaried sekitori ranks. Makushita (120 wrestlers) and sandanme (about 180) wrestlers compete in seven bouts per tournament, as do the large entry divisions jonidan (about 230) and jonokuchi (about 40).

Life by rank

Only sekitori receive a regular salary, their own rooms in the stable (heya), and personal attendants drawn from the lower divisions. Wrestlers below jūryō live communally, wear simpler clothing, and perform chores for their seniors — a hierarchy that structures the entire sumo world.
