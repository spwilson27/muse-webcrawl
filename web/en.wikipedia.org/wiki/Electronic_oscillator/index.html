Electronic oscillator


A simple oscillator circuit

An electronic oscillator is an electronic circuit that produces a periodic, oscillating or alternating current (AC) signal, usually a sine wave, square wave, or triangle wave. Oscillators convert direct current (DC) from a power supply to an AC signal. They are widely used in many electronic devices ranging from simplest clock generators to digital instruments (like calculators) and complex computers and peripherals etc. Common examples of signals generated by oscillators include signals broadcast by radio and television transmitters, clock signals that regulate computers and quartz clocks, and the sounds produced by electronic beepers and video games.

Types

Harmonic oscillators

Harmonic (linear) oscillators produce a sinusoidal output. The two main families are LC oscillators, which use an inductor-capacitor tank circuit — examples include the Hartley, Colpitts, and Clapp circuits — and RC oscillators, such as the phase-shift and Wien bridge oscillators, used at lower frequencies. Crystal oscillators use the mechanical resonance of a piezoelectric quartz crystal to achieve exceptional frequency stability.

Relaxation oscillators

Relaxation oscillators produce non-sinusoidal waveforms such as square, sawtooth, and triangle waves, using a switching device and an RC timing network. The classic 555 timer astable circuit is a relaxation oscillator.

Conditions for oscillation

For a feedback oscillator to sustain oscillation, the Barkhausen stability criterion must be met: the loop gain must be at least unity and the total phase shift around the loop must be 0° or a multiple of 360° at the oscillation frequency. Amplitude is stabilized by nonlinearity or automatic gain control.

Applications

Oscillators provide carrier waves for radio transmitters, local-oscillator signals in receivers, clock signals in digital systems, sweep signals in test equipment, and timing references in watches and instruments. Voltage-controlled oscillators (VCOs) are central to phase-locked loops and frequency synthesizers.
