Heterotroph

A heterotroph (from Ancient Greek héteros, "other", and trophḗ, "nutrition") is an organism that cannot produce its own food, but instead takes nutrition from other sources of organic carbon, mainly plant or animal matter. Heterotrophs are known as consumers in the food chain and contrast with autotrophs, such as plants and algae, which fix carbon from inorganic sources by photosynthesis or chemosynthesis. Animals, fungi, and most bacteria and protists are heterotrophs.

Animals are heterotrophs that ingest other organisms for carbon and energy

Types

Heterotrophs are classified according to the source of their energy and carbon. Photoheterotrophs, such as purple non-sulfur bacteria, use light for energy but organic compounds for carbon. Chemoheterotrophs obtain both energy and carbon from organic compounds; this group includes all animals and fungi. Based on how they obtain food, heterotrophs are also divided into herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, and detritivores, and by feeding mechanism into organisms that ingest food (holozoic nutrition), absorb dissolved nutrients across the body surface (saprotrophic nutrition, as in fungi), or live on or in a host (parasitic nutrition).

Role in ecosystems

In a food chain, primary producers form the first trophic level; heterotrophs that eat them are primary consumers (herbivores), those that eat herbivores are secondary consumers, and so on. Saprotrophic heterotrophs — the decomposers, chiefly fungi and bacteria — break down dead organisms and waste, releasing inorganic nutrients that producers can use again, and are essential to nutrient cycles such as the carbon and nitrogen cycles.

Origin and evolution

It is generally thought that the earliest organisms on Earth were heterotrophs, feeding on organic molecules in the primordial environment, and that autotrophy evolved later as those molecules became scarce. Mixotrophs — organisms such as the Venus flytrap and some protists that combine photosynthesis with the capture of prey — blur the boundary between the two categories.
