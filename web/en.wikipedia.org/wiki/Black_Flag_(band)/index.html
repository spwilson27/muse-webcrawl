Black Flag (band)

Black Flag is an American hardcore punk band formed in Hermosa Beach, California, in 1976 by guitarist Greg Ginn. One of the first and most influential hardcore bands, Black Flag built a national underground following through relentless touring and Ginn's independent label, SST Records. Vocalist Henry Rollins, who joined in 1981, became the band's best-known frontman. The band's four-bar logo, designed by Raymond Pettibon, is one of punk's most recognisable symbols.

History

Originally called Panic, the band cycled through singers — Keith Morris, Ron Reyes, and Dez Cadena — before Rollins, a fan from Washington, D.C., joined in 1981. The debut album Damaged (1981), featuring songs such as "Rise Above" and "TV Party," became a hardcore landmark despite legal battles with MCA/Unicorn Records that blocked releases for two years, during which the band issued material "by everybody but" itself.

Later work and breakup

In 1984 the band released three albums, including My War, whose slow, heavy second side influenced sludge and grunge, and Slip It In. Exhausted by touring and internal conflict, Ginn disbanded Black Flag in 1986. Members went on to the Rollins Band, Circle Jerks (Morris), and other projects.

Reunions and legacy

Competing reunion line-ups in 2013 — Black Flag under Ginn and "Flag" featuring Morris, Cadena, and Bill Stevenson — led to litigation. Black Flag's DIY ethos, touring circuit, and SST catalogue helped create the American independent music network of the 1980s.
