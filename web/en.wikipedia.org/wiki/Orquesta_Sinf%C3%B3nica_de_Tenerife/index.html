The Orquesta Sinfónica de Tenerife (Tenerife Symphony Orchestra) is a Spanish symphony orchestra based in Santa Cruz de Tenerife on the island of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. It is regarded as one of Spain's leading orchestras and serves as the resident orchestra of the Auditorio de Tenerife, the striking sail-like concert hall designed by Santiago Calatrava that opened in 2003.

History

The orchestra's origins lie in the Orquesta de Cámara de Canarias, founded in 1935. It grew over the following decades into a full symphony orchestra under the sponsorship of the island government, the Cabildo de Tenerife. Long-serving chief conductors included Armando Alfonso and, most notably, Víctor Pablo Pérez, who led the orchestra from 1986 to 2006 and raised its national profile through extensive touring and recording. Later music directors included Lü Jia and Michał Nesterowicz; Pablo González became chief conductor in the 2020s.

Activities

The orchestra performs a subscription season at the Auditorio de Tenerife, appears at festivals across Spain, and has toured internationally. It collaborates with the Ópera de Tenerife and the Festival Internacional de Música de Canarias, and maintains education and outreach programmes throughout the island. Its discography includes recordings of Spanish and Latin American repertoire as well as standard symphonic works.
