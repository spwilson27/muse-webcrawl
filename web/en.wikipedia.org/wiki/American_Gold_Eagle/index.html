The American Gold Eagle is an official gold bullion coin of the United States. Authorised by the Gold Bullion Coin Act of 1985, it was first released by the United States Mint in 1986. It is produced in 1/10, 1/4, 1/2, and 1 troy ounce denominations with face values of $5, $10, $25, and $50 respectively, although their market value is far higher, based on the spot price of gold.

Composition and design

By law the gold must come from sources within the United States. The coins are struck in 22-karat gold — 91.67% gold alloyed with silver and copper for hardness — and each coin contains its full stated weight of pure gold, so the gross weight exceeds one troy ounce for the largest denomination (33.93 g). The obverse adapts Augustus Saint-Gaudens' celebrated design for the 1907–1933 double eagle, depicting Liberty striding forward with a torch and olive branch. The reverse featured a family of eagles designed by Miley Busiek from 1986 to 2020; in 2021 a new reverse, a close-profile eagle's head by Jennie Norris, was introduced.

Production and collecting

Gold Eagles are struck at the West Point Mint in bullion, proof, and uncirculated finishes, and are among the most popular bullion coins in the world. They are eligible for inclusion in individual retirement accounts (IRAs) in the United States. Sales peaked during periods of financial uncertainty, such as 1999, 2009, and 2020. A special "burnished" uncirculated version with the W mint mark was issued for collectors between 2006 and 2008 and again from 2011.
