Cleethorpes is a seaside town in North East Lincolnshire, England, on the south bank of the Humber Estuary. It adjoins the port town of Grimsby, with which it forms a conurbation.

History

Originally a small fishing village, Cleethorpes developed rapidly as a Victorian seaside resort after the arrival of the railway in 1863. The pier, opened in 1873, and promenades made it a popular destination for holidaymakers from the industrial towns of Yorkshire and the Midlands.

Features

Landmarks include the pier, the Ross Castle mock fortification, a boating lake, a light railway and the Greenwich Meridian line, which passes through the town and is marked by a signpost and plaque.
