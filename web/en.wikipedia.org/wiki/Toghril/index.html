Toghril, also spelled Tughril, Toghrul or Tughrul (Turkish: Tuğrul; full name Rukn al-Dunya wa'l-Din Abu Talib Muhammad Tughril ibn Mika'il), better known as Tughril I, was a Turkoman chieftain who founded the Seljuk Empire, ruling from 1037 to 1063.

Rise to power

Toghril was the grandson of Seljuk, the eponymous founder of the dynasty. Together with his brother Chaghri Beg he led the Seljuk Turks from the Central Asian steppe into Khurasan, defeating the Ghaznavids decisively at the Battle of Dandanaqan in 1040. From there the Seljuks expanded across Persia, taking Nishapur, then Ray and Isfahan.

Entry into Baghdad

In 1055 Toghril entered Baghdad at the invitation of the Abbasid caliph al-Qa'im, ending Buyid domination of the caliphate. The caliph granted him the title of Sultan — the first ruler to bear it prominently — and proclaimed him "King of the East and the West". Toghril thus became the effective protector and ruler of the Abbasid heartlands, while the caliph retained religious authority. He later married the caliph's daughter.

Legacy

Toghril united most of the Islamic Middle East under Seljuk rule and established the pattern of a Turkish military sultanate ruling in the name of the Abbasid caliph. He died childless in 1063 and was succeeded by his nephew Alp Arslan. The Tughril Tower near Ray in Iran is traditionally associated with his era.
