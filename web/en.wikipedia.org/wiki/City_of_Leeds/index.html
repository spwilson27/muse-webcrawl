The City of Leeds is a city and metropolitan borough in West Yorkshire, England. The metropolitan borough includes the administrative centre of Leeds and the towns of Horsforth, Morley, Otley, Pudsey, Rothwell, Wetherby and Yeadon, as well as extensive rural areas. With a population of around 800,000 it is one of the largest local government districts in England. It is governed by Leeds City Council, and forms part of the West Yorkshire Combined Authority.

History

Leeds received city status in 1893 as a county borough. The modern district was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, merging the former county borough of Leeds with the municipal boroughs of Morley and Pudsey, several urban districts, and parts of rural districts. The name Leeds derives from the Brittonic Loidis, originally referring to the district around the river crossing on the Aire.

Geography

The district covers about 552 square kilometres, stretching from the Pennine fringe in the west to the Vale of York in the east. The main urban area centres on Leeds city centre, a major centre for financial, legal, and professional services, retail, and higher education, with two large universities and several other institutions.

Governance

Leeds City Council is elected by thirds in most years, with 99 councillors representing 33 wards. The city returns Members of Parliament to Westminster and has a directly influential role in regional transport and economic policy through the combined authority.
