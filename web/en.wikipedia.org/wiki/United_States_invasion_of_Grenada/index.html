United States invasion of Grenada

The United States invasion of Grenada, codenamed Operation Urgent Fury, began on 25 October 1983. The invasion followed the overthrow and execution of Grenadian Prime Minister Maurice Bishop by a hard-line faction of the ruling New Jewel Movement, which had led to the formation of a military government under Hudson Austin.

The United States, reacting to the construction of a large airfield at Point Salines with Cuban assistance and fearing for the safety of several hundred American medical students on the island, intervened alongside forces from several Caribbean nations organised through the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS). The operation took roughly four days of major combat and resulted in the collapse of the Revolutionary Military Council.

Background

Maurice Bishop's People's Revolutionary Government, which had come to power in a 1979 coup, developed close ties with Cuba and the Soviet Union. A power struggle within the party in October 1983 led to Bishop's house arrest and, after crowds freed him, his execution on 19 October along with several cabinet members.

The invasion

US forces, including Army Rangers, Navy SEALs, Marines, and the 82nd Airborne Division, landed beginning in the early hours of 25 October. They met resistance from the Grenadian People's Revolutionary Army and Cuban military advisers and construction personnel. American casualties numbered 19 killed and 116 wounded; Grenadian and Cuban forces suffered heavier losses, including 45 military and at least 24 civilian Grenadian deaths, and 25 Cuban killed.

Aftermath

The UN General Assembly condemned the invasion as a violation of international law, while the operation was broadly popular with Grenadians and with the American public. An interim government was installed, and democratic elections were held in December 1984. The date of the invasion, 25 October, is now celebrated in Grenada as Thanksgiving Day.
