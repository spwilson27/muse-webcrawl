Flemish literature

Flemish literature is literature from Flanders, historically a region comprising parts of present-day Belgium, France and the Netherlands. Up to the second half of the 18th century, Flemish literature was not clearly distinct from Dutch literature as a whole. From the 19th century, as Belgium became independent and the Flemish Movement asserted Dutch-language culture, a distinct Flemish literary tradition developed.

Early literature

Medieval Flemish works include the verse romance Van den vos Reynaerde (Of Reynaert the Fox), one of the masterpieces of Middle Dutch literature, and writings by the mystic Hadewijch. Jacob van Maerlant was a prolific 13th-century author of didactic works.

19th and 20th centuries

Hendrik Conscience's novel De Leeuw van Vlaanderen (1838, "The Lion of Flanders") helped awaken Flemish national consciousness. Guido Gezelle was a major poet of the era. In the 20th century, authors such as Hugo Claus, Louis Paul Boon and Gerard Reve gained international recognition, and Flemish writers have continued to win major Dutch-language literary prizes.
