Asexuality

Asexuality is the lack of sexual attraction to others, or low or absent interest in or desire for sexual activity. It may be considered a sexual orientation or the absence of one. Asexuality is distinct from celibacy, which is a behavioural choice to abstain from sexual activity, and from sexual dysfunction, though these can overlap.

Definitions and spectrum

People who identify as asexual ("aces") vary widely: many form romantic relationships and describe a separate romantic orientation (for example, aromantic, biromantic or heteroromantic), distinguishing romantic from sexual attraction. The "asexual spectrum" includes demisexual people, who experience sexual attraction only after forming an emotional bond, and "grey-asexual" people with rare or low-intensity attraction. Researcher Anthony Bogaert estimated around 1% of the population might be asexual, based on a 2004 British survey.

Community and research

The modern asexual community coalesced around the Asexual Visibility and Education Network (AVEN), founded by David Jay in 2001. Academic and public recognition has grown, and asexuality is included within some formulations of the wider LGBTQ umbrella. The asexual pride flag, adopted in 2010, consists of black, grey, white and purple horizontal stripes.
