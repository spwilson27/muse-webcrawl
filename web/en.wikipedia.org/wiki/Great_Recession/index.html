Great Recession

[Image: a row of suburban houses with 'foreclosure' sale signs during the 2008 financial crisis]

The Great Recession was the severe global Recession that followed the financial crisis of 2007–08 — the worst downturn since the Great Depression. Triggered by the collapse of the US housing bubble and the failure of Lehman Brothers in September 2008, it produced a synchronised fall in world output in 2009, US unemployment of 10%, and a long, uneven recovery.

Causes and response

Causes identified by official inquiries include predatory and subprime lending, securitisation that dispersed and obscured risk, excessive leverage, failures of rating agencies and regulators, and a shadow banking system prone to runs. Governments responded with bank rescues and guarantees, near-zero interest rates, quantitative easing by the Federal Reserve and other central banks, and fiscal stimulus — measures credited with preventing a second depression but followed by austerity debates and the euro-area sovereign debt crisis (2010–12), which nearly broke the euro. Regulatory reform followed (Dodd–Frank 2010, Basel III capital rules, stress tests). The recession's political aftershocks — populism, distrust of elites, the Occupy protests — and its long shadow on wages, housing and public debt defined the 2010s.
