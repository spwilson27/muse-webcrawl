Post-Hartree–Fock

In computational chemistry, post-Hartree–Fock methods are the set of methods developed to improve on the Hartree–Fock (HF) approximation by accounting for electron correlation — the difference between the exact non-relativistic energy of a system within a given basis set and the Hartree–Fock limit.

Motivation

Hartree–Fock theory treats each electron as moving in the average field of the others, so it neglects the instantaneous correlation of electron motions. Although HF recovers most of the total energy, the missing correlation energy is comparable in magnitude to chemical reaction energies, so chemically accurate results require post-HF treatment.

Methods

Common post-Hartree–Fock methods include Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2, MP3, MP4, ...), configuration interaction (CISD, and its size-consistent quadratic variant QCISD), coupled cluster theory (CCSD, CCSD(T) — often called the "gold standard" of quantum chemistry), and multi-configurational approaches such as CASSCF used when a single determinant is a poor starting point. These methods are generally more computationally expensive than Hartree–Fock and scale steeply with system size, so they are complemented in practice by density functional theory for larger systems.
