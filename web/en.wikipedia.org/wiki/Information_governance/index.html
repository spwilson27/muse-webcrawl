Information governance

Information governance
ScopeRecords management, privacy, security, compliance, e-discovery
Key frameworksARMA Generally Accepted Recordkeeping Principles; IGRM

Information governance (IG) is the overall strategy within an organisation for managing information to support business outcomes while balancing risk and value. It encompasses the policies, procedures, roles, and technologies by which an organisation creates, uses, retains, protects, and disposes of information across its lifecycle.

Scope

Information governance brings together disciplines that were traditionally separate: records and information management, information security, data privacy and protection, regulatory compliance, litigation readiness and e-discovery, data quality, and enterprise content management. Its goals include reducing the cost and legal risk of retaining excessive or uncontrolled data ("dark data" and redundant, obsolete, and trivial content), ensuring defensible deletion, protecting personal and sensitive data under laws such as the GDPR, and making information findable and trustworthy for business use.

Frameworks

The Information Governance Reference Model (IGRM), developed by the Electronic Discovery Reference Model community, diagrams the links between legal, records, IT, and business stakeholders. ARMA International's Generally Accepted Recordkeeping Principles — accountability, transparency, integrity, protection, compliance, availability, retention, and disposition — are widely used as a maturity framework. In UK healthcare and public services, the term is also used for the framework governing the handling of personal and patient information, including the Caldicott principles and the role of the Caldicott guardian.
