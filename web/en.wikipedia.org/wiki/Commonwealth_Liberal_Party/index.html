The Commonwealth Liberal Party (CLP), often called the Fusion Liberal Party, was an Australian political party formed in 1909 by the merger of Alfred Deakin's Protectionist Party and Joseph Cook's Anti-Socialist Party. The "Fusion" united the two non-Labor parties against the growing Australian Labor Party in the years after Federation.

History

For the first decade of the Commonwealth, Australian politics was a three-party contest between Protectionists, Free Traders (later Anti-Socialists), and Labor. In 1909 Deakin and Cook agreed to merge, creating the first united non-Labor party. The Fusion won the 1910 election's Senate contest but lost government to Andrew Fisher's Labor; Deakin retired in 1913 and Cook led the party to a one-seat victory that year, before losing the 1914 double-dissolution election.

Dissolution

During the First World War the Labor Party split over conscription in 1916. Prime Minister Billy Hughes and his supporters joined with Cook's Liberals to form the Nationalist Party in February 1917, ending the Commonwealth Liberal Party's existence. The modern Liberal Party of Australia, founded in 1944, traces part of its ancestry to the Fusion tradition.
