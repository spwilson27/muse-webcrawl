Walking bass

A walking bass line is a style of bass accompaniment or line, common in jazz, blues, rockabilly, and other genres, in which the bass plays a note (principally a quarter note) on each beat, creating a steady, propulsive rhythmic foundation. Walking bass lines typically outline the chord progression by combining chord tones with passing tones, approach notes, and scale tones.

Technique

A walking bassist aims to create a sense of forward motion. Common patterns include "two-feel" playing (half notes) in early sections building to full walking lines. Lines frequently move by step or by small intervals, target chord roots on strong beats (especially beat one), and use chromatic approach notes to lead into the next chord change.

History

The style developed in the 1920s and 1930s as string bass replaced tuba in jazz ensembles. Players such as Walter Page with Count Basie's orchestra established the four-to-the-bar walking style central to swing; later masters include Ray Brown, Paul Chambers, Ron Carter, and Charles Mingus. Walking bass techniques also appear in blues, country, and rock and roll.
