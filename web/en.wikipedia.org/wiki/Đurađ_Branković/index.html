Đurađ Branković

Đurađ Branković (1377 – 24 December 1456) was the Despot of Serbia from 1427 to 1456, the last great medieval Serbian ruler before the Ottoman conquest. He navigated precariously between the Ottoman Empire and the Kingdom of Hungary, to both of which he was at times a vassal.

Reign

Succeeding his uncle Stefan Lazarević, Đurađ returned Belgrade to Hungary and built a new capital at Smederevo, whose vast fortress on the Danube was completed in 1430. Serbia was overrun by the Ottomans in 1439–1441, but the Crusade of Varna and subsequent peace restored his despotate in 1444. Under pressure he declined to join the second crusade of John Hunyadi, and his relations with Hungary remained fraught.

Legacy

In Serbian folk tradition Đurađ was remembered ambivalently — blamed in epic song for the fall of Serbia, yet also revered as a builder and diplomat who preserved the state for three decades against overwhelming odds. His dynasty's marriage alliances stretched from the Hungarian court to the Byzantine imperial family.
