Resurrection of Jesus

The resurrection of Jesus (Biblical Greek: ἀνάστασις τοῦ Ἰησοῦ) is the Christian belief that God raised Jesus from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion, starting — or restoring — his exalted life as Christ and Lord. According to the New Testament writings, early Christians believed that after his crucifixion, Jesus appeared to his followers, beginning with the discovery of the empty tomb, and that these appearances convinced them he had been raised from the dead.

New Testament accounts

The earliest surviving mention is in the letters of Paul, who quotes a creed (1 Corinthians 15:3–8) naming appearances to Cephas (Peter), the Twelve, more than five hundred others, James, and finally Paul himself. The canonical Gospels describe women — in Mark, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome — finding Jesus's tomb empty, followed by appearances in Jerusalem and Galilee. The resurrection is commemorated at Easter, the central feast of the Christian year.

Theological significance

For Christians, the resurrection is the cornerstone of faith: Paul wrote that "if Christ has not been raised, our preaching is useless and so is your faith" (1 Corinthians 15:14). It is understood as God's vindication of Jesus, the defeat of death, and the guarantee of the future resurrection and eternal life of believers. Most Christian denominations confess it in the Nicene Creed.

Historical study

Historians debate how to explain the rise of resurrection belief — many accept that Jesus was crucified, that some followers soon had experiences they took to be appearances, and that the empty tomb tradition is early, while disagreeing about supernatural conclusions, which lie beyond historical method. In Islam, the Quran denies that Jesus was crucified or killed, teaching instead that he was raised to God; in Judaism the resurrection of Jesus is not accepted.
