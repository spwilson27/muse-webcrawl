Soil conditioner

A soil conditioner is a product which is added to soil to improve the soil's physical qualities, usually its fertility and sometimes its mechanics. In general usage, the term "soil conditioner" is often thought of as a category of soil amendments under which certain mulches fall.

Purpose

Soil conditioners improve soil structure — its aggregation, porosity, aeration, and water retention — rather than primarily supplying plant nutrients as fertilizers do. They can relieve compaction, improve drainage in heavy clay soils, increase moisture retention in sandy soils, and support the soil microbiota.

Types

  • Organic matter: compost, manure, peat, coir, and biosolids.
  • Mineral amendments: gypsum (for sodic soils), lime and sulfur for pH adjustment, perlite and vermiculite for aeration and retention, and sand for texture modification.
  • Biochar: charcoal-like material that can raise water-holding capacity and cation exchange capacity.
  • Synthetic polymers: superabsorbent polyacrylamides used in some agriculture and erosion control.

Application

Conditioners are typically incorporated into the topsoil before planting or applied as top dressing. Benefits accrue over repeated applications, since organic conditioners decompose and must be replenished to maintain soil organic matter.
