Sportswear


Technical fabrics dominate modern sportswear.

Sportswear, or activewear, is clothing designed for sports and physical exercise, including footwear. Typical garments include tracksuits, shorts, leggings, T-shirts, polo shirts, and specialist items for particular sports. In the United States, "sportswear" historically also denoted a category of casual separates for women; this article concerns athletic clothing.

Function

Sports clothing is engineered for comfort, safety, and performance: breathable and moisture-wicking fabrics move sweat away from the skin, stretch materials permit a full range of movement, and compression garments are claimed to support muscles and recovery. Sport-specific designs — padded, aerodynamic, thermal, or protective — address the demands of each activity.

Industry

Sportswear is a major global industry led by companies such as Nike and Adidas. Since the late twentieth century, athletic clothing has increasingly been worn as everyday fashion, a trend dubbed "athleisure", blurring the boundary between exercise gear and casual dress.

History

Dedicated sporting dress emerged in the nineteenth century with the codification of sports: flannels for cricket, knitted jerseys for football, and specialised boots and shoes. Synthetic fibres in the twentieth century — nylon, polyester, and elastane — transformed design and performance.
