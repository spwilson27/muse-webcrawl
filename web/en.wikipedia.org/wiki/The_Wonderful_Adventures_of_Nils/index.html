The Wonderful Adventures of Nils (Swedish: Nils Holgerssons underbara resa genom Sverige, "Nils Holgersson's Wonderful Journey Through Sweden") is a children's novel by Swedish author Selma Lagerlöf, published in two volumes in 1906 and 1907. Lagerlöf, who would become the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1909), wrote the book on commission from the Swedish National Teachers' Association as a geography reader for schoolchildren.

Plot

Nils Holgersson, a lazy and cruel fourteen-year-old farm boy in Skåne who delights in tormenting animals, is shrunk to thumb-size by an elf (tomte) he tries to trap. Able now to understand the speech of animals, he clings to the farm's white gander Mårten as the goose joins a flock of wild geese led by the old Akka of Kebnekaise on their spring migration north to Lapland. Over the journey across every province of Sweden — pursued by the fox Smirre and facing many dangers — Nils learns empathy, courage, and the geography, wildlife, folklore, and history of his country. Returning home in the autumn grown to full size and reformed, he must part from the geese, no longer able to understand them.

Legacy

The book became a classic of Swedish literature and has been translated worldwide. Nils and the geese appeared on the Swedish 20-kronor banknote (with Lagerlöf's portrait), and the story was adapted into a popular Japanese anime series, The Wonderful Adventures of Nils (1980–1981).
