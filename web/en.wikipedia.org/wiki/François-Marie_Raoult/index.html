François-Marie Raoult (10 May 1830 – 1 April 1901) was a French chemist best known for research on the behavior of solutions, particularly for formulating Raoult's law governing the vapor pressure of solutions.

Life

Raoult was born in Fournes, in the Nord department of France. He taught in secondary schools while pursuing his studies, earning his doctorate in Paris in 1863. In 1867 he joined the University of Grenoble, where he held the chair of chemistry from 1870 until his death.

Work

Raoult studied the freezing points and vapor pressures of solutions. In 1882 he published his finding that dissolving a substance in a solvent lowers the solvent's freezing point by an amount proportional to the amount dissolved — a general method (cryoscopy) for determining molecular weights. In 1887 he formulated Raoult's law: the partial vapor pressure of a component in an ideal solution equals the vapor pressure of the pure component multiplied by its mole fraction in the solution. His experimental work provided crucial support for the emerging theories of van 't Hoff and Arrhenius and helped establish physical chemistry as a discipline.

Honors

Raoult received the Davy Medal of the Royal Society in 1892 and was made a commander of the Legion of Honour. A type of laboratory condenser and the unit of measure in ebullioscopy and cryoscopy bear his name in the older literature.
