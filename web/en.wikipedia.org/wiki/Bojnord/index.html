Bojnord

Country: Iran
Province: North Khorasan
County: Bojnord
Elevation: approx. 1,070 m
Population (2016): approx. 199,000

Bojnord (Persian: بجنورد, also romanised as Bojnourd and Bojnord) is a city in northeastern Iran and the capital of North Khorasan Province. It lies in a valley surrounded by the Aladagh and Kopet Dag ranges, roughly 700 kilometres northeast of Tehran, near the border with Turkmenistan. The city is known for its ethnically diverse population of Kurds, Persians, Khorasani Turks, Turkmen and Tats.

History

The region around Bojnord has been inhabited since antiquity and was part of historical Khorasan. The modern town grew around the village of Charmaghan, and the present city is often said to have been founded, or refounded, in the Safavid period; some traditions attribute older origins to a settlement destroyed by the Mongol invasions. An earthquake devastated the older town in the late 19th century, after which it was rebuilt on roughly its present site. Bojnord became a provincial capital in 2004 when Khorasan Province was divided into three provinces.

Geography and climate

Bojnord sits at about 1,070 metres above sea level. It has a semi-arid continental climate with cold winters, warm summers and modest rainfall, much of it in spring. The surrounding mountains make the area a summer retreat, and nearby attractions include the Baba Aman park, a chain of springs and pools shaded by old plane trees, and the Besh Qardash springs.

Culture

The city is a centre of Kurdish culture in Khorasan, and Khorasani Kurdish music, particularly the dotar lute tradition, is prominent. Local handicrafts include Turkmen-style carpets and felt work. The population speaks Persian alongside Kurdish (Kurmanji), Khorasani Turkish and Turkmen.

Economy

Bojnord's economy is based on agriculture — including wheat, barley, sugar beet and orchards — food processing, and a petrochemical complex and power plant in its industrial zone. The city is served by Bojnord Airport and by road links to Mashhad and Gorgan.
