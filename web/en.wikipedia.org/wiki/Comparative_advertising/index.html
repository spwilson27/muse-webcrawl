Comparative advertising

Comparative advertising, or combative advertising, is an advertising strategy in which a product or service specifically mentions a competitor by name to show why the competitor is inferior in terms of price, quality, features, or other attributes — or why the advertiser's offering is superior.

Forms

Direct comparisons name the rival brand (e.g., the Pepsi Challenge, Mac vs. PC). Indirect comparisons refer to "the leading brand" or "Brand X" without naming. Negative comparative ads focus mainly on the competitor's flaws and are more common in political campaigning.

Legal treatment

United States

The Federal Trade Commission has since the 1970s encouraged truthful, substantiated comparative advertising as pro-competitive. False or misleading comparisons can be challenged under Section 43(a) of the Lanham Act and through industry self-regulation (NAD). See consumer protection.

European Union and United Kingdom

Comparative advertising was historically restricted in many European countries but was harmonised by EU directives (now Directive 2006/114/EC), permitting it if it is not misleading, compares goods meeting the same needs, and does not discredit trademarks or create confusion.

Effectiveness

Research finds comparative ads can increase attention and brand differentiation, especially for challenger brands and new entrants, but may also increase scepticism, source derogation, and retaliation by market leaders.
