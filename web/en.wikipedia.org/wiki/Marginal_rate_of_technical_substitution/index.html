Marginal rate of technical substitution

In microeconomic theory, the marginal rate of technical substitution (MRTS) — or technical rate of substitution (TRS) — is the rate at which a factor of production must decrease and another must increase in order for a producer to maintain the same level of output. It is the production-theory analogue of the marginal rate of substitution in consumer theory.

Definition

For two inputs, conventionally capital (K) and labour (L), the marginal rate of technical substitution of labour for capital is defined as the negative of the slope of an isoquant:

MRTSLK = − dK/dL = MPL / MPK

where MPL and MPK are the marginal products of labour and capital respectively. Moving along an isoquant, output is constant, so any increase in one input must be exactly offset by a decrease in the other; the ratio of marginal products gives the rate at which the producer can substitute one input for the other while holding output fixed.

Properties

Under the standard assumptions of a smooth, convex production technology, the MRTS diminishes as more of an input is substituted for the other — the principle of diminishing marginal rate of technical substitution. This diminishing MRTS is reflected geometrically in isoquants that are convex to the origin, and it arises from the diminishing marginal product of each factor.

At a cost-minimising input combination, a firm equates the MRTS with the ratio of input prices:

MRTSLK = w / r = MPL / MPK

where w is the wage rate and r is the rental rate of capital. This condition states that the last dollar spent on each input yields the same addition to output.

Special cases

Perfect substitutes

If the two inputs are perfect substitutes, the isoquants are straight lines and the MRTS is constant. Output depends only on a weighted sum of the inputs.

Fixed proportions

With a Leontief (fixed-proportions) production function, isoquants are L-shaped; the MRTS is zero along the horizontal segment, undefined at the corner, and infinite along the vertical segment — no substitution is possible.

Cobb–Douglas

For a Cobb–Douglas production function Q = A Kα Lβ, the MRTS is MRTSLK = (β/α)(K/L), which declines smoothly as L is substituted for K.
