Sophia Charlotte of Hanover (30 October 1668 – 1 February 1705) was the first Queen consort of Prussia as the wife of King Frederick I. She was the daughter of Ernst August, Elector of Hanover, and Sophia of Hanover, and thus a sister of King George I of Great Britain.

Life

Sophia Charlotte grew up at the cultured court of Hanover, where the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz was a family friend; the two maintained a lifelong correspondence, and she was known for her intelligence and intellectual curiosity. In 1684 she married Frederick, Electoral Prince of Brandenburg, who in 1701 crowned himself King in Prussia, making her its first queen.

Charlottenburg

Sophia Charlotte presided over a brilliant court at the palace of Lietzenburg near Berlin, which after her death was renamed Charlottenburg Palace in her honour; the surrounding town, now a district of Berlin, also bears her name. At Charlottenburg she hosted operas, masquerades, and philosophical discussions, and Leibniz's Theodicy grew partly out of conversations with her. She is also associated with the founding of the Prussian Academy of Sciences (1700), which Leibniz served as first president.

Death

Sophia Charlotte died of pneumonia on 1 February 1705 while visiting her mother in Hanover, aged only 36. Her only surviving son became King Frederick William I of Prussia, the "Soldier King".
