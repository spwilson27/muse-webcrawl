Shravanabelagola

Shravanabelagola (Śravaṇa Beḷagoḷa) is a town in Hassan district, Karnataka, India, and one of the most important pilgrimage centres of Jainism. The town lies between two granite hills, Chandragiri and Vindhyagiri, beside a large tank whose "white pond" (beḷagoḷa) gives the town its name.

The Gommateshwara statue

On Vindhyagiri stands a monolithic statue of the Jain figure Gommateshwara (Bahubali), about 17–18 m (57 ft) tall, carved from a single block of granite around 981 CE under the Western Ganga minister Chavundaraya. It is considered the world's largest monolithic free-standing statue of its kind. Every twelve years or so, hundreds of thousands of devotees gather for the Mahamastakabhisheka, the ceremonial anointing of the statue with milk, saffron, sandalwood paste and other substances poured from scaffolding above its head; the festival was notably held in 2006 and 2018.

History

Tradition holds that the Maurya emperor Chandragupta retired here in the 3rd century BCE with the Jain monk Bhadrabahu and fasted to death (sallekhana) on Chandragiri hill, which preserves early Jain basadis (temples) and memorials. Shravanabelagola also preserves hundreds of inscriptions in Kannada, Sanskrit and Tamil that are foundational for South Indian epigraphy.
