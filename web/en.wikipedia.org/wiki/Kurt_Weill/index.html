Kurt Weill

Kurt Julian Weill (2 March 1900 – 3 April 1950) was a German-born American composer active from the 1920s in his native country, and in his later years in the United States. He was a leading composer for the stage, best known for his fruitful collaborations with Bertolt Brecht, including The Threepenny Opera (1928).

Died: 3 April 1950, New York City, US
Occupation: Composer
Spouse: Lotte Lenya

Early life and German career

Born in Dessau, the son of a cantor, Weill studied composition with Ferruccio Busoni in Berlin. His early works included symphonies and chamber music, but he found his voice in theatre music. With Brecht he created The Threepenny Opera (1928), a modern adaptation of The Beggar's Opera whose "Mack the Knife" became famous worldwide, and Rise and Fall of the City of Mahagonny (1930).

Exile and American career

Weill, who was Jewish and whose work was denounced by the Nazis, fled Germany in 1933, living in Paris and London before settling in New York in 1935. On Broadway he composed Johnny Johnson, Knickerbocker Holiday (with "September Song"), Lady in the Dark, One Touch of Venus, Street Scene, Love Life and Lost in the Stars, working with writers including Maxwell Anderson, Moss Hart and Langston Hughes.

Legacy

Weill sought to bridge popular and classical idioms; his American operas such as Street Scene helped shape the modern musical. He died of a heart attack in New York in 1950.
