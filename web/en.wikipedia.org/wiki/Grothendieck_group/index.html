Grothendieck group

In mathematics, the Grothendieck group construction is a systematic way of constructing an abelian group from a commutative monoid, generalising the construction of the integers from the natural numbers. It is named after Alexander Grothendieck, who introduced it in the context of algebraic K-theory.

Construction

Given a commutative monoid (M, +), the Grothendieck group K(M) is formed from formal differences m − n of elements of M, subject to the equivalence relation (m, n) ~ (m′, n′) if and only if m + n′ + k = m′ + n + k for some k in M. Addition is defined componentwise, and the map M → K(M) sending m to the class of (m, 0) is a homomorphism. The group K(M) has the universal property that every homomorphism from M to an abelian group factors uniquely through it.

Examples

The Grothendieck group of the natural numbers under addition is the group of integers. In algebraic geometry and topology, applying the construction to the monoid of isomorphism classes of vector bundles over a space X gives the group K⁰(X), the zeroth group of topological K-theory. Similarly, the monoid of finitely generated projective modules over a ring yields K₀ of the ring.
