John Eliot (c. 1604 – 21 May 1690) was a Puritan missionary to the American Indians who was called "the apostle to the Indians" and the founder of Roxbury Latin School in the Massachusetts Bay Colony in 1645. He is best remembered for translating the entire Bible into the Massachusett language, published in 1663 as the first Bible printed in British North America.

Early life and ministry

Eliot was educated at Jesus College, Cambridge, and emigrated to Boston in 1631. He became teacher of the church at Roxbury, a post he held for nearly sixty years, alongside his missionary work.

Mission to the Indians

Beginning in 1646, Eliot preached to the Massachusett people in their own language, which he learned with the help of Native interpreters such as Cockenoe. Converts were gathered into "praying towns", of which Natick (founded 1651) was the first of fourteen. He trained Native teachers and ministers and wrote catechisms and other works in Massachusett, devising an orthography for the previously unwritten language.

The Eliot Bible

Supported by the English Corporation for the Propagation of the Gospel in New England, Eliot's translation of the New Testament appeared in 1661 and the complete "Eliot Indian Bible" (Mamusse Wunneetupanatamwe Up-Biblum God) in 1663 — the first Bible printed in the Americas. A second edition followed in 1685.

King Philip's War and later life

King Philip's War (1675–1676) devastated the praying towns; many Christian Indians were interned on Deer Island, where many died, and the mission never fully recovered. Eliot protested against the enslavement of Native captives and continued ministering until his death at Roxbury in 1690.
