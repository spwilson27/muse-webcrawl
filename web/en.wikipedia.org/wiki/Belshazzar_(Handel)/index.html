Belshazzar (Handel)

Belshazzar (Handel)
Composer: George Frideric Handel
Librettist: Charles Jennens
Premiere: 1745, London

Belshazzar, HWV 61, is an oratorio by George Frideric Handel in three acts, with a libretto by Charles Jennens based on the biblical story of Belshazzar's feast in the Book of Daniel.

Composition

Handel composed Belshazzar in 1744. It was first performed on 27 March 1745 at the King's Theatre in London during the Lenten season.

Plot

The work dramatises the fall of Babylon to Cyrus the Great, the feast at which Belshazzar sees writing on the wall, and Daniel's interpretation foretelling the king's downfall.

Music

The oratorio is noted for its characterisation, choruses representing Babylonians, Jews and Persians, and dramatic confrontations between Belshazzar, his mother Nitocris, Daniel and Cyrus.
