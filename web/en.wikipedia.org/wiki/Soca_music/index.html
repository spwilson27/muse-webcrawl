Soca music

Soca is the soundtrack of Trinidad Carnival
Stylistic originsCalypso, soul, funk, cadence-lypso, East Indian rhythms
Cultural originsEarly 1970s, Trinidad and Tobago
Typical instrumentsDrum kit, bass guitar, keyboards/synthesisers, brass, iron/steel percussion
SubgenresGroovy soca, power soca, rapso, chutney soca, bouyon soca, parang soca

Soca music is a genre of music originating in Trinidad and Tobago in the early 1970s, fusing calypso with soul, funk and East Indian rhythmic influences. The word "soca" was originally spelled "sokah", said to combine the first letters of "soul" and "kah" (from kaharwa, an Indian tabla rhythm) — often summarised as the "soul of calypso". Soca is the dominant music of Caribbean carnivals, built for dancing (or "wining") in street parades and fetes.

History

The genre is credited largely to Garfield Blackman, known as Lord Shorty (later Ras Shorty I), who in the early 1970s began accelerating calypso's tempo and adding Indian instruments and rhythms drawn from Trinidad's large Indo-Trinidadian community. His 1973 recordings and the 1974 album Endless Vibrations, with the track "Sokah", are widely regarded as the birth of the style. In the following years calypsonian Lord Kitchener dominated the road marches, while bands such as Byron Lee and the Dragonaires helped spread modern soca across the Caribbean.

Styles

Modern soca is broadly divided into groovy soca, a slower, melodic style suited to radio play, and power soca, a fast, high-energy style typically above 160 beats per minute designed for jumping and waving in carnival fetes. Related and fused forms include rapso (the oral-poetry style of Brother Resistance), chutney soca (fusing soca with Indo-Caribbean chutney), bouyon soca from Dominica, and parang soca performed at Christmas.

Carnival and the Road March

Soca releases are concentrated in the months before Trinidad Carnival, when artists compete for the Road March title — the song played most often as masquerade bands cross the judging stages. Leading soca performers include Machel Montano, often called the "King of Soca", Bunji Garlin, Fay-Ann Lyons, Destra Garcia, Alison Hinds of Barbados, and Kes the Band. Soca has spread through the Caribbean diaspora to carnival celebrations in Toronto, London (Notting Hill), New York (Brooklyn's Labor Day Parade) and Miami.
