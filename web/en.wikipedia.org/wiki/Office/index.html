Office

An office is a space where the employees of an organization perform administrative work in order to support and realize the various objects and goals of the organization. The word "office" may also denote a position within an organization with specific duties attached to it (see officer, office-holder, official); the latter is in fact an earlier usage, office as place originally referring to the location of one's duty. In the adjective form as office supplies, the term refers to the associated set of basic consumable items used in an office environment, including pens, paper, staplers and printers.

When used as an adjective, the term "office" may refer to business-related tasks. In legal writing, a company or organization has offices in any place where it has an official presence, even if that presence consists of a storage silo rather than a chair and desk. An office is also an architectural and design phenomenon: ranging from a small office bench in the corner of a small business of extremely small size (see small office/home office), through entire floors of buildings, up to and including massive buildings dedicated entirely to one company.

History

The structure and shape of the office is impacted by both management thought as well as construction materials and may vary based on the size of the company. Early offices resembled the palaces and temples of antiquity, where records were kept in designated rooms. Medieval chanceries and scriptoria served similar functions. With the rise of large, complex organizations such as the Royal Navy and the East India Company in the 18th century, purpose-built office spaces were constructed. The Old Admiralty (Ripley Building) in London, built in 1726, was one of the earliest purpose-built office buildings.

The Industrial Revolution intensified clerical work, and railways and telegraphs brought administrative functions together in dedicated buildings. The 20th century saw open-plan offices, the German Bürolandschaft (office landscaping) concept of the 1950s, cubicle farms following the Action Office design of the 1960s, and later the telecommuting and hot-desking movements enabled by personal computers and the internet.

Types of office space

Modern offices are commonly classified as private offices, shared offices, open-plan offices, cubicles, team spaces, and virtual offices. A home office is a space set aside in a person's residence for official business purposes, and became widespread with remote work. The primary purpose of an office building is to provide a workplace and working environment for administrative and managerial workers, supporting collaboration, communication and concentration.

Office buildings

While offices can be built in almost any location in almost any building, some modern requirements for offices make this more difficult, including requirements for light, networking and security. The main purpose of an office building is to provide a working environment for workers, and its form is strongly influenced by the need to maximize rentable floor area, leading to the familiar multi-storey blocks of central business districts.
