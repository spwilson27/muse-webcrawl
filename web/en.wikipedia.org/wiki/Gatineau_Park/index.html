Gatineau Park (French: Parc de la Gatineau) is a federal park administered by the National Capital Commission in the Gatineau Hills of Quebec, immediately northwest of Ottawa and Gatineau. Covering 361 square kilometres of Canadian Shield forest, lakes, and escarpment, it is the largest green space in Canada's National Capital Region and its most visited natural attraction.

Features

The park contains the Mackenzie King Estate, the summer retreat of Prime Minister William Lyon Mackenzie King, with its ruins and gardens; the Champlain Lookout atop the Eardley Escarpment overlooking the Ottawa River valley; Pink Lake, a rare meromictic lake; and Meech Lake, beside which the failed Meech Lake Accord was negotiated. It offers hundreds of kilometres of hiking and mountain-biking trails, cross-country ski trails in winter, beaches, campgrounds, and the Philippe Lake area.

History and conservation

Land acquisition began in 1938 amid concern over logging and development; the park expanded under federal planning for the capital region after the Second World War. Unlike Canada's national parks, Gatineau Park was created administratively rather than by statute, and a minority of its land remains privately owned, an ongoing source of debate. It is a refuge for species such as the peregrine falcon along the escarpment cliffs.
