Épée

The épée (French for "sword") is the largest and heaviest of the three weapons used in the sport of fencing, the others being the foil and the sabre. It developed from the duelling sword of the 19th century, and the entire body is a valid target area in épée bouts.

Description

A modern fencing épée has a stiff triangular or V-shaped blade about 90 cm long, a large bell guard, and a total weight under 770 grams for competition. Hits are registered electrically when the tip is depressed with a force of at least 750 grams. Unlike foil and sabre, épée has no right-of-way rules: if both fencers land within 1/25 of a second, both score, except that in the final point double hits do not count.

Technique

Because the whole body is target, épée fencing rewards patience, timing and counter-attacks, with frequent hits to the wrist, arm and foot. It is often considered the most tactically conservative of the three weapons.
