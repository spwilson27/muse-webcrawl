The M4 carbine is a 5.56×45mm NATO, gas-operated, magazine-fed carbine developed in the United States in the 1980s and 1990s. It is a shorter and lighter variant of the M16 rifle, sharing roughly 80 percent of its parts with the M16A2.

History

The M4 was adopted in 1994 and progressively replaced the M16 as the primary individual weapon of the U.S. Army and Marine Corps. It saw extensive service in the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, where its shorter barrel and collapsible stock made it handier in vehicles and urban terrain than the full-length rifle.

The M4A1 variant features a fully automatic fire control group and a heavier barrel, and became standard for U.S. forces. Rail-mounted accessories such as optical sights, lasers, and the M203 or M320 grenade launchers are commonly fitted under the Special Operations Peculiar Modification (SOPMOD) programme.

Design

The M4 has a 14.5-inch barrel with a distinctive step cut that accommodates the M203 grenade launcher. Its direct impingement gas system and rotating bolt follow the AR-15 pattern designed by Eugene Stoner. The weapon's effective range is about 500 metres against point targets.

Users

The M4 and its derivatives are used by military and law-enforcement forces in dozens of countries, and it is one of the most widely recognised modern carbines.
