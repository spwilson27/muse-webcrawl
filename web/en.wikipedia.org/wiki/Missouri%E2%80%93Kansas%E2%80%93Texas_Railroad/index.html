The Missouri–Kansas–Texas Railroad (reporting mark MKT), commonly known as the Katy, was a major American railway that operated in the central United States. Chartered in 1865 as part of the Union Pacific system, it was the first railroad to enter Texas from the north, crossing Indian Territory in 1870.

History

The Katy built and acquired lines linking Kansas City and St. Louis with Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio and Galveston, becoming a key carrier of Texas cattle, grain and cotton. Long controlled by interests associated with Jay Gould, the railroad went through several reorganisations. In 1988 it was merged into the Missouri Pacific Railroad, by then part of Union Pacific; the Katy's name survives in the Katy Trail State Park in Missouri and the Katy Trail in Dallas, both rail trails on former right-of-way.
