Regina Manifesto

The Regina Manifesto was the founding programme of the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF), Canada's democratic socialist party, adopted at the party's first national convention in Regina, Saskatchewan, in July 1933. Drafted largely by members of the League for Social Reconstruction — including Frank R. Scott, Frank Underhill and Eugene Forsey — in the depths of the Great Depression, it remains a landmark document in Canadian political history.

Contents

The manifesto called for the replacement of capitalism by a planned, socially owned economy. Its fourteen points included:

  • Public ownership of banks, railways, utilities and natural resources, and of key industries operated through public corporations.
  • A national labour code, social insurance, unemployment insurance, pensions and a comprehensive public health system.
  • Support for farmers through marketing boards and price stabilisation, and reform of the monetary system.
  • Constitutional reform, civil liberties, and a foreign policy of disarmament and international cooperation.

Its closing words declared that "no CCF Government will rest content until it has eradicated capitalism and put into operation the full programme of socialized planning."

Legacy

The Regina Manifesto defined the CCF through its breakthrough in Saskatchewan in 1944 under Tommy Douglas, whose government pioneered hospital insurance and medicare in Canada. As the party moderated, the manifesto was superseded by the Winnipeg Declaration of 1956, which accepted a mixed economy. When the CCF merged into the New Democratic Party in 1961, the manifesto's legacy of democratic socialism continued in attenuated form.
