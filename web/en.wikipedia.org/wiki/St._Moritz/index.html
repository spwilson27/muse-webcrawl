St. Moritz (German: Sankt Moritz; Romansh: San Murezzan) is a high Alpine resort town in the Engadin valley of the Swiss canton of Grisons. Situated at 1,822 metres above sea level on the shore of Lake St. Moritz, it is one of the world's most famous winter sports and luxury tourism destinations, celebrated for its sunny climate — averaging around 322 days of sunshine a year — and its mineral springs, known since the Bronze Age.

History

The springs of St. Moritz were a pilgrimage site in the Middle Ages; Paracelsus praised their waters in 1535. Winter tourism began in 1864 when hotelier Johannes Badrutt famously wagered with English summer guests that the Engadin winter was mild enough to enjoy outdoors, founding the resort's cold-season trade. The town hosted the Winter Olympic Games in 1928 and 1948, and has repeatedly hosted the FIS Alpine World Ski Championships, most recently in 2017. The Cresta Run skeleton track, built in 1884, and the natural-ice Olympic Bob Run remain unique institutions of the town.

Sport and events

Skiing areas including Corviglia, Corvatsch, and Diavolezza surround the town. On the frozen lake, winter events include the White Turf horse races, polo, and cricket on ice. The Engadin Skimarathon, founded in 1969, draws thousands of cross-country skiers each March. In summer the resort attracts hikers, mountain bikers, and sailors, and is a terminus of the Glacier Express and Bernina Express railway journeys.
