Consumer unit

A consumer unit is a type of distribution board used in the United Kingdom to distribute electricity within a building, particularly a home. It is the modern successor to the older fuse box, and contains a main switch together with circuit protection devices such as miniature circuit breakers (MCBs) and residual-current devices (RCDs) or combined RCBOs, each protecting an individual final circuit.

TypeDistribution board
Used inUnited Kingdom
ComponentsMain switch, MCBs, RCDs/RCBOs, busbar
Regulated byBS 7671 (IET Wiring Regulations)

Design

Modern consumer units are typically made of metal following Amendment 3 of the 17th Edition of the Wiring Regulations (2015), which required non-combustible enclosures for domestic units to reduce fire risk. Devices mount on a DIN rail. In a "split load" board, circuits are divided between two RCDs so that a single fault does not disconnect the whole installation; increasingly, individual RCBOs provide separate overcurrent and earth-leakage protection per circuit.

History

Earlier boards used rewirable or cartridge fuses in wooden or metal boxes. From the 1960s, MCB-equipped consumer units became standard, and RCD protection for socket outlets became required under successive editions of BS 7671. Since 2005, most domestic electrical work in England and Wales has been notifiable under Part P of the Building Regulations.
