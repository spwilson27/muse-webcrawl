Middle Bass Island

Middle Bass Island is an island in Lake Erie, in the U.S. state of Ohio, administered by Put-in-Bay Township, Ottawa County. It is located in the western basin of the lake, just north of South Bass Island (home to Put-in-Bay) and south of the Canadian border. The island covers about 805 acres and has a small year-round population that grows substantially in summer with vacationers.

History

Like the neighboring Bass Islands, Middle Bass was settled in the 19th century and became known for vineyards and wine production, taking advantage of the lake-moderated climate. The island was the site of the Lonz Winery, long a popular tourist destination housed in a castle-like winery complex built in the 1930s after earlier structures burned; winery operations have since closed and been revived in part. During Prohibition, the Lake Erie islands were centers of grape growing and rum-running.

Geography and access

Middle Bass Island State Park, established on the island's former marina and camping area, provides public access. The island is reached by ferry from Catawba Island and Port Clinton (Miller Ferry) and by inter-island ferry from Put-in-Bay; it has a small airstrip, Middle Bass Island Airport. Middle Bass Island lies within the Lake Erie Islands archipelago and is known for quiet, rural character compared with the livelier South Bass Island.
