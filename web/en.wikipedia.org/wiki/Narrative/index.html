Narrative

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A narrative, story, or tale is any account of a series of related events or experiences, whether non-fictional (as in a memoir, biography, news report, documentary, or travelogue) or fictional (as in a fairy tale, fable, legend, thriller, or novel). Narratives can be presented through written or spoken words, images, or a combination of media, and the study of narrative structures and modes of presentation is the subject of narratology.

The word derives from the Latin verb narrare, "to tell," and is related to gnarus, "knowing" or "skilled." Along with argumentation, description, and exposition, narrative is one of the four rhetorical modes of discourse. More narrowly defined, narrative is fiction writing characterized by a manner in which the narrator communicates directly with the reader.

Overview

A narrative is a sequence of events described in a particular order, typically involving a protagonist who faces some kind of conflict or change. Oral storytelling is the earliest method of sharing narratives, and stories have served as a principal means of transmitting cultural knowledge, values, and history across generations.

Semioticians distinguish between story (the content: a chain of events, characters, and settings) and discourse (the expression: how the content is communicated). Roland Barthes argued that narrative is international, transhistorical, and transcultural — "simply there, like life itself."

Narrative structure

Many analyses describe narrative structure in terms of exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution — a scheme associated with the German novelist Gustav Freytag's analysis of five-act drama, often called Freytag's pyramid. Other models include the three-act structure and the hero's journey identified by Joseph Campbell in The Hero with a Thousand Faces (1949).

Point of view

Narratives may be told in the first person, second person, or third person, and narrators may be reliable or unreliable. The Russian formalists and later theorists such as Gérard Genette developed detailed typologies of narrative voice, focalization, and time.
