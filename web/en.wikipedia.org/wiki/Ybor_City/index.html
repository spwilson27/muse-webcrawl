Ybor City (pronounced /ˈiːbɔːr/ EE-bor) is a historic neighbourhood in Tampa, Florida, United States, located just northeast of downtown. Founded in 1885 by Spanish cigar manufacturer Vicente Martinez Ybor, it was built up by thousands of immigrants, mainly from Cuba, Spain, and Italy, who worked in its cigar factories and made Tampa the "Cigar Capital of the World".

History

Ybor, a prominent manufacturer, moved his operations from Key West to escape labour unrest and high costs, purchasing land in 1885 and building a company town of factories and cottages (casitas) for workers. The first cigars were rolled in 1886, and other manufacturers followed. Ybor City was annexed by Tampa in 1887. Its immigrant community built distinctive mutual-aid social clubs — the Centro Español, Centro Asturiano, L'Unione Italiana, El Círculo Cubano, and La Unión Martí-Maceo — which provided medical care, libraries, theatres, and social life.

In the factories, lectores read newspapers and novels aloud to the rollers, sustaining a literate and politically engaged workforce. José Martí visited repeatedly in the 1890s, raising support for Cuban independence; the steps of the Ybor factory where he spoke are preserved in a small park maintained with the Cuban government's cooperation.

Decline and revival

The Great Depression, mechanisation, and the shift to cigarettes devastated the cigar industry, and urban renewal in the 1960s demolished much of the neighbourhood. From the 1980s onward, preservation efforts — including the Ybor City State Museum and designation as a National Historic Landmark District — revived the area as an arts, dining, and nightlife district centred on Seventh Avenue (La Séptima). Ferall chickens, descendants of residents' backyard flocks, roam protected under city ordinance.
