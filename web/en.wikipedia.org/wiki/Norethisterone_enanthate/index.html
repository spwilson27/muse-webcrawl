Norethisterone enanthate

Norethisterone enanthate (NETE), also known as norethindrone enanthate, is a progestin medication used as an injectable contraceptive for women. A long-acting ester of norethisterone, it is given by intramuscular injection — typically 200 mg every two months — and has been marketed under brand names such as Noristerat and Norigest.

Medical use

NETE was the first long-acting injectable progestin-only contraceptive, introduced in the 1970s and used mainly in parts of Europe, Latin America, Africa and Asia. It is highly effective, with failure rates below 1 per cent in the first year of typical-perfect use. Because it contains no estrogen, it can be used by women who cannot take combined contraceptives, though it commonly causes irregular bleeding, amenorrhea and weight gain, and return of fertility after discontinuation can be delayed.

Pharmacology

After injection in oil, the enanthate ester is slowly hydrolysed to norethisterone, which suppresses ovulation, thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium. A combined formulation with estradiol valerate (Mesigyna) provides monthly injections.
