Thayer School of Engineering

The Thayer School of Engineering at Dartmouth College is one of the oldest engineering schools in the United States and one of the four professional schools of Dartmouth. Founded in 1867 with a bequest from Brigadier General Sylvanus Thayer — the West Point superintendent known as the "Father of West Point" — it began instruction in 1871 and describes itself as the first graduate school of engineering in the country.

Programs

Thayer offers undergraduate Bachelor of Arts and accredited Bachelor of Engineering degrees, as well as Master of Engineering, Master of Engineering Management (offered jointly with the Tuck School of Business), Master of Science and Doctor of Philosophy programs. Undergraduates typically complete a liberal-arts AB first and then the BE, in a combined five-year course. The school's curriculum emphasizes project-based learning, interdisciplinary study and entrepreneurship, and it has historically enrolled among the highest proportions of women in engineering in the United States — in 2016 it reported the first majority-female graduating class of engineers at a national research university.

Research strengths include biomedical engineering, materials science, energy systems, cold-regions engineering (in association with the U.S. Army's Cold Regions Research and Engineering Laboratory in Hanover), and engineering management.

Facilities

The school is based in Hanover, New Hampshire. Its facilities expanded substantially with the opening of the Class of 1982 Engineering and Computer Science Center in 2022, which Thayer shares with Dartmouth's computer science department and the Arthur L. Irving Institute for Energy and Society.
