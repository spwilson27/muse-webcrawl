Mahabali

Mahabali (IAST: Mahābalī), also known as Bali, is a benevolent asura king in Hindu mythology, a descendant of the sage Kashyapa and grandson of the devotee Prahlada. Celebrated for his generosity, devotion and just rule, he conquered the three worlds before being humbled by Vishnu in the dwarf avatar Vamana. Mahabali is the central figure of the Kerala festival of Onam, which celebrates his mythical annual return to visit his people.

The Vamana legend

Having conquered heaven and earth through devotion and might, Bali performed a great sacrifice. The gods appealed to Vishnu, who was born as the Brahmin dwarf Vamana. At the sacrifice, Vamana asked Bali for just three paces of land. Despite the warnings of his guru Shukracharya, who recognised the dwarf's true identity, Bali granted the request. Vamana then grew to cosmic size (Trivikrama): with two strides he covered heaven and earth, and Bali, having nowhere left to offer, humbly offered his own head for the third step. Pleased by his devotion, Vishnu granted Bali rule of Sutala, a netherworld realm, and promised him the position of Indra in a future age.

Onam

In Kerala tradition, Mahabali's reign is remembered as a golden age of equality and prosperity, summarised in the saying that in his time "all men were equal". The harvest festival of Onam, Kerala's state festival, marks his yearly homecoming, welcomed with flower carpets (pookalam), feasts (sadya), boat races and the costumed figure of Maveli. The legend is also honoured elsewhere in India on Balipratipada, the fourth day of Diwali.
