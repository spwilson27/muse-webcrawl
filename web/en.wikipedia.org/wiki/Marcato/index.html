Marcato

Marcato (Italian for "marked", abbreviated marc.) is a musical instruction indicating that a note, chord or passage is to be played louder or more forcefully than the surrounding music. It is a form of accent, stronger in emphasis than the ordinary accent (>), and is commonly notated with an open horizontal wedge or caret-like symbol (^) placed above or below the affected note.

Usage

The marcato symbol is often described as a combination of the accent and staccato articulations: the note is both attacked strongly and given a degree of separation or shortened duration, though exact interpretation depends on the instrument and style. Orchestral string players execute a marcato with a firm, accented bow stroke; brass and wind players use a hard, "dah"-like tonguing. In piano music it calls for a decisive, weighted attack from the arm.

Composers from Beethoven onward used the written direction marcato or ben marcato ("well marked") to demand that a theme stand out clearly, for example in march-like or declamatory passages. In modern notation software the marcato is available as a distinct articulation, visually a vertical wedge rather than the horizontal accent.
