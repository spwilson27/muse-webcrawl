Wonders of the World

Various lists of the Wonders of the World have been compiled throughout history to catalogue the most spectacular constructions and natural things in the world. The best known is the list of Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, a guidebook for Hellenic travellers compiled in the second century BCE. Numerous successor lists — of the medieval world, the natural world, the underwater world, and the modern world — have followed the same pattern.

Only the Great Pyramid of Giza, the oldest of the seven, survives today. The list reflected the world known to Greek writers around the Mediterranean and Near East, and the number seven — held to be magical or complete — fixed the length of the catalogue.

Other lists

Medieval lists substituted wonders such as the Colosseum, the Great Wall of China, Hagia Sophia, the Leaning Tower of Pisa, and Stonehenge. In the modern era the American Society of Civil Engineers named seven engineering wonders including the Channel Tunnel, the Panama Canal, and the Empire State Building. Lists of natural wonders have included the Grand Canyon, the Great Barrier Reef, Mount Everest, and Victoria Falls.

New7Wonders

In 2007 the New7Wonders Foundation announced the results of a worldwide poll in which more than 100 million votes were cast, naming the Great Wall of China, Petra, Christ the Redeemer, Machu Picchu, Chichén Itzá, the Colosseum, and the Taj Mahal as the New Seven Wonders of the World; the Great Pyramid of Giza was given honorary status. A companion campaign in 2011 selected seven wonders of nature, among them the Amazon rainforest, Ha Long Bay, Iguazu Falls, and Table Mountain.
