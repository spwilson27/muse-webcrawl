Engineering design process

Typical stages of the engineering design process, with iteration loops

The engineering design process is a common series of steps that engineers follow in creating functional products and processes. It is an iterative method: designs are generated, tested against requirements, evaluated, and refined — often many times — rather than produced in a single linear pass.

Typical stages

While models vary, the process is usually described with the following stages:

  1. Define the problem: identify the need, the client or users, and the context.
  2. Research and specify: gather background information and write a design specification with criteria and constraints (cost, weight, safety, standards, environment).
  3. Generate concepts: brainstorm and develop several candidate solutions, often with sketches and morphological analysis.
  4. Select a concept: evaluate alternatives against the specification, using decision matrices or weighted criteria.
  5. Develop and model: carry out detailed design — calculations, computer-aided design (CAD), simulation and analysis.
  6. Prototype and test: build prototypes or models and test them against the requirements.
  7. Evaluate and iterate: compare results with the specification, identify failures and weaknesses, and redesign as needed.
  8. Communicate and manufacture: produce final documentation, drawings and manufacturing or implementation plans.

Characteristics

Design is distinguished from routine analysis by being open-ended: there is rarely a single correct answer, and engineers must balance competing objectives and make judgements under uncertainty. Formal frameworks include systems engineering's V-model, stage-gate processes in product development, and the Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle. In education (for example in STEM curricula and the Next Generation Science Standards), the process is taught as a cycle of asking, imagining, planning, creating and improving.
