Connecting flight

A connecting flight is an itinerary in which a passenger reaches their destination by taking two or more successive flights, changing aircraft at an intermediate airport (the connection or transfer point), as opposed to a direct or nonstop flight. Connections are the basis of the hub-and-spoke networks used by most large airlines.

Types

On an online connection both legs are operated by the same airline; on an interline connection different airlines operate the legs under agreements allowing through check-in of the passenger and baggage. A "direct flight" in airline terminology may keep a single flight number yet still land and sometimes change aircraft en route; a nonstop flight makes no intermediate landing.

Connection times

Airports and airlines publish a minimum connect time (MCT) for each transfer type, varying with whether the journey is domestic or international and whether the passenger must clear immigration, customs, or security screening. Passengers booked on a single ticket are normally protected: if a delay causes a missed connection, the airline must rebook them and, under regimes such as EU Regulation 261/2004 or carrier policies, provide care or compensation. On separate tickets no such protection applies.
