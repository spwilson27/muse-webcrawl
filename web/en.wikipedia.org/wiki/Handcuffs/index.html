Handcuffs
Type: Restraint device
Used by: Law enforcement, corrections
Mechanism: Ratchet with double lock

Handcuffs are restraint devices designed to secure an individual's wrists close together. A standard pair consists of two metal cuffs linked by a short chain or hinge, which close around the wrists with a ratchet mechanism and can be double-locked to prevent further tightening.

Design and use

Modern handcuffs descend from adjustable designs patented in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Police and correctional officers use them during arrest and transport. Rigid models give greater control; oversized and disposable plastic restraints (flex-cuffs) are also common. Improper or prolonged use can cause nerve injury ("handcuff neuropathy"), so guidelines require checking fit and double-locking.

Regulation

Handcuffs are standard, generally unregulated police equipment in most countries, though their use on children, pregnant people and vulnerable detainees is often restricted by policy.
