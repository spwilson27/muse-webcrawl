Pannalal Ghosh (31 July 1911 – 20 April 1960) was an Indian flute player and composer in the Hindustani classical tradition. A disciple of Allauddin Khan of Maihar, he is credited with transforming the bansuri from a folk instrument into a full-fledged vehicle for classical raga performance, and is regarded as the pioneer of Hindustani classical flute.

Life and career

Born in Barisal (now in Bangladesh), Ghosh showed early musical talent, first learning sitar before taking up the flute. He worked in Calcutta, providing music for theatre and early films with New Theatres, and travelled in Europe in 1938 with a dance troupe. In 1940 he moved to Bombay, where he composed music for films and became an established concert artist. From 1956 until his death he worked as a producer of music at All India Radio in Delhi. He died suddenly in 1960 at the age of 48.

Innovations

To render the slow, deep elaboration of ragas, Ghosh developed a much larger bansuri — over 80 cm (32 inches) long — with an additional seventh hole, allowing him to play in a lower octave and to reproduce vocal-style ornaments (meend and gamaka) characteristic of the Maihar style. He adapted compositions and techniques from vocal music and instruments such as the been and surbahar to the flute, and composed new ragas including Deepawali, Pushpachandrika, Hansanarayani, and Chandramauli.

Legacy

Ghosh's recordings set the standard for classical bansuri playing, and his innovations paved the way for later flautists, most famously his younger contemporary Hariprasad Chaurasia.
