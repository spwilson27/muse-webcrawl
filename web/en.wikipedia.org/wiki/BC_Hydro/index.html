The British Columbia Hydro and Power Authority, operating as BC Hydro, is a Canadian electric utility in the province of British Columbia. It is the main electricity distributor in the province, serving more than four million people and some 95 per cent of the provincial population. A Crown corporation of the provincial government, BC Hydro generates, transmits and distributes electricity, the overwhelming majority of it from large hydroelectric dams on the Columbia, Peace and other river systems.

History

BC Hydro's roots lie in the British Columbia Electric Railway Company, a private utility founded in the 1890s, and the publicly owned BC Power Commission created in 1945 to extend electrification to rural areas. In 1961, Premier W. A. C. Bennett's government nationalised BC Electric and merged it with the Power Commission to create BC Hydro, pursuing a "two rivers" policy of developing both the Columbia and Peace rivers. The Columbia River Treaty with the United States (1964) financed major dams including Mica and Revelstoke, while the W. A. C. Bennett Dam on the Peace created Williston Lake, one of the largest reservoirs in North America.

Operations

BC Hydro operates more than 30 hydroelectric facilities plus a small number of thermal plants, with installed capacity above 12,000 megawatts, and maintains roughly 18,000 km of transmission lines. Its trading subsidiary Powerex buys and sells electricity in western North American markets. Major recent projects include the controversial Site C dam on the Peace River, an 1,100-megawatt project approved in 2014 and completed in the mid-2020s. Because most generation is hydroelectric, BC Hydro's grid is among the lowest-carbon in North America, though drought years can force greater reliance on imports and gas-fired generation. Rates are regulated by the British Columbia Utilities Commission.
