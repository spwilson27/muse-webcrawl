2016 Turkish coup attempt
Date: 15–16 July 2016
Location: Turkey (mainly Ankara and Istanbul)
Outcome: Coup attempt failed
Deaths: ~300
Injured: ~2,100

On 15 July 2016, a faction within the Turkish Armed Forces, calling itself the Peace at Home Council, attempted a coup d'état against the government of Turkey and President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. The attempt failed within roughly a day.

Events

On the evening of 15 July, soldiers seized the Bosphorus bridges in Istanbul, tanks appeared in Ankara, and jets flew low over the capital. The plotters seized the state broadcaster TRT and forced an announcement that the military had taken power. Erdoğan, speaking to CNN Türk via FaceTime, called on citizens to take to the streets. Loyalist forces and large crowds resisted; the parliament building and police headquarters in Ankara were bombed by coup aircraft. By the morning of 16 July the coup had collapsed and participating soldiers surrendered.

Aftermath

Around 300 people were killed and over 2,000 injured. The Turkish government blamed the Gülen movement, which denied involvement. A state of emergency was declared and mass purges followed: tens of thousands of soldiers, judges, teachers and civil servants were dismissed or arrested, and many media outlets were closed. The events were followed by a 2017 constitutional referendum expanding presidential powers.
