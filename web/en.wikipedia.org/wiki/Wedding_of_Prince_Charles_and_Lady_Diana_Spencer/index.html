Wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer

The wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer took place on Wednesday, 29 July 1981, at St Paul's Cathedral in London, United Kingdom. The groom was the heir apparent to the British throne, and the bride was a member of the Spencer family. The ceremony was a traditional Church of England wedding service, presided over by the Archbishop of Canterbury, Robert Runcie.

Described as the "wedding of the century" and a real-life fairy tale, the event was watched by an estimated global television audience of 750 million people. Some 600,000 spectators lined the route from Buckingham Palace.

Engagement

Charles proposed on 3 February 1981 at Windsor Castle; the engagement was announced on 24 February, with Diana wearing a sapphire and diamond engagement ring that had belonged to the Queen Mother.

The ceremony

Diana arrived at St Paul's in the Glass Coach with her father, Earl Spencer. Her wedding dress, designed by David and Elizabeth Emanuel, was an ivory silk taffeta gown with a 25-foot (7.6 m) train — the longest in royal history at the time. Diana slightly stumbled over Charles's names during the vows, and Charles made a small error in his own, moments that drew wide comment.

Aftermath

The couple honeymooned aboard the royal yacht Britannia in the Mediterranean before staying at Balmoral. They had two sons, William (born 1982) and Harry (born 1984). The marriage broke down in the late 1980s; the couple separated in 1992 and divorced in 1996. Diana died in a car crash in Paris in August 1997.
