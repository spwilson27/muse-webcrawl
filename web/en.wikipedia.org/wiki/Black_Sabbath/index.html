Black Sabbath

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Black Sabbath in the early 1970s: four working-class Birmingham men in black, crosses around their necks]
Black Sabbath in the early 1970s: four working-class Birmingham men in black, crosses around their necks

Black Sabbath were an English rock band formed in Birmingham in 1968 by guitarist Tony Iommi, bassist Geezer Butler, singer Ozzy Osbourne and drummer Bill Ward. Widely regarded as the founders of heavy metal, their down-tuned, doom-laden sound — forged in factory grime and horror films — produced landmark albums including Paranoid (1970) and Master of Reality (1971), and influenced virtually every metal subgenre.

Formation and sound

Working-class Aston teenagers playing blues as Earth, the band renamed themselves after a Boris Karloff film (Black Sabbath, 1963) and leaned into occult dread audiences unexpectedly loved. Iommi, who lost two fingertips in a factory accident, down-tuned his guitar to loosen the strings — accidentally creating metal's sludgy, detuned weight. Butler's lyrics mixed horror, war protest ('War Pigs'), drugs and Catholic-tinged guilt.

Career

The debut album (recorded in a day, released Friday 13 February 1970) and Paranoid — its title track a three-minute afterthought that became their signature — made Sabbath arena stars in America despite critical hostility. Internal chaos and cocaine ended the classic line-up in 1979; Osbourne launched a huge solo career, Ronnie James Dio fronted a acclaimed reboot (Heaven and Hell, 1980), and the band rotated singers for decades. The original four reunited periodically, released the chart-topping 13 (2013) and played a farewell tour ending in Birmingham in 2017; a final hometown farewell concert was held in 2025.

Legacy

Every doom, stoner, thrash, sludge and black metal band descends from Sabbath's tritone and tempo; they were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006. Osbourne's death in July 2025, weeks after the farewell show, closed the founding chapter of heavy metal.
