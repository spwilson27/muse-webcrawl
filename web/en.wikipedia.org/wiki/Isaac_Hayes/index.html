Isaac Lee Hayes Jr. (August 20, 1942 – August 10, 2008) was an American singer, songwriter, composer, actor, and record producer. One of the creative forces behind the Southern soul music label Stax Records, Hayes helped define the sound of soul music and was a major influence on disco and hip-hop. He won an Academy Award for the "Theme from Shaft" (1971), becoming the first African American to win the Oscar for Best Original Song.

Early life

Hayes was born in Covington, Tennessee, to a sharecropping family. Raised in poverty, he taught himself piano, organ and saxophone and began singing in church. In the early 1960s he moved to Memphis and played in local bands before joining the Stax Records house band as a session musician.

Stax Records

At Stax, Hayes formed a legendary songwriting and production partnership with David Porter. Together they wrote hits for Sam & Dave, including "Hold On, I'm Comin'" (1966) and "Soul Man" (1967). Hayes' own recording career took off after an impromptu jam session produced his debut album Presenting Isaac Hayes (1968).

Solo career

Hayes' second album, Hot Buttered Soul (1969), was a landmark: its four long, orchestrated tracks — including an 18-minute version of "By the Time I Get to Phoenix" — redefined the soul album as an artistic statement and influenced generations of producers. His lush arrangements, spoken-word "raps" and deep baritone became his signature.

In 1971 Hayes composed and recorded the soundtrack to the blaxploitation film Shaft. The "Theme from Shaft", with its wah-wah guitar and hi-hat groove, reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 and won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and three Grammy Awards. Hayes became an icon of the era — shaved head, gold chains and sunglasses — and was crowned honorary king of a Ghanaian village after performing there in 1973.

Later career

After Stax's collapse in 1975, Hayes continued recording, with disco-era hits such as "I Can't Turn Around". He also acted, notably as the Duke in John Carpenter's Escape from New York (1981). From 1997 to 2006 he voiced Chef, the soul-singing school cook, on the animated series South Park, recording the novelty hit "Chocolate Salty Balls" (1998). A Scientologist, Hayes left the show in 2006 following an episode satirising Scientology.

Death and legacy

Hayes died on August 10, 2008, of a stroke, ten days before his 66th birthday. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002. His music has been sampled extensively in hip-hop, and his Stax songwriting is canonical in soul music.
