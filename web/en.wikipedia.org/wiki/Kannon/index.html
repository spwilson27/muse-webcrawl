Kannon

Kannon (観音, also Kanzeon) is the Japanese form of the bodhisattva of compassion known in Sanskrit as Avalokiteśvara and in Chinese as Guanyin. Kannon is one of the most widely venerated figures in Japanese Buddhism, embodying mercy and the vow to save all sentient beings from suffering.

Forms

Kannon appears in many forms, including Shō Kannon (the basic form), Jūichimen Kannon (eleven-headed), Senju Kannon (thousand-armed), Nyoirin Kannon, Jundei Kannon, Batō Kannon (horse-headed) and Fukūkenjaku Kannon. These forms are often grouped as the six or seven Kannon. Pilgrimage circuits such as the Saigoku Kannon Pilgrimage of thirty-three temples in the Kansai region are dedicated to Kannon worship.

Worship in Japan

Devotion to Kannon spread from the Nara period onward and became central to popular Buddhism. In the Edo period, Kannon pilgrimages flourished among commoners. During the era of anti-Christian persecution, hidden Christians sometimes venerated statues of "Maria Kannon", outwardly Buddhist images representing the Virgin Mary. Major temples dedicated to Kannon include Sensō-ji in Asakusa, Tokyo, and Hase-dera in Kamakura.
