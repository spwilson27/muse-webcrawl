Night-watchman state

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A night-watchman state is a form of government in which the state's functions are limited to protecting citizens from aggression, theft, breach of contract and fraud, through the military, police and courts. It is the model of the state in minarchist and many libertarian theories: government exists only as a “night watchman” guarding rights, providing no welfare, education, infrastructure or economic management.

The phrase translates the German Nachtwächterstaat, used mockingly by the socialist Ferdinand Lassalle in an 1862 speech to describe the liberal ideal he opposed; nineteenth-century liberals and later libertarians adopted the label. Robert Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia argues that exactly this minimal state — and no more — can be justified. Critics from the left argue that such a state cannot secure the conditions of real freedom or prevent private domination; anarchist critics argue that even the night-watchman monopolises force and tends to grow, so it is unstable in the other direction.
