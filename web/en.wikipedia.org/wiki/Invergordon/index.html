Invergordon

Invergordon is a port town on the Cromarty Firth in the Scottish Highlands.

Invergordon (Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Ghòrdain or An Rubha) is a town and port in Easter Ross, in Ross and Cromarty, Highland, Scotland. It lies on the north shore of the Cromarty Firth, one of the finest natural harbours in Europe.

History

The town was founded in the 18th century by Sir William Gordon and developed around its harbour. Invergordon became an important naval base during both world wars. In September 1931 it was the scene of the Invergordon Mutiny, when sailors of the Atlantic Fleet protested against pay cuts during the Great Depression — an event that shook the British government and contributed to Britain abandoning the gold standard.

In the 1970s an aluminium smelter was built at Invergordon, powered by the North Sea oil industry's growth, but it closed in 1981. The port remains important for cruise ships, oil rig maintenance, and the offshore renewables industry.

Transport

Invergordon railway station lies on the Far North Line between Inverness and Wick. The town is served by the A9 road.
