Fashion is a popular aesthetic expression at a certain time and in a certain context, especially in clothing, footwear, lifestyle, accessories, makeup, hairstyle and body proportions. Whereas a trend often connotes a very specific aesthetic expression, and often lasting shorter than a season, fashion is a distinctive and industry-supported expression traditionally tied to the fashion season and collections. Style is an expression that lasts over many seasons and is often connected to cultural movements and social markers, symbols, class and culture.

History

Distinctive changing dress became a feature of European court culture from the late Middle Ages; by the 17th century Paris set styles for Europe. The 19th century saw the rise of the couturier, with Charles Frederick Worth — generally considered the founder of haute couture — dressing Empress Eugénie and pioneering the fashion show and the designer label. Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel liberated women's dress in the 1910s–20s; Christian Dior's "New Look" of 1947 restored opulence after wartime austerity. Post-war decades brought ready-to-wear (prêt-à-porter), youth subcultures, and designer licensing. From the 1990s, globalised "fast fashion" brands such as Zara and H&M compressed design cycles to a few weeks.

The fashion system

Fashion operates through seasonal collections presented at fashion weeks in the "Big Four" capitals — Paris, Milan, London and New York (women's: February–March and September–October). Media — magazines such as Vogue (founded 1892), photographers, influencers and retailers — mediate between designers and consumers. Roland Barthes's The Fashion System (1967) analysed fashion writing as a semiotic code.

Social function

Theorists from Georg Simmel to Pierre Bourdieu have read fashion as a play of imitation and differentiation: elites adopt styles to signal status, lower strata imitate, and elites move on. Dress also expresses gender, occupation, religion, subculture and personal identity, and it is regulated in many societies by sumptuary norms and dress codes.

Sustainability and criticism

The fashion industry is among the world's most polluting: textile waste, microplastic fibres, water-intensive cotton and leather, and poor labour conditions in garment factories (highlighted by the 2013 Rana Plaza collapse in Bangladesh, which killed over 1,100 workers) have driven movements for slow fashion, circular design and supply-chain transparency.
