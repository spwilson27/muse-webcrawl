Ibn Battuta (Arabic: ابن بطوطة; born 24 February 1304, died 1368/1369), in full Abū ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh al-Lawāṭī al-Ṭanjī ibn Baṭṭūṭa, was a Maghrebi traveller, explorer, and scholar from Morocco. Over a period of thirty years from 1325 to 1354, he visited much of Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and the Iberian Peninsula. Near the end of his life, he dictated an account of his journeys, titled A Gift to Those Who Contemplate the Wonders of Cities and the Marvels of Travelling, commonly known as The Rihla ("The Travels").

Ibn Battuta is widely recognised as one of the greatest travellers of all time; the distance he covered, roughly 117,000 km (73,000 mi), surpasses that of his near-contemporary Marco Polo.

Early life

Ibn Battuta was born into a family of Muslim legal scholars in Tangier, in present-day Morocco, during the reign of the Marinid dynasty. As a young man he studied Islamic law (sharia) and in June 1325, at the age of 21, departed on the Hajj, the Muslim pilgrimage to Mecca.

Journeys

First Hajj and the Middle East

Travelling overland along the North African coast through the sultanates of Tlemcen and Tunis, he reached Alexandria and Cairo in Egypt, then continued through Syria, visiting Damascus, Aleppo, and Mosul, before reaching Mecca in 1326.

Persia, Iraq and East Africa

After the pilgrimage he travelled through Iraq and Persia, visiting Baghdad, Tabriz, and Shiraz, then sailed down the East African coast as far as Kilwa in present-day Tanzania, before returning to Mecca.

Anatolia, the steppe and India

He crossed Anatolia and the Black Sea to the lands of the Golden Horde, visited Constantinople, then crossed Central Asia and Afghanistan into India, where he was appointed a judge (qadi) by Sultan Muhammad bin Tughluq of Delhi and served for several years.

China, return, and later travels

Appointed ambassador to Yuan China, Ibn Battuta travelled via the Maldives, Ceylon, Bengal, and Southeast Asia, claiming to have reached Beijing. After returning to Morocco in 1349, he made further journeys to al-Andalus and across the Sahara to the Mali Empire, returning in 1354.

The Rihla

At the request of the Marinid sultan Abu Inan, Ibn Battuta dictated his travel account to the scholar Ibn Juzayy, producing the Rihla, one of the most famous travel books of the medieval world and a major source on the 14th-century Islamic world.

Legacy

The lunar crater Ibn Battuta, the Ibn Battuta Mall in Dubai, and Tangier's Ibn Battuta Airport are named after him.
