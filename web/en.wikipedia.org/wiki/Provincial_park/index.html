Provincial park

A provincial park (or territorial park) is a park in a province or territory of Canada. Provincial parks are administered by the governments of the provinces and territories, in contrast to national parks, which are managed by Parks Canada, a federal agency. The term is also used in South Africa for parks administered by provincial conservation authorities.

Canada

Canada's provincial parks number in the hundreds and protect a greater total area than the national parks system. Among the oldest and largest are Algonquin Provincial Park in Ontario (established 1893), Strathcona Provincial Park in British Columbia (1911), and Dinosaur Provincial Park in Alberta, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. British Columbia's system, BC Parks, manages over 1,000 protected areas covering about 14% of the province.

Classification

Provinces classify their parks variously as wilderness parks, natural environment parks, recreation parks, wildland parks and heritage rangelands, balancing conservation with recreation such as camping, hiking and boating. Hunting and resource extraction rules differ by class and province.

South Africa

In South Africa, provincial parks and nature reserves are managed by provincial bodies such as Ezemvelo KZN Wildlife and CapeNature, complementing the national parks administered by SANParks.
