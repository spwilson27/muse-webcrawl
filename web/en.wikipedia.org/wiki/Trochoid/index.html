Trochoid

A trochoid is a roulette curve formed by a point attached to a circle as that circle rolls along a straight line. The point may lie inside the circle (a curtate trochoid), on the circumference (in which case the curve is a cycloid), or outside the circle (a prolate trochoid, which loops back on itself).

Equations

For a circle of radius r rolling along the x-axis, with the tracing point at distance d from the centre, the curve is given parametrically by x = rθ − d sin θ and y = r − d cos θ. When d < r the result is a smooth wavy curtate trochoid; when d = r it is a cycloid with cusps; when d > r it is a prolate trochoid with loops.

Applications

Trochoidal curves occur in the design of gear tooth profiles, rotary (Wankel) engine housings — the epitrochoid, traced by a point on a circle rolling around another circle — and in describing the shape of water waves in fluid dynamics, where Gerstner waves follow trochoidal forms.
