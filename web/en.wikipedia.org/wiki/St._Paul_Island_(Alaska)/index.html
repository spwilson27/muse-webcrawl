St. Paul Island (Alaska)

St. Paul Island (Aleut: Tanax̂ Amix̂) is the largest of the Pribilof Islands, a group of four volcanic islands in the Bering Sea off the coast of Alaska. It lies about 480 km (300 mi) west of the Alaska mainland and 70 km (43 mi) north of St. George Island. The island covers about 100–110 km² (40 sq mi). Its only community is the city of St. Paul, with a population of roughly 400–480, making it one of the largest Aleut (Unangan) communities in the world.

History

Russian explorer Gavriil Pribylov reached the island in 1788, two years after discovering St. George, and named it for Saints Peter and Paul. The islands' enormous northern fur seal rookeries drew Russian fur traders, who settled Aleut hunters on St. Paul to conduct the harvest. After the United States purchased Alaska in 1867, the Pribilofs were administered directly by the federal government for over a century, with the Aleut community dependent on the government-managed seal harvest. During World War II, residents were evacuated to camps near Juneau. The commercial sealing era ended in the 1980s, and the community has since transitioned toward commercial fishing (especially snow crab and halibut), tourism, and support services for the Bering Sea fishing fleet.

Geography and wildlife

St. Paul is a low, rolling island of volcanic origin, with five volcanic cones, the highest being Rush Hill at about 203 m (665 ft). Its shores are famed for the largest northern fur seal rookeries in the world — roughly half the global population breeds in the Pribilofs. The island is also renowned among birdwatchers: more than 240 bird species have been recorded, including nesting tufted and horned puffins, parakeet auklets, and red-legged kittiwakes, plus rare vagrants blown from Asia. Wild reindeer, introduced in 1911, and arctic foxes roam the tundra. St. Paul is part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge, and its salt lagoon is a notable habitat for migratory birds.

Community

The city of St. Paul is served by an airport with flights to Anchorage, a harbor that supports a major crab processing plant, and the notable Saints Peter and Paul Russian Orthodox Church, a National Register-listed landmark. The Tribal Government of St. Paul Island (the Aleut Community of St. Paul Island) plays a major role in local governance and wildlife co-management.
