Insert (filmmaking)

In film and television, an insert is a shot of part of a scene as filmed from a different angle and/or focal length than the master shot. Inserts cover action already shown in an establishing shot, but from a closer vantage, emphasizing a detail — for example, a hand turning a key, a newspaper headline, or a clock face.

Purpose

Inserts direct the audience's attention to information important to the story, provide editors with cutting options to compress time or smooth over continuity problems, and add production value by enriching coverage. Because an insert usually excludes the actors' faces, it can often be filmed separately, sometimes with stand-in hands, after principal photography of the main scene.

Relation to other shots

An insert differs from a cutaway, which shows something outside the main scene, and from a reaction shot, which shows a character's face responding to events. A close-up of a detail within the scene is the typical insert.
