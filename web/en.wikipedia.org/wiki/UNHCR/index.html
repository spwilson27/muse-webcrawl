UNHCR

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), also known as the UN Refugee Agency, is a United Nations agency mandated to aid and protect refugees, forcibly displaced communities and stateless people, and to assist in their voluntary repatriation, local integration or resettlement to a third country. Created by the General Assembly in 1950 for an initial three-year term to help Europeans displaced by the Second World War, it has been renewed ever since and now operates in well over a hundred countries with a staff in the tens of thousands, overwhelmingly field-based.

Work

UNHCR's Statute ties it to the 1951 Refugee Convention system: it supervises states' application of the Convention, provides material protection and assistance (registration, camps and increasingly cash-based aid), negotiates access in conflicts, runs emergency responses, and publishes the standard statistics (its Global Trends reports count over 120 million forcibly displaced people in the mid-2020s, by its methodology, including internally displaced and asylum-seekers). Twice awarded the Nobel Peace Prize (1954, 1981), the agency is funded almost entirely by voluntary contributions — chiefly a handful of Western governments — leaving it chronically short of assessed needs and structurally exposed to donor politics, the standing tension of a protection agency dependent on the states whose policies create displacement.
