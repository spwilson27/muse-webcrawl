Exclusive or

Exclusive or, exclusive disjunction, or XOR is a logical operation that is true only when its operands differ: one is true and the other false. It is symbolized by ⊕, XOR, or EOR, and written in logic as p ⊕ q. Unlike inclusive or, XOR excludes the case where both operands are true.

Uses

Bitwise XOR is fundamental in computing: it is used in parity checks and error-correcting codes, in cryptography (the one-time pad combines plaintext with a key by XOR), in checksums and hash functions, and in graphics (XOR drawing modes). XOR is addition modulo 2, and a chain of XOR operations can swap two variables without a temporary. In digital circuits it is implemented by the XOR gate.

Properties

XOR is commutative and associative, with false as its identity element; every value is its own inverse under XOR. In set theory, the corresponding operation is the symmetric difference.
