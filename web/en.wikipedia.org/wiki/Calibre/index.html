Calibre

In guns, particularly firearms, calibre (or caliber in American English) is the specified nominal internal diameter of the gun barrel bore — regardless of how or where the bore is measured and regardless of whether the finished bore matches that specification. It is measured in inches or in millimetres.

Measurement

In the United States, calibre is often expressed in hundredths of an inch (e.g. .45 calibre), while most other countries use millimetres (e.g. 9 mm). For rifled barrels the diameter may be measured between the lands or between the grooves. Shotguns are traditionally sized by gauge, an indirect system based on the number of lead balls of bore diameter that make a pound — the exception being the .410 bore.

Cartridge naming

Cartridge names often incorporate the calibre along with other figures, such as case length (9×19 mm Parabellum) or the year of adoption (.30-06 Springfield). Nominal names do not always match the actual bullet diameter exactly, owing to historical conventions.

Other uses

The term is also used outside firearms, for example for the diameter of artillery shells, the width of watch movements, and — by analogy from the bore — in describing the diameter of tubes and fibres.
