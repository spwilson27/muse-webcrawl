Ultra HD Blu-ray

Ultra HD Blu-ray (commonly known as 4K Ultra HD, or 4K UHD) is a digital optical disc data storage format that is an enhanced variant of Blu-ray. Ultra HD Blu-ray discs are incompatible with existing standard Blu-ray players, though most Ultra HD Blu-ray players can also play standard Blu-ray discs, DVDs and CDs. The format supports 4K resolution (3840 × 2160) video at up to 60 frames per second, high dynamic range (HDR), and the wider Rec. 2020 colour gamut, using the HEVC (H.265) codec.

Released: 2016
Capacity: 66 GB (dual layer), 100 GB (triple layer)
Codec: HEVC/H.265
Resolution: 3840 × 2160 (4K UHD)
HDR: HDR10 (mandatory), Dolby Vision, HDR10+ (optional)

Specification

The Blu-ray Disc Association finalised the specification in 2015. Discs come in 66 GB (dual-layer) and 100 GB (triple-layer) forms, spinning at higher rates than Blu-ray, with video bitrates up to 128 Mbit/s. HDR10 is the mandatory base HDR format; Dolby Vision and HDR10+ are optional enhancement layers. Audio support includes object-based formats Dolby Atmos and DTS:X. Copy protection uses AACS 2.0/2.1.

Launch and market

The first players (from Samsung, Panasonic and Philips) and film titles arrived in early 2016. Studios including Warner Bros., Sony, Universal, Paramount, Disney and Lionsgate released catalogues of films, and game consoles PlayStation 5 (standard model) and Xbox One S/X and Series X incorporated UHD Blu-ray playback or drives. The format found a durable niche among home-cinema enthusiasts even as streaming became the mainstream route to 4K, prized for bitrates and audio quality exceeding those of streamed equivalents.
