Côte de Beaune

The Côte de Beaune is the southern half of the Côte d'Or wine region in Burgundy, France, stretching south from the town of Beaune. It is especially renowned for white wines from Chardonnay, as well as Pinot noir reds.

Geography

The escarpment runs roughly north–south with east- and south-east-facing limestone slopes giving drainage and ripening. Together with the northern Côte de Nuits it forms the Côte d'Or.

Wines

Villages such as Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet produce some of the world's most celebrated dry white wines, around the grand cru Montrachet. Red villages Pommard and Volnay are famed for contrasting styles — Pommard firmer, Volnay more delicate. Other appellations include Aloxe-Corton, Beaune, Saint-Aubin, Santenay and the Hautes-Côtes de Beaune.

Classification

Burgundy's system ranks vineyards as regional, village, premier cru and grand cru, reflecting terroir rather than producer. See Burgundy wine.
