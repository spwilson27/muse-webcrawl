Leonard H. "Bones" McCoy is a fictional character in the Star Trek franchise, portrayed by DeForest Kelley in the original series and the first six feature films, and by Karl Urban in the reboot ("Kelvin timeline") films beginning in 2009. McCoy serves as chief medical officer aboard the starship USS Enterprise under Captain James T. Kirk, and with Kirk and the Vulcan science officer Spock forms the central trio of the original series.

Character

McCoy is a passionate, irascible, and deeply humane physician from the American South (born in Georgia in 2227). His frequent arguments with the logical Spock—whom he calls a "green-blooded hobgoblin"—provide both comic relief and the philosophical heart of the series, with McCoy representing emotion and humanism against Spock's reason. The nickname "Bones" derives from "sawbones", an old slang term for a surgeon; the Kelvin timeline films re-interpret it as a joke about his divorce leaving him "nothing but my bones".

McCoy is credited with several of the franchise's signature phrases, including "He's dead, Jim", "I'm a doctor, not a …" (followed by some incongruous profession), and "Damn it, Jim!" He professes a distrust of transporters and other advanced technology despite depending on it.

Appearances and legacy

McCoy appeared throughout Star Trek: The Original Series (1966–1969), the animated series, and films from Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979) to Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country (1991). A 137-year-old Admiral McCoy made a cameo in "Encounter at Farpoint" (1987), the pilot of Star Trek: The Next Generation. Kelley, a former Western actor, regarded McCoy as the role of his career; the character has been widely cited as an inspiration by physicians and was ranked among the most beloved figures in science-fiction television.
