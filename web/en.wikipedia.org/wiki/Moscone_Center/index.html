The Moscone Center is the largest convention and exhibition complex in San Francisco, California. It comprises three main halls within the Yerba Buena development in the South of Market neighbourhood: Moscone North, Moscone South, and Moscone West. The complex is named after George Moscone, the mayor of San Francisco assassinated in November 1978 — see Moscone–Milk assassinations.

History

Moscone South opened in 1981 on the site of redevelopment that had been fiercely debated through the 1960s and 1970s; the underground exhibition hall was designed by Hellmuth, Obata + Kassabaum (HOK), with gardens and the Yerba Buena Center for the Arts above. Moscone North, also underground beneath Howard Street, opened in 1992, connected to the South building beneath the street. Moscone West, a freestanding three-storey hall at Fourth and Howard, opened in 2003. A major expansion completed in 2019 added contiguous space spanning the North and South buildings, topped by extensive solar arrays, and made the centre one of the most environmentally certified convention facilities in the United States.

Events

The centre hosts many of the technology industry's largest conferences. It was the long-time home of Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference and Macworld Expo, and hosts or has hosted Salesforce's Dreamforce, the Game Developers Conference, RSA Conference, Oracle OpenWorld, Google I/O (in some years), and numerous medical, scientific, and trade conventions. It also hosts political events and has served as a venue for presidential addresses and, in emergencies, as a shelter and vaccination site.
