John Arbuthnot Fisher, 1st Baron Fisher (25 January 1841 – 10 July 1920), commonly known as Jacky Fisher, was a British naval officer and reformer. As First Sea Lord from 1904 to 1910 he modernised the Royal Navy, scrapped hundreds of obsolete ships, championed oil fuel, submarines and torpedo craft, and introduced the revolutionary all-big-gun battleship HMS Dreadnought (1906) and the battlecruiser.

Early career

Born in Ceylon and fatherless at an early age, Fisher joined the navy at 13, served in the Crimean and Opium wars, and became a leading gunnery and torpedo specialist. He commanded HMS Inflexible at the bombardment of Alexandria (1882) and rose through commands including the Mediterranean Fleet.

First Sea Lord, 1904–1910

Fisher's reforms redistributed the fleet to home waters against the growing German navy, raised personnel standards, and drove construction of Dreadnought — built in a year and a day — which rendered all existing battleships obsolete and reset the naval arms race. His volcanic energy, press campaigns, and mottoes ("Hit first, hit hard, keep on hitting") made him both famous and divisive. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Fisher of Kilverstone in 1909 and retired in 1910.

Recall and resignation

Recalled as First Sea Lord in October 1914 after the outbreak of the First World War, Fisher worked furiously with First Lord Winston Churchill on shipbuilding, including the monitors and the projected Baltic landing plan. He resigned in May 1915 over the Gallipoli campaign, precipitating the fall of the Liberal government. He later chaired the Board of Invention and Research and died in London in 1920, receiving a state funeral at Westminster Abbey.
