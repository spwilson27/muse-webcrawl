Jordan's lemma

In complex analysis, Jordan's lemma is a result frequently used in conjunction with the residue theorem to evaluate contour integrals and improper integrals. It is named after the French mathematician Camille Jordan, who described it in his Cours d'analyse.

Statement

Consider a complex-valued, continuous function f defined on the semicircular contour of radius R in the upper half-plane, centred at the origin. If f is of the form f(z) = eiazg(z) with a > 0, and if g tends uniformly to zero on the arc as R → ∞, then the integral of f over the semicircular arc vanishes in the limit as R → ∞.

Application

Jordan's lemma allows integrals of the form ∫−∞∞ eiaxg(x) dx — which include many Fourier transform and oscillatory integrals — to be computed by closing the contour with a large semicircle in the half-plane where the exponential factor decays, and applying the residue theorem to the poles enclosed. The lemma is sharper than the standard estimation lemma because it exploits the decay of the exponential rather than merely bounding the integrand by its maximum modulus. A corresponding statement holds for the lower half-plane when a < 0.

The key step in the proof uses the inequality sin θ ≥ 2θ/π for θ in [0, π/2], which controls the integral of the exponential over the arc and yields the vanishing result even where the ordinary ML bound is insufficient.
