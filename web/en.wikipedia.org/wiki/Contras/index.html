Contras

The Contras (from Spanish la contrarrevolución, "the counter-revolution") were the various U.S.-backed armed groups that were active from 1979 to 1990 in opposition to the communist government of Nicaragua, the Junta of National Reconstruction and the Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN). Among the separate contra groups, the Nicaraguan Democratic Force (FDN) emerged as the largest by far.

History

After the Sandinistas overthrew the Somoza dictatorship in 1979, opponents of the new government — including former National Guardsmen, disaffected former Sandinistas and indigenous groups on the Atlantic coast — formed armed movements based mainly in Honduras. The United States, under President Ronald Reagan, authorized the Central Intelligence Agency to organize, fund, train and supply the contras as part of the Reagan Doctrine of supporting anti-communist insurgencies.

The ensuing war devastated Nicaragua's economy and society, with widespread atrocities documented against civilians by both contra forces and government troops. In 1984 the U.S. Congress passed the Boland Amendment prohibiting further military aid, prompting officials in the Reagan administration to seek covert funding, including through arms sales to Iran — the Iran–Contra affair, a major political scandal when revealed in 1986. The International Court of Justice ruled in 1986 (Nicaragua v. United States) that U.S. support for the contras and the mining of Nicaraguan harbours violated international law.

Demobilization

Following the Esquipulas peace process, a ceasefire was agreed in 1988. After the Sandinistas lost the February 1990 elections to Violeta Chamorro's UNO coalition, the contras demobilized under United Nations supervision, formally ending the conflict by mid-1990. Tens of thousands of Nicaraguans had died in the war.
