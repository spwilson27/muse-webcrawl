Yokohama (横浜市, Yokohama-shi) is the capital of Kanagawa Prefecture and, with a population of about 3.7 million, the largest incorporated city in Japan by population. Located on Tokyo Bay less than 30 kilometres southwest of central Tokyo, it forms part of the Greater Tokyo Area and is one of Japan's principal ports and commercial centers.

History

Yokohama was a small fishing village until 1859, when it was opened as a port to foreign trade following the Convention of Kanagawa and the Harris Treaty between Japan and the United States. A foreign settlement grew rapidly, and Yokohama became Japan's gateway for imports, exports, and new technology during the Meiji era; Japan's first railway, opened in 1872, linked the port to Tokyo. The city was devastated by the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923 and again by air raids in 1945, but rebuilt each time. After the Second World War it served as a major base and supply port for American forces.

Geography and economy

The city occupies the western shore of Tokyo Bay and comprises 18 wards. Its modern waterfront district, Minato Mirai 21, built on reclaimed land from the 1980s, contains the Landmark Tower — for a time Japan's tallest building — along with museums, convention facilities, and the historic red-brick warehouses. Yokohama's Chinatown is the largest in Japan. The port handles automobiles, containers, and cruise ships, and the city hosts the headquarters of companies in the automotive, electronics, and biotechnology sectors.

Culture and sport

Landmarks include the Sankeien garden, the Cup Noodles Museum, and Yamashita Park. Yokohama Stadium is home to the Yokohama DeNA BayStars baseball team, while the International Stadium Yokohama (Nissan Stadium) hosted the finals of the 2002 FIFA World Cup and the 2019 Rugby World Cup.
