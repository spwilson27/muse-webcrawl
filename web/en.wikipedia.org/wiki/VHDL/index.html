VHDL

VHDL (VHSIC Hardware Description Language) is a hardware description language that can model the behavior and structure of electronic systems at multiple levels of abstraction, from the system level down to logic gates. It is widely used in the design of digital circuits, particularly for field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) and application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs).

History

VHDL originated in the United States Department of Defense's Very High Speed Integrated Circuits (VHSIC) program in the early 1980s, to document the behaviour of ASICs supplied by contractors. It was standardised as IEEE 1076 in 1987 and has been revised several times since, most recently as IEEE 1076-2019.

Features

Unlike a conventional programming language, VHDL is inherently concurrent, reflecting the parallel nature of hardware. Designs are structured as entities with architectures, communicating through signals and processes. VHDL supports simulation for verification as well as synthesis of designs into physical hardware, and it has a strongly typed, Ada-like syntax.
