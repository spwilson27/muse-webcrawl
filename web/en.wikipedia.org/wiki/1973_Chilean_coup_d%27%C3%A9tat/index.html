1973 Chilean coup d'état

Date11 September 1973
LocationChile
ResultGovernment of Salvador Allende overthrown; military junta installed

The 1973 Chilean coup d'état was a military coup in Chile that deposed the Popular Unity government of President Salvador Allende. On 11 September 1973, after a period of social and political unrest, a junta led by General Augusto Pinochet seized power, bombing the presidential palace, La Moneda, in Santiago.

Background

Allende, a socialist, was elected in 1970 and pursued rapid nationalization of copper mines, banks, and industries, land reform, and price controls. His program polarized Chile, contributing to inflation, shortages, and strikes. The United States, under President Richard Nixon, worked to undermine his government economically and supported opposition groups.

The coup

On the morning of 11 September, the armed forces moved against the government. Hawker Hunter jets bombed La Moneda Palace. Allende refused to surrender and died in the palace that day. The junta dissolved Congress, banned political parties, and began a campaign of repression in which thousands were killed or disappeared and tens of thousands imprisoned and tortured.

Aftermath

Pinochet ruled Chile until 1990, presiding over a dictatorship marked by human rights abuses and free-market economic reforms. A 1988 plebiscite rejected his continued rule, leading to a transition to democracy.
