Ardabil Province

The Sheikh Safi al-Din shrine ensemble in Ardabil, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Ardabil Province (Persian: استان اردبیل) is one of the 31 provinces of Iran, located in the northwest of the country on the border with Azerbaijan. Its capital is the city of Ardabil. The province has an area of about 17,800 km² and a population of roughly 1.3 million, the majority of them Iranian Azerbaijanis speaking Azerbaijani Turkish. It was separated from East Azerbaijan Province in 1993.

Geography and climate

The province is mountainous, dominated by Mount Sabalan (4,811 m), an inactive stratovolcano with a permanent crater lake and popular hot springs at Sarein. The climate is cold in winter and mild in summer, making the province a summer retreat; agriculture, animal husbandry, carpets and honey are important to the economy.

History

Ardabil is historically significant as the home of Sheikh Safi al-Din, founder of the Safavid Sufi order from which the Safavid dynasty — which established Twelver Shi'a Islam as Iran's state religion — took its name. His shrine and khanegah ensemble in Ardabil is a masterpiece of Iranian architecture and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The famous Ardabil Carpet, made for the mosque of the shrine in the sixteenth century, is now divided between the Victoria and Albert Museum in London and the Los Angeles County Museum of Art.
