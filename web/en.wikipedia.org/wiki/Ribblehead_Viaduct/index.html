Ribblehead Viaduct

The Ribblehead Viaduct or Batty Moss Viaduct carries the Settle–Carlisle railway across Batty Moss in the Ribble Valley at Ribblehead, in the Yorkshire Dales, North Yorkshire, England. It is the longest and the third tallest structure on the line, and a Grade II* listed building and scheduled monument.

Construction

Designed by Midland Railway engineer John Sydney Crossley, the viaduct was built between 1870 and 1875. It is 440 yards (400 m) long, has 24 arches, and rises 104 feet (32 m) above the valley floor. Around 1,000 navvies worked on the structure, living in shanty towns around the site; more than 100 died from accidents and outbreaks of smallpox, and are commemorated in the nearby churchyard at Chapel-le-Dale.

History

The viaduct was at the centre of a proposed closure of the Settle–Carlisle line in the 1980s; claimed repair costs were controversial, and the line was reprieved in 1989 and the viaduct restored. Today it carries regular passenger services, freight trains, and steam-hauled excursions, and is one of the most photographed railway structures in Britain.
