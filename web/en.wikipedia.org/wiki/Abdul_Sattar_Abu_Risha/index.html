Abdul Sattar Abu Risha

Born1972, Ramadi, Iraq
Died13 September 2007 (aged 35), Ramadi, Iraq
NationalityIraqi
Known forLeader of the Anbar Awakening

Abdul Sattar Abu Risha (Arabic: عبد الستار أبو ريشة; 1972 – 13 September 2007) was an Iraqi Sunni tribal sheikh who led the Anbar Awakening, the alliance of tribes in Al Anbar Governorate that turned against al-Qaeda in Iraq and cooperated with United States forces during the Iraq War.

Background

Abu Risha belonged to the Dulaim tribe. His father and two brothers were killed by al-Qaeda in Iraq, which sought to dominate the smuggling routes and tribal life of Anbar. In September 2006 he helped found the Anbar Salvation Council, bringing together dozens of tribes against the insurgent group.

Anbar Awakening

Under Abu Risha's leadership, tribal fighters provided intelligence and recruits for Iraqi security forces, and violence in Anbar fell dramatically during 2007. The "Awakening" model was later extended to other provinces as the "Sons of Iraq" programme. Ten days before his death, Abu Risha met US President George W. Bush during Bush's visit to Al Asad Airbase.

Assassination

On 13 September 2007, Abu Risha was killed by a roadside bomb outside his home in Ramadi. Al-Qaeda in Iraq claimed responsibility. His brother, Ahmed Abu Risha, succeeded him as leader of the movement.
