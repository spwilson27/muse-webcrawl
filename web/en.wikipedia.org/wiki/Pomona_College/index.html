Pomona College is a private liberal arts college in Claremont, California. Founded in 1887 by a group of Congregationalists, it was the first of the seven institutions that now compose the Claremont Colleges, a consortium modeled on the collegiate structure of Oxford and Cambridge. Pomona is consistently ranked among the top liberal arts colleges in the United States and is known for highly selective admissions and a large endowment per student.

Academics

Pomona offers the Bachelor of Arts in about 48 majors across the arts, humanities, social sciences, and natural sciences, with a student–faculty ratio of roughly 8:1. Students may cross-register for courses across the Claremont Colleges consortium (Scripps, Claremont McKenna, Harvey Mudd, Pitzer, and the graduate institutions) and participate in study abroad through the college's extensive programs. The college requires study in a set of breadth areas and a first-year critical inquiry seminar.

History

The college was incorporated on October 14, 1887, and held its first classes in Pomona before moving to Claremont. It grew under presidents including James A. Blaisdell, who championed the consortium plan in the 1920s. Pomona's campus is noted for its Spanish Colonial Revival and collegiate architecture, gardens, and the Smith Campus Center and Bridges Auditorium.

Student life and athletics

Nearly all students live on campus. Pomona's athletic teams compete jointly with Pitzer College as the Pomona–Pitzer Sagehens in NCAA Division III. Traditions include the college's founding-date celebration and the number 47, an object of campus lore associated with a 1964 proof jokingly suggesting 47 is the quintessential random number.
