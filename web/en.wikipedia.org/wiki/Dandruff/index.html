Dandruff

Dandruff is a common scalp condition in which dead skin cells are shed from the scalp in visible white or yellowish flakes, often accompanied by mild itching. It affects roughly half of the adult population at some point and is considered a mild, non-inflammatory counterpart on the spectrum of seborrhoeic dermatitis.

Dandruff
SymptomsWhite/yellow scalp flakes, mild itching
Associated organismMalassezia yeasts (esp. M. globosa)
Risk factorsOily scalp, adolescence/young adulthood, infrequent washing
TreatmentAnti-dandruff shampoos (zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, salicylic acid)

Causes

Dandruff is linked to the scalp's response to Malassezia yeasts, which are normal skin residents. These fungi metabolise sebum and release oleic acid, to which susceptible individuals react with accelerated skin-cell turnover, producing clumps of cells that shed as flakes. Dry weather, irritant hair products and infrequent shampooing can aggravate the condition.

Management

Regular use of medicated shampoos containing antifungal agents (ketoconazole, zinc pyrithione, selenium sulfide) or keratolytics (salicylic acid, coal tar) controls most cases. More severe, inflamed presentations may indicate seborrhoeic dermatitis or psoriasis and warrant medical assessment.
