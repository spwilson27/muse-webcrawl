Ian Thorpe

Ian James Thorpe AM (born 13 October 1982) is an Australian former competitive swimmer who specialised in freestyle and is regarded as one of the greatest swimmers of all time. Nicknamed the "Thorpedo", he won five Olympic gold medals — the most won by any Australian at the time — including three at the Sydney 2000 Games. Thorpe won eleven World Championship gold medals and set 23 world records (including long-course records in the 200 m, 400 m, and 800 m freestyle) during a career that made him a national icon.

Early life

Thorpe was born in Sydney and grew up in Milperra. A large child — he eventually wore size 17 shoes — he overcame an allergy to chlorine to enter competitive swimming, initially following his older sister into the sport. At 14 he became the youngest male ever selected for the Australian national team, and at the 1998 World Championships in Perth, aged 15, he became the youngest male world champion in history by winning the 400 m freestyle.

Career

Thorpe dominated middle-distance freestyle around the turn of the century. At the 1998 Commonwealth Games in Kuala Lumpur he won four gold medals. The 1999 Pan Pacific Championships saw him break the 400 m freestyle world record, the first of many records. At the Sydney 2000 Olympics, amid enormous home expectations, he won gold in the 400 m freestyle in world record time, gold in the 4×100 m freestyle relay — where Australia famously upset the United States, with Thorpe swimming the anchor leg — and gold in the 4×200 m freestyle relay, plus silver in the 200 m freestyle behind Pieter van den Hoogenband.

At the 2001 World Championships in Fukuoka he won six gold medals, an unprecedented haul at the time. After changing coaches and a quieter 2002–2003, he regained form at Athens 2004, winning the "Race of the Century" — the 200 m freestyle final against van den Hoogenband and Michael Phelps — and retained his 400 m title, adding relay medals to take his Olympic tally to nine medals (five gold).

Retirement and comeback

Taking a break after Athens, Thorpe announced his retirement on 21 November 2006, aged 24, citing loss of motivation. In February 2011 he announced a comeback targeting the London 2012 Olympics, but he failed to qualify at the Australian trials in March 2012, ending the attempt. He later worked as a television commentator and in mental-health advocacy, speaking publicly about his experience of depression.

Personal life

Thorpe was appointed a Member of the Order of Australia (AM) for services to swimming and charity. In a July 2014 television interview he came out as gay, explaining that he had previously denied his sexuality publicly. He has been an ambassador for children's education charities in Australia and Japan and an advocate for Indigenous reconciliation.
