Appius Claudius Caecus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Via Appia, built under Appius Claudius Caecus

Appius Claudius Caecus (fl. c. 312–279 BC) was a Roman statesman of the patrician Claudian gens, famed as censor (312 BC) and twice consul (307 and 296 BC). The epithet Caecus ("the Blind") refers to his blindness in old age, which tradition said was divine punishment for his opposition to peace with Pyrrhus.

Achievements

As censor he built Rome's first great road, the Via Appia (312 BC) from Rome toward Capua, and its first major aqueduct, the Aqua Appia. He reformed the tribal rolls, enrolling the sons of freedmen in the Senate's electorate and distributing landless citizens among all tribes, measures that angered the aristocracy. He also published the calendar of legal court days and the forms of legal actions, and is credited with early Latin literary prose — including a famous speech against Pyrrhus quoted for centuries — and with orthographic reforms such as the distinction between R and S.
