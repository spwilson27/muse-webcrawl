Land yacht (automobile)

Land yacht

A 1970s American full-size sedan, archetypal "land yacht"

Land yacht is a slang term for an exceptionally large, luxurious automobile, typically a full-size American sedan or coupe produced from the 1950s through the 1970s. The expression plays on the size, smooth ride and opulent fittings of such cars, likening them to yachts sailing along the highway: long hoods, generous chrome, plush interiors, soft suspension and powerful V8 engines were defining traits.

Classic examples include the Cadillac Fleetwood and DeVille, the Lincoln Continental, the Imperial, the Buick Electra 225 — whose "225" badge advertised its length in inches — and the Oldsmobile Ninety-Eight. The largest land yachts exceeded 5.8 metres (19 ft) in length and weighed well over two tonnes.

Characteristics

Land yachts prioritised straight-line comfort and interior space over handling and fuel efficiency: wallowing ride quality, heavily assisted steering, front bench seats and lavish ornamentation were the norm. Their era peaked before the 1973 oil crisis and the 1975 introduction of U.S. Corporate Average Fuel Economy standards, after which Detroit's full-size cars were progressively "downsized" in the late 1970s.

Legacy

Although modern SUVs and luxury flagships can match or exceed their bulk, the term retains a nostalgic, slightly ironic flavour, evoking the chrome-laden flagships of post-war American motoring such as the 1959 Cadillac with its towering tailfins. Well-preserved land yachts are popular in the classic-car scene.
