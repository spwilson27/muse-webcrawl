Italian resistance movement

Period1943–1945
OpponentsNazi Germany; Italian Social Republic
Coordinating bodyCLN / CLNAI

The Italian resistance movement (Resistenza italiana) was the armed and civil resistance of Italians against Nazi German occupation and Mussolini's Italian Social Republic after the armistice of 8 September 1943, during the Italian Civil War phase of the Second World War.

Formation

After Germany occupied northern and central Italy and freed Mussolini, anti-fascist parties formed the Committee of National Liberation (CLN) in Rome and the CLNAI in Milan. Partisan brigades — communist Garibaldi brigades, Action Party Giustizia e Libertà formations, socialist Matteotti, Catholic and autonomous units — waged guerrilla war in mountains and cities, perhaps 250,000 strong by April 1945, aided by escaped Allied prisoners and SOE/OSS missions.

The war

Partisans liberated zones and short-lived partisan republics (e.g. Ossola, 1944), sabotaged transport, and fought alongside the advancing Allies, suffering brutal reprisals such as the Ardeatine Caves (Rome, 1944), Marzabotto and Sant'Anna di Stazzema massacres. Urban groups (GAP) carried out actions including the Via Raselli attack.

Liberation

On 25 April 1945 — now Liberation Day — the CLNAI called a general insurrection; partisans liberated Milan, Turin and Genoa before Allied arrival. Mussolini was captured and executed on 28 April. The Resistance underpinned the founding of the Italian Republic (1946) and its 1948 anti-fascist constitution, celebrated in the song Bella ciao.
