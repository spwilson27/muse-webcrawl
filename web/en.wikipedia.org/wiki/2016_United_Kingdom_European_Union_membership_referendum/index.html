2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum

The 2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum, commonly referred to as the EU referendum or the Brexit referendum, took place on 23 June 2016. Voters were asked: "Should the United Kingdom remain a member of the European Union or leave the European Union?" By 51.89% to 48.11%, the electorate voted to leave the European Union, on a turnout of 72.2%.

Campaign

Prime Minister David Cameron, who had promised the referendum in his 2013 Bloomberg speech and renegotiated the UK's membership terms in February 2016, campaigned for Remain, as did the official Britain Stronger in Europe campaign. Leave was championed by Vote Leave (associated with Boris Johnson and Michael Gove) and by UKIP's Leave.EU. The campaign was marked by disputes over immigration, sovereignty and the economy, and by the murder of MP Jo Cox a week before polling day. Leave's messaging featured the claim that the UK sent £350 million a week to the EU, displayed on its campaign bus.

London, Scotland and Northern Ireland voted to remain, while England outside London and Wales voted to leave.

Aftermath

Cameron resigned the next morning; Theresa May succeeded him in July. Sterling fell sharply and markets dropped worldwide. The government triggered Article 50 in March 2017, beginning a two-year negotiation process; after repeated parliamentary deadlocks, the UK finally left the EU on 31 January 2020 (Brexit), with a transition period ending on 31 December 2020. The referendum remains one of the most consequential votes in modern British history.
