No-hitter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Celebrations typically follow the final out of a no-hitter.

In baseball, a no-hitter (also known as a no-hit game and colloquially as a no-no) is a game in which a team was not able to record a single hit through at least nine innings. A pitcher who prevents the opposing team from achieving a hit is said to have "thrown a no-hitter". Basq runners may still reach base by walks, errors, hit batsmen, or other means; if the pitcher allows no runners at all, the game is the rarer perfect game.

Definition

Major League Baseball's official definition, formalised in 1991, requires a game of at least nine innings in which the opposing team records no hits. No-hitters thrown in shortened games, such as doubleheader seven-inning games, are no longer officially recognised, a change that removed several games previously counted, including some by famous pitchers. Combined no-hitters, in which two or more pitchers from the same team share the feat, are recognised.

Frequency and history

More than 320 no-hitters have been thrown in Major League Baseball history. The first recognised no-hitter was thrown by Joe Borden in 1875 (or George Bradley in 1876, in the National League's first season). No-hitters occur on average a few times per season, though the frequency has varied: nine were thrown in 2021, a single-season record.

Nolan Ryan holds the major league record with seven no-hitters, thrown between 1973 and 1991. Sandy Koufax threw four, including a perfect game in 1965. Only one pitcher, Johnny Vander Meer, has thrown no-hitters in consecutive starts (1938). Two no-hitters have been thrown in World Series play, most famously Don Larsen's perfect game in 1956; Roy Halladay threw a no-hitter in the 2010 National League Division Series.

Superstitions

A well-known baseball superstition holds that players and announcers should not mention a no-hitter in progress, for fear of jinxing it; teammates traditionally avoid sitting next to or speaking with the pitcher between innings while a no-hitter is intact.
