Formulary (pharmacy)

A formulary in pharmacy is a list of prescription drugs covered by a health plan, hospital, or health system, together with the conditions under which they will be supplied or reimbursed. Modern formularies are maintained by pharmacy and therapeutics committees that evaluate drugs for safety, efficacy, and cost-effectiveness. Historically, a formulary was a book of recipes and standards for preparing medicines — the sense preserved in national formularies such as the British National Formulary (BNF) and the United States Pharmacopeia–National Formulary (USP–NF).

Drug benefit formularies

In insurance-based systems, especially the United States, a formulary determines what a plan pays for. Drugs are usually arranged in tiers: generic drugs carry the lowest copayments, preferred brand-name drugs a higher tier, non-preferred brands higher still, and specialty drugs the highest, often with coinsurance rather than a flat copay. Drugs not on the formulary may not be covered at all, though prescribers can request exceptions or prior authorisation. Step therapy rules may require trying a cheaper formulary drug before a costlier alternative is covered.

Hospital formularies

Hospitals maintain closed or restricted formularies to standardise prescribing, control inventory, and promote cost-effective therapy. A pharmacy and therapeutics (P&T) committee reviews evidence and decides additions and deletions; therapeutic interchange policies may substitute an equivalent formulary drug for a non-formulary order.

National and reference formularies

Some countries publish authoritative prescribing references: the British National Formulary gives guidance on uses, doses, interactions, and cautions for medicines available in the UK; the WHO publishes a Model Formulary linked to its Model List of Essential Medicines. The World Health Organization's essential medicines concept has led many countries to maintain national formularies guiding public-sector procurement and reimbursement.
