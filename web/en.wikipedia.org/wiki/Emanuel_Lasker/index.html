Emanuel Lasker

Emanuel Lasker
Born: 24 December 1868, Berlinchen, Prussia
Died: 11 January 1941, New York City
Title: World Chess Champion 1894–1921
Other pursuits: mathematician, philosopher

Emanuel Lasker (1868–1941) was a German chess player, mathematician, and philosopher who was the second World Chess Champion, holding the title for 27 years — from 1894 to 1921 — the longest reign of any officially recognised champion.

Chess career

Lasker won the championship from Wilhelm Steinitz in 1894 and defended it against Marshall, Tarrasch, Janowski, and Schlechter, among others, while also winning elite tournaments including London 1899, St Petersburg 1914, and New York 1924, where he finished ahead of Capablanca. He finally lost the title to José Raúl Capablanca in 1921. Renowned for a psychological, pragmatic style, he often chose moves that made his opponents uncomfortable rather than those a computer would rate best.

Mathematics and philosophy

Lasker earned a doctorate in mathematics in 1902; the Lasker–Noether theorem on primary decomposition of ideals is named for him and Emmy Noether. He wrote books on philosophy and on games, co-invented the board game Lasca, and was a strong bridge player.

Later life

Fleeing Nazi Germany in the 1930s, he lived in the Soviet Union and then the United States, dying in New York in 1941.
