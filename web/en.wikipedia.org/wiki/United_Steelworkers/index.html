United Steelworkers

USW
Full name: United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union
Founded: 22 May 1942
Headquarters: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania

The United Steelworkers (USW), in full the United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union, is the largest industrial labor union in North America, representing workers in the United States, Canada, and the Caribbean across metals, mining, rubber, paper, oil refining, chemicals, and other industries.

History

The union was founded in Cleveland in 1942 by the Steel Workers Organizing Committee of the CIO, building on the organizing drives of the 1930s and on the older Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers, defeated in the Homestead strike of 1892. Philip Murray was its first president. The USW led a nationwide steel strike in 1959, the last industry-wide steel strike, lasting 116 days.

Mergers

Through mergers — with the United Rubber Workers (1995), the Aluminum, Brick and Glass Workers, and in 2005 the Paper, Allied-Industrial, Chemical and Energy Workers (PACE) — the union diversified far beyond steel, becoming the USW in its present form. It has a strategic alliance with the British union Unite and Mexico's Los Mineros.

Activities

Beyond collective bargaining, the USW is active in trade policy, workplace safety campaigns, and politics, and is affiliated with the AFL–CIO and the Canadian Labour Congress.
