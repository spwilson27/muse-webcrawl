El Alamein (Arabic: العلمين, lit. "the two flags") is a town in northern Egypt on the Mediterranean Sea in Matrouh Governorate, about 106 kilometres west of Alexandria and 240 kilometres northwest of Cairo.

World War II

El Alamein is famous as the site of two major battles in 1942 during the Western Desert campaign. The First Battle of El Alamein (July 1942) halted the Axis advance into Egypt. The Second Battle of El Alamein (23 October – 11 November 1942), in which the British Eighth Army under Bernard Montgomery defeated Erwin Rommel's Panzer Army Africa, was a turning point of the war in North Africa. Winston Churchill wrote: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat."

Memorials

The town hosts Commonwealth, German, and Italian war cemeteries and memorials and an El Alamein War Museum. Minefields from the battles long affected the region.

Modern development

Today El Alamein is a resort area, with marina developments, and nearby New Alamein City is a major Egyptian new-city project.
