Yasser Arafat

Yasser Arafat (Arabic: ياسر عرفات; born Mohammed Abdel Rahman Abdel Raouf al-Qudwa al-Husseini; 24 August 1929 – 11 November 2004), known by the kunya Abu Ammar, was a Palestinian political leader. He was chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) from 1969, the dominant faction Fatah from 1969, and the first president of the Palestinian National Authority from 1994 until his death. In 1994 he shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Shimon Peres and Yitzhak Rabin for the Oslo Accords, and he is regarded by most Palestinians as the national symbol of their liberation movement, while viewed by Israel and others for much of his life as a terrorist leader.

Early life and Fatah

Arafat was born in Cairo to a Palestinian family with roots in Gaza and Jerusalem. He studied civil engineering at the University of Cairo, became a student leader, and served briefly in the Egyptian army during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Working as an engineer in Kuwait in the late 1950s, he co-founded Fatah, a nationalist group committed to the liberation of Palestine by armed struggle, and published the magazine Our Palestine. After the 1967 Six-Day War, Fatah took over the PLO at the 1969 Cairo session, and Arafat became its chairman.

Leader of the PLO

Arafat led the PLO through its bases in Jordan — expelled in Black September (1970) — and Lebanon, where he built a quasi-state until the 1982 Israeli invasion forced the PLO's evacuation to Tunis. In 1974 he addressed the United Nations General Assembly wearing a pistol and famously declared he came bearing "an olive branch and a freedom fighter's gun". After the First Intifada (1987–1993), he negotiated the Oslo I Accord (1993) with Israel, recognising the state of Israel and renouncing terrorism in exchange for limited self-rule in the West Bank and Gaza.

President of the Palestinian Authority

Returning to Gaza in 1994, Arafat was elected president of the Palestinian National Authority in 1996. The peace process culminated and collapsed at the 2000 Camp David Summit; the Second Intifada that followed destroyed much of the Oslo framework. Israel confined Arafat to his Ramallah compound (Mukataa) from 2002, accusing him of orchestrating violence. He fell ill in October 2004, was flown to a military hospital near Paris, and died on 11 November; the cause of death remains disputed, with French medical records pointing to a massive haemorrhagic stroke following an unidentified infection. He is buried in Ramallah.
