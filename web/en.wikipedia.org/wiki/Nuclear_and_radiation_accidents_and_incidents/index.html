Nuclear and radiation accidents and incidents

A nuclear and radiation accident is defined by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) as "an event that has led to significant consequences to people, the environment or the facility", while an incident is an event with less severe consequences. Examples of accidents include lethal effects to individuals, large radioactivity releases to the environment, and reactor core melt. The severity of nuclear events is classified using the International Nuclear and Radiological Event Scale (INES), which runs from level 1 (anomaly) to level 7 (major accident).

Major accidents

The two accidents rated at INES level 7 are the Chernobyl disaster of 1986 in the Ukrainian SSR and the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster of 2011 in Japan. The Kyshtym disaster of 1957 in the Soviet Union and the Windscale fire of 1957 in the United Kingdom were rated at levels 6 and 5 respectively. The Three Mile Island accident of 1979 in the United States was rated level 5.

Radiation incidents

Radiation incidents have also occurred outside the nuclear power industry, including accidents involving orphan sources, medical radiotherapy overdoses, and lost radioactive material. Notable examples include the Goiânia accident in Brazil in 1987, in which a discarded caesium-137 source from a radiotherapy clinic was broken open, killing four people, and the Ciudad Juárez cobalt-60 contamination incident in Mexico in 1983.
