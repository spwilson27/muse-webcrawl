Arch Colson Chipp Whitehead (born November 6, 1969) is an American novelist. He won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction twice — for The Underground Railroad (2016) and The Nickel Boys (2019) — becoming only the fourth writer to win the prize twice for fiction.

Early life and career

Whitehead was born in New York City and grew up in Manhattan. He graduated from Harvard University in 1991 and worked as a television and pop culture critic for The Village Voice. His debut novel, The Intuitionist (1999), a noir about elevator inspectors, won acclaim; John Henry Days (2001) was a finalist for the Pulitzer Prize. His other novels include the zombie novel Zone One (2011) and the coming-of-age Sag Harbor (2009).

Major works

The Underground Railroad reimagines the network of safe houses as an actual subterranean railway; it won the National Book Award and the Pulitzer Prize and was adapted into a 2021 limited series directed by Barry Jenkins. The Nickel Boys, based on the abuses at Florida's Dozier School for Boys, won the Pulitzer in 2020. Whitehead then published a Harlem crime trilogy: Harlem Shuffle (2021), Crook Manifesto (2023), and Cool Machine (2025).
