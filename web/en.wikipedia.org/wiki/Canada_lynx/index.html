Canada lynx

Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis)
Family: Felidae
Range: boreal forests of Canada, Alaska, northern US
Diet: mainly snowshoe hares
Status: Least Concern (IUCN); threatened in contiguous US

The Canada lynx (Lynx canadensis) is a medium-sized wild cat of the boreal forests of North America, ranging across Canada and Alaska with smaller populations in the northern contiguous United States. It is recognised by its long ear tufts, black-tipped short tail, large padded paws that act like snowshoes, and dense silvery-brown winter fur.

Ecology

The Canada lynx is a specialist predator of the snowshoe hare; lynx populations rise and fall in a famous roughly ten-year cycle tracking hare abundance, a classic of population ecology documented in Hudson's Bay Company fur records. In lean years lynx take other prey or disperse long distances.

Conservation

While abundant in Canada and Alaska, the species is listed as threatened under the US Endangered Species Act in the lower 48 states, where habitat fragmentation, roads, and competition with bobcats and coyotes are concerns. Canada lynx are still legally trapped for fur in much of Canada and Alaska.
