My Beautiful Laundrette

Directed by: Stephen Frears

Written by: Hanif Kureishi

Starring: Gordon Warnecke, Daniel Day-Lewis, Saeed Jaffrey, Roshan Seth

Released: 1985

The laundrette "Powders" in South London.

My Beautiful Laundrette is a 1985 British comedy-drama film directed by Stephen Frears from a screenplay by Hanif Kureishi. Set in South London during the Thatcher era, it follows Omar, a young British Pakistani man, who is given charge of a run-down laundrette by his wealthy uncle Nasser and transforms it with his lover and former school friend Johnny, a white ex-skinhead — a relationship that cuts across lines of race, class and sexuality in 1980s Britain.

Plot

Omar lives with his father Hussein, a disillusioned former journalist and socialist who has fallen into alcoholism, in contrast to his brother Nasser, a prosperous and ruthless businessman. Nasser gives Omar the failing laundrette "Powders" to manage. Omar rekindles his relationship with Johnny, once part of a racist gang, and the two refurbish the shop into a glittering success, funding it partly by stealing and selling drugs from Nasser's associate Salim. Tensions between family loyalty, ambition and community hostility build to a violent confrontation outside the laundrette, after which Omar and Johnny remain together.

Reception and legacy

Made for Channel 4's Film on Four strand on 16mm with a modest budget, the film was a surprise hit at festivals and in cinemas, and Kureishi's screenplay was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. It was Daniel Day-Lewis's breakthrough film role. The film is widely regarded as a landmark of 1980s British cinema for its frank treatment of race, sexuality and Thatcherite enterprise culture, and it appears in the BFI's list of the 100 greatest British films of the 20th century.
