Horace (play)

Horace
Written by: Pierre Corneille
First performed: 1640
Genre: Tragedy (five acts, in verse)
Setting: Early Rome, reign of Tullus Hostilius

Horace is a five-act tragedy in verse by the French dramatist Pierre Corneille, first performed in 1640 and dedicated to Cardinal Richelieu. One of the great works of French classical theatre, it dramatizes the Roman legend of the combat between the Horatii and the Curiatii, drawn from Livy's history of Rome.

Plot

Rome and Alba Longa, instead of waging full war, agree that the conflict will be decided by combat between three brothers from each city: the Horatii for Rome and the Curiatii for Alba. The families are bound by marriage — Horace is married to Sabine, sister of the Curiatii, while his sister Camille is betrothed to one of the Curiatii. Horace alone survives the combat, killing all three opponents after feigning flight. On his return, Camille curses Rome for the death of her fiancé, and Horace kills her in a rage. His trial forms the final act: defended by his father and by the claims of his service to Rome, he is acquitted, "Rome" — in the king's judgment — owing him more than his crime demands.

Themes and reception

The play explores the conflict between private affection and patriotic duty, a signature Corneillian dilemma. Contemporary critics, including the Academy under Richelieu, debated whether Camille's killing and Horace's acquittal violated dramatic propriety, and Corneille revised the ending in later editions. Horace remains a cornerstone of the French classical repertoire.
