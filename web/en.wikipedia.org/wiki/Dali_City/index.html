Dali City

Country: China
Province: Yunnan
Prefecture: Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture
Area: about 1,815 km²
Population: roughly 770,000 (2020)

Dali City (Chinese: 大理市) is a county-level city in, and the seat of, the Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture in northwestern Yunnan province, China. It lies on the western shore of Erhai Lake, beneath the Cangshan mountain range, and is one of China's most popular domestic tourist destinations, famed for its well-preserved old town and Bai culture.

History

The area was the centre of the Dali Kingdom (937–1253), which succeeded the Nanzhao kingdom before falling to Mongol forces under Kublai Khan. The old town of Dali, with its Ming-dynasty city walls and gates, sits a few kilometres from the modern administrative centre at Xiaguan.

Landmarks

The city's emblem is the Three Pagodas of Chongsheng Temple, built between the 9th and 10th centuries. Other draws include the old town's Foreigner Street, Erhai Lake cruises, Cangshan's cable cars and the region's tie-dye (zharan) workshops at Zhoucheng, produced by the Bai people.
