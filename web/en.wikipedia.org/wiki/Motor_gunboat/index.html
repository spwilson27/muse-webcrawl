Motor gunboat

A Fairmile D motor gunboat of the Royal Navy

Motor gunboats (MGBs) were small, fast naval vessels used by the Royal Navy during the Second World War. Closely related to motor torpedo boats (MTBs), they differed in carrying gun armament rather than torpedoes, and were intended to fight enemy fast attack craft, particularly German E-boats, in coastal waters.

Design and service

Motor gunboats were operated by the Royal Navy's Coastal Forces. Early examples were converted motor anti-submarine boats and short hulls of about 63–70 feet, armed with a mixture of 2-pounder pom-poms, 20 mm Oerlikon cannon and machine guns. The later Fairmile D type, a much larger boat of 115 feet, carried a heavy gun armament including a 6-pounder gun and could combine the roles of MGB and MTB. MGBs operated from bases along the English coast, patrolling the Channel and North Sea, escorting coastal convoys, and engaging E-boats attacking shipping. Commanders such as Robert Hichens, who was killed in action in 1943, became well known for aggressive night actions.

Legacy

After the war the MGB designation was dropped as surviving boats were reclassified, and the concept merged into the post-war fast patrol boat.
