Moria (video game)

Moria is a roguelike computer game first developed in 1983 by Robert Alan Koeneke at the University of Oklahoma. Based largely on J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings and named for the Mines of Moria, the game's goal is to descend to the lowest level of the mines and kill the Balrog. Moria is one of the first roguelikes, following Rogue, and the direct predecessor of Angband.

Gameplay

Moria begins with the player creating a character from a selection of races (Human, Elf, Half-Elf, Dwarf, Hobbit, Gnome, Half-Orc, Half-Troll) and classes (Warrior, Mage, Priest, Rogue, Ranger, Paladin). The player starts in a town above the mines, where supplies can be bought, then descends into procedurally generated, persistent dungeon levels. Experience, combat, spellcasting, traps, hunger and a huge variety of monsters and treasure feature prominently; death is permanent (permadeath).

Development and legacy

Koeneke wrote Moria in VAX Pascal on a VAX-11/780, frustrated that Rogue was unavailable on that system. After releasing the source code in 1987, it was ported to many platforms, notably as UMoria for Unix and DOS by Jim E. Wilson. Moria introduced or popularised many conventions later standard in roguelikes, including a town level with shops and increasingly ambitious monster design. Its open-source descendant Angband (1990) became even more influential, spawning numerous variants.
