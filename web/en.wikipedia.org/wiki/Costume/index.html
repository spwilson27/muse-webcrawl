Costume

Costume is the distinctive style of dress of a particular people, class, period or character. The word is used especially for clothing worn by actors and performers, for festive and ritual dress, and historically — as in French, where costume simply means a suit — for the characteristic dress of a group or era, as in "national costume".

Theatrical and film costume

In theatre, film and television, costumes are designed by a costume designer to express character, period and mood, and to function under the practical demands of performance — quick changes, movement, lighting and camera. Costumes may be built in a workroom, adapted from stock, rented or purchased. The Academy Award for Best Costume Design has recognised film costume since 1948; noted designers include Edith Head, Milena Canonero and Colleen Atwood.

Festive and ritual costume

Costumes mark festivals and rites of passage worldwide: carnival dress, Halloween costumes, mumming and morris dancing outfits, wedding dress, religious vestments, and the traditional folk costumes (such as the German Tracht or Norwegian bunad) worn at national celebrations. Cosplay — costume play originating in Japanese fan culture — involves dressing as characters from anime, games and other media.

History of the term

English borrowed "costume" from French in the 18th century, initially for the correct depiction of period dress in painting. Costume history as a scholarly field studies surviving garments, images and texts to reconstruct how people dressed.
