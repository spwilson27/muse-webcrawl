Nineteen Eighty-Four

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A huge telescreen poster: BIG BROTHER IS WATCHING YOU, over a drab London of victory gin and rationed chocolate

Nineteen Eighty-Four (also published as 1984) is a dystopian novel and cautionary tale by the English author George Orwell. It was published on 8 June 1949 as Orwell's ninth and final book completed in his lifetime. Thematically, it centres on the consequences of totalitarianism, mass surveillance and repressive regimentation of people and behaviours within society.

In the superstate of Oceania, the Party, led by the possibly fictional Big Brother, rules through the Thought Police, perpetual war, the rewriting of history and the controlled language Newspeak. Winston Smith, a records clerk who falsifies the past, drifts into forbidden love and dissent and is destroyed in Room 101. Written in 1948 on Jura as Orwell was dying of tuberculosis, the novel contributed 'Orwellian', 'Big Brother', 'thoughtcrime' and 'doublethink' to world language.
