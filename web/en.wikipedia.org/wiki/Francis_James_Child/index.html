Francis James Child (February 1, 1825 – September 11, 1896) was an American scholar, educator, and folklorist, best known today for his collection of folk songs known as the Child Ballads. He was a long-serving professor at Harvard University, where he held the Boylston Professorship of Rhetoric and Oratory.

Life

Child was born in Boston, Massachusetts, the son of a sailmaker. He attended the Boston Latin School and Harvard, graduating in 1846 as the top scholar of his class. After tutoring at Harvard, he studied in Germany, at Berlin and Göttingen, where he developed his interest in English literature and comparative philology. On returning to Harvard he became Boylston Professor in 1851.

The Child Ballads

Child's enduring monument is The English and Scottish Popular Ballads, published in ten parts (five volumes) between 1882 and 1898, the final part appearing after his death. It contains 305 traditional ballads from England and Scotland, with their variants, extensive headnotes, and comparative material drawn from the folk traditions of many countries. Ballads in the collection are still referred to by their "Child numbers" — for example "Barbara Allen" (Child 84), "Tam Lin" (Child 39) and "Lord Randall" (Child 12).

Legacy

Child's collection became the foundation of ballad scholarship and a key source for the twentieth-century folk revival; collectors such as Cecil Sharp compared songs they gathered in the field against his canon. Harvard's Child Memorial Library, devoted to English literature, was named in his honour. He was also a noted authority on Chaucer and on the language of Spenser.
