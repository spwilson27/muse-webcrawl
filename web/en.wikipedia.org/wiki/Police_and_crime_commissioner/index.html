Police and crime commissioner

A police and crime commissioner (PCC) is an elected official in England and Wales responsible for securing efficient and effective policing of a police area. The posts replaced police authorities in 2012, following the Police Reform and Social Responsibility Act 2011. The first elections took place in November 2012, and PCCs are elected every four years.

Role and powers

PCCs set the police precept (the policing element of council tax), appoint and, if necessary, dismiss the chief constable, and publish a police and crime plan for their area. They hold the chief constable to account but do not direct operational policing, which remains the responsibility of the chief constable. Each PCC is scrutinised by a police and crime panel made up of local councillors.

Criticism and reform

Turnout in the first PCC elections was very low, around 15 per cent, prompting debate about the visibility of the role. Some combined authority mayors have taken on PCC functions, notably in Greater Manchester and London (through the Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime). Following the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts reforms, the government announced plans to abolish PCCs and transfer their functions to mayors and councils.
