Lumen gentium, the Dogmatic Constitution on the Church, is one of the principal documents of the Second Vatican Council. The Latin title means "Light of the Nations (or Peoples)", from the opening words of the text, which quote Christ as the light of all nations. The constitution was promulgated by Pope Paul VI on 21 November 1964, having been approved by a vote of 2,151 to 5 of the assembled bishops.

The Second Vatican Council, which promulgated Lumen gentium in 1964

Contents

The document is organised into eight chapters: the mystery of the Church; the People of God; the hierarchical structure of the Church, with special reference to the episcopate; the laity; the universal call to holiness; religious life; the eschatological character of the pilgrim Church; and the Blessed Virgin Mary in the mystery of Christ and the Church.

Key teachings

Lumen gentium presents the Church primarily as the "People of God" and emphasises the collegiality of the bishops together with and under the pope. Its statement that the Church of Christ "subsists in" (subsistit in) the Catholic Church became one of the most debated phrases of the council. The constitution also teaches the universal call to holiness of all Christians, affirms the role of the laity in the Church's mission, and presents Mary as a model and member of the Church rather than apart from it.
