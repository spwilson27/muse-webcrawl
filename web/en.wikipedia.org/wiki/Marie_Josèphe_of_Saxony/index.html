Maria Josepha of Saxony (4 November 1731 – 13 March 1767) was Dauphine of France as the second wife of Louis, Dauphin of France, son of Louis XV. She was the mother of three kings of France: Louis XVI, Louis XVIII, and Charles X. Although she never became queen — her husband predeceased his father — she was one of the most respected figures at the court of Versailles.

Early life and marriage

Born in Dresden, Maria Josepha was the fifteenth child of Frederick Augustus II, Elector of Saxony and King of Poland (Augustus III), and Archduchess Maria Josepha of Austria. The marriage to the widowed Dauphin Louis was promoted by Maurice de Saxe, her uncle, the famous Marshal of France, and was intended to strengthen ties between France and Saxony. They were married by proxy on 10 January 1747 and in person at Versailles on 9 February 1747.

Dauphine

The Dauphin was still grieving his first wife, the Spanish infanta Maria Teresa Rafaela, but Marie Josèphe won his affection through tact and patience, and the marriage became a happy one. Intelligent and devout, she learned French quickly, took an interest in music and science, and navigated the court's factions with care, maintaining good relations with Queen Maria Leszczyńska and even with Madame de Pompadour, whom she avoided offending. She bore eight children, five of whom survived infancy, including three future kings and two daughters: Clotilde, later Queen of Sardinia, and Élisabeth, executed during the Revolution.

Death

The Dauphin died of tuberculosis on 20 December 1765, leaving Marie Josèphe devastated. Her own health collapsed, and she died at Versailles on 13 March 1767, aged thirty-five, fifteen months after her husband. She was buried beside him in Sens Cathedral, while her heart was placed at Saint-Denis.
