Tdap vaccine

Tdap is a combination vaccine that protects against three bacterial diseases: tetanus, diphtheria, and pertussis (whooping cough). The name derives from the components: tetanus toxoid (T), reduced diphtheria toxoid (d), and acellular pertussis (ap). The lower-case "d" and "p" indicate smaller quantities of diphtheria and pertussis antigens than in the childhood DTaP vaccine.

Usage

Tdap is recommended as a one-time booster for adolescents and adults who have completed the childhood DTaP series, typically given at age 11 or 12, followed by Td boosters every ten years. It is also recommended during each pregnancy, ideally between 27 and 36 weeks of gestation, to pass protective antibodies to the newborn.

History

Tdap vaccines were first licensed in the United States in 2005, marketed under names such as Boostrix and Adacel. Their introduction was prompted by waning immunity to pertussis among adolescents and adults, who could transmit the infection to vulnerable infants.

Side effects

Common side effects include pain, redness, or swelling at the injection site, mild fever, and fatigue. Severe allergic reactions are rare.
