Battle of Lipany

Date30 May 1434
LocationLipany near Český Brod, Bohemia
ResultDecisive Utraquist–Catholic victory; end of the Hussite Wars
BelligerentsUtraquist and Catholic Bohemian nobility vs. Taborites and Orphans (radical Hussites)
CommandersDiviš Bořek of Miletínek vs. Prokop the Great (killed)

The Battle of Lipany, also called the Battle of Český Brod, was fought on 30 May 1434 near the village of Lipany in central Bohemia. It was the decisive final battle of the Hussite Wars, in which an army of moderate Hussites (Utraquists) allied with Catholics destroyed the radical Hussite field armies of the Taborites and the Orphans.

Background

After fifteen years of war, negotiations at the Council of Basel offered the moderate Utraquists terms (the Compacts) recognising communion under both kinds. The radical Taborites and Orphans, led by the priest-general Prokop the Great, rejected compromise and continued the war, bringing the conflict to a final confrontation between Bohemian armies.

The battle

The allied army under Diviš Bořek of Miletínek feigned a retreat after an exchange of fire, luring the radicals out of their wagon fort. When the Taborites opened their wagenburg to pursue, allied cavalry that had been concealed charged into the gap and turned to attack the wagon fort from inside while the main body assaulted it from the front. The radical army collapsed; Prokop the Great and Prokůpek, leaders of the Taborites and Orphans respectively, were both killed.

Aftermath

Lipany ended Tabor's military power and cleared the way for the Compacts of Prague (1436) and the recognition of Sigismund as king of Bohemia. The battle remains a symbol of national tragedy in Czech tradition — Hussites destroyed by Hussites — though it brought peace after a generation of war. A monumental memorial mound now marks the battlefield.
