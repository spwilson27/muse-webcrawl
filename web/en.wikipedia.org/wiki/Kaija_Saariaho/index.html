Kaija Saariaho

Kaija Anneli Saariaho (née Laakkonen; 14 October 1952 – 2 June 2023) was a Finnish composer based in Paris. She is regarded as one of the most significant composers of her generation, known for a richly textured orchestral style influenced by spectral music and for pioneering work combining live instruments with electronics. Her first opera, L'Amour de loin (2000), brought her international renown.

Life and career

Saariaho was born in Helsinki and studied composition at the Sibelius Academy with Paavo Heininen, before further studies in Freiburg with Brian Ferneyhough and Klaus Huber. In 1982 she moved to Paris to work at IRCAM, the institute for electronic and computer music founded by Pierre Boulez, where research into the analysis and synthesis of sound shaped her mature style. Works such as Verblendungen (1984), Stria (1982), and the orchestral diptych Du cristal… à la fumée (1989–90) established her reputation.

Operas

Saariaho composed five operas, frequently collaborating with the librettist Amin Maalouf and the director Peter Sellars:

  • L'Amour de loin (2000) — based on the life of the 12th-century troubadour Jaufré Rudel; premiered at the Salzburg Festival.
  • Adriana Mater (2006) — set in a war-torn country.
  • Émilie (2010) — a monodrama about the scientist Émilie du Châtelet.
  • Only the Sound Remains (2016) — based on Noh plays translated by Ezra Pound.
  • Innocence (2021) — a large-scale ensemble opera premiered at Aix-en-Provence, confronting the aftermath of a school shooting.

Style

Saariaho's music is characterised by slowly evolving timbral masses, glissandi, and a continuum between harmony and noise. She described her interest in "timbre as a structural element" and frequently drew inspiration from visual art, nature, and poetry.

Death and legacy

Saariaho died in Paris on 2 June 2023, aged 70, from glioblastoma, a diagnosis she had kept private. In a 2019 BBC poll of fellow composers she was voted the greatest living composer of the time. Her honors included the Grawemeyer Award (2003) for L'Amour de loin and the Polar Music Prize (2013).
