The X-Men film series consists of American superhero films based on the Marvel Comics team of the same name, centred on a subspecies of humans known as mutants who possess superhuman abilities. Produced by 20th Century Fox, the series began with X-Men (2000) and ran for thirteen films over twenty years, concluding with The New Mutants (2020). It is widely credited, along with the contemporary Spider-Man films, with launching the modern era of superhero cinema.

Overview

The original trilogy — X-Men (2000), X2 (2003) and X-Men: The Last Stand (2006), directed by Bryan Singer and then Brett Ratner — followed Professor Charles Xavier's school for mutants and its conflict with Magneto's Brotherhood, with Hugh Jackman's Wolverine emerging as the breakout character. A prequel series beginning with X-Men: First Class (2011) recast younger versions of Xavier (James McAvoy) and Magneto (Michael Fassbender), and X-Men: Days of Future Past (2014) united both casts in a time-travel story. Spin-offs included the Wolverine trilogy — capped by the acclaimed Logan (2017) — and the two Deadpool films (2016, 2018) starring Ryan Reynolds, which became the highest-grossing R-rated films of their time.

Reception and legacy

The series grossed over US$6 billion worldwide. Early entries were praised for their serious tone and themes of prejudice and outsiderdom, and the franchise's success helped establish comic-book films as a dominant Hollywood genre. Following Disney's acquisition of 21st Century Fox in 2019, control of the X-Men characters passed to Marvel Studios; elements of the Fox series were later integrated into the Marvel Cinematic Universe through multiverse storylines, including Reynolds's Deadpool and Jackman's Wolverine in Deadpool & Wolverine (2024).
