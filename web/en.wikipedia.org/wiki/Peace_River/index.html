Peace River

The Peace River is a 1,923-kilometre-long river in northern British Columbia and Alberta, Canada. It is a major tributary of the Mackenzie River system and flows from the Rocky Mountains to join the Slave River near Lake Athabasca.

SourceWilliston Lake, British Columbia
MouthSlave River, Alberta
Length1,923 km

Course

The river begins at the confluence of the Finlay and Parsnip rivers, now flooded by Williston Lake behind the W. A. C. Bennett Dam. It flows east through the Rocky Mountain Trench, across the Alberta plateau past the town of Peace River, and into Wood Buffalo National Park before joining the Slave River.

History

The river takes its name from Peace Point, where the Cree and Dane-zaa (Beaver) peoples negotiated a peace settlement in the 18th century. Alexander Mackenzie travelled the river in 1793 on his overland journey to the Pacific.
