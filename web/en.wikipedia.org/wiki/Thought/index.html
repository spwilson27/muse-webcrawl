Thought

Thought, or thinking, is the process of considering, reasoning about, or reflecting on something. In philosophy and cognitive science it encompasses mental activities such as forming concepts, solving problems, making judgements and decisions, imagining, planning and remembering. Thought is one of the central topics of the philosophy of mind, psychology and cognitive science.

Types of thought

Thinking is often divided into types including convergent and divergent thinking, abstract and concrete thinking, analytical and creative thinking, and fast, automatic "System 1" processes versus slow, deliberate "System 2" reasoning, in the terminology popularised by Daniel Kahneman. Other forms include critical thinking, lateral thinking and wishful thinking.

Philosophical accounts

Descartes made thought the indubitable foundation of knowledge in his cogito ergo sum ("I think, therefore I am"). Debates continue over the relationship between thought and language: the language-of-thought hypothesis holds that thinking occurs in a mental representational system ("mentalese"), while strong versions of linguistic relativity suggest language shapes thought. The extended mind thesis argues that cognitive processes can partly occur outside the brain, in notebooks and devices.

Neuroscience and psychology

Cognitive psychology studies thinking through experiments on reasoning biases and heuristics, while neuroscience maps thought to activity in networks including the prefrontal cortex and the default mode network active during mind-wandering. Disorders of thought content and form are central features of several psychiatric conditions.
