Pen y Fan

Elevation: 886 m (2,907 ft)
Location: Brecon Beacons, Powys, Wales
Range: Brecon Beacons

Pen y Fan is the highest peak in south Wales and in the Brecon Beacons (Bannau Brycheiniog) National Park, rising to 886 metres (2,907 ft). Its name means "the top of the peak" in Welsh. The mountain is composed of Old Red Sandstone and has a distinctive flat-topped summit plateau.

Walking and military use

The most popular ascent is the well-graded path from the Storey Arms on the A470, making Pen y Fan one of the most climbed mountains in Britain. Together with neighbouring Corn Du it forms the centerpiece of the Beacons. Pen y Fan is also used in the "Fan Dance," a famous endurance march in British special forces selection, in which candidates cross the mountain twice with heavy packs.

Archaeology

A Bronze Age burial cairn stands on the summit; when excavated it contained a cist with cremated remains. The mountain and surrounding land are cared for by the National Trust.
