Steve Wynn

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen Alan Wynn (born January 27, 1942) is an American real estate developer and art collector. He is widely credited with leading the resurgence and expansion of Las Vegas in the 1990s through his company Mirage Resorts, whose properties — the Mirage, Treasure Island, and Bellagio — set a new standard of luxury for casino resorts. He later founded Wynn Resorts, developing Wynn Las Vegas, Encore, and properties in Macau and Boston Harbor.

Early life and beginnings

Wynn was born in New Haven, Connecticut, and raised in upstate New York. After graduating from the University of Pennsylvania in 1963, he took over his father's bingo parlour business in Maryland following his father's death. In 1967 he moved to Las Vegas, acquired a stake in the Frontier Hotel, and in 1973 became CEO of the Golden Nugget, which he expanded into one of downtown's most profitable casinos and later replicated in Atlantic City.

Mirage Resorts

Financed in part through junk bonds arranged by Michael Milken, the Mirage opened on the Las Vegas Strip in 1989 with an erupting volcano attraction; its success sparked a building boom of themed megaresorts. Wynn followed with Treasure Island (1993) and the Bellagio (1998), at the time the most expensive hotel ever built, famous for its lake and fountain show. In 2000, Mirage Resorts was acquired by MGM Grand Inc. for about $6.4 billion, forming MGM Mirage (now MGM Resorts International).

Wynn Resorts and later events

Wynn's new company opened Wynn Las Vegas in 2005 on the site of the Desert Inn, and expanded to Macau (Wynn Macau, 2006; Wynn Palace, 2016). In January 2018, The Wall Street Journal reported allegations of sexual misconduct by Wynn spanning decades; Wynn denied the allegations but resigned as CEO and chairman of Wynn Resorts in February 2018 and sold his stake in the company. Regulators in Nevada and Massachusetts subsequently fined the company and found Wynn unsuitable. He has been a major Republican donor and served as finance chair of the Republican National Committee in 2017–2018. Wynn suffers from retinitis pigmentosa, a degenerative eye disease that has progressively impaired his vision; he is a prominent collector of modern art, and in 2006 he famously damaged Picasso's Le Rêve with his elbow shortly after agreeing to sell it.
