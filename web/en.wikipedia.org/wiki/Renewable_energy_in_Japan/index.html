Renewable energy in Japan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Solar power leads Japan's renewables growth

Renewable energy in Japan has been expanding rapidly since the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster prompted the closure of the country's nuclear reactors and a reorientation of energy policy. Renewables — chiefly solar, hydroelectric, geothermal, wind and biomass — supply roughly a fifth to a quarter of Japan's electricity in recent years.

History

Before 2011, nuclear power supplied about 30% of Japan's electricity. After Fukushima, all reactors were progressively shut down, and fossil fuel imports surged. In July 2012 Japan introduced one of the world's most generous feed-in tariffs for renewable energy, triggering a boom in solar installations that made Japan one of the largest solar markets in the world.

Sources

Solar power is the largest source of new renewable capacity, despite Japan's mountainous terrain and dense population, which have driven distinctive floating solar and agrivoltaic projects. Hydroelectricity, long established, provides about 8% of electricity. Japan has the world's third-largest geothermal resources after the United States and Indonesia, but development is limited, partly because many resources lie within national parks and onsen (hot spring) areas. Offshore wind is a major growth area, with government targets of 10 GW by 2030 and up to 45 GW by 2040.

Targets

Japan's Strategic Energy Plan targets renewables supplying 36–38% of electricity by 2030, alongside restarted nuclear reactors and reduced fossil fuel use, as part of a pledge to reach carbon neutrality by 2050.
