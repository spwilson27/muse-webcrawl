Antipasto

Antipasto (plural antipasti) is the traditional first course of a formal Italian meal. The name means "before the meal" (from Latin ante and pastus). A typical antipasto includes cured meats, olives, pepperoncini, mushrooms, anchovies, artichoke hearts, various cheeses, pickled meats and vegetables in oil or vinegar.

Varieties

Regional antipasti vary widely: Piedmont offers vitello tonnato and bagna càuda; Tuscany is known for crostini with chicken-liver pâté; coastal regions serve seafood antipasti such as marinated anchovies. In Italian-American cuisine, the "antipasto salad" combines many of these elements over lettuce. Antipasti are generally served at room temperature, arranged on platters for sharing, and are intended to stimulate the appetite rather than satisfy it.
