Jacqueline Mary du Pré (26 January 1945 – 19 October 1987) was a British cellist, widely considered one of the greatest cellists of the twentieth century. Her career was cut short in her twenties by multiple sclerosis.

Career

Du Pré studied with William Pleeth and, briefly, with Paul Tortelier and Mstislav Rostropovich. She made her formal debut at the Wigmore Hall in 1961 and quickly became known for intense, emotionally direct performances. Her 1965 recording of Elgar's Cello Concerto with Sir John Barbirolli and the London Symphony Orchestra became a landmark of the catalogue and remains the work's benchmark recording. She married the pianist and conductor Daniel Barenboim in 1967 and performed widely with him and with musicians including Itzhak Perlman, Pinchas Zukerman and Zubin Mehta; the film of their performance of Schubert's "Trout" Quintet became famous.

Illness and legacy

Loss of feeling in her fingers forced her to stop performing in 1973, and multiple sclerosis was diagnosed. She died in 1987 aged 42. Her life inspired the film Hilary and Jackie (1998), based on her siblings' memoir, and her name is borne by the Jacqueline du Pré Music Building in Oxford.
