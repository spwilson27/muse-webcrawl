Gould designation

A Gould designation is a star designation similar to a Flamsteed designation, consisting of a number followed by the letter "G" and the Latin genitive of the constellation, as in 41G Eridani. The designations were assigned by the American astronomer Benjamin Apthorp Gould in his Uranometria Argentina (1879), a catalogue of southern stars observed from Córdoba, Argentina.

History

John Flamsteed's numbers had covered only stars visible from Britain, leaving the far southern sky largely unnumbered. Working at the Argentine National Observatory in Córdoba, Gould catalogued some 73,000 southern stars and assigned numbers, in order of right ascension within each constellation, to the brighter ones. Because Gould's numbering overlaps in form with Flamsteed's, the "G" suffix distinguishes his designations.

Usage

Gould designations survive for stars that lack a Bayer designation and are still used for many southern stars, although modern catalogues more often use Henry Draper (HD) or Hipparcos numbers. Gould also introduced many of the modern boundaries and names later adopted for southern constellations.
