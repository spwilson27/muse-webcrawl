The Lords Spiritual (Women) Act 2015 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that gives priority to female diocesan bishops of the Church of England in filling vacancies among the Lords Spiritual — the 26 bishops who sit in the House of Lords — during the ten years following the Act's commencement.

Background

Twenty-six Church of England bishops sit in the House of Lords: the Archbishops of Canterbury and York, the Bishops of London, Durham and Winchester (automatic seats), and 21 further diocesan bishops selected by seniority of consecration. In 2014 the Church of England's General Synod approved the consecration of women as bishops, and the Bishops and Priests (Consecration and Ordination of Women) Measure 2014 removed the legal barrier. However, because appointment to the Lords previously followed strict seniority, it would have taken many years before any woman bishop qualified. The Act was introduced to accelerate women's entry.

Provisions

For ten years from 18 May 2015, where a vacancy arises among the 21 seniority-based places and a female diocesan bishop is eligible, she is to be selected in place of the longest-serving male bishop (the five ex officio sees are unaffected). The Act originally applied until May 2025; in 2024 Parliament passed legislation extending the arrangement for a further five years, to 2030, given that the proportion of women among the Lords Spiritual remained below what the original Act's supporters had envisaged.

Effects

The first woman to sit as a Lord Spiritual was Rachel Treweek, Bishop of Gloucester, introduced to the House in October 2015 following her consecration that July. Subsequent women bishops entering the Lords under the Act include the Bishops of Newcastle, Derby, Bristol and Chelmsford, among others.
