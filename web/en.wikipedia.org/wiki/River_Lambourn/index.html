River Lambourn

The River Lambourn is a chalk stream in the English county of Berkshire, and a tributary of the River Kennet. It rises in the Berkshire Downs near the village of Lambourn and flows about 15 miles (24 km) south-east to join the Kennet at Newbury.

A winterbourne

The upper reaches of the Lambourn are a winterbourne: the source migrates up and down the valley according to the level of the chalk aquifer, and in dry summers the topmost section, near the Uffington White Horse end of the valley, may cease to flow. Below Lambourn village the flow becomes perennial, fed by springs at Lynch Wood and elsewhere.

Ecology

The Lambourn is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Special Area of Conservation, celebrated for the characteristic chalk-stream plant community of water-crowfoot, water starwort and watercress beds, and for fish such as bullhead, brook lamprey and brown trout, along with the Desmoulin's whorl snail in the valley wetlands. The valley of the Lambourn is equally famous for horse racing: Lambourn is one of Britain's principal racehorse training centres, the "Valley of the Racehorse".
