Hill climbing

In numerical analysis, hill climbing is a mathematical optimization technique which belongs to the family of local search. It is an iterative algorithm that starts with an arbitrary solution to a problem, then attempts to find a better solution by making an incremental change to the solution. If the change produces a better solution, another incremental change is made to the new solution, and so on until no further improvements can be found.

Problems

Hill climbing can get stuck on local maxima, plateaus, and ridges, where no single incremental step improves the solution even though a better solution is nearby. Variants such as stochastic hill climbing, random restarts, and simulated annealing attempt to mitigate these problems.

Variants

Simple hill climbing examines neighbouring solutions one at a time and takes the first improvement; steepest-ascent hill climbing evaluates all neighbours and takes the best. Hill climbing is widely used in artificial intelligence and operations research for problems where a good local solution is acceptable.
