Saz (musical instrument)

Saz
Classification: String instrument, lute
Related: Bağlama, bouzouki, dutar

The saz is a family of long-necked lutes played in Turkey, Azerbaijan, Iran, Armenia, and parts of the Balkans and Central Asia. The word "saz" comes from Persian, where it means an instrument or a set of instruments. The best-known member of the family is the Turkish bağlama, often simply called the saz, which is central to Turkish folk music and to the tradition of the âşık (minstrel poets).

Description

The saz has a pear-shaped or rounded bowl body, traditionally carved from a single piece of mulberry or other hardwood, a long neck with tied movable frets, and typically three courses of strings (often seven strings in total) tuned in groups. The movable frets allow the player to adjust for the microtonal intervals of Turkish makam music. The instrument is usually played with a long thin plectrum called a mızrap or tezene, though fingerstyle playing (şelpe) is also traditional.

Sizes and types

The bağlama family comes in several sizes, from the small cura to the larger tambura and the deep-bodied divan saz. In Azerbaijani music, the saz is the principal instrument of the ashiq tradition, recognized by UNESCO as part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Cultural significance

The saz accompanies epic poetry, folk songs, and Alevi religious ceremonies (cem), where it holds spiritual significance. Legendary performers such as Âşık Veysel made the instrument emblematic of Anatolian folk culture.
