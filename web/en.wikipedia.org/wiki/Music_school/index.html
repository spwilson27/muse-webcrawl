Music school

A music school is an educational institution specialised in the study, training, and research of music. Such institutions include conservatories (or conservatoires), university music departments and schools of music, community music schools, and specialist schools for children. Students receive instruction in performance (instrumental and vocal), music theory, composition, music history, and pedagogy, typically involving both individual lessons and ensemble participation.

An orchestra rehearsal at a conservatory.

History

The first institutions called conservatories were orphanages in Naples in the 16th century — such as Santa Maria di Loreto — where children received musical training; their graduates dominated European music, and "conservatory" (from conservare, to preserve) later came to mean an advanced school of music generally. The Paris Conservatoire, founded in 1795, became the model for the modern institution. In the 19th century, conservatories spread across Europe: Leipzig (1843, founded by Mendelssohn), Vienna, and the Royal Academy of Music in London (1822).

Types

Conservatories

Independent conservatories focus intensively on professional performing training. Famous examples include the Juilliard School in New York, the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia (which has charged no tuition since 1928), the Royal College of Music in London, and the Moscow Conservatory. Admission is by competitive audition.

University music schools

Many universities house schools of music combining performance with academic degrees (BMus, MMus, DMA, PhD), such as the Indiana University Jacobs School of Music and the Eastman School of Music. These offer broader studies in musicology, ethnomusicology, music education, and music technology.

Specialist and community schools

Specialist schools nurture gifted children, such as the Yehudi Menuhin School and the Chetham's School of Music in Britain. Community music schools and Saturday schools provide instruction to amateurs and pre-college students of all ages.

Curriculum

Study typically centres on one-to-one principal-study tuition, supported by ensemble work (orchestra, choir, chamber music, opera, jazz and popular music groups), aural training, harmony, counterpoint, analysis, and music history. Many schools also teach Alexander technique, performance practice, and career skills.
