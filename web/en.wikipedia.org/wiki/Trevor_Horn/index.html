Horn, dubbed "the man who invented the eighties."

Trevor Charles Horn (born 15 July 1949) is an English record producer and musician. He first achieved fame in 1979 as half of the new wave band the Buggles, whose single "Video Killed the Radio Star" was the first music video played on MTV. He went on to become one of the most influential producers of the 1980s and beyond, pioneering the use of sampling and digital production.

Career

After the Buggles, Horn briefly joined the progressive rock band Yes as lead singer for the album Drama (1980). Turning to production, he produced ABC's The Lexicon of Love (1982) and co-founded ZTT Records in 1983 with Paul Morley and Jill Sinclair. With the Art of Noise and Frankie Goes to Hollywood — whose singles "Relax," "Two Tribes" and "The Power of Love" dominated 1984 — Horn defined a maximalist, sample-heavy pop sound. He also produced Yes's hit album 90125, including "Owner of a Lonely Heart."

Later work

Horn produced Seal's early albums, winning Grammy Awards for "Kiss from a Rose" (1995), and has worked with artists including Grace Jones (Slave to the Rhythm), Pet Shop Boys, t.A.T.u., Cher, Tina Turner, Robbie Williams and LeAnn Rimes. He was appointed CBE in 2011 for services to the music industry and has continued to perform with the Trevor Horn Band.
