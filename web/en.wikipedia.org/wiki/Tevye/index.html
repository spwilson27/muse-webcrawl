Tevye

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tevye the Dairyman (Yiddish: טביה דער מילכיקער, Tevye der Milkhiker) is a fictional character created by the Yiddish writer Sholem Aleichem (Solomon Rabinovich). He is the protagonist and narrator of a series of stories, written between 1894 and 1916, in the form of monologues addressed to the author himself. Tevye is a pious, poor Jewish milkman in the fictional village of Anatevka (in some stories, Boiberik and Yehupetz/Kyiv) in the Russian Empire's Pale of Settlement, father of seven daughters, whose struggles with poverty, faith, tradition and his daughters' marriages form the heart of the cycle.

The stories

The major stories include "Tevye Strikes It Rich", "Tevye Blows the Whistle", and those centred on his daughters: Tzeitel, who marries the poor tailor Motel instead of the wealthy butcher Lazar Wolf; Hodel, who joins the revolutionary Perchik in Siberian exile; Chava, who marries a Christian, Fyedka, and is cast out; and the later, darker episodes involving Shprintze, Beilke, and Tevye's expulsion from his village in the era of anti-Jewish pogroms and expulsions. Tevye constantly quotes — and cheerfully misquotes — the Torah, Talmud and liturgy, debating with God in a tone that mixes reverence, irony and lament.

Adaptations

The stories were adapted for stage and screen many times, most famously as the Broadway musical Fiddler on the Roof (1964), with music by Jerry Bock, lyrics by Sheldon Harnick and book by Joseph Stein, and its 1971 film adaptation directed by Norman Jewison, starring Chaim Topol as Tevye (Zero Mostel originated the role on stage). Earlier adaptations include Maurice Schwartz's Yiddish film Tevya (1939).

Legacy

Tevye has become an archetype of the Eastern European Jewish father and of the old world of the shtetl on the eve of its destruction; his monologues ("If I Were a Rich Man" in the musical) frame questions of tradition and change that the character famously balances "on the one hand... on the other hand".
