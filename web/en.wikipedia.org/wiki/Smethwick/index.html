Smethwick

Smethwick is an industrial town in Sandwell, West Midlands, England, historically part of Staffordshire. It lies immediately west of Birmingham and had a population of around 48,000 at the 2021 census.

County: West Midlands
Metropolitan borough: Sandwell
Historic county: Staffordshire

History

Smethwick grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution, powered by the Birmingham Canal Navigations and firms such as Matthew Boulton and James Watt's Soho Foundry (built 1795), the Chance Brothers glassworks at Spon Lane — which glazed the Crystal Palace — and later the Guest, Keen and Nettlefolds (GKN) screw works, once the largest of its kind in the world.

Politics

The town gained national attention in the 1964 general election, when Conservative candidate Peter Griffiths won the Smethwick constituency amid a notoriously racially charged campaign during early post-war immigration debates. The constituency is now represented within Sandwell's parliamentary seats.

Transport and sport

Smethwick is served by Galton Bridge and Smethwick Rolfe Street railway stations. Sandwell Aquatics Centre in the town hosted swimming events for the 2022 Commonwealth Games.
