Ensenada, Baja California

Ensenada is a city in Ensenada Municipality, Baja California, situated on the Pacific Coast of Mexico. Located in Bahía de Todos Santos, the deep-water port lies about 125 kilometres (78 mi) south of San Diego. The city had a population of roughly 330,000 in 2018, and its municipality is the largest by area in Mexico.

History

The bay was first reached by sea in 1542 by the expedition of Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo, who named it San Mateo. In 1602 Sebastián Vizcaíno renamed the bay Ensenada de Todos Santos ("inlet of all the saints"). The modern settlement dates to the 19th century: Ensenada was founded in 1882 and briefly served as capital of the Baja California Territory from 1882 to 1915, when the capital moved to Mexicali.

Economy and tourism

Ensenada is Mexico's principal Pacific port south of the US border for cruise ships, and a major commercial fishing and cargo port. It is the gateway to the Valle de Guadalupe wine country, Mexico's best-known wine region, and is famous for fish tacos, which are said to have originated in its markets. The nearby La Bufadora marine geyser is a popular excursion. The city is the start and finish of the Baja 1000 off-road race.

Geography and climate

Ensenada has a semi-arid climate, moderated by the Pacific Ocean, with mild winters and warm, dry summers; most rain falls between November and March.
