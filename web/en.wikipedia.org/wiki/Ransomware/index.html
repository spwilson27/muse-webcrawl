Ransomware

Ransomware
TypeMalware, cyber extortion
First major examplesAIDS Trojan (1989), CryptoLocker (2013)

Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts the victim's personal data or blocks access to a system until a sum of money is paid. Crypto-ransomware encrypts files with keys held by the attacker; locker ransomware locks the device interface. Modern attacks add "double extortion" — exfiltrating data and threatening to leak it, a digital form of blackmail.

History

The 1989 AIDS Trojan is usually cited as the first ransomware. CryptoLocker (2013) popularised strong encryption and Bitcoin payment. WannaCry and NotPetya in 2017 spread worldwide, hitting hospitals, shipping and government. Later, ransomware-as-a-service groups such as LockBit, Conti and REvil targeted companies, pipelines (Colonial Pipeline, 2021), schools and hospitals, prompting international law-enforcement takedowns.

How it works and prevention

Infection vectors include phishing, exposed remote desktop services, and software vulnerabilities. Defences include offline backups, patching, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation and incident-response planning. Authorities generally advise against paying, as payment does not guarantee recovery and funds crime; in some jurisdictions paying sanctioned groups is itself unlawful.
