Penne alla vodka


Course: Main course
Place of origin: United States / Italy
Main ingredients: Penne, vodka, tomatoes, cream, garlic, chili flakes

Penne alla vodka is a pasta dish made with penne in a creamy sauce of vodka, tomatoes, and heavy cream, often seasoned with garlic, red pepper flakes, and basil. It became one of the most popular Italian-American restaurant dishes of the late twentieth century and remains a staple of the cuisine.

History

The dish's origins are disputed, with claims from both Italy and the United States. Several New York restaurants and chefs claimed to have invented it in the 1970s and 1980s, including Orsini in Manhattan and James Doty. Italian accounts trace a similar dish, sometimes called pennette all'infuriata or penne alla russe, to the 1970s, when vodka producers promoted the spirit as a cooking ingredient. The dish peaked in popularity in the 1980s and 1990s, when pink vodka sauce became a signature of Italian-American dining.

Preparation

The sauce combines tomato sauce or crushed tomatoes with vodka, which is cooked off briefly; the alcohol helps emulsify the sauce and extract flavor compounds from the tomatoes that are soluble in alcohol but not water or fat. Heavy cream is then stirred in to give a pale pink-orange sauce. The pasta is tossed in the sauce, often with parmesan, basil, and a pinch of crushed red pepper. Some versions add pancetta or prosciutto.

Cultural revival

In 2020 the dish saw a viral resurgence on social media after a spicy rigatoni alla vodka recipe was widely shared, prompting restaurants and home cooks to revisit the classic.
