The Confédération Mondiale des Activités Subaquatiques (CMAS), known in English as the World Underwater Federation, is an international federation for underwater activities, founded in Monaco in January 1959. It is one of the world's largest recreational diver training organisations and the international governing body for underwater sports including finswimming, underwater hockey, underwater rugby, spearfishing, and freediving disciplines.

History

CMAS grew out of the underwater committee of the Confédération Internationale de la Pêche Sportive; delegates from fifteen countries, among them Jacques-Yves Cousteau, who became its first president, constituted it as an independent body. The federation established the "star" system of diver certification (one-star, two-star, three-star divers and instructors), which many national federations still use.

Activities

CMAS sets rules and world-championship programmes for underwater sports, publishes training standards for scuba diving through its member national federations, and has scientific and technical committees concerned with underwater archaeology, marine science, and diving safety. It is recognised by the International Olympic Committee as the governing body for its sports.
