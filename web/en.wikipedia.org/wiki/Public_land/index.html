Public land

Public land is land owned by a government on behalf of its citizens, as opposed to private property. In many countries public lands include national parks, forests, military reservations, wildlife refuges, public rights-of-way and the beds of navigable waters. The term is particularly associated with the United States, where the public domain historically comprised lands acquired by the federal government through purchase, treaty, cession and conquest, much of which was later transferred to states, homesteaders, railroads and other private owners.

United States

The federal government owns roughly 640 million acres — about 28 per cent of the land area of the United States — concentrated in Alaska and the eleven western states, where federal ownership ranges from about 45 per cent of California to more than 80 per cent of Nevada. Four agencies administer most of it: the Bureau of Land Management (about 245 million acres), the Forest Service (about 193 million acres), the Fish and Wildlife Service and the National Park Service.

Early federal policy favoured disposal: laws such as the Homestead Act of 1862 transferred land to settlers, and vast grants went to railroads and states. From the late nineteenth century the policy shifted toward retention and management, with the creation of forest reserves, national parks and grazing districts, and the Federal Land Policy and Management Act of 1976 formally declared that public lands would generally remain in federal ownership and be managed for multiple use and sustained yield. Continuing debates concern grazing fees, mineral leasing, conservation designations, access, and periodic campaigns to transfer federal land to the states.

Other countries

In Australia and Canada, analogous government land is called Crown land; in Canada about 89 per cent of the land area is Crown land, administered mainly by the provinces. In England and Wales, common land and village greens form a distinctive category of land over which the public or commoners hold rights, and legislation since 2000 has created a statutory right of access on foot to mapped open country.
