433 Eros is a stony asteroid of the Amor group and the first discovered, and second-largest, near-Earth object. It was discovered by the German astronomer Carl Gustav Witt at the Berlin Urania Observatory on 13 August 1898. It is named after Eros, the Greek god of love. Measuring approximately 16.8 kilometres (10.4 miles) in mean diameter, Eros is elongated, with dimensions of about 34.4 × 11.2 × 11.2 kilometres.

Discovery and history

Eros was the first asteroid found to come inside the orbit of Mars, and early observations of its position were used to refine the value of the astronomical unit and the mass of the Earth–Moon system. During its favourable opposition in 1930–1931, an international campaign measured its parallax with high precision. For the first half of the 20th century it was the principal object used to determine the scale of the Solar System.

NEAR Shoemaker visit

Eros was the first asteroid to be orbited and landed upon by a spacecraft. NASA's NEAR Shoemaker probe, launched in 1996, entered orbit around Eros on 14 February 2000 after a flyby in December 1998. Over a year in orbit, NEAR mapped the asteroid's irregular, cratered surface, measured its composition and internal structure, and determined that Eros is a consolidated but fractured S-type body rather than a loose rubble pile. On 12 February 2001, the spacecraft descended to a soft landing on the asteroid's surface — the first landing on a small Solar System body — and returned data from the surface for about two weeks.

Physical characteristics

Eros's surface is covered with craters and strewn with boulders, some tens of metres across, thought to be ejecta from the large Shoemaker impact crater. Its bulk density of about 2.67 g/cm³ is close to that of ordinary chondrite meteorites, supporting the link between S-type asteroids and the most common meteorites found on Earth.
