Presidential yacht

A presidential yacht is a vessel of a country's navy, or otherwise designated, used by the president of a republic for travel, ceremonies, or leisure. Presidential yachts serve a role similar to the royal yachts of monarchies.

United States

American presidents used a succession of naval yachts, most famously USS Potomac, Franklin D. Roosevelt's "floating White House," and USS Sequoia, which served presidents from Herbert Hoover to Jimmy Carter, who sold it in 1977 as a cost-cutting gesture. No dedicated presidential yacht has been in U.S. service since.

Other countries

The Philippines maintains BRP Ang Pangulo; Turkey preserves Atatürk's yacht Savarona; Egypt's El Mahrousa, launched in 1865, remains among the oldest commissioned yachts in the world. Croatia, India, Finland, and Italy have also maintained vessels for presidential or state use.
