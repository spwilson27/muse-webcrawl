Ivan III of Russia

Reign1462–1505, Grand Prince of Moscow
Born22 January 1440
Died27 October 1505
SpousesMaria of Tver; Sophia Palaiologina

Ivan III Vasilyevich (1440–1505), known as Ivan the Great, was Grand Prince of Moscow from 1462 to 1505. He tripled Muscovy's territory, ended its subordination to the Mongol Golden Horde, rebuilt the Kremlin, and laid the foundations of the Russian state, styling himself "Sovereign of all Russia".

Gathering of the Russian lands

Ivan annexed or subdued rival principalities — Yaroslavl, Rostov, Tver (1485) — and conquered the wealthy republic of Novgorod (1471–1478), deporting its elites and seizing its vast northern lands. Wars with Lithuania pushed Muscovy's borders westward; many Orthodox princes defected to Moscow.

End of the Horde

In 1480 Ivan refused tribute to Khan Akhmat; the armies faced off across the Ugra River in the "Great Stand", after which the Horde withdrew — traditionally marking the end of the "Mongol yoke". Ivan also campaigned against Kazan and the Livonian Order.

Government and ideology

His marriage to Sophia Palaiologina (1472), niece of the last Byzantine emperor, brought Byzantine ceremonial and the double-headed eagle emblem. Ivan issued the Sudebnik law code of 1497, built the Kremlin's cathedrals and walls with Italian architects such as Aristotele Fioravanti, and corresponded with European powers as an independent sovereign. The "Third Rome" idea developed under his successors.
