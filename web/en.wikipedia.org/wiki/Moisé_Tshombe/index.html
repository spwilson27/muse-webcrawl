Moïse Tshombe

Born10 November 1919, Musumba, Belgian Congo
Died29 June 1969 (aged 49), Algiers, Algeria
OfficePresident of the State of Katanga (1960–1963); Prime Minister of the Republic of the Congo (1964–1965)

Moïse Kapenda Tshombe (sometimes spelled Tshombé; 10 November 1919 – 29 June 1969) was a Congolese businessman and politician who led the secession of the mineral-rich Katanga province during the Congo Crisis, and later served as prime minister of the reunified Republic of the Congo. One of the most controversial figures of decolonisation, he was admired by some Western conservatives as an anti-communist and pro-business leader, and condemned by others for his reliance on Belgian support and white mercenaries and for his role in the crisis that ended with the murder of Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba.

Early life and Katanga secession

Born into a wealthy Lunda trading family in Musumba and educated at an American Methodist mission school, Tshombe built a chain of stores in Katanga before entering politics, founding the CONAKAT party in 1958 to represent "authentic" Katangese — especially his Lunda people — against both the centralist national movement and immigrants from Kasaï. When Congo became independent on 30 June 1960 and the army mutinied, Tshombe proclaimed Katanga's secession on 11 July 1960, backed by Belgian troops and the Union Minière mining company, whose taxes financed his state. UN forces (ONUC) intervened in the Congo Crisis, and, after the assassination of Lumumba in Katanga in January 1961 — a crime in which Tshombe's government was implicated — international pressure mounted. The secession collapsed in January 1963 under UN military action, and Tshombe went into exile in Spain.

Prime minister and exile

Recalled in July 1964 to head a government of national reconciliation against the Simba rebellion, Tshombe served as prime minister of the whole Congo, relying again on mercenaries and South African and Rhodesian pilots, and rescuing hostages in the Stanleyville airlift with Belgian and American help. President Joseph Kasa-Vubu dismissed him in October 1965; Mobutu seized power weeks later. Tshombe fled to Spain, was sentenced to death in absentia for treason, and in June 1967 was kidnapped when his chartered plane was hijacked to Algeria, where he was held under house arrest. He died in Algiers on 29 June 1969, officially of a heart attack, before extradition proceedings concluded. His body was returned to Belgium, and a Katangese figure of continuing fascination, he was played by Erik Hell in the film Lumumba and remains at the centre of debates over the Congo Crisis.
