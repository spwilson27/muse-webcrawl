The Grand Canal (Irish: An Chanáil Mhór) is the southernmost of a pair of canals that connect Dublin, in the east of Ireland, with the River Shannon in the west, the other being the Royal Canal. The Grand Canal stretches from Grand Canal Dock at the Liffey in Dublin through the midlands towns of Sallins, Tullamore and Daingean to Shannon Harbour in County Offaly, a distance of about 132 kilometres with 43 locks; branches once reached Naas, Athy (connecting to the River Barrow), Mountmellick and Kilbeggan.

History

The Grand Canal Company was incorporated in 1756, and construction began that year under engineer Thomas Omer. Financial difficulties repeatedly stalled the work, but the canal reached Sallins in the 1770s and Tullamore in 1797; in 1804 the final link crossed the Bog of Allen to join the Shannon, opening a through route for trade boats carrying turf, grain, coal and Guinness from Dublin to the west and south. A network of canal hotels served passengers on the swift "fly boats". Competition from railways in the mid-nineteenth century steadily eroded passenger traffic, but freight by barge continued; the canal was nationalised under Córas Iompair Éireann in 1950 and the last commercial through-traffic ceased in 1960.

The canal today

The canal is now maintained by Waterways Ireland and is used for leisure cruising, angling, cycling and walking — the Grand Canal Way follows its towpath. The Dublin section between the locks at Suir Road and Lucan is a busy urban amenity. The Barrow Line branch to Athy remains navigable, linking the canal system to the River Barrow navigation. Restoration campaigns in recent decades have kept the full main line open, and barges once again pass from Dublin to the Shannon.
