Catechism of the Catholic Church

Author: Catholic Church
Published: 1992 (French), 1994 (English)
Promulgated by: Pope John Paul II

The Catechism of the Catholic Church (Latin: Catechismus Catholicae Ecclesiae; commonly called the CCC) is a reference work summarising the beliefs of the Catholic faith, promulgated by Pope John Paul II on 11 October 1992 by the apostolic constitution Fidei depositum. It is intended primarily as a teaching tool for bishops and catechists.

Structure

The Catechism is organised in four parts: The Profession of Faith (the Creed), The Celebration of the Christian Mystery (sacraments), Life in Christ (morality, including the Ten Commandments), and Christian Prayer (including the Lord's Prayer). It contains 2,865 numbered paragraphs, extensive scriptural and patristic citations, and cross-references.

History

A universal catechism had not been produced since the Roman Catechism of 1566, issued after the Council of Trent. An extraordinary Synod of Bishops in 1985 recommended a new compendium. A commission under Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, later Pope Benedict XVI, oversaw its drafting. A revised Latin typical edition appeared in 1997.

Adaptations

The Compendium of the Catechism (2005) offers a concise question-and-answer summary. Youcat (2011) adapts its content for young people. National bishops' conferences have produced local catechisms based on it.
