The Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) is an American professional association and standards organization for electronics engineering, electrical engineering, and related disciplines. Formed on 1 January 1963 by the merger of the American Institute of Electrical Engineers (founded 1884) and the Institute of Radio Engineers (founded 1912), IEEE describes itself as "the world's largest technical professional organization dedicated to advancing technology for the benefit of humanity." It has more than 400,000 members in over 160 countries and is headquartered in New York City, with major operations in Piscataway, New Jersey.

Activities

IEEE publishes a large share of the world's technical literature in its fields — over 200 peer-reviewed journals, transactions, and magazines, plus conference proceedings — accessible through the IEEE Xplore digital library. Its standards arm, the IEEE Standards Association, develops widely used standards including the IEEE 802 family for local area networks (Ethernet and Wi-Fi), POSIX, and standards for power systems and floating-point arithmetic (IEEE 754). The organization is structured into technical societies (such as the IEEE Computer Society), geographic regions, sections, and student branches, and confers awards including the IEEE Medal of Honor.
