Boeing Field, officially King County International Airport (IATA: BFI, ICAO: KBFI), is a public airport owned and operated by King County, five miles (8 km) south of downtown Seattle, Washington. The airport has some passenger service, but is mostly used by general aviation and cargo. It is named after the Boeing Company's founder, William E. Boeing, and was constructed in 1928.

The airport's property is mostly in Seattle, with a portion lying in the city of Tukwila. The airport covers 594 acres (240 ha) and has two asphalt runways. It has more than 375,000 operations annually and is a hub for cargo operations in the Puget Sound region.

History

Before the airport opened, Seattle's principal aviation site was a seaplane and land facility on Lake Union and the field at Sand Point. Boeing Field served as Seattle's main passenger airport from its opening in 1928 until the late 1940s, when Seattle–Tacoma International Airport opened. During the Second World War the field was a major centre for the production and test-flying of Boeing bombers, including the B-17 and B-29.

The airport remains closely associated with Boeing: the company has delivery and test facilities on the field, and new Boeing airliners typically make early flights from nearby plants to Boeing Field. The Museum of Flight, one of the largest private air and space museums in the world, is located on the west side of the airport.

Facilities and operations

Boeing Field handles general aviation, corporate aviation, air cargo and military traffic. Tenants include flight schools, helicopter operators, fixed-base operators and the King County Sheriff's helicopter unit. UPS and other cargo carriers use the field, and it serves as an alternate airport for the Seattle area.
