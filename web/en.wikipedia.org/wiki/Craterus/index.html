Craterus (Greek: Κρατερός, Kraterós; c. 370–321 BC) was a Macedonian general under Alexander the Great and one of the Diadochi, the successors who partitioned Alexander's empire after his death. Immensely popular with the Macedonian soldiery, he was regarded as loyal, traditional and brave, though perhaps lacking in political subtlety.

Career under Alexander

Craterus commanded a brigade of the phalanx (the pezhetairoi) in Alexander's Persian campaigns, fighting at Granicus (334 BC), Issus (333 BC) and Gaugamela (331 BC). At the Hydaspes (326 BC) he commanded the force left on the western bank of the river, crossing to complete the victory over Porus. He led major detachments during the return from India, taking one column through Arachosia and Drangiana while Alexander crossed the Gedrosian desert.

After Alexander

In 324 BC Alexander sent Craterus back to Macedon with a force of discharged veterans, intending him to replace Antipater as regent of Europe — a transfer interrupted by Alexander's death at Babylon in 323 BC. In the ensuing Lamian War, Craterus instead aided Antipater, marrying his daughter Phila, and defeated the Greek rebels at Crannon in 322 BC.

In the first war of the Diadochi, Craterus joined the coalition against the regent Perdiccas and marched into Asia Minor against Eumenes of Cardia, Perdiccas's loyal general. In 321 BC, fighting near the Hellespont, Craterus was thrown from his horse and killed — according to tradition, Eumenes's men, many of them Macedonians who revered Craterus, were deliberately matched against non-Macedonian troops so that they would not recognise and spare him. Eumenes gave him an honourable funeral.
