Jerash

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The colonnaded Oval Plaza of Jerash, its Ionic columns curving around the forum
The colonnaded Oval Plaza of Jerash, its Ionic columns curving around the forum

Jerash is a city in northern Jordan famous for the ruins of the Greco-Roman city of Gerasa, one of the best-preserved provincial Roman cities in the world. A member of the Decapolis, Gerasa flourished in the first to third centuries AD; its colonnaded streets, oval plaza, two theatres, temples and hippodrome survived burial under sand to dazzle excavators from the 1920s. The modern city (population about 50,000) faces the ruins across a wadi.

The Roman city

Gerasa's grid: a cardo maximus with 500 surviving columns, an oval forum unique in the Roman world, the monumental Artemis temple, a nymphaeum, and Hadrian's Arch (129–130 AD) marking an imperial visit. Byzantine churches with fine mosaics, then an Umayyad mosque, layered the site's sacred geography before earthquakes (notably 749) ended its urban life.

Excavation and festival

Systematic excavation by British, American and Jordanian teams continues; the Jerash Festival (since 1981) fills the South Theatre with concerts each summer, and the ruins rank with Petra among Jordan's signature heritage sites — though the modern city's sprawl presses at their edges.
