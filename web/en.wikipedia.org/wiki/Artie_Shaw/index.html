Artie Shaw (born Arthur Jacob Arshawsky; May 23, 1910 – December 30, 2004) was an American clarinetist, composer, bandleader, actor and author. Widely regarded as one of the finest jazz clarinetists of the swing era, he led one of the most popular big bands of the late 1930s and early 1940s, rivalling Benny Goodman, with whom he was often compared — Shaw was marketed as the "King of the Clarinet" to Goodman's "King of Swing".

Career

Shaw left home at fifteen to tour as a saxophonist and clarinetist, working in dance bands before settling in New York as a much-in-demand session musician. A breakthrough came in 1936 with his unusual "Interlude in B-flat" for clarinet and string quartet. His big band scored an enormous hit in 1938 with Cole Porter's "Begin the Beguine", followed by "Stardust", "Frenesi", "Dancing in the Dark" and the "Concerto for Clarinet" (1940). Unusually for a white bandleader of the era, he hired Black singer Billie Holiday as his band's vocalist in 1938.

Style and later life

Shaw was a restless perfectionist who repeatedly disbanded his orchestras at the height of their success, served in the U.S. Navy during the Second World War (leading a band that toured the Pacific), and experimented with bebop-inflected small groups such as the Gramercy Five. He largely retired from performing in 1954 to devote himself to writing; his autobiography The Trouble with Cinderella (1952) is candid about the music business. His many marriages, including to film stars Lana Turner and Ava Gardner, kept him in the public eye. In 2004 he received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award, and his recordings are held in the National Recording Registry.
