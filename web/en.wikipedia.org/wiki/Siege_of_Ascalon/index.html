Siege of Ascalon

Date: 19–22 August 1153
Location: Ascalon, Fatimid Caliphate
Result: Crusader victory; Ascalon surrenders

The siege of Ascalon took place in 1153 and culminated in the capture of the Fatimid coastal fortress of Ascalon by the Kingdom of Jerusalem under King Baldwin III. It was one of the last major Crusader conquests in the Levant and eliminated the principal Fatimid bridgehead in Palestine.

Background

Ascalon had survived the First Crusade and remained a Fatimid outpost from which raids were launched into the kingdom. Repeated Crusader attempts to take it had failed, partly because of division among the Crusader leadership. In January 1153 Baldwin III invested the city with the full host of the kingdom, supported by a fleet blockading the harbour.

The siege

The siege lasted about seven months. The Crusaders built siege towers and battered the walls; when a tower was burned by the defenders, the flames brought down a section of wall, but a quarrel between the Templars and other troops over the spoils of the breach allowed the garrison to seal it. Eventually the exhausted defenders negotiated surrender.

Aftermath

On 22 August 1153 the inhabitants were permitted to evacuate to Egypt, and Ascalon became a lordship of the kingdom, granted to Amalric, the king's brother. The conquest removed a long-standing threat but also removed a buffer whose weakness had, in part, restrained Egyptian intervention in Palestine.
