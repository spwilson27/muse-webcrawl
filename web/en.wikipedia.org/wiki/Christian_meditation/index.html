Christian meditation

Christian meditation is a form of prayer in which a structured attempt is made to become aware of and reflect upon the revelations of God. The term is used for practices, rooted in Scripture and developed in monastic tradition, that focus the mind on a passage of the Bible, a divine attribute, or an episode in the life of Christ.

Traditions

In the Western tradition, lectio divina — slow, prayerful reading of Scripture — developed in Benedictine monasticism, and later methods were systematized by figures such as Ignatius of Loyola, whose Spiritual Exercises employ imaginative contemplation. In the Eastern Orthodox tradition, hesychasm centers on the repetition of the Jesus Prayer.

Modern practice

The 20th century saw a revival of Christian meditation, including the teaching of Benedictine monk John Main, who promoted meditation with a mantra ("maranatha"), leading to the World Community for Christian Meditation. Christian meditation differs from many Eastern techniques in being directed toward communion with God rather than the emptying of the mind.
