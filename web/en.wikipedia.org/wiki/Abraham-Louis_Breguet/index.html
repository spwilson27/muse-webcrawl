Abraham-Louis Breguet

Abraham-Louis Breguet (10 January 1747 – 17 September 1823) was a Swiss horologist who founded the watch company Breguet in Paris. Working for royalty and the scientific elite of Europe, he made a series of inventions that permanently shaped watchmaking, including the tourbillon, the self-winding watch, the overcoil hairspring that bears his name, and the modern guilloché dial.

Life

Breguet was born in Neuchâtel, then a Prussian principality, and was apprenticed to a Versailles watchmaker after moving to France as a boy. In 1775 he opened his own workshop on the Quai de l'Horloge in Paris. His refinement and taste brought him commissions from Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette — for whom legend holds he made the celebrated No. 160 "Marie Antoinette" pocket watch, a grand complication not finished until decades after both their deaths.

During the French Revolution his friendship with Jean-Paul Marat helped him escape to Switzerland in 1793; he returned in 1795 and rebuilt the business. Napoleon Bonaparte, Joséphine, the Tsar Alexander I and the Prince Regent of England became clients. In 1814 he was appointed watchmaker to the French Royal Navy, and he was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1816. He died in Paris in 1823; his name is inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.

Inventions

  • Self-winding watch (1780): the perpétuelle, wound by an oscillating weight as the wearer moved.
  • Tourbillon (patented 1801): a rotating carriage housing the escapement and balance, designed to average out the effects of gravity on a pocket watch's rate.
  • Breguet overcoil (1795): a hairspring whose outer coil is raised and curved inward, improving concentric "breathing" and timekeeping.
  • Pare-chute (1790): one of the first shock-protection devices for the balance staff.
  • Guilloché dials: engine-turned gold and silver dials, with the slender hands with an open "moon" tip and Arabic numerals that became known as Breguet hands and Breguet numerals.
  • The first wristwatch, made in 1810 for Caroline Murat, Queen of Naples.

Legacy

The firm Breguet continued under his descendants and remains a leading name in haute horlogerie, today part of the Swatch Group. The Marie Antoinette watch, stolen from a Jerusalem museum in 1983, was recovered in 2007 and is displayed at the L.A. Mayer Museum for Islamic Art.
