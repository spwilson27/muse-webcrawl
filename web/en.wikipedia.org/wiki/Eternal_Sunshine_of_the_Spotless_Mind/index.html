Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind is a 2004 American romantic science-fiction drama film directed by Michel Gondry and written by Charlie Kaufman, from a story by Kaufman, Gondry and Pierre Bismuth. It stars Jim Carrey as Joel Barish and Kate Winslet as Clementine Kruczynski, former lovers who each undergo a medical procedure to erase all memories of their relationship.

Plot

After a painful breakup, Clementine has her memories of Joel erased by the firm Lacuna, Inc. Joel, learning of this, undergoes the same procedure; but as his memories of Clementine are deleted one by one during a night of monitored sleep, he relives them and realises he does not want to lose her. Within his own mind he tries to hide Clementine in unrelated memories to preserve her. The attempt fails, yet after the procedure the two meet again by chance and, hearing recordings of the bitter things they once said about each other, choose to begin again anyway — "Okay." / "Okay."

Themes and production

The title quotes Alexander Pope's poem Eloisa to Abelard: "Eternal sunshine of the spotless mind! / Each pray'r accepted, and each wish resign'd". The film explores memory, identity and the pain that gives love its meaning. Gondry favoured in-camera effects, practical sets and improvised camerawork; the score is by Jon Brion, with songs by Beck and others. Tom Wilkinson plays Lacuna's Dr. Mierzwiak, with Kirsten Dunst, Mark Ruffalo and Elijah Wood as the technicians.

Reception

The film was a modest box-office success but became one of the most acclaimed films of its decade, appearing on numerous critics' lists of the greatest films of the 21st century. Kaufman, Gondry and Bismuth won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Winslet was nominated for Best Actress for her performance as the free-spirited, ever-changing-hair-coloured Clementine.
