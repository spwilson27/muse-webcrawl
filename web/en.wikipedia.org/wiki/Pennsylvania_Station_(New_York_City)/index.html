Pennsylvania Station (New York City)

LocationMidtown Manhattan, New York City
Opened1910 (original); 1968 (current)
OperatorsAmtrak, NJ Transit, Long Island Rail Road
RankingBusiest rail station in North America

Pennsylvania Station, commonly known as Penn Station, is the main intercity railroad station in New York City and the busiest transportation facility in the Western Hemisphere, serving more than 600,000 passengers on a typical weekday.

The original station

The original station, opened in 1910 by the Pennsylvania Railroad, was a Beaux-Arts masterpiece designed by McKim, Mead and White, with a vast travertine colonnade and a steel-and-glass train shed inspired by Roman baths. Its demolition between 1963 and 1966, to make way for Madison Square Garden and an underground station, galvanised the historic preservation movement in the United States and led to New York's landmarks law.

The current station

Today's Penn Station operates entirely underground beneath Madison Square Garden, serving Amtrak, NJ Transit and the Long Island Rail Road. Long criticised as cramped and confusing, it has been partly relieved by the Moynihan Train Hall, opened in 2021 in the former James A. Farley Post Office across Eighth Avenue, and further redevelopment plans continue to be debated.
