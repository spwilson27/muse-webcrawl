Coequalizer

In category theory, a coequalizer is a generalisation of a quotient by an equivalence relation to objects in an arbitrary category. It is the categorical dual of an equalizer: where an equalizer identifies where two morphisms agree, a coequalizer is a universal way of forcing two parallel morphisms to become equal by quotienting their common codomain.

Definition

Given a pair of parallel morphisms f, g : X → Y in a category, a coequalizer is an object Q together with a morphism q : Y → Q such that q ∘ f = q ∘ g, and which is universal with this property: for any object Z and morphism m : Y → Z with m ∘ f = m ∘ g, there exists a unique morphism u : Q → Z with m = u ∘ q. As with all universal constructions, a coequalizer, if it exists, is unique up to a unique isomorphism. Every coequalizer is an epimorphism, and a coequalizer of an equivalence relation's projections recovers the quotient object.

Examples

In the category of sets, the coequalizer of f, g : X → Y is the quotient of Y by the smallest equivalence relation identifying f(x) with g(x) for all x, together with the quotient map. In the category of groups, the coequalizer is the quotient of the codomain by the normal closure of the elements f(x)g(x)−1. Coequalizers, together with finite coproducts, suffice to construct all finite colimits.
