Harbor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A harbor (American English) or harbour (British English) is a sheltered body of water where ships, boats and barges can dock. The term is often used interchangeably with port, though a port is usually a man-made facility built for loading and unloading vessels and dropping off and picking up passengers, whereas a harbour may be natural or artificial and primarily provides safe anchorage and shelter from wind and waves.

Types

Natural harbours are formed by features of the coastline such as bays, estuaries, rias and fjords; notable examples include Sydney Harbour, Rio de Janeiro's Guanabara Bay and San Francisco Bay. Artificial harbours are deliberately constructed with breakwaters, sea walls or dredging, such as the harbour at Cherbourg or the Mulberry harbours used after the Normandy landings. Some harbours, such as Plymouth Sound and Pearl Harbor, serve major naval bases.

Works and maintenance

Harbour engineering includes the construction and upkeep of breakwaters, moles, jetties, quays, navigation channels and aids to navigation. Channels must often be dredged continuously to accept ships of deep draught, and harbour authorities regulate traffic, pilotage and anchorage within their jurisdiction.

Environmental issues

Because harbours are sheltered and often adjacent to cities, they accumulate pollutants, and dredging, ballast water discharge and introduced species are significant management concerns.
