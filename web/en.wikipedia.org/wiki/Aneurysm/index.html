Aneurysm

An aneurysm is an abnormal localised widening of a blood vessel, usually an artery, caused by weakness in the vessel wall. Aneurysms can occur anywhere but are most significant in the aorta (abdominal and thoracic aortic aneurysms) and in the arteries of the brain (intracranial aneurysms). Their chief danger is rupture, which causes life-threatening internal bleeding.

SpecialtyVascular surgery, neurosurgery, cardiology
TypesTrue, false (pseudoaneurysm), dissecting
Risk factorsAtherosclerosis, hypertension, smoking, connective-tissue disorders, family history
DiagnosisUltrasound, CT/MR angiography

Causes and types

True aneurysms involve all layers of the wall. Saccular aneurysms bulge from one side (the "berry" aneurysms of the cerebral circulation), while fusiform aneurysms dilate the whole circumference. Degenerative change associated with atherosclerosis is the commonest cause; genetic conditions such as Marfan and Ehlers–Danlos syndromes, infections (mycotic aneurysms), and trauma also contribute.

Management

Small, asymptomatic aneurysms are monitored with imaging and controlled for blood pressure and smoking. Large or symptomatic ones are repaired by open surgery (graft replacement or surgical clipping in the brain) or by endovascular techniques — stent-grafts for aortic aneurysms and coiling or flow diversion for intracranial aneurysms. Ruptured aneurysms are emergencies with high mortality.
