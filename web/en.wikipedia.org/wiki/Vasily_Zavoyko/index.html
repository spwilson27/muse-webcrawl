Vasily Stepanovich Zavoyko (Russian: Василий Степанович Завойко; 1809 – 28 February 1898) was a Russian admiral and the first military governor of Kamchatka. He is best remembered for organising the successful defence of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky against a British-French squadron in 1854, during the Crimean War.

Early career

Zavoyko joined the Imperial Russian Navy as a young man and served in the Black Sea and the Pacific. He took part in long-distance voyages, including expeditions connected with the Russian-American Company, and spent time in Russian America (Alaska), where he gained experience of Russia's remote Pacific possessions.

Governor of Kamchatka and the siege of Petropavlovsk

In 1849 Zavoyko was appointed the first military governor of Kamchatka, with his capital at Petropavlovsk. He developed the port, encouraged agriculture and trade, and strengthened its defences. When an Anglo-French squadron under Rear Admirals David Price and Auguste Febvrier-Despointes attacked Petropavlovsk in August–September 1854, Zavoyko's small garrison of roughly a thousand men, supported by shore batteries and the frigate Aurora, repelled two landing attempts and forced the allied squadron to withdraw — one of the few Russian successes of the Crimean War in the Pacific theatre.

In 1855, anticipating a larger attack, Zavoyko evacuated the settlement and moved the population to the Amur estuary, where he founded the post that became Nikolayevsk-on-Amur.

Later life

After the war Zavoyko served in the Baltic and in administrative posts, rising to the rank of admiral. Zavoyko Bay on the Kamchatka coast and other geographic features bear his name, and a monument in Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky commemorates his defence of the city.
