The Beijing Subway is the rapid transit system of Beijing, China. First opened in 1969, it is one of the oldest metro systems in mainland China and, after a vast expansion program in the 2000s and 2010s, has become one of the longest and busiest urban rail networks in the world, carrying billions of passenger trips per year.

History

Construction began in 1965 under a plan that combined civilian transport with civil-defense functions. The first line — the western portion of today's Line 1 — opened on National Day, 1 October 1969, initially restricted to officials before public service expanded. Line 2, following the Ming dynasty city wall around the old city, was completed in stages through 1987. The network grew slowly until the late 1990s; an enormous building program followed Beijing's successful bid for the 2008 Olympics. New lines opened nearly every year thereafter, and the system passed 500 km in 2014 and 800 km in the early 2020s. Beijing was the first Chinese city whose metro exceeded the Shanghai Metro in length at various points, and the two systems have traded the title of world's longest.

Network

The network comprises more than 25 lines, most numbered (Lines 1–19 and beyond), plus the Capital Airport Express, Daxing Airport Express, the Yizhuang and Fangshan suburban lines, modern tram lines, and the Xijiao light-rail line. Lines 1 and 2 form the core of the old city; ring Line 10 is among the world's longest circle lines. Stations feature full-height platform screen doors on newer lines, and fares are distance-based, payable by the Yikatong card, mobile apps, and QR codes.

Operations

Most lines are operated by the state-owned Beijing Mass Transit Railway Operation Corporation; several newer lines are operated under public–private partnerships, including by the Beijing MTR joint venture. Trains run roughly from 5:00 to 23:00, at headways as short as about two minutes on the busiest lines.
