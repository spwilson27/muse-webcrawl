Dōgen

Dōgen Zenji (道元禅師; 26 January 1200 – 22 September 1253), also known as Eihei Dōgen, was a Japanese Zen Buddhist monk, writer, poet, and philosopher who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. He is regarded as one of the most important religious thinkers in Japanese history and the author of the Shōbōgenzō ("Treasury of the True Dharma Eye"), a landmark of Japanese philosophy.

Life

Born into an aristocratic family in Kyoto, Dōgen was orphaned young and ordained as a Tendai monk on Mount Hiei at about age twelve. Dissatisfied with the teaching he found, he became a disciple of Eisai's successor Myōzen at Kennin-ji and in 1223 travelled with him to China, where he studied Caodong (Sōtō) Chan under the master Rujing at Mount Tiantong. There he experienced enlightenment on hearing Rujing's phrase "cast off body and mind" (shinjin datsuraku).

Teaching

Returning to Japan in 1227, Dōgen emphasised the practice of shikantaza — "just sitting" meditation — and taught that practice and enlightenment are one (shushō-ittō), rather than practice being a means to a separate goal. In 1243 he moved his community to the mountains of Echizen Province, founding the temple Eihei-ji, which remains one of the two head temples of Sōtō Zen. His writings, including the Bendōwa, the Genjōkōan fascicle, and instructions for monastic life such as the Tenzo Kyōkun, explore time, being, language, and the unity of practice and realisation with unusual philosophical depth.
