Distance measuring equipment

Distance measuring equipment (DME) is a radio navigation technology that measures the slant range distance between an aircraft and a ground station by timing radio signals in the UHF band. It is commonly paired with a VOR beacon, allowing pilots to determine both bearing and distance from the station.

Operation

The airborne equipment interrogates the ground transponder with pairs of pulses; the ground station replies after a fixed delay (50 microseconds). The aircraft measures the round-trip time, subtracts the delay, and computes the distance, displayed in nautical miles. DME operates between 962 and 1213 MHz and provides accuracy typically better than 0.2 nautical miles.

Uses

DME distances define fixes, holding patterns, and instrument approach procedures. Combined DME/VOR stations and the military TACAN system, which provides both bearing and distance, form a backbone of conventional air navigation, though GNSS-based navigation is increasingly primary. The distance shown is slant range — the straight-line distance to the station — which slightly exceeds horizontal distance at high altitude near the station.
