Social skills

Social skills are the skills people use to communicate and interact with each other, both verbally and non-verbally, through gestures, body language, and personal appearance. They include speech, listening, turn-taking, empathy, cooperation, and the ability to read social situations. Human beings are social creatures, and the development of social skills is a central part of childhood development and a predictor of success in education, work, and relationships.

Components

Social skills are often grouped into several categories:

  • Communication skills — speaking clearly, listening actively, and interpreting nonverbal cues such as facial expressions, eye contact, and posture.
  • Empathy and perspective-taking — understanding and sharing the feelings of others.
  • Assertiveness — expressing one's own needs and rights without aggression or passivity.
  • Interpersonal problem-solving — negotiating, compromising, and resolving conflicts.
  • Relationship skills — initiating and maintaining friendships, cooperating in groups, and observing social norms and manners.

Development

Social skills develop from infancy through attachment, play, and imitation. Peer interaction in childhood and adolescence is particularly important: children learn turn-taking, sharing, and conflict resolution through play. Difficulties with social skills are associated with a range of conditions, including autism spectrum disorder, social anxiety disorder, and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, and may also follow from social isolation or deprivation.

Social skills training

Social skills training (SST) is a form of behaviour therapy used to improve social competence, particularly in people with mental health conditions such as schizophrenia, social anxiety, and depression, and in autistic people. Techniques include instruction, modelling, role-play rehearsal, feedback, and homework assignments practising skills in real-life settings.

Measurement

Social skills are measured with rating scales, behavioural observations, and role-play tests. Instruments include the Social Skills Inventory and various parent- and teacher-rated questionnaires for children.
