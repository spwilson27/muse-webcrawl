Rootes Group

The Rootes Group was a British automobile manufacturer founded in 1913 by William Rootes as a car sales business, which grew into one of Britain's largest motor groups, owning the Hillman, Humber, Singer, Sunbeam, Talbot and Commer marques. From the 1960s the company was progressively acquired by the American Chrysler Corporation, becoming Chrysler UK in 1970, before its remnants passed to Peugeot in 1978.

History

William and Reginald Rootes expanded from a Hawkhurst dealership into distribution and then, in 1928, took control of Hillman and Humber after the Wall Street crash era reshaping of the industry, later adding Singer (1956) and the Sunbeam-Talbot marques. During the Second World War Rootes factories built aircraft, including Blenheim bombers, and armoured cars. Postwar successes included the Hillman Minx, the Sunbeam Alpine sports car, and the rear-engined Hillman Imp (1963), built at a new Linwood plant in Scotland.

Decline and takeover

Losses on the Imp, aging models and industrial disputes weakened the group. Chrysler took a controlling stake in 1964 and full ownership by 1967, renaming it Chrysler UK in 1970. In 1978 Chrysler's European operations were sold to PSA Peugeot Citroën, which revived the Talbot name for the former Rootes range; the last Linwood production ended in 1981.
