The Knavesmire is a large area of common grassland in the south-west of York, England, lying to one side of the Tadcaster Road (A1036). It is best known as the home of York Racecourse and, historically, as the site of York's place of public execution.

History

The Knavesmire was common pasture on the edge of the medieval city. From the 16th century until 1801, executions for the county were carried out at the Tyburn on the Knavesmire, near the Tadcaster Road; the highwayman Dick Turpin was hanged there on 7 April 1739. The gallows site is marked by a small plaque and mound.

Horse racing

Horse racing moved to the Knavesmire in 1731, making York Racecourse one of the oldest centres of the sport in Britain. The course hosts the Ebor Festival in August, including the Ebor Handicap (first run in 1843), and other major meetings such as the Dante Festival in May and the John Smith's Cup meeting in July.

Other uses

The open space is used for recreation, fairs and public events, and parts of it are managed as grassland habitat. York Crematorium stands on the edge of the Knavesmire at Bishopthorpe Road.
