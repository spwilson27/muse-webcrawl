Business ethics

Business ethics (also known as corporate ethics) is a form of applied ethics that examines ethical principles and moral problems arising in a business environment. It applies to all aspects of business conduct — the behavior of individuals and of organizations — and draws on normative ethical theory as well as descriptive study of actual practices.

Theoretical debate

A central debate opposes shareholder primacy — Milton Friedman famously argued in 1970 that the social responsibility of business is to increase its profits within the rules — against stakeholder theory (R. Edward Freeman), which holds that firms owe duties to employees, customers, suppliers, communities, and the environment as well as investors. Archie Carroll's pyramid of corporate social responsibility layers economic, legal, ethical, and philanthropic responsibilities.

Areas of concern

Business ethics covers accounting and financial fraud (as in the Enron and WorldCom scandals), insider trading, bribery and corruption, discrimination and workplace rights, executive pay, marketing and product safety, privacy and data protection, environmental responsibility, and the ethics of globalization and supply chains.

Practice

In practice, companies implement ethics through codes of conduct, ethics and compliance officers, whistleblower protections (strengthened by laws such as the Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002), ethics training, and sustainability reporting. Business ethics is a standard subject in business schools and the focus of academic journals such as the Journal of Business Ethics.
