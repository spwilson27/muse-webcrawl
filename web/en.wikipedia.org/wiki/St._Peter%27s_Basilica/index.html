The Papal Basilica of Saint Peter in the Vatican, commonly known as St. Peter's Basilica, is a church of the Italian High Renaissance located in Vatican City, within Rome, Italy. It is regarded as one of the holiest sites of Christianity and the largest church in the world by interior measure.

History

The basilica stands on the site of Old St. Peter's Basilica, built in the 4th century over the traditional burial place of Saint Peter, chief of the apostles. Pope Julius II commissioned the new church in 1506. Successive architects — Donato Bramante, Raphael, Michelangelo, Carlo Maderno, and Gian Lorenzo Bernini — shaped the building over 120 years.

Architecture

Michelangelo designed the great dome, completed by his pupil Giacomo della Porta in 1590; at 136.6 metres it remains the tallest dome in the world. Maderno extended the nave and added the façade, while Bernini designed the vast elliptical St. Peter's Square and the bronze baldacchino over the papal altar. Inside are masterpieces including Michelangelo's Pietà and Bernini's Cathedra Petri.

Significance

As the burial site of Saint Peter and most popes, and the venue of major papal liturgies, the basilica is the foremost pilgrimage church of Roman Catholicism, though the pope's cathedral is actually Saint John Lateran. It can hold some 60,000 people and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of Vatican City.
