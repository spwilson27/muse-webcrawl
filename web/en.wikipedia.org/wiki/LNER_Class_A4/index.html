LNER Class A4

LNER Class A4
Designer: Nigel Gresley
Built: 1935–1938
Number built: 35
Configuration: 4-6-2 Pacific

The London and North Eastern Railway (LNER) Class A4 is a class of streamlined 4-6-2 steam locomotives designed by Nigel Gresley for the LNER in 1935. Thirty-five were built to haul express passenger trains on the East Coast Main Line between London and Edinburgh.

Speed record

The most famous member of the class, No. 4468 Mallard, set the world speed record for steam locomotives on 3 July 1938, reaching 126 mph (203 km/h) on Stoke Bank, a record that still stands.

Design

The locomotives featured a streamlined casing inspired by wind-tunnel testing, three cylinders, and large 6 ft 8 in driving wheels. Initially fitted with valances covering the wheels, these were removed during the Second World War to ease maintenance.

Preservation

Six A4s survive in preservation, including Mallard at the National Railway Museum in York, Sir Nigel Gresley, Union of South Africa, Bittern, and two locomotives in North America, Dwight D. Eisenhower and Dominion of Canada.
