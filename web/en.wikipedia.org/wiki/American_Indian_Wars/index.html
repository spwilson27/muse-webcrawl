American Indian Wars

The American Indian Wars, also called the American Frontier Wars or Indian Wars, were a series of conflicts between Native American nations and European colonial powers and later the United States, fought from the colonial period into the early twentieth century. They were not a single war but hundreds of campaigns, raids, treaties, removals and massacres connected with land, sovereignty and expansion.

Course

Early conflicts included King Philip's War, the French and Indian War's Indigenous dimensions, and Pontiac's War. In the nineteenth century the United States pursued removal in the Southeast, fought wars in the Old Northwest and Florida, and after the Civil War campaigned across the Great Plains and Southwest in conflicts involving the Lakota, Cheyenne, Apache, Nez Perce and many other peoples.

Consequences

Disease, warfare, forced relocation and destruction of food sources such as the bison devastated Native populations and political independence. Reservations, allotment and assimilation policies followed military conquest. Modern scholarship and Native nations emphasise resistance, survival and the continuing legal force of treaties rather than treating the wars as an inevitable prelude to settlement.
