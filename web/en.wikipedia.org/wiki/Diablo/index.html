Diablo

Image: Diablo cover art

Diablo is a 1996 action role-playing dungeon crawler video game developed by Blizzard North and published by Blizzard Entertainment for Windows. The first instalment of the Diablo franchise, it is set in the fictional world of Sanctuary, in the town of Tristram in the kingdom of Khanduras. The player controls a lone hero who battles the demon Diablo, Lord of Terror, in the catacombs beneath the town's cathedral.

The game was a critical and commercial success, and is credited with popularising the action role-playing game genre. It introduced procedurally generated levels, a point-and-click interface, and an emphasis on collecting randomised magical items ("loot").

Gameplay

The player chooses one of three character classes — the Warrior, the Rogue, or the Sorcerer — and descends through sixteen levels of a dungeon beneath Tristram Cathedral, fighting monsters, completing quests, and acquiring increasingly powerful weapons, armour, and spells. Character statistics (strength, magic, dexterity, and vitality) improve as the player gains experience. The final level pits the hero against Diablo himself.

Plot

Diablo's soulstone was buried beneath Tristram by the Horadrim. King Leoric, corrupted by the demon's influence, descended into madness, and his younger son Albrecht was kidnapped and possessed by Diablo. The hero ventures into the labyrinth to destroy the demon; after defeating him, the hero removes the soulstone and drives it into their own forehead in an attempt to contain Diablo's essence — an act that sets the stage for the sequel.

Development and release

The game was developed by Condor Games, which was acquired by Blizzard and renamed Blizzard North during development. It was originally conceived as a turn-based game before being redesigned as a real-time title. Diablo was released in North America in January 1997, following a December 1996 ship date, and later ported to the PlayStation and Mac OS. An expansion pack, Diablo: Hellfire, developed by Synergistic Software, followed in 1997.

Reception and legacy

The game received widespread acclaim for its atmosphere, gothic art direction, musical score by Matt Uelmen, and addictive gameplay loop, and it sold millions of copies. It spawned the sequels Diablo II (2000), Diablo III (2012), and Diablo IV (2023), and its design influenced numerous later "Diablo clones" and loot-driven action RPGs.
