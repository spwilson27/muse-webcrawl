Broadmoor Hospital is a high-security psychiatric hospital in Crowthorne, Berkshire, England. It is the oldest of England's three high-security hospitals, the others being Ashworth Hospital in Merseyside and Rampton Secure Hospital in Nottinghamshire. Broadmoor treats men detained under the Mental Health Act 1983 who present a grave and immediate danger to the public, including patients transferred from prison and those sent by the courts.

History

Broadmoor opened in 1863 as the Broadmoor Criminal Lunatic Asylum, the first institution built under the Criminal Lunatic Asylums Act 1860; its first patient was a woman detained for infanticide. The architect was Joshua Jebb, and the site was chosen for its rural isolation within reach of London. Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Broadmoor held many notorious patients, including the artist Richard Dadd, the "Ripper" suspect-related cases of the Victorian era, and later figures such as the Moors murderer Ian Brady (later moved to Ashworth), Peter Sutcliffe, Robert Maudsley, Kenneth Erskine and Ronnie Kray, who died there in 1995.

Modern hospital

The hospital was long administered centrally; after the 1989 Reid report it became a special health authority and later passed to NHS trust management. A major redevelopment replaced the Victorian buildings with a new facility on the same site, opened in 2019, with about 240 beds across wards for patients with mental illness and personality disorder. Broadmoor no longer admits women, who are treated at Rampton and in medium-secure services. Despite its popular image, the majority of patients have committed less notorious offences or no offence at all and are detained purely for treatment because of the risk they pose.
