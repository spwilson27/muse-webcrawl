Battle of Bataan

The Battle of Bataan (7 January – 9 April 1942) was fought by the United States and the Philippine Commonwealth against Japan during the Philippines campaign of the Second World War. After the Japanese invasion of Luzon in December 1941, American and Filipino forces under General Douglas MacArthur withdrew into the Bataan Peninsula and the island fortress of Corregidor, where they held out for three months against the Imperial Japanese Army under General Masaharu Homma. The surrender of Bataan on 9 April 1942 — the largest surrender in American military history — was followed by the infamous Bataan Death March.

The campaign

MacArthur's forces — about 80,000 American and Filipino troops, soon joined by tens of thousands of civilian refugees — established successive defensive lines across the peninsula, from the Abucay–Mauban line in January to the final Orion–Bagac line. Starved of food and medicine and ravaged by malaria and dysentery, the defenders nonetheless repulsed the first Japanese offensives, delaying Tokyo's timetable for conquest of Southeast Asia. MacArthur was ordered to Australia in March, leaving General Jonathan Wainwright in command; General Edward King commanded on Bataan itself. After a heavy Japanese offensive beginning 3 April with massed artillery and air attack, the defence collapsed, and on 9 April King surrendered some 76,000 troops.

The Death March and aftermath

Japanese forces marched the prisoners roughly 100 kilometres (the Bataan Death March) from Mariveles and Bagac to San Fernando, then by rail to Camp O'Donnell. Thousands of Filipino and American prisoners died of disease, starvation, beatings and summary execution along the way; estimates of deaths range from several thousand to around 18,000, most of them Filipinos. Corregidor held out until 6 May 1942. Homma was later tried and executed for war crimes connected with the march. Bataan remains a potent symbol of sacrifice in both the United States and the Philippines, commemorated each 9 April as the Day of Valour (Araw ng Kagitingan).
