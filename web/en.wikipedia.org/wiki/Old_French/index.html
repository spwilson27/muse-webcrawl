Old French

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A manuscript of a chanson de geste in Old French.

Old French (franceis, françois, romanz) was the language spoken in most of northern France from roughly the ninth to the fourteenth century. It developed from the Vulgar Latin spoken in Gaul, heavily influenced by the Gaulish substrate and by the Frankish language of the Germanic conquerors — from which it took, among other things, its very name and much military and administrative vocabulary. Old French was not a single uniform language but a continuum of dialects (the langues d'oïl), including Francien (of the Île-de-France), Norman, Picard, Champenois and Anglo-Norman, the variety carried to England after 1066.

History and texts

The earliest surviving text in a recognisably French vernacular is the Strasbourg Oaths of 842, sworn between the grandsons of Charlemagne. Major literary monuments include the Chanson de Roland (c. 1100), the oldest major work of French literature; the Arthurian romances of Chrétien de Troyes (twelfth century); the fabliaux, courtly lyric and the Roman de la Rose. Old French was a prestige language far beyond France: it was the language of the English court for centuries, of the crusader states, and of international chivalric literature, and it massively influenced English vocabulary — by some estimates a large fraction of modern English words entered through French.

Linguistic features

Old French differed from modern French in several striking ways. Most nouns preserved a two-case system inherited in simplified form from Latin: a nominative (subject) case and an oblique case. Verb conjugation was richer, word order freer, and null subjects common. Its phonology included diphthongs and affricates (such as /ts/ and /dʒ/) that have since simplified, and nasal vowels were developing from vowel + nasal sequences. Over the Middle French period (14th–16th centuries) the case system collapsed, pronunciation and spelling diverged, and the Francien dialect, codified by royal administration, became the basis of the modern standard language.
