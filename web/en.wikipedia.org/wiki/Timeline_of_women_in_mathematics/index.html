Timeline of women in mathematics

This is a timeline of women in mathematics, recording notable firsts and achievements despite the historical barriers women faced in entering the field.

Antiquity and the Middle Ages

c. 350–415 CE: Hypatia of Alexandria, the earliest woman mathematician of whom substantial knowledge survives, taught astronomy and philosophy and edited mathematical works. c. 1150: In India, Bhāskara II's works were later associated in legend with his daughter Lilavati.

18th and 19th centuries

1748: Maria Gaetana Agnesi published Instituzioni analitiche, an influential calculus textbook. Late 1700s: Sophie Germain corresponded with Legendre and Gauss under a male pseudonym and made foundational contributions to number theory and elasticity. 1888: Sofia Kovalevskaya won the Prix Bordin for her work on the rotation of a solid body.

20th century

1918: Emmy Noether proved Noether's theorem, a cornerstone of modern physics and abstract algebra. 1943: Grace Hopper joined the US Navy and became a pioneer of computer programming. 2014: Maryam Mirzakhani became the first woman to receive the Fields Medal, for her work on the dynamics of Riemann surfaces.
