Product (category theory)

The product is characterised by a universal property involving projection morphisms.

In category theory, the product of two (or more) objects is a notion designed to capture the essence behind constructions in other areas of mathematics such as the Cartesian product of sets, the direct product of groups and the product of topological spaces. Essentially, the product of a family of objects is the "most general" object that admits a morphism to each of the given objects.

Definition

Let C be a category with objects X1 and X2. The product of X1 and X2 is an object X, typically denoted X1 × X2, together with two morphisms π1 : X → X1 and π2 : X → X2 called the canonical projections, such that for every object Y and every pair of morphisms f1 : Y → X1 and f2 : Y → X2 there exists a unique morphism f : Y → X with f1 = π1 ∘ f and f2 = π2 ∘ f.

Products need not exist in an arbitrary category, but when they do they are unique up to a unique isomorphism. The dual notion, obtained by reversing all arrows, is the coproduct.

Examples

  • In the category of sets, the product is the Cartesian product, with the usual coordinate projections.
  • In the category of groups, it is the direct product of groups.
  • In the category of topological spaces, it is the product space with the product topology.
  • In a partially ordered set regarded as a category, the product of two elements is their greatest lower bound (meet), if it exists.

Distributivity and limits

The product is a special case of the general categorical notion of a limit — specifically the limit of a discrete diagram. Categories with all finite products are called Cartesian categories, and a category with finite products in which morphisms can be "curried" is a Cartesian closed category, the categorical model of typed lambda calculus.
