Kapellmeister

Kapellmeister (from German Kapelle, "chapel" or musical ensemble, and Meister, "master") is a German word designating the leader of an ensemble of musicians. In English it is sometimes rendered "chapelmaster" or "music director". The term was most common in the 16th to 19th centuries, when musicians were employed by courts and churches.

Role

A Kapellmeister was responsible for composing music for court or church occasions, directing rehearsals and performances, training singers and instrumentalists, maintaining instruments and music libraries, and sometimes for general administrative and even domestic duties for the patron. The position carried great prestige in the German musical world but typically involved the status of a servant within the court household.

Notable holders

Famous Kapellmeisters include Johann Sebastian Bach (at Weimar and Köthen, and as Kantor in Leipzig), George Frideric Handel (appointed by the Elector of Hanover), Joseph Haydn (at the Esterházy court), Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart's father Leopold Mozart (vice-Kapellmeister at Salzburg), Carl Maria von Weber (at Dresden), and Richard Wagner (at the Dresden court).

Modern usage

In modern German-speaking musical life, Kapellmeister survives as a title for conductors in opera houses, below the Generalmusikdirektor, and the word is occasionally used by critics in English for historical conductors.
