Operation Vistula

Operation Vistula (Polish: Akcja "Wisła") was a military and resettlement operation carried out in 1947 by the communist authorities of Poland, with support from the Soviet Union and Czechoslovakia, involving the forced deportation of approximately 140,000–150,000 Ukrainians, Lemkos, Boykos and other members of the Ukrainian minority from south-eastern Poland to the northern and western territories (the so-called Recovered Territories) acquired from Germany after the Second World War.

Background

Following the border changes of 1945, a substantial Ukrainian minority remained within Poland's new frontiers. The Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA) was active in the region, and population exchanges between Poland and Soviet Ukraine in 1944–1946 had already transferred hundreds of thousands of people. The immediate pretext for the operation was the assassination on 28 March 1947 of General Karol Świerczewski, a deputy minister of national defence, in an ambush attributed to the UPA near Baligród.

The operation

Beginning on 28 April 1947, military units surrounded Ukrainian and Lemko villages, giving inhabitants only a few hours to pack before transporting them to collection points and railway stations. Deportees were scattered in small groups across the Recovered Territories, deliberately dispersed so that no settlement held more than a small proportion of Ukrainian families, with the aim of forced assimilation. Suspected UPA members and sympathisers were sent to the Central Labour Camp in Jaworzno, where several thousand were imprisoned and around 160 died.

Consequences

The operation destroyed the centuries-old Ukrainian and Lemko communities of the Carpathian foothills; villages were abandoned, churches left to ruin, and cultural property lost. In 1990 the Polish Senate condemned Operation Vistula, and subsequent Polish presidents have expressed regret for the deportations. The operation remains a sensitive subject in Polish–Ukrainian relations and in Lemko memory.
