Puerperal infections

Puerperal infections, historically called childbed fever or puerperal fever, are bacterial infections of the female reproductive tract following childbirth or miscarriage, typically occurring in the first ten days after delivery (the puerperium).

History

Childbed fever was a leading cause of maternal death in maternity hospitals until the 19th century. Ignaz Semmelweis demonstrated in 1847 that hand disinfection with chlorinated lime dramatically reduced deaths in Vienna, and Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr. had argued for its contagious nature in America. Germ theory and antiseptic practice later confirmed their insights.

Causes and treatment

The most common site is the uterus (endometritis); organisms include streptococci, staphylococci and anaerobes. Modern treatment is with antibiotics, and the condition is now uncommon where hygienic delivery care is available, though it remains a significant cause of maternal morbidity worldwide.
