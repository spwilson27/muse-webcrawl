Albert V (29 February 1528 – 24 October 1579), known as Albert the Magnanimous, was Duke of Bavaria from 1550 until his death. A committed champion of the Catholic Counter-Reformation, he turned Munich into a leading centre of Catholic culture, music and collecting in Renaissance Germany.

Reign and the Counter-Reformation

Albert succeeded his father William IV in 1550. Educated by Catholic tutors and married to Anna of Austria, daughter of Emperor Ferdinand I, he invited the Jesuits to Bavaria, granted them colleges in Ingolstadt and Munich, and enforced Catholic orthodoxy while initially tolerating some concessions to Protestant nobles — concessions he later withdrew after defeating the so-called Counts' conspiracy of 1563–64. His enforcement of the decrees of the Council of Trent made Bavaria the bulwark of German Catholicism.

Patronage of the arts

Albert spent lavishly on culture. He appointed the Franco-Flemish composer Orlande de Lassus to his court chapel, making Munich a musical capital of Europe. He founded the ducal library (the nucleus of today's Bavarian State Library), amassed a famous Kunstkammer of art and curiosities, and built the Antiquarium in the Munich Residenz to house his collection of classical sculpture — one of the largest Renaissance halls north of the Alps.

Death and succession

Albert's spending left heavy debts, but his policies secured Bavaria's Catholic identity for centuries. He died in 1579 and was succeeded by his son William V.
