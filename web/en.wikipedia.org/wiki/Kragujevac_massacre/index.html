Kragujevac massacre

Šumarice Memorial Park, where thousands of Kragujevac civilians were shot on 21 October 1941

The Kragujevac massacre was the mass murder of between about 2,300 and 2,800 Serb men and boys of the city of Kragujevac in German-occupied Serbia by Wehrmacht soldiers on 21 October 1941. It was one of the largest reprisal massacres of the Second World War in Yugoslavia, carried out under a policy of executing 100 hostages for every German soldier killed by the resistance.

Date21 October 1941
LocationKragujevac, German-occupied Serbia
PerpetratorsWehrmacht (717th and 724th infantry regiments)

Background and massacre

Following a joint Partisan–Chetnik attack on Gornji Milanovac in which German soldiers were killed and wounded, the military commander in Serbia, Franz Böhme, ordered reprisals at the ratio of 100 executions per dead German and 50 per wounded. On 19–20 October, troops rounded up the male population of Kragujevac and surrounding villages — workers, teachers, priests, Jews, Roma, and entire classes of high-school boys taken directly from their classrooms. On 21 October the prisoners were marched in groups to the Šumarice meadows and shot throughout the day. In all, roughly 2,300 to 2,800 people were killed in Kragujevac, in addition to about 500 in nearby villages.

Memory

The massacre became one of the defining national traumas of Serbia. The poet Desanka Maksimović's poem "Krvava bajka" ("A Bloody Fairy Tale"), written in response to the killing of the schoolboys, is learned by Serbian schoolchildren to this day. The Šumarice Memorial Park (October in Kragujevac Memorial Park), established in 1953 over the mass graves, contains a museum (the 21 October Museum) and a series of monuments, including the Monument to the Murdered Schoolchildren and their Teachers. Every 21 October a commemoration, the Great School Lesson, is held at the site.
