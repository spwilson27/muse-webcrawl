Sone

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The sone is a unit of loudness, the subjective perception of sound pressure. Doubling the perceived loudness doubles the sone value. The unit was proposed by Stanley Smith Stevens in 1936 as part of a scale of loudness measurement based on listeners' judgements, and it remains the standard unit in which the noise output of equipment such as fans and appliances is rated in North America.

Definition

One sone is defined as the loudness of a pure tone of frequency 1 kHz at 40 dB sound pressure level — equivalently, a loudness level of 40 phons. Loudness in sones, S, is related to loudness level in phons, L, by S = 2(L − 40)/10. Thus a sound of 50 phons is 2 sones, 60 phons is 4 sones, and 30 phons is 0.5 sones. For sounds other than 1 kHz tones, the loudness level in phons is obtained from equal-loudness contours and then converted.

SourceApprox. loudness (sones)
Quiet bedroom at night0.1–0.5
Refrigerator hum0.5–1
Normal conversation at 1 m1–4
Busy street traffic8–32

Because the sone is a psychoacoustic (perceptual) quantity, its measurement depends on the listening conditions and the standardised equal-loudness contours used (see ISO 226). Appliance ratings in sones, for example for bathroom exhaust fans (where quiet models are rated below 1.0 sone), let consumers compare perceived noise directly rather than raw decibel values.
