Electronic dance music

Electronic dance music (EDM), also referred to as dance music, club music, or simply dance, is a broad range of percussive electronic music genres made largely for nightclubs, raves, and festivals. It is generally produced for playback by DJs who create seamless selections of tracks, called a DJ mix, by segueing from one recording to another. EDM producers also perform their music live in a concert or festival setting in what is sometimes called a live PA.

History

EDM's roots lie in 1970s disco and the electronic experimentation of groups such as Kraftwerk. In the early 1980s, producers in Chicago created house music using drum machines such as the Roland TR-808 and TR-909; in Detroit, techno emerged from artists including Juan Atkins, Derrick May, and Kevin Saunderson, while acid house — built on the squelch of the Roland TB-303 — ignited the UK's "Second Summer of Love" in 1988 and the rave era. The 1990s saw the proliferation of trance, jungle/drum and bass, big beat, and superstar DJ culture.

Mainstream boom

In the late 2000s and early 2010s, EDM achieved massive mainstream popularity in the United States — the term "EDM" itself was popularized by the American music industry in this period. Festivals such as Electric Daisy Carnival, Ultra Music Festival, and Tomorrowland grew into stadium-scale events; artists like Skrillex (dubstep), Avicii, Calvin Harris, Swedish House Mafia, and David Guetta crossed over to pop charts, and EDM sounds permeated mainstream pop and hip hop production. Analysts valued the global industry in the billions of dollars annually.

Characteristics

EDM tracks are typically built in a digital audio workstation around a repetitive four-on-the-floor or breakbeat rhythm, synthesized basslines, and structures optimized for DJ mixing: long intros/outros, breakdowns, and climactic "drops." Tempos range from around 110 BPM (some house) to 160–180 BPM (drum and bass, hardcore). The culture surrounding EDM — clubs, warehouse raves, festivals, and PLUR ("peace love unity respect") ethos in US rave culture — is integral to the genre's identity.
