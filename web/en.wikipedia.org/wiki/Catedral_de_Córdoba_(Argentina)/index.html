Catedral de Córdoba (Argentina)

Location: Córdoba, Argentina
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Consecrated: 1784
Style: Baroque, Neoclassical

The Córdoba Cathedral (Spanish: Catedral de Córdoba), formally the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Assumption, is the central church of Córdoba, Argentina, and one of the country's oldest colonial churches still in use. It stands on the Plaza San Martín, facing the historic Cabildo.

History

The first church on the site was built in 1580. Construction of the present building began in 1582 but progressed slowly and suffered collapses; Jesuit architects including Gian Battista Primoli contributed to its design. The church was consecrated in 1784, though its facade was completed later, and its towers were finished in the 19th century.

Architecture

The cathedral mixes Spanish Baroque and Neoclassical elements, with a prominent dome, twin towers, and a richly decorated interior featuring paintings by Emilio Caraffa and other Argentine artists. It was declared a National Historic Monument.
