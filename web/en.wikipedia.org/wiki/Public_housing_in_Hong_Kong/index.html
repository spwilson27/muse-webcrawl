Public housing in Hong Kong


A public housing estate in Hong Kong

Public housing in Hong Kong is a set of mass housing programmes through which the Government of Hong Kong provides affordable housing for lower-income residents. It is one of the largest public housing programmes in the world: around 45 per cent of Hong Kong's population lives in public rental housing or subsidised sale flats, and roughly 30 per cent live in public rental estates alone.

History

The programme began after the Shek Kip Mei fire on Christmas night 1953 left nearly 60,000 squatters homeless. The government responded with a large resettlement programme, building simple multi-storey resettlement blocks. The Hong Kong Housing Authority was established in 1973 under Governor Murray MacLehose, whose Ten-year Housing Programme aimed to provide self-contained housing for 1.8 million people.

Types

Public rental housing is provided mainly by the Hong Kong Housing Authority and the Hong Kong Housing Society, allocated by waiting list to eligible low-income households at subsidised rents. Subsidised sale schemes include the Home Ownership Scheme (HOS), launched in 1978, which sells flats to eligible buyers at a discount to market price.

Estates

Well-known estates include Choi Hung Estate, famous for its rainbow-coloured blocks, the now-demolished Kowloon Walled City site, and Wah Fu Estate. Early resettlement blocks (Mark I and Mark II) have largely been redeveloped into modern estates with community facilities.
