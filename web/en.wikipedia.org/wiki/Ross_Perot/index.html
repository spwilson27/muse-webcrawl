Ross Perot

Henry Ross Perot (June 27, 1930 – July 9, 2019) was an American businessman and politician. He founded Electronic Data Systems (EDS) in 1962 and Perot Systems in 1988, becoming a billionaire. He ran for president of the United States as an independent candidate in 1992 and as the candidate of the Reform Party in 1996. His 1992 campaign won 18.9% of the popular vote — the strongest showing by a third-party or independent presidential candidate since Theodore Roosevelt in 1912 — though he won no electoral votes.

Early life and business career

Perot was born in Texarkana, Texas, and attended the United States Naval Academy, graduating in 1953. After leaving the Navy in 1957 he worked as a salesman for IBM, where he excelled but became frustrated with the company's bureaucracy. In 1962, with an initial $1,000, he founded Electronic Data Systems in Dallas, which provided computer data processing services to businesses and government agencies. EDS went public in 1968; its share price rose dramatically, making Perot a billionaire and earning him the nickname of the "fastest, richest Texan" from Fortune magazine.

In 1979, after two EDS employees were imprisoned in Iran during the Iranian Revolution, Perot organised and financed a private rescue mission led by retired Army Colonel Arthur "Bull" Simons, which freed the men; the episode was chronicled in Ken Follett's book On Wings of Eagles. In 1984 General Motors bought a controlling interest in EDS for $2.5 billion, but clashes with GM management led to Perot's departure in 1986 in a buyout worth about $700 million. He then founded Perot Systems in 1988, which was sold to Dell in 2009 for about $3.9 billion. Earlier, in 1984, his foundation bought a copy of Magna Carta, which was displayed at the National Archives and eventually sold at auction in 2007 for $21.3 million.

Political career

Perot became known in the 1960s and 1970s for his advocacy on behalf of American prisoners of war in Vietnam. In February 1992, on CNN's Larry King Live, he announced he would run for president if volunteers placed him on the ballot in all 50 states. Running as an independent against President George H. W. Bush and Arkansas Governor Bill Clinton, he campaigned against the federal budget deficit — using televised infomercials with charts to explain fiscal policy — and against the proposed North American Free Trade Agreement, which he memorably described as creating a "giant sucking sound" of jobs going to Mexico.

Perot briefly led in national polls in mid-1992, then abruptly withdrew in July before re-entering the race in October. In the November election he won 19.7 million votes (18.9%) but carried no states and won no electoral votes. In 1995 he founded the Reform Party and ran as its candidate in 1996 with Pat Choate as his running mate, winning 8.4% of the popular vote against Clinton and Bob Dole. Perot's campaigns are credited with pushing deficit reduction to the centre of American politics in the 1990s.

Death and legacy

Perot died of leukemia in Dallas on July 9, 2019, aged 89. He left a fortune estimated at over $4 billion and a reputation as a self-made entrepreneur whose plain-spoken, anti-establishment presidential bids reshaped third-party politics in the United States.
