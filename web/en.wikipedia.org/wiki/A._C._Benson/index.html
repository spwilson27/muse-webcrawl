A. C. Benson

Arthur Christopher Benson
1862–1925
English essayist, poet, diarist and academic
Master of Magdalene College, Cambridge.

Arthur Christopher Benson (24 April 1862 – 17 June 1925), usually published as A. C. Benson, was an English essayist, poet and diarist. The son of Edward White Benson, Archbishop of Canterbury, he became a schoolmaster at Eton, a fellow and later Master of Magdalene College, Cambridge, and one of the most read Edwardian writers of reflective prose. He wrote the words to “Land of Hope and Glory” for Elgar’s coronation ode.

Life

Benson was educated at Eton and King’s College, Cambridge. After teaching at Eton for many years, he returned to Cambridge and became Master of Magdalene in 1915. His public manner was polished and sociable, but his enormous diary records recurrent depression, religious questioning and acute self-scrutiny. The diary, running to millions of words, was later mined for studies of Benson and of Cambridge life.

Writing

Benson published volumes of essays such as The Upton Letters, From a College Window and Escape and Other Essays. Their appeal lay in a cultivated, conversational voice reflecting on friendship, work, landscape, ambition and tranquillity. He also wrote poetry, biography and ghost stories; the last connect him with his brothers E. F. Benson and R. H. Benson, both also authors. Modern readers often find his essays gentle and dated in assumptions, but they document an influential ideal of civilised, contemplative academic life.

“Land of Hope and Glory”

For the 1902 coronation of Edward VII, Edward Elgar adapted the trio melody from Pomp and Circumstance March No. 1, and Benson supplied the patriotic lyric. The song became a fixture of the Last Night of the Proms and of British ceremonial culture, while also attracting modern debate about empire and national identity.
