Ashoka Chakra (emblem)

Ashoka Chakra
DesignNavy-blue wheel with 24 equally spaced spokes
Adopted15 August 1947 (as centre of the national flag of India)
OriginLion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath

The Ashoka Chakra is a depiction of the Dharmachakra, the "wheel of dharma", with 24 spokes, shown in navy blue at the centre of the national flag of India. It takes its name from the emperor Ashoka, whose pillars and capitals — most famously the Lion Capital at Sarnath, where Buddha first taught the dharma — bear sculpted wheels of this form.

Adoption

When India became independent in 1947, the Constituent Assembly adopted a national flag based on the Congress tricolour but replaced the spinning wheel (charkha) of the earlier flag with the Ashoka Chakra. The change, adopted on 22 July 1947 and effective from 15 August 1947, was explained by Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan: the wheel signifies that there is life in movement and death in stagnation, and that India should move forward.

Symbolism

The 24 spokes represent the twenty-four hours of the day and the eternal, universal law of dharma (righteousness and motion). The navy blue colour is said to represent the depth of the ocean and the universality of the sky. The Chakra also forms the centre of India's highest peacetime gallantry award, the Ashoka Chakra, instituted in 1952, and appears in the State Emblem's abacus derived from the Sarnath capital.
