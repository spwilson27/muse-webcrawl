Luis Miguel Valdez (born 26 June 1940) is an American playwright, screenwriter, film director, and actor regarded as the father of Chicano film and theater in the United States.

Career

Born in Delano, California, to Mexican migrant farmworker parents, Valdez graduated from San José State University. In 1965 he joined César Chávez's United Farm Workers during the Delano grape strike and founded El Teatro Campesino, a theater troupe that performed short bilingual actos on flatbed trucks in the fields to support the strikers. The company became the most influential Latino theater group in the country and remains based in San Juan Bautista, California.

His play Zoot Suit (1978), dramatizing the 1942 Sleepy Lagoon murder trial and the 1943 Zoot Suit Riots in Los Angeles, was the first Chicano play produced on Broadway and was adapted into a 1981 film. Valdez wrote and directed the biographical film La Bamba (1987), about rock and roll singer Ritchie Valens, a major commercial success. He received the National Medal of Arts in 2016.
