Scarface is a 1983 American crime drama film directed by Brian De Palma and written by Oliver Stone. A remake of the 1932 film of the same name, it stars Al Pacino as Tony Montana, a Cuban refugee who arrives in Miami during the 1980 Mariel boatlift and rises to become a powerful cocaine kingpin before his empire destroys him.

Plot

Tony Montana arrives in Miami and, with his friend Manny Ribera, works his way from dishwashing and petty crime into the organisation of drug lord Frank Lopez. Tony takes over Lopez's empire, marries his wife Elvira, and builds a vast cocaine operation with Bolivian supplier Alejandro Sosa. Paranoia, addiction and violence spiral: Tony kills Manny after discovering his secret marriage to Tony's sister Gina, refuses Sosa's order to cooperate in an assassination, and is finally killed in a spectacular shootout at his mansion when Sosa's hitmen storm it, dying beneath the "The World Is Yours" globe in his foyer.

Reception and legacy

Initial reviews were divided, with criticism of the film's violence and profanity, but Scarface has since been reappraised as one of the greatest gangster films ever made and Pacino's performance is regarded as iconic. The film became a major influence on hip-hop culture and popular culture generally, and lines such as "Say hello to my little friend!" are widely quoted.
