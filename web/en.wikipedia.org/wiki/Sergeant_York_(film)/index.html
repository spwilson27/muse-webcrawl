Sergeant York (film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sergeant York is a 1941 American biographical film about the life of Alvin C. York, one of the most decorated American soldiers of World War I. It was directed by Howard Hawks and starred Gary Cooper in the title role, with Walter Brennan and Joan Leslie. The film was a critical and commercial success, released as the United States stood on the verge of entering World War II, and won Cooper the Academy Award for Best Actor.

Plot

The film tells of Alvin York, a poor Tennessee mountaineer and crack shot who, despite his rowdy youth, undergoes a religious conversion and registers for the draft as a would-be conscientious objector rooted in his church's pacifist reading of the Bible. Denied exemption, he struggles with the morality of killing, but during the Meuse-Argonne offensive of 8 October 1918 he single-handedly attacks a German machine-gun position, killing many enemy soldiers and capturing 132 prisoners — becoming an American hero.

Production and reception

York had refused for years to sell his story; producer Jesse Lasky persuaded him, with York insisting that Gary Cooper play him and that proceeds support his charitable Bible school. The film was the highest-grossing film of 1941 and received eleven Academy Award nominations. Cooper's performance and the film's themes of duty and faith resonating with wartime audiences made it one of the most prominent patriotic films of the era.
