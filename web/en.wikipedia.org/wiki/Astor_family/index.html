Astor family

OriginWalldorf, Germany
FounderJohn Jacob Astor (1763–1848)
Known forFur trade, New York real estate

The Astor family achieved prominence in business, society and politics in the United States and the United Kingdom during the 19th and 20th centuries. Descended from German immigrant John Jacob Astor, who made a fortune in the fur trade and New York real estate, the Astors were for a time among America's richest families and were known as the “landlords of New York.”

John Jacob Astor

John Jacob Astor (1763–1848) emigrated from Walldorf in 1784, built the American Fur Company, and invested profits in Manhattan land. At his death he was the richest man in America.

Later generations

His son William Backhouse Astor Sr. expanded the real estate holdings. Later members included William Waldorf Astor, who moved to England and became Viscount Astor; his wife Nancy Astor, the first woman to take her seat in the British House of Commons; and John Jacob Astor IV, who died on the RMS Titanic in 1912. Caroline Schermerhorn Astor, “the Mrs Astor,” dominated Gilded Age New York society.

Legacy

The family name survives in the Waldorf Astoria hotel (combining Astor family properties), Astor Place in Manhattan, and Astoria, Queens and Oregon.
