The Lincoln Continental is a series of mid-sized and full-sized luxury cars produced by Lincoln, a division of the American automaker Ford Motor Company. The model line was introduced in 1939 as a personal luxury car developed by Edsel Ford as a one-off convertible; over ten generations it served for decades as Lincoln's flagship sedan. The Continental nameplate was retired in 2002, revived for a tenth generation in 2017, and discontinued again after the 2020 model year as Lincoln shifted to SUVs.

History

The original Continental grew from a custom 1939 Lincoln-Zephyr convertible styled by Eugene T. "Bob" Gregorie for Edsel Ford, distinguished by its long hood, short deck and exposed rear spare tire — the "continental kit". Production models followed from 1940 to 1948 with V12 power. The name returned in 1956 on the ultra-luxury Continental Mark II, sold by a short-lived Continental Division.

The fourth generation (1961–1969), styled under Elwood Engel, became an icon of understated slab-sided design and introduced rear-hinged "suicide doors" on the four-door convertible; a 1961 Continental was the Dallas parade car associated with the Kennedy era. Subsequent generations downsized in 1980, moved to front-wheel drive with a V6 in 1988, and adopted a V8 again for 1995–2002. The tenth generation (2017–2020), previewed by a 2015 concept, revived the name with a twin-turbo V6 and coach doors on the limited 2019 Coach Door Edition.
