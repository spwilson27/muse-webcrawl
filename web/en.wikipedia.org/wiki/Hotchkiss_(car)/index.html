Hotchkiss was a French automobile manufacturer, part of the armaments firm Hotchkiss et Cie founded by the American gunsmith Benjamin B. Hotchkiss. The company built luxury cars between 1903 and 1955 at its factory in Saint-Denis, near Paris.

History

Hotchkiss cars were renowned for solid engineering and conservative elegance; the marque's emblem, crossed cannons, reflected its armaments heritage. Well-known models included the pre-war six-cylinder cars and the post-war Anjou and the Grégoire, designed by Jean-Albert Grégoire with front-wheel drive and aluminium construction.

Decline

France's punitive horsepower taxation and the cost of the advanced Grégoire undermined the business. Car production ended in the mid-1950s after a merger with Delahaye, although military vehicle and armaments work continued under the Hotchkiss-Brandt name.
