Milnor conjecture

Milnor conjecture
FieldAlgebraic geometry, algebraic K-theory
Conjectured byJohn Milnor, 1970

The Milnor conjecture is a theorem of algebra, proposed by John Milnor in 1970 and proved by Vladimir Voevodsky, which asserts that the mod-2 Milnor K-theory of a field is isomorphic to its mod-2 Galois (étale) cohomology ring — equivalently, to the graded Witt ring of quadratic forms over the field, via the natural maps sending a symbol to the corresponding Pfister form and cohomology class. The result links three previously separate subjects: algebraic K-theory, the theory of quadratic forms, and Galois cohomology.

Statement

For a field F of characteristic not 2, Milnor defined a graded ring KM*(F) generated by symbols {a} for a in F× subject to the Steinberg relation. The conjecture states that the natural map from KMn(F)/2 to the Galois cohomology group Hn(F, μ2) — which by related work is also the n-th graded piece of the Witt ring — is an isomorphism for all n. Special cases had been verified earlier: n = 0 and 1 are elementary, n = 2 was established through work of Tate, Merkurjev and Suslin, and n = 3 by Merkurjev–Suslin and Rost.

Proof

Voevodsky proved the conjecture using a new theory of motivic cohomology, developed with Suslin and Friedlander, and the machinery of A1-homotopy theory created with Fabien Morel. A key ingredient, the construction of motivic cohomology operations and the "Milnor conjecture on Milnor K-theory" reduced to the Beilinson–Lichtenbaum conjectures in the needed ranges. Voevodsky received the Fields Medal in 2002 in part for this work. The generalisation to odd primes is the Bloch–Kato conjecture, proved by Voevodsky with Markus Rost and Charles Weibel (the norm residue isomorphism theorem).
