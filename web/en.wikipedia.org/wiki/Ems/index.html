Ems

Ems may also refer to the Ems (river) in Germany and the Netherlands, the town of Bad Ems, or other uses; this article is about the river.

Ems (German: Ems; Dutch: Eems)
Length: about 371 km (231 mi)
Source: Senne, near Schloß Holte-Stukenbrock, North Rhine-Westphalia
Mouth: Dollart, North Sea, near Emden

The Ems (German: Ems; Dutch: Eems) is a river in northwestern Germany and the northeastern Netherlands. It runs from the state of North Rhine-Westphalia to the Dollart bay and the North Sea, with a total length of about 371 kilometres (231 miles). It flows through the states of North Rhine-Westphalia and Lower Saxony, and forms the border between the Dutch province of Groningen and Germany near its mouth.

Course

The Ems rises in the Senne, a sandy heathland region near Schloß Holte-Stukenbrock, and flows west and then north through towns including Rheda-Wiedenbrück, Warendorf, Greven, Rheine, Lingen, Meppen, Papenburg, and Leer before reaching Emden and the sea. The river is navigable for much of its course and is linked to the Ruhr and the Rhine basin by the Dortmund–Ems Canal, completed in 1899.

Economic significance

The river has long served industry and shipping. Papenburg is home to the Meyer Werft shipyard, from which large cruise ships are conveyed down the river to the sea — an operation requiring careful management of water levels, assisted since 2002 by the Ems barrage (Emssperrwerk) near Gandersum. The estuary contains the port of Emden, historically a major outlet for the Ruhr.
