École Polytechnique

École Polytechnique (often nicknamed "l'X") is a French grande école founded in 1794 during the French Revolution. It is one of France's most prestigious institutions for science and engineering education, supervised by the Ministry of the Armed Forces. Since 1976 its campus has been located in Palaiseau, south of Paris. It is a founding member of the Institut Polytechnique de Paris.

History

The school was founded in 1794 as the École centrale des travaux publics, with Gaspard Monge among its creators, and renamed École polytechnique the following year. Napoleon gave it a military status in 1804 and its motto, Pour la Patrie, les Sciences et la Gloire ("For the Fatherland, Science and Glory"). Generations of leading French scientists, engineers, and statesmen have passed through its highly selective entrance examination, the concours.

Education

The flagship cycle ingénieur polytechnicien is a four-year programme combining advanced mathematics, physics, computer science, economics, and humanities with military and sports training; students in the French corps track hold officer-cadet status. The school also offers bachelor's, master's, and doctoral programmes and hosts major research laboratories.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Henri Poincaré, Sadi Carnot, Augustin-Louis Cauchy, André-Marie Ampère, and several presidents and prime ministers of France, as well as founders of major companies.
