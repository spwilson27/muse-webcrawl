The Aztec Triple Alliance was a political and military alliance formed in 1428 between the city-states of Tenochtitlan (the Mexica), Texcoco (the Acolhua) and Tlacopan (the Tepanecs) in the Valley of Mexico. It formed the core of what became the Aztec Empire.

Formation

The alliance arose from the Tepanec War, in which Itzcoatl of Tenochtitlan and Nezahualcoyotl of Texcoco overthrew the dominance of Azcapotzalco. Tribute from conquered cities was divided between the partners — two-fifths each to Tenochtitlan and Texcoco, one-fifth to Tlacopan — though Tenochtitlan gradually became the senior partner.

Conquest and fall

Over the following decades the alliance expanded by conquest across central Mexico. It collapsed with the Spanish conquest led by Hernán Cortés, ending with the fall of Tenochtitlan in 1521.
