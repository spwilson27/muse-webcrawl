Battle of Cable Street

The Battle of Cable Street was a series of clashes that took place in the East End of London on Sunday, 4 October 1936. It was a clash between the Metropolitan Police, sent to protect a march by members of the British Union of Fascists (BUF) led by Oswald Mosley, and anti-fascist counter-protesters — including local Jewish residents, Irish dockers, communists, anarchists and trade unionists. An estimated 100,000 to 300,000 anti-fascists mobilized; barricades were erected in Cable Street, and the police, unable to clear a route, advised Mosley to abandon the march, which he did.

Background

The East End in the 1930s contained London's largest Jewish community. Mosley's Blackshirts deliberately targeted the area with antisemitic propaganda and marches. When the BUF announced a march in uniform through the district on 4 October 1936, a petition of some 100,000 signatures asking the Home Secretary to ban it was rejected, but the local population — organized under the slogan "They shall not pass!" (¡No pasarán!, borrowed from Republican Spain) — determined to stop it physically.

Events

Counter-protesters blocked the fascists' likely routes at Aldgate and built barricades — including an overturned lorry, furniture and paving stones — across Cable Street near its junction with Christian Street. Mounted police and foot police charged repeatedly to clear the way, met by marbles thrown under horses' hooves, improvised missiles and fierce resistance. With the route impassable, the police commissioner, Sir Philip Game, ordered Mosley to disperse his marchers westward; the fascists marched out through the City instead. The day's fighting was mostly between the police and anti-fascists, not against the fascists themselves.

Aftermath and legacy

The battle helped prompt the Public Order Act 1936, which banned political uniforms and gave police greater powers to control marches. Cable Street remains a celebrated symbol of community resistance to fascism, commemorated by a large mural (painted 1979–1983) on the side of St George's Town Hall and by annual anniversaries.
