William Peers

Born: 14 June 1914, Stuart, Iowa
Died: 6 April 1984
Service: United States Army; OSS Detachment 101
Known for: Peers Commission inquiry into the My Lai massacre

William Ray Peers (June 14, 1914 – April 6, 1984) was a United States Army lieutenant general best known for leading the army's investigation into the My Lai massacre and its cover-up, and earlier for his guerrilla operations with OSS Detachment 101 in Burma during the Second World War.

Second World War

Peers, an ROTC graduate of UCLA commissioned in 1938, volunteered for the Office of Strategic Services and commanded Detachment 101 in Burma from 1943 to 1945. The unit, working with Kachin guerrillas behind Japanese lines, rescued downed Allied airmen, gathered intelligence, and conducted ambushes, becoming one of the most effective irregular units of the war.

Peers Commission

After the war Peers commanded the 4th Infantry Division in Vietnam. In 1969 the Chief of Staff appointed him to head a commission of inquiry into the handling of reports of the My Lai massacre of March 1968. The Peers Report, completed in 1970, documented failures up the chain of command and recommended charges against numerous officers; much of it remained classified for several years.

Later life

Peers retired in 1973 and co-authored a book on Detachment 101. He died in 1984.
