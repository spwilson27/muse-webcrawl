Sarah Churchill, Duchess of Marlborough (née Jennings; 5 June 1660 – 18 October 1744) was an English courtier who rose to become one of the most influential women of her age through her close friendship with Queen Anne. As wife of John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough — Britain's greatest general of the War of the Spanish Succession — and holder of several great court offices, she directed patronage, managed the royal finances, and played a central role in the politics of the early 18th century.

Rise and influence

Sarah Jennings came to court as a maid of honour and formed an intense friendship with the young Princess Anne, with whom she corresponded using pet names — Anne was "Mrs. Morley", Sarah "Mrs. Freeman" — meant to put them on equal footing. She married John Churchill in about 1678. After Anne's accession in 1702, Sarah became Mistress of the Robes, Groom of the Stole, and Keeper of the Privy Purse, and the Marlboroughs stood at the apex of power while the Duke won his victories at Blenheim (1704), Ramillies (1706), and Oudenarde (1708). Sarah was a committed Whig and pressed the Queen toward Whig ministers.

Fall and later life

The friendship deteriorated as Sarah's abrasive candour clashed with Anne's growing reliance on Abigail Masham, Sarah's own cousin and protégée. In 1711 Sarah was dismissed from her offices and the Marlboroughs fell from favour. Widowed in 1722, she spent two decades completing Blenheim Palace — the nation's gift to her husband, whose construction she supervised through bitter disputes with the architect Vanbrugh — quarrelling with descendants, publishing a self-justifying memoir (An Account of the Conduct of the Dowager Duchess of Marlborough, 1742), and dying one of the richest women in Europe.

Legacy

Sarah Churchill is an ancestor of Winston Churchill and of Diana, Princess of Wales. She was portrayed by Rachel Weisz in the 2018 film The Favourite.
