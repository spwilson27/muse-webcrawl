HD DVD

HD DVD (short for High Density Digital Versatile Disc) was a high-density optical disc format designed for the storage of data and high-definition video. Developed principally by Toshiba, it was the successor to the standard DVD and competed with Blu-ray Disc, backed by Sony, in a format war during the mid-2000s.

Format war and discontinuation

Like Blu-ray, HD DVD used a blue-violet laser to achieve higher storage density than the red laser of DVD, offering 15 GB on a single-layer disc and 30 GB dual-layer. HD DVD players and discs launched in 2006 with support from studios including Paramount, Universal and Warner Bros. The contest turned in early 2008 when Warner Bros. announced it would release exclusively on Blu-ray, followed by major retailers dropping HD DVD. On 19 February 2008 Toshiba announced it would discontinue development, manufacture and marketing of HD DVD players and recorders, ending the format war; about one million players and several hundred film titles had been sold.
