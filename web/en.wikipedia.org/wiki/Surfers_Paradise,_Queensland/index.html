Surfers Paradise is a seaside suburb and the tourism and entertainment heart of the Gold Coast in Queensland, Australia. It is famous for its sweeping surf beach, dense skyline of high-rise apartment and hotel towers — one of the most recognisable in Australia — and a year-round holiday economy of attractions, restaurants, nightclubs and events.

History

The area, originally known as Elston, developed after hotelier Jim Cavill built the Surfers Paradise Hotel in 1925 and successfully lobbied to rename the locality in 1933 to promote its appeal. Post-war prosperity, the meter maid phenomenon of the 1960s (introduced to soften new parking meters), and a high-rise building boom transformed it into a mass tourism destination; it also became controversially associated with property development and, later, a lively nightlife precinct. Landmarks include Q1 (for a time the world's tallest residential tower, at 322.5 m), Soul, and the beachfront Cavill Avenue mall.

Events and culture

Surfers Paradise hosts the annual Gold Coast 500 motor race on a street circuit, rounds of the World Surf League at nearby breaks, and each November–December the Schoolies celebrations marking the end of secondary school for thousands of school leavers. The suburb is served by the G:link light rail, and its beach is patrolled by surf lifesavers, continuing the surf culture from which it takes its name.
