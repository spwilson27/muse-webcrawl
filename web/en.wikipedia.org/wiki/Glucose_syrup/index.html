Glucose syrup

Glucose syrup, also known as confectioner's glucose, is a food syrup made by the hydrolysis of starch. It is a clear, viscous, sweet liquid consisting mainly of glucose, maltose and higher oligosaccharides. It is produced commercially from maize (corn), wheat, potato, rice or other starch sources, using acid or enzyme hydrolysis.

Properties and grades

Glucose syrups are graded by dextrose equivalent (DE), a measure of how far hydrolysis has proceeded. Low-DE syrups are mildly sweet and give body; high-DE syrups are sweeter. Typical confectionery syrup has a DE around 42. Because it inhibits crystallisation and retains moisture, glucose syrup is widely used in sweets, jams, ice cream, baked goods and brewing.

Related products

Further processing with the enzyme glucose isomerase converts part of the glucose to fructose, producing high-fructose corn syrup, widely used in soft drinks in the United States. Glucose syrup should not be confused with golden syrup or corn syrup in all its forms, though North American "corn syrup" is essentially a glucose syrup.
