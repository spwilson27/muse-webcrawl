Breeder's equation

In quantitative genetics, the breeder's equation predicts the response of a trait to selection:

R = h²S

where R is the response to selection (the difference in mean trait value between offspring of selected parents and the original population), h² is the narrow-sense heritability of the trait, and S is the selection differential (the difference in mean trait value between the selected parents and the population as a whole).

Interpretation

The equation shows that evolutionary change under artificial or natural selection depends both on how strongly selection acts (S) and on how much of the phenotypic variation is attributable to additive genetic effects (h²). If h² = 0, no response occurs however strong the selection; if h² = 1, the offspring mean equals that of the selected parents.

Extensions and limits

The univariate breeder's equation generalises to the multivariate form Δz̄ = Gβ, where G is the additive genetic variance–covariance matrix and β the selection gradient (Lande's equation). The simple equation assumes, among other things, that heritability is constant across generations and ignores mutation, drift, genotype-by-environment interaction and changes in linkage disequilibrium (the Bulmer effect) that build up under selection.
