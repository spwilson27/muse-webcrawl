The Mayflower carried the Pilgrims to New England in 1620.

The Mayflower was an English sailing ship that transported a group of English Puritan separatists, known today as the Pilgrims, from England to the New World in 1620. After a gruelling ten-week voyage, the ship anchored at Cape Cod in November 1620, and its passengers founded Plymouth Colony in present-day Massachusetts — one of the earliest successful English settlements in North America and a foundational episode in American history.

The voyage

The Mayflower, a Dutch-built fluyt of about 180 tons burden and 27.4 m in length, was chartered by a group of London merchants to carry the Leiden Separatists and other emigrants ("Strangers") recruited in England. She departed Plymouth, England, on 16 September 1620 (Old Style) with 102 passengers and a crew of roughly 30 under Captain Christopher Jones. Storms drove her far north of the intended destination near the Hudson River; she sighted land at Cape Cod on 19 November.

The Mayflower Compact

While anchored in Provincetown Harbor, the male passengers signed the Mayflower Compact, an agreement to form a "civil body politic" governed by just and equal laws of their own making — often regarded as a founding document of American self-government. The settlers then chose Plymouth as their permanent site, and the first winter killed about half the colonists. With help from the Wampanoag people, notably Tisquantum (Squanto), the colony survived, and the harvest feast of autumn 1621 is commemorated as the first Thanksgiving.

Later history

The Mayflower returned to England in April 1621. Captain Jones died in 1622, and the ship was probably broken up at Rotherhithe a few years later. A full-scale replica, Mayflower II, was built in Devon and sailed to America in 1957; she is preserved at Plymouth, Massachusetts, by Plimoth Patuxet Museums.
