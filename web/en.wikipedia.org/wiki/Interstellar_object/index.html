Interstellar object

An interstellar object is an astronomical object (such as an asteroid, comet, or rogue planet) located in interstellar space that is not gravitationally bound to a star. Such objects pass through the Solar System on hyperbolic trajectories, which reveal their extrasolar origin.

Known objects

The first interstellar object detected passing through the Solar System was 1I/ʻOumuamua, discovered by the Pan-STARRS telescope in October 2017. Its elongated shape, lack of a cometary coma despite non-gravitational acceleration, and high eccentricity (~1.20) prompted extensive study and speculation. In August 2019, amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov discovered 2I/Borisov, an unambiguous interstellar comet with a visible coma and tail. A third object, 3I/ATLAS, was discovered in July 2025 by the ATLAS survey on a strongly hyperbolic path.

Origin and significance

Interstellar objects are thought to be planetesimals ejected from their home systems during planet formation or by later gravitational encounters. Studying them gives direct samples of material from other planetary systems, including their composition and isotopic ratios, without the need for interstellar probes.

Detection

Because such objects are fast-moving and faint, only a few have been found; surveys such as the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time are expected to increase the discovery rate, and mission concepts such as ESA's Comet Interceptor are designed to visit one on short notice.
