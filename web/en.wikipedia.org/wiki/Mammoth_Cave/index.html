Mammoth Cave

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mammoth Cave in south-central Kentucky is the centrepiece of Mammoth Cave National Park and the world's longest known cave system, with more than 420 miles (680 km) of surveyed passageways. The cave formed in thick Mississippian limestone strata beneath a sandstone caprock.

History

Native Americans used the cave for thousands of years, mining minerals and leaving artefacts and preserved remains. Settlers rediscovered it in the 1790s; it was mined for saltpetre during the War of 1812. In the 19th century the cave became a famous tourist attraction, guided in part by the enslaved explorer Stephen Bishop, who mapped much of the cave and discovered features such as the Echo River. Mammoth Cave was established as a national park in 1941 and designated a World Heritage Site in 1981.

Features

Notable features include the Rotunda, Frozen Niagara, Fat Man's Misery, and the Bottomless Pit. The cave supports a rich subterranean ecosystem, including the endangered Kentucky cave shrimp and several bat species.
