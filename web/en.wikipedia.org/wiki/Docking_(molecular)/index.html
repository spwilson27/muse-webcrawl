Docking (molecular)

Molecular docking
A ligand (green) docked into a protein binding pocket
FieldComputational chemistry, drug discovery
PurposePredict binding orientation and affinity

In the field of molecular modelling, docking is a method which predicts the preferred orientation of one molecule to a second when a ligand and a target are bound to each other to form a stable complex. Knowledge of the preferred orientation in turn may be used to predict the strength of association or binding affinity between two molecules using, for example, scoring functions.

The associations between biologically relevant molecules such as proteins, peptides, nucleic acids, carbohydrates, and lipids play a central role in signal transduction. Furthermore, the relative orientation of the two interacting partners may affect the type of signal produced (e.g., agonism vs antagonism). Therefore, docking is useful for predicting both the strength and type of signal produced.

Applications

Molecular docking is one of the most frequently used methods in structure-based drug design, due to its ability to predict the binding conformation of small molecule ligands to the appropriate target binding site. Characterisation of the binding behaviour plays an important role in rational design of drugs as well as to elucidate fundamental biochemical processes. Docking is routinely used in virtual screening of large compound libraries to identify candidate hits for experimental testing.

Approaches

Docking methods combine a search algorithm, which explores possible binding modes (poses), with a scoring function, which estimates the binding affinity of each pose. Two broad strategies are used: shape complementarity (geometric matching) and simulation-based approaches in which the ligand and target are allowed to move. Proteins may be treated as rigid or partially flexible; explicit treatment of flexibility in both partners remains computationally demanding.

Scoring functions

Scoring functions are typically classified as force-field-based, empirical, or knowledge-based. They approximate the free energy of binding from contributions such as van der Waals interactions, electrostatics, hydrogen bonding, desolvation, and entropic terms. Machine-learning-based scoring functions have become increasingly popular.

Software

Widely used docking programs include AutoDock, AutoDock Vina, DOCK, Glide, GOLD, and FlexX. Blind docking refers to docking over the entire surface of a protein when no binding site is known in advance.
