Petroleum engineering is a field of engineering concerned with the activities related to the production of hydrocarbons — crude oil and natural gas — from subsurface reservoirs. It combines geology, geophysics, fluid mechanics, thermodynamics and economics to locate, drill into and extract oil and gas efficiently and safely.

Sub-disciplines

Reservoir engineers estimate reserves and design strategies to maximise recovery, using geological models and numerical simulation. Drilling engineers plan and supervise the drilling of wells, selecting casing, drilling fluids and well-control practices. Production engineers manage wells once drilled, optimising flow and artificial lift, while completions engineers design how wells are finished to connect the reservoir to the wellbore. Enhanced oil recovery methods, such as waterflooding, gas injection and hydraulic fracturing, fall largely within the discipline.

History and profession

The field emerged in the early twentieth century alongside the modern oil industry; the first petroleum engineering degree programs were established in the United States in the 1910s. The Society of Petroleum Engineers, founded in 1957, is the main professional society. Petroleum engineers work for oil companies, service companies such as SLB and Halliburton, and consultancies, and are among the highest-paid engineering graduates, though employment is strongly cyclical with oil prices.
