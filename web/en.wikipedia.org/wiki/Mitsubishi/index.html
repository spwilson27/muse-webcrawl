Mitsubishi

The Mitsubishi three-diamond mark

The Mitsubishi Group (三菱グループ, Mitsubishi Gurūpu) is a group of autonomous Japanese multinational companies in a variety of industries, sharing the Mitsubishi brand and trademark. Founded in 1870 by Iwasaki Yatarō as a shipping firm, it grew into one of Japan's great zaibatsu conglomerates before being broken up under the Allied occupation after the Second World War; its successor companies remain loosely linked through cross-shareholding (keiretsu) and the Friday Conference of executives.

Major companies

Core members include Mitsubishi Corporation (trading), Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, MUFG (Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group), Mitsubishi Electric, Mitsubishi Motors, Mitsubishi Estate, Mitsubishi Chemical Group and Nikon. The name combines mitsu ("three") and hishi ("water chestnut", read as bishi), reflected in the three-diamond logo.

History

Starting from shipping, Mitsubishi expanded into mining, shipbuilding, banking, insurance and wartime aircraft production (including the Zero fighter). After 1945 the holding company was dissolved, but the firms regrouped around the Mitsubishi name from the 1950s.
