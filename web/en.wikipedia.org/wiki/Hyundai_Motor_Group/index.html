Hyundai Motor Group

Native name: 현대자동차그룹
Type: Chaebol (conglomerate)
Headquarters: Seoul, South Korea
Key brands: Hyundai, Kia, Genesis, Ioniq
Chairman: Chung Eui-sun

The Hyundai Motor Group (Korean: 현대자동차그룹) is a South Korean multinational conglomerate headquartered in Seoul. It is the world's third-largest automobile manufacturer by sales volume, behind Toyota and the Volkswagen Group, and the largest carmaker in South Korea. The group was formed through the purchase by Hyundai Motor Company of a controlling stake in Kia in 1998, following the Asian financial crisis, and the two brands operate as its principal automotive arms alongside the Genesis luxury marque and the Ioniq electric sub-brand.

Structure and affiliates

Beyond finished vehicles, the group spans the automotive supply chain: Hyundai Mobis (parts and service), Hyundai Steel, Hyundai Rotem (rail and defence), Hyundai Wia (machinery and components), Hyundai Transys (powertrains and seats), Hyundai Capital and Hyundai Card (finance), and Hyundai Engineering & Construction interests through affiliates. Its innovation arms include Boston Dynamics (acquired in 2021), the autonomous-driving joint venture Motional, and the advanced air mobility company Supernal.

Electrification and global operations

The group has invested heavily in electrification around its E-GMP platform, with models such as the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Kia EV6 winning international awards. Major manufacturing hubs include Ulsan — among the world's largest car plants — plants in the United States (Alabama; the Georgia "Metaplant"), India, Czechia, and elsewhere. Hyundai and Kia together sell several million vehicles per year in more than 190 countries.
