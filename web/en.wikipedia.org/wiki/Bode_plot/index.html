Bode plot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Bode plot is a graph of the frequency response of a system, widely used in electrical engineering and control theory. It is named after Hendrik Wade Bode of Bell Labs, who developed the technique in the 1930s while designing amplifiers with feedback.

Description

A Bode plot consists of two aligned graphs sharing a logarithmic frequency axis. The upper graph, the Bode magnitude plot, shows the magnitude of the system's transfer function in decibels against log frequency. The lower graph, the Bode phase plot, shows the phase shift in degrees against the same axis.

Construction and use

Bode plots can be sketched by hand from straight-line asymptotic approximations: each pole or zero contributes characteristic slopes (–20 dB/decade per pole) and phase transitions, and the individual contributions are added together. Because gains multiply and phase shifts add, logarithms make this additive decomposition natural. From a Bode plot engineers read stability margins directly: the gain margin and phase margin measure how close a feedback system is to instability, and the crossover frequencies where gain reaches 0 dB or phase reaches –180° are central to classical control design.
