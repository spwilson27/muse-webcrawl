A drinking song is a song sung while drinking alcoholic beverages, typically in company, with themes celebrating drink, fellowship and good humour. Drinking songs are among the oldest song genres, found from the convivial poetry of ancient Greece and the medieval Carmina Burana to modern student, military and pub traditions worldwide.

History

The Latin goliard songs of the 12th and 13th centuries — including the famous "In taberna quando sumus" — celebrated tavern life, and Renaissance and early-modern broadsides overflowed with ale songs. In German-speaking student culture, the Kommers songbooks codified hundreds of Trinklieder, of which "Gaudeamus igitur" — originally a drinking and fellowship song — became the international academic anthem. Scandinavian students maintain the tradition of snapsvisor, short songs sung before drinking snaps at dinners.

Forms and examples

Drinking songs are typically strophic, tuneful and easy to join, often with call-and-response, toasts, or cumulative verses. Well-known examples include "99 Bottles of Beer", "Beer, Beer, Beer", sea shanties sung over grog, the Irish repertoire of pub songs such as "Whiskey in the Jar" and "The Wild Rover", rugby songs, and university and fraternity repertoires. Composers have also stylised the genre in art music, from operatic brindisi — the famous drinking duet "Libiamo ne' lieti calici" in Verdi's La traviata — to choral drinking rounds.

Cultural role

Anthropologists note drinking songs' role in bonding and ritualising communal drinking; temperance movements answered with parody and counter-songs, rewriting popular tunes with anti-alcohol lyrics.
