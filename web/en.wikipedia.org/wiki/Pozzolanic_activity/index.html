Pozzolanic activity


Volcanic ash, a natural pozzolan

Pozzolanic activity is a measure of the degree of reaction over time, or the reaction rate, between a pozzolan and calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2) in the presence of water. A pozzolan is a siliceous or siliceous-and-aluminous material which, in finely divided form and in the presence of moisture, reacts with calcium hydroxide to form compounds with cementitious properties.

Reaction

The pozzolanic reaction converts soluble calcium hydroxide, released during Portland cement hydration, into insoluble calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H), improving the strength and durability of concrete and reducing its permeability.

Measurement

Activity is assessed by strength activity index tests, electrical conductivity methods, or by measuring calcium hydroxide consumption, for example with the Frattini test or thermogravimetric analysis. Common pozzolans include fly ash, silica fume, metakaolin and natural volcanic ashes.
