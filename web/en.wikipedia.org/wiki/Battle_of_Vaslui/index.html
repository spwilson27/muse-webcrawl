The Battle of Vaslui, fought on 10 January 1475, was a major victory of Stephen III of Moldavia — Stephen the Great — over the forces of the Ottoman Empire. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest victories of a medieval Christian army over the Ottomans and cemented Stephen's reputation across Europe.

Background

Stephen had refused to pay tribute to Sultan Mehmed II and had intervened in Wallachia, provoking an Ottoman punitive expedition. The Ottoman army under Hadım Suleiman Pasha, Beylerbey of Rumelia, invaded Moldavia in the winter of 1474–75. Stephen applied a scorched-earth policy, evacuating settlements, poisoning wells and drawing the Ottomans deep into Moldavian territory.

The battle

Stephen chose a strong defensive position in the marshy, wooded valley of the Bârlad River near Vaslui, fortified with ditches, wagons and artillery. On a foggy January morning the Moldavians lured the Ottoman advance guard forward with music played from the opposite side of the valley; when the main Ottoman force committed itself into the narrow, mist-covered terrain, Stephen's troops attacked from the flanks while his artillery fired into the crowded ranks. The Ottoman army broke and fled, suffering very heavy casualties during the pursuit and in the marshes.

Aftermath

Pope Sixtus IV hailed Stephen as a "Champion of Christ" (Athleta Christi). The victory bought Moldavia a brief respite, but Mehmed II returned in person the following year; after the Battle of Valea Albă in 1476 the Ottomans ravaged Moldavia, though they failed to take its key fortresses. Vaslui remains a defining episode of Romanian national history.
