Clear Channel Communications

Clear Channel Communications, renamed iHeartMedia in 2014, grew from one San Antonio station into America's largest radio group.

Clear Channel Communications, Inc. was an American mass media corporation headquartered in San Antonio, Texas. Founded by Lowry Mays and B. J. "Red" McCombs in 1972, it grew from a single FM station into the largest owner of radio stations in the United States, at its height owning more than 1,200 stations, as well as a dominant outdoor advertising business and the live-entertainment giant that became Live Nation. On September 16, 2014, the company rebranded itself as iHeartMedia, taking the name of its iHeartRadio digital platform; its former outdoor division continues as Clear Channel Outdoor.

History

Clear Channel's growth was driven by deregulation. After the Telecommunications Act of 1996 lifted most limits on station ownership, the company embarked on an acquisition spree, most notably the $23.5 billion merger with AMFM Inc. in 1999–2000, becoming the dominant force in American commercial radio. Critics charged that its scale homogenised programming — through voice-tracking, syndicated formats and centralised playlists — and reduced localism, while the company argued consolidation was necessary to compete with new media.

In 2006, Clear Channel agreed to be taken private by Bain Capital and Thomas H. Lee Partners in a leveraged buyout worth about $26.7 billion including debt; the deal closed in 2008 just as the financial crisis and declining radio revenues hit. The company had meanwhile spun off its live-entertainment arm as Live Nation (2005) and separated its outdoor advertising unit.

Controversies

Clear Channel attracted criticism after the September 11 attacks when a list of more than 150 "lyrically questionable" songs circulated internally among its stations, and in 2003 when its stations sponsored pro-Iraq War rallies and the Dixie Chicks were dropped from many playlists following the band's criticism of President George W. Bush. The company denied formal bans in both cases. The heavy debt from the buyout led its successor, iHeartMedia, to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2018, from which it emerged in 2019.
