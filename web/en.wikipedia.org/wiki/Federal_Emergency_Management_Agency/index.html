The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) is an agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security, initially created under President Jimmy Carter by Presidential Reorganization Plan No. 3 of 1978 and implemented by two Executive Orders on April 1, 1979. The agency's primary purpose is to coordinate the federal government's response to a disaster that has occurred in the United States and that overwhelms the resources of local and state authorities.

The governor of the state in which the disaster occurs must declare a state of emergency and formally request that the president declare a federal disaster so that FEMA and federal resources can respond. FEMA also provides assistance for housing, disaster unemployment, crisis counselling, and rebuilding of public infrastructure through its Public Assistance and Individual Assistance programs, largely under the authority of the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act of 1988.

History

Federal disaster aid in the United States dates to 1803, when Congress provided relief to Portsmouth, New Hampshire, after a devastating fire. Through the 20th century, disaster functions were scattered among dozens of agencies. FEMA consolidated civil defence and disaster programs in 1979. In the 1990s, under director James Lee Witt, the agency was elevated to cabinet-level status in practice and was widely praised for its responses.

In 2003 FEMA was absorbed into the new Department of Homeland Security. Its preparedness functions were partly stripped, and its performance during Hurricane Katrina in August–September 2005 was heavily criticised, leading to the Post-Katrina Emergency Management Reform Act of 2006, which restored and strengthened the agency's autonomy and capabilities within DHS. FEMA coordinates the National Flood Insurance Program and the U.S. Fire Administration, and it has responded to major events including the September 11 attacks (through state support), Hurricane Sandy (2012), Hurricane Maria (2017), and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Organization

FEMA is headed by an Administrator appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. It maintains ten regional offices, a National Response Coordination Center in Washington, the National Emergency Training Center and Emergency Management Institute in Emmitsburg, Maryland, and a large cadre of reservist "disaster assistance employees" who deploy on call.
