Republic (pressure group)

Republic is a British republican pressure group advocating the replacement of the monarchy of the United Kingdom with an elected head of state. Founded in 1983 as a grassroots membership organisation, it campaigns for a democratic republic in Britain, arguing that the monarchy is undemocratic, unaccountable and expensive, and that the head of state should be chosen by and answerable to the people.

History

Republic was founded in 1983 and operated for many years as a largely volunteer-run campaign. It grew in prominence during the 2000s and 2010s, professionalising its campaigning around royal weddings, jubilees and the question of the cost of the monarchy. The group gained wide media attention during the Diamond Jubilee of Elizabeth II in 2012, when it organised a protest on the Thames, and again during the coronation of Charles III in 2023, when its chief executive Graham Smith was among protesters arrested in London under public order legislation, prompting debate about the right to protest.

Aims and arguments

Republic proposes an elected, ceremonial head of state — broadly on the model of Ireland or Germany — chosen by popular vote for a fixed term. It argues that monarchy is incompatible with democratic principles, that the royal household lacks transparency in its finances, and that powers exercised in the monarch's name (the royal prerogative) should be subject to democratic control. Supporters of monarchy dispute the group's cost figures and argue that the institution provides continuity and soft power.

Organisation

Republic is a membership organisation funded by subscriptions and donations. It is not affiliated to any political party, and republicanism in Britain draws support across the political spectrum. It publishes reports on royal finances and polls on public attitudes to the monarchy.
