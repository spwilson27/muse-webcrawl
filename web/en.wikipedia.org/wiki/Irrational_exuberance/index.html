Irrational exuberance

"Irrational exuberance" is a phrase used by Alan Greenspan, then Chair of the US Federal Reserve, in a speech on 5 December 1996, warning that stock market valuations might be unsustainably high: "But how do we know when irrational exuberance has unduly escalated asset values, which then become subject to unexpected and prolonged contractions as they have in Japan over the past decade?" The phrase became emblematic of debate over the dot-com bubble.

Context

Greenspan delivered the remarks at the American Enterprise Institute, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average around 6,400. Markets in Asia fell sharply on the comment, but US stocks quickly recovered and continued rising for more than three years, peaking in March 2000 before the dot-com crash. The episode is often cited in debates about whether central banks should lean against asset bubbles and about the limits of officials' market-timing judgements.

Shiller's book

Economist Robert Shiller adopted the phrase as the title of his 2000 book Irrational Exuberance, published near the market peak, which argued using long-run valuation measures such as the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings (CAPE) ratio that US stocks were historically overvalued. Later editions (2005 and 2015) extended the analysis to housing bubbles and behavioural finance, discussing psychological anchors, herd behaviour, and media amplification as drivers of speculative episodes.
