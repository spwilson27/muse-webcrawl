Dune is a 1965 epic science fiction novel by American author Frank Herbert, originally published as two separate serials (1963–64 and 1965) in Analog magazine. It tied with Roger Zelazny's This Immortal for the inaugural Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1966 and won the first Nebula Award for Best Novel. It is the first instalment of the Dune Chronicles and is regarded as one of the greatest science fiction novels ever written, and the best-selling science fiction novel of all time.

Plot

Set in the distant future in a feudal interstellar society, the novel follows Paul Atreides, heir of House Atreides, whose family is given control of the desert planet Arrakis — the only source of the spice melange, a drug that extends life and expands consciousness and is essential for interstellar navigation. When the Atreides are betrayed by their enemies, the Harkonnens, Paul flees into the desert with his mother Jessica, a Bene Gesserit, and joins the native Fremen. Rising as the messiah Muad'Dib, Paul rides the giant sandworms, masters the spice, and launches a jihad that overturns the Imperium.

Themes

The novel explores ecology, politics, religion, and the danger of charismatic heroes. Herbert drew on research into desert ecology and Oregon sand dunes, and on his interest in messianic movements and resource politics — the spice has often been read as an analogue for oil.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted by David Lynch as the 1984 film Dune, as a 2000 television miniseries, and by Denis Villeneuve in the films Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024), which together were major commercial and critical successes.
