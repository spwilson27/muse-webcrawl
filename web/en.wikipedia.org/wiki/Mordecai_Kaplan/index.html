Mordecai Kaplan

Mordecai Menahem Kaplan (June 11, 1881 – November 8, 1983) was a Lithuanian-born American rabbi, essayist and Jewish educator and the co-founder of Reconstructionist Judaism along with his son-in-law Ira Eisenstein.

Life

Kaplan was born in Švenčionys, Lithuania, and emigrated to the United States with his family in 1889. He studied at City College of New York, Columbia University and the Jewish Theological Seminary of America, where he was ordained in 1902 and taught for over fifty years. In 1917 he founded the Society for the Advancement of Judaism, and in 1922 he helped establish the first Jewish community centre housed in a synagogue.

His daughter Judith Kaplan's 1922 bat mitzvah — the first in America — became a landmark event in Jewish religious life.

Thought

In his major work Judaism as a Civilization (1934), Kaplan argued that Judaism should be understood not only as a religion but as an evolving religious civilization, encompassing language, culture, art, ethics and peoplehood. He rejected supernaturalism, conceiving of God naturalistically as "the Power that makes for salvation" — the sum of processes that help human beings achieve fulfilment. He also rejected the doctrine of the Jews as a chosen people, a position that led to his excommunication (herem) by Orthodox rabbis in 1945, when his Sabbath Prayer Book was ceremonially burned.

Legacy

Kaplan's ideas gave rise to Reconstructionist Judaism, formally organised with the founding of the Reconstructionist Rabbinical College in 1968, and influenced all the non-Orthodox movements, including the synagogue-centre model, the havurah movement, and egalitarian prayer.
