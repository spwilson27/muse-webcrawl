Anthony Kennedy

Anthony McLeod Kennedy (born July 23, 1936) is an American lawyer and jurist who served as an associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1988 until his retirement in 2018. Nominated by President Ronald Reagan, he was often the Court's swing vote and authored landmark opinions on gay rights, including Obergefell v. Hodges (2015).

Early life and career

Kennedy graduated from Stanford and Harvard Law School, practised in Sacramento, and taught constitutional law. President Gerald Ford appointed him to the Ninth Circuit in 1975. After the failed nominations of Robert Bork and Douglas Ginsburg, Reagan nominated Kennedy, confirmed 97–0.

Jurisprudence

A conservative with libertarian leanings on social issues, Kennedy co-authored the plurality in Planned Parenthood v. Casey (1992), and wrote the majority opinions in Romer v. Evans, Lawrence v. Texas, United States v. Windsor and Obergefell, expanding LGBT rights. He also joined majorities on campaign finance (Citizens United) and gun rights, while favouring limits on the death penalty for juveniles and people with intellectual disabilities.

Retirement

He retired July 31, 2018, and remains a senior figure in American law, receiving the Presidential Medal of Freedom's counterpart honours in legal circles and publishing a memoir in 2025.
