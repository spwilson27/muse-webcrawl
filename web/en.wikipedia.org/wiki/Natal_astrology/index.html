Natal astrology

Branch ofAstrology
Core documentNatal (birth) chart
InputsDate, time and place of birth

Natal astrology is a branch of astrology concerned with the individual, based on the construction of a natal chart (also called a birth chart or nativity): a map of the positions of the Sun, Moon and planets at the exact moment and place of a person's birth. Astrologers interpret the chart as a symbolic picture of the person's character, talents, challenges and life themes.

The natal chart

The chart is drawn as a wheel divided into twelve signs of the zodiac and twelve houses, with the ascendant (rising sign) determined by the birth time. Interpretation weighs the sign and house placement of each planet, the angular relationships between them (aspects such as conjunctions, trines and squares), and the overall distribution of elements. Popular "sun sign" astrology, based only on the Sun's sign, is a greatly simplified offshoot of this tradition. Transits and progressions — comparing the moving planets to the natal positions — are used in predictive work.

History and criticism

Natal astrology developed in the Hellenistic world, drawing on Babylonian and Egyptian antecedents, and was systematised in works such as Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos. It was practised for royalty and commoners alike through the medieval and early modern periods, and revived in psychologised form in the twentieth century, when figures such as Dane Rudhyar and Liz Greene recast it as a tool of self-understanding rather than fate. Mainstream science regards astrology as a pseudoscience: controlled studies, including the well-known 1985 double-blind test published in Nature, have found natal-chart interpretation to perform no better than chance.
