Wrocław (German: Breslau) is a city in southwestern Poland and the capital of the Lower Silesian Voivodeship. It is the fourth-largest city in Poland and stands on the banks of the Oder River, spread across twelve islands connected by more than a hundred bridges, which has earned it the nickname "the Venice of Poland".

History

A settlement existed at the site by the 10th century; tradition attributes the founding of the bishopric in the year 1000, when the Congress of Gniezno established a diocese at Wrocław under the early Polish state. The city received municipal rights in the 13th century and prospered as a trade centre on routes linking Bohemia, the Baltic, and eastern Europe. It passed from Polish Piast dukes to the Bohemian crown, then to the Habsburgs, and in 1741 to Prussia under Frederick the Great, becoming the German city of Breslau — one of the largest cities of the German Empire.

In 1945 Breslau was declared a fortress (Festung Breslau) and held out against the Red Army until 6 May 1945, days before the end of the Second World War in Europe; much of the city was destroyed. Under the Potsdam Agreement the city became Polish, its German population was expelled westward, and Poles — many displaced from Lwów and other eastern territories ceded to the Soviet Union — resettled it, bringing institutions such as the Ossolineum library and the Lwów polytechnic tradition.

Sights and culture

The Old Town centres on the Market Square (Rynek), one of the largest in Europe, ringed by colourful burgher houses and the Gothic Old Town Hall. Across the river, Ostrów Tumski (Cathedral Island) holds the twin-spired Cathedral of St John the Baptist and is the city's oldest district. The Centennial Hall (1913), a pioneering reinforced-concrete structure by Max Berg, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Since 2005, hundreds of small bronze dwarf statues (krasnale) have been placed around the city as a playful symbol with roots in the anti-communist Orange Alternative movement. Wrocław was a European Capital of Culture in 2016 and hosted matches of UEFA Euro 2012.

Economy and education

Modern Wrocław is a centre for information technology, business services, and manufacturing, and home to the University of Wrocław, Wrocław University of Science and Technology, and numerous other institutions, with a large student population.
